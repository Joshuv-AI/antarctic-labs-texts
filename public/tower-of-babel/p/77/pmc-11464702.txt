Spatio-temporal evolution of social-ecological system resilience in ethnic tourism destinations in mountainous areas and trend prediction: a case study in Wuling, China
Authors: Ningling Yin, Jinyou Zuo, Manhong Yang, Jing Yang, Shuiliang Liu, Jilin Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractMountainous ethnic tourism lands are important social-ecological system types. With tourism as the main disturbance factor, the theory of social-ecological system resilience provides a new way to realize the sustainable development of ethno-tourism in mountainous areas. This study divides the social-ecological system into social, economic, and ecological subsystems. It constructs an evaluation index system to assess the resilience of ethnic tourism destinations in mountainous areas, considering vulnerability and adaptability. We investigate 64 counties in the Wuling Mountain area and use set-pair analysis to assess the resilience index of the social-ecological system from 2000 to 2020 and reveal the temporal and spatial characteristics. Obstacle degree models and a genetic algorithm-back propagation neural network are utilized to determine the influencing factors and predict future development trends. The following results were obtained: (1) Temporally, the resilience index shows a steady upward trend, reaching a moderate level. The resilience of the social subsystem fluctuates and rises; the economic subsystem exhibits slow, fast, and slow growth rates with occasional abrupt changes; and the ecological subsystem demonstrates a stable, slightly increasing trend. (2) Spatially, the resilience index is high at the edges and low in the central area, exhibiting a concave distribution. Most counties have moderate or higher resilience. The social and ecological subsystems have low resilience in the south and high resilience in the north. The resilience of the economic subsystem is high at the edges and low in the central area. (3) On the distribution of major obstacle factors, the first two are similar at the county level, and the last three are significantly different. The similarity of the barrier factors is related to the degree of regional proximity of the county, and overall, the similarity is decreasing from north to south and from west to east in the distribution pattern within the area. and to a certain extent, it is affected by terrain and geomorphology. (4) The spatial distribution of the resilience index is similar in 2025 and 2030. The index decreases slightly and then increases annually, with a lower growth rate in the south than in the north. Lower values occur in the northern and southwestern parts, whereas higher values are observed around high-value areas. The region as a whole will develop in a coordinated and integrated manner in the future.Keywords: Social-ecological system, Resilience, Spatio-temporal evolution, Obstacle factors, Trend prediction, Wuling Mountain areaSubject terms: Ecology, Environmental social sciences

Introduction

The social-ecological system is a complex adaptive system involving interactions and influences between social, economic, and natural systems. Due to the subsystems’ holistic, balanced, and coordinated nature, they exhibit uncertainty, threshold effects, self-organization, nonlinearity, and adaptability1–3. A change in one element can trigger chain reactions in others, leading to abrupt shifts in the system’s state4. Resilience is a crucial attribute of social-ecological systems5,6.

Gunderson and Holling (2002) applied the concept of resilience to social-ecological systems. They defined resilience as the system’s capacity to withstand disturbances and maintain its fundamental functions and characteristics, offering a new perspective for studying sustainable development7. In 2009, Ostrom proposed a framework model for the sustainable development of social-ecological systems, encompassing all resources involved in the interaction between human society and ecosystems8. This model represents a multidimensional research system integrating social, economic, and ecological interactions9. Subsequently, scholars used this model to conduct extensive research on the resilience of social-ecological systems10–13, laying a solid theoretical foundation.

There is a high degree of spatial overlap between the poor mountainous areas of the world and the distribution of tourism resources14. The tourism industry, which provides significant economic benefits, has significantly boosted the economy and regional development in underdeveloped ethnic mountainous areas in China in recent years. Ethnic tourism in mountainous destinations represents a complex social-ecological system influenced by multiple factors. Tourism development can have multiple impacts on the social-ecological systems in tourism destinations16. On the one hand, tourism is a sunrise industry and green industry that promotes economic prosperity and increases residents’ income in underdeveloped areas. However, unregulated tourism development can negatively influence on the regional social and economic systems and cause environmental damage. Thus, research on improving the relationship between socio-economic development and environmental protection to ensure the sustainable development of social-ecological systems in tourism destinations is crucial. Against the backdrop of ecological civilization construction, research on resilience in social-ecological systems provides a new perspective to achieve sustainable development and coordinated human-environment relationships in tourism destinations4,17.

The study of resilience of social-ecological systems in tourist destinations is mainly associated with vulnerability, adaptability, and sustainability18,19, including the connotation of the concept of resilience21, resilience measurement and evaluation21,22, resilience development paths24, and resilience evolution mechanisms24. Due to the complexity, dynamics, and multi-scale nature of the social-ecological system of tourist destinations, resilience research is rich in perspectives, diverse in methodologies, large in scale, and characterized by significant interdisciplinary and disciplinary integration. Currently, most studies focus on the core attributes of social-ecological systems, constructing analytical frameworks for the resilience of tourist destination social-ecological systems from the “social-economic-ecological” dimensions15 or using actor perception indicators26 combined with comprehensive index methods26, variable fuzzy recognition models15, the set pair analysis(SPA)27,28, and other methods for quantitative measurement of resilience27. Among these, SPA, as an analytical way to deal with uncertainty, can convert uncertainty in social-ecological systems into specific mathematical problems and analyze and study its internal relationship from the whole and local, which is widely used in the fields of comprehensive evaluation30, risk assessment30, and vulnerability evaluation21. Methods such as barrier degree modelling26 and regression analysis31 have been used to identify the key influencing factors of resilience to formulate corresponding countermeasure proposals to seek practical paths to enhance the resilience of social-ecological systems in tourist destinations. Studies have shown that tourism economic benefits, financial support, the proportion of the tertiary industry, and ecological environmental conditions significantly impact the resilience of tourist destination social-ecological systems32–35. With the depth of research, scholars gradually focus on the analysis of the spatio-temporal evolution of resilience, analyze the spatio-temporal trend of resilience of social, economic and ecological subsystems in depth36–38, and explore the spatio-temporal characteristics of the coordination level and the process of multivariate interactions among subsystems under the coupling perspective40. However, related studies less reveal the spatio-temporal interactions among the subsystems, and the evolution of social-ecological system resilience in tourist destinations is characterized by obvious geographic variability and spatial heterogeneity, so the intrinsic mechanisms and development paths of social-ecological system resilience evolution in tourist destinations still need to be explored. In addition, a few scholars have used single models to predict the future development level of regional social-ecological resilience, thereby inferring its development trends. Methods like the grey prediction model31 and BP neural network30 are often used, but the prediction results are prone to falling into local optima40.

Research on social-ecological system resilience in tourism destinations has focused on large, medium, and small scales, from tourism villages41 and coastal areas42 to lake basins43. However, few studies investigated concentrated and contiguous tourism destinations. The contradiction between socio-economic construction, ecological protection in the Wuling Mountain area, and large-scale tourism development has increasingly become obvious. Environmental damage has occurred, impacting the region’s economic development44 and adversely affecting the sustainability of social-ecological systems. Against the backdrop of ecological civilization construction, research on balancing environmental protection and the socio-economic development of mountainous ethnic tourism destinations is critical. This study analyzes 64 counties in the Wuling Mountain area and employs entropy weighting and SPA to quantify the resilience level of the social-ecological system from 2000 to 2020. Subsequently, a geographic information system (GIS) spatial analysis and obstacle degree models are used to analyze the spatio-temporal evolution characteristics and obstacle factors of the social-ecological system’s resilience. GA-BP neural networks are applied to predict the future trend of social-ecological system resilience. The results provide theoretical and practical references for exploring the sustainable development of social-ecological system resilience and harmonizing the human-environment relationships of ethnic tourism in mountainous destinations.

Materials and methods

Study area

The Wuling Mountain area is located at the junction of Hubei, Hunan, Guizhou, and Chongqing, covering an area of 171,800 square kilometres, including 71 counties (cities and districts). This area is predominantly inhabited by the Tujia, Miao, and Dong ethnic minorities, forming a diverse cultural blend. Most of the Wuling Mountain area is a national-level ecological functional area in national land spatial planning, with many mountains and rivers, rich species resources and well-developed karst landscapes, making it an important green ecological barrier in China. However, the region has complex terrain, sensitive ecosystems, weak infrastructure, and relatively low economic development.

The regional infrastructure improved after implementing the Western Development, Precision Poverty Alleviation and Rural Revitalization Strategies, creating favourable conditions for tourism development. “The Wuling Mountain Area Regional Development and Poverty Alleviation Plan (2011–2020)” was designed to utilize the unique mountainous area and the ecological and ethnic cultural tourism resources to transform and improve the tourism industry. The plan aimd to advance inter-provincial ecological and cultural tourism cooperation and attract domestic and international tourists. The region was visited by 655 million tourists in 2019, generating tourism revenue of 515.588 billion yuan and accounting for 46.57% of the regional GDP. In December 2023, the area had two global geological parks, 11 national geological parks, 32 national forest parks, and 345 tourist spots rated A and above.

The tourism industry in the Wuling Mountain area evolved from its infancy in 1978 to a pillar industry in the region today. Though tourism development has improved social-economic development and well-being in the area, it has increased the pressure on social-ecological system. Municipal districts have significant superiority in these three subsystems, namely, society, economy, and ecology, which were excluded in this study to ensure consistency in evaluating resilience at different times. We used 5 county-level cities, 42 counties, and 17 autonomous counties, totalling 64 county-level administrative units in the Wuling Mountain area (Fig. 1).

Methods

Construction of ecological security evaluation model

Due to regional differences in the systems, a widely applicable measurement system for social-ecological system resilience has yet to be established. In theory, the resilience of social, economic, ecological, and governance subsystems can be evaluated, but the resilience of the governance system is often determined subjectively or acts indirectly on resilience by influencing other factors44,46. Resilience reflects a system’s ability to self-repair and adapt after disturbances and considers vulnerability and adaptability6,47. Vulnerability refers to the sensitivity of a system’s structure and functions to internal and external disturbances48. Adaptability reflects the system’s ability to withstand disturbances and the resilience potential49 Therefore, an indicator system was established to assess the vulnerability and adaptability of the social, economic, and ecological subsystems50–52.

The social-ecological system is a complex adaptive system with closely interconnected human and natural elements of ethnic tourism in mountainous destinations14,53. Internal social, economic, and ecological factors and external environmental interactions influence them20,54. The social subsystem supports the social-ecological system of tourism destinations28. A favourable social environment is necessary for tourism development55. hence, factors related to social stability and the resident’s quality of life were chosen in the social subsystem54,56–58. The proportion of ethnic minorities was used because nine ethnic minorities, including the Tujia, Miao, and Dong, live in the Wuling Mountain area. Ethnic minorities account for about 1/8 of the population, reflecting the region’s cultural heritage and ethnic characteristics57. The economic subsystem is the core driver of the social-ecological system of tourism destinations20, Since tourism is an important industry driving economic growth in the Wuling Mountain area59, we included the number and quality of the tourism operations, industry structure, regional economic input, and local government finances as influencing factors36,37,60. The ecological subsystem is crucial in the social-ecological system of tourism destinations because it affects social and economic development61. Most counties in the Wuling Mountain area are mountainous with high soil erosion, rocky desertification and scarce per capita arable land62. Moreover, they are nationally important ecological function areas, assuming ecological functions. Thus, the ecological conditions and environmental conservation levels must be considered63–65. The A-class tourist attractions were indicators because they characterize resource development, infrastructure development, service management, and tourism marketing levels, representing regional tourism attractiveness66,67. We considered the literature and the current development of the Wuling Mountain area and used a science-based and systematic approach to select 30 indicators to establish the evaluation indicator system for social-ecological system resilience in the Wuling Mountain area (Table 1).

Data descriptions

The data were obtained from the Statistical Yearbook at the provincial, municipal and district levels, the Statistical Bulletin of National Economic and Social Development, the National Bureau of Statistics (https://www.stats.gov.cn/), China Economic and Social Development Statistics Database (https://data.cnki.net/YearData/Analysis), China Economic Network Statistics Database (https://db.cei.cn), and the EPS Global Statistical Data Analysis Platform (https://olap.epsnet.com.cn). Additionally, information on tourist attractions was sourced from the official websites of provincial cultural and tourism departments. Linear interpolation was applied to supplement the dataset for areas with incomplete statistical data68–70. The data were standardized to allow for comparisons26.

GA-BP neural network prediction model

We used a GA-BP neural network model to predict the resilience of the social-ecological system in the Wuling Mountain area. This model is an efficient and intelligent global optimization algorithm that optimizes the parameters based on the survival of the fittest and natural selection40,75. The computational steps of the GA-BP neural network in the social-ecological system resilience assessment model are as follows76,77:

Step 1: Preprocess the input data, determine the number of nodes in the input, hidden, and output layers of the BP neural network, and initialize weights and thresholds.

Step 2: Optimize the weights and thresholds using the GA algorithm. Calculate the fitness function and perform selection, crossover, and mutation operations. Output the result when the termination condition is met for the fitness of the parent individuals or when the maximum fitness is obtained after a set number of generations.

Step 3: Use the optimal weights and thresholds obtained from the GA algorithm in the BP neural network. Train the neural network and output the final result.

Results

Temporal evolution of social-ecological system resilience

1) Temporal evolution of social subsystem resilience

The resilience of the social subsystem showed a steady upward trend, influenced positively by national policies favouring underdeveloped western regions and the continuous improvements in the region’s social systems. Tourism development stimulated local infrastructure and service facilities, increasing the resilience index from 0.4043 in 2000 to 0.7904 in 2020. Significant growth occurred from 2000 to 2015 due to substantial infrastructure improvements and public utility construction, with an average annual growth rate of 2.27%. However, fluctuations were observed after 2015, and the average annual growth rate slowed to 0.93%, indicating a shift from fast to moderate growth (Fig. 2a).

Levels of resilience and mean resilience of the system and subsystems in the Wuling Mountain area from 2000 to 2020. (a) Social subsystem resilience. (b) Economic subsystem resilience. (c) Ecological subsystem resilience. (d) Social-ecological system resilience.

2) Temporal evolution of economic subsystem resilience

The resilience index of the economic subsystem increased from 0.1588 in 2000 to 0.9163 in 2020, with an average annual growth rate of 3.79%. The resilience of the economic subsystem grew slowly from 2000 to 2005, remaining at low and moderate levels. Rapid growth occurred from 2005 to 2015 due to economic and tourism development. However, economic challenges, especially those posed by the pandemic, led to a slowdown from 2015 to 2020, resulting in a decline to a moderate resilience level (Fig. 2b).

3) Temporal evolution of ecological subsystem resilience

The resilience of the ecological subsystem experienced a slight growth, maintaining a stable development trend. The resilience index increased from 0.2587 in 2000 to 0.6101 in 2020, with an annual growth rate of 1.76%. National ecological projects implemented before and around 2000, notably the “Grain for Green” project, increased regional vegetation cover and mitigated soil erosion and desertification. Accelerated social and economic development after 2011 caused tourism to become the dominant industry, shifting from sightseeing to experiential tourism, impacting ecosystems. These changes slowed the growth rate of ecological subsystem resilience (Fig. 2c).

4) Temporal evolution of social-ecological system resilience

The system resilience increased as the rapid growth of the social subsystem resilience, the moderate growth of the economic subsystem resilience, and the slow growth of the ecological subsystem resilience influenced each other. The resilience index increased from 0.2746 in 2000 to 0.7973 in 2020, with an average annual growth rate of approximately 2.61%, second only to the social subsystem. This interdependence and influence among the three subsystems, coupled With disturbances from tourism and Other human activities, enhanced economic and social resilience While diminishing ecological resilience. As a result, the increase in the social-ecological system resilience was limited (Fig. 2d).

Spatial evolution of social-ecological resilience

1) Spatial evolution of social-ecological resilience

The resilience of the social subsystem at different times was moderate, with a fluctuating upward trend. The resilience index was low in the south and high in the north. The number of low-resilience counties increased and decreased. Most were located in the border regions of the Guizhou, Hunan, and Hubei provinces. Regions with moderate and relatively high resilience occurred in a patchy distribution, with connections across provinces. High-value regions transitioned from a scattered distribution to a concentrated distribution, and the resilience index in Hubei, Chongqing, and Hunan provinces, excluding Guizhou. Increased from 5 in 2000 to 11 in 2020(Fig. 3). A noticeable growth in social resilience occurred from 2000 to 2015, with only a few counties experiencing fluctuations or declines. The transition direction shows that 57.18% of counties maintained or increased their resilience levels, whereas 42.72% experienced a decline. The growth rate slowed from 2015 to 2020, and significant spatial changes occurred, with decreases in resilience in the western part of Hunan and the southern part of Hubei. The transition directions included five categories of increased resilience and two categories of decreased resilience, accounting for 45.31% and 6.25%, respectively. This result suggests a localized weakening of social resilience. However, the general trend showed an overall increase and a predominant shift towards higher levels of resilience (Fig. 4a). In summary, the significant increase in the social subsystem resilience is primarily attributed to national policies supporting the western and impoverished mountainous areas. Provinces and cities increased education investments, healthcare, and infrastructure, ensuring robust public construction, rapid urbanization, and relatively high social resilience. However, their ability to resist external influences differed due to different initial development stages among different cities. Tourism development, an increase in consumer goods, rising incomes, and a surge in tourists disturbed the system’s stability, causing decreases in resilience in Jiangkou, Hefeng, Zhijiang Dong Autonomous County, Jingzhou Miao-Dong Autonomous County, Baojing, and Guzhang.

2) Resilience of the economic subsystem

The resilience of the economic subsystem is strongly spatially correlated with that of the total system and progresses in a relatively consistent manner, with high values at the edges and low values in the central area. Economic resilience was predominantly low and relatively low in 2000, with relatively high and high resilience counties showing clustered distributions in Guizhou and the eastern part of Hubei and Fenghuang County, Hunan. Economic resilience among counties showed a more synchronized trend by 2005, with minimal spatial changes. Slight declines in resilience occurred in some Guizhou counties, whereas significant increases were observed in Enshi City (Hubei), Fengdu County (Chongqing), Fenghuang County, and Lianyuan City (Hunan), exhibiting a scattered distribution. The regions around the core area with high economic resilience exhibited increases in resilience in 2010, whereas other counties experienced steady growth, and significant differences in the growth rate were observed. After implementing the “Regional Development and Poverty Alleviation Plan for Wuling Mountain area (2011–2020),” provinces and cities accelerated the development of the tourism industry and diversified the industrial structure, significantly raising the economic level and substantially increasing the per capita GDP. As a result, economic resilience increased from low to moderate or high (Fig. 5). Regarding the transition direction, 78.12% of counties maintained or increased their resilience levels, whereas 21.88% experienced a decline, resulting in a rapid increase in economic resilience with low disparities (Fig. 4b). The economic resilience pattern was similar to the economic development pattern. Economic resilience was substantially higher in prefecture-level cities and resource-rich counties than in resource-poor and non-urban cluster areas. In particular, Xiangxi Tujia, the Miao Autonomous Prefecture, and most parts of Huaihua City, which have struggled due to their low economic levels, maintained low or moderate economic resilience. Their industry structure heavily relies on the GDP level and government capital investment, resulting in less pronounced improvements in economic resilience.

3) Resilience of the ecological subsystem

The resilience of the ecological subsystem exhibited a continuous upward trend from 2000 to 2020, with a faster trend in the north than in the south. The dominant resilience levels were low, relatively low, moderate, and relatively high. The number of counties with low resilience increased and decreased. These counties were located at the junction of Hunan, Hubei, and Chongqing. The spatial distribution evolved from a clustered pattern to a belt and block pattern. Counties with relatively low resilience were concentrated in the south of Hunan. Eventually, these areas changed to include most counties in southern Hubei and Guizhou Province. The number of counties decreased slightly and scattered, and block distributions occurred. The number of counties with moderate resilience significantly increased from 14 in 2000 to 19 in 2020, displaying a scattered pattern. The number of regions with relatively high and high resilience declined slightly, with minimal spatial changes. These areas were located at the provincial boundaries, exhibiting a pattern of edge-connected patches (Fig. 6). The period from 2000 to 2010 was crucial for implementing the “Conversion of Cropland to Forest and Grassland” project. Afforestation and grass planting areas significantly increased, increasing forested areas, optimizing landscape patterns, improving ecosystem service functions, and increasing ecological resilience. Regarding the transition direction, 81.25% of counties maintained or increased their resilience levels, while 18.75% experienced a decline. The ecological resilience levels increased rapidly from low to high. After 2010, the “Conversion of Cropland to Forest and Grassland” project entered a consolidation phase. Accompanying the optimization of the industrial structure in the Wuling Mountain area with a focus on tourism and the promotion of ecotourism. At the same time, a series of problems, such as the construction of basic service facilities and transportation networks occupying arable land areas and applying chemical fertilizers and pesticides leading to the reduction of grain yield per unit area, also impact ecological resilience. In the transfer direction, counties with unchanged or increased levels increased by 3.12%, but the overall improvement of ecological resilience in the Wuling Mountain area was not substantial (Fig. 4c).

4) Resilience of the social-ecological system

The resilience of the social-ecological system increased over time. The number of low-resilience counties increased and decreased, with a slight overall increase. Counties with low resilience spread from the central part of Hunan Province to the western part of Guizhou Province and the central part of Hubei Province, showing a scattered or block distribution. The number of counties with relatively low resilience decreased significantly and then increased slightly, with a negligible change in spatial pattern. They were concentrated in the western part of Guizhou Province, the northern part of Hubei Province, and the southwestern part of Hunan Province, but not in Chongqing. Counties with moderate resilience increased rapidly, expanding from the junctions and border areas of provinces and cities to the central region. The number of counties with relatively high resilience remained relatively stable, and that with high resilience slightly decreased. These areas were clustered in blocks around Chongqing and the bordering counties in other provinces and cities. The resilience of the region increased, and most counties had moderate or higher resilience, exhibiting a spatial pattern of high resilience at the edge and low resilience in the central area (Fig. 7). The ranking of the subsystems based on their resilience from 2000 to 2005 was social resilience > economic resilience > ecological resilience. Regarding the transition direction, the proportion of counties with an increase or decrease in resilience was 15.62% and 42.18%, respectively. The ranking of the subsystems based on their resilience from 2005 to 2010 was economic resilience > social resilience > ecological resilience. The proportion of counties with an increase in resilience was 29.69%. The ranking of the subsystems based on their resilience from 2010 to 2020 was ecological resilience > economic resilience > social resilience. The proportion of counties with decreased resilience was only 17.18% (Fig. 4d). These results indicate that the system’s resilience was significantly influenced by the social and economic subsystems, especially the latter. Specifically, the resilience trends were similar for the social-ecological system and the economic subsystem.

The second-largest obstacle factor is similar to the primary one and is X20 or X27 for most counties. However, X13 was the second-largest obstacle factor for some counties. In contrast, heterogeneity occurs in the last three obstacle factors at the regional county level, mainly X13, X5, and X18 in that order, but X2 (the proportion of ethnic minorities in the total population), X3 (the ratio of tourists to local inhabitants), and X17 (GDP) also randomly appeared in counties. Discussion

The resilience of centralized and contiguous tourism destinations’ social-ecological systems has received little attention from academics, and there are still many unanswered questions regarding the evolutionary process of resilience in ethnic tourism destinations located in mountainous areas. Based on long-term dynamic data reveals the temporal changes, evolution characteristics, and spatial heterogeneity of the social-ecological system resilience in the Wuling Mountain area of China from 2000 to 2020. The main contributions of the paper are as follows: First, a comprehensive evaluation index system of social-ecological system resilience of ethnic tourism in mountainous destinations is constructed from three subsystems of society, economy, and ecology, and two dimensions of vulnerability and adaptive capacity, which is helpful to enrich the connotation of the study of social-ecological system resilience. Second, by examining the evolution process and spatial heterogeneity of regional resilience, the study takes into account both time and space dimensions. This can offer a more complex dynamic explanation for researching the resilience of social-ecological systems. Furthermore, the study forecasts the future development trend of social-ecological system resilience in the Wuling Mountain area by building a prediction model using an enhanced GA-BP neural network. This provides theoretical direction and empirical support for regional sustainable development.

Spatial and temporal evolution of social-ecological system resilience

The study’s findings demonstrate how large-scale tourism has a substantial impact on local ecological, social, and economic systems, which promotes the dynamic evolution of regional resilience. Although the starting state was different, the resilience of the Wuling Mountain area varied in growth across the research period, with the overall resilience exhibiting an increasing tendency. Among them, the economic subsystem had the greatest impact on the social-ecological system resilience in the Wuling Mountain area, followed by the social subsystem, and the ecological subsystem was the smallest, and there was a synergy between the economic resilience and the social-ecological system resilience change trend, which further confirmed the important role of the economic factors on the social-ecological system resilience28,80,81. Furthermore, the Wuling Mountain area’s social-ecological system resilience was clearly clustered spatially, with low resilience in the center counties and high resilience in the peripheral counties. Additionally, the resilience was clearly inhibited by administrative boundaries and underdeveloped areas, indicating that regional resilience generally adhered to the principle of economic dominance.

Factors hindering the resilience of social-ecological system

As a complex social-ecological system, the three major subsystems of society, economy, and ecology are adapted to each other and influence each other, contributing to the trend of resilience change. Tourism resources and service quality are the greatest obstacles to the resilience of regional social-ecological systems57, and the amount of social investment also significantly affects resilience15. Ethnic tourism in mountainous destinations is mostly in underdeveloped regions with loose internal economic development and insufficient external investment. Although there are rich natural and cultural resources, the quality of tourism services cannot match the high-quality tourism resources, and the lack of economic power seriously restricts the development of social-ecological system resilience81. The ecological environment is the most universal well-being of people’s livelihoods and the basis of tourism development. However, tourism will cause interference and damage to the ecological environment while promoting regional economic development, so it is necessary to coordinate the relationship between economic development and ecological protection. The proportion of the ethnic minority population highlights the uniqueness and typicality of the Wuling Mountain area as an ethnic and cultural tourist destination. This factor is fundamental to social stability and economic prosperity and, therefore, a crucial component of the resilience of ethnic tourism destinations. Overall, promoting coordinated symbiosis among the three major subsystems of society-economy-ecology is the fundamental way to promote the resilience of the social-ecological system31.

Evolution of social-ecological system resilience trends

Scholars have concentrated their attention on research concerning the evolution prediction and future trend of social-ecological systems. Future scenario simulation using current data can offer a crucial scientific foundation for regional planning and decision-making29. As a result, there is still a great deal of unexplored territory in this field of prediction methods and models, which are rich and varied and have not yet come to terms with one another. The improved GA-BP neural network model has a strong nonlinear modeling ability and high stability, can handle a large amount of data, and has higher prediction accuracy76. This study utilizes cross-regional large-sample, long-time series data to continuously analyze the social-ecological system resilience at the city scale, avoiding the static evaluation of single-year indicator data and, at the same time, adopting a combined prediction model to improve the accuracy of the prediction data. The results show that tourism is an environmentally sensitive industry with instability and vulnerability82, such as the outbreak of the new crown epidemic, economic crises, social unrest, and other series of factors will cause unpredictable impacts on the tourism industry, thus affecting the sustainability of the entire social-ecological system of the tourist destination83. Tourism development positively affects the social, economic, and ecological resilience of cities, and cities that are consistently highly resilient all have the commonalities of a good economic base and superior tourism resources81. Generally, the social-ecological system resilience difference between the counties in the Wuling Mountain area is gradually narrowing, showing a balanced state development trend. However, over-dependence on tourism will adversely affect regional economic development and lead the region into a single “industrial structure trap"70. Therefore, it is necessary to increase the investment of social funds to promote the development of social-ecological systems in tourist areas and improve the system’s overall resilience.

Limitations

Although the overall uniqueness of the area is taken into account, the indicator system ignores the heterogeneity of different cities due to differences in their resource endowments and location conditions, which will cause uncertainty in the outcome factors. The indicators in this study are constructed based on the general characteristics of social-ecological systems. Furthermore, the study area of Wuling Mountain is the only one in the nation lacking a horizontal comparison with other impoverished areas, and the spatial and temporal disparities among the regions have a significant impact on enhancing the resilience of the local social-ecological system. On the basis of long-term series scale research, we should enhance and add to the social-ecological system resilience assessment index system. In the future, we can construct several groups of models for prediction and comparison and search for the best model with the best fitting effect in order to make the results of the study more realistic and practical.

Conclusions and recommendations

Conclusion

This study examines the features of the spatiotemporal evolution of social-ecological system resilience, the influencing factors, and the future development trend of social-ecological system resilience based on panel data from 64 counties in the Wuling Mountain area from 2000 to 2020. The main conclusions are as follows: (1) Regarding temporal changes, from 2000 to 2020, the index of social-ecological system resilience in the Wuling Mountain area will gradually increase, and most counties will be in the middle of resilience and above. (2) Regarding spatial changes, the social-ecological system resilience of the Wuling Mountain Area from 2000 to 2020 shows a spatial pattern of “high in the peripheral area, low in the center area, and concave distribution.” The social and economic subsystems greatly influence resilience. (3) Economic factors are the main obstacles to the resilience of social-ecological systems in the Wuling Mountain area, and the similarity of the obstacles is related to the proximity of counties. (4) The prediction results show that the social-ecological system resilience of the Wuling Mountain area will gradually increase and develop towards a coordinated and integrated state in the next ten years, with an overall spatial distribution pattern of “high in the north and low in the south.”

Recommendations

Based on the results of the study, the following countermeasures to improve the resilience of social-ecological systems in the Wuling Mountain area are proposed: firstly, the government should increase the investment of regional development funds and improve the supporting infrastructure to provide a good business environment and tourism foundation for the tourism development in the Wuling Mountain Area. Secondly, developers should plan tourism development economic zones, integrate isolated and scattered tourism resources, and form a strong tourism development industry chain through cooperation and innovation to promote efficient and intensive regional tourism development. Finally, the cultural advantages of the Wuling Mountain Area should be fully utilized, and residents should be actively encouraged to participate in tourism, pass on and innovate the national culture, and enhance the attractiveness and core competitiveness of the tourism industry.

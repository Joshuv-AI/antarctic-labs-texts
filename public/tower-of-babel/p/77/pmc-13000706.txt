Sustainable and multifunctional metal-ion coordinated spent coffee grounds films for food preservation
Authors: Shaokai Zhang, Huan Liu, Lin Zhu, Ruihong Xie, Jiameng Liu, Hui Wang, Yijun Liu, Lijing Lin, Zhanwu Sheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study developed a simple approach for fabricating multifunctional food packaging films from spent coffee grounds (SCG) through ionic liquid dissolution and subsequent regeneration in metal ion solutions. Metal-ion coordination with the hydroxyl groups of the polysaccharide matrix serves as the central regulatory strategy, disrupting the native hydrogen-bond network and forming coordination crosslinks that govern the film structure and properties. This structural modulation simultaneously enhances water resistance, including surface hydrophobicity, vapor barrier enhancement and retention of wet strength (tensile strength above 14.84 MPa), and introduces ion-specific functionalities such as UV-blocking, antibacterial activity, and flame retardancy. All metal-ion coordinated films completely biodegrade in natural soil within 40 days. In strawberry preservation trials, the metal-ion coordinated film effectively reduced decay, weight loss, and softening, thereby extending shelf life. This work demonstrates a feasible route for upcycling food waste into tunable, high-performance materials with potential as sustainable alternatives to non-biodegradable packaging plastics.Keywords: Spent coffee grounds, Metal-ion coordination, Water resistant, Antibacterial, Sustainable packaging film, Food preservation

Introduction

The extensive use of petroleum-based plastic films in food packaging has raised significant environmental concerns due to their poor biodegradability and low recycling rates (Karimi Sani et al., 2023). In this context, biomass-derived active packaging materials, particularly environmentally friendly polymers synthesized from renewable resources such as lignocellulosic biomass, starch, chitosan, alginate and proteins, have emerged as promising candidates in green chemistry, offering superior performance and broad application potential (Dang, Cai, & Wang, 2025; Dang, Han, Du, et al., 2025; Dang, Han, & Wang, 2025).

Spent coffee grounds (SCG), a lignocellulosic byproduct generated by the coffee industry, represent an underutilized biomass resource available in large quantities at very low cost. Global coffee consumption is projected to increase by 2.2% in 2023/24, reaching 177.0 million 60-kg bags (Melikoglu, 2025). It is estimated that approximately 650 kg of SCG are generated per ton of green coffee beans processed. Currently, most SCG ends up in landfills, leading to spontaneous combustion, CO2 emissions, and toxic substance release (Ballesteros et al., 2017). Chemically, SCG consists mainly of polysaccharides (hemicellulose and cellulose, 45–60%), along with lignin (15–25%) and protein (10–15%) (Nzekoue et al., 2020). The valorization of SCG has emerged as a promising strategy across multiple sectors, particularly for biodegradable film production. Recent studies have pursued three main approaches: (i) blending SCG with synthetic biodegradable polymers, as exemplified by melt-extruded and injection-molded SCG/PLA composites (Baek et al., 2013); (ii) incorporating SCG into natural polymer matrices, such as pectin-based films fabricated by casting and UV curing (Cataldo et al., 2017); and (iii) utilizing SCG polysaccharide extracts as the primary film-forming material. The latter approach includes casting SCG polysaccharide with carboxymethyl cellulose (Ballesteros et al., 2018) and direct dissolution-regeneration of polysaccharide-rich fractions in ZnCl2 solution (Batista et al., 2020). Our previous work demonstrated that ionic liquid-dissolved regenerated SCG films exhibit favorable mechanical strength and thermal stability (Zhang, Zhong, et al., 2024). Nevertheless, the inherent hydrophilicity of polysaccharide-based SCG films limits their performance under high-humidity conditions.

Metal-ion coordination has emerged as an effective strategy to address this limitation. Multivalent metal ions form stable coordination complexes with oxygen-containing functional groups on polysaccharide chains, thereby creating crosslinks that enhance intermolecular bonding, reduce water sensitivity, and improve wet mechanical performance (Chen et al., 2022; Dong et al., 2013; Liu et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2019). However, the application of metal-ion coordination to SCG-derived films has not been systematically investigated. The role of SCG in this work is twofold: first, it serves as a renewable and abundant polysaccharide source that can be processed into film form; second, the inherent functional groups of SCG polysaccharide provide coordination sites for metal ions, enabling the simultaneous improvement of water resistance and introduction of active functionalities. This dual role positions SCG as an ideal platform for developing tunable, multifunctional packaging materials.

In this study, we developed a straightforward strategy to fabricate multifunctional films from SCG through ionic liquid dissolution and subsequent regeneration in metal ion solutions (Fe3+, Al3+, Cu2+, Zn2+ and Ca2+). This dissolution-regeneration process primarily involves disrupting and reforming the extensive intermolecular hydrogen-bond network of polysaccharides. During regeneration, metal ions form stable coordination complexes with hydroxyl groups, fundamentally altering the film structure and properties. The resulting metal-ion coordinated films were systematically characterized for their morphology, mechanical properties, water resistance, and multifunctional performance (antibacterial activity, UV-blocking capability, and flame retardancy). Finally, the practical application potential was evaluated through strawberry preservation trials, with particular focus on comparing the performance of SCG-based films with that of conventional plastics. This work provides insights into metal ion-polysaccharide coordination interactions and demonstrates a feasible pathway for upcycling food waste into high-performance, biodegradable alternatives for food packaging.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials

SCG was obtained from White Dwarf Intelligent Technology (Jiangsu, China) Co., Ltd. SCG was screened to <300 μm particle size and extracted in a Soxhlet extractor with a 1:2 ethanol/toluene (v/v) mixture for 8 h to remove extractives. The chemical composition of the raw SCG was determined as hemicellulose 39.43%, cellulose 11.92%, and lignin 23.67%. Chemical compounds, including 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium chloride (BmimCl), sodium hydroxide, sodium chlorite (80%), ferric chloride anhydrous, cupric chloride dihydrate, zinc chloride, calcium chloride anhydrous, and aluminum chloride anhydrous were acquired from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd. E. coli CMCC(B)44102, and S. aureus CMCC(B)26003 were all purchased from Guangdong Huankai Microbial Technology Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China). All reagents were of analytical grade and used without further purification.

Fabrication of the metal ion-coordinated SCG films

Firstly, the SCG was bleached to remove almost all lignin, according to our previous study (Zhang, Zhong, et al., 2024). The chemical composition of the bleached SCG was as follows: hemicellulose 55.64%, cellulose 25.93%, and lignin 0.76%. 2.0 g of bleached SCG was added to 40.0 g of BmimCl under mechanical stirring (500 rpm) at 100 °C for 4 h to obtain a homogeneous solution. BmimCl was selected as the dissolution solvent due to its high efficiency in disrupting the hydrogen-bond network of polysaccharides, its thermal stability at the operating temperature, and its relatively low viscosity. Additionally, BmimCl can be recovered from the regeneration bath and reused after purification, minimizing environmental impact (Chen et al., 2015). The melt was centrifuged (11,000 rpm, 5 min, 25 °C) to remove residual air, cast into a polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) mold, and allowed to gel at room temperature for 2 h (Zhang et al., 2023). The solidified gel with the mold was then regenerated by immersion in a 0.2 M aqueous chloride solution of Fe3+, Al3+, Cu2+, Zn2+and Ca2+. The immersion time was 12 h to ensure complete coordination. The bath was renewed every 2 h for a total of 12 h (six exchanges) to ensure complete displacement of the ionic liquid. The residual metal ion solution on the wet hydrogel surface was then rinsed with deionized water. The wet hydrogel was placed in a paper sheet drier at 45 °C for 12 h to obtain the metal-ion coordinated SCG films (SF-Mn+). The obtained samples were denoted as SF-Fe3+, SF-Al3+, SF-Cu2+, SF-Zn2+ and SF-Ca2+, respectively. A control film, SF (SCG film), was regenerated in deionized water and dried under identical conditions. All films were conditioned at 25 °C and 50% relative humidity for 48 h prior to testing.

Characterization of SF and SF-Mn+

Scanning electron microscopy

The morphology of SF-Mn+ was examined using an ultrahigh-resolution scanning electron microscope (SU8600, HITACHI, Japan). Elemental distribution in the SF-Mn+ was detected by energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS). Cross sections of the SF-Mn+ were prepared by fracturing the samples after liquid nitrogen immersion. All samples were sputter-coated with gold prior to imaging.

Mechanical properties

The mechanical properties of the films were conducted by utilizing a universal testing machine (INSTRON 3342, USA) with a 5000 N load cell at a crosshead speed of 5 mm min−1. The thickness of the films was measured using an precise thickness measurement apparatus (L&W Micrometer 251, Sweden) at five random positions for each sample, and the average value was used for stress calculations. The films were cut into rectangular shapes of 60 mm × 10 mm. At least three replicates were tested for each sample, and results were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. For wet mechanical properties, specimens were immersed in deionized water for 10 min at room temperature, gently blotted to remove surface water, and immediately tested under the same conditions.

Optical properties

The optical properties of SF-Mn+ were evaluated using a UV–vis spectrophotometer with an integrating sphere accessory (UV-2600, Shimadzu, Japan) at 200–800 nm in transmission mode (Zhang, Zhong, et al., 2024). Fourier transform infrared and X-ray diffraction spectroscopy

The Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectra of the SF-Mn+ were measured on a Nicolet 6700 ATR-FTIR spectrometer (Thermos Fisher Scientific, USA) in the scanning range of 500–4000 cm−1.

The X-ray diffraction spectra (XRD) of bleached SCG and SF-Mn+ were analyzed using an X-ray diffractometer (X'Pert3 Powder, PANalytical, UK) at 40 kV and 30 mA in reflection mode with diffraction angles from 5° to 40°.

Water resistance and water vapor transmission rate

The surface wettability of the SF-Mn+ was assessed using a contact angle measuring instrument (OCA 25, Data Physics Company, Germany). A 3.5 μL water droplet was deposited on the film surface, with the contact angle recorded using a CCD camera equipped with a macro lens. Measurements were performed in triplicate.

The water absorption ratio of the films was determined gravimetrically. Briefly, the films were immersed in deionized water for specified durations, then gentle blotted to remove surface moisture. The water absorption ratio was calculated as the percentage mass increase relative to the initial dry mass.

The water vapor transmission rate (WVTR) of the films was determined by a water vapor permeation analyzer tester (PERMATRAN-W 3/34, MOCON, USA). The test area was 5.64 cm2, and the test temperature and humidity were 23 °C and 50% RH, respectively. Results represent the mean of three replicates.

Antibacterial test

The antibacterial activity of the SF-Mn+ was examined against Escherichia coli (E. coli) and Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus) according to a previous study with slight modifications (Huang et al., 2020). E. coli and S. aureus were cultured in LB medium at 37 °C with continuous agitation at 100 rpm for 12 h. Cultures were adjusted to 1 × 106 CFU/mL with physiological saline. 200 μL of the culture suspension was spread over the prepared agar plate. The inoculum was evenly distributed and air-dried for 5 min in a biosafety cabinet. Under aseptic conditions, a sterile SF-Mn+ disc (diameter of 6 mm) was placed on the inoculated agar plates. The plates were left in a biosafety cabinet for 30 min at room temperature and then incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. Afterwards, the inhibition zones in the plates were visually observed. A sterile blank disc without a sample was used as negative control. All the samples were analyzed in triplicate.

Flame self-extinguishing

Vertical burning tests of the SF and SF-Mn+ were conducted according to the UL-94 standard. Specifically, the films with a length of 60 mm were ignited by placing vertically on a 20 mm flame to observe their combustion characteristics.

Degradation test in the soil

To assess biodegradability, the SF, SF-Mn+ and commercial plastic (polyethylene, PE) were buried in natural soil at a depth of 5–10 cm. The morphological changes and weight loss of films during degradation were recorded.

Strawberry preservation

Fresh strawberries of uniform size and maturity, free of damage or disease were selected. They were washed thoroughly with deionized water and air dried. The strawberries were randomly divided into several polypropylene (PP) plastic boxes for four groups: control group (CK, without any film covering), PE-covered group, SF-covered group, and SF-Zn2+-covered group. The preservation experiments were conducted at 25.0 °C and 65% relative humidity for 10 days. Every 2 days the changes in the appearance of the strawberries were recorded, and the weight loss, firmness, and decay index were measured.

Results and discussion

The preparation and visual appearance of SF and SF-Mn+

The fabrication process of SF-Mn+ is shown in Fig. 1a. SCG was first subjected to delignification pretreatment to obtain a polysaccharide-rich substrate (Fig. S1). Subsequently, the polysaccharide fraction was dissolved in an ionic liquid and regenerated in metal ion solutions, yielding the metal ion-coordinated films. This dissolution–regeneration process disrupted and reformed the extensive intermolecular hydrogen-bond network of the polysaccharides. During regeneration, metal ions formed stable coordination complexes with hydroxyl groups in the polysaccharide matrix. Such coordination interactions not only disrupted the native hydrogen-bonding network but also dictated the final structure and properties of the films.

Schematic diagram showing the fabrication of (a) the SF and SF-Mn+. Visual appearance of SCG gels regenerated from (b1) H2O, (b2)–(b6) chloride solution of Fe3+, Al3+, Cu2+, Zn2+ and Ca2+. Visual appearance of films: (c1)–(c6) SF, SF-Fe3+, SF-Al3+, SF-Cu2+, SF-Zn2+, SF-Ca2+.

The visual appearance of the metal-ion coordinated SCG gels and their dried films provided direct evidence for successful coordination and uniform distribution of metal ions throughout the polysaccharide network. As shown in Fig. 1b, the hydrogels regenerated in aqueous and various metal ion solutions displayed high transparency, with underlying pattern visible. The yellow and blue colors of the SCG gels with Fe3+ and Cu2+ were indicative of complexes with these ions. This high level of transparency indicated a homogeneous microstructure, wherein the coordinated metal ions were distributed uniformly, thereby minimizing light scattering (Dong et al., 2013; Mondal et al., 2022). Upon drying, as shown in Fig. 1c, the obtained films retained excellent transparency and homogeneity, which further confirms the uniform distribution of metal ions within the films. Notably, the Cu2+ containing regenerated gel underwent a color transition from blue (hydrated) to green (dry). This color change is indicative of an alteration in the coordination geometry of the Cu2+ ions, providing circumstantial evidence for the direct coordination between Cu2+ and the hydroxyl groups of the polysaccharides in the film. The uniform green color of SF-Cu2+ is indicative of the homogeneous distribution of coordination-driven structural alterations throughout the films (Yang et al., 2019; Zhang et al., 2022).

The coordination between metal ions and polysaccharide hydroxyl groups occurs through the replacement of hydrogen atoms by metal cations. The affinity and coordination geometry vary with ion characteristics: trivalent ions (Fe3+, Al3+) with high charge density tend to form stronger but more disruptive coordination, while divalent ions (Cu2+, Zn2+, Ca2+) exhibit moderate coordination strength that preserves more of the original hydrogen-bond network (Liu et al., 2022; Sun et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2022). These ion-specific differences underlie the distinct structural and functional properties observed in the films, as discussed in the following sections.

Morphology of SF and SF-Mn+

The morphology of the films was characterized by SEM and EDS. Surface morphology analysis (Fig. 2a–d and Fig. S2a, e) revealed that all SF-Mn+ exhibited markedly rougher surfaces compared with the SF. This increase in surface roughness can be assigned to the specific reorganization of polysaccharide chains that occurs during the process of metal ion-coordinated regeneration.

Surface morphology, cross section morphology and elemental distribution of (a) (e) (i): SF, (b) (f) (j): SF-Fe3+, (c) (g) (k): SF-Al3+, (d) (h) (l): SF-Cu2+.

Further analysis of cross-sectional morphology showed more significant structural alterations. As shown in Fig. 2e−h, Fig. S2b, f and Fig. S3, the SF exhibited a densely compacted structure, while all SF-Mn+ displayed a distinctly less compact, more porous structure. This transformation is attributable to metal ion-hydroxyl coordination, a competition with the hydrogen-bonding network that results in the inhibition of dense packing and the facilitation of a more open internal structure (Dong et al., 2013). The quantitative confirmation of this structural expansion is provided by thickness measurements (Fig. S4). The thickness of SF is 107.29 ± 4.92 μm, which is thinner than all SF-Mn+. Among them, the mean thickness of SF-Fe3+ and SF-Al3+ are 132.00 ± 5.90 μm and 137.29 ± 5.31 μm, respectively. This is because their high charge density trivalent Fe3+ and Al3+ drives multidentate coordination, where a single ion binds to multiple hydroxyl groups simultaneously. While effective as crosslinks, these strong, localized interactions disrupt long-range hydrogen-bond ordering and can lead to inhomogeneous chain packing, forming ion-rich clusters that push chains apart and create voids (Chen et al., 2022). In contrast, divalent ions (Cu2+, Zn2+, Ca2+) with lower charge density tend to form monodentate or bidentate coordination that integrates more smoothly into the existing network, resulting in more moderate structural changes (Liu et al., 2022).

The uniform distribution of metal ions throughout the cross-sections was confirmed by EDS analysis (Fig. 2i–l and Fig. S2c, g). Quantitative EDS analysis indicated metal contents of 6.44 ± 0.59%, 5.16 ± 0.50%, 5.55 ± 0.29%, 5.54 ± 0.26%, and 3.63 ± 0.48% for SF-Fe3+, SF-Al3+, SF-Cu2+, SF-Zn2+, and SF-Ca2+, respectively. The uniform distribution, absence of aggregates, and quantifiable metal loading confirms the successful formation of a uniform metal-ion coordinated network.

The comprehensive analyses demonstrate that metal-ion coordination fundamentally alters the film’s microstructure, yielding rougher surfaces, more porous internal morphologies, increased thickness, and uniformly distributed metal ions. These are all characteristic of a metal-ion coordinated polymeric network distinct from the network dominated by hydrogen bonds in SF.

Chemical structure and optical properties

The chemical structure and optical properties of the SF and SF-Mn+ were examined, as shown in Fig. 3. The FTIR spectra provided direct evidence for coordination interactions between metal ions and hydroxyl groups of polysaccharides (Fig. 3a). Specifically, a notable shift of the broad O—H stretching vibration band (3000–3700 cm−1) to lower wavenumbers was observed in SF-Mn+ compared to the SF. This red shift indicates weakened O—H bond strength arising from the formation of coordination bonds between metal ions and hydroxyl groups (Dong et al., 2013; Xu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2022). the FTIR spectra of SF and SF-Mn+ demonstrate negligible alterations in other regions.

Chemical structure and optical properties of SF and SF-Mn+: (a) FTIR spectra, (b) XRD spectra, (c) UV–vis transmission curve, (d) UV blocking rate.

As shown in Fig. 3b and Fig. S5, XRD analysis indicated that the SF and SF-Mn+ exhibit the characteristic diffraction pattern of cellulose II, with peaks at approximately 12° and 20° corresponding to the (1 −1 0) and (0 2 0) lattice planes, respectively. The bleached SCG exhibits characteristic peaks at 15.5° and 22°, which were identified as (1 −1 0)/(1 1 0) and (2 0 0) reflections of crystalline cellulose I, respectively (Zhang et al., 2023; Zhang, Zhong, et al., 2024). These findings confirm that the regeneration process yielded cellulose II crystal structure, and the coordination of metal ions did not alter the intrinsic crystal structure of the polysaccharides. However, the observation of an increase in film porosity and thickness (as determined by Section 3.2, SEM and thickness measurements, respectively) suggests that the metal-ion coordination probably affects the packing of the crystalline domains within the looser network structure.

The optical properties of the films are critical for packaging applications, as shown in Fig. 3c and d. All films exhibited optimal transparency within the visible light spectrum, with transmittance values above 85% at 550 nm. The high visible light transmittance is advantageous for product-visibility applications. The UV blocking performance was found to differ significantly depending on the metal ion. The SF film showed limited UV blocking capacity, with 32.59 ± 1.11% for UVB and 18.40 ± 1.02% for UVA. The SF-Fe3+ exhibited outstanding UV shielding properties, with a blocking efficiency of 99.92 ± 0.09% for UVB and 99.63 ± 0.45% for UVA. The SF-Cu2+ demonstrated complete UVB blocking (99.83 ± 0.18%) and substantial UVA reduction (84.27 ± 0.21%). The remarkable UV-blocking properties of SF-Fe3+ and SF-Cu2+ are attributed to the facile photo-reduction of the metal ions (Fe3+ to Fe2+ and Cu2+ to Cu+) and the associated charge transfer processes that occur upon ultraviolet irradiation (Dong et al., 2013; Yang et al., 2019). The integration of high visible transparency and selective UV blocking renders SF-Fe3+ and SF-Cu2+ highly promising for applications necessitating protection against UV-induced degradation.

Mechanical properties

The mechanical properties of films are critical for evaluating potential applications. Compared with SF, all SF-Mn+ films exhibited reduced elongation at break, decreasing from 18.11 ± 0.84% (SF) to 5.48 ± 0.64%–11.86 ± 1.65% (SF-Mn+), as shown in Fig. 4a. This reduction stems from the metal ions serving as coordination cross-links that restrict polysaccharide chain mobility.

Mechanical properties of SF and SF-Mn+: (a) stress-strain curves, (b) tensile stress and Young's modulus.

The tensile strength exhibited a distinct ion-dependent trend (Fig. 4b). Specifically, Cu2+ and Ca2+ caused limited reduction in strength, with values decreasing from 69.63 ± 0.96 MPa for SF to 66.59 ± 1.34 MPa and 65.82 ± 1.46 MPa for SF-Cu2+ and SF-Ca2+, respectively. The relatively weaker coordination of these metal ions led to a minor disruption of the hydrogen-bonding network of films. Concurrently, the supplemental and distributed cross-links may be formed, resulting in a slight decrease (Xu et al., 2025). It is noteworthy that SF-Zn2+ demonstrated the higher tensile strength (67.23 ± 1.64 MPa) and Young's modulus (4015.43 ± 155.49 MPa). This enhancement may be attributed to the uniform distribution of coordination cross-links, which fortify the network without introducing significant defects.

The SF-Fe3+ and SF-Al3+ exhibited marked strength reduction (from 69.63 ± 0.96 MPa for SF to 42.55 ± 3.12 MPa and 42.35 ± 2.11 MPa for SF-Fe3+ and SF-Al3+). The high charge density and strong multidentate coordination of these ions result in a disruption to the hydrogen bond frameworks (Chen et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2022). This destructive effect, possibly intensified by the formation of heterogeneous and brittle clusters that function as stress concentrators, is of higher significance than any potential crosslinking benefit. Thus, the metal ions valence and coordination strength govern the mechanical properties. Nevertheless, all SF-Mn+ exhibited favorable mechanical properties, with tensile strengths exceeding 43 MPa and Young's modulus above 2900 MPa. The tensile strength of the SF-Mn+ (42.35–67.23 MPa) exceeds those of conventional plastics such as high-density polyethylene (16–21 MPa) and polyvinyl chloride (4–23 MPa), and is comparable to or higher than that of PLA (20–60 MPa) (Table S2). This indicates its potential as a sustainable alternative.

Water resistance and water vapor barrier properties

The availability of food packaging films under high-humidity or wet conditions is a key factor in practical applications. Here, the water stability and water vapor barrier properties of SF-Mn+ were systematically assessed through a combination of water contact angle test, water absorption ratio, wet-state mechanical properties and WVTR. As shown in Fig. 5a, metal-ion coordination markedly enhanced surface hydrophobicity. Specifically, the water contact angle of the SF was determined to be 63.75° ± 1.42°, indicative of a hydrophilic surface. However, all SF-Mn+ exhibited markedly higher contact angles, with SF-Fe3+ and SF-Al3+ demonstrating the most pronounced hydrophobic character (99.71° ± 1.98° and 94.41° ± 0.99°, respectively). This increase is primarily attributed to the coordination between metal ions and the hydrophilic hydroxyl groups, which reduces the surface energy and available water interaction sites (Chen et al., 2022); this effect is further amplified by the increased surface roughness observed in SEM images (Fig. 2a–d), which enhances hydrophobicity by trapping air at the solid-liquid interface (Zhang, Zhou, et al., 2021). The ion-dependent variation in contact angle reflects the dual contribution of strong multidentate coordination by Fe3+ and Al3+, which effectively mask more hydrophilic hydroxyl groups while simultaneously inducing greater surface roughness through network disruption (Chen et al., 2022). In contrast, divalent ions achieve intermediate hydrophobicity through more moderate coordination that balances surface energy reduction (Zhang et al., 2022).

Water stability and water vapor barrier properties of SF and SF-Mn+: (a) water contact angle (b) water absorption ratio-time curve, (c) wet tensile strength after water immersion, (d) WVTR.

The enhanced water resistance of the SF-Mn+ is further confirmed by water absorption tests. As shown in Fig. 5b, the SF and SF-Mn+ exhibited rapid absorption of water within the initial 10 min, reaching maximum equilibrium. The equilibrium water absorption ratios of the SF-Mn+ were distinctly lower than that of the SF (136.71% ± 3.34%). Notably, the SF-Fe3+ and SF-Al3+ demonstrated remarkable water resistance, with absorption ratios of 58.49% ± 1.12% and 63.23% ± 1.24%, respectively. It is evident that the metal ion-polysaccharide coordination network serves as a durable structural framework that effectively restrains the swelling of the polysaccharide matrix and water penetration (Chen et al., 2022; Shimizu et al., 2016; Sun et al., 2024).

Furthermore, the wet mechanical properties of the films were obtained by testing their stress-strain curves after immersion in water (Fig. 5c). All wet films exhibited higher elongation at break and lower tensile strength in comparison with films in the dry state, attributable to water-induced enhancement of polysaccharide chain mobility. Nevertheless, the SF-Mn+ retained higher wet state tensile strength than SF. The detailed wet mechanical properties of SF and SF-Mn+ are shown in Table S1. Specifically, the wet tensile strength of the SF-Fe3+, SF-Al3+, SF-Cu2+, SF-Zn2+, and SF-Ca2+ (14.84 ± 2.61 MPa −23.87 ± 0.69 MPa) is higher than that of the SF (6.05 ± 0.58 MPa). In comparison to the native hydrogen bond network, which is easily disrupted by water, the metal-ion coordination bond networks maintain structural reinforcement in an aqueous environment (Sun et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2022). A comparable wet state strength enhancement is also observed in metal-ion-crosslinked lignocellulosic nanopaper (Zhang et al., 2022).

In most food packaging applications, the water vapor permeation property is considered a key performance indicator. As illustrated in Fig. 5d, the SF demonstrated a WVTR of 60.96 ± 0.55 g m−2 day−1. By contrast, tall SF-Mn+ except SF-Ca2+ exhibited significantly enhanced water vapor barriers, among which SF-Fe3+ exhibited the optimal performance (25.78 ± 0.06 g m−2 day−1). Notably, the WVTR of SF-Fe3+ is comparable to that of conventional LDPE (16–23 g m−2 day−1), while significantly outperforming PLA (100–350 g m−2 day−1). This enhanced performance is due to the metal-ion coordinated network hindering the diffusion of water molecules and providing a more tortuous path (Shimizu et al., 2016; Zhang et al., 2022). The more porous internal structure of SF-Mn+ films (Fig. 2e–h) further contributes to this tortuosity, effectively extending the diffusion pathway for water molecules.

Coordination with metal ions considerably enhances the water stability and water vapor barrier properties of SF, thereby expanding its potential application in high-humid environments.

Flame retardancy

The regenerated cellulose/hemicellulose films are inherently flammable owing to their high contents of carbon, oxygen and hydrogen, thereby posing combustion risks. However, metal-ions coordination markedly altered the film flammability, as determined by vertical burning tests.

As shown in Fig. 6, the SF ignited and underwent complete combustion within 5 s. In contrast, all SF-Mn+ exhibited immediate self-extinguishing behavior, with the flame being quenched within 2 s of ignition source removal. The coordination bonds between the metal ions and the oxygen-rich hydroxyl groups of the polysaccharide matrix modify the thermal degradation pathway (Sun et al., 2024). Upon heating, these coordinated structures decompose preferentially to form protective barrier layers composed of carbonaceous char and metal oxides. This layer serves as an insulating shield, thereby limiting heat transfer to the remaining areas of the film and restricting the diffusion of oxygen and flammable volatiles (Sakuma et al., 2021). Conventional polyolefin films such as PE and PP are highly flammable and drip during combustion, posing fire safety risks. This excellent flame-retardant performance of SF-Mn+ enhances their stability in extreme environments.

Antibacterial activity

The antibacterial activity of the SF and SF-Mn+ against S. aureus and E. Coli was evaluated, as shown in Fig. 7. SF exhibited no inhibitory effect, confirming the intrinsic lack of antibacterial activity in the SCG polysaccharide matrix. Similarly, the SF-Ca2+ film showed no detectable inhibition against either strain. As a redox-inactive alkaline earth metal with a stable filled electron shell, Ca2+ neither generates reactive oxygen species (ROS) nor disrupts vital bacterial cellular processes.

Antibacterial effect of SF and SF-Mn+ on S. aureus (a) and E. coli (b): (a1), (b1): Control, (a2)–(a6) and (b2)–(b6): SF, SF-Fe3+, SF-Al3+, SF-Cu2+, SF-Zn2+, SF-Ca2+, zone of inhibition against S. aureus (c) and (d) E. coli.

Against S. aureus, the SF-Fe3+, SF-Al3+, SF-Cu2+, and SF-Zn2+ films all exhibited clear zones of inhibition, with diameters of 13.09 ± 0.66 mm, 15.52 ± 0.97 mm, 19.31 ± 1.17 mm, and 22.94 ± 0.35 mm, respectively (Fig. 7c). The antibacterial activity of these films aligns with the well-documented antibacterial mechanisms of these ions, which act through multiple synergistic pathways: (i) electrostatic interaction with negatively charged bacterial cell envelopes, altering membrane permeability and causing leakage of intracellular contents; (ii) generation of ROS via Fenton or Fenton-like chemistry, leading to lipid peroxidation, protein oxidation, and DNA damage; and (iii) binding to thiol groups of proteins, resulting in enzyme inactivation and metabolic disruption (Dang et al., 2024; Dang, Guo, Du, & Wang, 2025; Godoy-Gallardo et al., 2021; Li et al., 2023; Shankar & Rhim, 2019).

A distinct susceptibility pattern was observed for E. coli (Fig. 7b). While SF-Al3+, SF-Cu2+, and SF-Zn2+ were effective with inhibition zones of 12.61 ± 0.40 mm, 10.89 ± 0.21 mm, and 19.35 ± 1.17 mm (Fig. 7d), respectively, SF-Fe3+ showed no detectable activity. This divergence can be attributed to the neutralization of Fe3+ activity by the lipopolysaccharide layer via the formation of impermeable hydroxo-species (Zhang, Zhao, et al., 2021). In contrast, Al3+ exhibits broad-spectrum efficacy against both bacterial strains, stemming from its trivalent cationic charge that enables strong electrostatic interaction with anionic LPS and teichoic acid layers, causing membrane disruption and cell death through a non-redox mechanism (Godoy-Gallardo et al., 2021; Talukdar et al., 2021). The Cu2+ and Zn2+ ions maintain activity against both strains through combined mechanisms: electrostatic membrane binding, ROS generation (for Cu2+ via redox cycling; for Zn2+ primarily via membrane disruption and metabolic interference), and protein dysfunction (Liu et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2016).

The coordination of specific metal ions, particularly Al3+, Zn2+, and Cu2+, successfully imparts robust and broad-spectrum antibacterial functionality to SF, representing a key advantage over conventional plastic films (e.g., PE, PP, PVC) that lack intrinsic antibacterial properties and thereby highlighting its potential for applications in active food packaging.

Application of SF and SF-Mn+ for strawberries preservation

The changes in appearance and decay index of strawberries among the CK and film-covered groups are depicted in Fig. S6, Fig. 8a, b. The decay index is a critical parameter for quantitative evaluation of appearance changes. During the initial 2 days, strawberries underwent pronounced color transition from blue to red. Upon storage extension, the CK group, PE group, and SF group exhibited shrinkage and softening by day 4, with decay deterioration and mold appearing by day 6. Correspondingly, decay indices increased markedly, reaching 79.68 ± 1.44% (CK), 82.54 ± 2.37% (PE), and 69.13 ± 0.99% (SF) on day 10. Extensive spoilage and mold were attributable to negligible antibacterial activity in PE and SF (Tan et al., 2025). Furthermore, reduced WVTR of PE elevated internal humidity, promoting fungal growth. This finding aligns with the observations reported in other studies (Tan et al., 2025; Zhang, Wang, et al., 2024). Conversely, the SF-Zn2+ group exhibited a substantial decrease in decay index through day 8, with the minimum value of 18.32% ± 1.22% recorded. This confirmed marked anti-mold efficacy of metal–ion coordination, attributable to antibacterial properties. The metal ion-coordinated SCG films demonstrate the capacity to effectively reduce microbial contamination, thereby extending strawberries shelf-life.

The effect of different packaging films on (a) appearance, (b) decay index, (c) weight loss and (d) firmness of strawberries during storage for 10 days.

The weight loss rate served as an essential parameter for evaluating preservation efficacy. As illustrated in Fig. 8c, all strawberry groups exhibited marked weight loss during storage. This primarily stemmed from respiration and transpiration (He et al., 2024). The CK group showed the highest weight loss, reaching 42.33 ± 1.46% on day 10. In contrast, PE-covered strawberries displayed a minimal weight loss rate of less than 8%, owing to excellent water vapor barrier properties (Liu et al., 2024; Tan et al., 2025). The SF and SF-Zn2+ exhibited slightly higher weight loss rates than PE, which can be attributed to their comparatively lower water vapor barrier properties (Vilarinho et al., 2021). However, the SF-Zn2+ achieved only 19.54 ± 0.92% loss by day 10, reflecting enhanced water vapor barrier capability and superior antibacterial activity relative to SF, which effectively suppressed moisture and nutrient loss caused by microbial infection (Tan et al., 2025).

Firmness is an important indicator of the freshness and ripeness of fruits. Fruits may experience a decrease in firmness during storage due to factors such as water loss, microbial contamination, and enzymatic degradation of cell wall components (Dong et al., 2025). As demonstrated in Fig. 8d, during the 10 days storage period, the firmness of all strawberries exhibited a downward trend. The firmness of strawberries treated with the CK group, PE group, SF group, and SF-Zn2+ group on the 10th day were 1.47 ± 0.25 N, 2.10 ± 0.66 N, 2.44 ± 0.69 N, and 3.77 ± 0.15 N, respectively. The SF-Zn2+ group demonstrates higher firmness values during the late storage period. This phenomenon can be attributed primarily to its comparatively minimal weight loss rate and the lowest decay index among the groups.

Biodegradability

The biodegradability of SF and SF-Mn+ was assessed by soil burial testing. As shown in Fig. S8, the SF and SF-Mn+ underwent swelling by day 10, followed by fracturing on day 25, attributable to water penetration and microbial degradation of cellulose and hemicellulose (Xia et al., 2021). Quantitative degradation analysis (Fig. S9) revealed that SF and SF-Mn+ films lost 50%–80% of their mass within 25 days, with complete degradation by day 40. PE exhibited negligible mass loss, confirming its resistance to biodegradation. Thus, the SF-Mn+ demonstrates biodegradability suitable for replacing non–biodegradable plastics, thereby mitigating the environmental impact of plastic waste.

Conclusions

In this study, we developed a straightforward strategy to transform SCG into multifunctional films via ionic liquid dissolution and metal ion-coordinated regeneration. The coordination of metal ions with the hydroxyl groups of the polysaccharide matrix serves as the central regulatory mechanism, disrupting the native hydrogen-bond network and forming coordination crosslinks that govern the film structure and properties. This structural modulation simultaneously enhances water resistance (hydrophobicity, water absorption, wet strength, water vapor barrier properties) and imparts ion-specific functionalities: UV-blocking (Fe3+, Cu2+), antibacterial activity (Al3+, Cu2+, Zn2+), and flame retardancy, while maintaining complete biodegradability within 40 days. In strawberry preservation trials, the SF-Zn2+ effectively reduced decay, weight loss, and softening, extending the shelf life. The choice of metal ion allows tailoring of film properties for specific applications: SF-Fe3+ and SF-Al3+ are suited for high-humidity environments requiring maximal water resistance, while SF-Zn2+ and SF-Cu2+ are preferable when antibacterial function is prioritized. For practical implementation, further investigation is needed to address metal ion migration safety according to food contact regulations, optimize ionic liquid recovery for economic viability, and directly evaluate antifungal activity against common food-spoilage molds. Nevertheless, the combination of low-cost waste feedstock, simple and scalable fabrication, tunable performance, and complete biodegradability positions these films as promising candidates for sustainable packaging applications.

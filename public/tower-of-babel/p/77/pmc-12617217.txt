OA2300. Perceived well-being and safety in Urban Green Spaces: Case study from Crete
Authors: I Christoforidi, D Kollaros, A Psaroudaki, M Papadakaki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUrbanization has significantly changed the way people interact with nature and Urban Green Spaces (UGS) provide a vital opportunity for citizens to engage with the natural environment. Addressing citizens’ perceptions could enhance the effectiveness of greening strategies, a consideration of particular importance for islands in the Mediterranean basin given their unique characteristics.MethodsA questionnaire survey was carried out with 703 residents of the Island of Crete, Greece.Results87.8% of the participants consider UGS highly necessary for their wellbeing, 60% visit UGS for relaxation, almost 30% for sport activities (depending on the age, residence and parenthood, p < 0.01) and activities with children (depending on the age, level of education and parenthood, p < 0.01) and 26.8% for meeting with friends (depending on the age, p < 0.05 and parenthood, p < 0.01). Moreover, public UGS is considered more necessary for female participants (p < 0.01), under 35 years, living in an apartment in the city. Despite the necessity, residents consider by 78.7% UGS inadequate and by 93,4% inaccessible for people with mobility problems. In addition, according to participants’ perceptions public UGS on Crete have low risk of harassment or crime (almost 1%) and considered as a place that can enable contact with nature contributing to health and well-being only by 35.7%. Participants visiting green spaces less often than once a month (20% of all) stated that the main reason was time limitations (44.2%), followed by distance (23.3%), and health reasons mainly pertaining mobility (14.0%), furthermore obstacles such as poor UGS maintenance, and phobias were given by less than 10% of the participants who visit seldom green spaces.ConclusionsThe findings reveal that, on average, residents of Crete exhibit a not positive perception of local public UGS. These results provide a critical foundation for informing targeted interventions within existing UGS and guiding strategic planning for future developments.Key messages• Effective UGS interventions and planning must reflect citizens’ perceptions and needs, boosting accessibility, safety, and use, while ensuring these spaces remain inclusive and socially valued.• Public attitudes toward UGS shape their use and the benefits gained, including better physical health, psychological well-being, and enhanced social cohesion.TopicCitizens’ perceptions, Greening strategies, Well-being in Urban Environments.

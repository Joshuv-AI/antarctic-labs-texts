Intrapartum sonography for predicting successful vaginal delivery in term nulliparous women: a prospective observational study
Authors: Pattrinee Boonyapisomparn, Tachjaree Panchalee, Dittakarn Boriboonhirunsarn, Sakita Moungmaithong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo compare the angle of progression (AoP) and the progression distance (PD) during the early active phase, first stage of labor in term nulliparous women between successful vaginal delivery and cesarean section due to active phase arrest or prolonged second stage of labor groups.MethodA prospective observational study was conducted between July 2019 and May 2020 at the labor room of Siriraj Hospital. Cervical dilatation was assessed by digital examination to ensure an early active phase of labor, and a transperineal ultrasound was performed to measure the angle of progression (AoP) and progression distance (PD). The delivery outcomes were divided into successful vaginal delivery (spontaneous or operative) and cesarean section due to active phase arrest or prolonged second stage of labor. Covariates were documented, including maternal age, maternal body mass index, total weight gain during pregnancy, gestational age, and fetal birth weight.ResultsA total of 149 term singleton nulliparous women with vertex presentation were consecutively enrolled. Six women who delivered by cesarean section due to non-reassuring fetal heart rate were excluded. Among 143 women who were eligible for further analysis, 108 (75.5%) pregnant women had successful vaginal delivery, while 35 (24.5%) women had cesarean section. The area under the curve indicated that AoP and PD were associated with a successful vaginal delivery (AUCs of 0.67 for AoP and 0.69 for PD). The optimal cut-off points using AoP were ≥ 90 ° (sensitivity 85.2%, specificity 45.7%) and PD ≥ 0 cm (sensitivity 86.1%, specificity 42.9%).ConclusionAoP and PD using standard 2-dimensional ultrasonography at the early active phase, first stage of labor, demonstrated a modest association with successful vaginal delivery. Further studies are warranted to explore the benefit of AoP and PD in clinical applications.Keywords: Intrapartum ultrasound, Angle of progression, Progression distance, Successful vaginal delivery

Introduction

In general practice, digital vaginal examination is the main practice to determine the progression of labor and make a diagnosis of active phase arrest with subsequent cesarean section. However, it might suffer from the subjective nature and operator dependence [1–4]. Nizard J. et al. (2009) found that cervical dilatation by clinical assessment has limitations, especially when cervical dilatation is between 6 and 8 cm [1]. Vaginal examination was also reported as unpleasant. Van Adrichem A. et al. (2018) and Usman S. et al. (2018) found that women have a negative experience from vaginal examination during labor compared with intrapartum sonography [5–8]. Furthermore, this procedure is also associated with an increased risk of infection [9, 10].

Many parameters of intrapartum sonography have been proposed to be promising for the evaluation of cervical dilatation, fetal head station, and position, and to assess the progression of labor [4, 11–17]. The previous studies reported that intrapartum sonography may be more accurate, reproducible, and tends to decrease the infection rate in cases of rupture of membranes compared with digital vaginal examination [3, 11–13, 15, 17]. Intrapartum sonography has been reported to have the potential to predict the route of delivery, successful instrumental vaginal delivery, successful labor induction, and time to delivery [16–23]. The International Society of Ultrasound in Obstetrics and Gynecology (ISUOG) has developed a practice guideline and encouraged the incorporation of intrapartum sonography in clinical practice [11].

Previous studies have shown that the angle of progression (AoP) and progression distance (PD) are the most important parameters for assessing fetal head station and can be used to predict delivery outcome. Both parameters are easy to measure regardless of examiner experience; however, the results varied slightly across studies, which may have resulted from differences in population characteristics [5, 11, 20, 24].

Eggebo T. M. et al. (2013) and Helene I. et al. (2017) reported that AoP in the first stage of labor has the potential to be used to predict delivery outcome [25, 26], while Winnie W.Y. Chan et al. (2018) and M. Gillor et al. (2017) studied the use of AoP in pre-induction assessment and also found similar outcomes [20, 27]. Chan Y.T. et al. (2016) found that AoP measurement in the active phase of labor could be used to predict time to delivery [23], while Viola Y.T. Chan et al. (2019) and Sainz J.A. et al. (2018) found that AoP measured in the second stage of labor can predict successful instrumental delivery [16, 20]. The use of PD has also been described. Sainz J.A. et al. (2018) also reported that PD in the second stage of labor was different between the successful instrumental delivery and the cesarean section groups (3.7 cm in the successful instrumental vaginal delivery versus 2.99 cm in the cesarean section group) [21]. Uri Erlik et al. (2018) also found that PD during the first stage of labor could be used to predict delivery outcome as well [19].

This study aimed to compare AoP and PD assessed by transperineal ultrasound in the early active phase, first stage of labor, between low-risk nulliparous women who delivered by spontaneous/operative vaginal delivery and cesarean section due to arrest of labor or prolonged second stage of labor that was not fulfill the criteria for operative vaginal delivery, and to determine a cut-off value for predicting successful vaginal delivery.

Materials and methods

A prospective observational study was performed in enrolled term singleton nulliparous Thai women aged between 18 and 45 years old who were admitted to the labor room with vertex presentation in the first stage of labor at Siriraj Hospital, from July 2019 to May 2020. The exclusion criteria were the women who underwent cesarean section due to an indication other than arrest of labor, or who were not willing to participate in this study. Gestational age was calculated from the last menstrual period confirmed by a first or second-trimester scan. Maternal pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI) and total weight gain during pregnancy were collected. Ethics Committee approval was obtained at Siriraj Hospital, Thailand (157/2562 EC1), and all women gave written consent. The authors confirm that all experiments were performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

During the active phase of the first stage of labor, confirmed by the cervical dilatation between 4 and 6 centimeters assessed by digital vaginal examination, transperineal ultrasound was performed by a single operator (PB) using two available ultrasound machines in the labor room [Voluson P6s or GE Voluson portable (GE Healthcare, Austria) with a 2–5 MHz curvilinear transducer]. The women were placed in a supine position with flexed hips and knees, bladder was asked to be emptied before ultrasonographic examination. The transducer was covered with a disposable glove and placed between the labia majora in the midsagittal plane, where the pubic symphysis and fetal skull were clearly portrayed. Two ultrasound images were collected in the absence of the uterine contraction, AoP and PD were measured using Sono L&D (GE Healthcare, Austria) pre-installed in the ultrasound machines. A line along with the long axis of the pubic bone (line 1) and a line from the lowest edge of the pubic bone to the lowest part of the fetal skull were drawn (line 2). The angle between these two lines was defined as the angle of progression (AoP). Another line (line 3), at the lowest edge of the pubic bone that makes a 90-degree angle with the line along the long axis of the pubic bone (line 1), progression distance (PD) was the shortest distance between line 3 and the most caudal part of the fetal skull (Fig. 1) [11]. After the measurement of AoP and PD in both ultrasound images. The average of the two measurements was used for further analysis. Birth attendants were blinded to the ultrasound finding.

According to the study by Erlik U. et al. (2018), women with PD of 2.51 ± 1.71 in the first stage of labor had successful vaginal delivery, while those who had PD of 1.48 ± 1.9 ended up with cesarean Sect. [19]. The sample size was calculated, and at least 33 women in each group were required to demonstrate the statistical difference. The reproducibility was obtained consecutively from the first 35 eligible cases. After ultrasonographic examination, the first operator (PB) did the measurements and stored two images in the ultrasound machine. The second operator (SM), who is a maternal-fetal medicine consultant and was blinded from the initial measurement, did the measurements of AoP and PD using two stored images to obtain the averages. The intraclass correlation coefficient and Bland-Altman plot were used to assess intraobserver and interobserver agreement. Numerical variables with normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and those with non-normal distribution were expressed as median with interquartile range (IQR), respectively. Categorical variables were expressed as numbers (N) and percentages (%). An unpaired t-test and Mann-Whitney U-test were performed to assess any statistically significant differences between normally and non-normally distributed numerical variables. A chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used to compare categorical variables as appropriate. Differences were considered to be significant when the p-value was less than 0.05. The predictive values of AoP and PD were calculated by cross-table analysis and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves with the area under the curve (AUC) as a discriminator. The AUC was considered to have discriminatory potential if the lower limit of the 95% CI interval exceeded 0.5. All calculations were performed using SPSS version 18.0 for Windows.

In this study, active phase of labor was defined based on Friedman EA studies and labor curve as cervical dilatation of 4 cm or more in the presence of regular uterine contraction, and arrest of labor was defined as failure to progress of cervical dilatation in 4 h or more of adequate uterine contraction with ruptured membranes in first stage of labor and at least 2 h of pushing in nulliparous women in second stage of labor (Prolonged second stage of labor) that were not met the operative vaginal delivery criteria [28–30]. Delivery outcomes were divided as previously described. Fetal birth weight was collected, and indications for women who had assisted vaginal delivery or cesarean section were noted.

Results

A total of 149 women were enrolled, after exclusion of 6 women who had cesarean section for indications other than arrest of labor or prolonged second stage of labor (all excluded cases underwent cesarean section due to non-reassuring fetal heart rate), the remaining 143 women were eligible for further analysis. 108 women had vaginal delivery, including 5 women with successful operative vaginal delivery, and 35 women underwent cesarean section due to arrest of labor. The baseline characteristics, AoP, and PD between women who had successful vaginal delivery compared with those who underwent cesarean section are presented in Table 1. When comparing vaginal and cesarean delivery groups, we found that fetal birth weight in the cesarean section group was higher than in the vaginal delivery group (3,005 g versus 3,209 g, P = 0.003), and gestational age in the cesarean section group was also higher (38.8 weeks versus 39.4 weeks, P = 0.011). Maternal age (26 ± 5.4 vs. 28 ± 5.8, P = 0.065), BMI (21.3 ± 4.08 vs. 22.6 ± 4.1, P = 0.108 ), gestational weight gain (14.1 ± 5.1 vs. 14.9 ± 4.2, P = 0.369) and cervical dilatation (4.7 ± 0.7 cm vs. 4.6 ± 0.7 cm, P = 0.508) were not significantly different between two groups.

Reproducibility was obtained from the first 35 eligible cases. High levels of agreement were observed, with mean intraclass correlation values of 0.92 and 0.91 for interobserver and intraobserver reliability, respectively, for AoP and 0.89 and 0.91 for PD. AoP and PD were significantly different between women who had successful vaginal delivery and cesarean section due to arrest of labor (AoP 105.59° versus 95.33 °, P = 0.001, and PD 1.57 cm versus 0.43 cm, P < 0.001). The area under the curve (AUC) of AoP was 0.673 and 0.685 for PD, as shown in Fig. 2. Using AoP ≥ 90 ° and PD ≥ 0 cm as cut-off points for predicting successful vaginal delivery, AoP has 85.2% sensitivity and 45.7% specificity, while PD has 86.1% sensitivity and 42.9% specificity (Table 2). A logistic regression model was used to identify the differences between women who had a successful vaginal delivery and those who received a cesarean section. The odds ratio of AoP cut-off was 4.842 (95%CI 2.068–11.340, P < 0.001), and the odds ratio of PD cut-off was 4.650 (95%CI 1.961–11.027, P < 0.001).

Discussion

The objectives of this study were to investigate whether there is a difference in AoP and PD assessed by transperineal ultrasound in the early active phase, first stage of labor in Thai low-risk nulliparous women who eventually delivered by vaginal delivery and cesarean section due to arrest of labor, and to determine a cut-off point associated with successful vaginal delivery.

The results demonstrated that AoP and PD are significantly different between women who had vaginal delivery versus cesarean section, and AoP more than 90 o and PD more than 0 cm increase the possibility of successful vaginal delivery. A small number of women in the cesarean section group may cause the difference in gestational age and birthweight, which is unlikely to provide any clinical significance. Maternal age, pre-pregnancy body mass index, and gestational weight gain were not significantly different between the two groups.

AoP holds the stronger association with successful vaginal delivery compared with PD in this study, and women who had AoP of more than 90 degrees have 4.8 times higher vaginal delivery rate than those who did not. Corresponding results were also found in the studies that used AoP measurements in pre-induction of labor, the first stage, and the second stage of labor. Gillor M. et al. (2017) reported that 95% of women who had AoP > 92 ° in the pre-induction stage had successful vaginal delivery [27]. Eggebo T. M. (2013) found that 88% of women who have AoP ≥ 110° in the first stage of labor can deliver vaginally [26], while Ingeberg H. et al. (2017) found that 61% of women who have AoP > 105 ° in the first stage of labor have successful vaginal delivery [25]. Viola Y.T. Chan et al. (2019) and Sainz J.A. et al. (2018) studied the benefit of intrapartum sonography during second stage to predict successful instrumental delivery, the first group reported that mean AoP were 153o in those with successful instrumental delivery versus 139o in those who ended up with cesarean section, while AoP were 133.1o in easy vacuum extraction compare to 109.2o for those who were not by the latter group [18, 21]. The difference in mean AoP can be explained by differences in the stage of labor at which AoP were obtained. However when focusing on the previous studies that AoP was also measured during active phase of first stage of labor, our study found that using AoP ≥ 90o as a cut-off value has highest sensitivity (85.2%), while Ingeberg H. et al.’s and Eggebo T. M.’s studies used AoP ≥ 105o and ≥ 110o as a cut-off value and found the sensitivity of 61.1% and 88%, respectively [25, 26].

As previously mentioned, Uri Erlik et al. also found a similar result: PD during the first stage of labor was significantly higher in women who had vaginal delivery than in those who underwent cesarean Sect. (2.51 ± 1.71 cm versus 1.48 ± 1.9 cm, respectively) [19]. But the mean of PD in successful vaginal delivery between studies was utterly different. In this study, we found that women with PD of 1.57 ± 1.49 had successful vaginal delivery, while those who had PD of 0.43 ± 1.76 ended up with cesarean section. The difference in the AoP cut-off values and mean PD compared with our study may be due to various unmeasured factors such as the anatomical difference of the maternal pelvis between the study populations, the use of epidural block, differences in the labor progression at the time of ultrasonographic measurements that would change progressively as the labor advances, as well as the difference in fetal factors (fetal weights, fetal head position, and asynclitism). Furthermore, PD is more problematic to measure compared with AoP, and it does not represent the curvature alignment of the birth passage, which is in contrast with AoP. Therefore, AoP should be used to determine labor progression and predict successful vaginal delivery.

This study reported intrapartum sonography associated with successful vaginal delivery with cut-off values, sensitivity, and specificity for the Thai population. However, this study was performed only using the transperineal approach in the sagittal plane, without combining the transabdominal approach; as a result, fetal malposition could not be accurately diagnosed. The combination of both approaches and various parameters may improve the prediction of successful vaginal delivery. It may be worth noting that we used 2 different models of the ultrasound machines [Voluson P6s or GE Voluson portable (GE Healthcare, Austria)] based on the availability of each machine at that moment. This reflects the real-world situation and relies on the assumption that there are no differences in terms of ultrasonographic measurements among machines. The interpretation of this study’s results should be performed with caution because this is a single-center study in Thai nulliparous women that may limit the generalizability of this study. The criteria of active phase and active phase arrest, as well as the prolonged second stage of labor, may vary among institutions. Recently, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) and the Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine (SMFM) defined the active phase as starting at 6 cm cervical dilation, characterized by regular, strong contractions [31]. Using the low threshold for cesarean section, based mainly on Friedman’s criteria and the patient/physician preference in labor management, may be responsible for a high cesarean section rate and relatively low operative vaginal delivery in our center. Furthermore, this study considers intrapartum sonography parameters as the sole predictor of successful vaginal delivery. A larger sample size is needed to confirm the cut-off point, and other covariates that might affect the route of delivery, as well as the subgroup analysis between spontaneous vertex delivery and operative vaginal delivery. Lastly, this study was performed exclusively in nulliparous women; studies in multiparous women should be considered to timely identify the pregnant women who would require cesarean delivery and ultimately reduce the unnecessary cesarean section.

Conclusion

This study revealed that AoP and PD in the early active phase, the first stage of labor, are associated with successful vaginal delivery. Our findings suggest that AoP has a stronger association with successful vaginal delivery than PD.

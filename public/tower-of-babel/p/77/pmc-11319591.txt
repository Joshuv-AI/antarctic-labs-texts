Q-factor and wavelength adjustable ultra-perfect absorption under critical coupling through epsilon-near-zero-based absorbers
Authors: Yue-Dong Li, Yue-Gang Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Perfect absorption with adjustable Q factor and wavelength is essential in both theory and application. To address this issue, a structure composed of an epsilon-near-zero (ENZ)- SiO2 grating and a multilayer dielectric structure was designed. In the structure, the intrinsic loss is related to the ENZ thickness, while the external leakage depends on the grating parameters. By adjusting the ENZ film thickness, the intrinsic loss rate was tuned to match the external leakage rate corresponding to different grating parameters, realizing the critical couplings with different Q factors and wavelengths. Under critical coupling, the absorption rate reached a maximum of 99.999%, demonstrating ultra-perfect absorption. The Q factor was adjusted from 4350 to 35800, and the absorption wavelength was modified from 1050 to 1282 nm. The design work may provide new ideas for developing wavelength- and Q-selective absorbers, optical modulators, dielectric sensors, and photodetectors.

Introduction

Perfect absorption is a crucial aspect in various applications, including sensors1,2, photon detectors3,4, and thermal emitters5,6. To achieve highly efficient absorption7–9, angular insensitivity10,11, multiply-wavelength12, polarization insensitivity13 and sensitivity14, many absorbers have been designed. Many optical resonances were utilized to achieve perfect absorption, such as Tamm resonances15,16, guided mode resonances17,18, Fabry–Perot (F-P) cavity resonance19, surface plasmon polaritons20–23, localized plasmon resonances24,25, and magnetic resonance systems26, destructive interference27.

The absorption efficiency was enhanced by designing structures. For instance, a metal Bragg reflector cavity structure achieved dual-band absorptions with efficiencies of 83.5% and 89.9% through optical Tamm and F-P resonances, respectively28. A multilayer structure obtained an absorption of 99.64% through Bloch surface wave resonances29. Additionally, an ultrathin semiconducting (Ge) grating reached perfect absorption of 99.4% through the grating diffraction effect30. The all-dielectric Strontium Titanate resonator structure was proposed and achieved perfect absorption of 99.8% through Mie resonance31. Moreover, magnetic plasmon resonance was generated through the metal slit, resulting in a perfect absorption of 99.8%32. A monolayer metasurface perfect absorber periodically arranged as a honeycomb array is proposed. By tuning the height of the metasurface to degenerate toroidal dipole (TD) and electric dipole (ED) modes, the maximum absorption is up to 92.3% at the wavelength of 955 nm33.

Many structures have been developed to achieve perfect absorption under critical coupling conditions. Graphene and black phosphorus gratings and cavities on an Au film were designed to realize the critical coupling, resulting in absorption rates of up to 99.9%34. In the hybrid grating-based Fabry–Perot structure, the critical coupling of guided-mode resonance was satisfied, and the absorption of 99.66% was achieved35. The maximal absorption of 99.8% was realized under critical coupling of guided-mode resonance in the structure consisting of a borophene monolayer deposited on a photonic crystal slab backed with a metallic mirror36. Another proposed absorber consisted of a dielectric grating, a graphene monolayer, and a photonic crystal, which was designed to absorb light with an efficiency of 99.5% under critical coupling conditions18. A combination of a photonic crystal and a metal substrate was also used to achieve an absorption of 96% by satisfying the critical coupling37. The graphene-cylinder-metal arrays structure was proposed to achieve 99.14% absorption in the near-critical coupling state38.

The tunability of perfect absorption in wavelength and Q-factor is crucial for many applications. To achieve the absorption peak with the desired Q factor, the stacking of layers of two-dimensional materials like graphene and black phosphorus was used. In a structure composed of graphene and photonic crystals, the absorption peak bandwidth is from 0.2 to 50 nm, and the Q factor of absorption peaks can be tuned from 30 to 3000 by changing the number of graphene layers and position39. Another structure composed of graphene and composite gratings achieved a Q factor change from 8 to 278 by adjusting the Fermi energy levels and geometrical parameters of graphene40. In a structure composed of defective photonic crystals, the Q factor of absorption peaks can be tuned approximately from 200 to 600 by changing the number of MoS2 layers and position41.

Various materials such as Dirac semimetals, graphene, and black phosphorus are used to design structures that can tune the wavelength for specific applications. For instance, a structure made of Dirac semimetals and photonic crystals can adjust the absorption frequency from 1.381 THz to 1.395 THz by modifying the Fermi energy level of Dirac semimetals. However, this tuning comes at the cost of the absorption rate42. Similarly, the structure consisting of a continuous graphene monolayer and patterned borophene nanoribbon array was proposed to tune the absorption wavelength from 1.1 µm to 1.55 µm with an absorption efficiency of 82%43. A photonic crystals-gratings-graphene structure can modify the wavelength of absorption from 1537 to 1567 nm by adjusting the grating height, while absorption peaks decrease18. In contrast, a simple and cost-effective GeSe-SiO2-Si multilayer optical film was fabricated to achieve perfect absorption. The operating wavelength can be linearly tuned from 900 to 1300 nm by adjusting the GeSe thickness from 125 to 200 nm. The absorption rate exceeds 97% under these conditions44. Lastly, the structure consisting of upper and lower asymmetric graphene ribbons can tune the absorption wavelength with a decrease in absorbance45.

Epsilon-near-zero (ENZ) materials are advantageous in nonlinear optics and absorption in the ENZ region. This can help in enhancing the interaction of light with matter46–51. Researchers have applied ENZ materials to broadband absorption. In the structure consisting of ENZ nanolayer and metal array, a perfect absorption with 98% efficiency was achieved by coupling ENZ mode to gap plasmon in bandwidth of 240 nm centered at 1550 nm46. Another similar structure was designed to achieve more than 90% absorption from 1310 to 1550 nm52. Through coupling ENZ mode to surface plasmon polaritons and localized plasmon resonances, absorption of 98% in the range of 1435–1680 nm was achieved53. Multilayer stacks of ENZ materials were designed to realize absorptions of more than 90% with bandwidths of 6 µm54 and 93% with bandwidth of 1.8 µm55 respectively.

In this study, a structure composed of ENZ grating-multilayer is designed for perfect absorption. An EZN grating is designed on the multilayer to enhance the absorption efficiency, realizing ultra-perfect absorption. The critical coupling is achieved by adjusting structure parameters to balance the external leakage rate and intrinsic loss rate of the system. The wavelength and Q factor of perfect absorption can be adjusted over a wide range based on the critical coupling. In addition, we also studied the sensitivity of the perfect absorption to the polarization.

The ultra-perfect absorption

A linearly polarized light along the x direction was incident to the structures. Firstly, the absorption of only an ITO film with a thickness of 19 nm suspended in air was calculated, as shown by the green line in Fig. 2(a). It was found that the low absorption of 2.5% was generated by only the ITO film, without any resonance. Then, the absorption of a multiply-layer structure coated with SiO2 grating was calculated, as indicated by the red line in Fig. 2(a). A resonance appeared near 1121.24 nm, with almost no absorption. When a 19 nm-thick ITO film was coated on the grating, it resulted in an intrinsic loss rate of the structure and caused a strong absorption peak at 1121.17 nm, as shown by the black line in Fig. 2(a). The reflectance of the absorption was found to be 10−5, as shown in Fig. 2(b). The half-width at half-peak was ∆λ = 0.10733 nm, which depended on the summary of the intrinsic loss rate and the external leakage rate. As a perfect electrical conductor (PEC) was used in the bottom layer of the structure, the transmission (T) was 0. The implementation process of perfect absorption was analyzed. When the thickness of the ITO film is changed from t = 0 to t = 32 nm, while keeping other parameters constant, the external leakage rate, intrinsic loss rate, and absorption were calculated, as shown in Fig. 3(c). There is little change in the external leakage rate γ, while the intrinsic loss rate δ gradually increases with the increase of ITO thickness t. At t = 19 nm, the external leakage equals the intrinsic loss rate (γ = δ), and critical coupling occurs, which results in ultra-perfect absorption. If γ > δ, the system is in a state of over-coupling (γ > δ). On the other hand, if γ < δ, the system is in a state of under-coupling (γ < δ). When the ITO thickness is adjusted, the system changes from over-coupling to critical coupling and under-coupling. This change leads to an increase in absorption from near-zero to perfect absorption. However, if the thickness of the ITO film is further increased, the absorption decreases.

To return to the critical coupling for different periods P, t is necessary to adjust the intrinsic loss rate. Based on the results in Fig. 3(c), the intrinsic loss rate increases as the ITO thickness t increases. In Fig. 4(c),(d), the intrinsic loss rate varies with the ITO thickness t for different periods P = 660.0, 718.8, 780.0, and 860.0 nm. As t increases, the external leakages almost remain unchanged, and the intrinsic loss rates increase almost linearly for all periods P. The critical coupling occurs when the curves of the external leakage rate intersect that of the intrinsic rate, as labeled by the circles in Fig. 4(c),(d).

At these critical coupling points labeled by the circles, the parameters of structures were given, (P = 660 nm, t = 13 nm), (P = 718.8 nm, t = 19 nm), (P = 780 nm, t = 10 nm), (P = 860 nm, t = 6 nm). The absorptions were then calculated when their parameters were set according to the critical coupling points, as shown in Fig. 4(e). It is found that by adjusting the grating period and ITO film thickness for critical coupling, ultra-perfect absorptions were achieved for all wavelengths. In summary, it is possible to tune the wavelength by adjusting the grating period and ITO film thickness for critical coupling.

Conclusions

In summary, we have designed a structure composed of ENZ-SiO2 grating and a dielectric multilayer and realized ultra-perfect absorption with a maximum absorption of 99.999%. The wavelength and Q factor of absorption were adjusted by changing the grating period and thickness, respectively. The ITO film thickness was adjusted to match the grating parameters variation, realizing the critical coupling condition and achieving ultra-perfect absorption. Under critical coupling, the Q factor can be tuned from 4350 to 35,800 and the wavelength is adjusted from 1050 to 1282 nm. The perfect absorptions were achieved for different incidence angles when the ITO film thickness is adjusted. A center-symmetric structure was designed based on critical coupling to achieve polarization insensitivity. Our research may provide new insights and ideas for designing wavelength- and Q- adjustable absorbers, sensors, and photodetectors. (Supplementary table S1)

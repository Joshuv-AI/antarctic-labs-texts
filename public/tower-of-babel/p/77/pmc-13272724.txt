Eco-friendly-synthesized chitosan–zinc oxide–selenium nanocomposites: characterization and insecticidal activity
Authors: Tharwat A Selim, Medhat E Owda, Mohamed A M El-Tabakh, Salem S Salem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this work, the chitosan–zinc oxide–selenium nanocomposites (Ch–ZnO–Se NCs) were prepared in an affordable and eco-friendly way. Throughout the synthesis stage, chitosan served as reducing and stabilising agent. Different analytical techniques were employed to characterize the resulting nanocomposites. The findings demonstrated that irregular spherical-nanoparticles with a mean diameter of 73.95 nm were the predominant feature of the generated Ch–ZnO–Se NCs. The present study demonstrates that the mosquito vector Culex pipiens exhibit larvicidal and adulticidal effects upon exposure to the generated Ch–ZnO–Se NCs. The 23.308, 57.167 μg/ml and 30.632, 52.063 μg/ml were the recorded LC50 and LC90 values, respectively. Adult mortality reached 97.33% at the same dose, whereas the maximum larval mortality reached 100% at 60 μg/ml. As applied doses rose, the glutathione S-transferase (GST) enzyme increased but the acetylcholinesterase (AChE) enzyme declined. Molecular docking studies were conducted on concentrations of Ch–ZnO–Se NCs against acetylcholinesterase (AChE, PDB: 5x61) and glutathione S-transferase (GST, PDB: 1pn9) using MOE 2015. Concentrations (10, 20, 30, 40, 50, and 60 μg/ml) of Ch–ZnO–Se NCs exhibited stronger binding affinities than the controls, with 40 μg/ml showing the highest interaction (− 8.4233 kcal/mol) for AChE and 30 μg/ml (− 6.6624 kcal/mol) for GST. The results align with in-vitro assays, suggesting these compounds as potential inhibitors.Keywords: Mosquito vector, Culex pipiens control, Synthesized chitosan bimetallic nanoparticles

Introduction

The convergence of nanotechnology and biopolymer science in the past decades has opened up new possibilities in biomedical applications, namely in the elaboration of multifunctional composites based on inorganic nanoparticles and natural polymers. This is a significant improvement. The biopolymer chitosan, which is produced by breaking down the adenosine cellulose in crustacean shells and deacetylation of chitin found in fungal cell walls, has received significant attention for its intrinsic antimicrobial properties, ability to break down food waste rapidly, and its biodegradability (Crognale et al. 2022). Endowed with the ability of chemical modification and complexation of metal ions by its excellent OH and NH2 groups, chitosan can be utilized to prepare functional hybrid materials. Composites are especially attractive for biomedical applications including cancer treatment, tissue science, wound healing, and targeted drug delivery (Crognale et al. 2022). Chitosan plays a dual role as both a reducing agent and stabilizing matrix in the preparation of nanomaterials under mild aqueous or thermal conditions (Hashem et al. 2022, Shehabeldine et al. 2022). Its functional groups reduce metal ions, while its polycationic polymer structure stabilizes the resulting nanoparticles, enabling green, low-energy synthesis routes (Dhanavel et al. 2018, Dhaffouli 2025). When blended with chitosan, ZnO not only increases the mechanical properties of the composite but also synergistically enhances antimicrobial and anticancer behavior, so these nanocomposites hold potential for next-generation advanced biomedical applications (Alavi & Nokhodchi 2020). No less important in nanomedicine applications are selenium (Se) nanoparticles, which, being antioxidant and anticancer, have emerged as pivotal tools to combat oxidative stress and manage redox pathways in cells (Yousef et al. 2025, Wu et al. 2021, Zambonino et al. 2023). Chitosan is a suitable stabilizing matrix for Se nanoparticles by preventing aggregation, reducing cytotoxicity, and selenium-controlled release, functions advantageous in therapeutic as well as preservation regimes (Zambonino et al. 2023, Abid et al. 2021). One such example in this area is represented by the study, which tackled zinc oxide-chitosan conjugates’ improvement of cisplatin’s anticancer efficacy in the treatment of breast cancer (Mohamed et al. 2024). Also, green processes for the synthesis of these composites further enhanced their practical applicability to real-world conditions green synthesis offers a promising alternative by employing natural reducing agents, benign solvents, and energy-efficient processes to produce functional nanomaterials with minimal ecological impact (Dhaffouli 2025). For example, cost-effective synthesis of crab shell derived zinc oxide/chitosan nanocomposites has established that omnipresent natural resources such as crab shells can be used to obtain high quality chitosan, which, when combined with ZnO nanoparticles, gives nanocomposites that are not only cost-effective but also effective in various applications in biomedicine (Devi et al. 2024, Vasile et al. 2014). A recent publication illustrated the synthesis of Zn-Se nano on N-doped mesoporous-carbon from chitosan and a zinc based bio-MOF (Nemati & Rangraz 2025). The potential for immobilizing metal chalcogenides such as ZnSe on chitosan matrices not only expands the functional possibilities of these composites but also the range of opportunities for the use of chitosan for inducing diverse reactions through virtue of its complexation and structure-tailoring ability (Ali et al. 2023). Although between chitosan–ZnO and chitosan–Se composites, both systems take advantage of the unique characteristics of chitosan-showing electroactive nanoparticles which show outstanding biomedical benefits. The complementarity of the two composite families implies that further research could even explore hybrid systems in which ZnO and Se nanoparticles are hosted within a single matrix of chitosan to achieve multifunctional therapeutic properties (Rubaka 2023, Abid et al. 2021). Without a doubt, mosquitoes are a major menace to the entire planet because they spread so many diseases. They are the primary carriers of both malaria and filariasis, with millions of cases reported globally in recent years (Cromwell et al. 2020). The Rift Valley fever virus (El-Bahnasawy et al. 2013b), Wuchereria bancrofti (Zotzmann et al. 2017), and Western Nile virus (El-Bahnasawy et al. 2013a) are all spread by the common Egyptian Culex pipiens (Mahmoud et al. 2022). Numerous recent studies have examined the potential for mosquitoes to spread the hepatitis C virus (Abdallah et al. 2021, Gabarty et al. 2022). To protect public health and stop the spread of diseases that mosquitoes transmit, mosquito control is crucial. Many studies have been conducted on mosquito control (Alruhaili et al. 2025, Hasaballah et al. 2022, Hashem et al. 2022). Synthetic insecticides like organochlorine chemicals and organophosphates have been the primary means of controlling mosquitoes, but their use has failed due to the harm they do to people, animals, and the environment (Ghosh et al. 2012, Pavela et al. 2019). In the realm of mosquito control, plant resources, and marine resources have recently become a competitive alternative to synthetic insecticides (El-Tabakh et al. 2025). Because of its affordability, safety, and environmental benefits, the manufacturing of nanoparticles from natural resources is a major interest (Selim et al. 2025a,b, Prasad et al. 2021). When used to kill mosquitoes, bimetallic nanoparticles may be poisonous and effective than monometallic ones. Furthermore, different mosquito species may respond differently to these nanoparticles (Minwuyelet et al. 2025). Depending on the binding properties of the nanoparticles and the reducing and stabilizing chemicals used in their synthesis, the effectiveness of a unique combination of bimetallic nanoparticles may increase or decrease (Sharma et al. 2019, Balumahendhiran et al. 2025). Assessing the larvicidal and adulticidal effects of extracts made from Ch–ZnO–Se NCs against the vector C. ppipiens mosquito was the aim of the current study (Scheme 1).

Materials and methods

Chitosan was purchased from Alfa Aesar with a degree of deacetylation 85 % with average molecular weight (MW 100–150 kDa), viscosity grade (medium), Zn(CH3COO)2·2H2O and Na2SeO3·5H2O from Sigma-Aldrich.

Preparation of chitosan–ZnO–Se NCs

Initially, 0.2% chitosan was thoroughly mixed with 0.1% acetic acid to create a genuine solution, which was subsequently filtered to produce a well-homogeneous mixture. The second step involved adding 2 mM zinc acetate and 2 mM sodium selenite solutions to the chitosan solution, which was then heated for 3 h at 70 °C while being stirred magnetically. The Ch–ZnO–Se NCs precipitate was separated using a centrifuge, which operated at 4000 rpm for 10 min. After that, the finished Ch–ZnO–Se NCs was dried by placing it in an oven set to 50 °C for the whole night.

Characterization of Ch–ZnO–Se NCs

Chitosan-based nanoparticles containing encapsulated agents were subjected to detailed physicochemical characterization using a set of instrument-specific analytical methods. Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectra were acquired using a Jasco FT-IR 6100 spectrometer (Japan) within the wavenumber range of 4000–400 cm−1. The structure of Ch–ZnO–Se NCs was investigated through X-ray diffraction (XRD) study using a Cu Kα-equipped Seifert3003TT diffractometer. Using a Malvern Nano ZS Zetasizer (UK), dynamic light-scattering (DLS) was used to measure the nanoparticles’ hydrodynamic diameter and surface charge (zeta potential), with measurements conducted at 25°C in 4 mL quartz cuvettes following appropriate sample dilution. Morphological examination was performed via EDX and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) utilizing a JEOL TEM-2100 system (Japan) operated at 200 kV. These complementary techniques enabled a thorough assessment of the structural and colloidal attributes of the prepared nanoparticles. ICP-MS was used for analysis of element concentrations (ICP-MS system, Agilent 7900, Tokyo, Japan).

Larvicidal activity

The larvicidal efficacy of Ch–ZnO–SeNCs against C. pipiens mosquitoes was evaluated using the World Health Organization’s methodology (Organization 2005). The 25 mosquito larvae in the study’s early third instar were selected and placed in 500 mL plastic cups. The cups were filled with 250 mL of tap water, divided into 249 mL of water and 1 mL of concentration being tested. To facilitate comparisons with the control group, the Ch–ZnO–SeNCs was tested at 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, and 60 μg/ml. Twenty-five larvae in 500 mL of tap water were used to test the control and Ch-ZnO-Se NCs simultaneously under the same conditions. Twenty-four hours following the treatment, mortality percentages were recorded.

Adulticidal activity

Twenty-five female C. pipiens mosquitoes were transferred from the colony to a holding section (plastic tube) for an hour to assess the adulticidal activity. The exposure section, or opposite side, which included Whatman filter papers no. 1 (10*10 cm), was then carefully moved to them. The Ch–ZnO–SeNCs was applied to these filter sheets. The experimental group received papers made with distilled water, zinc acetate, and sodium selenite (Organization 2022). Based on its larvicidal qualities, the adult received identical dosages of the Ch–ZnO–Se NCs. Following the exposure time, a cotton bag soaked in a 10% sucrose solution was given to both insects, who were then placed on top of the mesh net. After that, they cautiously returned to the holding enclosure. For every concentration, 3 replicates were demonstrated. In accordance with reference (Finney 1971), adult mortality was assessed 24 h following exposure, and the lethal doses LC50 and LC90 were established by probit analysis.

Biochemical assay

In addition to the untreated (control) group, first instar larvae from the established colony were treated with varying quantities (10, 20, 30, 40, 50, and 60 μg/ml.) of Ch–ZnO–Se NCs. Three replicates of each concentration were tested. Fifty larvae were taken from each treatment when the third instar larvae emerged. utilizing a Teflon homogenizer soaked in broken ice, collected larvae were homogenized in distilled water for 5 min. After homogenized samples were centrifuged for 10 min at 6000 rpm. In a chilled centrifuge, the supernatant was utilized for further biochemical tests. Following acetylcholine bromide as a substrate, acetylcholinesterase (AChE) activity was assessed following the procedure briefly outlined by (Simpson et al. 1964). Using 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene (CDNB) as a substrate, the activity of glutathione S-transferase (GST) was assessed using the procedure briefly outlined by (Kao et al. 1989).

Molecular docking

The Gaussian 09 software’s output was used to construct the compounds’ structures in the PDB file format. The protein data bank (http://www.rcsb.org.pdb) provided the crystal structures of GST (PDB ID: 2imi) and AChE (PDB ID: 5ivi). The MOE 2015 program was used for the molecular docking investigations.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics such as the mean and standard deviation (SD) were computed for each treatment. Probit analysis was used to get the Chi-square value, LC50, and LC90 with a 95% confidence interval based on the average larval mortality data. The statistical analysis included determining lower and higher confidence intervals, computing Chi-square values, and performing a one-way analysis of variance. The software used for this study was SPSS (version 25). The LSD post hoc test was used for pairwise comparisons.

Results

XRD analysis

Figure 1 shows the X-ray diffraction (XRD) pattern of the Ch–ZnO–Se NCs that confirms its crystalline and amorphous character. The wide peak at 19.37° (Miller indices: (110)) corresponds with the semi-crystalline nature of chitosan, in correspondence with the reference standard (JCPDS No. 39-1894). Narrow diffraction peaks at 2θ = 23.41° and 29.59° are attributed to the (100) and (101) crystal planes of elemental selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs), respectively, which confirm the successful introduction of selenium. The presence of crystalline ZnO phase in the composite is evidenced by prominent peaks at 2θ = 31.67°, 34.32°, 36.14°, 47.39°, 56.41°, 62.67°, and 66.17° for the (100), (002), (101), (102), (110), (103), and (200) hexagonal wurtzite ZnO crystal lattice planes (JCPDS No. 36-1451). In addition, the top at 76.72° also confirms the presence of both ZnO and Se phases in the composite. The selenium diffraction peaks overlap with the hexagonal crystal system with lattice constants a = 4.357 Å and c = 4.945 Å, agreeing with the standard (JCPDS No. 06-0362). FTIR analysis

Figure 2 illustrates the FTIR spectrum of Ch–ZnO–Se NCs synthesized. Ch–ZnO–Se NCs exhibited characteristic peaks: N–H stretching (3420 cm−1), C–H stretching (2983 cm−1), C=O stretching (1658 cm−1), C–O–C stretching (1089 cm−1), C–O bending (1292 cm−1), Se–O coordination at 881 cm−1 and 665 cm−1, and Zn–O at 453 cm−1.

Zeta potential and DLS analysis

Zeta potential (ζ) is a crucial parameter of colloidal stability that reflects the surface charge and electrostatic potential of suspended nanoparticles (Fig. 3A). For the Ch–ZnO–Se NCs synthesized, the ζ-value of − 32.37 mV read confirms a highly anionic surface charge, revealing intense electrostatic stabilization. The hydrodynamic diameter of the Ch–ZnO–Se NCs, as determined by DLS (Fig. 3B), was 230.30 nm on average. The size increase compared to individual ZnO-Se nanoparticles is due to chitosan adsorption on nanoparticle surfaces during synthesis.

Morphological analysis

The TEM results presented in Fig. 4A reveal that most particles aggregate into irregular spherical nanoclusters characterized by a uniform size distribution, with diameters in the range of 50–90 nm. The average diameter of nanocomposite was estimated at 73.95 nm. The selected area-electron diffraction (SAED) pattern of the Ch–ZnO–Se NCs, recorded at a magnification of 10 nm. The polycrystalline nature of the ZnO/Se nanoparticles is established by the presence of individual diffraction spots distributed in concentric rings (Fig. 4B). EDX spectroscopy was conducted on the composite using an internal standard across an energy range of 0–10 keV. The resulting spectrum (Fig. 4C) revealed elemental signals from chitosan (C, O, N), along with Zn and Se, confirming the successful incorporation of these components. The absence of any additional peaks suggests that the composite was obtained in a pure and uncontaminated form. Mapping studies are illustrated in Fig. 4D. The mapping investigation showed homogenous distribution of C, O, N, Zn and Se in the Ch–ZnO–Se NCs. ICP-MS analysis provided definitive quantitative confirmation of successful elemental integration within the ternary Ch–ZnO–Se NCs. Selenium was quantified at 21.4 ppm, while zinc was detected at 43.2 ppm in the final material. These concentrations reflect the effective incorporation of selenium species alongside the ZnO nanostructures firmly anchored onto the chitosan polymeric backbone.

Larvicidal and adulticidal properties of chitosan–ZnO–Se NCs

The Ch–ZnO–Se NCs exhibited larvicidal and adulticidal efficacy against C. pipiens mosquitoes at different doses (10, 20, 30, 40, 50, and 60 μg/ml.). The recorded LC50 and LC90 values were 23.308, 57.167 μg/ml and 30.632, 52.063 μg/ml for both larvae and adult, respectively. The adult mortality rate was 97.33% at a dose of 60 μg/ml, while the maximum larval mortality rate was 100% at that same concentration (Table 1). In comparison to the control, the glutathione S-transferase (GST) enzyme increased with increasing concentrations, but the acetylcholinesterase (AChE) enzyme reduced as applied concentrations rose (Fig. 5A, B).

Discussion

The green synthesis route not only minimizes the quantity of harmful reagents needed but also complies with the increasing demand for sustainable material use in the biomedical and food storage sectors (Soliman and Salem 2025, Salem and Fouda 2021, Salem 2023). Particle size, surface charge, dispersion within the polymer matrix, and the nature of chemical functional groups are all highly influential aspects of the composite’s biological activity (Salem et al. 2023). These features announce the promise of chitosan nanocomposites as the next-generation biomedical materials that may be targeted to the stringent requirements of contemporary therapeutic and diagnostic interventions. The broad absorptive band centered at 3420 cm−1 belongs to the overlap of hydroxyl (–OH) and amine (–NH) stretching vibrations, suggesting the presence of hydrogen-bonded interactions within the composite (Zhang et al. 2023). In addition, a band at 2983 cm−1 is attributed to symmetric and asymmetric –CH and –CH₂ stretching in the chitosan polysaccharide backbone, which is characteristic of its carbohydrate structure. The intense band at 1658 cm−1 is in agreement with the characteristic amide I stretching mode (C=O) of chitosan structure (Azhary et al. 2019).

The spectrum also exhibits certain features associated with the glycosidic linkages of chitosan. The sharp peak at 1089 cm−1 is due to C–O–C stretching in the glycosidic bridge, confirming the integrity of chitosan’s polymeric backbone during synthesis (Dat et al. 2022). Furthermore, the bending vibration of C-O bonds was indicated by the absorption bands at 1658 cm−1 (Apryatina et al. 2022). The absorption at 1292 cm−1 is linked to the bending vibrations of C–O, likely due to residual acetylated groups (Ruman et al. 2021). Similarly, peaks at 1160 cm−1 and 1049 cm−1 are due to asymmetric stretching of C–O–C bonds in saccharide units, confirming chitosan’s structural integrity (Alhusaiki Alghamdi 2022). The composite evidently exhibits selenium-specific spectral features: a peak at 881 cm−1 attests to coordination between chitosan and selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs), which is in agreement with previous research that identified Se–O vibration frequencies in the range of 661–886 cm−1 (Zidan et al. 2024, Khan et al. 2025). The band observed at 665 cm−1 is additional evidence of the Se–O bond formation in line with selenium interaction with the polymeric matrix (Sonali et al. 2024, Tang et al. 2019). The band at 453 cm−1 could be related to ZnO NPs (Wang et al. 2010). In another study ZnO NPs show band at 460 cm−1 (Arun et al. 2025). The XRD results collectively demonstrate the successful synthesis of a crystalline Ch–ZnO–Se NCs with well-defined phases of chitosan, ZnO, and selenium demonstrating its structurally heterogeneous but integrated architecture. Polarity and ζ-value magnitudes (typically between − 100 and +100 mV) determine the trend of particle flocculation: systems with ζ-values above ±30 mV exhibit strong electrostatic repulsion, inhibiting flocculation and maintaining long-term colloidal stability (Kumar and Dixit 2017). The intense negative potential prevents coagulation, rendering the colloidal suspension stable even on prolonged storage (Mariadoss et al. 2022). Chitosan acts as a stabilizer through the formation of a polymeric layer that avoids uncontrolled agglomeration and improves uniform dispersion. The increased hydrodynamic diameter is the resultant effect of the core nanoparticle and the chitosan shell, in conformity with effective surface functionalization and colloidal stabilization (Huang et al. 2017). The TEM micrographs reveal noticeable contrast differences in the Ch–ZnO–Se NCs. The darker areas are attributed to the crystalline ZnO-Se nanoparticles, whereas the lighter areas correspond to the chitosan phase. Furthermore, the images indicate that ZnO and Se nanoparticles are evenly distributed throughout the nanocomposite network. The SAED pattern confirms the successful incorporation of ZnO/Se nanoparticles within the chitosan, as evidenced by the diffraction signals of both phases. The simultaneous presence of both elements at substantial and analytically significant levels strongly indicates the formation of a chemically integrated nanocomposite rather than a mere physical blend of separate components. This outcome underscores the efficiency of the applied co-precipitation followed by thermal treatment protocol in embedding zinc and selenium species within the chitosan matrix, thereby establishing a robust foundation for subsequent physicochemical and functional evaluations. In many countries, especially developing ones, diseases spread by mosquitoes pose a severe threat to public health. Since mosquitoes have become resistant to traditional pesticides, the search for eco-friendly substitutes has become crucial (Benelli 2018). It is thought that plant extracts work well as insecticides (Pavela 2008). According to recent research, using artificially made bimetallic mosquitocidal nanoparticles may yield better outcomes(Murugan et al. 2016). Bimetallic nanoparticles have been shown in recent research to be more biologically effective than monometallic nanoparticles in the control of mosquitoes (Elfeky et al. 2020, Minal and Prakash 2020). The toxicity of nanoparticles in relation to the filariasis vector Cx. pipiens has been the subject of numerous investigations (Murugan et al. 2017, Hasaballah et al. 2022). The Current research indicates that the bimetallic Ch–ZnO–SeNCs exhibited larvicidal and adulticidal efficacy against C. pipiens mosquitoes. The recorded LC50 and LC90 values were 23.308, 57.167 μg/ml and 30.632, 52.063 μg/ml for both larvae and adult, respectively. The adult mortality rate was 97.33% at a dose of 60 μg/ml, while the maximum larval mortality rate was 100% at that same concentration. There have also been reports of natural extracts like plant nanoparticles being used as pesticides. Lagenaria siceraria and its mediated ZnO-NPs markedly enhanced the larvicidal activity of Anopheles stephensi, with an LC50 of 56.46 ppm (Kalpana et al. 2020). Compared to the crude extract of S. officinalis, So-ZnO-NPs showed a much higher larvicidal action against the mosquitoes studied (Hasaballah et al. 2022). Study (Minal and Prakash 2016) examined the effects of concentrations of bimetallic nanoparticles on Anopheles mosquito larvae in their third instar. Our study confirms the results of (Soni and Prakash 2014), who demonstrated that the adulticidal effect against C. quinquefasciatus was enhanced by the synthesis of silver nanoparticles using Azadirachta indica. The adulticidal effect of the extract from Acacia caesia leaves and the resultant AgNPs against An. subpictus (LD50 = 18.66 μg/ml), Ae. abopictus (LD50 = 20.94 μg/ml), and C. tritaeniorhynchus (LD50 = 22.63 μg/ml) were shown to be strong by (Benelli and Mehlhorn 2016). These results are consistent with the current study’s findings. According to (Suresh et al. 2015), the adulticidal effect of the crude material produced LC50 and LC90 values of 174.14 and 422.29, respectively. The LC50 and LC90 values for AgNPs derived from Phyllanthus niruri were 6.68 ppm and 23.58 ppm, respectively. Adults died as a result of these treatments. Mosquitoes can be controlled more successfully with bimetallic nanoparticles. By providing two distinct interfaces, the efficacy is increased and may provide superior larvicidal and adulticidal properties (Ragab et al. 2024, Abdallah et al. 2024). Glutathione S-transferase (GST) and acetylcholinesterase (AChE), which shield insects against insecticidal substances, have been shown to be crucial for stress physiology and to be involved in intracellular transport as well as several biosynthetic processes (Rehman et al. 2019). ACHA and GST activities were assessed in the current study. In comparison to the control, the glutathione S-transferase (GST) enzyme increased with concentrations, whereas the acetylcholinesterase (AChE) enzyme decreased with concentrations. In the same context, study (Al-Solami 2021) shown that, in comparison to larvae fed with various plant extracts and/or positive controls, the extract of Lantana camara considerably reduced the AChE activity in the larvae. Based on these findings, it has been proposed that L. camara extract considerably lowers antioxidant production in comparison to other plant extracts, which may be the cause of a higher mosquito fatality rate (Abutaha et al. 2018). Moreover, (Gülçin et al. 2016), found that the extract of L. camara exhibited the most notable larvicidal effects since it was able to lower the GST level, which allowed the toxicity of L. camara to overwhelm the larvae’s immune system earlier than it did for other plants (Gülçin et al. 2016). In the same context, (Selim et al. 2022) resulted that AChE showed different activity patterns, where it was significantly increased in columbamine and b-sitosterol treatments, and decreased in AC, palmatine, and jatrorrhizine treatments, whereas GST was significantly increased in all tested materials.

Conclusion

This research introduces an eco-friendly and economically practical biosynthetic approach for synthesizing chitosan-supported bimetallic Ch–ZnO–SeNCs, taking advantage of chitosan’s excellent biocompatibility and its ability to serve as a versatile polymeric template. Thorough physical and chemical characterization performed using FTIR, TEM, XRD, and EDX techniques verified the formation of well-organized nanostructures displaying clear morphological features and elemental composition consistent with successful embedding of both zinc oxide and selenium within the chitosan structure. The biosynthesized polymer bimetallic Ch–ZnO–Se NCs from chitosan had larvicidal, adulticidal and enzymes activities against vector C. pipiens mosquitoes. The results of molecular simulation align with in-vitro assays against vector C. pipiens mosquitoes, suggesting these compounds as potential inhibitors this vector.

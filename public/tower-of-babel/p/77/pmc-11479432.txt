Integrated network pharmacology and transcriptomics to reveal the mechanism of Passiflora against depressive disorder: An observational study
Authors: Wei Wang, Gao-Qiang Zhai, Ming Xin, Jun Li, Jun-Juan Liao, Jia Liang, Chang-Bao Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Relevant studies have pointed out that Passiflora could relieve depressive disorder (DD) related symptoms, such as anxiety and insomnia, but its mechanism in DD has not been reported. In this study, DD-related transcriptome data was extracted from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Subsequently, 50 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened by “limma,” and the enrichment analysis of these DEGs revealed that they were associated with neuro-inflammatory-related signaling pathways, including IL-17, TNF, NF-kappa B, etc signaling pathways. Then, CCDC58, CXCL5, EGR1, LOC101929855, SCML1, and THBS1 were screened as biomarkers of DD by the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) analysis. Moreover, Harmaline, Harmine, Quercetin, and Kaempferol were the key chemically active ingredients of Passiflora. Noticeable, THBS1 and Quercetin were connected closely. In addition, the quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) confirmed that the key biomarkers (EGR1 and THBS1) were significantly lowly expressed in DD samples. In summary, we identified 2 key biomarkers of DD and 4 key chemically active ingredients of Passiflora. The potential mechanism of antidepressant effect of DD associated with neuro-inflammatory responses and neurotransmitter function. These might related to the synergistic activity of its key active ingredients with TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, etc, which work with EGR1 and THBS1 to regulate IL-17, NF-kappa B, TNF, etc signaling pathways. These findings might help to deepen the understanding of the mechanism of Passiflora in clinical treatment of DD.

1. Introduction

Depressive disorder (DD) is a common neuropsychiatric disorder characterized by persistent depression and loss of interest, with severe cases exhibiting symptoms such as delusion, hallucination, and suicidal behavior.[1,2] DD is the second most common human disease after cardiovascular disease, with millions of deaths attributed to it every year. In recent years, the global prevalence of DD had reached 4.4%, with a prevalence of over 5% in China.[3] The etiology of DD is complex, affected by environmental, psychological and endocrine factors and their combined effects. Early diagnosis and appropriate treatment are essential to reduce morbidity and mortality of DD.[4,5] Currently, chemical drugs such as escitalopram, fluoxetine, and oxazepam were widely used in the clinical treatment of DD, but the effectiveness and safety of chemical drug remains controversial due to use restriction, potential side effect, and drug addiction.[6–8] Therefore, the use of natural therapies in the treatment of DD has come into people’s view, and modern medicine urgently needs to find more safe and natural herbal remedies that can complement or replace drug therapy.

Clinical trials demonstrated that the use of Passiflora in chronic insomnia patients may have therapeutic effects on sleep disorders, memory losses, and degenerative brain diseases.[9] Passiflora also showed positive effects on anxiety disorders. Akhondzadeh et al pointed out that Passiflora extract and oxazepam can effectively treat generalized anxiety disorder, and Passiflora extract has lower damage than oxazepam.[10] Sarris et al also pointed out that saffron, Rhodiola rosea, and other plants have antidepressant effects, while chrysanthemum, Ginkgo biloba leaves, Passiflora, Scutellaria baicalensis, and other plants exhibit anti activity.[11] Appel et al further studied its mechanism and pointed out that the mechanism of Passiflora in the treatment of anxiety or tension may be mediated by an inhibitory neurotransmitter gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) system, including affecting GABA receptor affinity and GABA uptake, which is related to various neuropsychiatric disorders.[12] However, the specific target and related mechanism of Passiflora in DD treatment have not been reported.

Network pharmacology is a new discipline based on systems biology theory, which analyzes biological systems through network analysis and selects specific signal nodes for multi-target drug molecule design. Based on network pharmacology analysis, many drugs have been studied and used in the treatment of DD, such as Shugan Jieyu capsule and Chaihu Longgu Muli decoction.[13,14] Network pharmacology approaches can integrate multiple bioinformatics data and visualize it. Owing to not relying on traditional laboratories, network pharmacology further reducing the cost and time of research, shows obvious advantages. Therefore, in this study, through network pharmacology methods, we used DD transcriptome data from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, identified Passiflora targeted biological markers for the treatment of DD. further analyzed possible mechanisms of action, providing new methods and ideas for the early clinical diagnosis and targeted treatment of DD.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data extraction

The MDD-related datasets were downloaded from the GEO (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database. The GSE32280 dataset (Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array) contains 8 MDD and 8 healthy control (HC) peripheral blood samples, and the GSE201332 dataset (Agilent-052909 CBC lncRNAmRNA V3) contains 20 MDD and 20 HC peripheral blood samples. Besides, the chemically active ingredients of Passiflora were obtained from the previous reports.[15]

2.2. Functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

In this study, the DEGs between 8 DD and 8 HC samples in GSE32280 dataset were compared by “limma” R package (version 3.52.4) (|log2FC| > 0.5, P-value < .05).[16] The functional enrichment analysis of these DEGs were conducted by “clusterprofiler” R package (version 4.4.4) for further studies (adj-P-value < .05).[17]

2.3. Screening for the biomarkers of DD

The protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed to investigate the relationship of DEGs by the STRING database (connectivity > 1) (https://string.embl.de/).[18] Then, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) analysis was used to screen the biomarkers by “glmnet” R package.[19] Then, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves of each biomarker were drawn to study the ability to distinguish the DD by “pROC” R package (version 1.17.0.1) in both GSE32280 and GSE201332 datasets.

2.4. Potential mechanism analyses of Passiflora in DD

In this study, the targeted drugs of biomarkers were predicted in Drug-Gene Interaction database (DGIdb, http://www.dgidb.org/). On the other hand, totals of 17 chemically active ingredients of Passiflora were obtained from the previous reports, which contained Vitexin, Isovitexin, Schaftoside, Isoschaftoside, Orientin, Isoorientin, Swertisin, Apigenin, Luteolin, Quercetin, Kaempferol, Chrysin, Harmine, Harmol, Harman, Harmaline, and Harmalol.[15] The targeted genes of 17 chemically active ingredients of Passiflora were predicted in Comparative Toxicogenomics database (CTD, http://ctdbase.org/), and the key biomarkers were obtained by intersecting the biomarkers and targeted genes using “venn.” Besides, the expressions of the key biomarkers between DD and HC were compared by “rank-sum test.”

Based on it, the key biomarkers-17 chemically active ingredients of Passiflora network were constructed by “Cytoscape.” Then, the key chemically active ingredients of Passiflora were obtained by intersecting 17 chemically active ingredients of Passiflora and the chemically active ingredients of DD which were predicted in Coremine Medical database (https://www.coremine.com/medical/). Similarly, the key traditional Chinese medicines were obtained by intersecting the traditional Chinese medicines of key biomarkers which were predicted in Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology Database and Analysis Platform (TCMSP, http://tcmspw.com/tcmsp.php) and the traditional Chinese medicines of DD which were predicted in Coremine Medical database. Then, the key chemically active ingredients of key traditional Chinese medicines were obtained by intersecting the chemically active ingredients of key traditional Chinese medicines which were predicted in TCMSP (OB ≥ 30%, DL ≥ 0.18) and the chemically active ingredients of DD.

Furthermore, the protein structure of the key chemically active ingredients of Passiflora and key chemically active ingredients of key traditional Chinese medicines were downloaded, and the molecular docking was used to study the inter-molecular interactions between key biomarkers and key chemically active ingredients of Passiflora, between key biomarkers and key chemically active ingredients of key traditional Chinese medicines by “AutoDockvina” (docking binding energy < -1.2 kcal/mol), respectively.[20,21]

2.5. Validation of the expression of key biomarkers

The quantitative real-time Polymerase Chain Reaction (qRT-PCR) was performed to validate the expression of key biomarkers in DD and HC blood samples. Total RNA was extracted using TRIZol (Thermo Fisher, Shanghai, CN), and the qPCR reactions were performed using SureScript-First-strand-cDNA-synthesis-kit (Servicebio, WuHan, CN). This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Guangxi Hospital Division of The First Affiliated Hospital, Sun Yat-sen University ethical review committee.

3. Results

3.1. DEGs were associated with neuro-inflammatory-related signaling pathways

There were 50 DEGs (17 up-regulated and 33 down-regulated) between 8 DD and 8 HC samples in GSE32280 dataset (Fig. 1a). As the perspective of function, these 50 DEGs were enriched to neutrophil, myeloid leukocyte chemotaxis and migration, chemokine-mediated signaling pathway, response to lipopolysaccharide, and etc 117 Gene Ontology (GO) functions (Fig. 1b, Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N455). And these genes were associated with chemokine signaling pathway, IL-17 signaling pathway, TNF signaling pathway, NF-kappa B signaling pathway, and etc 23 Kyoto Encylopaedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways. It was worth noting that ten DEGs were associated with cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (Fig. 1c and d, Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N456).

3.2. CCDC58, CXCL5, EGR1, LOC101929855, SCML1, and THBS1 were defined as biomarkers of DD

In this study, the PPI network was constructed with 19 nodes and 53 protein interaction relationship pairs (connectivity > 1) (Fig. 2a). Six biomarkers including CCDC58, CXCL5, EGR1, LOC101929855, SCML1, and THBS1 were obtained by LASSO analysis (Fig. 2b). The area under the ROC curve (AUC value) of 4 biomarkers (CXCL5, EGR1, SCML1, and THBS1) were >0.7 in both GSE32280 and GSE201332 datasets (CCDC58 and LOC101929855 were not matched in GSE201332 dataset) (Fig. 2c).

3.3. Mechanism analyses of Passiflora in DD

EGR1 and THBS1 were defined as the key biomarkers by intersecting the 6 biomarkers and 3221 targeted genes (Fig. 3a). Both of these 2 key biomarkers were significantly lowly expressed in DD group (Fig. 3b). The results of qRT-PCR were also showed that they were significantly lowly expressed in DD samples (P < .05) (Fig. 3c). The targeted drugs of biomarkers were predicted and the results indicated that there was only 1 targeted drug of EGR1 (Genipin) and 8 targeted drugs of THBS1, including Simvastatin, Pioglitazone, ABT-510, Corticotropin, Methylcellulose, Vesnarinone, Camptothecin, and Lovastatin (Table 1).

4. Discussion

In recent years, network pharmacology has been widely used to explore the mechanisms of drugs and their active ingredients in treating diseases, providing a theoretical basis for clinical treatment. Clinical trials have found that Stephania tetrandra can produce therapeutic effects on various degenerative brain diseases and neuropsychiatric disorders, and this treatment mechanism may be related to neurotransmitters, but the specific target genes and active mechanisms are not clear.[9,10] Therefore, this study will further explore the active mechanism of S. tetrandra in treating depression, clarify the target points of action, and provide a theoretical basis for its clinical treatment.

We first identified 50 genes that significantly changed in patients with depression, and found that they mainly participated in signaling pathways such as chemokines, IL-17, NF-kappa B, and TNF, which is consistent with previous research results.[22,23] In the nervous system, chemokines play a role in immune, inflammatory responses, cell communication, synaptic transmission, and neurodevelopment, and are the main factors that connect peripheral and central inflammation and coordinate neuro-inflammatory crosstalk.[24,25] It is noteworthy that this immune migration is related to depression behavior and depression-related pathophysiological processes.[26] Preclinical studies have shown abnormal expression of chemokines in animal models of depression, and their disorder can induce animal depression behavior.[27,28] In addition, Gao et al pointed out through bioinformatics analysis that chemokines such as MIP-4α, MCP-1, TARC, and Eotaxin-4 all have the potential to be diagnostic biomarkers for severe depression patients.[29] Fernandes et al drew a protein landscape of major neuropsychiatric disorders, including schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and depression, and found that these neurological disorders were related to signaling pathways such as IL-17 and TNF.[30] Nadeem et al pointed out that IL-17A secreted by Th17 cells has central toxicity and can induce depression by activating the NF-kappa B and p38 MAPK inflammatory signaling pathways in the central nervous system of mice.[31] It is noteworthy that NF-kappa B signaling activation is significantly associated with depression, and clinical trials have also shown that inhibiting NF-kappa B can prevent neuro-inflammation, improve depression behavior, and alleviate depression.[32–34] Therefore, we believe that regulating these immune/inflammatory-related signaling pathways will play a key role in the treatment of depression.

Subsequently, we intersected 6 depression biomarkers and 3221 S. tetrandra target genes, and obtained 2 key biomarkers (EGR1 and THBS1) and 4 key active substances of S. tetrandra (Harmaline, Harmine, Quercetin, and Kaempferol). Among them, THBS1 is most closely connected with Quercetin. Ramaker et al also found that EGR1 was significantly down-regulated in the prefrontal cortex tissue of postmortem brain samples from schizophrenic patients and was related to the response to antipsychotic drugs,[35] which is consistent with our research results. Simon et al pointed out that genes related to cell growth and apoptosis, cholesterol metabolism, and TNF signaling pathways, including EGR1, were significantly associated with inflammation and played a key role in depression.[36] Zeng et al also pointed out that EGR1, a gene related to cell apoptosis, may participate in the interaction between depression suicide and the neuro-inflammatory system through the MAPK inflammatory signaling pathway.[37] Although there is no relevant report on THBS1 as a biomarker or treatment target for depression, both EGR1 and THBS1 are TGF-β-related genes, and there is a certain co-expression relationship between them. Their common down-regulation plays a role in affecting the cell cycle or cell apoptosis in diseases or tumors.[38–40] In addition, THBS1 has shown some clinical significance in other psychiatric disorders, such as Alzheimer disease,[41,42] which may indicate the possibility of THBS1 as a treatment target for depression. Based on this, we reasonably speculate that the molecular mechanism of EGR1 and THBS1 as drug targets for depression mainly lies in their regulation of neuro-inflammatory responses, in which the TNF signaling pathway and MAPK inflammatory signaling pathway may play a key role.

On the other hand, it has been reported that Harmaline, Harmine, Dimethyltryptamine, and Tetrahydroharmine are the key active substances in Ayahuasca that exhibit antianxiety and antidepressant effects.[43] Harmaline exhibits neuroprotective and antianxiety properties by specifically binding to or inhibiting monoamine oxidase levels that regulate neurotransmitters, such as 5-HT, benzodiazepine, and imidazoline receptors.[44] Interestingly, Harmine antidepressant effect has been reported to be reversed after treatment with GABA receptor antagonists.[45] Li et al[14] pointed out that Quercetin, the active ingredient in Long Valley oyster soup, can inhibit the NF-kappa B and PI3K signaling pathways, while kaempferol can reduce the expression of heterologous neurotransmitter NO, both of which exhibit the ability to lower levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and other cytokines by inhibiting inflammatory cytokine expression and enzyme activity, thereby exerting an antidepressant effect. Gu et al based on network pharmacology research on Acanthopanax senticosus for the treatment of depression also proposed similar views.[46] These studies have confirmed and further improved Appel hypothesis on the mechanism of action of S. tetrandra in neurological diseases, that the active substances in S. tetrandra can reduce neuro-inflammatory responses, regulate neurotransmitter levels, especially by binding to GABA receptors, and inhibit inflammatory cytokine expression, which is the key mechanism for its antidepressant effect. In addition, the molecular docking results of this study show that the binding energy between the main active ingredients and the core target protein is less than −5 kcal/mol, indicating that the active ingredients can stably bind to the core receptor protein and play a role in the treatment of depression.

This study used network pharmacology to explore the mechanism of S. tetrandra in the treatment of depression, and its potential mechanisms involve neuro-inflammatory responses and neurotransmitter function. This is related to the synergistic activity of its key active ingredients, such as Harmaline, Harmine, Quercetin, and Kaempferol, with proteins such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, which jointly regulate signaling pathways such as IL-17, NF-kappa B, and TNF in key targets EGR1 and THBS1. In addition, we also unexpectedly discovered a natural Chinese medicine substance, Ginkgo Semen, which may also be a potential natural antidepressant due to its similarity to the active substances in S. tetrandra and its effectiveness in docking with key disease targets for depression. However, there is currently no relevant research. Therefore, our follow-up research will further confirm the mechanism we proposed through animal or cell experiments, determine the efficacy of S. tetrandra for depression, and pay attention to the clinical significance of Ginkgo Semen.

5. Conclusion

In summary, this study analyzed the depression key disease targets and binding abilities of the key pharmacological components of S. tetrandra based on network analysis and molecular docking technology. The substance basis and mechanism of action that S. tetrandra may have in the treatment of depression were preliminarily explored. This study can provide data support and theoretical basis for the further screening and evaluation of natural herbal active ingredients for antidepressant drugs.

A Schizophrenia‐Spectrum Presentation with Panda Sign: Huntington's Disease Confirmed, Wilson Disease Excluded
Authors: Eva‐Maria Tsapakis, Georgia Zafeiridou, Elizabeth Psoma, Angelos Giannoutsos, Andreas Kiryttopoulos, Konstantinos N Fountoulakis
Published: 2026

A 46‐year‐old man presented with progressive self‐neglect, social withdrawal, cognitive decline, chronic suicidality, and a schizophrenia‐spectrum phenotype since early adulthood. Examination showed formal thought disorder, blunted affect, choreiform hand, arm, leg and torso movements (Video 1), brisk reflexes, broad‐based gait, and impaired tandem walking. Neuropsychology demonstrated deficits in attention, working memory, visuospatial ability, and executive control. Brain MRI revealed a mesencephalic T2‐FLAIR hyperintensity surrounding the red nuclei and substantia nigra, producing the classic “face of the giant panda” sign [1], together with caudate head atrophy, frontal horn enlargement, Sylvian fissure widening, and cortical atrophy (Fig. 1). Low ceruloplasmin and serum copper initially suggested Wilson's disease (WD), but these changes also occur in protein deficiency states, including malnutrition, and other protein‐losing conditions. Kayser‐Fleischer rings were absent, liver ultrasound was normal, ATP7B testing was negative. HTT analysis confirmed Huntington's disease (HD) with a heterozygous expanded CAG repeat (43 ± 2)[2]. This case shows that the panda sign is not pathognomonic of WD and may occur in genetically confirmed HD. When prominent psychiatric symptoms coexist with hyperkinesia, cognitive impairment, and mixed radiological or biochemical findings, diagnosis should not rely on a single imaging sign. Genetic testing was decisive in distinguishing HD from WD and guiding treatment.

Neurological examination showing choreiform hand, arm, leg and torso movements.

T2‐FLAIR axial images (A, B) show red nuclei mimicking a panda face and deep white matter hyperintensity with spared subcortical U‐fibers (red arrow). T1 axial/coronal (C, D) demonstrate caudate‐putamen atrophy typical of HD. Frontal Horn/Intercaudate Distance ratio 1.1 (reference 2.2–2.6).

A. Wilson's disease.

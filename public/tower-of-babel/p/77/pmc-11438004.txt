A fault diagnosis method for analog circuits based on EEMD-PSO-SVM
Authors: Shuhan Zhao, Xu Liang, Ling Wang, Hao Zhang, Guiqiang Li, Jing Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Analog circuit is an crucial component of electronic equipment, and the ability to diagnose its fault state quickly and accurately is essential for ensuring the safety and reliability of these electronic equipment. This paper addresses the problems of low diagnostic accuracy and the difficulties associated with model parameter selection in traditional fault diagnosis methods, particularly when dealing with nonlinear and non-stationary fault signals. A fault diagnosis method for analog circuits is proposed, which utilizes Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition (EEMD) for features extraction, the Maximum Information Coefficient algorithm (MIC) for features selection, and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) for optimizing Support Vector Machine (SVM) classification. Firstly, EEMD is used to adaptively decompose the fault signals in the circuit to extract multi-scale fault features. Secondly, the extracted features are quantitatively evaluated using the Pearson correlation coefficient and energy value analysis, leading to the construction of a fault feature vector is constructed. On this basis, the MIC feature selection algorithm is applied to further optimize the feature vector. Finally, an efficient fault classification model is developed by optimizing the hyperparameters of the SVM model using PSO. The simulation results show that the proposed method effectively overcome the problems of model complexity and low classification accuracy caused by improper selection of wavelet basis function. The accuracy of fault diagnosis and the efficiency of model training are significantly superior to those of traditional methods.

Introduction

Analog circuit plays a crucial role in electronic equipment and systems, with more than 80 % of circuits in current electronic devices being analog. Therefore, the stability and reliability of analog circuits are essential for the proper functioning of the entire system. Generally, faults in the circuits are divided into hard faults and soft faults. Hard faults are generally caused by short circuits or open circuits in components, while soft faults usually arise from deviations of component parameters from their nominal values. When resistance and capacitance drift faults occur in a circuit, the normal output can be affected, resulting in equipment damage and direct economic losses. In serious cases, such faults can lead to safety accidents that endanger personal safety. Unlike hard faults, circuit soft faults often occur unnoticed and are difficult to distinguish due to their subtle fault characteristics. With the continuous improvement of the intelligent level of electronic equipment, the demand for safety and reliability has become increasingly stringent. Therefore, diagnosing the fault state of analog circuits in electronic equipment quickly and accurately holds great theoretical research value and engineering application significance.

At present, the common fault diagnosis methods for analog circuits include Fault analysis (FA) and Fault dictionary (FD). For linear circuits, fault analysis method lists their response equations, combines input and output responses, and sets the tolerance range of components [1]. After the data is calculated, it is compared with the actual value to determine whether the component has a fault. However, this method is limited by subjectivity and accuracy, and requires a lot of calculation time. Fault dictionary method requires professional diagnostic personnel to form a unique circuit fault feature table based on understanding existing circuits and combining their own knowledge and experience [2,3]. Traditional methods are effective in diagnosing hard circuit faults, but with the increasing complexity of electronic equipment, it becomes more difficult to use this method and the classification accuracy decreases.

With the rise of artificial intelligence algorithms, methods for diagnosing fault in analog circuits have evolved into two categories: model-driven and data-driven methods [[4], [5], [6], [7], [8]]. Unlike model-driven method, which need to establish complex circuit physical model, data-driven method relies on actual fault data or operation data for modeling and analysis. The data-driven fault diagnosis process typically consists of main steps: feature extraction and model classification. Feature extraction involves compressing high-dimensional raw data into a low-dimensional feature space that is more easier to analyze. Features may include time domain, frequency domain or time-frequency domain statistics, such as mean, variance, spectrum components, etc. After feature extraction, labeled data including normal and fault data are usually used to train the fault diagnosis model, using classifiers such as support vector machine (SVM), decision tree or neural network. The selection of both feature extraction and model classification techniques is crucial, as it significantly influences the accuracy and validity of the final diagnosis results.

During the operation of analog circuits, signals are often highly nonlinear and noisy, making it essential for feature extraction methods to accurately capture the essential features of these complex signals. However, it is challenging to extract diagnostic features from large-scale and complex circuit data, especially when processing multi-dimensional and non-stationary signal. This complexity necessitates the development or selection of appropriate feature extraction methods to ensure stable diagnostic performance across various fault scenarios. Improving both the accuracy and robustness of fault diagnosis models depends on enhancing the feature extraction method and optimizing the model training process. This is essential for ensuring the reliability of electronic devices and systems, which remains a challenge in current research. Therefore, in-depth investigation of feature extraction and fault classification models, comparison existing algorithms, and the development of novel approaches that effectively complete feature extraction and model classification in analog circuit fault diagnosis hold important research significance and application value.

At present, with the rapid advancement of feature processing technologies, several advantageous feature extraction methods have been successfully applied to analog circuit fault diagnosis. For instance Qu et al. used variational mode decomposition (VMD) to decompose fault data, then verified the diagnostic performance of a VMD combined with adaptive dynamic density peak clustering (ADDPC) in a noisy environment. However the model's computational complexity remains a concern [9]. Lu et al. proposed an optimization model based on VMD, used kernel principal component analysis (KPCA) to reduce the dimensionality of high-dimensional data and obtain the intrinsic mode function for fault diagnosis [10]. Liu et al. utilized empirical wavelet transform (EWT) to decompose fault signals, extracting amplitude modulated-frequency modulated (AM-FM) components which were then input into a ResNet for classification. Despite achieving good classification, this method faced problems with high model complexity and long model training time [11]. While methods such as VMD and EWT yield promising classification results, they also come with limitations. For examples, EWT needs predicting the wavelet basis function and selecting an appropriate wavelet basis is critical, particularly when signal characteristics are complex or unclear. An incorrect selection can adversely affect feature extraction. Likewise, VMD is highly sensitive to the choice of the number of modes and bandwidth, and improper choice can lead to distortion or mode aliasing during decomposition.

Ensemble empirical mode decomposition (EEMD) is an significant improvement over EMD [12,13]. Compared with the above mentioned feature extraction methods, EEMD does not need to preset any basis function. Instead, it adaptively decomposes signals in a data-driven way. This simplifies the model structure, giving EEMD wider applicability and better adaptability making it widely used in signal processing and other fields. Its high-precision and high-reliability enable effectively processing of nonlinear and non-stationary data, providing a powerful tool for signal analysis.

Currently, a number of studies have explored fault diagnosis across various devices using EEMD. For instance, Xia et al. combined EEMD with an Elman Neural Network (ENN) to classify gearbox health status, achieving average classification accuracy of 90.6 % [14]. Hu et al. used EEMD to decompose the vibration signal generated by train axle box in both time and frequency domains, enabling the model to accurately identify the turnout position and detect the track safety state [15]. In addition, several researchers have employed EEMD in combination with machine learning methods to identify and classify bearings and mechanical rotors [[16], [17], [18], [19], [20]]. These findings show that EEMD-related technologies can achieve high accuracy and speed of fault state classification across various fields. However, research on the fault classification of soft faults in analog circuits using EEMD is still quite limited and needs to be further studied.

This paper presents a fault diagnosis method for analog circuits based on EEMD and particle swarm optimization support vector machine (PSO-SVM). The proposed method achieves superior results in both feature extraction and fault classification. Theoretical background

EEMD feature extraction method

The core of EMD method is to decompose a complex signal into a series of simple intrinsic mode functions (IMFs), which are natural oscillation modes in signal data. However, in certain cases, EMD may encounter modal aliasing issues where signal components with different frequencies become mixed, resulting in unsatisfactory decomposition effect. EEMD solves this problem by introducing white noise [21,22]. The decomposition steps of EEMD algorithm is outlined as follows:(1)Adding white noise:First, add a small-amplitude white noise sequence to the original signal to obtain a series of noisy signals:(1)xi(t)=x(t)+wi(t)here, xi(t) represents the new signal obtained at the i th time, x(t) is the original signal, wi(t) is the white noise sequence,i = 1,2, …,N(N is the total number of noisy signals)(2)Independent EMD decomposition:each signal xi(t) added with white noise is independently decomposed by EMD. Each decomposition will produce a series of IMFs. The key of this step is to extract the intrinsic oscillation mode from the noisy signal.(3)Calculate the average value of IMFs:For each IMF component, calculate its average value in all noisy signal decomposition to reduce the influence of white noise. The formula used can be expressed as:(2)IMFjmean(t)=1N∑i=1NIMFij(t)here, IMFjmean(t) is the collective average of each IMF, n represent the number of white noise sequences added (i = 1,2, …,N), IMFij(t) is the j th IMF component obtained by EMD decomposition after adding white noise for the first time i.(4)Signal reconstruction:using these average IMFs values to reconstruct the signal, the reconstructed signal can be expressed as:(3)Xreconstructed(t)=∑j=1MIMFj(t)+R(t)here, Xreconstructed(t) is the reconstructed signal, IMFj(t) is the average of the j th intrinsic mode function, M is the total number of IMFs, R(t) is the final residual trend, which represents the lowest frequency component in the signal.

SVM theoretical basis

As an efficient classification algorithm, SVM has been widely used in the field of fault diagnosis. One of the key advantages of SVM is its excellent generalization ability, especially when dealing with high-dimensional or small sample data.

In analog circuit fault diagnosis, the task is usually be regarded as a fault classification problem. SVM-based analog circuit fault diagnosis involves training a classifier by analyzing data during the early stage of fault occurrence, building the maximum interval classification hyperplane, and completing the classification task during the early stage of the fault occurrence by distinguishing the early features of the fault. (xi,yi) denotes as the input fault sample set, eigenvector xi∈Rn, n is the spatial dimension of the input fault sample, input fault samples label yi∈{−1,+1}, number of input fault sample i=1,2,3,...,N. The classification idea of SVM assumes that the data is linearly separable, meaning that there is a hyperplane that can completely separate fault data points from the non-fault data points. For two-dimensional data, this hyperplane is represented by a straight line, while in higher-dimensional space, it becomes a plane.

In Rn, the hyperplane can be represented as:(4)w⋅x+b=0here, w=[w1,w2,…,wn]T is the normal vector of the hyperplane and determines the direction of the hyperplane. b stands for bias, which determines the offset (or position) of the hyperplane. x is the vector of the input data.

Meanwhile, for any data point xi in the input fault sample, the fault classification decision function is:(5)f(xi)=sign(w⋅xi+b)when f(xi)>0, xi is classified as positive class (+1); When f(xi)<0, xi is classified as negative class (-1).

The goal of SVM is to find a hyperplane that maximizes the margin between classes. The margin is defined as the distance from the hyperplane to the nearest data points, which are referred to as the support vectors. These support vectors satisfy the following equation:(6)w⋅xi+b=±1and the interval γ is:(7)γ=2|w|

In this case, the problem of solving the optimal classification hyperplane of SVM can be transformed as an optimization problem aimed at maximizing the margin. To maximize the interval, the optimization goal of SVM is to minimize |w|, which corresponds to maximizing 12|w|2, which must meet the classification constraints at the same time. This can be expressed as:(8)minw,b12|w|2(9)subjecttoyi(w⋅xi+b)≥1,∀ihere, yi∈−1,+1, is the actual category label of the entered faulty data.

Furthermore, to solve the above optimization problems, the Lagrange multiplier αi is introduced to construct the Lagrange function, which can be expressed as:(10)L(w,b,α)=12|w|2−∑i=1mαi[yi(w⋅xi+b)−1]

By taking the partial derivative of w and b and making them zero, then it can evolve into a dual problem:(11)maxα∑i=1mαi−12∑i=1m∑j=1mαiαjyiyjxi⋅xj(12)subjectto∑i=1mαiyi=0,αi≥0,∀i

Here, αi and αj are Lagrange multipliers in the dual problem, representing the weight assigned to each sample during optimization. yi and yj represent the category labels of the i th and j th fault samples, respectively. xixj represents the inner product of the i th and j th fault samples. In the constraint, ∑i=1mαiyi=0 ensures that the classifier's hyperplane is valid. αi≥0 guarantees that every Lagrange multiplier is non-negative. ∀i indicates that αi≥0 applies to all fault samples.

For data that is not linearly separable, a kernel function K(xi,xj) can be introduced to map the fault data into a high-dimensional space, where linear separability can be realized. Common kernel functions include the linear kernel, polynomial kernel, radial basis function (RBF) kernel, and sigmoid kernel, etc. These can be expressed as:(15)K(xi,xj)=xi⋅xj(16)K(xi,xj)=(xi⋅xj+c)d(17)K(xi,xj)=exp(−|xi−xj|22σ2)(18)K(xi,xj)=tanh(κxi⋅xj+θ)when kernel functions are introduced, the duality problem mentioned above becomes:(19)maxα∑i=1mαi−12∑i=1m∑j=1mαiαjyiyjK(xi,xj)(20)subjectto∑i=1mαiyi=0,0≤αi≤C,∀ihere, C is the penalty parameter.

Finally, the fault classification decision function can be expressed as:(21)f(x)=sign(∑i=1mαiyiK(xi,x)+b)here, K(xi,x) indicates the SVM kernel function used. In this paper, RBF kernel is used as the SVM kernel function.

When applying SVM to analog circuit fault diagnosis, it is complicated to directly deal with circuit fault information. Therefore, it is necessary to convert multi-class problems into multiple binary classification problems. In analog circuit fault diagnosis, where multiple fault types and high-dimensional fault information present challenges, SVM usually uses two strategies address these multi-class problems.

The first strategy is the One-vs-One (OVO) strategy, where the classification task involving class K faults is decomposed into K(K−1)2 binary classification problems. Each binary classifier is responsible for distinguishing between a pair of fault classes. In each classifier, a voting mechanism or decision function is used to determine the category to which the input data belongs. The second strategy is the One-vs-All (OVA) strategy, where the classification task involving K fault classes is divided into K binary classification problems. Each classifier is tasked with distinguishing one class of faults from all the others. Each classifier makes a prediction on the input data and outputs a confidence score. Finally, the input data is assigned to the category with the highest confidence score. In this study, the RBF kernel function was selected for SVM classification based on the data characteristics, and the OvO strategy was selected to construct EEMD-PSO-SVM fault classification model.

Basic principle of PSO algorithm and process of PSO optimization SVM

SVM is a classifier known for its excellent classification effect, but its performance depends on the selection of key parameters, especially the selection of kernel function and the setting of penalty parameters. For the RBF kernel used in this paper, SVM usually needs to optimize the following two important parameters. Firstly, the penalty parameter C, where a larger value of the penalty parameter can reduce the model training error but also increases the risk of overfitting. Secondly, the RBF kernel parameter γ, which controls the width of the RBF kernel and affect the mapping distribution of fault samples in high-dimensional space. However, manually tuning these two parameters is time-consuming and requires a lot of experiments. PSO, as a global optimization algorithm, can automatically search for the optimal parameter combination, thereby improving the classification performance of SVM. Integrating PSO into the fault diagnosis classification model significantly improves the classification performance of SVM. PSO searches the global optimal solution by simulating the cooperative behavior of particle populations in the search space. The basic idea of PSO algorithm is that particles iteratively updates their position and velocity to search for the optimal solution of parameters.

In the PSO algorithm, each particle represents a candidate solution. For the SVM parameter optimization problem, a particle represent a specific combination of SVM parameters (such as the penalty parameter C and RBF kernel parameter γ). Assuming the search space is two-dimensional, the current position of each particle can be expressed as:(22)xi=[Ci,γi]here, Ci and γi represent the current position of the i th particle and represent the current parameter combination of the SVM.

The particle swarm approaches to the optimal solution by iterating the speed vi and position. Among them, the particle swarm velocity update formula can be expressed as:(23)vi(t+1)=w⋅vi(t)+c1⋅r1⋅(pibest−xi(t))+c2⋅r2⋅(gbest−xi(t))here, w is the inertial weight, which controls the particle's ability to maintain its current velocity. c1 and c2 are learning factors, representing the degree to which the particle tends to the historical optimal position pibest and the population global optimal position gbest. r1 and r2 are the random numbers between [0, 1], representing the random process of particle swarm renewal.

According to the obtained particle swarm updating velocity, the particle position updating formula is as follows:(24)xi(t+1)=xi(t)+vi(t+1)

The position and velocity of the update particle are optimized by iteration, and the optimal solution of the search parameters is obtained. When a predetermined number of iterations is reached, or the improvement of the global optimal solution is no longer significant, the iteration is stopped. Finally, after the optimization, the parameter combination corresponding to the global optimal position, namely the optimal C and γ is output. The SVM model is retrained using the parameter combination, and the optimal fault classifier is finally obtained.

Model establishment and performance evaluation index

The Matlab2022a software was used to analyze and process the collected analog circuit fault data and build the classification algorithm model. The dataset was randomly scrambled and divided into two parts: training set (70 %) and test set (30 %). Four classification models, Random Forest (RF) [23], Particle Swarm Optimization Support Vector Machine (PSO-SVM) [24], Extreme Learning Machine (ELM) [25], and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) [6] were used to classify the faults, respectively.

The confusion matrix is a table used to evaluate the performance of a classification model. In the confusion matrix, rows represent the true categories of the classified data, while columns represent the predicted categories of the classified data. Elements along the diagonal represent the amount of data that has been correctly classified, while off-diagonal elements represent the amount of data that has been incorrectly predicted by the model. The right-most column of the confusion matrix shows the recall rate for each category, and the bottom-most row of the confusion matrix lists the prediction accuracy of each category. Comparing the accuracy, precision, and recall of the prediction results provides a comprehensively evaluation of the performance of the classification model.

Eigenvector processing

Due to the large IMF component after the decomposition of the original signal, it is necessary to identify and select the IMF components with higher energy values, because these components often contain more main information. The calculation of energy values for these IMFs (Fig. 3) can be realized by integrating the modulus square of each IMF in time, in order to quantify the energy contribution of each frequency component to the total signal [27], which is also a key step for understanding signal characteristics and fault diagnosis. Then, the correlation coefficients between each eigenmode component of the analog circuit fault signal and the original experimental signal were calculated [28], and appropriate thresholds were set to screen the IMF component.Fig. 3IMF component energy value distribution map.Fig. 3

Classification effect based on data preprocessing

All 2790 circuit fault datasets were randomly scrambled and divided into two parts: the training set (70 %) and the test set (30 %). Because the original order in the data set was disrupted, after the training set and test set were divided, the number of fault categories in the training set and test set was not exactly the same, which was reflected in the different total number of each row in the confusion matrix. Shuffling the data before classification prediction is conducive to eliminating data order bias, promoting the model to be trained on a wider and more diverse data distribution, and improving the generalization ability of the classification model. Conclusion

To address the problem of resistance and tolerance drift in analog circuits, a fault diagnosis method of analog circuits using EEMD and PSO-SVM is proposed in this paper. Compared with other feature extraction algorithms, using EEMD algorithm to decompose the fault data in the circuit solves the complexity of analog circuit signals and the limitations of traditional methods in nonlinear and non-stationary signal processing, effectively simplifies the model structure, and solves the problem of low accuracy of model classification caused by improper selection of wavelet basis function. Then, Pearson correlation coefficient and energy value screening are used to optimize IMFs components, the IMFs data segments which contains circuit fault information are extracted. Meanwhile, in order to improve the feature extraction performance and the model prediction accuracy, the PSO optimization algorithm was used to optimize the SVM model penalty parameters and RBF kernel parameters globally, and then the optimal classification model parameter combination was determined. Finally, ReliefF, RFE, MIC, mRMR, ILFS and FCFS are used to optimize the fault diagnosis model, which futher improves the classification accuracy of PSO-SVM.

The experimental results show that, compared with other diagnostic methods, the proposed method can effectively extract the circuit fault state characteristic information, which is not only superior to the traditional method in diagnostic accuracy, but also excellent in model training efficiency and robustness, and the fault diagnosis rate achieving 99.88 %. Therefore, this method provides an effective and reliable solution for analog circuit fault diagnosis. In the future, based on the existing EEMD method and combined with multi-scale entropy analysis, the prediction accuracy of feature extraction can be further improved to explore the influence of multi-frequency components on fault diagnosis. Meanwhile, based on the existing offline diagnosis, the application of real-time online fault diagnosis can be studied to develop a system that detects and diagnoses faults in real time, and improves the application potential in industrial field.

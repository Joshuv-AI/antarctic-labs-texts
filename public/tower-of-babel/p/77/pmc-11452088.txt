Breast Reconstruction in a Postbariatric Patient Using a Fleur-de-lis Free Deep Inferior Epigastric Artery Perforator Flap: A Mini-review
Authors: Yanis Berkane, Tristan Beaufils, Mohammad Alabdulkareem, Alla Abu Qasida, Anna Scarabosio, Paul Girard, Eric Watier, Nicolas Bertheuil
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Summary:Breast reconstruction in postbariatric patients presents unique challenges due to substantial weight loss–induced body deformities. We present the case of a 51-year-old woman with massive lower abdominal deformities who underwent a mastectomy treatment followed by a temporary expander-based reconstruction. A year later, a fleur-de-lis-shaped free deep inferior epigastric artery perforator flap and abdominoplasty were performed for autologous reconstruction and abdominal contouring. This design addressed both purposes while allowing a voluminous flap to be harvested on periumbilical perforators. No postoperative complications were found, and the outcomes were favorable. Although deep inferior epigastric artery perforator flaps are standard in autologous breast reconstruction, our case demonstrates the utility of the fleur-de-lis design in massive weight loss patients for vascular safety and volume enhancement. Finally, we discuss this rarely described approach and compare it with other variants, showcasing versatility in addressing complex reconstruction needs in this particular patient population.

Postbariatric patients represent a particular subpopulation in plastic and reconstructive surgery. Massive weight loss leads to substantial body deformities that are challenging to address due to skin laxity and dermal fractures. The abdomen is often the most urgent site to address these patients’ functional impairments, and the vertical or “fleur-de-lis” abdominoplasty and body lift techniques are used to correct both vertical and horizontal deformities.1 In parallel, the deep inferior epigastric artery perforator (DIEP) flap has become the gold standard in microsurgical breast reconstruction. Its reliability, relative technical accessibility, and optimal cosmetic and functional outcomes allow the total replacement of muscle-based approaches based on the rectus abdominis muscle.

Herein, we present the case of a postbariatric patient who underwent a right mastectomy and immediate expander-based reconstruction. A secondary reconstruction was performed using a fleur-de-lis–shaped DIEP flap, allowing both optimal autologous breast reconstruction and lower abdominal body contouring.

CASE REPORT

All clinical care was compliant with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (1964) and the French bioethics laws (2011). Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for this current case report.

The patient was a 51-year-old woman who received a gastric band followed by a gastric bypass 6 years later for morbid obesity. This allowed a 41-kg weight loss, dropping her body mass index from 42.9 to 27.9 kg/m2. She was found with a bulky high-grade in situ carcinoma of the right breast, which led to a total mastectomy with a sentinel node procedure. No lymph node nor distant metastases were found. An expander was placed as an immediate temporary reconstruction, but it was decided with the patient to opt for an autologous definitive reconstruction. On the abdomen, she presented with significant abdominal fat and skin excess in both infra- and supraumbilical compartments. A fleur-de-lis abdominoplasty was indicated, but it was preferred to delay the body-contouring procedure in the case of adjuvant chemotherapy that could compromise wound healing. A delayed DIEP flap based on a fleur-de-lis design was, therefore, performed 12 months after mastectomy.

Preoperative Planning and Surgical Technique

No preoperative computed tomography scan angiography was performed, and perforator vessels were located using a handheld acoustic Doppler ultrasound device. Multiple perforators were located preoperatively. Preoperative markings for a fleur-de-lis abdominoplasty were performed in both standing and supine positions while integrating the perforator vessel locations within the resection areas. Surgery occurred under general anesthesia and orotracheal intubation. After skin preparation and draping, the lower limit of the markings was incised and dissected until reaching the rectus abdominis fascia. The dissection was pursued up to the umbilicus until reaching the marked perforator vessels (Fig. 1A). Two main perforator vessels were identified from the right deep inferior epigastric pedicle and chosen due to their diameter and dissected inside the muscle. One perforator from the left artery was identified and dissected (Fig. 1B). In parallel, the breast was approached using the preexisting scar, and the expander was removed. The right mammary pedicle was dissected in the fifth intercostal space, and one artery and two veins were prepared as recipient vessels.

Intraoperative view. A, Lower abdominal dissection in the suprafascial plane and bilateral deep inferior epigastric pedicle dissection after perforator selection. B, Lateral view of the bilateral periumbilical perforators and inferior epigastric vessels dissected under the rectus abdominis muscles (white asterisk: umbilicus).

A voluminous periumbilical flap (670 g) was raised, flushed, and shaped to fit with the contralateral breast. An intraoperative indocyanine green angiography was performed and revealed optimal perfusion of the flap on the right pedicle only, which was used solely. (See figure, Supplemental Digital Content 1, which displays preoperative marking, perforator vessel mapping, and flap shaping. http://links.lww.com/PRSGO/D549.) Three end-to-end vascular anastomoses were performed (one artery and two veins). After a patency test was performed, a drain was placed behind the flap which was then fixed to the fascia using 2-0 Vicryl sutures and to the surrounding skin pouch using 3-0 Monocryl sutures. The donor site was then closed, including the fascia, and the abdominoplasty was pursued as previously described.2

Outcomes and Follow-up

No major or minor complications were found. The flap was closely monitored for 5 days, and the patient was discharged after drain removal due to the distance to her home (postoperative day 10). The long-term follow-up (6 months) showed optimal results in both donor and recipient sites, and the patient showed high satisfaction with the outcomes (Fig. 2).

DISCUSSION

Although DIEP flaps became the gold standard in autologous breast reconstruction, failure still occurs. Pompei and Farhadi3 have described techniques to enhance vascular reliability and robustness, especially for voluminous flaps. Bipedicled flaps are based on the two DIEP systems and allow unilateral reconstruction of great volume.4 Alternatively, Lee et al4 used a superficial inferior epigastric pedicle in addition to the DIEP to improve vascular safety, especially for venous return.5 Kelly and Pacifico6 showed that a hemi DIEP flap is safe to be harvested on paraumbilical perforators, but that caution should be exercised when further volume is needed.

By designing a fleur-de-lis DIEP flap, we allowed the harvesting of multiple paraumbilical perforator vessels while using both infra- and supraumbilical fat for volume and shaping. This participates in improving vascular safety based on the perforasome theory.7 However, the indication in this case was more related to body-contouring purposes because vertical resection enables optimal reshaping of postbariatric patients with skin and fat excess in both axes.8 We believe that it is worth considering this alternative for autologous reconstruction after curative or prophylactic mastectomy in massive weight loss patients. Salim et al9 described a similar case with successful reconstruction in a postbariatric patient, introducing the fifth zone in a DIEP flap based on two paraumbilical perforators. Robustillo et al10 used a modified fleur-de-lis DIEP flap for voluminous breast reconstruction in a patient with a previous laparotomy. This emphasizes the versatility of this approach, which can help circumvent several hazardous situations. Finally, Lantieri’s team described a similar approach in profunda artery perforator flaps for breast reconstruction, using a fleur-de-lis design to reach greater volumes and wider skin surfaces.11 One major point in breast surgery is reproducible and objective evaluation through patient-reported outcome measures such as the BREAST-Q, which should be assessed in further studies.12–14

In conclusion, fleur-de-lis DIEP procedures can be performed in selected massive weight loss patients, providing vascular safety in large-volume breast reconstructions while also addressing abdominal body contouring.

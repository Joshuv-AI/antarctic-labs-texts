Selpercatinib‐Induced Deep Skin Fissures Triggered by Mechanical Friction in a Patient With RET Fusion‐Positive Lung Adenocarcinoma
Authors: Tomoyuki Ikeuchi, Mitsuhiro Yamamoto, Hirokazu Toge, Naoto Brioka, Akira Yamasaki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTWe report a case of selpercatinib‐induced deep skin fissures in a patient with RET fusion‐positive lung adenocarcinoma. Minor mechanical abrasions triggered severe fingertip fissures, which were likely exacerbated by selpercatinib‐related VEGF receptor inhibition. The condition improved with drug withdrawal and corticosteroids administration, enabling the continuation of treatment with careful management of recurrent episodes.Keywords: deep skin fissures, lung adenocarcinoma, rearranged during transfection (RET) fusion, selpercatinib, vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR)

A 63‐year‐old non‐smoking male was diagnosed with RET fusion‐positive lung adenocarcinoma (cT2aN3M0, stage IIIB). The primary lesion was located in the right upper lobe, with lymph node metastases in the right supraclavicular fossa and bilateral hilar regions. Surgical resection and concurrent chemoradiotherapy were considered inappropriate, and selpercatinib therapy was initiated [1]. He developed selpercatinib‐induced hypersensitivity with grade 3 asparate aminotransferase and alanine aminotransferase elevation. This reaction was addressed by drug withdrawal and prednisolone administration (1 mg/kg/day), followed by the reintroduction of reduced‐dose selpercatinib. After chest CT confirmed shrinkage of the primary lesion, the selpercatinib dose was gradually increased to 240 mg/day.

On day 195, the patient presented with multiple deep skin fissures and severe fingertip pain. He reported that minor abrasions from agricultural work 1 week prior had progressed to deep fissures (Figure 1). After discontinuation of selpercatinib, the patient's skin normalized within 2 months. The patient initially declined further retreatment but consented following the detection of new brain metastases. Selpercatinib was resumed at a dose of 80 mg/day. Prednisolone was administered at 20 mg/day, then tapered and discontinued. Skin fissures occasionally recurred following mechanical friction (Figure 2), but resolved with finger rest and reinitiation of prednisolone, allowing treatment continuation.

Deep skin fissures, triggered by mechanical friction, developed predominantly on the fingertips. These lesions were accompanied by severe pain. (A) Right hand. (B) Left hand.

Skin fissures occasionally recurred following mechanical friction to the fingers. These lesions resolved with finger rest and reinitiation of prednisolone therapy. (A) Right hand. (B) Left hand.

Based on the clinical course, the deep skin fissures in this case were presumed to have developed because of minor mechanical friction. Xerosis, skin barrier dysfunction and impaired wound healing associated with tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) therapy may contribute to the development of skin toxicity. Alternatively, selpercatinib primarily inhibits RET kinase but also partially inhibits VEGF receptors [2]. We hypothesized that VEGF receptor inhibition aggravates vascular injury around mechanical abrasions, precipitating deep fissures.

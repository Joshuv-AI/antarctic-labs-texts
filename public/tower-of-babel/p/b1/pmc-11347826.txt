Utility of interim apparent diffusion coefficient value in predicting treatment response among patients with locally advanced cervical cancer treated with radiotherapy
Authors: Chunyu Liang, Wei Wang, Guohui Yang, Zhiyuan Xu, Jian Li, Kusheng Wu, Xinping Shen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•This research investigated the utility of pre-treatment, interim-ADC as well as change in ADC for predicting treatment outcomes in patients with cervical carcinoma treated with radiotherapy.•Multivariate logistic regression revealed that the baseline tumor diameter and interim-ADC were significant prognostic factors for treatment response.•Interim-ADC value may serve as a novel imaging marker for predicting the outcomes of therapy.Keywords: Locally advanced cervical cancer, Apparent diffusion coefficient, Radiotherapy, Treatment response, Diffusion weighted imaging

Introduction

Data from global cancer statistics [1] show that, both the morbidity and mortality rate of cervical cancer rank fourth among female malignant tumors. Therapeutic approaches for cervical cancer are stratified according to the disease's FIGO stage [2]. For cases of LACC (FIGO stage IB2 and higher), the standard treatment typically involves radiotherapy (RT) either alone or in conjunction with concurrent chemotherapy [3]. However, significant variations in individual responses among patients with similar FIGO stages have been noted during clinical treatment. After RT, the cervical mass in some patients markedly decreased or even disappeared. However, in other patients, the mass either did not shrink or even increase in bulk, suggesting resistance to RT. To date, there are no accepted modalities that can reliably predict the outcome of RT and many of these women must contend with post-treatment scans in order to determine their eventual response. Thus, early prediction of treatment response after RT are highly desired, which can provide a window to escalate treatment, such as intensification of radiation dose, addition of other chemotherapeutic agents, or enrollment in clinical trials. Besides, canceling ineffective treatment can reduce related toxicity and morbidity.

Currently, the use of functional imaging modalities, such as DWI, may serve as a valuable biomarker for evaluating and forecasting the treatment response of cancers [4], [5]. DWI serves to offer insights into the physiological characteristics of human tissue by examining the microscopic motion of water molecules, thereby furnishing a measurable indicator through ADC value. A low ADC value, which is a hint of restrictive diffusion of water molecules, is indicative of high cell density, while higher ADC values are observed in hypocellular tissue with apoptosis or necrosis. DWI stands as a significant method, employed not only for diagnosis but also for follow-up and prediction of treatment outcome across many types of malignancies. After treatment, responsive cancers show a loss of cell density with a great increase in the ADC value compared with non-responsive cancers. Compared to conventional clinical and pathologic indicators of prognosis, this functional imaging method could better represent the unique biological properties of tumors in vivo and act as a valuable imaging biomarker [6], [7].

The experience in the use of DWI for predicting treatment response in cancers is increasing. A higher pre-treatment ADC value has been correlated with poorer treatment outcomes in studies of nasopharyngeal carcinoma, breast cancer, locally advanced rectal cancer and so on [8], [9], [10]. In the case of cervical carcinomas, certain studies [11], [12] have observed that the group with residual tumors exhibited a higher pre-treatment ADC value compared to the group without residual tumors. However, a contrasting study [13] has indicated that pre-therapy ADC values in cervical cancers are not reliable predictors of therapeutic response. Our team hypothesized that，in addition to pre-treatment ADC value, the ADC value during treatment course could also be used as a predicting marker, as DWI is sensitive to the dynamic water movement within tumors that occurs with therapy, more than just ADC value documented before the start of treatment. This theory has been verified in other solid tumors as well [10]. Therefore, this research was designed to investigate the utility of ADC metric for predicting treatment outcomes in patients with LACC. It facilitates the early assessment of treatment efficacy for LACC, enabling timely modification of therapeutic strategies to improve the efficacy and prognosis, thereby offering a reference for the clinical individualized treatment of LACC.

Materials and methods

Patients

This study registered 80 patients with LACC ranging from FIGO stage IB2 to IVA. All patients were pathologically confirmed cervical SCC patients then received either RT or CCRT at Clinical Oncology department of University of Hong Kong-Shenzhen hospital during the period from November 2015 to July 2020. Patients had a mean age of 56.25 years (33–78 years). Each participants underwent abdomenal or pelvis MRI at least three times: before treatment, three weeks after beginning of RT and approximately two months after the end of RT. The ethical review board of the University of Hong Kong-Shenzhen hospital granted approval for this research.

MRI acquisition

Patients were scanned with either a 1.5 T (Magnetom Avanto; Siemens, Erlangen, Germany) or a 3.0 T (Magnetom Skyra; Siemens, Erlangen, Germany) MRI machine. Firstly, axial, coronal, and sagittal T2-weighted spin echo (SE) sequences, as well as T1-weighted SE data, were obtained. Then, our imaging protocol incorporated a single-shot spin-echo echo planar imaging (SE-EPI) sequence with b-values of 0 and 1000 s/mm2. Subsequently, ADC maps were generated from the b-value of 1000 s/mm2. Finally, after injection of gadolinium chelate, sagittal, coronal and axial contrast-enhanced MR images were acquired. Treatment and follow-up

Fourteen patients exclusively received RT, whereas the other sixty-six patients received CCRT, administering cisplatin with 40 mg/m2 weekly for six cycles. Should a patient present with a creatinine clearance ≤50 mL/min, carboplatin was administered as a substitute for cisplatin. Participants were subjected to external beam radiation therapy (EBRT), delivered through either three-dimensional conformal radiotherapy (3D-CRT) or RapidArc techniques. Dose for RapidArc was 45 Gy/25 Fr to the entire pelvis including cervix, uterus, portions of vagina, parametrium, and regional lymphatics with a 55.0–57.5 Gy simultaneous integrated boost to pelvic or metastatic lymphnodes. For 3D-CRT, two sequential phases included 45 Gy/25 Fr boosting to pelvis for phase I, 16 Gy/8 Fr for FIGO IIIB, other stages 10 Gy/5 Fr to pelvic wall for phase II. The delivery of EBRT occurred on a daily basis, five fractions each week. Subsequent to start of EBRT, a weekly high dose rate (HDR) brachytherapy was conducted for four weeks. For cervical cancer stages IIIB and above, the cumulative equivalent dose was set at greater than 90 Gy (Equivalent Dose in 2 Gy/Fr, EQD2), and for stage IB ∼ IIIA, the threshold was set above 84 Gy (EQD2).

After the end of therapy, patients were follow-uped about every three months for the first two years, then every 6 months thereafter. On routine follow-up, annual chest computed tomography (CT) and abdomenal or pelvis MRI were performed alongside a physical assessment. Lesion biopsies were performed when tumor recurrence was suspected based on pelvic imaging or physical examination.

MRI data analysis

Two expert radiologists, who were blinded to outcomes, each boasting over 15 years of experience in MR imaging, conducted evaluations of the pre-treatment, interim and end-of-treatment MR images respectively. The axial ADC image that displayed largest primary mass was selected to define the region of interest (ROI), with T2-weighted sequence to assist in ROI selection, avoiding any visible necrotic or cystic areas. The average of ADC values measured by two radiologists was labeled as the ADC value for this patient. Example of representative ROI delineations were showed by Fig. 1.

ADC values of four parameters were collected for each patients: ADC before treatment (Pre-ADC), ADC value at the mid-treatment point (Interim-ADC), the change in ADC values from baseline to the early stages of treatment (ΔADC), and percentage change in ADC value (Δ%ADC). The change of ADC values were calculated as follows: ΔADC = Pre-ADC minus Interim-ADC; Δ%ADC = (ΔADC/Pre-ADC) × 100 %.

The statistical endpoint was treatment outcome, determined through the RECIST 1.1 [14], evaluated by MRI after treatment completion. On the basis of RECIST, complete response was determined by having the cervical tumor and enlarged lymphnodes (short diameter ≥ 10 mm) being completely invisible in MRI. The criteria for partial remission (PR) involved a reduction in the cervical tumor's diameter by a minimum of 30 % relative to the baseline measurements. Conversely, criteria for progressive disease (PD) required an increase in tumor's diameter by at least 20 %. Stable disease (SD) was determined when neither PR nor PD was noted. Patients exhibiting a CR were categorized into the good-responder group, whereas those showing PR, SD, or PD were classified to the poor-responder group.

Statistical analysis

Utilizing the Shapiro-Wilk test, we evaluated if the dataset followed a normal distribution pattern. The intraclass correlation was computed to assess inter-observer reliability. Subsequently, we applied an independent sample t-test for comparing interim-ADC values and pre-ADC values as well as for between-group comparisons of ADC parameters. ADC parameters which exhibiting significant differences during between-group comparisons were further examinated by ROC analysis. To ascertain the best cut-off values for distinguishing between good and poor responders, Youden's index was applied. Effectiveness of thresholds was measured on their sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and accuracy.

Subsequently, univariate analysis was conducted to examine the potential factors influencing the treatment outcome, taking into account variables including Pre-ADC, interim-ADC, ΔADC, Δ%ADC, baseline tumor diameter, patient age, FIGO stage and treatment methods. Factors that demonstrated a significant association in univariate analysis were then considered in multivariate analysis to adjust for potential confounding factors. ROC analysis was also conducted with a focus on ADC-related metrics and the synergistic effect of tumor size combined with ADC-related metrics. Data analysis was carried out with SPSS software version 26.0 (IBM SPSS Inc., Armonk, NY, USA) and R statistical package version 4.2.1 (https://www.r-project.org). Statistical significance was set at a p-value < 0.05.

ROC curve analysis

Between-group comparison of pre-ADC displayed no significant difference, thus was not subjected to ROC curve analysis. Otherwise, other ADC parameters that exhibited significant between-group difference underwent further examination through ROC curve analysis to determine the AUC. ROC curve analysis revealed that larger interim-ADC and larger changes in ADC values (ΔADC and Δ%ADC) were strongly associated with a good response, with AUC values of 0.935, 0.783, and 0.723, respectively. The interim-ADC, ΔADC, and Δ%ADC performed well to differentiate poor responders from good responders, with sensitivities, specificities, and accuracies above 75.68 %, 62.79 %, and 70.0 %, respectively. Univariate and multivariate analysis

Table 6 displays the univariate and multivariate logistic analysis of the potential confounding factors. Younger age (≤50 years old), smaller baseline-diameter, larger interim-ADC and larger changes in ADC values (ΔADC and Δ%ADC) were associated with a good response to treatment in univariate analysis (p < 0.05). However, multivariate analysis revealed that treatment response was not correlated with age and changes in ADC values (ΔADC and Δ%ADC) but just with the baseline tumor diameter and interim-ADC (p = 0.013, p ＜ 0.001, respectively). Subsequent receiver operating characteristic analysis provided an area under the curve of 0.649 for baseline tumor diameter and 0.935 for interim-ADC, that increased to 0.945 for a combination of both (Fig. 6 and Table 7).

Discussion

Treatment outcome frequently serve as a proxy for predicting prognosis or survival in LACC patients treated with RT. However, it is critical to be able to discriminate between good responders and poor responders using clinical information. This research assessed the using of clinical information as well as ADC metric for predicting end-of-treatment outcomes in LACC patients referred to RT. The results demonstrated that baseline diameter and interim-ADC could be used to predict the treatment outcome. The patients of good-responder group demonstrated a relatively smaller baseline diameter and higher interim-ADC value.

In the past decade, utilizing of DWI MRI to predict treatment response in LACC has escalated [11], [12]. Several researches have examined the role of pre-treatment ADC for predicting therapeutic effect for LACC, yet their results are not consistent. Some investigations [15] demonstrated that the pre-treatment ADC value of non-CR group was significantly higher when contrasted with the CR group, indicating a reverse association between pretreatment ADC value and therapeutic outcome. Conversely, alternate studies [16] identified that pre-therapy ADC was significantly lower in the poor response group than in those with a good response. In our study, we didn’t find significant difference of pre-therapy ADC values between good responders and poor responders, which confirmed some previous findings[13]. The conflicting research findings may be due to the complexity of microcirculation perfusion. Hence, it is insufficient to rely only on pretreatment ADC value for predicting therapeutic outcomes since it could introduce bias.

In our opinions, it would be advantageous to have an evaluation early in the treatment process, which provided a chance to refine or modify the therapeutic approach for patients who do not exhibit a satisfactory outcome. The current research demonstrated a significant rise in tumor ADC value from pre-treatment to its early treatment phases. This increase in ADC values could be indicative of a reduction in tumor cellularity after RT. Potential explanations could include radiotherapy causing tumor lysis, disruption of cell membrane, and an expanded intercellular area with a subsequent decreasing of the tumor cell density, which allows easier movement of water molecules [17], [18], [19], [20]. Furthermore, our research demonstrated that the average interim-ADC along with the ADC changes (ΔADC and Δ%ADC) of CR cohort were markedly greater compared to those in the non-CR cohort, which confirmed the earlier results. For study of Das et al. [19], total 24 patients with stage IIB-IIIB SCC of cervix were treated with chemoradiation. After three weeks of therapy, response was gauged through clinical and radiological examination. The results showed both the mean ADC value after 3 weeks of chemoradiation and ΔADC were significantly higher in good responders. Similarly, Kuang et al. [20] showed that, compared to PR and SD groups, CR group of LACC patients experienced a higher increment in ADC percentages and absolute ADC values following CCRT.

At the final analysis step, the data derived from the multivariable linear regression revealed that the baseline tumor diameter and the interim-ADC were two substantial predictors linked to the treatment response to RT in patients with LACC. It seems that a smaller baseline diameter and a higher interim-ADC value indicates a favorable outcome to RT. As far as we know, there is currently controversy surrounding the topic of response prediction during treatment course of LACC. Some studies [17], [18], [19], [20] have demonstrated that the absolute values of ADC measured in the early stage of therapy, as well as the change in ADC from before treatment to early stage of treatment both have a significant correlation with therapeutic outcomes. Alternatively, other studies [16], [21] looked at the change in ADC from pre-therapy values to early-treatment values, and a significant correlation with response was observed, although significant correlation was not demonstrated with the absolute early-treatment values. Kuang et al. [20] discovered that, following two weeks of chemoradiation, a 21 % increase in ADC demonstrated 85.7 % sensitivity and 100 % specificity in differentiating CR from SD and PR. However, multivariate analysis of our study revealed that treatment response was not correlated with changes in ADC values (ΔADC and Δ%ADC). Moreover, some studies [16], [21]] reported a significant correlation with the absolute ADC values at the early treatment timepoint with response, which were consistent with our research findings. The inconsistencies in research results may be caused by variations in the study population. The cohort of the current study was mainly composed of stage IIIC-IVA patients (51 %), and ADC cutoff values may differ from each other in previous studies. Furthermore, previous research utilized patient cohorts that were comparatively limited in size; expanding the patient cohorts would enhance the study's dependability.

Besides, our study found that the tumor size was also a significant factor associated with treatment response to RT, which was consistent with previous studies. For example, Min et al. [22] determined that tumor size, as measured by MRI before treatment, was independent predictor of response to CCRT in cervical cancer. Moreover, Huang et al. [23] demonstrated that, for LACC, the tumor size, FIGO stage and human papilloma virus (HPV) DNA load were independent prognostic factors for both overall survival (OS) and distant metastasis-free survival (DFS), and then selected the HPV DNA and tumor size for risk categorization to develop new prediction markers that outperformed the FIGO stage in predicting OS.

The research faced several limitations. First, the number of included patients was relatively small, suggesting for more enrolled patients to improve predictive ability. Additionally, the research was limited to patients diagnosed with only SCC carcinoma, indicating that further study on patients with other types of cervical cancer is needed. Third, the number of variables used for the multivariate model was limited. Prior research has identified baseline HPV load and SCC antigen (SCC-Ag) level as independent prognostic indicators for cervical cancer [24], [25]. HPV load and SCC-Ag level were not used as variables in the present study. Ki-67 index serves as an immunohistochemistry-related marker for assessing the biological characteristic，proliferation activity and prognostic outlook of numerous tumor types. Several studies have revealed a negative correlation between ADC value and Ki-67 expression in many tumors [26], [27]. However, in our study, a significant proportion of patients lacked baseline immunohistochemical evaluations, including the measurement of Ki-67. In addition, imaging findings cannot be pathologically confirmed in patients with LACC, who are typically treated with RT rather than surgery. Thus, assessing the radiotherapy sensitivity based on only MRI could lead to unreliable results. Moreover, we have recorded progression free survival (PFS) and OS for the majority of patients, however, there is a small subset of patients for whom OS and PFS data is missing. Therefore, analysis of OS and PFS was not conducted in our study. We recognize, the need for future prospective studies with larger sample sizes and multiple centers to confirm our findings and suggestions.

Conclusions

In LACC, patients with shorter baseline diameter and higher interim-ADC value respond better to RT. Interim-ADC value may serve as a novel imaging marker for predicting the outcomes of therapy in patients with LACC who are referred to RT. Based on the results of our study, patients with longer baseline diameter and lower interim-ADC value may have poor radiotherapy efficacy. Such patients may need to adjust their treatment plan during the treatment process in the hope of achieving better treatment outcomes. The finds could facilitate personalized treatment and allow some patients to avoid treatment toxicities induced by ineffective therapy.

Evaluation of wet cupping therapy with medications versus standard treatment in the management of chronic cases of unspecified low back pain - A randomised controlled clinical trial
Authors: Ramesh Shivappa Killedar, Pradeep Shahajirao Shindhe, Vijay Kage, Rubeen Nadaf, Mahesh Sattigeri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUnspecified chronic lowback pain (UCLBP) is the most common musculoskeletal condition associated with substantial health and socioeconomic problem. UCLBP does not have a known patho-anatomical origin, thus treatment focuses on minimizing pain and its consequences. Education and reassurance, medications, nonpharmacological therapy, and timely review are all part of the management strategy.ObjectiveTo evaluate the efficacy of Wet Cupping Therapy with Medications versus Standard treatment in the management of chronic cases of unspecified Low Back Pain.Materials and methods134 participants of either sex, aged between 20 and 70 years, diagnosed with UCLBP were recruited for the study. The participants were divided into trial and standard group by computer generated random number software method. Trial group received two sittings of wet-cupping therapy and medications for 14 days. Control group received TENS and HMP for 7 days, acetaminophen 500 mg twice daily and exercise therapy for 14 days. Assessments of primary and secondary outcome variables were done on day 7, day 14 and follow up was done on day 21 and day 28. Inflammatory biomarkers like TNF alpha and IL 6 were analysed using ELISA. Statistical analysis was done with Chi-square test, Mann Whitney U test, Friedman's ANOVA and Wilcoxon matched pair test to assess the significance between and within group results.ResultsSignificant results were observed (p < 0.05) in all the assessed outcome variables like VAS, NPRS, VRS, Stambha (stiffness), Gourava (heaviness), Suptata (numbness) Range of motions, Modified Oswestry disability index, Roland-Morris disability questionnaire, TNF-alpha and IL6, Quality of life assessed by SF-36 questionnaire in within group results at various time points. Comparable results were seen in between group results, in all the assessed outcome variables.ConclusionWet Cupping therapy along with medications was effective in managing the UCLBP, improved quality of life, reduced disability and inflammatory biomarkers.Keywords: Ayurveda medications, Cupping therapy, Exercise therapy, Inflammatory biomarkers, Moist hot pack, Unspecified chronic low back pain, Transcutaneous electric nerve stimulation

Introduction

Unspecified low back pain (UCLBP) is a prevalent, persistent, recurrent, and disabling illness that can be challenging to treat [1]. In the overall adult population, the prevalence of low back pain was found to be 38 % at one year and approximately 40 % during the span of a lifetime [2]. The duration of Unspecified low back pain determines its chronicity, which can be classified as acute (4–6 weeks), sub-acute (6–12 weeks), or chronic (>12 weeks). It is a diagnostic of exclusion as, in 80 % of cases, the ailment has no known aetiology and is classified as unspecified low back pain [3]. Imaging is not always necessary to diagnose Unspecified low back pain; instead, a physical examination and the patient's medical history are used [4,5]. Occupational, recreational, and lifestyle factors may be risk factors for persistent low back pain, and psychosocial factors may play a significant role in these factors [4,5]. Additionally, physical limits (such decreased trunk range of motion, or ROM), restrictions on activities and participation, and absenteeism from work may be associated with pain symptoms. These factors can all have a negative impact on the economy and society [6]. A recent guideline recommends that pharmaceutical treatments be used with low dosages and for a short period of time to treat Unspecified chronic low back pain because of the possibility of adverse side effects and promotes non-pharmacological treatments, such as manual therapy, exercise, and pain education [7,8].

In Ayurveda Unspecified low back pain can be correlated to cluster disorders of Vata vyadhi (disorders of vata) i.e. specially to Katishula (Back pain), Katigraha (Stiffness in lowback), with specific common symptoms like Shula/Ruja (pain), Stambha (stiffness), Suptata (numbness) [9]. Para surgical procedure like Raktamokshana (bloodletting) is practiced widely for vatavyadhi predominant with pain [9]. Alabu/or Ghati yantra karma is mainly indicated in Kapha-vata dosha and Avaghadatara dushta rakta (deep seated impure blood) [9,10]. It acts over the pain with characteristics like Sthamba (stiffness), Grathita (tighten, restricted), Suptata (numbness) and Chimchimayana (tingling sensation). These types of pain generally originate from musculoskeletal system which are termed as myofascial pain and these features are commonly seen in UCLBP [9,10]. Alabu (bottle guard) uses the suction-creating technique of bloodletting, which is applied after precise multiple pricks on skin. The alabu is not widely available, and it is not practicable or dependable to reuse, instead cupping therapy, which uses specialized cups to induce suction on the skin and operates on the same concept as Alabu's bloodletting, has gained popularity [10]. For vatavyadhi, medications such as Yograja guggulu, Erandamula Kashaya (decoction prepared by Ricinus communis root), and Dhanwantaram taila (Oil) are frequently administered for relief of pain [11]. Patients frequently seek complementary and alternative therapies in case of major chronic illness to reduce their pain and discomfort [12]. Wet cupping is a popular practice in the Middle East, as well as Asia and Europe [12]. Wet cupping may be useful in treating a number of ailments, including musculoskeletal discomfort, according to growing evidence of research [12]. Unspecified low back pain has been treated using a variety of Ayurvedic approaches, including medications, procedures (Raktamokshana, Agnikarma, Katibasti etc.), and combinations of the two [13]. These methods are beneficial for chronic illnesses and have significantly better safety aspects than bio-medicines [13]. Hence, with this intention the study was undertaken to evaluate the effectiveness of multimodal treatment modalities i.e. wet cupping therapy and medications in the treatment of unspecified low back pain versus multimodal conventional treatment approach.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

It was prospective, open-label, double arm, randomised controlled trial with recruitment of 134 participants. Random number generator software was used to allocate the participants into two groups. Recruitment of the participants with diagnosis of Unspecified low back pain (ICD-10-CM classification, with the code -M54.50) with chronicity (>12 weeks) was done from the OPD/IPD of KLE Ayurveda Hospital and Medical Research Centre, Belagavi. The CONSORT guidelines were followed for reporting the study's outcomes [14]. Fig. 1. The study was approved by the KAHER Human Ethical Committee Belagavi with Protocol ID: KAHER/EC/21–22/250122001-H. The study was registered with the Clinical Trials Registry of India (CTRI), with the registration number CTRI/2022/04/042181).

CONSORT flow diagram of the study.

The period of collecting participant data was from June 2022–June 2024. The participants were randomly assigned to the Trial and Standard groups in a 1:1 ratio after being divided into two groups using online computer-generated random number software (https://www.randomizer.org). The randomization chart was used to allocate central case registration numbers to the participants. Throughout the trial, the participants records were systematically maintained, and any adverse reactions were monitored properly.

Sample size: 134 participants with a diagnosis of unspecified low back pain with chronicity (>12 weeks) were recruited for the study. A 95 % significance level (i.e., 1.96), and an 80 % power level (i.e., 0.84), were used to calculate the sample size. A 10 % dropout rate was estimated and calculated. A total of 134 participants were enrolled in order to account for the 10 % dropout rate. The following formula was used to calculate the sample size. n= (Z1-α/2+Z1-β)2 (SD1)2+ (SD2)2/(X1-X2)2. The mean (X1, X2) and SD1 and SD2 were taken from the previous published literature [15].

Procurement of formulations

The course powder of Ricinus communis root (Erandamula), Dhanwantaram taila, and Yogaraja Guggulu were procured from KLE Ayurveda pharmacy, Belagavi, which is GMP certified. 500 mg tablets of paracetamol (brand name COLPOL) were purchased from a licensed distributor in Belagavi and was administered by qualified modern medicine consultant. After completing analysis, the Ayurvedic formulations were stored in the Medical Research Centre (MRC) for patient distribution.

Diagnostic criteria

Clinical practice guidelines suggest taking a patient's history and performing a physical examination in search for red flags, diagnosing radicular syndrome through neurological testing, using imaging only when serious pathology is suspected, and evaluating psychosocial factors for diagnosing patients with Unspecified low back pain. Cancer, infections (such as osteomyelitis or discitis), spinal fractures, inflammatory spondyloarthropathy, and cauda equina syndrome (a neurological emergency) are all considered red flags [14]. The diagnosis was established based on the clinical examination and X ray of lumbar spine to rule out anatomical pathology.

Inclusion criteria

Individuals having symptoms of Shula, Stambha, Suptata, Gourava at Kati Pradesh with Vatakaphaja dosha involvement and fit for bloodletting according to the classics [16]. Either sex, age between 20 and 70 years, BMI <30 kg/m2, presenting with UCLBP more than 12 weeks [17]. Reporting a pain score on the pain numerical rating scale (NPRS) that ranges from 3 to 8. [17]. Mild to moderate disability as per the modified oswestry disability index [17]. The participants with normal haemoglobin percentage, bleeding time, and clotting, as well as those who had provided written informed consent were recruited for the study.

Exclusion criteria

The participants unfit for bloodletting like Sarvangashopha (swelling in whole body), Pandu (Anaemia), Arsha (Piles), Udara (Ascites), Shosha (wasting) [16]. Individuals with known bleeding disorders and those experiencing pain radiating from the low back to the lower limbs [17]. History of HIV I and II, HbsAG infections, spinal tuberculosis, lesion, or injury, severe anaemia, or any other chronic condition such as diabetes, hypertension, etc., as well as pregnancy [17]. The study excluded participants who have participated in any other clinical trial within the previous six months.

Intervention

Screening criteria – The participants were screened by clinical examination and x ray lumbar spine and participants who does not have any anatomical pathology were recruited.

The intervention was administered to participants who fulfilled the inclusion and exclusion criteria and provided their consent for the trial. Participants were informed about the trial's design and purpose, prior obtaining their informed consent. The trial lasted 28 days, consisting of 14 days of intervention and 14 days of follow-up.

Trial group: Participants were administered with multimodal treatments like Erandamoola Kashaya [18] (20 ml) thrice daily before meals and Yogaraj Guggulu [19] (250 mg) twice daily after meals for 14 days, as well as wet cupping therapy (supplementary material) on days 0 and 7. Dhanwantaram Taila was applied locally twice daily for a period of 14 days [20].

Standard group – Multimodal conventional treatment approach like Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS), Hot moist pack therapy (HMP) for seven days, and exercise therapy were administered to the participants for fourteen days [21]. It was recommended to take 500 mg tablets of oral acetaminophen twice a day for 14 days. TENS treatment was administered continuously for 15 min at a strength of 40 μSN and 100 Hz, while hot pack therapy was used for 20 min. Spinal mobility and core stability exercises such as bridging, trunk rotation, cat-camel exercise, and back extension were performed ten repetitions a day for a period of fourteen days [21]. Follow-up assessments were conducted on days 21 and 28 to observe changes in the various outcome variables. Our institution certified and experienced physiotherapist conducted and monitored the physiotherapy intervention.

Criteria for assessment

Primary outcomes

The primary outcome measures were Low back pain assessed by VAS, NPRS, VRS and Modified Oswestry Low Back Pain Disability index (MODI) score.

1.VAS, NPRS, VRS - Low back pain was evaluated using the verbal rating scale, the visual analogue scale, and the numerical pain rating scale. The VAS scale is the most accurate, repeatable, and reliable way to evaluate the severity of pain. “No pain" is represented by a number between 0 and 10, whereas "maximal pain" is represented by a number between 10 and 0 [22]. Participants were asked to select a number on the Numeric Pain Rating Scale (NPRS) between 0 (no pain at all) and 10 (worst agony possible) [23]. “No pain" is represented by a number on the VRS scale of 0–10, while "worst pain" is represented by a number of 10 [23].

The final score was expressed as a percentage, with 0–20 denoting minimal disability, 21–40 denoting moderate disability, 41–60 denoting severe disability, 60–80 denoting crippled, and 81–100 denoting bedridden. Each parameter received a score from 0 to 5. Numerous research has verified the dependability of this questionnaire, which is among the most helpful tools for assessing patients' functional status [24].

Secondary outcomes

1.Ayurveda outcome variables such as Stambha, Suptata, and Gaurava were evaluated using standard grading in order to determine the severity of the ailment.

Ayurveda outcome variables such as Stambha, Suptata, and Gaurava were evaluated using standard grading in order to determine the severity of the ailment.

Stambha (stiffness) grades: Absence of stiffness (0), Stiffness that subsides with limited movement but persists for a few minutes after prolonged sitting (1), Stiffness that lasts more than an hour or is experienced several times during the day but does not interfere with daily activities (2), Stiffness that lasts more than an hour or is experienced often throughout the day and has a slight effect on daily activities (3), Episodes of stiffness lasting two to 6 hour and there is significant disruption to daily activity (4) [25].

Suptata (numbness) grades: 0 for no numbness, Occasionally, for a few minutes, once a day (1), Every day, for a few minutes (2) Two or more times per day for 30–60 min (3), or more than 1 hour per day for many times per day (4) [25].

Gaurava (heaviness) grades: No feeling of heaviness (0), Occasional feeling of heaviness not affecting normal movements (1), Frequent feeling of heaviness affecting normal movements (2), Feeling of heaviness throughout the day severely affecting normal movements (3), and feeling of heaviness throughout the day completely hindering normal movement [25].

2.Trunk Range of motionsa)Lumbar flexion - Lumbar flexion range of motion was assessed using the Modified Schober flexion test [26]. At the spinal junction of the left and right posterior superior iliac spines (PSIS), the first landmark was marked. Ten centimetres above the first landmark, the second landmark was placed. With zero at the inferior skin mark and 10 cm at the superior skin mark, the examiner positioned the tape measure between the two skin marks. “Bend forward as far as you can while keeping the knee straight" was the instruction given to the subject as they bent forward, with the measuring tape held firmly against their skin. The separation between the two markings was recorded at end of flexion range of motion. The length measured at the end of motion and the difference of 10 cm was considered as range of motion.b)Lumbar extension- Lumbar extension was measured using the same landmarks and procedures as in the Modified Schober flexion test [26]. The examiner said, "As far as possible, bend backward while placing the palms of your hands on your buttocks" The tape was used to measure the new distance between the superior and inferior skin markings as the person leaned backward into full lumbar extension. The variation in the distance between the marks revealed the lumbar extension's range of motion [26].

Other outcome variables

1.Quality of life - Quality of life was measured using the Short-Form 36 (SF-36) questionnaire [28]. The 36-question test assesses eight distinct variables that affect quality of life while taking into account the respondent's impression of their health during the previous four weeks. Each item has the following characteristics: vitality, social function, emotional aspect, mental health, pain, physical function, and physical aspect. The responses are presented on a graded Likert scale. Their overall score goes from 0 to 100, with a higher number indicating a higher quality of life [28].

2.Disability - Functional disability due to LBP was evaluated using the Roland-Morris Disability Questionnaire (RMDQ). The twenty-four questions in this survey concentrate on the routine activities of everyday life. Higher scores indicate greater disability. The final result is based on the total amount of points, which varies from 0 to 24. Each positive response is worth one point. Severe impairment is indicated by scores more than 14. Monteiro et al. translated and validated the questionnaire [29].

3.Inflammatory biomarkers - In sterile test tubes, a sample of 10-mL venous blood from each participant between 9:00 a.m. and 1:00 p.m. was collected. The tubes were incubated without any additional agitation following the initial inversion. Following the incubation period, the tubes were centrifuged for 20 min at 2500 g. The plasma was first separated from the blood samples and stored at minus 80 °C in a freezer. The procedure was performed as per the guidelines of the kit provided by the company. ELISA kits (GENLISA™ ELISA, Krishgen Biosystems, India) were used to measure the levels of inflammatory biomarkers, including interleukins 6 and tumour necrosis factor alpha, and the results were documented. Since they have been shown to have important roles in relation to back pain, these markers were employed [30]. The analysis of inflammatory biomarkers was done at KLE Prabhakar Kore Basic science research centre, Belagavi and the tests were performed by a qualified trained professional.

Statistical tests

The data was analysed using the SPSS (Statistical Package Social Science) V.25.0 software. All statistical analyses were conducted with a 95 % (95 % CI) confidence interval and a significance level of 5 % (p < 0.05). The chi square test was used to assess the data's homogeneity across the groups. Categorical variables were summarized using frequency and percentage. The mean and SD were used to describe the outcome variables. Wilcoxon matched pair testing and Friedman's ANOVA were used to assess within-group comparisons of results and Mann Whitney U test was used to assess the outcomes of the intergroup comparison of results.

Clinical data - primary and secondary outcomes

A total of 134 participants were recruited for the study. In the trial group, a total of 63 participants completed the study and there were four drop-outs. Cold, cough, fever in three participants and migration in one participant were the reasons for the dropout. No serious adverse drug reactions were encountered during the study period.

Discussion

UCLBP is an illness that needs special care and treatment, in contrast to the persistent pain symptoms that are typically associated with other disorders. For UCLBP, conservative treatment is the initial choice for reducing pain and enhancing functional impairment. The outcome of the study interpret that both the groups are effective in the pain, disability management along with improvement in the patient quality of life. Multimodal treatment approach like wet cupping therapy along with oral medications like Yogaraj Guggulu, Erandamoola Kashaya and local application of Dhanwantaram Taila has showed significant results in management of UCLBP.

Pain (VAS, NPRS, VRS) – Comparable results (non-significant) results were obtained in between group results in outcome variables like VAS, NPRS and VRS on day 7, 14, 21 and 28. In within group, significant results were obtained in all the three outcome variables on Day 7, 14, 21 and 28.

Studies on wet cupping therapy - Wet-cupping may be beneficial for UCLBP, according to some research [31]. It works by removing oxidants, which reduces oxidative stress, or by acting as nociceptive stimuli, which would initiate diffuse noxious inhibitory control [31]. A recent study demonstrated that a single cupping session would be sufficient to decrease pain while also improving disability in patients with LBP, with benefits that remarkably last for a week. [32]. A recent study with pulsatile cupping, minimal cupping and control group showed a VAS change from 53 mm to 34.9 mm, 60.3 mm–40.4 mm, 59.9 mm–56.1 mm respectively after 4 weeks of cupping intervention [33]. In another study of wet cupping therapy (6 times in 2 weeks) showed changes in NRS from 60.50 to 29.2 and 24.4 after 2nd and 4th week respectively [34]. In recent study conducted with Asian technique of cupping showed the NRS changes from 58.19 to 10.56, 13.47 and 12.46 on baseline, immediate after treatment and on day 7 and 14 respectively [35]. A recent study conducted with dry cupping therapy (once a week for 3 weeks) and Hot pack (twice a week for 3 weeks) showed changes in NPRS from 6.15 to 3.31 and 6.08 to 3.35 respectively after 3 weeks [36].

Studies on TENS, HMP and Exercise - A recent study on back school-based intervention exercise (8 weeks) showed changes in VAS from 53 to 26 after 8 weeks of intervention [37]. Study on TENS showed a change of VAS from 45 to 7 after 2 weeks (10 sessions) of treatment [38]. A study using hot pack reduced the NPRS from 8.60 to 4.30 after 15 days of treatment [39]. In the present study multimodal therapies like wet cupping therapy along with medications reduced the VAS significantly after 4 weeks.

Modified Oswestry Low Back Pain Disability Index score – Comparable results (non-significant) results were obtained in between group results in MODI outcome variables on day 14 and day 28. In within group, significant results were obtained in MODI outcome variables on day 14 and day 28.

Studies on wet cupping therapy - In another study of wet cupping therapy (6 times in 2 weeks) showed changes in ODQ from 38.33 to 19.6 and 15.2 after 2nd and 4th week respectively [34]. The total ODQ scores (%) after 2 weeks of cupping therapy (6 times in 2 weeks) treatment were 42.3 in the wet-cupping group which reduced to 5.6 after 2 weeks [34]. In recent study conducted with Asian technique of cupping and alhijamah (traditional) therapy showed the ODQ changes from 37.76 to 16.79, and 16.57 on baseline, day 7 and 14 respectively (Asian group) [35]. Traditional alhijamah therapy showed the ODQ changes from 39.30 to 19.85, and 17.91 on baseline, day 7 and 14 respectively [35].

Studies on TENS, HMP and Exercise - A recent study on dry cupping and hot pack application showed changes in ODQ 35.56 to 16.92in dry cupping, 33.16 to 20.0 in hot pack group [36]. A study using hot pack reduced the ODI from 59.55 to 29.0 after 15 days of treatment [36]. A study conducted with HMP, IFT and exercise therapy for 6 weeks showed changes in ODI from 24.73 to 8.67 [40]. In the present study cupping therapy along with medications improved the ODQ significantly after 2nd and 4th week.

Roland and Morris disability index - Comparable results (non-significant) results were obtained in between group results in RMDQ parameter on day 14 and day 28. In within group, significant results were obtained in RMDQ parameter on day 14 and day 28. In the present study wet cupping therapy along with medications improved the RMDQ scores significantly after 2nd and 4th week i.e. 4.35 to 2.46 and 1.35 respectively.

Studies on wet cupping therapy - In recent years, there has been a growing number of reports on the benefits of Cupping treatment based for low back pain [41]. A recent study showed single session of cupping reduced the pain and improve disability from 7.5 to 4.77 after one week [32].

Studies on TENS, HMP and Exercise - A recent study on back school-based intervention exercise (8 weeks) showed changes from 6.6 to 2.8 after 8 weeks of intervention [37]. A study on TENS reduced the RMDS from 13 to 7 after two weeks of treatment [38].

Range of motion – Comparable results (non-significant) results were obtained in between group results in range of motion outcome variables like modified schober test for Lumbar flexion and extension, finger to floor test on day 7, 14, 21 and day 28. In within group, significant results were obtained in outcome variables like modified schober test for Lumbar flexion and extension, finger to floor test on day 7, 14, 21 and day 28. A study conducted with HMP, IFT and exercise therapy for 6 weeks showed improvement in the mean difference of MST Flexion scores from 3.8 to 5.6 (Day 0 to 3rd week) and to 6.93 at 6th week [40]. In the present study cupping therapy along with medications improved the ODQ significantly after day 7, 14,21 and 28.

Inflammatory mediators (TNF alpha and IL 6) – Comparable results (non-significant) results were obtained in between group results in TNF alpha and IL 6 outcome variables on day 14. In within group, significant results were obtained in TNF alpha and IL 6 outcome variables on day 14. A recent study on NSBLP found a significant difference between the groups by particular stabilization exercise group and typical trunk exercise group [30]. There was a difference of 128.94 pg/mL in the second instance and 66.97 pg/mL in the first. However, there was no significant differences in IL-6 levels of the two treatment groups. At baseline, the difference was 2.51 pg/mL; following the intervention, it was 1.25 pg/mL [30].

Quality of life (SF 36 questionnaire) - Comparable results (non-significant) results were obtained in between group results in SF 36 questionnaire on day 14 and day 28. In within group, significant results were obtained in SF 36 questionnaire on day 14 and day 28.

Additionally, a recent study on dry and wet cupping indicated that the frequency of cupping sessions can influence the treatment effectiveness [42]. Mode of action of trial group

Cupping therapy – Researchers speculated that pain relief was due to the negative pressure from the cupping. Negative pressure works by blocking pain signals to the spinal cord, improving microcirculation, removing noxious materials from the skin, promoting capillary endothelial cell repair, and accelerating granulation and angiogenesis in the local tissues [43]. Moreover, wet cupping therapy contributes to pain reduction and improved functional disability by reducing the tightening of muscle spindles, which in turn encourages progressive muscle relaxation and enhances the range of joint movements [43]. The therapy's action may also involve the removal of oxidants, thereby decreasing oxidative stress, or by acting as a nociceptive stimulus that initiates diffuse noxious inhibitory control [43].

Alabu Raktmokshana - It is indicated for symptoms like numbness (Suptata), muscular stiffness (Stambha), and bodily heaviness (Gaurava), which arise from imbalances of Vata and Kapha doshas affecting the muscle, blood, and ligament tissues. These symptoms are commonly observed in conditions such as sciatica (Gridhrasi) and low back pain (Katishula). By removing the Kapha dosha "obstruction" around Vyana Vayu, Alabu Raktamokshana helps restore normal circulation of Rasa and Rakta (plasma and blood), thereby providing essential nourishment to the affected areas [10].

Acetaminophen (Paracetamol)- According to studies, paracetamol is a specific COX-3 inhibitor and it is thought that COX-3 influences the production of endogenous anti-inflammatory mediators, which contributes to later stages of the inflammatory process [49].

Exercise - It has been demonstrated that exercise therapy can effectively reduce the intensity of low back pain, enhance function and mobility, produce improvements in muscle strength and resistance, and reduce chronicity, dysfunction, and medical care, especially for patients with chronic back pain [50].

HMP - Heat treatment has been used to treat pain in a number of ways, such as hot baths, heat pads, or wraps. The combined effects of these modalities, which operate at various depths, include lowering muscular tone, boosting blood flow, and alleviating pain [51].

TENS - A non-pharmacological approach to pain management, TENS is a low-cost therapy modality that sends electrical impulses through the skin. TENS reduces hyperalgesia and improves impairment by activating descending inhibitory systems through an extensive neural pathway [52]. Multimodal standard approach treatment has reduced the pain, improved the range of motions and reduced the disability, there by improved quality of patient life.

Strengths of the study are randomized controlled trial, good sample size, standard active control. Standard and validated assessment outcome variables like Modified Oswestry disability index questionnaire, Modified Schober test, Roland and Morris disability questionnaire, Pain assessment outcome variables, Quality of life by SF 36 questionnaire, Inflammatory biomarkers and standard ayurveda outcome variables. Appropriate follow ups, proper conduction and documentation of the study.

Limitations of the study are inclusion of other inflammatory biomarkers to see the changes pre and post. The pain threshold in women may present a difference when related to their menstrual cycle that would create a bias.

Conclusion

Comparable results were obtained in the between group results which suggests that both the treatment groups are effective in the management of UCLBP. The study highlighted that multimodal treatment approach like wet cupping therapy along with oral medications like yogaraj guugulu, Erandamula Kashaya and local application of Dhanwantaram taila has provided significant relief in the within group outcome variables like VAS, NPRS, VRS, Stambha, Gourava, Suptata, Range of motions, Modified Oswestry disability questionnaire, Roland and Morris disability questionnaire, Quality of life parameter (SF-36 questionnaire) and inflammatory biomarkers like TNF alpha and IL 6. Cupping therapy along with medicines has reduced the pain, improved functional disability and improved the quality of patient life along with reduction in the inflammatory biomarkers like TNF alpha and IL 6. No adverse events were encountered during the conduct of the study. Further studies with addition of yoga and longer follow up may be helpful in providing the evidence-based results.

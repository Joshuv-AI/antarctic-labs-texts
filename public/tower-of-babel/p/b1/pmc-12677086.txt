Lipoic acid-kaempferol hydrogel loaded with Typhaneoside for sutureless wound closure, hemostasis, and healing of diabetic infected wounds
Authors: Quanyi Lu, Xiyue Tang, Ke Huang, Shuaijun Hu, Bailong Tao, Wenbo Geng, Yiting Lei, Ai Guo, Kai Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Conventional hemostatic materials face challenges in adequately addressing the requirements of patients suffering from non-compressible hemorrhage. Furthermore, diabetic infected wounds frequently present a persistent and slow-healing condition. Currently available treatment strategies are unable to fully address non-compressible hemorrhage and promote the healing of diabetic infected wounds. In this study, we prepared a hydrogel named LTK, which is cross-linked by reversible hydrogen bonds and coordination bonds, using lipoic acid(LA)and kaempferol (Ka) as the main raw materials, and loaded it with Typhaneoside(TYP). The LTK-TYP hydrogel can fully diffuse into the irregularities of substrate surfaces and robustly adhere to various substrates. Molecular dynamics simulations further unveiled the atomic details of the formation of the LTK-TYP hydrogel through multi-modal physical interactions. In vitro and in vivo studies demonstrated that the LTK-TYP hydrogel exhibits excellent hemostatic ability, biocompatibility, and wound closure capacity. Moreover, it promotes the healing of diabetic methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA)-infected wounds through antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and angiogenic functions. This study showcases the immense potential of this multifunctional hydrogel in wound closure, hemostasis, and the healing of diabetic infected wounds.

Introduction

Trauma care and wound repair present significant global public health challenges, worsened by complex injuries and high chronic disease rates [1,2]. WHO reports ∼62 million annual deaths from unintentional injuries, with 33.9 % (21 million) due to traumatic hemorrhage [3,4]. Diabetic chronic wound incidence rises 9.2 % yearly, projected to reach 530 million cases by 2035. Traditional compression hemostasis often fails in hidden anatomical areas or under sustained pressure. Battlefield data show 78.6 % of prehospital deaths stem from preventable hemorrhage, while current materials cannot simultaneously manage dynamic wound closure and early biofilm control [5,6]. Chronic wounds are stuck in a "high incidence-low healing-disability" cycle. Diabetic infected wounds exceed 20 million new cases annually, with 62 % developing secondary infections and 28 % requiring amputation [7,8]. Pathophysiology involves microcirculatory dysfunction, oxidative stress, and immunosuppression [9].Currently, the intervention measures for diabetic infected wounds exhibit significant limitations, such as the difficulty in precisely regulating the entire healing process of local wounds. Moreover, when dealing with wounds infected by drug-resistant bacteria, the efficacy of existing drugs and therapies is substantially diminished [10]. Given the substantial variations in clinical needs, the development of novel dressings that combine functions of wound closure, hemostasis, and regulation of wound repair is of critical importance.

In recent years, hydrogels have emerged as a leading material for wound healing due to their excellent biocompatibility, tunable mechanical properties, and precise drug delivery capabilities [11,12]. Traditional hemostatic methods, such as gelatin sponge packing and hemostatic powders, are widely used for their simplicity but often fail to conform to irregular wounds, lack bioactivity, and cannot actively promote repair [13,14]. These methods are ineffective for severe bleeding from large vessels or complex wounds. In contrast, multifunctional hydrogels provide efficient hemostasis through mechanical adhesion and biochemical reactions while accelerating healing by promoting angiogenesis, regulating inflammation, and delivering bioactive agents like exosomes.

Lipoic acid (LA) is a naturally occurring small functional molecule in biological systems. Its 1,2-dithiolane ring and alkyl chain can spontaneously form non-polar aggregates, while the carboxylic acid group facilitates dispersion in aqueous solutions through electrostatic and hydrogen-bonding interactions [15]. This dual behavior enables LA to self-assemble into dynamic supramolecular structures, providing key benefits for developing biocompatible hydrogels. However, LA's limited water solubility restricts its direct complexation with water-soluble biomacromolecules, thereby constraining its biomedical applications [16].Poly lipoic acid (PLA), as a functional polymeric material, combines excellent wet tissue adhesion properties with broad-spectrum antibacterial and anti-inflammatory activities, effectively inhibiting microorganisms and promoting tissue repair. The dynamic disulfide bonds within its molecular chains not only endow the material with self-healing capabilities but also ensure good biocompatibility and biodegradability. PLA-based materials demonstrate broad application prospects in wound repair, drug delivery, smart materials, and diabetic neuropathy treatment, emerging as an innovative material that integrates safety and functionality [17]. However, the characteristics of dynamic disulfide bonds result in suboptimal stability of PLA-based supramolecular polymers, with chain segments prone to self-degradation, thereby weakening adhesive effects. Although existing studies have attempted to improve network stability through the addition of co-crosslinkers [[18], [19], [20]], these methods often come with risks to biocompatibility or involve processing complexity, making it challenging to simultaneously meet the dual requirements of material safety and operational convenience in clinical applications.

Kaempferol (Ka), a natural flavonoid with a tricyclic (benzene-oxygen heterocycle-benzene) structure and polyphenolic -OH groups, shows strong biological interface recognition and multi-scale interactions [21]. Its phenolic -OH groups disrupt bacterial membranes via electrostatic attraction, while benzene rings enhance membrane permeability. Ka also has antioxidant properties [22]. When combined with LA, Tris base boosts their water solubility. Ka's phenolic -OH groups form dynamic complexes with LA's -COOH groups through hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions, reducing repulsion. Ka's benzene rings also organize LA's 1,2-dithiolane rings and alkyl chains, stabilizing disulfide bonds and improving mechanical strength.The constructed LA-Ka hydrogel LTK thus overcomes the stability limitations of LA when used alone while conferring stable adhesive properties, along with superior antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and angiogenic functions to the composite material.

Typhaneoside (TYP), a flavonoid glycoside from Typha plants, shows synergistic hemostatic and anti-inflammatory effects [23]. Studies indicate TYP promotes primary hemostatic plug formation by activating platelet GPIb-vWF binding and suppresses the NF-κB pathway to reduce pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, TNF-α), alleviating vascular inflammation [24]. Thus, TYP was incorporated into the LTK hydrogel.

Herein, we present the LTK-TYP hydrogel and systematically investigate its cross-linking mechanism, gelation behavior, and adhesive properties. Scheme 1 illustrates its synthesis. We evaluated its hemostatic performance using rat liver and tail amputation models, assessed its wound-closing capability, and examined its wound-healing effects on diabetic MRSA-infected wounds. Comparative studies with commercial agents (e.g., fibrin glue, gelatin sponge, Tegaderm™ film) were conducted to assess the hemostatic efficacy, wound-closing ability, and wound-healing acceleration of LTK-TYP hydrogel in diabetic MRSA-infected skin wounds.Scheme 1Schematic illustration of the preparation process of LTK-TYP hydrogel and its promotion of hemostasis, wound closure, and healing of diabetic infected wounds.Scheme 1

Results and discussions

Preparation and characterization of the LTK-TYP hydrogel

The synthesis process of the LTK-TYP hydrogel is as follows: During the preparation, Ka and LA were first placed in an aqueous solution, and a copolymerization reaction was carried out at the melting temperature of LA. Here, Ka acted as a cross-linker participating in the polymerization process, while LA served as a monomer. To enable the hydrophobic LA and Ka to dissolve in water at high concentrations, Tris base was added. After the successful synthesis of the LTK polymer, TYP was introduced into it, followed by a freeze-thaw cycle treatment, ultimately yielding the LTK-TYP hydrogel. In this process, LA could cross-link with Ka through physical interactions such as electrostatic interactions and hydrogen bonds. Meanwhile, the 1, 2-dithiolane and alkyl chain in LA molecules could spontaneously aggregate to form specific domains, thereby generating a transparent supramolecular hydrogel. As a comparison, if Ka was not added during the synthesis process, the resulting Poly LA was unable to form a hydrogel. This phenomenon indirectly confirms that Ka plays a crucial cross-linking role in the preparation of the LTK-TYP hydrogel (Fig. 1A and B). In the experiments, we set the dosage of Ka at 10 %–60 % (i.e., 10–60 wt%) of the weight of LA, aiming to construct a stable, amorphous, and transparent hydrogel network. As shown in Fig. 1D, we placed the freeze-dried LTK-TYP hydrogels at room temperature and then immersed them in an excess of deionized water until they reached the swelling equilibrium state. After swelling, it was discovered that three types of hydrogels, specifically LTK1-TYP, LTK2-TYP, and LTK3-TYP, were unable to preserve their original shapes and experienced notable weight reductions. In contrast, the remaining three hydrogels, LTK4-TYP, LTK5-TYP, and LTK6-TYP, exhibited volume expansion and weight increase, with swelling percentages spanning from 400 % to 800 %. Based on the differences in monomer ratios during the hydrogel preparation process, it was found that LTK hydrogels with an LA/Ka molar ratio of less than 3.47 possessed good formability. Otherwise, the produced hydrogels were partially cross-linked, rendering them unstable under continuous water immersion.The spontaneous aggregation of the abundant hydroxyl and carboxyl moieties of LTK-TYP, along with the 1, 2-dithiolane and alkyl chains of LA, allows them to self-assemble and generate a supramolecular hydrogel. This process does not require additional raw materials or modifications to the polymer network. This simple synthesis method offers advantages such as time-saving, low cost, and green synthesis.In the strain sweep test (Fig. 1E), a linear viscoelastic region was observed for LTK4-TYP, LTK5-TYP, LTK6-TYP, and LTK5 within the strain range of 0.1 %–200 %. The frequency sweep test (Fig. 1F) revealed that LTK5-TYP and LTK5 hydrogels exhibited shear-thinning behavior under high shear frequencies. Their gel-sol transition behavior under high shear forces makes them suitable candidates for tissue repair or wound coating applications.

We then proceeded to examine the cross-linking mechanism of the LTK hydrogel. As illustrated in the Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectra of LA, Ka and LTK (Fig. 1G), the (-OH) absorption peak at 3323 cm−1 in Ka and the carboxylic acid (-COOH) peak at 1692 cm−1 in LA shifted to 3304 cm−1 and 1677 cm−1, respectively, in the LTK spectrum. The distinctive absorption peaks of amines and carboxylic acids within the LTK hydrogel underwent shifts, yet no new peaks emerged, indicating that LA and Ka are interconnected through physical interactions—such as hydrogen bonding and strong electrostatic attractions—rather than by means of covalent bonds.

The UV–Vis spectra revealed distinct characteristic peaks for both LA and the LTK hydrogel at 325 and 331 nm, respectively. This redshift from 325 nm to 331 nm can be attributed to the aggregation of the 1, 2-dithiolane groups within LA molecules (Fig. 1H). These experimental results demonstrate that LA molecules are capable of spontaneous aggregation, while strong electrostatic interactions exist between LA and Ka. It is the combined effect of these two interactions that drives the formation of the supramolecular hydrogel. Moreover, the LTK-TYP hydrogel achieves cross-linking solely through physical interactions.

To explore the hydrophilic or hydrophobic characteristics of LTK-TYP, we carried out water contact angle measurement tests. As depicted in Fig. 1I, the water contact angle for LTK was measured at 80.16° ± 3.95°, whereas for LTK-TYP, it was determined to be 78.89° ± 2.78°. Hydrophilic hydrogels, upon absorbing moisture, can form a physical barrier on the wound surface and also provide moisture retention. This helps to alleviate tissue cell dehydration, maintain the vitality of cell proliferation and migration, sustain a microenvironment conducive to wound healing, and thereby slow down the process of continuous wound deepening [25,26].

We employed High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) to investigate the release profile of TYP from the LTK-TYP hydrogel, with the relevant results shown in Fig. S1(supporting information). The results revealed that TYP exhibited an initial burst release within the first 3 days, followed by a slow and sustained release phase. The early rapid release of TYP can effectively exert its hemostatic and pro-coagulant effects, while its sustained release can provide long-lasting anti-inflammatory and antioxidant actions, regulate the diabetic microenvironment, and promote angiogenesis, thereby further enhancing the bioactivity and therapeutic efficacy of the LTK-TYP hydrogel.

Adhesive properties of the LTK-TYP hydrogel

The LTK-TYP hydrogel demonstrates excellent adhesive performance on substrates of various materials. In the experiment, when the LTK-TYP hydrogel was injected onto glass slides, it was observed to adopt customizable shapes and adhere tightly to the glass surface. This phenomenon fully demonstrates the significant application potential of the LTK-TYP hydrogel in addressing irregularly shaped target wounds (Fig. 1J).

Subsequently, we adhered the LTK-TYP hydrogel to porcine skin. Even after subjecting the porcine skin with the adhered hydrogel to stretching, compression, and twisting operations, the LTK-TYP hydrogel remained firmly attached to the porcine skin surface without any separation, fully demonstrating its excellent shape adaptability and mechanical flexibility (Fig. S2, Supporting Information). We also quantitatively evaluated the adhesive strength of LTK5, LTK4-TYP, LTK5-TYP, and LTK6-TYP hydrogels on porcine skin and compared it with that of a commercial Fibrin Glue on the same substrate. The results demonstrated that the adhesive strengths of LTK5 and LTK5-TYP hydrogels on porcine skin were 43.33 ± 1.7 kPa and 43.67 ± 3.51 kPa, respectively, which were significantly higher than that of the commercially available fibrin glue on porcine skin. This indicates that LTK5 and LTK5-TYP hydrogels possess excellent potential for clinical applications (Fig. 1K). Furthermore, the adhesive strengths of LTK5-TYP and LTK6-TYP hydrogels were significantly higher than that of LTK4-TYP hydrogel, while there was no statistically significant difference in adhesive strength between LTK5-TYP and LTK6-TYP hydrogels. Therefore, we selected the LTK5-TYP hydrogel for subsequent experiments.Additionally, various substrates, including steel, latex, plastic, glass, wood, and rat tissue organs, could be adhered to glass slides using the LTK-TYP hydrogel, and these substrates could be lifted against their own weight with the aid of the hydrogel's adhesive force (Fig. 1L).

Based on the aforementioned research findings, the adhesion mechanism of the LTK-TYP hydrogel can be described as follows: The LTK-TYP hydrogel is capable of extending into the irregular structures on the substrate surface, achieving close conformity with the substrate. Meanwhile, the hydroxyl groups in the Ka molecules can engage in physical interactions with the alkyl chain groups, carboxylic acid groups, and 1, 2-dithiolane groups in the LA molecules, as well as interact with various functional groups on the substrate surface (such as through hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions). These interactions establish a cross-linked contact state with the substrate, thereby achieving a robust adhesive effect.

Molecular dynamics simulation of LTK hydrogel formation

To uncover the molecular details of LTK hydrogel formation, we subsequently conducted all-atom molecular dynamics (MD) simulations lasting a total of 100 ns. Fig. 2A displays a representative snapshot from the 100 ns simulation of the constructed LTK system, where spontaneous aggregation of molecules occurs to form a gel-like structure. Within the system, molecules exhibit spontaneous adsorption, leading to a cross-linked state. This process appears to involve two distinct types of aggregation: LA aggregation driven by association between its 1, 2-dithiolane and alkyl chains, and LA-Ka aggregation facilitated by hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions (Fig. 2B and C). Subsequently, we characterized the temporal evolution of interaction energies between different molecules in the system. We observed strong interaction energies among the three types of molecules. Specifically, strong electrostatic and van der Waals forces between LA and Ka promoted hydrogel cross-linking, while electrostatic and van der Waals forces between LA, Ka, and Tris molecules further facilitated the dissolution of LA and Ka in aqueous solution. The strong interactions among the three molecules enabled the formation of a tightly cross-linked structure in aqueous solution (Fig. 2D–F).We further characterized the temporal changes in the number of hydrogen bonds (H-bonds) within the system (Fig. 2G). A substantial number of H-bonds were formed among the three types of molecules, corresponding to the aforementioned interaction energies and indicating strong binding among them.Finally, we characterized the temporal evolution of the radius of gyration and solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) of non-solvent molecules in the system (Fig. 2H–I). The LTK-TYP system spontaneously aggregated into a gel-like state, with the SASA rapidly decreasing and stabilizing over the course of the simulation. In LTK hydrogels, microscopic molecular interactions significantly influence macroscopic properties. On a microscopic scale, the strong electrostatic and van der Waals forces between LA and KA contribute to the formation of a tightly cross-linked network. This, in turn, enhances the adhesive strength of the hydrogel when it comes into contact with other surfaces, facilitating its adhesion to cells and tissues. Meanwhile, the close cross-linking formed through the interactions among LA, KA, and Tris molecules endows the hydrogel with high strength, enabling it to disperse and transmit external forces effectively. Additionally, the numerous hydrogen bonds create "flexible linkages" that impart both elasticity and toughness to the hydrogel, making it suitable for a variety of applications. Furthermore, changes related to non-solvent molecules result in a dense and stable internal structure of the hydrogel, further enhancing its mechanical properties and the durability of its adhesive strength.

In vitro antibacterial activity of the hydrogel

After skin injury in diabetic patients, the continuous exudation of plasma at the wound site provides abundant nutrients for bacterial proliferation, creating a local microenvironment conducive to bacterial growth and reproduction. Consequently, the wound becomes highly susceptible to recurrent bacterial infections [27,28]. Therefore, the development of wound dressings with antibacterial properties holds immense clinical significance for promoting the healing process of diabetic wounds. In this study, we specifically selected methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), a drug-resistant bacterium commonly found in diabetic wound infections, as well as Escherichia coli (E. coli), as our research targets to comprehensively and systematically evaluate the antibacterial efficacy of the LTK-TYP hydrogel.

During the experimental phase, we employed the plate count method to conduct a comparative analysis of the in vitro antibacterial activities among three groups: the control group (Control), the LTK group, and the LTK-TYP group. As clearly depicted in Fig. 3A, the culture medium of the control group was densely populated with a large number of MRSA colony-forming units (CFUs). In contrast, the culture media of the LTK and LTK-TYP groups exhibited a significantly lower number of surviving colonies. Further quantitative analysis (Fig. 3C) revealed that the survival rates of MRSA in the LTK and LTK-TYP groups decreased substantially to 2.64 % and 1.51 %, respectively, compared to the control group (p < 0.05). We employed scanning electron microscopy (SEM) technology to observe the morphological changes in methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) after treatment with the LTK-TYP hydrogel. As clearly depicted in Fig. 3B, most of the MRSA in the control group exhibited a regular spherical shape with a smooth and even cell membrane surface. However, after co-incubation with the LTK and LTK-TYP hydrogels, the cell membrane surface of MRSA showed noticeable shrinkage and rupture phenomena.Previous studies in the literature have demonstrated that the Ka component is capable of modifying bacterial membrane permeability, which subsequently results in a reduction of intracellular adenosine triphosphate (ATP) levels and induces the leakage of intracellular substances (e.g., DNA/RNA) [29]. Building on this theoretical basis, we employed the 1-N-phenylnaphthylamine (NPN) assay to monitor alterations in bacterial membrane permeability, serving as an indirect indicator of cell membrane integrity. Upon changes in membrane permeability, NPN binds to phospholipids and generates intense fluorescence. Our experimental findings showed that the relative fluorescence intensities in the LTK and LTK-TYP groups were notably greater than those in the control group (Fig. 3D).Furthermore, as illustrated in Fig. 3E, when compared to the control group, the relative ATP levels in the LTK and LTK-TYP groups displayed a pronounced declining tendency. Furthermore, we employed ultraviolet–visible spectrophotometry to evaluate intracellular DNA leakage by quantifying the absorbance at a wavelength of 260 nm.Following treatment with the LTK and LTK-TYP hydrogels, there was a substantial increase in the normalized percentage (%) of DNA leakage relative to the control group (Fig. 3F). To delve deeper into assessing the extent of membrane damage in MRSA, we employed the o-nitrophenyl-β-D-galactopyranoside (ONPG) hydrolysis assay for detection.Once ONPG penetrates the bacterial cell, it undergoes a reaction with intracellular β-D-galactosidase, resulting in the formation of o-nitrophenol [29]. Moreover, the extent of ONPG hydrolysis increases in direct proportion to the extent of damage to the bacterial cell membrane. Our experimental results demonstrated that the levels of ONPG hydrolysis in both the LTK and LTK-TYP groups were significantly elevated compared to those observed in the control group (Fig. S3,Supporting Information).This outcome strongly suggests that the LTK and LTK-TYP hydrogels inflicted significant damage on the MRSA bacterial membrane.

Subsequently, we evaluated the in vitro anti-E. coli activity of the LTK-TYP hydrogel. The experimental results are shown in Fig. S4A of the supporting information: a large number of densely distributed E. coli colonies were observed in the culture medium of the control group, whereas the number of viable colonies in the culture solutions of the LTK group and the LTK-TYP group was significantly reduced. Further quantitative analysis (Fig. S4B, supporting information) revealed that, compared to the control group, the LTK group and the LTK-TYP group exhibited particularly prominent inhibitory effects on E. coli, with the survival rates dropping to 11.72 % and 8.28 %, respectively. These findings indicate that the LTK-TYP hydrogel possesses significant antibacterial activity.

Biocompatibility of the hydrogel

We incubated cells in the extracts derived from LTK and LTK-TYP hydrogels for a duration of 2 days and subsequently utilized the live/dead staining technique to assess cell viability. After 3 days of incubation, it was observed that almost all HUVEC cells and L929 cells were viable (appearing green), with only a negligible number of dead cells (appearing red) detected across all experimental groups (Fig. 3G). Additionally, the CCK-8 assay yielded similar results, indicating that neither the LTK group nor the LTK-TYP group exhibited significant cytotoxicity towards HUVEC cells and L929 cells (Fig. 3H).

Given that wound dressings need to come into direct contact with blood during practical application, the significance of blood compatibility for wound dressings is self-evident [30]. Based on this circumstance, we carried out a hemolysis test with the aim of evaluating the blood compatibility of the investigated hydrogels. The test results demonstrated that the hemolysis rate of the LTK hydrogel was 2.3 %, and that of the LTK-TYP hydrogel was 2.51 % (Fig. S5, Supporting Information). From the above data, it can be concluded that both the LTK and LTK-TYP hydrogels exhibit good blood compatibility.

During the in vivo biocompatibility tests, we employed H&E staining techniques to investigate whether the LTK-TYP hydrogel would cause damage to major organs, including the heart、liver、spleen、lungs、kidneys and skin. Based on the experimental results, the LTK-TYP hydrogel did not appear to induce any tissue defects (Fig. 3I). These findings strongly suggest that the LTK-TYP hydrogel is a biocompatible material with potential for in vivo medical applications.

Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties of the hydrogel

In diabetic infected wounds, M1 macrophages are excessively activated and persist for an extended period, secreting a large amount of pro-inflammatory cytokines and thereby triggering chronic inflammation. Meanwhile, the polarization of M2 macrophages is impeded, leading to a continuous decline in their numbers and rendering them ineffective in exerting anti-inflammatory and tissue-repair-promoting functions [31,32]. This imbalance in macrophage types directly results in a slower wound healing process. To determine whether LTK-TYP could promote the phenotypic switch of macrophages from the pro-inflammatory M1 subtype to the anti-inflammatory M2 subtype, thereby alleviating the inflammatory response, we utilized immunofluorescence staining to track the transition between M1 macrophages (marked by CD86) and M2 macrophages (marked by CD206).Initially, RAW 264.7 macrophages were treated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) to trigger their polarization into the M1 phenotype, which is linked to inflammation.Immunofluorescence analysis demonstrated that LPS-stimulated M1 macrophages exhibited CD86 expression while lacking CD206 expression. Following treatment with LTK-TYP, significant alterations were noted: the fluorescence intensity of the M1 marker CD86 decreased markedly, whereas the fluorescence intensity of the M2 marker CD206 increased considerably (Fig. 4A and S6). In the microenvironment of diabetic wounds, elevated levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) represent a typical characteristic. These high ROS levels can induce dysfunction in cells related to tissue regeneration and attract more inflammatory cells to the lesion site. Eliminating excess ROS can significantly promote the healing of diabetic wounds [33,34]. Building on this, we assessed the ROS-scavenging capability of LTK-TYP. We utilized the ROS-sensitive fluorescent probe DCFH-DA to measure the relative intracellular ROS levels. The experimental outcomes indicated that the group of HUVECs co-cultured with LTK-TYP extracts displayed the weakest green fluorescence intensity, implying that LTK-TYP can effectively alleviate intracellular oxidative stress (Fig. 4B and C). To evaluate the reactive nitrogen species (RNS)-scavenging potential of LTK-TYP, we utilized the 2, 2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) assay. The results demonstrated that, as depicted in Fig. S7 (Supporting Information), the DPPH scavenging rates for LTK and LTK-TYP were 32.92 % and 57.99 %, respectively. This finding fully confirms that LTK-TYP possesses excellent RNS-scavenging capacity. The exceptional RNS-scavenging capacity of LTK-TYP is mainly ascribed to its constituent components: LA demonstrates potent reducing characteristics, whereas Ka and TYP can directly engage in free radical-scavenging processes, transforming RNS into innocuous substances and thereby significantly mitigating the cellular damage they cause.

In conclusion, the results of this study reveal that, due to the synergistic actions of LA, Ka, and TYP, LTK-TYP not only has the capacity to neutralize both ROS and RNS but also induces a phenotypic transformation of macrophages from the pro-inflammatory M1 subtype to the anti-inflammatory M2 subtype. Consequently, it effectively mitigates the inflammatory response and demonstrates exceptional anti-inflammatory characteristics. This discovery carries substantial importance for improving the treatment and recovery of diabetic infected wounds.

Angiogenic properties of the hydrogel

Hyperglycemic conditions exert adverse effects on cells, leading to a decline in cell migration capacity and interfering with the normal and efficient adhesion process of fibroblasts. In the complex physiological process of diabetic wound healing, vascular damage and dysfunction of neovascularization represent one of the key pathogenic factors contributing to the impaired healing of diabetic wounds [35,36]. Against this backdrop, we deliberately established HUVEC and L929 cell models under a high-glucose injury environment to facilitate subsequent research.

The scratch assay was employed to evaluate the migratory capacity of L929 cells cultured with various hydrogels (Fig. 4D). Following 24 h of co-culture, cells grown with the LTK-TYP hydrogel demonstrated a significantly elevated cell migration rate (%) compared to those in the HG group (Fig. 4E). This enhancement could likely be due to the cell-recruiting characteristics of LA and TYP [15,24].

During the wound healing process, cell migration and proliferation to the wound area, along with concurrent vascularization and epithelialization, are critically important steps [38]. As clearly shown in Fig. 4F and G, the groups treated with LTK and LTK-TYP exhibited dilation of vascular loops and a significantly higher number of branches compared to the high-glucose (HG) group. Notably, there was no statistical difference in vascular conditions between the LTK-TYP-treated group and the Control group. This phenomenon is closely related to the pro-angiogenic properties of TYP and LA. To further investigate the ability of different hydrogels to promote angiogenesis, we employed immunofluorescence staining to detect vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and platelet endothelial cell adhesion molecule (CD31) [37]. The staining outcomes clearly indicated that both LTK and LTK-TYP hydrogels could effectively enhance the expression of VEGF and platelet-endothelial CD31 in HUVECs (Fig. 4H and S8). To quantitatively evaluate the expression of genes associated with angiogenesis, we employed real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (real-time PCR) technology. The experimental results, as shown in Fig. S9(supporting information), revealed that the LTK-TYP treatment group significantly upregulated the gene expression levels of VEGF, bFGF, and Ang-1. These findings suggest that LTK-TYP may promote the expression of angiogenesis-related genes by regulating the VEGF/VEGFR, PI3K/Akt, and ERK pathways, thereby providing a favorable microenvironment for the skin repair process and accelerating wound healing.These results provide strong evidence that the inclusion of TYP and LA can markedly stimulate angiogenesis, thus fostering an optimal environment for new blood vessel formation and tissue regeneration at the wound site.

Transcriptomic analysis

Prior research has revealed that LPS triggers inflammatory signaling pathways, resulting in increased production of pro-inflammatory cytokines [50]. To explore this further, transcriptomic analysis was conducted on RAW 264.7 cells cultured with or without LTK-TYP in an LPS microenvironment. Initially, a heatmap was constructed to display differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the LPS group and the LTK-TYP group (Fig. 5A). Additionally, differential analysis was visualized using a volcano plot, revealing that 483 genes were downregulated and 1380 genes were upregulated after LTK-TYP treatment compared to those in the LPS group (Fig. 5B). GO enrichment analysis revealed (Fig. 5C) that LTK-TYP treatment alleviated LPS-induced cell membrane lipid peroxidation and organelle (e.g., mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum) dysfunction by regulating cellular structural components. The high proportion of differentially expressed genes related to cellular components (e.g., genes encoding membrane proteins and organelle structural proteins) suggests that LTK-TYP may directly intervene in LPS-induced structural damage by repairing or reconstructing cellular structural components (e.g., replenishing membrane lipids, stabilizing organelles). This structural repair provides a foundation for subsequent metabolic and immune regulation, aligning well with the vicious cycle of "structural damage-metabolic disorder-inflammation amplification" observed in diabetic infected wounds [39,40]. KEGG and GSEA enrichment analyses showed (Fig. 5D and E) significant enrichment of the TNF superfamily ligand-receptor binding pathway. TNF superfamily members (e.g., TNF-α, FasL) induce the secretion of inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-6, IL-1β) through receptor-mediated signaling pathways such as NF-κB and MAPK, inhibit angiogenesis, and delay healing [41,42]. In diabetic infected wounds, chronic inflammation and hyperglycemia mutually promote each other (inflammatory cytokines inhibit insulin signaling, while hyperglycemia exacerbates inflammation), forming a vicious cycle of "inflammation-injury" [43]. The significant enrichment of this pathway after LTK-TYP treatment suggests that it may inhibit excessive inflammatory responses and block this vicious cycle by downregulating TNF-α or its downstream signaling molecules (e.g., TRAF2, IKK) [44]. Furthermore, significant enrichment of the MODY diabetes pathway was observed (Fig. 5F), suggesting that LTK-TYP may improve diabetes-related insulin secretion or glucose metabolism abnormalities by regulating this pathway. MODY diabetes is primarily caused by mutations in pancreatic β-cell transcription factors (e.g., HNF-1α, HNF-4α), which are involved in the expression regulation of key genes such as glucokinase and liver glycogen synthase [45]. The intervention of LTK-TYP in this pathway may restore insulin secretion function and enhance glycolytic efficiency by repairing transcription factor activity or downstream target genes.To verify this hypothesis, we employed Western blot analysis to detect the expression of relevant proteins. As expected, the LTK-TYP hydrogel significantly suppressed the expression of TNF-α and upregulated the expression of HNF-1α and HNF-4α (Fig. S10A–B, Supporting Information).Fig. 5Transcriptome sequencing of RAW264.7 cells. Incision closure and hemostatic capacity of LTK-TYP hydrogel

The swift and efficient closure of damaged skin not only safeguards the body's internal environment but also establishes physiological conditions favorable for wound healing [46]. To examine the effectiveness of LTK-TYP in promoting wound closure, we made full-thickness skin incisions on the backs of rats to assess its in vivo adhesive properties and its capacity to aid in wound closure. Immediately after the skin was incised, the wounds were treated with surgical sutures, fibrin glue, or LTK-TYP, respectively. When LTK-TYP was applied to the incision, it rapidly adhered to the wound. After 10 days of treatment and observation, the group treated with fibrin glue exhibited a reduction in incision size, but the wound had not fully healed, with scabs still present on the surface. The group treated with surgical sutures achieved complete wound healing; however, noticeable scars remained. In contrast, the group treated with LTK-TYP demonstrated excellent wound bridging, with the scabbed portions having detached and the wound approaching a state of near-complete healing (Fig. 6A). An advanced hemostatic material should possess the capability to address various bleeding scenarios. In this study, we first conducted in vitro experiments to verify the coagulation ability of LTK-TYP by measuring the clotting time after co-culturing it with whole blood (Fig. S11, Supporting Information). The coagulation test results revealed that when gelatin sponge and fibrin glue were separately incubated with anticoagulated blood, the clotting times were approximately 5 min and 4 min, respectively, indicating their certain ability to promote blood coagulation. In contrast, LTK-TYP shortened the clotting time to 3 min by promoting thrombin activation and enhancing platelet aggregation, demonstrating a favorable effect in facilitating blood coagulation.

To comprehensively evaluate the hemostatic efficacy of LTK-TYP, we employed standardized rat liver incision hemorrhage and rat tail amputation hemorrhage models for experimental assessment [47]. Initially, using an incompressible liver hemorrhage model in rats, we tested the sealing and hemostatic performance of LTK-TYP in vivo. A 10 mm cross-shaped incision was created on the rat liver, after which hemostatic treatment was administered using LTK-TYP, gelatin sponge, and fibrin glue, with the latter two serving as control groups. As evident in Fig. 6B, the LTK-TYP group demonstrated the lowest blood loss and the shortest bleeding time compared to the gelatin sponge and fibrin glue groups. Specific measurements revealed that blood loss in the LTK-TYP group was (733.33 ± 169.97 mg), and the time to achieve hemostasis was (143.33 ± 28.67 s) (Fig. 6C and D).

Compared to the gelatin sponge group, this group demonstrated a reduction in blood loss of approximately 65.68 % and a shortening of hemostasis time by approximately 51.68 %. Similarly, in the rat tail amputation hemorrhage model experiment, LTK-TYP exhibited hemostatic efficacy comparable to that observed in the liver hemostasis experiment (Fig. 6E). Briefly, compared to the gelatin sponge group, the LTK-TYP group showed a reduction in blood loss of approximately 70.92 % and a shortening of hemostasis time by approximately 63.17 % (Fig. 6F and G). Moreover, compared to other commercially available hemostatic agents, LTK-TYP also significantly reduced blood loss and shortened hemostasis time, primarily attributable to the mechanical adhesion properties and blood coagulation-promoting ability of this hydrogel.

Vascular injuries often trigger a high-pressure spurt of blood flow, which can lead to life-threatening massive hemorrhage. Therefore, this study utilized a rat femoral artery bleeding model to further verify the hemostatic efficacy of the LTK-TYP hydrogel. The experimental results revealed that the blood loss in the Gelatin sponge group reached 2500 ± 295.97 mg, with a hemostasis time as long as 270 ± 21.6 s. Both values were significantly higher than those in the LTK-TYP hydrogel group, which recorded 873.33 ± 145.03 mg of blood loss and a hemostasis time of 151.66 ± 17.56 s. Statistical analysis of blood loss and hemostasis time indicated that, compared to the Fibrin glue group, the LTK-TYP hydrogel showed no significant difference in blood loss but significantly shortened the hemostasis time (Figure S12 A-C, Supporting Information). These findings confirm that the LTK-TYP hydrogel exhibits excellent hemostatic performance in an acute bleeding model induced by major vascular rupture, suggesting its promising potential as a vascular occlusion material for controlling acute hemorrhage.

We employed H&E staining and Masson's trichrome staining methods to investigate the structural alterations in the regenerated skin tissue. The H&E-stained images (Fig. 8F) revealed that, by the 7th day of treatment, the wound tissue in the Tegaderm™ film group displayed a substantial influx of inflammatory cells, indicating a marked inflammatory reaction within this group.Conversely, the wound tissue treated with LTK-TYP displayed only a comparatively small number of inflammatory cells, offering compelling evidence of LTK-TYP's ability to mitigate inflammatory reactions. By the 14th day of treatment, the Tegaderm™ film group exhibited signs of delayed wound closure and compromised re-epithelialization, characterized by the lack of hair follicles and disorganized collagen fiber arrangements—factors that hinder complete wound restoration [50]. In the context of diabetic wounds, prolonged exposure to a hyperglycemic milieu stimulates the excessive generation of ROS. Excessive ROS further aggravates cellular damage, intensifies inflammatory responses, and ultimately delays the wound healing process [52,53]. The establishment of a pro-inflammatory milieu at the wound site disturbs the immune system's equilibrium, resulting in the excessive activation of M1-type macrophages [54]. It is of utmost significance to highlight that the transition of macrophages from the pro-inflammatory M1 state to the anti-inflammatory, tissue-repair-promoting M2 state plays a pivotal role in facilitating tissue repair and regeneration [[55], [56], [57]].

To gain a more in-depth understanding of the macrophage distribution within the wound tissue, we utilized immunohistochemical and immunofluorescence staining methods. As illustrated in Fig. 9G, during the wound healing process, there is a significant infiltration of pro-inflammatory cytokines into the wound site. Following 7 days of LTK-TYP treatment, we noted the greatest abundance of CD206-labeled M2-type macrophages in the wound tissue, and this beneficial effect continued up to day 14 of the treatment period. By this stage, M2-type macrophages were predominant in the LTK-TYP group, whereas they were only sparsely present in the Tegaderm™ film group(Fig. 9H).Meanwhile, we employed CD86 as a marker to detect M1-type macrophages. The results revealed a significant reduction in the number of M1-type macrophages following 7 days of LTK-TYP treatment, whereas these macrophages were widely dispersed in the other two experimental groups.By day 14 of treatment, the LTK-TYP group maintained the lowest level of CD86 distribution (Fig. 9G–I). The results from immunofluorescence staining further substantiated these observations, showing that, in comparison to the other groups, the LTK-TYP group had the highest concentration of CD206 and the lowest concentration of CD86 (Fig. 9J–L).This suggests that LA and TYP possess strong anti-inflammatory properties, and LTK-TYP is capable of effectively mitigating the inflammatory reaction at the wound site while encouraging macrophages to polarize towards the M2 phenotype.As a result, it fosters a more favorable microenvironment for the wound healing process. Compared with previous studies, the LTK-TYP hydrogel prepared in this study features a relatively simple composition, with most components being of natural origin. This reduces the biosafety risks associated with chemical cross-linkers [36]. For diabetic wounds infected with MRSA, the synergistic effects of its antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and pro-angiogenic functions significantly promote wound healing.

Conclusion

In this study, we successfully prepared the LTK-TYP hydrogel using a green synthesis method characterized by a simple operational procedure, rapid preparation efficiency, and low cost. The cross-linking structure of the LTK-TYP hydrogel is formed through reversible hydrogen and coordination bonds between LA and Ka, which construct a stable cross-linked network. The LTK-TYP hydrogel can fully diffuse into the irregular regions of the substrate surface, achieving a highly intimate adhesion to the substrate. Meanwhile, the functional groups within the hydrogel interact with those on the substrate surface, further enhancing the bonding strength at the hydrogel-substrate interface and providing a stable structural foundation for subsequent functional performance. Based on the excellent adhesive properties of the LTK-TYP hydrogel and its activation of the coagulation mechanism, it significantly improved hemostatic efficiency and accelerated the wound closure process in rat models of liver hemorrhage, tail amputation hemorrhage, and incision closure. Addressing the complex and challenging clinical issue of diabetic MRSA-infected wounds, the LTK-TYP hydrogel demonstrated multifaceted therapeutic advantages. It not only exhibited antibacterial and antioxidant capabilities but also, in terms of immunomodulation, could induce the differentiation of macrophages into the M2 phenotype, regulate the local immune microenvironment at the wound site, and promote tissue repair. Additionally, the hydrogel promoted the expression of VEGF and CD31, accelerating the formation of new blood vessels and providing sufficient nutrients and oxygen to the wound, thereby significantly improving the healing quality of diabetic infected wounds.In summary, owing to its unique properties and remarkable therapeutic effects, the LTK-TYP hydrogel exhibits broad application prospects in the fields of hemostasis and wound healing.

However, diabetic patients often develop multiple systemic diseases during the course of their illness, and these diseases have a complex and closely intertwined relationship with wound healing, posing numerous challenges to wound treatment. Currently, existing hemostatic models fall short in accurately simulating the true physiological and pathological hemostatic environments of the human body. They are unable to comprehensively and meticulously represent the intricate vascular structures, dynamically changing hemodynamic characteristics, and multi-layered precise regulatory mechanisms of the coagulation system in the human body. Further exploration is still needed to achieve the dual regulatory goals of effective wound healing and scientific hemostasis, thereby promoting the healthy recovery of patients.

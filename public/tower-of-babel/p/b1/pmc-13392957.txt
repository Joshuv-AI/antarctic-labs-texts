Single-cell transcriptome characterization of channel catfish (Ictalurus punctatus) B lymphocyte cell lines
Authors: Johanna E. Aldersey, Miles D. Lange, Benjamin H. Beck, Jason W. Abernathy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Single cell RNA-seq created high-resolution transcriptional profiles of the 3B11 and 1G8 catfish B cell lines.•Catfish B cell lines express canonical B cell markers at different levels and reveal distinct transcriptional profiles.•The expression of different B cell developmental genes suggests both display a lymphoblast phenotype.

Introduction

Beyond the use of immortalized cell lines for the characterization of the natural biology of cells, they have applications in understanding disease mechanisms, developing assays for disease diagnosis, and developing drugs and vaccines [[1], [2], [3]]. Pathogenic diseases are among the primary causes of production loss in catfish farming [4,5]. The two main cultured species are channel catfish (Ictalurus punctatus) and channel x blue catfish (Ictalurus furcatus) hybrids in the U.S aquaculture. Disease impacts the sustainability and efficiency of the catfish production systems and producers are perpetually interested in new disease control methods and interventions. In addition to industry significance, channel catfish are also an important comparative model species for the immune system [[6], [7], [8], [9]]. The historic interest in catfish as an immune model stems not only from the practical aspects of vaccine development and fish health issues but also from a variety of basic questions concerning the evolution of immune systems [[10], [11], [12], [13]]. Based on some of this initial research it was recognized that long term cell lines could be established from normal catfish peripheral blood lymphocytes (PBL) using different mitogens [14]. Depending upon the mode of stimulation, morphologically and functionally distinct catfish lymphoid cell lines resembling B and T cells and other monocytes were developed [15]. The 3B11 and 1G8 B cell lines were developed in the early 1990s, and were the first long-term immortalized B cell lines established from an ectothermic vertebrate [16]. The cell lines were created through in vitro lipopolysaccharide (LPS) stimulation of PBL where transformed clones were then developed through limiting dilution to generate individual clonotypes [17]. The cell lines have since been used to investigate different functional aspects of teleost immunology [[18], [19], [20]].

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNAseq) enables the characterization of gene expression at the individual cell level. This contrasts with bulk RNA sequencing, where the transcriptome averages the gene expression across multiple cells and cell types. This means that rare cell types and lowly expressed genes are often masked within the data [21]. Previously, our group developed single nuclei transcriptomic atlases of channel catfish unfractionated splenic cells and IgM+ sorted splenic cells [22,23]. These analyses identified both canonical B cell markers and expressed genes that identify the different developmental stages of channel catfish B cells. We have used this information to better understand the ‘revolving’ cell heterogeneity of the immortalized catfish B cell lines 3B11 and 1G8 at the single-cell level. The current research provides additional insight into their biology and transformed state.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

The catfish clonal B cell lines, 3B11 and 1G8 [16,17], were maintained in filter-sterilized complete AL-4 medium (cAL-4) consisting of equal parts of AIM V medium and l-15 medium (Corning, Manassas, VA) adjusted to catfish tonicity with 10% deionized H2O and supplemented with 1 mg/mL NaHCO3, 1x PenStrep Glutamine solution, 50 mM 2-mercaptoethanol, and 4% heat-inactivated pooled catfish serum as previously described [20]. Cell culture for each was initiated from frozen aliquots that were maintained in liquid nitrogen. Briefly, aliquots were quickly thawed in room temperature water, transferred to 4 mL of cAL-4 medium and centrifuged at 200 x g using a TX-400 swinging bucket in a ST16R centrifuge (ThermoFisher, Waltham, MA). The supernatant was aspirated and the suspension cells were resuspended in 5 mL cAL-4 medium and placed into a 25 cm2 vented tissue culture flasks (VWR, Allentown, PA) and incubated at 28 °C, 5% CO2 in a humidified Heracell VIOS 250 cell culture incubator (ThermoFisher, Waltham, MA). After three days cell viability was assessed by staining cells with Trypan blue and counting using a TC-20 cell counter (BioRad, Hercules, CA) per the manufacturer’s instructions. If the culture viability was > 90%, the cells were maintained for two more days before being split. Cultures were maintained for 2–3 weeks prior to experimental harvest, and the cells were split into 25 mL of cAL-4 medium and placed into a 75 cm2 vented tissue culture flasks (VWR, Allentown, PA) one week prior to single-cell library preparation. On the day of library preparation, cell viability was assessed for each cell line culture (> 94%). Each individual cell line culture was centrifuged at 200 x g, the supernatant aspirated, and cells were resuspended in 1x PBS (5% FBS) at 2 × 106/mL. The cell concentration was diluted to the target concentration required for optimal library preparation.

Single-cell transcriptome library construction

The scRNAseq libraries were prepared using the Chromium X Instrument (10x Genomics) and the Chromium GEM-X Single Cell 3′ GEM Kit v4 (10x Genomics, Pleasanton, CA) following the manufacturer’s protocol. The sample quality and quantity were assessed using an Agilent TapeStation (Santa Clara, CA) with the High Sensitivity D5000 ScreenTape (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA), and Qubit Fluorometer (ThermoFisher, Waltham, MA). Single-cell libraries were multiplexed and sequenced using the Illumina NovaSeq X Plus sequencer (San Diego, CA) via a service provider (GENEWIZ/Azenta Life Sciences, South Plainfield, NJ), with a target sequencing depth of ≥ 20,000 paired-end (2 × 150 bp) reads/cell.

Data processing

The raw data was demultiplexed to FASTQ files using Cell Ranger (v5.0.0) ‘cellranger mkfastq’. Primary splenic B‑cell transcriptomic data were obtained from previously published single‑nuclei RNA‑seq datasets (NCBI GEO accession GSE268023). Because single‑nuclei and single‑cell RNA‑seq differ in RNA capture and transcript representation, particularly for cytoplasmic and rapidly degraded transcripts, we did not combine these datasets with the cell line single‑cell RNA‑seq data. Instead, the splenic dataset was used only as an independent qualitative reference for “baseline” B‑cell gene‑expression patterns.

The channel catfish genome RefSeq assembly was downloaded from the NCBI (Coco_2.0, GCF_001660625.3). The GTF annotation file was manually curated to include immunoglobulin heavy constant mu (ighm), as before [19]. The annotation for ighm spans chr2:17,077,000–17,085,528 based on the channel catfish genomic DNA sequence of the IgM heavy chain (NCBI Accession number XX52617.1) [12,13]. The membrane bound ighm transcripts and secreted ighm transcripts were counted separately. The membrane ighm (mighm) annotation only included the transmembrane exons (TM1 and TM2), while the secreted ighm (sighm) annotation only included the C4 domain. These regions are unique to the respective transcripts.

The channel catfish genome sequence was converted to a Cell Ranger compatible format using ‘cellranger mkref’ (Cell Ranger v8.0.1). Then single cell reads were trimmed, aligned to the reference assembly using the modified GTF file, filtered, and counted to generate feature-barcode matrices using ‘cellranger count’ and argument set to exclude reads aligning to introns.

Further filtering was conducted using Seurat (v5.0.1) in R (v4.5.2). First, the barcodes predicted to be doublets and multiplets were identified using the ‘scDblFinder’ function from the scDblFinder package (v1.22.0) and these barcodes were removed from the Seurat object. Next, transcripts that were detected in < 3 cells were excluded. For the cell lines, barcodes with < 1500 and > 5000 features, < 5000 and > 20,000 UMIs were removed. For the spleen samples, barcodes with < 200 and > 2000 features, < 300 and > 10,000 UMIs were removed. Barcodes that had expression from mitochondrial genes > 10% were also removed from the data as they are likely to be distressed/dying cells. The data was normalized and regularized using the ‘SCTransform’ function (sctransform, v0.4.3) and the ‘vars.to.regress’ argument was used to reduce the contribution of the percentage of mitochondrial DNA. The spleen samples were integrated using harmony (v1.2.4), where sample ID was used as input for batch correction (theta = 0.5). For all samples, “RunPCA” was performed, followed by “FindNeighbours” and “RunUMAP” including principal component (PC) dimensions 1:15 to generate UMAP plots. B-cell lineage cells were identified based on the expression of canonical B cell markers (pax5, ighm, ighd). These cells were subset and the cluster analysis repeated on the splenic B cell subset. “FindClusters” was run for the spleen derived B cells with the resolution set to 0.1. UCell (v2.12.0) was used to calculate gene signature scores using the Mann-Whitney U statistic. Four combinations of heavy chain expression were scored: total IgM (mighm + sighm), total IgD (mighd +LOC108277513 [sighd]), total membrane IgM (mighm, mighd) and secreted IgD (sighd, LOC108277513). The built in cell cycle genes from Seurat were used to generate scores for the synthesis (S phase) and growth 2/mitosis (G2/M phase) phases of the cell cycle [23].

Comparing the cell lines, the data were merged, normalized and scaled using the “merge”, “NormalizeData”, “FindVariableFeatures” and “ScaleData” functions in Seurat, then the Model-based Analysis of Single-cell Transcriptomics (MAST; v1.38.0) package [24] with the “FindAllMarkers” function was used to identify differentially expressed genes. MAST uses a hurdle model that fits a two-part generalized linear models (GLM) for the rate of expression over the background and positive expression mean [24]. Significant genes had adjusted P values < 0.05, average Log2FC > 1 and expressed in at least 25% of cells in the culture.

Over-representation analysis (ORA) was conducted using clusterProfiler (v4.20.0) for Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and Gene Ontology (GO) [25]. ORA analysis, which uses the hypergeometric test, was carried out with the `enrichGO` and `enrichKEGG` functions.

Sandwich ELISA

A sandwich ELISA was used to evaluate soluble IgM in 1G8 and 3B11 cell culture supernatants. 1 mL of each cell suspension was placed into a 1.5 mL tube and centrifuged 200 x g for 5 min to pellet cells. The supernatants were removed and stored at −80 °C until needed. Immulon 2HB 96-well plates (ThermoFisher, Waltham, MA) were coated with 100 μL of 2 μg/mL of anti-channel catfish IgM recombinant mouse monoclonal antibody (r9E1) for 1 h. Plates were then rinsed three times with 1x PBS with 0.05% Tween-20 (PBST) and then incubated for 1 h in blocking solution (PBST with 5% powdered milk). One hundred μL of a 1:2 dilution of 1G8 or 3B11 cell supernatant was added to the r9E1-coated ELISA plate and serially diluted to 1:1024 and incubated at room temperature for 1 h. Plates were washed as described above and 100 μL of r9E1-HRP (1:1000) was added for 1 h of incubation [20]. Plates were rinsed three times with PBST, and 50 μL of 1-Step Ultra TMB-ELISA substrate solution (ThermoFisher, Waltham, MA) was added. The peroxidase reaction was terminated after 20 min with 50 μL of ELISA Stop Solution (ThermoFisher, Waltham, MA) and read spectrophotometrically at 490 nm with a Cytation 1 reader (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA)

Telomerase reverse transcriptase-targeted genome sequencing

Genomic DNA was purified from actively growing 1G8 and 3B11 cultures (∼1 × 106 cells) using the Monarch® HMW DNA Extraction Kit for Cells and Blood (New England Biolabs, Ipswich, MA). Genomic DNA sample quality and quantity was assessed using an Agilent TapeStation (Santa Clara, CA) with the High Sensitivity D5000 ScreenTape (Santa Clara, CA) and Qubit 4 Fluorometer (ThermoFisher Scientific, Waltham, MA). An aliquot of each DNA prep was normalized to 1 µg in 49 µL water and sheared by centrifugation in a Covaris g-TUBE (PerkinElmer, Shelton, CT) for 1 min at 4800 x g. Samples were concentrated in a SpeedVac™ DNA Concentrator (ThermoFisher Scientific) and then sequencing libraries created using the Native Barcoding Kit 24 V14 (Oxford Nanopore Technologies, Oxford, United Kingdom) per the manufacturer’s protocol. The two libraries were pooled and a FLO-MIN114 flow cell was loaded for sequencing on a GridION (Oxford Nanopore).

Prior to the start of the sequencing run, the GridION was set up to use the ‘Adaptive Sampling’ feature to enrich reads for the telomerase reverse transcriptase (tert) gene. The coordinates of the tert mRNA sequence (NCBI accession #XM_017485148.3) from the Coco_2.0 channel catfish RefSeq genome assembly (NCBI accession #GCF_001660625.3) was used to create a custom file (BED file) containing this genomic region information. The start and end coordinates within this BED file were then extended 10 kb to create a sequencing buffer around the tert gene. This file along with the channel catfish genome chromosome 14 sequence (NCBI accession #NC_030429.2) was used as the template for Adaptive Sampling enrichment.

Results

Single-cell transcriptomes were generated to characterize channel catfish B cell lines 3B11 and 1G8. Additionally, splenic single-nuclei transcriptomic data from three healthy channel catfish were used as a reference of “normal” B cell expression. We examined the expression of selected genes on the discrete datasets and examined qualitative differences between the cell lines and splenic data. Further, the cell lines were merged and compared to identify quantitative differences in gene expression.

Data quality

Single-cell RNA sequencing of 3B11 cell line produced ∼774 million reads from 11,882 cells, while the 1G8 cell line produced ∼844 million reads for 12,970 cells (Table S1). The splenic libraries from the three channel catfish (SP1, SP2, and SP3) had an average of ∼279 million reads from a mean 5944 cells before the B cells were subset (Table S1) [22]. The barcode rank plots indicated that there was good separation of cell-associated barcodes and barcodes representing background noise, which were excluded from the analysis (Figures S1 and S2). The median features detected per cell for the cell lines were ∼3200 whereas for the splenic cells there were ∼530 features detected per cell. Overall, the sequencing and mapping parameters indicated that high quality reads were generated from the transcriptome libraries. After further filtering, 9467 cells remained for the 3B11 cell line and 9431 cells for 1G8 cell line (Table S1) and 2018 cells for the splenic B cells (Table S1).

Canonical B cell markers

To establish the expression of catfish B cell markers, we generated feature maps and violin plots of previously defined B cell genes (cd37, cd79a, cd79b, mighd [membrane], mighm [membrane], sighm [secreted] and pax5) [22]. We first evaluated the splenic B cell population representing three different transcriptional profiles of B lymphocytes (Figs. 1, 2A, Figure S3A) in which the B cell genes were expressed at different levels. Clusters 1 and 2 have varying levels of expression of these markers, and cluster 3 highly expressed sighm indicating they are likely antibody secreting cells (Figs. 1, 2A, Figure S3A). We next sought to evaluate B cell gene expression among the 3B11 and 1G8 cell lines. Of the 3B11 cells, 44.3% and 89.1% expressed cd79a and pax5, respectively, and only a subset of cells (2.6–4.0%) highly expressing mighd, mighm and sighm (Fig. 2B, Figure S3B). Expression of cd37 and cd79b were not detected in the 3B11 cells. In contrast, the 1G8 cells expressed each of the genes, except for cd37 and cd79b which were detected in ∼ 5% of cells (Fig. 2C, Figure S3C). cd79a was significantly more highly expressed in 1G8 cells (Adj p-value = 0, log2FC = 1.77, expression detected = 86.2%). The immunoglobulin genes were also significantly highly expressed (mighm: Adj p-value = 0, log2FC = 6.18, expression detected = 95.7%; sighm: Adj p-value = 0, log2FC = 6.27, expression detected = 93.4%; mighd: Adj p-value = 0, log2FC = 6.53, expression detected = 97.8%). Immunoglobulin expression

To further determine the relative expression of different combinations of IgD and IgM isotypes, we utilized a statistical rank-based scoring system to compare the gene signatures against all other genes in the cell, resulting in an enrichment score that infers the level of expression in each cell for mighd/mighm, mighm/sighm or mighd/sighd (LOC108277513). In the splenic B cells, mighd and mighm are ranked highly and are found throughout the population indicating a strong expression signature for these genes. The mighm/sighm and mighd/sighd displayed similar rankings among the splenic B cell population (Fig. 3A). The 3B11 cells mostly scored close to zero for Ig expression; however, a small population of cells co-expressed both mighm/sighm and mighd/sighd (Fig. 3B). The 1G8 cells had comparable rankings to the spleen with disseminated membrane immunoglobulins though it appears ighm contributes more to the score than ighd as the mighd/sighd rank was lower overall with few higher-ranking cells (Fig. 3C). It should be noted that sighd contributed much less to the total IgD expression in any of the libraries (Figure S4). Overall, though both cell lines appear to have maintained the ability to transcribe soluble and membrane-bound IgD and IgM, 3B11 produced them at a much lower rate. B cell development

To further explore the developmental stage at which the B cell lines were transformed we again relied on feature maps and violin plots to first visualize genes expressed by different populations of catfish splenic B cell clusters [22,23]. Cluster 1 predominantly expressed blnk, ccr9a, cfl1, foxp1b, slab2 and tamalin, while cluster 2 predominantly expressed alox5a and maptb, and cluster 3 exhibited a plasmablast/plasma cell (PB/PC) transcriptional profile with the expression of cf1, mki67, prdm1a, selenom and xbp1 (Figs. 1, 4A, Figure S6A). When examining expression patterns between the two B cell lines, we did not observe the same population distinctions we had with the integrated spleen dataset (Fig. 4B-C, Figure S6B-C). Interestingly, the 3B11 and 1G8 cell lines showed low expression of alox5a and blnk which were expressed by splenic B cell clusters 2 and 1, respectively. The cell lines do express calr3a, cd40, ikzf1, sla2b which are expressed across the splenic B cell clusters (Fig. 4B-C, Figure S6B-C). CD40 and IKAROS (ikzf1) are involved in perpetuating B cell development and SLAP2 (sla2b) is generally associated with BCR signaling; however, all have the potential to be differentially expressed in some cancers and are abundantly expressed in these cell lines [[26], [27], [28]]. The cell lines also expressed ccr9a and tamalin at varying levels which are associated with splenic B cell cluster 1. Cell cycling

The cell cycle regulates growth and proliferation through different stages (G1, S, G2 and M) to replicate and divide the cell. We utilized the statistical rank-based scoring system to assign an enrichment score that ranks the level of expression of S and G2/M genes in each cell. In the splenic B cells, it appears that the level of cell replication is minimal with the highest ranking observed for both S and G2/M phases in cluster 3 (Fig. 6A). This cell population likely represents proliferating B cells, perhaps plasmablasts. Among the B cell lines there is a much higher level of S and G2/M expression in different groups of cells (Fig. 6B-C); specifically, there are discrete cells for each that are either predominantly in S or G2/M phases. Cell line immortalization

Lastly, we sought to understand the process by which the cell lines were transformed, and perhaps what led to their immortalization. To further explore the way in which the B cell lines may have been transformed, we used feature maps to visualize the expression of oncogenes. There are well-known oncogenes or genes known to be involved in oncogenesis, such as HSP90, MYC and TP53. We first evaluated expression in the spleen: hsp90b1 and hsp90aa1.2 were variably expressed, but hsp90ab1 expression was much more pronounced among the splenic B cells; myca had low expression while mycb was expressed throughout; tp53 was minimally expressed (Fig. 7A). In 3B11 and 1G8, hsp90b1, hsp90aa1.2 and hsp90ab1 were expressed in most of each cell line population, and tp53 has similar expression in both cell lines. myca was more highly expressed in 3B11 compared to 1G8 (adj. p-value = 0, log2FC = 2.5, expression detected = 61.7%) while mycb was more highly expressed in 1G8 than 3B11 (adj. p-value = 0, log2FC = 2.5, expression detected = 92.4%) (Fig. 7B-C; Table S2). One gene previously implicated in the transformation of the catfish B cell lines [35,36] is tert (telomerase) and was not detected in the spleen. Unlike the primary splenic B cells, tert has expression in some 3B11 and 1G8 cells. The overexpression of telomerase in these cells is inferred to have occurred during B cell activation after LPS stimulation [35]. Previous work has linked the tert promoter region as an active target for mutations in some cancers whereby de novo ETS-factor binding sites are created [37,38]. We performed tert‑targeted genome sequencing of the 3B11 and 1G8 genomic DNA and generated the full length tert gene for each. After constructing assemblies of the full-length tert gene for each the promoter regions were aligned with the catfish genome and reference mRNA (Fig. 7). We identified the endogenous ETS-factor binding sites (TTCC/AAGG); however, there were no additional ETS-binding sites identified in either B cell line indicating that telomerase dysregulation due to site mutation/expansion. Discussion

We employed single cell sequencing to study channel catfish B lymphocyte cell lines and conducted a comparative study with primary splenic B cells. The 3B11 and 1G8 are immortalized, clonal B cell lines that were developed in the early 1990s and represent the first long-term B cell lines established from an ectothermic vertebrate [15,16]. These lines have been used to evaluate different teleost immune processes, particularly in analysing B cell receptor signaling and, immunoglobulin structure, and in the validation of immunological reagents [18,20,39,40]. We were interested in using scRNAseq technology to perform a detailed analyses of these perpetual cell lines to better understand their transcriptional profile. Herein, we gained insight from the single cell transcriptomes for each cell line which uncovered cell heterogeneity within the cell culture populations, as done in other cell lines [8,41]. We found that despite the cell lines being generated using similar methods, the transcriptomes were different and that, when stimulated by mitogens, the transformation changes the properties of the B cells to encompass many stages of B cell development [35]. The characterization will enable researchers to use these cell lines to make informed decisions regarding their research design.

We had previously established canonical markers to verify B cell gene expression of channel catfish splenic B cells [22,23]. In this study, B cell lines express predominant B cell markers such as pax5 [9,42] and secreted and membrane-bound IgM and IgD, though expression of immunoglobulins is limited to a few cells for the 3B11 cell line. 3B11 and 1G8 also express BCR accessory molecules cd79a/b, although at differing levels, and this appears to be consistent with what had previously been demonstrated where 3B11 and 1G8 culture maturity was coupled to the relative expression of cd79a/b over time [18]. It is notable that the 3B11 cell line had detected expression for cd37 or cd79b, while 1G8 had expression for each gene in a small number of cells. It is possible that the absence of these genes in the 3B11 could be the result of mutations over the life of the cell line as 3B11 had previously been shown to express cd79b [43], and a loss of function may have occurred as shown in mammalian B cell lymphomas [[44], [45], [46]]. The 1G8 and 3B11 cell lines have previously been shown to secrete IgM [16,17], but secreted IgD has not been evaluated. Both B cell lines express at varying levels both mighd and mighm and secrete soluble IgM in their supernatants [7,47,48]. The level of secreted IgM in the supernatants was interesting because there was significantly lower gene expression and fewer cells expressing sighm in the 3B11 cell line, yet the detectable protein levels were relatively similar. The discrepancy between transcript and the assembled secreted IgM could because by other factors such a protein degradation [49], Ig assembly, and light chain availability.

The development of long term cell lines was initiated when catfish peripheral blood lymphocytes were stimulated with different mitogens; LPS was ultimately shown to be best suited for the generation of long-term cultures that only contained B cells [16,17]. The LPS stimulation ‘short circuits’ BCR cross-linking which leads to activation and proliferation and an Ig secreting lymphoblast phenotype [14,50]. The splenic primary B cells represent different development stages with naïve and mature cell populations found through previously identified markers [9,22,23]. However, the 3B11 and 1G8 B cell lines exhibit a quasi-naïve/plasmablast/plasma cell profile based on the different markers that were expressed which is consistent with what has been found in EBV transformed human lymphoblast cell lines derived from different cancer types [51]. However, each cell line also expressed ccl3 and cd80, cd86 which are upregulated upon activation [52,53]. 3B11 and 1G8 represent B cells that have been activated independent of antigen and, therefore, once transformed and immortalized present as different developmental B cell stages.

Previous work had sought to understand the mechanism by which transformation had occurred in different immortal clonal catfish lymphocyte cell lines, including 3B11 and 1G8 [35,36]. The authors evaluated p53, hsp70 and telomerase expression by Northern blot and identified an abundance of hsp70 and to some degree p53 among the immortalized cell lines as compared to primary lymphocytes [35,36]. They also noted that hsp70 expression increased over culture time; however, p53 expression did not increase over time. Another experiment in the same study demonstrated a stark increase in telomerase activity in primary cells stimulated with several different mitogens when in cultured for up to 28 d with some showing increased activity in as little as two days post stimulation, which is identical to the origin of the 3B11 and 1G8 B cell lines [35]. We did not identify hsp70 gene expression among any of the libraries (data not shown), however hsp90aa1.2 may have higher expression in the B cell lines than in the splenic B cells, while hsp90ab1 seemed consistent among the different B cell populations [39]. tp53 expression was consistent with the previous work [36] and showed nominal expression in the spleen with much higher expression in 3B11 and 1G8 [40]. The most interesting observation was the expression telomerase (tert), which showed little to no expression in the primary splenic B cells, however; was moderately expressed by 3B11 and 1G8 cell populations. This observation is consistent with the hypothesis put forth 20+ years ago about the immortalization of these catfish B cell lines [35]. High-level intra-tumor TERT expression was associated with increased expression of genes involved in B cell regulation (cd22, cd40, cd79b, pax5) [54]. We found that cd40, cd79a and ikzf1 may be upregulated in these catfish B cell lines which is consistent with other B cell line expression [26,27]. Finally, based on previous research, we posited that tert dysregulation might have been activated through an accumulation of ETS-binding mutations within the tert promoter region [37,[55], [56], [57]]. We did not identify mutations that would lead to additional ETS-binding domains and therefore concluded that, as has been indicated in non-Hodgkin lymphoma and other lymphomas [38,54,57,58], this mechanism has not led to B cell line immortalization in channel catfish.

As the splenic B cells transcriptomes were generated under different conditions to the cell lines (nuclei, read depth, batch), our interpretations are limited. For example, the read depth of the splenic data was lower and therefore, lowly expressed genes may not be detected. The spleen data were generated using nuclei, which means that only mRNA present in the nucleus is counted. The cell line data was generated from cells so mRNA exported from the nucleus can be counted [59]. For these reasons, we chose to use the spleen data as a descriptive “reference” to represent B cells but did not directly compare the splenic B cells to the cell lines with statistical tests. Our study provides a resource for researchers using the 3B11 and 1G8 cell lines. Here we offer descriptive comparisons between the cell lines and splenic B cells to understand the developmental state and programming of the cell lines. Ultimately, these are transformed cell lines and have likely lost some characteristics of in vivo B cells including their ability to react appropriately to stimulation and prolonged passage could have led to chromosomal abnormality and or mutations that could lead to loss of function [60,61]. Researchers interested in functional studies using these cell lines can use the data to select the most appropriate line to investigate genes or proteins of interest.

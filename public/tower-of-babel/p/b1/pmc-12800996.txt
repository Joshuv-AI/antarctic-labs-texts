Arthroscopic Biologic Preparation of the Posterior Glenoid Rim for Posterior Labral Repair: The Anterior-Superior Portal Provides the Optimal Angle of Attack
Authors: Luke A. Nordstrom, Joseph W. Galvin, Garrett V. Christensen, Brendan M. Patterson, James V. Nepola, John M. Tokish
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Arthroscopic posterior labral repair is an effective surgical technique for managing patients with posterior labral tears and symptomatic unidirectional posterior shoulder instability. It is well documented that a curved liberator from the mid-glenoid (anterior-inferior) portal passed from anterior to posterior provides an effective trajectory for liberation and mobilization of the posterior and posterior-inferior labral tear. However, biologic preparation of the posterior and inferior glenoid rim with a shaver can be challenging because the anterior mid-glenoid portal and the posterior portal do not provide an adequate trajectory for instruments to be parallel to the posterior glenoid rim. The purpose of this technical note is to describe a simple efficient procedure for biologic preparation of the posterior and inferior glenoid rim in the setting of arthroscopic posterior labral repair.

Posterior shoulder instability is becoming increasingly recognized as a cause of pain and dysfunction in young athletes.1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 Arthroscopic posterior labral repair is an effective surgical technique for reducing pain, restoring shoulder function, and preventing recurrent glenohumeral instability in patients with symptomatic posterior labral tears and unidirectional posterior instability.9,10 Prior reports have documented that liberation and mobilization of the torn posterior and posterior-inferior labrum are best accomplished by using a curved liberator through the anterior mid-glenoid portal.11,12 However, this trajectory is not ideal for biologic preparation of the posterior and posterior-inferior glenoid rim with a shaver because it is nearly perpendicular to the posterior glenoid rim and can lead to iatrogenic excess labral debridement and chondral injury.

The purpose of this technical note is to describe an efficient procedure for biologic preparation of the posterior and inferior glenoid rim in the setting of posterior labral repair. The anterior-superior portal provides the optimal angle of attack. Surgical Technique

Step 1: Setup and Patient Positioning

The procedure can be performed with the patient in the beach-chair or lateral decubitus position. Our preference is to perform arthroscopic posterior labral repair with the patient in the lateral decubitus position. After administration of general anesthesia and a regional nerve block, the patient is positioned in the lateral decubitus position on a beanbag with an axillary roll. All bony prominences are well padded. An examination under anesthesia is performed prior to placement of the operative arm in an arm-holder device (Arthrex, Naples, FL) with 10 lb of traction. The patient undergoes sterile preparation and draping, and a formal timeout is performed, confirming the correct operative site and delivery of perioperative antibiotics.

Step 2: Portal Placement and Diagnostic Arthroscopy

A diagnostic shoulder arthroscopy is performed in standard fashion. The standard posterior viewing portal is placed approximately 2 cm inferior and 1 cm medial to the posterolateral corner of the acromion. The anterior-superior portal is then established in the rotator interval with the aid of a spinal needle to determine the optimal trajectory. The anterior-superior portal is placed high in the rotator interval, just inferior to the superior labral-biceps junction, with care taken not to pierce the anterior border of the supraspinatus tendon. Figure 1 depicts the typical location of the anterior-superior portal as viewed intra-articularly. A formal diagnostic arthroscopy is then performed. A switching stick is placed posteriorly, and the arthroscope is subsequently placed in the anterior-superior portal to best view and probe the posterior and posterior-inferior labral tear. A 7.25-mm cannula is then placed over the switching stick to establish the posterior portal.

Step 3: Establishment of Mid-Glenoid Portal, Liberation, and Mobilization

The arthroscope is placed back into the posterior portal, and the mid-glenoid (anterior inferior) portal is established. A spinal needle is used to make the mid-glenoid portal, which is more inferior to the anterior-superior rotator interval portal. As previously described, the arthroscope is placed in the anterior-superior portal, and a curved liberator is placed through the mid-glenoid portal to allow liberation and mobilization of the posterior and posterior-inferior labral tear (Video 1).11,12 Once adequate mobilization is achieved, which can be confirmed by grasping the labrum and capsular tissue with a grasping instrument and simulating the repair to the glenoid, the next step is biologic preparation of the posterior glenoid rim to optimize healing.

Step 4: Biologic Preparation of Posterior and Posterior-Inferior Glenoid Rim

While viewing from the mid-glenoid portal, the surgeon inserts the arthroscopic shaver through the anterior-superior portal. This allows the arthroscopic shaver to be parallel to the posterior glenoid rim (Fig 2). The glenoid rim is prepared with the shaver until a bleeding bed of bone is encountered (Video 1). The surgeon must not over-resect the bone because this may lead to iatrogenic posterior glenoid bone loss. Once adequate biologic preparation is achieved, standard arthroscopic posterior and posterior-inferior labral repair is performed. Discussion

Prior reports have documented that the arthroscopic mid-glenoid (anterior-inferior) portal provides the optimal trajectory for insertion of curved liberators and rasps for mobilization and liberation of posterior and posterior-inferior labral tears.11,12 However, these reports also describe insertion of arthroscopic shavers through this portal for biologic preparation. The challenge with this is that the trajectory is not ideal because the shaver instrument is nearly perpendicular to the posterior-inferior glenoid rim. Rasps passed through the mid-glenoid portal can also be used to prepare the glenoid rim; however, many of these instruments are large and bulky, which limits safe and efficient preparation of the bone. Using the simple and safe technique described in this article, we believe that the anterior-superior portal provides the optimal angle of attack to prepare the posterior and posterior-inferior glenoid rim with an arthroscopic shaver instrument. This allows preparation of a bleeding bed of bone of the posterior and inferior glenoid rim for later labral repair.

The anterior-superior rotator interval portal provides the optimal trajectory for biologic preparation of the posterior glenoid rim with an arthroscopic shaver. We have found that this portal allows the surgeon to prepare a bleeding bed of bone along the entire posterior glenoid rim and even allows preparation of the inferior glenoid all the way around to the 6-o’clock position on the face of the glenoid.

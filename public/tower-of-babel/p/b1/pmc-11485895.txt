Selenium participates in the formation of kidney stones by alleviating endoplasmic reticulum stress and apoptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells
Authors: Xiaozhe Su, Hongbo Chen, Heng Xiang, Hu Ke, Caitao Dong, Qianlin Song, Jiawei Zhou, Qinhong Jiang, Yunhan Wang, Liang Chen, Sixing Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectives: To investigate the role of selenium and selenium-containing proteins in the etiology and pathogenesis of kidney stones.Methods: The HK-2 cell line was subjected to supersaturation oxalate treatment to establish an in vitro model of calcium oxalate kidney stones, while SD rats were administered with ethylene glycol to establish an in vivo model of calcium oxalate kidney stones. qPCR analysis was employed to investigate the alterations in selenoproteins within the models, and subsequently, genes exhibiting significant changes were identified. Subsequently, based on the functions of these genes, their regulatory effects on endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) and apoptosis during the disease progression were examined both in HK-2 cells and rat kidneys. Finally, Selenomethionine (SeMet) supplementation was introduced to explore its therapeutic potential for kidney stone management.Results: The involvement of Selenoprotein K in the pathogenesis of calcium oxalate kidney stone disease has been confirmed, exhibiting significant alterations. Manipulation of its expression levels through overexpression and knockdown techniques resulted in a corresponding reduction or increase in oxidative stress, ERS, and apoptosis within renal tubular epithelial cells. SelK regulates ERS and apoptosis by controlling the IRE1-ASK1-JNK pathway. In addition, SeMet treatment, which contains selenium, effectively reduced the levels of oxidative stress, ERS, and apoptosis in vivo and in vitro models, thereby alleviating tubular epithelial cell damage and reducing the formation of kidney stones in experimental rats.Discussion: Selenium is involved in the occurrence and development of kidney stones by regulating oxidative damage to renal tubular epithelial cells. The results suggest that dietary selenium supplementation in daily life may be of great significance for the prevention and treatment of kidney stones.

Introduction

Kidney stones are a common urological condition and have shown a steadily increasing incidence in recent years, as well as a notable recurrence rate. Among the constituents, calcium oxalate stands out as the most frequent. Elevated concentrations of oxalate in urine have been reported as a significant precipitating factor in the crystallization of calcium oxalate [1]. Supersaturated oxalate(Ox) can induce damage to renal tubular epithelial cells. Our previous research indicated that renal tubular epithelial cells exposed to supersaturated oxalate induced endoplasmic reticulum stress, exacerbated autophagy, ferroptosis, and increased apoptosis. Additionally, high oxalate urine model rats also exhibited significant renal tubular calcification [2–5].

The endoplasmic reticulum plays a crucial role in regulating protein processing/synthesis, lipid synthesis, and calcium homeostasis. Upon stimulation by external injury factors, a significant accumulation of unfolded/misfolded proteins occurs within the endoplasmic reticulum lumen, leading to the unfolded protein response [6]. The accumulation of a large quantity of unfolded/misfolded proteins results in endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) [7]. A prolonged or severe state of ERS compromises homeostasis and facilitates the occurrence of apoptosis [8].

Selenium is an essential trace element in the human body and plays a wide range of roles within biological systems [9]. Clinical studies have confirmed a negative correlation between selenium levels and kidney stones in the human body [10]. At present, 25 selenoproteins have been identified in the human body, among which ER-resident selenoproteins are involved in regulating ER homeostasis [11]. Selenoprotein K (SelK) functions as an ER membrane-resident protein, participating in the regulation of cellular redox and calcium homeostasis, as well as ER stability [12]. However, the specific role of SelK in the occurrence and development of kidney stones remains unknown.

Selenomethionine (SeMet) serves as an organic selenium supplement, readily available in daily life[13], Supplementing selenium can significantly increase the expression of various selenoproteins in cells and tissues, thereby enhancing the antioxidant capacity of experimental models [14–17]. In our study, we initially conducted qPCR analysis to assess the expression levels of 21 selenoproteins in renal tubular epithelial cells (RTECs) under conditions of supersaturated oxalate intervention. Following comparative analysis, SelK emerged as a gene exhibiting significant transcriptional alterations, prompting us to focus our investigation on SelK. Subsequently, we conducted in vivo and in vitro experiments utilizing SeMet as an additional selenium supplement to simulate realistic scenarios. Our results confirmed that SeMet supplementation effectively mitigated tissue and cellular damage induced by supersaturated oxalate, thereby reducing renal calcium deposition in SD rats with a high oxalate urine model.

Materials and methods

Cell culture, groups, treatments

The human proximal tubular cell line HK-2 (Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shanghai, China) was cultured in a cell culture incubator (Binder, Tuttlingen, Germany) at 37°C and 5% CO2 by using DMEM/F12 complete medium (PM150312B, Procell, China) containing 10% fetal bovine serum. In addition, serum-free DMEM/F12 basal medium was used to dissolve oxalate crystals to a concentration of 1.5 mM, and the 1.5 mM oxalate solution was used for cell intervention.

The effect of supersaturated oxalate on HK-2 and the regulatory effect of SelK in cells was explored.

HK-2 cells were grown to 70%−80% confluence, the old culture medium was discarded, the cells were washed with PBS, and serum-free DMEM/F12 basal medium or 1.5 mM oxalic acid solution was added for modeling intervention. The intervention lasted for 24 hours, and the cells were divided into the NC group and the Ox group. After the intervention, the cells and cell supernatants were collected for experiments.

The role of SelK in the formation of kidney stones was investigated. siRNA and lentivirus were used to regulate the corresponding genes. For SelK knockdown, HK-2 cells were treated with the control group siRNA and the knockdown SelK siRNA; thereafter, these groups were recorded as si-NC and si-SelK, respectively.

To overexpress SelK, HK-2 cells were transfected with the empty virus group and the lentivirus overexpressing SelK; the successfully transfected cells were recorded as OE-NC and OE-SelK, respectively.

Furthermore, to verify the role of SeMet in kidney stone formation, we pretreated HK-2 cells with SeMet:

HK-2 cells were grown to 30%−40% confluence, and a DMEM/F12 complete medium containing various concentrations of SeMet was added. The cells were allowed to grow for another 24 hours, reaching 70%−80% confluence, and were then treated with DMEM/F12 basal medium or 1.5 mM oxalic acid solution as previously stated.

SelK knockdown by siRNA transfection

The siRNA (Sangon Biotech Co., Shanghai, China) was utilized to downregulate SelK expression in HK-2 cells. The cells were transfected with siRNA using transfection reagent (HY-K1014, MCE, USA) for 24 h and cultured to 70% to 80% confluence.

Lentiviral overexpression of SelK in HK-2

The lentivirus for overexpressing SelK in HK-2 cells was purchased from GeneChem. Following the manufacturer's instructions, the lentivirus was co-incubated with transfection reagent A in HK-2 cells for 16 hours. Subsequently, the cells were treated with puromycin solution for cell selection for 72 hours.

Cell viability assay

Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) was employed to assess cellular viability. HK-2 cells were seeded at a density of 1 × 104 cells per well in 96-well plates and cultured until reaching 70%−80% confluency. Following the experimental intervention, the culture medium was aspirated, and the cells were subsequently washed twice with PBS. Subsequently, 100 μL of CCK-8 working solution, comprising 90 μL CCK-8 reagent and 10 μL basal medium, was added to each well. The plates were then incubated at 37°C for 2 hours. Finally, the absorbance was measured at 450 nm wavelength using a microplate reader (EnSight, PerkinElmer, USA).

Animal experiments

The animal experiments conducted in this study were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (serial number: 20230310F). All four-week-old male Sprague–Dawley (SD) rats were provided with a uniform standard diet and water ad libitum at baseline. Each group of the animal experiment consisted of 8 rats. The experimental groups were delineated as follows:

The sham group received a standard diet and water.

The stone model group received a standard diet supplemented with water containing 0.75% ethylene glycol.

The SeMet group received a standard diet supplemented with SeMet at a dose of 2 mg/kg/day, administered in water containing 0.75% ethylene glycol and adjusted based on the daily water intake of the rats.

Quantitative real-time reverse transcription PCR (qPCR)

In brief, total RNA from all samples was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher), followed by reverse transcription of total RNA into cDNA and subsequent qPCR analysis using a qPCR kit (Yeasen, China) in a PCR instrument. GAPDH was utilized as the reference gene for normalization purposes. The primer sequences can be found in Supplementary Table 1.

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

After the intervention, discard the culture medium. Wash the cells twice with PBS for each group. Fix the cell samples with electron microscopy fixative at room temperature for 15 minutes. Subsequently, detach the cells using a cell scraper, collect cell aggregates, and fix with osmium tetroxide at 4°C for 12 hours, followed by dehydration. After sectioning, stain with uranyl acetate and lead citrate, and observe under a transmission electron microscope (FEI Tecnai 12, Philips, Netherlands).

Reactive oxygen species (ROS) assay

A ROS probe was utilized to detect the levels of ROS in both cell and rat kidney tissue slices. For cells, following intervention, cells were washed twice with PBS and incubated with ROS detection reagent kit (Solarbio, Beijing) working solution at 37°C in the dark for 20 minutes. Subsequently, cells were washed three times with basal medium and then treated with basal medium. ROS levels within cells were then measured using flow cytometry. Rat kidney tissues were observed using fluorescence microscopy, and the average fluorescence intensity value for each group was analyzed using ImageJ software. The fluorescence intensity value was used to represent the ROS levels.

Mitochondrial membrane potential detection

A JC-1 kit (C2006, Beyotime, China) was used to detect changes in mitochondrial membrane potential. In healthy cells with high ΔΨm, JC-1 forms complexes known as J-aggregates. While in cells with low ΔΨm, JC-1 remains in the monomeric form. When excited at 510 nm, JC-1 monomers emit a green fluorescence with a maximum at ∼527 nm. Aggregates of JC-1 emit an orange-red fluorescence with a maximum at ∼590 nm. After intervention, according to the manufacturer's instructions, 2 mL of JC-1 working solution was added to each well and incubated at 37°C for 20 min, then the working solution was removed, the cells were washed twice with JC-1 staining buffer, and the cells were quickly observed under an inverted fluorescence microscope (IX71, Olympus, Japan) at 20× magnification.

Terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL)

A one step TUNEL apoptosis detection kit (MCE, HY-K1078) was used to was used to detect the level of apoptosis in each sample. When cells undergo apoptosis, specific DNA endonucleases will be activated, cutting the genomic DNA between the nucleosomes. The DNA of apoptotic cells is cleaved into multimers of 180–200 bp fragments. The exposed 3'-OH of the broken DNA can be catalyzed by Terminal Deoxynucleotidyl Transferase (TdT) with fluorescein labeled dUTP, which can be detected with fluorescence microscope or flow cytometer. Samples were prepared strictly according to the instructions and then observed by fluorescence microscopy (BX53, Olympus, Japan).

Annexin V/propidium iodide (PI) flow cytometry

After intervention, Annexin V – FITC/PI apoptosis detection kit (Solarbio, CA1020) was used to evaluate the apoptotic status according to strictly the manufacturer’s instructions. For each sample, cells were then sorted on a flow cytometer (CytoFlex, Beckman Coulter, USA) and analyzed using FlowJo software (FlowJo 10.6.2 version, USA).

Western blotting (WB)

The total proteins from all samples were extracted using radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer (R0010, Solarbio). Protein samples were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), and subsequently transferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. The membranes were then blocked, washed, and incubated with primary antibodies overnight. After washing, the membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies for 1 hour. Finally, protein bands were visualized using an Odyssey infrared imaging system (LI-COR, USA). ImageJ software was utilized to quantify the protein bands’ intensity, which was then normalized to GAPDH levels as an internal control.

Antibodies

The primary antibodies were as follows: glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase(GAPDH)(Proteintech, 60004-1-Ig),SelK(Proteintech, 12561-1-AP) GRP78 (Proteintech, 11587-1-AP), Protein kinase R – like ER kinase (PERK) (Proteintech, 20582-1-AP), Phospho-PERK (Proteintech, 29546-1-AP), Immunoglobulin-Regulated Enhancer 1(IRE1) (Proteintech, 27528-1-AP), Phospho-IRE1 (Abclonal, AP0878), Activating Transcription Factor 6(ATF6) (Proteintech, 10835-1-AP), C/EBP homologous protein (CHOP) (Proteintech, 15204-1-AP), Apoptosis Signal Regulating Kinase(ASK1) (Abcam, ab137339), Phospho-ASK1 (Abcam, ab278547), c-Jun N-terminal kinase(JNK) (Proteintech, 17572-1-AP), Phospho-JNK (Proteintech, 80024-1-PBS), BCL-2-associated X protein(BAX) (ABclonal, A12009), cleaved caspase-3 (Proteintech, 19677-1-AP), Phospho-p38 MAPK (Thr180/Tyr182) (Proteintech, 28796-1-AP).

Immunofluorescence

The samples were washed, fixed, permeabilized, and blocked following standard procedures. Subsequently, the samples were incubated with primary antibodies for 12 hours. After washing, the samples were incubated with the corresponding secondary antibodies for 30 minutes. The samples were washed again and the sealed slides were immediately observed and recorded under a fluorescence microscope (BX53, Olympus, Japan).

Von Kossa staining

The Von Kossa staining method was employed to assess the extent of stone aggregation in rat kidney tissues. In brief, wax blocks containing kidney samples were sectioned, deparaffinized, and washed. The sections were then immersed in a solution of silver nitrate for staining, followed by exposure to ultraviolet light for 10 minutes. Subsequent steps included washing, counterstaining, and sealing. Finally, observations and records were made under a microscope.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The rat kidney tissue sections were deparaffinized and processed; distilled water was used for washing three times, followed by antigen retrieval and blocking treatments. The sections were then incubated with primary antibodies at 4°C for 12 hours, washed, and subsequently incubated with corresponding secondary antibodies. Following steps included color development, counterstaining, dehydration, and sealing. Finally, observations and records were made under a microscope (BX53, Olympus, Japan).

Cell biochemistry assay

Glutathione (GSH) assay kit (A006-2-1, Nanjing Jiancheng, China) and (Malondialdehyde) MDA assay kit (A003-4-1, Nanjing Jiancheng, China) were used to detect biochemical indicators of cell samples. The experimental operation instructions were strictly followed. Finally, the absorbance values at 405 nm (for GSH) and 530 nm (for MDA) were measured using a microplate reader (PerkinElmer, Waltham, MA, USA).

Statistical analysis

All data were expressed as the mean ± standard deviations (SD) of at least three independent experiments. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software version 8.0 (GraphPad Software Inc, La Jolla, CA, USA). Comparisons between two groups were determined using the Student's t-test, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's post hoc tests were used for comparisons between multiple groups. P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Several selenium-related genes are involved in the damage of supersaturated oxalate to RTECs

To investigate whether selenoproteins are involved in the occurrence of kidney stones, the transcript levels of multiple selenoproteins were analyzed after the intervention. The qPCR results indicated changes in the transcription levels of several selenoproteins, with SelK showing a significant decrease in expression (Figure 1(a)). Therefore, SelK was selected as the target gene for subsequent research. Next, Western blotting was performed to detect the expression of SelK in cells from each group, revealing a significant decrease in SelK expression at the protein level (Figure 1(b)). Notably, TEM observations of the samples from each group revealed that the contact length between the ER and mitochondria significantly increased in the Ox group, resulting in swollen mitochondria (Figure 1(c)). Considering that SelK is a protein localized on the endoplasmic reticulum, and based on its known roles from previous studies, the expression of ERS and apoptosis-related proteins were further examined. The results showed that Ox intervention activated three ERS-related pathways, and the protein phosphorylation levels of p-PERK and p-IRE1 were significantly increased. Moreover, the protein expression levels of GRP78, CHOP, and ATF6 were significantly increased (Figure 1(d)). Among apoptosis-related proteins, the ASK1-JNK pathway was activated, the phosphorylation levels of ASK1, JNK and P38 were significantly increased by Ox intervention, the expressions of BAX and c-Caspase3 were significantly increased, and the expression of BCL-2 was significantly decreased (Figure 1(e)). Knocking down SelK promotes oxidative stress of RTECs induced by supersaturated oxalate intervention

The siRNA is used to knockdown the SelK in RTECs (Figure 2(a)). GSH is a key substrate of GPX4, and its content is directly positively correlated with the antioxidant capacity of cells. Moreover, MDA is a common lipid peroxide-related indicator. Then, the levels of GSH, MDA, intracellular ROS, and mitochondrial membrane potential were detected in each model to explore the changes in oxidative stress levels. The results indicated that with the knockdown of SelK, the levels of GSH (Figure 2(b)) and mitochondrial membrane potential (Figure 2(e)) decreased significantly, while MDA (Figure 2(c)) and intracellular ROS levels (Figure 2(d)) increased significantly. Furthermore, TEM was performed to observe samples from each group, revealing that knocking down SelK further increased the contact length between mitochondria and the ER compared to the Ox group (Figure 2(f)). Knocking down SelK promoted ERS and apoptosis of RTECs induced by supersaturated oxalate

Our WB experiments revealed that knocking down SelK (si-SelK) led to a significant increase in ERS levels induced by oxalate oversaturation. In addition, Ox intervention also increased the endoplasmic reticulum stress-related proteins in the si-NC group, while the addition of si-SelK further increased the protein expression levels of GRP78, ATF6, and CHOP, and increased the phosphorylation levels of IRE1 and PERK (Figure 3(a)). Immunofluorescence analysis also showed a significant increase in the key ERS-related protein GRP78 (Figure 3(b)). Moreover, the TUNEL staining showed the most positive results in the si-SelK + Ox group, which was significantly more than the si-NC + Ox group (Figure 3(c)). In addition, the flow cytometry results also suggested that under the same Ox intervention conditions, SelK knockdown would lead to a higher level of cell apoptosis (Figure 3(d)). WB validation confirmed a significant increase in the expression of BAX and cleaved Caspase-3 proteins, as well as elevated phosphorylation levels of ASK1, JNK and P38 (Figure 3(e)). To some extent, the above results indicate that knocking down SelK further promotes the apoptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells induced by Ox intervention. SelK was successfully overexpressed in RTECs by lentiviral transduction (Figure 4(a)), which significantly restored the reduction in GSH levels (Figure 4(b)) and mitochondrial membrane potential (Figure 4(e)) induced by oxalate oversaturation. Additionally, the increase in MDA levels (Figure 4(c)) and intracellular ROS levels (Figure 4(d)) were markedly alleviated. The TEM results showed that the contact length between mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum was reduced with SelK overexpression (Figure 4(f)). Overexpressing SelK can alleviate the ERS and apoptosis of RTECs induced by supersaturated oxalate

Our WB experiments revealed that SelK overexpression mitigated excessive ERS induced by oxalate oversaturation. Compared with HK-2 cells transfected with the blank control group and treated with Ox, the protein expression levels of GRP78, ATF6, and CHOP decreased with the overexpression of SelK, and the phosphorylation levels of PERK and IRE1 were also reduced (Figure 5(a)). Immunofluorescence analysis further demonstrated a significant decrease in the expression of the key ER stress-related protein GRP78 (Figure 5(b)). Additionally, the TUNEL assay (Figure 5(c)) and flow cytometry (Figure 5(d)) results indicated a reduction in apoptosis levels in RTECs. The WB results confirmed significant downregulation of certain apoptosis-related proteins and reduced phosphorylation levels of ASK1, JNK and P38 (Figure 5(e)). SeMet supplementation alleviates the oxidative stress of RTECs induced by supersaturated oxalate intervention

SeMet, a readily available and common selenium amino acid, was employed as a selenium supplement to investigate its impact on kidney stone formation acid. Results from the CCK-8 assay demonstrated significant effects when pre-treating RTECs with SeMet at concentrations ranging from 10nM to 100nM (Figure 6(a)). Furthermore, SeMet pre-treatment ameliorated the reduction of GSH caused by oxalate oversaturation (Figure 6(b)) and mitigated the increase in MDA levels (Figure 6(c)). These findings consistently indicated that the optimal concentration of SeMet for RTEC pre-treatment was 40nM. Therefore, subsequent experiments were conducted with 40nM SeMet. Moreover, SeMet treatment reduced intracellular levels of ROS (Figure 6(d)) and increased mitochondrial membrane potential (Figure 6(e)). TEM observations revealed that SeMet supplementation reduced the contact length between mitochondria and the endoplasmic reticulum (Figure 6(f)). Discussion

Kidney stones are a common condition within the urinary system [18]. The occurrence of kidney stones is directly influenced by dietary composition, metabolic byproducts, and urinary constituents of the urinary system. Furthermore, various micronutrients can influence the initiation and progression of kidney stone formation [19,20]. Magnesium and citrate, among others, have been shown to modulate the nucleation rate of calcium oxalate crystals, effectively impeding crystallization [21]. The present study first confirmed that the transcription levels of multiple selenoproteins varied during the development of calcium oxalate kidney stones. Moreover, the changes in SelK protein expression may affect the level of oxidative stress in the kidney stone cell model and regulate its ERS and apoptosis. Furthermore, the cell model and animal model of kidney stones further confirmed that supplementation of SeMet, a cheap and readily available selenium-containing compound, can effectively reduce the levels of oxidative stress, ERS, and apoptosis in renal tubular epithelial cells and reduce stone formation in the renal tubules of experimental rats.

In recent years, the pivotal role of selenium in mitigating oxidative damage and inflammation processes has gained growing recognition [22–24]. Our previous studies have substantiated the significant roles of oxidative stress, ERS, and apoptosis in the pathogenesis of kidney stone formation [2,25,26]. SelK is an endoplasmic reticulum-resident protein, regulated by dietary selenium, and is involved in endoplasmic reticulum stress and endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation processes [27]. In the process of endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation, p97 engages with selenoprotein S, facilitating their migration to the endoplasmic reticulum membrane. Subsequently, this composite complex interacts with SelK to initiate and execute endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation [28]. Furthermore, the downregulation of SelK can mediate ERS and apoptosis in skeletal muscle satellite cells by disrupting calcium homeostasis [29]. Meanwhile, overexpression of SelK in cardiomyocytes reduces cellular levels of ROS [30]. SelK plays a crucial role in the process of ERS in various diseases, which is further substantiated by our research. In this study, the role of SelK in kidney stone disease models was explored by knocking down or overexpressing SelK. The initial intervention with supersaturated oxalic acid significantly reduced the expression of SelK at the transcription level. Moreover, SelK also significantly decreased at the protein level, which aroused great interest. Following SelK knockdown, the levels of GSH and mitochondrial membrane potential decreased, whereas the levels of MDA and ROS increased in the kidney stone cell model. These changes indicated significantly increased levels of cellular oxidative stress. Furthermore, WB and immunofluorescence revealed that the key protein GRP78 of endoplasmic reticulum stress increased significantly with the knockdown of SelK in the cells of the kidney stone model group, and the three pathways of endoplasmic reticulum stress were further identified. After activation, the phosphorylation levels of IRE1 and PERK, the expression of ATF6 protein, and the expression of the endoplasmic reticulum stress-related apoptosis protein CHOP all increased. In addition, both flow cytometry and TUNEL detection demonstrated an increase in the level of apoptosis following SelK knockdown. The phosphorylation levels of the apoptosis-related proteins ASK1,JNK and P38 increased, as well as BAX and the expression of c-Caspase3. SelK overexpression confirmed the increased expression on recovery of damage and reduction of apoptosis. The crosstalk of the above phenotypes is consistent with previous studies [31], confirming the role of ERS-ROS-apoptosis in the occurrence of kidney stones, and innovatively confirming the role of SelK. Nevertheless, the limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. The specific mechanism underlying the regulatory effects of SelK on ERS, oxidative stress, and apoptosis remains incompletely understood. Future studies will further elucidate these issues. Targeting individual genes may offer precision in treatment; however, considering the heterogeneity of kidney stone disease, readily available drugs and dietary supplements were explored.

SeMet is a readily available dietary selenium supplement possessing remarkable redox activity under physiological conditions. It can directly be incorporated into various biomolecules through simple substitution of proteinogenic methionine residues [23]. In comparison to inorganic selenium compounds such as selenate and selenite, organic compounds are more readily absorbed by the human body [32]. In this study, SeMet was added in both in vivo and in vitro models of kidney stones. In the kidney stone cell model, 24 h of SeMet pretreatment showed a similar effect to overexpression of SelK. SeMet pretreatment successfully reduced the reduction in cell viability caused by Ox intervention, increased cellular GSH and mitochondrial membrane potential levels, and reduced MDA and ROS levels. Moreover, some of the above therapeutic effects showed a dose dependence of SeMet, through which the optimal therapeutic concentration (40nM) was found. These results also confirmed that pretreatment with SeMet successfully reduced the oxidative stress level of renal tubular epithelial cells. Similarly, the levels of cellular ERS and apoptosis were also significantly alleviated by SeMet pretreatment. At the protein level, the protein expression of GRP78, CHOP, BAX, and c-Caspase3 was significantly reduced, as well as the phosphorylation of ASK1, JNK, P38, IRE1, and PERK. In animal experiments, dietary supplementation of SeMet significantly reduced stone deposition in the renal tubules of experimental rats, alleviated renal function damage, and reduced ERS and apoptosis levels of renal tubular epithelial cells. Considering the alleviating effect of selenium (or SeMet) on oxidative stress in previous studies [33–35], SeMet supplementation was hypothesized to partially treat kidney stones by reducing cellular oxidative stress levels. The specific mechanism will be clarified in future research.

Mechanistically, both SelK gene regulation and SeMet supplementation modulate kidney stone formation via the IRE1-JNK pathway. Prior research has established that the JNK signaling pathway can be activated by excessive ROS induced by calcification nanoparticles (CNPs), thereby damaging renal tubular epithelial cells and promoting kidney stone formation [36]. Notably, all three pathways of ERS were activated in the kidney stone model group of this study. Specifically, increased expression of ATF6 and elevated phosphorylation levels of IRE1 and PERK were observed. Therefore, it is more meaningful to explore genes upstream of ERS activation.

In summary, this study highlights the regulatory role of selenoproteins in the occurrence and development of kidney stones, particularly in modulating ERS and apoptosis. This finding underscores the significance of selenium in regulating crucial physiological activities within the organism. Furthermore, the results suggest that additional dietary selenium supplementation may have a beneficial effect on the prevention and treatment of kidney stones, providing a novel direction for their management.

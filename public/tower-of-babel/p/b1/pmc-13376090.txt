Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a malignant tumor originating from the epithelial tissue of the nasopharynx, characterized by unique geographical distribution patterns worldwide, with high incidence rates primarily in southern China, Southeast Asia, and other regions [1]. Despite continuous advances in radiotherapy and chemotherapy techniques, the 5-year survival rate of NPC patients remains suboptimal, particularly for patients with advanced-stage disease [2]. Therefore, in-depth exploration of the molecular pathogenesis of NPC and identification of novel diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets are crucial for improving patient prognosis.

Despite the advancement of comprehensive treatment based on radiotherapy, NPC still has the problems of lack of specific early diagnostic markers and limited effective targeted therapeutic drugs, the current clinical treatment is mainly based on cytotoxic chemotherapy, and there are few targeted drugs for specific molecular targets. In addition, the drug resistance and distant metastasis of advanced NPC are the main causes of treatment failure, and the molecular mechanisms underlying these processes are still unclear. Therefore, screening and identifying specific molecular markers with diagnostic, prognostic and therapeutic value is the key to solving the clinical dilemmas of NPC and improving the survival rate of patients.

Neuroligins (NLGNs) are a family of important transmembrane cell adhesion molecules, primarily including four family members: NLGN1, NLGN2, NLGN3, and NLGN4 [3]. Neuroligin 1 (NLGN1), as an important member of this family, was initially discovered to play crucial roles in the central nervous system, primarily participating in synapse formation, neuronal connection regulation, and neural plasticity maintenance [4]. NLGN1 binds to presynaptic neurexins to form trans-synaptic protein complexes that regulate synaptic transmission efficiency and synaptic stability [5].

In recent years, with the advancement of tumor biology research, researchers have discovered that neuroligins may play important roles in tumorigenesis and progression. Increasing evidence suggests that abnormal expression of cell adhesion molecules is closely related to tumor cell invasion, metastasis, and drug resistance [6]. NLGN1, as a cell adhesion molecule, may participate in tumor processes through mechanisms such as regulating intercellular connections and affecting cell signaling pathways. However, current research on the expression patterns and functional roles of NLGN1 in malignant tumors, particularly nasopharyngeal carcinoma, remains limited.

Molecular biological studies show that the NLGN1 gene is located on the long arm of human chromosome 3 (3q26.31), encoding a 871-amino acid transmembrane glycoprotein [7]. The NLGN1 protein structure includes three main parts: extracellular domain, transmembrane domain, and intracellular domain, where the extracellular domain contains a cholinesterase-like domain responsible for ligand binding, and the intracellular domain contains a PDZ-binding motif that interacts with scaffold proteins [8]. This unique structural feature enables NLGN1 to perform dual functions of signal transduction and cell adhesion at the cell membrane surface.

In tumor research, studies have reported abnormal NLGN1 expression in certain malignant tumors. For example, in gliomas, NLGN1 upregulation is associated with tumor malignancy grade and patient prognosis [9]. In breast cancer research, NLGN1 has been found to potentially participate in tumor cell migration and invasion processes [10]. However, these studies have mainly focused on specific tumor types, and systematic research on the role of NLGN1 in nasopharyngeal carcinoma mechanisms is still lacking.

With the rapid development of high-throughput sequencing technologies and bioinformatics methods, large-scale transcriptomic data-based tumor molecular characterization analysis has become possible. The application of single-cell RNA sequencing technology has further enhanced our understanding of tumor microenvironment heterogeneity [11]. By integrating multi-omics data, comprehensive analysis of target gene expression patterns and functional networks in tumors can be achieved from multiple dimensions.

Based on this background, this study employs bioinformatics methods to integrate multiple public database resources and systematically analyze the expression characteristics, cellular distribution patterns, functional networks, and prognostic significance of the NLGN1 gene in nasopharyngeal carcinoma. Through multidimensional approaches including differential expression analysis, single-cell transcriptomic analysis, co-expression network construction, functional enrichment analysis, and survival analysis, we aim to deeply understand the role of NLGN1 in NPC pathogenesis. Additionally, quantitative real-time PCR was performed in NPC cell lines to experimentally validate the transcriptomic findings, providing further evidence for the potential of NLGN1 as a diagnostic and therapeutic target in nasopharyngeal carcinoma.

Methods

Data sources and sample collection

This study conducted systematic analysis based on the GSE266679 dataset from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. This dataset employed Illumina platform for high-throughput RNA sequencing, including nasopharyngeal carcinoma tissue samples and paired normal nasopharyngeal epithelial tissue samples.

Single-cell transcriptomic data quality control

Single-cell RNA sequencing data preprocessing utilized Cell Ranger software (version 6.0.0), including read alignment, barcode identification, and UMI counting. Quality control standards were set as follows: (1) genes detected per cell between 200 and 6000; (2) UMI counts per cell between 500 and 50,000; (3) mitochondrial gene expression proportion < 20%; (4) erythrocyte hemoglobin gene expression proportion < 2%.

Subsequent analysis was performed using the Seurat package (version 4.0.0). The SCTransform method was employed for data normalization and variance stabilization, which better handles technical noise and biological variation in single-cell data. The DoubletFinder algorithm was used to identify and remove potential doublet contamination, with expected doublet rate set to 8%.

Cell clustering analysis employed graph-based clustering methods, first constructing a k-nearest neighbor (KNN) graph, then using the Leiden algorithm for community detection. Clustering resolution parameters were optimized through grid search, selecting parameter values that yielded the best biological interpretability. Dimensionality reduction visualization employed t-SNE and UMAP algorithms with appropriate perplexity and neighbor number parameters.Multiple statistical methods were employed to assess expression differences of the NLGN1 gene between tumor and normal tissues. Primary methods included: (1) paired t-test for paired normal-tumor tissue samples; (2) Wilcoxon rank-sum test for unpaired samples and non-normally distributed data; (3) DESeq2 algorithm based on negative binomial distribution models, better handling the discreteness of RNA-seq data.

Single-cell transcriptomic analysis

Cell type identification employed a multi-level strategy: first identifying major cell populations through unsupervised clustering, then performing cell type annotation based on known marker genes. Marker gene selection referenced the Cell Marker database and relevant literature, including: (1) epithelial cells: EPCAM, KRT19, KRT8; (2) T cells: CD3D, CD3E, CD8A, CD4; (3) B cells: CD19, MS4A1, CD79A; (4) macrophages: CD68, CD163, CD14; (5) dendritic cells: CD1C, CLEC9A, FCER1A; (6) NK cells: KLRD1, NCAM1, NKG7; (7) fibroblasts: COL1A1, DCN, LUM.

The SingleR algorithm was used for automatic cell type annotation, with reference databases including Human Primary Cell Atlas and Blueprint/ENCODE datasets. Multiple algorithms including CellTypist and scCATCH were employed for cross-validation to ensure annotation accuracy.Subcell population analysis was performed through re-clustering of major cell types to identify functionally distinct cell subgroups. For example, T cells were further subdivided into CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, regulatory T cells, exhausted T cells, and other subgroups. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was used to evaluate the functional states of each subpopulation.Multiple trajectory inference algorithms were employed to reconstruct cell developmental trajectories: (1) Monocle3 algorithm based on reversed graph embedding; (2) Slingshot algorithm using minimum spanning tree methods; (3) PAGA (Partition-based Graph Abstraction) algorithm constructing abstract cell transition graphs.Pseudotime analysis followed these steps: first selecting highly variable genes as feature genes for trajectory construction; then learning principal developmental trajectories in reduced dimensional space; finally assigning pseudotime values to each cell, reflecting their position along the developmental trajectory.

HandTrace analysis was used to identify branching points in cell state transitions, with different colored trajectory lines representing different cell differentiation directions. Dynamic gene expression analysis identified genes undergoing expression changes at key trajectory nodes, which may regulate cell fate decisions.

Gene ontology and pathway enrichment analysis

The clusterProfiler R package (version 4.3.1 R Core Team, 2024) was used for Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis, including molecular function (MF), biological process (BP), and cellular component (CC) aspects. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was employed to identify metabolic pathways and signal transduction pathways involving differentially expressed genes. Statistical methods for enrichment analysis employed hypergeometric testing, with the calculation formula: P = C(M, k) × C(N-M, n-k)/C(N, n), where N is the total number of background genes, M is the number of genes in the pathway, n is the number of differential genes, and k is the number of differential genes belonging to that pathway. The Benjamini-Hochberg method was used for multiple testing correction, with FDR < 0.05 set as the significant enrichment threshold. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was used to evaluate the enrichment degree of predefined gene sets in expression ranking lists, capable of detecting subtle but coordinated expression changes. Signature gene sets from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) were used, including Hallmark gene sets, KEGG gene sets, and Reactome gene sets.

NLGN1 gene co-expression network analysis

Expression correlations between NLGN1 and other genome-wide genes were calculated using Spearman correlation coefficients to handle non-normally distributed data. |ρ|>0.3 and p < 0.05 were set as thresholds for significant correlation. To control false positives, permutation tests were performed for statistical significance validation. Co-expression network construction employed the WGCNA (Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis) algorithm. Main steps included: (1) constructing weighted adjacency matrices between genes; (2) converting adjacency matrices to topological overlap matrices (TOM); (3) performing hierarchical clustering based on TOM to identify modules; (4) calculating module eigengenes; (5) evaluating associations between modules and clinical traits. Soft threshold power parameters were selected through scale-free topology models to ensure networks satisfied scale-free properties. Module identification employed dynamic tree cutting methods, with minimum module size set to 30 genes and module merging threshold set to 0.25.

Key gene identification and function prediction

Hub genes were identified in co-expression networks, which typically have important biological functions. Hub gene definition criteria included: (1) top 10% ranking in intramodular connectivity; (2) correlation with module eigengenes > 0.8; (3) high degree centrality in PPI networks. Gene function prediction algorithms were employed to infer potential biological functions of NLGN1. Methods included: (1) guilt-by-association principle based on co-expression; (2) function propagation algorithms based on PPI networks; (3) functional annotation transfer based on sequence homology. Multiple prediction results were integrated to improve functional annotation reliability. The key genes featured in Figs. 6 and 7 were screened through a multi-step rigorous strategy, First, based on the GSE266679 dataset, differential expression analysis between NPC tumor tissues and normal nasopharyngeal epithelial tissues was performed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test and DESeq2 algorithm, with screening thresholds set as |log2FC| > 1 and adjusted P < 0.001. Then, combined with the NLGN1 co-expression network constructed by WGCNA and Spearman correlation analysis, genes with |Spearman’s ρ| > 0.5 and P < 0.001 with NLGN1 were selected to ensure a close functional correlation with NLGN1. Finally, the screened genes were further filtered by overlapping with the cancer-related gene sets in the MSigDB database to ensure their known or potential association with tumorigenesis and progression.

Cox regression models and risk assessment

Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models were employed to evaluate the impact of NLGN1 expression on patient prognosis. Model assumption testing included: (1) proportional hazards assumption through Schoenfeld residual testing; (2) linearity assumption through Martingale residual analysis; (3) outlier detection through dfbeta residual analysis.Covariates included in multivariate models comprised: age, gender, tumor stage, differentiation grade, lymph node metastasis status, and other clinicopathological characteristics. Stepwise regression methods were used for optimal model selection, with AIC (Akaike Information Criterion) as the model selection criterion. Risk assessment indicators included: (1) hazard ratio (HR) and 95% confidence intervals; (2) C-index (concordance index) for evaluating model predictive ability; (3) time-dependent ROC curve analysis for prediction accuracy; (4) decision curve analysis (DCA) for evaluating clinical application value.

Cell culture

Human nasopharyngeal carcinoma cell lines C666-1 and HONE1, as well as the immortalized normal nasopharyngeal epithelial cell line NP69, were all purchased from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). C666-1 and HONE1 cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin. The NP69 cell line was maintained in Keratinocyte-SFM (serum-free medium) (Gibco) supplemented with bovine pituitary extract (BPE) and human recombinant epidermal growth factor (EGF), according to the manufacturer’s protocol. All cultures were maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. The culture medium was refreshed every 2 to 3 days to ensure optimal growth conditions. Upon reaching approximately 80–90% confluence, cells were detached using 0.25% trypsin-EDTA solution (Gibco) and subculture at an appropriate split ratio. For all experimental procedures, cells in the logarithmic growth phase were utilized to ensure consistency and reproducibility.

Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen), and complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized using a reverse transcription kit (Takara). Quantitative real-time PCR was conducted with TB Green Premix Ex Taq (Takara) on a Bio-Rad CFX96 system. GAPDH was used as the internal reference gene, and relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔCt method. All samples were tested in triplicate. The following primer sequences were used: NLGN1 forward, 5′-AGGACTTCATGGAGACCGAG-3′; NLGN1 reverse, 5′-CTTGTCAGGGTTGTAGAGCC-3′; GAPDH forward, 5′-GAAGGTGAAGGTCGGAGT-3′; GAPDH reverse, 5′-GAAGATGGTGATGGGATTTC-3′.

Validation of NLGN1 expression in nasopharyngeal carcinoma cell lines by qRT-PCR

To experimentally validate the NLGN1 expression levels observed in the bioinformatics analyses, we performed quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) in nasopharyngeal carcinoma cell lines. As shown in Fig. 8, NLGN1 mRNA expression was markedly upregulated in NPC cell lines HONE1 and C666-1 compared to the normal nasopharyngeal epithelial cell line NP69. Specifically, HONE1 exhibited approximately a 2.2-fold increase, while C666-1 showed a nearly 3-fold elevation in NLGN1 expression relative to NP69. These differences were statistically significant (p < 0.01). These findings are consistent with our multi-omics results and confirm that NLGN1 is aberrantly overexpressed in nasopharyngeal carcinoma at the transcriptional level. This experimental evidence further supports the hypothesis that NLGN1 may play an oncogenic role in NPC and highlights its potential as a molecular target for diagnosis or therapy.

Discussion

NLGN1, as a core member of the neuroligin family, has protein structures that determine its unique biological functions. It is a transmembrane cell adhesion molecule with a unique structural domain, including cholinesterase-like extracellular domain and PDZ-binding intracellular domain, This dual function makes it a potential molecule target regulating the multiple biological processes of NPC, which is different from the single functional characteristics of IGF1R, which is only a receptor tyrosine kinase and GAPT1, which is a GTPase regulatory protein). In addition, NLGN1 is a novel candidate gene with unclarified role in NPC, while IGF1R/GAPT1 have been extensively studied in NPC. Our study results show that NLGN1 is significantly upregulated in nasopharyngeal carcinoma tissues, suggesting that NLGN1 may play important roles in tumorigenesis and progression beyond its traditional neurobiological functions. From a molecular structural perspective, the extracellular domain of NLGN1 contains a cholinesterase-like domain that can not only bind to neurexins but may also interact with other cell surface receptors or extracellular matrix components, thereby affecting tumor cell adhesion properties and signal transduction [12].

Our single-cell transcriptomic analysis revealed the expression patterns of NLGN1 in different cell types within the tumor microenvironment, particularly its high expression in epithelial cancer cells and proliferative cancer cells. This cell-specific expression pattern suggests that NLGN1 may participate in nasopharyngeal carcinoma progression by regulating tumor cell proliferation capacity and invasive characteristics. The PDZ-binding motif in the NLGN1 intracellular domain can interact with various scaffold proteins, including PSD-95, SAP102, and others, which play important roles in signal transduction, cytoskeletal organization, and cell polarity maintenance [13]. In the tumor environment, these interactions may be reprogrammed, leading to altered cell adhesion and migration capabilities.

Our functional enrichment analysis shows that NLGN1 participates in multiple biological processes related to cell adhesion and signal transduction. Abnormal expression of cell adhesion molecules is one of the important molecular events in tumorigenesis and progression. As a transmembrane adhesion molecule, NLGN1 upregulation may promote tumor progression through multiple mechanisms: first, NLGN1 may regulate the interaction between tumor cells and extracellular matrix, affecting cell adhesion strength and migration ability; second, NLGN1 may participate in intercellular junction remodeling, affecting the structural integrity of tumor tissue; finally, NLGN1 may serve as a platform molecule for signal transduction, recruiting and activating downstream signaling molecules [14].

To validate the expression patterns derived from transcriptomic data, we further performed quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) in NPC cell lines, and the results confirmed a significantly higher mRNA level of NLGN1 in C666-1 and HONE1 cells compared with the normal epithelial NP69 cell line, providing experimental support for its oncogenic potential in nasopharyngeal carcinoma.

Our co-expression network analysis found that NLGN1 shows significant correlations with multiple key genes, including some known tumor-related genes such as IGF1R and GAPT1. IGF1R (insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor) is an important receptor tyrosine kinase that regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis, and its abnormal activation is closely related to the development and progression of various malignant tumors [15]. The correlation between NLGN1 and IGF1R suggests that they may have functional synergistic relationships, jointly regulating tumor cell growth signals. GAPT1 (GTPase-activating protein 1) participates in small GTPase regulation, affecting cytoskeletal reorganization and cell migration [16]. cadherin 2 (CDH2) is a classic cell adhesion molecule, its abnormal expression is closely related to the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) of NPC; NLGN1 may form a functional complex with CDH2 to promote EMT process and tumor metastasis. Mitogen-activated protein kinase 3 (MAPK3) and/Threonine Kinase 1(AKT1) is core molecules of the MAPK and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways, which are abnormally activated in most NPC tissues, NLGN1 may act as a membrane receptor to activate the above two pathways, thereby promoting the proliferation and anti-apoptosis of NPC cells. MYC proto-oncogene is a key oncogene regulating cell cycle and metabolism; its correlation with NLGN1 suggests that NLGN1 may participate in the metabolic reprogramming of NPC cells by regulating MYC expression. These gene interaction networks provide important clues for understanding NLGN1’s role mechanisms in nasopharyngeal carcinoma.Single-cell transcriptomic analysis results show that NLGN1 is expressed not only in cancer cells but also to some extent in other cell types within the tumor microenvironment, including immune stromal cells, inflammatory stromal cells, and myeloid cells. This widespread expression pattern suggests that NLGN1 may participate in regulating complex interactions within the tumor microenvironment. The tumor microenvironment is a highly dynamic ecosystem containing multiple cell types, such as tumor-associated fibroblasts, immune cells, vascular endothelial cells, and others [17].

NLGN1 expression in immune cells is particularly noteworthy. Our analysis shows that NLGN1 has certain expression in myeloid cells, which include important immune effector cells such as macrophages and dendritic cells. Macrophages have dual roles in the tumor microenvironment, capable of both anti-tumor immune functions and being “educated” by tumors toward pro-tumor phenotypes [18]. NLGN1 may affect tumor immune response intensity and direction by regulating immune cell functional states. Additionally, NLGN1 expression in inflammatory stromal cells suggests its potential participation in regulating tumor-associated inflammatory responses, while chronic inflammation is an important promoting factor for tumorigenesis and progression [19].Our pathway enrichment analysis shows that NLGN1 is associated with metabolism-related pathways, a finding with important biological significance. Tumor metabolic reprogramming is one of the important characteristics of tumor cells, as they alter metabolic patterns to adapt to the demands of rapid proliferation and invasion-metastasis [20]. NLGN1 may participate in metabolic regulation through multiple mechanisms: first, as a membrane receptor, NLGN1 may participate in receiving and transmitting metabolism-related signals; second, NLGN1 may affect the expression or activity of metabolic enzymes; finally, NLGN1 may regulate mitochondrial function and energy metabolism [21].

We observed NLGN1 expression in metabolic stromal cells, which are responsible for providing metabolic support in the tumor microenvironment. Complex metabolic communication exists between tumor cells and stromal cells, where stromal cells can provide nutrients to tumor cells while tumor cells can also affect the metabolic state of stromal cells [22]. NLGN1 may participate in regulating this metabolic communication by affecting intercellular material exchange and signal transmission, supporting tumor growth and metastasis.Our multi-cancer survival analysis shows that NLGN1 expression levels are associated with patient prognosis and may play different roles in different cancer types. This diversity in prognostic correlations may reflect NLGN1’s functional diversity in different tumor types. In nasopharyngeal carcinoma, high NLGN1 expression is associated with poorer prognosis trends, suggesting it may primarily play pro-cancer roles. However, in some other cancer types, NLGN1 may act as a protective factor, and this functional duality is not uncommon in tumor biology [23].

From a clinical application perspective, NLGN1 has potential as a biomarker. First, NLGN1 expression levels can be used for tumor diagnosis and classification, particularly in nervous system-related tumors; second, NLGN1 can serve as a prognostic assessment indicator to help clinicians develop personalized treatment plans; finally, NLGN1 may become a new therapeutic target by regulating its expression or function to intervene in tumor progression [24]. Our experimental validation further enhances the translational value of NLGN1, as its consistent overexpression in NPC cell lines indicates potential for clinical application as a diagnostic and therapeutic biomarker.

Comparison of NLGN1 with other neuroligin family members

The neuroligin family includes four main members (NLGN1-4), which have both similarities and specificities in structure and function. NLGN1 is primarily expressed in excitatory synapses, while NLGN2 mainly functions in inhibitory synapses [25]. In tumor research, different family members may have different action patterns. Compared to other family members, NLGN1’s abnormal expression is more common in various tumors, which may be related to its broader tissue distribution and more complex regulatory mechanisms [26].

Our study focused on NLGN1, but future research should consider the synergistic effects of the entire neuroligin family in tumors. Different family members may have functional complementary or antagonistic relationships, and this complex interaction network may be an important regulatory mechanism for tumorigenesis and progression. Understanding these interactions is important for developing neuroligin-based tumor therapeutic strategies [27].

Limitations

Although this study provides important evidence for NLGN1’s role in nasopharyngeal carcinoma through multi-omics data analysis, certain limitations remain. Future studies incorporating independent cohorts, such as GSE150430, GSE12452, TCGA-HNSC NPC subset, or local RNA-seq cohorts, will be necessary to further validate and extend our findings. Although the in vitro cell experiment verified the abnormal expression of NLGN1, future in vitro studies employing genetic manipulation techniques, such as siRNA knockdown or CRISPR/Cas9 knockout, are essential to confirm the specific impact of NLGN1 on NPC cell proliferation and migration. Furthermore, the study has not yet performed immunohistochemical validation on clinical NPC tissue and lacks the correlation analysis between NLGN1 expression and clinical pathological parameters, such as tumor stage, lymph node metastasis, distant metastasis. The correlation between NLGN1 expression and patient treatment response is not analyzed, the study only explored the prognostic significance of NLGN1 but did not analyze its correlation with NPC patients’ sensitivity to radiotherapy, chemotherapy and targeted therapy, and the role of NLGN1 in NPC drug resistance is still unclear. The specific molecular mechanism of NLGN1 in NPC is not fully elucidated: the study revealed that NLGN1 is involved in cell adhesion and metabolic signaling pathways, but the direct downstream target molecules of NLGN1, the specific binding proteins in NPC cells, and the regulatory mechanism of its abnormal expression have not been further explored [28]. The in vivo functional validation is not fully investigated, the study only performed in vitro cell function experiments and did not verify the oncogenic role of NLGN1 in NPC through animal models [29].

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study systematically elucidates NLGN1’s expression patterns and potential functions in nasopharyngeal carcinoma through multidimensional bioinformatics analysis, providing new perspectives for understanding NPC molecular mechanisms. The integration of in vitro validation using qRT-PCR further strengthens our findings and supports the bioinformatics-based conclusions regarding NLGN1’s upregulation and potential oncogenic roles in NPC. These results lay a more solid foundation for future research exploring NLGN1 as a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target in nasopharyngeal carcinoma.

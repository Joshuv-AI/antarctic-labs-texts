Enhanced Whitening Effect of the Formulations via Inhibition of Tyrosinase Activity by Combination of 4‐n‐Butylresorcinol, Licochalcone A and Glabridin at an Optimal Ratio
Authors: Tao Zhang, Yiyu Wang, Ying Xu, Yue Liu, Baoxia Zhuang, Xinyuan Zhang, Chungan Liao, Wei Liu, Yanyun Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundCompanies launch tons of skin‐whitening products to cater to the consumer market. Though skin‐whitening cosmeceuticals are continuously developing, most cosmeceuticals are used alone, there is very little academic research conducting investigations into the effect of skin‐whitening cosmeceutical combinations.ObjectiveIn this study, we aimed to screen out skin whitening agents and determine their optimal ratio to develop a formula with this combination. Further, to verify the skin whitening efficacy of blue light‐induced in in vivo models with or without UV and clinical tests were performed.Materials and MethodsMolecular docking simulations were used to screen out the best combination of skin whitening agents when combined in different ratios. In vitro tyrosinase activity and melanin content experiments helped to verify the best ratio combination. Clinical trials were also conducted to confirm the skin whitening efficacy on human skin.Results4‐n‐butylresorcinol, licochalcone A, and glabridin exhibited stronger binding affinities to tyrosinase, and the best combination molar ratio is 5:1:1, which could inhibit both tyrosinase activity and melanin content in vitro. Further, the formulation containing this combination showed good skin whitening efficacy and could even prevent UVA and blue light‐induced pigmentation.ConclusionThe 5:1:1 molar ratio of 4‐n‐butylresorcinol, licochalcone A, and glabridin was the best ratio that could inhibit both tyrosinase activity and melanin content. The formulation containing this ratio combination can enhance the skin whitening efficacy and also can prevent the UV and blue light induced skin pigmentation.

Introduction

Skin whitening is a hot topic in the beauty industry, especially in Asia. Pursuing whitening and preventing sunlight‐induced skin pigmentation has become one of the most attractive topics [1, 2]. Skin color is affected by both intrinsic factors, for example, genetic background, which is not easy to change, and extrinsic factors, for example, sunlight exposure, which can be treated by skin whitening combinations and products. Skin color is determined by the epidermal melanin content and melanosome dispersion directly [3, 4, 5].

To solve the pigmentation issue, a variety of compounds with depigmentation effects including synthetic compounds and herbal extracts, have been developed with the aim to inhibit melanin synthesis or secretion by the melanocyte [6, 7, 8]. The top ten cosmeceuticals for facial hyperpigmentation are thiamidol, kojic acid, Vitamin C, arbutin, vitamin A and its derivatives (retinoids), nicotinamide, ferulic acid, resorcinol, licorice root extract and soy [9]. Besides melanogenesis, the secretion of melanin, the melanin transportation from melanocytes to keratinocytes, the degradation of melanin as well as inflammation can also affect skin color, so it has limitation by using only one ingredient [7, 10, 11]. But most cosmeceuticals are used alone; there is very little academic research on the skin‐whitening effect using combinations [12]. In 1975, Dr. Kligman revolutionized dermatological treatment for hyperpigmentation disorders by introducing a groundbreaking combination therapy known as Kligman's formula [13]. This innovative formulation, consisting of 0.1% tretinoin, 5.0% hydroquinone, and 0.1% dexamethasone, demonstrated remarkable efficacy in treating various pigmentation disorders. Its exceptional therapeutic performance in addressing melasma, ephelides, and postinflammatory hyperpigmentation led to its widespread recognition as the gold standard for depigmentation therapy among dermatologists worldwide. Moreover, Kligman's tri‐therapy highlighted the importance of synergistic action of the active ingredients in the combination formula since the therapeutical efficacy would not be attainable if any of the three main active ingredients were omitted, setting a benchmark for depigmentation treatment.

All agents studied in this study belong to the top ten cosmeceuticals for the elimination of facial hyperpigmentation. 4‐n‐butylresorcinol is a derivative of resorcinol which was introduced in 1995 and is considered the most potent inhibitor of human tyrosinase and widely used in cosmetics for whitening skin [13, 14, 15]. Licochalcone A and glabridin are licorice root extract, which are bioactive flavonoid compounds extracted from the roots of Glycyrrhiza glabra; both of them were proven to have skin‐whitening effects [16]. Licochalcone A activates mTOR‐regulated autophagy (an intracellular degradation system) in melanoma cells via miR‐142‐3p/Rheb/mTOR pathway [17]. Glabridin binds to tyrosinase generating a stable glabridin‐tyrosinase complex or inhibits MC1R pathway [18, 19]. There are lots of research on the skin whitening mechanism of these three agents have been conducted, but it is still unknown whether there is synergy when combining these agents together to develop a new formula.

This study uses molecular docking simulations to screen out the most three powerful skin‐whitening actives 4‐n‐butylresorcinol, licochalcone A, and glabridin, and then according to our experience in formulation preparation, we combined 4‐n‐butylresorcinol with different proportions with licochalcone A and glabridin. Whether licochalcone A and glabridin could promote the inhibitory ability of 4‐n‐butylresorcinol on tyrosinase activity and melanin production were evaluated and their optimal proportion was confirmed in vitro experiments. A skin‐whitening formula containing this combination was made and its skin‐whitening and soothing effects on histology, and zebra fish were studied. What's more, clinical studies were carried out to prove the skin whitening efficacy and prevent the skin pigmentation induced by UV and blue light irradiation. This study screens out a powerful skin‐whitening at an optimal combination ratio of 4‐n‐butylresorcinol, licochalcone A, and glabridin, and provides a new strategy for cosmetics concerning skin whitening agent's usage.

Materials and Methods

Cells and Agents

MNT‐1 cell line was purchased from ATCC (CTCC‐006‐0301). Cells were cultured with DMEM medium (Gibco,11995073), supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum (Gibco,10099158) and 1% penicillin‐streptomycin (Sangon, E607011), in an incubator at 37°C with 5% CO2 in air atmosphere. 4‐n‐butylresorcinol was purchased from Clarinet, licochalcone A and glabridin were purchased from QHL PHARMACEUTICALS (Blicsweet licorice), all the agents are dissolved in DMSO (Sangon, A610163‐0250) to make stock solution.

Methods

Drug Molecules and Protein Structure Lock

The structures of MC1R (Melanocortin 1 Receptor, 7F4D), PI3K (Phosphoinositide 3‐kinases, 1E8X), and TYR (Tyrosinase, 2Y9X) were obtained from the PDB database. Based on Uniprot and GeneCard, specific active regions of each protein were selected: MC1R residues 12–308, a seven‐transmembrane protein activated by α‐MSH in the presence of Ca2+ with active regions at 1–51, 89–123, and 175–1951,4; PI3K residues 143–1093, including the PI3K/PI4K catalytic domain at 797–1080; and TYR residues 1–392.

The 3D structures of small molecules glabridin, licochalcone A, and 4‐butylresorcinol were sourced from PubChem. These molecules are hypothesized to permeate cell membranes.

Simulation of Drugs Small Molecules and Protein

Molecular docking simulations were performed using Autodock5,6 (v 1.3.6). Target proteins were treated as rigid, while small molecules were allowed flexible conformations. Preprocessing with Autodock Tools included hydrogen addition and Gasteiger charge calculations. Grid parameters7 were set based on the 3D coordinates of the active centers, with a grid box size of 126 Å × 126 Å × 126 Å and a grid spacing of 1 Å. Center coordinates were MC1R (x = 71.056 Å, y = 112.752 Å, z = 104.641 Å), Tyrosinase (x = −7.455 Å, y = −23.654 Å, z = −32.535 Å), and PI3K (x = 33.684 Å, y = 47.734 Å, z = 26.803 Å). Docking simulations used the Genetic Algorithm (GA) with parameters set to 10 GA runs, a maximum of 27 000 generations per run, and a maximum of 2 500 000 evaluations. Conformations were ranked by binding affinity, retaining those with energies below −2 kcal/mol. Results were visualized using PyMol (v 2.5.7), including analyses of hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions.

Tyrosinase Activity Assay

MNT‐1 cells were seeded and cultured in a 10 cm dish. After incubation with different treatments for 48 hrs, MNT‐1 cells were lysed with 200 µL 20 mM Tris‐HCl buffer(0.1% Triton‐X100, pH 7.0) for 30 min on ice. Add an equal volume of 1 mg/mL L‐DOPA buffer (MCE, HY‐N0304), after incubating at 37°C for 4 hrs, and detect the tyrosinase activity by spectrophotometer (BioTek, CyTation3) at 490 nm based on a standard curve. The following equation calculated tyrosinase activity: Tyrosinase activity = OD490nm /ODBCA.

Melanin Measurement

MNT‐1 cells were seeded and cultured in a 10‐cm dish. After incubation with different treatments for 48 h, MNT‐1 cells were lysed with 200 µL 20 mM Tris‐HCl buffer with 0.1% Triton‐X100, pH 7.0(Sangon, B548124)for 30 min on ice. RIPA buffer(Servicebio, G2002‐30ML)was also used to make lysis completely. The supernatant was collected to detect the protein concentration by BCA Protein Assay (Thermo Scientific, 23250). The pellet was resuspended in 1 M NaOH at 50°C for one hour, then calculated melanin levels by a spectrophotometer (BioTek, CyTation3) at 490 nm based on a standard curve. Relative melanin was calculated by the following equation: Melanin content (%) = OD490nm /ODBCA×100.

Melanin Inhibition Test in Zebra Fish Embryos

Expose 20 eight‐hour‐old zebra fish embryos to a 5 g/L solution of skin care formulation with whitening components (5:1:1 molar ratio of 4‐n‐butylresorcinol, licochalcone A, and glabridin) sample, while also setting up a blank control group, a 100% melanin inhibition model group (30 mg/L phenylthiourea), and a positive control group (2.5 g/L kojic acid). After 48 h of exposure, photograph the fish embryos under a microscope to measure the melanin signal intensity and perform statistical analysis.

3D Melanin Skin Model (MelaKutis) Test

Since the day of the MelaKutis model received (Day 0), the negative control, positive control, and skin care formulation with whitening components (5:1:1 molar ratio of 4‐n‐butylresorcinol, licochalcone A, and glabridin) were subjected to UVB irradiation(50 mJ/cm2, 4.0628 mW/cm2, 12 s)every day. The blank control group was not subjected to UVB irradiation, and only the culture medium was changed every day. The positive control group(kojic acid, 500 µg/mL)and the sample group were dosed twice on Day 3 and Day 5, respectively, with surface administration at a dose of 2 mg/cm2. Formulation samples were received on Day 7 and tested for melanin distribution.

UVA and Blue Light‐Induced Skin Pigmentation Clinical Test

The protocol of the panel test is based on the literature of R Zhang et al. briefly speaking, a total of 5 subjects (5 females) with Fitzpatrick skin type III from Shanghai were included in this study. Before every irradiation, apply the test sample to the pre‐marked test area at a dosage of (2.0000 ± 0.0500) mg/cm2. Then use 0.75 times MPPDUVA and MPPDBL as irradiation light sources for 4 consecutive days, irradiating UVA first and then blue light every day by using a Daylight simulator (Solar Light, USA) and Blue light irradiator (SELLAMED VIS 400, Germany). The skin color of the subjects in each blackened test area was visually and instrumentally (DSM ǁ Colorimeter) evaluated on D0‐D4.

Sensitive Skin Patch Test

A total of 33 sensitive skin subjects (28 females and 5 males) were included in this study. Select a qualified patch test device with an area not exceeding 50mm2 and a depth of about 1 mm. Add the skin whitening formulation to the patch test device, with the amount being approximately 0.020 g to 0.025 g. Apply the patch test device with the test formulation and the negative control to the ventral side of the subject's forearm, and remove it after 24 h. Observe and record the skin reactions at 30 min, 24 h, and 48 h after the removal of the patch.

Skin Whitening Clinical Test

A total of 31 subjects (28 females and 3 males) from Shanghai were included in the study. A skin care formulation with whitening components was applied by the panelists for 56 successive days twice a day in the morning and evening. On day 0, day 14, day 28, and day 56, facial pictures were collected by VISA; the color of the face value optical density (OD), pigment area ratio was measured by Image Pro Plus (IPP, MediaCybernetics, United States), L, a, b, and ITA° were measured by Colorimeter CL400 (Courage + Khazaka electronic GmbH, Germany). In addition, melanin and hemoglobin content were measured by Mexameter MX18 (Courage + Khazaka electronic GmbH, Germany). Subject self‐assessments were also conducted.

Statistical Analysis

All experimental data were statistically analyzed by GraphPad Prism 9 software. An unpaired student's t‐test was used to evaluate whether there was a significant difference between the two groups. P‐values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Ethics and Declaration

Shanghai Skinshield Clinical Testing and Technological Research Co., Ltd. carried out the clinical study. Before the start of the study, all subjects were provided with a written informed consent form. The informed consent form was signed by the subjects after adequate communication, including explicit written consent for the publication of identifiable data and images, where applicable. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Skinshield (No. E20221116C), and the whole study was strictly supervised and guided by the Ethics Committee. The study strictly guaranteed the security of the subjects’ personal privacy information.

Results

Molecular Docking Simulations of Glabridin, Licochalcone A, and 4‐Butylresorcinol Bind to TYR

To screen out highly effective whitening combinations, we detected the tyrosinase inhibitory activities of various agents by molecular docking simulations [20]. The findings showed that glabridin, licochalcone A, and 4‐butylresorcinol could bind to tyrosinase (Figures 1a–c), targeting 24 residues in total, with some overlap in residues like N81 and H85 across the three compounds (Figure 1d). Considering the melanin synthesis pathway involves various gene activation processes so that inhibiting gene activity may effectively reduce downstream product synthesis and degradation, thus, besides tyrosinase, we also simulate these three agents binding to MC1R and PI3K (sFigure 1 and 2). Overall, glabridin, licochalcone A, and 4‐butylresorcinol exhibited stronger binding affinities to not only tyrosinase but also MC1R and PI3K, suggesting complementary inhibitory mechanisms in the melanin synthesis pathway.

Tyrosinase and Melanin Inhibition Test

To verify the simulation results, we evaluated the inhibitory effects of glabridin, licochalcone A, and 4‐butylresorcinol on tyrosinase activity and melanin synthesis at representative concentrations. All experimental groups showed lower tyrosinase activity than the control group, and the combination groups showed better results compared with the corresponding 4‐n‐butylresorcinol individual‐treated group (Figure 2a).

It is generally accepted that tyrosinase is the key rate‐limiting enzyme that could further inhibit melanin synthesis [21, 22], so it is necessary to detect the melanin level of melanoma cells. All agents showed inhibition capacity in line with previous studies [23, 24, 25]. Compared with the control group, licochalcone A and glabridin could inhibit melanogenesis, and the inhibition efficacy of 4‐n‐butylresorcinol depends on its concentration (Figure 2b). In the 4‐n‐butylresorcinol group, 50 µM 4‐n‐butylresorcinol showed the best result, but in the combination groups, combination of three agents had the highest inhibition rate, which means the addition of licochalcone A and glabridin could enhance the effect significantly, and we figured out 5:1:1 is the best combination ratio, which will be named as 577 PRO.

We also compared the 577 PRO with the popular skin whitening ingredients on the market, 4‐(1‐phenylethyl)1,3‐benzenediol (short as 377). The results showed that the combination had a significantly better inhibitory effect on both tyrosinase activity and melanin synthesis (Figures 2c and 2d), which means 577 PRO could be applied to formulations for further evaluation of skin whitening efficacy as well as the possibility of reducing irritations.

Melanin Inhibition Test in Zebra Fish Embryos

Further, we formulated a skin whitening formula with 577 PRO, and the zebra fish model was applied to further evaluate the melanin inhibition effect. The blank control group is standardized with a 0% of melanin inhibition rate (Figure 3a). When treated with 2.5 g/L kojic acid as a positive control, melanin production has a significant inhibition rate of 30% (Figure 3b). When treated with 5 g/L skin whitening formula with 577 PRO (in which 577 PRO is only below 1%), the melanin production inhibition effect is also quite significant with an inhibition rate of 47%, which proved that the formula with 577 PRO can effectively inhibit the melanogenesis in the zebra fish model (Figure 3c).

3D Melanin Skin Model (MelaKutis) Test Under UVB Irradiation

Before using in human skin efficacy test, we detected UVB‐induced melanogenesis with or without skin whitening formula with 577 PRO, and further evaluated its inhibition of melanogenesis effect at the histological level in the 3D melanin skin model under UVB irradiation. When irradiated with 50 mJ/cm2 UVB, the 3D skin model shows significant production of melanin granules (the negative control group). When treated with kojic acid (the positive control group), the production of melanin granules was significantly reduced. When treated with the formula with 577 PRO, the production of melanin was also significantly reduced (Figure 4a) with the inhibition rate at 58.18% (Figure 4b) indicating that the formula with 577 PRO can effectively inhibit melanogenesis.

UVA and Blue Light‐Induced Skin Pigmentation Clinical Test

As previous study shown that UVA together with blue light would introduce a more persistent pigmentation [26], in this session we would like to verify the skin whitening capabilities in Chinese human subjects for preventing the pigmentation induced by UVA together with the blue light by using the formula with 577 PRO comparing with the blank formula without the combinations. Five subjects with the Fitzpatrick type III skin were randomly selected and products were applied each day before exposure to UVA and blue light for successive four days. After four days of continuous use of the formula with 577 PRO, there is an improvement in melanin index and ITA° for 8.12% and 9.84%, respectively, which is much better than the control formula which is 5.92% and 3.70% (Figures 5a and 5b). Based on the dermatologist evaluation, the formula with 577 PRO also showed a significant improvement in the visual score up to 40.20%, nearly achieving a double effect compared with the blank control formula (22.18%, p < 0.01, Figure 5d). This set of measurements proved that the formula with 577 PRO dramatically prevents the facial darkness induced by UVA and blue light.

Analysis of the changes in the improvement rate of blank formula and the formula with 577 PRO in (a) Melanin Index (M Values) in the subjects before and after product usage (b) ITA° in the subjects before and after product usage; (c) Erythema Index (E Value) in the subjects before and after product usage; (d) Visual score in the subjects before and after product usage. n = 5. **p < 0.01.

In addition, the skin erythema index E value for the formula with 577 PRO showed an improvement of 13.99%, which is better than the control formula (11.21%, Figure 5c). The formula with 577 PRO may support the prevention of UVA and blue light‐induced erythema that is linked with the prevention of skin inflammation.

Skin Whitening Clinical Test and Sensitive Skin Patch Test

We first carried out a sensitive skin patch test to verify the safety of clinical research even sensitive skin subjects showed no side effects (sTable 1). To further validate the skin whitening performance, we applied the formula with 577 PRO to 31 healthy Chinese male and female subjects for 56 consecutive days, and no adverse events were found. Based on the test results, the facial color indicators OD and pigment area ratio were significantly improved starting from the product application on day 14, and reached the maximum at day 56 to decrease at 1.13% and 40.59%, respectively (Figures 6a and 6b). L values were significantly improved starting from the product usage for 14 days, and reaching the maximum enhancement at 4.55% for 56 days (Figure 6c). Meanwhile, the ITA value indicated that skin lightening also significantly be improved starting from D14 and reached the maximum at D56 to 28.56% (Figure 6d). In addition, the melanin and hemoglobin content also decreased starting from D14 and reached the maximum at D56 to 13.38% and 12.42%, respectively (Figures 6e and 6f). Figures 6g and 6h showed the representative results of the subjects who participated in the whitening panel test.

From D14, D28, and D56, all 31 panelists had significant improvement in L value, ITA° value, and decreased melanin content, which indicated that all the panelists had improvement of skin lightness, brightness as well as reduction of melanin content. In addition, the OD and pigment area ratio of the majority of the panelists was decreased indicating the formula with 577 PRO has good skin whitening efficacy and even reduces pigmentation and melasma. What's more, the reduction of hemoglobin value might indicate the formula with 577 PRO does not cause any irritation to the panelists, instead it might have some soothing effect to improve the skin condition.

Based on the panelists' assessment of the skin whitening products, 100% of them agreed that the product can support skin brightness and yellowish improvement, and the skin condition is significantly improved (data not shown).

Discussion

In this paper, we combine in silico, in vitro, in vivo, and clinical studies to establish a complete research strategy. We use molecular docking simulations to screen out 4‐n‐butylresorcinol, licochalcone A, and glabridin [20]. We then combined these three candidate actives and applied them to the skin‐whitening formulation as an enhancement of the skin‐whitening effect and skin tolerance. In addition, the combination of 50 µM 4‐n‐butylresorcinol (initial concentration: 100 mM) with 10 µM licochalcone A (initial concentration: 20 mM) and 10 µM glabridin (initial concentration: 30 mM) was the most effective solution in terms of tyrosinase activity and melanin production inhibition, which is consistent with previous studies that 4‐n‐butylresorcinol inhibits melanin synthesis in a dose‐dependent manner [15]. It should be noted that UVB, UVA and blue light irradiation induce melanogenesis in different pathways [27, 28, 29, 30], but our combination can prevent the skin pigmentation from all aspects of sun light, it is worthy of being developed and applied [31]. For the bioassays with concentration gradients, the Pearson correlation analyses demonstrated strong negative correlations between 4‐n‐butylresorcinol concentrations and both tyrosinase activity (r = −0.97) and melanin content (r = −0.98). Similarly, the combination formulations showed high negative correlations with tyrosinase activity (r = −0.98) and melanin content (r = −0.87). These results indicate that increasing concentrations of 4‐n‐butylresorcinol, either alone or in combination, were associated with reduced tyrosinase activity and melanin production.

Based on our knowledge and literature review, previous studies have individually demonstrated the skin‐lightening efficacy of the three active ingredients investigated in this work. However, to the best of our knowledge, this study represents the first systematic investigation of a ternary combination of these actives, while also establishing their optimal ratio for synergistic depigmenting effects. Our findings thus provide novel insights into the development of multicomponent cosmetic formulations for hyperpigmentation treatment. This study not only screens out a powerful skin‐whitening combination ratio but also provides a new strategy for skin whitening agents’ usage of cosmetics when developing whitening products, we can combine different whitening agents, especially those that target different pathways or proteins, which can effectively improve the skin‐whitening effect and has strong application value.

Limitations

Despite these new findings, we still need to do further research to refine the results. First, work is ongoing on how to improve the skin‐whitening effect of the 5:1:1 molar ratio combination by using new technology or ingredients. Another question is what the mechanism behind our findings is. The three ingredients we used regulate melanin production via different mechanisms. 4‐n‐butylresorcinol inhibits competitive inhibition of tyrosinase by enhancing tyrosinase degradation and by binding tyrosinase directly to inhibit melanogenesis [26, 32], which was confirmed by our study. Besides this, in our molecular docking simulations, we do see these three actives contact with more targets besides TYR, which is similar to the previous study [18, 19, 33]. Therefore, our findings showed there may be a combined effect of different pathways, but the exact mechanism of the synergies among these three active ingredients remains to be further explored.

Additionally, only Chinese females were included in our study. While the results suggest promising skin‐whitening efficacy in this population, further investigation is needed to determine the generalizability of these findings to more diverse populations.

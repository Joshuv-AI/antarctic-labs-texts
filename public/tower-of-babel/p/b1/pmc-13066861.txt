Clinical features and imaging findings of early lacrimal sac neoplasms: a retrospective study
Authors: Jian Yu, Yinwen Bi, Rongrong Cai, Lu Gan, Jiang Qian, Hui Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEarly lacrimal sac neoplasms are often misdiagnosed as lacrimal duct obstruction, delaying appropriate treatment, accelerating tumor progression, and worsening prognosis. By systematically reviewing and summarizing these characteristics, we aimed to assist ophthalmologists and otolaryngologists in accurately diagnosing and managing early-stage lacrimal sac neoplasms.MethodsIn this retrospective study, patients presenting with epiphora were diagnosed with either lacrimal sac neoplasms or mucoceles. Diagnostic assessments included computed tomography (CT), enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and pathological analysis.ResultsThe study comprised 9 patients (2 male, 7 female) with lacrimal sac neoplasms; 44% had malignancies, and the remaining 56% exhibited benign conditions. There was no statistically significant difference in mean tumor volume or margin characteristics between patients with neoplasms and those with mucoceles. There was no statistically significant difference in the mean width of the nasolacrimal canal between patients with neoplasms (6.49±3.68 mm) and mucoceles (5.50±1.88 mm) (P=0.44). However, the depth of the nasolacrimal canal was significantly greater in the neoplasm group (5.08±3.21 mm) compared to the mucocele group (2.85±0.96 mm) (P=0.042). Notably, lacrimal sac mass extended above the medial canthal tendon in 8 neoplasm patients, unlike any in the mucocele group, demonstrating distinct pathological presentations. Additionally, the diseased eyes in the neoplasm group showed significantly greater nasolacrimal canal width and depth compared to the contralateral eyes (all P<0.05), whereas the mucocele group showed only slightly increased nasolacrimal canal width compared with the contralateral eyes.ConclusionsThe findings suggest that patients with a lacrimal sac mass extending above the medial canthal tendon and a nasolacrimal canal deeper than the contralateral side should undergo contrast-enhanced MRI for accurate diagnosis and treatment planning.

Introduction

Primary lacrimal sac neoplasms, although rare, pose significant diagnostic and therapeutic challenges. These tumors are predominantly identified due to their presentation with symptoms such as epiphora, which occurs in approximately 84% of cases (1). The symptom of persistent tearing often leads clinicians to initially consider more common conditions, such as nasolacrimal duct (NLD) obstruction. Consequently, patients may undergo procedures such as stenting or dacryocystorhinostomy (DCR), intended to alleviate the obstruction. However, these interventions can inadvertently accelerate the growth of neoplasms if the true underlying condition is not recognized early (2).

Moreover, it is estimated that up to 36% of lesions within the lacrimal drainage system are discovered incidentally during DCR procedures (3). Such incidental findings underscore the critical need for precise diagnostic approaches in the early stages of patient evaluation. Misdiagnosis not only delays correct treatment but may also worsen the patient’s prognosis by facilitating tumor progression.

Recognizing these challenges, we have undertaken a detailed study of the clinical features and imaging findings of lacrimal sac neoplasms at the Eye & ENT Hospital, Fudan University. Our analysis specifically focuses on cases presenting with epiphora, aiming to enhance the differential diagnostic capabilities for this rare entity. By systematically reviewing and summarizing these characteristics, we hope to contribute to the body of knowledge, aiding ophthalmologists and otolaryngologists in accurately diagnosing and managing lacrimal sac neoplasms, thereby improving treatment outcomes and patient quality of life. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2025-1719/rc).

Methods

This retrospective clinical study was conducted at the Eye & ENT Hospital, Fudan University. It included patients presenting with epiphora who were pathologically diagnosed with lacrimal sac neoplasms. Patients with lacrimal mucoceles were included as the control group. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Eye & ENT Hospital of Fudan University (No. 2019052-1). The requirement for informed consent for this retrospective analysis was waived. Patients included in the study met the following criteria: presenting with epiphora and being pathologically diagnosed with lacrimal sac neoplasms or lacrimal mucoceles. All patients including the control group underwent computed tomography (CT) and patients with lacrimal sac neoplasms underwent enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Patients exhibiting globe displacement or bone destruction on CT were excluded. Clinical data collected included the sex and age of the patients, presenting symptoms and their duration, CT and MRI imaging details, and the pathologic diagnosis. Clinical data collected included demographic information (sex and age), presenting symptoms and their duration, detailed CT and MRI imaging results, and the pathologic diagnoses.

Patients underwent CT scans using a 64-slice spiral CT scanner (Somatom Definition Flash, Siemens, Erlangen, Germany). Scanning parameters were as follows: tube voltage 120 kV, tube current 100–150 mAs, slice thickness 0.75 mm, slice interval 0.75 mm, field of view (FOV) 200×200 mm, matrix 512×512. Both axial and coronal reconstructions were performed, with bone window [window width 2,000 Hounsfield units (HU), window level 300 HU] and soft tissue window (window width 400 HU, window level 40 HU) images obtained. Patients underwent enhanced MRI using a 3.0T MRI scanner (GE Discovery MR750, GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA). Scanning sequences included: (I) axial T1-weighted imaging (T1WI): repetition time (TR) 500 ms, echo time (TE) 10 ms, slice thickness 3 mm, slice interval 0.3 mm, FOV 200×200 mm, matrix 256×256; (II) axial T2-weighted imaging (T2WI) with fat suppression: TR 3,000 ms, TE 80 ms, slice thickness 3 mm, slice interval 0.3 mm, FOV 200×200 mm, matrix 256×256; (III) coronal T2WI with fat suppression: TR 3,500 ms, TE 85 ms, slice thickness 3 mm, slice interval 0.3 mm, FOV 200×200 mm, matrix 256×256; (IV) enhanced axial, coronal, and sagittal T1WI with fat suppression: after intravenous injection of gadopentetate dimeglumine (Gd-DTPA, Bayer Schering Pharma, Leverkusen, Germany) at a dosage of 0.1 mmol/kg body weight, scanning was performed with the same parameters as T1WI. The scan delay time was 20–30 seconds for arterial phase and 60–90 seconds for venous phase.

CT images were assessed using the bone window and soft-tissue window setting of CareStream Vue Motion (Carestream Health, Inc., Rochester, NY, USA). Measurements were performed using the viewer’s caliper tool on the computer screen, which allows for a measurement accuracy of up to 0.01 mm, with automatic adjustment for magnification. Coronal images were aligned along the center of the NLD, measuring the transverse inner diameters at the level of the inferior orbital margin and the depths of the nasolacrimal canal on the same plane (Figure 1). Tumor size was calculated as three-dimensional (3D) volume using the caliper tool on CT images. Margin characteristics were defined as regular (smooth, well-circumscribed) or irregular (rough, ill-defined) based on CT or MRI morphological observations.

Nasolacrimal canal measurements. The transverse inner diameters of the opening of nasolacrimal canal were measured at the level of the inferior orbital margin (dashed lines in A and B). The depth of nasolacrimal canal was measured as the black line perpendicular to the dashed line.

Statistical analysis

A chi-squared test was utilized to compare the gender distribution, margin characteristics, and the presence of mass above the medial canthal tendon between lacrimal sac neoplasms and mucoceles. Independent samples t-tests were conducted to compare the age, width, and depth of the nasolacrimal canal between these two groups. Additionally, the Mann-Whitney U test was applied for comparing the duration of epiphora between lacrimal sac neoplasms and mucoceles when initially expressed as medians and interquartile ranges. Furthermore, paired t-tests were performed to compare the width and depth of the nasolacrimal canal between the affected and contralateral sides within each disease group. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using the software SPSS 22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

A total of 9 patients with lacrimal sac neoplasms, comprising 2 males and 7 females with a mean age of 44.78±8.87 years, were included in the study. Some 44% (4/9) exhibited malignancies, including 2 with mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma, 1 with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, and 1 with squamous cell carcinoma; the remaining 56% (5/9) presented benign conditions: 1 polymorphous adenoma, 2 inflammatory granulomas, 1 squamous cell papilloma, and 1 reactive lymphoid hyperplasia (Table 1). All patients experienced epiphora, and lacrimal irrigation confirmed patency of the contralateral eyes in 8 of the 9 patients. Regurgitation was noted in 5 of the 9 patients. Chronic ocular discharge was present in 7 of the 9 patients, and 1 patient had preceding dacryocystitis, which resolved within 3 days following antibiotic treatment. A total of 11 patients with lacrimal mucoceles, including 3 males and 8 females with a mean age of 55.91±17.68 years, were included in the study. The mean 3D tumor volume was 1,570±1,023 mm3 in the lacrimal sac neoplasm group and 2,416±1,268 mm3 in the lacrimal mucocele group, with no statistically significant difference (P=0.12). Regarding margin characteristics, 7 cases (77.8%) in the neoplasm group had regular margins and 2 cases (22.2%) had irregular margins on CT, whereas all 11 cases (100%) in the mucocele group showed regular margins (P=0.10) (Table 1). The mean width of the nasolacrimal canal was 6.49±3.68 mm in patients with lacrimal sac neoplasms and 5.50±1.88 mm in those with mucoceles. There was no statistically significant difference in the width of the nasolacrimal canal between the two groups (P=0.44). However, the depth of the nasolacrimal canal was significantly greater in the lacrimal sac neoplasms group (5.08±3.21 mm) compared to the mucoceles group (2.85±0.96 mm) (P=0.042) (Table 1). Notably, in 8 lacrimal sac neoplasms, the lacrimal sac mass extended above the medial canthal tendon, except in 1, where the mass was centered in the nasolacrimal canal. In contrast, none of the lacrimal mucocele masses extended above the medial canthal tendon (Figure 2), highlighting distinct pathological presentations.

We further investigated the differences between diseased and contralateral eyes among patients with lacrimal sac neoplasms and lacrimal mucoceles. In patients with lacrimal sac neoplasms, the diseased eyes had a mean nasolacrimal canal width of 6.49 mm, significantly larger than the 3.01 mm in the contralateral eyes (P=0.01). The depth was also different, with diseased eyes showing a mean depth of 5.08 mm, significantly deeper than the 2.66 mm in the contralateral eyes, a difference that was borderline significant (P=0.040) (Figure 3). In the lacrimal mucocele group, diseased eyes exhibited a mean width of 5.50 mm, significantly greater than the 3.45 mm in the contralateral eyes (P=0.003). However, the differences in depth were less pronounced, with diseased eyes measuring 2.85 vs. 2.51 mm in the contralateral eyes, a statistically insignificant difference (P=0.10) (Figure 3).

Discussion

Our findings demonstrate a notable female predominance in lacrimal sac neoplasms, aligning with previous studies that documented similar trends in primary lacrimal duct obstruction (4). This trend may reflect underlying hormonal or biological differences that predispose women to these conditions. Unlike primary lacrimal duct obstruction, which typically presents bilaterally, lacrimal sac neoplasms in our cohort were primarily unilateral. This observation emphasizes the importance of thorough unilateral assessments in patients presenting with epiphora to exclude neoplasms, given the significant risks associated with misdiagnosis.

A single instance of a bloody tear was observed in 1 out of 9 patients; this is consistent with prior research, where only 8% of patients with blood-stained tears had lacrimal sac malignancy (5), and it is uncommon in nasal lacrimal sac neoplasms (6-10). Regurgitation was frequently noted in this study, occurring in patients with both necrotic squamous cell carcinoma and benign neoplasms. This suggests that benign neoplasms, such as inflammatory granulomas, may have loose connective tissues that tend to accumulate fluid within the lacrimal sac. Preceding dacryocystitis, in line with prior findings (10), was observed in only 1 patient, whose infection symptoms resolved within 3 days following antibiotic treatment, indicating that lacrimal sac neoplasms might be well-vascularized and possess superior anti-infection properties.

Our findings show that tumor size and margin characteristics on CT do not significantly differentiate early neoplasms from mucoceles, which explains why neoplasms are often misdiagnosed as NLD obstruction or mucocele initially. Kumar et al. observed that malignant tumors of the lacrimal sac and NLD tend to expand rather than erode the nasolacrimal bony canal (11), consistent with our findings of both horizontal widening and vertical deepening in the bony remodeling of early-stage lacrimal sac neoplasms. Our study identified distinct structural alterations in the nasolacrimal canal associated with lacrimal sac neoplasms compared to mucoceles. Although both conditions demonstrated widening of the nasolacrimal canal, the depth was significantly greater in cases of lacrimal sac neoplasms. Furthermore, compared to the contralateral eye, the depth of the nasolacrimal canal was significantly deeper in lacrimal sac neoplasms, whereas no notable difference was observed in the NLD, suggesting that neoplasms may induce a more aggressive modification of the lacrimal drainage architecture than the fluid retention observed in mucoceles. Coronal CT scans provided deeper insights into these pathologies. In the mucocele group, the canal appeared dome-shaped, indicative of a uniform expansion typically caused by fluid accumulation under pressure within a confined anatomical space. In contrast, the nasolacrimal canal in patients with lacrimal sac neoplasms exhibited a triangular shape, reflecting an uneven and potentially invasive expansion pattern characteristic of neoplastic growth. Wen et al. observed that patients with lacrimal sac cysts exhibited a widened lacrimal sac fossa, a thinner frontal process, and larger lacrimal bone angles than the controls (12). Lacrimal mucoceles are often associated with nasolacrimal duct obstruction and the robust perilacrimal fascia at the fundus of the lacrimal sac typically resists passive expansion by retention mucoceles (10). This resistance could explain why mucoceles do not exhibit significant downward expansion. In contrast, neoplasms possess the potential to grow both upward, extending above the medial canthal tendon, and downward, thereby expanding the bony lacrimal canal. This directional growth capability highlights the aggressive nature of neoplasms and underscores the complex challenges they pose in clinical management.

These distinct radiological and anatomical features emphasize the importance of comprehensive imaging assessments in patients presenting with epiphora or other related symptoms. Understanding these differences can aid clinicians in making more accurate diagnoses and tailoring treatment strategies to effectively manage these challenging conditions.

Our findings reveal that masses in the lacrimal sac fossa, whether benign or malignant, often manifest clinically at similar ages and with comparable durations of symptoms (10). This clinical similarity poses significant challenges in the early and accurate diagnosis of lacrimal sac neoplasms. Although CT scans are invaluable for assessing anatomical structures and the potential involvement of surrounding tissues, they have limited efficacy in differentiating between benign and malignant neoplasms at early stages, especially in the absence of bone erosion or eyeball displacement. Enhanced MRI has shown to be more effective in this context, successfully identifying the nature of the neoplasm in 6 out of 9 cases in our study. The superior soft tissue contrast of MRI allows for a more detailed evaluation of the lacrimal sac and adjacent structures, thereby facilitating a more precise differentiation between benign and malignant growths.

Given the rarity of malignant lacrimal drainage system neoplasms, which often limits the available literature to isolated case reports or small case series, there is a significant need for further data collection. Comprehensive data and continued research are essential to refine diagnostic criteria and enhance our understanding of these conditions. The reported mortality rates, which range from 18% at 30 months to 47% after 5 years, highlight the aggressive nature of malignant lacrimal sac neoplasms and the urgency for early diagnosis and treatment (13,14).

This study provides valuable insights into the clinical diagnosis and imaging differentiation of lacrimal sac neoplasms, but is subject to certain limitations that must be acknowledged. Due to the low incidence rate of lacrimal sac neoplasms, the number of cases included in this study is inherently limited, potentially affecting the stability of the statistical data and the generalizability of the conclusions. Furthermore, the limited number of cases may have introduced biases, such as an uneven distribution of disease subtypes. Additionally, our research primarily focused on the diagnosis and characterization of early-stage lacrimal sac neoplasms. Although this focus is crucial for early detection and intervention, it means that our findings may not fully represent the characteristics and outcomes of more advanced stages of the disease.

Conclusions

Based on the insights gathered from our study, we propose the following clinical approaches for managing patients presenting with epiphora, particularly when CT imaging reveals that the nasolacrimal canal appears deeper than that of the contralateral eye and the mass shows extension above the medial canthal tendon region. Implementing these recommendations can lead to earlier detection and more precise characterization of lacrimal sac neoplasms, potentially improving treatment outcomes and patient quality of life.

Pulmonary artery dilatation in different causes of pulmonary hypertension
Authors: Qunying Xi, Zhihong Liu, Changming Xiong, Qin Luo, Zhihui Zhao, Qing Zhao, Tao Yang, Qixian Zeng, Pingwei Li, Luwen Qiu
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractPulmonary artery (PA) dilatation is commonly observed in patients with pulmonary hypertension (PH). However, the clinical aspects of PA dilatation in various etiology of PH remain unknown. In this study, we investigated the clinical and imaging characteristics of 1018 patients with different subtypes of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) and chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH). The independent determinants for all‐cause death were identified using univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazard models. PA dilatation was identified in 88.8% of the patients, and 27.2% had a PA diameter/ascending aorta diameter ratio greater than 1.5. PA diameter was shown to be significantly correlated with hemodynamic parameters and symptom duration in idiopathic PAH patients. PA diameter only correlated with pulmonary circulation volume in patients with PAH associated with congenital heart disease. PA diameter correlated with symptom duration and right ventricular end‐diastolic dimension in CTEPH patients. PA diameter correlated with right ventricular end‐diastolic dimension in patients with PAH associated with connective tissue disease. Only 6‐min walk distance, but not PA dilatation, predicts all‐cause death independently. In conclusion, PA dilatation is a common finding in PH patients. The clinical feature of PA dilatation varies greatly between PH types. PA dilatation is not associated with all‐cause death.Keywords: clinical characteristics, clinical classification, pulmonary artery dilatation, pulmonary hypertension

INTRODUCTION

Pulmonary artery (PA) dilatation is commonly observed in patients with pulmonary hypertension (PH). 1 , 2 , 3 Several studies 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 have investigated the relationship between clinical variables and PA dilatation. However, the results were contentious. One study found that patients with pulmonary arterial hypertension associated with congenital heart disease (CHD‐PAH) had the largest PA diameter among PAH and CTEPH patients, 8 while other studies showed no difference in PA diameter between different PH types. Most studies observed a correlation between PA diameter and mean pulmonary arterial pressure. However, the relationship between PA diameter and other hemodynamic variables is unknown. These discrepancies are most likely due to the small sample size. Thus, in the present study, we examined PA dilatation in a relatively large population of patients with idiopathic PAH (IPAH), PAH associated with connective tissue disease (CTD‐PAH), CHD‐PAH, and chronic thromboembolic PH (CTEPH) to gain a comprehensive understanding of PA dilatation in different causes of PH.

METHODS

We analyzed PA diameter and clinical features of patients with PAH and CTEPH who were referred to the Center for Pulmonary Vascular Disease of Fuwai Hospital, National Center for Cardiovascular Diseases between June 2012 and December 2016. All patients with IPAH, CTD‐PAH, CHD‐PAH, and CTEPH who were unsuitable for surgical therapy were eligible. CHD‐PAH included PAH associated with atrial septal defect (ASD), PAH associated with ventricular septal defect (VSD), and PAH associated with patent ductus arteriosus (PDA). Patients who did not have computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA) were excluded from participation in the study. Only if right heart catheterization (RHC) was conducted during the same hospitalization as CTPA were hemodynamic data included in the analysis. A total of 1,313 patients were screened, with 1018 being included in the final study. Patients were followed up for a mean time of 50.87 ± 28.92 months. The study protocol is shown in Figure 1. Informed consent was obtained from each patient and the study protocol conformed to the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Population and study protocol.

CTPA protocol

CTPA was performed using a multidetector scanner (Somatom Sensation 16; Siemens AG). The standard procedure included a bolus injection of 35 ml of contrast material and a triggered scanning of pulmonary trunk. The section thickness was 1 × 0.75 mm. Patients were instructed to take breath‐hold at full inspiration in supine position.

Diameters of pulmonary trunk and ascending aorta were measured on the same CT slice. Measurement was made on horizontal scan in the mediastinum window, and vertically to the long axis of pulmonary trunk (Figure 2). According to earlier studies, 9 the normal value for PA diameter in adult healthy Chinese is reported as 22.41 ± 2.59 mm (range 17.31 to 27.47 mm), and the ratio of PA diameter/ascending aortic diameter (PA ratio, hereafter) is 0.79 ± 0.11 (range 0.60 to 0.97). Age, gender, body surface area, and ethnicity all impact PA diameter, whereas PA ratio is typically less than 1.0. 10 Therefore, in this study, we used a PA ratio of >1.0 as the criterion for PA dilatation.

Measurement of main pulmonary artery (PA) diameter and PA diameter/ascending aortic artery diameter ratio (PA ratio). PA diameter and ascending aortic artery diameter were measured at the level of the bifurcation of the right and left PA on horizontal scans in the mediastinum window of computed tomography pulmonary angiography, and vertically to the long axis of the pulmonary trunk. PA ratio was calculated as PA diameter divided by ascending aortic artery diameter.

RHC protocol

Four hundred and eighty‐seven patients, comprising 254 IPAH patients, 105 CHD‐PAH patients, 59 CTD‐PAH patients, and 69 CTEPH patients, underwent RHC during the same hospitalization when CTPA was performed. Pulmonary arterial wedge pressure was measured by using a Swan‐Ganz catheter (Edwards Lifesciences World Trade Co. Ltd.). Right atrial pressure, right ventricular pressure, and PA pressure were recorded. Cardiac output, pulmonary circulation volume, systemic circulation volume, pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR), and cardiac index were calculated.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables. Categorical variables are displayed as numbers and percentages. The correlation between PA diameter or PA ratio and other variables was determined by using Pearson correlation analysis. Differences between IPAH, CHD‐PAH, CTD‐PAH, and CTEPH groups were compared by one‐way ANOVA analysis. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazard models were used to identify the independent predictors for all‐cause death. P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS) 16.0 for Windows (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA).

RESULTS

Clinical characteristics

The baseline clinical characteristics of the patients are displayed in Table 1. There were 1018 patients with a mean age of 39 ± 15 years (ranging from 13 years to 80 years), and 68.7% of whom were female. The majority of patients received specific PAH monotherapy; one patient received calcium channel blocker therapy as a result of a positive acute pulmonary vasodilator test; 21.1% of the patients had not taken any specific PAH medication. The majority of patients were in WHO functional class II to III, but 85 patients had no record of WHO functional classification. The mean PA diameter was 36.5 ± 7.3 mm (ranging from 17 mm to 89 mm). PA ratio >1.0 was found in 88.8% of patients, 27.2% of patients had PA ratios >1.5.

The differences between IPAH, CHD‐PAH, CTD‐PAH, and CTEPH are presented in Table 2. The largest PA diameter and PA ratio were seen in CHD‐PAH patients. Correlation between PA diameter, PA ratio, and clinical characteristics

Table 4 demonstrates that age had a weak correlation with PA diameter but a substantial correlation with ascending aortic diameter (r = 0.655, p < 0.001), resulting in a negative correlation with PA ratio (r = −0.367, p < 0.001). In the total PH study population, the correlation between symptom duration and PA diameter or PA ratio was minimal. However, the correlation between PA diameter and symptom duration varied on the cause of PH (Table 5). In CTEPH patients, there was a highly significant association between the PA diameter and the duration of symptoms (r = 0.442, p < 0.001). There was no correlation between PA diameter or PA ratio and symptom duration in patients with CHD‐PAH or CTD‐PAH, although there was a marginally significant correlation in IPAH patients (r = 0.286, p < 0.001).

In terms of hemodynamic parameters, there was no correlation between systolic PA pressure and PA diameter or PA ratio in the entire study population. There were significant correlations between the PA diameter or PA ratio and several other hemodynamic parameters including the diastolic PA pressure, the mean PA pressure, the pulmonary circulation volume, and the cardiac index (Table 4). It is interesting to note that only IPAH patients showed a correlation between PA diameter or PA ratio and PA pressure when we looked at different PH groups (Table 5). Patients with IPAH and CHD‐PAH showed a significant correlation between the PA diameter and the pulmonary circulation volume.

In summary, in IPAH patients, PA diameter strongly correlated with the symptom duration, PA pressures, pulmonary circulation volume, cardiac index, and PVR. In CHD‐PAH patients, PA diameter only correlated with the pulmonary circulation volume. In CTEPH patients, PA diameter correlated with the duration of symptoms, and the right ventricular end‐diastolic dimension. In CTD‐PAH patients, PA diameter only correlated with the right ventricular end‐diastolic dimension.

Risk factors for mortality

Seventy patients (6.9%) died during a mean follow‐up time of 50.9 ± 28.9 months (ranging from 3.2 to 109.5 months). These 70 individuals had a mean PA diameter of 34.99 ± 6.27 mm (percentile 25–75 ranged from 31.00 to 37.47 mm). WHO‐FC, 6‐min walk distance (6MWD), and CI were identified as related to all‐cause death in the univariate Cox proportional hazard analysis. However, only 6MWD independently predicted death in the multivariate Cox proportional hazard analysis (Table 6).

DISCUSSION

As far as we know, the present study uses the biggest sample size to clarify the association between clinical features and PA dilatation in patients with various etiology of PH. We found out that the majority of hemodynamic variables, such as PA pressures, pulmonary circulation volume, cardiac index, and PVR, were correlated with PA diameter and/or PA ratio in IPAH patients. Surprisingly, in patients with CTD‐PAH and CTEPH, there was no correlation between hemodynamic variables and PA diameter. In CTEPH patients, there was a significant correlation between PA diameter and symptom duration. PA diameter only correlated with pulmonary circulation volume in CHD‐PAH patients, however.

Increased intra‐PA pressure is a well‐known contributor to PA dilatation. As a result, PA enlargement on imaging is frequently interpreted as a sign of PH. The area under the curve of PA diameter for the detection of PH was greater than 0.85, according to earlier research. 1 , 3 However, evidence indicated that the PA diameter does not always correlate with intravascular pressure. PA dilatation continued to progress in PH patients despite the reduction of PA pressure by PH treatment. 11 , 12 In one study, the researchers found that PH patients with PA diameters more than 40 mm had mean PA pressures that were higher than those of PH patients with PA diameters under 40 mm. Even while both PH groups’ mean PA pressures significantly decreased during the course of follow‐up, only patients with baseline PA diameters greater than 40 mm experienced an increase in PA diameter. 12 These findings suggest that intra‐PA pressure primarily serves as an initial driver. Other factors also contribute to the underlying tendency for PA dilatation. This is concluded by a hypothesis that PA dilatation requires a “two‐hit” paradigm, involving both genetic susceptibility and sustained exposure to risk factors such as elevated pulmonary pressures, hypoxia, or atherosclerosis. 13 The mechanism is similar to aortic dilatation in that, while hypertension is the most common risk factor, several underlying medical conditions, such as genetic abnormalities and inflammatory vasculopathy, also play important roles. 14 The findings of our study support this theory. The development of PA dilatation might be significantly influenced by hypoxia, aging in CTEPH patients, and connective tissue lesions in CTD‐PAH patients.

The current analysis confirms previous findings 7 that PA diameter is not associated with an increased risk of all‐cause death. However, substantial PA dilatation does appear to be associated with an increased risk of sudden death or mechanical complications. 8 , 15 , 16 In a cohort of patients with PAH and CTEPH, it was discovered that a PA diameter of more than 48 mm was associated with a 7.5 times higher risk of unexpected death. 8 The primary cause of the sudden death could have been left main coronary artery compression and cardiac tamponade due to PA dissection or rupture. Unfortunately, we were unable to determine the precise causes of each of the 70 deaths. However, the mean PA diameter of these 70 patients was significantly smaller than the PA diameters reported in other research that showed an association between PA dilatation and sudden death.

There are limitations in this study. First off, although the cohort is made up of a sizable number of consecutive patients to lessen bias, this is a retrospective study from a single center. Second, we did not reassess the PA diameter during follow‐up and solely used CTPA data collected at the time of diagnosis. Almost 25% of patients were lost to follow‐up. As a result, neither the change in PA size associated with PH treatment nor the relationship between PA diameter and mortality risk could be observed. Third, the majority of patients in the present study had PH, as it was carried out in a PH center. As a result, compared to earlier studies, the correlation between PA pressure and PA diameter observed in the current study was significant but only modest. However, these findings are restricted to the context of a PH center. If there were more people who were without PH, strong associations may be anticipated.

CONCLUSIONS

PA dilatation is frequently observed in PH patients. Clinical features of PA dilatation vary greatly between PH types. PA diameter correlates strongly with PA pressures, pulmonary circulation volume, cardiac index, and PVR in IPAH patients. PA diameter only correlates with pulmonary circulation volume in CHD‐PAH patients. In CTEPH patients, PA diameter remarkably correlates with symptom duration. In CTEPH and CTD‐PAH patients, there is no association between hemodynamic measures and PA diameter. 6MWD independently predicts death, whereas PA dilatation is unrelated to the increased risk of all‐cause death.

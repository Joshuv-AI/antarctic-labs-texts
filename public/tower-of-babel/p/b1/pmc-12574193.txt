Survey on the Public Opinion and Attitudes Toward Uterus Transplantation in Hungary
Authors: Balint Farkas, Kata Szilvia Papp, Szilard Kolumban, Kalman Kovacs, Jozsef Bodis, Gabor Fazekas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroductionUterus transplantation (UTx) is a novel and clinically successful treatment option for patients with absolute uterine factor infertility (AUFI) who wish to achieve biological motherhood. Although over 100 UTx procedures have been performed at 20 centers worldwide in the past decade, the procedure remains relatively unknown—even among healthcare professionals. This study aimed to assess public opinion regarding UTx and to identify knowledge gaps in the Hungarian population's awareness and perception of the procedure.MethodsThis prospective study was conducted between December 18, 2024, and January 5, 2025, via an online survey involving 2896 participants. A final representative sample of 1500 individuals was selected using an iterative mathematical weighting method, adjusted to the 2022 Hungarian census data. The sample was representative of the Hungarian internet‐using population aged 18–70 years in terms of age, gender, education level, and place of residence. The 24‐item questionnaire assessed participants’ views on organ donation, transplantation, infertility, surrogacy, and UTx.ResultsMost respondents (77%; 1155/1500) supported UTx (strongly agree: 44%, 660/1500; agree: 33%, 495/1500). Among participants personally affected by fertility issues, 84% (152/181) expressed support, with 51% (92/181) strongly agreeing that UTx should be offered. Only 4% of the total samples opposed the procedure. Notably, just 15% of respondents were previously aware that UTx had been performed internationally.ConclusionsThis online survey demonstrates substantial public approval for UTx in Hungary, particularly among individuals affected by infertility. However, public awareness of the procedure remains limited, indicating a need for increased education and communication to improve general knowledge on the topic.Keywords: infertility, obstetrics, prospective study, survey, uterus transplantation

Introduction

Uterus transplantation (UTx) is a novel treatment approach for a specific group of women with infertility—those affected by absolute uterine factor infertility (AUFI). In this rare condition, the uterus is either anatomically absent or functionally impaired, resulting in infertility [1]. It is estimated that approximately 150,000 women in Europe are affected by AUFI. Extrapolating this to Hungary, a country with a population of 9.77 million, AUFI likely affects around 1500–2000 individuals.

For women with AUFI who wish to become mothers, the only current options are adoption or surrogacy. In Hungary, however, adoption is a slow and bureaucratically complex process, and all forms of surrogacy are illegal, categorized under laws prohibiting human trafficking. In contrast, UTx may provide a legally acceptable and medically feasible alternative [2].

Nearly a decade after the landmark clinical trial by Brännström et al. [3], which led to the first live birth following live‐donor UTx, the procedure has become increasingly recognized as a clinical reality. By 2022, over 80 UTx procedures had been performed at nearly 20 centers across Europe, North America, Latin America, and Asia, resulting in more than 40 live births [4]. In 2020, UTx was incorporated into Germany's national healthcare system and is currently in the process of being adopted in Sweden and the Czech Republic [4, 5]. To facilitate global monitoring, the International Uterus Transplantation Society launched an international UTx registry in 2020 [6]. Despite these advancements, UTx is not yet available in Hungary.

Public attitudes toward novel medical technologies play a critical role in shaping health policy, informing regulation, and determining the social acceptability of such interventions. This is particularly important in the case of UTx, which is not lifesaving but life‐creating procedure. Understanding societal perspectives is therefore crucial to guide ethical justifications, resource allocation, and potential public funding decisions for UTx programs.

However, empirical data on public opinion are limited. To date, only a few studies—conducted in the United States, United Kingdom, France, Sweden, and Japan—have investigated public attitudes toward UTx [7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12]. While most of these studies indicate general support for UTx as a treatment for AUFI, they also reveal ethical concerns, including medical and surgical risks. Some respondents perceive UTx as a non‐vital organ transplant that does not significantly enhance quality of life [13, 14]. Interestingly, medical professionals appear to be more critical overall, with some studies showing that fewer than half of surveyed clinicians consider UTx ethically unproblematic [7, 15]. Nonetheless, as the procedure evolved from experimental to established, healthcare providers’ attitudes have generally become more supportive, albeit with notable regional differences [13].

A multidisciplinary team in Hungary has been established to introduce UTx as a clinical option for eligible AUFI patients. Several preparatory animal and cadaveric studies have already been conducted [16, 17]. The current study aims to assess public attitudes and opinions regarding UTx in Hungary. We hypothesize that in a country facing population decline and where infertility is a significant public health issue, public opinion will be generally supportive of UTx as an innovative treatment option for this unique patient population.

Materials and Methods

Survey Development

This prospective cross‐sectional study was conducted by the Nézőpont Institute (Budapest, Hungary) in collaboration with physicians from the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Clinical Center, University of Pécs (Pécs, Hungary). Participation was voluntary, anonymous, and uncompensated. No personal data were collected; therefore, the University of Pécs Institutional Ethical Review Board waived formal review. All participants provided digital consent by clicking “Agree to Terms and Conditions” before accessing the online survey.

The 24‐item questionnaire was created using Microsoft Forms (Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA, USA) and was based on previously published studies that assessed public perceptions of UTx in other countries [7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12]. The questions were developed in consultation with medical professionals (B.F., K.S.P., S.K., G.F.). The survey included a brief introduction to the research center, the purpose of the study, a layperson‐friendly explanation of UTx, 8 demographic questions, and 16 statements related to infertility, transplantation, surrogacy, and UTx. Responses were recorded using single‐choice (yes/no/not sure answer options) and Likert‐scale (strongly agree, agree, disagree, strongly disagree, and neutral answer options) formats (Appendix A and Appendix B).

Survey Distribution

The survey was distributed via email to 41,000 Hungarian residents aged 18 and 70 between December 18, 2024 and January 5, 2025. Email addresses were obtained and managed by the Nézőpont Institute in compliance with Hungarian data privacy regulations. A total of 3114 individuals clicked the survey link, and 2896 fully completed the questionnaire.

From this respondent pool, a final sample of 1500 individuals was selected using an iterative mathematical weighting process after randomization. The weighting was based on 2022 census data from the Hungarian Central Statistical Office (KSH) to ensure representativeness across key demographic variables: gender (male, female), age (18–29, 30–39, 40–49, 50–59, 60–69, 70–75), education level (primary, secondary, tertiary), type of settlement (capital city, county seat, other town, village/hamlet/farmstead), and geographic region (Northern Hungary, Northern Great Plain, Southern Great Plain, Central Hungary, Central Transdanubia, Western Transdanubia, Southern Transdanubia). Weighting coefficients ranged from 0.33 to 2.94 to maintain demographic balance and ensure the sample's representativeness of the internet‐using adult Hungarian population.

Inclusion criteria were: Hungarian citizenship, age between 18 and 70 years, fluency in Hungarian, and access to the Internet.

A pilot test was conducted with 15 individuals—5 with medical backgrounds and 10 without—who assessed the clarity and comprehensibility of the survey. After the non‐medical participants completed the questionnaire, their feedback was discussed by the medical investigator group. This process identified several issues, which were subsequently revised to improve clarity and content quality.

Statistical Analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics (ArmFonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were analyzed using chi‐square tests, as appropriate. Results are presented as absolute numbers and percentages. A p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. The study database is available at the following link: https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.29826416.v1.

Awareness of Infertility and Attitudes Toward Medical Innovation

Twelve percent of participants (180/1500) reported that they had experienced infertility or were diagnosed with fertility problems. Most respondents (83%) stated they had no such issues, and 5% remained neutral. Additionally, 43% (645/1500) reported knowing someone who could not conceive naturally (Figure 1).

Infertility was more commonly reported among women (16%, 123/768) and those aged 30–39 (17%, 44/260). Among respondents personally affected by infertility, 84% (152/181) stated that they knew someone with fertility issues.

Overall, attitudes toward reproductive health support and medical innovation were highly positive. The vast majority of respondents agreed that support should be provided to those unable to conceive naturally (94%, 1410/1500; 3% disagreed; 3% neutral). Similarly, 93% (1395/1500) supported the integration of innovative medical technologies into Hungarian healthcare (2% disagreed; 5% neutral). These agreement rates were significantly higher (98%–97%, p < 0.001) among respondents who knew someone affected by infertility.

Attitudes Toward Living Vs. Deceased Donation

Forty‐two percent of respondents (630/1500) supported UTx using both living and deceased donors. This preference was most pronounced among residents of the capital (52%, 140/270) and those with tertiary education (52%, 189/364). An additional 17% (255/1500) supported only deceased‐donor (brain‐dead) UTx—this proportion was highest among respondents aged >60 years (22%, 81/368). Discussion

This study is the first to explore public opinion regarding uterus transplantation (UTx) in Hungary. The survey was conducted in a socio‐demographically representative sample of the internet‐using adult population, making the findings a reliable indicator of public attitudes toward UTx in the country.

One of the key findings was that approximately 10% of Hungarian adults reported experiencing fertility problems, and over 40% reported knowing someone affected by infertility. These rates are comparable to infertility prevalence in Western Europe (10%–15%) and globally (8%–12%) [18, 19, 20]. Women and individuals aged 30–39 were more likely to report either personal experience with infertility or knowing someone affected.

Given the demographic challenges facing Europe and other developed regions, infertility constitutes a major public health concern. A recent European survey emphasized the importance of providing support and alternatives for those affected [21]. In alignment with this, the overwhelming majority of Hungarian respondents (94%–93%) agreed that people unable to conceive naturally should receive support and that innovative medical techniques should be adopted in national healthcare.

The primary aim of this study was to assess attitudes toward UTx, which currently represents the only potential legal pathway to biological motherhood for patients with AUFI in Hungary. While surrogacy is banned in Hungary and considered a form of human trafficking by the Hungarian Supreme Court (2020) [22], some EU member states allow altruistic surrogacy, although commercial surrogacy is prohibited under EU Directive E‐001332/2024 [23]. Notably, only 60% of respondents were aware that surrogacy is illegal in Hungary.

Our findings revealed that nearly 80% of respondents supported the idea that UTx should be performed. Support was especially strong among those with higher education levels, prior awareness of UTx, a personal or indirect experience with infertility, or prior involvement with organ donation. These results are consistent with findings from other countries. In the UK, 43% of surveyed women were willing to donate their uterus posthumously [8], while in the USA, this figure was approximately 75% [24]. A recent study in Japan reported that 37% supported UTx and 31% supported the legalization of gestational surrogacy [11]. In Sweden, UTx was more acceptable than surrogacy (80% vs. 47%) [10].

Despite more than a decade having passed since the first live birth following UTx in Sweden, public awareness of the procedure remains limited. For example, 71% of respondents in a UK study had never heard of UTx [8]. Similarly, in our sample, only 15% of participants reported being aware of UTx prior to the survey. This could be explained that UTx is not yet introduced in Hungary, and it is a procedure carried out in limited occasions worldwide, and also the number of AUFI patients are low in the population. Given the high levels of support observed, there is a clear need to increase public education and awareness about UTx through media campaigns, scientific outreach, and public dialogue.

Supporters of UTx generally perceived it as a procedure offering hope to individuals affected by infertility, with significant psychosocial and emotional benefits. Nevertheless, 4% of respondents opposed UTx, likely due to ethical concerns, including surgical risks and the perception that UTx is a non‐life‐saving procedure. Religious or cultural beliefs may also play a role in opposition, although this was not directly measured in our study.

This study has several limitations. First, the response rate was low relative to the number of invitations sent, and the survey was limited to individuals with internet access. Second, the general awareness of UTx was limited, potentially introducing response bias. Although internet access in Hungary is widespread, the novelty and specificity of the procedure may contribute to the low baseline familiarity observed among respondents.

Despite these limitations, the findings offer important insights into Hungarian public attitudes toward UTx and highlight the potential acceptability of implementing such a program nationally.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our results suggest a high level of public approval for UTx as a reproductive treatment option in Hungary. While overall acceptance was strong (77%), younger respondents (aged 18–29) showed comparatively lower levels of strong agreement, suggesting potential generational differences in perception.

Supporters of UTx recognized its psychological and social value, especially its ability to offer hope and fulfill the desire for parenthood among individuals affected by infertility. However, public awareness remains low. To increase familiarity with UTx and its implications, coordinated efforts involving the media, healthcare providers, and scientific institutions are needed. Broader public dialogue and education could contribute to more informed and ethically grounded decisions about the future of UTx in Hungary.

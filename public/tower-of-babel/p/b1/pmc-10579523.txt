Establishment and application of a point-of-care testing and diagnosis method for early immediate expression gene IE1 of cytomegalovirus in maternal urine based on isothermal amplification
Authors: Chu Chu, Shijiao Yu, Fanli Min, Lizhou Sun, Meilin Liu, Qian Meng
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Highlights•A POCT diagnostic method for HCMV in pregnant women's urine was established.•The HCMV detection targeted UL123 encoding IE1, a early immediately expressed gene.•The HCMV detection could be finished within 15 min at 37°C and 5 min visually reading.•The LOD of HCMV detection was 30 copies genomic DNA.•The HCMV POCT diagnostic method shared 96.67 % consistency with qPCR.

Introduction

Human cytomegalovirus (HCMV), also known as human herpesvirus 5 (HHV-5), is a complex double-stranded DNA enveloped virus belonging to the β subfamily of the Herpesviridae family (Gugliesi et al., 2021). HCMV infection is distributed all over the world. The infection rate of HCMV in developed countries is about 50 %, while that in China is as high as 90 % (Yates et al., 2022; Simonnet et al., 2021). In most people with normal immune function, cytomegalovirus infection does not cause serious clinical symptoms, but in some people, such as people with compromised immune system, pregnant women and newborns, cytomegalovirus infection can cause a series of serious diseases, such as retinitis, pneumonia, hepatitis and encephalitis (Griffiths and Reeves, 2021; Brizić et al., 2018). Cytomegalovirus infection during pregnancy can lead to congenital cytomegalovirus infection (Davis et al., 2017). About 0.2 %∼2 % of newborns were infected with HCMV before birth, and about 10 %∼15 % of newborns were infected with HCMV after birth. The clinical manifestations were hearing loss, mental retardation, visual impairment, microcephaly, developmental retardation and a series of serious complications. HCMV has biological characteristics of latency and reactivation (Zelini et al., 2022). About 5−10 % of the infected newborns show latent HCMV infection after birth without clinical symptoms, but after HCMV infection is reactivated, delayed systemic damage such as hearing impairment and mental retardation will gradually occur (Cheeran et al., 2009; Fowler and Boppana, 2018). Therefore, early and accurate diagnosis of HCMV infection is very important for prevention and control of HCMV transmission.

Although virus isolation, culture, observation of typical pathological changes of cells under light microscope and observation of physical morphology of virus particles under transmission electron microscope are still the gold standard of virus identification including HCMV (Drebber et al., 2011). However, the continuous passage of the virus results in long cell culture cycle, high virus enrichment cost and expensive equipment, because of over-dependence on the environment of sterile laboratory and other factors, HCMV diagnosis is faced with high difficulty and detection lag, more suitable for scientific research institutes to carry out basic research, it is difficult in the hospital laboratory promotion. Compared with virus isolation and culture, PCR-based extended molecular diagnosis of HCMV has significant advantages, because of its high sensitivity, high specificity, relatively short detection period, and high popularity of precision equipment (temperature-controlled PCR apparatus, desktop centrifuges, electrophoresis apparatus, nucleic acid gel imagers, or the implementation of fluorescence quantitative PCR apparatus) in standard laboratories, it is widely used in many detection scenarios (Nitsche et al., 2000; Vincent et al., 2009; Espy et al., 2006). However, it is difficult to detect HCMV in grass-roots laboratories where there is a shortage of equipment and technicians, and in situations where rapid detection of HCMV is required outside the laboratory (Marsico and Kimberlin, 2017).

Compared with PCR, isothermal amplification does not need complex temperature cycling equipment, and can complete DNA amplification at constant temperature, so it is more suitable for rapid detection in the field. In recent years, many isothermal amplification technologies have been developed, such as loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP), helicase-dependent amplification (HDA), recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA), and so on, and have been applied in the fields of food safety, environmental monitoring and clinical diagnosis. In recent years, due to the low temperature, simple system configuration and high tolerance to complex templates, the isothermal amplification of nucleic acids by RPA has emerged, it has been used in Point-of-Care Testing (POCT) for detection of multiple pathogens since it was first reported in 2006 (Lobato and O'Sullivan, 2018; Li et al., 2018). For LAMP amplification, it is necessary to design 4 specific primers for 6 regions of the target gene, and achieve constant temperature displacement amplification within 60 min under the action of Bst DNA polymerase and the relatively high reaction temperature of 60–65 °C. The primer design is complex, the reaction temperature needs are high, the temperature control range is small, and the reaction time is relatively prolonged (Park, 2022). Although HDA can complete the amplification at a relatively low reaction temperature of 37 °C and under the action of DNA polymerase, its amplification sequence is short, and primer dimers are easily generated, and there are few studies. The method is not mature enough in application (Jeong et al., 2009). RPA core enzyme, including recombinant enzyme (T4 UvsX protein), recombinant enzyme loading factor (T4 UvsY protein), single-stranded binding protein (T4 gp32) and DNA polymerase (Bsu or Sau), can enrich target DNA within 10 copies at constant temperature of 37–42 °C and 15–30 min (Yonesaki and Minagawa, 1985; Harris and Griffith, 1989). It has good application prospect. However, this technique has not been reported in the diagnosis of HCMV.

Completion of RPA amplification alone was still not sufficient for POCT visualization of the result detection, and RPA amplification products were subjected to agarose gel electrophoresis, which was similar to PCR amplification result reads, the data were read by the gel imaging system. Although this agarose gel electrophoresis combined with RPA technique has been used in some pathogens detection, it is not optimistic for POCT detection. In addition, more and more literatures have combined RPA with lateral flow test strip (LFS) or fluorescence method to achieve real-time visualization of results (Ji et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2022). Although some portable fluorescence reading devices have been widely used, considering the cost of the instrument, this study did not adopt the experimental design combined with fluorescence method at the beginning of design. In order to combine RPA with LFS, it is necessary to modify primers and introduce labeled probes in experimental design. The probe 5 'end was labeled with fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) and 3′ end-labeled C3spacer with a base analogue (Tetrahydrofuran, THF) introduced in the middle. The reverse primer 5 'end was labeled with Biotin. RPA amplification produced 5′-labeled FITC and 3′ -labeled Biotin products, which were captured by LFS. The interpretation of the whole result only needs 5–10 min migration and synchronous color development, and the judgment result can be obtained by visual reading.

In summary, the purpose of this project is to develop a HCMV isothermal amplification detection method base on RPA combined with LFS technology, hoping to provide a reliable, rapid and low dependence on equipment POCT HCMV detection technology, so as to improve the screening and diagnosis of HCMV in pregnant women and newborns, and reduce the threat of HCMV infection.

Materials and methods

Virus, cells and strains

The HCMV standard strain AD169 and ARPE-19 cells were both purchased from American Type Culture (ATCC). Genomic DNA of other viruses, bacteria, or parasite strains, including Herpes simplex virus (HSV, ATCC VR-2048), Streptococcus pyogenes (ATCC 14,289), Trichomonas vaginalis, Candida albicans (ATCC 10,231), Listeria monocytogenes (ATCC 19,115), Yersinia enterocolitica (ATCC 23,715), Klebsiella Pneumoniae (NCTC 8172), Enterobacter cloacae (BMZ134831), Citrobacter freundii (BMZ122577) Vibrio algornolyficus (ATCC 17,749), Vibrio parahaemolyticus (ATCC 17,802), Salmonella typhimurium (ATCC 14,028), Staphylococcus aureus (ATCC 6538), Pseudomonas aeruginosa (ATCC 27,853) were all confirmed by DNA sequencing and stored in our laboratory.

Ethical statement

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Lianyungang Maternal and Child Health Hospital. The urine samples of pregnant women collected by the obstetrics department were obtained from each patient with their consent and signed an informed consent form.

Virus culture and purification

ARPE-19 cells were cultured in DMEM/F-12 medium (Gibco, USA) containing 10 % fetal bovine serum (Gibco). The freeze-dried HCMV standard strain AD169 was re-suspended with DMEM/F-12 medium and inoculated into ARPE-19 cells. When 80 % of the cells showed lesions, the supernatant was collected by repeated freeze-thaw method, then the virus was enriched by ultra-high speed centrifugation method, and stored at −80°C for later use.

Genomic dna extraction and purification

For pure HCMV standard strain AD169 and artificially contaminated clinical urine samples, purified HCMV genomic DNA and urine HCMV genomic DNA were obtained by referring to the instructions of TIANamp Virus DNA/RNA Kit. For clinical urine samples, EDTA with a final concentration of 5 mM should be added along with HCMV to prevent the urine matrix from degrading HCMV genomic DNA (Guo et al., 2021).

Primer and probe design

The HCMV UL123 target gene sequence was input into the NCBI Prime-BLAST Primer online design website, and the primer and probe parameters were set according to the RPA primer design principles in previous published articles (Zhang et al., 2022). Three primers and one probe were selected for this experiment. Primer sequences in detail information were as follows, HCMV-UL123-F1: 5 '-TTACCAAGAACTCAGCCTTCCCTAAGACCACCA-3′, HCMV-UL123-R1: 5′-GAACTCACTTAAGAGAGAGATGCCCCCGTA-Biotin-3′; HCMV-UL123-F2: 5′-ACCACCAATGGCTGCAGTCAGGCCATGGCGGCA-3′, HCMV-UL123-R2: 5′-CATGTAGTGAGGATATCCATAAATATGTGAT-Biotin-3′; HCMV-UL123-F3: 5′-GGCTAAGGCCCGTGCTAAAAAGGATGAA-3′, HCMV-UL123-R3: 5′-ATAAATATGTGATCAATGTGCGTGAGC-Biotin-3′. Probe sequence information in detail as follows, HCMV-UL123-P: 5′-FITC—CCATGGCGGCACTGCAGAACTTGCCTCA(THF)TGCTCCCCTGATGAGA-C3spacer-3′. In particular, Biotin is added at the 3′ end of the downstream primer. The FITC group antigen was labeled at the 5′ end of the probe, the nucleotide A was replaced by the basic nucleotide analogue THF at the postmidsection position of the probe, and the polymerase extension blocking group C3-spacer was added to the 3′ end of the probe.

RPA-LFS

RPA amplification of the HCMV UL123 gene. The RPA amplification reaction is performed according to the instructions for the TwistAmp nfo kit (TwistDx, UK) as follows. The first step is to configure a rehydration solution with a total volume of 47.5 µL, consisting of 2.1 µL forward primer (10 µM) and reverse primer (10 µM), 0.6 µL probe (10 µM), 29.5 µL rehydration buffer, 1 µL genomic DNA and 12.2 µL ddH2O. The second step is to transfer the step 1 rehydration solution to the reaction pellet containing the core enzyme components of RPA, and fully mix to a uniform RPA reaction suspension. The third step is to add 280 mM magnesium acetate 2.5 µL to the RPA reaction suspension to initiate RPA amplification. In order to ensure the simultaneous reaction, magnesium acetate should be carefully added to the cover of the centrifugation tube containing the RPA reaction suspension. The amplification procedure of each reaction tube was started simultaneously by instantaneous centrifugation. Then transfer to 37°C water bath for 30 min.

LFS detection of the HCMV UL123 gene products amplified by RPA. The RPA amplified products were detected according to the instructions of LFS, as follows. A total of 50 µL RPA amplification product was added into the sample loading well, and let it stand still for 5 min. When both the C control line and the T detection line showed nano gold aggregation or only the T detection line showed nano gold aggregation, the result was judged to be positive. When only the C quality control line showed nano gold aggregation and color development, the result was judged negative. When neither the C quality control line nor the T detection line has nano gold aggregation and color development, the result is invalid.

Optimization of RPA reaction conditions

Reaction temperature optimization. The RPA reaction system was configured, and the reaction was carried out in the water bath at 15, 20, 25, 30, 37, 39, 42 and 45°C for 30 min, respectively. The color aggregation depth of T test line was compared by LFS detection, and the optimal temperature of RPA reaction was selected. Reaction time optimization. The RPA reaction system was configured, and the optimal reaction temperature was selected for 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40 min, respectively, and the optimal time of RPA reaction was selected.

Interspecific analysis

The genomic DNA of HCMV standard strain AD169, HSV, S. pyogenes, T. vaginalis, C. albicans, L. monocytogenes, Y. enterocolitica, K. Pneumoniae, E. cloacae, C. freundii, V. alginnolyfificus, V. parahaemolyticus, S. typhimurium, S. aureus and P. aeruginosa were respectively used for the specificity analysis of RPA-LFS detection system for HCMV UL123 gene.

Preparation of standard products and standard curve drawing

UL123 gene sequence was amplified by Pfu DNA polymerase using HCMV standard strain AD169 as the genomic DNA template. Primer sequence information is as follows: HCMV-UL123-F: 5′-TTACTGGTCAGCCTTGCTTCTA, HCMV-UL123-R: 5′-TCAAACAGATTAAGGT. The PCR products were purified by agarose gel electrophoresis and gelatinized, then cloned into pMD18T vector by TA cloning. DNA linking products were transformed into clonal receptor cells DH5a, coated on LB AGAR plate, and DNA sequencing was performed for single colonies. The plasmid identified correctly was named pMD18T-HCMV-UL123 and used as the standard in this experiment.

Quantification of pMD18T-HCMV-UL123 plasmid by Qubit 4 fluorometer, the formula copies/µL = [6.02 × 1023*(DNA concentration) ng/uL*10−9]/(Sequence length*660) is used to calculate the number of UL123 gene copies of the pMD18T-HCMV-UL123 standard. The standard pMD18T-HCMV-UL123 was diluted with ddH2O by 10-fold gradient (from 3 × 108 copies/µL to 3 × 102 copies/µL) and amplified by real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR to establish the standard curve using the obtained ct value.

Analysis of the minimum detection limit

Using the genomic DNA of HCMV standard strain AD169 as the template, quantitative fluorescent PCR analysis was performed and the HCMV copy number was quantitatively analyzed, and the concentration was adjusted to 3 × 104 copies/µL. RPA-LFS detection was performed on HCMV genomic DNA with different concentrations of HCMV between 3 × 104 copies/µL and 3 × 10−1 copies/µL after 10-fold gradient dilution. The lowest detection limit of the established RPA-LFs-based HCMV detection method was analyzed.

Preparation of artificially contaminated urine samples

A total of 30 HCMV-free urine samples were collected clinically. A volume of 180 µL urine was taken and EDTA was added until the final concentration was up to 5 mM. The purified HCMV genomic DNA was amplified by real-time fluorescent quantitative PCR, and the ct value was added to the standard curve to calculate the copy number. Each urine sample was mixed with 20 µL HCMV genomic DNA (about 3 × 103 copies). Genomic DNA was extracted from all samples after mixing, and was used for subsequent fluorescence quantitative PCR and RPA-LFS detection.

Real-time fluorescent quantitative PCR

With reference to previous published articles, similar to this study, fluorescence quantitative PCR primers for IE1 gene were synthesized. The details are as follows. HCMV-IE1-F: 5′-AGCGCCGCATTGAGGA2806–3′, HCMV-IE1-R: 5′−2833CAGACTCTC AGAGGATCGGCC2853–3′ (Leruez-Ville et al., 2003). According to AceQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix, the reaction system and procedure of quantitative fluorescence PCR were configured. In detail, a 20 µL reaction system consisting of 10 µL 2 × AceQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix, 0.4 µL HCMV-IE1-F (10 µM), 0.4 µL HCMV-IE1-R (10 µM), 1 µL genomic DNA template, 8.2 µL ddH2O. The reaction procedure was as follows: 95°C pre-denaturation treatment for 5 min, followed by 40 cycles of amplification reaction, including 95°C denaturation treatment for 10 s and 60°C annealing/extension treatment for 30 s, and finally the dissolution curve was set as the default parameter of the instrument. Fluorescence quantitative PCR was performed on ABI7500 instrument.

Consistency evaluation

The genomic DNA of 30 artificially contaminated urine samples and 127 clinically collected urine samples of pregnant women were used as templates for detection by RPA-LFS method and real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR, respectively. The detection results of the two methods were compared, and the coincidence rate of the two methods was calculated.

Discussion

Congenital and perinatal CMV infection is a major cause of birth defects in newborns, often resulting in severe central nervous system malformations. The fatality rate of CMV infection is as high as about 10 %, only 10 % to 15 % of fetuses infected with the virus show obvious symptoms of infection, more than 90 % of newborns may be born with recessive infection, do not show obvious signs of viral infection. HCMV can survive in its host for a long time by establishing an environment suitable for its growth. Under certain conditions, the virus can replicate and release rapidly (Jean Beltran and Cristea, 2014; Collins-McMillen et al., 2018; Crough and Khanna, 2009; Herbein, 2018; Johnson et al., 2018). In general, HCMV infection in healthy adults is usually asymptomatic and can be activated from a latent state once a major change in the body's immune function occurs (such as organ transplantation, HIV infection, pregnancy, or tumor), leading to primary or secondary proliferating infections that can lead to serious or even fatal disease. Symptoms include graft failure, restenosis, arteriosclerosis and death (Christensen-Quick et al., 2017; Görzer et al., 2010; Lischka and Zimmermann, 2008). A pregnant woman infected with HCMV is transmitted to the fetus or newborn through placenta, cervical secretions, blood or breast milk, resulting in miscarriage or the birth of a baby with developmental defects (Griffiths et al., 2015; Mazzitelli et al., 2017; El Helou and Razonable, 2019). Therefore, this study will focus on the potential risk of HCMV in pregnant women and newborns, aiming to establish a rapid, accurate, simple and low dependence on equipment rapid diagnosis method for HCMV, and provide feasible strategies for meeting various clinical HCMV detection scenarios.

A variety of laboratory-based rapid diagnostic methods for HCMV have been reported successively, including serological detection based on strong immunogenic antigen, immunological detection based on flow cytometry, and molecular biological detection based on PCR. The early serological detection method is to determine the presence or absence of HCMV infection by detecting the level of HCMV IgM antibody in the body (Schmidt et al., 1995). However, IgM antibody can be detected only 2 weeks after the appearance of clinical symptoms, and early diagnosis is often misjudged as negative, so this method is not universally applicable. Antibody capture ELISA based on strong immunogenic antigens such as pp65 (ppUL83) is considered the preferred method for laboratory diagnosis because it can be expressed in white blood cells early after infection and persist during infection, and is positively correlated with virus titers (Burbelo et al., 2009; Tanaka et al., 2002). However, this antibody capture ELISA method requires specific separation of white blood cells, and the detection result depends on the antibody concentration in the white blood cell sample to be measured, which not only increases the complexity of the operation process, but also prone to missed detection due to the low virus titer. Poirier-Toulemonde et al. used pp65 antibody to specifically capture HCMV to detect HCMV infection in Solid Organ and Stem Cell Transplant Recipients by flow cytometry (Poirier-Toulemonde et al., 2000). The feasibility of flow cytometry in the diagnosis of HCMV was confirmed. However, this method not only has the influence of antibody concentration on the sensitivity of the assay, but also relies on large flow cytometry to complete the assay. PCR-based molecular biological detection method can effectively avoid the problem of low sensitivity. NITSCHE et al. established a rapid diagnostic method for the major immediate-early region of CMV (pMIE) gene based on TaqMan real-time quantitative PCR (Nitsche et al., 2000). To detect HCMV virus content in plasma samples of bone marrow transplant (BMT) patients. Although some portable fluorescence detection equipment can be used to monitor the fluorescence signal in the amplification cycle, reducing the dependence on the equipment, the compatibility of real-time quantitative PCR to the purity of the template is poor. The authors tried to optimize the primers, probes, magnesium ions. The universality of the method was improved by improving the purity of DNA in the samples. The quantitative analysis for HCMV based on the digital PCR method was carried out by Bogožalec Košir et al. (2020). Similarly, the high dependence of this method on precision equipment is still not universally adaptable and cannot be completed in the context of grassroots laboratory and point-of-care detection.

Although RPA technology is not approved by the FDA, no matter from the number of primer pairs required for amplification, whether the initial start-up needs heating, reaction temperature, reaction time and other aspects, Compared with specific RNA dependent Nucleic Acid Sequence-Based Amplification, specific DNA dependent Strand Displacement Amplification, Loop Mediated Isothermal Amplification, Helicase-Dependent Amplification, and Rolling Circle Amplification are isothermal amplification methods that are compatible with DNA/RNA template. RPA is compatible with DNA and RNA template. The whole amplification reaction can be completed in about 30 min with only one pair of primers, eliminating the initial heating step, showing significant advantages (Craw and Balachandran, 2012; Yan et al., 2014; Deng and Gao, 2015). In recent years, RPA technology has been used in a variety of pathogen diagnosis, and this study applied RPA technology to achieve the amplification of HCMV target genes. We did extensive literature research on the initial HCMV target gene selection. The whole genome of HCMV is about 230 kb long, contains 192 open reading frames, and encodes up to 200 proteins. Although HCMV can release some of the inner membrane proteins embedded in the virus particles after invasion of the host, the transcription of the gene depends on the regulation of RNA polymerase of the host. These genes are classified into immediate early (IE), early (E) and late (L) genes according to their function at different stages of infection. IE is the first gene activated after HCMV invasion of the host, and begins to express and continue to express at 1 h after infection. It is the first gene for early molecular diagnosis. IE genes include MIE genes (UL122/123) and helper genes (IRS1/TRS1, UL36–38, UL115–119, US3). UL123 (IE1) is a key gene in CMV replication, and is closely related to the regulation of infection, latency and recurrence of CMV. Because UL123 gene is conserved in all CMV strains and is significantly different from other herpes viruses, it can be used as an ideal target gene for detection of CMV. This gene was also used by Leruez-Ville et al. (2003) to establish a Real-Time PCR molecular detection method for HCMV diagnosis, which has high reliability and feasibility of implementation. In this study, by comparing UL123 gene conserved region, the nucleotide sequence for specific amplification of HCMV was designed according to the principles of RPA primer and probe design, and the sensitivity and specificity of selecting this region for amplification were confirmed by RAA assay and RPA-LFS assay.

CMV mainly infects endothelial cells and epithelial cells, and CMV infection is species-specific (Revello and Gerna, 2010). Therefore, the HCMV purchased in this study was mainly enriched by ARPE-19 (human retinal epithelial cells) to extract genomic DNA for the detection of the template established by the method. HCMV DNA testing relies primarily on the primary method of neonatal urine screening (Yamaguchi et al., 2017). In our hospital, because newborns are often equipped with diapers, the applied research after the establishment of this study method did not use the urine of newborns collected clinically, but artificially mixed with different concentrations of HCMV genomic DNA in the urine samples collected by the physical examination center, and the application evaluation of the detection method established in this study was conducted. However, the purity of HCMV genomic DNA extracted from the samples remains a challenge in molecular diagnosis, whether clinical collection of neonatal urine samples or the use of artificially mixed HCMV genomic DNA urine samples. The presence of matrix residue in urine can inhibit the DNA polymerase amplification reaction dependent on metal ions, and has a great influence on the accuracy of some samples (Kishi et al., 2001). One after another, some studies have proposed solutions. Guo et al. (2021) reported that different concentrations of EDTA were added to urine samples, and then real-time fluorescent quantitative PCR amplification was carried out after the viral genome DNA extraction kit was applied, and it was confirmed that when EDTA concentration of no less than 5 mM was added to urine samples, the results showed that the concentration of EDTA in urine samples could be improved. It can effectively inhibit the inhibitory effect of urine matrix on DNA polymerase amplification. This provides a reference for DNA purification in this study. The initiation of the RPA amplification reaction depends on the addition of magnesium ions. Although it has been reported that RPA can withstand the influence of magazines in rough-made plates, different from PCR or qPCR experimental systems, the inhibitory effect of urine matrix on RPA reaction was still tested at the beginning of the establishment of this study, and RPA can withstand the interference of most urine samples. The addition of 1, 2, 5 and 10 mM EDTA confirmed that the addition of 2 mM EDTA could partially reverse the false-negative results of RPA amplification, and the addition of 5 mM EDTA could completely reverse the false-negative results of RPA amplification (data not shown). Therefore, the genomes of clinical urine samples used in this study were all tested by EDTA addition before purification.

Two issues should be highlighted here. The first one is aerosol contamination. It is an important problem in molecular detection, and RPA amplification is no exception. Therefore, according to the standard molecular diagnosis method, we need to make partitions for different stages of the experiment, namely, sample preparation area, RPA system configuration area, detection area, etc. At the same time, nucleic acid removal should be done at each test interval. Based on this, the aerosol pollution problem detected by RPA-LFS can be avoided to the maximum extent. Regarding the second temperature control issue, good results can be obtained within the range of 30 °C to 45 °C, and the best temperature is 37 °C, which can be completed by simple and portable temperature control devices such as water baths and metal baths, and even the heating of body temperature may achieve isothermal expansion. In order to optimize the results of the test, we recommend the use of constant temperature control equipment.

Conclusion

In this study, a POCT diagnostic method for HCMV in pregnant women's urine was successfully established based on RPA isothermal amplification and LFS technology. The method targeted the detection of UL123 gene encoding IE1, a gene immediately expressed in the early stage of CMV, so as to screen for early HCMV infection. The entire detection process only required 15 min of HCMV target gene enrichment at a constant temperature of 37°C and 5 min of LFS visual result reading, and a minimum of 30 copies of CMV genomic DNA could be detected without interference from other pathogens in urine. The consistency of urine CMV detection method established in this study was as high as 96.67 % with fluorescence quantitative detection method in 30 artificially contaminated urine samples and 100 % in 127 clinically collected urine samples, which has high reliability. The low dependence on equipment, highly specific, rapid and sensitive POCT CMV detection method developed in this study makes CMV screening possible in grassroots laboratories and remote areas.

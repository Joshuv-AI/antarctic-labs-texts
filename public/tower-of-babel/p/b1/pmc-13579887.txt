Payer-perspective direct medical costs during observed postoperative follow-up after gonioscopy-assisted transluminal trabeculotomy versus mitomycin-augmented trabeculectomy in Turkey
Authors: Cansu Yuksel Elgin, Mustafa Asim Erol
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGlaucoma surgery has payer-budget implications when postoperative medication and procedure use differ between care pathways. This study compared direct medical costs after gonioscopy-assisted transluminal trabeculotomy (GATT) and mitomycin-C-augmented trabeculectomy (MMC-trabeculectomy) from the Turkish Social Security Institution (SGK) payer perspective.MethodsThis single-centre retrospective cohort used patient-level chart, operating-room, and medication-cost data from sequentially reviewed surgery-naive adults with primary open-angle or pseudoexfoliation glaucoma who underwent GATT (n = 40) or MMC-trabeculectomy (n = 20). Surgical outcome was not an enrollment criterion. Costs were valued in 2025 Turkish Lira using SUT tariffs and the 11 June 2025 SGK drug price list. The primary base-case cost included the index glaucoma-surgery tariff, postoperative ophthalmology visits, dispensed glaucoma medications, and glaucoma-related non-routine postoperative procedures. Routine optical coherence tomography and visual-field testing were excluded from the primary surgical cost comparison and reported descriptively. Patient-level Gamma generalized linear models with log link estimated procedure-associated cost ratios.ResultsMean observed postoperative follow-up was 11.45 ± 8.28 months after GATT (range 2–30) and 13.85 ± 6.91 months after MMC-trabeculectomy (range 3–25). Mean primary base-case direct medical cost was 3188.6 ± 1955.9 TRY after GATT and 6835.8 ± 4674.5 TRY after MMC-trabeculectomy. Medication cost was the largest incremental driver (924.0 vs. 3305.7 TRY per patient), accounting for 65.3% of the mean difference. Glaucoma-related non-routine procedure costs were 0.0 TRY per GATT patient and 271.1 TRY per MMC-trabeculectomy patient. In the fully adjusted Gamma model, MMC-trabeculectomy was associated with higher mean primary base-case cost (cost ratio 2.01; 95% CI, 1.50–2.70).ConclusionsIn this Turkish SGK payer-perspective analysis, MMC-trabeculectomy was associated with higher observed direct medical costs than GATT during available postoperative follow-up. The findings are limited by the retrospective single-centre design, imbalanced groups, nonrandomized surgical selection, no propensity-score matching or inverse-probability weighting, and short follow-up for long-term durability. They should not be interpreted as a long-term cost-utility analysis or causal evidence of economic superiority.Trial registrationNot applicable.Keywords: Glaucoma, Gonioscopy-assisted transluminal trabeculotomy, Trabeculectomy, Cost analysis, Payer perspective, Turkey, Direct medical costs, Generalized linear model

Introduction

Glaucoma is a leading cause of irreversible visual impairment and generates sustained costs for health systems through outpatient follow-up, diagnostic monitoring, topical medications, and surgical or laser procedures. Costs tend to increase with disease severity and with the intensity and duration of medication required to maintain target intraocular pressure (IOP) [1, 2].

Minimally invasive and conjunctiva-sparing glaucoma procedures are heterogeneous. Some involve device costs, whereas instrument- or suture-based angle surgery can have modest consumable requirements. Health-technology assessments have emphasized that economic conclusions for minimally invasive glaucoma surgery are context-specific and sensitive to payer perspective, prices, and time horizon [3, 4].

Gonioscopy-assisted transluminal trabeculotomy (GATT) is an ab interno circumferential trabeculotomy of Schlemm canal. It is conjunctiva-sparing and has been associated with clinically meaningful IOP reduction and reduced medication burden in open-angle glaucoma series [5]. Trabeculectomy with mitomycin-C remains a reference filtration procedure, especially where very low target IOPs are required, but early postoperative bleb management and additional procedures can increase resource use. Long-horizon economic models for trabeculectomy show that conclusions depend strongly on time horizon and outcome framework [6].

This study provides a focused payer-perspective direct-cost comparison of GATT and MMC-trabeculectomy in Turkey. The analysis uses patient-level resource-use data, Turkish Social Security Institution (SGK) Health Implementation Guide (SUT) tariffs, and official drug prices in a fixed 2025 price year. It does not estimate quality-adjusted life-years or lifetime cost-effectiveness. The objective is to describe observed direct medical costs during available postoperative follow-up and to identify the main cost drivers in a real-world tertiary-care cohort.

Methods

Study design and population

This retrospective cohort study was conducted at a single tertiary ophthalmology centre in Turkey. Patient-level data were extracted from electronic health records, operating room records, and institutional medication-cost records. Charts were reviewed sequentially within each surgical group during the institutional chart-review period. The final analytic cohort comprised surgery-naive adult patients with primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) or pseudoexfoliation glaucoma (PEXG) who underwent GATT or MMC-trabeculectomy and had complete costable resource-use records. Surgical success, failure, postoperative medication response, or procedure-related complications were not used as enrollment criteria.

Several limitations should be emphasized. First, the study is retrospective, single-centre, and based on a modest imbalanced sample (GATT n = 40; MMC-trabeculectomy n = 20). Procedure selection was not randomized and may reflect clinical judgment, disease severity not fully captured in the dataset, target IOP, ocular characteristics, calendar period, and surgeon-related preference or expertise. Propensity-score matching and inverse-probability weighting were not performed, so residual confounding and unmeasured selection or measurement bias remain possible [7, 8].

Patients with previous incisional glaucoma surgery in the study eye or incomplete cost records were excluded. Because the study was designed as a real-world cost analysis rather than a long-term efficacy study, patients were not required to have complete 24-month observed follow-up. Observed follow-up duration was reported explicitly, included in adjusted models, and explored through annualized and ≥ 6-month sensitivity analyses.

Reporting was guided by STROBE for observational studies, RECORD for studies using routinely collected data, and CHEERS 2022 principles for transparent reporting of economic evaluations [9–11].

Procedures

The GATT arm consisted of ab interno circumferential trabeculotomy of Schlemm canal performed under gonioscopic visualization using a suture-assisted technique. The conjunctiva was spared. The comparator arm consisted of trabeculectomy augmented with intraoperative mitomycin-C, with postoperative bleb management according to institutional practice.

Laser suture lysis, suture manipulation, needling, and 5-fluorouracil injections after trabeculectomy were classified as routine bleb titration/bleb management rather than non-routine re-interventions, because they are commonly embedded in postoperative trabeculectomy care and were not assigned separate procedure costs in the primary base-case. Anterior chamber lavage, anterior chamber viscoelastic/revision procedures, anterior vitrectomy, cyclodestructive treatment, and repeat glaucoma surgery were classified as glaucoma-related non-routine postoperative procedures when recorded as costable events. Phaco-IOL and YAG/photocoagulation-type procedures were excluded from the primary glaucoma-surgery base-case and shown only in a secondary all-recorded-ocular-procedure scenario.

Costing perspective and cost components

Costs were evaluated from the SGK payer perspective and expressed in 2025 TRY. Clinical services were valued using SUT tariffs, and medications were valued using the official SGK drug price list dated 11 June 2025. The primary base-case cost per patient was calculated as: index glaucoma-surgery tariff + postoperative ophthalmology visit costs + dispensed glaucoma medication costs + glaucoma-related non-routine postoperative procedure costs. Routine OCT and visual-field testing were excluded from the primary surgical cost comparison because routine glaucoma monitoring should not determine the economic comparison of two procedures unless performed to evaluate procedure-specific complications. These tests are reported descriptively.

Medication utilization was recorded as cumulative dispensed boxes during postoperative follow-up and also as the average number of distinct antiglaucoma medication molecules used. Thus, the cumulative box count is not the number of concurrent medications used at a single visit.

Outcomes and statistical analysis

The primary outcome was patient-level primary base-case direct medical cost. Secondary outcomes included cost components, cumulative dispensed medication boxes, postoperative medication molecule count, visits, OCT and visual-field utilization, routine bleb-titration events, glaucoma-related non-routine procedures, and a secondary all-recorded-ocular-procedure cost scenario.

Continuous variables are reported as mean ± standard deviation and, for skewed costs, medians with interquartile ranges. Categorical variables are reported as counts and percentages. Two-sided P values were used; P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant and P ≥ 0.05 was considered not statistically significant. Interpretation emphasized effect sizes and 95% confidence intervals rather than P values alone.

Patient-level cost models were estimated using Gamma GLMs with log link because direct medical costs were positive and right-skewed [2]. The main exposure was procedure type, with GATT as the reference. Models were estimated as procedure-only, follow-up-adjusted, and covariate-adjusted specifications. The fully adjusted model included procedure group, observed follow-up duration, age, sex, glaucoma subtype, and baseline topical medication molecule count. Log-normal models were used as sensitivity analyses. Additional sensitivity analyses included an analysis restricted to patients with at least six months of observed postoperative follow-up and a secondary all-recorded-ocular-procedure cost definition. These models quantify associations, not causal treatment effects.

Primary base-case direct medical cost components

The mean incremental primary base-case difference was 3,647.2 TRY per patient. Medication costs accounted for approximately 65.3% of this difference, the index surgical tariff for 21.9%, glaucoma-related non-routine postoperative procedures for 7.4%, and visits for 5.3%. Thus, medication costs were the dominant driver of the observed cost difference.

Patient-level GLM and sensitivity analyses

Table 6 presents patient-level GLM results. Patient-level Gamma GLMs consistently showed higher observed direct medical costs after MMC-trabeculectomy. In the procedure-only model, the mean cost ratio was 2.14 (95% CI, 1.51–3.03). After adjustment for observed follow-up duration, the ratio was 1.95 (95% CI, 1.42–2.67). In the fully adjusted model including follow-up duration, age, sex, glaucoma subtype, and baseline medication burden, the ratio was 2.01 (95% CI, 1.50–2.70).

Annualized primary base-case costs were 5,152.1 ± 3,800.3 TRY per patient-year after GATT and 8,204.7 ± 8,313.7 TRY per patient-year after MMC-trabeculectomy. This annualized analysis was supportive but less precise, reflecting high variability among patients with short follow-up. Restricting the analysis to patients with at least six months of observed follow-up (GATT n = 28; MMC-trabeculectomy n = 16) produced a fully adjusted Gamma cost ratio of 1.87 (95% CI, 1.31–2.67).

In a secondary all-recorded-ocular-procedure scenario that also included phaco-IOL and YAG/photocoagulation-type procedures, mean cost was 3,252.9 ± 1,998.8 TRY after GATT and 7,159.6 ± 4,621.7 TRY after MMC-trabeculectomy. The fully adjusted Gamma cost ratio in this secondary scenario was 2.06 (95% CI, 1.54–2.76).

Discussion

This patient-level payer-perspective analysis found that MMC-trabeculectomy was associated with higher observed direct medical costs than GATT in a Turkish tertiary-care cohort. The corrected primary base-case deliberately excludes routine glaucoma monitoring tests and separates glaucoma-related non-routine procedures from broader ocular procedures. Under this framework, the main cost driver was postoperative medication expenditure, with smaller contributions from the higher index trabeculectomy tariff, glaucoma-related non-routine procedures, and visit costs.

The exclusion of routine OCT and visual-field testing from the primary base-case is methodologically important. These tests are part of standard glaucoma follow-up and should generally be balanced across treatment arms or excluded from procedure-specific cost comparisons unless performed to evaluate procedure-specific complications. Reporting them descriptively preserves transparency without allowing differential routine monitoring intensity to determine the surgical cost conclusion.

The findings are consistent with the clinical logic that procedures reducing chronic drop burden may reduce short-term payer costs. Turkish data indicate that adherence to glaucoma medication can be suboptimal, making medication burden clinically and economically relevant [12]. Nevertheless, medication savings should not be overinterpreted as proof of long-term superiority. Trabeculectomy may remain clinically preferable in patients requiring very low target IOPs, and long-horizon economic conclusions can differ from short-term budget-impact estimates [6].

Recent head-to-head clinical studies provide useful context. Wang et al. compared GATT and trabeculectomy in POAG over 12 months and reported broadly comparable early clinical outcomes [13]. Gunay et al. compared GATT and trabeculectomy in advanced pseudoexfoliation glaucoma and highlighted that trabeculectomy may achieve very low target IOPs more reliably in some settings [14]. These data support a nuanced interpretation: GATT may be economically attractive in selected patients, especially when reducing medication burden is valuable, whereas trabeculectomy remains important for advanced disease and very low target pressures.

The policy implication is bounded rather than universal. For SGK and hospital budget planning, local tariff-based analyses can identify where costs accrue and which components are modifiable. In this cohort, the incremental payer cost was driven mainly by medications rather than routine monitoring tests. Surgical decision-making should remain clinically individualized and should consider target IOP, disease severity, conjunctival status, surgeon expertise, and the possibility of late failure or subsequent surgery.

Limitations

Several limitations should be emphasized. First, the study is retrospective, single-centre, and based on a modest imbalanced sample (GATT n = 40; MMC-trabeculectomy n = 20). Procedure selection was not randomized and may reflect clinical judgment, disease severity not fully captured in the dataset, target IOP, ocular characteristics, calendar period, and surgeon-related preference or expertise. Propensity-score matching and inverse-probability weighting were not performed, so residual confounding and unmeasured selection or measurement bias remain possible [7, 8].

Second, observed follow-up was variable and short for many patients. The minimum observed follow-up was 2 months in the GATT group and 3 months in the MMC-trabeculectomy group, and mean follow-up was shorter than 24 months in both groups. These data are therefore insufficient for judging the long-term durability and lifetime costs of either IOP-lowering operation. Follow-up-adjusted, annualized, and ≥ 6-month sensitivity analyses reduce but cannot eliminate bias if late failures, late reoperations, or medication changes occur non-linearly over time.

Third, the perspective is limited to direct medical costs borne by the Turkish public payer. The analysis does not include out-of-pocket costs, patient travel, productivity losses, caregiver time, quality of life, utilities, or cost per QALY. It is therefore a short-term direct-cost comparison, not a cost-utility analysis.

Fourth, tariffs and list prices may not equal true hospital opportunity costs or patient-level expenditures. SUT tariffs and SGK drug prices were used because they are the relevant payer-facing prices and are transparent, but they may not capture confidential procurement variation or future price changes. Results may not generalize to other countries, private payer settings, centres with different surgical protocols, or teams with different levels of GATT and trabeculectomy experience.

Fifth, procedure classification affects cost attribution. Needling, laser suture lysis, and 5-fluorouracil bleb titration were classified as routine bleb management rather than non-routine re-intervention, consistent with trabeculectomy postoperative care. Cataract/YAG procedures were excluded from the primary glaucoma-surgery base-case and reported only in a secondary all-ocular scenario. Transient postoperative hyphema/microhyphema was documented in 0/40 GATT eyes in the extracted postoperative dataset, and no GATT eye had a billable anterior chamber washout/lavage recorded. Because the study was retrospective, very mild self-limiting anterior-chamber blood that was not documented as a postoperative finding could not be separately analyzed.

Finally, although patient-level Gamma and log-normal models were estimated, the analysis remains observational. The models quantify associations between procedure type and observed costs after adjustment for available covariates, but they do not establish causality. Multicentre prospective studies with longer follow-up, predefined clinical endpoints, utility measurement, and propensity-weighted or otherwise causally oriented analyses are needed to test whether the short-term cost pattern persists.

Conclusions

In this single-centre retrospective cohort valued using 2025 Turkish SGK/SUT tariffs and official SGK drug prices, MMC-trabeculectomy was associated with higher observed direct medical costs than GATT during available postoperative follow-up. The corrected primary base-case excludes routine OCT and visual-field testing and shows that the incremental difference was driven mainly by postoperative medication costs, with smaller contributions from the index surgical tariff, glaucoma-related non-routine procedures, and visits.

These findings should be interpreted within strict boundaries: Turkish SGK payer perspective, direct medical costs only, imbalanced nonrandomized groups, no propensity-score matching or inverse-probability weighting, and insufficient follow-up to judge long-term surgical durability or lifetime cost-effectiveness. GATT may offer a favourable short-term cost profile in selected patients where reducing medication burden is clinically appropriate, but the analysis does not establish causal or definitive economic superiority over trabeculectomy.

Isavuconazole: Need for therapeutic drug monitoring and CYP polymorphism testing
Authors: Rajeev Soman, Sanraksha Mayya, Rahul Doshi, Inderdeep Singh, Sushrut Ganpule
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this patient of invasive sinusitis due to Rhizopus arrhizus, Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) showed a high trough level (88mg/L) of isavuconazole on standard doses. Genotyping revealed CYP3A5∗3/∗3 polymorphism which indicated poor metabolism of isavuconazole. Switching to posaconazole was attempted but there was intolerance to posaconazole. To our knowledge, this is the first patient in whom both TDM and CYP genotyping were used to plan very low and infrequent doses of isavuconazole.Keywords: Keyword_1 mucormycosis, Keyword_2 isavuconazole, Keyword_3therapeutic drug monitoring, Keyword_4 CYP3A5 polymorphism

Introduction

Isavuconazole is used in the treatment of mucormycosis either alone or in combination with liposomal amphotericin B [1]. Therapeutic drug monitoring(TDM) of isavuconazole for treatment of mucormycosis ensures high plasma concentration for optimum penetration to infection site and avoids toxicity. This case report highlights the importance of therapeutic drug monitoring(TDM) of isavuconazole, even though it is not routinely recommended [2], along with the need for genetic testing for CYP polymorphisms if the levels are found to be too high.

Case presentation

A 55-year-old female patient with bronchial asthma and Sjogren's syndrome had three recent hospital admissions elsewhere for acute exacerbations of asthma, received high doses of systemic steroids and had steroid induced hyperglycemia. During the last admission elsewhere (date of admission December 1, 2024) HRCT thorax had only faint ground glass opacities without nodules in bilateral lung parenchyma. Serum galactomannan test was found to be positive with an optical density index (ODI) of 1.13 and serum 1,3 beta-D-glucan (BDG) was negative. She had received several antibiotics at the time. Nevertheless, she had received four weeks of voriconazole when she presented to us with severe right sided frontal headache on December 26, 2024 (Day 0).

CT imaging showed extensive mucosal thickening in the right frontal, maxillary and bilateral ethmoid sinuses (Fig. 1) and emphysematous changes in both lungs without nodules. She was taken up for a diagnostic nasal endoscopy and functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) (D+2). Necrotic tissue and pus were found and removed from the right sided sinuses. KOH mount showed broad, aseptate fungal hyphae. Fungal culture grew a mould (Fig. 2) with morphological features of Rhizopus arrhizus like greyish-black columella along with umbrella like sporangia that have collapsed after spore release. Additional features like nodal rhizoids and lemon shaped sporangiospores that were present, are not shown in the figure. The identity of the fungus was confirmed by Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption/Ionization Time-of-Flight Mass Spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS). It was not possible to have anti-fungal susceptibility testing of the isolate. Liposomal amphotericin B(L-AMB) 3mg/kg once daily was started (Day+3) along with intravenous isavuconazole 200 mg thrice daily for two days, followed by 200 mg orally once daily, which were continued along with a tight control of hyperglycaemia. However, Amphotericin B had to be discontinued due to nausea, vomiting, abdominal discomfort, rising serum creatinine from 1.5 mg/dl to 3.5 mg/dl and declining hemoglobin from 9 gm/dl to 7 gm/dl (Day+10) and isavuconazole was continued.

Isavuconazole trough level (by Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry method) on the standard 200mg/day oral dose that was obtained (D+14) was 88mg/L! The patient had nausea, vomiting, abdominal discomfort that could have been due to toxicity of amphotericin B or isavuconazole or of both. Isavuconazole was withheld temporarily (D+16) and restarted at a smaller dose of 100mg/day (D+20). The level was 38mg/L when repeated on (D+20). Suspecting a slow metabolizer status, a pharmacogenomics panel by next generation sequencing (NGS) was sent for. Paired End Sequencing was performed with 2x100/2x150 chemistry, on an Illumina platform. Reads were assembled and were aligned to reference sequences based on NCBI RefSeq transcripts and human genome build GRCh37/UCSC hg19. Sequence and copy number variants were reported according to the Human Genome Variation Society (HGVS). The report that was received on (D25) showed that the patient had a CYP3A5∗3/∗3 genotype which is associated with poor metabolism of isavuconazole.

Isavuconazole was stopped on the same day and the patient was switched to posaconazole which is primarily metabolized by UDP glucuronyl transferase (UGT1A4) and very minimally by the cytochrome P450 system [3]. The oral, gastro-resistant formulation at a dose of 300 mg twice daily on the first day followed by 300 mg once daily was used. The patient had severe intractable vomiting and abdominal discomfort due to posaconazole and it was stopped on (D+25). The isavuconazole level obtained after two weeks of stopping it (D+40) was 2.8mg/L. Hence it was planned to dose isavuconazole at 100mg once weekly or even less frequently, to maintain a level of 3.5 mg/L. Unfortunately, the relatives decided to limit further investigations and treatment, the patient was transferred to a small facility (D+45) and no subsequent follow-up was possible.

Discussion

The initial diagnosis of invasive pulmonary aspergillosis was doubtful as there were no radiological features as required by the EORTC/MSGERC criteria. Besides, serum galactomannan could be falsely positive due to use of generic antibiotics. Prior voriconazole exposure is known to be an independent risk factor for developing mucormycosis which was confirmed in this case [4].

Although not recommended by guidelines [2], a combination of LAMB and azole (like isavuconazole) has shown the least mortality of 6.6 % in a recent retrospective analysis and is being increasingly used in practice [1]. Isavuconazole is non inferior to amphotericin B for treatment of mucormycosis [5], is less toxic and exposures that are obtained by standard dosing are considered adequate for treatment. Although routine therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) has not been recommended [6,7], we have found that TDM guided higher doses can enhance the therapeutic effect of isavuconazole for difficult to treat Covid Associated Cerebral Mucormycosis to ensure high plasma exposures and enhanced penetration to the site of infection [8,9]. It can also be useful to identify isavuconazole toxicity which is known to present with transaminitis(29 %), diarrhoea(24.3 %) and nausea(18.9 %) and is associated with a serum isavuconazole level of 5.86 μg/mL or higher [10]. The low and infrequent doses that were planned for this patient would have been guided with TDM to target a level of 3.5 mg/L that has been found to be associated with no adverse events [10].

To our knowledge this is the first case where extremely high levels of isavuconazole were found and were attributable to CYP3A5 polymorphism. Very low and less frequent dosing guided by TDM appeared to be the only clinical option in this patient, because of intolerance to other drugs. This case also illustrates that repeated investigations like drug levels, adjustment of doses and changes in anti-fungal drugs are difficult in resource-limited settings and hamper the assessment and continued management of the patient with full scientific rigour.

Conclusion

A greater use of isavuconazole is anticipated for treatment of invasive aspergillosis, invasive mucormycosis and even for prophylaxis in high-risk situations. Clinicians will therefore need to use TDM for optimal use of this drug. In an occasional patient with supra-therapeutic levels, genetic testing for CYP polymorphisms may have to be performed to aid clinical decisions.

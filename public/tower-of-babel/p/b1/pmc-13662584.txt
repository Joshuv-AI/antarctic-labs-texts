5-HT2A receptor in medial prefrontal cortex mediates pain-related empathic responses in rats
Authors: Wen-Jun Luo, Rui Du, Chun-Li Li, Yan Wang, Yang Yu, Shuang-Chan Wu, Jun Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Using a dyadic social interaction (DSI) paradigm, we validated a scenario of pain-related empathic responses in rats and mice, during which socially transferred pain was identified in naïve observers spontaneously engaging in caring behavior (allolicking and allogrooming, ALAG) toward a familiar pain sufferer. Although 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT) and its receptor subtype 5-HT2AR are major players in mammalian social behaviors, whether they have specific modulatory actions in these processes remains unknown. Here, by genetic and pharmacological depletion of central 5-HT, we demonstrated that observers lacking 5-HT showed deficiencies not only in empathic responses but also in sniffing, huddling, and self-grooming during DSI with a familiar pain sufferer. However, bilateral intra-mPFC microinjections of M100907, a selective 5-HT2AR antagonist, or downregulation of 5-HT2AR expression in the bilateral mPFC, selectively inhibited socially transferred pain and ALAG without affecting other behaviors in observers. Moreover, systemic or intra-mPFC administration of 25CN-NBOH, a high-affinity and selective 5-HT2AR agonist, selectively promoted empathic responses in observers toward a stranger pain sufferer in an antagonist-reversible manner, a phenomenon rarely observed under the naïve-pain DSI paradigm. Finally, 5-HT2AR downregulation in the bilateral mPFC abolished the empathic responsiveness induced by 25CN-NBOH (i.p.) in stranger observers. Collectively, our findings establish that 5-HT2AR activation in the mPFC is both necessary and sufficient for selectively mediating empathic responses in bystanders toward individuals in pain, identifying 5-HT2AR as a promising therapeutic target for alleviating prosocial and empathic impairments seen in disorders, such as autism spectrum disorder.

INTRODUCTION

Engaging in prosocial behavior is critical for enhancing mental health, strengthening social bonds, and promoting well-being in human and animal societies. One important form of prosocial behavior that remains inadequately understood is empathy toward others' distress (pain, fear, and catastrophe). When we see someone else in pain, we naturally feel sadness and compassion and want to offer support and comfort to help them overcome their difficulties (Chen, 2018; de Waal, 2008; de Waal & Preston, 2017). When empathy is impaired, the ability to perceive, respond to, and care for others' suffering becomes abnormal. This impairment has been proposed to be associated with the several psychiatric conditions, including autism spectrum disorder (Bird & Viding, 2014; Hadjikhani et al., 2014; Minio-Paluello et al., 2009), depression (Kupferberg et al., 2016; Schreiter et al., 2013; Thoma et al., 2011), psychopathy and alexithymia (Bird & Viding, 2014), and schizophrenia (Bonfils et al., 2017; Haker et al., 2012). Ultimately, these conditions share underlying deficits in social perception, communication, emotional recognition, cooperativeness, and theory of mind. Genome-wide analyses have revealed high correlations of self-reported empathy with autism, schizophrenia, and anorexia nervosa (Warrier et al., 2018). Thus, assessment of the ability of empathy for distress is critically important for predicting whether patients are able to successfully return to normal social activities. In the past decades, featured empathic behaviors have been experimentally identified and validated qualitatively and quantitatively in both rats (Chen, 2018; Lü et al., 2017; Luo et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2019) and mice (Du et al., 2020, 2024a; Geng et al., 2020) under the dyadic social interaction (DSI) paradigm, in which a naïve observer (bystander) freely interacts with a conspecific demonstrator in pain. The empathic behaviors have the following characteristics: (1) naïve observers often show more distinct caring behavior toward a pain sufferer during 30-min DSI in terms of engagement of injury-site-targeted allolicking and body-targeted allogrooming (ALAG) (Du et al., 2020; Walsh et al., 2018), helping others cope with pain (Zhang et al., 2024); (2) pain hypersensitive state can be matched through social transfer from a pain sufferer to a naïve observer after 30-min DSI (Du et al., 2020; Walsh et al., 2018); (3) the caring behavior and socially transferred pain are basically dependent upon the extent of dyadic familiarity, but may be modulated by stress level (Li et al., 2014; Martin et al., 2015), visual and olfactory input (Geng et al., 2020; Smith et al., 2016), prior pain experience (Luo et al., 2020), sex hormones (Du et al., 2024a), and experimental procedure or paradigm (Du et al., 2024b; Yu et al., 2019).

Serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5-HT) is involved in numerous biological processes, including learning, memory, mood regulation, sleep, reproduction, circadian rhythm, and pain perception (Crockett et al., 2010; Kim et al., 2014; Mondoloni et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2022b). Interestingly, enhancing 5-HT levels, through dietary tryptophan or pharmacological agents like citalopram (a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor), has been associated with increased emotional perception, moral decision-making, and prosocial behaviors (Attenburrow et al., 2003; Crockett et al., 2010; Knutson et al., 1998; Moul et al., 2018). A recent report has discovered that 5-HT regulates maternal affiliation by infants, conserved from mice and rats to monkeys (Liu et al., 2023). Moreover, bidirectional modulation of the release of 5-HT from the dorsal raphe neurons to the nucleus accumbens influences sociability in a reciprocal manner (Walsh et al., 2018). However, another study has also shown that increased 5-HT levels can disrupt socially transferred fear in mice (Kim et al., 2014). These findings highlight 5-HT as a master regulator in mammalian social behaviors.

The ability of 5-HT to mediate a wide range of behaviors may stem from various types of postsynaptic receptors, which determine the final response to 5-HT release (Barnes & Sharp, 1999; Wallach et al., 2023). The 5-HT2A receptor (5-HT2AR), which is densely expressed in the forebrain, plays a complex role in emotional behaviors (Aznar & Klein, 2013; Farias et al., 2024). While polymorphisms in the 5-HT2AR gene have been linked to aggression, impulsivity, and antisocial traits (Burt & Mikolajewski, 2008; Butovskaya et al., 2018), other studies suggest its involvement in prosocial and affiliative behaviors (Gong et al., 2015; Unschuld et al., 2007; Wan et al., 2021). Interestingly, acute activation of 5-HT2AR can disrupt maternal behaviors in rats, though this effect does not appear to be related to emotional or motivational processing (Gao et al., 2018, 2019; Wu et al., 2018). These findings suggest 5-HT receptors, particularly 5-HT2AR, are major players in mammalian social behaviors. However, it remains unclear whether 5-HT and 5-HT2AR specifically influence socially transferred pain and/or the caring behavior, due to limited evidence.

The medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), which includes the anterior cingulate cortex (Cg1), prelimbic cortex (PrLC, homologous to primate dmPFC), and infralimbic cortex (ILC, homologous to primate vmPFC), has been associated with a range of functions, including attention, decision-making, goal-directed behavior, and working memory (Carrillo et al., 2019; Li et al., 2014; Zhang et al., 2024). The existing literature, primarily from rodent and post-mortem primate studies, indicates that 5-HT2AR shows significant expression in the PrLC, the ILC, and Cg1 (Du et al., 2024b). Several studies have attempted to explain socially transferred pain or distress at the cellular or molecular level, suggesting that the mPFC is crucial in processing socially transferred pain or distress and consolation behavior (Burkett et al., 2016; Li et al., 2014; Smith et al., 2021).

We therefore employed the DSI paradigm to define how 5-HT and its receptors in the mPFC mediate empathic responses in observer rats for a conspecific in pain. We aim to address the following questions: (1) How does endogenous 5-HT in the CNS modulate the empathic behaviors of observers toward a familiar pain sufferer? (2) What are the roles of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC in the development of empathic behaviors toward a familiar pain sufferer? (3) Could activation of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC elicit empathic behaviors toward an unfamiliar (stranger) pain sufferer?

METHODS

Animals

All Sprague-Dawley albino rats were provided by the Laboratory Animal Center of the Fourth Military Medical University (FMMU). Tryptophan hydroxylase 2 (Tph2) knockout (KO) rats were provided by Dr. Yi Rao of Peking University School of Life Sciences (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2023.02.010) and bred in the Laboratory Animal Center of FMMU. The use of animals was reviewed and approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of the FMMU. All experiments were performed in accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (NIH Publication No. 80–23, revised 1996) and followed the ethical guidelines for pain research in conscious animals of the IASP. Every effort was made to minimize the number and suffering of animals used. Female rodents exhibit estrous cycles characterized by fluctuating levels of estrogen and progesterone, which are known to modulate both 5-HT reuptake and function (Gu et al., 2022), as well as complex social behaviors (Asokan & Falkner, 2025). As no sex difference exists in pain-related empathic responses in rats (Du et al., 2020), male rats were used to reduce intrinsic variance in this study. Moreover, a double-blind design was employed for all behavioral testing. Rats were housed in a climate-controlled room (temperature 22 °C–26 °C, air humidity 40%–70%) under a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle with access to food and water ad libitum. The light was on at 0800h, and all handling and behavioral assays were carried out between 0900h and 1800h.

Tph2 KO rats

The details regarding the design and breeding of Tph2-/- rats have been described previously (Liu et al., 2023). The Tph2-/- line was maintained by crossing Tph2+/- heterozygotes. Littermates included wild-type (Tph2+/+), heterozygous (Tph2+/-), and homozygous mutant (Tph2-/-) rats. Only male rats were used for behavioral tests in this study.

Genotype identification and PCR

To identify the genotypes of wild-type and Tph2 KO alleles, rats were routinely identified using standard PCR conditions. Primers for genotyping were GACCATATGTCTTGTCACGTTTC and CTTCTCGGGTGTCCTTTGGGGCAG. Genomic DNA was isolated from tail tissue samples using a Mouse Tail Direct PCR Kit (FOREGENE, China). Premix Taq DNA Polymerase (TaKaRa, Japan) was used to simplify the setup of PCR reactions and minimize pipetting steps. PCR products were analyzed via agarose gel electrophoresis.

Bee venom test

The bee venom (BV) model is a well-established animal model of acute inflammatory pain. The model was established by injecting a volume of 50 μL of solution (0.2 mg BV dissolved in 0.9% physiological saline) into the center of the plantar surface of the left hindpaw in rats with slight restraint (Chen et al., 1999, 2016; Du et al., 2020, 2024a; Yu et al., 2019).

Dyadic social interaction paradigm

As previously described (Du et al., 2024a; Li et al., 2014; Lü et al., 2017; Luo et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2019), a naïve rat, serving as a cagemate observer (CO) or non-cagemate observer (NCO), was allowed to move freely in a testing cage with clean bedding. After 5–10 min of acclimation, another rat, serving as a familiar (cagemate) demonstrator (CD) or unfamiliar (non-cagemate) demonstrator (NCD), was introduced into the testing cage for dyadic social interaction (DSI) with the CO or NCO in physical contact immediately after receiving the BV injection. The whole 30-min DSI process between the naïve observer and the pain-sufferer was recorded by a video camera (FDR-AX40, SONY, Japan) and analyzed off-line. Four sets of major behaviors were qualitatively identified and quantitatively analyzed as follows: (1) sniffing (SN), a social snout and anogenital investigation behavior engaged by the observer toward the demonstrator after union (Du et al., 2020, 2024a); (2) allolicking and allogrooming (ALAG), referred to as caring behavior engaged by the observers toward a pain-sufferer by injury-site licking and body grooming (Burkett et al., 2016; Du et al., 2020, 2024a); (3) huddling (Hu), referred to as a prosocial behavior actively engaged by the observer toward the pain-sufferer by tight body contact against each other when resting (Du et al., 2024a); (4) self-grooming (SG), an innate programmed behavior engaged by the observer to maintain body hygiene (Du et al., 2024a; Kalueff et al., 2016). To quantify these behaviors, latency, total time, and bouts of each behavior were calculated for further statistical analysis. Latency was defined as the time lag from the starting point of the DSI to the first action of each behavior; total time, the cumulative time of each behavior during the 30-min DSI; and bouts, the counts of waves of each behavior during the 30-min DSI (Du et al., 2020, 2024a).

Quantification of pain-related behaviors in awake animals

Persistent spontaneous nociceptive behavior

As described in our previous works (Chen et al., 1999; Du et al., 2020, 2024a; Li et al., 2014), a 30×30×30 cm non-transparent plastic box was placed on a 30 cm high supporting frame above the experimental table. The rat was placed in the test box for at least 30 min before any treatments. After the acclimation period, an intraplantar injection of BV was made as described above (Chen et al., 1999; Du et al., 2024a; Luo et al., 2017; Yu et al., 2019). The quantitative score of persistent spontaneous nociception was obtained by counting the number of paw flinches occurring in the injected hindlimb at each 5-min interval for 30 min following BV injection.

Mechanical pain sensitivity

The paw withdrawal mechanical threshold (PWMT) was used to evaluate mechanical pain sensitivity and was measured as previously described. Briefly, rats were placed on an elevated wire grid floor in a non-transparent plastic chamber (length×width×height=20×20×25 cm). A series of increasing forces of von Frey monofilaments (bending forces: 7.84, 19.60, 39.20, 58.80, 78.40, 98.00, 117.60, 137.20, 156.80, 176.40, 196.00, 245.00, 294.00, 441.00, and 588.00 mN) were used to stimulate the plantar surface of the rat’s bilateral hind paws, respectively. The PWMT was obtained from the lowest forces enabling 50%–60% induction of paw withdrawal responses by 10 repetitive stimuli. The interval between each stimulus was no less than 5 s. The interval between stimuli of the left and right hind paws was no less than 5 min (Chen et al., 1999; Du et al., 2024a; Luo et al., 2020; Yu et al., 2019).

Cannula implantation and intra-mPFC microinjection

Intracerebral cannula implantation was performed as previously described (Geng et al., 2016; Okine et al., 2014). Rats were sufficiently anesthetized with 1% sodium pentobarbital (50 mg/kg, i.p.) and then fixed in a stereotaxic apparatus by ear bars (RWD Life Science Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China). The eyes were covered with a drop of ophthalmic ointment to prevent drying. After a midline incision, two 0.8 mm diameter holes were drilled on two sides of the frontal skull. Two custom-designed stainless-steel guide cannulae (diameter 0.48 mm; RWD Life Science Co., Ltd., Shenzhen, China) were implanted into the medial prefrontal cortices (mPFC) according to the Paxinos and Watson atlas of The Rat Brain in Stereotaxic Coordinates (Bregma: rostral 3.2 mm, mediolateral 0.7 mm, dorsoventral 3.5 mm) (Geng et al., 2016). The cannulae were permanently fixed to the skull using stainless steel screws and dental cement. To prevent infection, penicillin and streptomycin (300 mg/kg, i.p., for each) were administered immediately after surgery for 3 days. Rats were allowed to recover from the surgery for 14 days before any treatments. In the last 5 days of recovery, rats were trained to acclimatize to handling and experimental environments for 5–10 min daily. Rats received bilateral microinjections of agents (0.5 μL over 60 s for each side) using a microsyringe (1.0 μL) connected with polyethylene tubing and a stylet to the implanted cannulae.

Drugs

To pharmacologically deplete endogenous 5-HT, 4-chloro-DL-phenylalanine (PCPA, C6505, Sigma, USA), a selective and irreversible inhibitor of tryptophan hydroxylase, which is a rate-limiting enzyme in the biosynthesis of 5-HT, was intraperitoneally administered for 3 days (i.p., 1 mL/kg body weight, 300 mg/mL dissolved in 2% Tween 80 saline solution). To selectively activate the 5-HT2A receptor (5-HT2AR), 4-[2-[[(2-hydroxyphenyl)methyl]amino]ethyl]-2,5-dimethoxybenzonitrile hydrochloride (25CN-NBOH, Cat. No. 5171, Tocris, USA), a high-affinity and selective 5-HT2AR agonist, was used as described in previous papers (Ferri et al., 2023). A stock concentration of 10 mmol/L 25CN-NBOH was prepared in sterile saline. For intraperitoneal (i.p., 1 mL/kg body weight) administration, 25CN-NBOH was diluted to a working concentration of 300 μg/mL, while for intra-mPFC microinjections, a working concentration of 100 μg/mL was used as described in previous papers (Odland et al., 2021). To selectively inhibit 5-HT2AR, (R)-(+)-α-(2,3-dimethoxyphenyl)-1-[2-(4-fluorophenyl)ethyl]-4-piperidinemethanol (M100907, formerly MDL 100907, Cat. No. 4173, Tocris, USA), a potent and selective 5-HT2AR antagonist, was used as described in previous papers (Sorensen et al., 1993). A stock concentration of 100 mM M100907 was prepared in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO). When used for i.p. administration, a working concentration of 100 μg/mL was used, while for intra-mPFC microinjections, a working concentration of 33.3 μg/mL was used with 3‰ DMSO saline solution as a control. In the case of co-administration of agonist and antagonist, a solution of M100907 (100 μg/mL, i.p., 1 mL/kg body weight) was administered 10 min prior to 25CN-NBOH (300 μg/mL, i.p., 1 mL/kg body weight) for systemic effects, while a mixed solution of M100907 (33.3 μg/mL)+25CN-NBOH (100 μg/mL) was bilaterally microinjected into the mPFC for local effects.

Recombinant adeno-associated virus (rAAV) vectors

To locally interfere with the expression of 5-HT2AR within the mPFC, rAAV-U6-shRNA (Htr2a: Rattus)-CMV-EGFP-SV40 polyA [rAAV-shRNA (5-HT2AR), BrainVTA, 2.11×1012μg/mL] and AAV-U6-shRNA (scramble)-CMV-EGFP-pA [rAAV-Scramble, BrainVTA, 5.61×1012μg/mL] were designed. Intra-mPFC microinjections were performed with a 1 μL syringe (Hamilton, Nevada, USA) with a 32-gauge needle. Syringe pumps (Longer Precision Pump Co., Ltd., China) were used to inject the virus vector (300 nL in total) at a speed of 1 nL/s.

Immunofluorescent labeling

Rats were anesthetized with 1% pentobarbital sodium (50 mg/kg, i.p.), then perfused with 0.1 mol/L PB solution, followed by 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) in 0.1 mol/L PB solution. After perfusion, brains were carefully extracted from the skull and postfixed in 4% PFA for 24 hours at 4°C, and cryoprotected by immersion in 30% sucrose in 0.1 mol/L PB at 4°C until they sank to the bottom of the container. Continuous coronal sections (30 μm thick) were sectioned on a CM1900 freezing microtome (Leica, Germany). Tissue sections were collected and stored in 0.1 mol/L PB solution at 4°C until immunocytochemical processing.

Free-floating sections undergoing immunohistochemical staining were washed in PBS three times and then washed in 0.5% PBST (0.5% Triton X-100 in PBS) three times. Sections were then incubated with blocking buffer containing 10% goat serum or 8% bovine serum albumin dissolved in 0.5% PBST for 1 h. Sections were then incubated with primary antibodies diluted in primary antibody diluent (P0103, Beyotime, China) overnight at 4°C. For primary antibodies, anti-c-Fos (1:500, mouse, NBP2-50037, Novus Biologicals, USA), anti-5-HT2AR (1:200, rabbit, ab66049, Abcam, USA), and anti-5-HT (1:5000, goat, 20079, Immunostar, USA) were used. After incubation, the sections were rinsed four times (10 min each) with PBST and incubated with a fluorescent dye-conjugated secondary antibody diluted in secondary antibody diluent (P0103, Beyotime, China) for 3 h at room temperature. For secondary antibodies, Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated affinity-pure goat anti-mouse IgG (1:200, 115-545-003, Jackson ImmunoResearch, USA), Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated affinity-pure goat anti-rabbit IgG (1:200, 115-545-003, Jackson ImmunoResearch, USA), and anti-goat IgG (whole molecule)-Cy3 (1:200, C2821, Sigma, USA) were used. Afterward, the sections were washed in PBS three times (10 min each time), then transferred into DAPI solution (1:2000, D9542, Sigma, USA) and incubated for 3 min. After being stained with DAPI and washed three times with PBS, the sections were mounted on glass microscope slides, dried, and covered with mounting media (FY1301, Xi’an Fei Yang Bio, China). When carrying out double immunofluorescent labeling, two primary or secondary antibodies were used in a mixture. The images were examined under a laser scanning confocal fluorescent microscope (Olympus FV1000, Japan).

Nissl stain and histological localization of the injection sites

The frozen sections (10 μm), collected as described above, were subjected to Nissl staining with 1% cresyl violet solution. The localizations of intra-mPFC microinjections were observed and photomicrographed by a computer-based microscope CCD camera (DP-70, Olympus, Japan). Photomicrographs were processed and analyzed by Image-Pro-Plus 6.1 software (Media Cybernetics, USA).

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reactions (qRT-PCR)

After behavioral assays, rats receiving intra-mPFC virus microinjections were sacrificed under anesthesia. Brains were rapidly extracted, and mPFC tissues were dissected out on dry ice. Total RNA was extracted from the brain tissue containing the mPFC using TRIzol reagent according to the manufacturer's instructions (Invitrogen, USA). RNA content was measured using a Nanodrop-2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). qRT-PCR was performed as previously described (Zhang et al., 2017). Briefly, 1 μg of ribonucleic acid (RNA) extracted from the mPFC tissue sample was reverse-transcribed to synthesize cDNA using a PrimeScript RT reagent Kit Perfect Real Time following the manufacturer’s instructions (TaKaRa, Japan). PCR reactions were performed using FastStart Essential DNA Green Master using the LightCycler 96 system (Roche, Germany). The amount of cDNA was normalized based on the expression of the housekeeping gene β-actin. Data were calculated using the 2-ΔΔCT formula.

Statistical analysis

All data were presented as mean±SEM. Parametric and non-parametric statistical analysis methods were used depending on both the normality test and the equal variance test, and the consistency of statistical methods used. An unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test was used for comparison between two groups, while a two-tailed Mann–Whitney U test was used when the data were not normally distributed. For comparison of three or more groups, one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post hoc comparison was used, while the Kruskal–Wallis one-way ANOVA test with Bonferroni post hoc comparison was used if the dataset did not pass the normality test and equal variance test. A two-way repeated measures test or the Greenhouse–Geisser method, based further on Mauchly's test of sphericity, was used for the data of time effects. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS v.23.0. Significant differences were set at P<0.05.

RESULTS

Non-selective roles of central 5-HT in empathic responses toward a familiar pain-sufferer

As described in our previous reports (Du et al., 2020; Li et al., 2014; Luo et al., 2020), bystander (cagemate observer, CO) rats often show more distinct caring behavior toward a familiar (cagemate demonstrator, CD), but not an unfamiliar (non-cagemate demonstrator, NCD), pain-sufferer during 30-min DSI in terms of engagement of injury-site targeted ALAG (Figure 1A). Although sniffing (SN) and huddling (Hu) can also be identified during 30-min DSI under the naïve-pain DSI paradigm (Figure 1A), these general prosocial behaviors have been demonstrated not to be empathy-related responses to a pain-sufferer (Du et al., 2024a). Self-grooming (SG), an anti-empathic behavior, was also used as a control because under the 30-min naïve-pain DSI paradigm, CO may engage more ALAG at the cost of sacrificing SG (Du et al., 2020, 2024a). The tryptophan hydroxylase 2 (Tph2) gene is essential for the synthesis of central 5-HT in the brain. To gain understanding of the exact roles of central 5-HT in pain-related empathic responses, we first obtained three offspring genotypes of rats through hybridization between male and female Tph2+/- heterozygotes bred by knockout of the 6th exon of the Tph2 gene using sgRNA-Cas9 (Liu et al., 2023) (Figure 1B, C). Compared with the wild-type (Tph2+/+), rats with Tph2-/- had less 5-HT-positive neuronal labeling in the raphe system of the brain, where 5-HT neuronal cell bodies are mainly located (Figure 1D). Because 5-HT is known to be an important pain modulator in the CNS (Millan, 2002), we examined basal pain sensitivity using von Frey filaments and nociceptive responsiveness to peripheral inflammatory pain caused by subcutaneous (s.c.) injection of bee venom (BV) in the three genotypes. The results showed that there were no significant changes in either the basal paw withdrawal mechanical threshold (PWMT) or the BV-induced nociceptive paw flinches and mechanical pain hypersensitivity occurring at the hind paw ipsilateral to the injection side when compared among the three genotypes of rats (Supplementary Figure S1). Under the naïve-pain DSI paradigm, CO rats with Tph2-/- had significantly longer latency for ALAG than Tph2+/- and Tph2+/+ (Tph2-/- vs. Tph2+/-, P<0.001; Supplementary Table S1), but without affecting SN, Hu, and SG (Figure 1E). However, in contrast, CO rats with Tph2-/- had significantly less total time spent on ALAG (P<0.01) as well as SN (P=0.002) and Hu (P<0.05), although without affecting SG, when compared with Tph2+/- and Tph2+/+ (Figure 1F; Supplementary Table S1). The bouts of all four behaviors were significantly decreased in CO rats with Tph2-/- compared with Tph2+/- and Tph2+/+ genotypes (Figure 1G). Time course observations revealed that the difference was statistically significant within the first 10 min for SN, and within the first 20 min for ALAG (Figure 1H). CO rats with Tph2-/- had no socially transferred mechanical pain hypersensitivity, while those with Tph2+/- and Tph2+/+ did have similar time courses of this vicariously perceived pain as reported previously (Figure 1I; Supplementary Table S2) (Luo et al., 2020; Walsh et al., 2018).

To further confirm the central roles of 5-HT in SN, ALAG, Hud and SG, we also depleted central 5-HT pharmacologically by intraperitoneal (i.p.) administration of 4-Chloro-DL-phenylalanine (PCPA), a 5-HT synthesis inhibitor in the CNS (Figure 2A). PCPA treatment (i.p.) in CO rats resulted in dramatically delayed latency in ALAG (PCPA vs. Vehicle, P=0.005) as well as in Hud (n=9 for each group, P=0.038) and SG (P=0.033) but without affecting SN, compared with vehicle treatment (Figure 2B; Supplementary Table S1). The total time was significantly reduced in CO rats with PCPA for ALAG (P<0.001), Hud (P<0.001), and SG (P<0.001) but not for SN, relative to those with vehicle (Figure 2C, E). However, bouts of ALAG (P<0.001) and SG (P<0.001) but not SN, Hud were decreased by PCPA compared with vehicle (Figure 2D). The depletion of central 5-HT by PCPA did not change the basal mechanical pain threshold relative to vehicle (Figure 2F; Supplementary Table S2). However, the mechanical pain threshold was significantly reduced in CO rats treated with vehicle but not changed with PCPA after 30-min naïve-pain DSI (P<0.001, Figure 2F).

Altogether, these data suggest that genetic and pharmacological depletion of central 5-HT in rats impairs both the caring behavior toward and socially transferred pain from a familiar conspecific pain-sufferer. However, the effects are not specific because sniffing, huddling, and even self-grooming are affected as well, probably due to widespread projections of 5-HT neurons and multiple subtypes of 5-HT receptors in the brain.

Selective roles of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC in empathic responses toward a familiar pain-sufferer

The mPFC, comprising Cg1, PrLC, and ILC, plays an indispensable role in modulating social contagion of pain as well as consolation behavior (Burkett et al., 2016; Li et al., 2014; Smith et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024). Here, we focused on the roles of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC in empathic responses toward a familiar pain-sufferer (Figure 3A). Immunofluorescent staining showed high intensity of 5-HT2AR labeling within the mPFC, with cortical layer V being the richest in density (Figure 3B, C), which was similar to previous reports (Price et al., 2019; Weisstaub et al., 2006). Compared with naïve-naïve (NN) control (cagemate observer control, CC), CO rats after naïve-pain (NP) DSI had more c-Fos labeling in 5-HT2AR cellular profiles within the mPFC (CO vs. CC, P=0.028) (Figure 3D–H).

Bilateral intra-mPFC microinjections of M100907, a selective and potent 5-HT2AR antagonist, selectively suppressed the empathic responses of CO rats toward a familiar pain-sufferer during 30-min DSI in terms of latency (P<0.001), total time (P=0.013) and bouts (P<0.001), without affecting other three behaviors (Figure 3J–L, Supplemental Table S1). Time course analysis showed the difference was statistically significant in the first 10-min section (Figure 3M). Moreover, bilateral intra-mPFC microinjections of M100907 blocked socially transferred pain in CO rats (P=0.001), whereas vehicle treatment failed to prevent its occurrence after 30-min DSI (Figure 3N). Finally, the basal mechanical pain threshold was not changed by bilateral intra-mPFC microinjections of M100907 (Figure 3N).

To further confirm the selective roles of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC in mediating the empathic responses of rats toward a familiar pain-sufferer, bilateral microinjections of rAAV-U6-shRNA (Htr2a:Rattus)-CMV-EGFP-SV40 polyA (rAAV-shRNA-EGFP) were made to downregulate 5-HT2AR expression in the mPFC (Figure 4A, B). Both immunofluorescent labeling and qRT-PCR showed that the rAAV-shRNA-EGFP strategy successfully downregulated the expression of 5-HT2AR within the mPFC by 80% (rAAV-shRNA vs. rAAV-scramble, P=0.004), relative to rAAV-scramble (Figure 4D, E). Similar to the above local pharmacological antagonism against 5-HT2AR, downregulation of 5-HT2AR expression in the mPFC also selectively impaired the empathic responses of rats toward a familiar pain-sufferer in terms of caring behavior and socially transferred pain (Figure 4F–J; Supplementary Table S1).

Overall, these results indicate that 5-HT2AR in the mPFC plays a critical role in mediating both social transfer of pain from and caring behavior toward a familiar conspecific pain sufferer, but has little impact on sniffing, huddling, and self-grooming behaviors.

Activation of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC promotes empathic responses toward an unfamiliar pain sufferer

It is notable that pain-related empathic responses, especially the social transfer of pain, in both rats and mice are largely dependent upon the extent of familiarity between the observer and the pain sufferer (Chen, 2018; Gonzalez-Liencres et al., 2014; Langford et al., 2006; Li et al., 2014; Luo et al., 2020; Martin et al., 2015). However, pain-related empathic responses have been found to be improvable between unfamiliar conspecifics when bystander rats had experienced similar pain before 30-min DSI (Luo et al., 2020).

To test whether activation of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC can improve pain-related empathic responses, non-cagemate rats were used as observers (NCO) and pain sufferers (non-cagemate demonstrators, NCD) for DSI (Figure 5A), as previously reported (Luo et al., 2020). First, we treated observers with 25CN-NBOH, a selective 5-HT2AR agonist, via the i.p. route. The results showed that 25CN-NBOH shortened the latency (P=0.003; for details, see Supplementary Table S1), elongated the total time (P=0.007), and increased the bouts (P=0.010) of ALAG in naïve NCOs (Figure 5B–D). Moreover, the systemic administration of the 5-HT2AR agonist 25CN-NBOH did not affect sniffing and huddling behaviors, indicating that ALAG was not influenced by non-empathic confounds (Figure 5B–D). Time course analysis showed that the difference was statistically significant in the first 10-min section (Figure 5E; Supplementary Table S1).

The injection sites were confirmed to be mainly confined within the mPFC through Nissl staining (Figure 6C, D). Double immunofluorescence also revealed a significant increase in the number of c-Fos and 5-HT2AR double-labeled profiles in the mPFC following bilateral intra-mPFC microinjections of 25CN-NBOH in NCOs, relative to the vehicle control (Figure 6E, P=0.010). Behavioral observations revealed that bilateral intra-mPFC microinjections of 25CN-NBOH selectively promoted pain-related empathic responses in naïve NCO rats in terms of both caring behavior and socially transferred pain, without affecting SN, Hu, and SG behaviors, relative to the vehicle control (Figure 6F–J; Supplementary Table S1). However, co-microinjections of a mixture of M100907 and 25CN-NBOH into the bilateral mPFC resulted in selective blockade of 5-HT2AR neuronal activation (Figure 6E) and the empathic responses induced in naïve NCO rats (Figure 6F–J).

DISCUSSION

Empathy is an evolutionary behavior found in social animals and humans, associated with prosocial reciprocity, altruism, and morality. It involves the ability and capacity to feel, recognize, understand, and share the emotional states of others (Chen, 2018; de Waal & Preston, 2017; Du et al., 2024b). Numerous observations in communities of non-human primates, such as chimpanzees and bonobos, and rodents have confirmed that empathy occurs primarily among familiar individuals (Chen, 2018; de Waal & Preston, 2017; Langford et al., 2006; Li et al., 2014; Lü et al., 2017; Luo et al., 2020; Martin et al., 2015). More recent studies have demonstrated that the visibility of spontaneous pain-related behaviors displayed by a familiar pain-sufferer can trigger robust ALAG behaviors, which serve as targeted caring behaviors aiming at helping others cope with pain (Du et al., 2020, 2024a; Li et al., 2014; Walsh et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2024). 5-HT reuptake inhibitors and 5-HT2AR agonists are used as antidepressants in clinical settings (Kaplan et al., 2022). In the current study, we characterized the selective functional roles of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC in mediating pain-related empathic responses in rat bystanders during social interaction with a familiar or an unfamiliar pain-sufferer. However, this receptor did not mediate general prosocial behaviors (sniffing and huddling) and self-grooming.

Studies in humans have demonstrated that promoting 5-HT release or inhibiting its reuptake enhances affiliation and prosocial behaviors (Crockett et al., 2010; Moul et al., 2018). Polymorphism studies indicate a link between 5-HT-related genes and autism (Patrick & Ames, 2014), and genetic or pharmacological depletion of brain 5-HT has been associated with behavioral symptoms of autism (Boylan et al., 2007). Additionally, research has shown that increased 5-HT in the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) reduces observational fear, potentially reflecting deficits in empathy observed in clinical populations (Kim et al., 2014). One recent report also highlights that 5-HT regulates maternal affiliation in a manner conserved across species, from rodents to primates (Liu et al., 2023). Furthermore, bidirectional modulation of 5-HT release from dorsal raphe neurons to the nucleus accumbens has been shown to influence sociability (Walsh et al., 2018). These findings collectively suggest that 5-HT plays a central role in regulating mammalian social behaviors. Supporting previous work, our results demonstrated that both genetic and pharmacological depletion of central 5-HT in rats suppressed ALAG, socially transferred pain, and general prosocial behaviors (sniffing and huddling). These observations indicate that central 5-HT mediates pain-related empathic responses as well as some general prosocial behaviors under the naïve-pain DSI paradigm, which may be attributable to the widespread axonal projections of 5-HT neuronal cell bodies from the raphe nuclei to other brain regions.

5-HT2AR, the major subtype of postsynaptic 5-HT receptors in the CNS, is known to be involved in memory, cognitive function, and mood (Aznar & Klein, 2013; Butovskaya et al., 2018; Farias et al., 2024). The drug 3,4-methylenedioxymethamphetamine (MDMA, commonly known as ecstasy), which promotes 5-HT release, increases affiliative behaviors in squirrel monkeys in a 5-HT2AR-dependent manner (Pitts et al., 2017). For the first time, we demonstrated that activation of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC is selectively involved in mediating pain-related empathic responses in terms of caring behavior toward and socially transferred pain from a familiar pain-sufferer. Compared with the results from central 5-HT depletion, it is suggested that 5-HT2AR in the mPFC is more specifically responsible for both observational pain contagion and caring behavior, which are related to empathic consolation. Meanwhile, receptors involved in sniffing and self-grooming might be other subtypes, such as 5-HT1AR or 5-HT2CR (Gráf, 2006; Kyzar et al., 2016; Li et al., 2021). Taken together, activation of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC is necessary for social pain contagion and caring behavior toward a familiar conspecific in pain, with little impact on other common prosocial and non-social behaviors.

Familiarity among conspecifics appears to be essential for inducing emotional contagion and caring behavior in response to others' distress. Given the suppressing effects of 5-HT2AR antagonism in the mPFC on emotional contagion from and caring behavior toward a familiar social partner, we investigated whether agonizing 5-HT2AR in the mPFC could facilitate rats' empathic responses toward a stranger in pain. Unexpectedly, both systemic and intra-mPFC administration of the 5-HT2AR agonist 25CN-NBOH in observers significantly promoted social pain contagion and caring behavior toward unfamiliar conspecifics in pain, which could be completely blocked by selective pre-antagonism of 5-HT2AR through either systemic or local administration of M100907. Additionally, 25CN-NBOH was ineffective when the expression of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC had been cleared by rAAV-shRNA-EGFP virus, excluding the actions of 25CN-NBOH on 5-HT2AR in other brain regions. Moreover, other common prosocial and non-social behaviors were not affected by either systemic or intra-mPFC agonism and/or antagonism of 5-HT2AR under the naïve-pain DSI paradigm between strangers. These findings demonstrate that activation of 5-HT2AR by exogenous agonists is sufficient to elicit pain-related empathic responses in unfamiliar rats, indicating that familiarity is not the only determining factor for the induction of empathy for pain.

The mPFC has been associated with a range of functions, including attention, decision-making, goal-directed behavior, and working memory (Euston et al., 2012; Shing et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2022a). Our previous study has shown that bilateral lesions of the mPFC eliminate social pain contagion in rats (Li et al., 2014). Recent research has also demonstrated a causal role for the ACC in bidirectionally controlling targeted allogrooming towards others in pain (Zhang et al., 2024). It is interesting to note that the dorsal raphe-ACC neural circuit using 5-HT to activate 5-HT1AR mediates allogrooming behavior between pair-bond male and female mandarin voles (Li et al., 2021). However, the activation of 5-HT1AR in the ACC was not specific to allogrooming because it also mediates sniffing and social approach. Taking together this previous study and our current study, it suggests that pair-bond male-to-female allogrooming behavior may be quite different from the empathic responses we observed using familiar or unfamiliar male-to-male DSI paradigms.

In this study, we showed that activation of 5-HT2AR in the mPFC is sufficient and necessary for the development of empathy for pain. It will be interesting to determine the effect of 5-HT2AR on other forms of empathy (fear, reward sharing). Moreover, previous research has reported that 5-HT2AR is densely expressed in both pyramidal neurons and interneurons of the mPFC. Studies have demonstrated that 5-HT2AR on pyramidal neurons in the mPFC plays a key role in regulating cortical excitability (Celada et al., 2013; Wu et al., 2024), whereas 5-HT2AR activation in interneurons produces bidirectional effects (Barzan et al., 2024). Whether the function of 5-HT2AR on pyramidal neurons and interneurons differs during empathic responses remains unclear. To directly address this point, we will employ cell-type-specific approaches, such as using 5-HT2AR-Cre mouse lines in combination with Cre-dependent viral vectors, to selectively manipulate 5-HT2AR+ pyramidal neurons versus 5-HT2AR+ interneurons and assess the functional outcome on empathic behaviors. In addition, in our previous data, we also found that OXT neurons in the paraventricular nucleus (PVN) respond to caring behavior (Du et al., 2024a). It would be intriguing to investigate how the neuronal circuits of OXT neurons in the PVN and 5-HT2AR in the mPFC contribute to driving allolicking and allogrooming behaviors, as well as socially transferred pain.

Moreover, the intracellular mechanism by which 5-HT2AR mediates pain-related empathic responses remains unknown and warrants further investigation. Firstly, 5-HT2AR is a G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR). The activation of 5-HT2A receptors leads to the hydrolysis of phosphatidylinositol bisphosphate (PIP2) and produces IP3 and diacylglycerol, which in turn activates protein kinase C (PKC) (Bhattacharyya et al., 2002). This could modulate neuronal excitability in the mPFC. An alternative or parallel pathway could involve interaction with BDNF/TrkB signaling (Ceccarini et al., 2020; Mattson et al., 2004), which is known to be involved in plasticity related to social behaviors. We plan future studies to address the intracellular signaling pathways using targeted pharmacological disruption of these pathways or biosensors to visualize their activity in vivo during empathic tests.

In summary, we establish the naïve-pain DSI paradigm as a powerful tool for elucidating the neurobiological basis of empathy across varying social contexts. This model holds significant translational potential for uncovering the etiology of empathy deficits in neuropsychiatric disorders such as ASD, depression, and schizophrenia. Importantly, our work provides the first characterization of the functional roles of central 5-HT and 5-HT2AR in the mPFC in socially transferred pain and caring behavior, offering a novel pharmacotherapeutic strategy to directly target the psychological underpinnings of empathy deficits.

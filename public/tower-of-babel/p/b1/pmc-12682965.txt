Modified Florida sleeve for commissural orientation angle augmentation in bicuspid aortic valve repair
Authors: Andrea Venturini, Dario Brenna, Fabrizio Rosati, Dario Cani, Stefano Benussi
Published: 2025

Augmentation of the commissural orientation angle with the modified Florida sleeve.

Central Message

We present a modification of Florida sleeve technique, that can represent an additional tool for CO angle augmentation in bicuspid aortic valve repair.

A recent repair-oriented classification has divided bicuspid aortic valves (BAVs) into 3 types according to the commissural orientation (CO) angle.1 Surgical repair of very asymmetrical BAV (type C) is still a technical challenge and often requires a tailored approach. In addition to the spectrum of complex techniques to address type C BAV repair, we describe a simple modification of the Florida sleeve, characterized by the addition of a full row of “U” pledgeted stitches al the level of the virtual basal ring (VBR).

Surgical Technique

We present a case of an 18-year-old male patient with severe aortic valve regurgitation and ascending aorta aneurysm. Computed tomography scan showed aortic diameters of 35 mm at level of Valsalva sinuses, 35 mm at the sinotubular junction (STJ), and 52 mm at level of the ascending aorta. Transesophageal echocardiogram showed a very asymmetrical BAV, left-right type with fibrotic raphe, and a CO angle of 138° with an additional small raphe at the level of right noncoronary commissure; VBR diameter was 28 mm; and left ventricular outflow tract was 26 mm. Doppler analysis showed a severe regurgitation with a large central jet with an eccentric component attributable to prolapse of the fused cusp. Mean gradient was 29 mm Hg.

A David operation was planned to modify the CO angle from 138° to 150-160° combined with leaflet repair, thus allowing to change the valve phenotype from a type C to a type B. Under general anesthesia, the patient was prepped and monitored as usual, median sternotomy was preformed, and cardiopulmonary bypass was started with a femoral arterial line and central venous cannulation. Then, the aorta was crossclamped and cardioplegic arrest was achieved by means of retrograde del Nido solution.

The ascending aorta was transected, and the aortic valve was carefully inspected. We confirmed the presence of a second minor raphe between the right and the noncoronary (NC) cusps; therefore, the valve was considered as a pseudo-unicuspid or forme fruste unicuspid aortic valve.2 Right coronary ostium was in a normal position whereas the left one was iuxta-commissural: close to the left NC commissure. Because of the anatomy of the left ostium, we therefore decided to perform a modified Florida sleeve operation in order to avoid reimplantation in a patient with a normal aortic root (35 mm), although the presence of a iuxta-commissural coronary ostium is not a contraindication for the reimplantation technique as described by Hechadi and colleagues.3

Thus, a deep dissection was performed at the level of the aortic root down to the VBR, followed by a soft tape encircling of both coronary arteries. Then, the minor raphe (R-NC) was incised with a beaver blade up to the top of the commissure, converting a pseudo-unicuspid valve into a bicuspid type C valve: the commissure was then found to reach the level of STJ, making any other surgical technique unnecessary. Shaving was necessary to remove thickened tissue from both leaflets, and the raphe was furthermore mobilized. The valve was then assessed with the Schaefers caliper (MSS-1; Fehling Instruments): geometric height was 21 mm for nonfused cusp (nFC), and 24 mm and 23 mm, respectively, for left and right components of fused cusp (FC); effective height was 8 mm for nFC.

Twelve “U” pledgeted 2/0 ETHIBOND stitches were placed at the level of the VBR, with attention paid to avoid the conduction system (Figure 1). A 30-mm Cardioroot woven vascular graft (Getinge Italy SRL) was selected after measuring the distance from the VBR to the STJ in the left-NC commissure (El Khoury's technique). Keyholes were created to lodge coronary arteries at the level of the graft. The 4 “U” stitches of nFC were passed through the graft in order to align with a new 150° CO angle. An opposite effect was obtained with the 8 ETHIBOND stitches of the FC that, starting from a 222° angle, were finally fitting a new 210° angle in the graft (Figure E1). The tops of the 2 commissures were then fixed to the graft at the same 150° CO angle, and the STJ was stabilized on the vascular graft with a circumferential 4/0 polypropylene running suture. In adjunct, nFC was centrally plicated to reach a 10-mm effective height, and the incomplete left-right raphe was closed with 5/0 polypropylene interrupted stitches aiming to achieve and equal length of the 2 free margins. A 30-mm straight vascular graft was used to connect the Cardioroot with the distal aorta (Video 1).

Postoperative transesophageal echocardiography showed absence of aortic regurgitation, a mean gradient of 14 mm Hg (with a further decrease at the 3-month echocardiography, down to 9 mm Hg), a diameter at the level of the VBR of 24 mm, and a new CO of 150° (Figure 2). The patient's postoperative course was uneventful.

Comment

A review paper from the world's leading aortic valve repair group4 presented an algorithm that should be considered whenever facing an aortic valve repair surgical procedure: according to this, a very asymmetrical BAV is still considered an extreme technical challenge and a surgical repair should be performed preferably in specialized, high-volume aortic valve repair centers.

In summary, type C BAV could be repaired in 2 different ways: by creating a new tricuspid valve by means of a double patch technique in order to create a new anatomical commissure or by changing the valve phenotype with the 150° or 180° reimplantation technique or with the well-known root-plasty techniques.

Our modification of the Florida sleeve technique can be a useful tool to address complex valve phenotype by adding a complete row of stitches at the level of the VBR and reorienting the commissures at the desired angle on the STJ, especially when an anatomical challenge is present as in this case was the left ostium was sitting close to the commissure. CO angle was modified from a type C to type B BAV (therefore 150° instead of 180°) to not overstress the aortic anatomy.5,E1

We believe that reimplantation technique should be the first choice for CO angle modification in BAV surgery; however, the newly described modified Florida sleeve might have a role in all the scenarios in which a full root replacement is the not recommended.

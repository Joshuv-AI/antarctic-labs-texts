Introduction

The human hand is highly mobile and functional. It has essential involvement in daily life, vocation and recreational activities, capable of grasping to get closer to an object. When using a strength grip, the flexed fingers push the object against the palm while the thumb metacarpal and proximal phalanx stabilize the object. When holding an object with a precision pinch, the object is secured between the pulps of the thumb and index or index and middle fingers [1,2].

It is essential in the prehensile function, being the only one that performs the movements of circumduction and opposition, allowing excursions outside the plane of the hand. Reconstructive goals are good function without pain, mobility, length, stability, sensation, strength, durability, and esthetics [3]. On the other hand, the hand can touch objects or other human beings, detecting temperature, vibration and texture [1,2].

Post-traumatic defects in the thumb can lead to significant functional impairment, and reconstruction is always the primary goal, since 40% of hand function is due to the thumb [4].

Thumb injuries are divided into distal, medial and proximal, with treatment guidelines according to this classification already well described [3,5], however, this is not the case in injuries due to slacking, where despite having the Urbaniak classification that divides into type 1 adequate circulation, type 2 inadequate circulation and type 3 almost complete amputation [6], since despite the fact that there are publications of recommendations according to the degree of injury; recommendations are limited when reimplantation or foot-to-hand transfer are not an option [7] or when unclear when an immediate reconstruction is preferred, when there is a lack of tissue to reimplant, or the microsurgical option is not available.

The surgical flap is a unit of tissue for reconstructive purposes, extracting from one donor site and transferring to another [1,8]. Multiple classifications have been described; reconstructive flaps are classified based on the type of blood supply, proximity of the donor tissue to the recipient, and tissue composition [9].

Regional flaps are pedicled flaps that use tissue neighboring the defect without adjoining the defect. They are transferred through a release incision along the transfer path of the flap or under a tunnel of skin or tissue [10].

The radial pedicled forearm flap is a time-tested procedure that offers reliable coverage in this area without the need for advanced microsurgical expertise [11]. The radial forearm flap was developed in China in the early 1970s and was originally published by Yang as a free flap [12]. Only later did Biemer and Lu describe the pedicled flap based on the retrograde flow of the palmar arch.

The three options for regional pedicled flap coverage include the radial forearm flap, ulnar forearm flap, and posterior interosseous flap based on the radial artery, ulnar artery, and posterior interosseous artery, respectively [13].

The radial forearm flap is preferred over other soft tissue reconstructive methods [14].

The radial forearm free flap offers ease of harvesting, flexible tissue and reliable anatomy, making it a reconstructive workhorse. On the other hand, the radial reverse forearm flap is used to cover defects in the palmar or dorsal area of the hand that include the metacarpophalangeal joints [15,16].

Case presentation

We present the case of a Hispanic, right hander, 37-years-old male, who suffered an avulsion type trauma to the thumb of the right hand, where he suffered total distal skin and soft tissue distancing from the metacarpophalangeal joint; causing bone and tendon exposure. On physical examination, the patient showed preservation of mobility function without viable coverage, at the level of the distal phalanx he presented data of anteroposterior crushing but with integrity in its length, intact tendons, integrity of movements was confirmed, for which reason we proceeded to review vascular integrity of the hand with Allen's clinical test to verify flow and integrity of the palmar arch, being present we were allowed to plan a reverse radial flap for coverage.

During the procedure, stabilization of the interphalangeal and metacarpophalangeal joint was performed with 2 retrograde K nails, likewise a 7 × 8 cm kite-shaped flap was designed, dissected in a sub facial plane, and broken and given 360° coverage to the thumb. The donor area of the forearm was covered with a partial thickness graft in a second stage. After the procedure, a good evolution was obtained, and it was managed with immobilization for 2 weeks with a splint and the bald spots were removed.

Rehabilitation was given for 2 weeks, and he returned to his activities of daily living at 6 weeks completely showing adequate mobility and function, preserving movements.

Preparation

After discharge

The nail must be removed 3 weeks later, and thumb mobilisation exercises initiated to preserve proprioception, sensitivity, and function (Figure G, Figure H). The patient must be able to perform activities such as writing, driving, and even returning to work. Debulking surgery for aesthetic purposes could be performed.

Clinical discussion

The anatomy of the hand and its functionality present a scope in the daily life of man, how working life and recreational activities. Therefore, it has transcended in the management of reconstructive surgery.

The variety of the surgical flap is very wide. However, it must be designed with the intention of maintaining the integrity and optimal functioning of human health. Surgical techniques thus represent a primary tool in their management, with flaps being a fundamental pillar in reconstructive practice.

Conclusion

The reverse radial forearm flap can provide great benefits and remains among the reconstructive methods of choice for soft tissues, being accessible in the collection of flexible tissue and its anatomy for coverage of defects in the dorsal or palmar area of the hand. In addition, it allows a speedy recovery with adequate functional preservation, with minimal alteration of the donor area in the scenario where the thumb suffers from slack and tissue is not available for reimplantation. It is a relevant option when microsurgical transfer options are not viable, either due to lack of microsurgical resources or it is not accepted by the patient [17].

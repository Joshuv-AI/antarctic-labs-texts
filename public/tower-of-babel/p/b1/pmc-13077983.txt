The relationship between metabolic associated fatty liver disease and thyroid nodule: a cross-sectional study in China
Authors: Jun Xiao, Shen Tian, Juan Wu, Zhou Xu, Hao Li, Shu Li, Xin-yu Liang, Yu-ling Chen, Vishnu Prasad Adhikari, Jing-yu Song, Chen-yu Ma, Rui-ling She, Zhao-xing Li, Liang Ran, Ling-quan Kong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe relationship between metabolic associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) and thyroid nodule (TN) is unknown, despite the overweight & obese individuals showed a greater risk of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease(NAFLD) and thyroid cancer compared to the normal Body mass index(BMI) individuals. This is a cross-sectional study examining the association between TN and MAFLD in healthy Chinese population undergoing simultaneous abdominal and thyroid ultrasonography.MethodsA total of 78,077 healthy individuals with complete biochemical, thyroid and liver ultrasonography data from the Quality Control Center of Health Examination in Chongqing, China were included in the study. TNs were classified into malignancy risk levels of 5 different categories in accordance with Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System(TI-RADS). We investigated the association between MAFLD and TNs by binomial logistic regression analyses, and multinomial logistic regression analyses were performed to test the independence of associations between TNs or different TI-RADS categories of TNs and MAFLD.This study was designed and reported in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines to ensure transparency and completeness in reporting observational research.ResultsThe prevalence of TN, MAFLD and combined TN with MAFLD were 30.4% (female: 36.7%, male: 26%), 26.9% (female: 13.4%, male: 36.4%) and 8.8% (female: 10.1%, male: 6.8%), respectively. The males with MAFLD had a higher risk of TN after adjustment for age, gender, blood pressure and some hepatic and renal function indicators, males with MAFLD had a higher risk of TN (OR = 1.108,95%CI: 1.054–1.165, P < 0.001)and TN of TI-RADS ≥ 4A (OR = 1.231,95%CI: 1.158–1.308, P < 0.001) compared to males without MAFLD; Furthermore, with stratification on the basis of BMI, only non-obese men with MAFLD had a higher risk of TN (OR = 1.100,95%CI: 1.016–1.191, P = 0.019) and TN of TI-RADS ≥ 4A (OR = 1.213, 95%CI: 1.099–1.340, P < 0.001). However, no statistical risk of TN or TN of TI-RADS ≥ 4A was observed in females with MAFLD.ConclusionThe prevalence of MAFLD and TN is high in Chinese healthy population with difference between males and females; The association between TN or TN of TI-RADS ≥ 4A and MAFLD is only observed in non-obese males.Keyword: Metabolic associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD),Thyroid nodule (TN),Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System(TI-RADS),Obesity

Background

Obesity has emerged as a global health crisis,driving the escalating prevalence of numerous chronic conditions. Extensive researches have established that obesity significantly contributes to the increasing prevalence of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), malignancies and many other chronic diseases [1–5]. Globally, NAFLD affects approximately 25% of the population, and its incidence continues to climb in parallel with increasing rates of type 2 diabetes, obesity and metabolic syndrome [6–8]. Projections indicate that NAFLD is poised to become the leading cause of liver transplantation within the next decade [9, 10]. Historically,NAFLD was regarded as the hepatic manifestation of metabolic disorders. However,reflecting a paradigm shift towards a more precise understanding of its core pathogenesis tootes in metaboloc dysfunction, an international expert panel proposed replacing the term NAFLD with metabolic dysfuntion-associated fatty liver disease(MAFLD) in 2020.This redefinition aims to delineate a distinct patient subset charaterized by liver steatosis linked to defined metabolic abnormalities [11].. Beyond hepatic complications, MAFLD is now recognized as a systemic disorder associated with a spectrum of extrahepatic manifestations, including cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and non-hepatic malignancies [12].Consequently,understanding the systemic impact of MAFLD constitutes a critical area of ongoing reasearch.

Concurrent with the increasing prevalence of MAFLD, thyroid nodules (TNs) have also witnessed a significant rise in clinical practice and health screenings. TNs are predominantly asymptomatic lesions,frequently detected incidentally during ultrasonography examinations, with the majority being benign [13]. However, a subset of TNs carries a risk of malignancy, which is systematically assessed using the Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System (TI-RADS). TI-RADS classifies nodules into grades 1 to 6 based on specific ultrasonography features, malignancy risk escalates with high er TI-RADS grades; we refer to TI-RADS 4 A,TI-RADS 4B,TI-RADS 4 C,TI-RADS 5 collectively as TI-RADS ≥ 4 A, nodules categorized as TI-RADS ≥ 4 A (malignancy risk 5–10% and above) warrant heightened clinical vigilance due to their elevated potential for progressing to thyroid cancer. Individuals with TN of TI-RADS ≥ 4 A warrant intensified public health focus to reduce the increasing incidence and mortality of thyroid cancer [14–17].Thyroid dysfunction and TN were familiar diseases in clinic,thyroid hormones erert a preminet influence on hepatic fatty acid and lipid synthesis and metabolism,hypothyroidism was associated with NAFLD [18, 19].But the association between thyroid nodule (TN) and MAFLD was undefined.Furthermore, no studies have explored potential effect modifications by gender or BMI in this association, which is critical given the known gender disparities in thyroid cancer and the well-documented,complex relationship between BMI and MAFLD.

This knowledge gap constitutes a critical oversight, as clarifying the MAFLD-TN relationship could inform targeted screening and prevention strategies for both conditions. The present study addresses these gaps by utilizing a large cohort of healthy Chinese individuals undergoing concurrent abdominal and thyroid ultrasonography. The innovation resides in its focus on MAFLD (asopposed to NAFLD), its stratification of TNs by TI-RADS malignancy risk, and its investigation of potential effect modification by gender and BMI. Specifically, this cross-sectional study aims to examine the association between MAFLD and TNs, particularly high-risk TNs (TI-RADS ≥ 4 A), in a large cohort of healthy Chinese adults, and to determine whether this association is modified by gender and BMI.

Method

Study design and characteristics of participants

This cross-sectional study utilized data from the Health Management Center of the First Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University which also serves as the Quality Control Center of Health Examination in Chongqing (China). Participants who underwent comprehensive health examinations inclusive of simultaneous abdominal and thyroid ultrasound scans from January 2015 to July 2018 were consecutively identified for enrollment. To prevent duplication, the patients were enrolled using their unique health examination ID and institution information, and only one examination dataset was included selected randomly in the study. Exclusion criteria included: less than 18 years old, history of malignancy, history of thyroid disease, history of liver surgery, history of undergoing radiation therapy of the head or neck region, and unavailable data (age, weight, height, or original descriptions/reports for thyroid and abdominal ultrasonography). Participants were subsequently categorized into overweight & obese and non-obese subgroups based on BMI. For females, menopausal status was defined as premenopausal (≤ 45 years), perimenopausal period (45–54 years) and postmenopausal (≥ 55 years) according to the mean menopausal period of Chinese female population [20].To facilitate age-matching comparisons between sexes,males were similarly stratified into three age groups by analogy with women's menopausal age: ≤ 45 years, 45–54 years, and ≥ 55 years. Thus,both males and females were age-matched and divided into 3 subgroups. This study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University and conducted in accordance with the Principles of the Helsinki Declaration. Requirement for informed consent was waived because all information was anonymous and no intervening measures were enforced on the population.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

For this study,267,645 people were included,first,we excluded 2,772 participants were below 18 years old,then we excluded 72,760 participants with incomplete anthropometric data on their height,weight,waist, blood pressure measurements(systolic and diastolic),46,717 participants who didn’t undergo liver ultrasonography and 51,521 didn’t undergo thyroid ultrasonography were excluded,as well as 12,399 participants who had incomplete hepatic function data and 3,099 had incomplete renal function data on high-density lipoprotein cholesterol,low-density lipoprotein cholesterol,albumin,alanine aminotransferase,aspartate aminotransferase,blood glucose,triglycerides,total bilirubin, blood urea nitrogen,creatinine, uric acid were excluded.Ultimately, our study included 78,077 participants. Procedure

Sex, age and other basic information were verified before examination. Self-reported symptoms, disease histories were recorded. Systolic and diastolic blood pressures and anthropometric measurements (height, weight, waist circumference, and hip circumference) were measured by experienced medical staff following standard procedures. BMI was also calculated, BMI groups of underweight (< 18.5 kg/m2), normal (≥ 18.5 kg/m2, < 23.0 kg/m2), overweight (≥ 23.0 kg/m2, < 25.0 kg/m2) and overweight & obese (≥ 25.0 kg/m2) were categorized based on the BMI criteria for Asians made by the WHO [21]. All blood specimens were taken after at least 8 h of fasting and analyzed in the laboratory of the First Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University, which has been certified by the College of American Pathologists (CAP No.: 7215494). Abdominal and thyroid ultrasound were performed using either a 4.8–11 MHz linear probe (Aplio500; Toshiba Medical Systems, Japan), 5–14 MHz linear probe (Aplio500; Toshiba Medical Systems, Japan), or 3–12 MHz linear probe (HD11XE;Philips Medical Systems, USA). All ultrasound examinations were performed and evaluated by well-trained board-certified sonographer. Ultrasound screening and complete diagnostic examinations were done simultaneously for all subjects. All data were collected and registered into the electronic medical record system of the Quality Control Center of Health Examination in Chongqing. Data were extracted and processed by Microsoft Excel 2016.

TN and MAFLD diagnosis

Participants underwent ultrasound scanning in the supine position. Thyroid size, parenchyma (homogeneous or heterogeneous echo) and existence of TN were recorded in detail. A TN was defined as a discrete lesion within the thyroid gland that is radiologically distinct from the surrounding parenchyma. Specific ultrasound characteristics were illustrated and recorded in detail [22]. Ultrasound features of TN contained following parameters, composition (solid, cystic, mixed or spongiform), echogenicity (hyper-, iso-, hypo- and marked hypoechogenicity), margins (well-circumscribed, microlobulated or irregular), calcifications (micro-, macro or no calcifications), and shape (wider than tall or taller than wide); each TN was examined a second time and classified on the basis of Kwak Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System (TI‐RADS) standards [23].

In this study, MAFLD was diagnosed according to a new international expert consensus statement on the basis of ultrasound diagnosed hepatic steatosis and with one of the following three criteria: overweight or obesity(defined as BMI ≥ 23 kg/m2 in Asians),type 2 diabetes mellitus, or metabolic dysregulation. Metabolic dysregulation was defined as the presence of at least two of the following metabolic risk abnormalities: 1) waist circumference ≥ 90/80 cm in Asian men and women; 2) blood pressure ≥ 130/85 mmHg or undergoing related treatment; 3) plasma triglycerides ≥ 150mg/dL(≥ 1.70 mmol/L)or undergoing related treatment; 4) plasma HDL-cholesterol < 40mg/dL(< 1.0 mmol/L) for men and < 50mg/dL(< 1.3 mmol/L)for women or undergoing related drug treatment; 5) prediabetes (i.e., fasting glucose levels 100 to 125 mg/dL (5.6 to 6.9 mmol/L), or 2-h postprandial glucose levels 140 to 199 mg/dL (7.8 to 11.0 mmol) or HbA1c 5.7% to 6.4% (39–47 mmol/mol)); 6) homeostasis model assessment (HOMA)-insulin resistance score ≥ 2.5; 7) plasma high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) level > 2 mg/L [11].

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were tested for normality and reported as median (interquartile range [IQR]). Kruskal‐Wallis testing was used to compare continuous variables by considering non-normal distribution. The Chi-square test was used to compare categorical variables. Age-specific prevalence and 95% confidence intervals (CI) of TN and MAFLD were calculated. Binary logistic regression was used to acquire odds ratios (OR) and 95% CI for TN and MAFLD. Basic multivariable models were adjusted for age, gender, body mass index (BMI), height, all the blood biomarkers which are associated with TN and MAFLD. Metabolic and biochemical markers were not included in the binary logistic models directly, and additional screening models were used to select covariates, in which both statistical and medical significance was taken into consideration. In multivariable adjusted models, age was categorized into groups according to their univariate logistic transformation values, and other indexes, including systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure(DBP), albumin(ALB), blood urea nitrogen(BUN), aspartate aminotransferase(AST), alanine aminotransferase(ALT), total bilirubin(TB), creatinine, uric acid were analyzed as continuous variables. A multicollinearity test was done (by calculating the proportion of variations and variance inflation factors) for models and no statistical collinearity was found. A two-tailed P < 0.05 was considered significant. All statistical analyses were done with SPSS 25.

Results

Of the 78,077 qualified individuals enrolled in this study, 23,759 (30.4%) had TN (female: 36.7%, male:26%), which was similar to our earlier study [24], 21,021 (26.9%) had MAFLD (female: 13.4%, male:36.4%), and 6,839(8.8%) had combined TN/MAFLD (female: 10.1%, male: 6.8%). Anthropometric and biochemical characteristics of participants are detailed in Table 1. The prevalence of TN or TN of TI-RADS ≥ 4 A increased gradually with age, and increased to 67.4% and 36.0%, respectively in those no more than 80 years old. A similar age-related increase was observed in males, and the prevalence of TN and TN of TI-RADS ≥ 4 A increased to 63.2% and 34.4%, respectively in those no less than 80 years old. In females,the highest prevalence of both TN(78.5%) and TI-RADS ≥ 4 A TN(42.6%) occurred in 75–79 years age group. The age-specific prevalence of TN and TN of TI-RADS ≥ 4 A in males and females are illustrated in Figs. 2 and 3. In contrast, the prevalence of MAFLD exhibited a different patter:it increased modestly with age,peaking at 36.5% in the 55–59 years age group, before gradually declining. The peak prevalence of MAFLD differed by sex,occurring in males aged 50–54 years (43.9%) and females aged 65–69 years (36.6%), as shown in Fig. 4.

Discussion

TN is a common and asymptomatic disease which is usually discovered by ultrasonography while undergoing physical health examination [25, 26]. The majority of individuals diagnosed with TN don’t require surgical treatment. However,TNs classified as TI-RADS ≥ 4 A warrant increased clinical vigilance due totheir addociated risk of thyroid cancer. Thyroid cancer is the most common and prevalent of all endocrine-based malignant tumors. It occupies more than 95% of all endocrine-related cancers [16, 17]. The incidence of thyroid cancer in the United States increased from 4.56 per 100,000 person during 1974–1977 to 14.42 per 100,000 person during 2010–2013, reflecting an average annual increase of 3.6%, this trend was predominantly driven by an increase in papillary thyroid carcinima. Concurrently, the incidence-based mortality increased from 0.40 per 100,000 person-years during 1994–1997 to 0.46 per 100,000 person-years during 2010–2013 [18]. It was worth noting that a study observed that thyroid cancer has become one of the most common malignant tumor in adolescent and young people with over 25% new thyroid cancer occurred in the 20 to 40 years age population in the united states in 2008 [19].

Obesity represents a critical global public health challenge and serves as a major risk factor for NAFLD. The global prevalence of NAFLD is approximately 25%, and has risen in parallel with che escalating prevalence of obesity, diabetes and metabolic syndrome. Previous research has established NAFLD as the hepatic manifestation of metabolic disorders, including obesity, insulin resistance, hypertension and dyslipidemia. Consequently,the diagnostic classification has shifted from NAFLD to MAFLD,defined by specific criteria [8, 11, 27].

Conclusions

This study explores the prevalence of TN and MAFLD. The prevalence of TN increased steadily with age, and decreased slightly after the age of 80 years in overall population. The prevalence of TN in females was similar to the overall population while males exhibited a sustained increase throughout the observed age range. The prevalence of MAFLD in our study is consistent with our proor researcn. In comparison to females, the males exhibited a significantly higher prevalence of MAFLD, and the age-specific prevalence showed a rapid increase in the prevalence of MAFLD until the age of 40, and the peaked within the 50–55 age group in males; Conversely,among females, prevalence rose tardily prior to 45 years of age while it rose rapidly after 45 years old. Moreover, we observed that the prevalence of MAFLD showed an opposite trend after 55 years age. The possible reasons for the difference between the prevalence of MAFLD in males and females were discussed in our earlier study [28, 29]. To our knowledge, this study represents the first investigation to focus on the association between TN and MAFLD in the urban Chinese population since the declaration of novel MAFLD dedinition. Since, no influence was observed with detection bias, which can be present in individuals undergoing screening tests due to preexisting TN and MAFLD, the results of the present study are believed to be robust and better reflect the intrinsic relationship between TN and MAFLD, the results of our study can inform tailored health recommendations,especially the population in higher risk of/TN ≥ 4 A subgroups,and future research avenues could be explored if the pathology results were accessible,Previous study found that estrogen receptors can be expressed in hepatic tissues, and the inactivation of estrogen mainly occurs in the liver. Estrogen has significant contribution to the lipid metabolism, which may contribute to the higher prevalence of MAFLD in males than females; This could account for the rapid increase in the prevalence of MAFLD in females after 50 years old; which is usually around the perimenopausal period [30]. Estrogen expresses in the thyroid tissues as well, and the expression in TN is higher than in natural thyroid tissue obviously. Estrogen has also been considered to play a role in stimulating growth and decreasing the differentiation of TN‐derived stem and/or progenitor cells [31–33]. In the pathgenesis of MAFLD, the estrogen metabolism in liver decreases, resulting in more estrogens residual in serum. This facilitates the germination of TN, which may be the reason for higher prevalence of TN/TN ≥ 4 A in MAFLD compared to non-MAFLD group [16, 34]. Furthermore, we observed another strange phenomenon, the difference of prevalence of TN/TN ≥ 4 A in MAFLD and non-MAFLD occurs only among males, especially in non-obese males. We hypothesize the possible reason could be that the residual estrogen in the non-obese men with MAFLD could have a higher estrogen concentration than among overweight & obese men; hence, the effect of estrogen facilitates the recession of TN. But why there is no significant difference between females with MAFLD and non-MAFLD? We surmised that the high cardinal estrogen concentration in women covered up the increased estrogen concentration caused by MAFLD.

The current study is subject to several limitations. Firstly, the participant cohort was exclusively drawn from an urban area, so the prevalence of TN and MAFLD predominately reflected the status in urban populations of southwest China. Secondly, the selection of participants underwent health examination may introduce selection bias,as this population inherently exbibits greater health-consciousness and proactive health-seeking behaviors.. Thirdly, the diagnosis of MAFLD was relied on ultrasonography,a modality with recongnized limitations in detecting.Nonetheless,ultrasongraphy remains the recommended first-line imaging technique in the clinical guidelines for MAFLD issued by the Association for the Study of the Liver (APASL), justified by its safety profile, non-invasive, widespread and acceptable diagnostic sensitivity and specificity for detection of hepatic steatosis.

In conclusion,our findings demonstrate a high prevalence of TN and MAFLD detected ultrasound screening within an asymptomatic population. Furthermore,a significant disparity in MAFLD prevalence was identified between males and females. specifically, males with BMI < 18.5, BMI ≥ 18.5 and < 23 showed higher risk of TN of TI-RADS ≥ 4A. Moreover,an increased prevalence of TN correlated positively with elevated ALT levels and inversely with aspartate AST levels. Further investigation into the interplay between these conditions and the potential underlying mechanisms is warranted,as it may yield valuble insights for enhancing the the early screening strategies for both TN and MAFLD.

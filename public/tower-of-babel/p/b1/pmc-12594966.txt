A study on parametric design of ceramic liquor bottles by combining grey relational analysis and particle swarm optimization support vector regression
Authors: Wei Guo, Xing Lu, Zhenwei Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractNowadays, with the vigorous development of cultural and creative industries, the design of traditional cultural products often depends on the designer’s personal artistic accomplishment and aesthetic judgment. However, the cognitive difference between designers and consumers will lead to the fact that cultural and creative products cannot be recognized by consumers when they are introduced to the market. In order to seek the design method with intelligence assistance and better design cultural products that can be in line with users’ emotional intentions, a parametric design method of cultural and creative products based on Kansei engineering, integrating grey relational analysis (GRA) and particle swarm optimization-support vector regression (PSO-SVR) is proposed in this paper. It aims at accurately quantifying users’ emotional needs and mapping them into product design parameters. Firstly, web crawler and expert interview are used to collect users’ emotional needs and product samples; and the morphological deconstruction of products is carried out as well. Secondly, GRA is used to calculate the optimal weight of users’ emotional needs and extract the key emotional needs. Finally, PSO-SVR is used to construct the mapping model of key users’ emotional needs and morphological characteristics so as to determine the optimal design parameters of ceramic liquor bottles. The result shows that the most critical emotional needs are “tough”, “fashionable” and “exquisite”. In addition, the evaluation results of the optimized design scheme have a high degree of fit with the prediction results of the mapping model, which verifies the feasibility of the proposed method. Compared with traditional cultural and creative products, this research can better balance the subjective aesthetics of designers and the objective needs of consumers, which can help enterprises build a communication bridge between users and designers, accurately predict the emotional needs of users, reduce the risk of product development failure and improve the market competitiveness of enterprises.Keywords: Traditional cultural product, Kansei engineering, Grey relational analysis, Particle swarm optimization-support vector regression, Ceramic liquor bottleSubject terms: Scientific data, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

Culture is acknowledged worldwide as a vital contributor to a sustainable creative economy. It not only fosters economic growth but also introduces the distinctiveness of global culture to the international market1,2. As a unique cultural carrier, cultural products not only carry rich history and traditional crafts, but also contain profound cultural connotations3. With exquisite design, delicate craft and diverse forms of expression, they show the artistic style and cultural characteristics of different regions and periods4. In modern society, cultural products are not only items in daily life, but also become an important part of cultural and creative industries. By combining traditional skills with innovative design, cultural products can attract more consumers’ attention; thus the market value of cultural products can be enhanced5. At the same time, this kind of integration also provides a new way for cultural transmission and promotes people’s cognition and appreciation of traditional culture6. Therefore, cultural products play an indispensable role in promoting the development of cultural industries and enhancing cultural influence.

Cultural products serve a dual role: they are both material consumer goods and vehicles for cultural reflection. To meet modern market demands, their design must prioritize product quality—balancing technical functionality, usability, and cultural depth to ensure user satisfaction7. In today’s market, purchasing decisions are no longer driven solely by practicality; consumers seek products that fulfill emotional needs, enable self-expression, and foster social connections. Thus, successful cultural products must achieve emotional resonance while excelling in functional performance8. User evaluations of cultural products extend beyond ease of use to include the cultural experience and emotional impact evoked by design. This first impression is critical to competitiveness9. To stand out, designers must integrate authentic cultural elements into products through systematic innovation—transforming traditional narratives into tangible features that align with user preferences10,11. However, current design practices often lack structured methodologies, relying instead on subjective designer judgment. This approach risks misalignment with user expectations, as it overlooks empirical analysis of emotional needs and market feedback12. The absence of objective evaluation mechanisms further exacerbates this gap. Without robust channels to capture user preferences, many cultural products fail to adapt to diversified demands, increasing development risks and market rejection12. To address this, the industry must shift toward user-centered frameworks that harmonize cultural authenticity with measurable quality standards. By embedding such parameters into the design process, products can achieve deeper cultural resonance while maintaining functional rigor13.

Among the research methods that arouse users’ emotional resonance, Kansei Engineering (KE) is a translation model that concerts users’ emotional needs into design product parameters14. Japan is the first country to apply KE to automotive modeling and interior design15, electronic furniture product design. Since then, KE has been widely used in service design16, fashion design17 and other fields. KE can help enterprises to obtain users’ emotional needs and build a communication bridge between designers and consumers. However, in the field of cultural and creative products, the relevant researches of KE mainly focus on how to explore culture and apply it to product design, but lacks a systematic design process. Therefore, the innovation of this research is to use KE to extract the emotional intention of specific cultural products, and then use intelligent parametric design method to transform the emotional intention into product parameters and apply it in the development process of cultural and creative products. In order to seek the design methods with the intelligence assistance and better design cultural products which can be in line with users’ emotional intentions18, a research framework for cultural and creative products based on KE in combination with grey relational analysis (GRA) and particle swarm optimization-support vector regression (PSO-SVR) is proposed in this paper. The weight and priority of emotional needs are determined by GRA; and the mapping function between products and emotional needs is constructed by PSO-SVR. The integration of GRA and PSO-SVR is motivated by the unique challenges in ceramic liquor bottle parametric design. First, GRA is adopted to rank design parameters and derive weights because ceramic production involves multiple interacting factors with limited historical data, which aligns with GRA’s strength in handling small, incomplete datasets and nonlinear relationships. Unlike entropy weight, GRA quantifies dynamic correlations without strict distribution assumptions. Second, PSO is employed to optimize SVR hyperparameters due to its global search capability and computational efficiency. Ceramic design requires balancing multiple Kansei objectives, and PSO avoids local optima traps common in grid search19. Finally, SVR is chosen as the predictor for its robustness in modeling small-sample, high-dimensional nonlinear problems. Its kernel function adapts to complex design constraints, outperforming linear regression or neural networks when training data is scarce20. A comprehensive review of intelligent design systems reveals a critical research gap in their application to cultural ceramic liquor bottles, particularly the lack of methodologies that simultaneously address Kansei emotional modeling, craftsmanship constraints, and data-driven optimization. While existing approaches treat these aspects in isolation, no prior study has developed an integrated framework combining GRA for quantifying nonlinear relationships between cultural design elements and user emotions with PSO-SVR to overcome the high-dimensional, small-sample challenges in Kansei-design mapping. This study bridges this gap by introducing a novel hybrid approach where GRA identifies key design features and PSO-SVR dynamically optimizes solutions while accounting for ceramic-specific production constraints, enabling both explainable and adaptive intelligent design. The proposed GRA-PSO-SVR pipeline represents the first attempt to systematically align traditional craftsmanship requirements with modern computational design optimization in this culturally significant product category, offering real-time refinement of designs based on evolving user preferences while maintaining manufacturing feasibility.The remainder of this study is structured as follows. The second section presents a literature review along with an introduction to key concepts such as KE, GRA, PSO-SVR, and other relevant methods. In the third section, the author outlines the research framework and its application to cultural and creative products. The fourth section includes an analysis and discussion of the research findings. Finally, the fifth section provides a summary of the conclusions drawn from this study and offers suggestions for future research.

Review of the relevant literature

Literature review on KE

KE is a design methodology with users’ emotion and experience as the core21. It aims to optimize the design of products and services by understanding and analyzing users’ emotional needs22. Since its rise in Japan in the early 1990 s, KE has gradually developed into an interdisciplinary research field, involving psychology, design, engineering and other fields23,24. First of all, the core concept of KE is “sensibility”, which not only includes users’ subjective feelings about products, but also covers users’ emotional reactions in the process of using products25. The emotional experience of users directly affects their satisfaction and loyalty to the product. Therefore, in the product design stage, understanding the emotional needs of users becomes particularly important. Secondly, the methodology of KE mainly includes steps such as emotion vocabulary construction, emotion evaluation model and design mapping. In recent years, with the development of science and technology, especially the progress of artificial intelligence and big data technology, KE is also constantly evolving. More and more studies have begun to explore how to use machine learning and data mining technology to extract value information from massive user data to better meet the emotional needs of users26. Lei et al.27proposed a method combining mixed Apriori with structural equation model system to quantity design features and Kansei evaluation; and case studies on a large number of cars, brands and reviews are conducted. Yang et al.28 used image generation algorithm to build product image data set; support vector regression is used to build prediction model; the ear thermometer is taken as an example to create a product form design method to meet users’ needs. Chen et al.29 deconstructed and encoded patterns based on morphological analysis, built a mapping model between users’ perception images and design elements through BPNN; and guiding clothing paper-cutting is taken as the research object to verify the feasibility of this system, aimed to help enterprises better understand consumers’ Kansei needs for patterns. Kang et al.30 extracted the symbolic elements of Jiangxi cultural and creative products by using semiotics theory, coded the symbolic elements by using interactive genetic algorithm, built the form system of cultural and creative produce, which integrated Jiangxi cultural genes into watches and guided the design practice. To sum up, KE is in combination with artificial intelligence technology has been widely used in product design, clothing design and other fields. Therefore, the dynamic adjustment of cultural and creative products’ design parameters can be realized by combining KE and artificial intelligence technology, which can provide an innovative method for the parametric design of cultural and creative products. By building a flexible parametric model, designers are able to continuously optimize product design based on real-time user feedback. This can not only improve the design efficiency, but also better meet the increasingly diversified and personalized needs of users, which can inject new vitality into the development of cultural and creative industry.

Literature review on GRA

GRA is a multivariate statistical analysis method derived from grey system theory, which is widely used in engineering, management, economics and environmental science and other fields31,32. Its basic principle is to reveal the internal relationship by calculating the correlation degree between different factors, which usually includes steps such as data standardization, establishment of reference sequence and comparison sequence, calculation of correlation coefficient and comprehensive evaluation33. With its simplicity and effectiveness, GRA can identify users’ emotional needs in product design and provide a basis for decision making34. On this basis, Xue et al.35 integrated GRA and quantitative theory type I into the design of train seat. Kang36 combined GRA and neural network to develop an automobile booth design that meets the emotional needs of users. Lin et al.37 combined KE, factor analysis, GRA and other methods to evaluate the subjective preference of AR dashboard of intelligent vehicles through factor experiments, and guide the design practice of AR dashboard. In other research domains, Dominika et al.38 developed a systematic model integrating GRA with multiple quality management methodologies. This framework effectively identifies root causes of quality instability in industrial materials and prioritizes corrective measures to enhance product quality consistency. Similarly, Andrzej et al.39 employed GRA in conjunction with Life Cycle Assessment to construct a predictive model. This innovative approach enables concurrent evaluation of both environmental impact and quality performance in new product designs, facilitating optimal selection of sustainable materials. In summary, GRA has the advantage of dealing with uncertainty and fuzziness, and can calculate the relative weight value and priority of key factors through quantitative analysis so as to provide a more reliable basis for decision-making. Therefore, the GRA is chosen to use in this study to more accurately identify the optimal Kansei needs in complex affective cognition and summarize the cognitive rules and priorities in consumer emotion.

Literature review on PSO-SVR

PSO-SVR is commonly applied in areas such as function approximation, pattern recognition, and forecasting40. SVR is valued for its powerful nonlinear ability and good generalization performance, but the selection of model parameters has a significant impact on the prediction accuracy. Traditional parameter selection methods mostly rely on experience or grid search, which may lead to low efficiency and local optimal solution problems. Therefore, PSO, as an optimization algorithm based on swarm intelligence, can effectively search for the global optimal solution by simulating the foraging behavior of birds, thus improving the performance of SVR method41. Fu et al.23 used PSO-SVR to construct an intelligent evaluation system between key design features and key emotional needs of Ming-style furniture so as to capture consumers’ optimal emotional needs for Ming-style furniture. Liu et al.42 used PSO-SVR to build a mapping model between new energy vehicle product elements and users’ Kansei needs to guide the development of practical design cases. Ren et al.43 integrated generative adversarial network and PSO-SVR; the design is used for the development of emotional social robots. To sum up, PSO-SVR has been widely used in the fields of Kansei needs analysis and product design optimization. Therefore, PSO-SVR is used to build nonlinear mapping model. By optimizing parameter settings, the model’s understanding and response ability to users’ needs are improved. The mapping relationship between users’ Kansei needs and product elements is accurately established.

Research framework of ceramic liquor bottles based on KE

This paper proposes a research framework for the parametric design of cultural and creative products by integrating KE, GRA, PSO-SVR. The framework addresses two key challenges in cultural and creative product design: (1) insufficient understanding of user needs and (2) difficulties in optimizing design parameters. By leveraging these methodologies, the study enables in-depth mining of complex user preferences and intelligent optimization of design parameters. The research unfolds in three stages: First, user requirements are collected and analyzed using GRA to identify key influencing factors. Second, PSO-SVR constructs a mapping model that correlates critical Kansei needs with product design parameters. Finally, the design team generates a series of product schemes based on the optimized parameters (Fig. 1).

The detailed research framework.

To ensure methodological rigor and applicability across diverse product domains, practitioners should follow a structured yet adaptable workflow: (1) Customize Kansei word selection and user surveys for the target product category, validating terms through expert review or preliminary studies; (2) Apply consistent normalization and distinguishing coefficients in GRA to objectively prioritize needs, with expert verification; (3) Optimize PSO-SVR hyperparameters and standardize design parameters for model robustness; and (4) Iteratively refine the model by comparing predicted user satisfaction with prototype evaluations, explicitly documenting parameter constraints. This replicable approach maintains methodological integrity while accommodating variations in product types.

Application of ceramic liquor bottles

Collection of ceramic liquor bottles’ Kansei images

To ensure efficient data collection, this study employed web crawler technology to systematically gather ceramic liquor bottle images from official brand websites and social media platforms. This automated approach addressed the inefficiency of manual searches while compiling a comprehensive dataset (N = 143 raw images) representative of current market offerings. The expert panel was rigorously selected based on three criteria: (1) minimum 3 years of professional experience in ceramic product design (for practitioners) or advanced coursework in cultural product design (for graduate students); (2) demonstrated knowledge of Kansei engineering principles; and (3) active involvement in liquor packaging design projects. The 30-member panel (15 professional designers + 15 graduate students) provided balanced perspectives combining industry practice and academic training. Through three iterative screening rounds, the panel eliminated duplicates (n = 37), low-resolution images (n = 46), and non-standard angles (n = 10), then evaluated remaining samples for cultural representativeness using a 5-point Likert scale (ICC = 0.82). Final selections (n = 50) underwent standardized preprocessing: background unification (white), size normalization (100 × 100 mm), and color calibration (sRGB profile) to isolate form-related perceptual features, as shown in Fig. 3.

Morphological analysis is used to analyze and deconstruct the design features of the ceramic liquor bottles. Design practice and evaluation of ceramic liquor bottles

Due to the need to get the products that best meet the emotional needs of users, the mapping model constructed in the previous stage is taken as the basis and three key emotional needs are taken as the drive. Namely, they are tough, fashionable and exquisite. The reliability of this method is verified.

A total of 5 morphological features and 6 × 3 × 4 × 4 × 3 = 864 design combinations are obtained by morphological deconstruction method. The design combinations are coded and respectively input into the PSO-SVR mapping model for prediction. Discussion

Under the research framework of KE, the GRA is integrated with the parametric design method of PSO-SVR in this study for cultural and creative products. The ceramic liquor bottle is taken as a case study to verify the feasibility of this method, which provides references and theoretical guidance for related research. The proposed method not only has wide applicability, but also can be effectively applied to local products with specific emotional semantics. For specific products, through the collection of a large number of samples and Kansei words, developers are helped from a variety of consumer needs to accurately extract the key emotional needs; thus the direction of product design can be guided. The morphological deconstruction method is used to deconstruct the form of the product so as to make clear about the design elements and form characteristics. A parametric evaluation system is established through PSO-SVR; a mapping function between morphological characteristics and key emotional needs is constructed so as to predict the latest product parameter combination that meets the users’ aesthetic preference and enhance the emotional value of the product.

Comparison of GRA and traditional KE methods of determining weights

In today’s fiercely competitive and ever-changing market environment, accurately identifying and evaluating product feature weights has become a crucial aspect of corporate product strategy formulation and design optimization. Traditional single-weight decision-making methods, such as Factor Analysis (FA), Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP)49, Conjoint Analysis (CA) and Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (Fuzzy AHP)50, often exhibit limitations when confronted with complex and dynamic market demands. Factor Analysis relies on linear relationships in data and is suitable for scenarios with strong correlations between variables, but in practical applications, it struggles to comprehensively reflect the influence of each feature on consumer decisions51. Although AHP determines the relative weights of factors by constructing a hierarchical structure model, it is highly subjective, prone to decision-maker bias, and faces challenges in maintaining consensus when multiple decision-makers are involved. Fuzzy AHP, while mitigating subjectivity to some extent by introducing fuzzy logic to handle uncertainty, involves complex computations and still depends on experts’ subjective judgments, thus remaining challenging in practical applications. In contrast, this study employs GRA to extract users’ optimal emotional needs. This method effectively handles small sample sizes and incomplete information by calculating the correlation degree between each factor and the target, providing a more comprehensive reflection of each feature’s importance. Additionally, GRA calculates correlation degrees based on objective data, effectively integrating expert subjective ratings with user demand-driven objective data, thereby enhancing the scientific rigor and accuracy of decision-making and aiding in identifying key emotional needs44. Meanwhile, CA, a commonly used preference modeling method in market research, is also frequently applied to obtain weights and part-worth utilities. By simulating consumer choice behavior among multiple attribute combinations, CA derives the relative importance of each feature, demonstrating high empirical validity and user behavior simulation capabilities. However, CA imposes higher requirements on sample size and experimental design, particularly when dealing with multidimensional and complex product features, significantly increasing experimental design costs and analytical complexity. Compared with GRA, CA is more suitable for large-sample and standardized feature sets, whereas GRA maintains robust performance even with limited samples and incomplete features. Therefore, GRA offers a more flexible and efficient approach to identifying product feature weights in complex market environments. The advantages of GRA over other methods are given in Table 9.

In recent years, research paradigms for mapping product features to users’ emotional needs have gradually shifted from traditional qualitative methods to data-driven intelligent algorithm models, particularly machine learning approaches represented by BPNN and SVR, along with their optimized variants. However, single-algorithm models still exhibit significant performance bottlenecks in practical engineering applications, prompting researchers to adopt meta-optimization strategies like “algorithm-optimizing-algorithm” to enhance model performance. Specifically, although BPNN possesses strong nonlinear fitting capabilities, its gradient descent-based training mechanism is highly sensitive to initial weights, making it prone to converge to local optima. Additionally, it demonstrates poor generalization stability in small-sample scenarios, and its hidden layer structural parameters often rely on trial-and-error experimentation, resulting in inefficient modeling60. On the other hand, while SVR models have a simple structure, their performance heavily depends on kernel function selection and parameter tuning. Traditional grid search methods for hyperparameter optimization are not only computationally expensive but also struggle to achieve global optima. Furthermore, as feature dimensions increase, the computational complexity of traditional SVR grows exponentially, severely limiting its application in complex product design scenarios. To address these limitations, this study employs PSO to enhance SVR’s parameter tuning process, offering distinct advantages over conventional approaches. The PSO-SVR hybrid model demonstrates superior performance in three key aspects: (1) Global optimization capability - PSO’s population-based search mechanism effectively avoids local optima traps common in gradient-based methods, enabling more reliable identification of optimal kernel parameters (C, γ, ε) for SVR; (2) Computational efficiency - Compared to exhaustive grid search, PSO reduces parameter tuning time by 40–60% through its intelligent parallel search strategy, as evidenced by benchmark tests on emotional need datasets; (3) Adaptive scalability - The PSO framework naturally accommodates high-dimensional feature spaces, maintaining stable optimization performance even when processing 50 + product characteristics.

Conclusions

In order to maximum benefits, companies need to accurately identify users’ needs and effectively translate these needs into product design and engineering features. The investment of enterprises is greatly determined by consumers’ satisfaction. In this study, a parametric design method integrating KE, GRA and PSO-SVR for cultural and creative products is proposed to quantify emotional needs and build a parametric design evaluation model. On the one hand, the previous users’ researches mostly used a single weight method, which could not accurately measure the users’ emotion, resulting in a direction of cheap product. Therefore, in this study, GRA is used to calculate the optimal weight and extract the key emotional needs; namely, tough, fashionable and exquisite. On the other hand, the traditional mapping model method has the problem of too high loss rate in the construction of nonlinear functions. In this study, PSO-SVR is used to construct the mapping model between key emotional needs and morphological features, and the optimal design scheme to meet the various Kansei needs is obtained. After the completion of the design scheme, it can be seen from the evaluation results of the subjects that compared with the predicted results, the error mean is within the reasonable error range of ± 0.5, which proves the reliability of the method. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

(1) A KE model integrating GRA and PSO-SVR is proposed to optimize the parametric design path of cultural and creative products.

(2) The comprehensive weight method of GRA is introduced to determine the key emotional needs while retaining the opinions of experts and users, which avoid the limitations of the previous single weighting methods.

(3) PSO-SVR method is used to build a mapping model, which not only combines the powerful ability of SVR is the modeling process and the advantage of PSO in searching the optimal model parameters, but also can effectively deal with a large number of variables involved in complex product design and their nonlinear relations.

However, we must acknowledge that this study still has certain limitations, which require further refinement in future research: (1)The product form is deconstructed only from a single two-dimensional perspective in this study. In the future, the introduction of computer-aided design can be considered to study three-dimensional space models. (2) The design process in this study relies on conventional modeling and rendering methods, which may limit efficiency. Implementing AI-assisted design tools, such as generative design algorithms or machine learning-based style transfer, could significantly accelerate prototyping while maintaining creative flexibility. Additionally, the current approach treats users as a homogeneous group without considering variations across different demographics, consumption levels, or cultural backgrounds. Future work should employ clustering analysis or consumer segmentation models to identify distinct user profiles, enabling more precise investigations into how specific product attributes align with different consumer needs. These enhancements would provide deeper insights for targeted product development strategies.

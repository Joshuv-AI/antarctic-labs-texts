Environmental sustainability and waste conversion of Prosopis juliflora fibre-reinforced ZnO nanofiller particulates PLA composite- mechanical and thermal analysis
Authors: Raja Thandavamoorthy, Vinayagam Mohanavel, A. Sivapragasam, Vipul Vekariya, Dipen Paul, Palanivel Velmurugan, Sami Al Obaid, Sulaiman Ali Alharbi, Nagaraj Basavegowda
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The presence of large quantities of Prosopis juliflora (PJ) fibers in natural habitats presents substantial threats to the environment and economies of numerous developing countries. Utilizing natural fibers in polymer composites can effectively enhance their characteristics. The primary objective of this study is to create a composite material by combining Prosopis Juliflora (PJ) fiber with a polylactic matrix that has been combined with zinc oxide nanofillers. The fabrication process will involve the hand layup technique. In order to have a comprehensive understanding of the mechanical characteristics, thermal behavior, and thermal stability of the PJ composite, it is necessary to undertake additional investigations. The results showed that the inclusion of zinc oxide filler enhanced the tensile strength (67.29 MPa), flexural strength (64.27 MPa), compressive strength (56.79 MPa), and impact energy (34 J) in sample S5. Additionally, the thermal properties, including thermal conductivity, thermal expansion, and short-term heat resistant capacity, were also improved by the addition of zinc oxide filler in sample S5. The deterioration temperature of the PJ composite was determined to be between 312 and 342 °C using thermogravimetric analysis. The failure mode of the PJ composite was investigated using scanning electron microscopy.

Introduction

There has been a significant surge of interest in natural fiber-reinforced polymer composites due to the growing emphasis on sustainable and environmentally responsible materials [1]. Conventional synthetic fibers, despite their impressive mechanical characteristics, frequently originate from finite resources and make substantial contributions to the environmental deterioration [2]. On the other hand, natural fibers derived from sustainable and biodegradable sources present a potentially advantageous option for addressing the increasing need for lightweight, environmentally friendly, and economically feasible materials in various sectors. Natural fiber polymer composites (NFPCs) are materials that integrate the advantageous characteristics of reinforcing fibers and polymer matrices, hence yielding hybrid materials with a unique array of features. These composite materials combine the inherent mechanical benefits of fibers, like high strength, modulus, and low density, with the adaptability, ease of processing, and corrosion resistance of polymers [3]. NFPCs can be customized to meet specific application needs by carefully choosing and optimizing the blend of fibers and polymers. This adaptability makes NFPCs suitable for various industries such as automotive, construction, aerospace, and consumer products. Combining natural fibers and polymers solves problems associated with waste management and carbon emission reduction simultaneously, as it lessens our dependency on nonrenewable resources [4]. Natural fibers have a less environmental impact than synthetic materials since they decompose quickly and can be processed with less energy. These features make NFPCs appealing to researchers, producers, and policymakers since they correspond with sustainability, circular economy, and reduced environmental impact [5]. Due to their economic worth and therapeutic advantages, members of the Prosopis plant family have been highly valued since ancient times. Scientific studies have demonstrated that the paste, gum, and smoke derived from the leaves and pods had advantageous properties in combating cancer, diabetes, inflammation, and infection. Identification of bactericidal and bacteriostatic interactions between antibacterial medicines and their bacterial targets is necessary [6]. Bacteriostatic drugs inhibit microbial growth momentarily, but the process restarts without treatment.

In contrast, bactericidal drugs are highly selective in the microorganisms they kill. Prosopis juliflora fiber (PJF) was analyzed, and it was determined to have a cellulose concentration of 61.65 %. The density of PJF was calculated to be 580 kg/m3, significantly lower than that of water [7].

Various bioactive compounds have been isolated from extracts of Juliflora leaves, including alkaloids, flavonoids, tannins, saponins, anthraquinones, and coumarins. Maximum elongation strength was 55.8 MPa, and an average microfibril angle of 10.64°. It was discovered that the mechanical properties of the final product were significantly affected by the aspect ratio of the PJ fibers. The best mechanical properties were achieved with a fiber aspect ratio of 136 and a fibre loading of 23.53 wt. Percent [8]. The biodegradable thermoplastic poly (lactic acid), or PLA, has great potential as a polymer matrix for composite materials because of its low cost and high recycling rate. Multifunctional composite materials with improved mechanical, thermal, and antibacterial properties may result from the synergistic coupling of Prosopis juliflora fibers and ZnO nanofiller particles within a PLA matrix [9]. Incorporating nanofillers can improve interfacial interactions and matrix reinforcement, increasing the mechanical properties of natural fiber-reinforced composites that are otherwise limited by variables like fiber-matrix adhesion and dispersion [10]. Adding ZnO nanoparticles, advantageous due to their high aspect ratio, unusual electrical properties, and antibacterial qualities, can provide the composite with a new level of utility [11]. Processing a bio-composite made of Prosopis juliflora fiber (PJF) and polylactic acid (PLA) took into account the use of coarse PJF (average particle size 15 m) and fine PJF (10–50 nm) as reinforcements. Each was mixed into a PLA matrix with a weight percentage of 10, 15, 20, and 25 %. As a result, the hybrid composite made from fine PJ fibers exhibited outstanding mechanical and thermal properties [12]. The characterization of Prosopis juliflora fibers involves several key properties measured through various methods. The fiber diameter is 120 μm as observed using Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM). Tensile strength is notably high, falling 18.7 MPa, with an elongation at break of 2–3 percent, both determined via tensile testing [13]. The fibers exhibit a density of 1.29 g/cm³ as determined by a pycnometer, and a moisture content of 8.9 percent as established by the oven drying technique [14]. Thermal stability of the fibers is observed at 200–250 °C using Thermogravimetric Analysis (TGA). Chemically, the cellulose content is about 45–55 percent, lignin content is around 20 to 25 percent, and ash content is between 1 and 2 percent, analyzed through chemical extraction, Klason Lignin Method, and incineration method, respectively [15].

The purpose of this literature review is to determine the appropriate materials for the production of a composite material consisting of Prosopis juliflora fibers reinforced (PJFR) with zinc oxide nanoparticles, which will be blended with an epoxy polymer matrix. The fabrication procedure will utilise the conventional manual layup technique. The aim of this work is to evaluate the mechanical, thermal, and morphological characteristics of a newly designed PJ composite, taking into account changes in fillers and matrix weight percentage. Additionally, the study aims to determine the most efficient sequence for fabricating automobile parts, medical instruments, and structural applications using this composite material.

Materials and methods

Go Green, based in Tamil Nadu, India, provides the Prosopis juliflora fibre for the reinforcement phase, which includes zinc oxide nanofiller. The present work involved the blending of polylactic acid (PLA) with tetramethyl guanidine (TMG), and its subsequent use as the matrix phase in a thermoplastic polyester resin. When the catalyst is introduced to the resin in a 10:2 proportion, a chemical reaction takes place [16], producing thermal energy and immobilizing (hardening) the resin. The matrix material was supplied by Javanthi enterprises situated in India.

Fabrication process of composite

The traditional manual layup method was used to manufacture composite laminates because of its simplicity, low operational costs, and capacity to maintain a uniform thickness of the laminate throughout time. Quantitative analysis of the mechanical, thermal, and morphological properties of this composite was conducted by fabricating five specimens with varying filler and matrix weight amounts. Upon thoroughly immersing the 20 × 20 cm2 hardwood mould box, the laminate sheet can be applied using a liquid wax mould release agent and then removed without causing any harm after it has solidified [17]. The PLA resin and filler components are homogeneously combined using an electric mixer and thereafter blended with the hardener in a 10:2 volumetric ratio. A layer of blended filler matrix is applied to the mould box, followed by a coating of bidirectional woven Prosopis juliflora fabric. The procedure is repeated until the desired thickness of five coatings of fiber reinforcement is achieved. In order to minimise the occurrence of air pockets and porosity in the composite laminates, a weight of 15 kg was imposed on the mould box and retained under compression for a period of 24 h. Following a 3-h curing time at 135 °C in a hot air oven with a heating rate of 3 °C per minute, the comprehensive wetted fibre laminated composite undergoes a subsequent 3-h post-cure at 115 °C [18]. Supplementary samples consisting of varying weight percentages of matrix and filler components were also produced. Experimental testing of PJ composite material

The influence of fibre weight fraction on the performance of the hybrid composite is quantified by experimental characterizations, including tensile, compression, flexural, and Izod impact tests. ASTM method D638 is standard for a tensile test of the hybrid composite, and UTM (FIE UTN-40) is used to conduct this test [19]. Moreover, the hybrid composite undergoes a compressive test utilizing the same UTM by ASTM method D695. A rectangular cross-sectioned bar is supported by a beam and deflected at a constant rate to evaluate the flexural characteristics of polymer matrix composites in a three-point bending test, as specified by ASTM D790 [20]. A Scanning Electron Microscope examines the hybrid composite's surface interaction and morphology under JSM-IT510-JEOL nano SEM. The hybrid composites' impact resistance is measured using the Izod impact test, which is conducted following ASTM method D256 [21].

At a mean temperature of 600 °C, a heat flow meter (Unitherm model 2022, ANTER corp., Pittsburgh, PA) is used to measure the thermal conductivity of the hybrid composite to determine its thermal insulation performance following the ASTM E1530 standard. The VICAT softening apparatus calibrates the heat deflection temperature of a hybrid composite according to the specifications of ASTM D648. It is used to identify the hybrid composite's short-term heat resistance and examine whether or not it can withstand minimal loads at high temperatures [22]. Dilatometer measurements of linear thermal expansion coefficientfollow the ASTM D696 standard for hybrid composites. The present methodology can be employed to ascertain the thermal expansion of the hybrid composite and identify the point of failure resulting from the thermal stress created. The analytical TGA required by ASTM E1131 is conducted using the NETZSCH STA 409 PC/PG system. In this study, a standard hybrid composite sample weighing 13 mg is heated at a rate of 5 °C/min in an inert gas (Argon) flowing at a rate of 10 ml/min inside a temperature range of 32–600 °C [23].

Results and discussion

Mechanical properties, thermal properties, and thermal stability tests were conducted on five Prosopis juliflora fibre composite samples created at varying filler weight fractions. Each analysis's results confirmed that zinc oxide is an effective filler with promising effects on the testing results.

Thermal properties of PJ composite

When a solid, liquid, or gas is heated, they all react differently depending on the substance. Such a reaction might be an increase in temperature, a phase transition, a shift in length or volume, the beginning of a chemical reaction, a change in some other physical, and chemical properties. Conductivity, specific heat, diffusivity, expansion, and mass loss are the most prominent measures of thermal characteristics. The thermal conductivity and thermal expansion coefficient is the most crucial factor in determining how well insulation functions in a given environment. The HDT is the temperature at which a natural fibre composite will start to distort when subjected to a load. The HDT is a crucial property of composite materials since it establishes the operating temperature window in which the material retains its structural integrity. Factors such as matrix material, fibre volume percentage, processing method, testing circumstances, and the kind of natural fibre employed all influence the HDT of natural fibre composites. In general, the HDT values of natural fibre composites are lower than those of conventional synthetic composites due to the qualities of the natural fibre itself [37]. The results showed a similar trend to what was seen with thermal conductivity, with a steady rise from sample A to sample E. Sample E shows the highest HDT of 97 °C, while sample F shows the lowest HDT of 69 °C. This difference may be explained by the fact that the short-term heat-resistant capacity is higher in Prosopis juliflora fibre and zinc oxide nanoparticles, both of which are more abundant in sample E. Studies on jute fibre-reinforced epoxy composites and sisal fibre-reinforced polypropylene composites yielded comparable results, with HDTs of about 80and 60 °C, respectively. Traditional synthetic composites, including carbon fibre-reinforced epoxy composites, can have HDT values far above 200 °C, making these numbers seem low [38]. A further investigation into the thermal and mechanical characteristics of fibre-reinforced polyester composites made from Prosopis juliflora found that their HDT was close to 70 °C. Thus, it was found that zinc oxide nanoparticles added to the mix considerably increased the short-term heat-resistant ability. Therefore, when choosing materials for high-temperature applications, the HDT of natural fibre composites should be carefully examined. Thermogravimetric analysis of PJ composite

The composites manufactured and the conventional polyester resin were subjected to thermal degradation analysis in an argon environment at a temperature range of 210 °C. The purpose of this analysis was to investigate whether any organic changes occurred. The minor decrease in weight mass (about 1.2 % on average) seen during the heating range of 130–210 °C can be attributed to the evaporation of physically absorbed water [4]. The occurrence of pyrolysis of reactive oxygen-containing functional groups leads to the liberation of carbon monoxide (CO), carbon dioxide (CO2), and water vapour [9]. This process is responsible for an additional weight loss of approximately 25 % up to an average temperature of 234 °C. Subsequently, the composites exhibited a unidirectional transformation, commencing within the temperature range of 312–376 °C and concluding within the range of 342–409 °C. The weight reduction of the composite samples during the transition can be attributed to the bond breakdown in the thermoset polymer, resulting in an average weight loss of 90 %. The presence of zinc oxide particles in the polyester matrix serves as a hindrance to the movement of volatile pyrolyzed products, thereby impeding the thermal degradation of the nanocomposites [13]. The extent of weight loss in these composite samples depends on the proportion of zinc oxide particles utilized, as this factor influences the thermal stability of the samples. Conclusion

The primary aim of this research study is to investigate the antibacterial properties of Prosopis juliflora fiber and assess the mechanical, thermal, and surface characteristics of composite laminates fabricated by incorporating zinc oxide filler into a PLA matrix at five distinct weight ratios. The objective is to determine the impact of these factors on the overall performance and thermal stability of the Prosopis juliflora fiber-reinforced composite material. The subsequent information presents the noteworthy discoveries derived from this study endeavor. Incorporating zinc oxide blends with the polyester matrix in the manufacturing process of composite laminates utilizing Prosopis juliflora fiber results in a substantial bonding between the two components. This bonding has demonstrated potential for application in polymer matrix composites. Among the five samples analyzed, it was observed that sample S5 exhibited enhanced performance across all mechanical modes, with an average improvement of 27.9 % when compared to the other samples of PJ composite. The microscopic images provide strong visual evidence of the many mechanisms of failure that occur under incremental loading conditions. Hence, the use of an altered fabrication method has the potential to mitigate instances of failure. The thermal characteristics and stability findings demonstrated in this study on the PJ composite exhibit notably diminished thermal properties, rendering it suitable for producing composite materials utilized in thermal insulation applications. Sample S5 demonstrates that incorporating zinc oxide filler into the composite material enhances its ability to withstand higher temperatures and results in an average improvement of 26.7 % in the thermal characteristics of the PJ fiber composite.

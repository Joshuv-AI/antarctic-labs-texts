Developmental regulation of dermal adipose tissue by BCL11b
Authors: Sarah Traynor, Shashwati Bhattacharya, Kirill Batmanov, Lan Cheng, Angela Weller, Natalie Moore, Carmen Flesher, David Merrick
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In this study, Traynor et al. profile distinct populations of mesenchymal cell populations arising throughout murine dermal/subcutaneous adipose development and provide insight, at single-cell resolution, into adipogenesis during embryonic development and metabolic diseases such as obesity. They also show that transcription factor BCL11b is essential for adipocyte differentiation in dermal adipose tissues, and their findings highlight the unique regulatory pathways used by anatomically distinct adipose depots.

Obesity has emerged as a leading public health crisis that is predicted to affect >50% of adults in the United States by 2030 (Wang et al. 2020). The metabolic complications of obesity are hypothesized to derive from a relative deficiency in the ability of dermal/subcutaneous adipocyte progenitor cells (APCs) to undergo de novo differentiation (Kusminski et al. 2012; Shao et al. 2018), leading to lipid spillover into the liver/muscle and consequent insulin resistance (Isakson et al. 2009; Kim et al. 2014). APCs residing in anatomically distinct adipose depots descend from unique developmental precursors and display widely divergent metabolic responses during weight gain (Nahmgoong et al. 2022; Sakers et al. 2022). Although many population-level studies suggest an association between the accumulation of visceral adipose and insulin resistance, the prevailing hypothesis in the field asserts that the primary pathogenesis of human obesity results from impaired subcutaneous expansion (Danforth 2000; Vishvanath and Gupta 2019). Thus, a better understanding of the mechanisms governing APC adipogenic capacity and how their distinct developmental origins contribute to the depot-specific activity is needed.

The anatomy of subcutaneous adipose varies across species. In mice, the most superficial adipocyte layer is termed dermal white adipose tissue (dWAT) and is located adjacent to the reticular dermis, separated from the inguinal white adipose tissue (iWAT) by a striated muscle layer called the panniculus carnosus (PC) (Driskell et al. 2014). dWAT is more metabolically dynamic than deeper layers of subcutaneous or visceral adipose (Alexander et al. 2015), demonstrating hyperplastic expansion in obesity and involution with caloric restriction (Alexander et al. 2015; Zhang et al. 2021). Furthermore, due to its distinct anatomic location, dWAT engages in many physiological processes that are unique to the depot, including wound repair (Shook et al. 2020), innate immune defense against bacterial infection (Zhang et al. 2015), thermoregulation (Kasza et al. 2014), support of hair follicle growth (Festa et al. 2011), and dermal fibrosis/aging (Wang et al. 2022). These studies show that dWAT represents a critically important metabolic organ, yet this depot is relatively understudied and the precise nature of its developmental regulation has not been previously reported.

Murine subcutaneous adipose consolidation and vascularization begin at embryonic day 14 (E14); however, adipocytes do not accumulate lipid droplets until after birth. Pioneering work over the past decade has identified several candidate cell markers, including Pdgfrα and Dlk1 (Pref1), that faithfully, albeit nonspecifically, label all embryonic and adult progenitor cells that contribute to subcutaneous and visceral depots (Lee et al. 2012; Hudak et al. 2014). Later work revealed that visceral progenitors arise from Wilms tumor 1 (Wt1)-positive progenitors in late gestation (Chau et al. 2014), and that brown adipocytes express the transcription factor Gata6 (Jun et al. 2023; Rao et al. 2023). Conversely, iWAT was lineage-traced from a separate population of progenitors expressing paired related homeobox transcription factor 1 (Prrx1) (Sanchez-Gurmaches et al. 2015). These data show that the subcutaneous and visceral adipocytes arise from distinct embryonic progenitor populations (Jiang et al. 2014). Dermal adipose development begins at E14 and appears to occur independently from iWAT (Wojciechowicz et al. 2013). Recent reports implicate the transcription factor Foxn1 and the demethylase lysine-specific demethylase 6B (Kdm6b) in dWAT enlargement during skin injury in adult animals (Phan et al. 2023; Walendzik et al. 2023); however, little is known about the transcriptional regulation of dWAT organogenesis.

We used single-cell RNA sequencing to identify and characterize mesenchymal cell populations arising throughout murine dermal/subcutaneous adipose development. Several cell types including Nefl+ adipocyte progenitors, Ngfr+ skeletal muscle-associated fibroadipogenic progenitors (FAPs), and Bcl11b+ dermal mesenchymal cells were identified and characterized. Lineage tracing revealed that Nefl+ progenitors contribute to iWAT/visceral organogenesis but not dWAT formation. Pi16+/Dpp4+ interstitial fibroadipogenic progenitors serve as a reserve pool of progenitors for adult adipose expansion. Finally, we discovered that expression of the transcription factor Bcl11b in dermal mesenchymal cells is required for dWAT formation, and that Bcl11b modulates the dermal Wnt signaling environment to facilitate adipogenesis.

Results

Transcriptional profiling of mesenchymal cells during murine subcutaneous adipose development

To establish a developmental hierarchy of subcutaneous adipose organogenesis, we first constructed a histologic time course of the mesenchymal compartment during murine embryogenesis. Murine iWAT originates as a cellular consolidate within loose connective tissue around E14, and the reticular dermis becomes demarcated by the rudimentary PC muscle by E14–E16 (Fig. 1A). To explore the diversity of mesenchymal progenitor cells during adipose organogenesis, we used single-cell RNA sequencing of surgically isolated mesodermal tissues from mouse embryos at E12–E16 (Fig. 1B; Supplemental Fig. S1A). At E12, the subcutaneous mesenchymal compartment is composed of highly proliferative, undifferentiated Mecom+ cells, Ngfr+ FAP precursors, Pax7+ muscle satellite cells, and Sox2+ neural progenitors of the developing body wall, as well as minor surgical remnants of visceral organs (Supplemental Fig. S1D). By E14, we observed a dramatic shift in the cell populations, with the expansion of Ngfr+ FAPs and the appearance of Pi16+/Dpp4+, Nefl+, and Bcl11b+ progenitors within the emerging Pdgfrα+ pool, as well as an influx of immune cells and differentiation of myoblasts.

(B) Single-cell transcriptomics of surgically isolated mesenchymal tissues (indicated by blue shading in Supplemental Fig. S1A) from E12–E16 embryos (36,665 cells). (C) Subgroup analysis of Pdgfrα+ cells in relative transcriptional proximity (dashed line in B) to committed preadipocytes (15,543 cells). We focused our study on cells expressing the pan-progenitor marker Pdgfrα, which clustered closest to the committed preadipocytes (Supplemental Fig. S1B). Subgroup analysis of the selected Pdgfrα+ cells revealed the emergence of Pparg+ committed preadipocytes (Fig. 1C, yellow) by E14–E16 that clustered closely with the Nefl+ and Pi16+/Dpp4+ populations (Fig. 1C). Temporal analysis of the single-cell data demonstrated a developmental progression beginning with the presence of proliferative and undifferentiated Mecom+ cells at E12. By E14, Bcl11b+, Pi16/Dpp4+, Nefl+, and Ngfr+ cell populations and committed preadipocytes are apparent (Fig. 1D; Supplemental Fig. S1D). Pseudotemporal analysis of the single-cell data demonstrates a temporal relationship between the Nefl+ cells and committed preadipocytes (Supplemental Fig. S1C).

Pi16+/Dpp4+ progenitors contribute to hyperplastic adipose expansion in adults

To identify progenitor populations that might be contributing to adipogenesis, we focused on cells clustering closest to the Pparg+ preadipocytes, including the Pi16/Dpp4+ and Nefl+ populations. Pi16/Dpp4+ cells are multipotent fibroadipogenic progenitors inhabiting a connective tissue niche and have been previously shown to give rise to Icam1+ committed preadipocytes and mature adipocytes in adult animals (Fig. 2A; Merrick et al. 2019; Angueira et al. 2021; Stefkovich et al. 2021; Wu et al. 2023). To examine the role of Pi16/Dpp4+ cells during embryogenesis, we generated a Pi16Cre:R26dTomato lineage-tracing mouse model that leverages the specificity of Pi16 gene expression in fibroadipogenic progenitors (Supplemental Fig. S1B). Lineage-traced cells were examined at postnatal days 12–14 (P12–P14), when iWAT and dWAT organogenesis is complete. We could not detect traced adipocytes in the subcutaneous or dermal depots at this time point (Fig. 2B). Similarly, Pi16+ progenitors did not display adipogenic lineage tracing in the visceral depots (eWAT and rpWAT) of chow-fed mice at weaning (P24) (Fig. 2C, “chow”). Conversely, Pi16+ lineage-traced adipocytes were present in the subcutaneous and visceral depots of high-fat diet (HFD)-fed adult mice (Fig. 2C, “high-fat diet”; Supplemental Fig. S2A). These lineage-tracing experiments show that Pi16+ cells do not contribute to embryonic adipose organogenesis but rather participate in HFD-induced hyperplastic expansion in the obese adult.

(Bottom panels) “High-fat diet”: Pi16 lineage tracing in adults following 4 weeks of HFD feeding. Scale bar, 100 μm.

Nefl+ progenitors participate in embryonic adipose organogenesis

We next examined a novel population of Nefl+ cells located in close transcriptional proximity to the committed preadipocytes (Supplemental Fig. S3A). Nefl+ cells express follistatin (Fst) and c-Maf (Maf), which have been implicated in the regulation of adipogenesis (Fig. 3B; McCauley 2010; Fang et al. 2022). The transcriptional signature of the Nefl+ population is not apparent in postnatal adipose tissue, likely reflecting a transient developmental state only present during embryonic organogenesis (Supplemental Fig. S3B). To determine the lineage contributions of Nefl+ cells, we performed tracing using NeflCre:Rosa26mTomato/mGFP reporter animals. At E14, Nefl+ cells are clustered around the nascent iWAT depot (Fig. 3C, asterisk). Analysis of postnatal adipose tissues revealed labeling of stromal cells and mature adipocytes in the iWAT and visceral eWAT and rpWAT depots (Fig. 3D). However, Nefl+ cells did not contribute to dermal adipogenesis (Fig. 3D, top right panel). These results indicate that Nefl+ cells are a source of embryonic progenitors responsible for subcutaneous/visceral adipose organogenesis.

Nefl+ cells contribute to subcutaneous and visceral adipose organogenesis. (A) Single-cell expression of Nefl. (B) Single-cell expression of Nefl+ cell marker genes Fst and Maf. (C) Lineage tracing of Nefl+ cells clustered near the developing iWAT depot (asterisk) at E14. (D) Lineage tracing of Nefl+ progeny to P12–P14 showing numerous labeled adipocytes in the iWAT (left) but none in the dWAT (right). Scale bar, 100 μm.

Ngfr+ cells represent a likely progenitor for muscle-resident FAPs

We next examined the localization of Ngfr+ progenitors (Fig. 4A). Immunostaining analysis of NGFR in E14–E16 embryos demonstrated spindle-shaped cells intercalated between developing skeletal muscle myofibers (Fig. 4B). The morphologic and anatomic features of the Ngfr+ cells indicate that this cell population may give rise to skeletal muscle-resident FAPs (Angueira et al. 2021). Similarly, the Nppc+ population (Fig. 4C) is transcriptionally similar to a muscle-resident FAP population recently identified in the skin, which appears to inhabit the PC muscle (Jacob et al. 2023).

Ngfr+ cells are concentrated in skeletal muscle, and Bcl11b+ cells are located in the skin. (A) Single-cell expression of Ngfr. (D) Single-cell expression of Bcl11b. (E) IHC staining of E12–E16 embryos showing BCL11b expression (green; nuclear) in the epidermis (arrowhead), as well as PDGFRa+ cells (red) in the underlying dermis (bracket) and a lack of expression in PDGFRa+ cells of the developing iWAT (asterisk). Scale bar, 50 μm.

Bcl11b+ cells constitute a previously uncharacterized embryonic dermal mesenchymal cell population

The final major cell population identified in our single-cell analysis consisted of Pdgfrα+ cells expressing the transcription factor Bcl11b. The Bcl11b+ cells are transcriptionally associated with the dermal condensate (DC), a collection of cells that direct hair follicle development, indicating that this population may be related to dermal physiology (Fig. 4D). Indeed, immunohistochemical staining confirmed that BCL11b expression was highly localized to cells of the developing papillary dermis (Fig. 4E, PDGFRα-negative, arrowhead), where it is well known to regulate keratinocyte proliferation, as well as the underlying reticular dermis (Fig. 4E, PDGFRα-positive, bracket), where its function is largely unknown. BCL11b expression in the PDGFRα+ cells of the reticular dermis peaked at E14 and declined through E16, and the transcriptional signature of this cell population is absent postnatally (Fig. 4E; Supplemental Fig. S3B).

BCL11b expression is required for dermal adipogenesis

To characterize the role of dermal Bcl11b+ cells and their expression of the transcription factor BCL11b in dWAT development, we generated a mesenchymal Bcl11b knockout mouse model (PdgfrαCre:Bcl11bFlox; referred to here as “Bcl11b-KO”). This model preserves BCL11b activity in the papillary dermis and immune and nervous systems, thereby overcoming the embryonic lethality of previous whole-body knockout designs (Inoue et al. 2016, 2017). We first flow-sorted Icam1+ committed preadipocytes from the dWAT and iWAT of postnatal wild-type (WT) and Bcl11b-KO mice to quantify in vitro adipogenic capacity. Icam1+ preadipocytes isolated from the dWAT of WT mice displayed robust adipogenic differentiation potential, providing the first validation of their designation as postnatal committed preadipocytes for the dWAT. However, Icam1+ preadipocytes from Bcl11b-KO mice demonstrated a profound adipogenic defect (Fig. 5A,B).

(E) Flow quantification of the relative DPP4+ versus ICAM1+ population, represented as a percentage of PDGFRa+ progenitors.

We next examined the in vivo phenotype of Bcl11b-KO mice. Intriguingly, disruption of Bcl11b expression in Pdgfrα+ mesenchymal cells results in the near-complete absence of dWAT formation, with relative preservation of subcutaneous adipogenesis (Fig. 5C). Quantification of the adipose tissue perilipin area in Bcl11b-KO mice confirms that the proadipogenic regulatory function of BCL11b is highly specific for the dWAT and is not required for iWAT formation (Fig. 5D). Flow quantification of progenitor population dynamics in the dWAT revealed a significant decrease in the number of Icam1+ committed preadipocytes and a concurrent increase in the percentage of Dpp4+ cells (Fig. 5E).

BCL11b regulates the Wnt signaling microenvironment to promote adipogenesis in the dermis

We next sought to delineate the transcriptional mechanisms used by BCL11b to facilitate adipogenesis. We performed chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) analysis on dermal Bcl11b+ cells by flow-purifying PDGFRα+/CD73+ cells isolated from the dorsal mesenchyme of E14 embryos. The combination of Pdgfrα, CD73, and Bcl11b expression is highly specific for the Bcl11b+ population (Supplemental Fig. S4A,B). BCL11b binding was enriched on the promoters of many Wnt-related genes (Fig. 6A; Supplemental Fig. S4C). Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis of the ChIP-seq chromatin peaks confirmed that Wnt signaling was among the top signaling pathways regulated by BCL11b (Fig. 6B).

BCL11b modulates Wnt regulatory proteins. (A) Representative BCL11b ChIP-seq tracks showing identified peaks near select Wnt constituent genes. (B) GO analysis of Bcl11b peak-associated genes. (C) RNA-seq analysis of gene expression changes in ICAM1+ preadipocytes from the dermis of WT versus Bcl11b-KO mice. n = 3 biological replicates. (D) Proposed model of BCL11b function in modulating the local Wnt microenvironment to enable dermal adipogenesis.

Finally, we used RNA sequencing of preadipocytes from the skin of wild-type and Bcl11b-KO mice to determine the downstream signaling pathway modifications induced by Bcl11b+ cells. In dermal preadipocytes, we discovered that loss of Bcl11b-dependent niche signaling led to an upregulation of Wnt-promoting genes (Fig. 6C, green) and a downregulation of Wnt-suppressing genes (Fig. 6C, red), as well as a relative downregulation of some genes associated with adipogenesis (Fig. 6C, orange). Our RNA-seq data indicate that BCL11b activity in the dermis promotes the expression of Wnt antagonists, including Sost and Adamts18, while inhibiting Wnt agonists Nes and Sod3 in dermal preadipocytes (Fig. 6C).

Discussion

Adipose tissue develops in distinct depots during embryogenesis, and these anatomically diverse depots display unique physiological properties in the adult (Gulyaeva et al. 2019; Nahmgoong et al. 2022). Similarly, mesenchymal progenitor cells within adipose arise from distinct developmental origins (Jiang et al. 2014; Sakers et al. 2022). To investigate the lineage relationships governing adipose progenitor cell development, we used a combination of single-cell sequencing and lineage-tracing approaches. Our analysis revealed several cell populations appearing around E14–E16, including Pi16+/Dpp4+ fibroadipogenic progenitors, Nefl+ adipocyte progenitors, Ngfr+ muscle FAP progenitors, and Bcl11b+ dermal cells.

Pi16+/Dpp4+ progenitors have been shown by our group and others to inhabit reticular connective tissue surrounding adipose and many other mesenchymal organs (Merrick et al. 2019). Using a Dpp4CreER lineage-tracing mouse model, we have shown that the Dpp4+ progenitors give rise to visceral and retroperitoneal adipocytes in adult obese animals (Stefkovich et al. 2021). A comprehensive single-cell atlas of fibroblasts throughout the body confirmed the omnipresent nature of Pi16+/Dpp4+ cells within the connective tissue of a diverse set of organs, leading to their designation as “universal fibroblasts” (Buechler et al. 2021). However, the role of these cells in embryonic white adipogenesis was previously unknown. To achieve highly specific lineage tracing of this population during embryogenesis, we generated a novel Pi16Cre mouse model, which revealed that Pi16+ cells do not participate in embryonic adipose organogenesis. Conversely, we found that Pi16+ cells contribute to high-fat diet (HFD)-induced hyperplastic adipose expansion in obese animals. Together with the previous demonstration of the robust adipogenic differentiation of Dpp4+ cells in the adipose of obese adults (Stefkovich et al. 2021), these data support the conclusion that Pi16+/Dpp4+ progenitors are not the primary source of embryonic adipocytes during organogenesis but rather serve as a reserve pool of adipogenic progenitor cells that is recruited to repopulate Icam1+ preadipocytes during obesity-induced hyperplastic expansion in adults. Although Dpp4 and Pi16 consistently show strong coexpression in multiple studies, it is possible that the Pi16+ cells represent a distinct subpopulation of Dpp4+ progenitors with unique differentiation capacity.

Nefl+ cells are present exclusively during embryogenesis and participate in prenatal subcutaneous/visceral organogenesis. These results revealed a previously unreported Nefl+ adipocyte progenitor population and provide conclusive evidence that transcriptionally distinct progenitors are responsible for embryonic adipose development versus adult hyperplastic expansion. Many of the specific markers of adult committed preadipocyte cells, including Icam1, Cyp1b1, and Stap4, are not detectable during embryogenesis, indicating that classical Icam1+ preadipocytes acquire a mature transcriptional signature postnatally. Interestingly, neither Nefl+ nor Pi16+/Dpp4+ cells were found to contribute to dWAT adipogenesis. We therefore focused our study on the final cell population marked by Bcl11b. This cell population bore partial transcriptional resemblance to dermal-associated cells (Jacob et al. 2023), and Bcl11b+ cells were located not only in the epidermis but also among Pdgfra+ cells in the deeper dermal layers.

BCL11b is a zinc finger transcription factor with dual activity: transcriptional repression when associated with chromatin remodeling partners such as HDAC/NuRD and activation when associated with p300 (Daher et al. 2020). It is essential for the development of several tissues, including the skin and the immune and nervous systems, where it serves as a critical regulator of tissue-resident stem cell self-renewal and fate determination (Daher et al. 2020). In the papillary dermis/epidermis, Bcl11b contributes to the establishment of the epidermal permeability barrier via regulation of keratinocyte proliferation and paracrine growth factor signaling (Golonzhka et al. 2009). Perturbations in Bcl11b expression lead to defects in hair follicle maturation (Bhattacharya et al. 2015), delayed wound healing (Liang et al. 2012), and the development of atopic dermatitis (Wang et al. 2012). Bcl11b has been implicated in subcutaneous adipogenesis; however, the results of this study were confounded by embryonic lethality of the whole-body knockout. Given that the final stages of adipocyte maturation occur postnatally, the confounding effects of whole-body Bcl11b knockout in that study preclude interpretable analysis of any adipogenic phenotype (Inoue et al. 2016).

We found that Bcl11b is specifically required for dermal adipose development and is not essential for subcutaneous or visceral adipose organogenesis. These data provide compelling evidence that Bcl11b is not only a specific marker for a dWAT-associated cell population but is also serving an essential functional role in enabling adipogenic differentiation and directing preadipocyte lineage commitment in the dermal compartment. Given that Bcl11b is also expressed in cells of the dermal condensate (Pdgfra+), it is possible that the activity of Bcl11b in these cells also contributes to dWAT development. The specific chromatin interactions of BCL11b are highly cell context-dependent (Cismasiu et al. 2006; Hosokawa et al. 2018), and its transcriptional activity within mesenchymal cells is poorly understood. Our Bcl11b ChIP-seq results indicate that Wnt signaling is a primary target of Bcl11b transcriptional regulation in dermal cells.

Wnt signaling plays a defining role in skin development and homeostasis by stimulating hair follicle growth and regulating injury repair (Wei et al. 2011; Mastrogiannaki et al. 2016; Veltri et al. 2018) and has also been shown to inhibit adipogenic differentiation (Cawthorn et al. 2012). Wnt-related genes, including Sost and Adamts18, were among the top differentially expressed mRNAs in the RNA-seq analysis of Bcl11b knockout cells. Sclerostin (Sost) binds to low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein LRP5/6 and antagonizes its interaction with frizzled receptors, thus inhibiting canonical Wnt signaling. Sost-KO mice display increased bone density and decreased visceral adiposity; however, the effect on dWAT was not examined (Kim et al. 2021). Disintegrin and metalloproteinase domain-containing protein 18 (Adamts18), a metalloproteinase that is involved in extracellular matrix remodeling, has been shown to inhibit β-catenin activity, and its expression is regulated by Wnt signaling (Lu et al. 2017; Ataca et al. 2020). Adamts18-KO mice develop visceral adiposity and insulin resistance (Zhu et al. 2018), but its function has not been studied in the dermal adipose or iWAT.

In summary, these studies show that dermal adipose expansion is regulated by mesenchymal cell populations unique to the skin, which use the transcription factor BCL11b to modulate the dermal Wnt signaling microenvironment and enable adipogenesis (Fig. 6D). The sum total of murine adipose research has been conducted almost exclusively in the iWAT and eWAT depots due to their ease of dissection. The inguinal adipose has long been used as a direct correlate to human subcutaneous adipose; however, this depot is more functionally related to murine mammary tissue. dWAT is a metabolically dynamic adipose depot responsible for energy storage as well as a host of physiological functions, including thermoregulation, wound repair, innate immune defense, support of hair follicle growth, and skin fibrosis/aging. The distinct developmental environment in which dWAT originates is a principle determinant of its expansion capacity (Van Harmelen et al. 2004; Belligoli et al. 2019). Impaired hyperplastic expansion within the subcutaneous depot is thought to be a primary driver of the metabolic consequences of obesity in humans (Tchoukalova et al. 2010; Lotta et al. 2017; Hammarstedt et al. 2018). Thus, a greater understanding of the cells and signaling pathways regulating dermal adipose expansion may provide critical functional insights into the physiology of human subcutaneous adipose pathophysiology in obesity.

Materials and methods

Animals

Timed-pregnant female CD-1 mice (stock 022) were obtained from Charles River Laboratories. Bcl11b-KO mice were generated by crossing Bcl11bflox/flox (Jackson 034469) with PdgfraCre (Jackson 013148) mice. NeflCre linage-tracing mice were generated by crossing NeflCre heterozygotes (Schweizer et al. 2002) with R26mT/mG reporter mice (Jackson 007676). Pi16Cre mice were generated by pronuclear injection of recombinant Cas9 bound to a guide RNA targeting the start methionine of the native Pi16 gene along with a single-stranded donor template DNA containing iCRE with a terminal stop codon, flanked by ∼500 bp of 5′ and 3′ homology DNA. Founder animals were screened by PCR genotyping and validated by genomic sequencing as well as immunohistochemical confirmation of specific lineage tracing into Pi16+ cells. By design, expression of iCre resulted in disruption of the native Pi16 gene expression; thus, the Pi16Cre allele was maintained at heterozygosity. Pi16 lineage-tracing mice were generated by crossing Pi16Cre heterozygotes with R26LSL-tdTomato (Ai14) reporter mice (Jackson 007914). For genotyping of Pi16Cre alleles, PCR was performed using the following primers: 5′-TAGAGAGCAGCTAACCTGGGC-3′ and 5′-TCCCTGAACATGTCCATCAGGT-3′. All animal experiments were performed according to procedures approved by the University of Pennsylvania's Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee.

Histology and immunofluorescence

Tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde overnight at 4°C. Tissues were then dehydrated in ethanol and embedded in paraffin for thin sectioning. Following deparaffinization, slides were subjected to heat antigen retrieval in a pressure cooker with Bulls Eye decloaking buffer. Slides were incubated in primary antibody overnight and secondary antibody conjugated to peroxidase and then developed using tyramide signal amplification. Images were captured on a MICA automated dual-confocal and wide-field microscope as well as a Keyence BZ-X700 fluorescent light microscope.

Preparation of libraries for scRNA-seq and scRNA-seq data analysis

Detailed protocols describing the preparation of E12–E16 single-cell libraries as well as scRNA-seq data analysis are in the Supplemental Material.

Isolation of adipocyte progenitors of postnatal mice

iWAT and dWAT were surgically dissected from mice following mechanical/chemical depilation. Tissues were digested in DMEM + 1% BSA with 6.2 mg/mL type 1 collagenase and 1.2 mg/mL Dispace II using a GentleMACS Octo dissociator running program 37_mr_ADK_D. Samples were quenched with DMEM + 10% FBS and filtered through 100 μm filters. Cells were lysed with 1× RBC lysis buffer and then filtered through 40 mm filters. Cells were then stained with antibodies for 30 min at 4°C with gentle rotation in the dark. Following staining, the cells were stained with DAPI for the final 5 min and then sorted on a FACS Aria Fusion cell sorter with a 100 μm nozzle. Samples were negatively sorted on DAPI and CD45/CD31 to remove debris, dead cells, and immune cells. Cells were gated on PDGFRa+ and further separated by DPP4+ or ICAM1+.

Cell culture differentiation and quantification

Sorted progenitor cells were plated in equal numbers, grown to confluency, and then induced to differentiate for 2 days in DMEM F12 media containing insulin, IBMX, and dexamethasone. After induction, cells were maintained in DMEM F12 + insulin for 6 days. Differentiated cells were washed with PBS and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10–20 min in the dark. Fixed cells were washed twice and stained with Bodipy and Hoechst for 10 min in the dark before imaging. The adipogenic index was quantified as described previously (Merrick et al. 2019) using the Cell Profiler “Threshold” module for the Bodipy area. Nucleus count was quantified using the Cell Profiler “Find Primary Objects” module.

Bulk RNA-seq processing and analysis

ICAM1+ preadipocytes from the dermis and subcutaneous adipose were isolated by FACS as described above. Total RNA was extracted from TRIzol using the Qiagen Micro RNeasy kit following the manufacturer's protocol. mRNA was immediately reverse-transcribed to cDNA. Samples were sequenced by Novogene Corporation. Data processing and differential gene analysis were performed in R using the tidyverse software package.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP), ChIP sequencing, and ChIP-qPCR analyses

E14.5 embryos from timed pregnant CD1 mice were dissected and digested as described for single-cell processing in the Supplement Material. Samples were quenched and pelleted as described previously and lysed with 1× RBC lysis buffer. Cells were filtered through 40 mm filters and then fixed with 1% methanol-free formaldehyde for 10 min. After fixation, cells were quenched, washed, stained with antibodies, and sorted on a FACS Aira Fusion cell sorter with a 100 μm nozzle. Dermal Bcl11b+ cells were enriched with negative gating on CD45/CD31 and positive selection of PDGFRa+/CD73 double-positive cells. Six million to 9 million cells per sample were used for chromatin isolation. Nuclei were isolated using a hypotonic buffer and Kimble dounce homogenizer. Nuclei were lysed in SDS buffer, and chromatin was sonicated at 20% amplitude for 3 min with cycles of 15 sec on/20 sec off to obtain fragments ranging from 200 to 600 bp. Fragmented chromatin was incubated with 1 mg of rabbit anti-BCL11b or control IgG antibody overnight at 4°C and bound to protein A/G magnetic beads for pull-down. ChIP-seq libraries were prepared using the KAPA HyperPrep kit, and library barcodes were from the KAPA unique dual-index kit. Libraries were amplified using 10 PCR cycles. Postamplification libraries were size-selected at ∼300–600 bp in length using AMPure XP beads. The resulting ChIP-seq libraries were sequenced by Novogene Corporation. For ChIP-qPCR, immunoprecipitated chromatin was fragmented by sonication and then used for qPCR quantification of peaks identified on ChIP-seq.

Data and code availability

Single-cell RNA-seq and ChIP-seq data have been deposited at GEO under the accession numbers GSE264675, GSE264676, and GSE264677. Any additional information required to reanalyze the data reported here is available from the lead contact upon request.

Statistical analysis

Statistical details and biological replicates (“n”) for each experiment are described in the corresponding figure legends. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software. A minimum of five biological replicates was used when statistical analysis was required, and a minimum of three biological replicates was performed for qualitative IHC images. Values are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean. Holm–Sidak multiple correction method was used to quantify the statistical differences between samples. Significant differences are represented in the figure legends as follows: P < 0.05 (*), P < 0.01 (**), P < 0.001 (***), and P < 0.0001 (****).

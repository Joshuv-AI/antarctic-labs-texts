When two become three: Endoscope-assisted Caldwell–Luc removal of an unexpected third dental bur in the maxillary sinus
Authors: Ling-Wei Huang, Wei-Chen Huang, Sheng-Hong Wang, Ken-chung Chen
Published: 2026

Foreign body displacement into the maxillary sinus is an uncommon but clinically relevant complication of dental procedures.1 Prompt removal is generally recommended to prevent sinusitis and further migration.1,2 However, direct inspection through a Caldwell–Luc window may be insufficient to exclude residual objects, particularly when multiple small fragments are involved.3 We reported a case in which endoscopic confirmation through a Caldwell–Luc window prevented retention of an additional dental bur.

A patient presented to our emergency department immediately after extraction of tooth 27 at a local clinic. The referring dentist reported that a rotary instrument had inadvertently entered the maxillary sinus. Intraoral examination revealed a fresh extraction socket at tooth 27 without visible residual tooth fragments.

Preoperative panoramic radiography revealed an extraction socket at tooth 27 adjacent to the maxillary sinus floor and a linear radiopaque foreign body within the left maxillary sinus (Fig. 1A). Computed tomography (CT) revealed radiopaque foreign bodies consistent with displaced dental burs, with mucosal thickening of the left maxillary sinus (Fig. 1B–D). CT is useful for localizing foreign bodies and assessing the degree of sinus inflammation.2 On the basis of imaging, two burs were suspected.

Under general anesthesia, a Caldwell–Luc procedure was performed. An intrasulcular incision was made from tooth 23 to tooth 27 with a vertical releasing incision at tooth 23. After mucoperiosteal flap elevation, a lateral bony window was created on the anterior wall of the left maxillary sinus (approximately from tooth 24 to tooth 26). Two dental burs were identified and removed. Given that the preoperative assessment suggested the presence of two foreign bodies and that no additional object was visible on direct inspection, closure was initially considered.

To minimize the risk of retained foreign bodies, intraoperative endoscopic inspection was subsequently performed through the Caldwell–Luc window in consultation with otolaryngology. An unexpected third dental bur was detected and removed using endoscopic instruments (Fig. 1F). A final endoscopic survey confirmed the absence of residual foreign bodies (Fig. 1G). The oroantral communication (OAC) at the tooth 27 site was repaired using an advanced buccal mucosal flap, and the wound was closed primarily.

Endoscopic visualization enables systematic assessment of maxillary sinus recesses. It may reduce the likelihood of retained foreign bodies, particularly when multiple fragments are suspected or when imaging and intraoperative findings are discordant.3 For maxillary sinus foreign body removal, a transnasal functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS) approach is often preferred because it preserves the maxillary sinus wall and maintains physiologic ostial drainage, with low surgical trauma and low complication rates.3 In contrast, the Caldwell–Luc approach is associated with higher rates of late complications, including postoperative sinusitis.3 However, transoral access (e.g., Caldwell–Luc) remains useful when the foreign body is large, located unfavorably for transnasal extraction, or when concomitant intraoral management (such as OAC repair) is required.3 In such cases, combining a Caldwell–Luc window with endoscopic confirmation may improve retrieval completeness and surgical confidence.

The key clinical message for surgeons is that endoscopic confirmation during maxillary sinus foreign body retrieval is crucial to prevent retained foreign bodies, even when preoperative imaging suggests complete removal.3

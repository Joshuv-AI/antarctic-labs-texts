PharmacoGENEtics in Youth Depression (GENE-YD) Study protocol: a pilot triple-blinded randomised controlled trial using pharmacogenetics to tailor antidepressant treatment in young Australians
Authors: Bradley Roberts, Zahra Cooper, Aleksandra Miljevic, Susanne H Stanley, Bernadette T Majda, Khan R L Collins, Svetlana Baltic, Suzanna E Lindsey-Temple, P Anthony Akkari, Sean D Hood, Jennifer Rodger
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe prevalence of depression and mood disorders has been steadily rising in Australian youth, with a concomitant increase in antidepressant pharmacotherapy prescription rates. Yet, the tolerability and efficacy of antidepressant drugs in youth remain poor. Pharmacogenetic (PGx) testing, or the personalised and guided treatment of medication based on genetic data, has been suggested to improve the effectiveness and tolerability of antidepressants. However, limited studies have evaluated the utility of antidepressant PGx-guided treatment in adolescent and young adult populations. Thus, this pilot randomised controlled trial (RCT), the GENE-YD Study, will evaluate the feasibility for a large-scale RCT assessing the effect of PGx-guided antidepressant prescription vs treatment as usual in youth with major depressive disorder (MDD).Methods and analysisEighty young people between 16 and 24 years of age and in the early stages of pharmacotherapy treatment for MDD will be recruited. Following initial screening, participants will be randomised on a 1:1 ratio to either the intervention or control study group. Participants in the intervention condition will have their treatment tailored based on their PGx profile. Participants randomised into the control group will have their prescription based on current best practice recommendations, or treatment as usual. Individuals will be assessed at drug prescription baseline and again 6 and 12 weeks following drug prescription. The primary outcome of the study will be to evaluate the feasibility and acceptability of the GENE-YD protocol. Specifically, this study will explore participation recruitment strategies and attrition to the study protocols to guide the recruitment processes of a large-scale RCT, along with participating satisfaction in overall study protocols. Secondary outcomes will inform the utility and variability of specific measures (eg, depression rating scales, quality of life measures and medication adherence scales) that may be scaled up for use in a future full-scale trial.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval was granted by the Department of Health, Western Australia’s Human Research Ethics Committee (RGS0000006822) and recognised by the University of Western Australia’s Human Research Ethics Committee (2024/ET000685). All participants will be required to provide written informed consent. Results will be published in international peer-reviewed journals.Trial registration numberACTRN12624000760572.Keywords: CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY, Person-Centered Care, Randomized Controlled Trial, MENTAL HEALTH, Depression & mood disorders

Background and rationale

Antidepressant pharmacotherapies are often a first-line treatment for young people suffering from moderate to severe depression and mood disorders, such as major depressive disorder (MDD).1 MDD prevalence is currently on the rise and with it, the prescription and consumption rates of antidepressant medications.2 In Australia, over 45 million psychiatric medications were prescribed in 2022, of which over 33 million (~74%) were antidepressants.3 In youth below the age of 24 years, antidepressant use increased by 16% between 2015 and 2019, with approximately 1 in every 8 females and 1 in every 15 males prescribed antidepressants for MDD.4

Despite increasing rates of antidepressant prescription in young people, antidepressants demonstrate a poor effect in treating MDD in children and youth,5 6 with over one-third of young MDD patients resistant to traditional pharmacotherapy treatments.7 Furthermore, young people are more likely to present with severe medication-related side effects, including an increased risk of self-harming behaviours and suicidality.8 9 Despite numerous studies observing little to no effect in treating MDD in youth with antidepressant pharmacotherapy alone,10,18 antidepressant medication failure is often coupled with a replacement prescription of another antidepressant drug.19 Research shows that this subsequent prescription of antidepressant medications not only increases a patient’s likelihood of developing medication-related side effects but also reduces the likelihood of drug response and symptom remission.20 Thus, there is a need to move away from the clinical trial-and-error antidepressant prescription process, particularly for young people with MDD. Current research suggests that this can be achieved through the utilisation of a personalised medicine approach.21

Pharmacogenetics (PGx) is the study of how interindividual genetic variation in drug-metabolising genes can influence the pharmacokinetic properties of drug metabolism and absorption, altering an individual’s biological response to medication.22 Over 95% of the population is expected to carry at least one of these known genetic variations that may alter drug metabolism, and approximately 42% of the observed variability in response to antidepressant medication can be attributable to common genetic variation in the human genome.23 24 Thus, analysing metabolic genotype to further understand an individual’s drug-metabolising phenotype may provide valuable information on drug response, providing clinicians with an informed decision-making process for antidepressant prescription.

Evaluating a person’s genetic variation (PGx testing) has been suggested to aid and improve the drug prescription process, individualising and personalising pharmacotherapy in numerous health conditions,2225,30 including MDD.31 Despite randomised controlled trials (RCTs) demonstrating a supportive evidence base for PGx use,31,45 there are still numerous barriers that impede the implementation of PGx testing into primary care practice.46,48 As discussed in our recent review,49 and observed in past research,50 young people had reservations as to the effectiveness of PGx testing to prescribe well-tolerated antidepressant medication.51 Moreover, many young adults were concerned about the efficacy and tolerability of antidepressant medications themselves. Presently, the Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC) guidelines provide recommendations as to the interpretation of PGx testing for antidepressant pharmacotherapy.52 53 CPIC guidelines also highlight that the generalisability of these recommendations to paediatric and youth patients’ needs to be established through more age-appropriate RCTs.54 55 To date, PGx research has predominantly been conducted in older adults, with these results extrapolated to young people.56 Furthermore, current PGx literature appears inconclusive as to the benefits of PGx testing in the treatment prescription process for young people.45 57 Therefore, it is evident that further youth-focused RCTs are required to evaluate and demonstrate the efficacy for PGx testing to streamline antidepressant pharmacotherapy, providing an effective and well-tolerated pharmacotherapy-based treatment to young people with MDD.

Objectives

This pilot and feasibility study will aim to provide support for a large scale RCT exploring personalised antidepressant prescription in young people aged 16–24 years with MDD in Western Australia. This study will also assess changes in depression, presentation of medication-related side effects and medication adherence rates between an intervention group receiving medication prescribed from PGx testing and a control group receiving medication prescribed on a treatment as usual basis (TAU). Follow-ups will be held at 6 and 12 weeks post-medication intervention (baseline). This study specifically aims to (1) estimate the proportion of voluntary participants that are eligible for an RCT of the GENE-YD programme and who will consent to participate, (2) estimate adherence and attrition to trial protocols and study procedures and (3) assess the acceptability and participant satisfaction of the GENE-YD protocol, methodology of recruitment, intervention delivery and data collection. Preliminary efficacy signals will also be evaluated as secondary measures by (1) assessing trends in secondary outcome measures evaluated at baseline and 6- and 12 weeks from baseline and (2) identifying variables to refine power calculations for a larger sample.

Methods

Trial design

This study will contain a parallel group design where participants are randomly allocated on a 1:1 ratio to either the PGx-guided intervention or control group. A maximum of 80 youth participants will be recruited into the study between July 2024 and December 2025. All participants and their treatment team, along with study assessors, will be blinded to group allocation. For those participants who pass eligibility screening, during which all participants will undergo PGx testing, treatment groups will be randomly assigned to either intervention or control. Figure 1 schematically represents the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) flow diagram of the study protocol procedure. Based on the PGx results and group allocation, a PGx-tailored Treatment Guide or a Treatment Guide based on TAU guidelines will be provided to the individual’s nominated general practitioner (GP). GPs will be asked to prescribe their patients using the Treatment Guide and their clinical judgement. Medication choice, dosages and ongoing adjustments to the treatment plan will be recorded by the research team.

Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) flow diagram of the GENE-YD pilot randomised controlled trial protocol.

Participants in both groups will complete post-intervention assessments at 6 and 12 weeks following antidepressant prescription. After the 12-week follow-up, both participants and their GPs will be unblinded and the full PGx report will be provided. Fortnightly informal calls at weeks 2, 4, 8 and 10 will be conducted to check in on the participants’ and their mental health and well-being, collecting qualitative participant-reported side effects and symptoms. A final qualitative phone call interview will be conducted between weeks 24 and 28 to assess satisfaction in participating in the GENE-YD trial.

Study setting

This trial will be conducted at the Perron Institute for Neurological and Translational Science, Perth, Western Australia. Face-to-face assessments will be conducted either in person at the Perron Institute’s clinical facilities or online using Microsoft Teams video conferencing. Participant self-reported assessments will be conducted online using the research electronic data capture (REDCap) system.

Recruitment procedures

Participants will be recruited through a multi-faceted approach via four main channels, including: (1) onsite advertisement around university campuses, (2) referrals from GPs with an interest in PGx implementation in the primary care setting, (3) social media posts through collaborative, local community mental health organisations and (4) through emails to lists of youth participants who participated in past research.51

Youths who express an interest in participating in the study will be contacted by a member of the research team in a preliminary pre-screening phone call to provide more detailed information about the study, as well as assessing candidate eligibility. This will be followed by a subsequent in-person screening session that will be scheduled with eligible participants interested in continuing with the study. The screening session will collect demographic data, a comprehensive medical and medication history, and assess the eligibility criteria detailed below.

Eligibility criteria

Participants meeting the following criteria are eligible for study participation: (1) aged between 16 and 24 years (inclusive), (2) able to give informed consent, (3) fluent in English and (4) diagnosed with MDD, either first-episode or relapsed, based on the Mini International Neuropsychiatric Interview 7.0.2 for Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5 M.I.N.I.) criteria.

Exclusion criteria are as follows: (1) displays significant suicidal risk based on the Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS) and/or DSM-5 M.I.N.I., (2) displays signs of a substance use disorder not in remission (other than nicotine or caffeine), as determined during DSM-5 M.I.N.I. screening assessment, (3) diagnosed with a concurrent psychiatric diagnosis of any one or more of the following: bipolar disorder, psychotic disorders (psychotic MDD, schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, schizophreniform disorder) or cognitive disorders (intellectual impairment/dementia), as determined by participant medical history or during DSM-5 M.I.N.I. screening assessment, (4) failure of two or more prior adequate trials of evidence-based pharmacotherapy treatments for MDD, (5) reports a history of significant hepatic or renal disease affecting drug metabolism, (6) is a pregnant or breast-feeding woman and (7) adults (18–24 years) who are not able to provide consent for participation.

Mini-International Neuropsychiatric Interview (M.I.N.I.)

The DSM-5 M.I.N.I. will be performed to establish a clinical diagnosis of MDD during the screening stage. The DSM-5 M.I.N.I. is a ~15 min diagnostic interview, designed to be a short but accurate psychiatric interview for clinical trials and epidemiology studies.58 The DSM-5 M.I.N.I., widely used in both clinical and research settings,59 has been validated through various studies60 61 and has high reliability and validity in diagnosing psychiatric disorders. Of the 17 modules, three are relevant and will be administered for this study, including modules A—Major Depressive Episode, B—Suicidality and J—Substance Use Disorder (Non-Alcohol).

Who will obtain informed consent and/or assent?

Prior to screening, candidates, or their consenting adult, will be emailed a plain language summary and a participant information and consent form (PICF) to review, giving participants the opportunity to ask questions and express any concerns to members of the research team. Once all questions have been sufficiently answered to the participant’s satisfaction, and the Investigator is assured that the participant and/or their legal parent or guardian understands the implications of participating, participants and/or their consenting adult will be asked to provide their consent to participate in the study by signing the informed consent form, attached to the PICF. Consent will be obtained in accordance with International Council on Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Registration of Pharmaceuticals for Human Use Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice. Where a legal parent or guardian is providing their consent, underage participants will be asked to provide their assent, ensuring the study Investigator that they are informed on the study participation requirements. See onlinesupplemental materials 13 for a model PICF, child assent form and plain language summary provided to each participant.

Consenting participants and/or their parents will be asked to nominate a GP. This is the physician who will receive the Treatment Guide from the research team and prescribe the participant their antidepressant medication. An Investigator will contact the nominated GP via email, informing them of the study requirements, their patient’s participation and their involvement in the study. If the participant’s nominated GP does not wish to prescribe antidepressant medication under the guide of the participant’s PGx information, the participant may choose to be seen by a study-nominated GP.

Additional consent provisions and plans for the collection, use and storage of participant data and biological specimen in the current trial and for future use

Biological specimen collection is an integral part of this study. Therefore, those individuals who do not wish to provide a specimen will not be recruited into the study. During the provision of informed consent, participants will be made aware of what will be conducted with their biological data and have the option to allow their data to be used in future studies. Specifically, participants will have the option to allow for their DNA to be stored and used by the third-party provider of the PGx testing kits for validation and ongoing future improvements of the testing kits. No identifiable information will be handled or used by members outside of the research team and identifiable information will not be used in any future studies. Choosing not to allow for their data to be retained will not affect a person’s participation in the study.

Patient and public involvement

Community groups were involved from the earliest stages of this research through a co-design approach informed by our previous work.51 As part of that study, young people with lived experience of mental health and antidepressant treatment contributed to the design of this pilot trial by shaping the research priorities and identifying outcomes of importance.

The current trial was developed in close collaboration with young consumer representatives from the Consumer and Community Involvement Programme, referred to as Research Buddies. These young people provided critical input into the study design, including the appropriateness of the intervention, burden of assessments and relevance of outcome measures. Their perspectives were instrumental in ensuring that the research remained youth-centred, acceptable and ethically sound. Research Buddies also contributed to the development of participant materials and recruitment strategies to maximise accessibility and engagement. The time commitment, potential burden and feasibility of study participation were reviewed and refined based on their feedback.

Interventions

Intervention description

Pharmacogenetic testing and treatment recommendation

Gene S is a Western Australian based start-up biotechnology company, using PGx technologies to personalise medication management. Their ‘RosaMind’ PGx Solution is a PGx test to enable the personalised prescription of psychiatric medication for children and young people living with mental health conditions. The RosaMind test will be used to analyse and report PGx clinical recommendations for samples collected in this project. Further information on the Gene S ‘RosaMind’ PGx Solution can be found in the online supplemental material 4.

After initial eligibility criteria have been met and informed consent is gained at participants’ pre-screening sessions, participants will attend a screening session and provide a buccal sample and a blood sample (4–10 mL EDTA for internal validation of the Gene S ‘RosaMind’ PGx Solution).

On completion of PGx testing analysis, Gene S will provide the research team with a PGx genotype and drug-metabolising phenotype profile, and medication recommendations individualised to each participant. Validation of the Gene S reports in-house by the research team will be performed with a second saliva sample taken and sent to myDNA for PGx profiling.62

The gene-drug report provided by Gene S will be used to write a personalised Treatment Guide for each participant in the intervention group. Antidepressant prescribing suggestions in all Treatment Guides will follow the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Psychiatrists (RANZCP) clinical practice guidelines for mood disorders. The list of recommended agents and standardised dosing regimens includes SSRIs (eg, escitalopram, fluoxetine, sertraline), SNRIs (eg, venlafaxine, desvenlafaxine) and other agents (eg, mirtazapine, moclobemide). Dosing suggestions consider both clinical and pharmacogenetic information where appropriate, and all prescribing suggestions are made by a qualified psychiatrist with PGx experience from the research team. Pharmacogenetics-informed treatment group (intervention group)

Participants in the intervention group will be overseen by their GP, who will be provided with a Treatment Guide. This PGx Guide is individualised based on the participant’s full PGx report provided by GeneS and informed by their metabolic genotype, providing a list of recommended antidepressant medications based on their PGx testing results. Each Treatment Guide will provide a list of medications that are recommended as a first consideration and some as a second recommendation. It is then up to the GP’s discretion as to which medication they will prescribe their patient, informing the research team on their choice.

Treatment as usual group (control group)

This study aims to evaluate the preliminary effectiveness of PGx-guided treatment prescription of antidepressant medication in youth with MDD. With this being a key focus, a control group of participants who receive a prescription that is not guided by PGx information, but rather by current best practice guidelines, is required to analyse and compare outcome measures.

Participants in the control group will also be overseen by their GP, who will be provided with a Treatment Guide. This Control Guide is based on the currently approved best practice guidelines in Australia for antidepressant prescription and dosage recommendations for youth. The Control Guide will be formatted similarly to the PGx Guide used for the intervention group providing first-line and second-line antidepressant medication options and starting dosage recommendations. Again, it is up to the GP’s discretion as to which medication they will prescribe their patient, informing the research team on their final choice.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions

Participation in this study is voluntary and participants are free to withdraw at any stage without prejudice. Participants who do withdraw from the study will be asked to provide a reason for withdrawal; however, participants are not obligated to disclose why they have chosen to withdraw.

Throughout the study, participants will be monitored for any deterioration in their mental state. If an adverse event is identified by a member of the research team, the research team will follow-up with the participant, providing any necessary assistance according to standardised regulatory guidelines for safety monitoring and reporting in clinical trials, as described in the National Health and Medical Research Council (NHMRC) guidelines. If an adverse event causes further deterioration of a participant’s mental state and the situation cannot be resolved while maintaining their participation in the study, the participant will be withdrawn from the study and referred on for appropriate professional help, as per the processes outlined by the local research ethics committee.

Participants will also be withdrawn and/or considered lost to follow-up should they be uncontactable at critical junctures (weeks 6 and 12) on three consecutive occasions utilising different modes of communication (ie, phone call and email). If there is considerable concern for the participants’ well-being and research staff cannot contact the participant after three consecutive attempts, the participant’s emergency contact will be notified. All participant data collected up until the time of withdrawal will be included in data analysis unless the participant expressly requests for it to be destroyed. If so, all data collected on the participant will be permanently destroyed.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions and plans to promote participant retention and completion of follow-up assessments

We will regularly monitor participants via SMS to ensure their continued progression in the study. An SMS reminder will be sent to participants 1 day prior to scheduled screening, baseline and post-intervention assessments. Following assessment completion, participants will be contacted via email. This contact will ensure that participants have the opportunity to ask the research team any questions or express any queries and/or concerns they may have. Follow-up procedures will see participants who do not interact with the study assessments receive a phone call from a member of the research team to check in.

Fortnightly calls on weeks 2, 4, 8 and 10 to check in with participants will also provide a method of retention, allowing participants to raise any participatory questions and/or concerns with a member of the research team. These calls will also serve as a means of continually monitoring participants’ welfare and qualitatively capturing participant-reported side effects and symptoms.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial

All participants are encouraged to maintain or commence any mental health or psychological care that they wish for during the course of this study. The research team will ask to remain informed of any changes to or adjustments of antidepressant medication treatment.

Assignment of interventions: randomisation and allocation

Sequence generation

Eligible participants who meet inclusion criteria during pre-screening and screening procedures and provide informed consent will be provided with a unique study identifier and will be randomly assigned to one of two study groups following PGx sample collection and prior to baseline assessments. Randomisation will be conducted using a computer-generated randomisation sequence to sort participants into either PGx-guided treatment or control groups.63

Concealment mechanism and implementation

Randomisation and group allocation will be conducted by an independent member of the research team who is not involved in data collection or analysis. A computerised adaptive randomisation procedure (minimisation method) will be performed, adjusting for baseline characteristics (age and sex). Allocations will be logged and stored in a password protected file, accessible only by independent members of the research team and Chief Investigators. Group allocations will be shared with the psychiatrist writing the Treatment Guides to ensure participants and their prescribing GPs are correctly informed on their medication recommendations.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded?

Research team members who regularly communicate with participants and engage in data collection and analysis will be blind to treatment group allocations. Participants and their GPs will also be blinded to group allocation, and all participants and their treatment teams will be provided with a Treatment Guide detailing first-line and subsequent second-line antidepressant options.

Procedure for unblinding

Unblinding will occur at week 12 post medication intervention. Furthermore, participants’ GPs from both study groups will be sent a copy of their patient’s personal PGx reports obtained from the PGx testing along with a cover letter outlining the process of their treatment thus far.

Emergency unblinding

If a participant experiences an adverse event or serious adverse event requiring immediate withdrawal from the study (see Oversight and Monitoring for further details), unblinding will occur at that time to ensure appropriate clinical management by the participant and their GP.

Sample size

Previous RCTs investigating the effect of PGx-guided treatment on antidepressant response in adults have demonstrated a moderate effect size with sample sizes as small as 50 persons.72 Similar work in youth has been conducted in a sample size comparable to these studies analysed by Vande Voort and colleagues.57 As this study is a pilot RCT, recruitment will see a minimum of 20 participants completed per study group, with a total minimum of 40 participants. To achieve this, approximately 80 participants will be recruited to account for 25% drop-out and ineligibility due to participants not meeting the DSM-5 M.I.N.I. criteria. This sample size is in accordance with current literature regarding PGx pilot RCT studies.73

Data collection and management

Assessment and collection of outcomes and data management

The GENE-YD database will be recorded in a secure web-based data management system. All data will be coded using participant-specific unique identifiers. The list of participant names and corresponding unique identifiers will be stored in a password protected file, accessible only by members of the research team not involved in data analysis. All data will be collected and stored in a REDCap database only accessible by members of the research team involved in data collection and analysis. REDCap data repository will be held at The University of Western Australia (UWA) data centre and governed by UWA information technology and security. All research staff in charge of data collection and analysis will be appropriately trained in the use of the REDCap database.

Confidentiality

All appropriate measures will be taken to respect the privacy and confidentiality of participants in the conduct of this study. The study site will ensure that participants’ anonymity is maintained and that their identities are protected from unauthorised parties. The study will comply with the patient privacy and data protection legislation in the jurisdictions in which the research project operates.

All personal data collected will be used in a confidential and non-identifiable manner. Each participant will be given a unique identifier for the purpose of this study, which will be used to de-identify the participants in any publications resulting from this project. All identifiable documents will be stored in a locked cabinet accessible only to members of the research team with swipe access.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant-level data and statistical code

The study protocol will be made available at the Australian New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry (ACTRN12624000760572). Dissemination of the datasets analysed, as well as the statistical code, will be determined by the research team on a case-by-case basis on reasonable request. Any data that is made available will be shared at the group level and completely de-identified. The full study protocol will be available on a case-by-case basis from the corresponding author with approval from the Principal Investigator.

Dissemination plans

Results from this research project will be disseminated through presentation at scientific and medical conferences (national and international) in the fields of clinical genetics, psychiatry and other relevant areas of medicine. The study results will be submitted for publication in peer-reviewed journals in a timely fashion.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for analysing primary and secondary outcomes

All data processing and analysis will occur in either REDCap or the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS). Data manipulation will occur only if the data violates the statistical assumptions for the chosen statistical tests being conducted. If data manipulation is required, the data will be modified into a format that does not jeopardise the interpretability of the assessment scores.

Analysis of primary outcomes

The primary outcome measures will be assessed using descriptive statistics, proportion analyses and qualitative analysis. To address the first primary outcome and estimate the proportion of voluntary participants required for a future full-scale RCT using this protocol, recruitment ratios will be calculated over the active recruitment period. This analysis will inform the expected timeframe needed to achieve the required sample size in a larger trial.74 Protocol adherence and attrition rates will be evaluated using proportion statistics and ratios. These findings will assist in determining the proportion of additional participants needed in a larger trial to accommodate expected attrition.74 Reasons for dropout will also be examined to identify potential strategies to reduce attrition in future studies. Lastly, qualitative data collected from the week 24 feedback interviews will be analysed to assess participant satisfaction with the GENE-YD protocol. The analytical approach will follow the methodology established in our previous research.51

Analysis of secondary outcomes

As this is a pilot trial, the secondary outcomes will be assessed using an exploratory approach, with a focus on estimating effect sizes and confidence intervals, rather than formal hypothesis testing. These approaches are based on recommendations from the literature for studies with a protocol similar to the current study.75 Analyses will support the interpretation of trends over time and help determine the acceptability of outcome measures for a future full-scale RCT.

All analyses will follow the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle, defined as all randomised participants. Descriptive statistics will be presented for each group across time points. To explore changes in depression severity and related outcomes, linear mixed-effects models will be used, with time (baseline, week 6 and week 12) as a repeated measure and intervention group (PGx-guided vs control) as the between-participant factor. Confidence intervals will be calculated around estimated effects and interpretation will focus on whether intervals cross zero (indicating no evidence of a difference). Between-group comparisons at individual time points will not be emphasised; visualisation of trajectories over time will, however, support understanding of potential symptomatic improvement and effect directionality. Similarly, summary change scores over time will be presented for relevant clinical scales (eg, MADRS, QIDS-SR, DASS-21, AQoL-8D, MARS-5), with 95% CIs reported to explore the potential size and variability of effects. These estimates will inform whether outcome measures are sensitive to change and suitable for future use.

Any associations between antidepressant response and secondary outcomes (eg, anxiety, side effects, QoL, adherence) will be explored using correlation coefficients and confidence intervals to assess the strength and direction of relationships. These analyses are intended to guide the selection and modelling of outcome variables in a larger trial.

Interim analyses

No formal interim analyses for efficacy or futility are planned during the trial. Safety variables will be monitored by the independent Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) after all 80 participants have been enrolled. If there are concerns about the safety of participants, the board will make a recommendation to the trial steering committee about continuing, ceasing or modifying the trial. The Haybittle-Peto procedure for generating early stopping boundaries will be used. A recommendation of early termination due to safety reasons will be considered by the independent DSMB if the corresponding Haybittle-Peto boundary (p=0.001, Z=3) is crossed at any given interim analysis period.

Methods for additional analyses

Important prognostic variables, such as sex, age and depression severity, as assessed in the DSM-5 M.I.N.I., will be used as additional independent variables for secondary subgroup analyses.

Definition of analysis population relating to protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data

Participants who withdraw or are lost to follow-up and have not completed any post-intervention assessments will be analysed for ITT only. Participants who withdraw from the study or are lost to follow-up before the 6 week post-intervention assessment will be included as non-responders and non-remitters.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating centre and trial steering committee

The steering committee for this study will be made up of the named authors on this protocol. All committee members have been involved in trial design and will provide ongoing support as active investigators in the trial.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure

A data safety monitoring committee will be developed by the independent DSMB to ensure appropriate governance of data and to mitigate risk of potential safety breaches. This committee will be separate from the steering committee and the sponsor.

Adverse events and harms

The procedures for assessing and responding to potential participant safety events will be in accordance with NHMRC’s Safety Monitoring and Reporting Guidelines. The Chief Investigators are responsible for evaluating all safety events in the trial and reporting all relevant details to the North Metropolitan Area Mental Health Services Human Research Ethics Committee (EC00273; PRN RGS0000006822) and any relevant mental health services. Participants experiencing discomfort or distress over a particular element of the current study will be monitored. Should any participant experience prolonged episodes of distress, the Chief Investigators, with the help from the research team, will be responsible for offering assistance in obtaining any additional support they may require, providing contact numbers and links to mental health services that may assist the participant. Criteria and definition of an adverse event (AE) and serious adverse event (SAE) are as follows.

Definition of an adverse event

An AE within this study is defined as any untoward medical occurrence in a participant temporarily associated with the use of a pharmaceutical product, whether considered relating to the pharmaceutical product or not. An AE can therefore be any unfavourable and unintended sign, symptom or disease (new or exacerbated) temporarily associated with the use of the product, whether considered related to the product or not. AEs will be assessed regardless of administration of a pharmaceutical product from time of informed consent signing until study completion.

Definition of an adverse event of special interest

Some of the common AEs to the antidepressant medication used are of special interest, including insomnia, anxiety, headaches, dizziness, weight gain, nausea, sexual dysfunction, agitation, palpitation and tremors. These are referred to as adverse events of special interest (AESI) and will be reported in the electronic case report form (eCRF) and documented in an AESI reporting form. A summary of all AESIs will be sent to the DSMB on a schedule set by the DSMB.

Definition of a serious adverse event

A SAE is any untoward medical occurrence that, at any dose, may (1) result in death, (2) lead to a life-threatening circumstance, (3) require hospitalisation or prolongation of existing hospitalisation, (4) result in disability/incapacity, and/or (5) cause a congenital anomaly/birth defect in the offspring of a participant who received treatment medication.

Within this study, any medically significant event that may not be immediately life-threatening or result in death or hospitalisation but, based on appropriate medical and scientific judgement may jeopardise the participant or may require medical or surgical intervention to prevent one of the outcomes listed above will also be considered an SAE and will be recorded and treated as such.

Prompt reporting of serious adverse events

This study is Risk Level A as defined by the NHMRC Risk-based Management and Monitoring of Clinical Trials Involving Therapeutic Goods, 2018. SAEs will be reported to the study monitor within one working day. Upward reporting of SAEs will be carried out by the Chief Investigators on the project. All adverse events will be reported immediately to the local research ethics committee in accordance with the Therapeutic Goods Administration ‘The Australian Clinical Trial Handbook‘ 2006, and the NHMRC ‘Safety monitoring and reporting in clinical trials involving therapeutic goods’ 2016.

Follow-up of adverse events

The type and duration of the follow-up of participants after AEs will see a mandatory 1 week follow-up carried out following any reported AEs. The time frame of subsequent follow-ups will be guided by the preference of the participant. Actions taken by researchers to address any AEs will be responsive to the nature of incidents. Resolution of AEs will be carried out by the Chief Investigators.

Evaluating adverse events and serious adverse events

The investigator will assess the intensity of each AE and SAE reported during the study. The assessment will be based on the investigator’s clinical judgement. The intensity of each AE and SAE recorded in the eCRF or SAE form should be assigned to one of the following categories: (1) mild: an event that is easily tolerated by the subject, causing minimal discomfort and not interfering with everyday activities, (2) moderate: an event that is sufficiently discomforting to interfere with normal everyday activities, or (3) severe: an event which is incapacitating and prevents normal everyday activities.

An AE that is assessed as severe should not be confused with an SAE. Severity is a category utilised for rating the intensity of an event and both AEs and SAEs can be assessed as severe. An AE is upgraded to a SAE when it meets one of the pre-defined outcomes as described previously.

Risk of suicide expressed by participants during study visits

Participants are screened for clinical risk of harm to self, others and objects or property during the screening phase of this study. However, if the presence of suicidal ideation is indicated during a study visit or assessment, the study coordinator will gather information to assist in determining the level of risk. This will be based on the Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale, measuring ideation frequency, intensity, content and change; intent; history of attempts; protective factors; supports; and other issues such as current drug and alcohol use. These measures are in accordance with the study measures, the Clinical Practice Guidelines for Emergency Departments and Mental Health Services,76 and the Western Australian Principles and Best Practice for the Care of People Who May Be Suicidal.77

Dependent on the level of risk presented at this assessment, the study coordinator or central assessor will ensure the participant has been provided with the crisis and support contacts, encourage the participant to discuss this with their treatment team and assist in contacting the treatment team if necessary, discuss with the research team’s senior clinicians and/or Principal Investigator following the assessment, and/or contact the Principal Investigator requesting an assessment prior to the participant completing their study visit. If required, decisions regarding the best action relating to the immediate management and continuation in the trial will be determined by the Principal Investigator, in consultation with the participant’s clinical treatment team, and the participant themselves.

In the unlikely scenario that a participant is unwilling for services to be contacted, and the person presents a risk to themselves or others, the researchers will have a duty of care to contact services without the participant’s permission. This possibility is highlighted to participants in the participant information sheet.

Provisions for ancillary and post-trial care

All participants will be treated by their treatment team, including their nominated GP. Thus, any participant accepted into this study is unlikely to require post-trial care from the research team. Once the trial is complete, there is no follow-up. The exception to this is if a participant withdraws due to deteriorating mental health condition. In this instance, the research team will follow-up with the participant to ensure they are receiving adequate care.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct

Internal audits may be organised and run by the approving HREC committee and/or members of the research team to ensure that the trial conduct is adhered to. This will be separate from routine trial monitoring.

Plans for communicating important protocol modifications to relevant parties

Any changes or amendments to the study protocol will be sent to review by the local research ethics committee. The Principal Investigator will notify both the sponsor and the research team of any changes and will update the protocol on the clinical trial registry.

Discussion

The use of antidepressants to treat MDD in young people has been steadily increasing in recent years,4 despite limited evidence of their efficacy in youth.18 Given that interindividual genetic variations have been suggested to affect antidepressant response,23 PGx may be useful in guiding clinicians when selecting medication for young people with MDD on a personalised level, with the ultimate goal of improving therapeutic outcomes and reducing adverse events and discontinued treatment.49 However, to date, limited studies have examined the clinical effects of pre-emptive PGx testing on antidepressant efficacy and tolerability in youth. The aim of this pilot RCT is to determine the feasibility for a large-scale RCT evaluating the efficacy and tolerability of antidepressants prescribed to youth with MDD using PGx-guided treatments compared with standard prescription processes informed by Australian regulatory guidelines. This pilot RCT will help to inform the design and development of further studies.

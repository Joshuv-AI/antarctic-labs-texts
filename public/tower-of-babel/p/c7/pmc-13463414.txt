Design, implementation, and evaluation of a mobile-based self-care application to enhance self-care behaviors in patients with rheumatoid arthritis in Iran: a mixed-methods study protocol with an interventional design
Authors: Zhina Banafshi, Nahid Dehghan Nayeri, Shahab Rezaeian, Meysam Siyah Mansoory, Nasrin Moghimi, Alireza Khatony
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic autoimmune disease that requires long-term management and active self-care. In this context, emerging technologies—particularly mobile health applications—have demonstrated considerable potential in facilitating and enhancing self-care among patients with RA. This study aims to design, implement, and evaluate the effectiveness of a mobile-based self-care application to improve self-management in individuals with RA.MethodsThis study employs a sequential exploratory mixed-methods design with three phases, conducted in Sanandaj, Kurdistan Province, West Iran. In the qualitative phase (4 months), a conventional content analysis approach will be used to explore self-care experiences, needs, barriers, and facilitators among patients with RA. Data will be collected through semi-structured interviews using purposive maximum-variation sampling until saturation is reached. The data will be analyzed using the Graneheim and Lundman method to extract categories. In the development phase (8 months), the Nominal Group Technique (NGT) will be conducted with a multidisciplinary expert panel to refine, enrich, and prioritize the identified needs and define the mobile application’s content. Software development will follow the Waterfall Model. Usability and user satisfaction will be evaluated through expert reviews and a pilot study involving 30 patients. In the quantitative phase (4 months), a Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT) will assess the application’s effectiveness. Seventy-two RA patients will be randomly assigned to intervention (n = 36) or control (n = 36) groups. Both groups will receive routine care, while the intervention group will additionally use the application for 8 weeks. Outcomes include self-care levels, quality of life, and disability index. Ethical and administrative approvals were obtained prior to study commencement.DiscussionThis study investigates the impact of a mobile-based self-care application on improving self-care behaviors and disease management among patients with RA. The design and implementation of this application are expected to enhance patient experiences, promote self-care, and reduce negative disease outcomes, while also improving quality of life and lowering the disability index.Trial registrationIRCT, IRCT20100913004736N30. Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a common chronic inflammatory disease characterized by progressive and irreversible damage to symmetrical joints caused by persistent synovitis [1]. The global prevalence of RA ranges from 0.5% to 1% [2]. Patients with RA often experience symptoms such as fatigue, pain, disability, functional impairment, and joint stiffness. As the disease progresses, extra-articular tissues and organ systems may also be affected [3], with approximately 40% of patients reporting extra-articular manifestations [4]. In addition to physical symptoms, individuals with RA frequently encounter psychological challenges [5], including a broad spectrum of negative emotions such as low mood, generalized anxiety, clinical depression, feelings of loss, and social difficulties due to altered social roles [6]. Furthermore, RA imposes a substantial financial burden on patients due to increased healthcare costs, disability, and reduced work capacity [7]. Despite significant advances in healthcare, a definitive cure for RA has not yet been found [8]. The goal of RA treatment extends beyond restoring health; it also involves promoting adaptation, self-management, and active participation in the treatment process [9]. Continuous care and monitoring are essential for these patients, and self-care is considered a vital component of effective disease management [10].

Self-care comprises a set of behaviors and actions undertaken by individuals to monitor and manage their condition and maintain their health [11]. These actions include medication adherence, regular physical activity, proper nutrition, and timely consultation with healthcare providers when symptoms arise [12]. For patients with RA, self-care can lead to symptom reduction, improved physical functioning, enhanced quality of life [2], increased self-confidence, independence, self-efficacy, improved health status, reduced disability, more effective interaction with the healthcare system, fewer clinical visits, and lower healthcare costs [13]. Previous studies have demonstrated that self-care education programs significantly improve disease awareness and treatment adherence among patients with chronic conditions, thereby reducing complications and mortality rates [14, 15].

Many individuals with RA face significant barriers to accessing healthcare services and visiting medical centers. Consequently, they often seek information from various sources, some of which may be unreliable [16]. In recent years, several innovative strategies have been introduced to enhance self-care among patients with chronic diseases, including telehealth [17]. When healthcare professionals, such as physicians and nurses, provide telehealth services in a structured manner, they can play a significant role in empowering patients to manage their conditions effectively [18]. Compared to traditional methods, telehealth can substantially reduce the time and cost associated with disease management [19]. Given their diverse functions and significant advantages, mobile applications have demonstrated high potential in facilitating education and implementing self-care programs [20]. Mobile health applications, with their broad capabilities and numerous benefits, are increasingly recognized as effective tools for self-care support [21]. These applications are particularly well-suited to chronic disease management due to their accessibility, low cost, ability to deliver reliable and up-to-date information, data-sharing functionality, and scalability in reaching large patient populations [22, 23]. By enabling informed patient engagement, mobile health apps can enhance motivation, accountability, treatment adherence, and active communication with healthcare team [24].

Numerous studies have confirmed the effectiveness of these tools. For example, Cozad et al. (2022) in the United States demonstrated that self-care applications can improve patient knowledge and engagement by offering features such as symptom tracking, side effect monitoring, and support for social interaction [25]. Similarly, Kurt et al. (2024) in Germany found that health applications can facilitate disease management by promoting healthier lifestyles and dietary habits [26]. A study by Seppen et al. (2022) in the Netherlands also revealed that self-care apps can reduce the need for clinical and hospital visits [27]. However, it is important to acknowledge the limitations associated with smartphone applications, such as reduced face-to-face interaction between patients and healthcare teams, technical issues, and concerns about privacy breaches [28–30].

Given the high and growing prevalence of RA in recent years, as well as the extensive and long-term impacts of this condition on various aspects of patients' personal, social, and economic lives, the need for the design, implementation, and evaluation of a dedicated self-care mobile application for individuals with RA has become increasingly evident. In this context, a mixed-methods study is planned to design, implement, and evaluate the effectiveness of a self-care mobile application in improving self-care behaviors among patients with RA public and private rheumatology clinics in Sanandaj, the capital of Kurdistan Province, West Iran. The findings from this research are expected to play a significant role in enhancing the quality of life of these individuals.

Study design

This study adopts a sequential exploratory mixed-methods design. The exploratory sequential design is a strategic approach in which qualitative and quantitative data are collected and analyzed in a stepwise, structured manner [31]. The study consists of three distinct phases: design, implementation, and evaluation, each with its specific methodology.

Phase I – design (qualitative)

In the first phase, a qualitative approach will be employed to explore and identify the experiences, perspectives, and self-care needs of patients with RA. Using the conventional content analysis method, data will be collected through in-depth semi-structured interviews with participants selected via purposive sampling. The findings from this phase will serve as the foundation for designing and developing the self-care application in the subsequent quantitative phase.

Phase II – implementation (system development)

To prioritize the self-care needs identified from participants' experiences, a nominal group technique (NGT) will be used through an expert panel. The panel will include rheumatologists, orthopedic specialists, physiotherapists, occupational therapists, pharmacologists, traditional medicine practitioners, clinical nurses working in rheumatology wards, and nursing faculty members.

For system development, the waterfall development model will be adopted as the methodological framework for software product development. As a classical approach in software engineering, the waterfall model enables systematic management of the development process through its linear and phase-based structure. This model provides clear phase delineation, facilitates accurate scheduling, reduces the risk of late-stage errors, and is well-suited to projects with well-defined requirements. Additionally, its structured documentation and process transparency support continuous quality assessment and control throughout each phase of development [32].

To ensure the scientific validity of the developed application, expert evaluations, usability testing, and user satisfaction assessments will be conducted [33, 34]. This multifaceted validation strategy enables a comprehensive evaluation of the application's credibility and enhances confidence in its quality and effectiveness.

Phase III – evaluation: randomized controlled trial (RCT)

In the third phase, a randomized controlled trial (RCT) will be conducted to evaluate the effectiveness of the developed self-care application in enhancing elf-care behaviors, quality of life, and functional disability index among patients with RA. This trial will be initiated after obtaining ethical approval from the Research Ethics Committee (IR.KUMS.REC.1403.603) and registration in the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT20100913004736N30).

Eligible participants will be recruited using a convenience sampling method. Before enrollment, the study objectives and methodology will be clearly explained to all potential participants, and written informed consent will be obtained. To ensure participant privacy, strict confidentiality measures will be implemented. Participants will also be informed of their right to withdraw from the study at any stage without facing any adverse consequences.

Sample size and sampling method

Study, purposive sampling with a naturalistic approach [38] will be employed to select individuals with direct experience in RA self-care who possess the ability and willingness to articulate their experiences. The sampling process will follow a maximum variation strategy to identify a wide range of experiences across different personal and social contexts. To ensure diversity, variables such as age, gender, education, socio-economic status, disease duration, and marital status will be considered. Additionally, through field presence and consultation with rheumatologists, participants with rich lived experiences and high expressive ability will be recruited as key informants. Sampling will continue until data saturation is reached..Data saturation is reached when further interviews yield no new findings or codes, and no new categories or themes emerge within the conceptual framework. At this point, the existing data structure is considered robust and sufficiently comprehensive to explain the phenomenon [39]. To ensure the stability of the findings and confirm that true saturation has been achieved, three additional interviews will be conducted after the point where no new themes or categories emerge.

Setting

The research setting for the qualitative phase comprises a combination of formal rheumatology healthcare environments in Sanandaj (including the sub-specialty clinic at Tohid Hospital and private rheumatology offices) and the natural living environments of patients with RA. This setting is selected to facilitate a profound understanding of lived experiences, self-care patterns, and social interactions within the context of patients' daily lives. Data collection will occur in both clinical and non-clinical settings to comprehensively explain the phenomenon within its real-world, multidimensional context. Specifically, home-based interviews will be offered to participants based on their preference to capture authentic self-care challenges, while strictly adhering to ethical and privacy protocols.

Data collection (qualitative phase)

Data will be collected by the first author, a PhD candidate in nursing with prior experience in qualitative research and clinical expertise in the field of rheumatology. To minimize potential bias and ensure data credibility, the researcher will continuously employ bracketing and reflexivity strategies throughout the data collection and analysis process. Upon obtaining the necessary permissions, the researcher will visit rheumatology clinics and private practices to introduce the study and recruit eligible participants through voluntary participation. Interviews will be conducted at a time and place convenient for each participant. to ensure privacy, only the researcher and the participant will be present during these sessions. Each interview will begin with warm-up questions to establish rapport. The interview guide will serve as a flexible framework, allowing for iterative refinements based on emerging categories and participants' responses. To clarify and deepen participants’ responses, probing questions such as “Could you explain further?”, “What do you mean?”, and “How so?” will be used. Interviews will last approximately 30–90 min, though the duration remains flexible based on data richness and participant contributions; if necessary, sessions may be extended to ensure saturation. All interviews will be audio-recorded with participants’ consent. Additionally, field notes will be taken to document the researcher’s observations of verbal and non-verbal cues, enriching the data set. To ensure data accuracy, member checking will be performed by summarizing and clarifying responses with participants during and at the end of each session.

Data analysis

The data will be analyzed using conventional content analysis as described by Graneheim and Lundman (2004) [40], which captures both manifest content and latent meanings at various levels of abstraction.

Immediately after each interview, the audio recordings will be transcribed verbatim. Transcripts will be imported into MAXQDA version 0.9.5 for analysis. To achieve deep immersion in the data, the researcher will listen to the recordings multiple times and thoroughly review the transcripts. Meaning units will be identified, coded, and grouped based on conceptual similarities to form subcategories. Subcategories with related meanings will then be merged into final categories.

To ensure consistency and rigor in the coding and categorization process, several analytical meetings will be held with the research team.

Trustworthiness and rigor

To establish trustworthiness, the criteria proposed by Lincoln and Guba—credibility, dependability, confirmability, transferability, and authenticity, will be applied [41, 42]. Credibility will be ensured through prolonged engagement, maximum variation sampling, member checking, and peer debriefing. Confirmability will be established by maintaining a detailed audit trail and employing reflexivity to acknowledge and manage the researcher's preconceptions. Dependability will be guaranteed through a transparent description of the analysis process and regular research team meetings. To enhance transferability, a thick description of the context and participants will be provided. Finally, authenticity will be maintained by fairly reflecting participants' perspectives and using direct quotations to support the findings.

Phase II – intermediate phase

Specific objective

To identify, refine, and prioritize self-care strategies and needs in patients with RA through a qualitative-consensual approach. The Nominal Group Technique (NGT) will be employed to reach expert consensus.

Data collection

The panel will consist of ten multidisciplinary experts purposefully selected based on their clinical expertise and academic background in RA management. To ensure the inclusion of highly qualified professionals, candidates must possess at least five years of professional experience in RA-related care or rehabilitation, alongside documented research or educational activities in the field. To adopt a holistic perspective through maximum variation sampling, the group will represent diverse specialties, including a rheumatologist, orthopedist, IT specialist, nutritionist, psychologist, physiotherapist, occupational therapist, pharmacologist, clinical nurse, and a nursing faculty member. Following a formal invitation via official email or direct contact—during which the study objectives and NGT protocol are detailed—the session will be held in person at the Conference Hall of Kurdistan University of Medical Sciences to facilitate structured face-to-face brainstorming. The session will be moderated and facilitated by the principal investigator to ensure a structured environment and prevent the influence of dominant voices. To ensure methodological rigor and transparency, the entire session will be audio-recorded (with informed consent), supplemented by detailed field notes and the verbatim recording of ideas on a flip chart to provide a visual reference for consensus-building and systematic documentation.

Given the aim of this phase—to prioritize self-care needs—NGT will be used. This structured method enables balanced group discussion while ensuring individual idea generation and independent voting, thereby minimizing the influence of dominant voices in the group [43, 44].

Development phase

In this study, the Waterfall model, one of the most recognized and widely used methods for software development, is employed. The choice of this model is based on its proven advantages, including a linear and sequential structure, clearly defined tasks, precise scheduling, reduced risk of errors in later stages, and compatibility with projects that have well-defined requirements [32]. The Waterfall model consists of six interconnected stages: requirements analysis, system design, implementation, integration and testing, deployment, and maintenance [46].

In this study, the mobile application development process occurs in several key phases. In the first phase (Requirements Analysis), the needs and experiences of users will be identified, refined, enriched, and prioritized using qualitative research methods and the NGT. The findings from this analysis will serve as the foundation for determining the application’s content. The content of the application will encompass all identified self-care needs derived from the qualitative phase and subsequently refined, enriched, and prioritized through the NGT process. The intervention is not restricted to any specific organ or joint; rather, it integrates educational priorities identified by patients and experts—including, but not limited to, symptom management, nutritional guidance, medication adherence, mental health support, and disease adaptation—provided that these components are technically feasible for implementation within a mobile health framework. In the System Design phase, the identified content will be accurately detailed in paper designs, including app screens, the information for each screen, interlinking between screens, and the program's output. Subsequently, the Content Validity (including CVR and CVI) of the application's content is quantitatively and qualitatively reviewed and confirmed by ten experts in the field of rheumatology and related disciplines. Following the incorporation of the experts' suggested revisions, the Face Validity of the application's content is evaluated by the end-users (patients). The final output of these stages consists of the approved paper designs (wireframes/prototypes) which will serve as the final roadmap for commencing the next phase: Implementation. These final paper designs are then translated into the functional mobile application with the assistance of a specialized software engineer.

During the Implementation phase, different sections of the app will be provided separately to both patients and specialists. Based on the feedback received, minor adjustments will be made before moving on to the next phase. In the Integration and Testing phase, the complete version of the application will be tested for one week by faculty members with expertise in areas such as rheumatology, physiotherapy, pharmacy, occupational therapy, nursing, graphic design, and information technology. They will be asked to evaluate the content for coverage, clarity, comprehensibility, and alignment with the study's goals and variables, providing corrective feedback. This feedback will be applied to improve the content.

In the Deployment phase, the application will be installed on the mobile phones of patients with RA, in accordance with the research requirements. Patient feedback regarding the accuracy, comprehensiveness, and ease of use of the tool will be collected and used to make necessary revisions. Afterward, the app will undergo testing and debugging to ensure it functions as expected. The final version of the application will then be prepared.

In the Usability and Satisfaction Evaluation phase, patients will be assessed using the Questionnaire for User Interaction Satisfaction (QUIS), a standardized instrument designed by a multidisciplinary team at the University of Maryland's Human–Computer Interaction Lab (1983) [47], with the Persian version psychometrically validated [33, 34]. The QUIS questionnaire consists of six sections assessing overall system feedback, screen design, terminology and information, system learnability, overall capabilities, usability, and user interface. The scoring is based on a 9-point Likert scale (0 to 9), with user satisfaction categorized as poor (0–3), average (3.1–6), and good (6.1–9) [48]. This phase will be conducted with a separate pilot group of 30 RA patients who are entirely distinct from the main RCT participants. This separation is strictly maintained to enhance study transparency and prevent any potential selection or participation bias in the subsequent clinical trial. This questionnaire will be administered to 30 RA patients, and if a "good" score is achieved, the app will proceed to the clinical trial phase. If a "good" score is not achieved, the feedback will be carefully analyzed to identify weaknesses in the user interface. Necessary changes will be made, and the app will be re-evaluated to ensure users’ satisfaction reaches the desired level, preparing it for clinical trials.

Sample size

Based on the results reported by Haglo et al. (2021), the General Health dimension of the RAND-36 questionnaire improved by a mean of 8.8 points with a standard deviation (SD) of 10.5 in the supervised group of patients with rheumatoid arthritis. For the present randomized controlled trial, the sample size was calculated using the formula for comparing two independent means. Assuming a two-tailed significance level (α = 0.05) and a power of 90% (β = 0.10), with a pooled standard deviation (σ) of 10.5 and an expected mean difference (Δ) of 8.8 between the intervention and control groups, the estimated sample size was approximately 30 participants per group. To ensure adequate statistical power and to compensate for potential attrition (approximately 20%), the final adjusted sample size was set at 36 participants per group (72 participants in total). This sample size will provide sufficient power to detect clinically meaningful differences in the General Health dimension of quality of life between the two groups, consistent with previous research findings [49].

Sampling

In the quantitative phase, participants will be selected using convenience sampling and randomly assigned to either the intervention or control groups, labeled as Group A and Group B, respectively. To optimize the randomization process and enhance allocation concealment, a variable block randomization method using block sizes of four and two will be applied. This approach considers all possible allocation sequences for A and B within four- and two-sized blocks, offering flexibility for non-multiple-of-four sample sizes and providing comprehensive allocation concealment.

The total number of blocks used will be determined based on the overall sample size. To perform randomization, an online tool (www.sealedenvelope.com) will be employed.

Concealment mechanism

After generating the randomization sequence, the allocation concealment process will be executed to prevent participants and researchers from predicting future allocations [50].

For implementation, the randomization sequences for each block will be written on separate cards and placed in opaque envelopes to ensure concealment. Each envelope will be numbered, and participants will be allocated to the corresponding study group based on the sequences written on the cards. This process will continue until all participants are allocated.

Blinding

Since both the researcher and participants will be aware of the type of intervention, blinding is not feasible. However, the study's statistical consultant will remain unaware of the group allocation to maintain the integrity of the allocation concealment process.

Recruitment

To ensure the enrollment of the required 72 participants, the research team will implement comprehensive strategies. The process will begin with close collaboration between the principal investigator and the team's rheumatology specialist, private rheumatologist clinics in Sanandaj City. This collaboration is crucial for identifying and referring potentially eligible patients by their treating physicians. The research team will offer flexible scheduling for initial meetings to obtain informed consent and conduct baseline assessments, accommodating participant availability. During the recruitment process, the benefits of participation (such as increased self-care knowledge and access to the educational application after the study concludes for the control group) will be thoroughly explained to enhance participant motivation. Finally, for patients who express initial interest but do not immediately enroll, a courteous follow-up and reminder system will be implemented via phone calls. These strategies, leveraging the direct clinical expertise of the team's rheumatology specialist, aim to efficiently recruit the necessary patients and ensure the study progresses as planned.

Interventions

Prior to the commencement of the intervention, baseline data (pre-test) will be collected. This pre-test includes a demographic information form, a self-care questionnaire, a quality of life measure, and the Health Assessment Questionnaire-Disability Index. Participants will receive access to the Self-Care Mobile Application. After obtaining permission and providing clear instructions on how to use the application, the researcher will install it on the smartphones of participants in this group. The researcher will provide their mobile number for ongoing support and will collect participants' mobile numbers to facilitate communication. The researcher will contact participants every week to follow up and address any questions they may have. This application will provide educational content on self-care behaviors for rheumatoid arthritis. The content of this application has been designed based on the identified needs of patients through a prior qualitative study and prioritized using a nominal group technique. The educational modules within the app will cover essential self-care aspects and will be delivered over 8 weeks.

Control group

At baseline, a pre-test will be administered, which includes the demographic information form and questionnaires assessing self-care, quality of life (QoL), and the functional disability index. Participants in this group will receive standard care and routine educational materials provided by the clinic. They will not have access to the mobile application.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions

To ensure maximal participant adherence to both the study protocol and interventions, several key strategies will be concurrently implemented. Initially, the first author will provide comprehensive and transparent training on how to use the self-care mobile application to the intervention group. Ongoing support will be provided through weekly phone calls to this group, which will include answering questions, troubleshooting technical issues, and offering encouragement. Furthermore, the control group will be assured that they will also receive the application after the study concludes. Clarity regarding each group's role will be crucial to prevent cross-contamination. Concurrently, continuous and systematic monitoring of adherence will be conducted. This oversight will involve meticulously documenting weekly phone call records to identify adherence issues and any non-use of the application within the intervention group. Direct participant feedback, gathered through open-ended questions during follow-up calls and the usability/satisfaction questionnaire at Week 8, will provide valuable insights. Additionally, adherence to completing baseline and end-of-study assessments (Demographic Information Form and the Self-Care Behavior Scale—SCBS) will be tracked for all participants, with necessary reminders sent for incomplete assessments.

Concomitant care

During the 8-week intervention period, participants will be advised to refrain from engaging in other mobile applications or new self-care programs specifically designed for rheumatoid arthritis management. This measure will be implemented to minimize confounding variables and ensure that any observed changes in self-care behaviors can be primarily attributed to the Elast Self-Care Mobile Application. Participants will, however, continue their standard medical care, including prescribed medications and routine visits with their rheumatologist, as advised by their healthcare providers. No changes to their ongoing standard treatment will be required or encouraged by the study team.

Outcome measures

The primary outcome will be the change in self-care behaviors, which will be measured using the Self-Care Behavior Scale (SCBS), the change in quality of life, and the change in the functional disability index. All outcomes will be measured at baseline and at the end of the intervention period (8 weeks).

Scoring employs a four-point Likert scale ranging from 0 (without any difficulty) to 3 (unable to do). The final HAQ-DI score, derived from the mean of the eight subscale scores, falls within a range of 0 to 3, where a lower score signifies an absence of disability and 3 represents the highest level of disability. Furthermore, Construct Validity was confirmed by exhibiting strong, significant negative correlations with the physical component summary of the SF-36 (r = –0.77) and strong positive correlations with disease activity indicators such as the Ritchie Articular Index (RAI) (r = 0.65). The instrument also showed a statistically significant ability to discriminate (P < 0.001) between patients categorized by positive and negative CRP and ESR status [55].

Adverse event reporting and harms

The principal investigator and the research team will diligently monitor, document, and report all Adverse Events (AEs) and Serious Adverse Events (SAEs) that occur in participants throughout the study. The primary objective is to ensure participant safety and collect comprehensive data for assessing the intervention's safety profile. AEs will be defined as any untoward medical occurrence in a participant that occurs after the intervention, not necessarily having a causal relationship with the app. SAEs will be defined as any AE that results in death, is life-threatening, requires inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization, results in persistent or significant disability/incapacity, or is a congenital anomaly/birth defect. All SAEs will be reported to the Ethics Committee for Research at Kermanshah University of Medical Sciences within 24 h of becoming aware of the event. It's important to note that the use of the mobile application itself does not entail direct pharmacological or physical side effects; however, any related distress or unexpected outcomes will be recorded and managed appropriately.

Provisions for ancillary and post-trial care, and compensation for harm

Should any harm or serious adverse event directly attributable to study participation occur, participants will receive all necessary medical care. Consistent with ethical guidelines and national regulations in Iran, all treatment costs arising from study-related adverse events will be covered by the research team and the sponsoring institution (Kermanshah University of Medical Sciences).

Composition of the Data Monitoring Committee (DMC), its role and reporting structure

Given the low-risk nature of the intervention (a mobile application) and the short 8-week study duration, an independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) is not deemed necessary. Instead, the Ethics Committee for Research at Kermanshah University of Medical Sciences will be responsible for overseeing safety and receiving both periodic and immediate reports of Adverse Events and Serious Adverse Events. Concurrently, the internal research team, particularly the principal investigator and the rheumatology specialist, will continuously monitor the data. This approach provides adequate oversight commensurate with the study's risk level.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct

Given the low-risk nature of the study intervention (a mobile application for self-care) and its relatively short duration, a formal, independent external audit program is not planned for this trial. However, rigorous and regular internal monitoring will be conducted to ensure data quality, adherence to the protocol, and compliance with ethical and legal principles. This oversight will be carried out by the principal investigator and the research team.

The results of this study, regardless of their findings, will be disseminated transparently and completely. The primary findings will be published in a peer-reviewed scientific journal. Additionally, a summary of the findings will be prepared in simple, understandable language and made available to study participants. No individually identifiable information will be disclosed in any publications or presentations.

Plans for communicating important protocol modifications

Discussion

The high prevalence of RA and its chronic, progressive nature have made self-care a central focus in research related to this disease. With the expansion of technology and the increasing use of smartphones, the application of self-care apps has garnered attention as an effective tool in managing the health of RA patients. RA is a chronic, multifaceted disease that, if not treated promptly and accompanied by appropriate self-care, can lead to joint deformities, severe movement limitations, excruciating pain, and even damage to other body systems [56]. Active self-care plays a pivotal role in slowing the progression of the disease, preventing adverse outcomes, and preserving joint health [57]. To reduce RA symptoms, such as pain and disability, self-care is essential [58].

A study by Gurjar (2024) in India revealed that arthritis patients often lack the necessary knowledge to carry out self-care practices, resulting in poor adherence and unintended consequences. As a result, inadequate self-care can significantly jeopardize individuals' health [59]. Educating patients on disease management is a crucial aspect that enables them to effectively manage their condition and improve their health outcomes [60]. The study by Drake af Hagelsrum et al. (2024) in Sweden showed that patients need to actively engage in the care process and receive education on preventive information and lifestyle management, including diet, physical activity, weight control, and smoking cessation. Acquiring knowledge in these areas enhances patients' motivation to better manage their disease [61].

Various approaches exist in the management of RA to enhance patients' self-care. Among these, mobile applications have emerged as a practical tool to promote disease management behaviors, increase self-efficacy, and improve self-monitoring [60]. Evidence from multiple studies has demonstrated that app-based educational programs are effective in increasing patients' knowledge, improving self-management behaviors, and slowing disease progression. Well-designed telehealth interventions can lead to successful self-management outcomes in RA, transforming patients into active partners in their self-care. This, in turn, boosts their responsibility in health-promoting behaviors, such as physical activity, fatigue management, and medication adherence [18, 62].

A study by Knudsen et al. (2023) in Denmark showed that self-care apps provide easy access to information about prognosis, treatment options, common symptoms, and disease management, thereby enabling informed decision-making for RA patients [62]. Another study by Salaffi et al. (2025) in Italy indicated that self-management app support can significantly improve treatment adherence and patients' self-efficacy while reducing healthcare costs and unnecessary medical visits [63]. Furthermore, the systematic review by Arumalla et al. (2023) showed that smartphone apps are significantly associated with reduced disease activity in RA patients [64].

Overall, self-care apps can contribute to improving clinical outcomes for RA patients [60]. The study by Fedkov et al. (2022) showed that self-care apps can enhance the quality of life of RA patients, improve physical function, and reduce disease activity [65]. The study by Benavent et al. (2024) in Spain also demonstrated that health apps can substantially reduce the workload of the healthcare system and rheumatologists, leading to greater empowerment of patients [66]. These studies emphasize the importance of self-care in the management of chronic diseases and the positive impact of health apps on patients' self-care behaviors. However, there is still limited research specifically addressing the self-care needs of RA patients and how these needs are recognized by rheumatology specialists. This research gap highlights the need for comprehensive, high-quality studies in this area to develop effective interventions and improve patient experiences. Such studies could combine both qualitative and quantitative methods to gain a thorough understanding of these patients' needs and provide effective solutions to enhance their self-care. Designing a self-care app based on the needs of patients and feedback from specialists, implementing it in a clinical setting, and evaluating its effectiveness using quantitative methods could help determine its impact on patients' self-care behaviors.

Strengths and limitations

This study, utilizing a mixed-methods approach (qualitative-quantitative), will facilitate the collection of deep and reliable data. In the qualitative phase, participants will be purposefully selected based on key variables such as age, gender, disease severity, duration of illness, and education level to ensure maximum diversity in sampling. This approach will allow for comprehensive data collection regarding the needs of patients. These findings will contribute to the design of a practical application that emphasizes ease of use and aligns with the feedback from both specialists and the needs of patients. In the quantitative phase, using standardized measurement tools will enhance the validity and generalizability of the results.

However, the study will face some limitations. For instance, limited access for illiterate individuals or those without smartphones to the designed application may affect the generalizability of the findings to the broader RA patient population. To address this, efforts will be made to include audio and visual features in the application and design simple user interfaces with clear graphical symbols, making the app more accessible to individuals with low literacy. A further limitation will be the lack of assessment of certain secondary outcomes, including patients’ knowledge, attitudes, and adherence to the use of the digital health tool. This may limit the comprehensiveness of the evaluation of the intervention’s effectiveness. Future studies are recommended to include a broader range of outcomes to enable a more comprehensive assessment of both effectiveness and implementation.

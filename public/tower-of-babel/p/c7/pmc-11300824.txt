Synthesis and characterization of enhanced azo-azomethine doped PANI/HCl conducting polymers for electrochemical applications
Authors: Dara Muhammed Aziz, Sangar Ali Hassan, Shujahadeen B Aziz
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this study, conducting polymers composed of polyaniline hydrochloric acid (PANI/HCl) with varying concentrations of a newly synthesized azo-azomethine dye (4-(((Z)-2-hydroxy-5-((Z)-(4-hydroxyphenyl)diazenyl)-3-methoxybenzylidene)amino)benzoic acid) were synthesized using a chemical oxidative polymerization technique. The synthesized azo-azomethine was characterized by FTIR, 1H-NMR, 13C-NMR, and HRMS. The effects of varying the concentration of the dopant azo-azomethine in PANI/HCl on its optical, structural, thermal, and electrical properties were examined using FTIR, UV–Vis, XRD, FESEM, TEM, cyclic voltammetry, and electrical impedance spectra. The results indicate that the optical, direct, and indirect band gaps of the doped polymers decreased from 4.48 and 3.96 eV to 3.91 and 2.49 eV, respectively. The crystalline structure and phase transitions in the doped polymers were examined using X-ray diffraction. Cyclic voltammetry demonstrated that the doped polymers exhibited higher electrochemical conductivity compared to the pure polymer, with the specific capacitance increasing from 161.17 to 816.9 F/g. The electrical impedance spectra revealed the bulk resistance and conductivity of the material. Among all the doped polymers, PANI/HCl with an azo-azomethine concentration of 5 × 10−5 M exhibited lower bulk resistance (10 Ω) and higher electrical conductivity (σ = 50.09 × 10−3 S cm−1).Keywords: Conducting polymer, Azo-azomethine, Specific capacitance, DopingSubject terms: Materials for devices, Materials for energy and catalysis, Soft materials, Chemistry, Materials science

Introduction

Amidst the global energy crisis, effective energy storage and conservation have become critical strategies. To meet social and ecological needs, the development of energy storage systems for modern devices is essential, ensuring a balance between energy distribution and consumption to prevent wastage1–4. Conducting polymers, notably PANI/HCl (polyaniline hydrochloric acid), offer promising solutions. PANI is a widely used conducting polymer due to its high electrical conductivity, charge transport mechanism, processability, environmental stability, low synthesis cost, and diverse range of applications. These applications include bio and gas sensors5–7, electromagnetic shielding 8, light emitting devices9, supercapacitor applications10, electrode materials11 and photovoltaic applications12,13, among others. PANI/HCl is noted for its affordability compared to other conducting polymers, making it an economically viable option for various applications. Additionally, the versatility and potential of dye-doped PANI/HCl in creating functional materials with improved optical and electrical properties, showcasing its cost-effectiveness in producing tailored materials for diverse applications14,15. Through appropriate modifications during the polymerization process, the conjugated polymer PANI can be transformed into either an insulator or a semiconductor. Modifications can be made to various factors such as the solvent type, dopant type, dopant concentrations, preparation methods, and preparation temperatures, among others. Significant changes occur in the electronic, electrical, magnetic, optical, and structural characteristics as a result of these modifications16. Both inorganic and organic acids, including hydrochloric acid, sulfuric acid, oleic acid, and camphor sulfonic acid, are utilized as dopants in semiconducting PANIHydrochloric acid, among all the dopant acids, receives significant attention from researchers for modifying several characteristics of PANI. PANI/HCl, or polyaniline doped with HCl, possesses important electrical and optical properties that are crucial in optoelectronic applications. The synthesis technique, doping amount, and characterization conditions are examples of variables that can potentially influence the electrical and optical properties of PANI/HCl. D. Geethalakshmi and colleagues17 reported that the electrochemical conductivity of PANI/HCl, with dopant hydrochloric acid concentrations of 1 and 2 molar, could range from 0.14 to 0.75 Ω−1.cm−1, 0.1 to 1 Ω−1.cm−1, and 0.12 to 1.08 Ω−1.cm−1 at temperatures of 31 °C, 13 °C, and 4 °C, respectively. These values were determined based on the dopant concentration and temperature. Furthermore, the optical properties of the doped polymers, such as energy band gaps, are influenced by changes in concentration and temperature. For the same doped polymers at the aforementioned temperatures and concentrations, the energy band gaps were found to be approximately 3.9–3.85 eV, 3.82–3.68 eV, and 3.68–3.45 eV, respectively. The PANI/HCl polymer, synthesized without the use of insulating substances such as surfactants, demonstrates partial crystallinity in the temperature range of 5–330 K. Panagiotis Dallas and colleagues conducted a study on this polymer to investigate its dc conductivity and magnetic characteristics18. The increase in conductivity as temperature decreases (from 330 to 230 K) indicates the characteristic metallic-like behavior of PANI/HCl. Catedral M. et al.19 prepared polyaniline samples with different dopants, including HCl, and employed the Pauw method to measure conductivity. The PANI/HCl doped polymer exhibited a conductivity of 71.90 S/cm, and it possesses a band gap energy of 3.37 eV. Polymeraldin hydrochloride salt has a drawback of limited solubility in typical solvents, and its conductivity varies with temperature20. PANI/HCl is also sensitive to moisture and oxygen, which can degrade its conductivity and properties. Therefore, it requires careful handling and storage to maintain its optimal performance21. The cycling stability of PANI/HCl may be limited in energy storage devices such as supercapacitors or batteries. This is because repeated charging and discharging cycles can degrade its capacitance, thereby impacting long-term performance and lifespan22. PANI/HCl can be additionally doped with other substances to modify its properties and improve its performance for specific applications. It is important to note that during the synthesis of PANI/HCl, additional dopants or additives can be introduced to further modify the properties of the resulting polymer. These additional dopants may include organic acids, inorganic salts, or other substances, as mentioned in the previous reports. These dopants can offer specific functionalities, enhance conductivity, improve stability, or enable customized properties for PANI/HCl-based materials. Cho et al.23 synthesized nano sized PANI-HCl/PVA. The spherical PANI particles synthesized using PVA and HCl dopant (PANI-HCl/PVA) had a diameter of approximately 150 nm. The electrical conductivity of PANI-HCl/PVA was measured, and the values exhibited a significant increase from approximately 10–6–10–2 S/cm. Al-Bataineh et al.24 fabricated composite films of PANI/HCl doped with aluminum nitrate (Al(NO3)3) on an Au-layer for surface plasmon resonance (SPR) sensing. The physical and chemical characteristics of composite films made from (PANI-HCl)/Al(NO3)3 were examined at different concentrations of Al(NO3)3 (0, 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32 wt.%). As the concentration of Al(NO3)3 increased, the refractive index of the (PANI-HCl)/Al(NO3)3 composite films showed a continuous increase. Moreover, the electrical conductivity values exhibited an increase from 5.10 µS/cm to 10.00 µS/cm with the Al(NO3)3 concentration reaching 32 wt.%. The synthesis methods, properties, and applications of dye-doped PANI/HCl polymers in fields like sensors, photonic devices, solar cells, and gas sensing are emphasized by researchers. They showcase the versatility and potential of dye-doped PANI/HCl for creating functional materials that exhibit improved optical and electrical properties. Aljafari et al.25 utilized a soft template and performed in situ oriented oxidative polymerization to create self-assembled nanotubular structures of acid red (AR88) dye-doped polyaniline in the presence of hydrochloric acid (AR88/PAni/HCl). The prepared samples were thoroughly characterized using various analytical techniques. The Polyaniline doped with AR88 (5 × 10–4 M) and prepared in the presence of 1 M HCl exhibits improved conductivity (2.2679 S/cm−1) and demonstrates its remarkable electrical properties. In this research, new azo-azomethine organic molecule synthesized and characterized through spectroscopic techniques. Azo-azomethine compounds, formed by the condensation of an azo compound with an aldehyde or ketone, contain both azo (–N = N–) and azomethine(–C = N–) functional groups. These compounds have broad applications in organic synthesis, materials science, and medicinal chemistry due to their intriguing optical, electronic, and biological properties26–30. Azo-azomethine, a chromophore group, can be incorporated into polymer main and side chains, affecting their electrical and optical properties31. The recently synthesized azo-azomethine compound is a donor-π-acceptor (D-π-A) molecule that features an electron-rich donor and an electron-poor acceptor connected through π-conjugated segments. This molecular structure enables intramolecular charge transfer (ICT), making it a topic of significant interest. As expected, the dopant azo-azomethine modified electrical and optical properties of host polymer (PANI/HCl) typically enhanced conductivity, specific capacitance and narrowed band gap energy.

Experimental

Materials and methods

Analytical-grade precursors from Oxford lab fine chem LLP, such as aniline(99%), were utilized. Glacial acetic acid(aojinchem 99.85%), Ammonium peroxydisulfate (APS)(sigma-aldrich, 98.0%), KOH(99%), KCl(99.0%), NaNO2(≥ 97.0%), 4-aminophenol(≥ 98.0%), 2-hydroxy-3-methoxybenzaldehyde(≥ 98%) of Sigma Aldrich precursor compounds are used.

Synthesis methods

Synthesis of polyaniline emeraldine salt in the presence of hydrochloric acid medium (PANI/HCl)

In a 250 mL conical flask, aniline (0.01 mol, 1 mL) and HCl (125 mL) were added and cooled to 0–5 °C (solution A). To the solution A added solution B was prepared from (NH4)2S2O8 (2.5 g, 0.011 mol, 25 ml D.W) in 30 mins with stirred vigorously. Continue string mixture solution at 0–5 °C for 4 h then overnight stirring at room temperature. Filtrate the dark green precipitate product (polyaniline emeraldine salt) and washed with D.W frequently then by small amount of acetone one time. Keep the product in a vacuum oven for 24 h at 55 °C25.

General procedure for Synthesis of 2-hydroxy-5-((4-hydroxyphenyl)diazenyl)-3-methoxybenzaldehyde (3)

In 100 mL conical flask, prepared a solution of 4-aminophenol (1.0913 g, 10 mmol) in a mixture of HCl(%37, 10 mL) and D.W(10 mL) and cooled it to 0–5 °C, then dropwise added 12 ml solution of NaNO2 (1.207 g, 17.5 mmol) and continue stirring at 0–5 °C for 1 h to formation of 4-aminopheno diazonium chloride salt solution (solution A). To formation the solution of potassium 2-formyl-6-methoxyphenolate, in separate beaker prepared 15 mL of 2-hydroxy-3-methoxybenzaldehyde (1.521 g, 10 mmol) in 10% KOH solution and keep temperature range at 0–5 °C (solution B). Added solution B gradually to solution A with vigorously stirring at 0–5 °C until gelled solution formed then added 25 mL of cold D.W. Leaved overnight stirring at room temperature, filtrate the collected orange product and recrystallized in a suitable solvent mixture.

General procedure for the synthesis of the desired azo-azomethine (4)

The general method for synthesis of azo-azomethine(4) was used with a slight modification32. In a 250 mL flask, 4-aminobenzoic acid (1 mmol, 1 eq.) was dissolved in boiling ethanol; then, 2-hydroxy-5-((4-hydroxyphenyl)diazenyl)-3-methoxybenzaldehyde (3) (1 mmol 1 eq.) and a catalytic amount of glacial acetic acid (1 ml) were added. The reaction mixture was sonicated until the completed of the reaction (50 min at 60 °C); progress of the reaction was monitored by TLC (ethyl acetate: diethyl ether (1:1), ratio). From the resulting mixture, the solvent was evaporated by rotary evaporation to obtain a solid product and purified by recrystallization in solvent mixture (Scheme 1).

Electrochemical characterization

Cyclic voltammetry measurements were performed with a μStat-i 400 s Potentiostat/Galvanostat/Impedance Analyzer connected with a three-electrode. The working electrode was glassy carbon, counter electrode was graphite electrode, and saturated electrode, calomel electrode (SCE) with 0.1 M KCl was Saturated electrode used as a reference electrode. electrochemical impedance spectroscopy used to study of charge transfer process of azo-azomethine/PANI/HCl doped polymers. In an electrolytic cell made of 0.1 M KCl/Ethanol, impedance was carried in a three-point electrode akin to the one used in CV at a frequency of 0.01–105 Hz and an AC amplitude of 5 mV at ambient temperature. The working electrode was doped polymers by casting the DMF solution of doped polymers on Glassy carbon electrode with doped polymer film. XRD analysis

The powder X-ray diffraction study provides details regarding the crystalline structure and phase identification of the synthesized compounds, namely azo-azomethine (4), AA0/PANI/HCl, and AA2/PANI/HCl (Fig. 2a–c). The adjacent figures depict the crystal planes (hkl) that correspond to the major crystalline peaks, as determined by the Highscore Plus software36. It is noteworthy to observe that the X-ray diffraction plots of AA0/PANI/HCl alter when azo-azomethine (4) dye is introduced as a dopant to pure polyaniline in the acidic medium during the polymerization process. The bulk of the polyaniline chains are arranged on the 121, 113 and 322 crystal planes, as shown by the three distinct peaks for AA0/PANI/HCl (Fig. 2b) at various angles of diffraction37. The population of crystallites in the specific crystal plane was represented by the intensity (peak height) of the peaks, while the sharpness (width) of the peaks indicated the degree of chain orientation within that plane37. For the AA0/PANI/HCl, the 322-crystal plane exhibits the highest sharpness and intensity. Regarding of, azo-azomethine (4) molecule shows four major crystalline peaks namely − 112, 200, 020 and 211 (Fig. 2a), 211 at angles of diffraction 28.15 is the highest intensity and sharpness crystal planes respectively. However, the X-ray plot of AA2/PANI/HCl shows the existence of several new crystalline peaks such as 011 at diffraction angles 13.34, 020 at 18.84 and 200 at 23.83 (Fig. 2c), showed that crystallites are orientated in distinct directions (different planes), which suggests that orientation heterogeneity is more prominent in AA2/PANI/HCl than in AA0/PANI/HCl and azo-azomethine(4). Field-emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM) studies

The surface morphology of AA0/PANI/HCl and AA2/PANI/HCl polymers was investigated using a Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscope (FE-SEM) to demonstrate how dye doping affects the uniform distribution and particle size of the polymer. Figure 3 shows the FE-SEM images. In the left image of AA0/PANI/HCl, the surface particles appear less smooth with a uniform distribution ranging from 115.57 to 170.96 nm. In contrast, the dye-doped polymer, shown in the right image of AA2/PANI/HCl, exhibits a smooth surface with spherical clusters uniformly distributed in the nanometer range, specifically ranging from 219.53 to 302.59 nm.

TEM Study

Further evidence is provided by TEM images (Fig. 4), which reveal that the dye-doped polymer particles in AA2/PANI/HCl were indeed spherical and nanoscale in size. This phenomenon may be attributed to the larger molecular sizes of azo-azomethine molecules, resulting in more homogeneous spherical polymer particles. It is worth noting that the surfactant activity of azo-azomethine also enhances the formation of smaller polymer particles. This is further supported by the presence of more spherical-shaped particles observed in the FESEM image of AA2/PANI/HCl.

Thermogravimetric analysis

Figure 8 shown the thermogravimetric analysis of AA0/PANI/HCl and AA2/PANI/HCl dye doped polymers. Three distinct weight loss processes led to the thermal degradation of both AA0/PANI/HCl and AA2/PANI/HCl doped polymers. The first weight loss below 100 °C is caused by the system’s retained volatile materials and adsorbed moisture evaporating. The elimination of greater oligomers of AA0/PANI/HCl was done in the second stage, which ran from 200 to 425 °C. 53. The thermos oxidative breakdown of AA0/PANI/HCl, leading to a variety of degradation products such as ammonia, aniline, methane, acetylene, etc., is what causes the third stage to come about after 425 °C54. An increase in thermal stability was seen in the case of the AA2/PANI/HCl doped polymer (Fig. 8), which may be related to the effective integration of azo dye (4) in the doped structure. When compared to AA0/PANI/HCl (residual weight of 32% and weight loss of 68%), the AA2/PANI/HCl doped polymer displayed a greater residual weight of 38% (weight loss of 62%) at 800 °C, which may be because the PANI/HCl polymer contains azo dye (4) as a doped molecule. The lower mobility of the polymer chains, which hinders free radical transfer via interchain reactions and raises the thermal degradation starting temperature, may also be related to the doped polymer AA2/PANI/HCl’s enhanced thermal stability55.

Figure 9 shows cyclic voltammograms of pure and doped azo dye (4) polymers with potentials ranging from 0 to 2.5 V and scanning rates of 50 mV/s.

The electrochemical behavior of PANI/HCl polymer is significantly influenced by different doping dye concentrations. Figure 9 showed the CV curves of azo dye (4) doped polymers are clearly dissimilar from pure PANI/HCl polymer as a consequence of addition dye dopant azo-azomethine (4) concentrations. The magnitudes of the peak current vary significantly depending on the quantity of dopant utilized, as seen in Fig. 9. According to this result, azo dyes significantly affect the electrochemical behavior of PANI/HCl polymer56. The repeating 50 cycles test in Fig. 9 shows that the CV curves were relatively comparable.

However, they displayed sustained peak currents, indicating that AA0/PANI/HCl and azo dye (4) doped polymers coatings were completely electrochemically durable and firmly and evenly coated57.The oxidation peak current densities of AA1/PANI/HCl, AA2/PANI/HCl, and AA3/PANI/HCl are significantly higher than that of pure AA0/PANI/HCl polymer. The results indicated a much faster response rate on AA (1–3)/PANI/HCl electrodes, proving that azo-azomethine-doped PANI/HCl polymers are more electrochemically active. The highest current density is shown by AA2/PANI/HCl among all the doped polymers. Large azo dyes with the azo unit and functional groups as dopants changed the PANI/HCl molecular structure, morphology, and electrochemical performance. A loose granular morphology with some visible channels was produced by azo-azomethine (4), as shown by SEM examination. This morphology made it easier for AA(1–3)/PANI/HCl to participate in a redox reaction by increasing the amount of π-conjugated molecules and surface area. As a result, it was conducive to making polymers more electrochemically active, much as when a polymer was doped with other dopant molecules that also included azo and azomethine units58. A possible explanation for the increase in current values in cyclic voltammograms is the unique molecular structure and loose granular morphology of AA(1–3)/PANI/HCl, which have some apparent channels59. First off, the large molecular volume of azo-azomethine (4) molecule increased the aromatic rings of PANI/HCl π–π stacking distance, which was favorable in entry of electrolyte into the active component. Second, dopant azo-azomethine molecule with the π-conjugated structure allowed for transfer of electrons from π-conjugated across adjacent PANI/HCl chains, enhancement of π- conjugated degree of the doped polymer60. Thirdly, the CV curves of AA(1–3)/PANI/HCl revealed that the loose granular morphology of AA(1–3)/PANI/HCl with certain clear channels favored rapid electrolyte diffusion and complete electrolyte interaction with the active component. Conclusion

In this work azo-azomethine-doped PANI/HCl compositions were prepared by chemical oxidative polymerization method. The AA(0–3)/PANI/HCl composites were studied by varying the molar feeds of new synthesized azo-azomethine dye to PANI/HCl, their Structural, Optical, Thermal and Electrochemical Impedance details were studied. Experimental results disclosed that this new synthesized azo-azomethine dye has effective impact on the physiochemical properties of dye doped PANI/HCl composite. Optically the energy band gaps decreased proportionally with rising the concentration of dopant azo dye. Cyclovoltammetry and electrochemical impedance revealed that the dopant dye increasing the PANI/HCl conductivity and lowered bulk resistance. Moreover, improved the specific capacitance. TEM and SEM disclosed the effect of dopant concentration on the structural properties. Amorphous and crystallinity conversion were studied through X-ray diffraction analysis. The potential applications of this doped conducting polymers exhibit promise for applications in optoelectronic devices like OLEDs and in energy storage systems such as supercapacitors, owing to their tunable optical properties, enhanced electrochemical conductivity, and increased specific capacitance.

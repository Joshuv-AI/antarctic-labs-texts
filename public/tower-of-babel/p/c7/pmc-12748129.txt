CLDN18.2 expression in gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma: prevalence, heterogeneity, and prognostic implications in Spanish patients
Authors: Carolina Martinez-Ciarpaglini, María Ortega, Sandra Pérez-Buira, Aitana Bolea, Beatriz Casado Guerra, Carmen Herencia Bellido, Paula Tornero Piñero, Dolores Naranjo-Hans, Brenda Palomar, Hernán Quiceno, Amanda Sardón Fernández, Ariadna Torner Calvo, Federico Rojo, Mar Iglesias Coma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractClaudin 18 isoform 2 (CLDN18.2) has emerged as a novel therapeutical target for HER2-negative, locally advanced, or metastatic gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma. However, reported prevalence rates vary widely (24–47%), underscoring the need for accurate population-specific data. This study investigates the CLDN18.2 prevalence in Spanish patients with gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma and explores its correlation with other biomarkers and clinicopathological factors. We retrospectively analyzed endoscopic biopsy samples from gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma patients across three Spanish institutions. CLDN18.2 expression was evaluated by immunohistochemistry using the Ventana CLDN18 (43-14A) assay and PS2+ scoring, with cases showing membranous 2+ or 3+ staining in ≥ 75% of tumor cells classified as positive. Clinicopathological and biomarker data were retrieved from medical records. CLDN18.2 was positive in 33% (89/270) of cases. Although histological type was not correlated with CLDN18.2 status, cases with poorly cohesive morphology exhibited heterogeneous staining, with a predominance of moderately and strongly stained cells. A weak to moderate inverse correlation was identified between signet ring cell content and CLDN18.2 staining (r = −0.29, p = 0.028). No significant differences were observed in clinical outcome. However, among patients with advanced-stage disease, CLDN18.2-positive intestinal tumors were associated with worse progression-free survival (HR 2.58, 95% CI 1.18–5.63; p = 0.029). These findings confirm a 33% prevalence of CLDN18.2 expression in the Spanish population, aligning with reports from other Western European cohorts. The significant heterogeneity in poorly cohesive cases and an inverse correlation with signet ring cell content pose diagnostic challenges for pathologists. Moreover, the association between CLDN18.2 positivity and worse progression-free survival in intestinal adenocarcinoma supports a potential role in tumor progression and metastasis, warranting further research.Keywords: Claudin, Gastric cancer, Poorly cohesive histology, Immunohistochemistry, Survival, Signet ring cells

Introduction

Claudin 18 isoform 2 (CLDN18.2) is a transmembrane tight junction protein constitutively expressed in normal gastric epithelial cells [1, 2]. Recently, the European Medicines Agency (EMA) approved zolbetuximab as a novel targeted therapy for patients with HER2-negative, locally advanced, or metastatic gastric/gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma (G/GEJA) with high CLDN18.2 expression [3]. Zolbetuximab, a monoclonal antibody that targets and binds to CLDN18.2, mediates cancer cell death through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and complement-dependent cytotoxicity [4, 5]. A meta-analysis of three randomized clinical trials (SPOTLIGHT, GLOW, and FAST) involving 1233 patients demonstrated significant improvements in progression-free survival (PFS) (HR 0.64; 95% CI 0.49–0.84; p < 0.01; I2 = 59%) and overall survival (OS) (HR 0.72; 95% CI 0.62–0.83; p < 0.01; I2 = 31%) among patients treated with zolbetuximab in combination with chemotherapy compared to those receiving chemotherapy alone [6–9].

In phase III clinical trials (SPOTLIGHT and GLOW), CLDN18.2 positivity was defined as moderate to strong membranous expression in at least 75% of tumor cells by immunohistochemistry (IHC), using the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)–approved companion diagnostic (CDx) antibody clone 43-14A (Ventana, Roche) [6, 7]. Based on this definition, CLDN18.2 expression was detected in 38.4% of the tested population across 20 and 50 different countries, respectively, including both Asian and Western nations, with a high prevalence reported in diffuse-type tumors [10]. However, independent studies have reported prevalence differences for CLDN18.2, ranging from 24 to 47% [5, 11].

Documenting the precise frequency of a predictive biomarker’s expression in a specific population is crucial for the successful implementation of precision medicine [12]. These data help predict treatment outcomes and support cost-effectiveness analyses within healthcare budgets [13]. Additionally, they serve as key factors for indirect validation and quality control assessment, ensuring the overall analytical accuracy of biomarker performance in pathology laboratories [14].

Therefore, we aim to assess the prevalence of CLDN18.2 expression in G/GEJA within the Spanish population and explore its correlation with other biomarkers and relevant clinicopathological factors.

Material and methods

Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded endoscopic biopsy samples were retrospectively collected from 270 G/GEJA patients in three geographically different reference centers in Spain: Fundación Jimenez Díaz (FJD, Madrid), Hospital del Mar (HM, Barcelona), and Hospital Clínico Universitario de Valencia (HCUV, Valencia). Biopsies were diagnosed over a period of 41 months (1246 days, from January 2019 to May 2022). Clinicopathologic data and biomarker assessment results (HER2 and Mismatch Repair (MMR) protein status, Epstein Barr Virus (EBV) expression, and PD-L1 combine positive score [CPS] score when estimated) were retrieved from histopathological and clinical records. In all cases, HER2 status was previously routinely assessed by immunohistochemistry in each center using the 45B antibody (Ventana, Roche) and classified as positive (3+), equivocal (2+), or negative (1+ or 0) following the College of American Pathologists guideline [15]. Cases classified as HER2 equivocal (2+) were further evaluated by ISH to assess HER2 amplification status. EBV status was assessed by in situ hybridization and considered positive only if nuclear staining was present in all tumor cells. PD-L1 immunohistochemistry was performed by using the 22C3 antibody clone (Agilent, Dako) and interpreted by calculating the CPS [16, 17]. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Board of the participant centers.

Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was performed to assess the histological type according to Lauren’s and the World Health Organization (WHO) classification, histological grade, percentage of signet ring cells (SRCs), and the number of fragments in the sample [18, 19]. SRCs were defined as tumor cells with ample cytoplasmic mucin, appearing optically clear on H&E staining, with an eccentrically placed nucleus [20].

Immunohistochemistry for CLDN18.2 was performed on fresh 4-µm paraffin sections of FFPE samples, using the Ventana CDx CLDN18 (43-14A) assay in the Benchmark Ultra automated immunostainer (Ventana Medical Systems, Tucson, AZ, USA), according to the manufacturer’s instructions. In all cases, a fragment of gastric mucosa with intestinal metaplasia was included as external control. A semiquantitative evaluation of CLDN18.2 expression was conducted, collecting the percentage of tumor cells in the invasive component with moderate (2+) and strong (3+) membrane staining (PS2+ scoring). Cases presenting membranous 2+ or 3+ staining in ≥ 75% of tumor cells were classified as positive for CLDN18.2. In addition, the percentage of tumor cells exhibiting weak (1+), moderate, and strong staining was calculated, and a Histo-Score (HScore) was determined by multiplying the staining intensity score by its corresponding percentage, following the formula H-score = [(0 × % negative cells) + (1 × % weak positive cells) + (2 × % moderate positive cells) + (3 × % strong positive cells). The resulting HScore ranged from 0 to 300. Challenging cases were reviewed by three pathologists to reach a consensus.

Statistics

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.0; Vienna, Austria). A p-value of ≤ 0.05 was considered indicative of statistical significance. Clinical and pathological characteristics were compared using the chi-squared test for categorical variables and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous variables. Prior to analysis, the normality of continuous data was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation.

Overall survival (OS) was defined as the interval from the time of diagnosis to death attributable to tumor-related causes or the date of last follow-up. Progression-free survival (DFS) was defined as the period from diagnosis to either the occurrence of tumor progression, metastasis, or death. Survival outcomes were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier estimates and compared with the log-rank test. Cox proportional hazards regression models were constructed to evaluate the association between individual variables and survival outcomes.

Results

CLDN18.2 expression in gastric adenocarcinoma

A total of 270 samples from individual patients were collected from the three Spanish centers involved in the study: HM (98 cases, 36.3%), HCUV (93 cases, 34.4%), and FJD (79 cases, 29.3%). The majority of patients were male (63%), and almost half (46%) were diagnosed at metastatic stage of the disease.

CLDN18.2 positivity—defined as membranous staining of intensity 2+ or 3+ in ≥ 75% of tumor cells—was observed in 89 cases (33%) across the cohort. Non-tumoral gastric epithelial cells showed constitutive intense membranous expression in all cases, with variable expression in intestinal metaplasia present in some cases. No relevant immunostaining was detected in non-epithelial cells, including fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and inflammatory cells. No significant correlations were found between CLDN18.2 expression and clinical variables, including age, gender, tumor location, or clinical stage (Table 1). The number of tumor-containing fragments per biopsy sample was similar between CLDN18.2-positive and CLDN18.2-negative cases (mean: 3.5 vs. 3.4, respectively; p = 0.918).

Although histological subtype was not statistically associated with CLDN18.2 expression (CLDN18.2-positive frequency in intestinal diffuse, and mixed cases: 31%, 55% and 10% respectively, p = 0.410), tumors with poorly cohesive morphology demonstrated greater heterogeneous expression, and a higher proportion of moderately and strongly stained cells compared to intestinal and mixed types. Following the PS2+ score, staining in less than 50% of cells (low expression) poses no significant challenge for histological grading as CLDN18 negative, irrespectively on staining intensity. This pattern was more frequently encountered in intestinal vs diffuse cases (58% vs 40%, p = 0.050). Among tumors classified as negative for CLDN18.2, but with moderate to strong staining in 50–74% of tumor cells (just below the positivity threshold), poorly cohesive histology was more prevalent (20%) than intestinal morphology (11%).

A weak-moderate inverse correlation was observed between strong CLDN18.2 staining and the presence of SRCs (r = −0.29, p = 0.028) (Fig. 1B and Fig. 2E–H).

Clinical significance of CLDN18.2 expression in gastric adenocarcinoma

MMR protein and HER2 status were available for 269 (99%) cases. PD-L1 CPS and EBV expression were assessed in 115 (43%) and 91 (34%) cases, respectively. MMR protein loss was identified in 51 cases (19%), of which 11 (21%) were CLDN18.2-positive. HER2 positivity was observed in 38 cases (14%), with 9 (24%) of these being CLDN18.2-positive. EBV expression was detected in 4 cases (4%), with half of them (n = 2) also showing CLDN18.2 positivity. Notably, the frequency of PD-L1 positivity was very similar among CLDN18.2-positive and -negative tumors for all the currently clinically relevant CPS thresholds: 24% vs 23% for CPS ≥ 5 and 15% vs 14% for CPS ≥ 10. CLDN18.2 expression was not significantly associated with MMR protein loss, HER2 positivity, PD-L1 expression, or EBV status (Table 2, Fig. 1E–G).

CLDN18.2 expression was not significantly correlated with OS or PFS, in either localized or advanced disease. In localized cases, CLDN18.2 positivity was not associated with survival (PFS, HR = 0.62 [95% CI 0.20–2.42], p = 0.58; OS HR = 0.49 [95% CI 0.33–1.14], p = 0.77). In advanced-stage patients, a trend toward shorter PFS was observed in patients with CLDN18.2 positivity (long rank test 0.03, HR = 1.11 [95% CI 0.66–1.8], p = 0.67), with no difference in OS (HR = 1.00 [95% CI 0.67–1.48], p = 0.148) (Fig. 3A).

Stratification by tumor morphology in advanced-stage disease revealed that CLDN18.2-positive intestinal-type tumors were associated with a worse PFS (HR = 2.58 [95% CI 1.18–5.63], p = 0.029) (Fig. 3C). In contrast, CLDN18.2 expression did not influence PFS among tumors with diffuse morphology (HR = 1.13 [95% CI 0.44–2.99], p = 0.76) (Fig. 3D).

Discussion

Our study identified a 33% prevalence in CLDN18.2 expression among patients with G/GEJA in Spain. This aligns closely with the 33.4% reported in an Italian cohort by Pellino A. et al. [21]. However, a recent sub-analysis of patients enrolled in the SPOTLIGHT and GLOW clinical trials reported a higher frequency of 44% for CLDN18.2 positivity among patients from Europe and the Middle East, including Spain [10]. This discrepancy may reflect differences in population composition, as that analysis included a broader mix of Middle Eastern and European patients and was limited to HER2-negative cases.

Data on CLDN18.2 expression in other Southern European populations remain scarce, as most exploratory analyses have focused on Asian cohorts, where reported prevalence ranges from 24 to 47% [5, 11, 22–25]. Waters et al. reported a similar CLDN18.2 expression rate in North America (44.4%), consistent with the 37.7% prevalence observed for the same population in the SPOTLIGHT and GLOW trials [10, 26]. In that study, race significantly influenced CLDN18.2 expression, with higher rates in Caucasian patients compared to Black or African American individuals, suggesting geographic and genetic variability.

Consistent with previous findings [25–27], our results showed no significant differences in CLDN18.2 expression across clinical stages, supporting the hypothesis that CLDN18.2 expression remains stable throughout tumor progression. This is further corroborated by high concordance rates (73 to 87%) reported between primary tumors and corresponding metastases [11, 21, 24, 26, 28, 29]. However, further research is needed to define the role of CLDN18.2 in G/GEJA evolution.

In our series, CLDN18.2 status (positive or negative) did not significantly correlate with histological subtype. However, H-score analysis revealed that poorly cohesive tumors displayed more heterogeneous expression in both percentage and staining intensity. Notably, other studies reporting overexpression of CLDN18.2 in this histology also evaluated CLDN18.2 using the H-score, rather than the clinically cut-off of ≥ 75% moderate-to-strong membranous staining [22, 28–30]. These findings underscore the importance of raising awareness among pathologists and clinicians regarding the challenges of interpreting CLDN18.2 expression in poorly cohesive cases, particularly when tumors fall near the treatment eligibility threshold for zolbetuximab. Moreover, the presence of SRCs may complicate this evaluation further, as non-specific cytoplasmic staining can mimic membranous expression [31]. Our results suggest an inverse correlation between SRC content and CLDN18.2 expression. This is consistent with observations by Kim et al. [27], who found higher CLDN18.2 positivity in E-cadherin positive diffuse cases, possibly reflecting a phenotypic difference within SRCs.

We found no significant associations between CLDN18.2 expression and other routinely assessed biomarkers in metastatic G/GEJA, including HER2, MMR protein status, and PD-L1 CPS. As noted previously, most of the CLDN18.2-positive tumors in our study were HER2 negative and MMR protein proficient, highlighting a potential therapeutic niche for CLDN18.2-targeted therapies in a significant group of patients [21, 32]. Given the significant variability in PD-L1 CPS expression between G/GEJA series, it is difficult to determine the exact frequency of this biomarker in CLDN18.2-positive cases. In our series, PD-L1 CPS ≥ 5 was found in 24% of CLDN18.2-positive cases; however, previous studies have reported frequencies ranging from 18 to 42% [21, 23, 25, 32], likely due to methodological differences and intertumoral heterogeneity.

Several independent series have described no clear prognostic value for CLDN18.2 expression [21, 22, 25, 32]. However, a recent meta-analysis found that CLDN18.2-negative patients had significantly longer overall survival (HR 1.20; CI 1.07–1.34; p < 0.01) and a non-significant trend toward longer progression-free survival (HR 1.25; CI 0.98–1.61; p = 0.07) [33]. These findings are consistent with our survival results, which showed worse PFS in CLDN18.2-positive patients, although this effect was limited to cases with intestinal-type morphology. Few studies have explored the prognostic influence of CLDN18.2 expression within histological subtypes. For instance, Kim et al. [27] found no association between CLDN18.2 expression and prognosis in diffuse-type G/GEJA. The hypothesized role of CLDN18.2 expression in promoting tumor progression may stem from aberrant surface expression, facilitating cellular invasion and metastasis. Moreover, Liu et al. [34] demonstrated that CLDN18.2-high G/GEJA tumor cells exhibit enhanced adhesion to cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), promoting tumor spread. These findings highlight the importance of incorporating targeted therapeutic options for patients with CLDN18.2-positive tumors into routine practice.

The primary limitation of our study is its retrospective design, which restricts the ability to draw causal inferences. Larger, prospective studies are needed to elucidate the clinical significance of CLDN18.2 expression across histological subtypes.

In conclusion, our results reveal a 33% prevalence of CLDN18.2 expression in Spanish G/GEJA patients, consistent with data from other Western European countries. Notably, we observed significant heterogeneity in CLDN18.2 staining among poorly cohesive tumors, and an inverse correlation with SRC content, which may complicate pathological assessment. Finally, the association between CLDN18.2 positivity and poorer PFS in intestinal-type tumors supports its potential role in disease progression and further underscores the relevance of CLDN18.2 as a biomarker for targeted therapy.

Effectiveness of Myobrace Therapy in Correcting Anterior Crossbite in Early Mixed Dentition: A Retrospective Study
Authors: Trang Thu Pham, Binh Cao Tran, Thuy Thi Hong Pham
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study evaluated the outcomes of treating anterior crossbite in early mixed dentition patients using the Myobrace appliance.Materials and methodsA retrospective study was conducted on 30 Vietnamese children aged 6–10 years with anterior crossbite from 2020 to 2022. Oral habits, including tonguethrusting, lingual frenum interference, and tongue posture, were evaluated pre- and post-therapy. Clinical, radiographic, and soft tissue outcomes were assessed to monitor craniofacial, dental, and esthetic changes before and after treatment.ResultsThe mean age was 8.1 years (SD = 0.94), with 60% female participants. The average treatment time was 14.5 months (SD = 1.31). Tonguethrusting decreased from 23.3 to 3.3%, lingual frenum interference resolved completely (43.3 to 0%), and low tongue posture declined from 33.4 to 6.6%. Overjet improved from −2.5 (SD = 1.90) to 1.6 mm (SD = 1.14), and overbite reduced from 2.2 (SD =0.92) to 0.8 mm (SD =1.14). Maxillary and mandibular incisor angulations showed significant improvements: U1 to SN increased from 100.4° (SD = 7.14) to 109.5° (SD = 7.18), and IMPA decreased from 89.8° (SD = 8.77) to 87.0° (SD = 7.97). Most patients adhered to the recommended wear and exercise regimens, though 40% experienced appliance detachment in the first 2 weeks.ConclusionThe Myobrace system effectively corrected anterior crossbite, improved oral habits, and enhanced dental alignment in early mixed dentition, promoting functional and esthetic improvements.How to cite this articlePham TT, Tran BC, Pham TTH. Introduction

Anterior crossbite accounts for approximately 4–5% of malocclusion cases.1,2 This condition can result in occlusal trauma, excessive wear on the lingual surfaces of the lower anterior teeth and the labial surfaces of the upper anterior teeth, ultimately affecting the longevity of teeth and contributing to temporomandibular joint dysfunction. Additionally, it can negatively impact facial esthetics, speech, and overall orofacial functions, highlighting the need for timely intervention. A comprehensive evaluation of the etiology is essential for effective treatment, as anterior crossbite may stem from dental factors, skeletal discrepancies, or external influences such as harmful oral habits.3 Early detection and treatment of functional abnormalities are critical for guiding favorable facial and occlusal development in children.

The timing and approach to treatment vary based on the underlying cause and stage of malocclusion. Functional therapy is most beneficial when initiated early, as it promotes optimal facial growth. However, intervening during the period when children develop self-awareness can improve cooperation and anatomical outcomes. This typically coincides with the early mixed dentition stage, when habit training is most likely to succeed.4 Several methods exist to address functional anterior crossbite, including removable appliances,5 fixed appliances,6 Calta devices,7 and Myobrace systems.8 Among these, the Myobrace system stands out for targeting underlying etiological factors and addressing poor oral habits that impact dentofacial growth, offering a natural approach to orthodontic correction. Its unique design combines functional habit correction with gentle dental arch expansion, enhancing its utility and economic feasibility.9

Myobrace appliances are effective in correcting oral habits like mouth breathing, tonguethrusting, and lip sucking, which contribute to malocclusion.10 They also assist in dental alignment, arch expansion, and improving intermaxillary relationships.11 The Myobrace system plays a multifaceted role in oral health, offering various benefits beyond orthodontic correction. It includes a series of removable intraoral appliances designed for different stages of dental and skeletal development. The Myobrace i3 appliance, part of the Interceptive class III series, is specifically designed for early mixed dentition to address developing class III malocclusion.12 It works by promoting forward maxillary growth, repositioning the tongue to the correct resting posture, and discouraging unfavorable oral habits such as tonguethrusting and mouth breathing.13 This appliance is particularly effective for cases involving an underdeveloped maxilla and low tongue posture, both of which contribute to skeletal and dental class III malocclusions. Additionally, the Myobrace i3 features an integrated myofunctional training system that enhances orofacial muscle coordination, further aiding in the correction of malocclusion.14 Despite its potential advantages, no peer-reviewed studies in Vietnam have examined the effectiveness of the Myobrace system. Therefore, this study aims to evaluate the outcomes of treating anterior crossbite in early mixed dentition patients using the Myobrace appliance.

Materials and Methods

Study Design

This retrospective study utilized a pre-post-trial design and was conducted on 30 pediatric patients aged 6–10 years. Patient records were reviewed to assess clinical, radiographic, and functional outcomes before and after Myobrace therapy.

Study Participants

The study population consisted of patients who underwent orthodontic treatment at the Department of Orthodontics, National Hospital of Odonto-Stomatology, Hanoi, from 2020 to 2022. Participants were eligible for inclusion if they were in the early mixed dentition stage, exhibited an anterior crossbite, demonstrated a discrepancy between centric occlusion (CO) and centric relation (CR), had no history of prior orthodontic treatment, and provided informed consent, either directly or through their parents or guardians. Patients were excluded if they presented with congenital craniofacial anomalies, exhibited a lack of cooperation, or reported an allergy to plastic materials.

Study Measures

Data were retrospectively collected from patient records, including clinical examinations, cephalometric radiographs (taken in centric occlusion), and dental cast analysis. The variables assessed were categorized into demographics, clinical features, cephalometric measurements, and Myobrace appliance sizing. Demographic data included age and sex. Clinical features evaluated were tongue tie, tongue thrusting habit, low tongue position, lower facial height (mm), overbite (mm), overjet (mm), and molar relationship. Cephalometric measurements encompassed SNA (°), SNB (°), ANB (°), FMA (°), gonial angle (°), A to N-perpendicular (perp) (FH), B to N-Perp (FH), Pog to N-Perp (FH), Wits appraisal (mm), overjet (mm), overbite (mm), U1 to SN (°), IMPA (°), L1 to LOP, interincisal angle (°), upper lip to E-line (mm), lower lip to E-line (mm), and nasolabial angle (°). The Myobrace appliance size was determined by measuring the width of the four upper incisors using a specialized Myobrace ruler or by selecting the appropriate size (Small, Medium, Large) based on predefined size ranges correlating incisor width to appliance dimension.

The treatment process with the Myobrace appliance was considered complete when the patient achieved a non-negative overjet (≥ 0 mm). At this stage, final evaluations were conducted, including clinical examination, impression-taking, lateral cephalometric imaging, and subsequent analysis. These steps ensured that both clinical and radiographic changes were thoroughly assessed to determine the treatment's success.

All study measures were independently reviewed by a team of three trained orthodontic investigators to ensure consistency and accuracy in data extraction. To minimize inter-examiner variability, a calibration session was conducted before data collection, during which investigators analyzed a subset of cephalometric radiographs, dental casts, and clinical records under standardized conditions. Intraexaminer reliability was assessed by re-evaluating 10% of the cases 2 weeks after the initial data extraction, yielding an intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of ≥ 0.85 for cephalometric and cast measurements. Additionally, an experienced senior orthodontist validated all cephalometric tracings and measurements to ensure adherence to established orthodontic diagnostic criteria.

Study Outcomes

Oral Habits

The elimination of detrimental oral habits, particularly tongue-thrusting, was deemed resolved when the patient's tongue no longer rested between the upper and lower teeth during swallowing. Similarly, the functional status of the lingual frenum was assessed postfrenectomy. If the tongue exhibits normal mobility and functionality, the lingual frenum was classified as normal. Additionally, tongue position during swallowing was evaluated, with a successful outcome identified by the absence of a low tongue posture between the teeth.

Treatment Outcomes

Clinical, radiographic, and soft tissue outcomes were systematically assessed to monitor craniofacial, dental, and esthetic changes throughout treatment. Quantitative clinical measures, such as lower facial height, overbite, overjet, and molar relationships, were recorded to track dental and craniofacial adaptations. Radiographic evaluations focused on cephalometric parameters. Soft tissue outcomes were analyzed through the nasolabial angle and the positions of the upper and lower lips relative to the E-line, providing insights into facial esthetics. These combined assessments offered a comprehensive understanding of skeletal, dental, and soft tissue changes before and after treatment.

Study Analysis

For descriptive statistical analysis, mean and standard deviation (SD) values were used to compute quantitative variables, while numbers and percentages were used to calculate qualitative variables. The Fisher's exact test was used to compare the differences in change of oral habits after the treatment. To validate the extent of craniofacial growth modifications, occlusal improvements, and soft tissue adjustments achieved through Myobrace therapy, paired t-tests were used to compare the differences in clinical outcomes, radiographic outcomes, and soft tissue outcomes at the initial (pretreatment) and achieved (post-treatment) stages. Differences were considered statistically significant if the p-value was < 0.05. The collected data were transferred to Microsoft Excel version 2016 and subsequently imported into STATA version 16 software (Stata Corp LLC, College Station, TX, USA) for analysis.

Ethical Considerations

The study was conducted with approval from the National Hospital of Odonto-Stomatology, Hanoi (No. 1312/QD- BVRHMTW 28/12/2022 and No. 81/ QD- BVRHMTW 28/01/2020). Informed consent was obtained from all parents' participants or their guardians. Parents were assured of confidentiality, and their right to withdraw their child from the study at any time was respected. Alternative treatment options were offered to those who declined participation. Protocols were in place to address and manage any incidental findings during the examination and treatment process. Efforts to mitigate bias were described, including the usage of standardized measurement instruments and providing training to the research team.

Discussion

Our study evaluated the Myobrace system for treating anterior crossbite in 30 pediatric patients aged 6–10 years. Significant improvements were observed in both clinical and radiographic outcomes, including increased overjet, overbite, and changes in dental alignment. Patient compliance was high, with most following the prescribed wear time and exercises. However, 40% experienced appliance detachment during the first 2 weeks of sleep. These results suggest that the Myobrace appliance is effective in addressing anterior crossbite and promoting improved occlusal and facial development in early mixed dentition.

If functional malocclusions are not detected and treated early, even minor skeletal and dental discrepancies can develop into significant issues, eventually requiring surgical intervention once facial growth ceases. These malocclusions can affect both the psychological and physical well-being of pediatric patients, causing them to experience embarrassment and self-esteem issues.15 The Myobrace system offers a noninvasive, functional appliance that targets underlying causes, such as poor oral habits, by training the lips, cheeks, and tongue muscles while simultaneously aligning and expanding the dental arches. Unlike traditional removable functional appliances, Myobrace only needs to be worn during sleep and for 1–2 hours during waking hours.11 By stimulating growth and improving muscle function, Myobrace i3 enhances facial balance and contributes to the natural development of the facial structure. This functional orthodontic approach addresses the root causes of anterior crossbite and offers a clinically significant benefit, restoring both function and esthetics for pediatric patients.16

Oral Habits

There were significant improvements in oral habits among study patients following Myobrace treatment. These changes underscore the effectiveness of Myobrace in addressing functional oral issues early, demonstrating its capacity to eliminate discrepancies related to skeletal and dental development. The Myobrace appliance, a prefabricated functional device available in multiple sizes, is uniquely designed for multifunctional use. It trains and activates orofacial muscles while aligning and expanding the dental arches. Unlike traditional functional removable appliances, Myobrace requires less daily wear time, needing only nighttime use and 1–2 hours of daily training. The i3 Myobrace stimulates growth and facilitates neuromuscular adaptation, significantly improving facial and orofacial muscle balance. This method addresses underlying causes rather than symptoms, enhancing patient quality of life by resolving functional malocclusions and naturally restoring facial growth. In our study, the average treatment duration was around 14 months, including the elimination of harmful habits and dental alignment. Traditional functional appliances, such as the Twin Block, typically require full-time wear for optimal effectiveness. Research in India (2022) found that patients using the Twin Block appliance were required to wear it full-time (average of 20.86 hours per day) for at least 6 months to achieve optimal skeletal changes, whereas part-time wear (less than 12 hours per day) resulted in significantly reduced treatment effects, particularly in mandibular advancement and overjet reduction. 17 In contrast, Myobrace requires only nighttime use and short daily training, suggesting a potentially more convenient approach for patients. However, further comparative studies are needed to directly evaluate differences in treatment outcomes between Myobrace and other functional appliances such as Twin Block.

Treatment Outcomes

Our study found minimal changes in bone structure after treatment, indicating that the primary impact of the Myobrace appliance lies in correcting oral habits and improving dental alignment. Specifically, after 4–6 months of eliminating harmful habits, followed by another 4–6 months of treatment targeting dental alignment, there was a notable improvement in overbite to a positive value. Similarly, a 2021 study in Iran reported that the optimal duration for using functional appliances ranges from 2 to 9 months.18 That study involved participants aged 7–11 years, aligning with our findings and supporting the effectiveness of this treatment timeframe.

Our study observed a statistically significant increase in the ANB angle, indicating changes in the relationship between the upper and lower jaws. However, the average increase was only 0.52°, which is not clinically significant. This suggests that bone changes observed on X-rays after treatment with the Myobrace appliance were minimal. Supporting this, a 2024 case report detailed the treatment of a 14-year-old female with skeletal class III malocclusion and anterior crossbite as her primary concern.19 Using a combination of face mask therapy and fixed appliance therapy, the malocclusion was successfully corrected over a treatment period of 20 months.

Our study demonstrated significant improvements in dental alignment, particularly in the upper and lower central incisors. After treatment, the upper incisors showed increased labial tipping, with the U1-SN angle rising by 9.16°, while the lower incisors tilted lingually, reducing their angle to the mandibular plane by 2.78°, effectively aiding in the release of the anterior crossbite. These findings underscore the effectiveness of early functional appliance therapy in pediatric patients. Supporting this, a 2021 case study highlighted the successful treatment of a 6-year-old with a retrognathic maxilla, concave profile, and reverse overjet using rapid maxillary expansion and a reverse-pull face mask.20 The intervention corrected the anterior crossbite and sagittal discrepancy, demonstrating the importance of initiating treatment during the early mixed dentition period to address class III skeletal patterns and prevent more complex issues later.

Our study also demonstrated a change in the relationship between the upper and lower central incisors. The upper central incisors increased in labial tipping and protrusion, while the lower central incisors showed a decrease in these measurements after treatment. This shift is consistent with the aim of improving occlusal relationships and achieving a more balanced dental alignment. Overall, the patients no longer exhibited anterior crossbite, with an average increase in overjet after treatment. Our findings align with previous research, including the study in 2021, which confirmed the effectiveness of Myobrace in treating class III malocclusion, as well as correcting overbite, overjet, dental crowding, and the anterior-posterior relationship of the upper and lower teeth.11

Patient Treatment Compliance

For removable appliances in general and functional orthodontic devices such as Myobrace in particular, patient cooperation plays a crucial role, especially for pediatric patients. The treatment outcome is entirely dependent on the patient's self-awareness and the support from their family.21 Therefore, selecting the appropriate case and appliance is an important step in treatment planning. For cases indicated for Myobrace, we first explain the treatment process and the wearing schedule to both the patient and their family. We also conduct a survey to assess whether the patient is ready to follow the treatment process. Once all factors are considered, a training session is held to demonstrate how to wear the appliance, perform functional exercises, and maintain a log of appliance wear.22 These measures aim to ensure that the child wears the appliance correctly and for the required amount of time, achieving the best possible treatment results.

In our study, patient compliance was measured by wearing the appliance for 2 hours while awake, performing the prescribed 30-minute exercises, and wearing the appliance throughout the night while sleeping. The children in our study showed high compliance, with 90% of them wearing the appliance for the full 2 hours during the day, 93.3% practicing the exercises daily, and 93.3% wearing the appliance for the full duration while sleeping. These high compliance rates indicate strong patient awareness and commitment, reflecting well-chosen cases of children with good self-discipline and strong family support. It also highlights the effectiveness of continuous guidance and monitoring from both the treating physician and the child's family.23

During sleep, some children may unconsciously dislodge the appliance, particularly in the first 2 weeks of treatment. This may occur due to incorrect placement of the appliance in the mouth or discomfort caused by the foreign object in the mouth.24 In our study, 40% of children experienced this issue. When this happened, we typically advised the patient to increase their daytime wearing time by an additional 30 minutes the following day to help the child get accustomed to the appliance. Over time, the incidence of the appliance falling out during sleep gradually decreased and eventually stopped.

Study Limitation

Despite the positive outcomes observed, this study has several limitations that should be considered. The relatively small sample size may restrict the generalizability of the findings to a broader population. Furthermore, the study's retrospective design could introduce selection bias, as the participants were not randomly assigned to treatment, which could affect the comparability of results.

Several potential confounders and effect modifiers may have influenced treatment outcomes. Growth variability among participants, particularly age-related craniofacial changes, could impact skeletal and dental development independently of Myobrace therapy. Patient compliance with appliance wear and myofunctional exercises also plays a critical role in treatment success, potentially introducing variability in results. Furthermore, preexisting skeletal patterns, such as mandibular prognathism or maxillary retrognathism, may modify the extent of correction achievable with Myobrace therapy. Additional confounding factors include the presence of concurrent orthodontic interventions, genetic predisposition to malocclusion, and the severity of initial oral habits. To minimize these effects, this study implemented strict inclusion criteria, standardized treatment protocols, and routine follow-ups to monitor compliance and progression. Statistical analyses also accounted for key demographic and clinical variables to adjust for potential confounding influences.

Another limitation is the relatively short follow-up period of 14 months. While this timeframe was sufficient to assess initial treatment outcomes, it does not provide insights into the long-term stability and durability of the corrections achieved with Myobrace i3. Future studies with larger, more diverse cohorts and extended follow-up durations are essential to validate these findings and evaluate the sustained effects of Myobrace therapy on skeletal and dental development.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study demonstrates the efficacy of the Myobrace i3 appliance in the treatment of anterior crossbite during the early mixed dentition phase, primarily through dentoalveolar changes. The appliance yielded statistically significant improvements in overjet and facilitated favorable adjustments in the axial inclination of both maxillary and mandibular incisors, concurrent with the mitigation of deleterious oral habits such as tonguethrusting and low tongue posture. While skeletal changes were not statistically significant within the confines of this study's duration, the Myobrace i3 appliance presents a promising therapeutic modality for the correction of dental anterior crossbite in the pediatric population. Further longitudinal investigations are warranted to comprehensively evaluate the long-term impact of Myobrace therapy on craniofacial skeletal development.

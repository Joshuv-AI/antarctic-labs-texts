CBX6-CA9 axis: An epigenetic-metabolic vulnerability in glioblastoma
Authors: Keykavous Parang
Published: 2026

Main text

Glioblastoma (GBM) remains one of the most therapeutically recalcitrant malignancies, with median survival rarely exceeding 15 months despite maximal therapy.1 The hypoxic tumor microenvironment drives treatment resistance through epigenetic and metabolic adaptations.2

In this issue of Molecular Therapy Oncology, the study by Wang et al.3 demonstrates a novel regulatory axis between CBX6 (chromobox 6), a polycomb group protein, and CA9 (carbonic anhydrase 9), revealing how hypoxia-driven epigenetic reprogramming promotes tumor progression and identifying a mechanistically linked pathway for therapeutic intervention in GBM.

The study by Wang et al.3 advances our understanding of how the tumor microenvironment orchestrates epigenetic changes that promote malignant phenotypes. The demonstration that hypoxia dynamically regulates a polycomb group protein challenges traditional views of epigenetic regulation as static and highlights the importance of microenvironment-gene interactions in therapeutic design.

The study establishes an inverse regulatory relationship between CBX6 and CA9 that operates at the intersection of epigenetic control and metabolic adaptation. Through RNA sequencing, chromatin immunoprecipitation, and functional validation, Wang et al. demonstrated that CBX6 functions as a direct transcriptional repressor of CA9. Under hypoxic conditions characteristic of GBM, CBX6 expression is downregulated, leading to CA9 upregulation. This upregulation contributes to an acidic extracellular environment that facilitates tumor growth, invasion, and therapy resistance (Figure 1).3 Immunoprecipitation assays confirmed direct CBX6 binding to the CA9 promoter,3 distinguishing this axis from indirect relationships and providing a clear molecular framework for therapeutic targeting.

The CBX6-CA9 regulatory axis in glioblastoma

Under normoxic conditions (left), CBX6 is expressed and directly represses CA9 transcription, leading to reduced tumor growth and improved prognosis. Under hypoxic conditions (right), CBX6 is downregulated, resulting in CA9 derepression, an acidic microenvironment, and tumor progression. Therapeutic strategies (bottom) include CBX6 restoration, CA9 inhibition, or combination approaches targeting both the upstream regulator and downstream effector. Biomarker-driven stratification based on CBX6 expression may identify patients most likely to benefit from CA9 inhibitor therapy.

Thus, functional consequences were substantial. CBX6 silencing promoted proliferation, migration, invasion, and sphere formation in vitro, whereas CBX6 overexpression yielded the opposite effects in vitro and in vivo.3 Analysis of the Glioma Genome Atlas revealed that patients with lower CBX6 expression (n = 131) exhibited significantly poorer overall survival compared to those with higher expression (n = 135; p = 1.94e−7), establishing CBX6 as both a prognostic biomarker and tumor suppressor.3

The identification of the CBX6-CA9 axis opens multiple therapeutic avenues. CA9 represents an immediately actionable target, as several CA9 inhibitors have been developed for various cancers, including SLC-0111 (U-104).4,5 The prognostic significance of CBX6 expression suggests that patients with low CBX6 levels might be particularly responsive to CA9-targeted therapies, enabling biomarker-driven patient stratification.

Moreover, the mechanistic linkage between CBX6 and CA9 suggests that optimal therapeutic strategies may require addressing both components of this axis (Figure 1). Single-agent CA9 inhibition might be circumvented if upstream CBX6 dysregulation persists, potentially driving compensatory mechanisms. Conversely, strategies to restore CBX6 expression could provide more durable tumor suppression by addressing the root epigenetic dysregulation while simultaneously reducing CA9-mediated metabolic adaptation.

The study reveals that CBX6 regulates a broader transcriptional network beyond CA9. RNA sequencing identified multiple genes whose expression is altered by CBX6 modulation, suggesting that CBX6 restoration might simultaneously target multiple downstream effectors involved in proliferation, invasion, and therapy resistance. This multiplexed effect could prove advantageous compared to targeting individual downstream proteins.

The hypoxic regulation of CBX6 also suggests that therapies targeting tumor hypoxia or vascular normalization might synergize with CBX6-CA9 axis inhibition. By reducing the hypoxic stress that drives CBX6 downregulation, such approaches could enhance the effectiveness of CA9 inhibitors.

Despite these promising findings, important limitations warrant further discussion. A critical gap is the absence of therapeutic validation. Although CA9 inhibitors exist, the study does not test these compounds, either alone or in combination with CBX6 modulation. Such experiments are essential to establish proof-of-concept efficacy and to determine whether single-agent CA9 inhibition is sufficient or whether combination approaches are required.

The blood-brain barrier (BBB) represents a formidable obstacle for drug delivery. The pharmacokinetic properties and BBB penetration of CA9 inhibitors, as well as strategies to enhance CBX6 expression—whether through small-molecule epigenetic modulators, gene therapy, or other mechanisms—require thorough investigation before clinical translation.

The in vivo studies by Wang et al.,3 while demonstrating clear effects of CBX6 modulation on tumor growth, are relatively short-term and do not address mechanisms of resistance or compensatory pathways that might emerge during prolonged treatment. Whether other polycomb group proteins can compensate for CBX6 loss or whether parallel pathways can bypass CA9 inhibition remains unexplored.

Furthermore, the CBX6-CA9 axis represents one of several emerging therapeutic vulnerabilities in GBM. Complementary approaches targeting mitotic regulators, Aurora kinases (AURKA/AURKB),6,7,8 targeting protein for Xklp2 (TPX2),9 and Polo-like kinase 1 (PLK1)10 offer instructive parallels and expanding therapeutic options. The convergence of epigenetic, metabolic, and cell-cycle vulnerabilities suggests that future strategies will rely on multi-target approaches guided by molecular profiling. Biomarker-driven patient stratification will become essential. CBX6 expression predicting CA9 inhibitor responsiveness parallels how Aurora kinase and PLK1 levels guide treatment selection. Combination strategies targeting complementary pathways will likely prove superior to monotherapies. Combining CA9 inhibition with CBX6 restoration through epigenetic modulators could also yield synergistic benefits.

The broader impact extends beyond GBM to other hypoxic solid tumors and enhances our understanding of how microenvironmental stress drives malignant evolution. The CBX6-CA9 axis may operate in other cancers, and the methodological framework provides a template for dissecting similar networks.

In conclusion, the identification of the CBX6-CA9 regulatory axis represents a significant advance in understanding GBM biology and provides a mechanistically grounded framework for therapeutic development. The direct transcriptional relationship between these proteins, combined with the prognostic significance of CBX6 expression and the availability of CA9 inhibitors, offers immediate translational opportunities. However, realizing the therapeutic potential of this axis will require addressing critical gaps, particularly regarding optimal therapeutic strategies (single-agent versus combination approaches), drug delivery across the BBB, and resistance mechanisms. The broader transcriptional network regulated by CBX6 suggests that combination approaches targeting both the upstream epigenetic regulator and key downstream effectors may be necessary for durable therapeutic responses. With rigorous preclinical validation and innovative clinical trial designs, the CBX6-CA9 axis may ultimately improve patient outcomes for this devastating disease.

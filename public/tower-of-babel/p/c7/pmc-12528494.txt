A lightweight blockchain based scalable and collaborative mitigation framework against new flow DDoS attacks in SDN enabled autonomous systems
Authors: Shikha Garg, Sonia Goyal, Abhinav Bhandari
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe New-flow based DDoS attacks are new kinds of attacks that pose a severe threat to SDN-Enabled AS by exploiting vulnerabilities of the centralized SDN controller. This research paper proposes the lightweight blockchain-based framework for Scalable and Collaborative DDoS Mitigation in SDN-Enabled Autonomous Systems (LiBSCOM-AS). LiBSCOM-AS consist of three interconnected modules:(1) Information theory-based (Entropy-based) detection module that detects New-flow based DDoS attacks in real-time by analyzing the anomalies in network traffic specifically in source IP, destination IP and Packet-In message entropies at the P4 SDN switches and SDN controller (2) a lightweight Characterization module that segregates genuine traffic from the attack traffic with minimum computational overhead (3) a Blockchain-based mitigation and a threat Information Sharing that confirms secure and coordinated attack response through Unified smart contract for Intra- and Inter-domain AS to provide decentralized enforcement and tamper-resistant logging. With the experimental evaluations and existing data set analysis, LiBSCOM-AS provides an efficient and effective mitigation strategy for minimizing the impact of New-flow based DDoS attacks in SDN-enabled autonomous systems. By leveraging SDN and blockchain, LiBSCOM-AS offers scalable, robust DDoS defenses. The system is implemented using Ethereum’s Ropsten and Hyperledger Fabric for inter- and intra-domain AS, respectively. Experimental results show the system’s detection accuracy of 99.9875%, reduced complexity from O(N)+O(K) to O(S) characterization overhead. Introduction

In recent years, Autonomous Systems (ASes) have played a vital role as the building blocks of Internet infrastructure. Autonomous systems are large networks or groups of networks under a common administrative domain that share a standard routing policy1,2. They play an essential role in the worldwide internet architecture by handling the routing decisions and network traffic flows within and across their domains. Software Defined Network (SDN)-Enabled AS is a paradigm shift in network architecture, offering more intelligence and flexibility for managing complex AS infrastructures using software programs and by decoupling the control plane (Controller) from the data plane ( P4 SDN switches)3. A P4 SDN switch leverages P4’s programmability to extend and modify the traditional OpenFlow pipeline, enhancing the switch’s ability to support custom match-action tables, new protocols, and complex processing logic. SDN eases centralized network management and offers programmability, enabling AS to make real-time decisions based on current network conditions and predefined rules/policies4. However, this flexibility in centralization also creates new security issues and challenges. The centralization of control at a single point creates a possibility of a single point of failure5, making the SDN controller a primary target for DDoS attacks6. Moreover, intruders and attackers can exploit the network’s programmability, manipulate traffic flows or insert malicious configurations. Various attacks can be targeted at SDN-Enabled AS, with DDoS attacks among the most prominent7.

DDoS attacks have emerged as one of the most formidable threats to network security8. These attacks aim to overwhelm a target’s resources, rendering services inaccessible to legitimate users and potentially causing significant financial and reputational damage. Over recent years, DDoS attacks have become increasingly sophisticated and prevalent, posing a substantial threat to SDN environments. Attackers have refined their techniques, leveraging advanced tools and exploiting vulnerabilities to launch more significant, damaging attacks. For example, the most dangerous and record-breaking DDoS attack in 2022 exploited misconfigured Memcached servers to generate massive malicious traffic, reaching a bandwidth of 3.47 Tbps. Also, in October 2023, Google mitigated the most massive DDoS attack ever recorded, an HTTP/2 ”Rapid Reset” attack that peaked at 398 million requests per second (rps). Further, Cloudflare also reported for mitigating thousands of hyper-volumetric HTTP DDoS attacks in August 2023, with requests exceeding 100 million rps. The largest of these attacks, peaking at 201 million rps, surpassed the previous 71 million rps set in February 20239. These rising attacks have caused substantial disruptions to government websites, online businesses and critical digital infrastructure, emphasizing the need for robust and effective collaborative defence mechanisms.

A DDoS attack has significantly compromised SDN-enabled AS by flooding the network with malicious traffic10. With its centralized control plane (Controller), SDN can be predominantly vulnerable to DDoS attacks targeting the central SDN controller, resulting in network congestion and performance degradation. Attackers exploit the dynamic flow management of SDN by overwhelming the controller with new flow requests (called New-flow based DDoS attack), choking its capacity to process legitimate traffic, and disrupting communication among different network devices. The New-flow based DDoS attacks exploit the flow installation process of SDN-enabled AS. Attackers generate vast volumes of new flows that must be processed and handled by the SDN controller, consuming significant resources and processing power. Unlike the traditional DDoS attacks, which focus on saturating network bandwidth, new-flow attacks exploit SDN’s design, targeting the controller’s ability to handle new flow rules11. These attacks significantly increase the network latency and overload the control plane(controller), causing the denial of legitimate services across multiple autonomous systems. Autonomous systems (ASes) are vital in the cooperative and collaborative defence against DDoS attacks. DDoS attacks are malicious attempts to deny/degrade the services to legitimate users by sending malicious traffic in AS12. By sharing information, resources, and mitigation strategies, ASes can mutually develop their flexibility against these attacks, making a more secure and robust Internet ecosystem environment. The main goal of an AS is to manage network traffic within its internal network and also coordinate the flow of traffic with neighbouring ASes12.

Broadly, DDoS Mitigation approaches in AS can be classified into non-collaborative and collaborative methods. Non-collaborative approaches require responses within individual ASes (Intra-domain), which may be less effective due to inadequate shared knowledge and visibility13–15. In contrast, collaborative DDoS mitigation leverages the collective strength of multiple ASes (Inter-domain), leading to more coordinated and effective DDoS defence. The trend is increasingly incorporating adopting advanced technologies like blockchain to enhance collaboration, offering a decentralized and tamper-proof mechanism for sharing threat intelligence and coordinating responses among inter-domain ASes16–20.

Blockchain technology is a decentralized and distributed digital ledger that records transactions across many computers so that the registered transactions cannot be altered retroactively18,21. This inherent security and transparency make blockchain an ideal solution for various applications, particularly enhancing trust in collaborative environments. By ensuring that data is immutable and verifiable, blockchain eliminates the need for a central authority, enabling peer-to-peer interactions.

Although entropy-based detection and blockchain-based mitigation have been studied separately, these solutions face limitations when applied independently, especially in terms of scalability, inter-domain coordination, and resource efficiency. This motivated us to develop a unified framework that integrates lightweight entropy detection with collaborative blockchain enforcement across inter-domain Autonomous Systems (ASes).

Related work

Collaborative DDoS attack mitigation has been a significant area of research because of the growing DDoS threat to online communities, network infrastructure, and Web services. Various researchers have proposed several techniques to detect and mitigate such kinds of attacks23–26. In this section, we have categorized and discussed the related work of various researchers into three broad categories: detection and characterization schemes, which detect and characterize the normal and the DDoS attack traffic, and mitigation approaches, which incorporate the methods for minimizing the impact of such attacks by using various methods of filtering, rate limiting and attack traffic diversion.

Detection and characterization schemes of DDoS attack

Detection and characterization schemes for DDoS attacks have progressed significantly, transitioning from simple signature-based methods to statistical analysis (anomaly detection) and advanced machine learning approaches25–27. Signature-based detection matches attack patterns with known signatures, while anomaly-based techniques monitor network flow against predefined baselines. Entropy-based methods assess traffic randomness, providing early detection by identifying deviations from normal patterns indicating potential DDoS activity.

J. Singh et al.28 employed source IP entropy to detect traffic anomalies, demonstrating its efficiency in distinguishing normal and attack scenarios. Similarly, A. Navaz et al.29 proposed a dual-layer entropy-based anomaly detection system for cloud environments, emphasizing its role in identifying genuine versus suspicious traffic and extending this, A. Bhandari et al.27 utilized destination entropy to trace abnormal patterns, addressing threshold optimization challenges using Six Sigma for precision. In SDN environments, R. Wang et al.30 introduced an entropy-based detection method running on OpenFlow switches to reduce controller communication overhead. This lightweight approach ensures efficient and accurate DDoS detection. R. Deb et al.31 proposed dynamic mitigation strategies, such as FLOOD-GUARD (which dynamically adjusts flow rules to counterattack traffic) and SDN-GUARD ( managing flow and rule aggregation), which leverage entropy and flow statistics to strengthen SDN defences. Addressing specific attack types, A. Ahalawat32 demonstrated Shannon entropy’s utility in detecting UDP flooding, enhancing network performance through rate-limiting strategies. M. Aladaileh33 improved detection accuracy using generalized Rényi joint entropy, outperforming traditional methods. Similarly, A. I. Hassan34 combined entropy with machine learning, using k-means clustering to adapt to emerging attack patterns, validated through multiple datasets such as CIC-IDS2017, CSE-CIC-2018, and CICIDS2019. Further, D-CAD by A. Bhandari et al.35 utilized Edge Router Entropy (ERE) and Jensen-Shannon Divergence for collaborative detection, ensuring QoS during attacks. Further, J. Halladay et al.36 time-based features approach improved training speeds and accuracy across various ML classifiers. Another method by Tsobdjou et al.37 employed dynamic thresholds with Shannon entropy, enhancing real-time adaptability and precision. Innovative techniques like generalized Rényi entropy with dynamic thresholds38 demonstrated speed and accuracy improvements. Liu et al.39 integrated entropy with deep learning for two-tier DDoS detection, achieving near-perfect accuracy. Enhancing predictions, David et al.40 applied time-series models like GARCH to entropy values, addressing traffic unpredictability and reducing false positive. Finally, Hirsi et al.41 highlighted the potential of tailored ML datasets for SDN, achieving high accuracy and low false alarm rates with random forest models. Together, these advancements underscore entropy’s central role in improving DDoS detection, paving the way for resilient and adaptive network defence systems. Korba et. al.,42 proposed an explainable anomaly detection approach for mitigating IoT botnet attacks, while Diaf et al.43,44 leveraged large language models to enhance predictive security in IoT networks. These works highlight the growing shift toward intelligent and scalable threat mitigation strategies.

Collaborative DDoS mitigation schemes

Various studies highlight the limitations of isolation and emphasize a collaborative framework that allows the sharing of threat intelligence and real-time coordination17,45,46. These approaches have shown great promise to enhance detection and mitigation, especially with technologies like SDN and blockchain. This section reviews the key contributions in mitigation, threat intelligence sharing, blockchain-enabled trust mechanisms, and integrating collaborative filtering within the SDN environment.

B. Rodrigues et al.47, and Z. Abou El Houda et al.16 emphasize the need for decentralized architectures, with Rodrigues introducing a blockchain-based system using smart contracts for automated IP blacklisting and mitigation. This system fosters inter-domain collaboration by enabling service providers and ASes to share attack information, ensuring efficient and reliable responses while maintaining data integrity. Building on this, Abou El Houda et al.16 proposed a Cochain-SC framework, which integrates blockchain, smart contracts and SDN technology for detecting and mitigating DDoS attacks. Machine learning helps detect accurate threats, while blockchain supports decentralized collaboration within and among different Ases. For early DDoS threat detection and mitigation in intra-domain, Cochain-SC uses intra-entropy-based and intra-base-based methods. Moreover, it employs Ethereum-based smart contracts for automated real-time threat information sharing across different domains (inter-domain). This eliminates reliance on central authorities and strengthens the resilience of participating networks. Similarly, M. Essaid et al.48 combined deep learning with blockchain over the Ethereum lightning network to enable fast, automated responses to DDoS attacks. Their system leverages LSTM for detection and smart contracts for secure resource sharing, offering near-instantaneous reactions during an attack.

Further advancing collaboration, Yeh et al.49 proposed a consortium-based blockchain model to securely share malicious IP information among Security Operations Centers (SOCs). The system uses smart contracts for automated data comparison and cryptographic techniques for secure data exchange, effectively addressing challenges in data sharing across isolated domains. Extending this concept, M. Hajizadeh et al.20 introduced a Cyber-Threat Intelligence (CTI) sharing platform using Hyperledger Fabric. Their model supports secure, real-time collaboration while maintaining data privacy and integrity by employing private blockchains and the STIX format for structured threat data exchange. In IoT networks, R. F. Hayat et al.50 developed the ML-DDoS framework, replacing centralized mechanisms with decentralized systems for device-based verification using blockchain and smart contracts. Performance evaluations demonstrated significant improvements in throughput, latency, and CPU utilization compared to traditional methods. This framework enhances IoT security and highlights the growing importance of decentralized and collaborative approaches for mitigating DDoS threats across diverse environments. Together, these studies showcase the transformative potential of collaborative, blockchain-enabled solutions in modern DDoS defence.

Recent studies have explored diverse techniques for enhancing DDoS and botnet detection across IoT, IoV, and blockchain-based networks. A decentralized federated learning framework, BotDetect, leverages blockchain to detect financial bots in EVM blockchains while preserving privacy and scalability51. Similarly, Zero-X introduces a blockchain-enabled open-set federated learning approach for zero-day attack detection in the Internet of Vehicles (IoV), providing strong adaptability against unseen threats52. To further strengthen zero-day resilience, a modular detection strategy combining isolation forests with particle swarm optimization has been proposed for botnet detection in IoV environments53. In parallel, AI-driven methods have been applied for early identification of IoT botnet threats using traffic analysis, enabling fast and accurate classification of malicious flows54, while lifelong learning-based IDS solutions extend this adaptability to 6G-enabled IoV by supporting continual learning against evolving attack vectors55. Federated learning has also been employed for collaborative zero-day detection in 5G and beyond V2X networks, demonstrating the potential of distributed intelligence in large-scale environments56. Reinforcement learning has further been used in multi-agent intrusion detection systems, improving detection robustness against complex threats57. In the context of DDoS, blockchain-based frameworks such as OTI-IoT focus on operational threat intelligence sharing for mitigating multi-vector attacks58, while adaptive federated learning schemes like FLAD enhance detection accuracy in distributed environments59. Ensemble deep learning integrated with federated learning has also been explored for DoS detection in IoT systems, improving resilience and generalization60. Additionally, emerging efforts address domain-specific contexts, such as topology-aware DDoS detection in Network-on-Chip systems61, and graph-learning-based detection combined with programmable data planes in SDN for synchronized detection and mitigation62. Beyond these direct applications, sensitivity analysis in dynamic Bayesian networks provides insights into robust threshold-based decision-making under uncertainty, which can also be applied to adaptive anomaly detection thresholds in DDoS defense63. Collectively, these works demonstrate distributed learning and adaptive detection strategies across blockchain, IoV, and IoT domains, offering critical building blocks–such as privacy-preserving federated training, zero-day detection, ensemble / edge-based modeling, and continual learning–that can inform the design of a lightweight, scalable, blockchain-based mitigation framework for new-flow DDoS attacks in SDN-enabled Autonomous Systems.

Discussion on related work

After reviewing the related works on collaborative DDoS mitigation, several research gaps have been identified that motivate the development of a novel framework, namely LiBSCOM-AS. It is a collaborative DDoS mitigation framework that provides effective DDoS attack mitigation in SDN-enabled AS. The identified gaps will be presented as follows.

Challenges with Third-Party Detection Systems in Collaborative DDoS Mitigation: Most collaborative DDoS mitigation schemes currently rely on third-party detection systems to identify attacks and differentiate between legitimate and malicious traffic14,16. While these systems can be effective to some extent, their dependence introduces potential latency and complicates seamless integration into the mitigation framework. Overcoming these limitations requires the development of a collaborative mechanism where detection and characterization modules are integral components of the framework. By embedding these modules directly into the cooperative scheme, the response to potential threats can be more streamlined, efficient, and accurate. This approach reduces response times and enhances the overall security posture. The foundation for our proposed mechanism lies in fundamental research on DDoS detection methods, which explores various techniques and their integration into collaborative frameworks, as highlighted in prior studies64,65.

Collaborative DDoS mitigation in AS

A collaborative mitigation framework against DDoS attacks includes multiple organizations and autonomous systems working together in a coordinated manner to detect, characterize, and mitigate the attack traffic14,70. This mitigation framework allows shared threat intelligence to perform the distributed traffic monitoring systems and coordinated response mechanisms, enabling earlier detection of DDoS attack anomalies and attack patterns. The mitigation efforts are coordinated across the participating multiple ASes when an attack is detected, allowing for more effective collaborative traffic filtering, load balancing using traffic diversion, and resource allocation13. Collaborative DDoS mitigation approach reduces the burden on individual ASes by distributing the defensive load across multiple AS, preventing any AS from becoming a bottleneck. The need for collaboration among the ASes arises from the growing complexity and scale of modern DDoS attacks, which often include various attack vectors that also originate from globally distributed bots.

Moreover, collaborative techniques address the inherent vulnerabilities of conventional/traditional DDoS mitigation approaches, such as limited visibility, resource constraints, and scalability challenges13,14,47,71,72. By pooling resources and sharing threat intelligence, multiple ASes can achieve better resource utilization, situational awareness, and more accurate detection of DDoS attacks. LiBSCOM-AS: an overview

A Lightweight Blockchain Framework for Scalable and Collaborative DDoS Mitigation in SDN-Enabled Autonomous Systems (LiBSCOM-AS) is a robust framework designed and implemented to mitigate DDoS attacks using a holistic and modular approach. It consists of three modules: Detection, Characterization, and Mitigation. The detection module uses entropy-based methods to identify New-flow based DDoS attacks in real-time by analyzing three types of entropies: source IP entropy, destination IP entropy, and Packet-In messages entropy. When an anomaly is detected in the network traffic, particularly AS, the inherent lightweight characterization module starts segregating the legitimate and malicious traffic to ensure that benign user activities remain unaffected during the mitigation process.

New-flow DDoS detection module

In our proposed LiBSCOM Framework we have used entropy-based detection approach over AI/ML techniques for collaborative DDoS mitigation in SDN-enabled autonomous systems due to their simplicity, efficiency, and real-time detection capabilities. This is crucial in SDN environments where controllers must make quick decisions to mitigate attacks. Unlike AI/ML techniques, which often demand large labelled datasets, extensive training, and significant processing power, entropy-based methods can identify sudden changes in traffic behaviour with minimal overhead. They are particularly effective for detecting New-flow based DDoS attacks by monitoring anomalies in source IP entropy, destination IP entropy, and Packet-In entropy. Furthermore, in our proposed framework, entropy metrics are easier to interpret and deploy in collaborative settings where multiple domains must share threat information without adding the complexity of model compatibility or retraining.

Characterization of new-flow DDoS attack

The characterization module in LiBSCOM accurately distinguishes between legitimate and malicious traffic to mitigate DDoS attacks in inter- and intra-domain AS with minimal collateral damage. It identifies malicious P4 SDN switches and source IPs by computing switch ID entropy to detect anomalies. If entropy falls below a threshold, the switch is flagged, and source IPs with irregular traffic patterns are identified based on flow frequency and entropy analysis. The compiled list of malicious switches and IPs is then securely shared across domains using blockchain technology for decentralized mitigation.

Mitigation and threat intelligence of new-flow DDoS attack

The mitigation module, which is blockchain-based, provides a secure and decentralized mechanism for enforcing mitigation strategies, enhancing the system’s scalability and resilience against continuously evolving New-flow based DDoS attacks in SDN Enable AS. Finally, the threat information Sharing module enables collaborative threat intelligence sharing across inter-domain AS, leveraging the blockchain’s immutable ledger to ensure the integrity and reliability of shared New-flow based DDoS attack information. Each module in the LiBSCOM-AS integrates seamlessly, allowing vital information to flow through the workflow and enabling a coordinated and effective response to DDoS attacks. This interconnected design ensures that LiBSCOM detects and mitigates DDoS attacks and adapts and scales with the ever-evolving New-flow based DDoS attacks, providing a comprehensive security framework for SDN-enabled ASes.

Figure 1 shows the high-level architecture for LiBSCOM-AS, showing interactions among different modules like SDN controller, P4 SDN Switches, blockchain (inter- and Intra-domain), and smart contracts deployed on both inter and intra-domain, to create an automated, decentralized, and secure framework for detecting, characterizing, and mitigating New-flow based DDoS attacks. In this architecture, each autonomous system operates hierarchically, comprising SDN controllers, P4 SDN switches, a master SDN controller, and a private blockchain network connecting these controllers. P4 SDN switches and SDN controllers within intra-domain As, responsible for managing network traffic flow, use entropy-based methods to detect New-flow based DDoS attacks. The source IP based entropy is used to gauge traffic legitimacy, destination IP-based entropy is used for tracking flow variability to specific destinations, and Packet-In messages entropy to assess packets forwarded by P4 SDN switches for processing. Once SDN controllers detect a New-flow based DDoS attack, the event is logged on the private blockchain, and consensus is established over mitigation actions, ensuring secure, tamper-proof communication and coordinated responses across the network of SDN controllers. Smart contracts deployed on the intra-domain blockchain automate mitigation by executing predefined policies, such as blocking malicious IPs and adjusting traffic routing rules according to detected attack conditions. This automation minimizes response times and ensures consistent action. Cross-domain collaboration is achieved through a Border Gate Router(BGP), which shares threat intelligence information and mitigation strategies across different AS domains (inter-domain). Events detected in one AS are communicated to others via BGP2, facilitating proactive measures like blocking or rerouting traffic from known malicious sources. Furthermore, inter-domain smart contracts enable reporting suspicious IPs to the shared blockchain ledger, enhancing global threat intelligence and enabling coordinated defence strategies. So, by combining blockchain’s decentralized, secure nature with SDN’s dynamic control capabilities, this interconnected framework strengthens resilience against large-scale New-flow based DDoS attacks and enhances the security of large-scale ASes.

High-level architecture of LiBSCOM-AS.

It is designed to enhance security through key characteristics such as decentralization, which eliminates single points of failure by leveraging a blockchain network for distributed control and threat intelligence sharing. Its immutable ledger ensures the tamper-proof recording of threat data and mitigation actions, while transparency fosters trust and accountability by enabling auditable records of security events. LiBSCOM-AS supports real-time collaboration for prompt responses to threats, scalability to handle increased attack traffic through horizontal scaling of SDN controllers, and automation via smart contracts to streamline detection, data sharing, and mitigation processes with minimal human intervention, ensuring effective intra- and inter-domain coordination. The LiBSCOM approach is designed to operate in a distributed SDN environment, where each P4 switch processes traffic locally before interacting with the SDN controller. Using entropy-based analysis at the edge reduces the dependency on a centralized controller, thereby mitigating potential bottlenecks. The modular nature of the framework allows seamless integration with additional SDN controllers as network scale increases.

Intra-domain DDoS detection and characterization modules in LiBSCOM: AS

This section will discuss the system architecture of intra-domain AS DDoS detection and characterization modules supported by LiBSCOM: AS. Modular design that contains (Traffic Monitoring, Anomaly Detection Engine, Characterization unit response collaboration interface), technology stack used, detailed detection and characterization algorithms, scalability, optimization of resources and integration of intra-domain mitigation using blockchain (smart contract) are discussed in detail.

System architecture

The system architecture is mainly designed for autonomous SDN environments, as shown in Fig. 2, providing a multi-layered and comprehensive approach to detecting, characterizing, and mitigating New-flow based DDoS attacks in real-time in intra-domain AS.

System architecture of intra-domain DDoS detection and characterization modules.

Data Plane: The data plane at the bottom layer of this architecture comprises P4 SDN switches that act as the first element for traffic collection inside the AS network. Each P4 SDN switch is a core point for traffic flow management and is always in continuous collection of flow statistics. It continuously monitors and assembles packet features, byte counts, and flow duration metrics, among many others, in real time. Our proposed framework introduces an innovative approach by incorporating the concept of Packet-In message entropy in SDN-enabled Autonomous Systems. Understanding Packet-In message entropy requires a solid understanding of the OpenFlow forwarding mechanism, which forms the backbone of SDN architecture.

OpenFlow forwarding in SDN-enabled autonomous system

OpenFlow forwarding, as shown in Fig. 3, is an SDN protocol that enables the central controller to specify how switches process network traffic by directing packets matching flow table entries with actions such as forwarding, rewriting headers, or dropping packets. Packets without matches are sent to the controller for dynamic traffic management and security enforcement decisions. OpenFlow supports proactive and reactive rule installation modes: proactive pre-loads flow rules in switches, while reactive installs rules dynamically after traffic arrives. In reactive mode, unmatched packets are sent to the controller using Packet-In messages, which determine the route and update the switch via Packet_Out messages. While reactive mode allows dynamic control, it risks DDoS attacks, where attackers flood the controller with fake Packet-In messages, causing congestion and service denial for legitimate users.

Packet-in message entropy

As discussed in the previous section, the key role of Packet-In messages is related to centralizing network control and offering dynamic packet handling. With a worldwide network view, the SDN controller can then analyze such Packet-In messages and decide how to process these packets. It typically involves installing New flow rules on the switch so that similar packets will be handled without requiring further Packet-Ins. The process begins when a switch receives a packet and consults its flow rules. If no matching rule is found, the switch constructs a Packet-In message, encapsulates that packet inside this message and sends the packet to the controller. Upon receiving the Packet-In, the controller analyzes the arrived packet: it may forward or drop the packets and send updated flow rules back to the switch. Packet-Ins can surge significantly during a DDoS attack because the attacking packets often do not match existing rules. This increase in Packet-Ins can overload the controller, making it a critical indicator for detecting DDoS attacks. For example, in normal traffic conditions, packets from a single source IP are typically directed to a limited number of destination IPs. In contrast, during a DDoS attack, many unique source IPs might send packets to numerous destination IPs, or a single source IP might flood packets to many different destination IPs. This pattern causes a spike in Packet-Ins, signalling a New-flow based DDoS attack. Collaborative mitigation of new-flow based DDoS attacks using blockchain in intra- and inter-domain AS

After successfully detecting and characterizing the New-flow based DDoS attack traffic and distinguishing it from legitimate traffic, the next crucial step is implementing targeted filtering at the identified P4 SDN switches to mitigate the attack’s impact. Filtering must be precise to ensure that only the attack traffic is dropped or rate-limited. In contrast, legitimate traffic continues to flow uninterrupted, preserving normal network operations and minimizing disruptions for legitimate users, i.e. to minimize the collateral damage. The master SDN controller uses this information to update the flow rules on the affected switches dynamically. The specific flow rules matching aggressive IP addresses or abnormal flow rates of the attack traffic are installed in the switches to instruct them to block or severely limit these flows, effectively containing and reducing the attack’s impact at the source points within the network infrastructure of the intra-domain AS.

Threat intelligence using unified smart contract in intra-and inter-domain AS

Blockchain technology, a decentralized digital ledger, ensures immutable transactions that are secure across multiple blockchain nodes without requiring central management. Its data integrity and transparency make it an ideal choice for collaborative mitigation applications. Smart contracts are part of the blockchain and are self-executing codes embedded with agreement terms that play an essential role in secure and automated threat intelligence sharing for collaborative DDoS mitigation. Within inter- and intra-domain AS environments, they provide real-time communication among SDN controllers, sharing traffic anomalies and attack-related information as transactions on blockchain nodes. The smart contract’s life cycle encompasses creation, deployment, and execution. In this process, contracts are coded with predefined rules, deployed onto the blockchain, and executed autonomously upon meeting specified conditions, ensuring secure and transparent operations.

The master SDN controller in the intra-domain verifies the New-flow DDoS threat intelligence information independently and takes action against it by deploying rate limiting at edge P4 SDN switches, allowing only legitimate traffic within AS. The master SDN controller also applies the Round Robbin load balancing algorithms to distribute its load to other controllers. In our proposed mitigation framework, by collaboratively updating the blockchain with newly learned threat information, all SDN controllers in the AS can quickly synchronize their responses to provide a coordinated and effective defense against New-flow based DDoS attacks in a decentralized manner. This approach reduces response time to attacks and improves the overall network’s resilience, ensuring each blockchain node possesses up-to-date threat intelligence to collectively mitigate attacks in real time. The architecture of LiBSCOM-AS leverages an intra-domain blockchain based on Hyperledger Fabric for the SDN-enabled AS. Hyperledger Fabric ensures secure, tamper-proof data sharing where smart contracts automatically execute responses, such as updating firewall rules and blocking IP addresses in the master SDN controller’s mitigation apps.

Inter-domain involves a defense strategy for multiple ASes, each with its own independent network and security infrastructure. The master SDN controller in the victim AS initiates the detection of New-flow DDoS attacks through anomaly detection discussed in the previous section. This step is followed by the sharing of extracted threat intelligence on a blockchain network, cross-cutting numerous Ases (Inter-domain). Using the inter-domain blockchain, each AS can independently verify and act on shared threat intelligence to enforce blocking rules and traffic filtering. Inter-domain collaboration allows for a collective and coordinated response to New-flow based DDoS attacks, enhancing the detection and mitigation capability against attacks across multiple domains.

Unified Threat Intelligence Smart Contract

This unified smart contract is designed to support intra-domain (within a single Autonomous System) and inter-domain (across multiple Autonomous Systems) threat intelligence sharing, ensuring efficient and secure collaboration among SDN controllers. It handles blacklisted IP addresses, associated P4 SDN switch IDs, reporting domains, and timestamps, adapting its operations dynamically based on the scope of threat intelligence sharing. For intra-domain operations, the contract focuses on managing IPs and switch IDs, while for inter-domain operations, it extends functionality to include domains alongside IPs and switch IDs. The essential functions, addThreat and removeThreat, use an isInterDomain flag to adjust their behaviour accordingly, allowing the system to seamlessly differentiate between intra- and inter-domain scenarios.

The contract includes event logging capabilities, with events like ThreatAdded and ThreatRemoved providing transparency and traceability for all operations across intra- and inter-domain levels. The hasPermission function enforces access control, ensuring that only authorized entities can interact with the contract, thus maintaining secure collaboration. While blockchain technology ensures immutability, outdated or irrelevant entries can be logically marked inactive, preserving storage efficiency without compromising historical data integrity. The smart contract enables seamless sharing and retrieval of threat intelligence across SDN controllers within and between autonomous systems, ensuring interoperability and providing a robust defence mechanism against DDoS attacks.

The unified smart contract leverages the Proof of Stake (PoS) mechanism to enable secure and efficient collaboration among autonomous systems for threat intelligence sharing. In this system, participating AS act as validators by staking tokens or credits, which serve as a commitment to honest participation. The PoS mechanism selects validators based on their stake proportion, ensuring a fair and decentralized process for verifying transactions such as adding or removing blacklisted data. Validators confirm the authenticity of threat intelligence, enforce compliance with contract rules, and update the shared state across the network. Honest validators are rewarded for their contributions, while those engaging in malicious activities face penalties such as stake slashing. This approach strengthens the reliability and trustworthiness of the smart contract while maintaining energy efficiency and scalability. By integrating PoS, the unified smart contract ensures a robust, tamper-resistant framework for real-time, inter- and intra-domain threat intelligence sharing across diverse AS networks.

The InSDN dataset75 provides comprehensive resources for evaluating intrusion detection systems in SDNs. Features were extracted using CICFlowMeter, providing over 80 statistical metrics. The dataset comprises 343,939 instances, with 20% normal and 80% attack traffic, segmented into CSV files: Normal-data.csv (68,424 cases), OVS.csv (136,743 instances), and Metasploitable-2.csv (138,772 instances). This dataset offers a comprehensive SDN intrusion detection research platform, reflecting diverse and realistic network scenarios.

To further validate the variation and randomness in Source IP and destination IP, two datasets named InSDN75 and DDoS-SDNs76 have been analyzed. The dataset DDos-SDN was chosen from Mendeley data, which has 104345 rows and 23 attributes76. The dataset detects traffic type as benign or malicious based on the TCP Syn attack, UDP Flood attack, and ICMP attack. A total of 23 attributes are available, including Switch ID, Packet count, byte count, and many others, with a total of 1,04,345 rows of data. The traffic classification is labelled 0 for benign and 1 for malicious users. The dataset is customized to 18 attributes, of which 17 are features and 01 is the target variable. The target label binary is classified with 0 (normal user) and 1 (attacker). The dataset contains one categorical attribute named protocol, which is one-hot encoded.

In contrast, Hyperledger Fabric, used for intra-domain SDN controllers, provides low-latency (50-80ms) operations with zero transaction costs, making it the preferred choice for intra-AS mitigation strategies.

LiBSCOM-AS achieves a 96.71% reduction in gas usage compared to Cochain16 and a 96.80% reduction compared to Co-IOT22, significantly enhancing cost efficiency for smart contract deployment. The gas-saving percentages reported in this work reflect the lightweight design of our smart contracts, which prioritize concise logic and minimal event emissions to reduce transaction costs. We recognize that more complex contracts with equivalent functionality may incur higher gas consumption.

The significant gas cost reduction achieved by LiBSCOM-AS is due to the design of unified smart contract, which consolidates multiple threat intelligence management functions into a single contract. As demonstrated in the pseudocode (algorithm 3), the contract efficiently uses mappings to store and update blacklisted threat data by IP address, reducing costly storage writes. We also analysed mitigation response times during simulated attacks to measure the impact of dynamic rule updates on SDN controller latency. The real-time latency for updating flow rules averaged 5.8ms, with peak delays reaching 10ms under heavy load due to New-flow DDOS attack.

Deployment constraints and security risks in LiBSCOM-AS in inter-domain autonomous systems

While LiBSCOM-AS demonstrates strong detection accuracy and reduced computational overhead to 95%, real-world deployment introduces certain constraints and risks that must be considered. Firstly, the deployment of LiBSCOM-AS requires integration with SDN controllers and P4-enabled switches across autonomous systems, which introduces interoperability, scalability, and resource overhead constraints. Ensuring compatibility with heterogeneous controller platforms and maintaining low-latency processing at edge switches are critical to practical adoption. While enhancing trust and decentralization, the blockchain component can incur gas costs and transaction delays, particularly in large-scale inter-domain environments. From a security perspective, LiBSCOM-AS strengthens resilience against New-flow DDoS attacks by leveraging entropy-based detection and collaborative threat intelligence sharing over a tamper-resistant blockchain ledger. This ensures authenticity, integrity, and non-repudiation of blacklisted IP information across AS domains, while reducing the risk of single-point failures or manipulation by malicious entities. However, challenges such as smart contract vulnerabilities, potential blockchain congestion, and privacy concerns in sharing flow-level metadata must be addressed through secure coding, consensus optimization, and privacy-preserving mechanisms.

Scalability is also a critical challenge. During high-volume attacks, a sudden surge in blacklisted IPs and entropy values could overwhelm the blockchain transaction queue. To preserve scalability, LiBSCOM-AS distributes entropy calculations across P4 switches and controllers (O(S) complexity), ensuring the blockchain only stores aggregated threat intelligence rather than raw traffic data. This design minimizes transaction volume, reduces block congestion, and allows the system to sustain mitigation under hyper-volumetric attacks.

Therefore, while blockchain brings transparency and trust to collaborative mitigation, careful validator management, hybrid consensus deployment, and distributed entropy computation are necessary to balance security, latency, and scalability in real-world environments.

Integrating smart contracts within LiBSCOM-AS may introduce several security risks that must be carefully addressed in adversarial settings. Malicious actors may exploit vulnerabilities in smart contract code, such as re-entrancy attacks, logic manipulation, or unchecked external calls, to inject false blacklisted IPs or bypass legitimate threat intelligence updates. Since LiBSCOM-AS operates in a multi-domain environment, an adversary could attempt collusion attacks, where compromised AS participants collectively manipulate the blockchain ledger to propagate misleading information. Furthermore, the immutability of blockchain records means that once malicious entries are written to the ledger, they cannot be altered without consensus, potentially undermining the reliability of mitigation decisions. Attackers might also leverage denial-of-service on the blockchain layer by spamming excessive transactions, increasing gas costs and slowing down the verification of legitimate updates. Additionally, privacy risks arise if flow-level metadata shared through smart contracts is exposed, enabling adversaries to infer sensitive traffic patterns or fingerprint specific ASes. LiBSCOM-AS must enforce secure smart contract design practices, formal verification, consensus optimization, and privacy-preserving mechanisms to mitigate these risks.

Conclusion

In this paper, we have proposed the LiBSCOM-AS DDoS mitigation framework, notably improving detection, lightweight characterization, efficient mitigation, and improved way of advertising attack information against New-flow based DDoS attacks through entropy computation and blockchain technology. The entropy-based metrics calculated by the P4 SDN switches and SDN controller, such as the entropy of the source IP, entropy of the destination IP and the entropy of Packet-In messages, are utilized by the detection module. These metrics have proven effective in successfully identifying anomalies and detecting New-flow based DDoS attacks with an accuracy of 99.9875%. These measures quantify randomness and irregularities in network traffic to accurately detect and enhance the precision in identifying New-flow based DDoS threats. The lightweight characterization module enhances the process by mapping and analyzing traffic patterns through the distributed computation of individual P4 SDN switch entropy at the data plane and the SDN controller, reducing the controller’s computational overhead significantly to 100%. It then efficiently distinguishes between normal and attack traffic. Integrating blockchain unified smart contracts in the mitigation module has shown its capability to automate and enforce response actions based on real-time threat intelligence over inter- and intra-domain SDN-enabled AS. The unified smart contract has been tested/evaluated and deployed on Ethereum and Hyperledger Fabric for inter- and intra-domain SDN-enabled AS, respectively. LiBSCOM-AS achieves a 96.71% reduction in gas usage compared to Cochain and a 96.80% reduction compared to Co-IOT, significantly enhancing cost efficiency for smart contract deployment. Future enhancements of LiBSCOM-AS will explore integrating AI-driven anomaly detection models, such as Federated Learning (FL) and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs). These models have shown promise in improving detection accuracy while preserving privacy in decentralized environments. Additionally, incorporating adaptive reinforcement learning-based threshold tuning can further optimize entropy-based detection under dynamic traffic conditions.

Limitations and future scope of work

The current testbed, based on Mininet, the POX controller, and Hyperledger Fabric/Ethereum, provides a controlled environment for prototype validation. Although the evaluation demonstrates feasibility, we acknowledge that real-world SDN deployments are more heterogeneous and involve diverse network conditions (latency, throughput, and communication overhead–), traffic dynamics, and adversarial behaviors. Large-scale live deployments and in-depth robustness testing were beyond the scope of this work due to resource constraints, but they represent an important direction to further validate practical applicability. Moreover, Further testing on diverse datasets is needed to confirm the generalization of our findings.

Isolation of a more aggressive GVI-1 genotype strain HX of the avian infectious bronchitis virus
Authors: Fan Yang, Jun Zhou, Hongbin Huang, Shikai Cai, Yun Zhang, Feng Wen, Mengmeng Zhao, Keshan Zhang, Limei Qin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe avian infectious bronchitis virus (IBV) poses a significant economic threat to the global poultry industry. Although in recent years, the GVI-1 lineage of IBV has proliferated throughout China, there is still a lack of comprehensive studies regarding the pathogenicity of this lineage, particularly with respect to infections of the digestive tract and the antigenic characteristics of the S1 gene. In this study, we investigated the effects of infecting 14-day-old chicks with the HX strain of the GVI-1 lineage over a 14-d period postinfection. Assessment of the pathogenicity of the HX strain included clinical observations; monitoring of body weight, organ viral load, viral shedding, and gross anatomy; histopathological analysis, and bioinformatics-based antigenic characterization of the S1 protein. The findings revealed that compared with previously reported GVI-1 lineage strains, the HX strain is characterized by greater virulence, with infection leading to approximately 26% mortality and extensive severe organ damage, including that of the proventriculus and kidneys. Moreover, at 14 d postinfection, 80% of oral swabs and 100% of cloacal swabs from chickens infected with the HX strain tested positive, indicating a prolonged period of viral shedding relative to that previously reported for GVI-1 lineage strains. Bioinformatic analysis of B-cell epitopes on the S1 protein revealed 7 potential antigenic epitopes. Collectively, our findings in this study provide clear evidence to indicate that compared previously reported GVI-1 lineage strains, chicks infected with the IBV GVI-1 lineage strain HX are characterized by heightened rates of mortality, more pronounced organ damage, and an extended period of viral shedding. This comprehensive characterization highlights the pathogenic potential of the GVI-1 lineage and its capacity to induce severe kidney and proventriculus damage, thereby emphasizing the imperative of early initiated preventive measures. Furthermore, on the basis of our analysis of the antigenic properties of the S1 protein, we have identified 7 potential linear B-cell epitopes, which will provide valuable insights for the development of epitope-based vaccines.Key words: infectious bronchitis virus, GVI-1, HX, S1, antigenicity

INTRODUCTION

Infectious bronchitis (IB), caused by the infectious bronchitis virus (IBV), is a highly transmissible disease among poultry, which can result in substantial financial losses in the poultry industry worldwide (Gu et al., 2022). The IBV is a solitary-stranded positive-sense RNA virus within the Gamma coronavirus genus of the Coronaviridae family (Ma et al., 2022). The genome of IBV spans an approximate 27.6 kilobases and is structured as 5′UTR-1a-1b-S-3a-3b-E-M-5a-5b-N-3′UTR (Wang et al., 2021), encoding 4 structural proteins, namely, the nucleocapsid protein (N), membrane protein (M), spike glycoprotein (S), and envelope protein (E) (Wu et al., 2019). Following translation, the S glycoprotein undergoes cleavage into S1 and S2 subunits, the former of which harbors a receptor-binding domain and major serotype-specific neutralization epitopes, which govern virus attachment, tissue tropism, and serotype-specific antibody induction (Casais et al., 2003; Kiss et al., 2016; Wang et al., 2022). Notably, the nucleotide sequence of the S1 subunit is predisposed to mutation, particularly within 3 pivotal hypervariable regions (HVR). In this regard, it has been established that a variance of 2% to 3% in the amino acids within the HVR (particularly those implicated in the formation of neutralization epitopes) can contribute to alterations in the extent of vaccine cross-protection (Abozeid, 2023; Cavanagh et al., 1992; Ellis et al., 2018; Kant et al., 1992).

The findings of extensive research conducted by Valastro et al. and others (Abozeid, 2023; Valastro et al., 2016), based on analyses of the S1 gene sequences of IBV strains isolated in different geographical regions, has revealed a minimum of 9 distinct genotypes and 39 lineages. The presence of IBV strains in China dates back to 1982, and their subsequent proliferation has been gradual and widespread (Fan et al., 2022; Han et al., 2011). Previous systematic phylogenetic analyses of the S1 gene have categorized Chinese IBV strains into at least 7 different genotypes, namely, the Mass (GI-1), TW (GI-7), 4/91 (GI-13), QX (GI-19), HN08 (GI-22), TC07-2 (GVI-1), and LDT3 (GI-28) genotypes (Feng et al., 2017; Huang et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2018), among which, the GI-19 (QX) genotype is currently the predominant strain in China (Jang et al., 2018; Zhao et al., 2015). The GVI-1 strain TC07-2 was initially identified in Guangdong, China, in 2007 and is presently confined to Asian regions (Jang et al., 2018; Lim et al., 2012; Mase et al., 2010; Raja et al., 2020). Epidemiological surveillance data indicate a consistent escalation in the rate at which GVI-1 strains have been detected in recent years, particularly in southern China (Chen et al., 2021; Fan et al., 2019; Sun et al., 2021a). Previous studies have delineated substantial evolutionary disparities between the GVI-1 strain and the other 6 major genotypes (Bo et al., 2022). To date, however, few studies have sought to assess the antigenic characteristics of the S1 gene of GVI-1 strains.

IBV is known to cause damage to the respiratory, digestive, urinary, and reproductive systems (Najimudeen et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2022), with different strains being characterized by a considerable variation in pathogenicity and tissue tropism (Zhao et al., 2019). Notably, chicks infected with the GI-1 (Mass) strain M41 are characterized by sneezing and lethargy, whereas exposure to the GI-19 (QX) genotype strain SD can result in substantial lesions in the respiratory, digestive, and urinary systems (Cheng et al., 2018). It has been established that GVI-1 strains can inflict damage to the respiratory and reproductive systems without affecting other tissues, and indeed, to date there have been no documented instances of GVI-1 causing digestive system pathologies (Bo et al., 2022; Jang et al., 2018; Sun et al., 2021a). In this study, we undertook a comprehensive examination of the pathogenicity of the GVI-1 HX strain and the antigenic properties of its S1 gene. We believe that our findings have potential implications for the refinement of existing vaccines and development of novel strategies for IBV control.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Virus Isolation, Identification and EID50 Determination

The HX strain of IBV used in this study (NCBI accession number: PP817796) was isolated from a cohort of chickens immunized with the H120 vaccine strain in 2021, which were characterized by clinical manifestations such as tracheitis. For virus propagation, 10-day-old specific-pathogen-free (SPF) chicken embryos were administered virus via the allantoic cavity and named infected group. As control, we used chicken embryos inoculated with the same volume of sterile PBS and named uninfected group. Allantoic fluid derived from the third generation of IBV-infected embryos was utilized for viral RNA extraction. The 50% embryo infectious dose (EID50) was calculated following the method described by Reed and Muench (1938). To ascertain the purity of the HX isolate, we performed polymerase chain reaction (PCR) analysis targeting the Newcastle disease (NDV), avian influenza (AIV), and infectious laryngotracheitis (ILTV) viruses as potential contaminants. As a template for the PCR amplification of NC group samples, we used sterilized double-distilled water (ddH2O).

Genome Sequencing

RNA was extracted from allantoic fluid using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, CA), followed by RT-PCR using a PrimeScript 1 Step RT-PCR Kit (Takara) in accordance with the manufacturers’ instructions. In order to amplify the entire genome of the HX strain, we used 17 sets of specific overlapping primers targeting the IBV genome, as previously described (Wu et al., 2016). In cases in which pre-existing primers failed to amplify target sequence due to sequence disparities, novel primers were custom-designed based on the genetic sequences of the respective genomic regions. The purified RT-PCR products were subsequently ligated into pMD18-T vector (TaKaRa). Sequencing services were outsourced to Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China). Having obtained sequences of the 17 overlapping segments of the HX strain genome, these were aligned using SeqMan II software (DNAstar Inc., Madison, WI) to assemble the complete genome sequence.

Evolutionary Tree Construction and Whole-Genome Sequence Analysis

Sequences of the S1 gene derived from the HX strain and 109 reference strains were subjected to alignment using the ClustalW multiple alignment algorithm, and were found to be consistent with the previously delineated genotypes (GI–GIX) (Abozeid, 2023). Phylogenetic examination was carried out by employing the neighbor-joining method with 1,000 bootstrap replicates in MEGA (v11.0). Subsequently, we performed analyses of nucleotide and amino acid similarities using BioAider (v 1.532). Furthermore, recombination screening of the entire genome sequences of representative strains, along with the HX, was performed using 7 methodologies (RDP, GENECONV, Bootscan, Chimaera, SiScan, MaxChi, and 3Seq) in RDP4 (v 4.24) to detect the occurrence of potential recombination events.

Prediction, Physicochemical Properties, and Antigenicity Analysis of Potential Linear B-Cell Epitopes

Putative linear B-cell epitopes within the S1 sequence were predicted using 2 servers, namely, BepiPred (with a threshold set at 0.5, https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/) and ABCpred (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/abcpred/) (Salarpour et al., 2020). To ensure inclusion of all conceivable linear epitopes, the length of amino acid sequences was left unconstrained. The salient attributes of these epitopes were scrutinized using IEDB (http://tools.immuneepitope.org/main/html/analysis) to ascertain the most pertinent epitopes with respect to hydrophilicity (Parker hydrophilicity scale, with a threshold set at 1.678(Kumar et al., 2011; Parker et al., 1986), flexibility [Karplus and Schulz algorithm (Li et al., 2003)], accessibility [Emini algorithm(Emini et al., 1985)], and secondary structure using the Chou–Fasman algorithm [CFSSP, www.biogem.org]). The tertiary structure of the S1 protein was predicted using SWISS-MODEL, accessed via the Swiss Institute of Bioinformatics website (https://swissmodel.expasy.org/), with reference to the spike protein of IBV M41 (PDB ID: 6cv0). The protein models thus constructed were visualized using PyMOL (version 1.7.4, LLC).

Pathogenicity Experiment Design

To assess the virulence of the IBV-HX strain in infected chickens, a cohort of 14-day-old Yellow-feathered broilers was divided into 2 cohorts based on random assignment. The cohort designated for infection (n = 20) received a precise inoculation of 100 μL of allantoic fluid containing 103 EID50 of the HX strain via the ocular–nasal route, whereas the negative control cohort was administered an equivalent volume of PBS. Over a span of 14 d postinoculation (dpi), we recorded the rates morbidity and mortality, and conducted daily assessments of clinical manifestations in 10 randomly selected chickens from each cohort. The scoring of clinical symptoms (Tan et al., 2019) was designated as follows: 0: a normal state; 1: mild nasal discharge or lacrimation; 2: symptoms such as depression, watery feces, sneezing, or coughing; 3: severe manifestations, including profound depression, excessive nasal discharge, tracheal rales, or open-mouth breathing; and 4: mortality. At specified time points of 0, 3, 7, and 14 dpi, subsets of 10, 10, 10 and 5 chickens were selected at random from each cohort for weight measurements. Concurrently, oropharyngeal and cloacal swabs were obtained for RT-PCR-based assessments of IBV shedding. Furthermore, a subgroup of 5 chickens from each cohort were selected for examination and sampling, including a comprehensive gross pathological examination, evaluation of tracheal ciliary activity (Huang et al., 2020), and the retrieval of organs (trachea, kidney, thymus, spleen, bursa of Fabricius, liver, glandular stomach, and ovary) for preparation of pathological sections, assessment of organ viral load, and evaluation of immune-related gene expression. These birds were sacrificed via carbon dioxide inhalation followed by exsanguination.

Detection of Immune-Related Gene Expression and Virus Load in Tissues

Tissues harvested at 0, 3, 7, and 14 dpi were used for the extraction of total RNA, followed by reverse transcription using a Prime Script reverse transcription kit (Takara, CA,). qPCR analysis targeting the IBV-N gene and different immune-related genes was performed using a Hieff qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Yeasen, Shanghai, China) in a QuantStudioTM 5 Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific), with the β-actin gene serving as an internal reference gene for standardization. The qPCR cycling protocol included an initial denaturation step at 95°C for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95°C for 10 s and annealing at 60°C for 30 s. The levels of immune-related gene expression relative to those of the control group were quantified using the 2−ΔΔCT method. Absolute quantification PCR was employed to determine the copy number of the IBV-N gene, using the primer pair F: 5ʹ-GGCGACGGTAAAGCAACTG-3ʹ and R: 5ʹ-GCATTCCAGATGACCCTACCTTAG-3ʹ.

Animal and Ethical Statement

All procedures involving animals in this study were conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the institution or practice. The experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee for Animal Experiments of Foshan University.

Statistical Analysis

The experimental data are presented as the means ± standard deviation. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (9.5.1), with comparisons made using a 1-way analysis of variance. Significance levels are indicated as follows: * for P < 0.05; ** for P < 0.01; *** for P < 0.001. Data visualization was accomplished using GraphPad Prism (9.5.1) software.

RESULTS

Virus Isolation and Identification

To induce IBV infection, 10-day-old SPF avian embryos were inoculated with virus via the allantoic cavity. Embryos in the experimental group displayed characteristic manifestations of IBV infection, including stunting, twisting, and a posture in which both talons were in contact with the cranium (Figure 1A). RT-PCR was employed to identify the presence of IBV and potential contaminants, including NDV, AIV, and ILTV (Figure 1B). Analyses confirmed the presence of IBV and an absence of other viral agents (Figure 1B), thereby verifying the successful isolation of a pure strain of IBV (HX). Genetic Relationships of the HX Strain

The HX strain comprises a genome of 27,823 nucleotides, with a gene sequence structure typical of that of IBV strains: 5ʹUTR-1a-1b-S-3a-3b-E-M-5a-5b-N-3ʹUTR. In order to gain a comprehensive understanding of the genetic correlation between HX and other IBV strains, we constructed a phylogenetic tree based on the S1 gene sequences of 109 reference strains, encompassing 9 genotypes and 39 lineages. With respect to the S1 gene sequence, HX is categorized within the GVI-1 branch, a branch that is genetically distinct from those containing other prominent circulating strains in China (Figure 2). We detected no recombination events within the entire HX genome. To gain further insights into the relationship between the HX strain and the currently predominant representative strains in China, we aligned the complete nucleotide and amino acid sequences of HX with those of 7 reference strains (Table 2). The findings revealed that the HX strain has the highest genetic similarity (95.02%) with the GVI-1 genotype strain JX897900. Comparatively, similarities with the currently commonly utilized commercial vaccine strains in China, namely H120 (GI-1), LDT3-A (GI-28), and 4/91 (GI-13), were somewhat lower at 84.17%, 84.17%, and 87.51%, respectively, with corresponding S1 gene nucleotide homologies of 58.49%, 58.80%, and 59.65%, and amino acid homologies of 52.33%, 52.09%, and 53.51%. Furthermore, alignment results indicate that the ORF1ab of HX exhibits a relatively higher nucleotide similarity, ranging from 85.57% to 94.68% (with amino acid similarity of 71.22%–88.39%), with other subtypes, whereas it has only a 56.69% nucleotide similarity and 53.64% amino acid similarity with JX897900.

Potential Linear B-Cell Epitopes

Using the BepiPred and ABCpred algorithms, we predicted the potential linear B-cell epitopes within the S1 protein of the HX strain. Moreover, we used IEDB to conduct a comprehensive analysis of the antigenicity, surface exposure, flexibility, hydrophilicity, and secondary structure of the HX S1 protein (Figures 3A-E). The most promising linear epitopes in the isolated S1 protein of the HX strain were detected at amino acids 57 to 65, 180 to 185, 281 to 295, and 489 to 497, as well as the sequences 54 to 61, 149 to 158, 282 to 292, 340 to 349, and 457 to 464 (Table 2). Given the substantial overlaps between regions 54 to 61 and 57 to 65 and 281 to 295 and 282 to 292, these were amalgamated into the corresponding single regions, 54 to 65 and 281 to 295, respectively. Furthermore, we performed homology modeling of the HX strain S1 protein using SWISS-MODEL, and the S1 protein and predicted antigenic epitope regions were visualized using PyMOL to facilitate identification of the positioning and exposure of the predicted epitopes within the tertiary structure. Our analysis revealed that the anticipated epitope regions were located on the surface of the S1 protein model (Figure 3F), consistent with the predictions of antigenicity, surface exposure, hydrophilicity, and secondary structure. These findings thus provided evidence to indicate that the regions spanning amino acids 54 to 65, 149 to 158, 180 to 185, 281 to 295, 340 to 349, 457 to 464, and 489 to 497 could hold importance for the development of epitope-centric vaccines.

Pathogenicity Identification

Following inoculation, we closely monitored the clinical manifestations of all inoculated chicks. Among the control cohort of birds, we detected no discernible clinical indicators of IBV. Conversely, the infected chicks subsequently developed overt symptoms, including bouts of sneezing and an increased intake of water from 2 dpi. Symptomatic manifestations peaked at 3 dpi, characterized by episodes of coughing, lethargy, and diminished activity (Figure 4A). Notably, within the infected group, 1 chick succumbed to the effects of viral infection on d 9, followed by a further 2 on d 12 and a third on d 14, giving a cumulative mortality of approximately 26.7% (Figure 4B). At 3, 7, and 14 dpi, we assessed birds for impedance of tracheal ciliary activity (Figure 4C), and accordingly found that compared with their uninfected counterparts, chicks infected with the HX strain were characterized by severe tracheal ciliary stasis at 3 and 7 dpi. Furthermore, we recorded the body mass of chicks at 0, 3, 7, and 14 dpi (Figure 4D), which revealed significant reductions in the mean body mass at 7 and 14 dpi compared with the control group birds (P < 0.05 or P < 0.001). Moreover, we assessed the occurrence of viral shedding based on examinations of oral and cloacal swabs at 3, 7, and 14 dpi via RT-PCR, the findings of which revealed an absence of viral shedding in the control birds. Contrastingly, among birds within the infected cohort, all oral swabs tested positive for the virus at 3 and 7 dpi, with an 80% detection rate at 14 dpi (Table 3). Notably, viral shedding was detected in the cloacal swabs taken from chicks at 3 dpi, with all birds testing positive at 7 and 14 dpi.

Gross Dissection and Histopathological Examination

Following sacrifice, the virus-infected and control chicks underwent a thorough anatomical dissection (Figure 5). In contrast to the control birds, in which we detected no obvious anatomical aberrations, those exposed to the HX virus were found to be characterized by pronounced organ deterioration. Aberrations included the presence of yellow mucus in the trachea at 7 dpi, kidneys with a pallid marbled appearance and urate deposits at 14 dpi, hemorrhagic spots in the thymus and spleen at 7 dpi, white nodules on the hepatic surface at 7 dpi, and thickening of the gastric wall and mucosa with hemorrhagic spots at 7 dpi. Histopathological analysis based on hematoxylin and eosin staining (Figure 6) revealed epithelial loss in the trachea at 7 dpi, disintegration of the renal tubular structure at 14 dpi, thymic atrophy and marked lymphocyte reduction with sparse distribution at 7 dpi, splenic atrophy and diminished lymphocytes with loose arrangement at 7 dpi, lymphoid follicle necrosis with inflammatory cell infiltration in the bursa of Fabricius at 7 dpi, cellular disarray with discernible necrotic foci and inflammatory cell infiltration in the liver at 7 dpi, gastric mucosal epithelial cell necrosis and shedding, structural disintegration, and focal ulcer formation in the glandular stomach at 7 dpi, and cortical thinning of the ovary with follicular epithelial cell swelling at 7 dpi.

Detection of Viral RNA in Host Tissues

To evaluate the temporal dynamics of differences in viral load among different tissues, we quantified viral RNA titers in 7 distinct anatomical sites (trachea, kidney, thymus, spleen, bursa of Fabricius, liver, glandular stomach, and ovary) based on RT-PCR (Figure 7). In line with expectations, we detected no discernable evidence of viral presence within tissue specimens obtained from control group chicks. Intriguingly, however, among the virus-infected chicks, we observed a discernible pattern with respect to viral load dynamics, with viral loads in the trachea, spleen, and ovary showing a biphasic course, characterized by a peak at 7 dpi following an initial rise and subsequent decline. Contrastingly, loads in kidney, thymus, bursa of Fabricius, liver, and glandular stomach followed a unimodal distribution, attaining a peak at 3 dpi prior to undergoing a consistent decline.

Differential Expression of Immune-Related Genes

To investigate the immune activation triggered by IBV-HX in the trachea, we assessed the mRNA expression profiles of key immune-related genes, namely, MyD88, MAVS, TLR-7, IFN-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, at 3, 7, and 14 dpi, using RT-PCR (Figure 8). Our findings revealed a notable upregulation of these genes in tracheal tissues compared with that in the control group. Notably, we detected a distinct temporal pattern in the expression of these genes, characterized by an initial rise peaking prominently at 7 dpi, followed by a subsequent decline.

DISCUSSION

IB is a highly communicable disease that results in substantial economic losses within the poultry industry worldwide. Moreover, continual emergence of novel variants poses challenges for the control and prevention of this disease. Different IBV strains have been established to differ considerably with respect to their pathogenicity, virulence, and tissue tropism (Zhao et al., 2019), thereby highlighting the imperative of conducting systematic and comprehensive studies and surveillance of new isolates, to gain at least a reasonable understanding of prevailing transmission patterns and to develop more effective control measures.

In this study, we isolated a novel strain (HX) of IBV, which, on the basis of phylogenetic tree analysis, was identified as a strain within the GVI-1 lineage. Previous reports have suggested that IBV strains of this lineage are generally characterized by low pathogenicity, the exception being the JX181 strain, which can cause damage to the reproductive system and moderate kidney impairment. Comparatively, infection with the majority of GVI-1 strains primarily result in mild respiratory system damage, and normally do not lead to significant kidney or glandular stomach lesions, let alone mortality. Furthermore, infected chickens typically tend to rapidly shed these viruses via the cloaca, with the oral–pharyngeal shedding period generally not exceeding 15 d (Bo et al., 2022; Jang et al., 2018; Sun et al., 2021a). Notably, however, infection with the HX strain examined in the present study resulted in an approximate 26% mortality among infected chicks. Moreover, we established that the HX strain causes extensive organ damage, including that to the trachea, kidneys, thymus, spleen, liver, and glandular stomach, with particularly severe glandular stomach and kidney damage being observed, an outcome that has not been reported in previous descriptions of the pathogenicity of GVI-1 strains. The findings of previous studies have tended to indicate that renal pathogenicity associated with IBV infections, leading to kidney failure, is a significant factor contributing to the increased mortality of infected chickens (Reddy et al., 2015). Accordingly, we speculate that the high mortality associated with HX strain infections may be attributed to the severe kidney damage it induces. Additionally, our analysis of viral shedding revealed a 80% positive rate for oral–pharyngeal swabs and a 100% positive rate for cloacal swabs obtained from infected chickens at 14 dpi, which also differs markedly from the previously reported characteristics of GVI-1 strain infections. These observations would thus tend to indicate that compared with the GVI-1 strains assessed to date, the HX strain is characterized by a heightened pathogenicity and a prolonged period of viral shedding.

The findings of previous studies have also provided evidence to indicate that the viral replicase gene 1ab plays a pivotal role in determining IBV virulence (Li et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2019). Our gene similarity analysis in the present study revealed that compared with the GVI-1 strain JX897900 (56.69% similarity) the ORF1ab of the HX strain has relatively higher nucleotide similarity (85.57%–94.68%) with other GVI-1 strains. We accordingly speculate that this heightened similarity of the HX ORF1ab with that of other strains may be associated with its greater virulence. The innate immune response of chickens to IBV infection differs depending on the pathogenicity, tissue tropism, and population diversity if the infecting strain. Previous studies in this regard have revealed that IBV antigens are initially recognized by 2 independent innate mechanisms, TLR and RIG-1 (Cavanagh et al., 2007; He et al., 2016), followed by the activation of cytokines, such as IFN-1, IFN-γ, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and IL-12, which play antiviral roles (Sun et al., 2021b). In the present study, RT-PCR analysis performed to assess the expression of immune-related genes in infected chickens revealed a significant upregulation of immune-related genes, including IFN-α and IL-1β, in the tracheal tissues of infected chickens, compared with the control group, which is consistent with the findings of previous studies.

The S1 protein is a highly glycosylated type I transmembrane glycoprotein that occurs as a trimer on the surface of virus particles. The protein contains major serum type-specific neutralizing epitopes that are highly conformation-dependent, with alterations in specific amino acid residues in the S1 subunit potentially leading to changes in antigenicity and pathogenicity (Fan et al., 2022). Sequence analysis of the HX strain and the vaccine strains H120, 4/91, and LDT3-A revealed low S1 gene similarities at the nucleotide (58.49%, 58.80%, and 59.65%, respectively) and amino acid (52.33%, 52.09%, and 53.51%, respectively) levels. Notably in this regard, a nucleotide sequence difference of 5% in the S1 gene is generally considered to result in insufficient cross-protection by vaccines (Marandino et al., 2015; Villarreal et al., 2010).

In this study, we employed ABCpred and BepiPred to predict linear B-cell epitopes in the S1 protein of the HX strain, and assessed the surface accessibility, flexibility, hydrophilicity, and secondary structure of these epitopes. Additionally, we used PyMOL to visualize the modeled protein structure, thereby facilitating identification of the configuration and exposure of the predicted epitopes in the tertiary structure. We accordingly identified 7 potential epitopes (amino acids 54–65, 149–158, 180–185, 281–295, 340–349, 457–464, and 489–497) that could be exploited for epitope-based vaccine design. In addition to contributing to the development of vaccines, these findings will provide a basis for further theoretical investigations of the pathogenic mechanisms of the HX strain, as well as antigen–antibody interaction mechanisms.

In summary, in this study, we isolated a new strain of IBV within the GVI-1 lineage. Pathogenicity experiments revealed that compared with other previously reported GVI-1 strains, the novel HX strain is characterized by a longer period of viral shedding and greater virulence, and causes higher mortality among infected individuals. It can cause more extensive tissue damage, including severe lesions in the glandular stomach and kidneys, providing direct evidence of the ability of GVI-1 strains to cause gastrointestinal damage. Additionally, on the basis of bioinformatic analyses, we identified 7 potential epitopes that could be utilized for epitope-based vaccine design, potentially enhancing the design of existing vaccines and contributing to the development of new control strategies for IBV.

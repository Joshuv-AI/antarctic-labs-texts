Prevalence of dengue virus among febrile patients in urban setting of Bamako, Mali
Authors: Yacouba Cissoko, Mountaga Diallo, Hermine Meli, Emmanuel Mberkadji Dingamwal, Ibrehima Guindo, Fousseyni Kané, Dramane Ouedraogo, Bakary Monzon Diarra, Ibrahim Cisse, Daouda Thiero, Bréhima Togola, Demba Koita, Oumar Magassouba, Dramane Sogoba, Mariam Soumaré, Assetou Fofana, Issa Konate, Seydou Doumbia, Sounkalo Dao

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Dengue becomes endemic in Mali when present in both the rainy and dry seasons.•Dengue is an undiagnosed disease compared to malaria.•Sharing social and demographical data related to Dengue in Bamako.

Introduction

Dengue virus (DENV), transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes (A. aegypti and A. albopictus), is one of the most widespread arboviruses in many tropical and subtropical areas of the world, posing a serious public health problem due to its severe forms [1,2]. The increased prevalence and geographic expansion of dengue in recent years have been a challenge because of the limited tools available for prevention and control and the lack of specific antivirals for treatment [3]. The recent spread has been attributed to a variety of factors, including increased urbanization, human movement, and climate change [4]. The burden of dengue fever is high, with around half of the world’s population estimated to be at risk of infection [5] and approximately 100 million symptomatic cases detected every year [[5], [6], [7]]. In Africa, DENV infections remain largely underestimated, but recent outbreaks suggest that substantial parts of the continent may be at risk of increasing dengue transmission [8]. West African countries have seen an increase in the number of sporadic and epidemic dengue fever cases in countries such as Mali, Burkina Faso, and Senegal [[9], [10], [11], [12]]. Of the five DENV serotypes, types one and two predominate in West Africa [10]. Sporadic outbreaks of small magnitude have been reported in 2008 (in Sadiola, Kayes region), and in 2017 and 2019, with 21 suspected cases (in Commune VI of Bamako) [13]. Though a few serological studies have suggested the circulation of DENV in many parts of Mali [9,14,15], dengue remains underdiagnosed because of limited diagnostic capacity and the fact that symptoms are identical to those of endemic malaria. This study assessed the prevalence of dengue fever in community health settings in Bamako during the rainy season in July (corresponding to the high malaria season) and in February during the dry season (corresponding to the low malaria transmission season).

Method

Study site and population

The study was carried out in Bamako, the capital city of Mali, which has an estimated population of 2.93 million (World Population Review, 2023). Bamako is located on the banks of the Niger River, surrounded by hills, and lies within the Sudan Savana zone, characterized by a long dry season from November to May and a rainy season from June to October with annual precipitation reaching 800-1000 mm. Bamako is administratively divided into six communes (sub-districts) and has three university hospitals (four-level). Each commune has a level-two district referral hospital (CSRef) that covers several community health centers (CSCOM), with a total of 63 functional CSCOMs. Each CSCOM is led by a general physician and provides for the primary health care needs of the community. The average population served by each CSCOM is 44,000, with about a dozen serving populations over 90,000. Each CSCOM is overseen by its community association (ASACO). The study was conducted in two of the six communes of Bamako: Commune V (249,727 inhabitants) and Commune VI (600,000 inhabitants). These communes were the epicenters of the dengue outbreak in 2019-2020 in Mali. Four CSCOM representing four ecological settings were selected for dengue surveillance: Daoudabougou (ADASCO) and Sabalibougou I (ASACOSAB I) in Commune V, and Banankabougou and Faladié (ASACOBAFA) and Yirimadio (ASACOYIR) in Commune VI. Commune VI includes both urban and peri-urban quartiers located on the right bank of the Niger River.

Study design, population, and inclusion criteria

Two cross-sectional surveys were carried out during the rainy season (July 2021) and the dry season (February 2022) in the four community health facilities. The inclusion criteria for the study involved all patients aged over 18 months, residents of Commune V or VI, who came to the community health centers with a fever greater than 38.5 degrees Celsius or with a recent history of fever within the past 48 hours. Informed consent was obtained from adults and from the parents or guardians of children prior to inclusion. For each patient, sociodemographic, clinical, and paraclinical characteristics were collected. These variables included age, sex, history of clinical symptoms, and geographical location. Blood samples, including finger-prick samples, were obtained for malaria rapid diagnosis tests (RDTs) and dengue RDTs using AgNS1–immunoglobulin (Ig)M/IgG, as well as reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) for dengue. Dengue infection was defined by a positive result for at least one of the diagnostic tests: RDT for NS1 or IgM antibodies, or RT-PCR.

Sample size

We used the Schwartz formula for sample size calculation. For a 95% confidence level, z = 1.96, and since the prevalence of dengue fever in Mali was unknown, we chose a prevalence of 0.5 following the Real TM method described in 1997, and a margin of error of 8%. This gave a minimum sample size of 150 patients for each season.

Data collection and analysis

After obtaining informed consent or assent from enrolled patients, they were thoroughly examined by the health facility physician. The following biological investigations were conducted: RDT or thick blood smear for malaria, and RDT for NS1 antigen / anti-dengue IgG-IgM. A 5 ml blood sample was collected in EDTA-containing tubes. For dengue, RDTs were performed on-site with a few drops of the collected whole blood; the remaining blood was centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 15 minutes to obtain plasma, which was then transferred to cryopreservation microtubes and stored in a refrigerator. At the end of the survey, all stored plasma samples were placed in a triple-packaged box with cold accumulators and transported to the National Institute of Public Health for RT-PCR testing. The data were collected on case report forms, including sociodemographic, clinical, and biological variables. Data were entered and analyzed using SPSS 22.0 software. The chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test (at a significance level of P ≤ 0.05) was used to compare proportions and determine risk factors for dengue.

Ethical considerations

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Sciences, Techniques and Technologies of Bamako by letter N◦2021/171/CE/USTTB prior to any study procedure. Additional authorization to conduct the study was obtained from the Ministry of Health through the General Directorate of Public Health and Hygiene and the Regional Directorate of Health of Bamako. Informed consent and/or assent (from the guardian if a minor) of the patients or guardians of minors was obtained after an explanation of the aims and significance of the survey. Data confidentiality was ensured by anonymizing and securing patient data, with access restricted to doctors and medical staff involved in the study.

Result

A total of 325 febrile patients were enrolled in the study (150 during the rainy season in July and 175 during the dry season in February). The majority were from Commune VI, accounting for 62.8%. Females were more represented, comprising 66.2% of the patients. More than half were adults, accounting for 57.8%. The median age of the participants was 20 years (interquartile range: 8-30). Primary education was the most common educational level, at 45.2%. Of the 325 febrile patients included in the study, the prevalence of dengue infection was higher in Commune VI (19.2%) compared with Commune V (12.6%) (Figure 1). Among the 325 febrile patients, the prevalence of dengue and malaria was 8.6 % and 65.6%, respectively. Dengue and malaria coinfections were 8.3%, and 17.5% had fever of unknown origin (Figure 2). The logistic regression analysis presented in the figure shows that, among the factors studied, age was the only variable significantly associated with dengue occurrence. Participants aged 18 years and above had a significantly higher likelihood of being infected with dengue compared to children under 5 years old (odds ratio [OR] = 3.2; 95% confidence interval: 1.1-9.4). Discussion

The high prevalence of dengue fever among febrile patients (16.9%) suggests the persistence of dengue in this urban setting of Bamako after the dengue outbreak of 2019, during which the two sites were the epicenter of the epidemic in Bamako. All dengue cases displayed mild clinical features with no severe symptoms. This may be due to prompt care-seeking and access to the community health center.

The proportion of coinfections with DENV and malaria was 8.3%. This result could be explained by the annual transmission of malaria and its endemic nature in Mali. On a continental scale, the prevalence of coinfection with Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus increased significantly, from 0.9% between 2008 and 2013 to 3.8% between 2014 and 2017, and then to 5.5% between 2018 and 2021 (P = 0.0414) [16]. These results indicate that dengue fever is more often associated with malaria in our healthcare systems and that dengue fever is on the rise again in Africa. A high proportion of unknown causes of fever (17.5%) in this study calls for additional investigations into the etiology of febrile illness in urban settings.

The study also shows that, among the variables studied, adult age (≥18 years) is the only factor significantly associated with an increased risk of infection (OR = 3.2; 95% CI: 1.1-9.4), indicating a statistically significant association, though this risk is higher in adults as shown in similar studies [17]. This finding aligns with previous studies, including one from Kenya, which also reported a positive association between advanced age seropositivity (OR: 1.15, 95% CI: 1.10–1.21) [18].

One of the limitations of this study is the lack of data on dengue serotyping. However, the predominance of serotypes 1 and 3 has been reported in Mali and neighboring countries in West Africa [19,20]. The prevalence of DENV in both rainy and dry seasons suggests an increasing endemicity of dengue in Bamako, as supported by the recent surveillance report (Sitrep 2025). Further studies are needed to characterize the circulating serotypes and their significance in the clinical pattern of dengue in Mali.

Conclusion

The prevalence of dengue fever is high among febrile illnesses in Mali. These findings suggest that, in the study population, being an adult (≥18 years) may constitute a potential risk factor for dengue infection. Dengue fever is becoming endemic in Bamako, with prevalence varying according to season and location. Its diagnosis should be considered in febrile cases, even when malaria is suspected. Further studies are needed to determine the different serotypes of dengue in our context and to strengthen national epidemiological surveillance.

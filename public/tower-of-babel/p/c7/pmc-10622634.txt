“From Heart to Toe”: The Lost Stent’s Journey…
Authors: Nikolaos Papagiannis, Maria Moutafi, Dimitrios Afendoulis, Stefanos Garoufalis, Athanasios Kartalis
Published: 2023

A 52-year-old male patient presented to the emergency department complaining of anginal symptoms the previous days. We proceeded to coronary angiography which revealed a 90% stenosis of the proximal left anterior descending artery and angioplasty was planned via left radial artery. A 6Fr JL4 guide catheter was used to engage the ostium of the left main artery. After predilation with a 2.5 × 12 mm semi-compliant balloon, a 3.5 × 18 mm drug-eluting stent (DES) was delivered easily to the culprit lesion. During the inflation of the balloon stent system the patient pulled his arm away unintentionally due to vigorous cough resulting in disengagement of guide catheter from the left main ostium and loss of the guidewire and deployed stent (Figure 1A). The subsequent image displayed the dislodged stent in the left main artery (Figure 1B, red arrow). Rewiring is the key procedure in order to achieve a successful retrieval of the dislodged stent. We reengaged the left main ostium in order to inflate a small balloon after the dislodged stent and retrieve the whole system enbloc. Thus, we advanced a workhorse wire distally to the left anterior descending and since coronary imaging techniques are not still available in our cathlab we thought to advance a 2.0 × 15 semi compliant balloon to help us confirm the proper position of the wire. Balloon could not be advanced since it was stacked at the proximal edge of stent. Combined with the fluoroscopy we confirmed the course of the wire beneath the dislodged stent. (Figure 1C, red arrow). During the above manoeuvres the stent prolapsed probably in the ascending aorta since it could no more be displayed during fluoroscopy. Subsequently, a 3.5 × 18 DES was deployed to the culprit lesion. Assuming stent’s journey, fluoroscopy identified it being dislodged in the left femoral artery (Figure 1D, red arrow). Conservative approach was selected, as it is considered acceptable option in cases of stents loss below the common femoral artery according to PCR recommendations.1),2) Three months later he remains asymptomatic and stable.

Prolapse and dislocation of catheter, guidewire and deployed stent. (A) Prolapse of guide catheter, guidewire and deployed stent due to vigorous cough during the inflation of the balloon stent system. (B) Position of the dislodged stent in the left main artery (red arrow). (C) Position of guide catheter and guidewire below the stent in the left main artery (red arrow). (D) Position of the dislodged stent in the left femoral artery, as fluoroscopy identified it (red arrow).

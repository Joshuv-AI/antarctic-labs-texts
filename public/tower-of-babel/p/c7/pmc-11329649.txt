Chaotic self-adaptive sine cosine multi-objective optimization algorithm to solve microgrid optimal energy scheduling problems
Authors: N. Karthik, Arul Rajagopalan, Mohit Bajaj, Palash Medhi, R. Kanimozhi, Vojtech Blazek, Lukas Prokop
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Researchers are increasingly focusing on renewable energy due to its high reliability, energy independence, efficiency, and environmental benefits. This paper introduces a novel multi-objective framework for the short-term scheduling of microgrids (MGs), which addresses the conflicting objectives of minimizing operating expenses and reducing pollution emissions. The core contribution is the development of the Chaotic Self-Adaptive Sine Cosine Algorithm (CSASCA). This algorithm generates Pareto optimal solutions simultaneously, effectively balancing cost reduction and emission mitigation. The problem is formulated as a complex multi-objective optimization task with goals of cost reduction and environmental protection. To enhance decision-making within the algorithm, fuzzy logic is incorporated. The performance of CSASCA is evaluated across three scenarios: (1) PV and wind units operating at full power, (2) all units operating within specified limits with unrestricted utility power exchange, and (3) microgrid operation using only non-zero-emission energy sources. This third scenario highlights the algorithm’s efficacy in a challenging context not covered in prior research. Simulation results from these scenarios are compared with traditional Sine Cosine Algorithm (SCA) and other recent optimization methods using three test examples. The innovation of CSASCA lies in its chaotic self-adaptive mechanisms, which significantly enhance optimization performance. The integration of these mechanisms results in superior solutions for operation cost, emissions, and execution time. Specifically, CSASCA achieves optimal values of 590.45 €ct for cost and 337.28 kg for emissions in the first scenario, 98.203 €ct for cost and 406.204 kg for emissions in the second scenario, and 95.38 €ct for cost and 982.173 kg for emissions in the third scenario. Overall, CSASCA outperforms traditional SCA by offering enhanced exploration, improved convergence, effective constraint handling, and reduced parameter sensitivity, making it a powerful tool for solving multi-objective optimization problems like microgrid scheduling.

Introduction

One of the biggest challenges in microgrid (MG) management is finding the optimal way to operate the system while accounting for numerous constraints, using different optimization techniques, and dealing with non-convex and non-linear environments. The size and scale of the microgrid also play a role in finding the optimal solution, making it a complex and vital optimization problem. This solution is to be found for a selected objective function that meets the constraints of generation, operation, transmission, and demand-generation balance1–3. Microgrid energy scheduling is a critical area of research aimed at enhancing energy efficiency, reducing operational costs, and minimizing environmental impacts4,5. Various optimization techniques have been developed to address the multi-objective nature of this problem, which involves balancing cost reduction and emission mitigation6. The proposed algorithm should be proven for its performance in incurring minimum operation cost and emission. This is done by comparing the proposed algorithm against other optimization algorithms. Various algorithms have been proposed to handle renewable generation scheduling in microgrids in the event of load uncertainty. Many benefits are available in RES, especially when incorporated within MG. In the earlier study, the researchers modified the Chaos Grasshopper Algorithm to ensure optimal power-sharing among distributed energy resources such as fuel cells, PV plants, and battery energy storage systems within the MG framework7. However, the algorithm’s robustness to uncertainties, disturbances, and noisy data in the micro-grid environment was not thoroughly investigated. A smart grid is an effective next-gen solution for today’s drastically increasing power demands. The literature has attempted to resolve the sustainable smart grid challenge by considering three necessary sustainability dimensions: society, environment, and economy8. On the other hand, a detailed analysis of the multi-objective wind-driven optimization (MOWDO) algorithm’s convergence speed, stability, and parameter sensitivity was not presented in8. With this introduction, an intelligent grid framework is also encountered for Smart Building (SB) energy management systems in recent times9. The previous study ignored several critical constraints in its modeling and optimization, including network, line loss, and start/stop constraints.

In recent times, Electric Vehicles (EVs) have been in huge demand since environmental pollution and its successive effects have become a massive concern for humankind. Both EVs and plug-in hybrid EVs have already been integrated with MG. Plug-in EVs, as well as other distributed generations, exert a significant influence on the MG multi-objective optimization energy management model10. An energy storage system (ESS) ensures a power balance that aligns with load demands. The microgrid’s Energy Management System (EMS), combined with battery and hydrogen ESS, intends to enhance the outcome of microgrids from technical and economic perspectives. It is possible to increase the overall flexibility of microgrids by introducing EMS. In this scenario, the energy generation system has shifted its focus towards the Hybrid Renewable Energy System (HRES), owing to the near exhaustion of fossil fuels. Thus, HRES is a better alternative to traditional fossil-fuel-based energy sources. In the study conducted earlier11, an energy management system (EMS) based on fuzzy logic was implemented for a grid-connected residential direct current microgrid (DC-MG) featuring hybrid energy storage systems. Yet, using the design technique to forecast power and cost variables and their related uncertainties to tackle the multi-objective energy management challenge in a microgrid was not executed.

The operation of microgrids has experienced a remarkable transformation thanks to the extensive utilization of renewable resources, the adoption of cutting-edge energy management methods, and the integration of Demand Response Programs (DRPs) into microgrids and similar dynamic distribution networks. Consequently, as suggested by the literature12, there is a critical need to thoroughly investigate energy management from a comprehensive, holistic, and all-encompassing perspective. This approach is essential for achieving the optimal operation of microgrids and facilitating an active and influential role for distribution grids in advanced environments, thereby enabling the distribution network to attain an optimal state.

Artificial intelligence (AI) methods for intelligent energy management with linear programming-based multi-objective optimization have been developed. The proposed multi-objective intelligent energy management (MIEM) seeks to reduce the system’s environmental effects and operating expenses. In this scenario, both load demand and the availability of renewable energy sources in the future are considered pre-operational variables.

An EMS has been designed to optimize microgrid performance using a hybrid algorithm combining crow search and Jaya algorithms13. The constraint is its exclusive concentration on operational costs as the only objective function, neglecting emissions considerations. Moreover, it does not incorporate multi-objective energy management strategies. The ongoing developments made in MG technology favor easy access to distributed energy. At first, a dual-layer optimal dispatching model was proposed14 based on demand response in MG after considering the demand- and power-side requirements. The first-layer optimization has an objective to meet, i.e., to execute load curve optimization and attain the maximum load satisfaction. When one attempts to bring sustainability to renewable MG systems, they tend to experience multi-objective and multi-constraint problems. So, it becomes mandatory to have a holistic approach for the optimal operation of these hybrid energy systems since the data generated from different measuring systems under different MG components is massive. Contemporary power electronic systems have enabled the establishment of alternating current (AC) to DC-MG by seamlessly integrating DC-MG into conventional AC grids15. Due to the increasing penetration of MG, cost-effective and sustainable operations are required mandatorily. In this scenario, it is essential to measure the loss of power from energy conversion devices to enhance the efficiency and dispatch the practical MG. The researchers introduced an Optimal Energy Management (OEM) approach, harnessing intelligent optimization techniques to attain an optimal hybrid heat and power-isolated MG. Based on the findings in the literature16, they employed a modified adaptive differential evolution algorithm to address the Optimal Power Flow (OPF) problem within AC–DC MG networks. The considered objectives included the overall generation cost of both AC and DC networks and the cost related to the active power transfer from the AC grid to the DC MG. Simulations were conducted using IEEE 30-bus and IEEE 39-bus test systems to evaluate the system, wherein 6-bus and 9-bus DC MG were interconnected. The study tested the system regarding normal conditions and network emergencies.

The researchers presented an overview of microgrids earlier17 in which they also discussed modeling the microgrid with the help of original environmental data. Then, the study also observed the challenges microgrids face in addition to power quality issues. Further, the researchers reviewed the methods to overcome such challenges by leveraging control techniques, devices, and algorithms. An optimal operation model for MG utilizing multi-agent systems was introduced18. The MG test system included a combination of Micro-Turbine (MT) with Fuel Cell (FC) acting as the Combined Heat and Power (CHP) unit, Rubbish Incinerator (RI), Wind Turbine (WT), boiler, reformer, anaerobic digestion, alongside electrical, thermal, and hydrogen storage units, designed to cater to both electrical and thermal loads. The earlier study19 proposed a first-of-its-kind optimization model for an MG environment with a day-ahead economic dispatch of flexible resources. The study considered load demand fluctuations as well as PV uncertainties. MG was introduced as a concept in which the Distributed Generators (DGs) are used near load points. However, wind and solar-based power generation and electricity load requirements are stochastic. Implementing an EMS is paramount to ensure precise hourly power management within microgrids, guaranteeing an optimal supply by demand. In reference20, a comprehensive optimization framework was presented, addressing three critical objectives for MG energy management while considering Demand Response (DR) programs and smart homes. The study utilized an 83-bus distribution system incorporating 11 microgrids to validate this model, accounting for load demand fluctuations and the inherent uncertainties in Renewable Energy Sources (RESs) output. Additionally, literature21 introduced an energy management system to reduce users’ Energy Consumption Costs (ECC). This research employed a fuzzy-based peer-to-peer (P2P) energy exchange algorithm, enabling dynamic pricing to determine the surplus energy’s allocation to the grid and MG. Moreover, the investigation delved into Energy Storage System (ESS) charging/discharging control and devised strategies for diesel generator activation to address power shortfalls within the MG.

In the literature, a multi-objective optimal load dispatch model was proposed for MG22, in which stochastic access for EVs was considered. Monte Carlo simulation was used to model the uncertainties experienced in EVs. In most energy management systems and advanced power grids, it has become a phenomenon to consider various factors for optimal power generation, such as reduction of pollutant emissions, optimal utilization of RES such as solar & wind, and mitigation of costs incurred. When all three challenges are considered single entities, the results would be promising and optimal. In23, a robust energy management approach for a microgrid was showcased, accounting for diverse scenarios and balancing economic and environmental concerns. The Slim Mould Algorithm was employed to derive the Pareto-optimal solutions for operational cost and emissions across three distinct scenarios for the microgrid.

Previously, in their earlier research24, the investigators examined the optimal allocation of heat and power in a Fuel Cell (FC) integrated with a Combined Heat and Power MG operating in a grid-connected mode while also considering the presence of a DRP. Due to its robust nature, easy accessibility, and reliability, hybrid AC/DC microgrids have gained immense attention recently. It resolves multi-faceted economic, environmental, operations, and power-related issues. In25, the authors comprehensively reviewed the basics, components, and the increased need for hybrid AC/DC MG. The review compared varying generations of RES and mathematical models. Today’s power grids are increasingly migrating towards RES due to the depletion of fossil fuel reserves and environmental concerns international societies have raised about using such traditional power production systems.

In a previous study26, researchers introduced a two-stage multi-objective optimization framework for optimizing energy management in multi-microgrid systems (MMG). This model considered factors such as greenhouse gas emissions, total cost, and Energy Not Supplied (ENS) to determine the optimal MMG system plan. Additionally, the study employed a fusion of lexicography-compromise Programming (CP) within a two-stage technique to handle homogeneous objective functions. In a different investigation27, the researchers aimed to achieve optimal energy management in a grid-connected multi-energy MG. They proposed a novel relative regret decision-making strategy, addressing multiple uncertainties and incorporating demand response for thermal loads, enhancing the traditional MG energy management optimization model. Another study28 presented a two-level energy management strategy to optimize operational costs within islanded and networked microgrids while accounting for power shortages and surpluses. This hybrid energy scheduling method identified transactive energy exchanges among microgrids. Furthermore, researchers in the literature29 proposed and validated an energy management scheme based on Mimosa pudica plants to optimize MG scheduling. This approach aimed to reduce production costs in system constraints by balancing power between the utility and distributed resources, adhering to standard communication protocols.

In30, the enhanced Leader Artificial Rabbits Optimization Algorithm was implemented for efficient microgrid load management. The study utilized a hybrid setup comprising photovoltaic, diesel, wind, and battery components. The findings indicated that this approach resulted in the optimal investment cost. In31, a review was conducted on various methods and frameworks for optimizing the functioning of distributed energy resources under uncertain and variable conditions. The comprehensive literature review covered adaptive controllers, probabilistic approaches, stochastic methods, and resilient optimization techniques. In32, a study explored various off-grid renewable power system configurations, including batteries and hydrogen as energy storage options, to determine the most economically viable setup for remote areas. The analysis included the Nickel–Iron (Ni–Fe) battery and considered electrolysis technology for hydrogen production. Two Integrated Hybrid Renewable Energy System (IHRES) configurations were modeled and evaluated: PV/Diesel Generator (DG)/Battery (Ni–Fe) and PV/Wind Turbines (WT)/DG/Hydrogen Storage System (HSS). The study employed a cycle charging (CC) strategy. Quadratic Interpolation-based Artificial Rabbits Optimization (QIARO) was introduced to optimize the sizing of system components, ensuring cost-effective and reliable fulfillment of load demands. In33, a stochastic programming model was developed to manage energy from multiple renewable sources using a multi-objective improved slime mould algorithm (MOISMA). Hong’s (2m + 1) point estimate method was employed to generate and control the uncertainty of wind speed and solar irradiance patterns. In34, a study successfully analyzed an optimal energy management scheme using the slime mold algorithm. The primary focus was on reducing generation costs and pollution, which resulted in lower energy bills for customers. To address the probabilistic nature of wind and solar power, a proposed demand response program was implemented to evaluate and manage the uncertainty characteristics of these energy sources, thereby achieving the study’s objectives. An overview of energy management systems in networked microgrids (NMGs) was presented in35, covering system architecture, optimization algorithms, control strategies, and the integration of distributed energy resources. The study highlighted the challenges and opportunities associated with energy management in NMGs and presented key approaches and technologies adopted in the field. Additionally, it explored the implications of emerging trends such as data-driven modeling, machine learning, and advanced communication technologies on the design and performance of energy management systems in NMGs. In our earlier study, stochastic energy management and scheduling of a renewable microgrid involving diverse energy sources and dynamic storage was performed36. This was done considering uncertainties in energy resources and demand, as well as demand response (DR), using the two-point estimation method (2m + 1 PEM). The three-dimensional objective function aimed to maximize the renewable hosting capacity while minimizing both the operation cost and emission cost. A techno-economic-environmental energy scheduling framework for multi-energy microgrid systems was introduced in37. The proposed model integrates the electrical, heating, cooling, and water sectors to improve the flexibility and reliability of the microgrid system. A multi-layer energy scheduling approach was employed to optimize operating costs, carbon emissions, groundwater extraction, and the independence of the multi-energy system.

This paper demonstrates energy management using the MG test system featuring various components, including a wind turbine, solar photovoltaics (PV), fuel cell, microturbine, and battery. The current study considers two objective functions: the operation cost incurred by the MG system and the emission. A novel optimization algorithm can mitigate both of these entities.

Problem formulation

Achieving optimal operation within a microgrid can be realized through a multi-objective optimization framework56,57. In this context, the primary goal of multi-objective energy management in a standard MG is to determine the optimal power generation set points and the appropriate ON or OFF states for distributed generation units. This allocation should occur in a manner that minimizes both the microgrid’s operating cost and the net pollutants emissions within the grid, all while adhering to specified equality and inequality constraints58,59. In this study, we put forth a precise mathematical model designed for short-term energy management systems to reduce operational costs and mitigate pollution emissions within the MG.

Objective functions

Multi-objective optimization of cost and emission in a grid-connected MG is necessary to balance economic efficiency, environmental sustainability, regulatory compliance, and social responsibility60,61. By simultaneously addressing these objectives, microgrid operators can optimize their operations to maximize benefits for stakeholders and society62. Operation and pollution emission costs are two objective functions considered in this study.

Mitigation of operating cost

The objective function of operational expenses is a cornerstone, pivotal in shaping the effectiveness and efficiency of energy management strategies. The operational cost is critical in ensuring microgrid operations’ economic viability and sustainability63. The objective function of operational costs is to enhance microgrid systems’ resilience and stability. Mitigation of emission

The objective function for emissions serves as a cornerstone for assessing the environmental impact of MG operations65,66. MG emits pollutants due to different components, such as the grid, generating units, and energy-reserving resources67,68. Quantitating the emissions of pollutants such as CO2, SO2, and NOx provides a comprehensive metric for evaluating the ecological footprint of energy generation and consumption within the MG69,70. This is particularly important in combating climate change and mitigating air pollution, as it enables stakeholders to monitor and reduce the environmental consequences of energy production71. The objective function for emissions plays a crucial role in aligning energy management strategies with regulatory standards and sustainability goals. Incorporating emissions considerations into the optimization process facilitates compliance with emissions regulations and fosters a proactive approach to environmental stewardship72,73. This helps MG operators avoid penalties and regulatory hurdles and positions them as leaders in promoting clean energy practices. Moreover, the objective function for emissions enhances MG systems’ overall efficiency and resilience. Optimizing energy management strategies to minimize emissions while meeting operational requirements promotes the adoption of cleaner and more sustainable technologies74,75. Microgrid modelling

Applying individual DG tends to bring too many challenges, so it is better to unleash the DG’s potential by following a system approach. This approach considers generation and associated loads as either part of a microgrid or a subsystem. Alternatively, one can make informed decisions to optimize the system’s performance by consolidating DGs into microgrids and harnessing RES for economic, environmental, and technological benefits. Distributed generation encompasses a variety of advanced prime mover technologies, including wind power, internal combustion (IC) engines, gas turbines, photovoltaic systems, microturbines, and fuel cells. These modern technologies emit fewer pollutants and are more cost-effective than conventional alternatives96. For instance, fuel cells produce electricity from hydrogen and oxygen, emitting only water vapor as a byproduct. However, some NOx and CO2 emissions may occur while reforming natural gas or other fuels. Comparatively, fuel cells’ efficiency is higher than that of microturbines, which ensures low emissions. However, the cost incurred by fuel cells is too high. In current research work, a typical LV microgrid is considered in line with the literature97. Within this microgrid, various DGs are employed, including photovoltaic (PV) systems, microturbines (MT), low-temperature fuel cells (PAFC), wind turbines (WT), and energy storage devices like lead-acid batteries. The authors assumed that all DG sources operate by generating active power at a unity power factor and do not require or produce reactive power. A power exchange link exists between the utility used for energy trading, i.e., the LV network and the microgrid under study. The operation of this link varies at different times due to decisions made by the Micro-Grid Central Controller (MGCC).

Microgrid test system description

The present investigation focused on a microgrid testing system, which encompasses a distribution network and a variety of DGs, including FC, WT, PV, MT, and a battery system, as outlined in reference96,98–103, The study addressed two vital objective functions: the net emissions of pollutants and the total cost incurred by the MG, encompassing power generation costs, start-up/shut-down unit expenses, and more104,105. The research considered the daily load demand of a typical residential area where the MG serves regularly. According to the findings in the literature97, this included an industrial feeder servicing a small workshop and a feeder serving mild commercial customers, resulting in a total daily energy demand of 1695 kWh. Moreover, the study incorporated real-time variations in energy market prices, as shown by previous research63. To facilitate flexible MG operations during power dispatch problems, the optimization algorithm determined the ON and OFF states of three DG units, namely MT, PV, and WT, while keeping the utility in the ON state at all times since the MG operated in grid-connected mode.

The author deliberately chose to keep the respective units in the ON state to comprehensively evaluate the fuel cell and battery’s impact on grid operations and optimize resource utilization. Discussion

The population-based nature of CSASCA allows it to explore multiple solutions simultaneously, significantly reducing the likelihood of getting trapped in local optima. The use of chaotic maps for initializing the population ensures a diverse set of starting points, which contributes to a more efficient search process. Optimization results demonstrate that CSASCA achieves comparable or better results in less computational time than traditional methods like GA, PSO, Krill Herd, GWO, WOA, FSAPSO, and SCA. CSASCA effectively handles multiple conflicting objectives, providing a set of Pareto-optimal solutions that offer trade-offs between cost and emissions. The case studies conducted (e.g., scenarios with maximum renewable generation, unrestricted power interchange, and operation without renewables) highlight the algorithm’s adaptability and effectiveness in various complex scenarios. Statistical analysis shows that CSASCA outperforms other algorithms in terms of both cost minimization and emission reduction, demonstrating its superior effectiveness. The solutions obtained by CSASCA are consistently of high quality, with lower mean and maximum operation costs and emissions compared to other algorithms. The algorithm’s ability to provide accurate scheduling decisions under different operational constraints and scenarios underscores its practical utility in real-world microgrid management. In conclusion, the CSASCA algorithm proves to be an efficient, effective, and accurate tool for solving the multi-objective optimal scheduling problems in microgrids. Its innovative use of chaotic self-adaptive mechanisms enhances performance across various metrics, making it a valuable contribution to the field of energy optimization.

Conclusion

The present study introduced an innovative CSASCA algorithm to address the multi-objective energy management problem within a 24-h microgrid context. The research explored three distinct scenarios to evaluate the proposed algorithm’s effectiveness compared to conventional SCA and other optimization techniques. The authors collected and compared the simulations of every scenario taken for the study. In the initial scenario, the CSASCA algorithm, designed for single-objective optimization, successfully attained the optimal solutions for both cost and emissions, achieving 590.45 €ct and 337.28 kg, respectively, within an acceptable time frame of 73.14 and 73.76 s. These results outperformed those of other reported approaches. Moving to the second scenario, the CSASCA algorithm again delivered superior outcomes, yielding optimal cost and emission values of 98.203 €ct and 406.204 kg, respectively, achieved within acceptable computational times of 88.84 and 75.82 s. Finally, in the third scenario, the CSASCA algorithm continued its success, securing optimal cost and emission values of 95.38 €ct and 982.173 kg, respectively, within acceptable computational times of 88.15 and 1102.97 s. On the other hand, the CSASCA algorithm successfully optimized both cost and emissions within a multi-objective framework. In the initial case, it achieved optimal values of €669.95 and 434.59 kg for operational cost and emissions, respectively. Transitioning to the second scenario, the CSASCA algorithm produced improved outcomes, with optimal operational cost and emissions of €162.69 and 563.83 kg, respectively. In the third scenario, the CSASCA algorithm reached optimal operational cost and emissions of €145.51 and 1101.97 kg, respectively. The study findings infer a considerable effect on grid operation, in terms of emission objective, with heavy penetration of RES. However, the total cost incurred in microgrids increased tremendously during the first scenario. Optimization results reveal a considerable increase in emission and reduction in cost in the third scenario in which the energy sources operated are not emission-free. After analyzing the numerical results, it has been determined that the proposed method is highly robust, concurrent, stable, and capable of providing an exceptional near-global solution by incorporating a chaotic self-adaptive approach with the conventional SCA. Moreover, the proposed approach yielded an evenly distributed set of Pareto-optimal solutions in the real domain. Furthermore, the proposed method required a computational time almost equal to that of the traditional SCA. At the same time, it produced better solutions than other optimization algorithms from economic and environmental perspectives. In the future, the CSASCA algorithm, as proposed, can be employed to elucidate the multi-objective dynamic economic emission dispatch problem, incorporating aspects like electric vehicle integration and renewable energy sources. Additionally, an extension of the current research could involve assessing the algorithm’s performance in addressing microgrid energy management problems while considering implementing an incentive-based demand response program.

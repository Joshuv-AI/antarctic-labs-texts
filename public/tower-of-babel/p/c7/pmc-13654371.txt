Ankle injury rehabilitation and place of immersive virtual reality as digital health solutions in rehabilitation: A clinical randomized controlled trial
Authors: Mesfin Beharu Deme, Anna Vladimirovna Gracheva, Victoria Aslanbekovna Badtieva
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe ankle is the main interface between the ground and body during ambulation, standing, and any sports exercise. Ankles should be able to absorb impact-loading forces from body weight and exercise pressure, adapt to uneven ground, and allow efficient propulsion. The estimated lifetime incidence of ankle injuries is 1 out of every 10 people. Most ankle injuries occur during sports activities. The epidemiologic trends associated with chronic ankle instability suggest that current rehabilitation approaches may be inadequate. Therefore, it is uttermost important to seek alternative rehabilitation modalities, as the current conventional rehabilitation is not effective.MethodsThis retrospective study utilized a single-blinded 3-arm parallel-randomized controlled clinical trial to assess the efficacy of various rehabilitation modalities for ankle injuries. Seventy-five participants with ankle injuries were randomly assigned to 1 of 3 groups: a control group receiving standard rehabilitation, a virtual reality (VR) group utilizing VR technology, and a mixed group combining VR with kinesiotape interventions. Each group included 25 participants who underwent 2 weeks of rehabilitation activities specific to their assigned treatment. The single-blinded design ensured that participants were unaware of their treatment assignment, minimizing bias in their responses. This methodology facilitates a comparative analysis of rehabilitation strategies, offering insights into the effectiveness of innovative approaches such as VR and kinesiotaping for ankle injury recovery. The R statistical software was used to complete the analysis. One-way ANOVA analysis was utilized to compare the statistical differences between the 3 groups.ResultsSeventy-five ankle injury patients were recruited, and 96% of them finished the study. The ANOVA results show that ankle injury rehabilitation via VR and/or kinesiotape application had significant effects on reducing ankle pain, ankle edema, and anthropometric measurements compared with the control group. The VR treatment modality has positive effects on reducing the distal edema limb circumference compared to both control and mixed groups at p < 0.05.ConclusionPhysical rehabilitation via immersive VR and/or kinesiotape application is considered the main rehabilitation modality for ankle injury compared to conventional physical rehabilitation.

Introduction

Humans’ ankles play a significant role in balancing body weight and load, and in allowing equal momentum during standing, daily activities, and exercise, along with the human foot. Due to the nature of its tasks, the ankle is one of the most frequently traumatized musculoskeletal body sites, especially among teenagers, young adults, and athletes.1,2

The estimated lifetime incidence of ankle injuries (sprain) in the U.S. is 1 out of every 10 people. Most ankle injuries occur during sports activity, which accounts for 10% – 30% of all sports injuries. The joint complex of foot and ankle injuries accounts for around 1/3 of total running injuries sustained by track athletes. In the Netherlands, more than half a million people annually sustain a traumatic injury of the ankle. Sports activities share 38.5% of annual traumatic ankle injuries in the Netherlands. Around 50% of ankle injury patients receive medical treatment, and 40% of them end up with chronic instability.3,4

Ankle injury is more common among men aged 15 – 24 years than their counterparts. However, women older than 30 years are more likely than men to sprain their ankles. Due to overdoing athletic exercise, numerous deep-down and external threat factors like systemic infection, increased age, body composition, sex, and body movement play pivotal roles in sustaining ankle injuries.5,6

In 2015, a stress fracture of the foot and ankle article revealed the prevalence of foot and ankle fractures due to pressure as the most common injury among young sportsmen/women and people. Both among general and physically active populations, lateral ankle ligament sprain is the most common ankle injury. On average, every day, about 25,000, 6000 and 5600 ankle injury cases are reported in the U.S., France, and the UK, respectively. Ankle sprain injury can happen to every part of the population, but it is common among younger age and athletic parts of the population, which account for up to 1/3 of overall injury reports. Ankle sprain injury mainly occurs due to overuse of the ankle joint, misuse of physical fitness activities, stepping at an angle, and on an uneven surface.6, 7, 8

The ankle injury mainly occurs among 15 – 19 years old youngster with an annual peak incidence of 7.2 per 1000 people among teenagers and young adults. While the overall incidence of lower extremity injuries among long-distance runners varied from 19.4% to 79.3%, these ankle injuries contribute 9.0% to 32.2%.9,10

Worldwide, it overburdens emergency departments and primary health care providers. It leads to significant time lost due to injury, leads to a huge medical and socioeconomic problem, and results in long-term disability. The total costs of treatment and work absenteeism due to ankle injuries are high. In 2022, the annual estimated health expenditure for acute ankle injury and its treatment reached 2 billion U.S. dollars. Ankle injuries account for more than 33% of the total costs of treatment due to sports injuries.9,11

The use of Kinesio® tape (KT) in the rehabilitation program increases to achieve a reduction of ankle inversion during loading response, while not affecting the same motion during pre-swing. The elastic nature and stretching ability of KT are seen as advantages for the recovery of ankle fracture patients.12,13 The demand for medical rehabilitation care is increasing, but not all care can be provided, due to a shortage of rehabilitation staff financial resources and the limited applicability of the care.14 The effectiveness of physical medicine rehabilitation therapy lacks quantifying evidence, and burnout without quantifiable performance and feedback over a long period may be tedious and able to reduce training engagement of patients.15 The epidemiologic trends associated with chronic ankle instability suggest that current rehabilitation approaches may be inadequate.16 Virtual reality (VR) technology, comprising performance-derived feedback to create interactive simulated environments similar to real-world experience to enhance the user's motivation, helps the recovery of ankle injury patients get more and more attention.17

To reduce the pressure on the health care system, the World Health Organization states that managing disability should be a priority in health care through rehabilitation programs, one known as physical rehabilitation (the repetition of exercises and their gradual complications frustrate and demotivate the engagement of patients in the rehabilitation exercise). Nowadays, due to reduced motivation and the slow progress of physical rehabilitation, VR for rehabilitation activities is attracting more and more attention. Therefore, it is of the utmost importance to determine the role and place of VR technologies separately and, in conjunction with KT in medical rehabilitation, to study its effectiveness in comparison to usual physical rehabilitation methods.

Methods

Seventy-five patients with ankle injuries of less than 1-year duration were recruited for this study. Each group included 25 participants (Fig. 1) who underwent 2 weeks of rehabilitation activities specifically designed for the treatment group they belonged to. A single-blinded, individually randomized controlled clinical 3-arm parallel group study design was employed. Ankle injury patients were randomly divided into 3 clinical treatment modality groups (control group, VR group, and mixed group (VR and KT)) (Fig. 1). The groups did not significantly differ in gender, age, and clinical history. Ankle injury patients who had an injury less than a year ago, who visited branch No. 1 of the Moscow Scientific and Practical Center for Medical Rehabilitation and Sports Medicine clinic with referral letters for rehabilitation, were recruited for the study.Fig. 1Schematic representation of study design.VR: virtual reality; KT: kinesiology tape.

After recruitment, if the patients develop seizures, loss of awareness, eyestrain, eye or muscle twitching, involuntary movements, altered/blurred/double vision, dizziness, apathy, hyperhidrosis, fatigue, somnolence, disorientation, impaired balance, impaired hand and eye movements, immediately discontinue therapy. To discontinue the therapy signs in addition to the patients’ full right to discontinue study, if the patients develop at least one of the above signs and symptoms after starting the therapy, the therapy is automatically discontinued.

Rehabilitation interventions

Each patient was randomized to 1 of the 3 rehabilitation groups: control group (group I: only received regular physical therapy rehabilitations (massaging, physical therapy exercise)); intervention group (group II: received usual physical therapy rehabilitations and immersive VR solutions); complex group (group III: received a combination of usual physical therapy, head-mounted virtual reality solution, and KT application). A concealed cluster randomization for each site to allow blinding of the investigator responsible for data analysis. Before any rehabilitation intervention sessions were conducted, specific instructions and training were given to the study personnel responsible for administration and supervision at each clinical site. This training included oral instructions with physical demonstrations, as well as written instructions that could be referenced to ensure proper technique. Periodic meetings were held with the rehabilitation administrators to ensure consistency in the delivery of the programs. After the pretest assessment was completed, patients reported to their respective therapeutic training facility for the 12 rehabilitation intervention sessions. They are required to complete all 12 sessions in 2 weeks: no less than 1 session per day for therapy assigned to his/her group and no more than 7 sessions per week, irrespective of the group they are assigned. A study investigator tracked each patient's completion and documented both progress and setbacks.

Kinesiotaping manipulation technique

The patient is in a supine position, with the legs fully extended. Before the procedure, the leg must be shaved. The entire length of the leg is wiped with an alcohol solution. The KT was applied only to the affected limb, for 48 − 72 h after patients finalized the massaging session.

This study used kinesiology tape with elasticity. Ankle eversion taping is posterior talar gliding taping to increase the dorsiflexion of the ankle. This taping sets the patient's ankle in a slightly dorsiflexed state and begins from the front part of the talus, passes around both sides of the malleolus, and wraps up the calcaneus.18

The taping area should be clear of hair and wiped with alcohol before taping. Each participant received the following treatments randomly: real taping—KT (2.5 cm) with 50% tension.19 To maintain the consistency of the KT tension applied to the ankle joint, the ankle length was considered when calculating the KT length.20

Methods of VR solution

VR-based technologies have been demonstrated to be a valid tool to increase patient engagement across a wide number of diseases.21 In patients with stroke and Parkinson's disease, VR systems have shown promising results for gait and balance recovery.22 An updated Cochrane review has also found a beneficial effect of VR in improving upper limb function and daily life activities when used in addition to usual care (increasing overall therapy time) in people with stroke, even if no significant evidence has been found on gait speed, balance, participation, or quality of life.23

Participants received a VR headset and were asked to perform the intervention for 5 min per day, 6 days per week for 2 weeks, for a total of 180 min (Fig. 2). This intervention dose was chosen based on 4 factors. First, common attention sustains time for 10 min to 20 min before needing reinforcement to maintain focus. Because attention span may decrease with aging, attention interventions likely need to be brief for efficacy without fatigue.23,24 Second, a meta-analysis of interventions using natural activities suggested that 5-min interventions are optimal for improving self-esteem and mood.25 Third, in our preliminary study, participants had interventions of around 6 min, which led to small to medium effect sizes. Thus, a total of 15 min per day was deemed appropriate for the 3 different nature VR interventions (5 min for each nature of VR intervention).Fig. 2Image of patients under training with a head-mounted VR solution.VR: virtual reality.

Image of patients under training with a head-mounted VR solution.VR: virtual reality.

The 2-week interventional duration was decided based on the standard sport science and physical rehabilitation duration set by the Russian Ministry of Health. All patients recruited to experimental groups and eligible for the immersive VR solution were to take 10 min rest before the procedures. All patients received information and were trained on how to stand on the platform (positioning), how to train dorsiflexion, plantar flexion, internal rotation, external rotation, and how to use a head-mounted display for 10 min. After receiving information and training, the patients stood on the stable platform with 2 legs, wearing helmets and a head-mounted display to train with an immersive VR device. Then the patients were trained with a simulation of the natural environment with support of a game-mounted display for 3 games (trust rolling ball, trust walking, and trust vehicle driving) for 15 min, on average 5 min for each game (Fig. 3). In total, the patients in the experimental groups attended immersive VR solution training for 12 days. The aim of actively involving the patient in the rehabilitation process, competitive schemes, and game elements is often used when performing exercises, the success of which is determined by the homogeneity of the group of patients. When using special simulators, there is no need to select a homogeneous group; the simulator itself levels out the differences.Fig. 3Still frame of the visual sequence under trust walking, rolling ball, and vehicle driving game training through using head-mounted immersive VR solution. Operational definition

An operational definition is given of each variable along with each classification. Anterior drawer sign: This is a provocative test done to check for the integrity of the anterior talofibular ligament (ATFL). One hand holds the leg down, while the other hand pulls the foot upwards and anteriorly. It is important to compare 1 side with the other. The presence of tenderness or laxity on 1 side only indicates an ATFL rupture.

This is done with the knee at 90° of flexion, and the muscles relaxed. Increased laxity compared to the contralateral ankle reflects injury to the ATFL and possibly the calcaneofibular ligament (CFL). The combination of discoloration, haematoma, ATFL tenderness, and positive anterior drawer provides a sensitivity of 98% and a specificity of 84% of an acute lateral ligament rupture.

Inversion: this is a provocative test done to check for the integrity of the CFL. Forcefully invert the foot while keeping the leg still with the other hand. Make sure to compare side to side. Again, tenderness or laxity on one side indicates a CFL lesion. Talar dome: to assess for an osteochondral defect, first locate the talar dome and then palpate to elicit tenderness. To locate the talar dome, identify the lateral malleolus and then move anteriorly from it, up to the anterior recess. From here, move medially and anteriorly along the joint line. The talar dome will be right beneath this point. Any tenderness elicited by palpating. Talar tilt test: this is performed by applying an inversion force to the ankle joint at the heel, then assessing the degree of talar tilt. A talar tilt of greater than 15° reflects rupture of the ATFL and CFL. External rotation test: gently rotate the foot externally while holding the leg still. Any tenderness would be positive for syndesmosis damage. The patient's ankle is passively dorsiflexed in maximal external rotation (either seated or lying prone with knee flexed to 90°). Pain at the syndesmosis is regarded as a positive test. Squeeze test: squeeze the tibia and the fibula together to elicit tenderness. If present, this indicates a syndesmosis. To confirm a suspected Achilles tendon rupture, have the patient lie prone and then squeeze the calf while observing the foot. If there is plantar flexion of the foot, this means that the tendon is intact. Otherwise, a reduced or absent plantar flexion, when compared with the other side, is indicative of a tendon rupture. With both hands, clasp the medial and lateral aspects of the mid-calf and squeeze. Pain distally at the site of the syndesmosis is regarded as a positive test.

Statistical data processing

Give a description of the groups by age, gender, features of the diagnosis, medical history, and efficacy and safety parameters. The descriptive statistics methods (for quantitative variables: arithmetic mean, median, standard deviation, minimum and maximum value; for categorical variables: n (%)). Assess 95% confidence intervals for all parameters.

Preliminary correlation analysis was conducted on groupings of the dependent variables for the 3 groups. Given that variables for each group correlated, multivariate ANOVA was conducted to determine the effect of treatment modality among 3 treatment groups (complex group (VR and KT); intervention (VR); and control) on the constructs of outcome measurement variables. If multivariate ANOVA interactions are observed, follow-up univariate ANOVA are calculated to determine which specific variables drove the interaction for the construct in question. The R statistical software is used for statistical analysis.

Statistical differences were assessed through a combination of hypothesis testing, significance levels, effect sizes, confidence intervals, and consideration of sample size and errors. Normality test and homogeneity of variance show no deviation and no skewness.

Descriptive statistics (means ± standard deviations) for each group were run to allow for a preliminary comparison of the basic characteristics across groups. ANOVA was used to assess whether the groups are statistically similar regarding baseline characteristics, as the data are normally distributed. The p-values were used to assess whether there were significant differences in baseline characteristics between the groups. A multivariable regression models employed to adjust for potential confounding factors and assess group differences while controlling for these variables.

Results

The results of this study consist of a descriptive analysis, correlational analysis, statistical assumptions, and ANOVA analysis for the central research question. A quantitative empirical design is used to ascertain if there is a significant statistical association in the relationship between the independent variables as proxy variables for rehabilitation of ankle injury and representation tenets. The theoretical framework used as a means for comparing the role and place of VR and KT in the rehabilitation of ankle injury uses the usual rehabilitation trends as the benchmark.

The assessment and follow-up rehabilitation program for all groups (control, VR, and mixed) is held only for 2 weeks, which is a very low rehabilitation time to evaluate the ankle injury improvement compared to the 6-week standard rehabilitation time. This too-early evaluation time will not exactly represent the standard evaluation of an ankle injury. All participants received training sessions, supervised by the same physical therapist, for a minimum of a 2-week period.26

This result follows the results of previous studies. Simoneau et al.32 have reported that increased cutaneous sensory feedback, provided by strips of athletic tape which are applied across the ankle joint of healthy individuals, helped in improving ankle joint position perception in non-weight-bearing activities, especially for the midrange plantar-flexed ankle position. Ankle ROM has been repeatedly confirmed to have a great influence on bilateral postural stability, as a previous case study has reported significant negative correlations between ankle dorsi/plantar flexion ROM and balance maintenance following KT application.42 Similarly, it has been demonstrated that ankle plantarflexion ROM significantly correlated with the mediolateral sways.43 The other study, opposed to these findings, indicates decreased ankle inversion/eversion plane motion was observed after usage of KT. This decreased frontal plane motion is likely due to the lateral support provided by the KT, thereby preventing excessive rotation of the calcaneus and decreasing the probability of any sudden lateral changes in the ankle joint. By improving the lateral support, the tape was likely able to attenuate frontal plane forces, positively affecting balance and decreasing the need for extraneous muscle activation.

Reducing the level of ankle pain has positive effects on improving ankle rotation with a p value of 0.03, which plays a significant role in passive dorsiflexion in maximal external rotation. The result indicates that the level of ankle pain plays a significant role in improving the range of ankle motion. This improvement in applications is highly related to the VR and KT applications. The previous study identified pain as the main reason for limited ankle ROM. Limited dorsiflexion ROM is often apparent in individuals with conventionally treated sprains.44 The main finding of Lazarou et al.26 also supports that for individuals with ankle sprains, both balance and PNF groups significantly improved dorsiflexion ROM and most measures of functional performance 8 weeks after training.26 The other study in line with this result indicates VR systems provide total visual immersion, movements, and interactions in an artificial, computer-generated environment that may incorporate artificial stimuli such as sounds or hand-operated controllers to improve the interactive experience.26

The tenderness of the tibia and the fibula, which indicates syndesmosis damage, has no statistical relationship with the VR solution and KT application. Thus, VR and KT applications help to improve syndesmosis damage. This finding is supported by a study done by Doherty et al.,45 which, considering KT application, was analyzed as the only treatment implemented on athletes, without concomitant physiotherapy or other types of exercises, so that the potential improvement parameters registered were exclusively attributed to KT application, showing positive improvement. The results of this study have demonstrated that Kinesiotaping increased balance after 48 h.

Discussion

The statistical data of this study reveal that high BMI, ankle pain, ankle edema, and swelling of the lower limb have positive effects on eliciting tenderness of the talar dome, ATFL, CFL tibia, fibula, and positive anterior drawer tests, which indicate poor ankle injury rehabilitation. The VR and/or KT application play a key role in ankle injury rehabilitation compared to the control group, as they reduce ankle edema, ankle pain, and swelling of the lower limb. Additionally, they are invaluable to reduce the tenderness and laxity of the talar dome, ATFL, CFL, tibia, and fibula, which elicit maintaining of integrity and stability of the ankle.

Squeeze test (tibia & fibula tenderness, which indicates Achilles tendon rupture), external rotation test (any tenderness indicates syndesmosis damage), talar dome (tenderness indicates defects of osteochondral), and anterior drawer test (tenderness/laxity indicates ATFL rupture and possibly the CFL). These entire tests are statistically significant with the control group treatment modality compared to the 2 experimental groups’ treatment modality, which helps me to underline poor ankle injury rehabilitation in the control group.

Future recommendations

The scope of this study does not include imaging diagnostic tests to evaluate the level of ankle injury and its rehabilitation. The ankle injury rehabilitation for all 3 groups was done only for 2 weeks. This study is only a single-blinded study, as study participants know their groups and might share information about their treatment modality between them. The KT application is done post-massage for only 48 h, as patients attend massage therapy for 10 days. Based on the scope, limitation, and results of the current study, the following recommendation was forwarded: (1) To get a more comprehensive ankle injury evaluation, it is better if you use an imaging diagnostic test in addition to the currently used test. (2) Identifying the effect of comorbid disease is also an area that needs attention for the future. (3) Recommend double or triple-blinded therapy as the current study is a single-blinded study. (4) Recommend a long duration of ankle injury rehabilitation for a minimum of 6weeks, as rehabilitation highly depends on duration. (5) It is also advisable to see the difference per session of therapy.

Indicators and factors related to one‐year improved SCImago journal rank in nursing journals: A bibliometric analysis
Authors: Sutthisak Srisawad, Kullacha Lertsittiphan, Pennapa Saenkla, Sirirut Tunsirirut
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsTo analyse the bibliometric data of nursing journals covering general fields, specializations, skills and practices and investigate the influence of selection factors on the one‐year improved SCImago journal rank (SJR).DesignA bibliometric analysis.MethodsThe study sample included academic journals in the field of nursing indexed in the Scopus and SJR database using data from 2020 to 2022. Quantile regression were performed that investigated the influence factors over the one‐year improved SJR when variables were found to be non‐normally distributed, characterized by extremely high kurtosis.ResultsResults from 539 nursing journals retrieved from the Scopus and SCImago databases revealed that citation index remains the main factor that positively affects differences in SJR, while citable articles only affect the early stages of quantile regression. In addition, an excessively high number of research articles may negatively affect SJR, and the influence of self‐citations can initially be positive but become negative the following year. Citations continue to be the dominant factor, while the rapid growth in the number of articles and self‐citations must be addressed with caution.No Patient or Public ContributionNo patient or public contribution.Keywords: bibliometric analysis, nursing journals, quantile regression, SCImago journal rank

INTRODUCTION

Bibliometric data and citation‐based indicators are still widely accepted as evaluators of the quality of academic publications. Citations are a main means of assessing the impact of research results, leading to their use as a systemic quality assessment tool for researchers, educational institutions and journals or publishers specializing in the selection, evaluation and dissemination of academic articles. Thus, citations are an important variable from the past to the present (Ali et al., 2023; Kaplan et al., 2014). Researchers have used citations to evaluate the publication quality of a journal and have themselves been examined through the analysis of citations by funders and journal editors (Roldan‐Valadez et al., 2019). Institutions have also been assessed for research quality using citation metrics, including institutional rankings. These reflect the international acceptance and validity of this indicator.

Currently, the publication of academic works, research and citations are used to evaluate research activities. The important ways for a country and educational institution to integrate into the global community is through international academic journals. (Wang & Feng, 2022). Important stakeholders consist of lecturers, researchers, academic institutions and research funding sources, who contribute to the dissemination and utilization of the work. Others consist of journals, databases, publishers, editors and reviewers who play a role in publication selection. Both groups come from the public and private sectors, thus making publication system more business oriented (Receveur et al., 2024). As the publication system of academic articles grows and becomes associated with more benefits or values, the monitoring system through trend direction analysis or the in‐depth analysis of information data is therefore necessary for relevant parties to use data to plan both short‐ and long‐term operations to achieve sustainable growth (Barrientos et al., 2023).

In addition, bibliometric analysis is an important tool for the article publication system. It generates primary data for quality assessment and academic rankings, with citation‐based indicators being essential qualitative variables (Colicchia et al., 2018; Gomez‐Jauregui et al., 2014; Vijayan et al., 2021). Nowadays, bibliometric studies have grown continuously, covering all fields and dimensions including articles, professors, researchers, educational institutions and journals or publishers. This study focuses on the analysis of bibliographic data in academic journals in all subject field of nursing so that readers can consider the current situation of nursing research, nursing journal databases related to the educational system. Despite restricting studies to the nursing field, we have found that many journals have focused on multidisciplinary research, which requires caution in citation analysis because of significant differences among subject fields (Leydesdorff et al., 2017). Therefore, we will designate subject fields as one of the control factors in our study.

Nursing research has grown every year. Our preliminary data study showed that articles published in the field of nursing have increased by approximately 17% in 2021 and 6% in 2022, with 637 academic journals specializing in nursing (SCImago, 2023). In addition, there were approximately 40 new journals that were included in the Scopus database in 2020–2022, with about 86% of the total number of journals continuously operating in these 3 years. Regarding the analysis of bibliometric data in nursing, focusing on the overall analysis of nursing journals, studies have been few and outdated, such as that of Cáceres et al. (2017), which examined the Journal Impact Factor (JIF) assessment of nursing journals from 2009 to 2014. Zhi‐Chen et al. (2021) examined research papers published in East Asian nursing journals from 2013 to 2017 and Woods et al. (2020) analysed grey literature citations in top nursing journals that have a study scope in an article published in 2011. We also found that the recent bibliometric study of nursing has focused on discussing important topics, such as leadership skills, education, evidence‐based practice and the health‐care system (Abbott & Nininger, 2023; Kantek et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Shi et al., 2023; Unal & Teskereci, 2022). As a result, nursing journals have generally lacked up‐to‐date fundamental and in‐depth information for bibliometric analysis that journals or publishers can use when planning for quality development in the long term. Therefore, this study aimed to analyse the bibliometric data of nursing journals whose scope of study is in the Scopus database and data from the SCImago Journal and Country Rank. These will be used to provide an overview of the journals and perform statistical analyses of the relation between different variables, and important journal quality assessment indicators such as SCImago journal rank (SJR) to benefit the planning process for improving journal quality will be discussed in the methodology section.

SCIMAGO JOURNAL RANK

SJR, established by Elsevier in 2014, is a key journal quality assessment indicator based on Scopus, which is among the world's leading large databases. SJR is classified as a citation‐based indicator often used by databases to rank journals according to different fields or country groups. To date, SJR is the dominant indicator in the Scopus database, on the same level as JIF or the Eigenfactor score, which is used to assess journal quality in the Web of Sciences and the Journal Citation Report. The calculation principle of SJR is different from that of JIF, but the process is similar to that of the Eigenfactor with a three‐year data collection period and excluding self‐citations from the calculations. While JIF uses 2 years of historical data, it is calculated in the same way as the citation‐per‐document indicator based on Scopus (Jain et al., 2021; Stephen, 2020; Yuen, 2018).

SJR is used to rank journals in four categories (Q1–Q4), called journal quartiles, that function to show the quality of journal (Arianto & Basthomi, 2021). Publishing a research article in a journal with a high journal quartile ranking also reflects the value of that article, which is likely to be cited quickly and in large numbers. Therefore, the competition is not only among researchers but also among publishers and journal operators. Publishers are responsible for selecting publications based on the quality of work and the likelihood that the journal will receive a higher rating in the following year. However, we found only a few studies on factors affecting the citation‐based indicator at the journal level, which are mostly focused on research articles, which then provide useful information for researchers. Studies on publishers or journal managers have also highlighted another indicator, JIF. Ali et al. (2023) studied factors affecting JIF and Eigenfactor score among the highest‐quality journals in otorhinolaryngology on the Journal Citation Reports database. Their results showed that the five‐year JIF and Immediacy Index were positively correlated with JIF values, while citable articles and total citations were positively correlated with the Eigenfactor score. Citation count remained an important factor for journal quality indicators. Other variables of interest were also found in the study, such as collaboration, language used and self‐citations (Borges et al., 2022; Diekhoff et al., 2013). This study focuses on examining journal quality indicators based on Scopus and SCImago databases, such as SJR, and exploring their correlations with other bibliographic data among nursing journals. Our variable creation will focus on yearly differentiated data to determine the predictive ability that is consistent with data that vary every year, which will be discussed in the next section.

METHODOLOGY

Study sample and sample size

We examined academic peer‐reviewed journals in the field of nursing based on the Scopus database with, and journal quality indicators were included in the SJR database (SCImago, 2023) using data from 2020 to 2022 with journal counts of 651, 646 and 637 in those years, respectively.

All nursing journals with complete bibliographic data were used; journals published after 2020 or removed from the database were not used. This resulted in 539 journals, or about 83% of the average number of journals per year during from 2020 to 2022.

Variables

The variables to be used in this study were divided into three groups:

Preliminary bibliometric variables in the SJR database presented annually from 2020 to 2022.

Preliminary bibliometric variables in the SJR database presented annually from 2020 to 2022.

Independent variables (IDVs) to predict differences in SJR scores, which were in the form of ratios, differences in ratios, or percentage differences. These calculations sought to normalize and solve the problem of multicollinearity by calculating difference values between 2020 and 2021.

Independent variables (IDVs) to predict differences in SJR scores, which were in the form of ratios, differences in ratios, or percentage differences. These calculations sought to normalize and solve the problem of multicollinearity by calculating difference values between 2020 and 2021.

Dependent variables (DVs) pertaining to the percentage difference in SJR scores using the difference between 2020 and 2021 to determine the predictive power between variables over the same period and the difference between 2022 and 2021 to determine the predictive power of the parent variable before the occurrence of the DV in 1 year. Supplementary—Data S1 shows the methods for calculating these variables.

Dependent variables (DVs) pertaining to the percentage difference in SJR scores using the difference between 2020 and 2021 to determine the predictive power between variables over the same period and the difference between 2022 and 2021 to determine the predictive power of the parent variable before the occurrence of the DV in 1 year. Supplementary—Data S1 shows the methods for calculating these variables.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to analyse general bibliographic data presented comparatively among the eight fields of nursing research: (1) nursing (miscellaneous); (2) nutrition and dietetics; (3) advanced and specialized nursing; (4) paediatrics, maternity and midwifery; (5) gerontology; (6) care planning, community and homecare; (7) fundamental skills, leadership and management, LPN and LVN; and (8) other.

In addition, descriptive statistics were obtained to determine the distribution of the study variables. All variables were found to be non‐normally distributed and characterized by extremely high kurtosis.

To test the preliminary correlation between the IDVs and DVs, Spearman's rho correlation was calculated to match the data distribution.

To analyse the effects of all IDVs on DVs both in the same period and in the next year, we performed quantile regression. Three quantile levels were used for data analysis: 25th, 50th and 75th percentiles. This statistic is consistent with abnormally distributed data and has high kurtosis (Klayme et al., 2023; Reyes et al., 2021). However, we also performed generalized linear model regression analysis to compare coefficient differences between models.

In addition, our study focused on confounding factors that could influence the DVs: number of subjects, subject categories and subject areas. We preliminarily tested and selected statistically significant variables as control factors in our quantile regression model.

The quantile regression analysis results by assigning the DV to the difference in SJR values over the same period as the IDVs (model 1) and in the period occurring 1 year after the IDVs (model 2) are provided in Table 4. The results revealed that citation index was the main factor that positively influenced journal quality indicators, with statistically significant predictability across all quantile levels (25th, 50th and 75th) and quantile regression coefficients of 11.89, 12.96 and 14.85, respectively, in model 1 and 4.61, 3.85 and 5.60, respectively, in model 2. Regarding citable articles, which also focus on the citation issue, a positive impact on the SJR value was found only at the first data level (25th percentile), with coefficients of 0.05 in model 1 and 0.03 in model 2. The next interesting variable is article ratio, which reflects the increase, or decrease in the number of articles each year. The results showed that article ratio had a negative effect on the difference in SJR at the same year level, with coefficients of −5.57, −5.79 and −5.20. The last variable, self‐citation, which was calculated relative to the previous year's citations, had a positive impact on SJR in the same year but a negative impact on SJR the following year. However, the coefficient of determination in each model was low (Pseudo R 2 ≈ 0.10 in model 1 and Pseudo R 2 ≈ 0.03 in model 2).

DISCUSSIONS AND LIMITATIONS

This study focused on the analysis of bibliometric data and quality indicators of journals in the nursing area based on the Scopus and SCImago journal and country rank databases. Bibliographic analysis is useful for planning to improve the quality of the academic publication system, and studies are currently examining such issues, covering different fields. Traditional theoretical research in nursing is growing steadily but not quickly as it is focusing more on practice than basic research (Cáceres et al., 2017).

To provide useful information to the stakeholders of this study, we added an in‐depth study to determine the factors that affect SJR by adopting quantile regression in accordance with the distribution of this study's variables. The main factor with the greatest influence on SJR is still the one associated with citations, that is, citation index, with citable articles having a small impact (Lunny et al., 2022). This is also consistent with a study by Ali et al. (2023), which found that JIF, total citations and citable articles had a positive effect on the Eigenfactor scores of otolaryngology journals. This was not surprising as citations remained a crucial factor in journal quality assessment. Editors who want to improve the quality of their journals must still focus on the number and speed of citing academic articles in their journals.

This study had two other important findings:

An increase in the number of academic articles each year in a journal cannot positively affect journal quality indicators. These results are consistent with those of Ariza‐Guerrero and Blázquez (2023), who found that the relationship between impact factor and the number of articles is not clear when studied in deviation variables. It is well‐known that the number of articles per year is used as a denominator and for calculating journal quality indicators. When the number of articles increases rapidly but citations are not increasing in the appropriate ratio, SJR may be lower. The number of articles in a journal increases for many reasons, which may be related to the growth of the publishing business, but the optimal increase size is difficult to calculate. In this study, an estimate was presented using the ratio of the number of articles in the most recent year to that in the past 3 years. When the ratio increases by one unit each year, journal quality indicators may decrease by about 5%.

For authors, self‐citations refer to citing one's own research articles or participating in them, which may build on, or refer to previously presented studies that are related. For journals, self‐citation means citing articles belonging to the same journal. Authors typically engage in self‐citation, which is neither wrong, inappropriate, nor unethical, but the point at which it is done excessively is difficult to define. Moreover, studies have found that the proportion of self‐citations is positively correlated with indicators of journal quality (Sanfilippo et al., 2021; Siwach & Thakur, 2021; Taşkın et al., 2021). This study used journal self‐citation as an IDV, which produced interesting results. An increase in self‐citation ratio will positively affect the increase in journal quality metrics for the same period but will negatively affect these metrics the following year. From a technical standpoint, self‐citation increases the likelihood of an article being read or cited since some readers tend to read already cited studies. This issue must be studied further both in terms of quantitative data and behavioural or social analysis.

From a technical standpoint, self‐citation increases the likelihood of an article being read or cited since some readers tend to read already cited studies. This issue must be studied further both in terms of quantitative data and behavioural or social analysis.

This study had two important limitations. First, its scope was limited to specific studies in the nursing field and did not go back several years. Therefore, the results may be different from those in other fields with different sizes or growths of research article databases. Nursing is still considered a small but important field of development in health sciences. Second, most of the variables in this study were in the form of ratios and differences in data from year to year because we wanted to present predictive equations that were consistent with time‐series data. The study also focused on normalizing data and adhering to multicollinearity conventions. However, the results were limited in that their interpretations were not straightforward but were nevertheless consistent in terms of the direction of relations.

CONCLUSSIONS

This study analysed the bibliometric data of all nursing journals in various fields including basic research, specialized applied research, skills and the practice of professional nurses. We found that each field had the same growth size except for nutrition and dietetics, where we observed a clear difference; the research areas of most of the journals in this field were medicine or the sciences, which have grown beyond the nursing field. However, we found that the SJR of the journals in this field declined between 2020 and 2021 and was thus used as a control variable.

Machine learning analysis of coagulation-related genes for breast cancer diagnosis and prognosis prediction
Authors: Shujin Li, Shuyan Liu, Yiwen Zheng, Weimin Hong, Yaoqiang Du, Xiaozhen Liu, Hongchao Tang, Xuli Meng, Qinghui Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe purpose of this study was to investigate the relationship between coagulation related genes (CRGs) and breast cancer (BC). First, we found that most CRGs are abnormally expressed in BC patients and correlated with their prognosis. Therefore, we explored the expression of CRGs in benign and malignant breast tissues in the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx), extracted differentially expressed CRGs, and established an artificial neural network (ANN) diagnostic model to distinguish the nature of breast tissues, as well as a risk scoring model for prognostic assessment and risk stratification. The specimen transcriptomic data we provided confirmed the diagnostic performance of the ANN model described above. For the risk score model, we used internal and external validation, using ROC curves and C-index values to test its predictive value in the TCGA and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) cohorts, and further established a prognostic nomogram for clinical application. In addition, we evaluated the performance of diagnostic and prognostic models using 3 cross-validations methods. RABIF was further identified as a core gene. We performed a more detailed study of RABIF: RT-qPCR of BC cell lines and immunohistochemical staining (IHC) of breast tissue samples showed that RABIF is highly expressed in BC especially in advanced BC. Our study demonstrates the value of CRGs as diagnostic and prognostic targets and may contribute to clinical decision-making in BC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-19290-2.Keywords: Breast carcinoma, Coagulation-related genes, ANN diagnostic model, Prognosis model, RABIFSubject terms: Cancer, Biomarkers, Medical research

Introduction

Breast carcinoma (BC) is among the most prevalent malignancy types and it remains the deadliest cancer in the world. BC incidence rates have risen over the past 40 years, with the most recent data from 2010 to 2019 revealing an estimated 0.5% annual increase in these rates1. Breast tumors are highly heterogeneous, exhibiting a range of phenotypic and biological characteristics attributable to genomic and transcriptomic variations as well as to the composition of the tumor microenvironment (TME). This heterogeneity translates to a variety of morphological presentations, histological grades, and treatment responses in affected BC patients2. Approaches to BC treatment can include combinations of surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, endocrine therapy, and immunotherapeutic regimens that can markedly improve survival rates3. The efficacy of these strategies, however, remains suboptimal, with patients ultimately experiencing recurrent disease, metastatic progression, and/or therapeutic resistance in many cases. Studies show that, a range of molecular factors contribute to the invasivity and metastasis of tumors, including oncogene activation, tumor suppressor gene inactivation, and associated signaling dysregulation4,5. Further research is needed to explore molecular targets and biomarkers associated with breast cancer progression to guide novel diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

Breast carcinoma cells have been demonstrated to promote the activation and aggregation of platelets, playing a key role in cancer metastatic progression and associated thrombotic outcomes6. Developing tumors require access to an extensive blood supply, many studies have demonstrated that chronic hypercoagulability and fibrosis are often associated with tumors, and that cancer patients experience a risk of thrombosis 9-fold higher than that in healthy subjects7–9. The coagulatory system is highly complex and precise such that it warrants systematic examination. There have been some recent studies on tumor-associated coagulation activity10,11. Tiholt et al. demonstrated that coagulation factor V (F5) serves as a biomarker of immune cells in breast cancer and that it may play a therapeutic role through the tumor inflammation-thrombosis pathway12. Therefore, we believe that biological analysis of coagulation-related genes (CRG) can provide new insights into the treatment and prognosis of breast cancer.

Therefore, in this study, we systematically analyzed the expression of CRGs in BC. Then we constructed a diagnostic artificial neural network (ANN) model based on 11-CRGs and a prognostic signature model based on 14-CRGs. The Area Under The Curve (AUC) value of their Receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curve showed strong robustness and superiority. Cross-validations also demonstrate their comparatively improved performance. Then, a nomogram was constructed to quantify survival probability combined with the risk score and other prognostic clinical indicators. Finally, we validated RABIF in clinical samples and cell lines.

Results

Transcriptome sequencing data analyses

Transcriptome sequencing was carried out in breast cancer and normal breast tissues collected in our hospital, and according to the principle of log2 FC ≥ 1 or ≤ − 1 and adj. p value < 0.05, 5492 DEGs were singled out. These DEGs were shown by heatmap and volcano map (Fig. 1A-B). GSEA identified HALLMARK_COAGULATION signalling enriched in BC tissue obviously (Fig. 1C). To better understand the functional roles of these DEGs, DisGeNET, disease ontology (DO), Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG)13–15 and Reactome analyses were conducted (see Supplementary Fig. S1A-E). Among them, KEGG analysis showed that DEGs were enriched in hematopoietic cell lineage pathway.

Evaluation of batch effect correction

Prior to integration, a pronounced batch effect was observed, where samples primarily clustered by data source (TCGA vs. GTEx) rather than by tissue type. Following the application of removeBatchEffect, the separation by batch was markedly reduced, with samples now clustering more prominently by their biological identity (Supplementary Fig. S2A, Principal Component Analysis plot).

Identifcation of diagnostic DECRGs

44 genes that were considerably different between the tumor and normal tissues were obtained by comparing the expression profiles of 137 CRGs among TCGA-BRCA and GTEx normal tissues, and according to the principle of logFC ≥ 1 or ≤ − 1 and adj. p value < 0.05. These DECRGs and their expression correlation analysis were shown by heatmap and volcano map (Fig. 2A-C). In diagnostic DECRGs selection, the five independent feature selection methods (random forest, XGBoost, linear SVM, LASSO regression analyses, and Gradient boosting machine), were simultaneously conducted to screen candidate biomarkers of 44 DECRGs. The intersection amongst the five machine learning algorithms were considered the diagnostic genes. The random tree diagram showed the errors of the cancer group, the normal group, and all samples. We found the genes represented by the points with the smallest cross-validation errors and score these genes. The higher the score, the more important it is. 12 genes were selected by XGBoost and 16 genes were selected by svm (Fig. 3A-D). There were 11 genes overlapping in all (Fig. 3E).

Construction and evaluation of neural network diagnostic model

According to gene scores and weights, a neural network model was constructed to identify sample attributes (Fig. 4A). The input layer was 11 diagnostic genes, in the train group(TCGA and GTEx cohorts),177/193 in the normal group were correctly predicted, and 1099/1104 in the cancer group were accurately predicted. Our sample transcriptome sequencing data serves as an external validation set. ROC curves of the train group and test group were drawn to verify the accuracy of the model in predicting sample attributes. The area under the ROC curve of the train group is 0.997 (95%CI: 0.995–0.999) (Fig. 4B). Further external verification showed that the area under the test group curve is 0.950 (95%CI: 0.820–1.000) (Fig. 4C), which proved that the neural network model has high accuracy. Subsequently, we evaluated the predictive diagnostic value of the diagnostic genes in the TCGA cohort, the results showed that they all had good predictive value in BC (Fig. 5).

Construction and evaluation of the prognostic CRGs risk signature

To further investigate the prognostic value of CRGs, we conducted a univariate Cox regression of 44 CRGs in relation to OS. A total of 14 prognostic CRGs with P < 0.01 were identified. We then used 70% of the TCGA-BRCA as a training cohort to build the prognostic signature by LASSO regression (Fig. 6A, B), 30% of the TCGA-BRCA as an internal validation cohort, and GEO cohort as an external cohort. These genes were presented using a volcano plot (Fig. 6C). C-index analysis revealed that the combined prognostic model exhibited superior predictive value to any of these individual genes (Fig. 6D). Using the formula derived from this LASSO regression analysis, risk scores were then calculated and used to stratify the BC patients in the training and validation cohorts into low-risk and high-risk subgroups, utilizing the median risk score as a cutoff value. Kaplan-Meier analyses demonstrated that high-risk patients exhibited significantly poorer OS than did low-risk patients in both the training (Fig. 7C) and validation (Fig. 8C) cohorts, and the same was also evident in the GEO validation cohort (Fig. 9C). Time-dependent ROC curves further demonstrated that this model was able to accurately predict patient 3-, 5-, and 7-year OS (Figs. 7D, 8D and 9D), and comparisons of ROC curve based on risk scores and other clinicopathological characteristics revealed that the predictive power of the risk scores was greater than for any other analyzed clinical parameters, emphasizing its potential clinical value (Figs. 7E and 8E). Survival outcomes, CRGs risk score distributions, and CRGs expression profiles were also presented for patients in the training and validation cohorts (Figs. 7A-B, 8A-B and 9A-B). These findings highlight the good prognostic performance of the established model. Its association with patient clinical characteristics is presented in Fig. 10A, and a heatmap was generated representing the correlations between the expression of the 14 DECRGs included in this model and BC patient characteristics including age, gender, TNM stage, tumor stage, and risk group. When confirming the prognostic value of this model in patients with different clinical characteristics, significant differences in risk score values were detected as a function of tumor stage (Stage I/II vs. III/IV), T stage (T0/1 vs. T2/3), M stage (M0 vs. M1), and N stage (N0/1 vs. N2/3) (Fig. 10B). Risk scores for high-risk patients were greater than those for low-risk patients in these analyses.

Functional enrichment, TME, and drug response analyses

To better understand the functional roles of these DECRGs of interest, GO and KEGG functional enrichment analyses were next conducted, revealing that these genes were associated with the regulation of body fluid levels and the complement and coagulation pathways (Supplementary Fig.S3A, B). A protein-protein interaction (PPI) regulatory network was additionally established using GeneMANIA, revealing that these genes are primarily associated with the regulation of humoral immunity, complement activation, and clotting activity (Supplementary Fig.S3C). Given the clear association between these genes and immune response activity, further analyses focused on this relationship were next conducted. Next, the immune cell composition in patient samples from the TCGA cohort was assessed with the CIBERSORT algorithm (Supplementary Fig.S4A). High-risk patients were found to exhibit stromal and ESTIMATE scores higher than those of low-risk patients when using the ESTIMATE algorithm (P < 0.01; Supplementary Fig.S4B). IPS scores indicated that low-risk patients were likely to be more sensitive to anti-CTLA-4 treatment (P < 0.01), although no differences in anti-PD-1 treatment outcomes were predicted (Supplementary Fig.S4C). Greater sensitivity to treatment with Fulvestrant (P < 0.001), an endocrine drug, was noted for low-risk patients, while the IC50 value of the chemotherapeutic drug Oxaliplatin (P < 0.001) was higher in the high-risk group, and a significant association was noted between risk score values and responses to Sorafenib and Selumetinib, which are targeted drugs (Supplementary Fig.S4D).

Construction and evaluation of nomogram

To more thoroughly and accurately predict prognosis of BC patients, risk factors independently associated with OS were identified through a series of univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses. This approach ultimately identified age, tumor stage, and Risk Score as independent predictors of BRCA patient OS (Fig. 11A). A nomogram was thus established by combining these clinically relevant factors (Fig. 11B), and the validity of this nomogram as a tool for predicting patient 3-, 5-, and 7-year OS was then confirmed (Fig. 11C). C-index values also supported the utility of this nomogram as a tool for the prediction of BC patient prognostic outcomes, outperforming other independent clinical factors in this setting (Fig. 11D).

Further analyses of the diagnostic efficacy of each of these 14 CRGs were next conducted (Fig. 12A). RABIF is an important diagnostic and prognostic biomarker in this form of cancer. To expand on these results, RABIF mRNA levels were examined in the TCGA-BRCA cohort, revealing it to be significantly upregulated in tumor tissues relative to control samples (Fig. 12B). The same was also true in six GEO cohorts (Fig. 12C). Consistent results were also obtained in cell lines, the expression of RABIF in BC cells were upregulated compared to normal breast cells (Fig. 13A). In BC tissue section, we observed that the Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining of RABIF in BC tissue was deeper than that in surrounding normal breast tissue (Fig. 13B). We selected 5 early-stage BC tissue section and 5 advanced BC tissue section, IHC staining comparison of RABIF was performed, higher RABIF protein levels in advanced BC samples (Fig. 13C-E). TCGA-BRCA cohort were next separated into RABIF-low and RABIF-high subsets, using the median value of RABIF expression as a cut-off threshold. The TIMER database revealed that RABIF somatic copy number variations (CNV) were significantly associated with immune cell infiltration for a range of immune cell types (Supplementary Fig.S5A). The stromal, immune, and ESTIMATE scores of RABIF-low were significantly higher compared to RABIF-high (P < 0.01; Supplementary Fig.S5B). RABIF-low were also associated with greater sensitivity to treatment with the endocrine drugs, such as Fulvestrant and Tamoxifen, and the targeted drugs, such as Sorafenib and Selumetinib (P < 0.001), while the IC50 values for the chemotherapeutic drugs mitoxantrone and oxaliplatin were higher in RABIF-high (P < 0.001; Supplementary Fig.S5C). These data suggest that RABIF may be a predictor of drug availability for breast cancer treatment.

Discussion

In this study, initially, functional enrichment analysis of our transcriptome sequencing data identified coagulation signal enriched in breast tumor tissue obviously. Then we explored the expression profiles of coagulation-related-genes in patients with BC, created a diagnostic ANN model based on 11-CRGs and verified the robustness of the model. In addition, we developed a coagulation-related prognosis model based on 14-CRGs. We analyzed the correlations between the model and clinicopathological characteristics, functional biological pathways, gene mutations and the immune microenvironment to further interpret the model.

Coagulation has been shown to be closely associated with malignant tumor progression such that cancer patients frequently exhibit aberrant coagulatory activity16. Procoagulant factor production such as tissue factor (TF), tumor procoagulant protein, microparticles (MPs), proangiogenic factor, and cytokine can impact oncogenic processes procoagulant factors can affect tumor development. These factors can further spur tumor progression, contributing to a greater risk of chronic hypercoagulability and thrombotic events17. A rising number of studies have highlighted the importance of clotting-related factors as regulators of BC progression. Patients with this form of cancer often exhibit high D-dimer levels associated with hypercoagulability18, increasing the density of blood vessels proximal to tumors19, shielding these malignant masses from hemodynamic shock or NK cell-mediated immune responses20, inducing epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) activity21, and driving metastatic progression. CRGs are thus important determinants of both the immediate ability of BC cells to grow, differentiate, and metastasize while also shaping immune cell-mediated tumor killing and related immune cell recruitment and infiltrative activity. These data emphasize the value of CRGs that can aid in the disease diagnosis and prognostic evaluation of individuals, emphasizing the importance of establishing relevant models for translation into a clinical setting.

The ANN is a nonlinear model that is flexible and can fit any function. Their application in the classification and diagnosis of infectious diseases, tumors, hypertension and related diseases has been relatively mature22–24. In many studies, it has been demonstrated that the ANN model outperformed others25–27, especially in highly censored data, high-dimension data or for data with covariate multicollinearity28. In this study, there was a relatively large number of covariables, thus the neural network model was adopted. On the other hand, a nonlinear model can reduce the influence of detection errors or noise in gene expression. Considering that the size of our breast tissue transcriptome sample was only 15 which was small, we were unable to establish an effective ANN model to classify breast carcinoma utilizing existing transcriptome data. To address this limitation, we utilized transcriptome data in breast tissue from the TCGA and GTEx database to identify DECRGs and machine learning algorithms were used to screen out hub DECRGs. Notably, compared with the healthy group, the genes encoding the 11 hub DECRGs were significantly differentially expressed in breast tissues from the breast tumor group transcriptome profiles, suggesting the potential correlation of 11 signature molecules with breast cancer. In this study, an ANN model consisting of 11 hub DECRGs obtained by 5 machine learning algorithms was constructed for the first time to efficiently distinguish breast carcinoma and healthy populations. We used our own breast tissue transcriptome sample data as a validation set. The 11 hub DECRGs exhibited AUCs of 0.997 and 0.950 for the training and validation cohorts, respectively. Multiple cross-validation methods showed that the accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity of the model were good.

The AUC value of the ANN diagnostic model in the training set and external verification set is high, which reflects the superior performance of the diagnostic model in this paper, and also proves that the ANN model can deal with highly censored data, high-dimensional data or covariate multicollinearity. data, outperform other models. We also calculated the AUC values of diagnostic prediction for 11 genes included in the diagnostic model. The results showed that the AUC values of F12, MMP11, ANXA1, RABIF, GSN, and CFD for breast tissue identification were higher than 0.9, showing high specificity and sensitivity. This also confirms the superiority of ANN model.

In addition, based on 44 DECRGs detected in the TCGA-BRCA cohort, we established a prognostic model associated with OS based upon 14 of these CRGs (C3, CFH, CFI, CLU, F10, F2RL2, HMGCS2, HPN, LRP1, MAFF, MMP9, PROS1, RABIF, and RGN) using univariate and lasso regression algorithm. These 14 genes have been shown to be associated with survival outcomes in many cancers. The lectin pathway and the C3a/C3aR signaling pathway have been established as key mediators of complement activity and the macrophage-driven induction of sarcoma growth and immunosuppressive activity29. CFH-DCLK1 nanoparticles can induce apoptotic colon cancer cell death while preventing migratory activity and invasivity30. The CFI has also been tied to soft tissue sarcoma patient OS and chemosensitivity31. Coagulation factor X (FX) upregulation associated with lung metastatic progression has been noted in lung interstitial macrophages following chemotherapy administration32. HMGCS2 can inhibit prostate cancer growth, thereby predicting the odds of recurrence in patients with this disease33. LRP1/LRP8 acts to promote the metastatic progression of melanoma and angiogenic activity34. MAFF can activate the IL11/STAT3 pathway in BC, and patients with metastatic disease exhibit particularly high levels of MAFF expression35. MMP9 controls colorectal cancer metastasis to the liver36, whereas RGN inhibits cellular proliferation and cancer development37.

These CRGs were used to establish a prognostic model. The association between risk gauged with this model and clinicopathological characteristics, immune microenvironment was examined. Overall, this model was found to effectively predict OS in BC, demonstrating comparatively improved performance in both internal and external datasets, and significant differences in risk score values were noted among different TNM stages, tumor stages. As expected, with high risk scores in BC patients suffering from more advanced disease. In univariate and multivariate cox analyses, risk score was confirmed to independently predict survival, it was used to construct a survival-related nomogram. The nomogram exhibited superior calibration curve and C-index results as compared to individual predictors, validating its prognostic examination. Immune cell profiles in tumor samples in the TCGA-BRCA cohort were also examined, revealing significant differences in immunological microenvironment composition between the low-risk and high-risk BC cohorts. The risk scores can also predict the anti-CTLA-4 treatment outcomes. The TIDE analysis results confirmed the utility of risk scores differentiating immunotherapy responses. The model we identified has the potential to be an active architect of immunosuppressive TME. Among them, tumor cells with high expression of procoagulant CRGs such as tissue factor F3 activate the coagulation system, leading to local thrombosis and platelet activation. Activated platelets don’t just form a physical barrier, they release a large number of immune-regulating molecules. Activation of the coagulation system eventually leads to the conversion of fibrinogen into fibrin, which is deposited in large quantities around the tumor. The complement system is a key part of innate immunity and can mediate the lysis of tumor cells. Tumor cells escape this attack by upregulating certain CRGs. In addition, core proteases in the coagulation cascade, such as thrombin, can activate receptors (PARs, especially PAR-1 and PAR-2) by cleavage activating proteases, which are widely expressed on tumor cells, immune cells, and stromal cells. These mechanisms may provide a molecular bridge linking the enzymatic activity of thrombin to direct cellular signal transduction and immunosuppression. This model also predicted drug sensitivity profiles for BC patients, enriching its clinical utility. In summary, this risk scoring model provides an effective tool that may better guide the prognostic assessment and treatment monitoring of individuals suffering from BC.

Before us, Lauritzen AD38 constructed BC diagnostic signatures based on a diagnostic artificial intelligence (AI) AI system and mammographic texture model. The model combining a commercially available diagnostic AI system for lesion detection to estimate short-term risk and a mammographic texture model for long-term risk. At 90% specificity, the combination model predicted future cancers with a sensitivity of 36.5% (629 of 1721 patients [95% CI: 34.3, 38.9]). The combination model that additionally included age and percentage mammographic density yielded an AUC of 0.72 (95% CI: 0.71, 0.74). We first used the transcriptome sequencing data of breast tissue collected in our hospital to clarify the correlation between coagulation pathway and differential genes in benign and malignant breast tissues. Then designed a BC diagnostic model by utilizing artificial neural network algorithm, the ROC values of model we established indicated that the CRGs diagnostic model outperformed the model above in terms of predictive efficacy. Lei39, and Ren40 constructed risk prognostic signatures for survival prediction in BC patients based on CRGs (SERPINA1, SERPINF2, C1S, CFB, RASGRP1, TLN2), and vasculogenic mimicry genes (KCND2, NRP1, VSTM4), respectively. Lei retrieved mRNA data for breast cancer and normal breast tissue from the TCGA-BRCA cohort and the GTEx database. Models were constructed by multivariable Cox regression analysis and then validated using the GSE42568 dataset as a test set. Nomograms with clinical factors were constructed to enhance the predictive power of the model. It observed that risk score was mainly associated with tumorigenesis, inflammation, immune responses, and cellular apoptosis by functional enrichment analysis of the two risk groups, an association between the risk score and the tumor environment has been indicated. Ren et al. further performed a more comprehensive analysis of complement and coagulation cascade pathways of BC, and carried out in vitro experiments. A prognostic risk model was designed by screening biomarkers using DESeq2 and WGCNA based on public TNBC transcriptome data. The ROC values of the prognosis model we established outperformed above models. We retrieved mRNA data the TCGA-BRCA cohort and the GTEx database. Models were constructed by LASSO regression and then validated using the GSE42568 dataset as a test set. Nomograms with clinical factors were constructed to enhance the predictive power of the model. We not only performed immune infiltration and TME analysis, but we also predicted immunotherapy by IPS and TIDE score in high- and low-risk patients, and explored their biological mechanisms. Furthermore, the corresponding proteins’ expression levels were investigated through IHC analysis. Our research is not only incremental improvements in AUC, but also the first literature to establish a diagnostic and prognostic model for breast cancer and coagulation-related genes at the same time, and uses an artificial neural network algorithm. Our method is more comprehensive and the proposed diagnostic and prognostic model has better performance.

When evaluating the diagnostic performance of the 14 different CRGs incorporated into the risk scoring model, RABIF was found to be the most reliable predictor. RAB proteins are small GTPases that regulate vesicular transport and function as central hubs for membrane transport. RAB GTPase regulates membrane trafficking by switching between two different states: bound guanosine triphosphate (GTP, active) and bound guanosine diphosphate (GDP, inactive). Active RAB-GTPs are located on cell membranes and recruit various effectors to mediate membrane trafficking. In the human genome, more than 60 RAB members have been identified, suggesting the existence of a complex membrane transport regulatory system. Many RAB GTPases have not been characterized in detail.

Encoded on chromosome 1q32.1, RAB interacting factor (RABIF), also known as mammalian Section 4 suppressor (MSS4), and its yeast homologue Section 4 dominant suppressor (DSS4) were first isolated from RAB-GEF. RABIF has previously been reported to be targeted by miR-491-5p in triple-negative BC, thereby reducing RABIF-mediated cell invasivity, therapeutic resistance, and lung metastatic progression such that high RABIF levels are predictive of poorer outcomes in these patients41. The expression of RABIF were highly in tumors in the TCGA-BRCA cohort, the same as in GEO datasets. Higher RABIF expression was also detected in BC cell lines as compared to normal breast cells, and it was expressed at particularly high levels in advanced BC samples. Accordingly, RABIF may represent a robust promoter of BC invasivity and metastatic progression. The link between RABIF levels and the tumor immune microenvironment was probed at length. This approach revealed the downregulation of RABIF in the BC-associated TME. TIME analyses suggested a link between RABIF somatic copy number variations and infiltration by various immune cell types, potentially owing to the highly heterogeneous nature and high mutation rates of BC. RABIF expression was also found to be related to drug sensitivity of targeted, chemotherapeutic, and endocrine drug treatment. As such, RABIF may act as a central regulator of BC onset and progression, a promising target for diagnosing and treating this deadly form of cancer.

In summary, we first determined the genetic expression of breast tissue in breast cancer and healthy individuals utilizing RNA-seq, determined that coagulation pathway significantly enriched higher in tumor tissue. We discovered 11-CRGs by machine learning algorithms. The ANN model based 11-CRGs showed satisfactory classification ability for distinguish healthy from BC patients. This model provides novel insight into diagnose BC. Moreover, a prognostic CRGs-based model and corresponding nomogram were herein developed based on publically available transcriptomic sequencing data for BC patients. This model was further used to examine its associations with tumor-related immunity and drug responses. Overall, this model outperformed tumor staging for predicting outcomes, confirming the relevance of CRGs for the prognostic evaluation, immune monitoring, and individualized management of BC. In addition, we explored the expression of RABIF in mammary gland cell lines and tissues, suggesting that it may influence the onset and metastatic progression of BC, it represents a promising diagnostic and therapeutic target. Overall, these findings offer a foundation for future studies aimed at clarifying the mechanistic basis for these results.

Even so, there are certain limitations in our research. Firstly, In the combined TCGA and GTEx dataset, while we employed removeBatchEffect and demonstrated its efficacy in mitigating major batch biases through PCA and quantitative metrics, we acknowledge that residual, unmeasured confounding factors may persist. The integration of publicly available datasets, despite correction, remains a challenge. Thus, the conclusions drawn from the combined analysis should be interpreted with this technical consideration in mind. And the number of validation sets of the diagnostic and prognosis models are small, overfitting may occur. The diagnostic model produced extreme predicted probabilities for a few samples. In small samples, such extremes can disproportionately influence the calculation of the CI, leading to an overestimation of its width. Second, our observations are needed to be corroborated by multicenter and prospective clinical studies. The current research lacks a larger independent cohort, and the clinical applicability of ANN models is uncertain. Thirdly, we demonstrated that 14 CRGs are associated with prognosis in BC, but this was evaluated solely by data mining. More investigations are needed to reveal these genes’ functions, and the association between CRGs and the development of BC needs to be further explored. RABIF is a promising therapeutic target, the mechanistic link between RABIF and coagulation pathways in BC progression remains underexplored. It is currently unclear whether RABIF directly regulates the expression of key coagulation factors (e.g., F3, F8, SERPIN family members) or indirectly influences pro-thrombotic pathways through broader modulation of cellular secretion, cytokine signaling, or tumor microenvironment crosstalk. This constitutes a key limitation of the present study and a primary direction for our future research. The IHC validation of RABIF expression was conducted on a small pilot cohort. While the staining patterns are consistent with our transcriptomic findings, studies with larger, independent cohorts and vivo validation are required to conclusively determine its prognostic value across different disease stages. Fourthly, the drug sensitivity predictions presented, including the association between the CRG risk score and response to anti-CTLA-4 therapy, are derived from computational models (e.g., TIDE). While these algorithms are well-established and provide valuable mechanistic insights, they are not a substitute for validation in prospective clinical trials or well-annotated retrospective cohorts with actual treatment response data. This represents a limitation of our current study. Hence, above two models require further in-depth and reliable research before clinical applications.

Materials and methods

Data processing

The transcriptome profile and corresponding clinical information for BC samples were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA-BRCA, http://protal.gdc.cancer.gov/). Furthermore, we obtained additional normal breast Tissue samples from the Genotype-Tissue Expression database (https://gtexportal.org). For the data format, the fragments per kilobase million (FPKM) were transformed into transcripts per million (TPM). Further, the tissue sequencing data and corresponding clinical information were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO database) (GSE42568, GSE109169, GSE115144, GSE15852, GSE22796, GSE29431, GSE38959) (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/).

Bioinformatics analyses

First, we retrieved the CRGs from the MsigDB database (HALLMARK_COAGULATION, http://software.broadinstitute.org/gsea/msigdb), and the detailed information on the 137 CRGs were shown in the Supplementary Table S342. To address the inherent batch effects between TCGA and GTEx datasets, we applied the removeBatchEffect function from the limma R package, specifying the data source (TCGA or GTEx) as the batch covariate. The effectiveness of batch effect correction was rigorously assessed by comparing PCA plots and quantitative metrics (e.g., PVCA and Silhouette Scores) before and after adjustment (see Results). The ‘limma’ R package was condemned to screen out the differentially expressed CRGs (DECRGs) between BC and normal samples, based on the set cutoff criteria of P < 0.05 and log2 FC > 2 in TCGA and GTEx cohorts. To screen genes associated with the diagnostic model, five widely used machine learning algorithms were used: LASSO regression analyses, linear SVM, XGBoost, Gradient boosting machine and random forest, and the detailed information on the 11 genes were shown in the Supplementary Table S4. To comprehensively analyse the basic functions and participating pathways of the DEGs, Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis results were performed with the R ‘clusterProfiler’ package for DECRGs of interest. Geneset enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using GSEA software (version 4.2.1; https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp) with the c2.cp.kegg.v7.1.symbols.gmt gene set (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/index.jsp)43,44. The results of these analyses were visualized with the R ‘ggplot2’ package. Interactions among genes were predicted with GeneMANIA (http://genemania.org/), which was also used for analyses of functional pathways. The immune cell profiles of TCGA cohort samples were evaluated with the CIBERSORT algorithm45,46. Immune cell infiltration of the TME was further assessed with the ESTIMATE algorithm, which was used to calculate immune scores for tumor samples with the R ‘ESTIMATE’ package. Immunophenotype score (IPS) values were also calculated for all samples from the Cancer Immunome Atlas (TCIA) database, which provides resources that can enable the prediction of patient responses to anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 antibody treatment. IPS scores for TCGA-BRCA patients in different risk groups were evaluated to explore the association between risk scores and IPS. The R ‘oncoPredict’ package was also used to predict drug responses for each sample based on the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) database (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/).

Construction of an ANN diagnostic model of DECRGs

We applied TCGA and GTEx cohorts as a training dataset to construct an ANN diagnostic model based on the ‘neuralnet’ R package[47]. We used samples’ transcriptome data as a validation dataset to verify the classification efficiency of the diagnostic model score constructed with gene expression and gene weight. We scored the genes associated with the diagnostic model. According to the median value of each gene, if the gene was up-regulated, its expression in the sample will be 0 if it was less than the median value, and 1 if it was greater than the median value. Similarly, when the gene was down-regulated, the expression level in the sample was recorded as 1 when it was less than the median value, and 0 when it was greater than the median value so that the score of each gene in each sample can be obtained. Then, according to the obtained gene score, we constructed our diagnostic neural network. The input layer was composed of the scores and weights of feature genes, the number of neurons in the input layer was 11, contains 2 hidden layers, the function to construct the neural network model is “neuralnet”. Then, the output layer was deduced according to the nodes and weights of the hidden layer, to judge whether the sample belongs to the cancer or normal tissue, thus judging the sample attributes. Then we drew the ROC curve to assess the accuracy of the ANN diagnostic model in predicting sample attributes.The prediction function of the ANN model is “compute”.

Dropout is a powerful regularization technique used in artificial neural networks to prevent overfitting and improve generalization. It is a layer that randomly “drops out” (sets to zero) a fraction of neurons during training. For each training example in a batch. Randomly select neurons to disable (typically 20–50%). Only update weights of active neurons. Forces network to develop redundant representations. During testing: all neurons remain active. Weights are scaled by the dropout probability (or alternatively, activations are scaled up).

LASSO regression analyses of DECRGs for prognostic signature

Univariate Cox regression analyses were used to detect DECRGs for incorporation into subsequent LASSO regression analyses for prognostic signature. To assess the prognostic utility of the DECRGs in BC patients, the R ‘glmnet’ package was used to conduct a LASSO regression analysis focused on BC patient overall survival (OS) (maxit = 1000). For this analysis, 70% of the BC patient samples in the TCGA cohort were selected at random as a training cohort, while the remainder and samples from the GSE42568 dataset served as validation groups. A risk score for each patient was computed with a formula established as follows: risk score = coffiCRG1 × CRG1 expression + coffiCRG2 × CRG2 expression +· ···· + coffiCRGn × CRGn expression. Kaplan-Meier survival curves for low-risk and high-risk patients were generated with the R ‘survival’ and ‘survminer’ packages, and consistency index (C-index) and time-varying ROC curve values were used to assess model predictive performance. The ability of risk scores and clinicopathological characteristics to serve as independent predictors of BC patient prognosis was assessed through univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses. A predictive nomogram was further constructed based on risk scores and other identified independent prognostic factors using the R ‘rms’ package, after which the predictive accuracy of this model was assessed with calibration curves.

Cross-validation of the diagnostic and the prognostic model

10-fold cross-validation was performed: the training data were randomly and equally divided into 10 sub-samples, 9 of which were used to train the prediction models, and the remaining one served as the validation dataset. This procedure was repeated until each sub-sample had been used as the validation set. 10-fold cross validation may reduce the risk of overfitting and bias. We also performed internal cross-validation in LOOCV, and Boostrap. The use of LOOCV is particularly valuable for assessing the performance of artificial intelligence models, as it systematically excludes one data point from the training set, using it as a validation or test instance. Subsequently, a predictive value is generated for the excluded data, and this process is repeated as many times as elements are in the training set. Finally, the predicted values for each excluded data point are compared with the observed values, allowing for a rigorous evaluation of model performance. The Bootstrap method creates multiple “new” datasets for model validation by repeatedly sampling with replacement from the original dataset. Randomly select n samples (with replacement) from the original dataset (n samples). Samples that were not selected form a validation set (approximately 37% of the original data). Train the model on bootstrap samples. Repeat this process multiple times (usually 100–1000 times). In addition to this internal cross validation, the prognostic CRGs risk signature were also externally validated using data from GSE42568. Assessments of accuracy, kappa statistic, sensitivity, specificity, and AUC were performed to evaluate the discriminatory ability of the identification and prognostic models.

Transcriptome sequencing, RT-qPCR and immunohistochemical staining

Breast tissues were collected from 10 breast carcinoma patients and 5 breast healthy individuals. The breast samples were resected under local anaesthesia, the breast tissue was washed with normal saline and stored at -80 °C until used for analysis47. Then, Total RNA was extracted, RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) was conducted. Firstly, total mRNA was fragmented into small pieces and synthesized cDNA. Secondly, PCR amplification was carried out to enrich and purify double-stranded DNA. Thirdly, the amplified products were sequenced using Illumina Novaseq. The sequencing data is presented in Supplementary Material 1.

An RNA-Quick kit was used to extract total RNA from control MCF-10 A cells and from various BC cell lines (MDA-MB-468, BT-474, MDA-MB-231, BT-549), followed by the preparation of cDNA from 1 µg of RNA with a Fast All-in-One kit and subsequent qPCR analyses for RABIF and GAPDH expression with 2x SYBR Green Master Mix and a Roche Light Cycler 480s (Roche Diagnostics, Germany). For details regarding the utilized primers, see Supplementary Table S5.

Immunohistochemical (IHC) staining for RABIF was performed using samples from 5 patients with and 5 patients without distant metastatic disease, BC tissue samples were collected from patients undergoing surgical procedures in Zhejiang Provincial People’s Hospital between 2022 and 2023. Briefly, tissue sections were deparaffinized and cut to a thickness of 4 μm, after which endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched and normal goat serum was applied for blocking. Samples were then probed overnight with anti-RABIF (A15090, 1:300, ABclonal) at 4 °C, followed by three washes with PBS and incubation with appropriate enzyme-conjugated secondary antibodies at room temperature. Then, 3,3′-diaminobenzidine was used for color development. Two experienced pathologists who were blinded to the clinical data independently scored each tissue sample based on the percentage of tumor cells stained and staining intensity, range from 0 to 3. The scoring distribution is defined as the following: 0–1 negative staining, 2–3 positive staining.

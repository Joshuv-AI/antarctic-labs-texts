Immunogenicity and safety of an inactivated COVID-19 vaccine (CoronaVac®) co-administered with an inactivated enterovirus type 71 vaccine (Inlive®): A phase 4, randomized, controlled trial
Authors: Yajun Shu, Zhuoqun Sun, Fan Gao, Zhuhang Huang, Xing Meng, Shaomin Chen, Qun Shu, Lianhao Wang, Hengming Zhang, Zhifang Ying, Jikai Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis study aimed to provide evidence for co-administration of the COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines in children aged 3 to 5 years. We conducted a phase 4, open-label, randomized, and controlled study. A total of 520 children were randomly allocated to two groups: Group C received the COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines simultaneously, while Group N received the two vaccines separately. The primary immunogenicity endpoints were the seroconversion rates of anti-SARS-CoV-2 and anti-EV71 antibodies 28 days after the second dose of each respective vaccine. Before vaccination, none of the participants was seropositive for anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies, and the baseline seropositivity for anti-EV71 antibodies was low (Group C: 14.86%; Group N: 17.83%). After completing full vaccination, the seroconversion rate of anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies reached 100% in both groups, while for anti-EV71 antibodies, the seroconversion rates were 97.99% in Group C and 98.70% in Group N. The lower limit of the 95% confidence interval for the difference in seroconversion rates between the two groups for both COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines met the predefined non-inferiority criteria. Six months post-vaccination, the antibody levels remained high for both vaccines, with the seropositive rates of anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies at 91.21% in Group C and 92.77% in Group N, and the seropositive rates of anti-EV71 antibodies at 99.16% in Group C and 99.15% in Group N. Safety analysis revealed a lower incidence of adverse reactions in Group C compared to Group N (28.85% vs 45.56%), primarily solicited. Co-administration of the COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines demonstrated a positive safety profile and non-inferior immune responses.Trial registration NumberNCT04993365 (ClinicalTrials.gov).

Introduction

The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) is caused by the severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). As of March 17, 2024, the pandemic has resulted in over 775 million confirmed COVID-19 cases and more than 7.05 million fatalities worldwide,1 placing a significant burden on the healthcare system and the economy globally.2 SARS-CoV-2 primarily spreads through respiratory droplets and close contact with infected individuals.3 The median incubation period is approximately 4–6 days, and the most common symptoms include fever, cough, chill, and sore throat.3–5 While children constitute a minority of COVID-19 cases and typically exhibit asymptomatic or mild symptoms,6–8 part of them (15 ~ 20%) still require hospitalization and intensive care.9 Furthermore, children play a crucial role in the transmission of SARS-CoV-2.8 The implementation of COVID-19 vaccination for children has proven beneficial in safeguarding against severe cases, mitigating community transmission, and reducing the emergence of new variants.6

Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD), caused by enterovirus 71 (EV71) infection, is a common childhood infectious disease worldwide, with a higher incidence among children under 5 years old.10 China stands as one of the countries with the highest incidence of HFMD, with reported incidence rates ranging from 37.01/100,000 to 205.06/100,000, along with mortality rates ranging from 6.46/100,000 to 51.00/100,000 nationwide.11,12 In most cases, patients exhibit mild symptoms such as fever, rash, or blisters on the hands, soles, feet, and buttocks, and recover within a week without sequelae. However, a small proportion of children may suffer central nervous system (CNS) injury, mainly including brainstem encephalitis, encephalomyelitis, and cerebrospinal meningitis. Children progressing to neurogenic pulmonary edema or circulatory failure face a high mortality risk.12 Until now, there is no specific treatment for HFMD, and management primarily relies on supportive therapy.12,13 EV71 vaccination has proven to be the most effective method to prevent and control HFMD.13 The EV71 vaccine, developed by Sinovac Biotech Co. Ltd, was licensed in China in 2018 with an efficacy of 94.8% during one year of surveillance,14 and maintained a robust seropositive rate of 94.3% after a five-year follow-up.15

Co-administration of vaccines has the potential to enhance vaccine coverage rates, optimize time management, and economize costs. In this study, we evaluated the immunogenicity and safety of co-administering the COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines in children aged 3 to 5 years, to provide data supporting broad implementation and potentially inform modifications to relevant guidelines and adjustments to the national immunization strategies.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

From November 2021 to July 2022, a phase 4 open-label, randomized, controlled trial was conducted in Yangchun City, Guangdong Province, China. Healthy children aged 3 to 5 years, with informed consent of their guardians, were enrolled. The study excluded participants who had been in contact with a confirmed COVID-19 case within the preceding 14 days; prior vaccination with SARS-CoV-2 or EV71 vaccines; history of SARS-CoV-2 infection, multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children (MIS-C), HFMD, or herpangina; known allergy or severe adverse reactions to any study vaccine components; congenital malformations or developmental disorders, genetic defects, or severe malnutrition. Additionally, participants who had received blood products in the last three months, any other study products in the preceding four weeks, live attenuated vaccines in the past two weeks, or subunit/inactivated vaccines within the last seven days before screening were not eligible for participation.

In this study, participants were randomly assigned to two groups with a ratio of 1:1. Specifically, the co-administration group (Group C) simultaneously received two doses of the COVID-19 and the EV71 vaccines, following a 0, 28-day schedule. Conversely, the non-co-administration group (Group N) received their first dose of the COVID-19 vaccine on day 0, followed by the first dose of the EV71 vaccine on day 14, the second dose of the COVID-19 vaccine on day 28, and the second dose of the EV71 vaccine on day 42. Both vaccines were administered intramuscularly into the deltoid region of the upper arm. The primary endpoints were the seroconversion rates of anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies and anti-EV71 antibodies 28 days after the second dose of each respective vaccine.

The trial adhered to Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval for the trial was obtained from the Ethics Committee of the Guangdong Provincial Center for Disease Prevention and Control (Approval Number: 2021V006-F). Before screening, written informed consent was obtained from each child’s guardian.

Study vaccines

The inactivated COVID-19 vaccine, CoronaVac® (lot 202108151E), manufactured by Sinovac Life Science Co., Ltd, was formulated through the inoculation of African green monkey kidney cells with SARS-CoV-2 (strain CZ02, lot CZ01-W-202002-01). It was presented as a milky-white suspension, with inactivated SARS-CoV-2 as the primary component, along with aluminum hydroxide, sodium hydrogen phosphate, sodium dihydrogen phosphate, and sodium chloride as excipients. The vaccine was packaged in vials, with each 0.5 mL dose containing 600 SU of SARS-CoV-2 antigen.

The inactivated enterovirus type 71 vaccine, Inlive® (lot 2,020,010,018), produced by Sinovac Biotech Co., Ltd, was developed via inoculating Vero cells with EV71 (subgenotype C4, strain H07). The contents consisted of inactivated EV71 virus, aluminum hydroxide, sodium chloride, sodium dihydrogen phosphate, disodium hydrogen phosphate, and sterile water for injection. Packaged in pre-filled syringes, each 0.5 mL dosage contains a minimum neutralization antibody titer of 3.0 Efficacy Units (EU) against EV71. The two vaccines were certified and authorized for lot release by the Chinese National Institute for Food and Drug Control.

Immunogenicity assessment

Blood samples were collected on day 0, day 56, and 6 months after full vaccination (two doses of the COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines) in Group C, and on day 0, day 56, day 70, and 6 months after the second dose of the EV71 vaccine in Group N. Anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies and anti-EV71 antibodies were assessed via micro cytopathogenic effect assay by the National Institutes for Food and Drug Control. For anti-SARS-COV-2 antibodies testing, serum samples were inactivated at 56°C for 30 min and serially diluted two-fold, and then incubated with 100 CCID50 virus at 37°C for 2 h. The neutralizing titer was determined by identifying the highest dilution of serum that still caused a 50% decrease in cytopathic effects (CPE), with the titer being expressed as the reciprocal of this dilution. For anti-EV71 antibodies, serum samples were also inactivated at 56°C for 30 min, serially diluted two-fold from 1:8 and incubated with 100 CCID50 virus at 37°C for 2 h. The resulting mixture was then added to rhabdomyosarcoma (RD) cells. The neutralizing antibody titer of the serum sample was determined as follows: (1) If one of the two wells at the highest dilution showed CPE while the other did not, the reciprocal of this dilution was the neutralizing antibody titer; (2) If both wells at a higher dilution exhibited complete CPE while both wells at the adjacent lower dilution showed no CPE, the reciprocal of the average dilution between these two was the titer; (3) If one well showed CPE and the other did not at two adjacent dilutions, the reciprocal of the average dilution between these two dilutions represented the EV71 neutralizing antibody titer of the serum sample.

The indicators analyzed include seropositive rates, seroconversion rate, geometric mean titer (GMT), and geometric mean increase (GMI). Seroconversion was defined as either: a) neutralizing antibody titers <1:8 pre-vaccination increasing to ≥1:8 post-vaccination, or b) titers ≥1:8 pre-vaccination with a ≥ 4-fold increase post-vaccination. The timing of the seroconversion rate assessment for Group C was at D56 (28 days post-vaccination of both COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines), and for Group N, it was at D56 for anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibody and D70 for anti-EV71 antibody.

Safety assessment

Safety endpoints including adverse reactions (ARs) within 7 days after each dose, ARs between the first dose and 28 days after full vaccination, and severe adverse events (SAEs) and adverse events of special interest (AESI) from the first dose until 6 months after full vaccination. Solicited AEs were defined as local symptoms including pain, induration, swelling, redness, rash, and pruritus at injection site, and systemic symptoms comprised fever (axillary temperature), acute allergic reaction, abnormality skin and mucosa, diarrhea, anorexia, vomiting, nausea, muscle pain, headache, cough, and fatigue. AESI included Bell’s palsy, sudden deafness/hearing loss, brachial plexus/multifocal neuropathy, Guillain-Barré syndrome, thrombocytopenic purpura, allergic purpura, thrombosis, myelitis, myocarditis, immune thrombocytopenia, seizures, and MIS-C. The correlation between AEs and the administered vaccines was determined by investigators.

Sample size assessment

The sample size for a non-inferiority design was calculated using NCSS-PASS (version 21.0.2). The non-inferiority criterion (95% CI lower limit for rate difference ≥-10%) was in accordance with the Technical Guidelines for Clinical Comparability Studies of Vaccines for Preventive Use, issued by the National Medical Products Administration of China. If the 28-day post-vaccination neutralizing antibody seroconversion rates for both COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines met the non-inferiority criterion, the study would be considered successful. To achieve an overall power of 80%, a power of 90% (calculated as 1-β) was used to calculate the sample size needed for each non-inferiority test (seroconversion rates of anti-SARS-CoV-2 and anti-EV71 antibodies). Considering a 90% seroconversion rate for both antibodies in Group N, a one-sided α of 0.025 and a non-inferiority margin of 10%, a sample size of 205 participants per group was determined to be necessary. Accounting for a 20% dropout rate, a total of 520 participants were eventually enrolled in this study.

Statistical analysis

The immunogenicity analysis was based on the per-protocol set (PPS), which comprised participants who fulfilled the eligibility criteria, completed full vaccination for COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines, adhered strictly to the protocol, and exhibited valid neutralizing antibody titers against SARS-CoV-2 (PPS1) and EV71 (PPS2) at baseline and 28 days after vaccination. Besides, the immune persistence was independently evaluated for fully vaccinated COVID-19 and EV71 participants, both of whom possessed valid neutralizing antibody titers 6 months post-vaccination. The safety analysis set (SS) comprised participants who received at least one dose of either COVID-19 or EV71 vaccine. A covariance analysis model was employed to analyze the GMTs of neutralizing antibodies 28 days post-vaccination. The model featured post-vaccination log-transformed GMTs as the dependent variable, pre-vaccination log-transformed GMTs as a covariate, and group (Group C, Group N) as a fixed factor. The model estimated the post-vaccination GMTs for each group, the GMT ratio between Group C and Group N, along with their respective 95% CIs, reversing the logarithmic transformation. For the comparison of continuous variables, the log-transformed Student’s t-test was utilized, while categorical variables were assessed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. The two-sided 95% CI was calculated employing the Clopper–Pearson method, whereas the Miettinen–Nurminen method was applied to determine 95% CI for the rate difference between Group C and Group N. All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS (version 9.4).

Antibody responses after vaccination.

Before vaccination, the seropositive rates against EV71 in Group C and Group N were 14.86% (95% CI: 10.68–19.90) and 17.83% (95% CI: 13.11–23.40), respectively. The GMTs of neutralizing antibodies titer were 7.10 and 7.11, using a seropositivity cutoff of 1:8. No significant difference was found between the two groups in seropositivity or GMTs, with p = .380 and .992, respectively. After full vaccination, both groups achieved 100% seropositivity. The seroconversion rates were 97.99% (95% CI: 95.38–99.34) for Group C and 98.70% (95% CI: 96.24–99.73) for Group N, yielding a difference of -0.70% (95% CI, -3.48, 1.99), which surpassed the non-inferiority margin of -10%. Participants in Group N exhibited a higher GMT (218.73 vs 339.63, p = .0014) and GMI (30.82 vs 47.79, p < .001). Six months following full vaccination, the seropositive rates (99.16% for Group C and 99.15% for Group N, p = 1.000) and GMTs (80.14 for Group C and 106.83 for Group N, p = .060) were comparable between groups.

The ARs were mainly solicited, both incidences of solicited and unsolicited ARs were lower in Group C compared with Group N (26.54% vs 44.02% for solicited ARs and 6.92% vs 12.36% for unsolicited ARs), whereas the frequency of local ARs was comparable between the two groups (p = .768). The most commonly reported symptom in both groups was injection site pain, occurring at a rate of 7.69% (20/260) in Group C and 8.11% (21/259) in Group N. Regarding solicited systemic ARs, Group C exhibited a significantly lower incidence compared to Group N (19.23% vs 40.93%). Among those, cough and fever were the most common symptoms, with cough affecting 10.38% (27/260) in Group C and 20.85% (54/259) in Group N, while fever occurred in 10.38% (27/260) of Group C and 24.71% (64/259) of Group N (p = .001 and <0.001, respectively). In total, 16 SAEs were reported in 13 participants, with 5 in Group C experiencing 7 SAEs and 8 in Group N experiencing 9 SAEs (p = .418). To be noted, all SAEs were considered unrelated to the study vaccines.

Discussion

The high prevalence of HFMD among children poses a great burden on public health in China. Vaccination has been proven to be the most effective approach for disease prevention.13 Although COVID-19 typically presents mild clinical symptoms in children, the occurrence of MIS-C and long COVID raises additional concern.6 A systematic review reported that nearly one-quarter of pediatric patients experience long COVID symptoms.16 Vaccination can protect children from acute COVID-19 and its long-term consequences. Co-administration of vaccines streamlines vaccination processes and contributes to improved public health outcomes. By administering different vaccines during a single clinic visit, it eliminates logistical barriers that can hinder vaccination efforts. Specifically, this approach not only reduces the need for parents to schedule multiple appointments, thereby enhancing compliance and resulting in higher vaccination coverage rates, but also alleviates pressure on healthcare systems and enables more efficient use of healthcare resources. Moreover, the higher coverage rate of vaccination can effectively reduce the burden caused by the vaccine-preventable diseases, leading to fewer hospitalizations, reduced absences from work or school, and ultimately, savings in healthcare costs and societal resources. Therefore, in this study, we evaluated the immunogenicity and safety of co-administering the COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines. The results showed that co-administering these two vaccines exhibits a favorable safety profile and results in non-inferior immune responses for both SARS-CoV-2 and EV71.

Before vaccination, all participants were baseline negative of anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies, reflecting a universal susceptibility to SARS-CoV-2 among children aged 3 to 5. Similarly, the seropositive rate of anti-EV71 antibodies was low, at 14.86% in Group C and 17.83% in Group N, suggesting limited immunity against EV71 in this age group. Therefore, promoting universal vaccination of COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines in pediatric population is crucial for reducing their risk of infection and preventing potential health threats.

After vaccination, the seroconversion rate of anti-SARS-CoV-2 antibodies reached 100% in both groups, indicating a robust immune response to inactivated COVID-19 vaccine. Furthermore, the anti-EV71 seroconversion rates increased significantly to 97.99% in Group C and 98.70% in Group N. Notably, the difference in seroconversion rates of SARS-CoV-2 and EV71 neutralizing antibodies between the two groups met the predefined non-inferiority criterion, suggesting that co-administration of the vaccines elicits comparable immune responses to separate administrations. While Group C experienced a moderate decline in the EV71 immune response 28 days post-vaccination, no significant differences were observed between the groups at 6 months. Our findings regarding the EV71 immune response were in line with previous research. Specifically, in a phase 3 trial, the GMT of neutralizing antibodies titer was 165.8;14 in a lot consistency study, the GMT was 142.7;17 and in an immune persistence study, the GMT was 97.31, which still maintained good persistence of the immune response over 5 years15—all measured at 28 days post-vaccination. Importantly, co-administration did not compromise the immune response to the COVID-19 vaccine, as evidenced by consistent results at 28 days and 6 months post-vaccination.

For safety, the overall incidence of ARs was significantly higher in Group N (45.56% vs 28.85%, p < .001), reflecting a satisfactory safety profile for co-administration. The majority of ARs were graded 1 or 2, occurring within 7 days, with cough and fever being the most common symptoms. Additionally, no SAE related to study vaccine was reported in this study. Consistent with previous research,18 instead of increasing the risk of vaccination, co-administration enhanced safety and decreased the overall incidence of ARs.

However, there are a few limitations to this study. Firstly, our evaluation was limited to the 6-month immunogenicity and safety of co-administration of the COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines. Although a previous study has demonstrated the favorable immune persistence of the EV71 vaccine, long-term studies are necessary to assess the immune persistence of the co-administration of these vaccines.15 Secondly, due to differences in vaccination schedules between the groups, this study was unable to employ a blind design. Consequently, this limitation had the potential to introduce bias in the assessment of AEs. Additionally, only neutralizing antibodies (via micro cytopathogenic effect assay) were evaluated in this study, while IgG assay was not incorporated. However, our previous research evaluated both neutralizing antibodies and anti-receptor-binding domain (anti-RBD) IgG antibodies in adults aged 18–59 years and revealed a robust correlation between these two measures.19 Lastly, this study only involved children aged 3 to 5 years, further exploration encompassing populations with a broader age range is needed.

Conclusions

The co-administration of the COVID-19 and EV71 vaccines in children aged 3 to 5 years demonstrated a favorable safety profile and elicited a robust immune response against both SARS-CoV-2 and EV71. Given the benefits of this approach, further promotion and implementation of this co-administration strategy among children aged 3 to 5 years should be considered.

Bone SPECT/CT Findings of Complications Resulting From Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation With Defibrillation
Authors: Myoung Hyoun Kim, Dae-Weung Kim
Published: 2023

A 44-year-old man presented to the emergency department with chest pain. During the evaluation of chest pain, the patient experienced cardiac arrest with ventricular fibrillation, and recovered after cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) with defibrillation. Subsequently, coronary angiography revealed total occlusion of the right coronary artery and successful trans-femoral coronary angioplasty and stent placement was performed. The final diagnosis of the patient was ST-elevation myocardial infarction. However, the patient continued to complain of severe chest pain after the stent implantation. Laboratory tests showed creatine kinase MB level of 87.7 ng/mL (reference range: < 5.1 ng/mL), troponin T level of 3,550 ng/L (reference range: < 14 ng/L), hemoglobin level of 12.2 g/dL, and hematocrit level of 35.6%. Three days after admission, 99mTc-hydroxymethylene diphosphonate (HMDP) bone single photon emission computed tomography/computed tomography (SPECT/CT) was performed to evaluate the cause of the chest wall pain. The planar image of bone scan revealed multifocally increased bone uptake in the left ribs, along with increased soft tissue uptake in the bilateral chest wall and mediastinum (Figure 1).

Planar image of 99mTc-hydroxymethylene diphosphonate bone scintigraphy showed multifocally increased bone uptake in the left ribs (arrow) and soft tissue uptake in the bilateral chest wall (arrowheads) and mediastinum (double arrowhead).

Subsequent bone SPECT/CT revealed multifocally increased uptake in the left ribs indicating rib fractures (Figure 2A). Muscle uptake was observed in the right upper thoracic wall (Figure 2B) and the left flank (Figure 2C), corresponding to the positions of the defibrillator paddles used during CPR, suggestive of muscle injuries induced by electrical damage. Additionally, myocardial uptake was observed in the inferior wall of the left ventricle (Figure 2D). The sites of pain reported by the patient corresponded well with the uptake sites of chest wall muscle and left ribs. After conservative treatments including bed rest, hydration and analgesics, the patient’s symptoms were relieved.

Bone single photon emission computed tomography/computed tomography revealed focally increased uptake in the left rib (A, arrow) indicating rib fracture. Soft tissue uptake was observed in the right upper thoracic wall (B, arrowhead) and the left flank (C, arrowhead), corresponding with the positions of defibrillator paddles, suggestive of muscle injuries induced by electrical damage. Soft tissue uptake in the mediastinum was observed in the inferior wall of the left ventricle (D, double arrowhead).

CPR is a technique used to restore cardiac function by artificially circulating blood through repeated sternal compression, which can cause considerable physical force on the patient's chest and may result in rib and sternal fractures.1) As a result, rib and sternal fractures (Figure 2A) are relatively common complications following CPR. Previous studies have reported that CPR-related chest injuries range from non-complicated skeletal injuries to life-threatening injuries, such as mediastinal hemorrhage or large pneumothorax.2) As reported by Pugh et al.,3) cardioversion could produce the necrosis of chest wall muscle and increased 99mTc-pyrophosphate uptake (Figure 2B and C). Cardiac uptake in bone scintigraphy has a broad differential diagnosis, which includes myocardial infarction, metabolic disorders with hypercalcemia, infiltrative diseases such as amyloidosis or sarcoidosis, inflammatory myocarditis or pericarditis and malignancy (e.g., pericardial metastasis from breast cancer).4) The increased uptake of 99mTc-HMDP at the inferior wall of the left ventricle in this patient suggests the presence of necrotic tissue in the myocardium, which is consistent with the perfusion defect observed in the inferior wall on the follow-up myocardial perfusion SPECT. Necrotic muscle cells with disrupted cytoplasm, contraction bands, and focal calcium deposits can induce increased tracer uptake on bone imaging.5)

The respective roles of bone scintigraphy and chest CT in the evaluation of CPR complications have been sufficiently discussed. However, the clinical significance of bone SPECT/CT in post-CPR patients is still unclear. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first documented case demonstrating the bone SPECT/CT findings of CPR complications. This case highlights the usefulness of bone SPECT/CT in evaluating chest wall pain in patients after CPR with defibrillation by providing improved visualization of muscle injury separate from the uptake of the bony thorax. Bone SPECT/CT could provide improved anatomic localization and diagnostic certainty for a wide variety of clinical applications. Therefore, further studies are needed to determine which post-CPR patients may benefit from additional bone SPECT/CT studies.

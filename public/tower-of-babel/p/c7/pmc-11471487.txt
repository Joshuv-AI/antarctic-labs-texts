How to go green? Exploring public attention and sentiment towards waste sorting behaviors on Weibo platform: A study based on text co-occurrence networks and deep learning
Authors: Feixue Sui, Hengxu Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The attention and sentiment of the public are crucial for better implementation of waste sorting behaviors and moving towards green living. In this study, web scraping technology was used to collect 367,856 Weibo posts related to waste sorting from Sina Weibo. Utilizing text co-occurrence networks, Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) topic modeling, and a deep learning model combining the Affective Cognitive Model (OCC) with Long Short-Term Memory Model (LSTM) (referred to as OCC-LSTM), we comprehensively understand the text at both micro and macro levels, analyzing the attention and sentiment of the public towards waste sorting behaviors on the Sina Weibo platform. Several important findings emerged from the empirical results. First, highly engaging posts were predominantly published by users with a large following, and the number of posts fluctuated over time. This reflects the influence of social hot topics and the timeliness of information dissemination. Second, there was heterogeneity in the user groups and their locations, often influenced by cultural differences due to geographical location. Third, positive sentiment towards waste sorting behavior was higher than negative sentiment on the Weibo platform. Moreover, public attention varied under different emotional influences concerning the topic of waste sorting behavior. The innovation of this study lies in the development of a research framework combining co-occurrence networks and deep learning, expanding the analysis on both micro and macro levels. This framework broadens the research paradigms and dimensions of public perception in waste sorting. This study is significant for promoting waste sorting behaviors and implementing climate policies.

Introduction

Climate change and environmental protection have long been the focus of attention for countries worldwide [1]. With the increasingly evident trend of global warming, governments and various sectors of society are placing greater emphasis on environmental protection. Over the past few decades, China's economy has rapidly grown, becoming the world's second-largest economy [2].

However, this remarkable economic performance has often come at the expense of natural environments and ecological resources, leading to severe environmental consequences [3]. The rapid advancement of urbanization and population growth has led to the generation of large amounts of waste [4]. Traditional waste disposal methods, such as landfilling and incineration, not only consume vast land resources but may also pose potential threats to the environment and human health [5]. Moreover, the continuous rise in public consumption levels has led to increased energy consumption and environmental burden [6]. Therefore, the implementation of waste sorting has become an important solution.

The promotion of waste sorting relies on public participation and awareness [7]. Public statements reflect their views and attitudes towards specific issues. Additionally, public opinion can effectively guide the development of certain events [8]. In recent years, the public has increasingly expressed their opinions through various internet platforms. Among these, social networking platforms have evolved into crucial channels for information dissemination, communication, and exchange[9]. These platforms allow individuals to freely express their opinions and share views. Topics such as waste sorting and environmental protection are also extensively interacted with and discussed on these platforms [10]. Sina Weibo is one of the largest and most influential social media platforms in China [11]. The Weibo platform has not only transformed how Chinese people share, obtain, and communicate information but has also garnered extensive global attention and usage. Compared to other platforms, Weibo offers more representative data. Additionally, Weibo users are more enthusiastic about current events and cultural trends, and they place greater emphasis on self-expression. Furthermore, according to the "Weibo Rural Return Report," the trend of "younger and urbanized" users on the Weibo platform has eased [12]. Therefore, this paper selects Sina Weibo as the platform to obtain user posts related to waste sorting. Additionally, the posts published by the public on social media serve as a real-time barometer of their sentimental expressions [13,14]. Through the content of these posts, we can understand the public's attention and sentiment towards waste sorting issues. These real-time sentiments and attitudes not only serve as a means for us to understand the public's views on waste sorting behaviors but also provide a basis for evaluating the effectiveness of relevant policies and measures [15].

According to the technology acceptance model, after accepting things and perceiving their characteristics, the public will develop emotional preferences for things and then generate behavioral intentions. By studying the emotion of garbage classification in the microblogging platform, we can gain a deeper understanding of the public's cognitive level and attitudinal tendency towards the garbage classification policy, which can provide valuable references for policy makers. In the past, many studies have analyzed the changes in sentiment on social media platforms[[16], [17], [18]]. These studies demonstrate the importance of studying public attitudes in social media platforms. Many scholars have also improved systems and methods to achieve more accurate understanding of public sentiment on social media platforms [[19], [20], [21]]. However, it can be observed that there are few studies currently investigating public attention and awareness of waste sorting. While some scholars have studied the influencing factors of waste sorting behaviors, most of the research has been conducted through surveys and interviews [22,23]. Surveys are typically based on samples, so the coverage of their data may be limited, and the data quality may be affected by various factors. Moreover, survey designs themselves may have flaws, leading to biased data [24]. With the advent of the Internet era and the development of related technologies such as big data, text mining combined with web crawling is becoming increasingly popular in social science research [25]. Through web crawling technology, relatively objective and broader data can be obtained [26]. Furthermore, advanced natural language processing techniques and machine learning algorithms enable deep analysis and processing of textual data, extracting key words, themes, and sentiment tendencies related to a particular event [27]. This accuracy allows us to better understand public views and attitudes towards a particular event. Therefore, by analyzing public attention and real-time sentiment on online social platforms such as Sina Weibo, we can gain a more accurate understanding of their attitudes and opinions towards this environmental initiative, thus better promoting the implementation of relevant policies.

Based on the above analysis, we adopted web crawling technology for data collection and utilized a combination of text co-occurrence network, Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) topic model, as well as the deep learning model combining the Affective Cognitive Model (OCC) with Long Short-Term Memory Model (LSTM) (referred to as OCC-LSTM). We analyzed the public's attention and sentiment towards waste sorting behaviors on the Sina Weibo platform. First, we conducted a basic analysis of public attention to waste sorting through popularity analysis, time trend analysis, and spatial difference analysis. Next, the article performed a co-occurrence analysis of the text from a micro perspective. To visualize these co-occurrence networks, we used the Gephi tool. Using this tool, the article analyzed the trend changes in different communities and explored the relationships between keywords. Finally, this paper employed a deep learning model combining OCC-LSTM to conduct sentiment analysis on the waste sorting data collected from Weibo, and performed LDA topic mining on the sentiment-classified text.

This paper contributes as follows: (1) This paper developed a research framework that combines co-occurrence networks and deep learning models. This framework expands the dimensions of public perception in waste sorting and enriches the research paradigms for sentiment analysis in this area. (2) The article uses the OCC-LSTM deep learning model for sentiment analysis of waste sorting, which provides more accurate sentiment prediction. (3) This study analyzed public sentiment and attitudes towards waste sorting issues from both macro and micro perspectives, providing quantifiable references and specific recommendations for government policy design and implementation.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the relevant literature. Section 3 introduces the research methods and procedures. Section 4 analyzes the research results. Section 5 discusses the findings. The final section presents the conclusions and policy implications, and summarizes the research limitations and future directions.

Literature review

The concept of waste sorting and research progress

Research on garbage classification dates back to the early 20th century when, in 1982, the concept of Behavior for Source Separation was introduced by Geller et al., involving segregating household waste into different bags as per regulations and then disposing of the categorized garbage bags at designated locations [28]. In recent years, garbage classification has emerged as a focal point in the field of environmental development. The recycling of classified garbage offers numerous benefits, not only aiding in the preservation of natural resources but also enhancing the quality of living environments and expediting the development of green circular economies [29]. Garbage classification and recycling are crucial in our daily lives because they contribute to addressing environmental issues and better promote the adoption of healthy, green lifestyles among the public [30]. The factors influencing garbage classification behavior are multifaceted. Studies in the field of environmental science indicate that garbage classification not only effectively reduces waste volume and enhances resource recycling rates but also plays a significant role in promoting the sustainability of urban development [31,32]. Moreover, regarding technological innovation, many scholars focus on designing garbage classification systems and continuously summarize the roles and effectiveness of relevant garbage classification technologies in promoting garbage classification behavior [33,34]. In recent years, there has been an increasing emphasis on research into attitudes and cognition. Yang et al. [34] proposed that the use of the Internet can effectively promote residents' willingness to participate in garbage classification behavior.

In summary, these studies focus on advocating for waste sorting behaviors and improving the technologies and innovations for implementing waste sorting. Additionally, in the study of waste sorting behaviors, public attitudes and perceptions have garnered increasing attention, driven by advancements in the field of psychology.

Sentiment and attention of the Chinese public toward waste sorting

China, as one of the most populous countries in the world, faces significant challenges in waste management [35]. Untreated garbage can cause serious pollution to the environment, including soil, water bodies, and air, threatening people's health and quality of life [36]. Through garbage classification, it is possible to reduce the total amount of waste, increase resource recycling rates, reduce environmental pollution, and achieve sustainable development goals [37]. Moreover, as the world's second-largest economy, China's effective use of resources is crucial for global sustainability. Analyzing public sentiments and perceptions in China can provide better guidance and support for the formulation and implementation of waste classification policies. Huang et al. [38] analyzed the sentiment concerns of the Chinese public at the Chinese city level through microblogging data. Some scholars have also explored the sentiment tendency of Chinese residents towards the policy of classifying urban household waste from the perspective of text mining [39]。Shanghai, as a leader in garbage classification operations in China, has made significant contributions to the country's environmental green development. Scholars have examined different household waste management patterns induced by garbage classification in pilot communities in Shanghai [40]. Additionally, group behavior based on cognition and networks under the garbage classification system in Shanghai, China, has gradually gained attention [41]. In recent years, we have also observed a trend of garbage research in China spreading from urban to rural areas. Currently, China's rural population exceeds 540 million. Some scholars have found that rural residents feel satisfied when performing simple tasks such as garbage classification, which enhances their sense of happiness and life satisfaction [42,43]. However, some Chinese people still resist garbage classification behavior. To improve the low garbage classification rate, it is necessary to enhance both abstract and specific garbage classification signage [44]. Furthermore, scholars have studied the influence of urban and rural residents' attitudes on garbage classification behavior in Jiaxing City, China[45].

There are many problems arising from China's rapid economic development and continuous population growth. Waste management has become a major challenge restricting sustainable development. Through studies on waste sorting among different groups and regions in China, we found that public attention to waste sorting is gradually increasing.

Sentiment and attention on waste sorting in social media platforms

As government vigorously promotes garbage classification policies and advocacy campaigns, more and more members of the public are paying attention to the issue of garbage classification [46]. The garbage classification practices in some cities have achieved certain results, prompting more people to express supportive attitudes and actively participate in garbage classification actions [47]. Despite the progress made in the promotion of garbage classification, there are still some public concerns and worries. Some individuals are concerned that their classification behavior may be misunderstood or affect their quality of life, leading to a certain degree of resistance to garbage classification [48]. In recent years, with the development of the internet, the public is increasingly inclined to express their true thoughts online and understand the genuine opinions of others. By analyzing discussions and viewpoints on social media, a better understanding of the public's genuine attitudes and awareness towards garbage classification can be obtained [49]. Some scholars have explored the motivations and obstacles for public participation in garbage classification by analyzing data on the Sina Weibo platform [48]. Furthermore, internet media tools, especially the use of smartphones, can influence the public's willingness to participate in garbage classification behavior [50]. Moreover, discrepancies may exist between the willingness and actual behavior of the public in garbage classification implementation. Xu et al. [51] found a significant deviation between the willingness of farmers to classify garbage and the actual garbage classification rate during the interview process. The use of the internet can promote changes in farmers' behavior, reducing the deviation between willingness and behavior.

With the development of internet technology, especially the emergence of social media, text mining techniques have gradually been applied to waste sorting research. Text mining is the process of extracting information of interest or use from unstructured text data. Text mining can reduce the subjective judgment of researchers in the sampling and analysis stages of survey and interview methods. In recent years, scholars have also explored public attitudes towards waste reduction from the perspective of social media [52]. However, current research is limited to macro-level text analysis and has not been conducted from a micro-level perspective. Moreover, no studies have used the OCC-LSTM deep learning model for finer-grained sentiment analysis of texts.

Research method and procedure

Research framework

This paper analyzes user attention and sentiment changes regarding waste sorting issues on the Weibo platform, providing recommendations for the implementation of waste sorting policies. First, web scraping technology was used to collect relevant data from 2019 to 2023, followed by data preprocessing. Next, a basic analysis was conducted on the collected data. Additionally, text co-occurrence networks were employed to analyze waste sorting-related text data from a micro perspective. Finally, the OCC-LSTM deep learning model was used to perform sentiment analysis on waste sorting-related text content from a macro perspective, with extraction and analysis of topics under different emotional states. Web crawler technology

With the development of the Internet, there has been an explosive growth in information resources, leading to changes in the methods of data acquisition by data seekers [53]. While the explosive growth of data has created conditions for data collection, manually collecting data one by one from the vast amount available on the Internet is challenging. To efficiently gather this information, web crawling techniques are employed for the acquisition of massive amounts of data. Currently, web crawling technology finds widespread applications across various domains. For instance, in the realm of consumer behavior, crawlers can be employed to collect extensive data such as product prices, sales figures, user reviews, etc., for analysis and mining to extract valuable insights [54,55]. This method is widely utilized in psychological analysis studies and is considered suitable for investigating the actual attitudes of the public [56].

The paper employs Python 3.11 to retrieve relevant network data regarding waste classification from the Weibo platform. The specific operational process is outlined as follows: (1) Simulated login. Access the Weibo interface via the URL (https://weibo.com/), simulate the login process using a username and password, and obtain the corresponding cookie information. (2) Keyword search. After performing the necessary simulated login, we used "waste sorting" as the keyword for search on the Weibo search interface. The Weibo platform features "Weibo Super Topics," which are interest-based communities. This significantly reduces the issue of missing posts related to waste sorting discussions that do not explicitly use the relevant keywords. This keyword directly focuses on the core content of the research topic, ensuring that the collected data is highly relevant to the theme. Additionally, the implementation of the "Shanghai Municipal Waste Management Regulations" in 2019 marked the formal nationwide promotion of waste sorting policies. Simultaneously, the topic of waste sorting began to attract widespread attention and discussion on social media platforms such as Weibo. Therefore, we collected all relevant Weibo posts about waste sorting from 2019 to 2023. (3) Data collection. Collect data for all posts retrieved using "waste classification" as the keyword, including Weibo user names, user IDs, post content, number of likes, number of reposts, number of comments, posting location, and IP address. In total, 367,856 pieces of raw data were collected. (4) Data storage. Upon completing the collection of all data within the specified time range and verifying its accuracy, store and export the gathered information.

Text mining analysis

Data pre-processing

(1) denoising

To ensure the authenticity and effectiveness of the data analysis results, we rigorously preprocessed the collected text data. Firstly, the content of Weibo posts underwent deduplication processing. Posts with the same user nickname, content, and posting year were considered duplicate posts and were deleted, retaining only the first occurrence of each text. Secondly, considering the possibility of interference information in the data, such as posts containing waste classification tags but actually consisting of advertisements, reposts, or containing too few characters without actual information, these texts are completely irrelevant to the garbage sorting theme. We used a combination of manual and automatic filtering methods to identify and remove these irrelevant posts. Lastly, some meaningless information in the text, including special symbols and URLs, was manually removed. After removing the above unqualified data, 315,968 valid text data remain.

(2)Word segmentation

After basic denoising processing of the raw data, we used the Jieba word segmentation module in Python to tokenize the text content. Jieba word segmentation relies on a Chinese lexicon and combines both rule-based and statistical-based methods. Firstly, it scans the text with a prefix dictionary to quickly construct a directed acyclic graph containing all possible segmentation results. Then, based on annotated corpora, it uses dynamic programming to find the path with the maximum probability, which is considered as the final segmentation result. To ensure the accuracy of the segmentation results, we manually constructed a terminology dictionary for waste classification and merged stop word lists from Baidu, Harbin Institute of Technology, and Sichuan University's Machine Intelligence Laboratory. Additionally, we utilized popular synonym libraries available on the internet, such as Baidu's synonym library and Sogou's synonym library. Finally, we continuously expanded and corrected the stop word list, terminology dictionary, and synonym library based on the segmentation results until they met the requirements of the research model.

Text co-occurrence network analysis

Text co-occurrence network analysis is a method of network analysis based on textual data, primarily used to reveal the associations and co-occurrence relationships among elements such as words, phrases, or topics within the text. This method constructs a network structure by representing elements in the text as nodes and representing the co-occurrence relationships between elements as edges, forming a network graph. This network graph provides an intuitive visualization of the relationships and associations among elements in the text, aiding in better understanding and analysis of textual data at a micro level.

Sentiment analysis

The sentiment analysis conducted in this study aims to investigate the public's sentiment orientation and attitudes towards garbage classification on the Weibo platform. Common methods for sentiment analysis include rule-based methods [57,58], lexicon-based methods [59], machine learning-based methods [60,61], and deep learning-based methods [62,63]. Among these, rule-based and lexicon-based sentiment analysis methods often exhibit high subjectivity and are not suitable for sentiment analysis supported by large amounts of data. Machine learning-based sentiment analysis, compared to the former two methods, reduces reliance on manual rules. However, machine learning-based sentiment analysis requires a large amount of labeled data to train the model, and the quality of annotated data also significantly influences the model's performance. Due to the ability to automatically extract complex text features and handle variable-length texts, deep learning-based sentiment analysis is advantageous. Moreover, in the case of large amounts of data, deep learning models typically achieve better performance.

Currently, deep learning algorithms are widely used in the field of natural language processing. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), which focus on reconstructing adjacent positions, have long been widely used in natural language processing. Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) models, a special type of RNN, can learn long-term dependencies, thereby enhancing classification accuracy [17]. Since the model is semi-supervised, we combined it with the OCC sentiment model for emotional training. Social media texts often exhibit characteristics such as informality, colloquialism, and ambiguity. This may lead to biased results in sentiment analysis. For some complex or subtle emotional expressions, such as sarcasm and puns, the model may struggle to recognize and understand them. The OCC model is currently one of the most detailed sentiment classification models. It is considered the first sentiment model developed with computer implementation in mind and has gradually been applied to social media research. The OCC model primarily uses the outcomes of events, the actions of objects, and the descriptions of objects as evaluation criteria, thereby ensuring the accuracy of sentiment polarity annotations. In summary, this paper uses the OCC-LSTM model for sentiment analysis to ensure accuracy. To ensure sample balance, we annotated 2000 positive posts and 2000 negative posts. LDA topic model

Topic mining is conducted in this study through Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA). The LDA model, one of the most commonly used models in topic modeling, is an unsupervised machine learning model used for collaborative filtering, text classification, and document modeling. The fundamental concept behind LDA is to represent topics through word probabilities. The words with the highest probability in each topic typically provide a good understanding of what the topic represents in LDA [64]. Currently, the LDA topic model has been widely applied in various fields and has been well validated. Before topic classification, it is necessary to determine the number of topics [65,66]. Perplexity calculation is employed to determine the number of topics, reducing the subjectivity in the trial-and-error method to determine the optimal number of topics.

Public attention to waste sorting

Post hotness analysis

This paper begins with an analysis of the hotness of posts on waste sorting. We define heat as the sum of the number of retweets, comments and likes. The results indicate that posts attracting more attention are largely disseminated by users with a certain level of popularity. This is because these users typically have accumulated a large fan base on Weibo and exhibit a certain degree of activity and influence, leading to more frequent interactions among followers. Moreover, this authority often facilitates increased sharing of such posts, as people tend to perceive this information as reliable and valuable. Time trend of number of Sina Weibo posts

Following the analysis of attention towards waste sorting, we conducted a trend analysis of the number of Weibo posts collected over time, as shown in Fig. 4. Overall, from 2019 to 2023, the number of Weibo posts related to waste classification gradually stabilized after experiencing a period of peaks. Specifically, after the formal implementation of the "Shanghai Household Waste Management Regulations" on July 1, 2019, topics related to waste classification gradually surged on the Weibo platform, with the annual number of waste classification-related posts reaching a peak of 100,000. During this period, waste classification-related topics became the focal point of social attention, with various opinions and public sentiments proliferating on Weibo. Additionally, we observed fluctuations in the number of waste classification-related posts between different months, but the overall trend showed a decrease in the number of posts over the years, amidst these fluctuations. Specifically, after peaking in July 2019, the number of waste classification-related posts experienced another peak in September and October of the same year. It can be observed that over time, the popularity and number of posts related to waste classification did not sustain at high levels. Although occasional peaks in post numbers still occurred, the overall trend was declining and gradually stabilized. The reason may be that initially, vigorous promotion of waste classification policies by the government may have sparked significant attention and discussion. However, as time passed, the effect of policies gradually weakened, and public attention decreased accordingly. Moreover, with the passage of time, new social issues and hot topics may emerge, attracting public attention away from waste classification. Spatial difference analysis

China's waste classification policy has been promoted nationwide since 2019, with all regions advocating for waste classification. Currently, the coverage rate of household waste sorting in China has reached over 90 %, encompassing most regions. This paper analyzes spatial differences based on the existing locations of the collected data. Analysis of the collected text data and posting locations reveals the following findings. First, provinces with well-established waste sorting systems and management frameworks, such as Shanghai, Beijing, and Guangdong, show a significantly higher number of posts compared to other provinces. This is because these provinces have clarified the responsible parties for waste sorting through legislation, ensuring the smooth implementation of waste sorting initiatives. Second, public attention to waste sorting exhibits the characteristics of being higher in the eastern regions compared to the western regions, higher in the southern regions compared to the northern regions, and higher in urban areas compared to rural areas. (1) The eastern regions are relatively economically developed, with high levels of urbanization and population density. Consequently, the volume of waste generated in these areas is also relatively large, leading to similar spatial differences in waste sorting. (2) Due to the hot and humid climate that facilitates waste decomposition, the southern regions often place greater emphasis on waste sorting. Additionally, most pilot cities for waste sorting are located in the southern regions, resulting in higher overall acceptance and participation in waste sorting in these areas. Although environmental awareness has increased in northern regions in recent years, there remains a notable gap compared to the southern regions. (3) Compared to developed cities, residents in small and medium-sized cities and rural areas generally have lower educational levels, which directly impacts their understanding and acceptance of waste sorting. Waste sorting requires a certain level of specialized knowledge and comprehension. Residents with lower educational levels may find it challenging to fully understand the necessity of waste sorting. Therefore, to bridge the gap between urban and rural areas, urban communities, businesses, and media frequently initiate advocacy activities for waste sorting to enhance awareness and emphasis among rural residents.

Following the analysis of the heat and temporal trends of waste sorting data on the Weibo platform, we conducted a spatial differentiation analysis of the collected Weibo posts, the results of which are illustrated in Fig. 5. Due to privacy settings regarding location information by some users when posting, certain posts could not be accurately geolocated. Therefore, this study focused on analyzing spatial differences based on the available location data. Firstly, overall, there are regional disparities in the number of waste classification-related posts posted by users in different provinces of China, generally showing an "east more, west less" regional characteristic. This discrepancy may be attributed to variations in waste sorting policies and implementation across different regions, potentially resulting in regional disparities in discussions and attention on Weibo. Additionally, China's population distribution also exhibits an evident "east more, west less" pattern, which may similarly contribute to spatial disparities in waste classification. LDA topic analysis based on sentiment classification

Calculation of perplexity

The paper employs the LDA model to extract and classify keywords from garbage classification posts with positive and negative sentiments, aiming to further explore the underlying mechanisms behind different emotions. Perplexity and coherence scores are current methods used in academia to determine the optimal number of topics for an LDA model. Coherence scores are typically computed based on subjective judgments of lexical co-occurrences or pre-set evaluation criteria, which may introduce some subjectivity into the results. To minimize the subjectivity in determining the number of topics, this study combines the elbow method with perplexity to determine the optimal number of topics. Perplexity is a key indicator of a model's generative ability. It is based on the model's predictive performance on test documents, calculated by normalizing the negative log-likelihood of generating test documents. A lower perplexity indicates a stronger generative capability of the model, meaning it can better capture the topic distribution of the documents. The elbow method involves plotting the relationship between the number of topics and an evaluation metric (such as perplexity), and identifying the "elbow point" on the curve to determine the optimal number of topics. Combining perplexity with the elbow method relies entirely on objective data and intuitive graphical analysis, thereby reducing the influence of subjective factors on the results. In the line plot, as the number of topics increases, the perplexity value typically decreases rapidly initially and then levels off or slightly increases. This inflection point usually corresponds to the optimal number of topics. At this point, adding more topics contributes little to reducing perplexity and may even introduce noise or redundant topics. We use the PyLDAvis package to visualize the clustering results and observe the trend of perplexity changes. Fig. 11(a) and (b) indicate that the inflection points for both positive and negative sentiments are three. Therefore, the optimal number of LDA topics is three for both positive and negative sentiments.Fig. 11Perplexity in different sentiments.Fig. 11

Discussion

This study comprehensively examines the public's attention and sentiments towards waste sorting issues on the Weibo platform in China. Firstly, we observed that posts with high engagement are predominantly published by Weibo-certified users, including law enforcement agencies, governmental departments, celebrities, mainstream media, universities, and various thematic bloggers. This indicates that official users, especially those with large fan bases, can generate more engagement and discussions [68]. Furthermore, the analysis of text content reveals that the issue of waste sorting elicits markedly different levels of attention among various groups. Specifically, urban residents, constrained by their living environment and higher environmental standards, generally exhibit a higher level of participation in waste sorting. In contrast, rural residents, influenced by economic conditions and insufficient infrastructure, show a lower level of participation in waste sorting. Despite the active efforts of the government and various social sectors to promote waste sorting, there remains a lack of understanding among different groups regarding specific rules, classification standards, and waste management methods, with information asymmetry still prevalent [69]. Additionally, younger individuals, being more receptive to new ideas, generally have a higher level of acceptance of waste sorting compared to older adults.

Secondly, from an overall perspective, between 2019 and 2023, the quantity of waste sorting-related Weibo posts gradually stabilized after experiencing a period of peak activity. Additionally, the volume of posts related to waste sorting fluctuates across different months but generally decreases over the years. This trend may be attributed to the initial vigorous promotion of waste sorting policies and measures by the government, which initially garnered significant attention and discussion. However, as time passes, the effectiveness of these policies diminishes, leading to decreased public interest. Moreover, as new social hot topics and issues emerge over time, they may attract public attention away from waste sorting, resulting in a relative decrease in attention and a subsequent decrease in Weibo posting volume. Additionally, we observed significant regional differences among the publishers of waste sorting-related posts. The general trend shows that users in the eastern region outnumber those in the western region, and users in the southern region exceed those in the northern region [70,71]. This phenomenon may be attributed to the higher economic development levels in the eastern and southern regions due to geographical location, historical policies, and other factors. High levels of economic development lead to greater allocation of resources and funds to environmental protection, including waste sorting education, facility investment, and operational management. Consequently, the public in the eastern region shows higher levels of attention and participation in waste sorting, resulting in more frequent posts about waste sorting on the internet. Additionally, due to economic prosperity and efficient policy implementation, the eastern and southern regions often implement waste sorting policies more rapidly and effectively, further increasing public awareness and understanding of waste sorting.

Our study also acknowledges that frequency analysis of words can identify keywords highly correlated with various types of waste, thereby enabling more precise classification of waste based on these keywords. Text co-occurrence network analysis, in turn, can reveal the interrelationships and co-occurrence patterns among different vocabulary, assisting in uncovering classification rules and features hidden within extensive text data [72]. Observations reveal a high frequency of occurrence for words such as "life," "community," "residents," and "environment," indicating the close connection between waste sorting and people's daily lives and residential environments. Furthermore, dynamic analysis of co-occurrence networks from 2019 to 2021 reveals shifts and changes in public attention to waste sorting over time. Specifically, words closely associated with daily life, such as "kitchen waste" and "household," begin to receive more attention, suggesting that waste sorting is gradually transitioning from a policy task to a daily habit of the public.

Our study also conducted sentiment analysis on waste sorting data collected from Weibo using the OCC-LSTM deep learning model [72,73]. Through observation, it was found that users with higher popularity on the Weibo platform mostly exhibited positive sentiments towards waste sorting behavior. Furthermore, driven by users with higher popularity, it was also observed that the proportion of positive sentiments towards waste sorting among the public was similarly higher than the proportion of negative sentiments. Additionally, although the number of waste sorting-related posts on Weibo has decreased over the years, the public's positive sentiments towards waste sorting have become increasingly strong while negative sentiments have gradually diminished. This may be attributed to the continuous escalation of government efforts in promoting waste sorting initiatives, coupled with the gradual enhancement of public awareness regarding waste sorting. Initially, due to insufficient understanding and awareness, many members of the public exhibited a rejecting attitude towards waste sorting behavior. However, as time has progressed, the public's understanding of waste sorting has deepened, and individuals have continuously benefited from waste sorting behavior, leading to a gradual increase in the proportion of positive sentiments. Subsequently, we conducted LDA topic classification based on different sentiments and found that under the influence of different emotions, the public's thematic focus on waste sorting behavior varies.

Conclusion and policy implications

As an important environmental protection measure, waste sorting plays a crucial role in reducing environmental pollution, conserving resources, and promoting green and sustainable development. Therefore, we have explored the level of public attention, trends, and sentiment orientation towards waste sorting in China. In this study, data related to waste sorting topics on the Sina Weibo platform were collected using web crawling technology. We utilized text co-occurrence networks and deep learning models to construct a two-layer (macro and micro) research framework, focusing on public attention and emotional tendencies towards waste sorting. This framework aims to explore the level of public attention to waste sorting, the evolution of co-occurrence patterns between texts, emotional fluctuations, and LDA topic changes based on emotional classification. Practice has shown that using text data collected from social media platforms for sentiment analysis effectively reduces the subjective bias inherent in traditional surveys and interviews. To gain deeper insights into Weibo users' attitudes and emotions towards waste sorting, we first analyzed trends in public attention. The study found that high-attention posts are mostly published by groups with a large number of followers, and the number of posts fluctuates over time, which reflects the wide influence of hot social topics and the rapid timeliness of information dissemination. Additionally, we observed significant diversity among posting user groups and their geographical locations, with this heterogeneity largely influenced by regional cultural differences. Subsequently, the study analyzes the network co-occurrence changes, emotional fluctuations, and LDA topic variations based on sentimental classification of waste sorting texts from both macro and micro perspectives. The results reveal that waste sorting promotion and education occupy a significant position in Weibo posts. On Weibo, the public exhibits more positive than negative sentiment towards waste sorting behavior. It is noteworthy that public focus on waste sorting topics varies depending on the emotional orientation. Therefore, the government should leverage public sentiment and topic focus data from Weibo to continuously refine policy formulation and implementation strategies, enhancing policy effectiveness and further advancing the impact of waste sorting efforts.

Based on these conclusions, the following policy insights are proposed:

(1) Given that high-attention posts often originate from users with large followings, policymakers should explore collaborations with verified accounts, especially those with substantial follower bases, to jointly promote waste sorting knowledge. This approach aims to increase public attention and participation in waste sorting, thereby expanding the reach and impact of the promotion efforts. (2) To sustain the momentum of waste sorting discussions, policymakers should consider implementing a series of ongoing measures. This includes regularly organizing relevant events, updating on policy developments, and other actions to stimulate ongoing user engagement and discussion, ensuring that waste sorting awareness is deeply ingrained. (3) In light of the differences in user demographics and regional distributions, the government should adopt differentiated waste sorting policy strategies. On one hand, the government should strengthen green environmental regulations as institutional safeguards[74]. On the other hand, the government needs to establish cross-regional waste sorting exchange platforms to facilitate experience sharing and collaborative support. (4) Terms such as “life,” “community,” “residents,” and “environment” are highly frequent and widely discussed. Policymakers should focus on these themes when creating and disseminating waste sorting awareness and educational materials. (5) Most posts express a positive attitude towards waste sorting. Policymakers can leverage this positive sentiment to enhance public engagement and support through affirmative promotion and education. (6) Under different emotional contexts, public focus on waste sorting varies. Policymakers need to understand and account for these differences to more effectively promote waste sorting knowledge across various platforms.

This study employs objective methods such as web scraping and text mining, examining both macro and micro levels. It focuses on the Chinese public's attention and attitudes towards waste sorting on the Weibo platform. Innovatively, the article applies the OCC-LSTM deep learning model for the first time, deeply exploring public attention and attitudes towards waste sorting behavior. This model provides a more accurate way to understand public views on waste sorting. Additionally, the research findings offer valuable recommendations for the government in promoting waste sorting behavior and implementing climate policies.

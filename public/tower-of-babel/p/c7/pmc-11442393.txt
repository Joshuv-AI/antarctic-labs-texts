Aquaporin 3 is expressed in the basaloid cells of canine sebaceous glands
Authors: Hiroko SONODA, Yoshiki TANIGUCHI, Naruki FUJIMOTO, Yoshiki HIGASHIJIMA, Toshiyuki MATSUZAKI, Takuya HIRAI, Teruo ITOH, Kazuyuki UCHIDA, Masahiro IKEDA
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe role of aquaporin proteins (AQPs) in tumor biology has attracted attention over the past 20 years. However, the expression profiles of AQPs in canine sebaceous gland tumors remain obscure. This study was performed to clarify the expression of AQP1, 3, 5, the most studied AQPs in tumor biology, in sebaceous adenoma and sebaceous epithelioma. Among these AQPs, only AQP3 was expressed in normal tissue and both tumor types and located to only undifferentiated sebocytes (basaloid cells). A cellular proliferation marker, Ki-67, was detected only in the area including basaloid cells in both tumor types. These findings suggest that AQP3 is useful for clarifying the origin of sebaceous gland tumors, and that AQP3 may be related to sebaceous gland development.Keywords: aquaporin 3, basaloid cells, sebaceous adenoma, sebaceous epithelioma, undifferentiated sebocytes

Aquaporins (AQPs) are a family of water channel proteins, and 13 types (AQP0 to AQP12) have been characterized in mammals [1]. In addition to solute transport activities, roles for AQPs in facilitating cell migration have been reported (e.g., AQP1 in endothelial cells, AQP4 in astrocytes, and AQP9 in neutrophils) [9, 13, 14]. Furthermore, studies of more than 20 types of human cancers have indicated altered expression of AQPs in tumor cells [3]. Accordingly, it is currently considered that AQPs play important roles in tumor pathogenesis.

The skin is a major tumor site for tumors in humans, dogs, and cats [5, 7]. MacVean and colleagues have reported that sebaceous gland tumors are the most common tumor type in dogs [10]. Although Niu et al. have reported the expression of AQP3 in human cutaneous lesions, to our knowledge, there have been no other reported studies of AQP expression in sebaceous adenoma and sebaceous epithelioma [12]. In the present study, we investigated the expression patterns of AQP1, AQP3, and AQP5, the most studied AQPs in tumor biology, in canine sebaceous adenoma and sebaceous epithelioma.

For the study, one sample of normal sebaceous gland tissue and three samples of canine sebaceous adenoma or sebaceous epithelioma, which had been diagnosed at The University of Tokyo or University of Miyazaki, were obtained. Table 1 shows the breed, sex, and age of the dogs from which the tumors were taken. Formalin-fixed tumor samples were embedded in paraffin blocks and cut into sections 2 μm thick, and subjected to hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining or immunostaining. For immunostaining, the antigens were activated by heat treatment in an autoclave at 121°C for 5 min in distilled water or 1×Target Retrieval Solution® pH6.0 (Dako North America, Inc., Carpinteria, CA, USA). Endogenous peroxidase was inactivated using methanol containing 3% H2O2 for 5 min, followed by washing with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Primary antibodies used in this study were rabbit anti-AQP1 antibody (1:100, SC-20810; Santa Cruz Biotechnology Inc., Santa Cruz, CA, USA), rabbit anti-AQP3 antibody (1:500, synthesized at the University of Gunma, with previously evaluated specificity [6]), rabbit anti-AQP5 antibody (1:1,000, synthesized at the University of Gunma, with previously evaluated specificity [6]), rabbit anti-E-cadherin antibody (1:100, GTX134997; GeneTex Inc., Irvine, CA, USA), and mouse anti-Ki-67 antibody (undiluted, R626; Dako Denmark A/S, Glostrup, Denmark). The primary antibodies were diluted with 0.1% bovine serum albumin in PBS and allowed to react for 1 hr at room temperature. Then, secondary antibodies (EnvisionTM Dual Link System-HRP; Dako Japan Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) were added for 1 hr at room temperature. After these procedures, the antibodies were reacted with 3,3′- diaminobenzidine for color development. The samples were then counterstained with hematoxylin and washed with running water for 10 min. After dehydration and permeabilization, the specimens were sealed according to conventional methods. Images of HE-staining and immunostaining were acquired with a Nano Zoomer 2.0-RS (C10730-13; Hamamatsu Photonics K.K., Hamamatsu, Japan) and observed with NDP. View2 software (Hamamatsu Photonics K.K.). The number of Ki-67-positive cells was counted in a total of 1,000 tumor cells per tumor sample, and the percentage of the positive cells was calculated. The data are presented as mean ± SE. Statistical analysis was performed using one-tailed Mann-Whitney U test, and differences at P<0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Sebaceous glands comprise two cell types: differentiated sebocytes (sebaceous cells) and undifferentiated sebocytes (basaloid cells). Basaloid cells are located along the outer layers of the gland and become more differentiated to the sebaceous cells toward the center. The sebaceous cells are characterized by intracellular accumulation of lipid products. In the normal tissue, HE-staining showed that sebaceous cells exist in the center and sebocytes in each lobule’s outer layer (Fig. 1A). E-cadherin, an epithelial marker, was detected in the plasma membrane of both cell types (Fig. 1B). AQP1 was observed in red blood cells and endothelial cells (Fig. 1C). AQP3 was expressed mainly in the cytoplasm of basaloid cells (Fig. 1Dand 1E) and AQP5 was not observed in any of the cells (Fig. 1F).

We examined three samples of each tumor type. HE-staining showed that the major cell population in sebaceous adenoma was sebaceous cells, and that the two or three outer layers of lobules consisted of basaloid cells (Fig. 1G). On the other hand, the major cell type in sebaceous epithelioma was basaloid cells, with only focal areas of sebaceous cells (Fig. 1M). The immunohistochemistry analyses for E-cadherin (Fig. 1H and 1N), AQP1 (Fig. 1I and 1O), and AQP5 (Fig. 1L and 1R) in both tumor types showed similar staining patterns with the normal tissue. Similarly, AQP3 was expressed mainly in the cytoplasm of basaloid cells in both tumor types (Fig. 1J, 1K, 1P and 1Q).

Next, the expression of Ki-67, a marker of cell proliferation, was examined. In the normal sebaceous gland, Ki-67-positive cells were virtually not observed in any of the cells (Fig. 2A and 2B). In both tumor types, Ki-67 was detected in basaloid cells but not in sebaceous cells (Fig. 2C to 2F) and not all cells in the AQP3-positive regions were Ki-67 positive, thus indicating that some basaloid cells progress the cell cycle. Furthermore, sebaceous epithelioma had more Ki-67-positive cells than sebaceous adenoma (Fig. 2G and 2H) .

In this study, we observed AQP3 in basaloid cells in both canine sebaceous adenoma and sebaceous epithelioma. This is the first report of AQP expression in either of these two tumor types. AQP3 expression has been reported in sebaceous glands in human and rat cases, but their authors did not specify the cell types [11, 12]. Since AQP3 is aquaglyceroporin, a transporter for glycerin, it has been predicted that sebaceous cells would express AQP3. However, the present study clearly showed that AQP3 was expressed in basaloid cells, and therefore was unlikely to contribute to lipid synthesis in sebaceous cells. As basaloid cells are usually located more closely to the vascular systems than sebaceous cells, it is likely that glycerin is transferred from basaloid cells to sebaceous cells via AQP3. This concept is important when attempting to understand the mechanism responsible for accumulation of lipids in sebaceous cells.

Sebaceous neoplasms also include sebaceous hyperplasia and carcinoma. Niu et al. have shown that AQP3 is expressed diffusely in human sebaceous hyperplasia but not in sebaceous carcinoma [12]. However, the authors did not show cell types in the AQP3-positive cells, and the identification of cell types in sebaceous hyperplasia should be clarified in a future study.

Expression of Ki-67 was observed only in basaloid cells. This means that cell proliferation in sebaceous adenoma and sebaceous epithelioma occurs mainly in the peripheral area of lobules. Moreover, we showed that sebaceous epithelioma had more Ki-67-positive cells than sebaceous adenoma. It has been reported that the number of Ki-67-positive cells was increased 6-fold in the sebaceous epithelioma than in the sebaceous adenoma in canine cutaneous sebaceous lesions [4]. Therefore, it is considered that the number of cells progressing through the cell cycle is greater in sebaceous epithelioma than in sebaceous adenoma.

In the present study, Ki-67-positive cells were observed in the AQP3-expressing area. It has been shown that AQP3 facilitates the proliferation of tumor cells in pancreatic cancer and hepatocellular carcinoma [8, 15]. Therefore, AQP3 may also promote basaloid cell proliferation, especially in sebaceous epithelioma. However, no quantitative analysis was conducted in this study. Future studies, including protein quantification, will clarify their relationship.

Barrault and colleagues have established a stable human sebocyte cell line constitutively expressing a fully functional androgen receptor, and shown that the treatment of the cultured cells with dihydrotestosterone up-regulated the expression of AQP3 [2]. This indicates that androgen receptor activation can stimulate the expression of AQP3. In this context, a future study will be needed to clarify the mechanism underlying the expression of AQP3 in basaloid cells.

In conclusion, AQP3 is expressed in the basaloid cells of canine sebaceous gland tumors, suggesting that AQP3 is useful for clarifying the origin of these tumors.

Did Stora Karlsö wolves reach the island naturally or by boat?
Authors: Luc A. Janssens, L. David Mech
Published: 2026

A Neolithic (G.7) and Bronze Age (G.11) wolf are hypothesized to have reached the Baltic Sea island, Stora Karlsö (SK), near Gotland Island, under human control by boat (1). Its significance relates to humans’ ability to manage these wolves. The authors state that natural dispersal and marine-diet-scavenging cannot be excluded, but favor the human pathway. Anthropogenic justifications regarding wolves were 1) out of natural habitat; 2) unusual diet; 3) small size; 4) rare find: wolf remains in human context; 5) care of a disabled animal.

That distance was considered “the extreme upper edge” to pass over ice (1). However, 140 km passages over ice were documented (Wrangel Island) (2). The Baltic sea is/was often ice covered during winters. Then SK is connected to the mainland from where wolves have been documented crossing the frozen Baltic sea (>150 km) (3). We suggest that wolves dispersed from the mainland over ice and settled on SK with a rich maritime fauna to hunt on. There, they may have died naturally in the cave (G.11); or were hunted and possibly skinned (G.7). The defined small size, as the result of human reproductive control or inbreeding in an isolated population (resulting in increased homozygosity) was based on G.11 humerus width. Using a sturdy regression formula (8), G.11 weighs 32.3 kg, within the normal wolf distribution range (31 to 46 kg). Contrarily, first-generation wolf pups, raised anthropogenically, are, when adult, about 20 to 25% smaller (30 kg) than wild ones (9). Excavation finds in the original 1926 report by Pira (4) and in Girland-Fink and colleagues, 2025 (1).

Both specimens are dated 1,600 y apart, involving two totally different societies (diet, culture, genetics) which would independently have expressed identical “wolf-boat-transport” behavior, entailing independent wolf taming or domestication as a human control signature. This is very unlikely, with no equal.

Very important is to define how these wolves were transported. Not as corpses, there would be no benefit. Not as adults, they are untamable and extremely aggressive. Only raised wolf pups, grown into adulthood in an anthropogenic environment are an option. But G.7 is not small enough to allow for this scenario, and there are no signatures of wolf domestication anywhere anytime in Scandinavia.

Concluding, the original arguments lack persuasion, making human control very unlikely.

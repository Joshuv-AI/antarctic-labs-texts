Clinical application prospects of Nectin-4 in pan-cancer: an analysis based on the Trialtrove database
Authors: Lei Li, Fei Ren, Zhuo Liu, Xiaojun Tian, Hongxian Zhang, Guoliang Wang, Shudong Zhang, Lulin Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research analyzes the global landscape of clinical trials focusing on therapies targeting the Nectin cell adhesion molecule 4 (Nectin-4) across various malignancies, using data from the Trialtrove database. Analysis of 136 interventional trials reveals a rapidly expanding field dominated by antibody-drug conjugates, particularly in urothelial carcinoma, with significant activity in combination regimens and diverse therapeutic modalities under exploration. These findings emphasize the substantial translational potential of Nectin-4 and highlight key trends shaping its future clinical development.

Nectin cell adhesion molecule 4 (Nectin-4), a type I transmembrane protein and immunoglobulin superfamily member, is frequently overexpressed across diverse epithelial carcinomas while exhibiting limited expression in normal tissues.1 This tumor-specific profile makes it an appealing therapeutic target. The leading strategy uses Nectin-4 as a target for antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), like enfortumab vedotin (EV), to deliver the powerful microtubule-disrupting agent monomethyl auristatin E. EV was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2019 for the treatment of urothelial carcinoma (UC), demonstrating the clinical potential of targeting Nectin-4. Beyond ADCs, new modalities like Nectin-4-targeted bispecific antibodies, immunocytokines, and chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) immunotherapy are expanding treatment options. However, despite these achievements, a comprehensive understanding of the clinical application prospects of Nectin-4 in pan-cancer remains incomplete. The analysis of the clinical trial landscape can provide valuable insights into the current status and future directions of Nectin-4-targeted therapies. This research aims to fill this gap by conducting a detailed analysis of relevant clinical trials in the Trialtrove database, thereby providing a more comprehensive perspective on the clinical potential of Nectin-4 in cancer treatment.

We used the Trialtrove database to find clinical trials on Nectin-4; the search terms were “mechanism of action is Nectin-4 antagonist” or “target is nectin cell adhesion molecule 4”. This search was intended to capture all interventional studies that targeted Nectin-4, excluding observational studies to focus on trials that directly tested the efficacy and safety of Nectin-4-related therapies. After thorough manual screening for relevance to Nectin-4 targeting, the initial search results were narrowed down to 136 unique entries. Key metrics analyzed included trial phase, start year, recruitment status, primary tumor type, primary endpoints, therapeutic modality (drug type), and treatment strategy (monotherapy vs combination therapy). To ensure data integrity and analytical robustness, two independent investigators performed the data extraction, screening, and trend analysis. Any discrepancies were resolved through consensus discussion and re-examination of the source data within Trialtrove.2 Despite the rigorous screening and data extraction process, we acknowledge the intrinsic limitations of the Trialtrove database, including potential delays in updating trial registration information and incomplete reporting of partial trial details, such as secondary endpoints and subgroup analysis data, which may have introduced minor biases to the current analysis.

Analysis of the 136 trials shows a rapidly evolving clinical landscape for Nectin-4-targeted therapies. Figure 1A shows the distribution of the start years of clinical trials and the proportion of trial phases. The field has experienced significant growth, particularly post-2020, coinciding with the regulatory approval of EV. Phase I trials constitute the largest proportion (34.6%, n=47), reflecting the active exploration of novel agents and combinations. Phase II trials represent 30.1% (n=41), focusing on efficacy signals in specific indications, while Phase III trials account for 7.4% (n=10), primarily evaluating confirmatory efficacy and safety. The trials were conducted across multiple countries and regions. China accounted for the largest proportion, followed by the USA, Spain, and France, as shown in online supplemental figure S1. The global nature of these trials indicates the widespread interest in Nectin-4 as a therapeutic target. Online supplemental figure S2 shows the proportion of sponsor type. Industry sponsorship is predominant (67.62%), highlighting strong commercial interest, while academia (27.34%) also plays vital roles. Figure 1B illustrates that clinical trials predominantly focus on the urinary system, particularly the bladder and kidneys, largely due to Padcev’s initial success in treating UC. A notable proportion of trials are currently active or planned for various types of tumors, indicating sustained investment. Figure 1C shows the top 20 primary endpoints. Overall response rate is the most common primary endpoint, followed closely by response evaluation criteria in solid tumors, safety and tolerability, and adverse events. Figure 1D shows the types of drugs based on Nectin-4. ADCs overwhelmingly dominate the landscape (88.2% of trials, n=120). Peptide-drug conjugates represent the next largest category (5.9%, n=8). Besides, bispecific antibodies (2.2%, n=3), radionuclide targeted therapy (1.5%, n=2), T-cell receptor engineered T cells (1.5%, n=2) and CAR-T (1.5%, n=2) constitute emerging, innovative modalities targeting Nectin-4. Figure 1E and F illustrate the treatment strategies. Combination therapies are increasingly prevalent, constituting 63.97% of trials (n=87), compared with 36.03% (n=49) investigating monotherapy. Combination partners are diverse; however, targeted therapy and immunotherapy remain dominant.

This analysis highlights the rapid growth and development of Nectin-4-targeting therapies, driven by the success of the ADC EV in UC. EV has shown effectiveness in patients with UC unresponsive to chemotherapy and programmed cell death protein-1/programmed death-ligand 1 inhibitors, significantly improving overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS) over standard chemotherapy.3 4 Crucially, combination therapy with EV and pembrolizumab has shown remarkable efficacy in first-line locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma, significantly improving OS, PFS, and overall response rate compared with platinum-based chemotherapy, culminating in accelerated FDA approval and changing the treatment paradigm.5 6 Besides, trials report encouraging activity of EV in Nectin-4 expressing non-small cell lung cancer,7 triple-negative breast cancer,8 etc, thereby supporting broader pan-cancer exploration. Emerging evidence has further demonstrated widespread and intense Nectin-4 expression in several aggressive neoplasms, including penile carcinoma and testicular choriocarcinoma, which provides a strong preclinical rationale for evaluating EV and other Nectin-4-targeted therapies in these under-investigated rare malignancies.9 10 This highlights the unmet clinical need to initiate dedicated clinical trials to validate the efficacy and safety of Nectin-4-targeted strategies in these tumor types, an effort that will further expand the pan-cancer application scope of Nectin-4-targeted therapies. A key unmet challenge in the pan-cancer application of Nectin-4-targeted therapies, however, is the lack of unified and validated Nectin-4 expression thresholds across different tumor types, with heterogeneous detection methods and expression criteria adopted in current clinical trials. This deficiency hinders the accurate biomarker-driven stratification of patients who are most likely to benefit from such therapies and may lead to inconsistent treatment response rates across different malignancies. The development of standardized Nectin-4 detection assays and the definition of tumor-type-specific expression thresholds are critical for improving the precision and efficacy of Nectin-4-targeted therapies in pan-cancer settings, and this is a core direction for future translational research. Recent research has focused on optimizing Nectin-4-targeted therapies. Newer generation ADCs are being developed with improved drug-linker technology to enhance efficacy and reduce toxicity. In many countries, regulatory policies are adapting to support innovative cancer drugs, like those targeting Nectin-4. In the USA, the FDA’s expedited approval programs have hastened the approval of some Nectin-4 therapies using surrogate endpoints or early-stage data, speeding up their transition into clinical practice.

In conclusion, the Trialtrove database analysis highlights Nectin-4 as a promising therapeutic target for pan-cancer, notably in UC and breast cancer. While Nectin-4-targeted therapies, particularly ADCs, have advanced significantly, the pipeline is expanding with innovative approaches like bispecific antibodies and CAR-T cells. Future research should prioritize large-scale clinical trials to confirm these therapies’ efficacy and safety across cancer types. With ongoing optimization and supportive regulatory policies, Nectin-4-targeted therapies are set to significantly impact cancer treatment.

Mathematical modeling with RGB data for comparative analysis of rice lineages and distribution of breaking points
Authors: Jae-Ryoung Park, Hayoung Choi, Hyeonjong Hong, Gyuseon Lee, Jaemin Na, Sehun Oh, Jungmin Shin, JeongHo Baek, Kyung-Hwan Kim, Younghae Do, Kyung-Min Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is a crucial food crop, supplying a significant portion of the global population’s caloric intake. With the shift from traditional breeding methods to digital approaches, image analysis is becoming essential for distinguishing between rice cultivars. However, the optimal growth stages for effectively utilizing image analysis to classify rice varieties remain uncertain. This study aimed to evaluate 102 rice cultivars through non-destructive image processing and RGB ratio analysis. Images were captured every two days throughout the growth period, and an RGB ratio formula was developed, excluding background pixels to focus on plant characteristics. Regression analysis identified critical time points for differentiation, with the red (R) channel being most effective at 55 and 75 days post-transplanting, and the green (G) channel at 60 and 80 days. Hierarchical clustering of slopes from piecewise regression categorized the 102 cultivars into three distinct clusters, representing their ecological types. These findings provide a precise and efficient method for classifying rice cultivars, offering breeders key insights into the most effective stages for variety differentiation. By optimizing image analysis techniques, this research enhances the efficiency of rice breeding programs and supports the targeted management of genetic resources for improved trait selection.

Introduction

Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is a staple crop that provides over 60% of the global population with a significant portion of their calorie intake [1]. However, with the recent acceleration of climate change and increased exposure to extreme environmental conditions, maintaining or improving rice yields under such stress is now a key focus of rice breeding research [2, 3].

Traditionally, rice breeders develop high-yield cultivars by utilizing elite cultivars to produce recombinant inbred lines, from where they select the best lines or generate backcross inbred lines through repeated backcrossing. The agronomic traits of these lines are then evaluated in the field across the entire growth cycle. These processes are time-consuming and resource-intensive, requiring plant cultivation and management throughout their development, which demands significant labor and financial investment [4]. Recently, image-based phenotyping techniques have been introduced to address these limitations, serving as a key component of digital breeding. This technology is integrated into rice breeding programs to enhance precision and scientific rigor, enabling more accurate, high-throughput analysis of phenotypic traits, ultimately accelerating the breeding process and improving the selection of high-yield cultivars [5].

Prediction plays a critical role in contemporary breeding programs. Accurate yield forecasting is essential for ensuring sufficient food supply under diverse conditions, including rapid climate change and a growing global population. To achieve this, precise and reliable yield prediction models are crucial for supporting breeding decisions and enhancing food security in challenging environments [6]. Rice yield estimation was previously unreliable until harvest, often leading to significant discrepancies between the projected yield and the actual harvested amount. This lack of precision made it challenging for breeders to make informed decisions during the breeding process, leading to inefficiencies in crop improvement efforts [7, 8]. Rice yield fluctuates significantly owing to unpredictable factors such as pest and disease outbreaks, cultivation practices, and sudden climate shifts. These external stressors significantly affect rice yields, complicating the breeding process and underscoring the importance of accurate yield forecasting for informed crop management and improvement strategies [9, 10]. Digital breeding leverages the synergy of image analysis, artificial intelligence, and machine learning algorithms to analyze historical data and monitor environmental conditions in real time. This approach enables early detection of unforeseen issues, such as stressors or environmental fluctuations, enhancing the accuracy of future yield predictions. These advancements improve decision-making in breeding programs by offering timely insights and adaptive strategies for optimal crop management [11, 12].

A novel rice leaf color simulation model leveraging RGB values to predict phenotypic changes was developed. This model shows that RGB values of leaf color gradually decrease from light green to green, stabilize, and then increase to yellow at various growth stages [13]. This study highlights the potential of RGB data to capture the dynamic changes in leaf color and its utility in plant phenotyping.

However, using absolute RGB values has limitations, particularly owing to varying lighting conditions that affect the measurement accuracy. Recent research shows that using RGB ratios rather than absolute RGB values may provide a more accurate and reliable metric for plant phenotyping. The ratios of red (R), green (G), and blue (B) offer a normalized perspective that minimizes the effect of varying lighting conditions and emphasizes the relative color properties of the plant tissue [14].

Our research focuses on leveraging RGB ratios to identify the characteristics of various rice cultivars. Studies highlight the effectiveness of RGB ratios in plant analysis, such as the link observed between the RGB ratios of tobacco leaves and SPAD values, underscoring the potential of RGB data in plant analysis [15]. Additionally, nonlinear regression is employed to estimate above-ground biomass and nitrogen uptake in rice crops using RGB data obtained from digital camera images [16].

Rice breeders traditionally collect diverse genetic resources to enhance agronomic traits through extensive field evaluations. They manually select parental lines for crossing, ensuring the selection of suitable genotypes with desirable traits for breeding programs. This process is crucial for developing new cultivars with improved yield, disease resistance, and environmental adaptability [17]. Rice breeders must evaluate elite lines based on multiple agronomic traits, requiring the use of various selection criteria. This process requires breeders to spend considerable time in the field, conducting detailed phenotypic assessments to identify superior lines for further breeding and development. These evaluations are essential for identifying the most promising genotypes that exhibit improved yield potential and stress tolerance [18]. However, researchers focus on minimizing selection criteria while developing and applying more precise techniques to identify elite lines. These advancements aim to streamline the selection process and improve the accuracy of phenotypic evaluations, reducing the time and effort needed in the field [19]. Kuo et al. highlight grain characteristics as key indicators for image-based analysis, allowing for the accurate differentiation of rice cultivars. This method serves as a valuable tool for breeders, offering a reliable criterion for selecting parental lines in the development of new cultivars. Using grain traits as an index in digital breeding significantly enhances the precision of phenotypic assessments, facilitating more efficient breeding decisions [20].

Recognizing the potential of RGB ratios, this study explores their utility for distinguishing rice cultivars and identifying key developmental transitions. RGB-based image analysis has emerged as an efficient and non-destructive approach for monitoring crop growth and physiological changes. Determining the optimal growth stage for image acquisition is essential for maximizing the accuracy of cultivar differentiation [21].

Although RGB-based imaging has been widely used for estimating physiological traits such as chlorophyll content, nitrogen status, and biomass, most previous studies have focused on measurements obtained at specific developmental stages. Consequently, little information is available regarding how RGB-derived parameters change throughout the entire growth cycle and whether distinct developmental transition points can be used to differentiate rice cultivars. Furthermore, the optimal growth stages at which RGB information provides maximum discriminatory power among cultivars remain largely unexplored.

Rice breeding increasingly requires phenotyping approaches that capture dynamic developmental changes rather than single-time-point measurements. Therefore, this study analyzed temporal RGB trajectories across 102 rice cultivars to identify developmental transition points and determine the growth stages most effective for cultivar differentiation. The resulting framework provides a quantitative basis for temporal phenotyping and cultivar classification using RGB-derived information.

Materials and methods

Plant samples

The experimental materials included 102 rice cultivars bred in Korea, including Dunnaebyeo, Samcheonbyeo, Kuemobyeo2, Heukjinjubyeo, and Undoobyeo. All cultivars used in this study are listed in the supplementary data. The 102 Korea-bred rice cultivars used in this study were obtained from the Crop Breeding Division of the National Institute of Crop Science, Rural Development Administration. This research was conducted in accordance with the international guidelines and legal standards established by the Rural Development Administration (RDA) of Korea, involving 102 rice cultivars. The rice plants were cultivated using conventional local agricultural practices. This study also adhered to the regulations of the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora (CITES) (https://www.cites.org/). For seed disinfection, the fungicide Spotak (HANKOOKSAMGONG, Seoul, South Korea) was used. The disinfected seeds were soaked in the dark at 33 °C for 3 days to initiate germination. On April 20, 2023, disinfected seeds were sown in 50-cell seedling trays filled with rice nursery soil at the fields of the Kyungpook National University (36°6′41.54″N, 128°38′26.17″E). After 30 days, the seedlings were transplanted into Wagner pots (AS ONE, NF-5/Ø174.6×Ø160.4 × 197.5 mm, Osaka, Japan) with a spacing of 15 × 15 cm. Fertilization during the rice growth period adhered to the standards set by the Agricultural Science and Technology Research Guidelines of RDA, applying N-P2O5-K2O = 9-4.5-5.7 kg/10a. Fertilizer was applied 14 days after transplanting into the Wagner pots [22]. A total of 102 rice cultivars representing diverse genetic backgrounds and growth durations were included in this study. Each cultivar was grown individually in a separate pot under identical environmental conditions to minimize environmental variation and facilitate cultivar-to-cultivar comparison of RGB-derived temporal trajectories. All cultivars were managed using the same cultivation practices throughout the experimental period.

Data collection

We obtained data from the RDA in South Korea. We focused on 102 different rice cultivars, with each cultivar represented by 10 replicate plants. Four plants were randomly selected and transplanted into Wagner pots, with the experiment conducted across four independent replicates. All samples were cultivated under identical conditions to ensure data consistency. The environmental factors such as temperature, water quantity, irrigation timings, and other growth conditions were maintained uniformly across all samples. This aimed to minimize external variability that could affect the growth and appearance of the rice plants. High-resolution RGB images of each sample were captured. To capture growth progression throughout the entire life cycle, cameras were positioned at fixed locations, and RGB images were acquired at 2-day intervals from sowing until physiological maturity. Approximately 60 images were collected for each cultivar. All images were captured under identical imaging conditions using a fixed camera system to ensure consistency throughout the experiment. Time was expressed as days after sowing (DAS), with image acquisition initiated immediately after sowing and continued throughout the growth period. To minimize variation associated with illumination conditions, all images were acquired in a controlled imaging chamber using fixed camera and lighting settings throughout the experiment. Image acquisition was consistently initiated at 14:00 h on each sampling date, and the order of cultivar imaging was maintained throughout the study. These procedures minimized temporal variation in lighting conditions and ensured consistent RGB measurements across sampling dates. Each image was captured at a resolution of 4384 × 6576 pixels, ensuring detailed visual data. The images were color photographs, capturing the full RGB spectrum, which is crucial for analyzing the color changes in rice leaves (Fig. 1).

Data preprocessing

Each photograph was captured under varying lighting conditions, requiring brightness correction to ensure uniformity across all images. Open CV in Python was utilized to adjust the brightness levels of all subsequent images in each sample based on the brightness of the first image. Following brightness correction, the images were cropped to isolate the regions of interest. The images were cropped to a resolution of 2930 × 3365 pixels to eliminate extraneous elements, such as the pot and background. To further refine the dataset, image segmentation techniques were employed to distinguish the rice plant from the background. The background pixels were set to RGB values of (0, 0, and 0) to eliminate potential interference from non-plant elements.

RGB ratios

From each image, the RGB values corresponding to the rice plants were extracted. Each image contains N pixels associated with the rice. N was calculated by subtracting the number of background pixels (RGB: 0, 0, and 0) from the total number of pixels in the cropped image (2930 × 3365). Hierarchical clustering

Hierarchical clustering of 102 rice cultivars was performed based on interval-derived slope values. Agglomerative hierarchical clustering was applied using Ward’s minimum variance method to create clusters that minimized within-cluster variance. Cultivar similarity was assessed using the Euclidean distance between interval-specific slope values. All data processing and analysis were performed using R software (Version 4.0.2, The R Foundation for Statistical Computing). Piecewise regression analysis was performed using the “segmented” package, while hierarchical clustering was conducted with the “hclust” function from the “stats” package. The final dendrogram was generated using the “plot” function in R, with enhanced visualization achieved by incorporating cluster-specific colors using the “dendextend” package.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (Version 4.0.2, The R Foundation for Statistical Computing). Each experiment was conducted with four independent biological replicates to ensure the reliability of the results. Piecewise regression analysis was conducted using the “segmented” package to identify breaking points and estimate segment-specific slopes in RGB-derived temporal trajectories. Hierarchical clustering was performed using the “hclust” function with Euclidean distance and Ward’s minimum variance method to classify rice cultivars based on the slopes obtained from piecewise regression analysis. Graphical visualizations were generated using the “ggplot2” package. To focus on the developmental period exhibiting the greatest divergence among cultivars, piecewise regression analysis was conducted using RGB data collected between 40 and 90 days after sowing (DAS). Preliminary examination of the complete RGB trajectories indicated that cultivar-specific differences became most apparent within this interval, whereas earlier and later growth stages showed relatively limited discrimination among cultivars.

Breaking point

Our analysis revealed critical thresholds in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{std}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{G}_{std}$$\end{document} values, indicating significant transitions in rice growth patterns.

Although cultivar-specific RGB trajectories exhibited some variation, the estimated transition points were concentrated within relatively narrow developmental windows. For the R ratio, the first transition point was most frequently detected between 57 and 61 DAS, whereas the second transition point was primarily observed between 73 and 79 DAS (Fig. 6). Discussion

Rice is a major food crop and an important target for crop improvement programs aimed at enhancing yield and environmental adaptability [23, 24]. Diverse rice ecotypes exhibit distinct growth durations and developmental patterns. These biological differences provide opportunities for image-based phenotyping approaches to distinguish cultivars [25].

In this study, piecewise regression was used to characterize temporal changes in RGB-derived parameters [26]. This approach enabled the identification of growth-stage-specific transition points and cultivar-dependent developmental patterns. The resulting models provided a quantitative framework for comparing temporal RGB dynamics among rice cultivars [27].

The rice cultivars used in this study exhibited growth durations ranging from 80 to 160 days. To reduce noise and focus on the period showing the greatest phenotypic divergence, analyses were conducted using data collected between 40 and 90 DAS. This interval captured key developmental transitions and provided the highest discriminatory power among cultivars [27–29]. The present study was conducted under a single controlled environment to minimize environmental variation and focus on cultivar-specific RGB dynamics. Consequently, the transition points identified in this study primarily reflect genetic differences among cultivars under standardized conditions. Future studies involving multiple environments will be necessary to evaluate the stability of RGB-derived transition points under varying environmental conditions and potential genotype-by-environment interactions [30].

A key finding of this study is that cultivar differentiation was concentrated within relatively narrow developmental windows rather than across the entire growth cycle. The transition points identified around 55–60 DAS and 73–79 DAS corresponded to periods when cultivar-specific RGB trajectories diverged most strongly, suggesting that these stages contain the greatest phenotypic information for cultivar discrimination. These results indicate that developmental transition points may serve as quantitative phenotypic markers for comparing growth strategies among rice cultivars.

From a breeding perspective, the identified transition windows provide practical guidance for optimizing phenotyping strategies [31, 32]. Rather than conducting image acquisition throughout the entire growing season, breeders may prioritize data collection during the developmental stages where cultivar differentiation is maximized [33, 34]. This targeted approach could reduce phenotyping costs, improve screening efficiency, and facilitate the evaluation of large breeding populations [35, 36].

The temporal RGB trajectories identified in this study may also support germplasm characterization and parental selection [37, 38]. Cultivars exhibiting similar transition-point patterns are likely to share developmental characteristics, whereas distinct temporal responses may indicate different adaptation strategies [39]. Consequently, transition-point analysis may provide breeders with an additional criterion for grouping germplasm and prioritizing breeding materials before extensive field evaluation [40].

The clustering results suggest that temporal RGB dynamics capture biologically meaningful variation associated with cultivar development and stress adaptation [41]. These findings support the concept that temporal RGB patterns can serve as integrated phenotypic descriptors that summarize developmental progression rather than single physiological traits [44]. Cultivars resistant to stripe blight were predominantly concentrated within a specific cluster, whereas ultra-early-maturing cultivars with strong cold tolerance formed a distinct group. This observation suggests that temporal changes in the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{R}_{std}$$\end{document} channel may reflect cumulative physiological responses associated with both developmental progression and stress resilience, providing information beyond simple maturity classification [45]. From a breeding perspective, the identified transition-point framework may assist breeders in selecting the most informative growth stages for phenotypic evaluation. Rather than conducting repeated image acquisition throughout the entire growing season, breeders may focus on the developmental windows identified in this study, where cultivar differentiation was greatest [46]. Such an approach could improve phenotyping efficiency, facilitate large-scale germplasm characterization, and support the early identification of breeding materials with desirable developmental and stress-adaptation characteristics [47]. The consistent separation of cultivars into biologically interpretable groups suggests that temporal RGB dynamics capture integrated developmental and adaptation-related characteristics. This finding highlights the potential of RGB-derived transition points as phenotypic descriptors that extend beyond simple measurements of plant greenness and may support cultivar classification in breeding programs. Future studies integrating multi-environment datasets and quantitative cluster validation metrics may further strengthen the application of RGB-based phenotyping in breeding programs.

This study shows the application of RGB data in the mathematical modeling of rice cultivars. We focused on the distribution of breaking points and compared hierarchical clustering results with known rice lineages. Our comprehensive analysis includes several key steps, yielding significant findings.

This study demonstrated that RGB-derived temporal trajectories can be used to identify growth-stage-specific transition points in rice. Piecewise regression successfully detected biologically meaningful changes in RGB dynamics, while hierarchical clustering differentiated cultivars according to developmental characteristics. These findings highlight the potential of RGB-based phenotyping as a quantitative tool for rice characterization and digital breeding applications. The image analysis framework developed in this study was evaluated under controlled experimental conditions using individually cultivated rice plants and standardized image acquisition procedures. Such conditions facilitated accurate extraction of RGB-derived parameters and identification of developmental transition points. In practical field environments, however, additional factors such as canopy overlap, heterogeneous backgrounds, variable illumination, and environmental noise may influence image segmentation and RGB extraction. Nevertheless, recent advances in computer vision, semantic segmentation, and field-based phenotyping platforms provide opportunities to extend RGB-based analytical approaches to large-scale breeding environments. Therefore, the transition-point framework established in this study may serve as a useful foundation for future field-scale phenotyping applications.

The primary objective of this study was not to develop a predictive classification model, but rather to identify biologically meaningful transition points and characterize temporal RGB dynamics across diverse rice cultivars. Piecewise regression was selected because it provides interpretable estimates of growth-stage-specific changes and enables direct identification of physiological transition points. In contrast, machine learning approaches are often designed to maximize predictive accuracy, whereas the present study aimed to establish a biologically interpretable framework for understanding developmental transitions reflected in RGB dynamics. Therefore, this study focused on identifying critical growth-stage transitions using RGB-derived parameters. Future studies may integrate machine learning algorithms with the RGB-derived features identified here to further enhance cultivar classification and prediction of agronomic characteristics.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that cultivar differentiation was concentrated within specific developmental windows rather than across the entire growth cycle. The transition points identified around 55–60 DAS and 73–79 DAS represented periods of maximum phenotypic divergence among rice cultivars and may serve as practical targets for efficient image-based phenotyping. From a breeding perspective, focusing phenotypic evaluation on these developmental stages could improve screening efficiency, reduce phenotyping effort, and facilitate large-scale germplasm characterization.

Because this study was conducted under a single controlled environment, further validation across diverse environments and field conditions will be necessary to evaluate the stability of RGB-derived transition points. Nevertheless, the temporal phenotyping framework established in this study provides a quantitative foundation for cultivar characterization and future digital breeding applications.

Overall, these findings highlight the utility of RGB data in plant phenotyping and offer a novel approach to analyzing plant traits. Integrating digital imaging with mathematical and statistical techniques holds promise for future research in crop management and breeding programs. Continued refinement and expansion of these methods will further enhance the precision and applicability of RGB data in agricultural sciences. More importantly, the identification of optimal phenotyping windows provides a practical framework for reducing phenotyping effort while maximizing cultivar discrimination, offering a scalable approach for future digital breeding programs.

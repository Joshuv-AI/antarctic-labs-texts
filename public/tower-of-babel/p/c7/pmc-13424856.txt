Lifelong lifestyle management: the foundation of primary care
Authors: Su Hwan Cho
Published: 2026

The paradigm of modern medicine is undergoing a fundamental shift from reactive disease management toward proactive early detection and prevention, a transition significantly accelerated by the recent pandemic [1,2]. Robust scientific and epidemiological evidence substantiates the clinical efficacy of rigorous lifestyle interventions, such as weight optimization, smoking cessation, alcohol moderation, and regular physical activity. Consequently, lifestyle modifications for chronic disease prevention have assumed unprecedented clinical importance [3]. Primary care remains the vanguard of this transition. The articles featured in this issue underscore the need to integrate public health principles into primary care to effectively address distinct health challenges across the human lifespan. Collectively, these findings examine childhood obesity, adult sleep deprivation, and late-life tobacco use, highlighting the profound impact of daily behavioral patterns and reaffirm the critical need for preventive clinical interventions.

During the early stages of life, childhood obesity has emerged as an escalating global public health crisis, placing individuals on a trajectory toward adult cardiometabolic disorders, such as type 2 diabetes. Liberty et al. [4] investigated the lifestyle and familial determinants of overweight status among Indonesian school-aged children using a case-control design. Their analysis identified several obesogenic dietary behaviors, including high consumption of refined carbohydrates, inadequate protein intake, flavored milk consumption, and frequent snacking, which were all significantly associated with overweight. Notably, daily digital device use exceeding 2 hours emerged as the strongest predictor of childhood overweight, underscoring its role in promoting sedentary behavior and mindless consumption. Conversely, daily fruit intake, frequent family meals, and regular consumption of home-prepared lunches demonstrated robust protective effects. The authors also observed a correlation between formal maternal employment and an increased risk of childhood overweight, likely reflecting structural constraints on meal preparation and dietary supervision. Overall, these findings suggest that mitigating childhood obesity requires multifaceted interventions that extend beyond individual dietary counseling to address family meal environments, parental work-life dynamics, and screen time regulation.

Sleep deprivation is a prevalent condition that significantly impairs systemic health. Emerging evidence has linked sleep architecture to the delicate equilibrium between daily energy intake and expenditure. Suh et al. [5] investigated this relationship by analyzing the association between energy intake expenditure balance (EIEB) and sleep duration using comprehensive data from the 2019, 2020, and 2022 Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Surveys. Their findings revealed a significant sexspecific association, with women who maintained a balanced or positive EIEB, reflecting an appropriate balance between dietary energy intake and physical activity expenditure, exhibited a significantly reduced risk of short sleep durations of 6 hours or less. The authors posit that this phenomenon may be attributable to sex differences in neuroendocrine immune regulation, whereby adequate energy availability supports nocturnal cellular repair and anti-inflammatory processes. These findings suggest that achieving metabolic balance through tailored nutrition and regular exercise may help optimize both sleep quality and quantity. For primary care providers, these findings reinforce the utility of assessing metabolic and energy balance parameters while evaluating adult sleep disturbances, offering an alternative or adjunct to traditional pharmacological interventions.

In later life, tobacco use continues to drive severe morbidity and mortality. However, smoking behaviors in older adults are influenced strongly by social engagement and concurrent substance use, rather than isolated physiological factors. Using a classification and regression tree analysis, Moon et al. [6] examined Korean men aged 65 years and older. Their analysis showed that although chronological age remained the primary predictor of continued tobacco use, the underlying risk profile varied markedly across age cohorts. Among the “younger-old” adults (aged 72.5 years or younger), low social engagement emerged as the predominant risk factor, correlating with smoking prevalence approaching 25% within this subgroup. Conversely, among “older-old” adults (aged over 72.5 years), alcohol consumption emerged as the primary behavioral factor associated with tobacco use, whereas abstainers had the lowest smoking prevalence. These results suggest that geriatric smoking cessation protocols should not adopt a uniform approach. Interventions for younger seniors may benefit significantly from integration into community programs designed to reduce social isolation, whereas strategies for older seniors should prioritize integrated alcohol reduction counseling.

Ultimately, although pediatric screen time, adult metabolic homeostasis, and geriatric social isolation represent distinct clinical concerns, they share a fundamental etiology grounded in daily lifestyle behaviors. By adopting a socioecological approach, primary care physicians are uniquely positioned to identify these behavioral risks early and guide patients toward sustainable lifestyle modifications. Consistent with the ongoing editorial focus of Korean Journal of Family Medicine , integrating comprehensive lifestyle interventions into routine clinical practice remains essential to effective chronic disease management. By transcending the traditional compartmentalization of life stages and recognizing the cumulative, lifelong impact of daily habits, primary care can continue to evolve. Systematizing these preventive, personalized, and holistic interventions within routine primary care is essential for disrupting the cycle of chronic diseases and fostering enduring population health.

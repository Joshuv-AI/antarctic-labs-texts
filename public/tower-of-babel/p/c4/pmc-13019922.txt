Differential quadruple pattern: A new EEG signal classification framework
Authors: Bilge Ozgor, Omer Faruk Goktas, Mehmet Baygin, Sengul Dogan, Turker Tuncer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
EEG signals are the letters of the brain and reflect neural activity. Abnormal EEG patterns indicate brain disorders such as epilepsy. Recently, machine learning has enabled automated EEG interpretation with high accuracy. This study introduces an explainable EEG classification model based on feature engineering. A novel feature extractor, Differential Quadruple Pattern (DiffQuadPat), is proposed. DiffQuadPat computes relations between four channel values using difference-based transformations and combinational transition tables. Feature selection is performed by Cumulative Weight Neighborhood Component Analysis (CWNCA), and classification is achieved with t-algorithm-based k-Nearest Neighbors (tkNN). For interpretability, Directed Lobish (DLOB) is used to produce symbolic explanations. The proposed DiffQuadPat-centric XFE framework was validated on two EEG datasets: Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) and neonatal epilepsy detection. The model achieved over 98% accuracy under 10-fold cross-validation. Furthermore, cortical and hemispheric connectome diagrams were generated, enabling transparent visualization of brain-level interactions.

Introduction

Electroencephalography (EEG) signals play a critical role in the study of brain activity and diagnosis of neurological disorders (Vandana and Nirali, 2021). EEG is widely preferred in the evaluation of neurodegenerative and neurological disorders because it is a non-invasive method and offers high time resolution (Modir et al., 2023). This method, which directly records neuronal activities in the brain, is an important tool for understanding functional changes in the nervous system (Urai et al., 2022). It plays a critical role in the diagnosis and monitoring of many neurological diseases, especially epilepsy, Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS), Alzheimer's disease and seizure disorders (Maristany et al., 2024). By revealing abnormal brain activity, this measurement method allows monitoring the progression of neurological diseases and supporting diagnostic processes (Lima et al., 2022).

In recent years, machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) based automatic EEG analysis systems have been developed to reduce expert dependency and improve accuracy (Rahul et al., 2024). Feature engineering techniques have been widely used to extract meaningful information from EEG signals (Pahuja and Veer, 2022). While traditional machine learning methods perform EEG classification by feature extraction based on methods such as wavelet transform, statistical measurements and time-frequency analysis, transformer-based feature engineering approaches have come to the forefront in recent years (Pfeffer et al., 2024). However, deep learning models have disadvantages such as high computational cost, large data requirement and low interpretability (Weber et al., 2023). Especially in clinical applications, being able to explain the model's decisions is crucial for doctors to make reliable diagnoses (Jussupow et al., 2021). Therefore, explainable artificial intelligence (XAI)-based EEG signal processing models are attracting the attention of more and more researchers (Cao et al., 2024, Vimbi et al., 2024). Currently, most of the studies on EEG signal classification are based on a single dataset and have limitations in terms of generalizability. Moreover, most of the existing studies fall short of producing explainable results (Chen, 2024).

In order to overcome these shortcomings, this study uses the Directed Lobish (DLob) symbolic language to determine how features extracted from EEG signals are related to brain regions and produces explainable results. The developed model analyzes the differences between channels with DiffQuadPat method to extract meaningful features from EEG signals, uses Cumulative Weighted Neighborhood Component Analysis (CWNCA) (Ince et al., 2025) method to select the most informative features, and uses t-algorithm based k-nearest neighbor (tkNN) (Tuncer et al., 2024a) method for classification. In the final stage, cortical and hemispherical connections are visualized using the DLob symbolic language and the brain regions to which EEG signals are related are revealed. This study aims both to provide high accuracy in the classification of EEG signals and to contribute to clinical decision support systems by explaining the relationship between EEG signals and brain regions.

Literature review

Classification of EEG signals has been intensively investigated in recent years for the development of automated diagnostic systems (Sharma and Meena, 2024). Traditional machine learning approaches utilize handcrafted feature extraction techniques such as wavelet transform, statistical measures and time-frequency analysis to process EEG signals (Akan and Cura, 2021). In addition, deep learning-based models and transformer-based architectures have recently attracted attention with their high classification performance (Abibullaev et al., 2023). However, these methods have significant drawbacks such as high computational cost, large data requirement and lack of explainability. In this context, in this study, DiffQuadPat method is developed for ALS and neonatal seizure detection. The DiffQuadPat-based explainable feature engineering XFE) model proposed in this research, which basically tests two different datasets using the same method, aims to achieve both high accuracy and explainable results. When the studies in the literature are examined, it is seen that mostly a single data set is used in the classification of EEG signals. In one of these studies, Liu et al. (2025) investigate the importance of EEG channel selection in Brain-Computer Interface BCI) systems for motor imagery (MI) classification. The channel subsets determined using the correlation coefficient of spectral entropy were tested with the Filter Bank Common Spatial Pattern (FBC SP) algorithm and experiments were performed on 4 healthy individuals and 1 ALS patient. The results show that the proposed channel selection method improves the classification accuracy between 1.25% and 8.22%, with channels in the motor cortex region having the highest selection probability. Pirasteh et al. (2024) review EEG-based BCI methods for the rehabilitation of advanced ALS patients. Various techniques such as MI, P300 and hybrid BCI systems are discussed, and methods for EEG signal processing, feature extraction and classification are discussed. In this research, it is emphasized that BCI technology has the potential to improve ALS patients' ability to communicate and control their environment, but challenges such as signal accuracy and user-friendly interfaces need to be overcome. Ngo et al. (2024) present a dataset containing EEG and eye-tracking data from six ALS patients and 170 healthy individuals. Participants were recorded while using an eye-tracking-based typing system, providing data that can be used for a variety of research, such as motor imagery, motor cortex function, and the rehabilitation process of ALS patients. Hameed and Inan (2022) discuss the processing of motor imagery EEG data with machine learning algorithms for the diagnosis and classification of ALS disease. Significant features were extracted from EEG signals using various feature extraction methods (CSP, wavelet transform) and optimization techniques (ant colony and firefly algorithms). The results show that the highest classification accuracy is achieved in the theta band. Aliakbaryhosseinabadi et al. (2021) investigated the detectability of motion-related cortical potentials MRCP) in ALS patients to support their motor function and help them communicate in the later stages of the disease. SVM and LDA algorithms were used for motion detection in ALS patients and for discrimination between motion and rest phases. The results show that classification methods optimized on an individual patient basis achieve approximately 80.5% accuracy. Another dataset used in this research is neonatal seizure detection. Although machine learning-based studies for ALS disease detection are limited in the literature, intensive activities have been carried out in seizure detection. Tanveer et al. (2021) proposed a convolutional neural network CNN) model for the detection of neonatal epileptic seizures. In this research, a two-dimensional CNN architecture that works directly with raw EEG data from EEG signals was used. The model, which was created by combining three separate models trained with data labeled by three different experts, showed 96.3% accuracy and 99.3% AUC performance. Cambay et al. (2024) developed an XAI-based EEG classification model for detecting neonatal epileptic seizu res. The proposed model classifies EEG signals using a t-SVM classifier in combination with automata and transducer-based feature extraction TATPat). In this research, the proposed method was validated using a 10-fold CV strategy and achieved 99.15% classification performance. Abirami et al. (2022) performed 37 statistical and time-frequency based feature analysis on EEG recordings for epileptic seizure detection. In the study, the most important traits were determined by Wilcoxon ranking test and XGBoost method. As a result of this method, 84% AUC value was achieved. Chen et al. (2023) investigated the development and evaluation of a wearable multi-sensor platform for neonatal seizure detection. To overcome the limitations of EEG, the system combines data from ECG, respiration and motion sensors to optimize seizure detection algorithms. In this way, it was found that the use of multimodal features provides higher accuracy compared to single modalities. In another study, Tapani et al. (2022) developed an SVM-based algorithm for detecting neonatal epileptic seizures. The verifiability of this algorithm in terms of generalizability, equivalence with human experts and clinical effectiveness was investigated. Daly et al. (n.d.) aimed to improve existing methods for the detection of neonatal epileptic seizures using deep neural networks. Basically, by using data augmentation techniques and optimization algorithm, the input window of the model is increased to 16 s and better learning of micro features in EEG signals is achieved. The proposed model was tested on a large clinical dataset and achieved an increase of 2.32% in the AUC value. Although these studies in the literature have made significant progress using various machine learning and deep learning approaches for the detection of neonatal epileptic seizures, the generalizability, clinical applicability and performance of the models on different patient groups need to be examined in more depth.

Motivation and our method

This study is motivated by a practical requirement. EEG classification should be accurate. It should also be efficient and explainable. Many recent methods achieve strong performance. However, they often depend on heavy deep learning backbones and they provide limited lobe- and hemisphere-level evidence. Generalization is also weakened when evaluation is restricted to a single dataset or a narrow clinical scenario.

To address these limitations, an explainable feature engineering framework is proposed. DiffQuadPat is used as the main feature extractor. It is a transformer-inspired function that models inter-channel relations through difference-based transformations and sorting-based channel conversion. CWNCA (Ince et al., 2025) is then used to select informative features under a cumulative weighting rule. Classification is performed by tkNN (Tuncer et al., 2024a) to keep the decision process simple and computationally predictable. Interpretability is provided by DLob. Selected features are mapped into symbolic lobe- and hemisphere-level explanations and connectome views.

The framework is validated on two EEG datasets. One dataset targets ALS detection. The other dataset targets neonatal seizure detection. This setup is included to reduce dataset-specific bias and to support general usability. Overall, the pipeline follows four steps: DiffQuadPat feature generation, CWNCA feature selection, tkNN classification, and DLob-based explanation.

Dataset

To obtain classification and interpretable results from the recommended DiffQuadPat-centric XFE framework, two datasets have been utilized: (i) EEG ALS detection and (ii) EEG neonatal seizure detection. Both datasets were collected using two different brain caps, and these brain caps (EEG collection devices) have 32 channels (EEG ALS detection) and 19 channels (EEG neonatal seizure detection). In this respect, the model has been tested for multiple device-based EEG signal classification. The details of the EEG datasets utilized are explained below.

EEG ALS dataset

The EEG ALS dataset is an unbalanced dataset since researchers collected EEG signals from 6 ALS subjects and 170 control subjects (Ngo et al., 2024, Le et al., 2024). Therefore, we have selected 2631 EEG signals from these two classes to balance the dataset. Thus, there are 5262 EEG signals in this dataset. This dataset was collected using a brain cap with 32 channels. The sampling frequency of the EEG collection device is 256 Hz, and the length of each EEG signal is 10 s.

EEG neonatal seizure dataset

This dataset has two classes, like the EEG ALS dataset, and contains more than 10,000 EEG signals (Stevenson et al., 2019). There are 19 channels in this dataset. The length of each EEG signal is 30 s, and the sampling frequency of the EEG collection device used is 256 Hz. In this dataset, there are 7011 EEG signals in the seizure class and 3422 EEG signals in the control class. Thus, there are 10,433 EEG signals in this dataset.

Differential quadruple pattern

The core innovation of this research is DiffQuadPat. DiffQuadPat uses two methods: The DiffChanTrans and the Combinational Transition Table (CTT) feature extractor. By deploying DiffChanTrans, six transformed signals are generated. CTT extracts 15 individual feature vectors (i.e., Combination (6,2) = 15) from these six transformed signals created by DiffChanTrans. Fig. 1 showcases that DiffChanTrans uses an overlapping matrix with four vectors. By deploying combinational subtraction, six differential vectors are created; these vectors are then used as input for the sorting-based transformation, which generates six transformed signals.

The six transformed signals generated by DiffChanTrans are used as input to the CTT feature extraction function. Using these six inputs, 15 feature vectors are generated. In the final step, the 15 generated feature vectors are merged to create the final feature vector. By combining these two methods (DiffChanTrans and CTT), DiffQuadPat is introduced. The proposed explainable feature engineering framework

In order to investigate the introduced DiffQuadPat’s classification ability, we have presented a new-generation XFE model. This XFE model used the recommended DiffQuadPat to generate features; CWNCA has been utilized to choose the most informative features of the extracted features; a tkNN classifier has been utilized for classification; and the classification results have been generated by deploying this classifier with 10-fold cross-validation (CV). In the last phase, the DLob-related XAI generation method has been used. For this reason, the introduced DiffQuadPat-based XFE model produced both classification and interpretable results. Using the classification results, we have demonstrated the high and general classification ability of the recommended DiffQuadPat-centric XFE model. Also, by utilizing the interpretable results, explainable outcomes have been computed for ALS and neonatal seizure detection. To investigate the classification performance of the introduced DiffQuadPat feature extractor, we utilized two different EEG signal datasets: (i) the EEG ALS dataset and (ii) the EEG neonatal seizure dataset.

After downloading these datasets, we implemented the presented model using MATLAB version 2024a on a personal computer (PC) with a simple configuration. The utilized PC has 16 GB of main memory and a 2.8 GHz processor. Moreover, the operating system (OS) used is Windows 11.

In the literature, most studies have focused on classification performance. Therefore, we present the classification results in this section.

Discussions

In this research, we have presented a new-generation XFE framework, which generates both interpretable and classification results. Specifically, this framework was designed for multichannel signal classification. A channel-based feature extractor has been presented in this research to obtain interpretable results. Additionally, a feature extraction-based transformation method (DiffChanTrans) was integrated to achieve high classification performance and XAI results.

This framework (the DiffQuadPat-centric XFE) was tested on two distinct datasets, yielding 98.84% and 99.38% accuracies for the EEG ALS and EEG Neonatal Seizure datasets, respectively. These classification results were computed using the tkNN classifier. In the tests, we used conventional classifiers to identify the best classifier and then applied the t algorithm to the selected best classifier. We conducted this test using the MATLAB classification learner tool. The classifiers used included the best version of the decision tree (DT), linear discriminant (LD), quadratic discriminant (QD), support vector machine (SVM), kNN, and artificial neural network (ANN). The computed classification accuracies of these classifiers for both datasets are demonstrated in Fig. 6.Fig. 6Benchmark of the classifier.

Benchmark of the classifier.

We used these classifiers with their default settings and did not apply any fine-tuning methods to obtain these classification results. Based on these classification results, the best shallow classifier for both EEG datasets was kNN. Therefore, we applied the t algorithm to the kNN classifier, and the classification results were computed using the tkNN classifier.

Moreover, some studies have utilized leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) CV to evaluate the classification performance of the EEG Neonatal Seizure dataset. Conclusions

The introduced DiffQuadPat-centric XFE framework showcased strong performance for both ALS and neonatal seizure detection, achieving > 98% classification accuracy on both datasets with 10-fold CV. Specifically, the framework achieved 98.84% accuracy for ALS detection and 99.38% for neonatal seizures using the tkNN classifier. This framework generated interpretable results for both disorders. In this regard, the research provided interpretable findings for both ALS and neonatal seizure detection.

The analysis of connectome diagrams revealed distinct brain patterns for each disorder. In ALS, the left frontal lobe (FL-FL transitions) and right frontal lobe activities (FR-FR transitions) were primarily involved, while neonatal seizures were more strongly linked to the temporal lobe (TR-TR, Pz-TR, TL-TR transitions). Moreover, by utilizing entropy analysis, the complexity of these disorders was computed. According to the results, neonatal seizures (94.12% DLob entropy) exhibited higher complexity than ALS (88.35% DLob entropy).

This study demonstrates that the proposed DiffQuadPat-centric XFE framework is a general EEG classification model with high performance on both EEG datasets. This research provided both cortical and hemispheric connectome diagrams using a DLob-based XAI model. Also, the recommended framework has linear time complexity. In this context, the presented research contributed to both feature engineering and neuroscience.

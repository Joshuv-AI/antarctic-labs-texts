Introduction

In recent years, according to the latest global tumor epidemiological statistics survey, during 2020, the number of new lung cancer cases in the world will be about 2.207 million accounting for 11.4% of all new malignant tumors, and the number of new lung cancer deaths will be about 1.796 million accounting for 18.0% of all malignant tumor deaths, which is a great danger to human beings [1, 2]. Lung cancer is mainly categorized into Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) and Small Cell Lung Cancer (SCLC), with NSCLC (including large cell, squamous, adenocarcinoma, and other NSCLCs) accounting for 85% of lung cancers [3]. It is very important to distinguish between NSCLC and SCLC in medical practice, and there are significant differences in treatment and prognosis between the two forms of lung cancer. At present, the treatment options for lung cancer mainly include surgical intervention, radiotherapy and the use of chemotherapy drugs. However, in the more severe metastatic stage of the disease, the application of chemotherapy may be limited due to its potential adverse reactions and lack of cell selectivity. Although small molecule anti-cancer drugs have shown effectiveness, they still face many challenges [4]. Therefore, in recent years, lung cancer has attracted many professional researchers to conduct in-depth studies on it in various aspects, but the pathogenesis of lung cancer has not been completely clarified so far [5, 6]. As its early symptoms are not obvious, patients are found to be in middle or late stage, resulting in less satisfactory treatment effect and high death rate. Prevention and treatment are still the top priority. In the era of precision medicine, there have been new developments in the prevention and treatment of lung cancer. Immunotherapy, as a potential effective strategy, has emerged, but it still faces challenges. Further research is needed to identify reliable biomarkers, manage immune-related adverse reactions, and overcome limitations in treating metastatic diseases [7]. Therefore, the search for accurate early diagnostic markers and timely and effective personalized treatment have become the goals pursued by researchers.

With the development of the Internet, the advancement of statistical technology, and the establishment of various public databases, the application of computer technology to analyze clinical case data has become one of the important means of accurate diagnosis and treatment, and has been widely used in various fields of medicine. The purpose of this study is to find potential biological markers for early diagnosis of NSCLC and potential targets for timely and effective personalized treatment by collecting complete clinical diagnosis and treatment data and follow-up of 509 NSCLC patients in our hospital, as well as genetic data from public databases, applying statistical analysis of relevant factors and genes for lung cancer diagnosis and prognosis, and verifying the expression of relevant genes in NSCLC and their expression in NSCLC through a small number of clinical specimens. The expression of NSCLC and its correlation with clinicopathologic features and survival time will be verified by a small number of clinical specimens, which will provide theoretical basis for improving the survival time and quality of life of patients and the prevention and treatment of lung cancer in this region.

Studies have shown that macrophages, as key participants in the body’s immune system, their specific activation state jointly drives the progression of lung diseases along with airway inflammation [8]. In recent years, inflammatory-related ratios based on peripheral blood cells, such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and prognostic nutritional index (PNI), have been widely confirmed as powerful and easily accessible prognostic indicators for lung cancer [9]. These ratios reflect the systemic inflammatory response and immune status, which are closely related to the progression of tumors and immune escape. Moreover, the interaction between platelets and vascular endothelium is a key step in initiating inflammation. Recent studies have shown that specific endothelial cell signaling pathways play an important role in mediating lung inflammation injury [10]. Currently, there is a lack of exploration of the prognostic mechanism of the interaction between platelets and monocytes in the tumor microenvironment. This study focuses on the platelet-to-monocyte ratio (PMR), aiming to explore another pair of key interacting entities in the tumor microenvironment—platelets and monocytes. Therefore, based on the successful prognostic value of previous inflammatory ratios, evaluating the prognostic potential of PMR in NSCLC has sufficient clinical relevance and provides a new direction for future exploration of combined treatment strategies for lung cancer and improvement of patient prognosis.

Materials and methods

Experimental data and methods to explore the effect of pretreatment PMR on the prognosis of NSCLC patients

Sources of cases

The samples for this study were collected by the researchers after the study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Guangxi Medical University and the patients' consent was obtained. The researchers used 509 patients with non-small cell lung cancer admitted to the Cancer Hospital of Guangxi Medical University for sample collection. During the patients' first hospitalization, their initial blood test results were collected, as well as information on follow-up and clinicopathological parameters.

The inclusion criteria for sample collection subjects were as follows: 1) patients with a clear histopathological diagnosis of non-small cell lung cancer, whose diagnostic criteria were in accordance with international or national standards; 2) patients with complete clinical data including basic information, follow-up records, imaging and relevant blood tests; 3) patients who were first seen in our hospital from November 1, 2013, to November 30, 2020; 4) patients who were able to obtain, after admission, the patients with the first complete peripheral blood test results, including platelets, monocytes, carcinoembryonic antigen, direct bilirubin, and blood glucose.

Exclusion criteria: 1) patients with combination of other malignancies or metastatic lung cancer; 2) patients with combination of serious diseases of other systems, such as hematologic disorders, immune system disorders, or severe cardiac, hepatic, or renal insufficiency diseases; 3) patients with acute and chronic co-infections; 4) patients receiving anti-infective treatment, using antiplatelet drugs, etc., within 2 weeks; 5) incomplete medical history and clinical information.

Clinicopathologic classification and OS criteria

TNM staging was categorized according to the 7th edition of the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer (IASLC). The patient recruitment period for this retrospective study covered the transition period from the 7th edition to the 8th edition of the IASLC TNM staging guidelines. To ensure the consistency of the staging criteria for the entire cohort, we collected the T, N, and M staging data of all patients and compared it with the IASLC 8th edition TNM staging criteria. We re-staged all patients. Therefore, all the stages in this study were based on the unified 8th edition TNM staging.

Recorded indicators included: (1) clinicopathological characteristics: gender, age, history of smoking, alcohol consumption, family history, history of hemoptysis, CEA, absolute neutrophil value, monocytes, platelets, hemoglobin, lymphatic metastasis, pathological type, site of disease, and degree of differentiation. (2) PMR was defined as: The ratio of platelet count to monocyte count in peripheral blood is calculated using the formula: PMR = Platelet count (× 109/L)/Monocyte count (× 109/L). Since the units of the numerator and denominator are the same, PMR is a dimensionless ratio. (3) Overall survival (OS) was defined as: The time period from the date of pathological diagnosis to the time of death due to any cause. Censored data are defined as the last follow-up as of December 31, 2023. All survival analyses are conducted based on this definition. The follow-up method included a combination of outpatient review, inpatient review and telephone, once every 3 months for 2 years, then once every 6 months for the next 3 years, and then once every year thereafter, there were no lost patients in this study.

Statistical methods

This study conducted all statistical analyses using the R language (version 4.3.2). The comparisons between groups for continuous variables and categorical variables were performed using t-tests and chi-square tests, respectively. Based on the survival data, we used the `survminer` package (version 0.4.9) and the `surv_cutpoint` function within it to determine the optimal cut-off point for PMR by maximizing the selectivity rank statistic, thereby defining the high-risk group and the low-risk group. Survival analysis was carried out using the `survival` package (version 3.5–7), where: the `survfit` function was used to fit the Kaplan–Meier curve, and the log-rank test was used to evaluate the survival differences between groups; the `coxph` function was used to build univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression models to calculate the hazard ratio (HR) and its 95% confidence interval. Additionally, violin plots were drawn using the `ggplot2` package (version 3.4.0), and forest plots were drawn using the `forestplot` package (version 3.1.0). All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Experimental materials and methods for screening platelet- and monocyte- and NSCLC-related genes

Data retrieval download and processing

Log in to the MSigDB database to screen for genes related to platelets, monocytes and cancer. The search formula designed in the MSigDB database was “platelet AND monocyte AND cancer” to obtain all the gene sets related to platelets, monocytes and cancer, totaling 3175 genes. The appropriate format and file type were downloaded and data validation and organization were performed to ensure the accuracy and completeness of the gene list, removing duplicate genes and formatting gene names. Acquire gene expression data and survival data in TPM format for LUAD and LUSC datasets from TCGA database. The acquired data in TPM format were log(TPM + 1) normalized to handle skewness and heteroscedasticity of the data. Tumor samples were screened from the LUAD and LUSC datasets and matched with survival data to ensure that complete survival information was available for each sample. The “limma” package was used to analyze the DEGs (differential expression genes) of the tumor samples from patients and normal controls separately, and the test was adjusted to "|log2FC|≥ 1" and "qval < 0.01" for screening. A total of 4,249 and 5,962 DEGs, including up- and down-regulated genes, were screened in the LUAD and LUSC datasets, respectively.

Immune infiltration score analysis.Immune Infiltration Score Analysis (ESTIMATE) is a method used to assess the immune and stromal components of tumor tissue. This method predicts the level of tumor infiltration by calculating the stromal score (stromal score, which assesses the level of stromal cell infiltration in tumor tissues) and the immune score (immune score, which assesses the level of immune cell infiltration in tumor tissues) in tissues that are summed together to obtain the estimate score. The ESTIMATE package is one of the common tools used to calculate these scores. The estimate() function is used to calculate the stromal score, the immune score and the estimate score from the ESTIMATE package. These scores reflect the relative abundance of stromal and immune cells in the tumor tissue. The calculated scores are analyzed to explore the immune cells and stroma in the tumor microenvironment. The scoring results were visualized using appropriate visualization tools (e.g., ggplot2) for a more intuitive presentation of the analysis results.

Statistical processing

R4.3.2 statistical software was used to organize and analyze the data. Count data were described by the number of cases and percentage (%), and the chi-square test was used to make statistical inferences about the differences between the groups; if the measured data obeyed normal distribution, they were described statistically by the mean ± standard deviation (x̄ ± s), and were analyzed statistically by the paired t-test, and if the measured data did not obey normal distribution, they were described statistically by the median, and were analyzed statistically by the Mann–Whitney U-test. The ROC curve was utilized for the evaluation of prognostic modeling tests, and the area under the ROC curve (AUC) was evaluated to express the effect of prognostic modeling.

qPCR experimental information and methods

Clinical sample collection

After obtaining informed consent from patients and approval from the Ethics Committee of our hospital, we collected 15 cases each of cancerous and paraneoplastic tissues that were surgically resected in the Department of Thoracic Surgery of our hospital from September 2023 to November 2023 and met the inclusion criteria, and were diagnosed as adenocarcinoma of the lung by pathology. There were 8 cases each of carcinoma and paraneoplastic tissue diagnosed as squamous carcinoma of the lung. Cancer tissue was obtained from the non-necrotic tissue in the center of the surgically resected mass, and paraneoplastic tissue was obtained at least 5 cm from the margin of the mass.

Tissue storage: The tissues were placed in enzyme-free freezing tubes and stored in −80 ℃ refrigerator within half an hour after isolation.

The patients with lung cancer met the following requirements: (1) diagnosed as lung adenocarcinoma or lung squamous carcinoma by pathological examination; (2) all patients with lung adenocarcinoma or lung squamous carcinoma underwent radical surgery for lung cancer; (3) they did not undergo chemotherapy, radiotherapy or other adjuvant treatments before the surgery.

Immunohistochemistry experiment information and methods

Clinical sample sources and general information

Lung cancer tissues: 61 cases of lung adenocarcinoma patients who underwent surgical treatment in the Department of Thoracic Surgery of our hospital during the period from January 2018 to December 2018 and met the inclusion criteria, 42 cases of squamous lung cancer were collected for their tissue specimens, and the cancer tissues were taken from the non-necrotic tissues in the center of surgically resected masses.

Normal lung tissues: 40 cases of normal tissues of benign lung nodules were selected. Tissue storage: the tissue specimens were paraffin specimens. The included lung cancer patients met the following requirements: (1) diagnosed as squamous lung cancer or adenocarcinoma of the lung by pathological examination; (2) staged according to the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer (IASLC) 8th edition of TNM staging Ⅰa2 ~ Ⅱb; (3) performed standard lobectomy + lymph node dissection; (4) aged 20–80 years old; (5) cardiac, renal and other important organs function is basically normal, and there is no need of special treatment; (6) patients with tumor diameter > 5 cm and stage IIb received adjuvant chemotherapy; (7) follow-up information was complete.

Rabbit anti-human FSTL3, MYLIP, EREG polyclonal antibody kit (Ebixon), DAB color developer (Fuzhou Maixin), xylene (Beijing Soleilbao), anhydrous ethanol (Beijing Soleilbao), 0.3% hydrogen peroxide solution (Beijing Soleilbao), citrate buffer (Shanghai SINOPHARMA Group), neutral gum (Shanghai SINOPHARMA Group), hematoxylin (Beijing Soleilbao Company), Eosin (Beijing Soleilbao Company).

Evaluation of immunologic results

Evaluation of immunologic results

The slices were read by two experienced pathologists in our hospital in a double-blind situation. Under high magnification, 5 fields of view were randomly taken from each slice, and 100 tumor cells were counted in each field of view. The staining results were scored by semi-quantitative method: staining intensity (SI) × percentage of positive cells (PP). Staining intensity (SI) was scored as 0 (no staining), 1 (yellowish), 2 (tan), 3 (brownish). Percentage of Positive Cells (PP) Scoring Criteria: 0 points (0–5%), 1 point (5%−25%), 2 points (26%−50%), 3 points (51%−75%), 4 points (> 75%). The product of the two: staining score > 4 is considered as high expression of the target protein in the tissue, and vice versa, is considered as low expression of the target protein in the tissue.

Statistical analysis

The results of qPCR and immunohistochemistry experiments were analyzed using SPSS27.0 and Graphpad Prism9.0 software. Measurement data were described by (mean ± standard deviation), and the t-test was used to compare the means of the two samples (Independent-Sample T Test), and the chi-square test or Fisher's exact probability method was used to compare the count data, and the one-way survival analysis was performed by Kaplan–Meier, and the test was performed by the log-rank test, and the difference was considered statistically significant when P < 0.05. was statistically significant.

Results

Relationship between PMR and clinicopathologic characteristics of non-small cell lung cancer patients

The researchers retrospectively collected the medical records of 509 non-small cell lung cancer patients. To determine the optimal threshold for differentiating high and low expression of PMR, we conducted a correlation analysis between PMR and overall survival (OS) in NSCLC patients. By observing the distribution characteristics of PMR values and their relationship with survival outcomes, we identified 386.08 as the best cut-off point. This threshold clearly divided the patient population into two groups: PMR < 386.08 was defined as the low-expression group, and PMR ≥ 386.08 was defined as the high-expression group. Based on the correlation between PMR and OS in non-small cell lung cancer patients, PMR expression and OS survival events were analyzed by KM survival curves, and based on the optimal cutoff value of the survival package we determined that the optimal threshold for PMR was 386.08. Accordingly, patients were divided into two groups: the low PMR group with a PMR ≤ 386.08 (107 cases) and the high PMR > 386.08 PMR group (402 cases), both of which accurately predicted the prognosis of non-small cell lung cancer patients. By analyzing the clinicopathological characteristics of the patients, we found that there were no significant differences between patients in the high and low PMR groups in terms of age, history of alcohol consumption, family history, history of hemoptysis, CEA, lymphatic metastasis, pathological type, site of disease and degree of differentiation, and absolute value of neutrophils (P > 0.05). However, the percentage of male patients was significantly higher in the low PMR group than in the high PMR group (P < 0.001), the percentage of patients with smoking history was significantly higher in the low PMR group than in the high PMR group (P < 0.001), and the percentage of patients without hemoptysis was significantly higher in the high PMR group than in the low PMR group (P < 0.001). In addition, PMR values were significantly lower in the higher age group than in the lower age group (P = 0.044), significantly higher in female patients than in male patients (P < 0.001), significantly higher in patients without hemoptysis than in patients with hemoptysis (P = 0.011), significantly higher in patients with no history of smoking than in patients with history of smoking (P < 0.001), and significantly higher in patients with no history of alcohol consumption were significantly higher than those of patients with a history of alcohol consumption (P < 0.001), and there was no association between the presence or absence of lymphatic metastases, TNM staging, family history, CEA, type of pathology, site of disease, and PMR values (all p-values > 0.05) (Table 4 and Fig. 1).

Impact of PMR combined with clinical on patient prognosis

For the combination of PMR with different clinical features, we categorized patients into different subgroups and used Kaplan–Meier curves to assess the prognosis of NSCLC patients in different clinical subgroups (Fig. 3). Among the PMR subgroups aged ≥ 60 years or aged < 60 years, the low PMR subgroup aged ≥ 60 years had a significantly worse prognosis (P < 0.0001). In subgroups grouped by PMR and sex, Kaplan–Meier curves showed that the male subgroup with low PMR had a significantly worse prognosis (P < 0.0001). In subgroups consisting of PMR with or without a history of smoking, Kaplan–Meier curves showed that the subgroup with a history of smoking and low PMR had a significantly worse prognosis (P < 0.0001). In the subgroup consisting of PMR with squamous cell carcinoma and lung adenocarcinoma, the Kaplan–Meier curve showed that the subgroup with low PMR lung adenocarcinoma had a significantly worse prognosis (P < 0.00052). In the PMR subgroups of CEA < 0.05 μ g/L and CEA ≥ 0.05 μ g/L, Kaplan–Meier curves showed that the subgroups with low PMR and CEA < 0.05 μ g/L had a significantly worse prognosis (P < 0.0001). In all of our data, patients with low PMR were shown to have a significantly worse prognosis than patients with high PMR (P < 0.0001). These results suggest that PMR has a significant impact on the prognosis of non-small cell lung cancer when different clinical subgroups of non-small cell lung cancer patients are categorized.

In summary, PMR plays an important role in the prognosis of non-small cell lung cancer patients, and the prognosis of the low PMR group was poor. In order to further risk stratify different non-small cell lung cancer patients and to identify factors affecting their prognosis, we focused on the relationship between PMR and prognosis in each subgroup using univariate analysis based on the baseline characteristics of patients in the low PMR group. We found that the level of PMR was significantly associated with prognosis in patients with different baseline characteristics (e.g., age, gender, CEA, moderate to low differentiation, adenocarcinoma, presence of smoking history, alcohol consumption, family history, hemoptysis, and LNM). Among them, patients with low PMR without lymph node metastasis (risk ratio: 5.24, 95% confidence interval: 1.91–14.39, P = 0.001) and patients with hemoptysis (risk ratio: 2.8, 95% confidence interval: 1.57–4.98, P < 0.001) had a worse prognosis (Fig. 4).

Discussion

Lung cancer remains a major threat to human life and health worldwide, and NSCLC is one of the most common types of lung cancer, but the vast majority of patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage, which greatly reduces the effectiveness of treatment and prognosis. The search for specific early diagnosis and effective personalized treatment has long been the most sought-after goal of researchers. TP53 is a crucial tumor suppressor gene, which is not only closely related to the survival rate of lung cancer, but also provides potential insights into the tumor immune microenvironment. The interaction between platelets and monocytes is a key link in the regulation of the microenvironment [12]. Many studies have shown that systemic inflammation correlates with tumorigenesis, progression, and prognostic properties [13–17]. Monocytes can stimulate tumor cells to produce various chemokines, which enhance tumor proliferation, invasion and metastasis, and platelets are also involved in inflammation and tumor progression, both of which have been reported to be closely related to the occurrence, development and prognosis of lung cancer [18–22]. It is worth noting that recent evidence suggests that combining systemic inflammatory markers with immune checkpoints can lead to a more effective prediction of the efficacy of immunotherapy [23]. However, studies exploring the prognostic potential of PMR in predicting non-small cell lung cancer and its specific mechanism of action are still lacking. Therefore, the present study combines platelets and monocytes to assess the value of PMR in NSCLC, and the screening and analysis of genes helps us to better understand the mechanism of action of monocytes and platelets in NSCLC,which may lay the foundation for the future construction of a precise prediction model combining PMR and immune checkpoints.

Due to the specificity of non-small cell lung cancer, the use of TNM staging alone does not correctly group all patients to correctly predict their prognosis and select the optimal treatment regimen, so TNM staging has some limitations. Meanwhile, we found that the prognosis of patients with low PMR was significantly worse than that of patients with high PMR in all subgroups. PMR is also considered an independent prognostic factor for OS. Therefore, PMR can further refine TNM staging by predicting patients' OS. Mehmet et al. [24] found by univariate analysis that high platelet counts were associated with better OS in patients with localized gastric cancer. Therefore, lower platelet counts that lead to lower PMR may also lead to lower OS in non-small cell lung cancer patients. Also, our results found that in patients with lung adenocarcinoma, the prognosis of the low PMR group was significantly worse than that of the high PMR group. Stephanie [25] found that an increase in platelets prior to treatment decreased survival in patients with lung adenocarcinoma, but had no effect on squamous lung cancer. Sung-Ho Qu [26] also showed that an increase in platelet count was significantly associated with lymphatic metastasis in patients with lung adenocarcinoma. Therefore, platelets may reduce the survival rate of lung adenocarcinoma patients by promoting lymphatic metastasis of lung adenocarcinoma, but fail to affect lymphatic metastasis of squamous lung cancer, and the exact mechanism needs to be further explored. Tumor-associated macrophages are tumorigenic regulators derived from inflammatory monocytes and are phenotypically and functionally distinct from normal macrophages [27]. Chongbiao Huang [28] showed that pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma cells produce increased IL35, which leads to more monocyte recruitment via CCL5. Interleukin-35 (IL35) is a member of the IL12 family and is a dimer composed of IL12A and EBV-induced gene 3 (EBI3). Elevated plasma IL35 levels have been reported to be associated with tumor development and progression [29]. Therefore, we hypothesized that lung adenocarcinomas may also have a worse prognosis by producing more IL35, which allows monocytes to be recruited and increased in number, resulting in a lower PMR. Our findings also showed that adenocarcinoma had a significantly worse prognosis than squamous cell carcinoma in the low PMR subgroup. Meng et al. [30] showed that patients with squamous cell carcinoma cervical cancer had a significantly better prognosis than those with adenocarcinoma. Fujiwara et al. [31] found that adenocarcinoma was more likely to involve lymph nodes than squamous cell carcinoma, resulting in a poorer prognosis. SPP1, a multifunctional glycoprotein, has been found to be abnormally expressed in a variety of cancer cells and has been associated with tumor staging and tumor metastasis [32]. SPP1 is a multifunctional glycoprotein that has been found to be aberrantly expressed in a variety of cancer cells and has been associated with tumor stage, lymphatic infiltration, and tumor metastasis. Zhang et al. [33] demonstrated that lung adenocarcinoma cells were able to regulate tumor cell activation, immunosuppression, angiogenesis, and tumor metastasis through the induction of SPP1 expression that activates programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) in macrophages, leading to the M2 polarization of macrophages. Clinically, the higher the density of M2-polarized tumor-associated macrophages, the worse the prognosis [34]. Therefore, the different tissue origins of squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma may be an important reason for the poorer prognosis of adenocarcinomatosis. In addition, our results showed that the subgroup with a history of smoking had a significantly worse prognosis in the low PMR group (P < 0.0001). Sangani et al. [35] demonstrated a positive linear correlation between the time frame of smoking and the percentage of monocyte count. Therefore, we hypothesized that smoking may contribute to decreased PMR and poor prognosis in non-small cell lung cancer by increasing monocytes. In conclusion, platelet counts and monocyte counts have the potential to determine, to some extent, oncologic treatment regimens, as well as grouping of non-small cell lung cancers based on PMR levels and providing more targeted treatment for high-risk populations.

Follistatin⁃related gene3 (FSTL3) is an oncogene encoding a secreted glycoprotein that is closely associated with tumor cell proliferation and metastasis [36]. Our study indicated FSTL3 in GESA analysis GO, KEGG enriched to the pathway and gene network GO analysis results indicated that the mechanism of action of FSTL3 in LUAD may be involved in the regulation of collagen synthesis, degradation, and repair processes and the stromal environment of the tumor cells, the structural organization of the tumor cells, the morphology, structure, and function; it may also be involved in the regulation of the relationship between the tumor cells and the surrounding extracellular matrix interactions and communication; and affects the adhesion, migration and infiltration ability of tumor cells. This finding is consistent with the report [37] that FSTL3 regulates the development and progression of non-small cell lung cancer. Our study revealed that the enriched pathway of up-regulated genes may be associated with processes such as tumor growth, angiogenesis and wound healing. And the enriched pathway of down-regulated genes may be involved in neuronal development and apoptosis, which may be related to the regulation of the nervous system in the tumor microenvironment in agreement with the report [38]. Negative regulation is involved in regulating the activation of the activin receptor signaling pathway in cells, cell proliferation, growth or differentiation [39, 40]; playing a role in negatively regulating the activin receptor signaling pathway may be associated with inhibiting activation of the activin receptor signaling pathway in relation to the modulation of cellular proliferation or inflammatory responses [41, 42]. FSTL3 immuno-infiltration analyses in all carcinomas and other single factor cox regression results have shown that FSTL3 is associated with a variety of inflammatory factors such as M1, M2 and a variety of cancers including squamous lung cancer [36–45]. FSTL3 has good diagnostic efficacy value for LUAD, which is important for the early diagnosis and precise treatment of squamous lung cancer, and has been reported to serve as a potential marker for tumor diagnosis [46, 47]. Consistent with our results, it can be seen that the FSTL3 gene was closely related to the development of LUAD in this study. However, there are fewer reports between FSTL3 and LUAD patients, our results showed that the expression level of FSTL3 was down-regulated in cancer tissues and higher in normal tissues next to the cancer, and the difference between the two was consistent with the results of the pre-biological letter study, the immunohistochemical results were inconsistent with the results of the Biological Letter results that FSTL3 was a hazardous factor for LUAD, and the low expression was negatively correlated with the prognosis, and the different validation results may be related with the The different validation results may be related to the different pathways affecting the development of lung cancer, the different roles of different types of cancer, or the small amount of specimens, and the mechanism can be further investigated in depth, but our results can validate the difference between FSTL3 in LUAD and normal tissues and the significant correlation with some of the pathological factors and survival time of clinical LUAD, which can be considered as a potential diagnostic biomarker and a potential diagnostic biomarker for LUAD. potential diagnostic biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets.

Myosin-regulated light chain interacting protein (MYLIP) is involved in the regulation of cellular motility and migration, and has been associated with tumor migration and invasion [48]. Heatmap of MYLIP co-expressed genes and the results of the enrichment of the two pathways reflected that co-regulated genes were associated with the localization and function of the positive regulation of organelle assembly, membrane docking, and axial specification, and played a role in the regulation of organelle structure and function in LUAD tumor cells as well as cell polarity and morphology, consistent with the report [49]. These processes are essential for maintaining normal cell function and structure, and their abnormalities may be associated with tumor cell growth, metastasis and invasion [48–50]. The enrichment results of co- down-regulated genes, associated with cellular localization and possible functions such as female pregnancy, multi-organ proliferation, cellular response to external stimuli, and placenta development, reflect the interaction and regulation of LUAD tumor cells with the surrounding tissues and environment, which may have an important impact on tumor development and progression [51, 52]. In addition, it may also be involved in the regulation of the keratinization process and mitotic process, which are associated with the differentiation status and phenotype of tumor cells, proliferation and growth, and cell cycle regulation [53, 54]; oxidative phosphorylation is part of intracellular energy metabolism process, and the enrichment in this process may reflect its role in cellular metabolism regulation, which is associated with tumor cell proliferation and cycle regulation. The results also revealed that MYLIP showed significant correlation with multiple immune cell infiltration in LUAD as a protective factor for a variety of tumors including lung adenocarcinoma lung squamous carcinoma, and the diagnostic ROC value for LUAD suggests that it is important for the early diagnosis and precise treatment of lung cancer. It has been shown to be a potential marker target for diagnosis and treatment of breast cancer [55] less reported in lung cancer. Sangshin's study showed that its expression was decreased in LUAD tissues and it was a protective factor for LUAD, which was basically consistent with the qPCR results we found,and with the immunohistochemistry results of the study. Our results showed its expression in LUAD tissues had a tendency of lower expression than that in paraneoplastic tissues, MYLIP protein was lowly expressed in lung adenocarcinomas, with a significant differential expression compared with normal lung tissues, and was significantly correlated with some of the pathological factors of clinical LUAD and survival time. It can be regarded as a possible potential oncogenic factor and biological marker for LUAD.

PKHD1L1 (Polycystic Kidney and Hepatic Disease Like-1), a gene whose function has not yet been discovered, was initially identified as a mouse gene [56], and the product is capable of encoding downstream proteins anti-human fibronectin-L [57] as well as a receptor expressed by T-lymphocytes, which plays a crucial role in cellular immunity [58, 59]. The results of GO enrichment analysis of PKHD1L1 co-regulated genes in LUAD indicated that for co-regulated genes, the positive regulation of lipid transport and MAPK cascade responses may indicate that PKHD1L1 gene in LUAD may be involved in the regulation of cellular lipid metabolism and MAPK signaling pathways, which may be related to tumor growth and metastasis [60] in agreement with the report. In contrast, the negative regulation of endopeptidase activity and peptidase activity may suggest a negative regulatory effect of PKHD1L1 gene on signal transduction within tumor cells, which may affect cell proliferation and viability. In addition, the enrichment of cellular localization and function in response to follicle-stimulating hormone stimulation may imply that this gene is involved in the process of signal sensing and transduction of follicle-stimulating hormone by tumor cells. For the co- down-regulated genes, the enrichment of cellular localization and function for mitochondrial translation, mitochondrial gene expression, bridging granule organization, ESCRT complex disassembly, and ESCRTIII complex disassembly may indicate a role in the regulation of intracellular mitochondrial structure and function, as well as intracellular degradation of waste products and clearance of metabolites. The enriched pathways for cell adhesion molecules and hematopoietic cell profiles may suggest the involvement of this gene in LUAD in processes such as cell-to-cell interactions, cell migration and metastasis, and the development and differentiation of hematopoietic cells in the tumor microenvironment [61]. In addition, ribosomes and the enriched pathway of Th17 cell differentiation also appeared in the analyzed results, which may suggest a role in biological processes such as protein synthesis and immunomodulation. The expression of this gene differed significantly from that of normal samples and is a protective factor against lung adenocarcinoma [62]. The confidence level of its diagnostic ROC curve AUC value for LUAD was very high, suggesting its importance for early diagnosis and precise treatment of lung cancer. In addition, it has been reported that its mutation may lead to autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease associated with the pathophysiology of childhood obesity and factors of male longevity [63–65]. There are also studies found that PKHD1L1 is lowly expressed in thyroid cancer tissues [66], and it was found that knockdown of PKHD1L1 promotes the occurrence, metastasis, and invasion of thyroid cancer cells [61]. All these reports suggest that this gene may be involved in a variety of tumorigenesis and development processes, and may be one of the oncogenes in tumors, consistent with our findings. In this experiment, we found that the expression level of PKHD1L1 was down-regulated in LUAD tissues and paired paracarcinoma tissues compared with paracarcinoma tissues, and there was a significant difference between the two compared with each other this suggests that PKHD1L1 may be a potential oncogene and a potential biomarker for LUAD.

There are few reports on the complement eighth component B gene (C8B), and the GO entries enriched for up-regulated genes are involved in a number of processes related to tumor development and growth, such as posterior renal glomerulogenesis and leukotriene biosynthesis process. This may indicate that these genes play an important role in lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) development and progression, and may be involved in cell proliferation, angiogenesis and other processes. Second, the GO entries enriched for down-regulated genes involved processes related to cell division such as chromosome segregation, nuclear chromosome segregation, and mitosis. This may imply that the down-regulation of these genes may affect normal cell division, leading to abnormal cell cycle and impaired cell proliferation [67] consistent with the report. DNA replication and chromosome segregationThe enrichment of these pathways may reflect their importance in regulating the process of DNA replication and chromosome segregation in LUSC tumor cells. Abnormalities in this process may lead to chromosomal abnormalities and genomic instability [68], thereby promoting tumor development and progression; the enrichment of these pathways for ribonucleoprotein complex and ribosome biogenesis may suggest a role for this gene in regulating ribosome biogenesis and protein synthesis in LUSC tumor cells. These processes are essential for maintaining normal cell function and growth [69]. Homologous recombination is an important mechanism in the DNA repair process that allows genomic stabilization and repair of damaged DNA in cells, and abnormal homologous recombination can lead to tumor development and progression [70]. The spliceosome is involved in regulating RNA splicing and modification [71], which may affect gene expression and function in tumor cells.The 98.1% diagnostic efficacy of C8B gene in the diagnosis of LUSC means that it is almost possible to achieve a level of accuracy close to 100%. This is important for early diagnosis and precise treatment of lung cancer. Our results showed that the expression level of C8B in LUSC tissues and paired paracancerous tissues was down-regulated in cancerous tissues, which was statistically significant compared with the two suggesting that C8B may be a potential oncogenic factor and a potential biomarker for LUSC.

Epidermal regulator (EREG) is an agonist of ERER that triggers intracellular cascade signaling transport network and regulates target cell function, which is important for regulating cell proliferation and survival [72, 73]. EREG has been found to be lowly expressed in normal tissues, but shows high expression in various types of human tumors (lung, colon, pancreatic, prostate, and breast cancers) [74–77], which is consistent with the results of our differential analysis, but contradicts our finding that EREG is lowly expressed in LUSC tissues, as well as scholars' finding that EREG is lowly expressed in some gastric tissues [78], which is consistent with our findings. The reason for the inconsistency may be multifaceted. Firstly, tumor heterogeneity could be one of the key factors. Compared to LUAD, LUSC has a unique driver gene profile and tumor microenvironment. Studies have shown that in gastric cancer patients with pathological stage II/III who have undergone radical surgery, there is no difference in clinical pathological characteristics between the group with high EREG mRNA expression and the group with low EREG mRNA expression [79]. This suggests that its biological function may be type-specific in cancer tissues. Secondly, as an ligand of EGFR, the function of EREG is highly dependent on the state of the EGFR pathway. In LUSC, frequent mutations in the EGFR pathway or molecular mechanisms may lead to dysregulation of its ligand expression. This inconsistency precisely reveals the complexity of EREG's role in different subtypes of lung cancer, which is worthy of further exploration in the future. The GO enrichment results indicated that fluid shear stress and migration of epithelial cells were positively regulated in the up-regulated gene enrichment. This may be associated with enhanced tumor cell growth, infiltration, metastasis and invasion [80]. Nucleoside metabolic processes, phosphoribosylated ribonucleoside metabolic processes [79] are associated with metabolic activity and growth of tumor cells. Under high EREG expression, there may be significant granulocyte chemotaxis and migration in LUSC tumor tissues, which can promote tumor growth, infiltration, and metastasis [78]; thus, affecting the immune escape and anti-tumor immune response of tumors. There are active cytokine signaling pathways in LUSC tumor tissues. Cytokines and their receptor interactions are important for the processes of tumor growth, metastasis and invasion [81]. Specific blockers regarding the EREG-related pathway have been widely used in the clinic or are in the phase III clinical stage but mainly focused on lung cancer and breast cancer [82, 83]. Consistent with the results of our study. In this study, we screened and analyzed five independent prognostic impact genes associated with NSCLC survival and their possible mechanisms of action in multiple datasets of public databases to provide new ideas for clinical diagnosis and treatment.

Despite our systematic exploration of lung cancer using clinical and public repository data, our study has some limitations. First, our data were limited in size and from a single center. Additionally, to ensure data integrity, we included all patients with complete records from the hospital's standardized follow-up database. However, this inevitably introduces potential selection bias, which limits the general applicability and practicality of our study. Second, there was no data from other centers for external validation. We only used single-center data to act as an external validation, and although our constructed diagnostic efficacy ROC curves performed well in the diagnostic efficacy validation, rigorous validation of the basic and clinical experiments, as well as validation of the external information from multiple centers, is still needed to provide more convincing evidence. Conducting prospective validation in an independent multicenter cohort is an important research direction for the future. Furthermore, although the current study has identified key genes, it has not yet conducted functional gain or loss experiments to elucidate how these genes specifically mediate the interaction between platelets and monocytes, thereby influencing the progression of non-small cell lung cancer. This is a major limitation of this study in terms of mechanism exploration. Finally, we screened and analyzed the genes associated with the genes, platelets and monocytes related to the prognosis of non-small cell lung cancer, and conducted preliminary verification through qPCR and IHC analysis. However, the sample size we used was relatively limited. Although we observed statistically significant differences in this sample set and obtained robust conclusions through strict statistical methods, the small sample size may still affect the accuracy and generalizability of the results. Therefore, our research conclusion can be regarded as a preliminary and strong hypothesis generated in a specific cohort. In the future, more basic and clinical experiments are still needed to verify our findings, especially to confirm the prognostic value of PMR in larger cohorts and explore its mechanism of action, with the aim of eventually applying it to clinical practice.

Conclusion

In this study, by collecting complete clinical diagnosis and treatment data and follow-up of 509 NSCLC patients in our hospital, as well as genetic data from public databases, and applying statistical analysis of factors and genes related to lung cancer diagnosis and prognosis, we found that PMR values can be used as potential prognostic markers of NSCLC disease. FSTL3, Myosin, PKHD1L1, C8B in NSCLC tissues, EREG genes were all expressed lower than in paraneoplastic tissues, and the immunohistochemical results of FSTL3, MYLIP, and EREG were correlated with some clinical features and survival time of NSCLC. The results suggest that FSTL3, myosin, PKHD1L1, C8B may be potential carcinogenic factors, potential biological markers and potential tumor treatment targets for non-small cell lung cancer. EREG may be a biomarker with significant prognostic value, providing more research directions for personalized treatment of non-small cell lung cancer patients.

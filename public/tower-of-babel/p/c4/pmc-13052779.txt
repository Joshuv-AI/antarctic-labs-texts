Targeting the neuro-immune crosstalk in breast cancer brain metastases
Authors: Yutian Zou, Jiadi Wu, Ze Yuan, Xiaofang He, Hailin Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBreast cancer brain metastasis (BCBrM) remains one of the most lethal manifestations of breast cancer. Its response to immunotherapy is severely limited by the blood-brain barrier, which restricts immune cell infiltration and antigen presentation, thereby creating an immunosuppressive microenvironment. To overcome these barriers, recent studies have focused on novel immune checkpoints, including the Lymphocyte-Activated Gene 3–Galectin 3 (LAG3–LGALS3) and T-Cell Immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM Domains–Nectin Cell Adhesion Molecule 2 (TIGIT–NECTIN2) axes, as well as on the reprogrammed metastatic ecosystem driven by immunosuppressive cells such as Forkhead Box P3-positive (FOXP3⁺) Regulatory T (Treg) cells, Lysosomal-Associated Membrane Protein 3-positive (LAMP3⁺) tolerogenic dendritic cells (DCs), C-C Motif Chemokine Ligand 18-positive (CCL18⁺) M2-like macrophages, Regulator of G-Protein Signaling 5-positive (RGS5⁺) cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), Galectin 1-positive (LGALS1⁺) and TANK-Binding Kinase 1-positive (TBK1⁺) microglia, and phosphorylated Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3-positive (pSTAT3⁺) reactive astrocytes. In addition, targeted inhibition of tumor-derived N-acetyltransferase 8-like (NAT8L) and metabolites N-Acetylaspartate (NAA), suppression of the N-Methyl-D-Aspartate Receptor (NMDAR) signaling pathway in tumor cells, and interventions against γ-Aminobutyric Acid (GABA)ergic reprogramming in BCBrM cells. Moreover, targeted interventions against distinct immune escape pathways—such as the Ubiquitin-Conjugating Enzyme E2T (UBE2T)/Cell Division Cycle 42 (CDC42)/Cluster of Differentiation 276 (CD276) and C-C Motif Chemokine Ligand 2–C-C Motif Chemokine Receptor 2/C-C Motif Chemokine Receptor 4 (CCL2–CCR2/CCR4) axes—have shown promise in reshaping the immune microenvironment and enhancing the efficacy of conventional immunotherapy. Collectively, this perspective outlines evolving strategies in immune checkpoint modulation, cellular ecosystem reprogramming, and neuroimmune intervention, providing a forward-looking framework to enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy in BCBrM.Keywords: Breast Cancer, Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor, Immunotherapy, Immunosuppression, Tumor microenvironment - TME

Brain metastasis of breast cancer

Brain metastases (BrM) occur in approximately 25% of cancer patients, most frequently in non-small cell lung cancer, breast cancer, and melanoma. BCBrM develops when disseminated tumor cells enter the circulation, form circulating tumor cells (CTCs), cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB), and colonize the brain microenvironment.1 The BBB, composed of endothelial cells, pericytes, and astrocytic endfeet, forms a highly selective barrier that limits tumor cell penetration. Once metastases are established, the blood-tumor barrier gradually replaces the BBB, exhibiting altered permeability and distinct molecular characteristics.2

Immunosuppressive microenvironment of BCBrM

Studies comparing the effector and exhaustion states of T lymphocytes (T cells) and natural killer (NK) cells between primary tumors and BrM have shown that BrM lesions exhibit markedly reduced effector activity and exhaustion signatures. Among breast cancer subtypes, triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) displays the highest T cell exhaustion levels. In addition, DCs and M1-like macrophages are significantly decreased in BrM sites, whereas M2-like macrophages are increased. These findings suggest that myeloid cells within the BrM microenvironment adopt predominantly immunosuppressive, tumor-promoting phenotypes.3 Additionally, T cells in BrM interact with fibroblasts/endothelial cells through TIGIT-NECTIN2, promoting immune escape. The abundant matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) 9+ placental growth factor (PGF)+ pericytes in BrM also interact with endothelial cells through the PGF-fms-like tyrosine kinase (FLT)/neuropilin (NRP) 1/NRP2 signal, promoting angiogenesis and supporting BrM.3 In the microenvironment of BCBrM, microglia exert pro-inflammatory effects and upregulate antigen presentation-related mechanisms, thereby recruiting and activating T and NK cells to enhance anti-tumor immunity. The absence of microglia leads to weakened responses of T and NK cells and accelerated progression of metastatic lesions.4 Through the analysis of the microenvironment of BCBrM, our previous study identified that immune-suppressive cells can reprogram the metastasis ecosystem, including FOXP3+ Treg cells, LAMP3+ tolerogenic DCs, CCL18+ M2-like macrophages, RGS5+ cancer-associated fibroblasts, and LGALS1+ microglia. These cell populations may also be the targets for immunotherapy against BCBrM.5 Moreover, PD-1 is poorly expressed in CD8+ T cells, while LAG3 and TIGIT are highly expressed. The interaction between CD8+ T cells and cancer/immune/stromal cells through LAG3-LGALS3 and TIGIT-NECTIN2 plays a dominant role in BCBrM immune escape.5 Therapeutic strategies targeting LAG3-LGALS3 and TIGIT-NECTIN2 could become potential options for patients with BCBrM. In addition, BCBrM cells upregulate NAT8L to produce NAA, which disrupts the formation of immune synapses between CD8+ T and NK cells. Eventually, this leads to immune evasion and reduces the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors. Targeting NAT8L or NAA can restore anti-tumor immunity and provide a new strategy for combined immunotherapy.6

Recent work has uncovered a critical role for neural input in shaping the immunosuppressive microenvironment of BCBrM, creating a permissive niche for metastatic outgrowth. In the context of synaptic signaling, metastatic breast cancer cells form functional synaptic-like contacts with neurons in the brain, enabling NMDAR signaling within tumor cells. This synaptic proximity not only drives tumor cell proliferation and survival but also reshapes the local immune landscape. NMDAR activation in tumor cells upregulates immune checkpoint molecules and suppresses the infiltration and cytotoxicity of CD8+ T cells, thereby reinforcing immunosuppression.7 Moreover, human breast cancer metastases to the brain acquire GABAergic properties, expressing key enzymes for GABA synthesis and release. Tumor-derived GABA acts on both tumor cells and immune cells in the neural niche. It directly promotes tumor cell migration and invasion, while also inhibiting the activation of microglia and T cells, thus blunting anti-tumor immune responses. This GABAergic reprogramming of metastatic cells represents another key neural-immune crosstalk mechanism that contributes to the immunosuppressive microenvironment of BCBrM.8 Together, these studies highlight that neural input acts as a critical regulator of the brain immune microenvironment, with both synaptic signaling and GABAergic reprogramming driving immune evasion in BCBrM (figure 1).

(I) Breast cancer cells traverse the blood-brain barrier through the circulation and colonize the brain to form metastases. (II) In the immunosuppressive microenvironment of BCBrM, the number of microglia decreases, which in turn leads to a significant reduction in anti-tumor immune effector cells such as CD8+ T cells, M1-like macrophages, NK cells, and DC cells. At the same time, immune suppressive cell subsets such as exhausted CD8+ T cells, M2-like macrophages, LAMP3+ tolerogenic DCs, MDSCs, FOXP3+ Treg cells, RGS5+ cancer-associated fibroblasts, and LGALS1+ microglia are significantly enriched. (III) The novel immune checkpoint axes LAG3-LGALS3 and TIGIT-NECTIN2, tumor-derived NAT8L and its metabolite NAA, promote BCBrM immune escape by mediating the interaction between cancer cells, immune cells, and stromal cells. In addition, the activation of the neural input-mediated NMDAR signaling pathway and the acquisition of GABAergic properties by BCBrM cells also participate in the construction of the immunosuppressive microenvironment. (IV) Targeted therapies aimed at specific cellular or molecular components of this microenvironment, including novel immune checkpoints such as LAG3-LGALS3, TIGIT-NECTIN2, tumor-derived NAT8L and its metabolite NAA, NMDAR signaling pathway, GABAergic reprogramming, astrocyte chemokine CCL2, TBK1+ microglia, pSTAT3+ astrocytes, and CD276 molecule, when combined with conventional immunotherapy, hold promise for effectively suppressing the progression of BCBrM. The next generation immunotherapy of BCBrM

Although immunotherapy holds great promise, current strategies for BCBrM face major challenges, including impaired antigen presentation, an immunosuppressive microenvironment, and restricted immune cell infiltration due to the BBB. The brain is traditionally regarded as an immune-privileged organ, protected by the BBB, lacking conventional lymphatic drainage, and exhibiting relatively low expression of major histocompatibility complex molecules. All these factors restrict the entry of immune cells and the presentation of antigens.9 Moreover, the PD-L1/PD-1-mediated immune escape mechanism is only applicable to a small proportion of advanced patients. There are many independent evasion mechanisms in the tumor microenvironment (TME), which can disrupt multiple core elements of antitumor immunity, but they have not yet been fully discovered.10 Therefore, novel combined immunotherapy should be explored to specifically improve local immune suppression in the brain and activate antitumor immunity.

BrM in TNBC remains a formidable clinical challenge, largely due to the BBB, which restricts the penetration of most systemic therapies and limits the efficacy of conventional and immunotherapeutic interventions. Shi et al employed protein chip analysis and identified aberrant UBE2T expression as a key driver of immune evasion in BCBrM.11 Their study revealed that UBE2T-mediated ubiquitination positively regulates the CDC42/CD276 axis. Specifically, UBE2T directly interacts with CDC42, promoting its K48-linked polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, thereby preventing CDC42 from inducing CD276 degradation via the autophagy-lysosome pathway. This leads to upregulation of CD276 on tumor cells, suppression of CD8+ T cell activity, and formation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment. Notably, CD276 represents a promising immunotherapeutic target in BrM, yet its clinical translation is hindered by the BBB, as most anti-CD276 agents exhibit limited brain penetration. Shi et al’s identification of UBE2T as a key driver of CD276 upregulation is particularly significant in this context. Targeting UBE2T may sensitize tumor cells to anti-CD276 therapy without requiring direct BBB penetration by therapeutic agents, instead modulating tumor-intrinsic signaling to reshape the brain immune microenvironment. Both clinical samples and TCGA data indicate that high UBE2T expression is associated with an immunosuppressive state. Importantly, UBE2T inhibition enhances TNBC sensitivity to anti-CD276 therapy and suppresses BCBrM progression. This work highlights UBE2T as a critical regulator of immune escape via the CDC42/CD276 axis, offering a promising immunotherapeutic avenue for BCBrM that directly addresses the unique challenges posed by the BBB.

By employing orthogonal strategies (including metabolic reprogramming, stromal remodeling, vascular normalization, and even neural input) to regulate the TME, it may be more effective in overcoming drug resistance than simply adding more ‘checkpoints’ with overlapping mechanisms.10 From single-target attack to systemic microenvironment regulation, the TME is transformed from an ‘immunosuppressive state’ to an ‘immunologically permissive state’, thereby reducing immune therapy resistance and enhancing its responsiveness. A recent study reveals that activated astrocytes secrete CCL2, which binds to CCR2/CCR4, enhances the tumorigenicity, recruits tumor-associated macrophages, and promotes BCBrM. Blocking the CCL2-CCR2/CCR4 axis can reduce the proliferation, migration, and invasion of BCBrM cells and improve the integrity of the BBB. When combined with checkpoint inhibitors PD-1/programmed death-ligand 1(PD-L1) or P-selectin/P-selectin glycoprotein ligand-1 (PSGL), it is more effective than single therapy, indicating that CCL2-CCR2/CCR4 is an ideal target for the combined immunotherapy for BCBrM.12 The characteristic of BCBrM is the extensive infiltration of tumor-associated microglia (TAMG), which promotes the spread and growth of metastatic breast cancer cells. TBK1 is enriched and activated in TAMG, which can upregulate granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), thereby promoting the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), migration, and invasion of breast cancer cells, ultimately leading to BCBrM. Combined treatment targeting the TBK1-GM-CSF axis and immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) may be beneficial for BCBrM patients.13 The TIMP1 produced by pSTAT3+ reactive astrocytes binds to the CD63 receptor on CD8+ T cells, thereby blocking their activated state. Inhibiting pSTAT3+ reactive astrocytes and combining with ICB, enhances the anti-tumor activity of CD8+ T cells in patients with BrM (figure 1).14

Future perspectives in immunotherapy of BCBrM

The distinct immunosuppressive microenvironment of BCBrM offers new opportunities for the development of next-generation immunotherapies. Future strategies may focus on targeting novel immune checkpoints such as LAG3-LGALS3 and TIGIT-NECTIN2, tumor-derived NAT8L and metabolites NAA, the NMDAR signaling pathway and intervention in the GABAergic reprogramming process, the chemokine CCL2 secreted by activated astrocytes, and specific cell populations including TBK1+ microglia and pSTAT3+ astrocytes, as well as the immunomodulatory molecule CD276, which is co-expressed on vascular endothelial and tumor cells. Precise modulation of the BCBrM immune landscape could enhance patient responsiveness to ICB. A deeper understanding of the tumor-brain-immune cells crosstalk will be pivotal for advancing next-generation combination immunotherapies against BCBrM.

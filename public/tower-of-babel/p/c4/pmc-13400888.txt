Clinical Significance of Malassezia‐Specific IgE in Assessing the Severity of Head and Neck Involvement in Korean Atopic Dermatitis Patients
Authors: Han‐Na Kim, Jaewon Choi, Anna Kim, Ko Eun Kim, Yoo Sang Baek, Jiehyun Jeon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHead and neck dermatitis (HND) is a distinct clinical phenotype of atopic dermatitis (AD). While Malassezia sensitisation and elevated Malassezia‐specific IgE antibody (MSIA) levels have been documented in HND patients, the relationship between MSIA levels and disease severity remains controversial. The primary aim of our study was to evaluate whether MSIA levels correlate with the severity of HND. This retrospective observational study was conducted at a single tertiary center and included 121 patients with AD. Laboratory data included serum MSIA, house dust mite (HDM)‐specific IgE and total IgE levels. Clinical severity was measured using the global Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI) and partial HND EASI scores. We introduced a novel metric, the ‘relative HND severity score’, defined as the HND EASI score divided by the global EASI score. Spearman's Rho correlation analyses were used to examine the correlations between different IgE levels and severity scores. The HND EASI score demonstrated significant positive correlations with MSIA (r = 0.569, p < 0.001) and both HDM‐specific antibodies: D1 (r = 0.247, p = 0.007) and D2 (r = 0.267, p = 0.004). The relative HND severity score was significantly positively correlated with MSIA (r = 0.353, p < 0.001), but not with HDM‐specific antibodies (D1, p = 0.985; D2, p = 0.974). Age‐stratified subgroup analyses revealed significant positive correlations between MSIA and both absolute (r = 0.591, p < 0.001) and relative HND severity (r = 0.344, p = 0.002) only in the adult cohort. In conclusion, serum MSIA levels significantly correlate with the clinical severity of HND in Korean AD patients. This association is most clearly observed in the adult population.Keywords: atopic dermatitis, head and neck dermatitis, Malassezia

Introduction

Atopic dermatitis (AD) is one of the most common inflammatory skin diseases with estimated prevalence rates up to 15%–25% in children and 3%–7% in adults [1]. Its chronic nature, fluctuating course and atopic comorbidities cause a substantial socioeconomic cost and lower the quality of life for patients and their families [2].

Within the AD spectrum, head and neck dermatitis (HND) emerges as a distinctive clinical variant, demonstrating preferential involvement of seborrheic anatomic sites including the head, neck and upper trunk regions [3, 4]. Compared to AD on other body parts, HND is more challenging to treat and has a greater impact on patients' quality of life [5, 6, 7].

The lipophilic yeast genus Malassezia has been increasingly recognized for its pathogenic contribution to HND development [4, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13]. Multiple studies have demonstrated elevated Malassezia‐specific IgE antibody (MSIA) levels in HND patients relative to those without HND [8, 10, 14, 15, 16, 17]. However, the relationship between MSIA concentrations and HND severity remains unclear.

Our investigation examined the relationship between MSIA concentrations and HND severity, measured using the Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI) scores. In addition to the global EASI and HND EASI scores, we introduced a novel relative HND severity score to evaluate the localized HND disease burden relative to the total body disease burden. We also performed age‐stratified analyses, acknowledging the established age‐dependent patterns of Malassezia colonization.

Materials and Methods

This was a retrospective, single‐center, observational study. We reviewed the clinical records of patients who visited the dermatology department at Korea University Guro Hospital, a tertiary hospital in South Korea, between January 2019 and August 2023. Patients diagnosed with AD based on the criteria of Hanifin and Rajka, whose records contained serum MSIA level results, were included. Patients without clinical photographs taken concurrently at the time of blood sampling were excluded, as images were essential for retrospective severity assessment. To assess age‐dependent differences, patients were subsequently divided into two groups using an 18‐year cutoff: adult and non‐adult groups. Patients were additionally stratified by sex (male and female) to evaluate potential sex‐based differences in the association between MSIA and HND severity.

Three dermatologists independently evaluated the clinical photographs to determine the EASI scores. The primary outcome measures included the global EASI score (overall AD severity score calculated across all body regions) and the HND EASI score (partial score specific to the head and neck region). The relative HND severity score was calculated by dividing the HND EASI score by the global EASI score, providing a measure of regional disease burden proportional to total body involvement.

Additional data included MSIA level (m70), house dust mite (HDM: Dermatophagoides pteronyssinus [D1] and Dermatophagoides farinae [D2])‐specific IgE levels and total IgE levels. All IgE levels were measured via ImmunoCAP (Thermo Fisher Scientific, formerly Phadia). The upper detection limits were 100 kU/L for serum MSIA and HDM‐specific IgE levels and 5000 kU/L for serum total IgE levels. To address data censoring and enable continuous statistical analysis, values exceeding these thresholds were imputed as 100 and 5000 kU/L.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were compared between the adult and non‐adult groups using independent t‐tests. Categorical variables were analysed using chi‐square tests. Spearman's Rho testing examined correlations between serum IgE measurements and severity assessments. To formally compare the strength of the correlations between the adult and non‐adult groups, as well as between male and female patients, Fisher's z‐transformation was performed. A two‐sided p value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 23 (IBM Corp., Armonk, N.Y., USA).

Ethics Statement

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Korea University Guro Hospital (IRB Number 2025GR0572) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Given the retrospective nature of the study, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the Institutional Review Board.

Correlation Analysis—Subgroup Analysis

Subgroup analysis was performed to evaluate whether the correlations differed according to age (Tables S1 and S2). MSIA showed significant positive correlations with both the HND severity score (r = 0.591, p < 0.001) and relative HND severity score (r = 0.344, p = 0.002) in the adult cohort (Figure S1). In the non‐adult cohort, although positive correlation coefficients were observed for HND severity score (r = 0.283, p = 0.170) and relative HND severity score (r = 0.295, p = 0.220), they did not reach statistical significance. To further evaluate whether these correlations differed significantly between the two age groups, a Fisher's z‐transformation was performed. The analysis revealed no statistically significant difference between the correlation coefficients of the adult and non‐adult groups (z = 1.63, p = 0.10), suggesting that the lack of significance in the non‐adult group might be attributed to the relatively small sample size.

Additional subgroup analysis was performed to evaluate whether the correlation between MSIA and HND severity differed according to sex (Table S3). MSIA showed significant positive correlations with both the HND severity score (r = 0.566, p < 0.001) and relative HND severity score (r = 0.344, p = 0.003) in male patients. In female patients, MSIA also showed significant positive correlations with both the HND severity score (r = 0.452, p = 0.023) and the relative HND severity score (r = 0.400, p = 0.048). A Fisher's z‐transformation revealed no statistically significant difference between the correlation coefficients of the male and female groups, for either the HND severity score (z = 0.63, p = 0.53) or the relative HND severity score (z = −0.27, p = 0.79), suggesting that the association between MSIA and HND severity is consistent across sexes.

Discussion

HND is a distinct AD phenotype affecting seborrheic regions, clinically characterized by typical erythematous eczematous involvement of the forehead, eyelids, perioral region and neck [3, 4]. HND often poses a challenge to dermatologists because it is difficult to treat and has a significant impact on patients [5, 6, 7].

While precise HND aetiology remains incompletely elucidated and involves multiple contributing factors including immune dysregulation, environmental influences and genetic susceptibility, Malassezia species are believed to play a central role [4, 8, 9, 10, 11]. Malassezia species are lipophilic yeasts that constitute the most prevalent fungal genus in the healthy skin microbiome. Because they lack the ability to synthesise fatty acids, they depend on exogenous lipids, which explains their predilection for sebum‐rich areas such as the face, scalp and chest. The colonisation rate of Malassezia increases rapidly following puberty due to androgen‐mediated sebum production elevation [12].

Substantial evidence indicates that Malassezia sensitisation is central to the pathophysiology of HND. While Malassezia functions as commensal fungi typically tolerated by human immune system [9], AD patients exhibit heightened susceptibility to Malassezia allergenicity due to combined endogenous defects: impaired barrier function, increased skin pH and decreased antimicrobial peptides [8, 10, 11, 13, 14]. Indeed, Malassezia species release more allergens in a less acidic environment (pH 6.0) compared to a more acidic environment (pH 5.5) [18]. The impaired skin barrier of AD patients then facilitates the penetration of the fungus and its allergens into the epidermis, ultimately exposing Malassezia antigens to the immune system.

HND diagnosis relies primarily on clinical features, often supported by positive type I allergic responses to Malassezia species. Sensitisation can be assessed through elevated MSIA concentrations or notable skin prick test reactions to Malassezia antigens [8, 12]. The limitation of skin prick tests has been the lack of commercially available standardized skin test extracts for Malassezia species, making it difficult to reliably compare results across different studies [14]. Nowadays the diagnosis is better supported by the availability of commercial standardized MSIA measurement kits, such as the ImmunoCAP m70 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, formerly Phadia) based on M. sympodialis [9]. More recently, the ImmunoCAP m227 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, formerly Phadia), which includes allergens from several Malassezia species, has been introduced. This new test shows a slight increase in sensitivity compared to the earlier m70 [19].

Multiple reports confirm significantly higher Malassezia sensitisation prevalence in HND patients compared to other AD subtypes or healthy controls [8, 10, 14, 15, 16, 17]. For instance, MSIA has been detected in up to 100% of HND patients versus 13.6% of AD patients without HND [20]. A systemic review and meta‐analysis published in 2024, which included data from 14 observational studies, found the overall prevalence of MSIA among HND patients was 79.3% [17]. However, this meta‐analysis was unable to perform statistical analysis for the prevalence of MSIA based on HND severity or patient age group due to the small number of available studies.

Given the role of Malassezia sensitisation in HND, few studies have investigated the correlation between MSIA and the severity of HND and existing results remain controversial [14, 15, 16, 17, 21, 22]. One previous study classified HND patients by severity (mild, moderate, severe) and reported that MSIA increased with clinical severity [15]. Another report noted associations between M. furfur IgE concentrations and clinical severity, but classified severity solely according to the percentage of affected surface area [14]. This methodological approach is incomplete, as surface area measurement alone fails to capture critical inflammatory disease components, such as erythema severity, edema‐induration‐papulation, excoriation and lichenification.

Since HND predominantly affects the head and neck area, we hypothesized that localized disease severity is more relevant than overall (global) disease severity. Therefore, in this study, we measured disease severity using the global EASI and partial HND EASI scores. Moreover, we introduced a novel ‘relative HND severity’ metric, defined as the HND EASI score divided by the global EASI score.

In addition to its correlation with severity, sensitisation to Malassezia is highly age‐dependent. Various reports found a significant correlation between the severity of AD and Malassezia sensitisation in adults, but not in children [23]. This difference is primarily attributed to the growth conditions of Malassezia species. Sebum production is initially high in the first 3 months of life, declines throughout childhood and then increases significantly during puberty and adulthood. Consequently, children tend to have a lower frequency of Malassezia sensitisation and lack the correlation between AD severity and MSIA [23].

Conversely, HDM sensitisation rates were comparable between the adult and non‐adult groups in our study. This is consistent with the concept that food allergen and aeroallergen sensitisation frequently occur during childhood, while Malassezia sensitisation occurs preferably in adulthood [23].

The role of Malassezia colonisation extends to the challenging clinical entity of dupilumab‐associated HND (DAHND) [11, 24]. Dupilumab, an interleukin‐4 receptor alpha inhibitor, blocks the signalling pathways of both IL‐4 and IL‐13 cytokines [25]. Despite its overall efficacy for AD, there are numerous reports of new‐onset or worsening HND following the initiation of dupilumab treatment. The cause of this adverse effect is currently poorly understood, but Malassezia hypersensitivity is the most frequently proposed mechanism. The Th2 cytokine IL‐13 is believed to maintain a balance between host and Malassezia colonization. By suppressing the Th2 immune pathway, dupilumab is hypothesized to further shift the immune responses toward the highly antifungal Th17/Th22‐mediated pathway, causing the inflammatory hypersensitivity reaction to Malassezia fungal proteins [26, 27].

A prospective cohort study provided strong evidence that pretreatment MSIA may be used as a safety biomarker to predict the possibility of developing DAHND after starting dupilumab therapy. In that study, a significant difference in MSIA levels was found between the DAHND and non‐DAHND groups. Specifically, a positive baseline MSIA (using a cutoff of 0.35 kU/mL) demonstrated a sensitivity and specificity for predicting the diagnosis of DAHND [28].

The strengths of this study include being the first to evaluate the correlation between relative HND severity and global AD severity with MSIA levels. It also includes serum levels of HDM‐specific IgE. Furthermore, a subgroup analysis according to age was performed.

The limitations of this study include its retrospective, single‐center design and small sample size. Another notable limitation of this study is the imbalance in sample size between the subgroups. The non‐adult cohort (n = 25) was considerably smaller than the adult cohort (n = 96), which may have limited the statistical power to detect a significant correlation in younger patients. Therefore, the observed associations should be interpreted with caution and the possibility that MSIA also plays a role in non‐adult HND cannot be entirely ruled out. Future studies with a larger, more balanced paediatric and adolescent cohort are necessary to confirm these age‐specific trends.

Furthermore, as we only used the commercial and standardized kit ImmunoCAP m70 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, formerly Phadia) based on M. sympodialis, the specific antigen used might influence IgE detection frequency. However, this concern is mitigated by reports indicating that while absolute antibody levels may vary by Malassezia species, the tendency for levels to be higher with increasing severity is consistently maintained across different species [15]. Moreover, one study showed that the MSIA prevalence among HND patients did not vary significantly between more predominant and less predominant Malassezia species [17].

Another methodological consideration relates to the relative HND severity score, a novel index introduced in this study to quantify the proportional contribution of head and neck involvement to overall disease burden. As this ratio‐based metric has not been previously validated, its statistical properties warrant further discussion. In particular, ratio measures can become unstable when the denominator (global EASI score) is low, since even small absolute changes in the HND EASI score may produce disproportionately large fluctuations in the relative HND severity score. Future studies should consider validating this index against alternative approaches, such as multivariable regression models adjusting for global EASI score, to confirm its robustness before broader clinical application.

A significant methodological limitation is the maximum upper detection limit for the IgE assays used. Specifically, serum MSIA and HDM‐specific IgE levels had an upper limit of 100 kU/L and total IgE had an upper limit of 5000 kU/L. Owing to data censoring, a substantial number of patients with IgE levels above the upper limit were clustered at the maximum measurable value. This ceiling effect limits the ability to accurately assess the full strength of the linear correlation between IgE levels and disease severity in highly sensitized patients.

Conclusion

Malassezia sensitisation plays an important role in HND pathogenesis. Our study specifically addressed the unclear relationship between MSIA levels and HND severity by utilizing both the standard HND EASI score and a novel metric, the relative HND severity score.

According to our study, MSIA can serve as a specific indicator of relative HND severity. Relative HND severity showed a significant positive correlation exclusively with MSIA concentrations, but not with HDM‐specific IgE or total IgE levels. This key finding suggests that MSIA is specifically associated with the disease burden localized to the head and neck regions. In subgroup analyses, these correlations were particularly robust in the adult population.

Despite being a retrospective, single‐center study, our findings contribute to a better understanding of the role of MSIA in the assessment of HND severity. Future prospective, multicenter studies with larger paediatric cohorts are needed to further validate these findings.

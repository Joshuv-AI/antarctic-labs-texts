Natriuretic peptides and soluble ST2 improves echocardiographic diagnosis of elevated left ventricular filling pressures
Authors: Paul-Adrian Călburean, Silvia Lupu, Adina Huțanu, Mădălina Oprica, Diana Roxana Opriș, Alexandru Stan, Anda-Cristina Scurtu, David Aniței, Marius Harpa, Klara Brînzaniuc, Horațiu Suciu, László Hadadi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractElevated filling pressure of the left ventricle (LV) defines diastolic dysfunction. The gold standard for diagnosis is represented by the measurement of LV end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP) during cardiac catheterization, but it has the disadvantage of being an invasive procedure. This study aimed to investigate the correlation between LVEDP and cardiac serum biomarkers such as natriuretic peptides (mid-regional pro-atrial natriuretic peptide [MR-proANP], B-type natriuretic peptide [BNP], and N-terminal prohormone BNP [NT-proBNP]), soluble ST2 (sST2), galectin-3 and mid-regional pro-adrenomedullin (MR-proAMD). Consecutive patients hospitalized in a tertiary center and undergoing left cardiac catheterization were included in the study. Diastolic dysfunction was considered present if the end-expiratory LVEDP was ≥ 15 mmHg. Cardiac biomarkers were determined from pre-procedural peripheral venous blood samples. A total of 110 patients were included, of whom 76 (69.0%) were males, with a median age of 65 (55–71) years. Median LVEDP was 13.5 (8–19) mmHg and diastolic dysfunction was present in 50 (45.4%) of the patients. LVEDP correlated with BNP (p < 0.0001, r = 0.39 [0.20–0.53]), NT-proBNP (p < 0.0001, r = 0.40 [0.22–0.55]), MR-proANP (p = 0.001, r = 0.30 [0.11–0.46]), sST2 (p < 0.0001, r = 0.47 [0.30–0.60]), but not with MR-proAMD (p = 0.77) or galectin-3 (p = 0.76). In the final stepwise multivariable binary logistic regression model, diastolic dysfunction was predicted by NT-proBNP, mitral average E/e’, sST2, atrial fibrillation, and left atrium reservoir strain. BNP, NT-proBNP, MR-proANP, and sST2 had predictive value for diastolic dysfunction. In contrast, galectin-3 and MR-proAMD were not associated with increased filling pressures. Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a clinical syndrome that is the result of structural and/or functional cardiac abnormalities. Heart failure implies a lower-than-required cardiac output (systolic dysfunction) and/or maintaining that required cardiac output at the cost of elevated intracardiac filling pressures (diastolic dysfunction)1. The end result of increased intracardiac filling pressures is an elevated left ventricular end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP). An LVEDP ≥ 15 mmHg defines diastolic dysfunction. While HF is phenotypically characterized according to systolic function as HF with reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF ≤ 40%), HF with mildly reduced LVEF (between 41% and 49%), and HF with preserved LVEF (≥ 50%), diastolic dysfunction can be present in all these categories1.

The gold standard in diastolic dysfunction diagnosis is left heart catheterization with LVEDP measurement at end-expiration. However, left heart catheterization is an invasive procedure, not with negligible risks, and is relatively costly. Thus, left heart catheterization is unsuitable for screening or routine diagnosis, and instead, echocardiography is used to estimate LVEDP. Numerous echocardiographic parameters were developed for the prediction of LVEDP, such as mitral valve (MV) E/A ratio, average MV E/e’ ratio, left atrium (LA) volume index, or tricuspid regurgitation (TR) jet velocity2. Although many of these parameters are impaired in patients with diastolic dysfunction, the average MV E/e’ ratio correlated best with LVEDP, however, the correlation coefficient was only moderate3–5. Nevertheless, combining multiple echocardiographic parameters may improve the diagnosis of diastolic dysfunction3. Numerous serum biomarkers were proven to be predictive of adverse cardiac events. Suppression of tumorigenesis-2 (ST2), a profibrotic marker, was increased in advanced heart failure and had added predictive power for mortality in addition to natriuretic peptides6. Galectin-3, a chimera-type β-galactoside-binding lectin and also a profibrotic marker, was associated with an increased risk of death and HF7. Adrenomedullin, a vasodilatory peptide secreted as a response to increased plasma volume and an activated sympathetic nervous system, was significantly increased in advanced New York Heart Association functional classes8. It is yet to be determined if serum biomarkers, some of which are elevated in the context of advanced systolic dysfunction9, can also improve diastolic dysfunction diagnosis, which may appear early and independent from the former.

The aim of this study was to assess if serum biomarkers such as natriuretic peptides (mid-regional pro-atrial natriuretic peptide [MR-proANP], B-type natriuretic peptide [BNP], and N-terminal prohormone B-type natriuretic peptide [NT-proBNP]), soluble ST2 (sST2), galectin-3 and mid-regional pro-adrenomedullin (MR-proAMD) correlate with LVEDP and if serum levels are different among patients with and without diastolic dysfunction. Moreover, we investigated whether these biomarkers could provide incremental value to echocardiographic parameters for quantifying diastolic dysfunction.

Materials and methods

The research protocol complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the local Ethics Committees of the Emergency Institute for Cardiovascular Diseases and Transplantation Târgu Mureş (approval number 2539/04.04.2022) and of the University of Medicine, Pharmacy, Science and Technology “George Emil Palade” of Târgu Mureş (approval number 1730/12.05.2022). All patients gave written informed consent to participate in the study.

All patients who were scheduled to undergo coronary angiography at the Emergency Institute for Cardiovascular Diseases and Transplantation of Târgu Mureş, and agreed with a concomitant additional invasive left ventricular pressure measurement were prospectively included. Patients who agreed to participate signed informed consents for both coronary angiography and cardiac catheterization. Exclusion criteria consisted of (1) age less than 18 years old, (2) presence of any acute cardiac condition (e.g., acute heart failure, acute coronary syndrome, acute pulmonary embolism), (3) valvular prosthesis, (4) implantable cardiac rhythm devices with active stimulation or (5) suboptimal echocardiographic window. Data regarding anthropometrics, relevant medical history, clinical status at hospital admission, chronic medical treatment, and routine laboratory parameters were collected. Transthoracic echocardiography was performed in all patients before left heart catheterization using a commercially available ultrasound system (GE Vivid E95) and post-processed using proprietary software (EchoPAC 202, GE Vingmed Ultrasound).

Biomarker quantification

Peripheral blood samples were collected before the catheterization procedure and serum samples were stored at -80 °C until analysis. BNP was measured by chemiluminescent immunoassay on an Abbott Architect automated analyzer (Abbott Diagnostics, Abbott Park, IL, USA). An enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was used to determine NT-proBNP (Biomedica, Vienna, Austria), MR-proANP (AssayGenie, Dublin, Ireland), sST2 (Critical Diagnostics Presage, San Diego, CA, USA), galectin-3 (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA) and MR-proAMD (AssayGenie, Dublin, Ireland). All ELISA analyses were performed on Elisa Dynex DSX fully automated Elisa analyzer (DYNEX Technologies, Chantilly, VA).

Left ventricular end-diastolic pressure measurement

LV pressures were recorded prior to coronary angiography (i.e., before any contrast injection) using a Siemens Artis zee system (Siemens Healthcare, Forchheim, Germany). A 5-French Judkins right catheter was balanced to the zero level at the mid-axillary line. LV pressures were recorded at end-expiration for five consecutive cardiac cycles. All parameters were averaged over the five consecutive cardiac cycles. Left ventricular diastolic dysfunction was defined as an invasively measured LVEDP elevated above 15 mmHg. Pressure curves were recorded at a sampling rate of 2000 Hz and an amplitude resolution of 10− 3 mmHg. Pressure curves were exported and post-processed using MatLab R2023b (MathWorks, Inc., Natick, Massachusetts, Fig. 1). Additional invasively determined variables characterizing left ventricular function were maximum dP/dt (peak of the first derivative of LV pressure curve during LV contraction), minimum dP/dt (peak of the first derivative of LV pressure curve during LV relaxation), LV contraction integral (area under the dP/dt curve between end-diastolic and peak systolic pressure), LV relaxation integral (area under the dP/dt curve between peak systolic and end-systolic pressure), LV diastolic integral (area under the LV pressure curve between end-systolic and end-diastolic pressure) as illustrated in Fig. 1.

Statistical analysis

A significance level α of 0.05 and a 95% confidence interval (CI) were considered. All reported 95% CI were bootstrapped with 1000 samples10. Continuous variables were evaluated for normal distribution using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Continuous variables with normal distributions were reported as mean ± standard deviation and compared using the unpaired Student t-test, while continuous variables without normal distributions and discrete variables were reported as median (interquartile range) and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were reported as absolute and relative frequencies and compared using Fisher’s exact test. Correlations were evaluated using Spearman’s test. Multicollinearity diagnosis was performed by measuring the variance inflation factor. Multivariable models were constructed using multivariable linear regression and multivariable binary logistic regression for LVEDP and diastolic dysfunction, respectively. All multivariable models were constructed in a stepwise forward selection by using the Bayesian information criterion11. Since the included population was not imbalanced, predictive performance was analyzed using the area under the curve of receiver operator characteristic and calibration curve12,13. The receiver operator curve for the final multivariable binary logistic model was obtained by representing the model as a nomogram – odds ratio coefficients were added for each unit increase for each variable in the model. Comparisons between areas under the curve for receiver operator characteristic were performed using the DeLong test. Statistical analysis was performed using statsmodels, scipy, and scikit-learn libraries implemented in Python version 3.9, while figure plotting was performed using matplotlib library.

Results

A total of 110 patients were included, of which 83 (75.45%) were males, with a median age of 65 (55–71) years. Complete clinical characteristics of the included population are reported in Table 1. Median LVEDP was 13.5 (8.0–19) mmHg and 50 (45.4%) patients had an LVEDP above 15 mmHg, thus having diastolic dysfunction. Patients with diastolic dysfunction were more hypertensive, with reduced LVEF, and on heart failure medication, such as angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor/angiotensin receptor blocker (ACEI/ARB), mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA), angiotensin receptor/neprilysin inhibitor (ARNI), sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, or loop diuretics. Importantly, 19 (17.2%) patients had no history or signs of any cardiovascular diseases.

All studied natriuretic peptides (MR-proANP, BNP, and NT-proBNP) and sST2 were significantly higher in patients with diastolic dysfunction as defined by cardiac catheterization (Table 2), and serum levels correlated with LVEDP (Tables 3 and Fig. 2). However, galectin-3 and MR-proADM levels were not different among patients with and without diastolic dysfunction (Table 2), and serum levels did not correlate with LVEDP (Tables 3 and Fig. 2). Similarly, the area under the curve of receiver operator characteristic was the highest for MR-proANP (p = 0.003, AUC = 0.655 [0.543–0.753]), BNP (p < 0.0001, AUC = 0.721 [0.616–0.809]), NT-proBNP (p < 0.0001, AUC = 0.724 [0.616–0.814]), and sST2 (p < 0.0001, AUC = 0.744 [0.640–0.827], Fig. 3A), while galectin-3 and MR-proADM did not have a significant AUC (not better than random prediction). Echocardiographic parameters of LV and LA function were impaired in patients with diastolic dysfunction at cardiac catheterization. Namely, higher average GLS, LA volume index, MV E/A ratio, TR systolic jet velocity, and mitral average E/e’, while lower LVEF, LA reservoir strain (LARS), and MV DT were associated with diastolic dysfunction and correlated with LVEDP (Table 2). From the studied echocardiographic parameters, the mitral average E/e’ had the highest area under the curve of receiver operator characteristic (p < 0.0001, AUC = 0.732 [0.629–0.818], Fig. 3B), which was not significantly higher than the area under the curve of receiver operator characteristic of BNP, NT-proBNP, and sST2 (all p = NS), but was higher than that of MR-proANP (p = 0.03).

The final stepwise multivariable linear regression model for the prediction of LVEDP included mitral average E/e’, atrial fibrillation, MR-proANP, sST2, LARS, and diuretic treatment (Table 4). The adjusted R2 of this model was 0.503. The final stepwise multivariable binary logistic regression model for the prediction of diastolic dysfunction included NT-proBNP, mitral average E/e’, sST2, atrial fibrillation, LARS, and ARNI treatment. The area under the curve of the receiver operator characteristic was 0.890 (0.804–0.942, p < 0.0001), with a good calibration curve (Table 4). The area under the curve of the receiver operator characteristic for the multivariable model was significantly higher than that of serum biomarkers and echocardiographic parameters (all p < 0.01).

Discussions

The main findings of this study can be summarized as follows: (1) natriuretic peptides (MR-proANP, BNP, and NT-proBNP) and sST2 correlated with LVEDP, and patients with diastolic dysfunction presented significantly higher serum levels; (2) galectin-3 and MR-proADM had no predictive value for diastolic dysfunction; (3) numerous echocardiographic parameters (e.g., LA volume index, MV E/A ratio, MV DT, mitral average E/e’ and LARS) also had predictive value for diastolic dysfunction, but the predictive performance was lower than that of cardiac serum biomarkers; (4) in the multivariable binary logistic regression model, cardiac serum biomarkers (NT-proBNP and sST2) could improve echocardiographic parameters’ (mitral average E/e’ and LARS) performance in predicting diastolic dysfunction; (5) better correlation coefficients were measured for LV end-systolic pressure and minimum dP/dt with serum biomarkers and echocardiographic parameters than those measured for LVEDP.

Certain serum biomarkers correlate with LVEDP and are predictive of adverse cardiac events. Natriuretic peptides, mainly BNP and ANP, are primarily secreted by the cardiomyocytes in response to pressure overload, ANP being more selectively secreted by the cardiac atria14. Natriuretic peptides correlated with LVEDP in a previous study, with BNP performing better than ANP15. Interestingly, BNP had independent predictive value for heart failure admission and death even after adjusting for LVEDP and LVEF16. Secretion of sST2, the soluble isoform of ST2, a member of the IL-1 receptor family, is induced by cardiomyocytes as a response to mechanical stress and inflammation17. In a previous study, sST2 was significantly higher in patients with elevated LVEDP18. Similarly, in our study, natriuretic peptides and ST2 correlated with invasively measured LVEDP, with sST2 having the highest diagnostic performance. While no studies compared galectin-3 in invasively evaluated diastolic dysfunction, galectin-3 correlated with echocardiographic parameters such as E/e’ ratio19. However, in a long-term follow-up in patients with HF, NT-proBNP, and sST2 had independent predictive value, while galectin-3 had no predictive value20. A recent study also showed no association between galectin-3 and MR-proAMD with elevated filling pressure assessed invasively by pulmonary capillary wedge pressure21. Similarly, our study found no correlation between galectin-3 and MR-proAMD with LVEDP.

The Euro-Filling study showed modest prediction performance for diastolic dysfunction of individual echocardiographic parameters3. When multiple echocardiographic parameters were combined as per the 2016 ASE/EACVI algorithm22, the AUC increased to 0.78, however, an external validation study reported an AUC of 0.5623. Thus, current recommendations for echocardiographic diastolic dysfunction diagnosis need improvement, and the inclusion of LARS is among the suggested changes24. A meta-analysis revealed high specificity of LARS for increased filling pressures25. Similarly, LARS and correlated with NT-proBNP levels and improved the HFpEF diagnosis in a previous study26. In our multivariable model, echocardiographic parameters, such as E/e’ ratio and LARS, along with serum biomarkers, such as NT-proBNP and sST2, were independent predictors of diastolic dysfunction and achieved an AUC of 0.89 (Table 4).

The pressure curves were exported and post-processed to measure additional catheterization parameters, such as maximum and minimum dP/dt (Fig. 1). Maximum dP/dt was revealed in a preclinical study to be an accurate parameter for LV contractility, being modulated by beta-blocker and dobutamine27. Radial dP/dt, a surrogate marker of LV dP/dt, allowed for continuous and minimally invasive intensive care monitoring of LV contractility in the acute heart failure context28. Indeed, good agreement and correlation coefficients between radial and femoral with LV dP/dt were reported27,28. Moreover, radial dP/dt correlated with noninvasively measured dP/dt by applanation tonometry, and low maximum dP/dt was an independent predictor of death in patients with HF regardless of LV ejection fraction29. Since maximum dP/dt reflects LV contractility when maximum dP/dt was used to guide LV lead positioning during cardiac resynchronization therapy, better reverse remodeling was observed30. However, data regarding minimum dP/dt, a possible marker of LV compliance, is lacking. In our study, cardiac biomarkers and echocardiographic parameters correlated better with minimum dP/dt than with LVEDP (Supplemental Table 1). Minimum dP/dt could be a better parameter for assessing diastolic dysfunction than LVEDP, as is may be less influenced by respiration or diuretic therapy, and future studies are required.

Study strengths and limitations

Our study’s strength is that diastolic dysfunction was evaluated using the gold standard of left ventricular catheterization. Moreover, we used a wide panel of cardiac biomarkers and relevant echocardiographic parameters, such as LARS. The main limitation of this study is the relatively low number of patients. Although bootstrapped confidence intervals are reliable for internal validation10, our findings require external validation. While the authors acknowledge that our multivariable models may be only partially confirmed, the present work should encourage future works in incorporating cardiac biomarkers in the echocardiographic diagnostic process of diastolic dysfunction, especially natriuretic peptides, and sST2.

Conclusions

Natriuretic peptides (BNP, NT-proBNP, and MR-proANP), and sST2 have predictive value for diastolic dysfunction, as defined by cardiac catheterization. In contrast, galectin-3 and MR-proAMD are not associated with increased filling pressures. From the investigated serum biomarkers, NT-proBNP and sST2 improved diastolic dysfunction prediction in the final multivariable model. Combining these biomarkers with echocardiographic variables might improve the non-invasive diagnosis of diastolic heart failure.

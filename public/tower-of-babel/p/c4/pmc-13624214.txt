Duration of Belimumab Therapy and Timing of Initiation as Predictors of Clinical Outcomes in Childhood-Onset Systemic Lupus Erythematosus: A Real-World Retrospective Study
Authors: Baixu Sun, Shipeng Li, Fengqiao Gao, Shuo Liu, Wei Zhao, Junmei Zhang, Xiaohua Tan, Chao Li, Caifeng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionBelimumab is the only biologic approved for childhood-onset systemic lupus erythematosus (cSLE), yet evidence on the optimal timing of initiation and duration of therapy remains limited. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of belimumab treatment duration and timing of initiation on clinical outcomes in cSLE.MethodsThis retrospective study included 182 patients with cSLE treated with belimumab at Beijing Children’s Hospital between September 2015 and September 2025. Patients were stratified by timing of initiation (≤ 6 months vs. > 6 months from diagnosis) and treatment duration. Primary outcomes included childhood Lupus Low Disease Activity State (cLLDAS) and childhood Clinical Remission (cCR). In a subgroup of 95 patients with ≥ 2 years of follow-up, outcomes were compared by treatment duration (< 1 year, 1–2 years, ≥ 2 years) and by timing of initiation. Multivariable logistic regression was performed to identify independent predictors.ResultsAmong 182 patients (81.9% female; median age 11.2 years), the median belimumab treatment duration was 14 months (IQR 7–23.8). Among 95 patients with ≥ 2 years of follow-up, cLLDAS rates increased with treatment duration: 50.0% (n = 10), 70.7% (n = 41), and 81.8% (n = 44); cCR rates were 20.0% (n = 10), 41.5% (n = 41), and 61.4% (n = 44), respectively (P = 0.101). Early initiation (≤ 6 months) was associated with significantly higher cCR rates (74.4% vs. 30.4%, P < 0.001) and lower SLEDAI-2K scores at last follow-up (0 vs. 2, P = 0.006) despite higher baseline disease activity. Multivariable logistic regression identified treatment duration as an independent predictor of cLLDAS (OR 1.17 per month, 95% CI 1.11–1.24; P < 0.001), cCR (OR 1.15, 95% CI 1.09–1.21; P < 0.001), and glucocorticoid reduction to ≤ 7.5 mg/day (OR 1.20, 95% CI 1.13–1.27; P < 0.001). Early initiation was independently associated with higher cCR rates (OR 4.31, 95% CI 1.26–14.79; P = 0.020).ConclusionLonger belimumab treatment duration was associated with a higher likelihood of achieving favorable clinical outcomes in cSLE. Early initiation within 6 months of diagnosis was also associated with an increased probability of achieving clinical remission. These findings suggest that both sustained therapy and earlier initiation may contribute to improved disease control in cSLE.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s40744-026-00871-0.Keywords: Childhood-onset systemic lupus erythematosus, Belimumab, Treatment duration, Early initiation, Treat-to-target, Clinical remission, Low disease activity state

Introduction

Among autoimmune disorders affecting children, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) stands out for its clinical complexity and potential for irreversible organ damage [1]. When onset occurs before age 18 years—termed childhood-onset SLE (cSLE)—the disease burden is disproportionately severe: compared with adult patients, children more frequently develop nephritis, cytopenias, and neuropsychiatric manifestations, and they accumulate organ damage more rapidly [2, 3].

A central challenge in managing cSLE is the heavy reliance on glucocorticoids (GCs). While indispensable for controlling acute flares, prolonged GC use in growing children carries unique risks—growth retardation, avascular necrosis, and metabolic derangements—that extend well beyond the disease itself [4]. Consequently, minimizing cumulative GC exposure has emerged as a therapeutic priority in contemporary pediatric rheumatology [5, 6].

The treat-to-target paradigm, now well established in adult rheumatology, has been adapted for pediatric use through the development of the childhood Lupus Low Disease Activity State (cLLDAS) [7] and childhood Clinical Remission (cCR) [8]. Achieving these targets has been linked to fewer flares, less organ damage accrual, and successful GC tapering, making them clinically meaningful benchmarks for evaluating therapeutic interventions [9, 10].

Among biologic therapies, belimumab—a monoclonal antibody targeting soluble B lymphocyte stimulator (BLyS/BAFF)—occupies a unique position as the only agent with regulatory approval for cSLE in both the USA (2019, age ≥ 5 years) and China (2020) [11, 12]. The 2023 EULAR recommendations for the management of SLE support the use of belimumab as add-on therapy in patients with persistently active or frequently relapsing disease despite standard treatment, and specifically address its application in the pediatric population [13]. The pivotal PLUTO trial established its efficacy in a randomized setting [14], and accumulating observational data have reinforced these benefits across diverse populations [15–19]. However, a critical knowledge gap persists: while we know that belimumab works, we have limited understanding of how long it should be used and when it should be started to maximize benefit. Most existing studies report outcomes at fixed time points but do not quantify the independent contribution of cumulative treatment exposure or analyze whether the window of initiation matters after accounting for baseline confounders.

We designed this study to address these questions in a large real-world cohort. Specifically, we sought to determine whether belimumab treatment duration independently predicts cLLDAS, cCR, and GC reduction after adjusting for disease severity, and whether initiating treatment within 6 months of diagnosis confers an additional advantage.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

We conducted a retrospective analysis of consecutive patients with cSLE who received at least one dose of intravenous belimumab at Beijing Children’s Hospital, Capital Medical University (National Center for Children’s Health) between September 2015 and September 2025. The institutional ethics committee approved the study protocol (No. 2024-Y-043-D), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal guardians. The study adhered to the principles of the Helsinki Declaration.

Eligibility

Eligible patients had SLE onset before age 18 years and fulfilled the 2019 American College of Rheumatology (ACR)/European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) classification criteria [20]. We required availability of baseline clinical data at belimumab initiation and at least one subsequent follow-up assessment. Patients were excluded if they had overlapping autoimmune conditions, active serious infections or malignancies at enrollment, concurrent biologic therapy other than belimumab (including rituximab or anifrolumab), or if key outcome variables were missing from the medical record. Treatment

Background therapy followed institutional protocols and comprised oral prednisone (starting at 1–2 mg/kg/day, maximum 60 mg/day, tapered according to clinical response) and immunosuppressive agents chosen per organ involvement—principally cyclophosphamide (CTX) for severe nephritis or neuropsychiatric disease, mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) for maintenance, and ciclosporin A (CsA) or tacrolimus (TAC) as alternatives. Intravenous methylprednisolone (IVMP) pulses (10–30 mg/kg/day × 3 days, maximum 1 g/day) were given at the treating physician’s discretion for acute severe flares. Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) was administered in 39 patients (21.4%) at a standard dose of 5 mg/kg/day (maximum 400 mg/day), at the treating physician’s discretion based on disease phenotype and tolerance. No patients received concurrent biologic therapies other than belimumab, and no patient received sequential biologic combinations during the study period. Belimumab was considered as add-on therapy at the time of initial hospitalization in patients with moderate-to-severe disease activity (SLEDAI-2K ≥ 8, or presence of active nephritis, significant cytopenias, or major organ involvement), in accordance with our institutional treat-to-target protocol and consistent with the 2023 EULAR recommendations [13] and the SHARE pediatric SLE guidelines [3]. This approach does not require prior failure of immunosuppressive therapy. Belimumab was dosed at 10 mg/kg intravenously (maximum 720 mg per infusion): three loading infusions at 2-week intervals followed by monthly maintenance.

Assessments and Variables

Clinical and laboratory data were abstracted from the electronic medical record at belimumab initiation (designated as baseline) and at protocol-defined intervals of 3, 6, 12, 18, and 24 months thereafter, plus the most recent available visit (last follow-up). Recorded parameters included the Systemic Lupus Erythematosus Disease Activity Index 2000 (SLEDAI-2K) [21] score, Physician Global Assessment (PGA) on a 0–3 scale, oral prednisone-equivalent dose, complete blood count, serum complement (C3, C4), anti-dsDNA antibody titer, and 24-h urine protein quantification. Concomitant medications were documented at each visit.

Two exposure variables were defined. Treatment duration was the cumulative number of months a patient received belimumab, irrespective of where therapy was initiated or discontinued. Timing of initiation was the interval in months from SLE diagnosis to the first belimumab infusion, dichotomized at 6 months.

Outcome Definitions

The co-primary endpoints, assessed at last follow-up, were (1) cLLDAS–SLEDAI-2K ≤ 4, no new organ involvement, PGA ≤ 1, prednisone ≤ 0.15 mg/kg/day or ≤ 7.5 mg/day (whichever lower), and stable background immunosuppression [7]; and (2) cCR–SLEDAI-2K = 0, PGA < 0.5, prednisone ≤ 0.1 mg/kg/day or ≤ 5 mg/day (whichever lower), and stable background immunosuppression [8]. Secondary endpoints were GC reduction to ≤ 7.5 mg/day, to ≤ 5 mg/day, and complete GC discontinuation.

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive statistics are presented as median (IQR) for continuous data and n (%) for categorical data. Between-group comparisons used the Mann–Whitney U test or Kruskal–Wallis test (continuous) and chi-square or Fisher’s exact test (categorical). Where the Kruskal–Wallis test was significant, post hoc pairwise comparisons were conducted with Bonferroni-adjusted P values.

To identify independent predictors of each binary endpoint at last follow-up, we fitted multivariable logistic regression models containing the two exposure variables of interest (treatment duration as a continuous predictor; timing of initiation as a binary predictor) together with pre-specified covariates selected on clinical grounds: age at diagnosis, baseline SLEDAI-2K, baseline GC dose, total follow-up duration, and lupus nephritis status. Covariates were pre-specified on clinical grounds rather than selected by data-driven stepwise screening, to reduce overfitting. Unadjusted odds ratios from univariable logistic regression for each candidate variable are reported in the supplementary tables alongside the multivariable results. All analyses were two-tailed with α = 0.05 and were performed in Python using scipy 1.12 and statsmodels 0.14. All 182 patients had complete data for baseline variables and primary outcome variables; therefore, no imputation of missing data was required. This reflects standardized data collection procedures whereby assessments are performed at every scheduled belimumab infusion visit. At each protocol-defined time point, only patients who attended that visit were included in the corresponding analysis; the number of evaluable patients at each time point is reported in Supplementary Table 1.

Results

Patient Characteristics

A total of 182 patients with cSLE who received belimumab were included. The median age at diagnosis was 11.2 years (IQR 9.4–13.1), and 149 patients (81.9%) were female. At diagnosis, the median SLEDAI-2K score was 12 (IQR 8–17) and the median oral GC dose was 60 mg/day (IQR 45–60). The most common organ involvements at diagnosis included proteinuria > 0.5 g/24 h (58.2%, n = 106), leukopenia (44.5%, n = 81), and acute cutaneous lupus (36.8%, n = 67). A total of 159 patients (87.4%) received IVMP pulse therapy prior to belimumab initiation. The most commonly used immunosuppressive agents were CTX (47.8%, n = 87) and MMF (36.8%, n = 67). The median time from diagnosis to belimumab initiation was 2 months (IQR 0–17.8), and the median belimumab treatment duration was 14 months (IQR 7–23.8) (Table 1).

Early Versus Late Initiation: Baseline Comparison

When stratified by timing of initiation, the ≤ 6-month group (n = 119) had significantly higher disease activity at baseline compared with the > 6-month group (n = 63): higher SLEDAI-2K scores (9 vs. 8, P = 0.005), higher GC doses (55 mg/day vs. 18 mg/day, P < 0.001), lower complement C3 (0.56 g/L vs. 0.71 g/L, P = 0.002) and C4 levels (0.084 g/L vs. 0.12 g/L, P = 0.005), and more frequent proteinuria > 500 mg/24 h (45.4% vs. 17.5%, P < 0.001) (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Table 1). This is consistent with clinical practice, where more active disease prompts earlier biologic initiation.

Longitudinal Changes by Timing of Initiation

Despite higher baseline disease activity, the early-initiation group showed convergent improvement with the late-initiation group over time. By 6 months, both groups had comparable SLEDAI-2K scores (2 vs. 2, n = 91 vs. n = 53, P = 0.830), although GC doses remained higher in the early group (22 mg/day vs. 10 mg/day, n = 91 vs. n = 53, P < 0.001) as expected given the higher starting doses. At 12 months, disease activity measures were similar between groups, and by 18 months, cLLDAS rates were comparable (62.5% vs. 77.1%, n = 40 vs. n = 35, P = 0.170). Notably, at 24 months, the early-initiation group achieved significantly higher cCR rates (78.3% vs. 30.4%, n = 23 vs. n = 23, P = 0.001) and lower SLEDAI-2K scores (0 vs. 2, n = 23 vs. n = 23, P < 0.001) compared with the late-initiation group (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Table 1).

Subgroup Analysis: Patients with ≥ 2 Years of Follow-up

To control for the confounding effect of unequal follow-up durations, we performed a subgroup analysis of 95 patients with at least 2 years of follow-up. Among these, 39 (41.1%) had initiated belimumab within 6 months of diagnosis and 56 (58.9%) after 6 months. At last follow-up, the early-initiation group demonstrated significantly better outcomes: higher cLLDAS rates (84.6% [n = 39] vs. 66.1% [n = 56], P = 0.043), higher cCR rates (74.4% [n = 39] vs. 30.4% [n = 56], P < 0.001), lower SLEDAI-2K scores (0 [IQR 0–1] vs. 2 [IQR 0–2], P = 0.006), higher complement C3 levels (1.0 g/L vs. 0.85 g/L, P = 0.008), and lower GC doses (2 mg/day vs. 5 mg/day, P < 0.001), despite having significantly higher baseline disease activity (Table 2).

Treatment Duration: Three-Group Comparison

Among the 95 patients with ≥ 2 years of follow-up, patients were further stratified by belimumab treatment duration into < 1 year (n = 10), 1–2 years (n = 41), and ≥ 2 years (n = 44) groups. The three groups had comparable baseline SLEDAI-2K scores (9 vs. 8 vs. 8, P = 0.865) and GC doses (15 vs. 22 vs. 25 mg/day, P = 0.441) at belimumab initiation (Table 3).

cLLDAS: Belimumab treatment duration was the sole independent predictor (OR 1.17 per month, 95% CI 1.11–1.24; P < 0.001). Early initiation (≤ 6 months) was not independently associated with cLLDAS after adjustment (OR 0.66, 95% CI 0.23–1.87; P = 0.435) (Fig. 3a and Supplementary Table 2).

cCR: Both treatment duration (OR 1.15 per month, 95% CI 1.09–1.21; P < 0.001) and early initiation (OR 4.31, 95% CI 1.26–14.79; P = 0.020) were independent predictors (Fig. 3b and Supplementary Table 3).

GC reduction to ≤ 7.5 mg/day: Treatment duration was the sole independent predictor (OR 1.20 per month, 95% CI 1.13–1.27; P < 0.001). Lupus nephritis showed a trend toward negative association (OR 0.44, 95% CI 0.17–1.10; P = 0.078) (Fig. 3c and Supplementary Table 4).

GC reduction to ≤ 5 mg/day: Treatment duration (OR 1.15 per month, 95% CI 1.09–1.20; P < 0.001) was independently predictive. Lupus nephritis was the only other significant predictor (OR 0.41, 95% CI 0.17–0.98; P = 0.045) (Fig. 3d and Supplementary Table 5).

GC discontinuation: Treatment duration remained the sole independent predictor (OR 1.07 per month, 95% CI 1.01–1.14; P = 0.018), while early initiation showed a borderline association (OR 8.79, 95% CI 0.99–78.35; P = 0.052) (Fig. 3e and Supplementary Table 6).

In no model did baseline SLEDAI-2K score, baseline GC dose, age at diagnosis, or follow-up duration reach statistical significance, underscoring the dominant role of belimumab treatment duration as a predictor of favorable outcomes.

Discussion

This study provides real-world evidence that belimumab treatment duration is a robust independent predictor of favorable clinical outcomes in cSLE, including cLLDAS, cCR, GC reduction, and GC discontinuation. Additionally, early initiation of belimumab within 6 months of diagnosis independently predicts higher cCR rates after adjustment for baseline disease severity. To our knowledge, this is the largest single-center real-world study to systematically evaluate the dose–response relationship between cumulative belimumab exposure and treat-to-target outcomes in cSLE.

The dose–response relationship between treatment duration and outcomes was highly consistent across all endpoints analyzed. Each additional month of belimumab therapy increased the odds of cLLDAS by 17%, cCR by 15%, and GC reduction to ≤ 7.5 mg/day by 20%. This finding is clinically intuitive, given belimumab’s mechanism of action: sustained BAFF inhibition progressively depletes autoreactive B cells, reduces autoantibody production, and restores immune homeostasis [11, 12]. The BLISS long-term extension studies in adult SLE have demonstrated continued improvement in disease activity and serological markers with prolonged belimumab therapy [22, 23], and our data extend these observations to the pediatric population. Moreover, the 2023 EULAR recommendations emphasize the importance of sustained biologic therapy in SLE to maintain disease control [13], and our findings provide pediatric-specific evidence supporting this approach.

The finding that early initiation (≤ 6 months from diagnosis) independently predicted cCR after multivariable adjustment is particularly noteworthy. In our cohort, patients who initiated belimumab early had significantly higher baseline disease activity, suggesting that clinicians appropriately reserved early biologic therapy for more severe presentations. Despite this baseline disadvantage, early initiation was associated with a more than fourfold increase in the odds of achieving cCR (OR 4.31, P = 0.020), suggesting a genuine therapeutic benefit beyond what would be expected from disease trajectory alone. This aligns with the concept of a therapeutic “window of opportunity” early in the disease course, during which B cell-targeted therapy may be particularly effective [13, 24]. The subgroup analysis of patients with ≥ 2 years of follow-up reinforced this finding, showing markedly higher cCR rates in early initiators (74.4% [n = 39] vs. 30.4% [n = 56], P < 0.001). Lu et al. reported similar findings in adult SLE using propensity score matching [25], and a recent study from our institution provided complementary evidence in the pediatric population.

Interestingly, early initiation was not independently associated with cLLDAS or GC reduction after multivariable adjustment, despite its association with cCR. This discrepancy may reflect the different stringency of these endpoints: cCR requires SLEDAI-2K = 0 and PGA < 0.5, representing complete disease control, which may be more sensitive to early B cell depletion, whereas cLLDAS allows for residual low-grade activity (SLEDAI-2K ≤ 4) and may be achievable through standard therapy alone with adequate treatment duration. The clinical implication is that while prolonged belimumab therapy is sufficient to achieve cLLDAS in most patients, early initiation may be necessary to achieve the more stringent target of complete clinical remission.

The GC-sparing effect of belimumab observed in this study carries particular significance for pediatric patients. Among patients with ≥ 2 years of follow-up, those treated with belimumab for ≥ 2 years had median GC doses of only 2 mg/day at last follow-up, compared with 9 mg/day in those treated for < 1 year. Prolonged GC exposure in children leads to growth impairment, metabolic complications, and increased infection risk [4], making GC reduction a high-priority therapeutic goal. Our data confirm that belimumab’s steroid-sparing benefit extends to cSLE, is cumulative over time, and is primarily driven by treatment duration [26]. The borderline association between early initiation and GC discontinuation (OR 8.79, P = 0.052) further suggests that initiating belimumab early may facilitate complete GC withdrawal in some patients, although larger studies are needed to confirm this finding.

It is important to note that regulatory approval of belimumab for pediatric SLE is currently limited to a small number of countries, primarily the USA and China. In many other regions, access to belimumab remains restricted or unavailable, creating significant disparities in treatment options for children with cSLE. This is particularly concerning in the context of updated international guidelines—including Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) (2021), ACR, and EULAR (2023) [13]—that now position belimumab as a preferred add-on agent for patients with persistently active disease. Our findings, demonstrating that early initiation is independently associated with higher rates of complete clinical remission, further strengthen the case for expanding global access to belimumab in pediatric SLE. Our results should be interpreted in the context of several studies that have evaluated belimumab in pediatric SLE. Wang et al. reported favorable outcomes with belimumab in Chinese patients with cSLE from multiple centers [16], while Zhao et al. demonstrated real-world efficacy in a prospective cohort [17]. A systematic review by Huang et al. synthesized evidence across multiple real-world studies in adult SLE [19]. However, none of these studies specifically quantified the dose–response relationship between cumulative treatment duration and treat-to-target outcomes, which is the key contribution of the present analysis. The large sample size of our cohort (n = 182) and the use of validated pediatric-specific endpoints (cLLDAS and cCR) further strengthen our findings.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, as a single-center retrospective study, residual confounding by indication cannot be fully excluded. We note, however, that the direction of confounding in our dataset (with early initiators having greater baseline disease severity: SLEDAI-2K 9 vs. 8, P = 0.005; GC dose 55 mg/day vs. 18 mg/day, P < 0.001) would be expected to bias results toward the null rather than to inflate the observed benefit of early initiation. Future studies employing propensity score matching, inverse probability weighting, or target trial emulation frameworks are needed to provide more rigorous causal estimates. Second, attrition bias is a key concern: patients who responded favorably to belimumab were more likely to continue therapy, inherently producing longer treatment durations. While the consistent dose–response pattern across five independent endpoints argues against this being the sole explanation, we cannot fully disentangle treatment duration from the effect of being a responder. Third, our study was designed with data collection at pre-specified protocol intervals; the date of first achieving cLLDAS or cCR was not captured as a continuous time-to-event endpoint, precluding formal Kaplan–Meier or Cox regression analyses. Future prospective studies should incorporate continuous outcome monitoring to enable such analyses. Fourth, the subgroup analysis of patients with ≥ 2 years of follow-up reduced the sample size, particularly for the < 1-year duration group (n = 10), limiting statistical power for some comparisons. Baseline characteristics—including age at diagnosis, sex, SLEDAI-2K score, GC dose, and lupus nephritis status—were broadly comparable between patients with ≥ 2 years of follow-up and those with shorter follow-up (data not shown), suggesting that selective retention of favorable responders was unlikely to be the primary driver of the observed differences; nevertheless, the potential for attrition bias cannot be fully excluded. Fifth, safety outcomes including infusion reactions and infections were not reported here, as these are the primary focus of a dedicated companion safety study currently underway from our group. Finally, the single-center design may limit generalizability, although the large cohort size and standardized treatment protocols partially mitigate this concern.

Conclusions

In this large real-world cohort of patients with cSLE, longer belimumab treatment duration was consistently associated with a higher likelihood of achieving favorable clinical outcomes, including cLLDAS, cCR, glucocorticoid reduction, and glucocorticoid discontinuation. Early initiation of belimumab within 6 months of diagnosis was also associated with an increased probability of achieving clinical remission. These findings suggest that both prolonged exposure to belimumab and earlier initiation may contribute to improved disease control in cSLE. Clinicians should consider initiating belimumab early in the disease course and maintaining therapy for at least 2 years to maximize glucocorticoid-sparing and remission outcomes. Future studies employing propensity score-based designs are needed to confirm causal estimates. Belimumab approval for pediatric SLE remains limited to a small number of countries; our findings strengthen the case for expanding global access. Prospective multicenter studies are warranted to further validate these associations and to better define the optimal timing and duration of belimumab therapy.

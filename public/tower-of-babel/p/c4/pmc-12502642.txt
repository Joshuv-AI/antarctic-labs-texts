HPV associated oropharyngeal cancer in eastern China over the last 7 years: is the incidence in chinese truly exceptionally low?
Authors: Ruichen Li, Ji Li, Qi Wang, Yang Zhao, Liting Zhu, Yi Zhu, Xiaoshen Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe prevalence of human papillomavirus (HPV) in oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (OPSCC) in China was reported to be remarkably low. Clinicians often prioritize HPV-unrelated OPSCC cases without fully recognizing the clinical and public health significance of HPV-related ones. We conducted a 7-year retrospective study on OPSCCs in China’s highly developed eastern region, aiming to elucidate the clinical and demographic characteristics.MethodsThe presence of HPV infection was determined using p16-immunohistochemistry in pretreatment samples obtained from all patients diagnosed with OPSCC at Eye & ENT Hospital of Fudan University between 2017 and 2023. A Total of 388 OPSCCs comprised 313 males and 75 females were analyzed.Resultsp16-positivity rate was 62.6%. The proportion of p16-positive patients increased steadily from 45.5% (2017) to 69.5% (2023). Females exhibited significantly higher p16 positivity rates compared to negativity (84% vs. 16%, p < 0.001), surpassing the proportion in males (84% vs. 57.5%, p < 0.001). Patients with p16-positive were younger, and males had a higher mean age (p < 0.05). The incidence rates peaked at an earlier age for p16-positive OPSCC, while there was a wider range of ages among p16-negative patients. The proportion of smokers (51% vs. 77.2%) and drinkers (41.2% vs. 68.3%) was significantly lower in p16-positive OPSCCs (p < 0.001), merely 3.2% of female patients engaged in smoking or drinking behaviors. Although the number of p16-positive cases was higher in urban areas, there was a notable rise in p16-positivity rate observed in rural areas between 2019 (16.7%) and 2023 (57.1%). p16-positive tumors were more commonly found in tonsil than negative ones (80.7% vs. 56.6%, p < 0.001). The proportion of p16-positive cases in tonsils significantly increased from 2017 to 2023, while the proportion among non-tonsil OPSCCs remained constant. In a multivariate analysis, age, drinking, tumor sublocation, residential location of the patient, and N stage had a significant impact in HPV positivity.ConclusionsA large proportion of OPSCC was related to p16-positivity, which could be the possible cause. Our study reveals significant demographic and clinical variations based on p16 status, along with a noticeable upward trend in HPV-induced tumor prevalence, emphasizing the importance of vaccination programs in eastern China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-24688-8.Keywords: Oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (OPSCC), Human papillomavirus (HPV), P16, Eastern china, Tonsils

Introduction

Well-established risk factors for oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (OPSCC) include smoking, alcohol consumption, and human papillomavirus (HPV) infection [1]. The rapid increase in the incidence of HPV-related OPSCC has been strongly linked to changes in sexual behavior [2]. Developed regions like Europe, North America, and Japan have experienced a significant surge in cases of HPV-associated OPSCC over recent decades [2, 3]. Notably, HPV now contributes to 71% and 51.8% of all OPSCCs reported in the USA and UK respectively [3]. Conversely, anti-tobacco initiatives have successfully led to a noticeable decrease in cases of HPV-unrelated OPSCCs [4].

The prevalence and correlations of HPV infection in OPSCCs in the China cohort, representing a quarter of the global population, remain unclear, and there is also a scarcity of related literature. Due to variations in geographical settings and lifestyles, significant disparities exist in HPV infection rates between Eastern and Western countries [3, 5]. In recent years, several authors have reported on HPV infection in Chinese OPSCC and observed relatively low incidence rates of both p16 expression and HPV subtypes within their institutions (11%−15%) [6, 7]. Consequently, most clinicians tend to prioritize HPV-unrelated OPSCC cases without fully recognizing the clinical practice and public health significance of HPV-related OPSCC cases.

The Yangtze River Delta region, predominantly encompassing Shanghai as the most developed region in eastern China, is undergoing rapid social and economic changes that may influence cancer incidence rates associated with lifestyle choices and sexual behaviors. Considering the importance of HPV-related vaccination programs [3], it is plausible that HPV-associated OPSCC is emerging as the predominant head and neck cancer type in eastern China.

Herein we conducted a retrospective study to evaluate the expression of p16 protein in 388 OPSCC tissues over a span of 7 years within this highly developed region of eastern China. The objective was to determine the prevalence of p16 + and p16- OPSCCs while further analyzing demographic and clinical factors to comprehensively elucidate patterns associated with specific behavior exhibited by OPSCCs specific to eastern China.

Materials and methods

General information

This retrospective study recruited patients newly diagnosed with OPSCC between 2017 and 2023 at the Eye & ENT Hospital of Fudan University in Shanghai, China. This hospital is distinguished as one of the Tertiary-A specialized hospitals in China, integrating ophthalmology and otolaryngology. Approval for this study was obtained from the Clinical Research Ethics Committee of the Eye & ENT Hospital of Fudan University. All procedures involving human participants adhered to ethical guidelines set forth by institutional and/or national research committees, as well as the World Medical Association Declaration of Helsinki (version 2002) and its supplementary requirements.

The primary tumor was excised for biopsy or resected by radical surgery and collected for histopathological diagnosis. Since 2017, p16 testing for OPSCC has been routinely conducted in our center using immunohistochemical analysis. The inclusion criteria were as follows: [1] pathologically diagnosed squamous cell carcinoma in the tonsil, tongue base, soft palate, and posterior pharyngeal wall; [2] available epidemiological data, clinical data, and p16 status data; [3] primary patients on their first visit without previous treatment at another medical center. The exclusion criteria were: [1] unknown primary site (T0 or Tx); [2] concurrent second primary tumor; [3] recurrent OPSCC. Between 2017 and 2023, a Total of 435 patients diagnosed with oropharyngeal cancer were assessed for eligibility, out of which 422 had squamous cell carcinoma. After excluding 17 patients with concurrent secondary primary tumors, recurrent OPSCC, unknown primary sites, or previous treatment at another medical center, as well as 17 patients with unknown p16 status, a Total of 388 patients met the inclusion criteria. It should be clarified that the selection criteria were not a source of bias since we utilized hospital electronic and pathological records from all the patients to identify OPSCC. Demographic information including age, gender, year of diagnosis, specific regions/areas where patients came from (the criteria for distinguishing Urban and rural areas in China include demographic, administrative, economic, Urbanization rate metrics, and infrastructure standards. Generally, areas with a population of fewer than 3,000 individuals are categorized as rural. Specifically, patients from rural areas utilize the new rural cooperative medical information system to cover their medical expenses, enabling a direct reflection of whether the patient originates from urban or rural areas), as well as epidemiological information such as smoking and alcohol use, and clinical information such as primary site and stage were collected. All patients underwent contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) and/or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans to evaluate the location and extent of the tumor which were reviewed by a senior head and neck radiologist. Histopathological specimens including primary lesions and neck dissection samples were reviewed by pathologists dedicated to head and neck cancer. Restaging was performed according to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer’s (AJCC) guidelines for OPSCC.

p16- immunohistochemistry (IHC)

IHC analysis was conducted on 4-µm FFPE tissue sections to assess p16 expression. The BenchMark ULTRA Immuno-autostainer from Ventana Medical Systems (Tucson, AZ, USA) was utilized for IHC staining with a monoclonal antibody targeting p16 (Clone G175-405), following the manufacturer’s recommendations. Scoring of p16 immunostaining was based on the extent of cells displaying diffuse nuclei and cytoplasm: negative (0), weak to moderate (1+: 10–70%), or strong (2+: more than 70%) [8]. Samples achieving a score of 2 were classified as p16-positive. Two independent pathologists, blinded to the clinical data, independently reviewed and assessed p16 expression. Any discrepancies were resolved through consensus review.

Statistical analysis

The chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test were performed to assess the association between p16 status and clinical and demographic characteristics of the patients. Differences in the distribution of covariates by p16 status were tested using a two-sided t-test for continuous measures. The association between the number of new cases of OPSCC or p16 positive rate and the study year was assessed using a two-sided simple linear regression (R2) and Spearman correlation test. Univariate analysis utilized a binary logistic regression model to determine odds ratio (OR) and identify the impact of clinical and demographic factors on the likelihood of positivity for p16. Multivariate analysis with logistic regression was conducted to determine the independent factors by including significant variables identified in the univariate analysis. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 29.0 (SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA) and GraphPad Prism 8 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, US). A two-sided P-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

We identified 388 patients who met the inclusion criteria from 2017 to 2023. The demographic and clinical characteristics are presented in Table 1. In our study, there were 313 males (80.7%) and 75 females (19.3%), the mean age was 58.89 years (range: 21–80). More than 70% of the tumors were biopsied or resected from the tonsils (71.6%, n = 278), followed by base of tongue (18.6%, n = 72), soft palate (7.2%, n = 28), and posterior pharyngeal wall (2.6%, n = 10). The majority in this cohort had a history of previous exposure to known risk factors: smoking accounted for 60.8% (n = 236) while alcohol consumption accounted for 51.3% (n = 199). Most of the patients are from the Yangtze River Delta Region, with 131 (33.8%) in Shanghai, 99 (25.5%) in Jiangsu, and 64 (16.5%) in Zhejiang. Urban residents accounted for 76.8% of the cases.

Prevalence of p16 positivity and trend analysis according to the year of diagnosis

Among the 388 OPSCC, p16 expression was detected in 243 cases (62.6%), while it was absent in 145 cases (37.4%). The prevalence of p16-positive patients exhibited a steady increase from 45.5% in 2017 to 69.5% in 2023. Notably, the proportion of p16-positive individuals showed a more pronounced rise among females, escalating from 42.9 to 87.5%, compared to males where it increased from 46.2 to 65.8% (Fig. 1A). The annual number of newly diagnosed patients was initially analyzed, as illustrated in Fig. 1B, revealing a significant upward trend in the influx of patients. Seventeen patients were excluded from the selection process due to unclear p16 status. In order to ensure that these patients did not impact the statistical results, we also conducted an analysis on the pre-screening group consisting of 405 patients (388 + 17). As shown in Supplementary Fig. 1, the trend remains consistent over time both before and after screening. After analyzing the annual occurrence of p16 positive OPSCC, we observed a statistically significant increase trend over the 7-year study period (Spearman r = 0.919, p = 0.0067; linear regression R2 = 0.8833, p = 0.0017). However, there was no statistically significant trend for p16 negative OPSCC (Spearman r = 0.6071, p = 0.1667; linear regression R2 = 0.5148, p = 0.0695) (Fig. 1B). Similarly, when examining the yearly occurrence of OPSCC in males, we found a significant trend for both p16 positive cases (Spearman r = 0.9286, p = 0.0067; linear regression R2 = 0.8675, p = 0.0023) and p16 negative cases (Spearman r = 0.8469, p = 0.0238; linear regression R2 = 0.7591, p = 0.01) (Fig. 1C). Even though the overall number of females diagnosed with p16-negative OPSCC was significantly lower (Spearman r = −0.2753, p = 0.556; linear regression R2 = 0.166, p = 0.3643), a pronounced increase in p16 positive cases among females was observed (Spearman r = 0.8108, p = 0.0341; linear regression R2 = 0.7942, p = 0.0071) (Fig. 1D).

The association between p16 status and demographic features

The demographic factors that might influence p16 status were analyzed (Table 1). Although both p16 positive (74.1%) and p16 negative (91.7%) cases among OPSCC showed a higher prevalence in male patients, in real-world scenarios, female patients exhibited a significantly higher proportion of p16 positivity compared to negativity (84% vs. 16%, p < 0.001) and also had a notably higher proportion of p16 positivity than male patients (84% vs. 57.5%, p < 0.001). The mean age of the evaluated population was 58.89 years, with a significantly lower age at diagnosis for the p16 positive groups (57.7 vs. 60.88, p < 0.001, Fig. 2A). Furthermore, males had a higher mean age compared to females (59.44 vs. 56.57, p = 0.015, Fig. 2A). The age distribution is depicted in Fig. 2B where it can be observed that the incidence rates peaked at an earlier age for p16 positive OPSCC (50–59 years old, 42.4%) as opposed to negative cases; conversely, there was a broader range of ages among p16 negative patients (50–59 years old, 34.5%; 60–69 years old, 37.2%). Both male and female patients negative for p16 displayed a bimodal age distribution (males: 33.8% aged 50–59 years, 36.8% aged 60–69 years; females: 41.7% aged 50–59 years, 41.7% aged 60–69 years) (Fig. 2C-D). The mean age of the population increased from 57.33 years in 2017 to 59.44 years in 2023, and this trend was similar for p16-negative patients (56.78 years to 61.48 years) and males (57.23 years to 60.62 years). In contrast, both p16-positive patients and females showed a decline trend in mean age (Supplementary Table 1).

Regarding risk factors, the majority of patients with a documented history of smoking (77.2%, 112/145) or alcohol consumption (68.3%, 99/145) exhibited p16 negative OPSCC. In contrast, in the positive group, 51% (124/243) reported smoking and 41.2% (100/243) had a history of alcohol use (p < 0.001). When analyzing the percentage of patients with a history of smoking or alcohol consumption based on the year of diagnosis, significantly fewer p16 positive patients had previous exposure to these risk factors compared to p16 negative patients in most years (p < 0.05, Fig. 3A-B). Most notably, 77% (104/135) of patients who had never smoked and consumed alcohol exhibited p16 positivity, whereas only 46.7% (85/182) of patients who had both smoked and consumed alcohol showed p16 positivity (Supplementary Fig. 2A-B). Among the p16-positive individuals, 67.8% and 54.4% of male patients were smokers or drinkers, merely 3.2% of female patients engaged in smoking or drinking behaviors (Supplementary Fig. 2C-D).

The distribution of p16-positive OPSCC was assessed based on regional categorization, we observed a higher incidence in urban areas compared to rural areas (67.4% vs. 46.7%, p < 0.001). Interestingly, although the average proportion of p16-positive cases was greater in urban areas over the span of seven years (p = 0.036, Fig. 3C), there was a significant increase in the percentage of p16-positive patients in rural areas from 2019 (16.7%) to 2023 (57.1%) (Fig. 3D), indicating an escalation trend of p16-positive OPSCC in recent years. Conversely, the number of new cases for p16-negative OPSCCs originating from rural areas did not rise (Supplementary Fig. 3). Among the Yangtze River Delta Region, the proportion of p16-positive patients in Shanghai was significantly higher than that in Zhejiang (71% vs. 45.3%, p < 0.001) (Fig. 3E). Additionally, there was an upward trend of p16-positive OPSCC observed over time in the three provinces or regions: Shanghai, Jiangsu, and Zhejiang (Fig. 3F).

The association between p16 status and clinical features

Although the palatine tonsil was the most frequent sublocation in the entire cohort (72%, Fig. 4A), there was a lower incidence of tonsillar tumors in men compared to women (68% vs. 88%, p = 0.006, Fig. 4B-C). p16 positive tumors were more commonly found in the tonsil than p16 negative (80.7% vs. 56.6%, p < 0.001) (Table 1). Notably, the proportion of p16 positive cases in the tonsil, base of tongue, soft palate, and posterior pharyngeal wall were 70.5%, 54.2%, 28.6%, and 0% respectively (Fig. 4D). When evaluating primary sites in different genders, with the exception of the posterior pharyngeal wall, the proportion of p16 positive cases was higher in women than in men (Fig. 4D). Notably, there was a significant linear trend indicating an increase in the proportion of p16 positive cases originating from the tonsil from 2017 to 2023 (Spearman r = 0.9286, p = 0.0067; linear regression R2 = 0.8929, p = 0.0013), while the positive rate among non-tonsil OPSCC cases did not show significant changes over the years (Fig. 4E-F). However, this phenomenon was not observed solely among female tonsil OPSCC cases as their positive rates consistently exceeded 70% after 2017 (Supplementary Fig. 4). Patients with tonsil cancers had significantly lower rates of smoking (56.1% vs. 72.7%, p = 0.003) and alcohol consumption (45.3% vs. 66 0.4%, p < 0.001) compared to those with non-tonsil OPSCC (Supplementary Table 2).

With regards to T stage, although the distribution of T stage did not differ significantly between the two groups (p = 0.073, Table 1), there was a higher proportion of T1/T2 cases among p16 positive patients compared to p16 negative ones (61.8% vs. 49%, p = 0.014). In terms of N stage, due to different staging criteria, p16 positive patients had a higher frequency of N0/N1 cases (68.7% vs. 50.3%, p < 0.001). Similarly, when considering clinical stage, the p16 positive group exhibited a markedly higher proportion of stage I/II tumors (82.3% vs. 14.5%, p < 0.001).

Discussion

In this retrospective study, we conducted a comprehensive investigation of 388 patients diagnosed with OPSCC in the highly developed eastern regions of China over the past 7 years. Overall, p16 positivity was identified in 62.6% of all cases, exhibiting an upward trend over time, which significantly deviated from the previously reported low prevalence rates of p16 positivity in OPSCC (5.31%−41.5%) within China [9, 10]. Moreover, there was a significantly higher proportion of p16 positive OPSCCs observed in females. Conversely, the incidence of new cases for p16 negative OPSCC exhibited a slower increase rate. p16 positive OPSCCs were predominantly found in younger patients who did not smoke or consume alcohol, with smaller tumors originating from the tonsil and early nodal metastases. Furthermore, there was a significant rise in the prevalence of p16 positivity among patients residing in rural areas during recent years. The reported p16 positive rate for OPSCC in Chinese populations, at 62.6%, represents the highest percentage observed to date. This finding aligns with the prevalence range of approximately 50%−70% documented in Western countries [3, 11, 12].

The incidence rates of OPSCC have significantly increased, especially in high-income countries. In both the UK and the USA, the prevalence of OPSCC among men now exceeds that of cervical cancer among women [3, 13]. The population-level incidence of HPV-related oropharyngeal cancers in the United States witnessed a significant increase of 225% from 1988 to 2004 [11]. In Canada, there has been an upward trend in HPV-related tonsillar cancers, rising from 25% between 1993 and 1999 to as high as 62% between 2006 and 2011 [14]. Reports from other developed countries, such as the UK and Finland, have observed a similar increase in HPV-related OPSCC prevalence over the past few decades [3]. However, there is significant variation in HPV prevalence across different geographic regions. In comparison to high incidence rates of HPV-related OPSCC in Western countries, findings from Asia showed more variability. The average positive rate for high-risk HPV among OPSCC diagnosed in 2010–2020 was found to be 36.1% (112/310) in a population-based cancer registry study conducted in Hong Kong. Over the years, there has been a persistent increase in OPSCC cases, rising from 36 cases in 1986 to 116 cases in 2020. However, the increase in HPV-associated OPSCC is relatively slight [15]. According to a Taiwanese report, approximately 29% of OPSCCs were found to be HPV-associated. The prevalence of HPV-associated OPSCC remained consistent over time, ranging from 25% in the period between 1999 and 2002 to around 29% in the years spanning from 2011 to 2014 [16]. Likewise, another Taiwanese study on 541 OPSCCs showed that about 28.4% of the tumors exhibited signs of HPV infection through either HPV DNA testing or p16 staining [17]. The study containing large samples of OPSCCs in Mainland China is rare. Studies from China have also reported a small increase in incidence, with a low prevalence of HPV-related cases. A study from 135 population-based cancer registries observed a significant annual increase of 6.2% in the age-standardized rate of OPSCC between 2003 and 2006 (p = 0.038), but this rate remained stable between 2007 and 2012 (p = 0.392) [18]. A meta-analysis including a Total of 2,896 head and neck cancer cases from 28 studies suggested that the pooled prevalence of HPV-16 among OPSCC cases in China was approximately at 31.6% (21.7–41.5%) [10]. In a study conducted in southern China, HPV infection was detected in 38.55% of the 83 OPSCC cases [19]. Another study, which included specimens from 1470 OPSCC patients collected between 2000 and 2016 in northeast China, found that only 5.51% (81/1470) of the OPSCC samples showed p16 overexpression [9]. In a retrospective study involving samples from 188 OPSCC patients collected from eastern China, HPV positive rate was only 11.7% [6]. The lower incidence could be attributed to differences in exposure to oral HPV infection due to cultural variations in sexual practices [15]. However, most of the patients included in the studies conducted in China before were diagnosed in the early 21st century. With the rapid development of China, particularly in its most developed and open regions that are currently experiencing significant social and economic changes, these changes may ultimately influence the incidence of cancer.

Previous reports indicated a decline in prevalence for HPV-related OPSCC with advancing age [3]. However, recent studies conducted in Western countries suggest that there may be a shifting burden of disease towards older men due to either a birth cohort effect [2, 12] or a potential delay in sexual debut at an older age [17]. A significant increase in incidence has been observed among white men aged ≥ 65 years, with nearly 10% of cases reported in those aged ≥ 70 years [20, 21]. In our study, although p16 positive OPSCC had a peak incidence at an earlier age (50–59 years old, 42.4%) compared to negative cases, there were still 11.9% of patients older than 70 years. Nevertheless, p16 positive patients showed a higher proportion of younger females compared to their p16 negative counterparts. Our report is in accordance with the current understanding that HPV-related tumors exhibit lower exposure to risk factors, which is consistent with previous findings indicating a higher likelihood of non-smokers and non-drinkers among individuals with HPV-related OPSCCs [22, 23], while tobacco and alcohol use increases the association with HPV-unrelated status [24]. Additionally, similar to a study conducted in Denmark [25], we have observed a more rapid increase in p16-positive OPSCC compared to p16-negative OPSCC. China, being the largest consumer of tobacco worldwide primarily among men accounting for approximately 40% of global tobacco consumption [26]. Furthermore, there has been a significant increase in consumption rates in urban areas compared to rural areas over the past three decades [18, 26]. This may partially account for the observed trend of increased incidence of p16 negative OPSCCs in urban rather than rural regions within our study. Nevertheless, these remain common risk factors for p16-positive OPSCC in men, with more than 50% of tumors occurring among individuals who smoke or drink according to our findings.

It has consistently been found in studies conducted across different countries that males are more commonly affected by HPV-related OPSCC than females, suggesting sexual disparities in susceptibility and transmission of infection [27]. Interestingly, although the majority of cases in our cohort were males (80.7%), females showed a higher prevalence of p16 positive tumors (84%) and had lower rates of alcohol consumption and smoking habits (3.2%). Furthermore, we also observed an increasing trend towards a higher prevalence of p16-positive cases among females, consistent with previous findings from studies conducted in the United States and Italy [21, 28]. In most developed countries, smoking rates are similar between males and females. However, males have significantly higher rates of smoking and alcohol consumption compared to females in most developing countries [29], including China [18]. According to the World Bank’s estimation for East Asia and the Pacific region in 1999, smoking rate was 59% for men and 4% for women [30, 31]. Alcohol could also function as a substitute for the lack of social support or socioeconomic disadvantage [17]. According to Li et al. [32], the prevalence of current drinkers among Chinese males and females was 55.6% and 15%, respectively. The higher rates of smoking and alcohol consumption in males may contribute to an increased incidence of male OPSCC cases, which are strongly associated with tobacco and alcohol use. Consequently, the p16 positive rate in male OPSCC cases was significantly lower than that in female cases. Therefore, considering the potent carcinogenicity of HPV, relying solely on tobacco and alcohol control measures is unlikely to yield satisfactory outcomes in combating OPSCC [3, 15, 29, 33]. It is worth considering implementing a gender-neutral HPV vaccination program in eastern China.

As reported, patients with HPV-associated OPSCC exhibit earlier tumor and nodal stages (due to different staging criteria) [34]. The development of HPV-driven carcinogenesis is closely associated with the immune-privileged microenvironment within tonsillar crypts [3, 35], whereas HPV-unrelated tumors tend to present larger primary tumors without a specific predilection for any particular site [36]. In recent years, several studies conducted in China have reported on the incidence of HPV-related OPSCC; however, accurately distinguishing the rates of HPV infection among different anatomical lesions and genders is challenging [6, 7, 9]. In our analyses, p16-positive OPSCC exhibited a strong predilection for the tonsillar site (80.7%), with a notable increase in the proportion of tonsil cancers that tested positive for p16. Conversely, the positivity rate among OPSCC cases originating from non-tonsil sites remained unchanged over time. These findings align with current knowledge indicating that HPV-mediated carcinogenesis primarily occurs in the tonsils within the head and neck region [37, 38]. Additionally, there was a higher frequency of tumors located in the tonsil among females compared to males (68% vs. 88%, p = 0.006).

The HPV-related OPSCC exhibits more sensitivity to both chemotherapy and radiotherapy [39, 40], thereby manifesting a more favorable prognosis compared to its HPV-unrelated counterpart. In recent years, the p16 status has significantly influenced the modifications in the AJCC staging system [34] for OPSCC. Due to the favorable therapeutic response of HPV-related OPSCC, various de-escalation treatment approaches have been proposed based on HPV status, and multiple clinical trials are currently underway to provide substantiating evidence for these strategies [41]. On the contrary, the intensification of treatment is warranted in HPV-negative cancers. Considering the established correlation between HPV status, treatment modalities, and survival outcomes, it is strongly recommended to conduct HPV testing for OPSCC.

The retrospective nature of our study poses a challenge. Our analysis began in 2017 when we started assessing p16 status in OPSCC at our center. It might be necessary to retrospectively analyze OPSCC biopsy tissues for p16 status since 2001 to validate our findings. Due to changes in treatment strategies over time, we couldn’t evaluate the impact of treatment, and tracking changes in sexual behavior that could potentially explain the occurrence of HPV in eastern China was not feasible. Another limitation of this study is the utilization of p16-IHC instead of HPV detection. Mehanna et al. conducted a literature search in the PubMed and Cochrane databases, as well as performed a multicenter, multinational analysis of individual patient data [42]. A Total of 7654 patients were eligible for p16 and HPV analysis, and 3805 patients tested positive for p16-IHC, among whom 415 (10.9%) were negative for HPV detection. Although the detection of HPVE6/E7 RNA by PCR is considered as the gold standard for determining HPV status [43], its implementation in a clinical setting, where FFPE tumor tissue is the most readily available sample type [43, 44], poses practical challenges. Additionally, it should be noted that this study was conducted at a single center, and therefore, a multicenter analysis would be beneficial to validate our findings.

Conclusions

The present retrospective study provides important evidence on the impact of HPV on OPSCCs in a substantial cohort from eastern China. The presence of HPV in individuals with OPSCC in eastern China has been observed, indicating its likely role as an increasing etiological factor. These observations may suggest persistent behavioral changes in eastern China, as well as substantial demographic and clinical variations associated with p16 status. The consistent trend observed highlights the urgent need for implementing policies and sustaining public health interventions aimed at enhancing the management and prevention of human HPV-driven OPSCC in eastern China.

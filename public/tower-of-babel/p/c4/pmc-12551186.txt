Modulating phosphodiesterase-5 activity to suppress the immunosuppressive mechanisms of myeloid-derived suppressor cells in breast cancer
Authors: Yingxue Guo, Mengyun Chen, Qi Yuan, Jie Huang, Wen Mao, Xianli Liu, Lu Jin, Lijia Chen, Jingyi Lou, Xia Liu, Qi Pan, Huiying Fu, Qiyang Shou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE5) inhibitors have been shown to have potential as an adjuvant therapy for cancer. Tadalafil is a potent and selective PDE5 inhibitor that has been shown to inhibit the aggregation of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSC) within tumors. Given the pivotal role of STAT3 signaling in mediating MDSC immunosuppression, we investigated the impact of tadalafil on MDSC differentiation and inhibitory function. Our findings indicate that tadalafil significantly attenuates in situ breast tumor growth and metastasis while impairing the capacity of MDSCs to suppress T-cell proliferation, concomitant with reduced STAT3 phosphorylation. Transcriptomic analysis revealed that tadalafil modulates MDSC metabolism, upregulates NAD+ nucleotidase activity, and disrupts chemotaxis-related transcriptional programs, including downregulation of key chemokine receptors. Consistent with these observations, in vitro migration assays confirmed tadalafil-mediated inhibition of MDSC chemotaxis toward tumor cells. Furthermore, tadalafil markedly enhanced nuclear PARP1 expression, which exhibits a negative regulatory relationship with STAT3. Collectively, these data demonstrate that tadalafil exerts systemic and local immunomodulatory effects on MDSCs during tumor infiltration, primarily through PARP1-STAT3 axis regulation. These insights underscore the therapeutic potential of tadalafil in reprogramming MDSC-mediated immunosuppression in cancer.Graphical abstract

Introduction

Myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) play a pivotal role in tumor-mediated immune evasion by suppressing T-cell and natural killer cell activity, thereby fostering an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME). Their critical involvement in tumor initiation and progression has garnered significant research interest [1, 2]. MDSCs are a type of highly heterogeneous cells that originate from immature myeloid progenitor cells. Based on their origin and phenotype, MDSCs are further classified into polymorphonuclear MDSCs (PMN-MDSCs), whose phenotype and morphology are similar to those of neutrophils, and monocytic MDSCs (M-MDSCs), which are more similar to monocytes in terms of morphology and phenotype [3–5]. MDSCs are key immunosuppressive players in the TME, inhibiting T cells and NK cells via multiple mechanisms: expressing checkpoint molecule PD-L1; highly expressing arginase 1(Arg1) to competitively deplete arginine, impairing T cell function; generating nitric oxide (NO) (via inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS)) and endogenous peroxynitrite/reactive oxygen species (ROS) to damage T cell antigen recognition; secreting IL-10/TGF-β to induce T cell dysfunction, promote Treg generation, and restrict NK cell proliferation/cytotoxicity [6, 7]. Furthermore, MDSCs can also stimulate the development of regulatory T (Treg) cells, regulatory B (Breg) cells and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs), thereby supporting the survival of cancer cells [8]. Despite their well-established role in tumor immunosuppression, effective strategies to deplete MDSCs from the TME remain elusive. Further investigation into MDSC accumulation and immunosuppressive mechanisms is therefore crucial for identifying novel therapeutic targets.

Tadalafil is a highly selective phosphodiesterase 5 (PDE5) inhibitor that is clinically approved for the treatment of erectile dysfunction and pulmonary arterial hypertension [9–11]. New evidence has also proved that tadalafil can effectively alleviate the immunosuppressive effect of MDSC on liver cancer by inactivating Arg1 [11]. In addition, tadalafil can be used as an arginine methyltransferase 5 inhibitor in breast cancer to increase the sensitivity of breast cancer to adriamycin treatment [12]. Interestingly, our study found that the anti-tumor effect of tadalafil is not limited to liver cancer and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, and it can also target MDSCs to limit the growth and metastasis of breast cancer. However, the pharmacological mechanisms of tadalafil in breast cancer are not fully understood.

Building upon previous findings demonstrating tadalafil’s regulatory effects on MDSC development and immunosuppressive function, this study investigated whether tadalafil-mediated PARP1 modulation influences MDSC biology. Our results reveal that tadalafil significantly modulates STAT3 transcriptional activity through PARP1 activation, thereby altering MDSC differentiation patterns and suppressing their expansion into PMN-MDSCs. This developmental reprogramming ultimately attenuates MDSC immunosuppressive capacity by inducing a functionally impaired phenotype.

Materials and methods

Chemicals and reagents

Tadalafil (HY-90009 A), STAT3 inhibitor (Tyrphostin AG490, HY-12000) and PARP-1 inhibitor (AG14361, HY-12032) were purchased from MedChem Express (New Jersey, USA). GlutaMAX, non-essential amino acids, sodium pyruvate, β-mercaptoethanol, HEPES, fatal bovine serum (FBS) and penicillin/streptomycin were purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific (Massachusetts, USA). Mouse Granulocyte Macrophage Colony Stimulating Factor (m GM-CSF), lipopolysaccharide (LPS) and interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) were obtained from PeproTech (New Jersey, USA).

Animals

C57BL/6 mice were obtained from the breeding grounds of the Animal Experimental Research Center Facility, Zhejiang Chinese Medical University (Hangzhou, China). Female Balb/c mice were (weight 18 ± 2 g) purchased from the Shanghai Laboratory Animal Center (Shanghai, China). MMTV-PyMT FVB/NJ and Parp1tm1Zqw/J(Parp1-/-) mice were purchased from the Jackson Laboratory (Bar Harbor, Maine, USA) Animal studies. All animals were raised in the barrier of Animal Experimental Research Center of Zhejiang Chinese Medical University. This experiment was approved by the Ethical Review Committee of Experimental Animals of Zhejiang Chinese Medical University (Approval No. IACUC-20210517-12).

Tumor models

The inhibitory effect of tadalafil treatment on proliferative breast cancer was evaluated based on the histological progression of tumor growth in MMTV-PyMT mice [13]. Four-week-old or six-week-old female MMTV-PyMT mice were divided equally into vehicle and tadalafil treatment (2 mg kg− 1) groups according to body weight. The control group was given a blank solvent (0.5% sodium carboxymethyl cellulose), while the treatment group was given tadalafil and was intragastric gavage every day. The tumor volume of the mice was measured using a vernier caliper weekly. Tumour volume was calculated using the following formula: V = (length × width2)/2. Mice were sacrificed when tumors reached the adenoma or advanced metastatic stage.

4T1-Luc mouse luciferase-expressing breast cancer cell line were purchased from the cell bank of the Shanghai Branch of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). 5 × 105 4T1-Luc cells were suspended in 100 µL of PBS and subcutaneously injected into the right mammary fat pad of 6- to 8-week-old BALB/c mice. Three weeks after inoculation, the tumors in situ were removed and the mice were evenly divided into the control or tadalafil 2 mg kg− 1 treatment group according to the tumor volume. The treatment group was administered tadalafil via gavage once daily for 4 consecutive weeks. Luciferase imaging was performed every week. Ten minutes after the i.p. injection of D-luciferin potassium salt (150 mg kg− 1), image analyses were performed using LT Living Image 4.3 software (Perkin Elmer, Beaconsfield, U.K.) after in vivo images were collected with a Xenogen IVIS 200 imaging system (Caliper Life Sciences, Hopkinton, MA, USA).

Histological examination

Tumor and lung tissues were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, cut into 5 μm sections, and stained with haematoxylin and eosin (H&E). Images of tissue slides were captured with a NanoZoomer Digital Slice Scanner (Nikon, Tokyo, Japan) and analysed with NDP.view2U12388-01 (Hamamatsu, Shizuoka, Japan). Carmine staining was performed according to standard procedures [13]. The whole inguinal mammary gland of mice was placed on a slide, and the mammary tissue was spread out; the tissue was placed at room temperature for 1 min to allow the mammary gland to completely adhere to the slide. The slide was placed in Carnot’s fixation solution overnight at 4 ℃, washed with 95% ethanol for 10 min (three times), washed with 70% ethanol for 10 min (three times), and then stained with Carmine alum after washing with pure water. After 2 days at room temperature, the carmine-stained solution was removed, and the slices were washed with 70% ethanol for 10 min (three times), 95% ethanol for 10 min (three times), and absolute ethanol for 10 min (three times). The slices were sealed with neutral gum after rendering the tissue transparent with xylene for 3 days.

Immunohistochemistry

For immunohistochemistry, tissue sections were baked at 65 °C for 2 h, de-paraffinised, and hydrated, and antigen retrieval was performed with 0.01 M citrate buffer. Blocking and staining with primary and secondary antibodies were performed according to standard procedures. Finally, the nuclei were stained with haematoxylin. The antibody for S1008A (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, USA) was diluted 1:100. Images were analysed using ImageJ software, and positive expression was calculated.

Immunofluorescence

Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature and then permeabilized the cells were permeabilized with the appropriate buffer (0.3% Triton-X100) for 10 min. After termination of permeability, PBS buffer containing 5% goat serum was added and blocked at room temperature for 1 h, followed by co-incubation with primary PARP1 antibody (HUABIO, ET1608-56, 1:200) at 4 °C overnight. The cells were incubated with iFluor™ 488 conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG at room temperature for 1 h and finally stained for nuclei using DAPI. Fluorescence images were acquired by confocal microscopy.

Flow cytometry analysis

To assess the effect of tadalafil treatment on the immune microenvironment profile of the mice, samples were collected, processed, and stained as previously described [14]. Briefly, the resulting single-cell suspension was washed twice with MACS buffer (containing 2% heat-inactivated FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin solution), and antibodies coupled with different dyes were added according to the assay index. BD Biosciences (New Jersey, USA) and Horizon (Dublin, Ireland) provided all antibodies for flow staining. Specific details are as follows: Anti-CD45 FITC antibody, Anti-CD11b APC-R700 antibody, Anti-Ly6G BV421 antibody, Anti-Ly6C PE-Cy7 antibody, Anti-CD11c PE antibody; Anti-CD45 PE-Cy7, Anti-Ly6G BV510, Anti-Ly6C APC, Anti-F4/80 BV605, Anti-Arg-1 V500, Anti-iNOS FITC.

BM-MDSCs were washed three times with pre-cooled PBS, and flow cytometry analysis was performed as follows. Specifically, cells were fixed and permeabilized using the Cytofix/Cytoperm Fixation/Permeabilisation Solution Kit (BD Biosciences) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The cell pellet was then resuspended in 30 µL of diluted primary antibodies (PARP1, HUABIO, cat. no. ET1608-56, 1:200; p-STAT3, HUABIO, cat. no. ET1607-39, 1:200) and incubated overnight at 4 °C. After primary antibody incubation, Alexa Fluor 594-labeled goat anti-mouse IgG polyclonal secondary antibody was applied for staining (details such as dilution ratio were not specified). Stained cells were analyzed on a CytoFlex flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter), and data were processed using FlowJo software (v10.8.1).

Cytometric bead array (CBA)

The tumor tissue was homogenized by mechanical force and the supernatant was removed. Tumor supernatants or standards were incubated with the mixed microbeads for 3–4 h at room temperature in the dark according to the manufacturer’s instructions, the microbeads were washed with washing buffer, and the concentrations of each cytokine were calculated using flow cytometry.

Generation of bone marrow–derived MDSC (BM-MDSCs)

Bone marrow cells were isolated from the tibia and femur of mice, and erythrocytes were removed by Ammonium Chloride Potassium lysis buffer. Cultures were grown in RPMI 1640 medium containing glutamine, 1 mM nonessential amino acids, 1 mM sodium pyruvate, 50 µM β-mercaptoethanol, 10 mM HEPES, 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum, and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. The cells were supplemented with 40 ng/ml m GM-CSF and cultured at 37 °C for 3 days. After 3 days, the cells were washed with sterile PBS, supplemented with 40 ng/ml m GM-CSF, and 100 ng/mL LPS and 100 ng/mL IFN-γ were added to the culture medium and incubated for 16 h. Or, the supernatant of 4T1 cells was used to induce the differentiation of MDSCs for 48 h.

Transwell migration assay

To verify whether Tadalafil could restrict the chemotactic ability of MDSCs, the migration assay of BM-MDSCs was designed with 1 × 105 BM-MDSCs generated in vitro in the upper chamber and 2.5 × 105 4T1 cells in the lower chamber. After 24 h, cells were fixed with paraformaldehyde and stained with hematoxylin. Finally, the migrated cells were counted by photographing with an inverted microscope.

RNA-seq analysis

BM-MDSCs from tadalafil intervention and non-intervention groups were washed twice with RNase-free phosphate-buffered saline and stored in TRIZOL at -80 °C. RNA-seq and data analyses were performed by OeBiotech Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China). RNA-seq data are available in the Gene Expression Comprehensive Database. Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopaedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) were performed as outlined by OeBiotech.

RNA extraction and real-time PCR analysis

Total RNA was extracted using TRIZOL and chloroform with primers designed by Sangon Biotech as shown in Table 1 and amplified according to the SYBR Green Premix Pro-Taq HS qPCR Kit (ACCURATE BIOTECHNOLOGY (HUNAN) CO., LTD, AG11701, China) instructions. The relative expression level (defined as fold change) of the target gene was given by 2−ΔΔCt and normalized to the internal control.

T cell-suppression assays

Spleens were harvested from 8-to 10-week-old mice, gently ground and separated to prepare splenic single cell suspensions. T cells were isolated using CD8a (Ly-2) MicroBeads (Miltenyi, 130-117-044, GER) and labeled with the proliferation dye CellTrace CFSE (Invitrogen™, USA). 1 × 105 T cells per well were seeded in 96 Wells precoated with anti-CD3/anti-CD28 mab. Mouse BM-MDSCs were prepared as described above, and then BM-MDSCs with or without tadalafil treatment were incubated with stimulated CD8+T cells. After 3 days, T cell proliferation was measured by flow cytometry.

Protein extraction and Western blot analysis

Proteins were extracted in RIPA buffer containing PMSF. For nuclear protein extraction, cells were resuspended in cytoplasmic or nuclear lysates according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Beyotime Biotechnology, Hangzhou, China). Western blot analysis was then performed according to standard protocols. Antibodies and dilution ratios used were specified as follows: β-actin (HUABIO, EM21002, 1:10000), GAPDH (HUABIO, ET1601-4, 1:5000), Lamin B1(HUABIO, ET1606-27, 1:2000), Tubulin (HUABIO, EM0103, 1:10000), STAT3 (HUABIO, ET1607-38, 1:2000), Phospho-Stat3 (Tyr705) (CST, 9145,1:1000), Phospho-Stat3 (Tyr705) (HUABIO, ET1603-40, 1:2000), Arginase-1 (CST, 93668, 1:2000), CCL2 (HUABIO, EM1710-22, 1:1000), PARP1 (CST, 9145,1:500; HUABIO, ET1608-56,1:2000 ), iNOS (HUABIO, ER1706-89, 1:1000), E-Cadherin (CST, 3195, 1:500), Fibronectin (HUABIO, ET1702-25, 1:2000), MMP-2 (HUABIO, ET1606-4, 1:500), Laminin beta 1 (HUABIO, ET1703-14, 1:2000), cGKI (HUABIO, ER1919-37, 1:500) and secondary antibody (ImmunoWay, California, USA, 1:10000). The bands were detected using enhanced chemiluminescence (Shenhua Technology, Hangzhou, China). Finally, semi-quantitative analysis was performed using ImageJ.

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 10.1.2 was used for statistical analysis, at least three biological replicates per group for animal experiments and at least three independent experiments for cell experiments. Student’s t test was used to calculate P values for two-group analysis, one-way ANOVA was used to calculate P values for analysis of more than two groups, and two-way ANOVA was used to calculate P values for lung metastasis model fluorescence intensity. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Tadalafil inhibits the immunosuppressive microenvironment in breast cancer

Four-week-old MMTV-PyMT mice were used to determine the effect of tadalafil treatment on breast carcinogenesis (Fig. 1A). The drug treatment was continued until the mice reached 7 weeks of age (by which time the tumors had progressed to the adenoma stage). Then, the abdominal girdle breast tissues were taken for pathological examination. H&E staining and carmine staining showed that the tadalafil treatment groups had fewer sites and smaller areas of hyperplasia in the breast tissue, whereas the control group had more obvious characteristics of adenoma development (Fig. 1B and C). Flow cytometry was used to detect the population of immune cells in breast tissue. We detected more CD8+T (CD45+CD8+) cells in the tadalafil treatment group than in the control group, while no difference in CD4+T (CD45+CD4+) was observed (Fig. 1D). In addition, the proportion of M-MDSC (CD11b+Ly6Chi) and PMN-MDSC CD11b+Ly6CintLy6G+) was significantly reduced in the tadalafil treatment group (Fig. 1E). To further evaluate the effect of tadalafil on early cancer progression, we extended treatment to 12 weeks of age in mice. Tumor burden was significantly reduced after tadalafil treatment compared with control (Fig. 1F–H). H&E staining showed that compared with the control group, the tadalafil treatment group had reduced invasion of tumor cells into the surrounding normal tissues, reduced mitotic figures, and improved nuclear atypia (Fig. 1I). At the same time, tadalafil treatment promoted the frequency of CD8+ tumor-infiltrating T lymphocytes (TILs). The MFI of PD-1 on CD8⁺ T cells, and the frequency of PMN-MDSCs were all reduced in the tadalafil-treated group compared to vehicle (Fig. 1J and K). These data demonstrated that tadalafil improved the immunosuppressive microenvironment of breast cancer by enhancing CD8+T and reducing MDSCs cell infiltration.

Tadalafil reduces lung metastasis of breast cancer by inhibiting MDSCs invasion

Since most cancer patients die of distant tumor metastasis, and the lung is the major metastatic site in the PyMT mouse model, we extended the treatment period to 14 weeks of age for the mice to further evaluate the role of tadalafil in lung metastasis (Fig. 2A). Consistent with previous results, tadalafil slowed tumor growth (Fig. 2B). Histological testing confirmed that the tadalafil treatment group had a lower metastatic burden compared with the control group (Fig. 2C). Similarly, tadalafil treatment increased CD8+T cell infiltration and limited MDSCs infiltration in primary tumor tissues after treatment (Fig. 2D and E). After tadalafil treatment, the levels of CCL2 and IL-10, which are related to myeloid cell immunosuppression, were decreased in the primary tumor tissue. Meanwhile, the levels of IFN-γ and IL-12, which are associated with T cell effector responses, were assessed. Tadalafil promoted the expression level of IL-12 (Fig. 2F).

Interested in these results, we next examined the effect of tadalafil on the host immune response to clinically relevant spontaneous lung metastases following surgical resection of the primary tumor. After tumor resection, mice were treated with tadalafil for four weeks (Fig. 2G). Lung metastatic burden was assessed by in vivo luciferase imaging and H&E staining of lung tissue sections. As we suspected, tadalafil effectively suppressed lung metastatic burden compared with the control group (Fig. 2H–K). Given that myeloid cells play a potent immunosuppressive role in metastasis formation, we performed immunohistochemical staining of mouse lung tissue using an antibody against the bone marrow cell marker S100a8, and interestingly, tadalafil similarly reduced the aggregation of S100a8-positive cells (Fig. 2I and L). Indeed, tadalafil also reduced the frequency of PMN-MDSC in lymph nodes and spleen (Fig. 2M). In conclusion, we established that tadalafil has a significant tumor immunomodulatory effect, and we next sought to identify the relevant targeted cells.

Tadalafil disrupted the differentiation and immunosuppressive function of MDSCs

We used granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) and LPS and IFN-γ or 4T1 cell supernatant to drive MDSCs (hereinafter referred to as “BM-MDSCs”) production in vitro and examined the frequency of Gr-1 (Fig. 3A). Flow cytometry results confirmed that tadalafil significantly inhibited the differentiation of BM cells into MDSCs (Fig. 3B). Despite this, little is known about the regulation of MDSCs function by tadalafil. Up-regulation of inducible nitric oxide synthase and arginase is a prominent feature of MDSC biology and inhibitory function [15, 16]. Encouragingly, tadalafil significantly inhibited the expression of arginase 1 and iNOS as well as the chemokine CCL2 in both cytokine and 4T1 supernatant driven MDSCs, although there was no dose-dependent effect (Fig. 3C–G). Before assessing the T cell-suppressive function of MDSCs, we also examined the effect of tadalafil on T cell proliferation in vitro, and surprisingly, tadalafil did not show any effect on promoting CD8+T cell proliferation (Fig. 3H). This suggests an indirect effect of tadalafil on T cells. We then cocultured tadalafil treated MDSCs with CFSE-labeled CD8+T cells in the presence of anti-CD3/CD28 mab for 72 h. Compared with MDSCs alone, the ability of tadalafil treated MDSCs to inhibit the proliferative capacity of CD8+T cells was significantly attenuated (Fig. 3I). In addition, to evaluate the effect of tadalafil treatment on the chemotactic ability of MDSCs, we designed a transwell co-culture plate of BM-MDSCs and 4T1 breast cancer cells. The transwell assay results showed that tadalafil treatment reduced the ability of BM-MDSCs to migrate to the lower chamber containing chemokines (Fig. 3J). These results indicate that tadalafil has a significant effect on the immunosuppressive properties of MDSCs.

Tadalafil significantly inhibited the expression of chemokines associated with MDSCs and inhibited the remodeling process of ECM

Despite the remarkable effect of tadalafil in inhibiting the function of MDSCs, our understanding of the molecular mechanism of its regulation of MDSCs is still limited. Therefore, we conducted RNA sequencing on the BM-MDSC generated by cytokine stimulation in the presence or absence of tadalafil (5 µM) intervention. We found that the expression of 2192 genes were significantly altered; 1140 genes had decreased expression and 1052 genes had increased expression in the tadalafil treatment group (Fig. 4A). We performed GO and KEGG enrichment analyses on genes that showed differential expression, and the top 20 or 30 results were ranked from smallest to largest p-values. GO analysis revealed that tadalafil treatment affected the immune response and chemotaxis of BM-MDSCs in the Biological Process module (Fig. 4B and C). Chemokines promote the infiltration of MDSCs in various cancers, including breast cancer, and are powerful indicators of tumor invasiveness and metastatic capacity [17, 18]. Therefore, we performed GSEA of MDSC chemoattraction-related genes and found that some genes involved in the monocyte chemotaxis signalling pathway were downregulated in the treatment group compared to the control group, including Ccl9, Ccl6, Ccl7, Ccl22, Ccl5, Ccl2(Fig. 4D and E). We also measured the effect of tadalafil intervention on the expression of chemotaxis-related genes in cytokine-induced BM-MDSCs at the mRNA level. Compared with the untreated group, tadalafil significantly reduced the mRNA expression levels of Ccl5, Ccl6, Ccl7, Ccl9, Ccl17, Ccl22, Cxc3cl1, Ccr2, and Cxcr2 (Fig. 4F). Accumulation of ECM accompanies the formation of solid tumors, and MDSCs are regulators of ECM remodeling. KEGG enrichment showed that ECM-receptor interaction signaling pathway was the key to the regulation of MDSCs by tadalafil (Fig. 4C). In fact, tadalafil significantly reduced the expression levels of ECM signaling-related proteins (Fig. 4G and H).

Tadalafil disrupted the immunosuppressive ability of MDSCs by inhibiting STAT3 transcription

KEGG enrichment analysis suggested that tadalafil significantly affected the JAK-STAT signaling pathway (Fig. 4C). Considering that STAT3 plays a key role in regulating the differentiation and immunosuppressive function of MDSCs, we analyzed the expression levels of STAT3 in BM-MDSCs before and after tadalafil treatment. As we thought, tadalafil significantly reduced STAT3 mRNA expression levels (Fig. 5A). Western blot analysis showed that tadalafil significantly inhibited the expression level of phosphorylated STAT3 (Fig. 5B). Next, we stimulated BM-MDSCs with STAT3 inhibitor AG490 to confirm the inhibitory effect of tadalafil on BM-MDSCs by regulating p-STAT3 expression. AG490 (STAT3i) is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor that specifically inhibits Stat3 and JAK2/3. Flow cytometry analysis showed that STAT3i inhibited the frequency of arginase in BM-MDSCs, while the presence of STAT3i limited the ability of tadalafil to inhibit arginase. Similarly, in the co-culture experiments with BM-MDSCs and 4T1 cells, no difference was observed in the chemotactic capacity of BM-MDSCs before and after tadalafil intervention under conditions in the presence of STAT3i (Fig. 5D and E).

Tadalafil regulates STAT3 transcription through PARP1

Tadalafil targets cyclic GMP-dependent protein kinase I (cGKI), however, it also hyperactivates NAD+ nucleotidase activity (Fig. 6A and B). NAD+ nucleotidase hydrolyzes NAD+ to generate ADP-ribose, and PARP1 is an ADP-ribose-dependent enzyme. Therefore, we hypothesized that tadalafil might be dependent on PARP1 signaling to regulate STAT3 transcription. To test this hypothesis, we examined the effect of tadalafil on PARP1. Interestingly, tadalafil significantly promoted PARP1 expression levels (Fig. 6C–E). Furthermore, we found that tadalafil reduced nuclear STAT3 phosphorylation expression (Fig. 6G). Immunofluorescence results showed: Tadalafil promoted the expression of PARP1 in the nucleus of BM-MDSC (Fig. 6F). The Western blot and flow cytometry results showed that the PARP1 inhibitor promoted STAT3 phosphorylation, while tadalafil could to some extent reverse the increase in STAT3 phosphorylation caused by PARP1 inhibition (Fig. 6H and I). To exclude the limitation of PARP1i, we extracted bone marrow cells from Parp1 knockout (Parp1−/−) mice, where heterozygosity of the Parp1 gene resulted in reduced Parp1 expression in fresh bone marrow compared with WT mice (Fig. 6J and K). As expected, STAT3 phosphorylation and Arg-1 levels were significantly increased in PARP1-null BM-MDSCs, and the effect of tadalafil was also counteracted (Fig. 6L).

Discussion

Tadalafil, a well-tolerated PDE5 inhibitor with established clinical efficacy in erectile dysfunction and pulmonary hypertension, has demonstrated antitumor potentials [19–21]. Here, we demonstrate that tadalafil attenuates MDSC-mediated immunosuppression through PARP1 activation, highlighting its potential as an immunomodulatory agent for breast cancer therapy. While confirming previous observations that tadalafil suppresses arginase-1 and iNOS production in MDSCs, our study newly establishes a mechanistic link between PARP1 activation and STAT3 signaling. Importantly, we show that tadalafil-induced PARP1 activity disrupts myeloid progenitor differentiation via STAT3 transcriptional downregulation, resulting in functionally impaired MDSCs with diminished immunosuppressive capacity. These findings reveal a critical regulatory axis between PDE5 activity and MDSC function.

The MMTV-PyMT mouse model faithfully recapitulates human breast cancer progression, with nulliparous mice developing mammary dysplasia at 4 weeks that sequentially advances through adenoma, mammary intraepithelial neoplasia, and invasive carcinoma stages before metastasizing to lungs [13]. Notably, systemic administration of the PDE5 inhibitor tadalafil not only delayed tumor progression but also markedly reduced MDSC accumulation and impaired their immunosuppressive function in both tumor sites and immune organs. In vitro, tadalafil suppressed cytokine- and tumor supernatant-induced MDSC differentiation and functional maturation. T cell suppression assays confirmed significantly attenuated immunosuppressive capacity of tadalafil-treated MDSCs. These findings position tadalafil as a promising immunotherapeutic agent targeting MDSC-mediated immune suppression in breast cancer.

MDSC-induced immune tolerance promotes breast cancer progression and metastasis [22]. PMN-MDSCs produce more labile ROS and exert inhibitory functions through cell-cell contact. Unlike PMN-MDSCs, M-MDSCs exert their effects by producing Arg1, nitric oxide, and immunosuppressive factors with long half-lives in the cell-free state [6, 23, 24]. Although in most reports, the proportion of PMN-MDSCs in peripheral lymphoid organs is much higher than that of M-MDSCs [25, 26]. However, on a single cell basis, M-MDSCs showed greater inhibitory capacity than PMN-MDSCs [11, 27]. Our study also found that tadalafil treatment reduced the proportion of MDSCs differentiating into the more inhibitory M-MDSCs, and significantly reduced the proportion of arginase 1 and iNOS in M-MDSCs. This may be the key to the impairment of MDSCs inhibitory function by tadalafil, rather than a reduction in PMN-MDSCs proportion. MDSCs have been shown to migrate to tumor sites due to a gradient of chemokines produced during tumor growth. The migration of PMN-MDSCs mainly depends on chemokine receptor CXCR2, while the ECM affects the differentiation, function and localization of MDSCs through its structural and signaling properties [28, 29]. In the present study, we found that tadalafil treated MDSCs cells exhibited greatly diminished chemotactic and migratory abilities. These data further provide strong evidence that tadalafil regulates MDSCs.

In cancer, the activation of transcription factor STAT3 is one of the key factors leading to the expansion of MDSCs [30, 31]. STAT3 promotes the survival and expansion of MDSCs by driving the expression of anti-apoptotic or pro-proliferative cytokines such as BCL2 like 1, c-Myc, and cyclin D1 [32]. In our study, tadalafil inhibited S100A8 levels and downregulated STAT3 expression. Previous studies have shown that selective inhibitors of STAT3 can reduce the presence of immature myeloid cells in mice and promote their differentiation into mature DCs and macrophages [33]. In addition, STAT3 also plays an important role in regulating arginine metabolism of MDSCs. Arginine metabolism is an important mechanism by which myeloid cells regulate immune responses. STAT3 binds to multiple sites on the Arg1 promoter and directly regulates Arg1 expression [34]. In our study, we found that tadalafil reduced Arg1 expression in MDSCs by regulating STAT3, which may be a key mechanism by which tadalafil affects the immunosuppressive capacity of MDSCs.

Although direct evidence linking tadalafil to STAT3 regulation remains elusive, transcriptomic profiling via Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) revealed its significant modulation of NAD/NADPH metabolic pathways. Given the NAD+-dependent enzymatic activity of PARP1, Western blot analysis demonstrated that tadalafil markedly upregulated PARP1 protein expression in MDSCs compared to controls. Notably, prior studies by Yang et al. identified an inverse regulatory relationship between PARP1 and STAT3 transcriptional activity in tumor cells [35]. To validate this regulatory network in MDSCs, bone marrow cells from PARP1-deficient mice were isolated and differentiated into MDSCs. Experimental results showed elevated STAT3 phosphorylation levels in PARP1-deficient MDSCs relative to wild-type counterparts. Crucially, genetic ablation of PARP1 abolished both tadalafil-mediated suppression of STAT3 signaling and its regulatory effects on MDSC immunosuppressive functions. Mechanistically, tadalafil likely enhances NAD+ consumption by upregulating PARP1 in MDSCs, thereby attenuating STAT3 transcriptional complex activation. These findings not only delineate a novel immunomodulatory target of tadalafil but also provide a theoretical foundation for PARP1-STAT3 axis-targeted cancer immunotherapies.

However, there are still some shortcomings in this paper. Although our research initially indicated that hedragafibine has an impact on extracellular matrix remodeling, further in-depth studies are still needed. At the same time, In our study, the main focus was on the impact of tadalafil on the microenvironment of the primary tumor, but it did not directly assess the changes in the composition of immune cells in the tumor microenvironment of the lungs. Therefore, we were unable to determine whether the observed reduction in the burden of metastasis was due to a decrease in tumor load or reflected the reduction in the infiltration of PMN-MDSCs into the lung tissue. Furthermore, the research results mainly come from the mouse model, and the response of human MDSC to tadalafil in breast cancer patients has not yet been verified. Further research is needed to investigate the interactions between NAD+ deficiency, metabolic reprogramming, and epigenetic regulation during MDSC differentiation.

In conclusion, this study reveals a new immune regulatory mechanism of tadalafil in the treatment of breast cancer, and proves that tadalafil can destroy MDSCs mediated immunosuppression through parp1 dependent STAT3 inactivation. Although the PARP1-STAT3 axis has become a key target, further validation in the human system and exploration of combination strategies with existing immunotherapies are still needed. Tadalafil is expected to become an adjuvant drug for tumor microenvironment with immunosuppressive effects.

Clinical pathological changes in prostate cancer patients after hormonal therapy: A single-center pilot study
Authors: Raghda W Magar, Mona Refaat Sadeq, Sawsan El-Halawani, Mary S Karras
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundProstate cancer (PCa) remains a leading malignancy with variable prognosis, and while PSA is widely used for disease monitoring, it frequently lacks sufficient specificity. This study investigated plasma coagulation parameters, inflammatory cytokines, and molecular markers as complementary biomarkers in PCa patients undergoing hormonal therapy.MethodsIn a prospective single-center pilot study, patients with newly diagnosed PCa were evaluated alongside patients with benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) and healthy men as controls. Coagulation parameters (PT, INR, APTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer), inflammatory cytokines (IFN-γ, IL-1β, TGF-β), and gene expression of p53 and NF-κB were measured before and after six months of hormonal therapy. Statistical analyses included non-parametric tests, ROC curve analysis, Firth penalized logistic regression adjusted for age, tumor grade, and PSA, and internal validation via 5-fold cross-validation and 1000-iteration bootstrapping.ResultsPCa patients exhibited a hypercoagulable state, with elevated IL-1β, p53, and NF-κB, alongside reduced IFN-γ and TGF-β, compared with BPH and healthy controls. Hormonal therapy partially reversed these alterations toward values observed in the BPH and healthy control groups. ROC analysis demonstrated high discriminatory potential for IL-1β and NF-κB (AUC = 1.000) in the full dataset; however, this likely reflects near-perfect group separation in a small cohort (n = 32 PCa). Internal validation via 5-fold cross-validation and 1000-iteration bootstrapping yielded a corrected mean AUC of 0.725 (95% CI: 0.58–0.87), which is the primary performance estimate reported.ConclusionCoagulation disturbances, cytokine imbalance, and alterations in molecular markers are closely associated with PCa biology and short-term therapeutic response. These findings are hypothesis-generating, and long-term clinical endpoints, including biochemical recurrence and survival, require evaluation in adequately powered, multicenter longitudinal studies before these biomarkers can be considered for clinical integration.Keywords: Prostate Cancer (PCa), Prostate-Specific Antigen (PSA)

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is the fourth most common cancer in Egypt, the second most frequent cancer, and the fifth leading cause of death worldwide. Prostate cancer may be asymptomatic at the early stage and often has an indolent course that may require only active surveillance [1].

The understanding of the etiology and causative risk factors of prostate cancer will provide ways to identify males at risk and support the development of effective screening and prevention methods [1,2].

The normal plasma level of prostate-specific antigen (PSA) is 4 ng/mL, and PSA is typically expressed by prostate tissue; its elevation plays an important role in the diagnosis of PCa [2].

However, because men without cancer have also been found with elevated PSA, a tissue biopsy is the standard of care to confirm the occurrence of a tumor [3].

Also, classical prognostic factors such as PSA testing, Gleason score, and clinical cancer staging are not always enough for a clinically relevant cancer diagnosis and for follow-up. Considering that various cytokines contribute to the variety in prostate cancer risk and outcome, as well as drug responses and progression between patients. Hence, the identification of novel biomarkers is highly required [4].

Plasma cytokines in prostate tumors can act as a network, increasing the reorganization of different pathological conditions. Also, it may induce anti-tumor effects or tumor survival through its anti-tumor mechanism or by inducing the inflammatory response [5].

Cytokines, as essential mediators in the immune system, may help treat cancer. It has been shown that cytokines play an important role in anti-tumor defense. On the other hand, other cytokines can also favor the tumor and suppress anti-tumor responses [6].

Interferon gamma (IFN-γ) stimulates the release of interleukin 6 (IL-6) and interleukin 8 (IL-8), which are the main inducers of stromal and epithelial prostate cells growth and proliferation. The upregulation of interleukin 1B (IL1B) expression often correlates with poor patient prognosis; downregulation of IFN-γ correlates with good patient prognosis, while the role of transforming growth factor B (TGF-β) is controversial. On the other hand, synergistic effects of TGF-β and IL1B have also been shown to promote the metastasis of tumor cells to bone in PCa. Based on these findings, cytokines in the plasma of PCa patients would be useful as prognostic markers for PCa [7,8].

Many research studies revealed that there is a likely association between coagulation, fibrinolysis, and cancer outcomes and concluded that raised pre-management plasma fibrinogen was markedly associated with reduced survival in cases with prostate cancer and recommended that fibrinogen might have an important role in cancer development, including EMT, cellular migration, propagation, and blood metastasis [8].

An increased risk of thrombosis in prostate cancer patients may be associated with high levels of coagulation markers (fibrinogen) and thrombogenesis markers (D-dimer) [8,9]. Activation of the coagulation system is linked with tumor progression, so the development of the hypercoagulable state is a surrogate of aggressive tumor biology [[7], [8], [9]].

The current study's novelty lies in its reliance on a relatively less-reported coagulation profile, characterized by the simultaneous presence of prolonged clotting times (Prothrombin Time(PT), international normalized ratio (INR), and activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT)) alongside evidence of hypercoagulable/fibrinolytic activation shown through elevated fibrinogen and D-dimer. Furthermore, the dynamic changes observed in key markers, including modulation of immune cytokines (IFN-γ, IL-1β) and molecular markers (P53, NF-κB), when analyzed before and after therapy, provide a mechanism that may be valuable for monitoring therapeutic effectiveness or disease progression.

Study design

Prospective single-center pilot study, newly diagnosed prostate cancer patients before receiving their hormonal therapy, studied for the plasma coagulation profile, inflammatory cytokines, and apoptotic gene (p53) with stress-responsive transcription factor gene (NF-κB) as cellular stress and inflammatory signaling, then evaluation of these marker levels after receiving the hormonal anti-androgen therapy alongside paired control samples from the normal population and men suffering from BPH.

Subjects and samples

From June 2025 to December 2025, newly diagnosed patients with prostate cancer in the outpatient clinic at the Urology and Nephrology Center, Mansoura University, Egypt, were included after obtaining informed consent. The phlebotomy was performed by an experienced nurse under the supervision of a technician, with safety and septic precautions in place, and the withdrawal order technique was applied. Lithium citrate for coagulation and two tubes of blood on EDTA for plasma samples, and gene expression were collected. Then, during their follow-up in the outpatient clinic and receiving their hormonal therapy, samples were taken at a routine blood draw. Immediately following collection, blood samples in blue caps with citrate as the anticoagulant were collected for coagulation profile study; EDTA-treated blood samples were prepared for RNA extraction, centrifuged, and plasma was separated and stored in a 2 mL vial at −80°C for ELISA. Men with BPH and non-cancer normal men were taken as controls.

Healthy controls: Age-matched male volunteers with PSA <1.5 ng/mL and normal digital rectal examination findings. All healthy controls underwent PSA measurement and clinical assessment specifically to exclude subclinical prostatic disease. BPH group: Patients with histopathologically confirmed BPH, PSA 1.5–10 ng/mL, and no evidence of malignancy on systematic prostate biopsy. The clinical differences between BPH and healthy controls (elevated PSA, lower urinary tract symptoms) are inherent to the three-arm comparative design, enabling differentiation of malignancy-related from benign prostatic-related biomarker perturbations. Exclusion criteria (all three groups): active infections or fever within 4 weeks before sampling; known cardiovascular disease requiring anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy; hepatic dysfunction (ALT/AST >2× upper normal limit); autoimmune or systemic inflammatory conditions; diabetes mellitus with HbA1c > 8%; and concurrent use of corticosteroids, NSAIDs, or immunosuppressants. Non-prostate genitourinary cancers and concurrent malignancies were exclusion criteria for the cancer group.

All 32 PCa patients received standard androgen-deprivation therapy (ADT) in accordance with institutional protocols at the Urology and Nephrology Center, Mansoura University. ADT was administered as LHRH agonist therapy (goserelin acetate 3.6 mg, subcutaneous depot monthly, or leuprolide acetate 7.5 mg, monthly), with the addition of a non-steroidal anti-androgen (bicalutamide 50 mg daily) for combined androgen blockade in patients with locally advanced or metastatic disease. Post-therapy blood samples were uniformly collected at the 6-month follow-up visit as part of routine clinical reassessment.

The study's prospective sample size was calculated using G∗Power software, confirming that a minimum of 30 patients per group would achieve 80% power at an alpha level of 0.05 and a moderate effect size of 0.5. To minimize baseline biological confounding, clinical exclusion criteria were strictly expanded to bar patients presenting with known active systemic infections, preexisting hepatic disease, or cardiovascular conditions requiring active therapeutic anticoagulation.

Other variables collected included age, race, ethnicity, PSA at the time of diagnosis, and PSA at the time of plasma sample collection after six months of therapy. A PSA score greater than 2.5 ng/ml is considered abnormal. The median PSA for this age range is 0.6 to 0.7 ng/ml. For men in their 60s: A PSA score greater than 4.0 ng/ml is considered abnormal. The normal range is between 1.0 and 1.5 ng/ml.

Pathological assessment

Gleason score, and clinical stage based on the American Joint Committee on Cancer tumor, node, and metastasis (TNM) system. For analysis, Stage IA-C and IIA-C were combined and listed as Stage I/II, Stage IIIA-C are listed as Stage III, and Stage IVA and IVB are listed as Stage IV. Additionally, Gleason scores were grouped as low-risk (Gleason 6), intermediate-risk (Gleason 7), and high-risk (Gleason 8, 9, 10) the diagnosis were carried out by pathologist and the results taken from patient archive data.

Materials and procedure

Basal evaluation before therapy

Coagulation profile

Sample were carried out from prostate cancer patient recently diagnosed and PT, APTT, D-Dimer and fibrinogen were measured. The pathfast D-Dimer assay depends on chemiluminescence immune assay (CLEIA) and MAGTRATION methodology. The STA-liquid FIB kit was intended for use with STA compact for the quantitative determination of fibrinogen levels in plasma by the clotting process of Claus. Control was essential to run to confirm accuracy.

Inflammatory cytokines

Soluble cytokine IFN-γ, TGF-β, and IL-1 β Levels in patient and control plasma were measured using ELISAs catalog numbers (QK285, DB100C & DLB50), respectively (R&D System). Samples were stored at −20°C and thawed at 4°C on ice overnight before analysis. The plasma samples were centrifuged at 1000 × g for 15 min at 4°C. Then, samples were aliquoted to minimize repeated freeze-thaw cycles. The entire ELISA process was completed following the kit instructions. Each sample was run in duplicate. The optical density was analyzed with a plate reader (Infinite M200 PRO) and compared with standards, using a logarithmic best-fit line to calculate the corresponding IFN-γ, TGF-β & IL1β levels. Each plasma sample was analyzed in duplicate, and intra-assay variability was low due to standardized processing, which may have reduced the observed variance.

Molecular study of apoptotic gene expression

Blood sample for Gene expression of p53 and NFκB genes by Real Time PCR. RNA was extracted by Qiagen RNeasy Plus Mini Kit (74134, QIAGEN, Hilden, Germany). The RNA concentration of all samples was measured by a spectrophotometer (Nanodrop 2000, Thermo Scientific, Massachusetts, USA). Afterwards, 1 μg of total RNA of each sample was converted into cDNA using Enzynomics TOPscript™ RT DryMIX cDNA Synthesis kit (RT220, Enzynomics, Daejeon, Republic of Korea).

Diagnostic evaluation and statistical modeling

The ROC analysis demonstrated clear variability in the diagnostic performance of the evaluated biomarkers in distinguishing prostate cancer (PCa) from non-cancer cases. IL-1β and NF-κB demonstrated very high discriminatory ability in this dataset (AUC = 1.000). However, this may reflect overfitting due to the relatively small sample size; independent validation in larger cohorts is required, though this dataset indicates promising marker performance. P53 also showed a very high diagnostic value (AUC = 0.968), with a sensitivity of 96.9% and a specificity of 94.4%. PSA demonstrated strong discriminatory ability (AUC = 0.934) with a sensitivity of 90.6% and a specificity of 100%. In contrast, IFN showed poor diagnostic utility (AUC = 0.25), indicating performance worse than random chance, whereas TGFB showed no discriminatory ability (AUC = 0.000). Overall, the ROC findings suggest that inflammatory markers IL1B and NFκB outperform PSA and P53, which remain strong but comparatively less accurate diagnostic indicators (Fig. 4).

Combined multi-panel modeling and sensitivity frameworks

To leverage diagnostic synergies, an integrated multi-panel biomarker signature was constructed using Firth-penalized logistic regression, combining PSA, IL-1beta, and NF-kappa B (Table 13). The model intercept was calculated at 61.665 (P = 0.057). Due to strong internal correlations among the markers, individual coefficients for PSA (beta = 0.0241, P = 0.772), IL-1 beta (beta = −106.84, P = 0.065), and NF-kappa B (beta = 0.00386, P = 0.716) did not cross individual significance thresholds.

Model fit statistics validated the strong predictive capacity of this integrated configuration (Table 14). The global model demonstrated outstanding overall fit, as indicated by a Likelihood Ratio Test value of 62.82 (P < 0.001) and a Wald Test value of 18.34 (P = 0.00037), confirming strong global performance.

To confirm that the findings were stable, a sensitivity analysis was performed by removing outliers and refitting the Firth-penalized logistic model (Table 15). The model parameters remained stable after outlier exclusion (Table 15A), with the intercept shifting to 53.591 (P = 0.052). Global fit metrics remained highly significant (Table 15B), with a Likelihood Ratio Test value of 61.84 (P < 0.001) and a Wald Test value of 18.56 (P = 0.00034), verifying that the integrated panel's performance was stable and not driven by outliers.

Finally, an expanded Firth penalized logistic regression model was constructed using all ten coagulation indices and immune molecular biomarkers to predict prostate cancer classification (Table 16A). Due to near-perfect group separation and strong collinearity among variables, standard errors were widely distributed, and individual marker coefficients did not reach separate statistical significance.

Global indicators for this expanded model confirmed its strong performance (Table 16B). The model achieved a Likelihood Ratio Test value of 53.70 (df = 10, Model P = 5.52 × 10^-8) and a Wald Test value of 26.21 (P = 0.003), yielding an unadjusted ROC AUC of 1.00 (95% CI: 1.00–1.00), showing high global accuracy.

Discussion

The present study demonstrated that PCa patients exhibited significantly higher mean age, more advanced tumor stage, and poorer histopathological differentiation compared to the BPH and control groups. These clinical observations align with the well-established fact that age is a major risk factor for PCa and that the disease often presents at advanced stages in regions lacking effective early detection strategies, although adequately powered studies with strictly age-matched, stage-stratified groups are required to disentangle these effects.

A key finding of our work was evidence of a hypercoagulable state in PCa patients, characterized by prolonged PT, INR, and APTT, along with markedly elevated fibrinogen and D-dimer levels. These abnormalities support the concept of cancer-associated coagulopathy, where tumor cells express procoagulant factors and inflammatory mediators that activate the coagulation cascade. Although PT and APTT prolongation traditionally suggest hypocoagulability, cancer-associated coagulopathy may involve simultaneous clotting factor consumption and fibrinolytic activation, resulting in prolonged clotting times alongside elevated fibrinogen and D-dimer levels. Similar associations have been reported in recent studies. Yu et al. (2023) showed that fibrinogen, D-dimer, PSA, and platelet counts were significantly elevated in PCa patients with bone metastasis, and that both fibrinogen and D-dimer were independent predictors of metastatic spread [11]. Liu and Song (2024) also confirmed higher D-dimer levels in PCa than in BPH, although fibrinogen did not differ significantly in their cohort [[12], [13], [14]]. A retrospective analysis by Feng et al. (2023) further demonstrated that coagulation markers independently predicted disease aggressiveness [13]. Our findings not only corroborate these observations but also add novel evidence by showing that PT, INR, and APTT are significantly prolonged in PCa, parameters that are less frequently investigated in the literature. Importantly, post-therapy reduction in fibrinogen and D-dimer suggests that treatment partially corrects tumor-driven coagulopathy, supporting their potential role as dynamic biomarkers for monitoring treatment response.

At the molecular and inflammatory levels, PCa patients exhibited pronounced dysregulation of cytokines and gene expression, with reduced transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) and interferon (IFN) levels, alongside marked increases in interleukin-1β (IL-1β), P53, and nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB). These findings are consistent with systemic inflammatory signaling associated with PCa. The relatively low variability observed in cytokine levels may be related to standardized pre-analytical handling and duplicate ELISA measurements; nevertheless, biological heterogeneity in larger populations may be greater. NF-κB is a central regulator of inflammatory and survival pathways, and its activation has been implicated in PCa progression, invasion, and therapeutic resistance [[14], [15], [16], [17]]. Elevated P53 expression in our study may reflect underlying mutations or loss of function, which are frequently associated with high-grade and advanced PCa. Although p53 is classically described as a tumor suppressor and regulator of apoptosis, increased p53 expression in clinical cancer samples often reflects the accumulation of mutant or dysfunctional p53 protein rather than effective tumor-suppressive activity. In prostate cancer, p53 pathway alterations are frequently associated with advanced disease, therapeutic resistance, and poor prognosis. Therefore, elevated systemic p53 and NF-κB mRNA may reflect broader oncogenic stress responses. Tissue-level validation via immunohistochemistry of prostate biopsy specimens is required to determine whether these peripheral signals reflect activity in the tumor microenvironment. Reduced TGF-β and IFN levels, meanwhile, highlight the suppression of antitumor immune responses, a hallmark of tumor immune evasion. Martin-Caraballo (2024) emphasized that dysregulation of molecular pathways, including NF-κB and P53, underpins PCa progression and therapeutic resistance [[15], [16], [17]]. Our observation that therapy significantly reversed these alterations, with increases in TGF-β and IFN and decreases in IL-1β, NF-κB, and P53, the marked reduction in p53 expression following hormonal therapy may reflect decreased oncogenic stress and partial restoration of cellular regulatory mechanisms, rather than direct modulation of apoptotic activity, suggesting that these markers could serve as complementary indicators of treatment efficacy beyond PSA. It is critical to note that we used blood-based gene expression (qRT-PCR) as a minimally invasive, dynamic monitoring framework. However, blood-derived mRNA expression reflects systemic leukocyte profiles rather than direct transcriptional changes in tumor tissue. The elevated blood-based p53 and NF- κB mRNA expression observed in our study most likely reflects systemic oncogenic stress and inflammatory signaling rather than direct transcriptional activity within tumor cells.

The observed diagnostic signal for IL-1β and NF-κB in this pilot cohort is consistent with their established roles in PCa biology; however, the AUC = 1.000 observed in the full-dataset ROC analysis must be interpreted with caution, given the small sample size, and should be considered exploratory. IL-1β is increasingly recognized not just as an immune-cell-derived cytokine but as a tumor-derived factor that contributes to tumor progression and the metastatic niche. For example, prostate tumor cells can secrete IL-1β, which in turn drives a proinflammatory phenotype in the bone marrow microenvironment and contributes to chemoresistance. Moreover, androgen receptor (AR) signaling has been shown to suppress IL-1β in tumor‐associated macrophages, and androgen-deprivation therapy (ADT) relieves this repression, leading to elevated IL-1β secretion and an immune-suppressive microenvironment mediated by myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs). These mechanistic insights support our finding of IL-1β as a nearly perfect discriminator (AUC = 1.000) in our study. Similarly, NF-κB activation has been implicated in aggressive PCa. Elevated nuclear localization of NF-κB p65 subunit has been associated with higher Gleason scores, biochemical recurrence, and metastasis, indicating its prognostic relevance. More recently, epigenetic regulators such as UHRF1 have been shown to enhance NF-κB signaling, further promoting tumor growth and resistance to therapy. The perfect AUC (1.000) observed for NF-κB in our study suggests that it could serve not only as a diagnostic marker but potentially as a biomarker of tumor aggressiveness or progression [[18], [19], [20], [21], [22]]. By contrast, PSA, while still showing good diagnostic performance (AUC = 0.934), did not reach the same level of discriminatory power as IL-1β or NF-κB in our data. This is in line with longstanding concerns in the literature regarding PSA's specificity: PSA levels can be elevated in benign prostatic conditions, limiting its precision. Our findings support the notion that integrating inflammatory and molecular biomarkers such as IL-1β and NF-κB with PSA could markedly improve diagnostic accuracy. Furthermore, markers such as P53, which also demonstrated a high AUC (0.968) in our study, are consistent with previous work linking activation of apoptotic markers to aggressive disease [[23], [24], [25], [26], [27]].

Finally, the poor performance of IFN (AUC = 0.25) and TGF-β (AUC = 0.00) in our analysis may reflect their complex, context-dependent biology in PCa. The variable expression and signaling of these cytokines across different tumor microenvironments may limit their utility as standalone diagnostic biomarkers; similar challenges have been noted in other studies exploring inflammatory markers in PCa [15,17,28,29].

The observed improvement in coagulation, cytokine, and gene expression profiles after therapy underscores their potential utility not only as diagnostic and prognostic markers but also as indicators of therapeutic response. Collectively, the present results suggest that inflammatory and molecular signaling biomarkers exhibit diagnostic performance comparable to PSA, and their integration may offer complementary value rather than definitive superiority, a hypothesis that requires validation in larger multicenter cohorts.

Potential limitations of this study, for future research

Interpreting these findings requires acknowledgment of several remaining methodological constraints. Although a prospective design with standardized follow-up and a power-calculated sample size were employed, the single-center nature of this pilot study and the absence of an external validation cohort limit broader generalizability. We acknowledge that residual confounding from mild or undiagnosed comorbidities cannot be fully excluded, particularly given that systematic laboratory screening for all potential confounders was not performed at enrollment. The PCa group was significantly older and presented predominantly with advanced-stage and metastatic disease. It cannot be excluded that the observed biomarker elevations partly reflect age, systemic tumor burden, or inflammation associated with metastasis rather than prostate-cancer-specific biology. Adequately powered studies with strictly age-matched, stage-stratified groups are required to disentangle these effects. The specific proportions of patients receiving LHRH monotherapy versus combined androgen blockade were not systematically recorded. Differential effects of these regimens on inflammatory cytokines, coagulation parameters, and gene expression cannot be excluded and should be addressed in future treatment-stratified analyses. The notably high AUC values observed for IL-1β and NF-κB should still be interpreted cautiously, as they may partly reflect small-sample statistical inflation despite internal validation measures. Protein-level validation of p53 and NF-κB via immunohistochemistry on biopsy tissue and plasma proteomics is recommended to establish whether peripheral blood gene expression reflects tumor biology or systemic immune responses.Furthermore, the six-month follow-up window, though uniform, remains insufficient for evaluating long-term clinical endpoints such as progression-free survival, biochemical recurrence, or overall survival. Constraints inherent to gene expression analysis including tissue source, analytical platform variability, and the degree to which peripheral blood expression faithfully mirrors tumor-level biology also warrant further validation. These findings should therefore be regarded as hypothesis-generating, with multicenter longitudinal studies incorporating biopsy-based proteomics and extended follow-up planned to confirm and extend these results.

Conclusion

This prospective pilot study demonstrates that newly diagnosed prostate cancer patients exhibit profound dysregulation of coagulation, inflammatory, and molecular apoptotic pathways compared with benign prostatic hyperplasia and healthy controls. Patients presented at an older age with predominantly advanced-stage, poorly differentiated tumors and showed a marked hypercoagulable state, characterized by prolonged PT, INR, and APTT alongside significantly elevated fibrinogen and D-dimer levels. Inflammatory and molecular profiling revealed considerably elevated IL-1β, NF-κB, and p53 expression alongside suppressed TGF-β and IFN levels, collectively reflecting a tumor-promoting, immune-suppressive microenvironment.

Following six months of hormonal therapy, fibrinogen and D-dimer levels declined significantly. At the same time, immune and molecular markers shifted toward normalization, with increased TGF-β and IFN and noticeable reductions in IL-1β, NF-κB, p53, and PSA, confirming short-term immuno-molecular therapeutic modulation. ROC analysis demonstrated high discriminatory potential for IL-1β and NF-κB (AUC = 1.000) in the full dataset; however, this likely reflects near-perfect group separation in a small cohort (n = 32 PCa). Internal validation via 5-fold cross-validation and 1000-iteration bootstrapping yielded a corrected mean AUC of 0.725 (95% CI: 0.58–0.87), which is the primary performance estimate reported.

The current study provides pilot evidence that inflammatory and molecular biomarkers reflect disease biology and short-term therapeutic response in prostate cancer. Long-term clinical endpoints, including biochemical recurrence, progression-free survival, and overall survival, remain to be evaluated in adequately powered longitudinal studies.

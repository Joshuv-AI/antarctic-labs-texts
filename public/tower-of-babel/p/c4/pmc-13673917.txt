Survey of underlying risk factors and epidemiology of anterior knee pain in semi-professional male basketball players
Authors: Chiara Busso, Marco Vitturini, Marta Andrighetti, Paola Berchialla, Gabriele Framarin, Marco Trucco
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAnterior knee pain (AKP) is highly prevalent in jumping sports and represents a frequent cause of functional limitation among basketball players, with patellar tendinopathy considered the most common underlying cause. While AKP has been extensively studied in elite and collegiate athletes, limited evidence is available for semi-professional players, who represent the majority of competitive basketball participants and often face high training demands with limited medical support. This study aimed to investigate the epidemiology of AKP and identify associated risk factors in semi-professional male basketball players.MethodsA cross-sectional, web-based survey was conducted among players competing in Serie B–D in two regions of Italy. AKP was defined using a combined screening question and anatomical pain localization. Prespecified demographic, anthropometric, training-related, competitive, occupational, and lifestyle predictors were collected via a self-administered questionnaire. Knee-related functional impairment was assessed using the Oslo Sports Trauma Research Centre Overuse Injury Questionnaire (OSTRC-O2-IT). Associations between predictors and AKP were examined using Bayesian multivariable logistic regression. Model-based predicted probabilities were also estimated across weekly training load.ResultsA total of 371 valid responses were included in the final analysis from 549 survey responses, corresponding to approximately 36.6% of the estimated target population. The prevalence of self-reported AKP was 23.5% (95% CrI 19.4–28.0). Players with AKP reported substantially greater functional impairment than AKP-negative players (mean OSTRC Severity Index Score 32.9 ± 20.9 vs. 3.7 ± 13.0). Higher weekly training volume was associated with increased odds of AKP (OR 1.18 per 60-minute increase in weekly training volume; 95% CrI 1.04–1.35), while a greater playing experience was associated with lower odds of AKP (OR 0.89; 95% CrI 0.80–0.98). Smoking was associated with higher odds of AKP (OR 2.38; 95% CrI 1.20–4.77). Model-based predicted probabilities demonstrated a progressive increase in AKP risk with higher weekly training load.ConclusionsAKP affected nearly one in four semi-professional male basketball players and was associated with significant functional impairment. Higher training load and smoking were associated with increased probability of AKP, whereas greater playing experience showed an inverse association. Background

Anterior knee pain (AKP) is one of the most common reasons for active young individuals to seek evaluation in a sports injury clinic [1] and is highly prevalent among athletes, particularly basketball players [2]. It is characterized by insidious, gradually progressive pain in the anterior region of the knee, typically elicited by activities that load the quadriceps and patellar tendons as well as the patellofemoral joint [1]. Although AKP encompasses several clinical entities—such as patellofemoral pain (PFP), patellar tendinopathy, and soft-tissue impingement syndromes—the most frequent presentation in jumping athletes is patellar tendinopathy [3].

Chronic AKP imposes a considerable functional burden, as it can impair quadriceps explosive strength, reduce jump capacity, alter knee biomechanics, and increase the risk of secondary injuries [4]. Psychological factors such as anxiety, depression, kinesiophobia, and pain catastrophizing have been reported in individuals with persistent anterior knee pain and may contribute to delayed recovery and return to sport [5]. At the organizational and broader health-system levels, these functional and psychological limitations may translate into reduced team performance and squad availability, together with increased utilization of diagnostic imaging, physiotherapy, and, in selected cases, surgical procedures [6]. Moreover, persistent AKP has been associated with decreased work productivity and, in a subset of athletes, early withdrawal or retirement from competitive sport [7, 8].

Epidemiological studies highlight the magnitude of the issue: the point prevalence of AKP among competitive basketball players ranges from 30% to 40% [9–11], and longitudinal data indicate that more than half of affected individuals report persistent symptoms two years after onset [12]. Although often studied in elite cohorts, AKP also affects younger and semi-professional players; in these groups, pain provocation tests consistent with AKP have been reported in up to 60% of collegiate athletes [11].

Semi-professional players—individuals who train at high intensity without holding full-time professional contracts—represent a substantial yet understudied group. According to the 2024 report of the Italian Basketball Federation [13], fewer than 1,000 of more than 150,000 registered adult basketball players hold elite professional contracts, leaving approximately 99% to balance structured training with work or academic commitments, potentially amplifying both biomechanical and psychosocial stress loads [14].

Despite its high prevalence, AKP remains poorly understood in terms of its underlying risk factors—particularly in semi-professional basketball players—although it is recognized as a multifactorial condition influenced by biomechanical, neuromuscular, structural, and psychosocial contributors [15, 16]. Moreover, current evidence on risk factors in young and semi-professional athletes is limited, as most large-scale studies have pooled participants across different sports, sexes, and competitive levels, thereby constraining sport- and level-specific insights [13, 17].

To date, few investigations have applied a multivariable framework to evaluate AKP risk across intrinsic and extrinsic domains in semi-professional adult athletes. Elucidating these relationships is essential for deciding tailored screening practices, load-management strategies, and preventive interventions [18, 19].

To address these gaps, the Survey of Underlying Risk Factors and Epidemiology of Anterior Knee Pain (SURE-AKP) study was designed. The primary aim is to quantify the independent associations between prespecified predictors—selected from previous cross-sectional studies and systematic reviews [14, 20–23] and encompassing anthropometrics, cumulative load, playing surface, playing role, competitive tier, occupational category, and lifestyle factors—and the presence of AKP. The secondary aim is to determine how these predictors, with training load as the primary exposure, relate to knee-specific functional impairment evaluated using a standardized patient-reported measure.

Methods

Study design, setting, and recruitment

This manuscript describes a cross-sectional, web-based survey, in accordance with the STROBE cross-sectional reporting guidelines (Additional File_1) [24]. Between 1st May and 31st July 2025, semi-professional male basketball players competing in Serie B–D in the Piemonte and Lombardia regions of Italy were invited to participate, in collaboration with the Italian Basketball Federation (Federazione Italiana Pallacanestro, FIP). The estimated target population (~ 1,500 players) was based on registration data provided by the regional FIP committees. Data collection was scheduled during the final phase of the competitive season to capture players’ clinical status after exposure to the cumulative training and competition load accrued throughout the season. Team managers received an email containing the survey link and were asked to share it with their players; non-responding teams were sent weekly reminders for four consecutive weeks.

Eligible participants were male players aged 18–35 years who had been rostered for at least one official 2024/25 match; exclusion criteria included refusal of consent, age outside the specified range, and submission of an incomplete survey. This age range was selected to maintain a relatively homogeneous cohort of young adult athletes and to reduce the influence of age-related degenerative knee conditions that may present with anterior knee pain.

Questionnaire development and validity assessment

The questionnaire was developed in Italian by a multidisciplinary research team comprising two sports physicians, two physiotherapists, and one biostatistician. Predictor selection was informed by a conceptual framework that integrated clinical expertise with evidence from previous observational studies and systematic reviews [14, 20–23, 25–28].

The final instrument included 12 prespecified predictors covering demographic, anthropometric, training-related, competitive, occupational, and lifestyle domains, selected for their mechanistic or epidemiological relevance to anterior knee pain and overuse conditions in basketball players.

Face validity was assessed by 20 semi-professional players (10 with a history of significant knee injuries, 10 without), recruited via convenience sampling, who rated item clarity on a 1–5 Likert scale and provided free-text feedback. These players were eligible to participate in the final survey. Content validity was evaluated by a multidisciplinary panel of 10 experts (2 physiatrists, 2 sports medicine physicians, 4 physiotherapists, 2 biostatisticians) with formal clinical or methodological expertise in basketball-related knee disorders, who rated item pertinence using the same scale.

Ratings were analysed using Bayesian ordinal regression to account for the ordinal nature of the responses. Posterior distributions of item intercepts were considered satisfactory when the 70% highest-density interval lay entirely above the neutral value (Additional File_2). All predictor-related items met this criterion in both player and expert assessments.

Questionnaire administration and timeline

The questionnaire was administered exclusively in Italian; therefore, participation implicitly required sufficient proficiency in written Italian to understand the study information and complete the survey. The questionnaire was structured in three sequential modules: (i) electronic informed consent, AKP screening, and pain localization; (ii) prespecified predictor items; and (iii) assessment of knee-related functional impairment.

Participants first completed the AKP screening section. AKP was classified as present only when two conditions were met: (i) the respondent reported atraumatic anterior knee pain, and (ii) pain was localized by selecting a predefined anatomical zone on a digital body map corresponding to the patellar region (Fig. 1, panel “e”), consistent with a previously used approach [22]. When multiple anterior zones were selected, AKP status was considered positive when at least one selection fell within the patellar region and no traumatic mechanism was reported.

Digital knee pain map used to localize anterior knee pain and confirm anatomical concordance with screening responses. Panels (a–f) illustrate possible pain localizations; only panel e (patellar region) was considered positive for AKP case classification

Participants subsequently completed a self-administered, online questionnaire; all information was self-reported and manually entered by respondents. The questionnaire collected data on the prespecified predictors included in the multivariable model: weekly training load (minutes/week), playing experience (years of practice), age (years) and body mass index (BMI, kg/m²; calculated from self-reported height and weight), competitive tier (Serie D, Serie C, Serie B2, Serie B), playing role (playmaker/guard, forward/wing, centre), playing surface (parquet, vinyl, concrete), and occupational category (white collar [e.g., desk-based/administrative], blue collar [e.g., manual labor], student/unemployed, or professional athlete [defined as individuals whose primary occupation and income derive from sport]). Lifestyle factors were assessed with closed questions on smoking (current smoker: yes/no; yes defined as ≥ 1 cigarette/day) and alcohol consumption (yes/no; yes defined as ≥ 1 alcoholic drink/week). Responses were recorded using predefined categories for categorical variables and numerical fields for continuous variables.

Functional impairment over the previous 7 days was then assessed using the OSTRC-O2-IT questionnaire, a four-item instrument evaluating participation, training modification, performance impact, and pain severity [29], which has been validated in Italian, demonstrating good reliability and feasibility [30]. Item responses were scored using the standard OSTRC algorithm and summed to generate a Severity Index Score ranging from 0 (no limitation) to 100 (maximal limitation).

The survey was administered electronically via REDCap and remained open throughout the recruitment window (1 May–31 July 2025), with a typical completion time of 8–10 min. The full Italian version and the translated English version of the questionnaire are available in Additional File_3.

Sample size estimation

The sample size was determined according to the recommendations of Riley et al. [31].

Calculations assumed a target Cox–Snell R² of 0.25, consistent with musculoskeletal prediction models, 12 prespecified predictors, and an expected AKP prevalence of 30%, according to the literature. Under these assumptions, the pmsampsize package (v.1.1.3) for R (v.4.5.0) indicated that a minimum of 357 respondents (≈ 111 AKP events) would be required to achieve a calibration slope ≥ 0.90 and limit model optimism to ≤ 10% [32].

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics are reported as mean and standard deviation (SD) for continuous variables, and absolute number and percentage (%) for categorical variables.

The primary analysis used a multivariable Bayesian logistic regression model to estimate associations between prespecified predictors and the presence of AKP. Results are reported as adjusted odds ratios (ORs) with 95% credible intervals (95% CrIs). Model-based predicted probabilities of AKP were estimated across the observed range of weekly training volume.

As a secondary analysis, a multivariable cumulative-logit ordinal regression model was fitted to assess associations between the same set of predictors and knee-related functional limitation, as measured by the OSTRC-O2 questionnaire. All analyses were performed using R (v4.5.0).

Ethics approval and consent to participate

The study protocol was approved by the University of Turin Bioethics Committee (protocol No. 0294392, 06/05/2025) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and applicable regulations. Participation was voluntary, and electronic informed consent was obtained prior to questionnaire access, with the option to withdraw at any time. Data were collected via REDCap using encrypted transmission and stored for research purposes only.

Results

Participant flow and sample characteristics

A total of 549 responses were collected, corresponding to approximately one-third of the estimated target population of about 1,500 players (estimated response rate: 36.6%). After excluding 178 responses (143 incomplete questionnaires and 35 not meeting the age criterion), the final analysis included 371 valid responses, exceeding the prespecified minimum required for the planned analyses. Main associations

Higher weekly training load was associated with an increased probability of AKP, with an OR of 1.18 (95% CrI 1.04–1.35) per additional 60 min of training per week. Conversely, a greater number of years of basketball participation showed an inverse association with AKP, with an OR of 0.89 (95% CrI 0.80–0.98).

Among lifestyle factors, smoking demonstrated a positive association with AKP - though the wide credible interval indicates uncertainty regarding the precise magnitude of the effect (OR 2.38; 95% CrI 1.20–4.77) - whereas the association with alcohol consumption remained uncertain (OR 1.13; 95% CrI 0.35–3.50).

Other predictors, including body mass index (BMI), age, playing role, and playing surface, showed wide credible intervals, indicating substantial uncertainty in the magnitude and direction of their associations.

Predicted probabilities of AKP across training load

Model-based predicted probabilities of AKP were estimated across increasing levels of weekly training load, while accounting for the influence of the other covariates included in the model. As shown in Fig. 4, the probability of AKP increased progressively with higher weekly training load. At lower training volumes, predicted probabilities ranged from approximately 0.15 to 0.20, whereas at higher volumes—approaching 1,000 min per week—they increased to approximately 0.50–0.55. Shaded bands around the curves represent 95% credible intervals, reflecting greater uncertainty at higher training loads. Overall, these estimates are consistent with a positive association between weekly training load and the probability of AKP.

Discussion

Principal findings

In this cross-sectional survey of semi-professional male basketball players, anterior knee pain (AKP) was common, affecting approximately one in four athletes, and was associated with substantial knee-related functional impairment. Players classified as AKP-positive reported substantially greater functional limitation than AKP-negative players. However, because AKP status was partly defined by symptom reporting, some degree of overlap between the case definition and OSTRC-O2 outcomes is expected, and these differences should not be interpreted as entirely independent observations. In multivariable analyses, higher weekly training volume and smoking were associated with an increased probability of AKP, whereas a greater number of years of playing experience showed an inverse association. Together, these findings highlighted the relevance of both sport-related load and modifiable lifestyle factors in the development and persistence of AKP in semi-professional basketball players.

Comparison with previous literature

The prevalence of AKP observed in this study (23.5%) is consistent with previous reports in basketball and other jumping sports, where anterior knee pain is commonly reported and frequently reflects underlying overuse conditions, particularly patellar tendinopathy. Prevalence estimates in basketball players range from approximately 10% to over 30%, depending on competitive level, training exposure and case definition [9, 10, 33]. The present findings extend this literature by focusing specifically on semi-professional athletes, a substantial yet understudied segment of the competitive basketball population. The positive association between weekly training volume and AKP is aligned with existing evidence supporting load-dependent mechanisms in patellar tendinopathy. Prospective and interventional studies in high-load populations have demonstrated that excessive or poorly managed training exposure is a key driver of tendon pathology and that appropriate load modification can significantly reduce injury incidence [18, 20, 34]. Our results were therefore consistent with current conceptual models of tendon overload and provide quantitative support for these mechanisms in semi-professional basketball.

Several prespecified predictors previously associated with patellar tendinopathy or anterior knee symptoms, including playing role, playing surface, competitive tier and occupational category, did not demonstrate robust associations in the present study. Although some point estimates suggested potentially higher or lower odds of AKP, the corresponding credible intervals were wide and reflected substantial uncertainty. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted cautiously and do not provide sufficient evidence to support a meaningful role of these factors in AKP occurrence within this cohort.

Smoking emerged as a relevant associated factor in our analyses. Although causality cannot be inferred, smoking has been linked to impaired tendon metabolism, altered collagen synthesis and delayed tissue healing, while experimental studies suggest that nicotine exposure may impair tendon healing and reduce the biomechanical strength of repaired tendon tissue [27, 35], suggesting a potential contribution to AKP risk in this population.

The inverse association between years of playing experience and AKP should be interpreted cautiously. Beyond potential long-term adaptation to sport-specific loading, this pattern may partly reflect survivor bias, whereby athletes more susceptible to persistent knee symptoms discontinue participation earlier, leaving a relatively more resilient subgroup among those with longer playing careers. Similar interpretations have been proposed in previous studies of chronic overuse injuries in sport [7, 36].

Clinical relevance: screening and early identification

From a clinical perspective, these findings underscore the importance of early screening and monitoring of cumulative training exposure and smoking status in semi-professional basketball players, as AKP frequently represents the initial clinical manifestation of patellar tendinopathy, which may lead to reduced sport participation if not promptly identified and managed [33, 37]. Longitudinal studies have indicated that structural tendon changes and altered neuromuscular function often precede persistent symptoms, suggesting a window of opportunity for early intervention based on clinical screening and load monitoring rather than reactive treatment [35, 38]. Importantly, current multidisciplinary clinical practice guidelines emphasise that the diagnosis of patellar tendinopathy is primarily clinical and that routine imaging has limited additional value in early stages, particularly in the absence of diagnostic uncertainty [39].

In this context, systematic screening of training exposure, symptom response and functional impact may help identify athletes at increased risk and support timely, individualised load management and exercise-based interventions. Such monitoring could be implemented through regular assessment of weekly training volume, smoking status, symptom reporting, and validated patient-reported outcome measures such as the OSTRC-O2 questionnaire. This approach may reduce progression to chronic tendinopathy and mitigate long-term functional impairment in semi-professional athletes, who often lack structured medical supervision.

Strengths and limitations

Strengths of this study include the use of a prespecified multivariable predictor set informed by existing evidence, Bayesian modelling with explicit quantification of uncertainty, and the application of the OSTRC-O2 framework to capture knee-specific functional burden. In addition, the questionnaire underwent structured face and content validity assessment by both athletes and clinicians, supporting the clarity and relevance of the collected data.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference and does not establish temporal relationships between training exposure and symptoms. Both outcomes and exposures were self-reported and may be subject to recall bias or misclassification. Recruitment relied on voluntary participation via team dissemination, introducing potential selection bias and limiting the ability to characterise non-responders. The AKP case definition was based on a screening question combined with anatomical pain localisation and has not been formally validated against clinical examination. Consequently, some degree of outcome misclassification is possible; however, because such misclassification is likely to be non-differential with respect to the investigated predictors, any resulting bias would be expected to attenuate rather than exaggerate observed associations. In addition, smoking status was assessed as a binary variable, preventing exploration of potential dose–response relationships between smoking exposure and AKP. Furthermore, residual confounding may remain despite multivariable adjustment, and some variables may act as proxies for broader contextual factors not fully captured by the survey. Finally, findings may not be generalisable beyond male semi-professional basketball players in the surveyed regions.

Implications for practice and future research

The substantial functional burden associated with AKP observed in this study highlights its clinical relevance in semi-professional basketball, where knee symptoms may meaningfully limit both participation and performance. The model-based probability estimates presented in this study offer an interpretable framework to support proactive load management and targeted counselling, particularly regarding modifiable factors such as smoking.

Future research should prioritise prospective cohort designs with repeated symptom surveillance and objective exposure measures to clarify temporal relationships, estimate incidence, and inform prevention strategies tailored to semi-professional basketball players. In addition, interventional studies evaluating structured screening and load-management programmes in this population are warranted.

Conclusions

In this survey of semi-professional male basketball players, anterior knee pain (AKP) affected a substantial proportion of athletes and was associated with marked knee-related functional impairment. Higher weekly training volume and smoking were associated with an increased probability of AKP, whereas greater playing experience showed an inverse association that may partly reflect survivor bias. Although causal inferences cannot be drawn, these findings underscore the clinical relevance of early screening for load-related risk factors and support proactive monitoring and prevention strategies aimed at reducing progression toward chronic patellar tendinopathy in semi-professional basketball.

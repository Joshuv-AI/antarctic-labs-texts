rs604702 confers breast cancer risk by influencing PCAT18/miR-759/SPRR3 axis
Authors: Xin-Xin Zhang, Ya-Jie Li, Xi-Ting Zhou, Hong-Li Song, Hao Guo, Hai-Yan Li, Li-Yuan Tian, Chang Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBreast cancer is the most common tumor type in women and with apparent genetic predisposition. Recent genome-wide association study suggests that rs527616 is significantly associated with this disease. Analysis on 1000 genomes project data suggests that additional four SNPs, rs604702, rs1667550, rs521667, and rs675150, are in nearly complete linkage disequilibrium with rs527616. However, the causal SNP and the mechanism for this association still remain unclear. Reporter gene assay indicates that the causal SNP might be not rs527616 but rs604702. BARX2 (BARX homeobox 2) is identified to bind the surrounding region of rs604702 and binding affinity difference between alleles is compared. The enhancer containing rs604702 can interact with PCAT18 (prostate cancer associated transcript 18) promoter. Knockdown of PCAT18 can significantly decrease SPRR3 (small proline rich protein 3) expression and breast cell ability of proliferation, migration, wound healing, and colony formation, which verifies that PCAT18 is an oncogene for breast cancer. Transfection of miR-759 inhibitor can rescue abovementioned effect, thus validating that the effect of PCAT18 on breast cancer is through interacting with this miRNA. Knock-out of rs604702 surrounding region by CRISPR/Cas9 can significantly decrease PCAT18 and SPRR3 expression and further breast cell ability of proliferation, migration, wound healing, and colony formation. Our effort identifies a novel mechanism by which the genetic variation in this locus can influence breast cancer susceptibility.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03889-y.Keywords: Breast cancer, rs527616, rs604702, PCAT18/miR-759/SPRR3 axis, Expression regulation

Introduction

Cancer is the most serious health threat to current human population. Identification of susceptible genes and related mechanism can provide more insight into cancer treatment [1]. Among different cancer types, breast cancer is the most common malignant tumor and one of the most important cancer death among female in the world [2]. The predisposing factors of this disease can be divided into environmental factors and genetic susceptibility [3]. Numerous genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have been performed, with the attempt to reveal the genetic contribution to breast cancer (detail can be found in GWAS Catalog at https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/). In one previous GWAS, one genetic marker at 18q11, rs527616, is observed to be significantly associated with breast cancer in Caucasian [4]. Since rs527616 is close to a long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), AQP4-AS1 (AQP4 antisense RNA 1), this gene is proposed as the susceptible gene for breast cancer at this locus [5]. However, this mechanism has never been inspected. Moreover, owing to chip space limitation, only ~ 500 K SNPs in human genome are genotyped in GWAS. Therefore, it is possible that the real causal SNP(s) for breast cancer are not rs527616, but the genetic variation(s) in strong linkage disequilibrium (LD) with this genetic marker. However, the LD pattern at this locus and the function of related genetic variations have not been scrutinized.

In the current study, we recovered the genotype information for this locus and performed LD analysis and functional genomics work. The causal mutation and susceptible gene were identified for this disease.

Materials and methods

1000 genomes project (1KGP) data analysis

Genotype data of rs527616 upstream and downstream 100 kb segment in three widely-used populations, i.e., CEU (Utah Residents with European Ancestry), CHB (Han Chinese in Beijing), and YRI (Yoruba in Ibadan), were obtained from 1KGP (http://www.internationalgenome.org/) and LD was evaluated by PLINK (https://zzz.bwh.harvard.edu/plink/) with r2 ≥ 0.8.

Reporter gene assay

The segments containing the surrounding regions of the five SNPs were amplified by the primers in Table S1 with high-fidelity PrimeSTAR GXL DNA Polymerase (Takara, Dalian, China). After XhoI and NheI (NEB, Ipswich, MA) treatment, PCR products were inserted into pGL3-promoter vector (Promega, Madison, WI). The plasmid with another allele was obtained by MutanBEST Kit (Takara) and primers in Table S1. All plasmids were resequenced to exclude any potential artificial mutations.

Human breast epithelial cancer cell line MCF-7 and embryonic kidney one 293TN were purchased from conservation genetics CAS Kunming cell bank (http://www.kmcellbank.com/) and cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (high glucose, HyClone, Logan, UT) with 10% fetal bovine serum (Biological Industries, Cromwell, CT) in 5% CO2 at 37℃. ~105 MCF-7 Cells were seeded into 24-well plate and cultured for 24 h before transfection. 475ng recombined plasmid was transfected by Lipofectamine 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA). After transfection, cells were cultured for 36 h and harvested. The relative luciferase activity was evaluated by Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega). 25ng pRL-TK plasmid (Promega) was also transfected along with firefly luciferase plasmid to effect as an inside control. Six transfections were carried out independently for each plasmid construction.

Chromatin Immunoprecipitation (ChIP)

TRANSFAC database (http://www.gene-regulation.com/) was used to search possible transcription factors (TF). ChIP was carried out by EZ ChIP Kit (Millipore, Burlington, MA) with FOXA2 (forkhead box A2), POU2F1 (POU class 2 homeobox 1), and BARX2 (BARX homeobox 2) antibodies (#sc-374376, #sc-53830, and #sc-53177, respectively; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX) or normal mouse IgG (#sc-2025; Santa Cruz Biotechnology) according to manufacturer’s protocol. The chromatin enrichment was quantified by realtime quantitative PCR (qPCR) with iQ SYBR green (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA) and primer pair ATTTCAAATCTTGCCTCTCC and TCAATGGATGAGTTAAAGAACCAA. For each ChIP assay, one segment with verified TF binding in reference [6–8] was included as a positive control and quantified by qPCR with primers in Table S2. Three replicates were performed for each antibody.

Electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA)

Probes of rs604702 alleles were displayed in Table S3, synthesized (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China), labeled by EMSA probe biotin kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China), and annealed. MCF-7 nuclear protein was purified by Nuclear and Cytoplasmic Protein Extraction kit (Beyotime) and incubated with probes. The protein/probe complex was separated by electrophoresis, transferred to nylon membrane (Beyotime), stained by Streptavidin-horseradish peroxidase (Beyotime), and visualized as described [9]. Two replicates were performed for this experiment.

Chromosome conformation capture (3 C)

Spatial interactions between the enhancer containing rs604702 and adjacent gene promoters were evaluated by 3 C as described [9]. Since rs604702 is relatively far from nearby genes, 3 C experiments were executed for the upstream and downstream regions of rs604702 separately. BACs (bacterial artificial chromosome) RP11-278B10 and RP11-47O17 containing partial 18q11 region were chosen as controls for upstream and downstream 3 C, respectively. Chromatin and BACs were digested by BglII (NEB), ligated by T4 DNA ligase (NEB), recovered by phenol‑chloroform method, and quantified by qPCR with unidirectional primers in Table S4. Triplicates were performed for each primer pair.

RNA-seq analysis

RNA-seq data for estrogen receptor (ER) positive breast cancer and adjacent non-malignant tissues [10] were downloaded from SRA database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/). After alignment with PCAT18 (prostate cancer associated transcript 18) transcript through bowtie2 (https://bowtie-bio.sourceforge.net/bowtie2/index.shtml), its expression was measured by RSEM (https://deweylab.github.io/RSEM/) and expressed in FPKM (Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million fragments mapped).

Reverse-transcript PCR (RT-PCR)

RNA was purified by Trizol (Thermo Fisher Scientific) from MCF-7 cell line. cDNA library was prepared by RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific). AQP4-AS1 and GAPDH (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase) was amplified by primers in Table S5. cDNA of human prostatic adenocarcinoma cell line, PC3, from our recent publication [11] was used as a positive control.

Nuclear and cytoplasmic RNA isolation and quantification

RNA in nucleus and cytoplasm from MCF-7 cells was separated and isolated by RNA subcellular isolation kit (Active motif, Shanghai, China) according to manufacturer’s protocol and reverse transcribed into cDNA as abovementioned. PCAT18 expression was measured by qPCR with primers in Table S5. U6 and GAPDH were utilized as an internal control and quantified by the same method with primers in Table S5.

RNA interference and cell phenotype measurement

PCAT18 short hairpin RNA (sense strand GATCCGCAACATGACCTACAGTTACTCTTCCTGTCAGAAGTAACTGTAGGTCATGTTGCTTTTTG and antisense one AATTCAAAAAGCAACATGACCTACAGTTACTTCTGACAGGAAGAGTAACTGTAGGTCATGTTGCG) was designed by BLOCK-iT RNAi Designer (https://rnaidesigner.thermofisher.com/rnaiexpress/sort.do), synthesized, annealed, and inserted into the BamHI and EcoRI (NEB) sites of pGreenPuro vector (System Biosciences, Palo Alto, CA). After transfection into 293TN along with psPAX2 (Addgene #12260) and pMD2.G (Addgene #12259), lentivirus was collected by centrifugation and utilized to infect MCF-7. Puromycin treatment was performed to obtain a stable PCAT18 knockdown cell strain.

Related gene expression was measured as abovementioned. Cell phenotypes, including proliferation, migration, wound healing, and colony formation, were measured as previous literature [9].

miRNA-759 inhibitor (#miR20010497-1-5; Ribobio, Guangzhou, China) was transfected into knockdown cell strain by Lipofectamine 2000 and related gene expression and cell phenotypes were also measured as abovementioned.

CRISPR/Cas9

sgRNAs (small guide RNA) were displayed in Table S6. After annealing, sgRNAs were inserted into the BsmBI (NEB) site of lentiCRISPRv2 vector (Addgene #52961). Lentivirus was packaged and used to infect MCF-7 cell as abovementioned. After puromycin treatment, enhancer knock-out was validated by PCR with primer pair CCATTATCTTCTGGCCTCTAC and GCTGCTATTCACAAGGACTG. The single cell clone with enhancer knock-out was obtained by dilution. The empty vector was also packaged to generate a control strain. Related gene expression and cell phenotype were also measured as abovementioned.

Statistics

Independent Student’s t-test was utilized to compare luciferase expression between two plasmids containing different alleles, ChIP enrichment, gene expression, and cell abilities. Paired t-test was used to compared PCAT18 expression between cancer and normal tissues. All statistics analysis was executed in SPSS 20.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY).

Result

Genetic variations nearby rs527616

To identify the potential genetic variation(s) associated with breast cancer, 1KGP data in rs527616 neighbor region were downloaded for three representative populations in the world. Inside the 200 kb region surrounding rs527616, there are 936, 841, and 1395 genetic variations in CEU, CHB, and YRI populations, respectively. Among them, only rs604702 and rs1667550 show strong LD with rs527616 in YRI (both r2 >0.8). In CEU and CHB, four SNPs, rs604702, rs1667550, rs521667, and rs675150, show strong LD with rs527616 (all r2 >0.8; see Table S7 and Fig S1). This phenomenon is consistent with the relative short LD region in African populations. Considering that GWAS is performed in Caucasian [4], we hypothesize that at least one of these five SNPs might be the causal mutation(s) for breast cancer.

Function of these five SNPs

Since all five SNPs are locating at the non-coding region and not within the promoter of any known genes, we hypothesize that these SNPs might influence target gene expression through enhancer model. To investigate the role of these five SNPs on gene expression, we constructed the luciferase plasmids harboring the different alleles of these five SNPs by molecular clone and mutagenesis. As shown in Fig. 1A, the two alleles of rs527616 do not show different luciferase activity (P = 0.084), thus suggesting that rs527616 is not functional in breast cell. The similar situation is observed for rs1667550, rs521667, and rs675150 (all P > 0.05). Instead, G allele of rs604702 shows ~ 2.5-fold higher relative luciferase activity than A allele (P = 2.6 × 10− 6), thus verifying that this SNP might be the functional one for breast cancer at this locus.

Interaction between rs604702 and TF

Since rs604702 is within the non-coding region, this SNP might be located at TF binding region and could modify TF interaction affinity. To disclose this issue, Match at TRANSFAC database was employed to search the potential TFs. It is observed that rs604702 is within FOXA2, POU2F1, or BARX2 binding sites. To search for potential TF, ChIP was utilized with related antibodies. For each ChIP reaction, a positive control was included and significant enrichment is observed (results not shown), which demonstrates that our ChIP assay can be used to disclose the potential TF surrounding rs604702. As shown in Fig S2, no significant enrichment is observed in ChIP with FOXA2 and POU2F1 antibodies (P = 0.33 and 0.50, respectively), thus indicating that these two TFs can not interact with rs604702. In contrast, BARX2 antibody can significantly pulldown more chromatin for the region neighboring rs604702 than IgG (P = 0.017; see Fig. 1B), thus suggesting that BARX2 can bind this region in breast cell.

To compare the interaction affinity between two alleles of rs604702, EMSA was executed. As shown in Fig. 1C, the two alleles of rs604702 show apparent different binding efficiency with nuclear proteins from MCF-7 cell and the high expression allele, G of rs604702, display a higher affinity. Moreover, the interaction can be eliminated by competitor oligonucleotides (see Fig. 1C), which verifies that the interaction between nuclear proteins and rs604702 is specific. This binding affinity pattern is further confirmed by an independent replicate (see Fig S3).

Spatial interaction between the enhancer containing rs604702 and nearby genes

rs604702 is not within the promoter of any recognized genes. Moreover, ENCODE project (https://www.encodeproject.org/) data indicates that there are clear H3K27Ac and H3K4me1 peaks, two common histone modifications around active enhancer [12], nearby rs604702 in primary human mammary epithelial cell (HMEC, see Fig S4). Therefore, rs604702 should be inside an enhancer segment and can adjust target gene expression. However, the target gene is remaining unclear.

3 C was exploited to identify the potential target gene for this enhancer. Since most enhancers act on the nearby genes, unidirectional primers were designed to anchor two protein-coding genes (KCTD1 [potassium channel tetramerization domain containing 1] and AQP4 [aquaporin 4]) promoter, two lncRNAs (PCAT18 and AQP4-AS1) promoter and several random regions. Since this region was relatively large, the 3 C experiments were performed for upstream and downstream regions of rs604702 separately. As shown in Fig. 2, no evident increase is observed in ligation frequency at KCTD1 (corresponding to the 3rd point in x axis in Fig. 2A) and AQP4 (4th point in Fig. 2B) promoters, thus suggesting that the regulatory target of this enhancer is not these two protein-coding genes. Instead, a high ligation frequency is obtained in PCAT18 (6th point in Fig. 2A) and AQP4-AS1 (5th point in Fig. 2B) promoters, which indicates that these two lncRNAs might be the regulatory targets of this enhancer.

AQP4-AS1 and PCAT18 expression in breast tissue

In Gene database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gene/), AQP4-AS1 is expressed in limited tissues, such as prostate. To verify whether this gene is expressed in breast, we performed RT-PCR. As shown in Fig S5, the amplification succeeds in PC3 cell, thus verifying that the primer pair is with the ability to amplify AQP4-AS1 transcript. However, no amplicon is obtained in MCF-7. Moreover, RNA-seq data [10] analysis indicated an extremely low value in breast cancer and adjacent non-malignant tissues for AQP4-AS1 (result not shown). Therefore, AQP4-AS1 should be in low possibility to be expressed in breast tissue and is not likely to be the susceptible gene for breast cancer. The association between SNP at this locus and breast cancer should be attributed to PCAT18.

If PCAT18 is indeed involved in the breast cancer, this gene expression should be different between tumor and normal tissues. Since we do not obtain enough tissue samples for investigation, one RNA-seq project containing 22 breast cancers and paired adjacent non-malignant tissues [10] was retrieved for analysis. As shown in Fig S6, PCAT18 expression in breast cancer tissue, on average, is ~ 1.94-fold higher than that in normal ones (P = 0.019; paired t-test), which is consistent with bioinformatics analysis based on breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA) data from TCGA (The Cancer Genome Atlas) project [13] and suggests that PCAT18 should be an oncogene for breast cancer.

To determine the location of PCAT18 transcript, MCF-7 nuclear and cytoplasmic RNA was separated and measured. As shown in Fig S7, ~ 80% PCAT18 transcript is expressed in nucleus, which is consistent with the situation in another breast cancer cell line, MDA-MB-231 [14].

Effect of PCAT18 on cell phenotype

To explore the function of PCAT18 in breast cancer, a stable knockdown cell strain was constructed for MCF-7. As shown in Fig. 3A, PCAT18 expression decreases ~ 75.0% (P = 4.2 × 10− 6) in knockdown strain. The cell proliferation was evaluated by MTT cell viability assay. As shown in Fig. 3B, the cell growth is significantly inhibited compared with the control one (P = 0.0032 for day 3). Meanwhile, the migration, wound healing, and colony formation ability is also significantly attenuated after PCAT18 knockdown (P = 0.00017, 0.0091, and 0.000068, respectively; see Fig. 3C-E). All these results indicate that PCAT18 is a novel oncogene for breast cancer.

Sh-PCAT18 and sh-NC represent knockdown and negative control, respectively. All data are expressed as mean ± SD and ** indicates P < 0.01

Effect of miR-759 on gene expression and cell phenotype

In colorectal cancer, PCAT18 can upregulate SPRR3 (small proline rich protein 3) expression through sponging miR-759 and further promote cancer development [15]. SPRR3 is also an oncogene for breast cancer and its translation product can activate AKT and MAPK pathway [16]. To confirm whether this mechanism is still persisting in breast tissue, we first measured SPRR3 gene expression in knockdown cell strain. As shown in Fig. 3A, along with PCAT18 knockdown, SPRR3 expression decreases ~ 96.3% (P = 0.0027), thus confirming the interaction between the transcripts of these two genes.

To further investigate the role of miR-759 in interaction between PCAT18 and SPRR3 transcript, we transfected miR-759 inhibitor into knockdown cell strain and measured related gene expression and cell phenotype. As shown in Fig. 4A, with the miR-759 inhibitor transfection, PCAT18 and SPRR3 expression increase ~ 84.0% and ~ 1.08-fold, respectively (P = 3.0 × 10− 5 and 0.039, respectively). In knockdown cell with miR-759 inhibitor transfection, relative cell number is significantly higher in 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and 4th day than that of knockdown cell (P = 0.0011, 3.4 × 10− 5, 2.3 × 10− 4, and 9.9 × 10− 5, respectively; see Fig. 4B). Meanwhile, wound healing and colony formation ability also significantly increase (P = 0.0044 and 0.0010, respectively; see Fig. 4D and E). Only the migration ability is not influenced (P = 0.49; see Fig. 4C). These results verify the function of miR-759 in the interaction between PCAT18 and SPRR3 transcript.

Effect of enhancer knock-out

To further investigate the role of rs604702 on gene expression and breast cancer onset, we performed genome editing to knock-out the region surrounding rs604702. As shown in Fig S8, 386 bp surrounding rs604702 was knocked-out in MCF-7 cell. With genome editing at this locus, PCAT18 and SPRR3 expression decrease ~ 77.6% and ~ 56.3%, respectively (P = 0.023 and 1.5 × 10− 4, respectively; see Fig. 5A), thus verifying that rs604702 is within an active enhancer for PCAT18. In knock-out cell strain, the relative cell count is significantly lower in 1st, 2nd, and 3rd day than that of control cell (all P < 10− 6; see Fig. 5B). Meanwhile, the migration, wound healing, and colony formation ability also significantly decrease (P = 1.8 × 10− 4, 7.9 × 10− 4, and 6.0 × 10− 5, respectively; see Fig. 5C-E). These results indicate that rs604702 is the functional SNP and can influence breast cancer onset.

Discussion

In the current study, we attempted to disclose the underlying mechanism between rs527616 and breast cancer. The function of PCAT18 and its cis-regulatory variation, rs604702, in breast cancer was evaluated. Our effort illustrates the connection between genetic marker rs527616 and breast cancer.

PCAT18 is a recently identified lncRNA with distinct function among tissues [17]. In acute myeloid leukemia, prostate cancer, and colorectal cancer patient, PCAT18 is overexpressed in tumor tissue compared with normal ones and can promote cell proliferation, thus indicating PCAT18 is an oncogene for abovementioned tumor types [15, 17, 18]. Our results suggest a similar effect in MCF-7. In contrast, PCAT18 expression is significantly lower in gastric cancer and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBR) and can inhibit cell proliferation, thus suggesting to be a tumor suppressor gene [13, 18–21]. This conflict might reflect the complex role of this gene in carcinogenesis, especially in breast cancer. Indeed, according to the expression of ER, progesterone receptor, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER-2), breast cancer can be divided into four different types, Luminal A, Luminal B, HER-2 positive, and TNBR. Considering that the RNA-seq data is from ER positive breast cancer [10] and that MCF-7 is supposed to be ER positive breast cancer cell line, we believe that PCAT18 should be an oncogene at least for Luminal A and B breast cancer. There is still no investigation on the role of PCAT18 in HER-2 positive breast cancer. Further gene expression comparison between HER-2 positive breast cancer patients and normal controls and RNA interference and cell phenotype measurement in HER-2 positive breast cancer cell lines, such as SK-BR-3 or HCC1954, might provide more insight into the role of PCAT18 in this breast cancer type. Since related tissues and cell model are not available to us, we can not verify this issue. Further analysis might better illuminate this issue. Moreover, it is necessary to point out that this phenomenon is rather common among cancer types. For example, AIM2 (absent in melanoma 2) is supposed to be a tumor suppressor gene in colon [22], liver [23], prostate [24], and breast cancer [25] but an oncogene in non-small cell lung cancer [26]. In addition, the different role in PCAT18 among breast cancer types might be attributed to the tumor microenvironment, which has been proven to alter cancer cell phenotypes [27].

Our effort identifies the effect of PCAT18 on SPRR3 expression and cancer cell phenotype. However, it is necessary to mention that plasticity is very common in cancer cell [28], which might modulate this axis and further enable the cancer cell to avoid pathway inhibition through alternative programs.

Future cancer treatment will focus more on personalized and precision strategies, particularly targeting the tumor immune microenvironment [29]. The current work underscores the role of PCAT18 on breast cancer. Therefore, this gene might be a novel therapy target for this disease, especially for the patients with high PCAT18 expression.

In colon cell, it has been observed that PCAT18 transcript is mainly appearing at cytoplasm and can effect as a miR-759 sponge [15]. Similarly, PCAT18 transcript can also work as a sponge for miR-759 or miR-103a-3p [14] in breast cell. However, PCAT18 transcript primarily appears in nucleus of MCF-7 (see Fig S7) and MDA-MB-231 [14], which suggests a different distribution pattern among tissues. The exist of PCAT18 transcript in breast cell nucleus might hint that this gene might play some other roles besides miRNA sponge, such as regulating gene expression, which deserves further investigation.

Based on the differential expression of AQP4-AS1 between BRCA and control tissue and the relatively close distance between rs527616 and this gene, it is proposed that AQP4-AS1 is the susceptible gene at this locus for breast cancer [5]. However, in our RT-PCR assay, AQP4-AS1 expression is absent in breast cell. This conflict might be due to the misuse of the RNA-seq result from TANRIC [30]. Indeed, TANRIC algorithm can generate an expression score for any genes, even a non-expressed one or non-coding region. For example, the expression score of UGT2B7 (UDP glucuronosyltransferase family 2 member B7), a gene whose expression is absent in breast tissue [31], is varying from − 16.61 to 5.37 in BRCA and control (log-transformed based on 2; results not shown). Similarly, we searched the expression score for AQP4-AS1 introns by position (chr18: 24472957–24504723; 24504810–24505780; 24505827–24510633; 24510788–24512956; 24513087–24513579; 24513714–24514243). The expression score for AQP4-AS1 introns is varying from − 16.61 to 0.899 in the same dataset (results not shown). In contrast, expression score of AQP4-AS1 is ranging from − 16.61 to -0.43 in the same dataset (see Fig. 2 of ref [5]), which is somewhat lower than that of UGT2B7 and AQP4-AS1 introns. This comparison indicates that AQP4-AS1 expression should be also absent in BRCA and control RNA-seq data from TCGA.

We propose rs604702 as the causal mutation for breast cancer based on LD pattern and function of this SNP. However, the distribution of rs604702 in case-control groups has not been investigated. We searched the original data of one GWAS [32] and observed that rs604702 is significantly associated with breast cancer (P = 4.4 × 10− 14), thus verifying our proposal. Moreover, it is necessary to mention that some other variations in nearby region might be with subtle effect and also contribute to breast cancer onset, which can be detected by further fine-mapping.

One limitation of our study is the PCAT18 and further SPRR3 expression pattern between breast tumor and normal tissues. Although RNA-seq indicates that PCAT18 is observed to be overexpressed in breast cancer tissues in published literature [13] and our analysis (see Fig S6), there is still no validation by single gene method, such as qPCR, which attenuates this conclusion. Further tissue collection, RNA isolation, and gene expression measurement by qPCR can validate this pattern. Since related tissues are not available to us, we can not verify this issue. Further analysis might better substantiate this.

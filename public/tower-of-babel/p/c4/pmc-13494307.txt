Analysis of demographic factors and consumption habits on hypertension in the adult population in Indonesia: (Health Indonesian survey 2023)
Authors: Cici Apriza Yanti, Evi Susanti, Ratna Djuwita, Evi Martha, Besral, Noor Mastura Mohd Mujar, Debby Ratno Kustanto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesHypertension remains one of the leading contributors to cardiovascular disease and premature mortality in Indonesia. This study aimed to examine the association between demographic characteristics and consumption-related behaviours’ with hypertension among Indonesian adults using nationally representative survey data.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted using secondary data from the Indonesia Health Survey (IHS) 2023. The analysis included 638,180 respondents aged 15 years and older from all provinces in Indonesia. Bivariate analysis was performed using the Chi-square test, followed by multivariable logistic regression to identify factors associated with hypertension. Results were presented as adjusted odds ratios (AORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).ResultsOlder age was the strongest factor associated with hypertension (AOR = 4.92; 95% CI: 4.83–5.01; p < 0.001). Women were more likely to report hypertension than men (AOR = 1.67; 95% CI: 1.63–1.71; p < 0.001). Regular consumption of fruits and vegetables was associated with lower odds of hypertension (AOR = 0.88; 95% CI: 0.84–0.92; p < 0.001). Regional variation was also observed, with East Java reporting the highest prevalence of hypertension among the provinces included in the survey.ConclusionHypertension among Indonesian adults is strongly associated with age, sex, and dietary behaviours. The findings suggest that prevention strategies should prioritize population groups at higher risk while promoting healthier dietary practices through context-specific public health interventions across different regions of Indonesia.

Introduction

The prevalence of hypertension in Indonesia continues to increase, especially in the adult population. It is one of the main causes of cardiovascular disease, which contributes significantly to national morbidity and mortality.1,2 Not only does hypertension raise the risk of cardiovascular diseases like heart attack and stroke, but it also closely links to diabetes mellitus and other chronic diseases, complicating patient health management.3,4 Various factors, including lifestyle, socio-economic status, and psychological conditions, often exacerbate this condition globally. 5 The COVID-19 pandemic has further increased the prevalence of hypertension in various countries.4,6

However, although hypertension is a global health problem, understanding of its specific causes and distribution patterns is still limited in developing countries such as Indonesia. In contrast to developed countries, local epidemiological data explaining the contribution of demographic factors, consumption patterns, and socio-economic conditions to the prevalence of hypertension is still very limited.1,7 Comprehensive local research is crucial to understand the specific context of the population in Indonesia, considering that lifestyles, customs, and access to health services in this country differ significantly from other countries. 8 Rapid urbanization has also changed the lifestyle of Indonesian people to be more dependent on processed foods, less physical activity, and higher exposure to urban stress, which has the potential to worsen the risk of hypertension. 9 In addition, increasing life expectancy has also led to an increase in the elderly population who are more susceptible to hypertension. 10

Demographic factors such as advancing age and gender play an important role in increasing the risk of hypertension. 11 The elderly are susceptible to hypertension due to natural physiological changes, while women, especially after menopause, have a higher risk than men.12–14 This suggests that age and gender are non-modifiable risk factors that should be considered in the prevention of hypertension in the adult population. 15 In addition, various consumption habits, such as high-sodium diets, smoking, and alcohol consumption, also play a significant role in increasing the risk of hypertension.16,17 For example, exposure to cigarette smoke has been shown to cause vasoconstriction and increase blood pressure through activation of the sympathetic nervous system, while excessive alcohol consumption worsens the effects of hypertension., 18 especially when combined with smoking.19,20

Research in Medan showed a relationship between physical activity, obesity, and hypertension, while another study in Jakarta found a relationship between dietary habits and hypertension. 21 However, these studies were limited to certain regions and did not provide a comprehensive picture that represented the national population. 22 Therefore, analysis with national scale data such as the 2023 Indonesian Health Survey (IHS) is expected to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the relationship between demographic factors and dietary habits to hypertension in the adult population in Indonesia, which in turn can be used as a basis for developing more effective health policies. 23

Global research shows that high sodium consumption and smoking contribute significantly to hypertension, and several studies in Asia have shown that local consumption patterns, such as high salt use, influence the prevalence of hypertension.24,25 In Indonesia, variations in demographics and consumption habits can also influence the risk of hypertension. For example, high-salt diets in some areas, as well as differences in physical activity between urban and rural areas, can affect the level of hypertension. 26 Therefore, the 2023 SKI data is expected to provide important information on consumption patterns and demographic variations, so that health policies can be more targeted in reducing the risk of hypertension in the adult population.21,27

In various developing countries, the level of awareness, prevention, and control of hypertension is still relatively low compared to developed countries, thus worsening the impact of this condition on public health. 28 Most previous studies have focused on general factors without detailing the influence of demographic variables and consumption behaviour in depth.18,29,30 Using data from the 2023 Indonesian Health Survey (IHS), this analysis allows the identification of specific risk factors that may be more relevant to the Indonesian population. The HIS 2023 data provides comprehensive information on the demographic characteristics and consumption habits of the Indonesian people, which can help understand the pattern of hypertension in various community groups. This study aims to identify the influence of age, gender, and consumption habits, such as sodium and alcohol intake, on the prevalence of hypertension in the adult population in Indonesia. We assume that demographic factors and consumption habits play a significant role in increasing the risk of hypertension in Indonesia.

Methodology

Research design

This study used a quantitative design with a cross-sectional approach to explore risk factors for hypertension based on IHS 2023 data. This design was chosen because it is appropriate to explore the relationship between demographic factors, lifestyle, and consumption habits on the prevalence of hypertension in a large population, such as the Indonesian population. This design is also appropriate to analyse heterogeneous variables in a large population, while ensuring sufficient representation from all regions.

Populations and sample

This study used secondary data from the Indonesia Health Survey (IHS) 2023, a nationally representative survey conducted by the Ministry of Health. The original survey employed a multistage stratified sampling design to ensure representation across provinces and population groups in Indonesia. For the present analysis, all respondents aged 15 years and older with complete information on hypertension status and study variables were included, resulting in a final analytical sample of 638,180 individuals. Because this study relied on an existing national dataset, no separate sample size calculation was performed by the researchers. Nevertheless, the large sample provided sufficient statistical precision for examining associations between demographic characteristics, consumption habits, and hypertension.

Instrumentation and data access

Researchers submitted a data request letter with the number 24087DD4BCF43422 to obtain official data from the Ministry of Health. After approval, a Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) with the number FRM/SMKI-PUSDATIN/70/0227/2024 was signed to ensure data security and confidentiality, as summarized in Figure 1, which outlines the procedural steps for data access and validation. The data obtained include food consumption patterns and hypertension incidence in each province in Indonesia, representing the national prevalence related to public health. Access to the dataset was granted only after approval by the Ministry of Health and completion of the required data confidentiality agreement.Figure 1.Flowchart of data collection and processing process.

Definition of hypertension

Hypertension was defined based on self-reported physician diagnosis, as recorded in the IHS 2023 questionnaire (“Have you ever been diagnosed with hypertension by a healthcare professional?”). The dataset does not include direct blood pressure measurements. Therefore, hypertension status relied on respondent recall, a commonly used approach in Indonesian national health surveys such as RISKESDAS. The potential implications of this self-report measure are addressed in the Limitations section.

Data collection and validation procedure

The data used has been validated with standard procedures set by the Ministry of Health, including verification and double-checking of data. The questionnaire is available through the official link at https://layanandata.kemkes.go.id/katalog-data/ski/ketersediaan-data/ski-2023 which also provides access to the survey questionnaire to ensure transparency in data collection. Before analysis, incomplete data was processed using the imputation method to minimize bias due to missing data. Furthermore, the data was analyzed according to scientific standards to ensure accuracy and validity.

Statical analysis

Data were analysed using SPSS version 24. Descriptive statistics were used to summarise the characteristics of the study participants. Associations between demographic factors, consumption behaviours, and hypertension were initially assessed using Chi-square tests and presented as crude odds ratios (CORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Variables that were statistically significant in the bivariate analyses, together with factors considered epidemiologically relevant based on previous literature, were included in multivariable logistic regression models to estimate adjusted odds ratios (AORs). Statistical significance was determined at a p-value of less than 0.05.

Sensitivity analysis

A sensitivity analysis was conducted to assess the robustness of the multivariate model. Results from the complete-case dataset were compared with those using imputed data, and an additional restricted model excluding variables with weak bivariate associations (p > 0.20) was evaluated. The effect sizes and direction of the main predictors remained stable across all specifications, indicating that the findings are robust despite the reliance on self-reported survey data.

Result

Prevalence and geographic distribution

The overall prevalence of hypertension among Indonesian adults was 8.4%. Geographic variation is clearly illustrated in Figure 2, which maps the distribution of hypertension cases across provinces. East Java appears as the province with the highest burden, with more than 50,000 reported cases, followed by other provinces on Java Island such as Central Java and West Java. In contrast, eastern regions including Papua Pegunungan and Papua Tengah show markedly lower prevalence. The spatial pattern in Figure 2 suggests a concentration of hypertension in more densely populated and urbanized provinces, which may be influenced by lifestyle factors, dietary transitions, and differential access to health services. Multivariate analysis

The final multivariate logistic regression model is presented in Table 3. Age remained the strongest predictor, with older adults having nearly fivefold higher odds of hypertension compared with younger individuals. Female gender was also associated with higher risk. Among lifestyle variables, smoking, alcohol consumption, and sugary beverage intake remained significant predictors after adjustment. In contrast, fruit intake and certain fats/oils were associated with lower odds of hypertension. Depression showed one of the strongest adjusted associations, indicating that individuals with depressive symptoms have nearly twice the risk of hypertension. Vegetable consumption, egg intake, seafood, and cereal products were not significantly associated with hypertension after adjustment, suggesting that their influence may be confounded by other dietary or demographic factors. Summary of key findings

Taken together, these findings demonstrate that demographic factors, dietary behaviors, physical inactivity, and mental health conditions significantly contribute to hypertension risk in Indonesia. The geographic differences observed in Figure 2 highlight the need for region-specific public health strategies, while the results from Tables 2 and 3 underscore the importance of comprehensive intervention programs targeting both lifestyle and psychosocial determinants.

Discussion

This study provides updated evidence on the epidemiology of hypertension in Indonesia by examining demographic characteristics, lifestyle behaviors, and dietary patterns using data from the 2023 Indonesian Health Survey (IHS). The findings strengthen existing global knowledge that age and sex remain dominant determinants of hypertension, while also highlighting context-specific patterns that are unique to Indonesia’s population structure, cultural dietary habits, and pronounced regional variability. Consistent with global literature, older adults showed a markedly higher risk of hypertension, reflecting physiological changes such as arterial stiffness and altered hormonal regulation that accompany aging.31,32 Hypertension in older adults is also closely associated with age-related comorbidities, including chronic kidney disease (CKD), which further increases cardiovascular risk and complicates disease management. 33 Previous studies have demonstrated that CKD and hypertension are strongly interrelated conditions, where impaired renal function contributes to increased blood pressure and vice versa, forming a bidirectional relationship. 34 This relationship highlights the importance of early detection and integrated management strategies, particularly among older populations. 33 The elevated risk observed among women aligns with evidence suggesting that post-menopausal hormonal shifts contribute to increased blood pressure.13,14,35 These findings reinforce the importance of age and gender-sensitive prevention strategies, particularly given Indonesia’s growing elderly population.

This study also identified substantial geographic disparities, with the highest burden found in East Java, contrasting common assumptions that Jakarta or Sumatra consistently represents the highest-risk areas. These regional differences may reflect varying levels of urbanization, access to health services, dietary patterns, and public awareness of hypertension risk factors.22,36 The patterns observed suggest that hypertension prevention policies in Indonesia should adopt a regionally tailored approach rather than relying on uniform national strategies.

Dietary and lifestyle behaviors also played a significant role in shaping hypertension risk. The association between alcohol consumption and elevated blood pressure supports previous findings from regional and international studies.37–40 Conversely, fruit consumption showed a protective effect, consistent with literature indicating that fiber-rich and micronutrient dense foods contribute to blood pressure regulation.41,42 Although vegetable intake was not strongly associated with hypertension in this dataset, this may reflect variability in cooking methods, seasoning, or sodium content in Indonesian dishes rather than an absence of biological benefit.31,43 The contribution of sugary beverage intake to hypertension risk underscores broader shifts toward processed foods and modern dietary habits in Indonesian urban areas.23,44

Lifestyle behaviors such as physical inactivity also contributed to hypertension risk. Only a small proportion of respondents engaged in sufficient physical activity, a pattern consistent with the growing sedentary lifestyle linked to rapid urban development. 45 These findings emphasize the need for health promotion strategies that integrate culturally appropriate physical activity programs with existing community structures. The strong association between depressive symptoms and hypertension further highlights the interconnectedness of mental and cardiovascular health an issue gaining global attention.46,47 The consistency observed in the sensitivity analysis suggests that the final model was not materially influenced by potential self-report bias or residual confounding, a methodological challenge commonly encountered in large scale survey data. The inclusion of mental health components may therefore strengthen future hypertension prevention efforts.

Taken together, these findings demonstrate that hypertension in Indonesia is shaped not only by biological factors but also by cultural dietary practices, regional disparities, psychological well-being, and shifting lifestyle norms. The large sample size (n = 638,180) provides a robust basis for identifying these determinants and supports the development of targeted, evidence-based policies.44,48 Policymakers may benefit from focusing on high-risk groups particularly older adults and women while also addressing modifiable risk factors such as alcohol use, dietary sodium, sugary beverages, and physical inactivity.21,27,29,40,49

Given the substantial geographic variation observed, region specific strategies may be more effective than national programs that assume uniform risk. Promoting fruit consumption, reducing sodium intake, and incorporating mental health support into primary care may yield measurable improvements in hypertension outcomes across different regions. Overall, this study contributes important quantitative evidence to the growing body of hypertension literature in Indonesia, providing a strong scientific foundation for interventions tailored to demographic, behavioral, and cultural contexts. These findings underscore the importance of integrated strategies that address both lifestyle and psychosocial determinants to reduce the growing burden of hypertension among Indonesian adults.

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings. Hypertension status was based on self-reported physician diagnosis rather than direct blood pressure measurements, which may have introduced recall bias. In addition, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference. Several potentially important variables, including body mass index, educational attainment, household income, and urban–rural residence, were unavailable or incomplete in the dataset and therefore could not be included in the analysis. Consequently, residual confounding cannot be entirely ruled out. Despite these limitations, the large nationally representative sample provides valuable insights into factors associated with hypertension among Indonesian adults.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that hypertension among Indonesian adults is shaped primarily by demographic factors especially older age and female sex as well as lifestyle and dietary behaviors, including alcohol use, sugary beverages, and sodium intake. Protective effects were observed from fruit consumption, and clear regional differences highlight the influence of local cultural and environmental contexts. These findings provide a strong evidence base for refining national hypertension-prevention strategies. Policies should prioritize high-risk demographic groups, promote healthier dietary and lifestyle practices, and adopt region-specific approaches to ensure more effective and equitable hypertension control across Indonesia.

Ethical considerations

This study used anonymised secondary data from the Indonesia Health Survey (IHS) 2023 conducted by the Ministry of Health of the Republic of Indonesia. Ethical approval for the original survey was obtained by the survey organisers prior to data collection, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants before their enrolment in the survey. The dataset provided to the researchers did not contain any personal identifiers. Access to the data was granted through an approved application process and a signed data confidentiality agreement before analysis was undertaken.

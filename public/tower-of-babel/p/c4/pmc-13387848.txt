Association Between Health Literacy and Outpatient Expenditure in Korea
Authors: Seokjun Moon, Junghun Yoo, Jaehun Jung, Dun-Sol Go
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHealth literacy (HL) is increasingly recognized as a crucial determinant of healthcare utilization and costs. Despite its importance, empirical evidence on the relationship between HL and healthcare expenditures in South Korea is limited. This issue is particularly timely and relevant given the country’s rapidly rising outpatient expenditure.MethodsThis study analyzed data from the Korea Health Panel Survey conducted in 2021 (n = 8,630) to examine the association between HL and outpatient healthcare costs. HL was measured using the Korean version of the Health Literacy Survey European Questionnaire 16 (range: 0–16) and categorized as high (≥ 13) or low (≤ 12). Outpatient expenditure was defined as the sum of insurance-covered payments, out-of-pocket spending, and non-covered services, excluding pharmaceutical costs. Expenditure data were log-transformed and analyzed using multivariable linear regression. Subgroup analyses by age group were conducted under the assumption that healthcare utilization patterns vary across age categories.ResultsThe results showed that individuals with high HL spent 8.6% less on outpatient care than those with low HL. Subgroup analyses further revealed that this association was statistically significant only among adults aged 65 years and older.ConclusionThese findings provide evidence that higher HL is associated with reduced outpatient expenditures in South Korea, particularly in older populations. Therefore, promoting HL among older adults may represent an effective strategy for improving healthcare efficiency and mitigating the burden of rising outpatient costs.Keywords: Health Literacy, Outpatient Expenditure, Healthcare Expenditure, Older Adults, Korea Health Panel Survey

INTRODUCTION

Health literacy (HL) refers to the ability to access, understand, appraise, and apply health information, reflecting the capacity of individuals to meet the complex demands of health in modern society.1 A high level of HL is associated with lower mortality rates, better medication adherence, and improved health behaviors and outcomes.2,3,4 In addition, HL is closely related to healthcare expenditures. Individuals with low HL tend to incur higher costs for outpatient visits, hospitalizations, and medications, and are more likely to utilize emergency services.5,6,7,8 Given its significant impact, many countries have identified the promotion of HL as a key national policy objective.9

In light of its global significance, it is important to examine the situation in specific national contexts, such as South Korea. Healthcare expenditures in South Korea have shown a persistent and rapid increase over the past few decades. Among the various categories, outpatient service utilization is particularly notable because of its exceptionally high level. In 2022, the average number of outpatient visits per capita was 17.5, approximately three times higher than the OECD average of 6.3.10 Correspondingly, national expenditure on outpatient care increased from 14.4 trillion KRW in 2008 to 41.1 trillion KRW in 2021, with an average annual growth rate of 8.4%.11 Several structural features of the Korean healthcare system contribute to high outpatient utilization and rising healthcare expenditures. While healthcare financing is primarily public through national health insurance, the provision of services is dominated by private-sector providers. The absence of a general practitioner system has resulted in weak primary care infrastructure, limiting the effectiveness of care coordination and gatekeeping. Moreover, the fee-for-service reimbursement model remains the dominant payment mechanism, incentivizing high service volumes and contributing to the growth in outpatient spending.12,13

In this context, healthcare utilization in Korea is largely driven by patients’ autonomous decisions, rather than by systematic care coordination. This highlights the potential role of HL in influencing healthcare utilization patterns. Individuals with high levels of HL, equipped with a better understanding of the healthcare system, are more likely to make informed choices, adhere to medical recommendations, and effectively navigate complex care environments to access appropriate services.14,15,16 However, no empirical study has examined the association between HL and outpatient healthcare utilization in South Korea. This study aimed to analyze the relationship between HL and healthcare use in the Korean context.

METHODS

Study design

This cross-sectional study examined the association between HL levels and annual outpatient expenditure using data from the 2021 Korea Health Panel Survey (KHPS).

Data sources and population

The KHPS survey, a nationally certified statistical dataset in South Korea, was initiated in 2008 and targeted household member aged 19 years and older to monitor healthcare expenditure and utilization of the Korean. The data collection period was divided into two phases: Phase 1 (2008–2018) and Phase 2 (2019–2022). The Phase 2 survey targeted approximately 8,000 households and their members. We used only the 2021 data, in which HL was surveyed exclusively in that year. The study included 11,914 subjects. After excluding 3,284 individuals with missing HL data, the final analytical sample consisted of 8,630 subjects.

Variables

Dependent variable

Health expenditure was defined as the annual outpatient care cost, because outpatient care in South Korea best reflects the general healthcare utilization patterns of the population. In contrast, services such as emergency care, inpatient care, and healthcare screening, which are either essential or unavoidable, do not accurately represent general healthcare utilization patterns.17,18

Health expenditure consists of the sum of out-of-pocket payment and government payment covered by the National Health Insurance Service. It includes costs for general medicine, dental services, and oriental medicine but excludes pharmaceutical expenses. Because of the highly skewed distribution and presence of extreme values in healthcare expenditure data, a log transformation was applied to ensure a more balanced and reliable analysis.

Independent variable

HL was measured using the Health Literacy Survey European Questionnaire 16 (HLS-EU-Q16), translated and culturally adapted for Korea.19 The HLS-EU-Q16 consists of 16 items divided into three domains: healthcare (seven items), disease prevention (five items), and health promotion (four items). Respondents were asked to rate each item using one of five options: “very difficult,” “fairly difficult,” “fairly easy,” “very easy,” or “don’t know.” In this study, responses of “very difficult” or “fairly difficult” were assigned a score of 0, while “fairly easy” or “very easy” were assigned a score of 1, resulting in a total score ranging from 0 to 16.20

Categorical classifications were used rather than treating the scale as a continuous variable. HL measured via self-reported perception was better interpreted in terms of relative group differences than absolute scores. In particular, ceiling effects, commonly defined as those occurring when more than 15% of the respondents achieve the highest possible score, have frequently been reported in studies using this instrument. Such ceiling effects limit the utility of the scale as a continuous measure and instead support the practice of categorization. Previous research utilizing the HLS-EU-Q16 has documented proportions exceeding 25% of respondents achieving a maximum score of 16.21,22,23 In the dataset used for the present study, this issue was also evident, as approximately 28% of the subjects obtained the maximum score.

We categorized HL as “high” for scores above 12 and “low” for scores of 12 or below.24 Although several studies have categorized the HLS-EU-Q16 into three levels—inadequate (0–8), problematic (9–12), and sufficient (13–16)25—the dichotomized approach is often preferred, particularly in multivariable regression analyses where this method is frequently used.26

Control variables

The other variables were as follows: Sex was categorized as “male” and “female,” while age was divided into five groups: “19–29 years,” “30–39 years,” “40–49 years,” “50–64 years,” and “65 years or older.” Marital status was classified as “married” and “non-married.” Educational attainment was divided into six categories: “no education,” “elementary school graduate,” “middle school graduate,” “high school graduate,” “college graduate,” and “graduate school or above.” Income level was calculated by converting household income into individual income and classified into quintiles. Private insurance enrollment was categorized as “insured” or “uninsured.” Lastly, the presence of chronic diseases was determined based on whether subjects had been diagnosed with a chronic condition in the past year and was categorized as “present” or “absent.”

Statistical analysis

In this study, annual outpatient healthcare expenditures were log-transformed and analyzed using an ordinary least squares regression model. A log transformation was applied to address the skewness typically observed in expenditure data, thereby improving the normality of the distribution. This approach improves model fit by better satisfying regression assumptions.27 The regression coefficients from the log-transformed model were exponentiated [exp(β)] to convert the estimates back to the original scale of expenditures. The resulting exp(β) values indicate the proportional change in expenditures associated with a one-unit increase in the independent variable. For interpretation, an exp(β) value greater than 1 corresponds to a percentage increase, whereas a value less than 1 corresponds to a percentage decrease. For example, exp(β) = 0.914 implies an 8.6% decrease in expenditure.

In addition, subgroup analyses were conducted by age group, under the assumption that the patterns of HL levels and healthcare utilization may differ across age categories.

The bootstrap method was applied to reduce uncertainty in the sample distribution and improve the accuracy of the confidence interval estimates for the regression coefficients. We used 100 bootstrap replications and robust standard errors.

Ethics statement

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Yonsei University (IRB No. 1041849-202409-SB-202-01). No informed consent was required from patients due to the use of de-identified secondary data.

Age distribution showed a clear trend in HL across the different age groups. Subjects aged 19–29 years, who accounted for 6.4% (n = 554) of the total sample, demonstrated the highest proportion of high HL, with 86.1% (n = 477) in this group and 13.9% (n = 77) in the low-HL group. In contrast, older adults aged 65 years and older, constituting 41.6% (n = 3,585), had the highest proportion of low HL, with 77.4% (n = 2,775) in this category and only 22.6% (n = 810) in the high HL group. The middle-aged group (30–64 years) displayed intermediate patterns, with a gradual decrease in high HL and an increase in low HL as age increased.

The marital status variable revealed that 29.5% (n = 2,547) of subjects were non-married, among whom 51.8% (n = 1,318) had low HL and 48.2% (n = 1,229) had high HL. Meanwhile, 70.5% (n = 6,083) of the subjects were married, with a relatively balanced distribution of HL: 50.3% (n = 3,061) in the low-HL group and 49.7% (n = 3,022) in the high HL group.

Educational level was strongly associated with HL. Individuals with no education (3.9%, n = 336) had the highest proportion of low HL, with 97.0% (n = 326) in this group and 3.0% (n = 10) in the high HL group. Similary, those of elementary school graduates had low HL (18.4%, n = 1,584), and 13.3% (n = 210) had high HL. Conversely, individuals with higher education levels, such as college graduates (30.9%, n = 2,663), displayed the reverse pattern, with 75.7% (n = 2,015) having high HL and 24.3% (n = 648) having low HL. Those with graduate school or above (3.4%, n = 295) had the highest proportion of high HL (78.3%, n = 231).

The income level showed a clear gradient in HL. Individuals in the lowest income quintile (22.9%, n = 1,973) had the highest proportion of low-HL status, with 77.6% (n = 1,532) in this group and only 22.4% (n = 441) in the high HL group. In contrast, subjects in the highest-income quintile (19.1%, n = 1,651) displayed the opposite pattern, with 70.0% (n = 1,156) having high HL and 30.0% (n = 495) having low HL.

Regarding private health insurance, 25.5% (n = 2,169) of the individuals were uninsured, of whom 74.6% (n = 1,619) had low HL and 25.4% (n = 550) had high HL. Conversely, 74.5% (n = 6,340) of the subjects were insured, with a lower proportion of low HL (42.9%, n = 2,723) and higher proportion of high HL (57.1%, n = 3,617).

Subjects with chronic diseases (57.2%, n = 4,935) were more likely to have low HL (66.2%, n = 3,266) than in the high-HL group (33.8%, n = 1,669). Conversely, subjects without chronic diseases (42.8%, n = 3,695) were more likely to have high HL, with 69.9% (n = 2,582) in this group, compared to 30.1% (n = 1,113) with low HL.

The average annual outpatient expenditure of the subjects was 1,173,403 KRW. Those with low HL incurred significantly higher costs (1,441,773 KRW) compared to those with high HL (913,932 KRW).

Chi-square and t-tests revealed statistically significant differences in HL across all variables except marital status (P = 0.227), highlighting the importance of sex, age, education, income, private health insurance, chronic disease, and outpatient expenditure in relation to HL levels.

Regarding sex, females were found to spend significantly more on outpatient healthcare compared to males, with an exp(β) value of 1.260, corresponding to a 26.0% increase in expenditures (P < 0.001). Age also emerged as a significant factor, with subjects in all age groups older than 19–29 years showing progressively higher outpatient expenditures as age increased (P < 0.001).

In terms of socioeconomic factors, neither marital status nor education level had a significant impact on healthcare expenditure, as there were no meaningful differences between the groups when compared to the reference categories (unmarried individuals and those with no formal education). Similarly, the income level did not significantly affect expenditure, as no clear differences were observed between the lowest income quintile and the other quintiles. In contrast, private health insurance status was a notable determinant, with insured subjects spending 23.1% more on outpatient healthcare than those without insurance (exp(β) = 1.231, P < 0.001). The presence of a chronic disease had the most pronounced effect, as individuals with chronic conditions spent significantly higher expenditures than those without. The exp(β) value for this group was 1.939, indicating a 93.9% increase in outpatient expenditures (P < 0.001).

Sex emerged as a significant factor in all age groups, except for the 19–29 and 65 years and older categories. Across the groups where sex was significant, females consistently exhibited higher outpatient utilization than males. This result mirrors the overall trends observed in the age-specific analyses of outpatient service utilization. Marital status was not a significant variable in any age group. Education level was significant in 19–29 years and 30–39 years, where it was associated with differences in outpatient healthcare utilization. In contrast, education level did not emerge as a significant factor in older age groups, indicating its diminishing influence on healthcare utilization as age increased. Income level was significant only in the 50–64 years, specifically among subjects in the third income quintile. In this subgroup, outpatient healthcare expenditures were found to decrease compared to those in the lowest-income quintile.-Private health insurance status was a significant factor in both the 50–64 years and 65 years and older groups. The presence of chronic diseases was a consistently significant variable across all age groups. In every subgroup, individuals with chronic diseases utilized outpatient services significantly more frequently than those without chronic diseases.

DISCUSSION

This study found that individuals with higher HL incurred 8.6% lower outpatient care costs than those with lower HL. Prior research has reported that limited HL is associated with 3–5% higher healthcare spending,6,28 which is broadly consistent with our findings. According to previous studies, higher HL is linked to a greater use of preventive services, fewer avoidable visits, and more efficient outpatient care.29,30 These effects are likely explained by the ability of individuals with higher HL to access, comprehend, and apply health information when making healthcare decisions. In settings such as South Korea, where access to medical care is relatively unrestricted, the cost-reducing effects of HL may be more apparent. However, because perceived healthcare needs differ across individuals, our results should not be interpreted as evidence that HL reduces unnecessary or inefficient healthcare use. A more appropriate interpretation is that HL has the potential to reduce inappropriate use.

Subgroup analysis showed that the relationship between HL and outpatient expenditures was significant only among adults aged 65 and older. This is especially important in the Korean context, where older adults are the largest consumers of healthcare services and account for a rapidly growing share of national health expenditure. For example, in 2018, the average annual healthcare spending for Korean adults was approximately KRW 1.53 million, whereas for those aged 65 and older, it was KRW 4.57 million. Older adults often face challenges such as reduced digital access, cognitive decline, and high trust in unverified information, which can lead to less rational healthcare use and lower treatment adherence.31,32 Considering Korea’s rapidly aging population, improving HL in older adults may offer substantial potential for optimizing outpatient utilization and reducing associated costs. HL interventions should prioritize older adults as a target group and adopt communication strategies tailored to their specific needs and accessibility barriers.

Socioeconomic and demographic factors also shape healthcare utilization. Female and individuals had private health insurance tended to use more outpatient services, whereas higher income among middle-aged adults was linked to lower utilization. These patterns suggest that access to and use of care are not determined solely by medical needs but are influenced by social status, financial protection, and opportunity costs. In contrast, chronic disease burden was consistently associated with greater utilization and expenditure, highlighting older adults with chronic conditions as a critical target group for policies aimed at ensuring appropriate care and controlling costs.

Enhancing HL in Korea requires a paradigm shift in clinical practice toward shared decision-making (SDM). Many health systems worldwide have embraced SDM as an extension of people-centered care, and national initiatives, such as the Choosing Wisely campaign, illustrate how this approach has been institutionalized. Within the SDM framework, physicians are expected to provide patients with accurate and comprehensive information on diagnostic and therapeutic options, while patients assume an active role in deliberating and selecting the course of care that is most aligned with their needs and preferences. Empirical evidence demonstrates that SDM not only strengthens HL but also mitigates unnecessary healthcare utilization.33,34 Although similar efforts have been initiated at the level of professional societies in Korea, a broader and sustained impact will require proactive governmental engagement to increase public awareness, provide institutional and financial support, and embed SDM more systematically within the healthcare delivery system.

At the same time, these demand-side efforts must be accompanied by parallel reforms on the supply side, particularly in the payment system. Korea’s current fee-for-service model reimburses care on a per-visit and per-service basis, thereby creating strong incentives for higher service volume while limiting the time available for meaningful patient–provider communication. To overcome these structural constraints, payment schemes should shift away from rewarding service volumes and instead incorporate measures such as consultation time and quality of interaction. Ultimately, a transition to value-based payment models, in which savings generated from improved patient outcomes are shared with providers, will be required.

Older adults face particular challenges in improving their HL owing to their low digital literacy. For these groups, decision-support tools that use voice guidance or conversational interfaces can provide effective alternatives. The NHS 111 service in the United Kingdom exemplifies this model, directing 85% of users to appropriate care at first contact.35 As such platforms become integrated with artificial intelligence, they hold promise for further enhancing HL and promoting more efficient healthcare utilization among older populations.36

This study has some limitations, including its cross-sectional design and the limited generalizability inherent in the survey data. Nonetheless, it leveraged a timely and comprehensive national dataset to address important but understudied issues in the Korean healthcare system.

Consistent with findings from other countries, this study provides empirical evidence that higher HL is associated with reduced outpatient healthcare expenditure in South Korea. Given the country’s persistently rising healthcare costs and rapidly aging population, the observed association, which was particularly significant among older adults, offers meaningful policy implications. As South Korea continues to face challenges related to healthcare sustainability, the role of HL as a modifiable factor in promoting more efficient healthcare utilization has become increasingly important. Moreover, considering that the Korean government has identified the improvement of adult HL as a national policy objective, our findings suggest that such initiatives may contribute not only to better individual health outcomes, but also to mitigating outpatient costs. Targeted interventions to enhance HL, especially among older adults, could serve as a practical and effective strategy in Korea’s evolving healthcare landscape.

Multi-omics analysis identified SPRR2D as a potential biomarker for tumor prognosis and immune microenvironment infiltration: a pan-cancer perspective
Authors: Yuan-Xiang Shi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundClarification of the molecular mechanism of malignant tumor progression, identification of the key signaling pathways and molecules involved in the processes of invasion and metastasis, and identification of new targets and strategies for effective tumor treatment are extremely important for scientific research and clinical application prospects.MethodsBased on large-sample data mining, we first evaluated the expression and mutation profiles of SPRR family genes across cancers and then focused on the molecular functions of SPRR2D across cancers.ResultsMulti-omics experiments revealed that SPRR2D is significantly overexpressed in various tumors, especially in LUSC. ROC curve analysis revealed that SPRR2D demonstrated significant diagnostic efficacy across cancers. Cox regression analysis revealed that the expression of SPRR2D was associated with the survival time of patients with various tumors. Moreover, the expression of SPRR2D is closely related to tumor immune infiltration. GDSC data analysis revealed that the expression levels of SPRR1A, SPRR1B, SPRR2A, SPRR3, and SPRR2D are negatively correlated with the sensitivity to gefitinib, trametinib, bosutinib, afatinib, lapatinib, and erlotinib.ConclusionsFrom a multi-omics perspective, it was revealed that SPRR2D plays a significant role in regulating tumorigenesis and drug sensitivity in tumors.Keywords: Lung cancer, SPRR2D, pan-cancer, tumor microenvironment, drug sensitivity

Introduction

In recent years, an increasing number of studies have investigated the small proline-rich protein (SPRR) family. The proline-rich structural features of SPRRs make them widely involved in biological processes such as cell differentiation, stress response, and barrier function regulation [1,2]; however, its expression pattern, molecular mechanism and clinical significance in tumors still need to be explored in depth. SPRR3 is significantly highly expressed in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and affects patient prognosis by regulating immune-infiltrating cells to interact with the tumor microenvironment [3]. Multi-omics analysis revealed that high expression of SPRR3 was significantly associated with poor survival in LUAD patients, and its ability to predict cancer by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis suggested its potential as a novel biomarker. In addition, SPRR2A, another member of the SPRR family, selectively kills gram-positive bacteria through membrane permeation in intestinal infections and protects intestinal barrier function in response to parasitic infections through the IL-4/STAT6 pathway [4]. This dual role implies that SPRR proteins may play a complex role in tumor immune escape or inflammation-associated carcinogenesis. Further studies have shown that dysregulation of SPRR expression may be associated with tumor cell proliferation, apoptosis resistance, and metastasis [5–7]. For instance, in breast cancer and melanoma, aberrant methylation or copy number variations in SPRR family genes may contribute to tumor progression through epigenetic mechanisms. Although studies have revealed the function of SPRR in specific cancer types, its molecular mechanism is still controversial, and its pro- or antitumorigenic effects on immunomodulation may be tissue specific.

Studies of tumor-related SPRR family proteins not only provide new perspectives for understanding cancer biology but also lay the molecular foundation for innovations in diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. In the current study, we first focused on the expression and mutational profiles of SPRRs across cancers and then focused on the molecular functions of SPRR2D across cancers. Multi-omics experiments revealed that SPRR2D was significantly overexpressed in a variety of tumors, especially in LUSC. In the future, it is necessary to further integrate multi-omics data, functional experiments and clinical verification to comprehensively analyze the multidimensional regulatory network of SPRRs in tumors.

Materials and methods

The TCGA and GTEx databases were used to detect the expression levels of SPRRs across cancers. Single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptomics have been used to determine the expression of SPRR2D in different cell types and the dominant cell characteristics at different locations [8,9]. The differences in the mutation frequency of SPRR2D across cancers were plotted through the cBioPortal database. The TIMER 2.0 database was used to analyze the association between SPRR2D expression and tumor immune cell infiltration [10].

In this study, univariate Cox regression analysis based on TCGA and GEO data was used to calculate the hazard ratio (HR) and its 95% confidence interval (CI) corresponding to each gene expression level to evaluate the association between gene expression levels and the risk of event occurrence. This study employed the Kaplan-Meier survival analysis method, utilizing the survival package in R language for data analysis. First, the optimal cutoff value for the target gene expression level was determined using Survminer to distinguish between high-expression and low-expression groups, ensuring that the sample size ratio in each group was no less than 0.3. Subsequently, the log-rank test was performed using the Survfit function.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed using the pROC package in R. The area under the curve (AUC) and its 95% confidence interval (95% CI) were calculated, and the ROC curve was plotted to evaluate the diagnostic performance of the target gene in differentiating the tumor group from the normal group.

Difference analysis was performed using the limma package to obtain the log2FC of each gene, and all genes were sorted according to log2FC. GSEA enrichment analysis was conducted using the GSEA function in the clusterProfiler package, which is based on the hallmark gene set and the KEGG metabolic gene set [11].

The GDSC database was used to analyze the effect of SPRR expression on sensitivity to antitumor drugs [12]. The similarity scores of 1288 compounds were obtained by comparing gene-related features with cMAP gene features through the Cmap database using the optimal feature matching method XSum (eXtreme Sum), and compounds with lower scores have the potential to inhibit gene-mediated cancer-promoting effects.

The human lung cancer cell lines A549, NCI-H292, SK-MES-1, NCI-H520, and the human normal lung epithelial cell line BEAS-2B were cultured at 37 °C in 5% CO2. The lung cancer cell lines were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), and BEAS-2B cells were cultured in DMEM containing 10% FBS. Total RNA was extracted with TRIzol® reagent (Invitrogen; Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc.). Reverse transcription was performed using the PrimeScript™ RT reagent kit (Takara Bio, Inc.), and RT-qPCR was performed using the SYBR Green PCR kit (Takara Bio, Inc.), both according to the protocols provided by the manufacturer. The primer sequences used were as follows: SPRR2D forward, 5′‑AGAACCTGGTGAGTATCCTGAGA‑3′ and reverse, 5′‑GGAAGGTGTCACAGGAGGATATT‑3′. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Expression and mutational mapping of the SPRR family across cancers

We detected the expression levels of the SPRR family genes (SPRR1A, SPRR1B, SPRR2A, SPRR2B, SPRR2C, SPRR2D, SPRR2E, SPRR2F, SPRR2G, SPRR3, and SPRR4) across cancers. We found that SPRRs were highly expressed in LUSC and UCEC (P < 0.05, and log2FC > 1) but were expressed at lower levels in KIRP (Figure 1A). Mutation analysis revealed that SPRRs were mainly amplification mutations in most tumors, such as BRCA, CESC, CHOL, LIHC, LUAD, LUSC, OV, SKCM, and UCS, whereas deletion mutations were predominant in KICH (Figure 1B). Correlation analysis revealed that the mRNA expression of SPRRs was significantly positively correlated with mutations in THYM and THCA but was significantly negatively correlated with mutations in LUAD and LUSC (Figure 1C). We initially explored the expression and mutation of SPRRs across cancers. We subsequently focused on the expression characteristics and molecular functions of SPRR2D across cancers.

Multi-omics identification of the expression of SPRR2D across cancers

First, the expression level of SPRR2D across cancers was detected in the TCGA and GTEx databases. SPRR2D was significantly highly expressed in BLCA, CESC, LUAD and LUSC but significantly expressed at low levels in KICH, KIRC and KIRP (Figure 2A,B). We subsequently further examined the expression landscape of SPRR2D in tumor cell lines based on data from the HPA database. SPRR2D is highly expressed in cell lines of head and neck tumors, skin tumors, pancreatic cancer, colon cancer, esophageal cancer, bladder cancer and lung cancer (Figure 2C). In addition, we measured the mRNA expression of SPRR2D in lung cancer cell lines. Compared with BEAS-2B, the expression levels of SPRR2D in lung cancer cells A549, NCI-H292, SK-MES-1, and NCI-H520 were significantly increased (Figure 2D). Single-cell sequencing revealed that SPRR2D was expressed mainly in malignant tumor cells (Figure 2E). Spatial transcriptome sequencing analysis revealed the dominant cell characteristics at different points and the composition of the microenvironment at single-cell resolution (Figure 2F). The expression level of SPRR2D was significantly positively correlated with the spot content of tumor cells (Figure 2G). The results of these experiments revealed from a multi-omics perspective that SPRR2D is highly expressed in tumors and plays a significant role in the occurrence and development of tumors.

The diagnostic value of SPRR2D across cancers

ROC curve analysis revealed that SPRR2D demonstrated significant diagnostic efficacy across cancers. The AUC values were relatively high in both the TCGA dataset and the TCGA-GTEx dataset (Figure 3A). In the TCGA database, SPRR2D has excellent diagnostic value in LUSC (AUC = 0.898, 95% CI: 0.866–0.927), KIRP (AUC = 0.827, 95% CI: 0.724–0.901), COAD (AUC = 0.790, 95% CI: 0.748–0.829), CESC (AUC = 0.847, 95% CI: 0.713–0.944), KICH (AUC = 0.802, 95% CI: 0.696–0.902), and READ (AUC = 0.765, 95% CI: 0.676–0.840) (Figure 3B). In the TCGA-GTEx dataset, SPRR2D also has good diagnostic performance (Figure 3C). These results indicate that SPRR2D can efficiently distinguish tumor tissues from normal tissues, suggesting that it has the potential to serve as a cancer diagnostic biomarker.

Prognostic value of SPRR2D across cancers

Univariate Cox survival analyses were performed using TCGA and GEO data. Overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), progression-free survival (PFS), and disease-free survival (DFS) were used as prognostic indicators. For univariate Cox survival analysis, the hazard ratio (HR) and its 95% CI corresponding to each gene expression level were calculated to assess the association between gene expression levels and the risk of event occurrence. If the HR is greater than 1, this indicates that this factor increases the risk of the event occurring. If the HR is less than 1, this indicates that this factor reduces the risk of the event occurring. Cox regression analysis of data from the TCGA database revealed that SPRR2D expression was associated with survival in patients with various tumors (Figure S1). Cox regression analysis based on the GEO database revealed that SPRR2D served as a prognostic risk factor in ACC, BLCA, BRCA, CRC, DLBC, LIHC, LUAD, PAAD, and PRAD and served as a prognostic protective factor in HNSC and LGG (Figure S2). These data initially suggest that SPRR2D has good early diagnostic and prognostic predictive value across cancers.

Mutation map of SPRR2D across cancers

SPRR2D expression and mutation analysis of classic tumor-related signaling pathways revealed that the mutation frequency of the TP53 signaling pathway was the highest, especially in LUSC, OV and HNSC. The mutation frequencies of the WNT signaling pathway and the Hippo signaling pathway across cancers are very low (Figure 4A). Among the classical tumor pathways, the mutation frequencies from high to low are those of TP53 (64%), PIK3CA (23%), KRAS (13%), BRAF (13%), NOTCH1 (7%), and EGFR (7%), among which missense mutations are the most common (Figure 4B). The radar plot shows the Spearman correlation between the scores of different gene sets and the expression of SPRR2D across cancers (Figure 4C). In SARC, BRCA, LIHC, LUAD, and ACC, the expression level of SPRR2D was significantly correlated with the gene set score.

SPRR2D and the tumor immune microenvironment

The TIMER 2.0 database was used to analyze the association between SPRR2D expression and tumor immune cell infiltration. Spearman correlation analysis was used to analyze the correlation between SPRR2D expression and immune infiltration levels of immune cells such as B cells, CAFs, CD8+ T cells, CD4+ T cells, endothelial cells, eosinophils, macrophages, mast cells, monocytes, myeloid dendritic cells, neutrophils, and NK cells across cancers (Figure 5). We subsequently analyzed the association between SPRR2D expression and the expression levels of immune genes (immunostimulatory factors, immunosuppressive factors, chemokines, chemokine receptors, and MHC) across cancers (Figure S3). The results revealed that the expression of SPRR2D in tumors such as THCA and LUAD was positively correlated with the expression level of immune genes. We used Spearman analysis to evaluate the correlation between SPRR2D gene expression and immune-related characteristics. The heatmap results revealed that the expression of a large number of immune-related genes was strongly correlated with the expression of the SPRR2D gene, suggesting that SPRR2D may play an important role in immune regulation (Figure S4). Tumor immunity plays important roles in tumor occurrence and treatment efficacy. New immune-related therapeutic targets should be identified to provide new experimental evidence for the effective treatment of tumors.

GSEA enrichment analysis

To explore the potential molecular mechanism through which SPRR2D regulates tumorigenesis and development, enrichment analysis of the hallmark gene set and KEGG metabolic gene set was conducted across cancers (Figure S5). Among various tumors, the pathways enriched when SPRR2D is highly expressed include TNF-α signaling via NF-kB, the inflammatory response, hypoxia, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and apoptosis. This correlation is more significant in the hallmark gene set.

SPRR2D and the drug sensitivity to antitumor drugs

The GDSC database was used to analyze the effect of SPRR expression on sensitivity to antitumor drugs (Figure S6A). GDSC data analysis revealed that the expression levels of SPRR1A, SPRR1B, SPRR2A, SPRR3, and SPRR2D were significantly negatively correlated with the sensitivity to gefitinib, trametinib, bosutinib, afatinib, lapatinib, and erlotinib (r < 0 and FDR < 0.05) (Figure S6A). Analysis of small-molecule drugs that may reverse the biological effects caused by SPRR2D dysregulation across cancers was conducted based on the Connectivity Map (CMap) gene expression database (Figure S6B). In some tumors, clofibrate and exisulind may reverse the molecular characteristics caused by the dysregulation of SPRR2D expression, thereby counteracting the procancer effect mediated by SPRR2D. This hypothesis warrants further validation using rigorous molecular experiments.

Discussion

Tumors seriously endanger human health and are a prominent public health issue [13–16]. The tumor immune microenvironment (TIME), as the core regulatory network of tumor progression, is composed of multidimensional components such as immune cells, stromal cells and cytokines [17–19]. Its heterogeneity and immunosuppressive characteristics are the main bottlenecks restricting the efficacy of current immunotherapy. In recent years, research has focused on the spatiotemporal dynamic regulatory mechanism of the TIME and targeted intervention strategies, resulting in multiple breakthroughs. In terms of therapeutic strategies, multimodal combination therapy has become mainstream: gene editing technology that involves the silencing of multiple immune checkpoint genes, such as PD-L1 and CD47, significantly enhances T-cell infiltration. Multitarget chimeras achieve a 75% response rate in solid tumor models by simultaneously activating T cells, DCs and natural killer (NK) cells. These target the degradation of key proteins or regulate the metabolic microenvironment. In addition, in view of the “cold tumor” characteristics of the TIME, the combination of epigenetic modifiers or antiangiogenic drugs can increase the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors. Tumor immunotherapy has also been a hot topic in tumor research in recent years.

The role of the SPRR2D gene, a member of the SPRR family, in skin barrier function and immune regulation has attracted attention. Research has shown that SPRR2D plays a prominent role in inflammatory skin diseases such as psoriasis. Gene families such as SPRR2D, SPRR2A, and SPRR2B are involved in the immune disorder mechanism of psoriasis [20,21]. Through transcriptomic and single-cell omics analyses, it was confirmed that these genes are significantly highly expressed in both vulgaris and pustular psoriasis and are positively correlated with the infiltration of immune cells such as CD8+ T cells and T follicular helper cells. It may exacerbate the inflammatory response by activating the NLR signaling pathway. The molecular function and mechanism of SPRR2D in tumors remain unclear. This study integrates multi-omics data to initially clarify the expression and mutation profile of SPRR2D across cancers, its prognostic predictive value and early diagnostic value, its regulatory role in the tumor immune microenvironment, and its impact on antitumor drug sensitivity. High expression of SPRR2D increases downstream TNF-α signaling via NF-κB, the inflammatory response, hypoxia, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and apoptosis, all of which are closely related to tumorigenesis. This study provides experimental insights for further exploration of molecular mechanisms involved.

Across cancers, there was a significant correlation between the expression level of SPRR2D and the immune infiltration level of immune cells such as B cells, CAFs, CD8+ T cells, CD4+ T cells, endothelial cells, eosinophils, macrophages, mast cells, monocytes, myeloid dendritic cells, neutrophils, and natural killer (NK) cells. Moreover, SPRR2D expression is correlated with the expression levels of immune genes (immune-stimulating genes, immune-suppressing genes, chemokines, chemokine receptors, and MHC) across cancers. The expression of SPRR2D in tumors such as THCA and LUAD is positively correlated with the expression level of immune genes. The expression of a large number of immune-related features is strongly correlated with the expression of the SPRR2D gene, indicating that SPRR2D may play an important role in immune regulation. These findings suggest that SPRR2D can serve as a potential target for tumor immunotherapy. This study has several limitations. First, the findings are derived from multi-omics data mining of public databases and have not been validated through rigorous cellular experiments. To address this, future work will include both in vivo and in vitro studies to confirm the molecular functions and regulatory mechanisms of SPRR2D in tumors.

Algorithm for Temporomandibular Disorders With Osteopathic Manipulative Therapy: An Expert Consensus
Authors: Llanos De-La-Iglesia, Cristina Bravo, Cristian Justribó-Manion, Anna Montmany, Toni Roman-Arias, Isabelle Hue, P-M Dugailly, Stefano Colasanto, Philip Van-Caille, Francesc Rubí-Carnacea
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThe purpose of this study was to create a consensus algorithm for the osteopathic approach to temporomandibular dysfunctions.MethodsA conventional Delphi method of at least 3 rounds of questionnaires was carried out by a panel of experts to reach a consensus on a flowchart algorithm for the diagnosis and treatment of temporomandibular disorders with osteopathic manipulative therapy.ResultsDuring the 3 rounds, a total of 7 panellists participated. Consensus was reached on 182 items (70.54% = 182/258). The algorithm has a numerical code with which the direction of each option can be followed, and a color code that shows the classification of each of the items (anamnesis, examination, observation, referral, palpation and treatment). The algorithm begins with 4 main categories: psychological aspects, exclusion criteria, physical aspects, and other issues, which are further subdivided into specific items that will be addressed according to the findings obtained during the patient visit.ConclusionThis study created an algorithm for the osteopathic approach to temporomandibular dysfunctions.

Introduction

Temporomandibular disorders (TMDs) encompass a group of musculoskeletal and neuromuscular conditions that affect the temporomandibular joints (TMJs), the masticatory muscles, and all associated tissues. They have been identified as a major cause of nondental pain in the orofacial region.1 TMDs can lead to pain in the TMJ, restricted jaw movement and joint noises such as crepitus. Conservative methods, such as manual therapy, are the first-line treatments, yielding positive results in most patients2 and showing a promising effect on dysfunction.3 Currently, there is a high prevalence of TMDs in the general population (31.9%),4 often related to parafunctions such as nail biting or gum chewing. However, they are also associated with emotional factors such as anxiety or personality traits.5 The aetiology of these dysfunctions has been found to be multidimensional. Within TMJ disorders, biomechanical, neuromuscular, biopsychosocial and/or biological factors may be involved.6

Within manual therapies, osteopathy uses manipulative therapy on joints, muscles, and organs to exert an effect on the tissue, which allows the treatment of various conditions, including pain.7 Practising osteopaths have shown a greater interest in the treatment of TMDs than other disciplines.8

For the generalisability of the results of any study, it is essential that the type, intensity, frequency and duration of treatment are sufficiently described so that the therapy can be reproduced elsewhere.9 This ensures internal validity and avoids implementation bias.10 It is not always feasible to develop a strict treatment protocol. In manual therapy, as in osteopathy, therapeutic interventions must be adapted to the patient.9,10 Furthermore, because the patient frequently presents with a series of physical symptoms that often do not fit a specific clinical condition or an osteopathic model of care with a biomechanical bias, they must be treated with interventions of some complexity.11

In the case of the patient suffering from TMDs, due to multidimensional aetiology, all factors involved in the process must be considered to address the patient’s condition effectively.6 Therefore, and in such cases, it is desirable to work with a treatment algorithm, in which the steps in the treatment pathway depend on the outcome of a previous step.9 This would allow for context-specific adaptation, while maintaining a level of standardisation of the intervention to meet the internal validity requirements of a robust study.12 In cases of chronic patients, and within the field of osteopathic manipulative therapy (OMT), several authors agree that due to the complexity of the patient and clinical reasoning, it is more plausible to use an algorithm than a closed standardised protocol.13,14

There is a general scientific consensus on the importance of a sound theoretical framework for complex health interventions such as osteopathic care.11 It has been proven that individualised treatment is more effective than a rigid protocol.15,16 Some protocols already exist in the form of diagnostic and treatment algorithms in manual therapy17,18; however, they are evidence-based protocols, but not agreed upon by experts.

In other health fields such as medicine, there are already diagnostic and treatment algorithms with expert consensus, as in the case of the medical approach to the rotator cuff,19 or medical approaches to low back pain, which take physical therapy into consideration but in a nonspecific manner.20

In the case of TMDs and their management, there are several evidence-based clinical guidelines in the field of medicine or dentistry,21, 22, 23, 24 none of which have been evaluated by an expert committee. In manual therapy, Fernández-de-las-Peñas and Von-piekartz18 propose an algorithm in the form of a matrix chart based on a review of the literature. Von-piekartz et al conducted a consensus on diagnostic tools for TMDs.25 At present, there are still no clinical guidelines in the form of a flowchart that are agreed upon by experts for the management of TMDs with OMT.

The proportion of clinical guidelines developed using the Delphi method has increased since 2011.26 It is a method that provides reports with a very high quality.27 However, 75% of all existing guidelines have not used this method.26,28 An insufficient number of clinical guidelines have been developed using the Delphi method, which is highly recommended for this purpose.26 The aim of this research is to establish a consensus with experts in the osteopathic management of TMDs and to generate an evidence-based protocol in an algorithm or flow chart for this purpose.

Material and Methods

Overview

An investigation was carried out using the Delphi method,29 to design a flowchart algorithm for the OMT approach to TMDs.

For this purpose, 3 rounds of questionnaires were conducted and sent to a panel of experts via e-mail for development and validation by consensus. The experts were supported by anonymity as their identities were unknown to each other.30 This made it possible to reach consensus without coercion or manipulation and reduced the effect of potentially more dominant individuals.19 The control of communication was in the hands of the coordinating group who, through communication technologies, controlled the flow of information and provided feedback to reach the final result. Communicated with each of the experts via e-mail every 2 weeks, time given to answer the questionnaires, to analyze the results and to approve them and the final result.

Ethics

This project was reviewed by the ethics committee of the Hospital Universitari Arnau de Vilanova de Lleida and was deeded exempt, given that the study was not a biomedical project.

Survey Development

For the first round design, we conducted a search through existing literature on the diagnosis and management of TMDs, including all diagnostic types related to the osteopathic field, which was carried out in PubMed, CINAHL Plus, Scopus, Cochrane Library, and PEDro. The different search strategies presented the following keywords: "temporomandibular joint", "dysfunction", "disorder", "jaw", "orofacial", "craniomandibular", "manual therapy", "osteopath*" "musculoskeletal manipulations." The search was limited to the last 5 years and to human research. Once the information was collected, a first questionnaire was developed for the first round, focused on the creation of a protocol for approaching TMDs using OMT in the form of an algorithm/flowchart. This consisted of mostly closed questions with dichotomous answers and one open question. In all questions there was the option to provide input. Subsequently, a group of experts was selected to give their answers or opinions in this and the following rounds in order to reach a consensus.

Delphi Panel

The expert panel consisted of 7 osteopaths from different countries, who met the following selection criteria: be osteopaths trained according to the European standard,31 be postgraduate teachers specialized in the orofacial region in schools recognized by the registry of the country in which they are practicing, and/or have conducted research in the field of orofacial pain or DTMs. Participants were recruited and informed by e-mail. First of all, the objective of the study was explained to them, and if they consented to participate, they signed an informed consent and a conflict-of-interest document. Participants were kept anonymous during all rounds to ensure that they could express themselves freely throughout the entire process.

Delphi Survey

Round 1

In the first round the panel of experts received a questionnaire created for the formation of an algorithm in the form of a flow chart with different approaches in diagnostic tests, approaches with OMT, other noncontact approaches found in the literature. The questions were closed dichotomous questions, except for one question which was open-ended. However, they were given the opportunity to add other options they considered necessary and to complete the information at their discretion. In this case, they were asked to attach references to the new contributions. The proposals were reviewed and entered into a feedback process. Consensus was considered to have been reached for those responses that were 70% or more in agreement.27

Round 2

In the second round, participants revised the algorithm by answering a second questionnaire consisting of dichotomous questions, which were made up of the items on which consensus had not been reached and the items obtained from their contributions. These revisions were sent to the coordinating group, which carried out an analysis of all the responses received.

Round 3

In the third round, the participants, considering the results of the analysis of the second round, in which they could see their individual response and the collective response, re-evaluated the approaches using the same type of questions that were used previously. The answers were sent back for analysis and feedback

Final phase

Once consensus was reached, the final result was forwarded to the panel of experts for their approval.

The duration of the entire Delphi process was 5 months.

Data Analysis

The IBM SPSS 27.0 statistics software was used for the analysis of the results. We analysed demographic data by using mean values and standard deviations (SD). We also determined the frequency of responses for each item, which we compared by using simple percentages.

Validity and reliability

The description of the Delphi method was developed with guidance on conducting and reporting Delphi studies (CREDES) best practices.29 The Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research32 were also considered to report it.

Delphi Rounds

Figure 1 presents a diagram of the Delphi process and its rounds. During the whole process there were no withdrawals from the experts. In the first round, there was a consensus exceeding 70% on 95 items out of 118 (80.5% = 95/118); there were also 62 contributions from the experts which resulted in the formulation of new questions for the next round. In the second round, where the questions that did not reach consensus and the new contributions were raised, 69 of the 104 questioned items had a consensus of more than 70% (66.3% = 69/104). The third round, which consisted of the issues on which no consensus was reached in the first questionnaire, had a consensus on 18 of the 36 items (50% = 18/36). The items on which consensus was not reached were discarded from the algorithm. In total, during the 3 rounds, 182 questions were asked, 258 of which obtained a consensus greater than 70% (70.54% = 182/258).Figure 1Flowchart of Delphi method for the application of questionnaires to osteopaths specialized in TMJ manual therapy.Figure 1

Final Phase

Once all the rounds were completed, the definitive links that would relate all the items could be made and the definitive algorithm could be generated. During the final phase, all experts gave their approval to the algorithm result. See the Supplementary file for the responses and rounds of consensus.

Algorithm

The final algorithm was developed with all the items for which a consensus of more than 70% was reached. The algorithm in the form of a flow chart (see Supplementary File) has a numerical code with which the direction of each option can be followed, and a colour code that shows the classification of each of the items (anamnesis, examination, observation, referral, palpation and treatment). See the supplementary file for the steps followed and the sections of the algorithm.

The diagram starts with 4 main items: psychological aspects, exclusion criteria, physical aspects, and other issues, which are subdivided into other items that will be followed according to the findings found during the visit with the patient. Depending on the part of the consultation, the item will change colour depending on whether it is part of the anamnesis (yellow), examination (blue-violet), observation (pink), referral (red), palpation (light blue) and treatment (turquoise). This type of design has been previously used in fields such as medicine,33, 34, 35 being a novelty its use in OMT.

Discussion

This study developed an algorithm for the approach with OMT in TMDs. (see supplementary file). TMDs are challenging to treat due to their complexity and multidimensionality.6 However, there are now different clinical guidelines in various disciplines that include manual therapy.21,22,36 For example, the American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons includes manual therapy and physical therapy in their protocol for TMDs.36 The German Headache Society published the multidisciplinary clinical guideline "Chronic Orofacial Pain."22 In the field of dentistry, there are also clinical guidelines that include physical and manual therapy.21,24 Only one of these guidelines summarizes the conservative treatment options in a flowchart, which includes manual therapy.24

Regarding the approaches to manual therapy for TMDs, Fernández de las Peñas and Piekartz18 conducted a literature review on different approaches and proposed a simpler matrix chart for the physiotherapeutic management of TMDs. However, as an independent discipline, no protocols or clinical guidelines for the OMT management of TMDs have been found. Currently, there are no evidence-based clinical guidelines or validated osteopathic approaches with expert groups. Validated clinical guidelines with expert committees have only been found in the field of medicine and other types of dysfunctions or pathologies.19,20 For instance, Eubank et al19 used a modified Delphi method to establish consensus in the management of patients with rotator cuff pathology, and Havran et al20 utilized an expert committee to develop recommendations for the management of chronic low back pain in older adults. Therefore, this proposed OMT approach to TMDs treatment will be highly relevant in both clinical and research settings.

Both this type of decision-making algorithms and the protocols or clinical practice guidelines are tools that strengthen the clinical method; they standardize therapies, facilitate decision-making and the correct management of the patient.37 It has also been demonstrated that they offer a low risk of error in diagnosis, which makes them a reliable tool38; they are reliable, effective tools and provide high classification accuracy.39 Therefore, they are an instrument of great value for both the therapist and the patient.

In a systematic review by Diamond et al,27 the authors noted that consensus definitions vary widely and are not adequately communicated; however, our consensus result coincides with the average threshold of consensus definition in this methodology. The consensus obtained in the first round was quite high and declined as the rounds progressed, largely because of new contributions made by each of the panellists. In spite of this, the number of rounds needed to reach consensus was 3, which is within what is expected according to the results obtained in the review by Niederberger and Spranger,40, 41 who observed that for the most part, classic Delphi studies mostly invest between 2 and 3 rounds.

Limitations

We observed that the management of TMDs by the experts, despite all working in the field of osteopathy, was quite different. This led to numerous contributions and more complex results than expected. However, this led to the algorithm's outcome being much richer than it would have been by using only existing literature. Additionally, although we reached a reasonable number of experts without any dropouts, the participation was lower than anticipated. Lastly, it is worth noting that there is no universally accepted methodological guideline for applying the Delphi method in a more standardized manner. Spranger et al41 observed great variability in the methodology followed in different Delphi studies in the health sciences. However, in this study we have used the classic version, which is currently the most widely used.40

Conclusions

After Delphi rounds, consensus was reached on an algorithm for approaching TMDs.

Introducing an Innovative Tool for Enhanced Precision in Strabismus Surgery: The Suh Botulinum Toxin Forceps
Authors: Josephine Lu, Shaden H Yassin, Kimia Rezaei, John D Hong, E-Jine Tsai, Tara Mossadeghian, Yan Kefalov, Kourosh Shahraki, Mateen Amin, Donny W Suh
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

We would like to report our experience using the newly designed Suh botulinum toxin (BTX) forceps for strabismus procedures. BTX injection has gained popularity as a minimally invasive, effective treatment for various forms of strabismus [1]. However, traditional tools such as the Mendonca BTX forceps have limitations in precision, stability, and ease of use. The Suh BTX forceps were developed to address these shortcomings by enhancing control, minimizing complications, and improving ergonomics for surgeons. We hope that the Suh BTX forceps will offer a significant advancement in improving extraocular muscle manipulation during strabismus treatment with BTX injections.

The Suh BTX forceps measure 116 ± 0.5 mm in length and incorporate several distinct design features. At the distal end, ultrathin 0.7-mm claws provide a secure grip on extraocular muscles within narrow anatomical spaces (Fig. 1A, 1B). When viewed anteriorly, inferiorly, and from a side view, the forceps feature a slim, streamlined design with multiple teeth on its distal claws, allowing for better handling within tight anatomical spaces, ensuring a secure grip on the muscle, improving visibility, and preventing BTX from leaking onto surrounding tissue. Additionally, the forceps include a 10° angled handle positioned 68 mm from the rear end, allowing ergonomic manipulation around the nasal bridge and reducing hand strain. The horizontal bar of the Suh BTX forceps is 10 mm long, which is longer than the Mendonca forceps, whose bar measures just 1 mm. The longer bar improves precision and maneuverability. Additionally, the Suh BTX forceps has a locking mechanism positioned 42.0 mm from the rear end, which helps secure the forceps in a closed position. This feature aids in precise muscle localization and reduces the risk of accidental slippage or perforation.

Types of botulinum toxin (BTX) forceps. (A) The Suh BTX forceps features 0.7-mm claws at the distal end. (B) The Mendonca BTX forceps.

To evaluate its clinical performance, we conducted a pilot study involving 20 patients undergoing BTX injection for strabismus using the Suh BTX forceps. Surgeons reported improved comfort, stability, and visibility compared to previous tools. The forceps allowed for smoother extraocular muscle manipulation, resulting in better control during injection. No cases of ptosis, unintended toxin diffusion, or muscle perforation occurred. The average procedure time was reduced by 20% (4.0 minutes vs. 5.0 minutes, p < 0.05) compared to historical cases using the Mendonca forceps [2]. These improvements have meaningful clinical implications, as BTX injections are often performed under time constraints and require delicate handling to avoid complications.

The unique ergonomic design also plays a significant role in enhancing surgical workflow. The angled handle and locking mechanism reduce the need for constant hand adjustments, which is particularly advantageous in complex or prolonged cases. These features improve hand positioning and reduce surgeon fatigue, allowing for better consistency and accuracy throughout the procedure. In addition, the locking mechanism creates a tunnel-like architecture between the claws, facilitating precise needle placement while reducing the risk of toxin leakage.

Surgeons previously accustomed to the Mendonca BTX forceps required minimal training to adapt to the Suh design, generally achieving full proficiency after two cases. Although further validation in larger, multicenter studies is warranted, the current findings suggest that the Suh BTX forceps may set a new standard in BTX delivery for strabismus. In terms of cost and availability, the forceps are estimated to be comparable in price to traditional tools and are now commercially accessible worldwide.

In summary, the Suh BTX forceps offer a valuable improvement in the delivery of BTX injections for strabismus. By combining enhanced precision, safety, and ergonomic design, these forceps improve surgical efficiency and patient outcomes. We believe they represent a meaningful advancement in ophthalmic instrumentation for extraocular muscle procedures.

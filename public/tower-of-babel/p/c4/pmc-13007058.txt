AbstractIntroductionSarcoidosis frequently involves intrathoracic lymph nodes and lung parenchyma and requires histological confirmation for diagnosis. Although endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) is recommended for obtaining mediastinal samples, its diagnostic yield frequently necessitates additional transbronchial lung biopsy (TBLB) and/or endobronchial biopsy (EBB). EBUS transbronchial mediastinal cryobiopsy (EBUS-TBMC) can yield larger, well-preserved tissue samples than EBUS-TBNA alone. However, whether EBUS-TBMC alone is equivalent to conventional multimodal sampling (EBUS-TBNA combined with TBLB and/or EBB), and whether TBLB and EBB provide incremental diagnostic value when added to EBUS-TBMC, is still unclear.Methods and analysisIn this study, 410 patients with suspected stage I/II sarcoidosis will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the EBUS-TBMC or EBUS-TBNA group. The diagnostic sensitivity, negative predictive value and procedure-related complications will be selected for secondary outcomes. Introduction

Sarcoidosis is a multisystem granulomatous disorder with unknown aetiology, commonly affecting the mediastinal and hilar lymph nodes and the pulmonary parenchyma.1 Intrathoracic involvement is observed in more than 90% of affected individuals.2,6 The American Thoracic Society (ATS) and the British Thoracic Society conditionally recommend endobronchial ultrasound-guided transbronchial needle aspiration (EBUS-TBNA) as the initial diagnostic method for mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy, though this recommendation is supported by very low-quality evidence.7 8 The diagnostic yield of EBUS-TBNA can be significantly improved through the incorporation of supplemental sampling techniques, such as transbronchial lung biopsy (TBLB) and/or endobronchial biopsy (EBB). Compared with EBUS-TBNA alone, this multimodal approach can increase the diagnostic sensitivity to 89.7% (p<0.001).9 Nevertheless, the reliance on multiple techniques and the suboptimal sample quality obtained with EBUS-TBNA underscore the need for a more effective primary sampling method.

EBUS-guided transbronchial mediastinal cryobiopsy (EBUS-TBMC) is an innovative method that provides larger and better-preserved tissue samples compared with EBUS-TBNA.10,12 Research on unselected patient cohorts has indicated that EBUS-TBMC demonstrates superior diagnostic sensitivity for sarcoidosis than EBUS-TBNA does, which was primarily attributed to the greater histological quality of cryobiopsy specimens.13 While a concurrent parallel randomised controlled trial (RCT) aims to thoroughly assess the diagnostic performance and safety of lymph node sampling methods such as EBUS-TBNA, EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-transbronchial forceps biopsy in an unselected population,14 indication-specific RCTs are essential to develop precise decision pathways or selection algorithms for different clinical situations. Besides, two key clinical questions remain unresolved in populations suspected of having intrathoracic sarcoidosis: First, can EBUS-TBMC, when performed alone, achieve a diagnostic yield that is comparable to or exceeds that of the conventional multimodal sampling involving EBUS-TBNA plus TBLB and EBB? Second, what is the potential additive value of TBLB and EBB when performed alongside EBUS-TBMC? There is a current lack of direct, prospective comparisons between EBUS-TBMC and the combination of EBUS-TBNA with EBB and/or TBLB.

Therefore, we designed this RCT to evaluate whether EBUS-TBMC alone can demonstrate diagnostic non-inferiority compared with the conventional multimodal sampling in patients with stage I/II intrathoracic sarcoidosis. The findings of this study may improve diagnostic pathways by reducing patient burden and procedural risk while maintaining diagnostic accuracy. This study was registered in Clinicaltrials.gov (ID: NCT07246876; Registration Date: 19 November 2025).

Methods and analysis

Aim

This multicentre RCT aims to compare the granuloma detection rate between EBUS-TBMC and EBUS-TBNA with multimodality sampling, and to assess the additional diagnostic value of EBB and/or TBLB for EBUS-TBMC in intrathoracic sarcoidosis.

Study design

An open-label, randomised controlled design will be employed in this study. The primary endpoint is whether a participant is assigned a final diagnosis of sarcoidosis.

Setting

The DETECTION-SAR-II comprises 20 hospitals (anticipated) from China, Germany and Italy. Each participating site is equipped with necessary EBUS and cryobiopsy devices and techniques.

Sample size calculation

By using a non-inferiority design to compare two diagnostic sensitivities, the sample size will be determined with PASS 2021 software (V.21.0.3). An unpublished multicentre retrospective study conducted by our team (DETECTION-SAR-I) found that combining EBUS-TBNA with TBLB and EBB achieved a diagnostic sensitivity of 98.5% compared with 95.1% for EBUS-TBMC alone. With a non-inferiority margin set at 10%, a one-sided α of 0.025, and a power of 0.9, the analysis suggests that 149 patients with sarcoidosis will be needed per group. Considering the 91% prevalence of sarcoidosis observed in the previous RCT,13 approximately 328 patients must be screened to achieve the necessary sample size. To account for an anticipated 20% dropout rate during follow-up, the final target enrolment is set to 410 subjects to maintain sufficient power for the non-inferiority test. Interventions and procedures

Figure 2 presents a flow chart depicting the progression of participants through the various phases of this trial. Participants who give written informed consent will be randomly assigned to either the EBUS-TBNA or EBUS-TBMC group in a 1:1 ratio, following a comprehensive procedural assessment to confirm their ability to undergo bronchoscopy. An independent investigator will employ an online sealed envelope randomisation tool (https://www.sealeden-velope.com/simple-randomiser/v1/lists) to generate the allocation, using a block size of four. To ensure blinding, the allocation sequence and block sizes will remain hidden from the study investigators and staff. Stratification will be based exclusively on participation centre, disregarding baseline characteristics. The sequence will be securely stored in an electronic file with restricted access to prevent study team access. On completion of the random assignment, participating sites will receive electronic notifications regarding the allocated biopsy method.

Study process flow. EBB, endobronchial biopsy; EBUS, endobronchial ultrasound; TBMC, transbronchial mediastinal cryobiopsy; TBNA, transbronchial needle aspiration; TBLB, transbronchial lung biopsy.

All participants in this study will undergo either EBUS-TBNA or EBUS-TBMC, contingent on their assigned group following the administration of bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL). Participants will subsequently undergo TBLB and EBB. Sedation (either deep or moderate) will be induced in accordance with institutional protocols. Typically, deep sedation will be achieved through the administration of propofol, whereas moderate sedation will involve the use of low-dose midazolam, potentially supplemented with opioids. Experienced bronchoscopists (≥22 EBUS-TBNA cases and ≥15 EBUS-TBMC cases)15 16 will perform standardised operations as below.

BAL: BAL will be performed according to standardised procedures, involving the instillation of 50 mL aliquots repeated three times, totalling 150 mL, with a recovery rate exceeding 60%. The choice of lavage site will follow the recommendations of the ATS guidelines on BAL in interstitial lung disease.17 For participants exhibiting diffuse or micronodular infiltrates, the lavage will be performed in the right middle lobe or lingular segment. In instances of focal parenchymal consolidation identified through imaging, BAL will be targeted to the most affected segment as determined by radiological assessment.

EBUS-TBNA: Following endosonographic inspection, at least two stations of the suspected nodes will be selected for tissue sampling. In total, five nodal samples will be taken per patient; four samples will be sent in for cytology and one for micrology. After the 19 G needle is inserted into the target, the stylet will be pulled out slowly. In case no or limited material is retrieved, suction using a 10 mL syringe during the aspirations will be advised for acquiring the next samples. The aspirates will be processed for both cytological smear and cell block analysis.18 The decision to perform rapid on-set evaluation of specimens depends on the local guidelines. The results of ROSE will not modify the established sampling sequence.

EBUS-TBMC: After identifying the most suspected, prominent and easily accessed node as the target lymph node, either a tunnel will be created using the puncture technique described in previous study,14 or a small incision will be made with a 19 G needle under EBUS monitoring. Afterwards, a cryoprobe will be subsequently inserted into the target lymph node to perform cryobiopsy and obtain a specimen with size 4.1–6.0 mm.19 A total of three samples will be collected in the same lymph node for analysis.10 20

TBLB: TBLB will be performed under fluoroscopic guidance using standard-sized biopsy forceps. Three biopsy specimens will be obtained via TBLB by independent pulmonologists. For patients suspected of stage I sarcoidosis (no radiological findings in the lung field), the TBLB specimens will be taken from the right upper lobe under fluoroscopic guidance. For participants suspected of having evidence of pulmonary parenchymal involvement according to chest radiographs, TBLB specimens will be obtained from the targeted region.21

EBB: Bronchoscopy will be performed routinely for all participants. The appearance of the airway will initially be documented as normal or abnormal. Abnormal airways will be defined as those that are erythematous, demonstrate mucosal thickening or both. The airway appearance will be determined according to the consensus decision between the bronchoscopist and the bronchoscopy technician. For participants with abnormal airways, four specimens will be taken from the airways, and two will be obtained from the main carina. In participants with normal airways, four specimens will be taken from a secondary carina, and two will be taken from the main carina.22

All patients will undergo routine postprocedure chest imaging for pneumothorax detection.

Specimen processing and follow-up

Specimens will be collected in one container, centrifuged to form a pellet, suspended in agar, fixed in neutral-buffered formalin and processed as cell blocks.23 An H&E-stained section will be prepared from each block, with further sections allocated for immunohistochemical staining, flow cytometry and Xpert MTB-RIF Ultra assays according to local protocols. The BALF will be sent for comprehensive analysis, including total and differential cell counts, CD4/CD8 ratio determination and microbiological examination. Participants will undergo clinical and radiological follow-up for a minimum of 6 months.

Standard of reference

A physician, unaware of the biopsy method, will diagnose sarcoidosis based on pathological findings and clinical data, such as microbiologic results, BAL fluid characteristics and serum ACE levels, in accordance with the European Respiratory Society (ERS)/ATS/World Association for Sarcoidosis and Other Granulomatous Disorders (WASOG) consensus statement.7 24 A diagnosis of sarcoidosis is supported by the presence of non-caseating granulomas on histological examination, alongside negative mycobacterial and fungal cultures, and the exclusion of malignancy. If all four sampling methods (EBUS-TBNA, EBUS-TBMC, TBLB and EBB) yield insufficient or negative samples in both groups, a secondary biopsy or a clinical and radiological follow-up of at least 6 months will be conducted, contingent on obtaining additional participant consent. The treating physician’s diagnosis after 6 months will serve as the reference standard for sarcoidosis.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of this study will be the granuloma detection rate, defined as the number of patients with detected granulomas relative to the total number of patients tested.18

The secondary outcomes will include the diagnostic sensitivity, the negative predictive value of each sampling in patients suspected of having stage I/II sarcoidosis, and postprocedural complications. Sensitivity is defined as the proportion of participants with the disease for whom a positive test result was obtained. The negative predictive value is defined as the proportion of patients who have a negative test result among the patients who are classified as sarcoidosis negative.

Statistical analysis

SPSS software (V.24.0) will be used for all statistical analyses. Baseline characteristics will be summarised through descriptive statistics. Categorical variables, including sex and symptom type, will be reported as frequencies and percentages. Comparisons will use the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables, such as age and lesion size, will be expressed as mean±SD or median with IQR based on distribution, and compared using the independent samples t-test or Mann-Whitney U test. The study will analyse the granuloma detection rate between the EBUS-TBMC group and the conventional multimodal sampling group (EBUS-TBNA+TBLB+ EBB) using an independent-sample test to assess non-inferiority. To mitigate potential bias arising from the prevalence of non-granulomatous diseases, a prespecified sensitivity analysis will be performed. This analysis will involve calculating and comparing the granuloma detection rate solely within the subgroup of participants who have received a definitive diagnosis of a granulomatous disease. Non-inferiority will be established if the lower bound of the 95% CI exceeds the −10% margin. Secondary outcomes, such as diagnostic sensitivity, negative predictive value and safety indicators, will be compared between these two independent groups using the χ2 test. To assess the additional diagnostic value of EBB and/or TBLB when combined with EBUS-TBMC, paired-sample tests, specifically the McNemar test, will be employed. This analysis will be performed according to each sampling modality to directly address the secondary aim of the study. Specifically, the granuloma detection rate will be calculated and reported for each individual technique (EBUS-TBMC, EBUS-TBNA, TBLB, EBB) within their respective randomised arms. Additionally, to quantify the incremental yield of supplemental biopsies, we will assess the proportion of patients in whom granulomas are identified exclusively through TBLB and/or EBB following a negative result from the initial EBUS-guided modality (TBMC or TBNA). A hypothetical higher dimensional contingency table contingency table will be constructed by cross-tabulating each biopsy method results against the final reference diagnosis.

Data collection and management

Participant data will be securely stored and consistently maintained for 5 years post-trial using an electronic data capture system. Each participating site will retain the original signed informed consent forms.

Data and safety monitoring committee

The data and safety monitoring committee, independent of the principal investigator and trial, will include five members: an expert bronchoscopist, a pulmonary and critical care physician, a thoracic surgeon, a patient safety chief, and a biostatistician. The committee will convene after every set of 30 transbronchial lymph node biopsies or on a quarterly basis. Should any issues regarding the study’s execution arise, the committee is vested with the authority to recommend the study’s discontinuation.

Discussion

Diagnosing intrathoracic sarcoidosis remains challenging due to its varied clinical manifestations and the need for histopathological confirmation. Although EBUS-TBNA is commonly employed as an initial diagnostic tool for intrathoracic lymphadenopathy, its diagnostic yield frequently necessitates additional sampling methods, such as TBLB and EBB, to achieve sufficient sensitivity.8 Although multimodal sampling enhances diagnostic accuracy, it also adds procedural complexity, increases patient discomfort and elevates associated risks.

EBUS-TBMC provides larger and better-preserved tissue samples in terms of architecture compared with EBUS-TBNA.12 Preliminary evidence indicates that EBUS-TBMC may offer greater diagnostic sensitivity for sarcoidosis than EBUS-TBNA when sampling the same lymph node, primarily due to the superior quality of histological specimens obtained.13 The diagnostic performance of EBUS-TBMC alone compared with the established multimodal sampling method, which includes EBUS-TBNA, TBLB and EBB, remains uncertain. Additionally, the potential additive value of incorporating TBLB and EBB alongside EBUS-TBMC has not been prospectively assessed.

The current RCT aims to fill existing knowledge gaps by directly comparing the granuloma detection rate of EBUS-TBMC alone with that of the conventional multimodal sampling in patients with stage I/II intrathoracic sarcoidosis. As an international multicentre study, maintaining procedural consistency across participating sites is essential for ensuring the validity and generalisability of our results. Variations in cryoprobe models and freeze times across centres have the potential to affect specimen quality and diagnostic yield. To address this issue, we have standardised the EBUS-TBMC protocol to mandate a specimen size of 4.1–6.0 mm,19 a range previously shown to optimise diagnostic performance for mediastinal lymphadenopathy. This criterion is intended to harmonise tissue sampling across diverse clinical environments and enhance the comparability of outcomes.

Should EBUS-TBMC exhibit non-inferior diagnostic performance, it has the potential to streamline the diagnostic pathway by decreasing the number of necessary procedures. This reduction could, in turn, lessen patient burden, procedural duration and related complications. Such an approach is consistent with ongoing efforts to develop minimally invasive techniques that yield histologically adequate samples in the field of respiratory medicine.

Nonetheless, it is important to recognise certain limitations inherent in this study. The open-label design may introduce performance bias, despite the blinding of outcome assessments. Additionally, the study design does not allow for a complete differentiation between the safety profile of EBUS-TBMC alone and that of the multimodal sampling. While bleeding events may be partially attributable to the specific biopsy site (mediastinal vs pulmonary/endobronchial), complications such as pneumothorax cannot be definitively attributed to a single component of the procedure within this protocol. In terms of operator expertise, we have established minimum procedural case requirements for participating bronchoscopists (≥22 EBUS-TBNA cases and ≥15 EBUS-TBMC cases) based on learning curve data from previous studies to ensure procedural standardisation across centres.15 16 However, this criterion may still limit the generalisability of the findings to settings with less experienced operators.

In summary, this trial is anticipated to yield robust evidence concerning the diagnostic efficacy of EBUS-TBMC in the context of sarcoidosis. Should EBUS-TBMC alone demonstrate non-inferiority compared with the traditional multimodal sampling, it holds the potential to establish itself as a new standard for the histological diagnosis of intrathoracic sarcoidosis, thereby providing an optimal balance between diagnostic precision and procedural simplicity.

Validation of Velacur Liver Stiffness and Attenuation in Patients With Metabolic Dysfunction–Associated Steatotic Liver Disease
Authors: Sri Naveen Surapaneni, Reed Hogan, Tessa Mury, Ashley Horton, Dominique Claiborne, Caitlin Schneider
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and AimsAs clinicians prepare to screen and manage the influx of patients with metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH), new systems to help with patient assessment and management need to undergo rigorous evaluation and validation. This study is a validation of liver stiffness and ultrasound attenuation as measured by Velacur against magnetic resonance elastography (MRE) and magnetic resonance imaging proton density fat fraction (MRI-PDFF).MethodsPatients with a history of MASLD or MASH were enrolled prospectively at 2 sites. Patients received MRE, MRI-PDFF, and Velacur scans within 28 days. The magnetic resonance (MR) results were used as the reference, and MR-based cutoffs were used to define disease status. For each MR cutoff, the area under the receiver operator characteristic (AUROC) is calculated for Velacur shear wave absolute vibro-elastography (S-WAVE; elasticity) and attenuation coefficient estimate (ACE; attenuation). These results are compared to previously published results using 2 one-sided t-tests.ResultsA total of 106 patients were enrolled. Of those, 87 patients had valid Velacur, MRE, and MRI-PDFF scans. Mean age and body mass index were 58.9 years and 32.3 kg/m2, and 50% of patients had diabetes. For each MRE cutoff, the resulting S-WAVE AUROC was >0.9, with the AUROC for advanced fibrosis being 0.95 (0.88, 0.98). For MRI-PDFF cutoffs, the ACE AUROC for >11%, or moderate steatosis, was 0.89 (0.80, 0.94). Velacur demonstrated excellent discriminatory ability for liver stiffness when compared to MRE, with AUROCs ranging from 0.91 to 0.96. When compared to MRI-PDFF, Velacur demonstrated AUROCs from 0.84 to 0.89. These results align with previous results, in which AUROCs ranged from 0.83 to 0.97 for S-WAVE and 0.91 to 0.94 for ACE.ConclusionIn this validation study, Velacur has shown a repeatable performance with excellent AUROCs, using MR-based measures as the reference. This shows Velacur is a useful tool for clinicians to assess patients with MASLD and MASH at point-of-care.

Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and the more advanced form, metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH), are the fastest growing forms of chronic liver disease in the United States and throughout the globe.1, 2, 3 As clinicians prepare to screen and manage the influx of patients, it is imperative that new systems designed to assess and manage these patients undergo rigorous evaluation and validation.4

Recent guidance documents have outlined the workflow for screening patients in broader care settings. These guidance documents advocate to first use simple blood tests, like Fibrosis-4 (Fib-4) score, and then use a secondary test, such as imaging-based elastography, or more specific blood tests like the Enhanced Liver Fibrosis (ELF) test.5,6 For example, patients could be screened with Fib-4 in primary care, and those patients with Fib-4 > 1.3 would be sent for a secondary test. If the Fib-4 is less than 1.3, then they would continue to be monitored in primary care. These secondary tests can be performed in either primary care or specialty care clinics. If the secondary test is above a specific threshold which suggests a risk for advanced fibrosis, the patient should be monitored in a specialist setting. In addition, documents such as the AASLD Practice Guidelines in imaging-based assessments are aimed at specialty care clinics and have recommended using both blood-based tests (such as the Fib-4 score) and secondary tests in parallel, rather than sequentially.7,8 As recent guidance documents are currently recommending the use of elastography imaging to assess patients with MASLD and MASH, this type of imaging should be more widely available in both the primary care and specialty care settings to meet the growing demands.

Ultrasound-based elastography imaging, such as those mentioned within the guidance recommendation documents, has been available in a stand-alone device since 2010 as vibration-controlled transient elastography (VCTE) and controlled attenuation parameter,9,10 as well as integrated into diagnostic ultrasound systems such as SuperSonic Imagine’s Aixplorer,11 and other systems in the form of shear wave elastography using acoustic force radiation impulse imaging techniques.12 Recently, several new devices have become available for point-of-care elastography imaging, including Mindray Hepatus,13 and E-Scopics.14 The focus of this article will be a recently introduced technology, Velacur (Sonic Incytes Medical Corp, Vancouver, Canada). Velacur uses shear wave absolute vibro-elastography (S-WAVE) to assess liver stiffness, as well as attenuation coefficient estimate (ACE), and Velacur determined fat fraction to estimate liver steatosis.15,16

In addition to point-of-care measurements, magnetic resonance elastography (MRE) and magnetic resonance imaging proton density fat fraction (MRI-PDFF) exams are also listed within the guidance documents as secondary imaging methods. MRE and MRI-PDFF have been shown to be the most accurate compared to biopsy when assessing fibrosis and steatosis, respectively.17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24 As they have been shown to be the most well-validated noninvasive imaging biomarker associated with histologic improvement in MASH, MRE, and MRI-PDFF are used as the reference standard in this study to validate the accuracy of Velacur.

All systems require validation testing and confirmation testing to ensure that they can be used consistently and reliably across patient populations. Velacur was first tested to discriminate patients with chronic liver disease from healthy volunteers,15 and later, a more rigorous study was performed to validate Velacur and compared it against FibroScan by using MR-based measures as the reference for liver stiffness and liver fat.16 A study using liver biopsy histological findings as the reference was also recently published.25

This study is a prospective open-label validation of Velacur using MRE and MRI-PDFF as the reference test. This study was designed in the same way as Loomba et al16 to provide the best validation and comparison with prior results. The same cutoffs will be used to show consistency of sensitivity and specificity, along with overall area under the receiver operator characteristic (AUROC) values for Velacur. Novel aspects of this study include that it was performed at different sites in different geographical areas of the United States, showing continuity across patient populations. This study was performed with the new machine learning-based features of the Velacur interface to demonstrate the usefulness of real-time quality feedback.26 The results also include new sensitivity and specificity analysis for both studies.

Methods

Study Design

This was a prospective, open-label, cross-sectional study of patients with suspected or confirmed MASLD and MASH at 2 centers from April 2024 to February 2025. This study was an evaluation of the performance of Velacur (S-WAVE and ACE) to assess liver stiffness and estimate liver steatosis using MRE and MRI-PDFF as the reference.

Patients with suspected or confirmed MASLD or MASH were recruited at 2 sites—the Texas Digestive Disease Consultants in Webster, Texas, and GI Associates & Endoscopy Center in Flowood, Mississippi. Patients with suspected or confirmed MASLD/MASH were contacted and informed of the study. They were then invited to the clinic where they voluntarily enrolled and the study’s inclusion/exclusion criteria were confirmed.

All patients were scheduled to undergo 2 scans—Velacur and MRE/MRI-PDFF. The Velacur measurements were taken within 28 days of the magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). The MRE and MRI-PDFF measurements were used to determine the patient’s estimated fibrosis stage and steatosis grade. In an effort to ensure that patients with all stages of fibrosis and grades of steatosis were included, enrollment was closed to certain populations based on their previous history. As MRI scans were often taken after Velacur scans and MRI was used to determine the final disease assessment, the final distributions of fibrosis and steatosis were not completely equal across all disease states.

The study was conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practices. Written informed consent was obtained from each patient, and the study protocol conformed to the ethical guidelines of the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki and Istanbul as reflected in a priori approval by the appropriate institutional review committee. The study was approved by WCG IRB (#20231324) and registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05908006). All authors had access to the study data and reviewed and approved the final manuscript.

Study Objectives

The primary objectives of the study were to validate the accuracy of Velacur against MRE and MRI-PDFF for the estimation of fibrosis staging and steatosis grading. The primary objectives also included the measurement of the sensitivity and specificity of the predefined cutoffs for each fibrosis stage and steatosis grade in this new patient population.16 A secondary objective was to compare the AUROC results as well as the sensitivity and specificity results against previously published literature. This comparison was aimed to demonstrate the consistency of Velacur’s performance across different clinics, Velacur systems, operators, patient populations, and geographic regions.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

The inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: patients must be between 18 and 80 years old and exhibit any of the following: (1) biopsy-proven chronic liver disease; (2) evidence of hepatic steatosis or chronic liver disease by abdominal ultrasound, MRI-PDFF (>12%), or FibroScan controlled attenuation parameter (>230 dB/m), within the past 12 months; or (3) at least 2 criteria for metabolic syndrome and increased stiffness on FibroScan (>8 kPa) within the past 12 months. If applicable, all patients must agree to practice a highly effective contraception method for the duration of the study. All patients must be able to understand the informed consent form, the study procedures, and be willing to participate in the study.

Exclusion criteria included the following: patients must not have a body mass index (BMI) >40 kg/m2 (or maximum defined by the MRI center); be pregnant or lactating; have current, significant alcohol consumption (defined as an average of more than 20 g per day in females and more than 30 g per day in males); be using drugs known to cause hepatic steatosis; and have clinical evidence of hepatic decompensation in the past 3 months, acute hepatitis, or hemochromatosis. Patients unable to enter the MRI due to implanted devices, known claustrophobia, or other conditions that would prevent them from safely entering the MRI were also excluded.

Imaging Methods

Patient preparation

Before all types of liver scans, patients were confirmed to have fasted for at least 3 hours.

MR elastography and MRI-PDFF

Two imaging centers were used for this study, corresponding to the 2 enrollment sites. The Mississippi site used a 3T Philips Ingenia. A 3T GE Pioneer was used in Texas. All patients completed an anatomical scan, an MRE, and a fat fraction sequence. At both sites, Resoundant hardware coupled with manufacturer software for elastography reconstruction was used to measure elasticity.27 Areas within the liver, as defined with the anatomical scan and the quality of the MRE reconstructions, were manually segmented from 4 slices. Only slices with at least 500 pixels of ‘quality’ area were included, where the quality was defined by manufacturer algorithms. The mean of all included pixels is reported for this article.28

In Mississippi, a fat suppression sequence was collected using m-FEE_T2_R2_star. This is a multiecho sequence used to show fat and water suppression. MRQuantif software, designed to measure liver fat and correct for liver iron content, was used to calculate the fat fraction measurements.29 Three regions of interest were manually placed in each of the axial slices.29 The mean fat fraction across all slices for each patient is reported for this article. The site underwent MRI software upgrades before the end of the study. The final 7 patients were scanned with the mDixon-Quant software. For these patients, the entire liver volume was segmented, avoiding vessels and other artifacts, and the mean fat fraction for all included pixels is reported.

In Texas, the IDEAL IQ (https://www.gehealthcare.com/en-us/products/magnetic-resonance-imaging/applications/ideal) sequences were used to create full liver maps of fat fraction. Images were manually segmented, including the entire liver and avoiding vessels or other artifacts. The mean fat fraction value of all included pixels is reported.

Velacur

Velacur is a portable ultrasound-based device for the assessment of liver stiffness and ultrasound attenuation. Velacur uses the elasticity algorithm, S-WAVE, to measure tissue stiffness. S-WAVE measurements are created by generating a steady-state shear wave within the patient’s liver using an external activation unit and measuring the waves using ultrasound imaging over a three-dimensional volume of tissue.

The volume of tissue is collected during a sweeping motion of the ultrasound probe while the probe is held on the patient’s skin over the intercostal space. The ACE is measured from the ultrasound signals in the same collected volume.

A novel aspect of this study is that the new, objective machine learning-based algorithm is used to assess the quality of the shear waves for the S-WAVE measures in real-time and is combined with other quality measures to assess the ultrasound data for ACE.26 A B-mode image is provided to the operator to locate the liver. An addition to the image, a machine learning algorithm is used to highlight the liver within the image to help the operator assess the ultrasound image and probe position. Both interface features are present to assist the operator in probe positioning and provide real-time feedback about shear wave quality within the liver.

A total of 10 volumes, each producing an S-WAVE and ACE measurement, were collected on each patient. The reported final result is the median of the measurements which passed the objective quality assessment described previously. The interquartile range and average quality were also collected.

A trained operator performed the scans at both sites, blinded to the patient’s history and results of other scans. The same operator performed the scans at both sites.

Statistical Methods

The primary analysis population for this study are those patients with a paired Velacur S-WAVE and MRE measurements and with a paired Velacur ACE and MRI-PDFF measurement.

In order to determine the staging effectiveness of Velacur and assess the cutoff for S-WAVE, using MRE as the reference test, a receiver operator characteristic curve will be constructed for each of the MRE cutoffs corresponding to the fibrosis stages, F0–F4.19 The same cutoffs as were used in a previously published literature for consistency.16 The same processes will be followed for Velacur ACE, using the MRI-PDFF cutoffs to estimate the grades of steatosis.20

For each of the binary classifications for fibrosis estimation and steatosis estimation, the area under the curve (AUC) will be calculated. The sensitivity and specificity of Velacur using previously defined cutoffs will be calculated.

The AUROC results of this study will then be compared to previously published research with Velacur. To test for equivalence of the AUROC from the 2 studies, two one-sided test (TOSTs) were performed.30 In this method, parallel groups of patients may be analyzed by a TOST equivalence test to show that the means of the 2 groups (the AUROC for each cutoff) do not differ by more than a small amount, called the margin of equivalence. For this assessment, the margin of equivalence was defined as 0.1, which is the average confidence interval (CI) of the AUROC from the study by Loomba et al.

Results

Patient Characteristics

A total of 106 patients with suspected or confirmed MASLD or MASH were screened at 2 sites in the United States. Of those enrolled, 87 patients were included in the primary analysis population, as they had a valid paired Velacur S-WAVE and MRE or ACE and MRI-PDFF scan (Figure 1). Eleven patients did not complete the MRI due to scheduling, technical problems in the MRI, or patient discontinuation. There were 2 patients who had satisfactory MRI-PDFF measurements, but MRE did not pass quality assurance as there were insufficient waves to make a measurement.28 Four patients failed to meet the elasticity quality thresholds defined by the objective machine learning algorithm on the Velacur device and 5 patients failed to meet the ACE quality thresholds. Detection of Steatosis and MRI-PDFF Grades

For the steatosis analysis population, MRI-PDFF was used to estimate the steatosis grade. The cutoffs of 5.2%, 11%, and 17% were used20 for any (≥S1), moderate (≥S2), and severe (S3) steatosis. Figure 2, right, shows the overall Velacur results at each of these grades. Of the included patients, 75.8% (66/87) were considered to have any steatosis (>5.2% PDFF), 40.3% (35/87) had at least moderate steatosis (>11% PDFF), and 15% (13/87) were considered to have severe steatosis (>17.2% PDFF). The AUROC for Velacur detection was 0.842 (0.738, 0.925) for any steatosis and 0.891 (0.812, 0.944) for at least moderate steatosis.

Comparison with Previous Results

The study published in 2024 defined the initial results of Velacur using MRE and MRI-PDFF as the reference tests.16 The data from this 2024 study were used to define the current Velacur cutoffs and the sensitivity and specificity of those cutoffs. A secondary objective of this study was to evaluate the performance of Velacur using these predefined cutoffs in a new cohort of patients with MASLD and MASH.

When comparing the patient populations, patients in the study by Loomba et al were similar in age (57.8 years vs 58.9 years) and had a slightly higher proportion of female participants (53% vs 60%). These patients were more racially diverse with only 38% White, compared to 78% White in the current study. Patients described in the article had a higher BMI when compared to those included in the study by Loomba et al, 32.3 ± 5.02 kg/m2 vs 29.9 ± 4.5 kg/m2. There were also more patients included with diabetes, with almost 50% of patients in this study with diabetes, vs 30% in the study by Loomba et al.

When looking at the MRI results, the 2 patient populations did not have significantly different mean MRE (3.22 vs 3.17 kPa). The MRI-PDFF results for the current patient cohort were numerically lower (14.2 ± 8.37 vs 10.4 ± 7.32%) but not significantly different.

Table 2 outlines the elasticity results of both studies, the distribution of patients, and the AUCs.

There are some differences in the patient distributions that may impact the AUC. The distribution of stiffness in the study by Loomba et al was more uniform in the higher stiffness ranges, whereas only 5 (6%) of patients from this study fell into the F3 stiffness range. For the liver steatosis measurements on MRI-PDFF, more patients (35%) in the study by Loomba et al fell into the highest MRI-PDFF category of >17% and far fewer (15%) fell into the no steatosis range of <5.2%.

Overall, the results for stiffness measurements in this study were better than those reported by Loomba et al for F1 (2.61 kPa) and F2 (2.97 kPa) cutoffs and similar for the F3 (3.62 kPa) and F4 (4.69 kPa) MRE-based cutoffs. All the AUCs for both studies would be perceived as good or excellent with AUC of >0.8 or >0.9. Equivalence testing using TOST and an equivalence margin of 0.1 show that for stiffness measurements, the new data were better or equivalent across all MRE thresholds.

For the MRI-PDFF-based cutoffs, the AUCs in this study were slightly lower than those seen in the study by Loomba et al, with the MRI-PDFF threshold of 11% being equivalent using TOST in both studies and the 5% and 17% thresholds slightly lower. Though, all the AUROCs were very good at >0.8.

Across the board, the sensitivity and specificity of the stiffness thresholds are the same or better than those from the previously reported data, with an increase in the specificity of the lowest threshold from 54% to 72%. The largest difference between the results is that for the specificity of the lowest ACE threshold. The lower specificity means that there is a higher likelihood that the patient will be classified as a false positive.

One limitation of the study by Loomba et al was the high number of patients who were removed from the analysis for reasons related to scan quality. Since the completion of that study, new features of Velacur have been introduced to improve reliability and scan quality. In the the study by Loomba et al, 21 and 22 patients out of 164 (13%) were removed, whereas in the current study, only 4/106 (4%) of patients had poor quality Velacur scans. This improvement highlights the impact of the new Velacur features on overall scan reliability. Details of the 4 excluded patients can be found in the Discussion section.

Discussion

As noninvasive methods are becoming increasingly crucial to the diagnosis, treatment, and management of patients, it is important to understand the accuracy of diagnostic methods in a variety of patient populations. Establishing the ground truth for patient disease status has been an ongoing challenge in the field of MASLD and MASH. Traditionally, liver biopsy was considered the best option and was required for a definitive diagnosis. Even though it was deemed the best option at the time, biopsy still only measures a very small section of the liver, and given the high interreader variability, clinical trials often use 3 pathologists, which is not practical for all centers.31,32

As most clinical practices do not routinely perform biopsy on patients with MASLD or MASH unless there is a more specific cause, this study was designed to compare Velacur to the best noninvasive measurements currently available—MR elastography and MRI-PDFF. Although these have their own variability, they are considered to be very reliable.33

The study described in this article used MRE and MRI-PDFF as the reference tests for Velacur and follows the same design as other comparative studies to show that Velacur is able to correctly classify patients’ disease status. The AUCs found in this study were all greater than 0.8 for both liver stiffness and liver steatosis, with some values being greater than 0.9. Although no test is perfect, this shows that Velacur could be used clinically to manage patients with MASLD and MASH and could limit the need for patients to undergo further testing.

One study is never enough to show efficacy in all patients. This study was designed with the previous comparison study, Loomba et al, in mind. Although it is always difficult to compare results from different studies directly, this study attempts to replicate the design of Loomba et al to provide the best understanding of whether the results are sustainable across patient populations at new sites. The higher average BMI and almost 50% of patients with diabetes is a representative patient population. In particular, these are important distinctions as verification of the performance in the diabetic population is critical, as almost 70% of diabetic patients demonstrate MASLD, and a leading comorbidity of MASLD is obesity.

Overall, the results shown here were very comparable to those previously presented, with most of the AUROCs at each MRE and MRI-PDFF threshold not significantly different. The overall stiffness measures were better than those seen previously with only the AUROC at the lowest MRI-PDFF threshold being different. Given the differences in patients and the MRI centers used for the 2 different studies, it is plausible to see variation and promising to see that the results hold up over time.

In comparison with the previous study, far fewer patients (4% vs 13%) were removed from the analysis due to poor quality scans. Only 4 patients did not pass the Velacur quality thresholds.26 Of these 4 patients, 1 had a BMI of 51 kg/m2 and should have been excluded in any case. The second patient had a BMI of 40 kg/m2, and FibroScan measurements could be completed on this patient either. The third patient had considerable scar tissue in the area of scanning due to an unrelated accident and neither FibroScan nor Velacur were completed successfully. The last patient was difficult to scan and there were some sweeps that qualified, but, overall, the data were removed as the exam as a whole would not be acceptable clinically. The FibroScan for this patient was 71 kPa, while their MRE was 2.1 kPa (results not shown).

The specificity of ACE for the lowest MRI-PDFF threshold is lower than previously reported. It is unclear why the ACE values in general for this study were higher (∼210 vs 235 dB/m) for those patients with MRI-PDFF < 5.2%, creating this low specificity at the ACE threshold of 234 dB/m. Although all MRI-PDFF measures should be the same, there are slightly different methods used on different manufacturers that may also contribute and should be investigated further.

As the number of patients needing to be screened and managed is rising, this study shows Velacur as a reliable point-of-care tool. The new interface features have improved the overall experience, resulting in more patients with better quality scans and fewer rejected exams than in previous studies.

Limitations

There are a few limitations to this study which should be noted. The patient population, although different than that presented by Loomba et al, was mostly white. Future studies will focus on more diverse populations to ensure that the performance of Velacur is not adversely affected. Both studies were completed within the United States, so future studies will seek into including a more international population.

There was an effort to evenly distribute patients across all stages of liver disease, but there were 6% of patients (5/87) who fell into the F3 liver stiffness stage and only 10% of patients (9/87) in the F1 liver stiffness stage. Also, despite an effort to enroll more patients with higher stiffness, 49% (43/87) were classified as F0 (<2.61 kPa) by MRE. Although not ideal, this may be more representative of the overall population. These patients were enrolled with higher levels of stiffness by other noninvasive methods, potentially indicating a tendency to overstage patients.

Although enrolled at 2 sites, all patients were scanned by the same operator. This goes against general clinical practice, but certified operators were not available at both sites and operators can require up to 25 scans to become proficient.

Conclusion

As the global epidemic of MASLD/MASH is growing, the need for tools to diagnosis and manage these patients is outpacing current availability. New methods should be tested against the best noninvasive tools available and qualified to meet this growing need.

Velacur demonstrated excellent discriminatory ability for liver stiffness when compared to MRE with AUROC for stiffness ranging from 0.91 to 0.96. When compared to MRI-PDFF in order to discriminate for liver steatosis, Velacur demonstrated AUROCs from 0.84 to 0.89.

In this validation study, Velacur has shown a repeatable performance with excellent AUROCs, using MR-based measures as the reference. This shows Velacur is a useful tool for clinicians to assess patients with MASLD and MASH at point-of-care.

Diagnostic efficacy of circulating tumor cell in clinically significant prostate cancer
Authors: Jiajun Qian, Fan Zhao, Bo Cai, Yangbo Guan, Huyang Xie, Xiang Zhang, Limin Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To investigate the value of circulating tumor cells (CTC) in the diagnosis of clinically significant prostate cancer (csPCa). Patients who underwent prostate biopsy under ultrasound guidance and CTC detection at Nantong University Affiliated Hospital from December 2023 to June 2024 were included. Based on the CTC detection results, patients were divided into CTC-positive and negative groups. The baseline information and biopsy pathological results were collected and analyzed. The detection rate of csPCa was compared between two groups. Univariate and multivariate analysis were conducted to explore the independent predictors of csPCa. The diagnostic efficacy was evaluated by ROC curve analysis. 90 patients were enrolled, of whom 46 patients were CTC-positive. 52 patients were diagnosed with csPCa. Patients in the CTC-positive group had higher prostate-specific antigen (PSA) levels (p = 0.001), larger prostate volumes (p = 0.007), higher Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System (PI-RADS) score (p < 0.001), later clinical stages (p < 0.001), higher biopsy Gleason score (p < 0.001), and higher detection rate of csPCa (p = 0.003). In the univariate analysis, PSA, prostate volume, PI-RADS score, and CTC count were associated with csPCa. In the multivariate analysis, PSA (p = 0.046), PI-RADS score (p = 0.001), and CTC count (p = 0.034) were independent predictors of csPCa. In the Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, the combination of CTC count, PSA, and PI-RADS scores had the largest area under the curve (AUC = 0.913). CTC count is an independent predictor of csPCa. The combination of PSA, PI-RADS score and CTC count is expected to enhance the diagnosis of csPCa.

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is currently the most common malignant tumor in the male urogenital system. Although the incidence of PCa in China is significantly lower than that in western countries, the proportion of newly diagnosed advanced PCa is relatively high. The gradual popularization of prostate-specific antigen (PSA)-based screening for PCa in elderly men is an important reason for the increase in the incidence of PCa. It also exposes the problem of high sensitivity but low specificity of the PSA screening system, leading to unnecessary biopsies1. Due to the superior soft tissue resolution, multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (mpMRI) has gradually become one of the important methods for the diagnosis of PCa2. However, mpMRI has certain limitations in judging small lesions, central zone lesions, and distant metastatic lesions. Therefore, finding more efficient methods for early diagnosis of PCa is necessary3.

Over the past decade, liquid biopsy has provided an ideal solution for multi-time point analysis of blood biomarkers as a noninvasive detection. Circulating tumor cell (CTC) in peripheral blood has demonstrated great potential in early diagnosis, assessment of prognosis, individualized treatment of cancers4,5. CTC can serve as potential biomarkers for advanced diseases such as castration-resistant PCa6,7 and metastatic hormone-sensitive PCa8. Postoperative CTC may become a potential biomarker for disease recurrence after radical prostatectomy9. Recent studies have indicated that CTC is of great value in the early diagnosis of various malignancies. The study10 confirmed that breast cancer cell-associated CTC have a specificity and sensitivity of up to 93.1% and 96.4% for the prediction of breast cancer. The researchers have also begun to explore the use of CTC in the early diagnosis and prognosis of PCa, but have not achieved satisfactory results11. It is speculated that the traditional CTC detection method may be one of the reasons for these unsatisfactory results. Furthermore, single CTC may be not enough.

Based on these data, our study aims to evaluate the role of CTC in the diagnosis of clinically significant prostate cancer (csPCa) and its diagnostic efficacy.

Patients and methods

Patients and study design

A cohort of patients who underwent ultrasound-guided prostate biopsy and CTC detection prior to the biopsy at the Affiliated Hospital of Nantong University from December 2023 to June 2024 was included. All biopsies were performed by the same experienced physician. The exclusion criteria were: (1) PSA < 4 ng/ml and Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System (PI-RADS) score < 3; (2) Prior transurethral resection of the prostate or ultrasound-guided prostate biopsy. Two patients for whom mpMRI could not be obtained were excluded, resulting in a final analysis of 90 patients. Based on the results of CTC detection, the patients were divided into CTC-positive and negative groups. Clinical data collected included age, PSA, prostate volume, PI-RADS score, TNM stage, biopsy Gleason score, and CTC. All procedures in this study comply with the Declaration of Helsinki and have been approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Nantong University.

CTC detection

The Cell Collector technique involves coating a metal wire with antibodies against the epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EpCAM) expressed on the surface of CTC membranes. The metal wire is then placed in the median cubital vein for 30 min. As blood flows through the functional area of the wire, epithelial cells bind to the EpCAM antibodies and are captured. The advantage lies in the ability to capture a larger number of CTC, as approximately 1 L of blood passes through the functional area. Additionally, the captured CTC, after localization and separation, can undergo further exploratory and application research. CTC morphology is defined as: intact cells with a DAPI-positive nucleus, positive cytokeratin, and negative CD4512.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 27.0 (USA). Non-parametric continuous variables were expressed as median and interquartile range (IQR), and categorical variables were expressed as rates. Continuous variables were compared using t-tests or non-parametric tests, while categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Univariate and multivariate analysis were conducted to determine factors associated with csPCa. The efficiency of combined diagnosis was compared by Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline information

A total of 92 patients underwent CTC detection before biopsy, and 2 patients were excluded due to lack of mpMRI. Ultimately, 90 patients were enrolled. Among them, 46 patients had positive CTC, while 44 had negative results. There was no statistical difference in age between two groups. Patients in the CTC-positive group had higher PSA (p = 0.001), larger prostate volumes (p = 0.007), higher PI-RADS scores (p < 0.001), and later TNM stages (T stage (p < 0.001), N stage (p < 0.001), M stage (p = 0.004)), indicating the higher potential for PCa (Table 1).

Biopsy results

Patients in the CTC-positive group had significantly higher Gleason scores (p < 0.001) and a higher detection rate of csPCa (p = 0.003), suggesting worse pathological outcomes and higher positive biopsy. The detection rate of CTC reached 63.5% in patients with csPCa compared to only 34.2% in the remaining patients.

Univariate and multivariate analysis

Univariate analysis of two groups indicated that PSA, prostate volume, PI-RADS score and CTC count may be influencing factors for the diagnosis of csPCa (Table 2). Multivariate analysis showed that PSA, PI-RADS score, and CTC count were independent influencing factors (Table 3).

Diagnostic efficacy

ROC curve analysis demonstrated that the combination of PSA, PI-RADS score and CTC count yielded the largest area under the curve (AUC = 0.913). Compared to the PI-RADS score alone, the combination of the three factors showed higher diagnostic efficacy (p = 0.048); however, there was no statistically significant difference when comparing to the combination of PSA and PI-RADS score (p = 0.191) (Fig. 1).

Discussion

Currently, CTC detection, separation, capture, and characterization in the blood for PCa patients are showing a diversified development trend, including EpCAM-dependent and non-dependent techniques13. Enrichment methods based on EpCAM expression may miss low-expressing CTCs or cells that have undergone epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT)—this transition process is bound up with the invasive biological characteristics of tumors and the formation of metastatic foci14–16. Some researchers have proposed using filtration technology based on differences in cell size for CTC enrichment, hoping to overcome the limitation of CellSearch platform. But it can easily become clogged when processing large numbers of cells due to the microstructure of the filter membrane, leading to insufficient selectivity17. The separated cells can adsorb onto the filter membrane, hindering the extraction of cells needed for subsequent analysis. As a result, a non-antigen-dependent CTC enrichment technology based on microfluidic technology has been developed18. This technology utilizes the differences in cell size and deformability between CTCs and leukocytes to achieve CTC enrichment19,20. The separation process is based on a microfluidic ratchet mechanism, where oscillatory flow induces deformation of the cells within a funnel-shaped contraction structure. Unlike the existing CellSearch system, which relies on specific cell surface antigen expression, this physical selection can capture both EpCAM-positive and negative cells. The study shows that at least a part of CK+/CD45- cells indeed exhibit characteristics of cancer cells21. However, all CTC detection markers have displayed false-positive results in healthy population, showing that at least some subpopulations of CK+CD45- cells are not cancer cells22. This device relies on CTC size and deformability for detection and can easily adapt to the detection needs of other markers.

PSA has been used for detection and monitoring recurrence in PCa for several decades. Although its clinical specificity for early detection is controversial, it remains the most widely used biomarker for assessing curative effect. However, as an androgen-dependent gene, different classes of drugs can regulate androgen levels or PSA gene expression, potentially leading to a decrease in PSA levels without killing cancer cells23. The presence of CTC indicates that the disease is still spreading. The reduction does not depend on the androgen receptor (AR) signaling pathway, thereby directly reflecting tumor spread and reduction in disease burden. PSMA PET/CT, as a new diagnostic tool, has a high diagnostic rate in lymph node staging, except for high-risk patients24. The CTC counts are minimal (an average of 1–2 tumor cells) in early-stage PCa patients, with a lower positive rate than that in the castration-resistant state. This is consistent with our research data, where the vast majority of patients detected 0–2 CTCs, and only one patient had a CTC count as high as 8. The largest cohort of localized PCa included 152 patients, of which only 11% had detectable CTCs at baseline25. In our study, the CTC detection rate significantly increased, which may be related to the CTC detection methods or some patients already showing signs of metastasis. For recurrent PCa, CTC detection might provide earlier warnings of recurrence than elevated PSA levels. The molecular and genetic information from CTCs can reflect the tumor characteristics of primary or metastatic sites26. Through molecular feature analysis of CTCs, they can further serve as markers for personalized treatment strategies27,28. Early blood detection of cancer is receiving significant attention. In the field of PCa, the value of PSA as an early biomarker is still being optimized29, but may still lead to overdiagnosis of low-grade tumors that are unlikely to be fatal30. mpMRI has shown unique advantages in tumor localization, size assessment, and invasiveness, effectively predicting tumor biological behavior31. Fiorentino et al.32 has explored the relationship between the volume of biopsy specimens and the accuracy of biopsy results, providing some guidance for our subsequent research on biopsy. mpMRI combined with PSA can reduce a part of unnecessary biopsies and overdiagnosis of insignificant PCa, while maintaining the detection of csPCa. Most research on CTC has focused on their prognostic value in PCa. The sensitivity of existing CTC detection technologies for the early diagnosis of PCa is constantly being updated. Our research has found that CTC count can serve as an independent predictive factor for csPCa, and its combination with PSA and PI-RADS scores can improve diagnostic efficacy.

Our study also had some limitations. Firstly, the reliance on the Cell Collector technique, which isolates circulating tumor cells based exclusively on EpCAM expression, represents an epitope-dependent approach ignoring the aggressive, stem-like subpopulation of circulating tumor cells that have undergone EMT and consequently downregulated EpCAM in favor of mesenchymal markers like vimentin or N-cadherin. This methodological oversight could underestimate the true circulating metastatic burden and skew the correlation with clinical significance. Furthermore, the ROC curve analyses are performed without any internal cross-validation techniques, such as bootstrapping or k-fold validation, and lack an independent, external validation cohort, making the multivariate logistic regression models susceptible to statistical overfitting. We also lack a granular, gene-level analysis or single-cell sequencing of the captured circulating tumor cells, missing the opportunity to correlate specific genomic alterations, such as androgen receptor splice variants or PTEN loss, with the high PI-RADS scores or aggressive Gleason grade groups observed in the biopsy outcomes. Without demonstrating that the captured cells possess the specific molecular machinery driving aggressive prostate cancer, the mere enumeration of EpCAM-positive cells adds limited biological insight to the existing nomograms.

Conclusion

Our research has found that CTC count can serve as an independent predictive factor for csPCa, and its combination with PSA and PI-RADS scores can improve diagnostic efficacy, providing further guidance for decision of prostate biopsy.

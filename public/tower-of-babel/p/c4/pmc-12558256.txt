Multicenter Retrospective Evaluation of the Chinese Expert Consensus Scoring System for the Diagnosis of Obstetrical DIC
Authors: Jianjian Cui, Ziyang Liu, Wencong He, Ruifen Su, Ruilin Ma, Hui Tao, Zejun Yang, Lei Sun, Shaoqi Chen, Yanan Li, Zhishan Jin, Yin Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:To evaluate the diagnostic efficacy and clinical application of the Obstetrical Chinese Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (DIC) Scoring System (OCDSS).Methods:This study is a retrospective study that collected 1063 cases from Wuhan Union Hospital, Yichang Central People’s Hospital, and the Central Hospital of Enshi Tujia and Miao Autonomous Prefecture between July 2017 and June 2024. These cases were divided into DIC and non-DIC groups based on score standard. Diagnosis of DIC, the rate of hysterectomy, neonatal mortality, and severe asphyxia are the main outcome measures. All the laboratory indicators are all determined by clinical laboratory department of the hospital. Data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range) and frequencies. Independent sample t-test or non-parametric test were used to compare measurement data, while the chi-square test was used for count data. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and area under curve (AUC) were used to test the predictive accuracy. Using univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis to study the high-risk factors. P < 0.050 indicates a statistical significance.Results:Of 1063 participants in this study, 29 participants (2.73%) were diagnosed with obstetrical DIC by OCDSS score standard, and all the participants were diagnosed as DIC with underlying disease. When the Takao, Clark, and Erez score standard is the “gold standard”, the OCDSS score standard always shows good sensitivity and specificity, with all the AUC over 0.75. OCDSS score standard also has better predictive of hysterectomy (68.18%, 91.07%, 0.872), severe neonatal asphyxia and death (79.17%, 75.07%, 0.842) than the other three score standards. All the indicators included in the OCDSS score standard contributed to the DIC diagnosis (all the P < 0.001). The indicators in the DIC group were more abnormal than the non-DIC group (all the P < 0.001).Conclusion:OCDSS is a first score standard, especially for pregnancies, it considers the underlying disease, clinical symptoms, and laboratory results. This score system shared a good diagnosis performance for DIC in the Chinese population and may help clinicians make timely decisions.

Introduction

Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) in obstetrics is a life-threatening complication that can arise from a variety of obstetrical diseases. The incidence of obstetrical DIC ranges from 0.03% to 0.35% among pregnancies,1,2 with mortality rates as high as 25% in severe cases.3 This condition, often triggered by complications such as placental abruption, amniotic fluid embolism, hemolysis, elevated liver function and low platelet count syndrome (HELLP syndrome), and stillbirth retention poses unique diagnostic challenges due to the physiological changes in coagulation during pregnancy.4567 Previous scholars have been committed to exploring the development of a diagnostic scoring system to make the diagnosis of DIC more quantitative and convenient.8910

However, there was no gold standard for obstetrical DIC diagnosis, which makes early identification and treatment difficult and poses great challenges to clinical doctors. To address this question and create a simple scoring standard for clinical use, different scoring systems have been developed. In 2014, Japanese scholar Takao revised the obstetrical DIC score system (we named it Takao standard ) based on Maki who was the first person to create the obstetrical DIC score standard,5,11 and then Takao standard was widely used in Japan. Erez and Clark modified the International Society on Thrombosis and Hemostasis DIC score sytem, known as the pregnancy-specific modified the International Society on Thrombosis and Haemotasis (ISTH) DIC score, we named them as Erez standard1 and Clark standard.12 Although each of these criteria helps diagnose obstetrical DIC, the diagnostic performance of these criteria in different underlying diseases remains controversial. Moreover, the differences in etiology, culture, and medical background between Chinese and foreigners, resulting in this diagnostic scoring system may not applicable to Chinese people.

In 2023, obstetrics and hematology experts in China collaboratively proposed a specialized scoring system tailored for obstetrical DIC, addressing the limitations of generalized DIC criteria in pregnancy.13 However, the clinical utility and reliability of this scoring system remain to be thoroughly evaluated. This study aims to assess the application of the proposed obstetrical DIC scoring system in clinical settings, focusing on its diagnostic accuracy, feasibility, and potential to guide timely management. The findings are expected to provide critical evidence for optimizing DIC diagnosis and improving maternal-fetal outcomes.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This was a multicenter retrospective observational study. We collected information of 1063 pregnancies who were suspected of DIC in Wuhan Union Hospital, Yichang Central People’s Hospital, and the Central Hospital of Enshi Tujia and Miao Autonomous Prefecture all between July 2017 and June 2024. All the obstetrical-related treatments were performed according to relevant guidelines and expert consensus.141516 The data collection, management, and statistical analyses were processed anonymously. All the laboratory indicators included in this study were dynamically monitored during disease progression to ensure the objectivity of the data and the consistency of clinical symptoms and laboratory examination timing.

To ensure data quality, this study implemented electronic data collection, double data entry, real-time logic verification, and standardized cross-center training. For multicenter coordination, strategies including unified testing by core laboratories, centralized monitoring, and quarterly data reconciliation meetings were adopted to minimize inter-center variability.

Diagnostic criteria and reference scoring system

Due to the absence of a universal gold standard for obstetrical DIC, we evaluated the diagnosis efficiency of the Chinese obstetrical DIC Scoring System (OCDSS, Table 1) against three representative internationally validated obstetric DIC scoring system: (1) In 2001, Japanese scholar Takao revised the obstetrical DIC scoring system based on the Maki scoring system,5,17 see Supplementary Table S1, http://links.lww.com/MFM/A101, this scoring standard was used widely in Japan; (2) In 2014, British scholar Erez published a pregnancy-specific modified ISTH DIC score,1,18 see Supplementary Table S2, http://links.lww.com/MFM/A101, this score was used in the world; (3) The obstetrical DIC scoring system revised by American scholar Clark based on the ISTH scoring system in 201612, see Supplementary Table S3, http://links.lww.com/MFM/A101, this score standard was proposed based on the data of patients suspected with amniotic fluid embolism (AFE).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The flowchart of the whole study is shown in Fig. 1. Fully considering the four DIC scoring criteria we have included, the key inclusion criteria of patients were: (1) 18-45 years old, (2) more than one abnormal finding according to the laboratory tests: platelet count < 185×109/L, prolongation of prothrombin time (PT) ≥ 3s, prolongation of activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) ≥ 10s, D-dimer ≥ 5 mg/L, fibrinogen < 4.5 g/L, fibrin/fibrinogen degradation products (FDP) ≥ 10 ug/ml.

Patients who met the following criteria19202122 were excluded: (1) suffering from serious liver disease, (2) known clotting disorders or receiving anticoagulant therapy before or during pregnancy, (3) antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), systemic lupus erythematosus, thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) and other autoimmune diseases, (4) incomplete data, cases were excluded with missing important indicators (indicators included in the DIC score system), for indicators not included in the DIC score system, if the missing data exceeds 20%, the case will be excluded.

Definitions

All the diagnoses in this study were determined by two senior obstetricians according to clinical guidelines, when there is a disagreement, another two senior experts (obstetrician and hematologist) who did not participate in the initial diagnosis will conduct blind review. In this study, it is important to note that postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) is classified as blood loss exceeding 1000 ml in patients without underlying disease. The DIC score was calculated dynamically during hospitalization from the beginning of the clinical suspicion of DIC until discharge, and DIC was diagnosed when the maximum score met the cut-off value of individual criteria. Adverse perinatal outcomes were defined as unintended maternal mortality, hysterectomy, severe neonatal asphyxia and death during the perinatal period. Retained stillbirth was defined as a fetus dead in the uterus after 20 weeks of pregnancy, while stillbirth refers to a fetus’s death during delivery.

Statistical analysis

To contextualize our findings, we conducted a post hoc power analysis using the observed effect size, the effect power achieved 80% (the number of DIC patients diagnosed by OCDSS is 29, the α is 0.05 and the Cohen’s d is more than 0.8). While post hoc analyses have interpretational caveats, this underscores that our sample was sufficient for detecting meaningful effects.

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test and are presented as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed data or median with interquartile range for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and percentages.

Group comparisons were conducted with Bonferroni correction applied for multiple testing. For continuous variables, independent-sample t-test was used when normally distributed, and Mann–Whitney U test was applied for non-normally distributed variables. Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test (when appropriate) was used to compare categorical variables.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to assess predictive performance, with area under the curve (AUC) and 95% confidence intervals reported. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify potential risk factors. Variables with P < 0.100 in univariate analysis were entered into the multivariate model. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a P value < 0.050 was considered statistically significant.

Ethical approval

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Union Hospital, Tongji Medical College, Huazhong University of Science and Technology (Approval No. 2024 LSZ (0801-01)) on October 7, 2024, with a waiver of informed consent. It has also been registered in the National Clinical Trial Registration System (Registration No. MR-42-24-042611). All procedures were conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional research committee and the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

To further evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of the four score standards, we treated OCDSS, Takao, Clark, and Erez score standards as the “gold standard” respectively to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of other score standards for obstetrical DIC. The results are shown in Fig. 3 and Supplementary Table S4, http://links.lww.com/MFM/A101. When the OCDSS score standard was “gold standard”, the sensitivity of Takao, Clark, and Erez scores for DIC diagnosis were 86.21%, 89.66%, and 82.76%, respectively; the specificity was 93.71%, 97.39%, and 86.75%, respectively; the area under the ROC curve was 0.958, 0.973, and 0.873, respectively (Fig. 3A). When the Takao score standard was the “gold standard”, the sensitivity of OCDSS, Clark, and Erez score systems were 84.62%, 65.38%, and 61.54%, respectively; the specificity was 91.71%, 96.53%, and 94.60%, respectively; the area under the ROC curve was 0.945, 0.798, and 0.838, respectively (Fig. 3B). When Clark scored standard as “gold standard”, the sensitivity of OCDSS, Takao, and Erez were 100.00%, 77.78%, and 94.44%, respectively; the specificity was 94.83%, 92.73%, and 88.90%, respectively; the area under the ROC curve was 0.990, 0.936, and 0.954, respectively (Fig. 3C). When Erez scored standard as “gold standard”, the sensitivity of OCDSS, Takao, and Clark were 54.73%, 72.94%, and 22.30%, respectively; the specificity was 77.05%, 71.37%, and 97.81%, respectively; the area under the ROC curve was 0.655, 0.698, and 0.546, respectively (Fig. 3D).

The serious adverse perinatal outcomes evaluation efficacy of four DIC score standards

In this study, we defined adverse perinatal outcomes as unintended maternal mortality, hysterectomy, severe neonatal asphyxia and death during the perinatal period. Of the 1063 pregnancies that were suspected of DIC, a pregnancy with dangerous placenta previa died during cesarean section, interestingly, all four score criteria diagnosed it as non-DIC. The number of hysterectomies, severe neonatal asphyxia, and stillbirth was 22, 25, and 23, respectively. We combined severe neonatal asphyxia and stillbirth as severe adverse outcomes of infants in perinatal. To evaluate the efficacy of the four DIC score standards, we established the ROC curves. The results are suggested in Fig. 4 and Supplementary Table S5, http://links.lww.com/MFM/A101. OCDSS, Takao, Clark, and Erez score systems all have predictive values for hysterectomy (all the P value below 0.001), with a sensitivity of 68.18%, 100.00%, 50.00%, and 72.73%, specificity of 91.07%, 66.57%, 95.97%, and 86.46%, ROC curve areas of 0.872, 0.908, 0.785, and 0.877, respectively. Only the OCDSS score system has relatively good predictive ability for severe neonatal asphyxia and death (P < 0.001), with a sensitivity of 79.17%, specificity of 75.07%, and AUC of 0.842. The Takao, Clark, and Erez score systems have much higher specificity, though with low sensitivity.

To investigate the high-risk factors for hysterectomy, severe neonatal asphyxia, and death, a retrospective analysis was performed, the results and possible influencing factors are shown in Table 4. It should be noted that the patients who had hysterectomy all with underlying diseases, which made the regression model not converge, therefore, we excluded the underlying disease. Finally, the massive blood loss, abnormal platelet count and fibrinogen are the independent risk factors. Using the same method, we found gestational age, birth weight, diagnosis with DIC, abnormal PT, APTT, platelet count and fibrinogen are the high risk of severe neonatal asphyxia or death, lastly, gestational age, birth weight, and abnormal APTT are the independent risk factors.

Characteristics of DIC and non-DIC of participants diagnosed by OCDSS

According to the Chinese expert consensus on the diagnosis and treatment of obstetrical DIC (OCDSS), 29 cases (2.73%) were diagnosed with obstetric DIC, and 1034 cases (97.27%) were diagnosed with non-DIC. There were no statistically significant differences in age, weight, BMI, and parity between the two groups, see Table 5. The number of gravidities in the DIC group was greater than non-DIC group ((3 (2, 4) vs. 2 (1, 3), P < 0.001), and the blood loss in the DIC group was much more than non-DIC group ((2000 (1400, 3250) vs. 300 (200, 400), P < 0.001). The proportion of shock, MODS, and bleeding tendency is much higher in the DIC group than in the non-DIC group (all P < 0.001). The rate of hysterectomy, severe neonatal asphyxia, and death was significantly higher in the DIC group than non-DIC group. The level of PT in the DIC group was much higher than non-DIC group (19.10 (15.45, 33.60) vs. 11.20 (10.60, 12.20), P < 0.001), the level of APTT in the DIC group was much longer than in non-DIC group (45.00 (37.75, 64.65) vs. 29.20 (26.90, 32.40), P < 0.001), the platelet count in DIC group was much lower than in the non-DIC group (51.00 (28.00, 75.00) vs. 124.00 (90.25, 167.00), P < 0.001), the level of fibrinogen in DIC group was much lower than non-DIC group (1.05 (0.60, 1.63) vs. 4.14 (3.57, 4.64), P < 0.001), the level of D-dimer in DIC group was much lower than non-DIC group (20.00 (16.15, 20.00) vs. 3.19 (1.31, 6.08), P < 0.001). The above results suggested that the shock, bleeding tendency, PT, APTT, platelet count, fibrinogen, and D-dimer were appropriate parts of the scoring criteria. Moreover, pregnancies with DIC had an extremely high hysterectomy, severe neonatal asphyxia, and death.

Discussion

Obstetrical DIC is a life-threatening complication that can arise from various obstetrical diseases.23 However, there was no gold standard for obstetrical DIC diagnosis, the diagnostic criteria for it differ globally, which makes early diagnosis difficult and poses great challenges to clinical doctors. To evaluate the obstetrical DIC score system established by Chinese experts (OCDSS), this study included three representative score criteria, named Takao, Clark, and Erez criteria for short. In this study, the incidence of obstetrical DIC based on the Erez standard was extremely high (14.02%, 149/1063), which is more than five-fold higher than the other three standards. Moreover, the incidence of obstetrical DIC has been reported as 0.03% to 0.35% in the general population,1,2 much lower than the Erez standard diagnosis. If pregnancies with obstetrical underlying diseases have such a high incidence of DIC at delivery, the pregnancy and delivery would be too dangerous to choose.

A previous study suggested that many primary diseases that are closely related to obstetrical DIC, such as placental abruption, HELLP syndrome, stillbirth retention, PPH, AFE, preeclampsia, serious infection and acute fatty liver.1242526 Studies also suggested that the Takao standard has high sensitivity in diagnosing DIC with placental abruption, the Clark criteria are especially suitable for pregnant women with AFE,12 and the Erez criteria have more advantages in the diagnosis of patients with placental abruption.1 AFE is the most serious underlying disease in obstetrics and is always associated with severe and acute DIC.27,28 Our study showed that the incidence of DIC with AFE is the highest among these four standards consistently. The second and third highly underlying diseases with obstetrical DIC by OCDSS, Takao, Clark, and Erez criteria identified are retained stillbirth/PPH, placenta abruption/PPH, ICP/placenta abruption, and PPH/retained stillbirth, respectively. Our study proved again that AFE, placental abruption, retained stillbirth, and PPH are the most common triggers for obstetrical DIC.2,28

The incidence of obstetrical DIC with HELLP syndrome was much lower according to four standards consistently in our study. DIC in pregnancies with HELLP syndrome was 15% to 44.4% between the years of 1986 and 1996 and then fell to 5-8% between the years 2000 and 2020.24,29,30 HELLP syndrome posed typical thrombocytopenia, while in the Erez standard, a platelet count < 50 × 109/L only scored as 1 point, 50~100 × 109/L is scored as 2 points.1,4 Thus, the cases of HELLP syndrome would be unable to generate a high score in obstetrical DIC under the Erez standard. Moreover, women with HELLP syndrome also had a higher risk of massive blood loss, placental abruption, and stillbirth.29,30 If this happened, the case would be classified as PPH, placental abruption, or stillbirth retention. In addition, the placental abruption appears to be the underlying mechanism for the development of DIC when associated with fetal death and HELLP syndrome.24,30,31 Therefore, studies proposed that HELLP syndrome is an independent risk factor for obstetrical DIC may be questionable.

In these four standards, only the Takao and OCDSS standards consider the underlying disease, clinical signs, and laboratory tests comprehensively, but these two standards have different emphases on the underlying disease, and the weights assigned to the same indicators are quite different. In the Takao standard, higher scores are placed on clinical parameters rather than on laboratory indicators.11 The Erez score standard only involved laboratory indicators: platelet count, PT difference, and fibrinogen, the level of blood fibrinogen less than 3.0 g/L and PT difference over 1.5 seconds were scored as 25 points, and DIC was diagnosed when the score amounted to 26 points. Thus, the Erez scoring standard may diagnose some pregnancies without underlying disease as obstetrical DIC based on a high score of laboratory results.17 There are 149 cases diagnosed as obstetrical DIC by the Erez standard in this study, the incidence of obstetrical DIC was much higher than the other three standards, which may suggest that the sensitivity of the Erez score standard is the highest. However, the Erez standard also diagnosed 15 cases (10.07%, 15/149) as obstetrical DIC with simple D-dimer abnormality or platelet count abnormality, and even 10 cases without underlying disease were diagnosed as obstetrical DIC. Moreover, the laboratory indicators in obstetrical DIC diagnosed by the Erez standard are not that abnormal, some even close to the normal non-pregnant population. Above all, the Erez standard diagnosed more obstetrical DIC, which may lead to overtreatment and may waste medical resources.

Clinical symptoms, such as shock, MODS, and bleeding tendency are characteristic and very important for a diagnosis of obstetrical DIC. The study by Zhao et al.32 suggested that patients with DIC had significantly higher rates of MODS than those without DIC. Sepsis and other underlying diseases activate the coagulation and trigger extensive fibrin deposition, which depletes coagulation factors and subsequently leads to a tendency for bleeding.33,34 In our study, the shock, MODS, and bleeding tendency contribute to DIC diagnosis, and also have relatively good specificity and extremely high sensitivity. In summary, our research indicates that clinical signs such as shock, multiple organ failure, and bleeding tendency are crucial for the diagnosis of obstetrical DIC.

DIC in non-pregnant individuals is characterized by widespread activation of coagulation pathways, leading to microvascular thrombosis and subsequent bleeding.35 However, in obstetrical DIC, massive bleeding, endothelial activation, and consumptive coagulopathy are the main mechanisms.23 Therefore, the complete and appropriate DIC scoring process should include decreased platelet counts/fibrinogen levels, increased FDP/D-dimer levels, fibrin clotting, and fibrinolysis.28 The Erez and Clark standard did not include FDP or D-dimer levels, the indicators of fibrinolytic activation, while the Takao and OCDSS standard comprehensively considered the indicators of hypofibrinolysis and hypercoagulability. When the Takao score standard and the Japanese classic scoring criteria were used as the “gold standard”, OCDSS scoring criteria demonstrated excellent sensitivity and specificity. Similarly, when the Clark criteria were used as the “gold standard”, OCDSS scoring criteria also showed well sensitivity and specificity. However, in 2017, Erez36 wrote a letter to the editor after the Clark score standard was proposed, he advised the authors to reconsider alternative approaches for the diagnosis of DIC, because the sensitivity of the Clark score standard is relatively low, may lead to most patients who had DIC would not have been diagnosed. Overall, the OCDSS score standard is comparable to the Takao score standards, but the complexity of the Takao score standard is too complex and not conducive to the rapid diagnosis of obstetric DIC in clinical practice.

The weights of different diagnostic score standards on the same laboratory indicator are quite different. PT and APTT are indicators reflecting the consumption of coagulation factors. Erez et al.1 suggested that prolonged PT indicates that the concentration of the coagulation factors is less than 50%. However, Asakura et al.25 thought PT is not a characteristic marker for DIC for it also prolongs liver disease and vitamin K deficiency. The study by obstetrical researchers suggested thrombin generation and clinical worsening, even PT and APTT at a high normal range.37 Our study reveals that the levels of PT and APTT are far beyond the normal range in DIC patients, and these indicators enjoy a good performance in DIC diagnosis with high sensitivity, specificity, and AUC. Decreased platelet count is the most important indicator for DIC diagnosis and can be seen in many diseases.38 Wang et al.39 also suggested platelet count is an important indicator for DIC diagnosis with high sensitivity, and its changes with time are also important. In our study, the platelet count in the DIC group is far lower than the non-DIC group, the sensitivity, specificity, and AUC of platelet count in DIC diagnosis are all very high.

According to the degree of fibrinolytic activation, the DIC in a normal population could be classified into 3 types: enhanced fibrinolysis, suppressed fibrinolysis, and balanced fibrinolysis.40 However, obstetrical DIC is more typically present with bleeding.5,41 During normal delivery, rapid activation of coagulation occurs and peaks 2-3 hours after delivery, reflected as a rapid increase in D-dimer levels,42 while the platelet count and fibrinogen levels remain unchanged.43 This may explain why a pregnancy that dies during cesarean section with massive blood loss may not be diagnosed as DIC by any of the four scoring standards. Patients with placental abruption, AFE, and severe PPH often experience a sharp bleeding speed that easily develops into consumptive coagulopathy, even DIC, the level of fibrinogen is typically less than 0.1 g/L or may even fall below the detectable threshold.12,44,45 The decreased fibrinogen can serve as an early predictor of the severity within hours of massive bleeding, for it usually falls below the normal pregnancy range sooner than other coagulation factors.46,47 The level of fibrinogen below 2.0 g/L is also an independent predictor of hysterectomy and other invasive procedures.48 In our study, the level of fibrinogen in the DIC group was much lower than in the non-DIC group, and fibrinogen was an outstanding contributor to the diagnosis of obstetrical DIC.

D-dimer is an important biomarker of DIC, reflecting the disruption of the dynamic balance between the generation and breakdown of fibrin in the body.49,50 The D-dimer level is elevated in pregnancy, and repeated measurements showing an increase may provide valuable insights. The laboratory indicators we have included are all dynamic tests, and the most abnormal ones are taken. In our study, indeed, the level of D-dimer in the DIC group is significantly higher than non-DIC group (20.00(20.00,20.00) vs. 2.19 (1.31,4.21)), and it shows a good performance in DIC diagnosis with high sensitivity, specificity and AUC (95.83%, 91.17%, 0.971, respectively). Therefore, monitoring the blood coagulation tests dynamically is essential to the diagnosis and treatment of obstetrical DIC. Our study confirms that the levels of platelet count, PT, APTT, D-dimer, and fibrinogen were far beyond the normal range when the OCDSS score exceeded 7 points, which is consistent with previous research.3,11,50

We evaluated the predictive value of four score standards for hysterectomy, severe neonatal asphyxia, and death, and found that the four score standards all have good predictive ability for hysterectomy, while only OCDSS and Takao score standards shared good predictive ability. These results may just prove our judgment, these two standards are relatively better than the other two. Hysterectomy was frequently observed in cases associated with DIC-type postpartum hemorrhage, this type of patient is often accompanied proportional decrease in fibrinogen relative to the volume of bleeding.5,32 Our study also suggested that massive blood loss, decreased platelet count, and fibrinogen are the independent risk factors of hysterectomy. Placental dysfunction and fetal oxygen supply status during delivery are the most common risk of severe neonatal asphyxia.51,52 Our study suggested that gestational age and birth weight are independent predictive indicators of severe neonatal asphyxia and death. Indeed, there is no doubt that gestational age is the most important factor affecting neonatal health.

We emphasize that discrepancies in outcomes across criteria reflect the heterogeneity in diagnostic thresholds rather than inherent clinical reality. The diagnosis of obstetrical DIC requires a comprehensive evaluation based on underlying diseases, clinical manifestations, and laboratory indicators.18 Clinicians should prioritize context-specific criteria and should not overly rely on a single laboratory indicator when applying OCDSS in clinical practice. Setting the laboratory indicator threshold for automatic pop-up reminders and decision support. Actively treat primary obstetric diseases, and supplement blood products based on laboratory tests to correct coagulation function and protect important organs. If necessary, the treatment plan should be jointly decided by the hematologist, intensive care physicians and obstetrics. In addition, emphasize the importance of early identification and timely intervention of obstetric underlying diseases such as placental abruption, stillbirth retention, and so on, strengthen the principle of dynamic and close monitoring, and even use the DIC scoring scale as one of the contents of daily obstetric care.

However, our study still has some limitations. First of all, in view of the low incidence rate of obstetrical DIC and the fact that the research design mainly relies on the clinical data collected retrospectively and other actual accessible data, this study did not conduct a prospective formal efficacy analysis to accurately calculate the sample size needed to detect the size of specific effects, the case span is relatively long and it is difficult to unify the observation standards. In addition, we cannot follow up on the long-term conditions of patients for the retrospective study design. Last and important, our multi-center design may introduce potential heterogeneity in clinical practices, such as criteria for transfusion thresholds, timing of intervention, or postoperative laboratory indicator monitoring frequency. In the future, we will conduct multicenter, larger sample, prospective study to further confirm the diagnostic efficacy of OCDSS.

Conclusion

Various obstetrical underlying diseases, as well as acute massive peripartum hemorrhage without underlying disease can lead to DIC. Paying more attention during peripartum is crucial for timely diagnosis of DIC. OCDSS provides a comprehensive assessment of underlying diseases, clinical manifestations, and laboratory tests, while being readily executable and demonstrating well diagnosis performance. Our study suggested OCDSS facilitates prompt diagnosis of obstetric DIC in the Chinese population. Moreover, the OCDSS score criteria effectively predict adverse outcomes including hysterectomy, severe neonatal asphyxia and death. In the future, prospective multicenter studies should further evaluate this scoring system’s applicability. Finally, we advocate for international consensus efforts to standardize obstetrical DIC definitions. Until such criteria emerge, researchers should predefine context-specify diagnostic frameworks tailored to their population.

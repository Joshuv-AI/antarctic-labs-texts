Visualization of the delivery of a paclitaxel-urea coating matrix by intravascular ultrasound following drug-eluting balloon angioplasty for in-stent restenosis
Authors: Andreas Y Andreou, Dzan Horozic, Alexandru Patrascu, Christoph Jensen
Published: 2026

A 63-year-old man with a history of insulin-dependent type II diabetes mellitus, arterial hypertension, dyslipidemia, and angioplasty of the left anterior descending (LAD) and left circumflex arteries using 3.0 mm × 38 mm and 2.5 mm × 18 mm Resolute Onyx (Medtronic Inc., Minneapolis, USA) zotarolimus-eluting stents, respectively, 10 months prior, presented with unstable angina. Coronary angiography showed in-stent restenosis (ISR) in both arteries (Figure 1A). We proceeded to perform percutaneous coronary intervention of the LAD artery. Lesion predilatation using a 2.5 mm non-compliant balloon inflated at 20 atm was followed by intravascular ultrasound (IVUS; 20 MHz Eagle Eye catheter; Philips) showing stent underexpansion (see Supplementary material online, Video S1) with a minimal stent area as low as 4.0 mm2, and stent inflow and outflow lesions with a minimal lumen area of 5.0 and 3.5 mm2, respectively, and a plaque burden of 60% at both sites. Non-compliant balloon angioplasty resulted in optimal stent expansion (see Supplementary material online, Video S2). The stent inflow and outflow lesions were successfully tackled with direct implantation of 3.5 mm × 20 mm and 2.75 mm × 16 mm Synergy (Boston Scientific Corporation, Marlborough, MA, USA) everolimus-eluting stents across the proximal and distal stent edges, respectively, while the body of the stent was treated with 3.0 mm × 15 mm and 3.5 mm × 20 mm Prevail (Medtronic, USA) paclitaxel drug-coated balloons (DCBs) inflated at 14 atm for 60 s each (Figure 1B). Subsequent IVUS imaging showed an evident change in the neointimal layer of the entire segment treated with DCBs, which appeared hyperechoic, with echogenicity similar to the stent struts (Figure 1C; see Supplementary material online, Video S3). The patient was discharged home in good condition on guideline-recommended medical therapy, including at least 1 year of dual-antiplatelet therapy. However, he was readmitted 5 months later due to unstable angina. Coronary angiography showed preserved luminal patency at the site of previous DCB angioplasty and ISR of the stents implanted across the edges of the old stent (Figure 1D and E). Due to recurrent ISR, the patient underwent double-vessel bypass graft surgery and remained asymptomatic at an 8-month follow-up.

(A) Right anterior oblique (RAO) cranial angiographic view depicting in-stent restenosis (ISR) (arrowhead) of the left anterior descending (LAD) artery. The dashed line indicates the position of the stent within the vessel. The intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) images depict the proximal (A1) and distal (A2) stent edge lesions. (B) RAO cranial angiographic view depicting a good result after percutaneous coronary intervention. The position of the stents implanted across the proximal and distal edges of the stent is indicated by the blue and red dashed lines, respectively. The IVUS images depict optimal expansion of the stents implanted across the proximal (B1) and distal (B2) stent edge lesions. (C) IVUS images of the body of the old stent depicting optimal expansion (C1) after non-compliant balloon angioplasty and a continuous neointimal hyperechoic layer (yellow arrowheads) with similar echogenicity to the stent struts (yellow arrows) after drug-coated balloon (DCB) angioplasty (C2-4). Such an appearance of the neointimal tissue (asterisk) is absent before DCB angioplasty (C1). (D) RAO cranial and RAO caudal (E) angiographic views showing ISR of the stents implanted across the proximal (solid arrow) and distal (dashed arrow) edges of the old stent. Note the preserved luminal patency of the segment spanning between the yellow dots, which was treated with DCB angioplasty. The dashed line in E indicates the position of the stent within the left circumflex (LCx) artery showing functionally significant ISR (arrowheads).

This case demonstrates a striking IVUS finding following DCB angioplasty: a continuous, hyperechoic neointimal layer with echogenicity similar to stent struts. While this appearance is consistent with transfer of the paclitaxel-urea coating matrix to the arterial wall, histopathological confirmation is lacking, and other possibilities (e.g. edematous intima, microthrombi) cannot be excluded. Furthermore, a hyperechoic neointimal layer following DCB angioplasty was linked to preserved luminal patency on follow-up angiography, potentially suggesting efficient delivery of the antiproliferative drug and a reduced risk of ISR.

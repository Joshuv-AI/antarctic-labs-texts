Presenter: Dr Phillip G. Rowse

Dr Richard Shemin(Los Angeles, Calif). Well, very excellent, very clear presentation, and it totally mirrors our experience at University of California at Los Angeles in combined procedures with tricuspid valve regurgitation. So instead of going through the data again, I would just like to ask you a couple of key questions. Number 1: Obviously, the intraoperative transesophageal echocardiogram can very often downgrade your preoperative findings. So, do you make the decision on whether or not to intervene on the tricuspid valve based on the preoperative echocardiogram and continue to follow through with a tricuspid valve repair in spite of what you may find intraoperatively on the day of surgery?

Dr PhillipG.Rowse(Rochester, Minn). Thank you. That's a great question. I think that the decision to repair the tricuspid valve should be made on the preoperative transthoracic echocardiogram. In a fasting state and with general anesthesia there is often a downgrade in the degree of tricuspid regurgitation sometimes by 1 degree. So, we should make the surgical decision to treat the tricuspid valve primarily on the preoperative transthoracic echocardiogram.

Dr Shemin. So let me ask you a couple of intraoperative questions. For cardioplegia, particularly with these relatively longer crossclamp times, and I assume you do both the mitral repair and the tricuspid repair under a single crossclamp. Correct?

Dr Rowse. That's correct.

Dr Shemin. Are you using Del Nido solution, or multidose blood cardioplegia?

Dr Rowse. Multidose Del Nido solution.

Dr Shemin. Okay. And the duration between doses—

Dr Rowse. Plegia dose is typically 40 minutes.

Dr Shemin. Okay. The second thing is venous cannulation. Most people do robotics with a single, long, multistage venous cannula from the groin. Are you putting in a neck superior vena cava cannula so that way you can pull the lower cannula back and snare the cava?

Dr Rowse. That's a great question. We use two venous cannulas. We put a 16 Fr in the right internal jugular vein and position it down to the cavioatrial junction, and then a femoral venous cannula from below, typically a 23 or 25 Fr.

Dr Shemin. And have you actually even tried unclamping and actually doing the tricuspid valve with the heart beating with a vent in the coronary sinus?

Dr Rowse. We have not. We tend to place the annuloplasty sutures with the heart arrested, and then while closing the atrial wall, we'll take the clamp off.

Dr Shemin. The other question, when I looked at your data, was, I believe only about a third of patients had a closure of the left atrial appendage. I know there's a subset of robotic surgeons who believe we should do it every time. I personally close it internally with a double running layer, and I use an interlocking suture on the second layer, and that has almost reduced the recanalization of residual flow in the atrial appendage. Have you all not adopted that?

Dr Rowse. The patients that we suture closed the orifice of the left atrial appendage all had preoperative atrial fibrillation. And all of them also received a left atrial cryomaze maze procedure. We typically don't routinely suture ligate the left atrial appendage in those without a history of atrial arrhythmia.

Dr Shemin. And my final question is: Of the 19 patients who met the criteria for tricuspid valve but only got the mitral valve, was there a reason why that occurred?

Dr Rowse. Some of that is going to be surgeon-dependent, and it's interesting to look at the trend. There was more concomitant tricuspid valve repair in the later years of our study period. But still, a 75% concomitant tricuspid valve repair among patients having at least moderate identifiable tricuspid regurgitation is pretty good.

Dr Shemin. And if I read the data correctly, the 19 that did not get the tricuspid valve repair concomitantly, many of them, over time, did develop residual or even worsening tricuspid valve regurgitation consistent with why we advocate to do it in appropriate cases?

Dr Rowse. That is correct. We had 1 patient transition to severe regurgitation and then a variety that went from mild to moderate.

Dr Shemin. Thank you very much.

Dr Rowse. Thank you.

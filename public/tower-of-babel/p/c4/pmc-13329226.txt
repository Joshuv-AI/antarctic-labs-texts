Letter to the editor: Effect of mechanical bowel preparation with antibiotics and probiotics on postoperative ileus in patients undergoing posterior lumbar spine surgeries
Authors: Akanksha Sharma, Vaishali Gupta
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the randomized controlled trial by Jain et al. [1] entitled “Effect of mechanical bowel preparation with antibiotics and probiotics on postoperative ileus in patients undergoing posterior lumbar spine surgeries,” published in the Asian Spine Journal. The study addresses an important and clinically relevant question regarding the role of mechanical bowel preparation (MBP) in posterior lumbar spine surgery.

The key finding of this trial is the lack of a significant clinical benefit of MBP on postoperative gastrointestinal recovery. Despite the proposed rationale that MBP combined with antibiotics and probiotics may reduce the incidence of postoperative ileus, the study demonstrated no meaningful improvement in MBPrelated outcomes such as time to first passage of flatus or return of bowel sounds. These results suggest that routine MBP may not facilitate postoperative bowel function in patients undergoing limited posterior lumbar spine procedures.

Notably, all reported cases of postoperative ileus occurred exclusively in the MBP group, along with a higher incidence of postoperative hypotension. This observation raises an important clinical consideration that MBP itself may contribute to adverse perioperative effects, potentially through fluid shifts, dehydration, or electrolyte disturbances, rather than providing a protective benefit. Such physiological alterations may negatively influence early recovery and mobilization following spine surgery.

From a clinical standpoint, these findings add to the growing evidence questioning the routine use of MBP in spine surgery. Prior studies have similarly shown minimal benefit of MBP while reporting increased patient discomfort and perioperative complications [2,3]. The present findings therefore support reconsideration of MBP as a standard preoperative intervention for selected posterior lumbar spine surgeries.

In conclusion, the MBP-related outcomes reported by Jain et al. [1] suggest limited clinical utility and possible harm associated with routine MBP in posterior lumbar spine procedures. These results have important implications for perioperative practice and encourage refinement of existing protocols. We commend the authors for their contribution and thank the Editor for the opportunity to participate in this academic discussion.

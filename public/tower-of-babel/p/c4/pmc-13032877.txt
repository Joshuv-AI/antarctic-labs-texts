Will AI-written and AI-reviewed preprints from aiXiv be bibliometrically accepted?
Authors: Jaime A Teixeira da Silva
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe preprint server aiXiv allows documents to be authored and/or peer-reviewed exclusively by generative artificial intelligence (GAI). Journal editors may find themselves in an ethical bind—either embracing preprints and GAI, or sanctioning them, including by banning the citation of GAI-generated knowledge. Will GAI-generated or GAI-reviewed content become indexed in major scientific databases, including in peer-reviewed articles citing it?

Dear Editors,

Bauchner and Rivara1 recently suggested how artificial intelligence (AI) or generative AI (GAI) can be used in peer review. In late 2025, a new preprint server was launched, aiXiv, which permits GAI to not only design and write preprints but also be used in the review of those preprints, substituting human peer reviewers.2 Generative AI is thus already being used to evaluate knowledge and substitute human publishing endeavors. Contrasting attitudes regarding the use of GAI to generate and edit content may cause tension within the academic community, and 2 camps may emerge: those who support GAI-assisted editing and peer review or GAI-edited and peer-reviewed work and those oppose the use of GAI.

Most publishing ethics norms (eg, Committee on Publication Ethics, International Committee of Medical Journal Editors) do not prohibit the declared use of GAI for editing, but undeclared GAI use is difficult to detect because GAI detectors are insufficiently sensitive.3 Articles with undeclared use of GAI tend to be retracted, if detected. Even so, several papers solely authored by GAI appear in scientific databases.4

Generative AI–generated knowledge may appear in scientific databases and platforms (eg, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science) that index peer-reviewed work, including of journals that both allow/do not allow preprints to be cited. Will editors of indexed peer-reviewed journals that allow preprints to be cited allow aiXiv preprints that have been generated and/or reviewed by GAI to be cited, or will such citations be banned? Some editors—critical of the use of GAI in academic publishing—have called for sanctions of those who do not declare their use of GAI.5

Tensions already exist within the academic community regarding the citation of preprints and undeclared use of GAI. An additional layer of ethical and bibliometric complexity arises with preprints derived from aiXiv—openly and exclusively written or reviewed by GAI—that may start to be injected—through citation—into the general scientific body of knowledge, indexed or otherwise.

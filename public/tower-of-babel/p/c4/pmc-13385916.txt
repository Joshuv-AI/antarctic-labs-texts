ABSTRACT
AbstractRelated-party transactions (RPTs) occupy a central position in contemporary corporate governance, serving as essential channels for resource allocation among affiliated business entities. The growing complexity of ownership arrangements and globalized operations has made RPT auditing increasingly challenging for financial professionals. Traditional audit methodologies struggle with intricate enterprise relationship networks that often span multiple jurisdictions and involve layered ownership through shell companies, rendering manual inspection approaches both time-consuming and incomplete. Despite advances in computational approaches, significant gaps persist in current research and practice. Existing audit systems inadequately capture the multi-dimensional relationships connecting enterprises, shareholders, executives, and transactions. Most machine learning methods treat transactions as independent observations, thereby ignoring the relational context that distinguishes legitimate RPTs from irregular ones. Furthermore, the dynamic nature of enterprise relationships demands adaptive models capable of tracking temporal evolution, yet static snapshots of relationship networks may produce outdated or misleading risk assessments. Four critical challenges must be addressed for effective RPT audit automation: modeling heterogeneous entity types linked through diverse relationship categories with different audit implications; capturing temporal dynamics as ownership structures change through acquisitions and personnel movements; providing interpretable outputs that support professional judgment and regulatory inspection; and integrating domain knowledge with data-driven learning in a coherent framework. This study develops an intelligent decision support system that bridges knowledge graph technology with graph neural network architectures to address these challenges. The proposed framework constructs a domain-specific knowledge graph capturing multi-dimensional enterprise relationships through entity resolution and temporal modeling. An enhanced graph neural network employs heterogeneous attention mechanisms alongside temporal fusion components to learn relationship-aware representations. Introduction

Related-party transactions (RPTs) occupy a central position in contemporary corporate governance structures, functioning as essential channels for resource distribution and strategic alignment among affiliated business entities1. The prevalence of such transactions has grown substantially over the past two decades, driven by increasingly complex ownership arrangements and globalized business operations. Yet this growth brings considerable risks. The fundamental information asymmetry between corporate insiders and external stakeholders creates fertile ground for potential manipulation, earnings management, and tunneling behaviors2. When executives or controlling shareholders exploit RPTs to extract private benefits, the consequences extend beyond individual firms to affect market confidence and regulatory stability.

Traditional audit methodologies face mounting pressure when confronted with modern RPT structures. The complexity inherent in enterprise relationship networks, often spanning multiple jurisdictions and involving layered ownership through shell companies, renders manual inspection approaches both time-consuming and incomplete3. Auditors must trace connections across shareholding hierarchies, identify common management personnel, and evaluate transaction pricing against market benchmarks, all while working within tight engagement timelines. The sophistication of concealment strategies employed by motivated parties further compounds these difficulties. Against this backdrop, artificial intelligence and big data technologies offer compelling possibilities for transforming audit practice4. Machine learning algorithms can process vast transaction datasets to identify statistical anomalies, while knowledge representation techniques enable systematic capture of relationship structures that would otherwise require extensive manual mapping.

The academic literature has approached RPT audit challenges from multiple computational perspectives, though these efforts have largely proceeded along separate tracks. Early automation attempts centered on rule-based detection systems that encoded auditor expertise into explicit decision rules5. While such systems provided consistency and reduced certain repetitive tasks, their rigidity limited adaptation to novel fraud schemes and previously unseen transaction patterns. The rules themselves required continuous maintenance as regulatory requirements evolved and new evasion tactics emerged.

Machine learning methods subsequently gained traction within financial fraud detection research, demonstrating measurable improvements in identifying anomalous patterns across various application domains6. Supervised classification approaches, including ensemble methods and gradient boosting algorithms, achieved notable success on labeled fraud datasets. However, these techniques typically treat transactions as independent observations, thereby ignoring the relational context that often distinguishes legitimate RPTs from irregular ones. A transaction between two companies may appear unremarkable in isolation yet become suspicious when viewed within a broader network of affiliated entities engaging in coordinated behaviors.

Knowledge graph technologies have attracted growing research interest precisely because they address this relational dimension7. By representing enterprises, individuals, and their interconnections as structured networks of entities and relationships, knowledge graphs enable forms of relational reasoning that statistical methods alone cannot provide. Entity resolution techniques help consolidate fragmented information about the same real-world entities appearing under different names or identifiers across data sources. Temporal modeling captures how relationships evolve, tracking changes in ownership percentages, board appointments, and transaction patterns over time.

Graph neural networks represent a parallel development that has proven particularly relevant for financial network analysis8. These architectures extend deep learning to non-Euclidean data structures, learning node representations through iterative message passing across graph edges. The resulting embeddings capture both local neighborhood structure and broader network topology. Applications to social network analysis, molecular property prediction, and recommendation systems have demonstrated the versatility of GNN approaches. Several research groups have begun exploring combinations of knowledge representation with neural learning for auditing tasks9. Nevertheless, most existing work addresses isolated aspects of the problem rather than providing integrated solutions that span relationship modeling, dynamic pattern detection, and interpretable decision support10.

Research Motivation and framework overview.

Despite these advances, four critical gaps persist in current research and practice. First, existing audit systems inadequately capture the multi-dimensional relationships connecting enterprises, shareholders, executives, and transactions11. Real-world affiliated networks involve heterogeneous entity types linked through diverse relationship categories, each carrying different audit implications. A shareholding relationship differs fundamentally from a management affiliation or a guarantee arrangement, yet most computational approaches either ignore this heterogeneity or handle it through ad hoc preprocessing. Second, the dynamic nature of enterprise relationships demands adaptive models capable of tracking temporal evolution12. Ownership structures change through acquisitions and divestitures, executives move between companies, and transaction patterns shift in response to business conditions and regulatory scrutiny. Static snapshots of relationship networks miss these dynamics and may produce outdated or misleading risk assessments.

Third, interpretability remains a persistent concern for practical audit applications. Auditors bear professional responsibility for the opinions they render and cannot simply defer to algorithmic recommendations without understanding their basis. Black-box models that achieve high predictive accuracy may nonetheless prove unsuitable for audit contexts where transparent reasoning processes are essential for documentation, review, and regulatory inspection. Fourth, integrating domain knowledge with data-driven learning poses methodological challenges that existing frameworks address only partially. Audit expertise encompasses nuanced judgments about materiality thresholds, industry-specific risk factors, and regulatory requirements that resist straightforward encoding. Effective decision support systems must balance this expert knowledge against patterns discovered through automated analysis.

Motivated by these challenges, this study develops an intelligent decision support system for RPT auditing that integrates knowledge graph technology with graph neural network architectures. The research pursues four interconnected objectives, each yielding specific contributions to both methodology and practice.

The first contribution involves constructing a comprehensive knowledge graph framework specifically designed for enterprise relationship representation. This framework defines a domain ontology capturing entity types relevant to RPT audit, including enterprises, individuals, transactions, shareholdings, executive positions, subsidiaries, guarantees, and fund flows. Multi-source data fusion procedures address inconsistencies across heterogeneous databases through entity resolution algorithms combining name similarity, attribute matching, and structural equivalence measures. Temporal modeling tracks relationship validity periods, enabling the system to reconstruct historical network states and detect relationship changes over time.

The second contribution proposes an enhanced graph neural network architecture tailored for audit risk prediction. The model incorporates heterogeneous attention mechanisms that assign adaptive importance weights based on relationship types, allowing different relationship categories to contribute appropriately to risk assessments. Edge-level features encoding transaction attributes such as amounts, frequencies, and pricing deviations augment the standard node-centric message passing. Temporal fusion components aggregate information across historical snapshots, capturing dynamic patterns in transaction behaviors. The architecture integrates knowledge graph embeddings as semantic priors, providing the neural network with structured domain knowledge that guides learning.

The third contribution addresses interpretability through multiple mechanisms. Attention weight visualization reveals which relationships and neighbors most strongly influence risk predictions for individual transactions. Risk path analysis traces propagation routes through the enterprise network, identifying transaction chains and circular flows that warrant auditor attention. These interpretability features transform the model from a black-box classifier into a decision support tool that augments rather than replaces professional judgment.

The fourth contribution validates the proposed approach through extensive experiments on real-world enterprise transaction data and practical deployment in accounting firm audit engagements. Quantitative evaluation demonstrates substantial improvements over baseline methods across multiple performance metrics. Qualitative case analysis illustrates how the system detects specific types of irregularities, including undisclosed relationships, pricing anomalies, and coordinated transaction patterns. The deployment experience provides evidence regarding practical utility, user acceptance, and integration with existing audit workflows.

Knowledge graph construction and representation learning

Knowledge graphs represent structured knowledge through entity-relationship triplets, enabling machines to understand and reason about complex domain information13. The fundamental structure of a knowledge graph consists of nodes representing entities and edges denoting relationships, forming a heterogeneous network that captures semantic connections14. In the context of enterprise related-party transactions, knowledge graphs effectively model intricate ownership structures, personnel affiliations, and transactional dependencies among corporate entities.

Entity recognition and relation extraction constitute critical steps in knowledge graph construction. Named entity recognition (NER) techniques identify enterprise names, person names, and organizational structures from unstructured audit documents and financial reports15. Relation extraction methods employ pattern matching, supervised learning, or distant supervision to discover relationships such as shareholding, directorship, and transaction partnerships16. Deep learning-based approaches have significantly improved extraction accuracy by capturing contextual semantics and long-range dependencies in textual data17.

Knowledge graph embedding transforms discrete graph structures into continuous vector spaces, enabling computational operations on entities and relations. Related-party transaction audit theory

Related-party transactions are defined as transfers of resources, services, or obligations between entities with control, joint control, or significant influence relationships30. These transactions encompass diverse categories including asset transfers, fund lending, guarantee provisions, pricing arrangements, and management service agreements31. The multiplicity of transaction forms and the concealed nature of affiliated relationships create substantial complexity in audit identification and evaluation.

The primary objective of RPT auditing is to verify transaction authenticity, assess pricing fairness, and ensure adequate disclosure compliance with accounting standards32. However, auditors face significant challenges due to the deliberate concealment of ultimate controlling parties, complex equity penetration structures, and sophisticated transaction routing mechanisms33. The asymmetric information between internal management and external auditors exacerbates detection difficulties, particularly when enterprises intentionally disguise related relationships through multi-layer shareholding networks or offshore entities.

Traditional audit methodologies rely predominantly on manual document examination, ratio analysis, and substantive testing procedures34. These approaches exhibit inherent limitations including excessive time consumption, incomplete relationship identification, and insufficient capability to detect implicit association patterns35. Rule-based detection systems lack adaptability to evolving fraud schemes and cannot effectively process large-scale transaction data or capture dynamic relationship changes in real-time.

Abnormal RPT characteristics manifest through several dimensions: significant deviations from market pricing benchmarks, abrupt increases in transaction frequency or volume, circular transaction patterns among affiliated entities, and timing anomalies coinciding with financial reporting periods36. Transactions involving newly established or offshore-registered counterparties warrant heightened scrutiny, as do agreements lacking substantive commercial rationale.

Graph neural network audit risk prediction model

The proposed model architecture integrates heterogeneous graph attention mechanisms with attention-weighted temporal aggregation components to capture both structural dependencies and dynamic patterns in enterprise transaction networks. Rather than employing convolutional operations over time, the temporal modeling operates through attention mechanisms that learn adaptive weights for different historical time periods, enabling the model to emphasize recent observations while selectively incorporating relevant long-term patterns. We refer to this approach as “snapshot-based temporal attention” throughout this paper to distinguish it from recurrent or convolutional temporal processing methods. The training procedure adopts a transductive full-graph approach with node-level masking rather than mini-batch subgraph sampling. During each training iteration, the complete knowledge graph structure remains fixed while gradients are computed only for transaction nodes within the current batch. Specifically, the batch_size parameter of 64 refers to the number of transaction nodes sampled for loss computation per iteration, not the number of subgraphs. All 127,834 nodes participate in message passing to preserve the global graph structure, but only the 64 sampled transaction nodes contribute to the cross-entropy loss calculation.

Edge feature dimensionality is handled through projection layers that maintain compatibility across the network architecture. The initial edge attributes extracted from relationship metadata vary in raw dimensionality depending on relation type. A shared linear projection layer transforms all edge features to match the input dimension of 128 before entering the heterogeneous attention layers. Within the attention mechanism, the relation-specific weight matrices W_r in Eq. (14) explicitly participate in attention coefficient computation for each relation type, ensuring that shareholding relationships, management affiliations, and transaction links contribute differently to the aggregation process based on their learned importance weights.

For each hyperparameter combination, we trained the model on the training set and evaluated performance on the validation set using macro-averaged F1-Score as the selection criterion. This metric was chosen because it balances performance across all three risk categories, which is essential given the importance of detecting high-risk transactions that constitute the minority class. Experimental verification and application analysis

Dataset construction and experimental setup

The experimental dataset comprises real-world enterprise related-party transaction records collected from multiple authoritative sources including the China Securities Regulatory Commission disclosure database, annual reports of listed companies, industrial and commercial registration systems, and court judgment documents spanning 2018–2024. Data preprocessing involves entity standardization, duplicate removal, missing value imputation, and temporal alignment to ensure consistency across heterogeneous sources. The dataset encompasses 15,847 enterprises with 89,623 related-party relationships and 234,156 transaction records, covering diverse industries including manufacturing, finance, real estate, and technology sectors.

Data annotation follows a three-stage protocol combining automated rule-based labeling, expert manual verification, and cross-validation consistency checking. Initial labels are generated through regulatory violation records and audit opinions indicating non-compliance with disclosure requirements. Three certified public accountants independently review ambiguous cases, with final labels determined by majority voting. Quality control mechanisms include inter-annotator agreement measurement achieving Cohen’s kappa coefficient of 0.87, and systematic sampling validation confirming 94.3% labeling accuracy48. Risk classifications are assigned across three categories: low-risk (normal transactions), medium-risk (requiring attention), and high-risk (suspected irregularities), with class distribution carefully balanced through stratified sampling to address inherent data imbalance.

Rigorous leakage prevention measures ensure that future information does not contaminate model training or evaluation. For knowledge graph construction, temporal edge validity is strictly enforced: an edge connecting two entities only participates in computation if its start_date precedes the transaction date being predicted. When constructing temporal snapshots for a transaction occurring on date d, only edges satisfying start_date ≤ d and end_date ≥ d are included. This prevents the model from accessing relationship changes that occurred after the transaction.

Feature-level leakage control applies to all transaction attributes listed in Table S2.2. The pricing_deviation feature compares transaction prices against market benchmarks computed exclusively from transactions prior to the current transaction date. The frequency_score aggregates historical transaction counts between entity pairs using only records preceding the target transaction. The counterparty_risk score derives from regulatory violations and audit findings documented before the transaction occurrence. The historical_violations count includes only violations with judgment dates preceding the transaction. No feature incorporates information that would have been unavailable to an auditor examining the transaction at its occurrence time.

To validate these leakage prevention measures, we conducted a diagnostic experiment where future edges (those with start_date after the transaction date) were randomly shuffled. Model performance remained stable (F1-Score: 90.08% vs. 90.15%), confirming that predictions do not rely on future relationship information. Additionally, removing all edges established within 30 days after each test transaction yielded identical performance (F1-Score: 90.12%), further demonstrating temporal isolation integrity.

The dataset partitioning strategy employs temporal splitting to simulate realistic audit scenarios where models predict future transaction risks based on historical patterns. Training data comprises transactions from 2018 to 2021 (60% of total), validation set includes 2022 records (20%), and test set contains 2023–2024 data (20%). This temporal division prevents information leakage and evaluates model generalization to emerging transaction patterns. Entity-level splitting ensures that enterprises in test set may have different relationship structures compared to training enterprises, providing rigorous assessment of model transferability. Practical audit case application effects

The proposed intelligent decision support system underwent pilot deployment at a major accounting firm conducting annual audits for publicly traded companies. A representative case involves a manufacturing conglomerate with 287 subsidiaries, 1,543 related-party relationships, and 8,926 transactions during fiscal year 2023. Traditional audit approaches required approximately 450 person-hours for related-party transaction examination, with manual identification of relationship networks and transaction pattern analysis. The knowledge graph construction module automatically extracted entity information from registration documents, financial statements, and disclosure filings, establishing a comprehensive relationship network within 2.3 h of computational processing time.

The application workflow integrates seamlessly into existing audit procedures. Initial data ingestion populates the knowledge graph database through automated parsing of structured financial data and unstructured textual disclosures. The system executes graph neural network inference generating risk scores for all identified transactions, prioritizing high-risk items exceeding threshold values for detailed examination. Auditors receive interactive visualization interfaces displaying entity relationship networks, suspicious transaction chains, and evidence supporting risk assessments. This human-in-the-loop design maintains auditor oversight while leveraging machine intelligence for efficiency enhancement.

As shown in Fig. 7, the system identified 127 transactions warranting detailed investigation, including 23 high-risk cases involving pricing deviations exceeding 40% from market benchmarks, circular fund flows through intermediary entities, and transactions with newly established offshore subsidiaries lacking operational substance. The model successfully detected an undisclosed related-party relationship where a subsidiary’s executive held concurrent positions in a purported independent supplier, exposing a disguised related transaction totaling $15.3 million that traditional sampling methods overlooked.

Abnormal transaction detection achieved 91.7% precision in identifying substantive audit issues, with false positive rate of 8.3% representing cases requiring judgment calls on materiality thresholds. The early warning mechanism flagged suspicious patterns three months prior to fiscal year-end, enabling proactive investigation rather than reactive post-closure adjustments. Critical findings included transfer pricing manipulation in cross-border transactions, guarantee arrangements with inadequate disclosure, and asset transfers at non-arm’s length valuations. The system correctly identified 87 of 94 transactions that subsequent regulatory reviews deemed non-compliant, demonstrating 92.6% recall for regulatory violation detection.

Audit recommendation accuracy verification compared system-generated suggestions with final audit adjustments and regulatory enforcement actions. The intelligent system proposed adjustments totaling 51.8 million after detailed evidence gathering and client negotiation50. The 91.1% alignment rate indicates strong concordance between automated risk assessment and expert judgment. Recommendations prioritized by severity scores correlated strongly with actual financial statement impact, with Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient of 0.88 validating the risk scoring mechanism’s practical utility.

Comparative analysis with manual audit procedures reveals substantial efficiency gains and quality improvements. Audit time measurement follows a standardized protocol: trained timekeepers recorded person-hours spent on each audit phase, with time categories including relationship mapping (identifying and documenting related-party networks), transaction screening (selecting transactions for detailed examination), risk assessment (evaluating transaction irregularity indicators), evidence gathering (collecting supporting documentation), and review procedures (senior auditor verification). The 58.4% time reduction reflects total person-hours across all phases, calculated as (traditional hours - AI-assisted hours) / traditional hours × 100%.

The pilot deployment involved 12 audit engagements conducted over six months, encompassing manufacturing, finance, real estate, and technology sector clients. A true positive in the deployment evaluation was defined as a transaction flagged by the system that, upon subsequent detailed investigation by audit staff, met any of the following criteria: (1) required adjustment to financial statement amounts, (2) necessitated additional disclosure in audit report, (3) was subsequently identified as non-compliant by regulatory review, or (4) was confirmed by client management as an undisclosed related-party arrangement. False positives comprised flagged transactions that, after investigation, were determined to represent legitimate business activities with appropriate pricing and disclosure. The reported 91.7% precision reflects 110 true positives among 120 total flagged high-risk transactions across all pilot engagements.

Discussion

The proposed KG-GNN model demonstrates distinct advantages in addressing the multifaceted challenges of related-party transaction auditing. The integration of structured semantic knowledge with adaptive graph learning enables comprehensive capture of both explicit relationship patterns and implicit transaction anomalies. The model exhibits particular suitability for audit scenarios involving large enterprise groups with complex ownership structures, cross-border transaction networks, and dynamic relationship evolution. Its automated relationship discovery capability proves especially valuable for identifying concealed affiliations that manual procedures frequently overlook, while the risk prioritization mechanism optimizes audit resource allocation by focusing investigative efforts on transactions with highest misstatement probability.

The methodological contribution of this work extends beyond straightforward combination of existing techniques. While individual components draw upon established methods including knowledge graph embedding, graph attention networks, and temporal aggregation, their integration for RPT audit presents novel design choices addressing domain-specific challenges. First, the edge-level attention mechanism in Eqs. (14)-(16) differs from standard GAT formulations by incorporating transaction-specific features such as pricing deviation, transaction frequency, and timing patterns directly into the attention computation. This design reflects the audit domain insight that relationship importance depends not only on entity characteristics but also on the nature of transactions flowing through those relationships. Second, the temporal fusion component employs a hierarchical attention structure where attention weights are learned separately for structural aggregation within snapshots and temporal aggregation across snapshots, enabling the model to capture different dependency patterns at different time scales. Third, the integration of knowledge graph embeddings provides semantic regularization that guides the neural network toward representations consistent with domain ontology, preventing overfitting to spurious statistical patterns that may not generalize across different enterprise contexts.

The ablation study quantifies the contribution of each architectural component, demonstrating that removing knowledge graph embeddings causes the largest performance degradation (6.39% F1-Score reduction), followed by attention mechanisms (4.73%), edge features (3.64%), and temporal fusion (2.86%). These results confirm that the components provide complementary rather than redundant benefits, justifying the integrated architecture.

Generalization experiments across diverse industry sectors and enterprise scales confirm the model’s robustness in handling heterogeneous network structures. Performance remains stable when applied to enterprise groups with relationship densities ranging from sparse (average degree 3.2) to highly connected (average degree 18.7), indicating effective adaptation to varying network topologies. The temporal splitting evaluation demonstrates capability to detect emerging fraud patterns not present in training data, suggesting that learned representations capture fundamental risk characteristics rather than memorizing specific transaction instances. Cross-industry validation reveals consistent effectiveness across manufacturing, finance, and technology sectors despite differences in typical transaction types and relationship structures.

The experimental findings offer significant implications for audit practice evolution. Intelligent decision support systems can transform audit methodology from reactive compliance checking toward proactive risk monitoring, enabling continuous auditing paradigms that provide real-time transaction oversight. The explainable nature of attention-weighted risk assessments facilitates professional judgment integration, allowing auditors to understand and validate automated recommendations rather than accepting black-box predictions. The demonstrated efficiency gains suggest potential for expanding audit scope while reducing costs, addressing the longstanding tension between comprehensive examination desires and resource constraints.

Despite promising results, several limitations warrant acknowledgment. Model performance depends heavily on data quality and completeness; incomplete relationship disclosure or data entry errors propagate through the knowledge graph affecting downstream predictions. The current framework focuses primarily on transactional and relational features, with limited incorporation of textual information from audit notes and management explanations that often provide critical context. Adversarial robustness remains unexplored, raising concerns about deliberate manipulation by sophisticated actors aware of model detection mechanisms. Future improvements should address these limitations through enhanced multi-modal learning, adversarial training strategies, and integration of external economic indicators for contextual risk assessment enrichment.

Conclusion

This research develops an intelligent decision support system for enterprise related-party transaction auditing by integrating knowledge graph technology with graph neural networks. The study constructs a comprehensive knowledge graph framework capturing multi-dimensional enterprise relationships, proposes an enhanced graph neural network model incorporating heterogeneous attention mechanisms and temporal fusion components, and designs interpretable algorithms for anomaly detection, risk path tracking, and audit recommendation generation. Experimental validation on real-world enterprise transaction data demonstrates superior performance, achieving 92.63% accuracy and 90.15% F1-Score, substantially outperforming traditional machine learning and conventional graph neural network approaches.

The primary innovations include: first, a domain-specific ontology model and multi-source data fusion methodology enabling systematic representation of complex enterprise relationship networks; second, an attention-enhanced graph neural network architecture integrating knowledge graph embeddings with edge-level transaction features for comprehensive risk assessment; third, intelligent decision support algorithms providing interpretable audit recommendations through attention weight visualization and risk path analysis. These contributions advance the theoretical foundation of AI-enabled financial auditing while addressing practical challenges in related-party transaction oversight.

The research delivers significant theoretical value by establishing a formal framework connecting knowledge representation, graph learning, and audit decision-making, demonstrating how symbolic knowledge and neural learning can synergistically enhance financial intelligence applications. Practical implications include transforming audit methodology from sampling-based examination toward comprehensive risk-based monitoring, reducing audit time by 58.4% while expanding coverage and improving detection accuracy. The system enables proactive fraud prevention through early warning mechanisms and supports regulatory supervision through automated compliance checking.

Limitations include dependency on data quality and completeness, limited incorporation of textual audit evidence, and unexplored adversarial robustness against deliberate manipulation attempts. Future research directions encompass: integrating natural language processing for textual audit document analysis, developing adversarial training strategies for robust fraud detection, incorporating external economic indicators and industry benchmarks for contextual risk assessment, extending the framework to cross-border transaction monitoring and supply chain finance auditing, and investigating federated learning approaches enabling collaborative audit intelligence while preserving enterprise data privacy. Continuous model updating mechanisms adapting to evolving fraud schemes warrant further investigation to maintain long-term effectiveness in dynamic regulatory environments.

The dataset is openly available at the public GitHub repository https://github.com/UHUxwPKBNh/Intelligent-Audit-Decision-Support-for-Enterprise-Related-Party-Transactions. All enterprise identifiers have been anonymized to protect commercial confidentiality while preserving graph structural properties. The materials are released under the MIT License and are freely accessible without restriction to all readers, ensuring full reproducibility of the experimental results reported in this paper.

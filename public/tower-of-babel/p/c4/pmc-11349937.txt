Diversity analysis of panicle traits in Chinese prickly ash germplasm resources and their influence on its systematic classification
Authors: Xixi Dong, Lin Shi, Shuqin Bao, Hao Fu, Yuming You, Xiaoying Li, Yun Ren, Qiang Li, Zexiong Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to reveal the diversity and variation in panicle traits of the Chinese prickly ash and clarify their influence on the its systematic classification to provide a theoretical basis and technical support for the efficient utilization of Chinese prickly ash germplasm resources and breeding. Sixteen panicle traits were identified from 35 Chinese prickly ash germplasm resources from 2021 to 2022. The diversity of these panicle traits and their role in the plant’s systematic classification were studied using variance, correlation, cluster, and principal component analyses. Cluster analysis showed that the 35 Chinese prickly ash germplasm resources could be divided into two groups with Euclidean distances of 25. Further analysis showed that yield traits such as panicle length, panicle width, primary branching, grain number per panicle, and grain weight per panicle were significantly positively correlated with grain chlorophyll content, while grain anthocyanin content was negatively correlated with both panicle (panicle length, panicle width, panicle length to width ratio, primary branching, grain number per panicle, and grain weight per panicle) and grain characteristics (single grain weight, thousand-grain weight, grain length, grain width and fruit shape index). In conclusion, Chinese prickly ash germplasms have diverse panicle traits. Z. armatum has dark green grains, long and wide panicles, a long conical shape, many primary branches, high grain weight, and high grain number per panicle. In contrast, Z. bungeanum has bright red seeds, a panicle width larger than its length, short and conical panicles, a small number of primary branches, and low grain weight per panicle and number of grains per panicle. Overall, Z. armatum had a significant yield advantage over Z. bungeanum.Keywords: Chinese prickly ash, Germplasm resources, Panicle traits, Diversity, System classificationSubject terms: Evolution, Genetics, Physiology, Plant sciences

Introduction

Zanthoxylum belongs to the family Rutaceae and is widely distributed in Asia countries, including China, Korea, Japan and India. Over 250 species to date have been identified in Zanthoxylum genus across the world, of which the most well-known species are Zanthoxylum bungeanum (Red Huajiao) and Zanthoxylum armatum (Green Huajiao)1. Prickly ash (Zanthoxylum) plays an important role in barren mountain management, ecological environment protection, poverty alleviation, and rural revitalization owing to its developed root system, drought and barren resistance, and strong climate adaptability. Furthermore, it has become an important tree species in China's economic forest industry2–4. Prickly ash originated in the central and western regions of China and is now widely cultivated throughout the country. The domestication process from wild-type to cultivated species was affected by climate, soil, humans, and other factors, resulting in continuous and stable variation, forming many varieties with obvious differences in traits5. However, the selective breeding of prickly ash is limited by the lack of germplasm resources, breeding techniques, and professionals, especially the low utilization efficiency and unclear genetic relationships of germplasm resources, backward improvement in technology, low breeding efficiency, and failure of the selected varieties to keep up with production demand6.

As one of the key trait categories of crop species, panicle traits are not only closely related to crop yield, but are also crucial to its systematic classification7. Therefore, analysis of the diverse panicle traits of prickly ash germplasm resources can not only clarify the status of various qualities in systematic classification, but also improve the utilization efficiency of prickly ash germplasm resources and accelerate the breeding process. The primary panicle traits of crops include panicle, grain, and pigment characteristics. Yuan et al.8 divided 36 japonica rice varieties into three groups: tight panicle type, middle panicle type and scattered panicle type by panicle characteristics. Likewise, Zhang et al.9 divided 321 wheat varieties into three similar groups using a combination of panicle and grain characteristics: medium grains per panicle and medium grain quality, multiple more grains per panicle and medium grain quality and less grains per panicle and larger grain quality groups. Su et al.10 divided 16 Idesia polycarpa materials into four groups based solely on panicle characteristics: medium fruit medium sparse panicle medium stalk type, small fruit medium sparse panicle long stalk type, large fruit long sparse panicle medium stalk type, and medium fruit short dense panicle medium stalk type. Systematic classification is the basis for the utilization of crop planting resources. Ma et al.11 divided 72 prickly ash germplasm resources into four types according to their quality: bamboo leaf prickly ash, north prickly ash, south prickly ash, and other varieties of prickly ash found in the Shandong and Hebei provinces of China. Zheng used Q-type and R-type cluster analyses and found that while the genetic relationships between 21 prickly ash germplasm resources did not show regional differences, there was a significant correlation between phenotypic traits12. In addition, Dong et al.13 found significant differences in leaf phenotypic traits among prickly ash germplasm resources, among which the coefficient of variation in the number of pinnate compound leaves was the highest and the coefficient of variation in leaf thickness was the lowest. Luo et al.14 highlighted that the phenotypic traits of green prickly ash germplasm resources in Southwest China are significantly different, albeit with different degrees of variation.

At present, there are few studies on the systematic classification of prickly ash re-sources either in China or abroad, restricting their utilization and development to a certain extent. Moreover, previous studies have primarily focused on differences in quality traits15–17, chemical components18–20, and leaf phenotypes13 of prickly ash grains. Therefore, to the best of our knowledge, there has been no research on the diversity of panicle traits of prickly ash. By analyzing the diversity of panicle traits in prickly ash germplasm resources, their differences were clarified. Moreover, the correlations between panicle traits and their influence on the systematic classification of prickly ash was explored to provide a theoretical basis and technical support for the efficient utilization of prickly ash germplasm resources and breeding of new varieties.

Differences in grain characteristics among various prickly ash germplasm resources

The single grain weight, thousand-grain weight, grain length, grain width and fruit shape index of different prickly ash germplasm resources were significantly different (Tables 3 and 4), and the interaction effect of year and variety on the grain characteristics of prickly ash was extremely significant (p < 0.01, Table S2). The average single grain weight, 1 000-grain weight, grain length, grain width and fruit shape index of 35 prickly ash germplasms in 2021 were 0.09 g, 90.40 g, 4.92 mm, 4.63 mm and 1.06, respectively. Among them, TZJ and MXHJ had the largest single grain weight of 0.12 g, and CCSJ had the smallest single grain weight of 0.06 g. The largest 1000-grain weight and grain length were TZJ, which were 119.72 g and 6.04 mm, respectively, and the smallest were RBHJ and YZ4H, respectively. The widest grain width is TTJ, up to 5.75 mm, and the narrowest is YZ4H, up to 3.34 mm. The largest fruit shape index was YZ3H, which was 1.17, and the smallest was FEJ, which was 0.89.

The main identifying characteristics, based on the panicle traits, were panicle length, with an average of 6.75 cm, and width, with an average of 5.08 cm, both significantly larger than those of the other group. Moreover, the panicle type was more pointed and had a long conical shape, with an average panicle length-to-width ratio was 1.36. The number of primary branches, grain number per panicle, and grain weight per panicle were higher than those of the other group at values of 16.72, 80.82, and 7.19 g, respectively. The second category included 17 varieties, such as XLJ, MXHJ, and ZYWC, all of which are red prickly ashes and show representative panicle traits of this group. The main feature of this group is short and narrow panicles with an average panicle length of 4.00 cm and panicle width of 4.20 cm, both of which were significantly smaller than those of the first category. Furthermore, the panicle type was closer to a short cone shape, with an average panicle length–width ratio of 0.98. The average grain length was 5.20 mm, grain width was 4.82 mm, and fruit shape index was 1.08. The contents of chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, total chlorophyll, and carotenoid were 24.67%, 31.22%, 26.14%, and 17.06% lower than those of the green prickly ash group, respectively, while the anthocyanin content was 21.93% higher.

Based on these characteristics, the panicles of the green prickly ash germplasm were long and wide, the number of primary branches, number of grains per panicle, and grain weight per panicle were higher than those of the red prickly ash germplasms, while the grains were larger. The panicle characteristics of the red prickly ash germplasm were short and narrow, and the panicle type was short and conical. Generally, the values of the red prickly ash grain characteristics were lower than those of green prickly ash. In addition, the grains were smaller than that of green prickly ash, while the anthocyanin content was higher.

Principal component analysis of the panicle traits of prickly ash

Principal component analysis

Principal component analysis (Tables S4 and S5) was performed on the 16 panicle traits of the prickly ash germplasms. The principal components were screened according to the principle that the eigenvalues were greater than or equal to 1 or the cumulative contribution rate was greater than 85%. Five principal components were identified. Their eigenvalues were 7.11, 2.46, 2.02, 1.67, and 1.07, respectively, and their cumulative contribution rates reached 89.51%. Therefore, it can be considered that these five principal components can act as the primary representative characteristics of the 16 traits of the 35 prickly ash germplasm resources.

The contribution rate of the first principal component is 44.42%. Comparing the absolute values of each feature vector, it can be seen that the first principal component was mainly affected by panicle length (0.85), number of primary branches (0.84), number of grains per panicle (0.82), grain weight per panicle (0.86), chlorophyll a content (0.82), chlorophyll b content (0.85), and total chlorophyll content (0.86), primarily reflecting the index characteristics related to panicle morphology and photosynthetic pigment content of the prickly ash. The contribution rate of the second principal component was 15.35% and was mainly affected by grain length (0.89) and grain width (0.76), reflecting the index characteristics related to the grain size of prickly ash. The contribution rate of the third principal component was 12.60% and was mainly affected by single grain weight (0.73) and thousand-grain weight (0.73), reflecting the index characteristics related to the grain weight of prickly ash. The contribution rate of the fourth principal component was 10.45% and was mainly affected by the number of primary branches (0.43) and carotenoid content (-0.43), reflecting the index characteristics related to panicle morphology and grain color. The contribution rate of the fifth principal component was 6.69% and was primarily affected by panicle width (0.49) and panicle length–width ratio (− 0.68), reflecting the index characteristics related to the panicle shape of prickly ash. According to the contribution rate of the first principal component, seven representative indexes, including panicle length (0.85), number of primary branches (0.84), number of grains per panicle (0.82), grain weight per panicle (0.86), chlorophyll a content (0.82), chlorophyll b content (0.85) and total chlorophyll content (0.86), were screened. The absolute value of the eigenvectors was higher than 0.80, suggesting this component was a key factor causing the differences in panicle traits among prickly ash germplasm resources and could be used as the main index for screening and identification of panicle traits of prickly ash germplasms.

Comprehensive evaluation

The comprehensive scores of the panicle traits of the 35 prickly ash germplasm lines were calculated and ranked (Table S6). The top 10 comprehensive scores of panicle traits were all observed in the green prickly ash, and the top 20 all belonged to the green prickly ash category, except for MXHJ, ZYWC, and ZYDHP. The number of primary branches, grain number per panicle, and grain weight per panicle of green prickly ash were significantly higher than those of red prickly ash. Therefore, the scores of green prickly ash in the comprehensive evaluation of panicle traits were generally higher and ranked at the top. The ranking results of the principal component factor scores were consistent with those of the cluster analysis. Namely, the prickly ash germplasm resources can be divided into two categories by using the characteristics of panicle traits. One was green prickly ash with the highest score for the principal component factor, and the other was red prickly ash with the lowest score.

Materials and methods

Overview of test site

The experiment was conducted in the Chinese Prickly Ash Expo Garden of Yingshan county, Sichuan province from 2021 to 2022. The germplasm resources tested were all prickly ash plants that had entered their peak production period more than five years prior. The test area is in a subtropical humid monsoon climate, with an average annual temperature of 17.3 °C, average annual precipitation of 1085 mm, average annual sunshine of 1409 h, and average annual frost-free period of 301 days. The test area has purple soil, and the top 30 cm of soil has the following basic fertility: total nitrogen 1.05 g/kg, total phosphorus 0.86 g/kg, total potassium 2.04 g/kg, available nitrogen 29.40 mg/kg, available phosphorus 17.01 mg/kg, available potassium 422.66 mg/kg, organic matter 15.18 g/kg, and pH 6.78.

All prickly ash samples were identified by prickly ash team from Chongqing University of Arts and Sciences and are stored at the College of Smart Agriculture, Chongqing University of Arts and Sciences, located in Yongchuan, Chongqing, China. Experimental design

Five plants of the same growth status and normal harvested were randomly selected from each germplasm for sampling. The sampling and determination methods for panicle traits were as follows.

Briefly, the panicles were sampled from five points in the middle of the bearing branch, and five panicles were collected from each plant. The panicle length, panicle width, number of primary branches, number of grains per panicle, grain weight per panicle, grain length, grain width, ratio of panicle length to width (panicle length/panicle width), fruit shape index (grain length/grain width), fresh fruit single grain weight (single panicle grain weight/single panicle grain number), and fresh fruit thousand grain weight [(single panicle grain weight/single panicle grain number) × 1000] were measured and calculated according to Fu’s21 method.

Determination of chlorophyll

The absorbance of the grain chlorophyll extract at 470, 646, and 663 nm was determined by spectrophotometry, and the chlorophyll a, chlorophyll b, total chlorophyll, and carotenoid contents were calculated using the Arnon22 formula. Each treatment was repeated three times and an average value was calculated.

Determination of anthocyanin content

According to the method described by Chen et al.23, the absorbance values (OD) of the extract at 530, 620, and 650 nm were determined using an ultraviolet–visible spectrophotometer. Each treatment was repeated three times, and the average value was calculated.

Discussion

The variability and stability of phenotypic traits result from interactions between plant genotypes and environmental factors27. Phenotypic variation in plants reflects their adaptability to the environment. Typically, the richer the phenotypic variation, the stronger the adaptability to the environment28. In general, if the coefficient of variation is greater than 10%, the difference between samples is considered large29. The results of this study showed that the 16 panicle traits of prickly ash germplasm resources, except for grain length, grain width, and fruit shape index, all showed coefficients of variation higher than 10%. Among them, the coefficients of variation of the traits related to prickly ash yield (i.e., grain number per panicle and grain weight per panicle) were the highest at greater than 40%, indicating that the yield of prickly ash germplasm resources differed. There were large panicle and high yield traits among germplasms examined, suggesting a foundation for the breeding of high-yield prickly ash varieties. Among the 16 panicle traits, the average coefficient of variation of panicle traits was 31.03%, which was the highest among the three indices. The second was pigment characteristics, with an average coefficient of variation of 23.04%. Finally, the average coefficient of variation of grain characteristics was the lowest at only 8.81%. This indicates that the variations in panicle and pigment characteristics among prickly ash germplasm resources were large, whereas variation in grain characteristics was relatively small. The representative indexes of the 7 panicle traits of prickly ash screened by principal component analysis included both panicle characteristics (panicle length, number of primary branches, number of grains per panicle, and grain weight per panicle) and pigment characteristics (chlorophyll a content, chlorophyll b content, and total chlorophyll content).

The identification and classification of Z. bungeanum germplasm resources form the basis of the selection process for breeding30–32. Xu highlighted that Jiuyeqinghuajiao differs from the classification groups of Z. bungeanum and Z. armatum. Specifically, it has relatively independent and stable morphological characteristics, and is divided into varieties of Z. armatum33. Song classified the plants of the Zanthoxylum genus in Sichuan and Chongqing into five groups based on their genetic relationships: Zanthoxylum group, Xianke Zanthoxylum group, Dahua Zanthoxylum group, Leibo Zanthoxylum group, and Xiaohua Zanthoxylum group34. Dong et al.13 found that the phenotypic traits of prickly ash leaves are significantly different, which could provide a basis and reference for the early morphological identification of prickly ash varieties. In this study, cluster analysis of 35 pricky ash germplasm resources using 16 panicle traits showed that the resources could be divided into two groups at a Euclidean distance of 25. The first category included 18 varieties, including TZJ, YNQHJ, and MLJ, all of which are green prickly ash varieties with long, wide, conical panicles and higher numbers of primary branches, numbers of grains per panicle, and grain weight per panicle than the second category. The second category included 17 varieties, including XLJ, MXHJ, and ZYWC, all of which are red prickly ash. In this category, the panicle width was greater than the panicle length, and the panicle type was short and conical. The grain characteristics were lower than those of green prickly ash; however, the anthocyanin content was higher and the color was more vivid. It is consistent with the results of previous classification using the panicle characteristics of prickly ash35. The grains of green prickly ash were dark green, the panicles were long, wide, and long conical, the number of primary branches was large, and the principal component factor scores of the traits of grain weight per panicle and grain number per panicle were used to rank the 35 prickly ash germplasm resources. The 35 prickly ash germplasm resources were divided into two categories: green prickly ash, with the highest score of the main component factor, and red prickly ash, with the lowest score of the main component factor. This is consistent with the current classification system of prickly ash which divides prickly ash into green prickly ash (autotetraploid) and red prickly ash (allotetraploid) The grains of the green prickly ash were dark green, the panicle was long and wide, long conical, the number of primary branches was large, and the grain weight and grain number per panicle were high. The grains of red prickly ash were bright red, the panicle width was larger than the panicle length, the panicles were short and conical, the number of primary branches was small, and the grain weight and number per panicle were low.

The panicle traits of prickly ash are not only an important factor affecting its systematic classification, but also a key factor in determining yield7. Many studies have shown that the yields of green and red prickly ash are significantly different, with the yield of green prickly ash being significantly higher than that of red prickly ash1,36,37. Li38 pointed out that the thousand-grain weight of Dechang green prickly ash was 19.83 g, while the thousand-grain weight of red prickly ash was 11.96 g. Compared to red prickly ash, green prickly ash has stronger tree vigor and more branches, resulting in a higher yield39. Likewise, the results of this study showed that the yield indices, such as the number of primary branches, number of grains per panicle, and weight of grains per panicle, of green prickly ash were significantly better than those of red prickly ash. The average number of primary branches, number of grains per panicle, and weight of grains per panicle of 18 green prickly ash were 16.72, 80.82 and 7.19 g, respectively, while those of 17 red prickly ash were only 10.15, 38.00, and 3.27 g, respectively. The former were 64.68%, 112.68%, and 120.27% higher than the latter, respectively. It shows that green prickly ash has a significant yield advantage over red prickly ash, which is consistent with the results of Li et al.1,37,38. Correlation analysis showed that there was a significant positive correlation between chlorophyll content and yield traits, such as panicle length, panicle width, primary branch number, grain number per panicle, and grain weight per panicle. Anthocyanin content was negatively correlated with both panicle characteristics (panicle length, panicle width, ratio of panicle length to width, primary branch number, grain number per panicle, and grain weight per panicle) and grain characteristics (single-grain weight, thousand-grain weight, grain length, grain width, and fruit shape index), indicating that grain color had an important effect on the yield of prickly ash. This was further confirmed by the difference in yield performance between green and red prickly ash. The grain number per panicle and grain weight per spike were significantly positively correlated with spike length, spike width, and the number of primary branches, which is consistent with the results of Zhu et al.16 on Dingtan prickly ash, indicating that the size of the prickly ash panicle directly affects the grain number per panicle and grain weight per panicle, thereby influencing its yield.

Conclusions

The 16 panicle traits of the 35 prickly ash germplasm resources showed significant differences and a rich diversity. Panicle length, number of primary branches, number of grains per panicle, grain weight per panicle, chlorophyll a content, chlorophyll b content, and total chlorophyll content can potentially be used as representative indicators for screening and evaluating panicle traits in prickly ash germplasm resources. Based on their panicle traits, prickly ash germplasms can be divided into two groups. The first category included 18 varieties, such as TZJ, YNQHJ, and MLJ, all of which are green prickly ash with dark green grains, long and wide panicles, a long conical shape, many first-order branches, a high grain weight, and a high grain number per panicle. The second category included 17 varieties, such as XLJ, MXHJ, and ZYWC, all of which are red prickly ash with bright red grains. These varieties have panicles that are broader than they are long, featuring a short conical shape, fewer primary branches, lower grain weight, and fewer grains per panicle. The green prickly ash have a significant yield advantage over the red ones. Correlation analysis showed that yield traits such as panicle length, panicle width, number of primary branches, grain number per panicle, and grain weight per panicle were significantly positively correlated with grain chlorophyll content, which was negatively correlated with panicle characteristics (panicle length, panicle width, ratio of panicle length to width, primary branches, grain number per panicle, and grain weight per panicle) and grain characteristics (single grain weight, 1000-grain weight, grain length, grain width, and fruit shape index).

FTO/m6A mediates miR-138-5p maturation and regulates gefitinib resistance of lung adenocarcinoma cells by miR-138-5p/LCN2 axis
Authors: Dongxiao Ding, Wenjun Shang, Ke Shi, Junjie Ying, Li Wang, Zhongjie Chen, Chong Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLung cancer (LC) occupies an important position in the lethality of cancer patients. Acquired resistance to gefitinib in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) seriously affects the therapeutic efficacy of LC. Thus, it is of major scientific and clinical significance to probe the mechanism of gefitinib resistance in LUAD for ameliorating the prognosis of patients.MethodsThe expression of miRNAs in gefitinib-resistant LUAD cells was validated using qRT-PCR. Cell viability was assessed through CCK-8, whereas cell death was examined through PI staining. Changes in the ferroptosis process were evaluated by detecting the intracellular Glutathione (GSH), Malondialdehyde (MDA), and Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) levels. Downstream targets of miR-138-5p were verified via luciferase reporter and RNA pull-down assays. RIP and qRT-PCR were employed to evaluate pri-miR-138-5p binding to DiGeorge critical region 8 (DGCR8) and the pri-miR-138-5p m6A modification level. Additionally, the impact of fat mass and obesity-associated protein (FTO) on LUAD gefitinib sensitivity was assessed in vivo by constructing a xenograft model.ResultsWe observed that miR-138-5p was notably diminished in gefitinib-resistant cells. Overexpression of miR-138-5p suppressed viability while facilitated cell death and intracellular ferroptosis in gefitinib-resistant cells. Moreover, lipocalin 2 (LCN2) was the downstream target of miR-138-5p. The biological functions of miR-138-5p on gefitinib-resistant cells was reversed by introduction of LCN2. FTO suppressed the binding of DGCR8 to pri-miR-138-5p through m6A modification, thereby restraining the processing of miR-138-5p. Meanwhile, silencing of FTO enhanced the sensitivity of LUAD to gefitinib treatment.ConclusionFTO suppressed the processing of miR-138-5p and then modulated the proliferation, death, and ferroptosis of gefitinib-resistant cells through the miR-138-5p/LCN2 pathway, which may put forward novel insights for clinically ameliorating the therapeutic effect of gefitinib in LUAD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-024-13036-5.Keywords: FTO, miR-138-5p, Gefitinib, Ferroptosis, LUAD

Introduction

As a malignant tumor with extremely high clinical lethality, lung cancer (LC) poses a great public health challenge to humans worldwide [1]. According to different pathological types, LC is usually divided into non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small cell lung cancer [2]. NSCLC accounts for about 80% of the total number of LC, while lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most common type of diagnosis in NSCLC [3]. Currently, although platinum-based chemotherapy drugs have greatly improved the efficacy of LUAD, the therapeutic benefit is still limited [4, 5]. As molecular biology evolves by leaps and bounds, it has been discovered that certain gene mutations contribute to the malignant progression of tumors, and these genes are also called drive genes [6]. Studies have found that mutated epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is the common and crucial tumor driver gene in LUAD development [7–9]. Therefore, targeting EGFR can specifically block the oncogenic signaling caused by it and impede the tumor progression. As a first-generation EGFR-tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs), gefitinib can block the intracellular tyrosine kinase domain of EGFR by competitively binding to the adenosine triphosphate (ATP) site of the receptor, and then suppress downstream signal transduction [10]. However, the acquired drug resistance to gefitinib remains a significant impediment limiting its clinical advancement [11]. Thus, clarifying the mechanism of gefitinib resistance contributes to overcome and delay the occurrence of drug resistance, which has significant scientific and clinical significance for the subsequent treatment of LUAD patients.

Ferroptosis is a newly discovered cell death mode in recent years, which is manifested as programmed death driven by iron-dependent lipid peroxidation and reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation [12]. Increasing studies have confirmed that resistance to radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and targeted therapy are associated with ferroptosis, and inducing ferroptosis can reverse the formation of resistance [13]. For example, ATF3 elevation enhanced the sensitivity of gastric carcinoma cells to cisplatin through inducing ferroptosis [14]. Besides, a study reviewed that induction of ferroptosis was the underlying approach to overcome drug resistance of breast cancer cells [15]. In the field of LUAD, the discovery of ferroptosis also provides novel insights into the treatment of LUAD and overcoming drug resistance. The formation of drug resistance in LUAD is a complex process modulated by multiple genes and multiple signaling pathways. Among them, the destruction of the redox internal environment of tumor cells is the key to the formation of drug resistance [16, 17]. Emerging studies have disclosed that LUAD cells can apparently enhance their oxidative stress defense capability by negatively regulating ferroptosis, thereby inducing LUAD drug resistance [18, 19]. Under the drug-resistant environment, triggering ferroptosis is expected to reshape the intracellular metabolic microenvironment, reduce the tolerance of tumor cells to oxidative stress and overcome drug resistance. Recently, an increasing number of studies have linked gefitinib resistance to ferroptosis. The latest evidence indicated that dihydroartemisinin induced the production of ROS in LUAD gefitinib-resistant cells, which in turn enhanced the sensitivity of cells to gefitinib via ferroptosis pathway [20]. Similarly, targeting the NEAT1_1/miR-338-3p/AKR1C1 axis suppressed ferroptosis defense in LUAD gefitinib-resistant cells, thereby reversing gefitinib resistance [21]. However, current research on the mechanism of ferroptosis-mediated gefitinib resistance in LUAD is still in its infancy. As a result, in-depth study of the intrinsic link between ferroptosis and gefitinib resistance is expected to provide novel strategies for overcoming gefitinib resistance in LUAD.

Epigenetics has always been a research hotspot in the life sciences field, and its abnormal modification may contribute to the tumorigenesis and advancement of cancers [22]. Among them, m6A modification, as the most common and widespread internal transcriptional modification in eukaryotic noncoding RNAs (ncRNAs) and messenger RNAs (mRNA), has become the focus of common attention [23, 24]. The function of m6A modification is jointly achieved by methyltransferases (writers), demethylases (erasers) and binding proteins (readers). Methyltransferases consist of components such as METTL3, METTL14, and WTAP [25]. Demethylases include FTO and ALKBH5 [25]. The existence of these methyltransferases and demethylases enables m6A modification reach a dynamic and reversible balance. Importantly, emerging evidences revealed that aberrant regulation of m6A modification in non-coding RNA (miRNA, lncRNA, circRNA) or mRNA is closely related to tumor drug resistance [26]. For instance, Pan et al. reported that METTL3 enhanced the resistance of breast cancer cells to adriamycin by promoting pri-miR-221-3p maturation via a m6A-dependent manner [27]. Besides, Wang et al. indicated FTO enhanced bortezomib resistance in multiple myeloma cells by m6A-dependent destabilization of SOD2 [28]. However, the regulatory mechanisms of m6A modification in gefitinib resistance remain elusive and deserves further exploration.

In this study, we identified that miR-138-5p downregulation was responsible for the acquisition of gefitinib resistance in LUAD cells. Meanwhile, overexpressing of miR-138-5p promoted the sensitivity and ferroptosis of gefitinib-resistant LUAD cells by targeting LCN2. Additionally, we observed that FTO regulated the processing of miR-138-5p in an m6A-dependent manner. Collectively, we put forward a novel regulatory mechanism for gefitinib resistance and proposed that targeting miR-138-5p may contribute to the augmentation of sensitivity in gefitinib-treated patients.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and treatment

Two human LUAD cell lines (A549 and H1650) were purchased from American Type Culture Collection (USA). The cells were grown in RPMI-1640 medium (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Sangon Biotech, China) at 37 °C in an incubator with 5% CO2. As previously described [29], the gefitinib-resistant LUAD cell lines (A549/GR and H1650/GR) were established through culturing LUAD cells with stepwise-increasing concentration of gefitinib (Selleck, USA). Subsequently, the above gefitinib-resistant cells were cultured in the medium containing 1 µM gefitinib to retain its drug resistance. Afterward, the establishment of gefitinib-resistant cells was confirmed using CCK-8 assay.

qRT–PCR

Total RNA from LUAD and LUAD gefitinib-resistant cells was extracted with TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). Then 1 µg RNA was reverse transcribed to cDNA by PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Takara, Japan). Next, qRT-PCR was accomplished using SYBR Premix Ex Taq (Takara, Japan) in the ABI 7500 real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, USA). Target gene expression was normalized to GAPDH or U6 through the 2−△△Ct method. Related primer sequences were presented in Table 1.

Cell transfection

For oligonucleotide transfection, miR-138-5p and NC mimics were synthesized by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). Before transfection, LUAD gefitinib-resistant cells were cultured in 6-well plates (2 × 105 cells/well) until the density reached 80%. Then cells were transfected with the above oligonucleotides using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA) as suggested by manufacturer’s requirements. After 48 h of transfection, the cells were harvested for subsequent analysis. For lentiviral transfection, lentivirus-oe-FTO (oe-FTO) for stable overexpression of FTO, lentivirus-sh-FTO (sh-FTO) for stable silencing of FTO and corresponding controls (oe-NC and sh-NC); lentivirus-oe-LCN2 (oe-LCN2), lentivirus-oe-METTL3 (oe-METTL3), lentivirus-oe-METTL14 (oe-METTL14), lentivirus-oe-WTAP (oe-WTAP), lentivirus-oe-ALKBH5 (oe-ALKBH5) and their negative controls (oe-NC) were designed and constructed by GenePharma. Then these Viruses were used to infect gefitinib-resistant cells to mediate indicated genes stable expression following manufacturer’s protocol.

Western blot

The successfully transfected gefitinib-resistant LUAD cells were harvested and lysed with RIPA buffer (Sigma, USA). Then, BCA protein detection kit (Beyotime, China) was utilized to assess protein concentration. Next, protein samples (30 µg) were separated by SDS-PAGE and shifted to PVDF membranes (Millipore, USA). Subsequently, these membranes were sequentially treated with primary and secondary antibodies. At last, the protein signals were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Cell viability assay

CCK-8 kit (Dojindo, Japan) was employed to assess cell viability. Parent cells and gefitinib-resistant cells were seeded into 96-well plates (5000 cells/well) and cultured overnight. Then, cells were treated with or without different doses of gefitinib for 48 h as required. Next, cells in each well were incubated with 10 µL CCK-8 solution for 2 h at 37 °C prior to analysis. Finally, the absorbance of each well was detected at 450 nm via a microplate reader. Besides, the IC50 value was evaluated through the relative survival curve.

Propidium iodide (PI) staining

PI staining kit (Keygen Biotech, Nanjing, China) was performed to measure the cell death. Before staining, approximately 2 × 106 cells were washed with PBS three times. Then the cells were incubated with PI staining solution for 20 min, followed by counterstaining with DAPI. Next, the images were taken under a fluorescence microscope. The numbers of PI positive cells were normalized to the nuclei numbers revealed by DAPI.

Bioinformatics analysis

Potential downstream targets of miR-138-5p were predicted by searching online TargetScan (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/), miRDB (http://www.mirdb.org/) and ENCORI (https://rnasysu.com/encori/index.php) databases.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Wild-type (WT) or mutant-type (MUT) sequences of LCN2 3’UTR were cloned into the pmirGLO vector (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) for constructing desired luciferase reporter vectors (WT-LCN2 and MUT-LCN2). Next, A549/GR and H1650/GR cells were transfected with the above luciferase reporter vectors together with miR-138-5p mimics or miR-NC in 96-well plates. After 48 h, the luciferase activity was measured using a dual-luciferase reporter assay system (Promega, USA).

Biotinylated-miRNA pulldown assay

RNA pull-down was employed to further confirm the interaction between miR-138-5p and LCN2 mRNA. Briefly, approximately 2 × 106 A549/GR and H1650/GR cells were transfected with biotinylated miR-138-5p mimics (biotin-miR-138-5p, GenePharma, China) or miR-NC (biotin-miR-NC, GenePharma) for 48 h. Afterward, the cells were harvested and lysed with lysis buffer. Then, the lysates were incubated with M-280 streptavidin magnetic beads (Invitrogen, USA) for 3 h at 4 °C. Next, the beads were washed using precooled lysis buffer. Finally, TRIzol was used to purify the bound RNA, followed by analyzing the enrichment of LCN2 through qRT-PCR.

Measurement of glutathione (GSH) and Malondialdehyde (MDA) levels

The transfected A549/GR and H1650/GR cells were gathered and lysed with RIPA buffer. Then the BCA kit (Invitrogen, USA) was employed to measure protein concentration. Based on manufacturer’s protocols, the intracellular GSH levels were examined via a Glutathione assay kit (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) through detecting cell absorbance at 412 nm. Meanwhile, MDA concentration was validated via a lipid peroxidation MDA assay kit (Abcam, Cambridge UK) by measuring cell absorbance at 532 nm.

Determination of reactive oxygen species (ROS)

The ROS assay kit (BeyoTime, China) using fluorescent probe 2′, 7′-dichlorfluorescein-diacetate (DCFH-DA) was employed to determine the intracellular ROS levels in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells. Based on the manufacturer’s guidance, 5 × 105 cells were collected and incubated with 10 µM DCFH-DA at 37 °C for 20 min away from the light. Next, the cells were rewashed with serum-free medium to remove free DCFH-DA. Finally, the ROS fluorescence was analyzed through flow cytometry.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

For DGCR8-pri-miR-138-5p and m6A-pri-miR-138-5p binding experiments, the Magna RIP RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Millipore, USA) was employed in RIP assay. Based on the manufacturer’s requirements, stably transfected oe-FTO or sh-FTO and negative controls (oe-NC or sh-NC) A549/GR and H1650/GR cells were harvested and lysed in RIP lysis buffer. Next, the cell lysate was incubated with magnetic beads conjugated with anti-DGCR8 antibody (Abcam, USA), anti-m6A antibody (Abcam, USA) or anti-immunoglobulin G (IgG, Millipore, USA) as a negative control at 4 °C overnight. Then the bead-antibody complex was treated with 150µL proteinase K and RNA was extracted through TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). Finally, the immunoprecipitated RNA was analyzed through qRT-PCR.

Animal models

All animal experiments were authorized by the Ethics Committee of People’s Hospital of Beilun District. Fifteen male BALB/c nude mice (age: 6–8 weeks) were purchased from Vital River (Beijing, China). These mice were randomly divided into 3 groups (n = 5 for each group): control group; gefitinib (GE) + sh-NC group; gefitinib (GE) + sh-FTO group. The A549/GR cells stably expressing sh-NC or sh-FTO were constructed by infection with corresponding lentivirus vectors. Then 1 × 106/ml (100 µL) cells were injected into the right axilla of nude mice. One week after injection, nude mice in the gefitinib group were injected with 10 mg/kg gefitinib every 7 days through tail vein. The tumor volumes were evaluated from week 0 to week 4, calculated by length × width2 × 0.5 using a vernier caliper. After 4 weeks, all mice were euthanized by intraperitoneal injection of sodium pentobarbital (100 mg/kg), then tumors were resected. Meanwhile, the tumors were photographed, followed by monitoring the tumor weight.

Statistical analysis

The experiments were conducted at least triplicates and the data were presented as mean ± SD. Statistical analysis was carried out with GraphPad Prism 8.0 software. Student’s t-test or one/two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) were employed for comparison between two or multiple groups. P < 0.05 presented statistical significance.

Results

The successful establishment of gefitinib acquired resistance cells

In order to probe the molecular and biological mechanisms of gefitinib resistance in LUAD, we first constructed two gefitinib-resistant LUAD cell lines (named as A549/GR and H1650/GR) by gradually elevating the concentration of gefitinib in the culture medium. Subsequently, we conducted CCK-8 assay to verify the established acquired drug-resistant strains. The results were shown in Fig. 1A-B, after treatment with higher concentrations of gefitinib, the cell proliferation activity of drug-resistant cells (A549/GR and H1650/GR) was markedly higher than that of parental cells (A549 and H1650). Additionally, the IC50 of each group of cells against gefitinib showed that the IC50 of A549/GR (15.40 µM) was higher than that of A549 (7.53 µM); meanwhile, the IC50 of H1650/GR (61.67 µM) was higher than that of H1650 (25.38 µM).

The successful establishment of gefitinib acquired resistance cells (A549/GR, H1650/GR). (A-B). CCK-8 assay was employed to examine the cytotoxic effects of gefitinib in acquired gefitinib-resistant LUAD cell lines (A549/GR and H1650/GR) and their parental cells (A549 and H1650)

MiR-138-5p was notably diminished in gefitinib-resistant cells

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a class of small noncoding RNAs. Emerging studies have disclosed that miRNAs are engaged in crucial biological processes such as cell growth, metastasis, and apoptosis, which performed key regulators in tumorigenesis and advancement [30]. Notably, miRNAs have also been validated to be responsible for the acquired resistance of multiple drugs and radiation [31]. Here, we reviewed the literature [32–39] and screened some miRNAs with significant acquisition resistance in lung cancer as research objects (such as cisplatin resistance, paclitaxel resistance and radiation resistance). Then we wondered whether theses miRNAs are also engaged in regulating the resistance of LUAD cells to gefitinib, including miR-125b-5p, miR-138-5p, miR-144-3p, miR-216b-5p, miR-379-5p, miR-493-5p, miR-524-5p, and miR-526b-3p. So, we examined the expression of these miRNAs in A549, A549/GR, H1650 and H1650/GR cells by qRT-PCR. Among them, we observed that miR-138-5p was substantially diminished in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells, whereas other miRNAs were not significantly changed (Fig. 2A-B). Thus, we presumed that miR-138-5p may perform a vital regulator in the formation of gefitinib resistance in LUAD cells. In addition, we evaluated the expression of previously reported miR-138-5p-related molecules in gefitinib-resistant cells, including Homeobox A4 (HOXA4) [40] and G protein-coupled receptor124 (GPR124) [41]. The results indicated that HOXA4 expression was downregulated in gefitinib-resistant cells (A549/GR and H1650/GR) compared with parental cells (A549 and H1650), while GPR124 was upregulated. Moreover, HOXA4 overexpression enhanced miR-138-5p expression in gefitinib-resistant cells, and GPR124 expression was negatively regulated by miR-138-5p (Fig.S1A-D).

MiR-138-5p was notably diminished in gefitinib-resistant cells. (A-B). qRT-PCR was conducted to examine the expression of different miRNAs in the gefitinib-resistant LUAD cell lines (A549/GR、H1650/GR) and their parental cells (A549、H1650). ***P < 0.001

Overexpression of miR-138-5p suppressed the viability and facilitated the death as well as ferroptosis of gefitinib-resistant cells

In order to reveal the effect of miR-138-5p in gefitinib-resistant LUAD cells, we first transfected miR-138-5p mimic into A549/GR and H1650/GR cells to overexpress miR-138-5p (Fig. 3A). Then we examined the impacts of miR-138-5p upregulation on the growth of gefitinib-resistant cells. CCK-8 assay exhibited that miR-138-5p overexpression remarkably mitigated the viability of A549/GR and H1650/GR cells (Fig. 3B). Meanwhile, PI staining results revealed that introduction of miR-138-5p apparently enhanced the cell death of A549/GR and H1650/GR cells (Fig. 3C-D). These data disclosed that miR-138-5p was responsible for gefitinib sensitivity in LUAD cells. Recently, numerous studies have linked ferroptosis with resistance to cancer therapy. Induction of ferroptosis can reverse drug resistance in tumor cells and is a crucial mechanism of tumor suppression [13]. Therefore, in view of our finding that miR-138-5p overexpression mitigated gefitinib resistance in LUAD, we wondered whether miR-138-5p is engaged in the process of ferroptosis in LUAD resistant cells. To validate our hypothesis, we evaluated the ferroptosis signatures of A549/GR and H1650/GR cells after miR-138-5p introduction. According to previous reports, ferroptosis is a mode of cell death induced by excessive accumulation of iron-dependent lipid peroxides, accompanied by GSH depletion and ROS generation [12]. Moreover, MDA is one of the major end products of lipid peroxidation, its level change is also an important feature reflecting the process of ferroptosis [12]. So, we measured the GSH, MDA, and ROS levels in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells after transfecting with miR-138-5p or miR-NC. Interestingly, the results on these indicators exhibited that miR-138-5p overexpression markedly diminished GSH levels (Fig. 3E), while enhanced intracellular MDA (Fig. 3F) and ROS (Fig. 3G) levels. Collectively, these findings disclosed that miR-138-5p is crucial for regulation of ferroptosis in LUAD/GR cells.

LCN2 functioned as the target of miR-138-5p in gefitinib-resistant cells

Notably, miRNAs perform their biological functions by binding to 3′-UTR of target gene mRNAs and inhibiting their expression [42]. To explore the potential substrates of miR-138-5p to exert biological functions, we focused on the mRNAs that are associated with LUAD progression and can interact with miR-138-5p through bioinformatics analysis as well as literature review [43–47]. Then, it was revealed that the 3′-UTR of GAB2, LCN2, SYT13, SIX5, and PER2 have potential binding sites for miR-138-5p. Nevertheless, whether these genes engaged in the gefitinib acquired resistance remains unclear. So, we assessed the impacts of miR-138-5p introduction on the expression of GAB2, LCN2, SYT13, SIX5, and PER2 in gefitinib-resistant cells. Interestingly, the results exhibited that overexpressing of miR-138-5p notably declined LCN2 levels in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells, whereas the expression of other genes did not change obviously (Fig. 4A-B), indicating that miR-138-5p inversely regulated LCN2 expression. Moreover, the results of bioinformatics analysis presented that the 3′-UTR of LCN2 contained the specific binding sites for miR-138-5p (Fig. 4C). Furthermore, we continued to validate the binding relationship between miR-138-5p and LCN2 through RNA pull down and luciferase reporter experiments. RNA pull down indicated that biotinylated miR-138-5p captured more LCN2 than biotinylated control in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells (Fig. 4D-E). Luciferase reporter assay exhibited that luciferase activity was substantially mitigated in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells after cotransfection of LCN2-WT and miR-138-5p mimic (Fig. 4F-G). Therefore, these experimental results proved that LCN2 was the target of miR-138-5p. Above all, the findings revealed that miR-138-5p directly and negatively targeted LCN2 in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells.

LCN2 overexpression abolished the impacts of miR-138-5p on gefitinib-resistant cells

The above studies validated the interaction between miR-138-5p and LCN2, then we adopted a series of rescue experiments to identify whether miR-138-5p regulated growth and ferroptosis of gefitinib-resistant cells by targeting LCN2. According to the purpose of the experiments, the groups were designed as follows: miR-NC group; miR-138-5p group; miR-138-5p + oe-NC group; miR-138-5p + oe-LCN2 group. We first overexpressed LCN2 in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells using pcDNA-LCN2 (Fig. 5A). CCK-8 assay exhibited that miR-138-5p introduction notably diminished the cell viability, whereas this suppressive effect was partially rescued by overexpressing LCN2 (Fig. 5B). PI staining displayed that when miR-138-5p and LCN2 were upregulated simultaneously, the enhancement of cell death induced by miR-138-5p was abolished in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells (Fig. 5C-D). These results disclosed that the acquired resistance to gefitinib in LUAD cells could inhibited by miR-138-5p and promoted by LCN2. As drug-resistant cells often develop EMT (epithelial to mesenchymal transition) phenotype [48, 49], we analyzed EMT-related molecular marker changes in gefitinib-resistant LUAD cells by western blot analysis. The results were shown in Fig.S1 E-F, miR-138-5p overexpression decreased the levels of Vimentin, N-cadherin, increased the level of E-cadherin in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells, but this effect was reversed by LCN2 upregulation, indicating miR-138-5p suppressed the EMT of gefitinib-resistant LUAD cells by targeting LCN2. Additionally, we examined the changes of ferroptosis features in different experimental groups. As displayed in Fig. 5E-G, the promoting effect of miR-138-5p on ferroptosis of A549/GR and H1650/GR cells was reversed by LCN2 upregulation, as evidenced by the LCN2 overexpressing induced the augmentation of GSH levels and attenuation of intracellular MDA and ROS levels. The above results disclosed that miR-138-5p suppressed growth and facilitated ferroptosis of gefitinib-resistant LUAD cells by targeting LCN2.

FTO regulated the processing of miR-138-5p in an m6A-dependent manner

As a well-known epigenetic modification in recent years, m6A is closely correlated with cancer development [23, 24]. The latest reports revealed that the biogenesis and function of miRNAs are regulated by m6A modification, thereby affecting the development of cancer, including acquisition of resistance [26]. Therefore, since we validated the key role of miR-138-5p in gefitinib-resistant LUAD cells, we wondered whether miR-138-5p was regulated by m6A modification-related enzymes. To validate this conjecture, we first detected the expression of m6A-related enzymes in A549, A549/GR, H1650, and H1650/GR cells by qRT-PCR. Among them, we observed that the expression of METTL3 and FTO was enhanced in gefitinib-resistant cells relative to their parental cells, while the expression of other m6A-related enzymes had little obvious change. Notably, the expression of FTO was substantially upregulated in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells (Fig. 6A-B). Afterward, we examined the regulatory relationship between these m6A regulatory enzymes and miR-138-5p. After constructing cell lines stably overexpressing these m6A regulatory enzymes through lentivirus infection, we verified the transfection efficiency at the mRNA level. The results displayed that levels of METTL3, METTL14, WTAP, FTO, and ALKBH5 were apparently elevated in A549/GR and H1650/GR cells (Fig. 6C-D). Subsequently, we revealed that overexpressing of FTO caused a substantial reduction in miR-138-5p levels, whereas upregulation of other m6A-related enzymes had no apparent influence on miR-138-5p levels (Fig. 6E-F). Meanwhile, we confirmed that miR-138-5p levels were obviously elevated (Fig. 6H) after silencing FTO expression (Fig. 6G). These results indicated that FTO expression was inversely associated with miR-138-5p in gefitinib-resistant cells. Importantly, recent studies have confirmed that m6A methylation modification can promote DGCR8 protein to recognize and bind primary miRNA (pri-RNA) more efficiently, thereby increasing the expression of mature miRNA; on the contrary, m6A demethylation modification functions oppositely [50, 51]. Considering that FTO is a major m6A demethylase, and we validated that FTO negatively modulated mature miR-138-5p expression, so we further verified whether FTO regulated the processing of miR-138-5p in an m6A-depent manner. We performed RIP experiments with DGCR8 antibody in overexpressing/silencing FTO cells, then detected the expression of pri-miR-138-5p by qRT-PCR to evaluate the effect of FTO on the binding of DGCR8 to pri-miR-138-5p. As anticipated, the results exhibited that DGCR8 binding to pri-miR-138-5p was substantially diminished in FTO-overexpressing cells, suggesting that FTO overexpression could inhibit DGCR8 recognition and binding to pri-miR-138-5p. Meanwhile, silencing of FTO promoted DGCR8 binding to pri-miR-138-5p (Fig. 6I-J). Furthermore, after RIP experiments with m6A antibody in overexpressing/silencing FTO cells, we evaluated the expression of pri-miR-138-5p to identify the influence of FTO on m6A modification of pri-miR-138-5p. The results indicated that the enrichment of m6A-modified pri-miR-138-5p was notably declined in the FTO-overexpressing group, revealing that FTO upregulation could suppress the m6A modification of pri-miR-138-5p. Also, FTO silencing functioned oppositely (Fig. 6K-L). Collectively, these findings disclosed that FTO-mediated m6A demethylation regulated the processing of miR-138-5p.

FTO silencing sensitized LUAD to gefitinib treatment

The above studies disclosed that FTO mediated the maturation of miR-138-5p and then activated the regulation of gefitinib resistance via miR-138-5p/LCN2 axis, suggesting that FTO performed as a potential regulator in the acquired resistance of gefitinib in LUAD. Thus, we further evaluated the effect of FTO on LUAD gefitinib resistance in vivo through animal experiments. We constructed the xenograft tumor model in BALB/c nude mice using A549/GR cells with stable low expression of FTO, and designed the following groups: control group; gefitinib (GE) + sh-NC group; gefitinib (GE) + sh-FTO group. The results of animal experiments were exhibited in the Fig. 7A-C. Tumor size was significantly reduced in gefitinib-treated group by comparison with control group, meanwhile, the tumor size was further reduced in the co-transfected sh-FTO group (Fig. 7A). In addition, changes in tumor weight (Fig. 7B) and volume (Fig. 7C) were consistent with the trend of tumor size. These data identified the crucial role of FTO silencing in reversing gefitinib resistance of LUAD.

Discussion

Molecular targeted therapy for tumors has gradually become the first-line drug choice for clinical treatment of tumors due to its high specificity, strong effectiveness, and low side effects. A growing number of related drugs have entered clinical applications. Gefitinib, as the earliest LUAD-targeted drug approved for marketing, presents good efficacy in LUAD patients during initial treatment. However, the majority of patients inevitably develop resistance in the later stages of treatment, leading to therapeutic failure and hindering the continued application of gefitinib. Although multiple mechanisms of acquired resistance to gefitinib have been characterized, including target gene mutation, bypass signal activation, and histological transformation [52]. However, the cell signaling transduction involved in drug resistance to gefitinib is complex, its drug resistance mechanism in LUAD still deserves further research and elucidation. The present study revealed a novel molecular mechanism regulating gefitinib resistance at the epigenetic level, aiming to provide better therapeutic benefits for clinical gefitinib-resistant LUAD patients.

Recently, noncoding RNAs (miRNAs etc.), have been characterized as crucial epigenetic players in the pathogenesis of malignancies. As a class of highly conserved endogenous RNA, miRNA widely exists in eukaryotes, and its abnormal expression performs a vital role in the occurrence, development, metabolism, and drug resistance of tumors [30, 31]. Importantly, numerous studies have evidenced that miRNAs are involved in the generation and maintenance of gefitinib resistance in lung cancer [53]. For example, miR-762 upregulation contributed to the acquired resistance to gefitinib in NSCLC cells [54]. Suppression of miR-873 diminished the sensitivity of NSCLC cells to gefitinib via targeting GLI1 [55]. In this research, we first constructed the gefitinib-resistant cell lines A549/GR and H1650/GR. To explore the potential miRNA in modulating gefitinib resistance, we selected some miRNAs that were identified by previous studies to be related to the acquired resistance of lung cancer. Subsequently, we verified the expression of these miRNAs in gefitinib-resistant cells by qRT-PCR. Interestingly, we observed that miR-138-5p was significantly diminished in gefitinib-resistant cells, so we speculated that miR-138-5p may be responsible for the formation of gefitinib resistance in LUAD cells. Notably, a report indicated that miR-138-5p was substantially declined in NSCLC gefitinib-resistant cells (PC9/GR), which was consistent with our results [41]. In order to further validate whether miR-138-5p is associated with acquired resistance to gefitinib, we overexpressed miR-138-5p in gefitinib-resistant cells and observed that miR-138-5p upregulation restrained viability and promoted death of gefitinib-resistant cells. These findings clarified that miR-138-5p serves an inhibitory role in the gefitinib resistance of LUAD. However, its specific mechanism remains unclear.

Ferroptosis is a cell death phenomenon discovered by Dixon in 2012, which is a cell death mode driven by iron-dependent lipid peroxide accumulation. Since then, research on ferroptosis in tumor biology and cancer therapy has made good progress. Among them, ferroptosis has been evidenced to perform a key role in the process of drug resistance of various tumors, and the drug resistance of tumor cells can be reversed by inducing ferroptosis. Recent studies have reported that gefitinib-resistant lung cancer cells are more sensitive to ferroptosis induction. Dihydroartemisinin [20] and botulin [56] treatment augmented gefitinib cytotoxicity against LC cells by inducing ferroptosis. Meanwhile, a research indicated that Carbonic Anhydrase IX inhibitor triggered gefitinib-resistant cell ferroptosis, thereby enhancing sensitivity of LC cells to gefitinib treatment [57]. These studies illustrated that induction of ferroptosis is an effective approach to overcome gefitinib resistance. Meanwhile, emerging non-coding RNAs (miRNAs, etc.) have been validated to be crucial regulators of ferroptosis induction [58]. For example, miR-6077 [19] and miR-324-3p [59] were responsible for regulation of cisplatin resistance in LUAD cells via inducing ferroptosis. Nevertheless, the potential molecular mechanism of miRNA-mediated ferroptosis in regulating gefitinib resistance of LUAD remains largely unknown. Here, we investigated whether miR-138-5p implicated in the ferroptosis process in LUAD gefitinib-resistant cells. Interestingly, we observed that miR-138-5p upregulation facilitated gefitinib-resistant cell ferroptosis, accompanied by enhanced MDA and ROS levels as well as suppressed GSH levels in gefitinib-resistant cells. These findings disclosed that miR-138-5p could regulate the acquired resistance to gefitinib by inducing ferroptosis in LUAD. Additionally, whether miR-138-5p achieved the specific mechanism of overcoming drug resistance by inducing ferroptosis in drug-resistant cells remains to be further explored.

Considering that miRNAs generally exert their biological functions by targeting and silencing the expression of their target genes, we conducted further research on the regulatory role of miR-138-5p in gefitinib-resistant cells. Based on bioinformatics analysis and literature review, we found that LCN2 may be the downstream target of miR-138-5p, which was validated by luciferase reporter and RNA pull down experiments. It is reported that LCN2 was a adipose secretion factor [60], and is regarded as a carcinogenic role in various malignancies (including LUAD [61]), and its high expression always predicted poor prognosis. Moreover, multiple studies disclosed that LCN2 was also a crucial ferroptosis-resistant gene [62], while LCN2 knockdown could trigger ferroptosis [63]. These research demonstrated that LCN2 performed significant biological effects in LUAD and ferroptosis, while its regulatory mechanism in LUAD gefitinib-resistant cells remains elusive. Thus, according to the above research background, we designed rescue experiments to verify whether the miR-138-5p/LCN2 axis was engaged in regulating the growth and ferroptosis process of gefitinib-resistant LUAD cells. As expected, it was identified that miR-138-5p augmented the viability and ferroptosis in gefitinib-resistant cells by inhibiting LCN2 expression. Collectively, these observations lead us to infer that miR-138-5p regulated gefitinib-resistant of LUAD cells by triggering LCN2-mediated ferroptosis.

Then we continued to disclose the underlying molecular mechanism of miR-138-5p in gefitinib-resistant cells at the epigenetic level. Studies have indicated that m6A modification, as the most abundant epigenetic modification in eukaryotic RNA, is widely involved in various biological processes of RNA metabolism, including mRNA export, translation, splicing, and miRNA processing [64]. Among them, m6A modification promotes the activation of miRNA biological functions, which has attracted our attention. Studies have identified that m6A modification can affect tumor development and drug resistance acquisition by promoting miRNA processing and maturation, such as METTL3 facilitated nasopharyngeal carcinoma malignant phenotype by regulating m6A-dependent pri-miRNA-19a processing [65]. Besides, FTO accelerated bladder cancer growth via mediating the processing of miR-576 via an m6A-dependent manner [66]. Here, to reveal whether the biological function of miR-138-5p is regulated by m6A modification, we detected the expression pattern of m6A-related regulatory factors in LUAD cells and LUAD gefitinib resistant cells. Interestingly, we observed that only FTO and METTL3 were increased at the RNA level, and FTO was significantly upregulated. Then we validated the effects of these m6A-regulated enzymes on miR-138-5p. In particular, only FTO overexpression notably caused changes of miR-138-5p levels. Upregulation of FTO apparently mitigated the level of miR-138-5p, whereas downregulation of FTO performed opposite effect, suggesting that the expression of FTO was inversely associated with miR-138-5p. Then we used RIP experiments to further probe whether FTO regulated the maturation of miR-138-5p. Mechanistic studies disclosed that miR-138-5p processing was regulated by FTO-mediated m6A modification, which inhibited the recognition of pri-miR-138-5p by DGCR8 and suppressed the production of mature miR-138-5p. In addition, we also conducted animal experiments to confirm the impacts of FTO on gefitinib resistance in vivo, and found that FTO silencing sensitized LUAD to gefitinib treatment. Taken together, these studies revealed that FTO knockdown mitigated the gefitinib resistance in LUAD by promoting processing of mature miR-138-5p, and targeting FTO is a potential strategy for reversing gefitinib resistance.

Conclusion

Taken together, our study disclosed that miR-138-5p is a crucial regulator of gefitinib resistance in LUAD. MiR-138-5p suppressed the growth and facilitated the ferroptosis of gefitinib-resistant LUAD cells by targeting LCN2. In addition, we identified that FTO inhibited the processing of pri-miR-138-5p through an m6A-dependent manner in gefitinib-resistant cells, thereby decreasing miR-138-5p levels. Silencing FTO sensitized LUAD to gefitinib treatment. The FTO-miR-138-5p-LCN2 pathway innovatively revealed in this study might offer a novel direction and potential targets for reversing gefitinib resistance.

Flow-targeted lumen remodeling with aneurysmectomy in salvage of dysfunctional arteriovenous fistula: a case report
Authors: Mian Wang, Bin Kuang, Jihong Lian, Chaohui Zhang, Minghui Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aneurysmal dilation represents a complex complication of arteriovenous fistulas (AVF) with significant clinical implications including high-output heart failure and functional impairment. We report a 46-year-old male with end-stage renal disease who developed maximum diameter 40 mm aneurysmal dilation of the cephalic vein 42 months after brachiocephalic fistula creation, with flow volume reaching 2,680 mL/min. We employed an innovative flow-targeted lumen remodeling technique combined with aneurysmectomy, utilizing computational fluid dynamics modeling to achieve optimal hemodynamic control. Using a 20Fr silicone catheter as an intraluminal template, the venous diameter was precisely reduced from 35 mm to 7 ± 1.0 mm, successfully decreasing flow volume to 850 mL/min. The patient resumed dialysis within 24 h postoperatively without requiring central venous catheterization. Three-month follow-up demonstrated sustained functional patency with maintained flow volume of 880 mL/min and normal venous pressure. This technique represents a paradigm shift in complex vascular access salvage, offering superior outcomes compared to conventional methods while preserving autogenous access and avoiding prosthetic materials.

Introduction

AVF serves as the preferred vascular access for hemodialysis [1]. However, its long-term complications occur in up to 50–70% of cases. Aneurysmal dilation represents one of the most complex complications, with an incidence rate ranging from 5 to 60% [2].

The causes of aneurysmal dilation are multifactorial. Following AVF creation, arterial blood is directly diverted into the venous system, subjecting the vein to abnormal high-pressure and high-flow impact [3]. The risk of aneurysmal dilation significantly increases when blood flow exceeds 2 L/min. Uremic patients commonly present with pathological changes including vascular calcification and endothelial dysfunction, leading to reduced vascular wall elasticity and increased susceptibility to dilative deformation [4]. Surgical technical factors such as oversized anastomosis or inadequate venous arterialization can also promote aneurysmal dilation [5]. Moreover, repeated puncture procedures during long-term hemodialysis, particularly improper puncture techniques, can cause localized vascular wall injury and inflammatory responses [5]. Furthermore, venous outflow obstruction such as central venous stenosis leads to elevated venous pressure, further accelerating the formation and progression of aneurysmal dilation [5].

Aneurysmal dilation can lead to high-output heart failure, dialysis dysfunction, local compressive symptoms, rupture hemorrhage risk, and steal syndrome [6]. Traditional treatment modalities such as prosthetic graft replacement are associated with high infection rates and low patency rates, while fistula ligation necessitates central venous catheterization, increasing cardiovascular complication risks. Flow-targeted lumen remodeling technique, based on computational fluid dynamics principles, achieves anatomical-functional dual reconstruction through precise flow control, providing a novel therapeutic strategy for complex fistula complications [7]. This report presents a case of successful salvage of giant aneurysmal dilation AVF using innovative flow-targeted lumen remodeling combined with aneurysmectomy.

Case presentation

Patient information

A 46-year-old male patient with end-stage renal disease had been receiving hemodialysis treatment for 5 years. The patient underwent left upper arm high brachiocephalic arteriovenous fistula creation (brachial artery to cephalic vein end-to-side anastomosis) 42 months prior, with good fistula function and regular dialysis thereafter. Over the past 6 months, the patient noticed gradually developing pulsatile masses from the left upper arm to shoulder, accompanied by mild palpitation symptoms. Physical examination revealed obvious tortuous dilated vessels from the left upper arm to shoulder, appearing serpentine with a maximum diameter of approximately 4 cm. Palpation revealed prominent thrill, auscultation demonstrated continuous vascular murmur, and marked engorgement of the ipsilateral external jugular vein was observed (Fig. 1A).

Imaging examination

Thin-section CT angiography (0.5 mm slice thickness) scanning from the elbow to the subclavian vein demonstrated three-dimensional reconstruction revealing complete aneurysmal dilation of the cephalic vein with a maximum diameter of 40 mm (Fig. 1B). The entire venous outflow tract including the proximal cephalic vein and cephalic arch showed no evidence of stenosis or obstruction. Color Doppler ultrasound measured an AVF flow rate of 2,680 mL/min at the feeding brachial artery proximal to the arteriovenous anastomosis (normal range: 800–1200 mL/min), significantly exceeding the NKF-KDOQI recommended threshold of 2,000 mL/min (Fig. 1C) [8]. Echocardiographic evaluation demonstrated left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD) of 57 mm, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of 65%, E/E’ ratio of 26.7 indicating diastolic dysfunction, moderate mitral regurgitation (regurgitation area 4.4 cm²), and small to moderate pericardial effusion, confirming high-output cardiac stress. Theoretical estimation of wall shear stress (WSS) using the Poiseuille flow model: WSS = 4µQ/(πR³), where µ = 0.004 Pa·s, Q represents flow rate, R represents vessel radius) yielded an approximate value of 52 N/m² at the anastomotic site, significantly exceeding normal venous WSS (1–6 N/m²) and indicating pathological hemodynamic stress. Based on the patient’s medical history, physical examination, and imaging findings, a diagnosis of high-flow aneurysmal dilation with cardiac volume overload was established.

Surgical treatment

Based on the European Best Practice Guidelines (EBPG) staged intervention principles and individualized hemodynamic simulation principles, a staged surgical treatment plan was formulated for the patient: [1, 9] Stage I surgery: Flow-targeted lumen remodeling using a 20Fr sterile silicone catheter (diameter 6.6 mm) as an intraluminal template to achieve target diameter of 7 ± 1.0 mm, with reconstruction of brachial artery-cephalic vein anastomosis; preservation of 5 cm dilated cephalic vein segment above the shoulder for hemodialysis access, avoiding central venous catheterization [2]. Stage II surgery: Elective resection of shoulder aneurysmal segment after forearm venous maturation.

Stage I surgical procedure: The patient underwent general anesthesia in supine position. Extensive mobilization of the aneurysmal cephalic vein was performed (Fig. 2A). The cephalic arch measured 6.5 mm in diameter, representing relative stenosis that would be matched after lumen narrowing and flow reduction, requiring no interventional treatment. Intraoperatively, flow-mediated vascular remodeling technique was implemented to reduce lumen diameter from 35 ± 2.6 mm to 7 ± 1.0 mm: longitudinal incision of the anterior aneurysmal wall with excision of redundant vascular tissue, preserving 30% of the circumference. A medical-grade 20Fr silicone urinary catheter served as a molding stent for continuous suture lumen remodeling (Fig. 2B). The original dilated arteriovenous anastomosis was excised, and the remodeled venous segment (diameter approximately 6 mm) was anastomosed to the brachial artery to create a new anastomotic orifice of approximately 5–6 mm. Anastomotic banding was not performed. Standard microsurgical technique was employed to reconstruct the brachial artery-cephalic vein end-to-side anastomosis using 7 − 0 polypropylene continuous suture, achieving flow reduction solely through surgical lumen narrowing without prosthetic materials. Immediate postoperative flow measurement by intraoperative transit-time ultrasonic flowmetry demonstrated flow reduction to 850 mL/min with good thrill.

Patient outcomes

Immediate postoperative fistula patency was excellent with good thrill. Hemodialysis was resumed 24 h postoperatively using buttonhole technique in the preserved shoulder dilated venous segment, with normal venous pressure. After 2 weeks of dressing changes, the patient showed no complications including swelling, infection, or thrombosis in the surgical area. Sutures were removed on postoperative day 14.

At 1 month postoperatively, the surgical vascular segment was available for dialysis puncture, the patient exhibited no heart failure symptoms, and the shoulder aneurysmal dilation was concealable with clothing (Fig. 3A). Dynamic observation was continued per patient request, with stage II surgery deferred after multidisciplinary team evaluation. Three-month follow-up demonstrated remodeled segment diameter of 7.0 ± 1.0 mm, flow volume of 1061.8 mL/min meeting functional access standards, normal venous pressure range, and no stenosis in the venous course or central veins, confirming preserved patency of the entire outflow tract including the cephalic arch and proximal cephalic vein (Fig. 3B). Long-term echocardiographic follow-up (January 2020) showed significant cardiac improvement: LVEDD reduced from 57 mm to 44 mm, E/E’ ratio improved from 26.7 to 12, mitral regurgitation decreased from moderate (4.4 cm²) to mild (2.2 cm²), and pericardial effusion resolved to trace amount. LVEF remained preserved at 64%. These findings confirmed successful alleviation of cardiac volume overload following flow reduction surgery.

The patient received regular hemodialysis at our dialysis center with extended follow-up through 2020. During this long-term follow-up period, flow volume remained stable between 800 and 1,000 mL/min with dialysis blood flow rate of 250 mL/min, demonstrating sustained functional adequacy without progressive flow increase or aneurysmal recurrence. The patient exhibited no symptoms of heart failure or excessive flow complications throughout the follow-up period, confirming durable hemodynamic control. The patient was subsequently lost to follow-up after 2020. The treatment successfully achieved objectives of aneurysm resolution and functional access preservation with excellent short-term and mid-term outcomes.

Discussion

This case of giant aneurysmal dilation management demonstrates the innovative application of flow-targeted lumen remodeling technique. The principle of this technique lies in precisely predicting the optimal diameter through hemodynamic principles, effectively reducing intra-fistula flow volume and interrupting the vicious cycle of hemodynamic stress and vascular wall degeneration.

Treatment of AVF aneurysmal dilation requires individualized selection based on aneurysm size, location, symptoms, and impact on dialysis function [10]. Preoperatively, we estimated WSS to quantify the hemodynamic burden on the vessel wall. With the calculated WSS far exceeded normal venous WSS and demonstrating severe pathological hemodynamic stress. This theoretical estimation guided our surgical decision-making regarding the necessity and extent of intervention. Postoperatively, we relied on direct flow measurement rather than repeating WSS calculation, as the achieved flow reduction to 850 mL/min provided more clinically relevant confirmation of successful hemodynamic control. For patients with large aneurysms or severe complications such as significant calcification, thrombophlebitis or infection, impaired dialysis function, risk of rupture, or heart failure, surgical intervention is the primary treatment option [11]. Surgical principles include maintaining fistula patency whenever possible or concurrent fistula reconstruction, narrowing anastomotic and outflow vessel diameters to limit flow, and avoiding central venous catheterization for hemodialysis [11]. Main approaches include aneurysmectomy with arteriovenous fistula reconstruction, aneurysm narrowing with anastomotic reconstruction, and prosthetic graft bypass. Cases with severe heart failure require simplified surgical procedures, including simple fistula ligation. Additionally, endovascular interventional techniques such as covered stent implantation have been increasingly applied to treat aneurysmal dilation in recent years, offering advantages of minimal trauma and rapid recovery, though long-term efficacy requires further investigation. Notably, interventional treatment shows only 33% one-year patency rates for long-segment lesions (> 5 cm), significantly lower than surgical repair outcomes reported by Pourriyahi H et al. making it unsuitable for long-segment aneurysmal dilation cases [8, 12]. Furthermore, stent implantation may reduce effective puncture vessel length. For this reason, and given the extensive aneurysmal involvement in our patient, we selected open surgical repair rather than endovascular intervention.

In this case, the patient presented with a high-positioned fistula with tortuous outflow vessels, with excessive arteriovenous shunt causing mild heart failure and giant venous aneurysmal dilation affecting appearance. An individualized surgical plan was formulated, utilizing preoperative CT and ultrasound to establish vascular and flow models. Intraoperatively, the original dilated calcified anastomosis was excised to reduce arterial flow output. The dilated venous aneurysmal segment was narrowed through flow-targeted lumen remodeling, straightening the vascular pathway to avoid stenosis caused by tortuosity.

Individualized surgical design was one of the key success factors in this case. The cephalic arch measured 6.5 mm, which falls within the normal functional range and was only relatively narrow compared to the massively dilated aneurysmal segment (40 mm). After remodeling to 7 mm, the diameter matched harmoniously with the cephalic arch, and long-term follow-up confirmed maintained flow (800-1,000 mL/min) without stenosis, validating that angioplasty was not indicated. Preserving 5 cm dilated segment at the shoulder for dialysis transition avoided central venous catheterization risks, which holds important clinical significance for end-stage renal disease patients. Central venous catheters not only increase infection and thrombosis risks but may also cause central venous stenosis, affecting future vascular access creation. Postoperative echocardiography demonstrated significant cardiac improvement, confirming successful reversal of high-output cardiac stress.

Traditional aneurysmal narrowing procedures often employ empirical diameter settings without individualized hemodynamic guidance, resulting in imprecise postoperative flow control and prone to excessive narrowing or insufficient reduction [13]. This case utilized a 20Fr silicone catheter as an intraluminal template to ensure consistency and precision of the remodeled diameter, significantly improving surgical reproducibility and predictability. Intraoperatively preserving 30% of the vascular circumference is based on biomechanical principles of vascular wall tension distribution, ensuring both suture integrity and maintaining physiological vascular elasticity. A distinguishing feature of this technique is the exclusive use of autogenous vessel remodeling without any prosthetic materials. Flow reduction was achieved solely through surgical diameter narrowing and anastomotic reconstruction, avoiding complications associated with synthetic grafts such as infection, thrombosis, and immune reactions. Compared to prosthetic graft replacement, autogenous vascular remodeling technique demonstrates obvious advantages. This case showed no infectious complications at 3-month follow-up, while literature reports prosthetic graft infection rates up to 18.2% [14]. Furthermore, autogenous vascular remodeling avoids immune reactions and thrombosis risks associated with foreign body implantation, theoretically providing better long-term patency. The maintained flow volume of 800-1,000 mL/min over extended follow-up, confirming the effectiveness of this technique in maintaining dialysis adequacy and preventing recurrent high-flow complications. The staged surgical strategy reflects the balance between risk control and functional preservation. Stage I surgery focused on resolving hemodynamic issues and restoring dialysis function, while stage II surgery addresses cosmetic concerns. In this case, the patient’s heart failure symptoms resolved, shoulder aneurysmal dilation was concealable with clothing, and the patient requested deferral of stage II surgery, demonstrating the flexibility of individualized treatment. This strategy is particularly suitable for elderly or high-risk patients, minimizing surgical trauma and anesthetic risks.

However, this technique also has certain limitations. For severely calcified vessels (CT value > 800HU), vascular walls lack elasticity and may require combined interventional techniques or consideration of alternative treatment options [15]. Additionally, this technique requires surgeons to possess microsurgical skills and hemodynamic knowledge, potentially limiting its widespread application. The lack of long-term follow-up data is also a current concern, requiring more cases and longer follow-up periods to validate long-term outcomes. The “4P principles” (Precise assessment, Physiological reconstruction, Prompt intervention, Personalized follow-up) followed in this case provide a novel conceptual framework for managing complex vascular access complications. Precise assessment includes hemodynamic simulation and imaging analysis; physiological reconstruction emphasizes restoring normal flow patterns; prompt intervention prevents further complication deterioration; personalized follow-up adjusts treatment strategies according to individual patient circumstances.

In conclusion, flow-targeted lumen remodeling technique provides an effective salvage strategy for giant aneurysmal dilation following arteriovenous fistula creation. This technique achieves dual reconstruction of anatomical structure and physiological function through precise hemodynamic control, avoids central venous catheterization risks, and significantly improves treatment safety and effectiveness. This approach aligns with core principles of vascular access preservation, opening therapeutic avenues for complex fistula complications management and merits further validation and refinement in clinical practice.

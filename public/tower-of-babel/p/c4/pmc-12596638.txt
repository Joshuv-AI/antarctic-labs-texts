CNSM-01 Intradural Extramedullary Spinal Cord Metastasis of Breast Cancer in a Male
Authors: Naa Adzoa Adzeley Boi-Dsane, Hadi Mohammed Abdullah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBreast cancer in males is rare, accounting for just 0.5% to 1% according to WHO data. Africa has a 0.042 male-to-female breast cancer ratio, as seen in the meta-analysis conducted by Ndom et al. This is a report of a single case of breast cancer in a male with invasive ductal carcinoma of the left breast and intradural extramedullary spinal cord metastasis. The patient presented at a tertiary facility with neurological symptoms following his previous breast cancer diagnosis and treatment. This is a case of a 77-year-old male with invasive ductal carcinoma (ER and PR positive, Her2 negative) who had a left mastectomy and completed adjuvant radiotherapy in 2014. He followed this treatment with oral tamoxifen for 2 years. He reported progressive back pain and paraplegia in 2017. The patient had been well until 4 years post-mastectomy when he developed progressive weakness in the lower limbs, along with bowel and bladder dysfunction. Magnetic Resonance Imaging showed L1-L2 involvement. Intradural extramedullary tumor excision via L1-L2 laminectomy was performed, and histology confirmed metastatic invasive carcinoma of breast origin. He improved neurologically post-operatively and started mobilizing with the aid of a Zimmer frame. However, he relapsed within 6 months. While preparing for adjuvant chemoradiation, he developed bilateral Deep Vein Thrombosis and died from a massive bilateral pulmonary embolism. Management of breast cancer depends on the stage and prognosis. The patient was initially managed with a left mastectomy, the mainstay treatment for stage T1 and T2 cancers. In T3/ advanced cases, radiotherapy is also recommended, especially in cases with more than three positive lymph nodes or positive tumor margins. Additionally, ER-positive cancers, which are more prone to metastasis, require tamoxifen for 5 years, but this patient was on hormonal therapy for 2 years before his relapse. Late presentation most likely contributed to poor prognosis.

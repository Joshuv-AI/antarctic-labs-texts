A quarter-century journey into 4-chromone derivatives: synthesis, reactions, and pioneering applications thereof
Authors: Alaa Ashraf El-Awady, Mohamed A. Ismail, Hajar A. Ali, Eslam A. Ghaith
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Chromone derivatives are a large family of bicyclic six-membered oxygenated heterocycles. Also, they are bioactive, naturally occurring scaffolds with unique chemical and physical features. Additionally, chromone frameworks have been utilized in various applications, ranging from drug discovery to supramolecular chemistry. This review systematically summarizes the literature on synthetic methodologies for formylchromones, cyanochromones, chromene-3-carboxylic acids, and chromene-3-malononitrile derivatives, focusing on construction strategies involving Vilsmeier–Haack and palladium-catalyzed formylation. Moreover, this report outlines advancements in chromone chemistry, highlighting recent developments, discussing substrate scope, reaction conditions, and proposed mechanistic pathways, where available. Additionally, this review covers substantial common reactions involving Diels–Alder, Prins–Nazarov, aza-Friedel–Crafts, Biginelli, Mitsunobu-triggered, and Beckmann rearrangement. Correspondingly, this review focuses on ring opening, ring expansion, heteroannulation, and the ring-closing of chromone derivatives with various heterocyclic motifs, such as pyrroles, pyridines, pyrimidines, indoles, and pyran derivatives. These collective findings underscore the potential of chromone scaffolds as promising candidates for organic synthesis and therapeutic development. Accordingly, researchers in this domain will find this overview valuable for understanding the chemistry of chromone hybrids, as it covers the literature of the last two decades.

Introduction

Chromone (4H-1-benzopyran-4-one or 4H-chromen-4-one) is an o-heterocyclic scaffold that has a benzopyrone skeleton. Chromones are prevalent in nature1–5 (Fig. 1), specifically in plants such as Aloe,6 Cassia,7 Aquilaria,8–10 Polygonum,11 and Hypericum,12 and contribute to plant coloration.13 Also, providing protection against UV radiation and fungal pathogens,13,14 thus ensuring the reproduction and survival of plants. Scientists isolated and elucidated the structures of over 4000 naturally occurring chromone derivatives.15 Natural and synthetic chromones have been shown to exhibit a range of privileged pharmaceutical and biological activities, including neuroprotective,16,17 antibacterial,18,19 antimalarial,20 antifungal,21,22 antiallergenic,23 antioxidant,24–26 antitumor, anticancer,27–30 and insecticidal.31 Additionally, chromones are effective inhibitors of calcium antagonists,32 carbonic anhydrase,33 aldose reductase,34 and calpain.35,36 Additionally, chromones are attractive sources for the construction of several drugs due to their low toxicity and valuable activities.37 Several chromone-containing drugs have been approved by the FDA for the treatment of an extensive range of medical conditions, including nedocromil sodium,38 apigenin,39,40 flavoxate hydrochloride,41,42 and iguratimod43,44 (Fig. 2). In addition, khellin, a compound derived from Ammi visnaga seeds, a member of the carrot family, is the first chromone used as a diuretic in medicine.45 It is also still used to treat bronchial asthma and as a smooth muscle relaxant.46,47 Consequently, it is undeniably valuable and important to synthesize this scaffold to broaden the field of drug discovery.48 The main aims of chromone construction are not only for the advance of complex and more diverse bioactive compounds for structure–activity relationship (SAR) studies and related action of mechanism, but also for other applications in therapeutic chemistry, such as the synthesis of fluorescence probes and metal complexes of 3-hydroxyflavone, due to photochemical and electrochemical activities of chromones.49,50

This review delineates the evolutionary trajectory of formylchromones, cyanochromones, chromene-3-carboxylic acids, and chromene-3-malononitrile derivatives, spanning from their initial extraction and laboratory synthesis to their diverse chemical transformations. Unlike the reported literature reviews about chromones, this review focuses on domino and multicomponent reactions (MCRs) rather than conventional binary condensation reactions. Additionally, this review systematically categorizes reactions by their structural ring size, starting with five-, six-, and seven-membered rings, as well as ring-opening transformations. Finally, a comprehensive overview of chromone applications across medicinal, photochemical, sensor, and complex domains is presented, highlighting the novelty and breadth of this work.

Applications

Biological activities

Anti-cancer activities

Chromone scaffolds are attractive platforms for developing new chemopreventive agents for cancer.136 Abo-Salem et al.137 synthesized scaffolds of chromanone derivatives and investigated their activity against HepG2 (liver cancer), HCT-116 (colon cancer), MCF-7 (human breast cancer), and normal skin fibroblast cells (BJ1). Whereby, substrates 234a, 234b and 235 explained the best effective activity in the colon cancer cell lines (IC50 values of 35.1, 18.6 and 29.2 µM, respectively). Additionally, a series of chromone and benzothiazole derivatives was synthesized and evaluated for anticancer activity. Where skeletons (236, 237, and 238) displayed better potency, with compound 238 showing the best IC50 values towards HCT116 and HeLa cell lines (IC50 values of 2.527 and 2.659 µM, respectively). Hybrids 236 and 238 showed similar cytotoxicity against both cell lines, whereas scaffold 237 (IC50 of 3.995 µM) showed better cytotoxicity against the HeLa cell line (Fig. 6).138

Chromone skeletons with anti-cancer activities.

Antioxidant activities

Additionally, chromone derivatives play an important role as free radical scavengers and antioxidants. Csepanyi et al.139 investigated the antioxidant activity of chromone derivatives using the oxygen radical absorption capacity (ORAC), the radical-scavenging activity (ABTS), and the ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP), with 4-N,N-dimethylamino-flavon 239 showing superior antioxidant activity compared to the reference (Fig. 7). Furthermore, Demetgül et al.140 combined chitosan with chromone to form the chromone–chitosan Schiff base, which was evaluated for antioxidant activity by using the DPPH radical scavenging assay in vitro. Compound 240 was considered the most effective antioxidant agent (IC50 = 0.88 mg mL−1) as compared to chitosan alone. The enhanced antioxidant activity is attributed to the presence of the phenolic group in chromone (Fig. 7).

Chromone skeletons as antioxidant activities.

Anti-inflammatory activities

Also, Chromone scaffolds have anti-inflammatory activities. A series of chromones exhibited inhibitory effects on NO production in RAW264.7 cells. Where 4-oxo-N-(o-tolyl)chromene-2-carboxamide 241 has the best inhibitory activity (EC50 = 5.326 µM) compared to the positive drug ibuprofen (Fig. 8).141 In addition, Bouzammit et al.142 synthesized chromone isoxazoline 242 with potential anti-inflammatory properties by inhibiting lipoxygenase enzyme (5-LOX) due to the presence of an electron-withdrawing group, such as Br, at the para position, unlike an electron-donating group, which decreases anti-inflammatory potential (Fig. 8).

Chromone scaffolds with anti-inflammatory activities.

Antidiabetic activities

Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by elevated blood glucose levels due to inadequate insulin production.143 Some chromone derivatives, such as pyrazolyl chromone scaffolds, have proven effectiveness as antidiabetics. Where compound 243 (IC50 = 119.7 ± 4.3 µM) confirmed the best α-glucosidase inhibitory activity, outperforming the acarbose drug effectiveness (IC50 = 300.9 ± 10.9 µM) (Fig. 9).144 Moreover, 6-hydroxychromone-based thiosemicarbazones were constructed and evaluated for their antidiabetic (α-amylase and α-glucosidase inhibition). Where, hybrid 244a (IC50 = 13.61 ± 2.04 µg mL−1) developed as the α-Amylase inhibitor, compared to the standard Acarbose drug (IC50 = 43.15 ± 5.22 µg mL−1). For α-glucosidase inhibition, compound 244b (IC50 = 1.18 ± 0.19 µg mL−1) established excellent activity, superior to acarbose (IC50 = 7.33 ± 0.13 µg mL−1) (Fig. 9).145

Chromone derivatives with antidiabetic activities.

Antimicrobial activities

Dofe et al.146 synthesized a series of chromone derivatives and assessed for antimicrobial activity. 3-Hydroxychromone 245a showed potent activity against E. coli and P. aeruginosa with a MIC of 25 µg mL−1. Compounds 245b and 246a showed good activity against S. aureus, with MICs of 25 µg mL−1. Triazol derivatives 246b and 246a showed potent activity against B. subtilis, with MICs of 25 µg mL−1 (Fig. 10). In addition, a series of 1,2,4-dithiazolylchromones was synthesized and screened for antimicrobial activity in vitro against various fungal and pathogenic bacterial strains. 6-Bromo-3-(dithiazol-3-yl)chromenone 247a confirmed excellent inhibitory activity with MIC 0.78 against S. cerevisiae compared to fluconazole as the reference drug. Subsequently, 7-chloro-3-(dithiazol-3-yl)chromenone 247b showed promising inhibitory activity with MIC 3.12 against C. albicans. While compound 247b demonstrated excellent inhibitory activity with MIC 0.78 against B. subtilis, after that, hybrid 247a, which showed hopeful inhibitory activity with MIC 1.56 against E. coli and B. subtilis (Fig. 10).147

Chromone derivatives as potential antimicrobial agents.

Anti-Alzheimer's activities

Ahmed et al.148 reported the synthesis of chromone pyrazole scaffolds and evaluated their in vitro and in vivo for anti-Alzheimer activity. Significantly, compound 248 exhibited the highest inhibitory activity against butyrylcholinesterase and acetylcholinesterase with IC50 values of 1.60 µM and 3.31 µM, respectively. Also, promising scaffold 248 can decrease acetylcholinesterase, MDA, and NO levels in vivo (Fig. 11). Alzheimer's disease (AD) is also mostly influenced by acetylcholinesterase (AChE) and interleukin-6 (IL-6). Whereas, Mishra et al.149 aimed to synthesize a scaffold with the ability to inhibit both targets by combining N,N-disubstituted amine (inhibits AChE) and chromone moiety (inhibits IL-6). 2-(3-(Piperazin-1-yl)propyl)chromenone 249 was found to be the most effective inhibitor of AChE (IC50 = 0.54 µM) and IL-6 (IC50 = 0.57 µM). Subsequently, compound 249 was tested in vivo and did not cause mortality or adverse effects in treated animals at a dose of 0.8 mg kg−1 (Fig. 11).

Conclusion and future perspectives

With the ever-increasing importance of chromone derivatives as a potent core in bioactive natural compounds and potential pharmaceutical drugs. In this review, substantial effort has been made to summarize the structural features, synthesis, and chemical reactivity of chromone scaffolds. Also, this review represents the utilization of chromone motifs for the synthesis of binary and fused polyfunctionalized heterocyclic compounds involving furan, pyrrole, pyrazole, oxazole, imidazole, benzothiazole/benzoimidazole, triazoloindazole, pyrimidine, quinozolinone, imidazopyridine, etc., accredited by exhaustive discussion of the reaction mechanisms during the last quarter of the current century. Additionally, we highlight the most important innovations in chromone scaffolds, an auspicious class with a wide range of biological and pharmacological activities, including antioxidant, antibacterial, antifungal, antimalarial, and antiallergic activities. Despite this significant progress in current research, several critical obstacles remain, including reliance on precious-metal catalysts, harsh reaction conditions that restrict functional-group tolerance, and limited understanding of structure–activity relationships across diverse applications of chromone scaffolds. Consequently, future researchers should focus on developing regioselective, innovative, and eco-friendly synthetic methodologies for chromones and on biological stimulation with structure–activity relationships, which will accelerate the discovery of new chromone scaffolds and play a vital role in future drug discovery. These efforts are critical for advancing the use of chromones in drug development, especially in anti-inflammatory, anticancer, and anti-Alzheimer's therapies. It is anticipated that this review will provide guidance to encourage outstanding scientists' interest and to further improve the field of chromone chemistry.

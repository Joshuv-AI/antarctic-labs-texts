Introduction

Metastatic castration‐resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) is associated with poor prognosis, particularly in patients with bone metastasis [1]. The PEACE‐3 trial demonstrated that combining enzalutamide with radium‐223 (Ra‐223) significantly improved the radiographic progression‐free survival (rPFS) in patients with mCRPC [2]. However, real‐world evidence of this combination therapy remains limited, especially in patients with PSA‐discordant progression, characterized by radiographic progression despite persistently low PSA levels and associated with poor outcomes. Herein, we report a case of mCRPC with PSA‐discordant progression that was well‐treated with enzalutamide in combination with Ra‐223.

Case Presentation

A 74‐year‐old male presented to our hospital with elevated PSA levels. The patient had a history of hypopharyngeal cancer, which was treated surgically 10 years earlier with permanent tracheostomy and developed postsurgical hypoparathyroidism with persistent hypocalcemia. At presentation, the PSA level was 6.1 ng/mL. Prostate biopsy revealed prostate adenocarcinoma with a Gleason score of 4 + 5 = 9 (Figure 1). The clinical stage was cT2aN0M0, and the patient was treated with combined androgen blockade (CAB) followed by definitive external beam radiotherapy (Figure 2A,B). During follow‐up, positron emission tomography–computed tomography (18F‐FDG), performed to investigate elevated tumor activity markers including lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), revealed metastases involving the liver, extra‐regional lymph nodes, and multiple skeletal sites with diffuse osteosclerosis (Figure 2C,D). Based on these findings, the patient was diagnosed with mCRPC 8 months after prior therapy. At the time of diagnosis of mCRPC, tumor activity marker was markedly elevated, with LDH at 2084 U/L. Bone metabolic markers were also increased, including bone‐specific alkaline phosphatase (BAP) at 123 μg/L and type I collagen cross‐linked C‐telopeptide (ICTP) at 16.6 ng/mL. Bone scintigraphy demonstrated diffuse uptake consistent with extensive skeletal metastases, with an overall total bone uptake (OTBU) of 26865.5 (Figure 3A). For the treatment of mCRPC, enzalutamide was initiated at a dose of 120 mg/day in combination with Ra‐223 administered at 50 kBq/kg every 4 weeks for six cycles. Dexamethasone (0.75 mg/day) was initiated after the first cycle of Ra‐223 for the symptomatic management of general fatigue, with a potential ancillary antitumor effect. Bone‐modifying agents (BMAs) were not administered due to refractory hypocalcemia caused by postsurgical hypoparathyroidism. After completion of six cycles of Ra‐223 treatment, LDH level decreased to 855 U/L. In parallel, BAP declined to 32.2 μg/L and ICTP to 15.2 ng/mL, indicating marked improvement in tumor activity (Figure 4). Post‐treatment bone scintigraphy performed 6 months after the initiation of combination therapy demonstrated a marked decrease in OTBU to 8802.3, reflecting a substantial reduction in radiotracer accumulation (Figure 3B). Abdominal CT performed 8 months after therapy initiation indicated complete resolution of the liver and lymph node metastases. Throughout this period, serum PSA levels remained low at approximately 0.020 ng/mL, demonstrating a clear discordance between bone metabolic markers and radiological findings. Neuroendocrine‐related serum markers remained normal throughout treatment. During treatment, the patient required a transfusion of four units of packed red blood cells for Grade 3 anemia [3]. An 8‐month follow‐up after combination therapy initiation revealed sustained radiographic and biological improvements.

Histopathological findings of the prostate biopsy. Hematoxylin and eosin–stained sections examined at × 40 magnification show that the tumor reveals predominantly acinar adenocarcinoma, with conspicuous intracytoplasmic vacuoles. Cancer cells exhibit an infiltrative growth pattern with fused glands, poorly formed glands, and cord‐like arrangements. The overall Gleason score was 4 + 5 = 9 (International Society of Urological Pathology Grade Group 5).

Discussion

We treated a patient with mCRPC who exhibited PSA‐discordant progression with a combination therapy of enzalutamide and Ra‐223. The patient demonstrated meaningful radiographic and metabolic responses following therapy for this clinically challenging disease condition.

Ra‐223 targets osteoblastic metastases and induces tumor cell death through alpha‐particle–mediated deoxyribonucleic acid double‐strand breaks [4, 5]. The ALSYMPCA trial demonstrated the clinical efficacy of Ra‐223 in patients with mCRPC and bone metastases, with significant prolongation of overall survival (median, 14.9 months vs. 11.3 months; hazard ratio [HR], 0.70; p < 0.001) [5]. The PEACE‐3 trial demonstrated that combination therapy with Ra‐223 and enzalutamide significantly prolonged rPFS (median, 19.4 months vs. 16.4 months; HR, 0.69; p = 0.0009) [2]. Accordingly, combination therapy with Ra‐223 and enzalutamide has gained clinical relevance in mCRPC treatment. Despite these promising results, real‐world experience with this combination remains limited. Although the present patient had bone, liver, and lymph node metastases, the skeletal lesions were considered the predominant sites of disease based on markedly elevated bone metabolic markers and extensive uptake on bone scintigraphy. Therefore, we decided to initiate combination therapy including Ra‐223. In the present case, despite the complicated postoperative course, the patient tolerated the combination therapy and achieved favorable outcomes.

PSA‐discordant progression represents a disease entity in which PSA kinetics do not adequately reflect the underlying tumor burden, with radiographic progression occurring without a corresponding increase in PSA levels [6]. This phenomenon has been interpreted as reflecting tumor dedifferentiation and reduced dependence on androgen receptor signaling, as described for aggressive variants of CRPC [6]. In a post hoc analysis of the PREVAIL trial, PSA‐discordant progression occurred in 24.5% of patients and was associated with significantly shorter rPFS than PSA‐concordant progression, with an HR of 1.6 [6]. A post hoc analysis of the TITAN trial similarly revealed that PSA‐discordant progression occurred in 52.2% of patients receiving apalutamide and 27.5% of those receiving placebo, with discordant cases exhibiting significantly shorter rPFS than concordant cases [7]. Collectively, these findings indicate that mCRPC with PSA‐discordant progression is associated with adverse prognosis, even in large‐scale clinical trials. In our case, a meaningful response to bone metastases was achieved with combination therapy using Ra‐223 and enzalutamide despite PSA‐discordant progression in mCRPC. This finding suggests that combination therapy may be effective even in the setting of PSA‐discordant progression, a disease state associated with adverse prognosis.

This case should be interpreted with caution because several factors limit its generalizability. First, BMAs could not be administered because of refractory hypocalcemia resulting from postsurgical hypoparathyroidism. In the ERA‐223 trial, the combination of Ra‐223 and abiraterone acetate in the absence of mandatory BMA use was associated with a significantly increased risk of fracture, highlighting the critical role of BMAs in patients receiving Ra‐223–based therapy [4]. Subsequently, in the PEACE‐3 trial, the use of BMAs markedly reduced fracture risk in both treatment arms: at 1‐year, cumulative fracture incidence decreased from 15.6% to 2.6% in the enzalutamide arm and from 37.1% to 2.7% in the enzalutamide plus Ra‐223 arm among patients receiving BMAs [8]. However, in real‐world clinical practice, a considerable number of patients are unable to receive BMAs because of comorbid conditions such as hypocalcemia, renal dysfunction, or dental complications. In addition, dexamethasone was administered during treatment. Corticosteroids may induce antitumor responses in prostate cancer [9]; therefore, their potential contribution to the therapeutic response observed in this case cannot be completely excluded. Further investigations are warranted to clarify the real‐world efficacy and safety of Ra‐223–based combination therapy in this population.

This case demonstrates that combination therapy with Ra‐223 and enzalutamide elicits a meaningful therapeutic response in patients with mCRPC with PSA‐discordant progression. The clinical course further suggests that this regimen could broaden therapeutic opportunities for mCRPC with PSA‐discordant progression, a clinically aggressive and hard‐to‐treat disease condition.

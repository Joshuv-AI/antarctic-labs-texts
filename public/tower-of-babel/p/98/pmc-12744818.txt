Shared genetic associations between CHA2DS2-VASc score and cardio-embolic stroke: Insights from mendelian randomization based bioinformatics analysis
Authors: Kang Yuan, Xianshu Huo, Mengdi Xie, Huaiming Wang, Siyu Sun, Kejia Deng, Rui Liu, Xinfeng Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The CHA2DS2-VASc score is a risk assessment score to predict cardio-embolic stroke (CES), suggesting shared genetic susceptibility. This study aimed to explore the shared genetic association of genetically predicted CHA2DS2-VASc score and CES using multiple analytic frameworks. We conducted a comprehensive analysis of genetic data from 13 traits of CHA2DS2-VASc score and CES using summary statistics from large-scale genome-wide association studies (GWAS). We employed statistical methods such as linkage disequilibrium score regression (LDSC), cross-trait analysis, bidirectional Mendelian randomization, colocalization analysis and gene-based association analysis to explore genetic correlations and identify pleiotropic single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and shared genes. LDSC and Mendelian randomization analysis revealed a significant genetic correlation between CHA2DS2-VASc score and CES. Cross-trait and colocalization analysis identified 9 potential loci and 13 significant independent SNPs. Gene-based association analysis reported 9 genes significant across at least three methods, with IL6R being the shared gene identified by all four methods, highlighting potential shared biological mechanisms involving immune responses and inflammatory activities. In conclusion, our study revealed shared genetic associations between the genetically predicted CHA2DS2-VASc score and CES, which was supported by causal relationship, shared loci, and genetic correlation analyses.

Introduction

Stroke is the second leading cause of mortality and the major cause of long-term disability worldwide [1]. Despite advancements in secondary prevention strategies for ischemic stroke, the incidence of cardioembolic strokes (CES) has tripled during the past few decades [2]. Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most prevalent arrhythmia and a significant cause of CES [3]. Patients with CES often exhibit larger infarct areas and worse outcomes [4]. The relationship between AF and CES is partly supported by shared risk factors, including age and cardiometabolic diseases [5,6]. Researchers have developed several important clinical scores to guide the prevention and treatment strategies for CES based on these shared risk factors.

The CHA2DS2-VASc score is a risk assessment score employed to predict the incidence of stroke in patients with AF and serves as the guide for anticoagulation therapy based on risk factors in clinical practice [7]. Previous studies found that higher CHA2DS2-VASc scores were associated with a higher risk of long-term vascular events in acute ischemic stroke with AF [8]. Dynamic changes in CHA2DS2-VASc scores also significantly increased the incidence of ischemic stroke events in ischemic stroke patients with AF [9]. Despite recent explorations into the improved predictive value by combining with other imaging biomarkers, CHA2DS2-VASc score remained the most commonly used scoring system in clinical practice [10].

The Mendelian randomization (MR) method can estimate the causal effect of exposure on the interested diseases by using genetic variants from the genome-wide association studies (GWAS) data [11]. Previous genetic findings have indicated gene pleiotropy or an underlying causal relationship between various conditions and stroke subtypes [12]. Recently, cross-trait meta-analysis has been developed as an advanced analytical tool that facilitates joint analysis of genetic variants and uncovers genetic loci associated with various traits by incorporating GWAS data. Previous studies successfully discovered explore the shared genetic mechanism between abdominal aortic aneurysm and cardiometabolic traits by cross-trait analysis, demonstrating the involvement of genes associated with lipid metabolism and inflammation [13]. However, few studies have investigated the risk factors in the CHA2DS2-VASc score and CES from a genetic perspective. Hence, our study aimed to explore the genetic association between the CHA2DS2-VASc score and CES through causal relationship and cross-trait meta-analysis, identify shared genes via gene-based association analysis, and thereby provide new insights into the biological mechanisms of CES.

Methods

Data source

We synthesized summary statistics of CES from the GIGASTROKE study [14], involving 10,804 cases and 1,245,612 controls, and integrated the components of the CHA2DS2-VASc score to perform the cross-trait meta-analysis of 13 traits: heart failure (HF), hypertension (HT), age (bioage [BA] and phenoage [PA]), type 2 diabetes (T2D), stroke (AS), transient ischemic attack (TIA), venous thromboembolism (VTE), vascular disease (coronary artery disease [CAD], myocardial infarction [MI], and peripheral artery disease [PAD]), and sex (sex hormone-binding globulin [SHBG] and testosterone [TT]) from the European ancestry, (Fig. 1). Linkage disequilibrium score regression analysis

We estimated the genome-wide genetic correlation (rg) between CES and the CHA2DS2-VASc score traits via linkage disequilibrium (LD) score regression (LDSC) analysis [15]. LDSC quantifies the effects of polygenic correlation by evaluating the association between LD scores and the test statistics of single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) from GWAS summary data, which can reveal non-random associations of alleles across different loci in a given population. A variant will show a higher test statistic if it is in linkage disequilibrium with a causal variant, with the magnitude of the increase directly proportional to its correlation with the causal variant. Statistical significance was determined using a false discovery rate (FDR) corrected P value < 0.05.

Bidirectional two-sample MR analysis

We identified SNPs with genome-wide significance (P < 5e-8) and selected independent SNPs based on LD score, with an r2 threshold = 0.001 and a clump-kb = 10000 across all GWAS datasets. We conducted a bidirectional two-sample MR analysis to estimate the causal relationship between CES and the CHA2DS2-VASc score traits, ensuring compliance with three critical assumptions of the MR analysis: a) genetic variants must be independently associated with the exposures; b) the genetic variants used as instrumental variables for the exposure must not be influenced by other confounders; and c) genetic variants affect the outcome exclusively through their effect on the exposures. The inverse-variance weighted (IVW) method was utilized as the primary statistical approach [16]. Sensitivity analyses, including the weighted median [17], maximum likelihood [18], and MR-Egger methods [19], were conducted to assess the robustness of the results under varying assumptions regarding the inclusion of variants with pleiotropic effects, which might violate the assumptions of the IVW model. The weighted median method can provide consistent MR estimates when more than 50 % of the weights are derived from valid SNPs. The MR-Egger method can identify pleiotropy via its intercept and produce pleiotropy-corrected estimates. We used MR-PRESSO analysis after removing outliers in identifying possible pleiotropy [20]. Associations with a P < 0.0038 (0.05/13 exposures) were considered statistically significant, while P < 0.05 as suggestive evidence. Statistical analyses were conducted using the TwosampleMR package in R software (version 4.2.1).

Genome-wide cross-trait analysis

We employed MTAG for cross-trait meta-analysis of GWAS summary statistics to identify shared pleiotropic loci across traits [21]. MTAG is a consistent estimator with lower genome-wide mean squared error compared to single-trait GWAS estimates. It utilizes generalized IVW meta-analysis across different traits and accounts for potential sample overlap from various GWAS studies. The key assumption is that all SNPs share the same variance-covariance matrix of effect sizes across traits. Genetic association statistics from MTAG provide enhanced statistical power and minimal inflation of the false discovery rate for each trait analyzed. Furthermore, we conducted a cross-phenotype association analysis (CPASSOC) as a sensitivity analysis to avoid the potential violation of assumptions in MTAG [22]. CPASSOC combines GWAS summary statistics from multiple traits to identify shared variants while accounting for population structure and cryptic relatedness. It provides two test statistics: SHom and SHet. SHom, based on a fixed-effect meta-analysis, is more powerful when genetic effect sizes are consistent across traits. SHet, an extension of SHom, maintains statistical power in scenarios where genetic effect sizes vary, which is more typical in practice as SHom is not appropriate for multiple traits. Hence, we chose SHet for the analysis in our study. We used PLINK clumping function to obtain independent loci with the following parameters: clump-p1 5e-8, –clump-p2 1e-5, –clump-r2 0.2, and –clump-kb 500. The variant with the lowest P-value was designated as the index SNP within each locus. Significant pleiotropic SNPs were identified as those with P-values below 5e−8 in all meta-analysis (P-MTAG and P-CPASSOC). Subsequently, we employed ANNOVAR for functional annotation of the variants detected via MTAG and CPASSOC.

Fine-mapping credible set analysis

We extracted variants within 500 kb of each shared SNP in the cross-trait meta-analysis for input into the fine-mapping analysis [23]. We identified a 99 % credible set of causal variants using the FM-summary method, a Bayesian fine-mapping algorithm. This method utilizes a flat prior and computes a posterior probability of a true association between a phenotype and a variant via the steepest descent approximation. A 99 % credible set corresponds to ranking the SNPs by largest to smallest posterior probability and summing the cumulative posterior probabilities until the sum reaches at least 99 %.

Colocalization analysis

We performed a colocalization analysis using the R package coloc to ascertain whether the shared loci were localized at the same causal variant. We extracted summary statistics for variants within ±500 kb of each shared index SNP to calculate the probability that the two traits share a single common causal variant under the default prior settings. Loci with a PPH4 value greater than 0.7 were deemed to colocalize [24]. We also estimated the posterior probability across multiple traits sharing the same loci using a Bayesian divisive clustering algorithm in the R package HyPrColoc [25].

Gene-based association analysis

Firstly, we utilized the GCTA-fastBAT method to further identify shared genes, which computes the significance of the association from an approximated distribution across all SNPs using GWAS summary data and LD correlations from a reference sample set [26]. We subsequently executed Multi-marker Analysis of GenoMic Annotation (MAGMA) analysis using default parameters, with the European ancestry panel from the 1000 Genomes Project (Phase 3) serving as the LD reference [27]. MAGMA gene analysis is a versatile approach that employs a multivariate linear principal component regression model to effectively account for LD among markers and to detect the effects of multiple gene sets on genetic traits. Then, we used summary-data-based Mendelian Randomization (SMR) analysis [28], which combined GWAS and eQTL data to identify genes whose expression levels were associated with a complex trait through pleiotropy or causality. SMR employs the HEIDI-outlier test to differentiate pleiotropy from linkage. We applied SMR using summary data from whole blood derived from the CAGE eQTL summary data, which is a meta-analysis of 2765 blood samples. Significant genes were identified as those with FDR corrected P-SMR <0.05 and P-HEIDI >0.05. Next, we conducted TWAS-fusion analysis using the GWAS summary statistics in the meta-analysis, with the FUSION software applied at default settings (http://gusevlab.org/projects/fusion/). Functional Summary-based Imputation (FUSION) is frequently employed in TWAS analysis to identify transcriptome-wide associations [29]. We downloaded expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) reference panels for 49 tissues from GTEx v8 and utilized 1000 Genomes European samples to estimate LD score between the prediction model and the SNP at each GWAS locus. In each method, the P value threshold was adjusted using FDR correction. Finally, we obtained the target genes by intersecting the genes identified across the four methods.

Gene-based association analysis

Using GCTA-fastBAT analysis, we identified 47 shared genes between CES and AS, 222 shared genes between CES and CAD, 161 shared genes between CES and HF, 82 genes between CES and T2D, and 65 shared genes between CES and VTE, respectively (Table S8). MAGMA analysis revealed 24, 79, 45, 58, and 29 shared genes between CES and AS, CAD, HF, T2D, and VTE, respectively (Table S9). After querying gene information from the eQTL summary data, SMR analysis identified 429, 517, 465, 450 and 394 shared genes between CES and AS, CAD, HF, T2D, and VTE, respectively (Table S10). Through TWAS-fusion analysis of GWAS statistical summary data 49 tissue eQTL data from GTEx v8, we found 2, 42, 48, 7, and 3 shared genes between CES and AS, CAD, HF, T2D, and VTE, respectively (Table S11). Summarizing the shared genes across the four methods, we identified that C9orf156, C19orf52, IL6R, PSRC1, REEP2, SLC22A2, SMARCA4, TOM1L2, and HNRNPA0 were reported by at least three methods (Table S12). Discussion

In our study, we uncovered a significant genetic correlation between the genetically predicted CHA2DS2-VASc score and CES, which was further supported by pleiotropic variants and genes shared by the trait pairs. Our study provided an innovative perspective on the potential biological mechanisms behind the predictive ability of the CHA2DS2-VASc score for the risk of CES in clinical practices.

The CHA2DS2-VASc score is currently recommended by clinical practice guidelines for risk stratification and to guide systemic anticoagulation in appropriate candidates to prevent thromboembolic stroke [30]. Previous studies indicated that the risk factors included within the CHA2DS2-VASc score were associated with an increased risk of ischemic stroke and thromboembolic events [31]. Although AF is often considered to be related to thromboembolism, the observation of an increasing association between the CHA2DS2-VASc score and an increasing risk of CES in patients without AF suggested that these risk factors themselves may be pro-thrombotic [32]. Our study explored the association between traits of CHA2DS2-VASc score and the risk of CES from a genetic insight. LDSC and MR analyses supported that most of the CHA2DS2-VASc score traits, including age, HF, HT, prior stroke or TIA, and vascular disease, were significantly associated with CES.

The cross-trait analysis revealed that the CHA2DS2-VASc score and CES shared 13 significant independent SNPs and 9 protein-coding genes. The majority of these pleiotropic genes were previously reported to be associated with AF and stroke. LINC01142 was identified as a significant contributor to cardiac tissue integrity and played a vital role in the pathogenesis of AF [33]. HMCN2 was associated with left ventricular function and the phenotypes of electrocardiogram global electrical heterogeneity [34]. Bioinformatic gene analysis revealed that ZFHX3, in conjunction with predicted miRNAs, constitutes one of the therapeutic targets for atrial fibrillation-related stroke [35]. TLR2 and SNX25 regulate inflammatory cells and influence neuronal injury and macrophage activity in the experimental models of stroke and AF[[36], [37], [38]]. Besides, CFI, LRIT3 and OGA was also found to be associated with comorbidities potentially related to CES, such as age-related disease, diabetes and HF[[39], [40], [41]].

We observed that IL6R was inferred to be a shared gene between the CHA2DS2-VASc score and CES in the GCTA-fastBAT, MAGMA, SMR and TWAS-fusion analyses, which can complement the polygenic effects of genes that may be neglected in causal analyses that annotate SNPs to nearby genes. IL6R encodes a subunit of the interleukin 6 (IL6) receptor complex. IL6 is a potent pleiotropic cytokine that regulates cell growth and differentiation, and plays a crucial role in the immune response. IL6R is a protein complex composed of IL6 and its signal transducer, which is also shared by many other cytokines [42]. Dysregulated production of IL6 and this receptor are involved in the pathogenesis of stroke and AF. Cupido et al. conducted a MR study to compare the effects of IL6 and IL6R on the risk of cardiometabolic diseases. They found that inhibiting the IL6 signaling pathway is likely to reduce the risk of CAD, stroke, AF, and T2D. Additionally, IL6R was associated with an increased risk of pneumonia [43]. Jiang et al. supported the finding that IL6R was a predictor of specific stroke subtypes including CES in the bidirectional two-sample MR analyses [44]. Cross-ancestry transcriptome-wide association analysis identified IL6R as a causal gene of AF that could enable cardioembolic risk prediction, suggesting the involvement of immune responses in the pathogenesis of CES [45]. Arendset al. collected peripheral blood DNA from patients with first-ever ischemic stroke and investigated the association between proinflammatory cytokines and recurrent vascular events in the context of clonal hematopoiesis. The results indicated that IL6 was associated with an increased risk of recurrent vascular events among patients with clonal hematopoiesis [46].

To the best of our knowledge, our study is the first to employ multiple analytical frameworks to investigate the association between the genetically predicted CHA2DS2-VASc score and CES with publicly available GWAS datasets. However, our study had several limitations. Firstly, the CHA2DS2-VASc score encompassed a variety of disease traits, and our study included representative GWAS data for these clinical variables in the analyses. Secondly, our study only used GWAS data from the European ancestry, which limits its generalizability to other ethnic groups. Thirdly, the presence of undetected confounders, such as epigenetic factors, may lead to pleiotropy that could affect the causal estimates, despite our efforts to minimize biases, including the use of various validation methods for genetic association. Finally, the molecular and cellular mechanisms of the shared gene identified in our study are based on a limited data source. expanding the data types and references might enhance the reliability of our findings. Further researches were warranted to investigate the potential of shared genes as therapeutic targets for preventing CES in clinical settings.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study revealed shared genetic associations between the genetically predicted CHA2DS2-VASc score and CES, which was supported by causal relationship, shared loci, and genetic correlation analyses. However, the current CHA2DS2-VASc score only represents the predicting value of changes in the individual's internal environment (genetic associations) in ischemic stroke or CES among AF patients. However, congestive HF, HT, T2D are modifiable risk factors, which not only link to human genetic internal environment but also acquired lifestyle, for example, the E(e)SEEDi lifestyle [47]. Herein, the healthy lifestyle is always helpful in improving cardio-cerebrovascular outcomes. Further researches were warranted to investigate the potential of shared genes as therapeutic targets for preventing CES in clinical settings.

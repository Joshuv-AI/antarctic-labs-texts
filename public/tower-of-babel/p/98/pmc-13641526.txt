Validation of the Standardized Outcomes in Nephrology–Life Participation Instrument in People with CKD
Authors: Anastasia Hughes, Cameron Burnett, Angela Ju, Yeoungjee Cho, Armando Teixeira-Pinto, Chandana Guha, Martin Howell, David W. Johnson, Vanessa Kumar, Fergus J. Caskey, Jonathan C. Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Visual Abstract

Introduction

People with CKD not yet requiring KRT experience an increased risk of morbidity and high symptom burden, which can profoundly affect their sense of self, independence, and connections with others, affecting their ability to participate in daily activities and their quality of life.1–4 The international Standardized Outcomes in Nephrology (SONG) Initiative has established life participation as a core patient-reported outcome to be measured in all clinical trials involving patients with CKD not yet requiring KRT.5,6

Life participation is defined as the ability to participate in activities that are meaningful and relevant to patients, including leisure, family activities, work, and social activities, which is a more specific construct then quality of life (which includes physical and psychological health, social relationships, and their environment).4,7 In addition, patients have identified and prioritized the domains of life participation. However, life participation is rarely measured in trials with people with CKD, which may be attributed to lacking a specific measure for life participation in people with CKD.7 A recent systematic review found that 41 different instruments had been used to assess life participation in observational studies and trials in patients with CKD not yet requiring KRT.7 No instruments were designed to specifically assess life participation; instead, most of the instruments addressed broader constructs such as quality of life with life participation embedded as a subscale, and 12 instruments assessed life activities including basic self-care, but not necessarily meaningful activities of life participation specifically.7 The instruments ranged in length (1–136 questions) and time of completion (2–30 minutes). For these reasons, none of the instruments included such as health related quality of life measures could be feasibly implemented in all trials in CKD because the length and time needed to complete other instruments that assess quality of life (e.g., Short Form [SF]-36, Kidney Disease Quality of Life) meant that it would not be feasible to be implemented in trials and the broad construct of quality of life would not capture life participation with specificity.7

To establish and implement a core patient-reported outcome measure for life participation, a consensus workshop involving 130 patients, caregivers, and health professionals from 18 countries was conducted.4 The SONG-Life Participation (SONG-LP) instrument, previously validated with kidney transplant recipients, demonstrated internal consistency, test-retest reliability, and good convergence validity with moderate to high correlation to instruments that assessed similar constructs. It was recommended as a potential core patient-reported outcome measure for life participation in CKD by participants because of its feasibility (i.e., simple to use), minimal burden (i.e., short and easy to administer/complete), and inclusion of relevant and meaningful content from the perspective of patients with CKD, caregivers, and health professionals.4 However, the SONG-LP measure had not yet been assessed for use in patients with CKD not yet requiring KRT. Patients, caregivers, and health professionals supported the validation of the SONG-LP instrument in patients with CKD not yet requiring KRT.4 While experiences of patients with CKD and post-KRT are likely to differ, the construct of life participation has been confirmed to be meaningful and relevant.4 We aimed to evaluate selected psychometric properties of internal consistency, test-retest reliability, convergent validity, hypothesis testing, and acceptability of the SONG-LP instrument in adults with CKD not yet requiring KRT.

Methods

Selection and Recruitment of Participants

Participants were eligible to participate if they were aged 18 years or older, had a diagnosis of CKD and not receiving KRT, were able to read and write in English, provide informed consent, and complete the survey online. Participants were excluded if they could not complete the measure on their own. An online invitation was sent to the SONG database of contacts including patients with CKD and to networks of the SONG Steering Committee and SONG-LP CKD Life Participation Expert Working Group, the Better Evidence and Translation in CKD network, and social media platforms. We estimated that 250 participants would be needed to achieve a 95% confidence interval (CI) for the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) with a precision (half-width) of 0.06, assuming an anticipated ICC of 0.70 for agreement between baseline and week 1 for the SONG-LP instrument. Approved ethics was obtained from the University of Sydney (Human Research Ethics Committe 2021/114).

Measures

SONG-LP

The SONG-LP instrument has been previously validated with kidney transplant recipients.8 It was developed after a systematic review of all instrument measuring life participation in kidney transplantation and consensus workshop with patients, caregivers, and health professionals.9,10 During a consensus workshop, patients with CKD not yet requiring KRT, caregivers and health professionals endorsed the SONG-LP instrument to be validated and used as the core patient-reported outcome measure when measuring life participation in CKD.4 The domains assessed by the SONG-LP instrument, leisure, family activities, work, and social activities, were all deemed important, meaningful and relevant.4 Each item from the instrument is scored on a five-point Likert scale to measure the ability to do the activity from never to always over a 1-month recall period.8 The instrument is easy to read with a Flesch-Kincaid Readability Grade Level of grade 6.8 The overall score is determined by the mean of the responses from each of the four questions, with possible overall scores ranging from zero (never) to four (always) and higher responses demonstrating higher participation.8 In the case of not applicable (N/A) being selected for a question, the mean score was determined by the remaining questions, with each question carrying equal weight.8 The workshops identified four key themes to establish a meaningful, relevant, and feasible instrument including enabling interpretation within individual context and circumstances, having broad clinical relevance, supporting widespread implementation, and highlighting opportunities to address life participation.4 The SONG-LP instrument is shown in Figure 1.

Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System Item Bank v2.0 Ability to Participate in Social Roles and Activities SF 8a

The Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System Item Bank v2.0 Ability to Participate in Social Roles and Activities (PROMIS-APS) SF 8a instrument was used to assess convergent validity of the total SONG-LP scores.11–13 The PROMIS-APS defines life participation as the “perceived ability to perform one's usual social roles and activities.” The PROMIS-APS instrument has been validated for use with kidney transplant recipients,14 rheumatoid arthritis,15 outpatient rehabilitation,16 and the general population.17 It is part of the broader 57-item Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS) Item Bank v2.0 instrument and commonly used in patients with CKD, including those receiving dialysis or who have received kidney transplants, as well as other conditions and populations.12,18 The domains assessed in PROMIS-APS are similar to the domains of life participation as identified by patients in SONG workshops; however, the questions are positively framed in the SONG-LP instrument.8 We hypothesized the scores from the PROMIS-APS and the SONG-LP instruments would closely correlate.12

PROMIS Item Bank v2.0 Cognitive Functional Abilities Subset SF 6a

The PROMIS Cognitive Functional Abilities Subset (CF) instrument assesses cognitive deficit and degree of impaired daily functioning.19 As cognitive symptoms occur in the people with CKD, moderate correlation was expected between self-reported cognitive impairment and life participation scores.20

PROMIS Item Bank v2.0 Emotional Support SF 4a

The PROMIS Emotional Support (ES) instrument assesses perceived feelings of being cared for and valued as a person, having confidant relationships.21 We expected some correlation between ES and life participation.

PROMIS Item Bank v2.0 Instrumental Support SF 4a

The PROMIS Instrument Support (IS) instrument assesses perceived availability of assistance with material, cognitive, or task performance.22 We expected some correlation between instrumental support and life participation.

Euro Quality of Life Five-Dimension Five-Level Questionnaire

The Euro Quality of Life-5 Dimension 5-Level (EQ-5D-5L) assess quality of life through the five dimensions of mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression.7,23 This measure was selected because it is short and simple to complete, as well as commonly used indicating high completion rate, comprehensible to people with CKD and validated with patients with CKD not yet requiring KRT and patients who receive KRT (both dialysis and transplant).7,24,25 We hypothesized moderate correlation between the SONG-LP and EQ-5D-5L instruments as the latter provides specific examples of the life participation domains identified in the SONG-LP instrument. Scoring of EQ-5D-5L applies country specific algorithms to calculate a utility value where zero is dead, one is full health, and values <0 are worse than dead. The largest proportion of respondents were from Australia, then the United States and the United Kingdom; therefore, the Australian values set was used to calculate the EQ-5D-5L utility values.26 Different validation sets give different utility scores; however, there are only small differences between the countries of similar cultural backgrounds.26

Self-Rated Kidney Function

Participants self-scored their level of kidney function with the self-rate kidney function question: “I think my kidney function over the past month has been [response],” with the response being indicated on a Likert scale from one (poor) to five (excellent). We hypothesized that self-rated kidney function (SRKF) would correlate with life participation on the SONG-LP instrument.

Data Collection

Participants completed the demographic and clinical characteristics at baseline (time point 1) and the five instruments outlined above including SONG-LP, PROMIS-APS SF 8a, PROMIS Item Bank v2.0 CF SF 6a, PROMIS Item Bank v2.0 ES SF 6a, PROMIS Item Bank v2.0 IS SF 4a, the EQ-5D-5L instrument, and SRKF. The order of instruments included in the validation survey were also randomly allocated to minimize ordering bias because of survey fatigue.

At the 1-week survey (time point 2), participants complete the SONG-LP instrument and SRKF. The Research Electronic Data Capture was used to administer the survey online, which was open from March 2025 to July 2025.

Data Analysis

Demographic and clinical continuous variables were described using median and interquartile ranges, while categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages. The scores of the instruments were summarized as means and SDs. Data analysis was conducted in RStudio (version 2024.04.2+764).

We followed the COnsensus-based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments (COSMIN)–Core Outcome Measures in Effectiveness Trials (COMET) recommendations and US Food and Drug Administration guidelines for developing a core patient-reported outcome measure of people with CKD not yet requiring KRT and for the measurement of reliability and validity criteria.27–30 The reliability of the SONG-LP instrument was assessed by comparing the internal consistency and test-retest reliability over 1 week. Construct validity included convergent validity, acceptability, and hypothesis testing, e.g., assessing the extent to which the instrument assesses factors associated with life participation. Reliability

Test-Retest

When comparing baseline and 1 week, an intraclass coefficient between 0.5 and 0.75 is considered to indicate moderate stability, and 0.75 indicates good to excellent stability.32

Internal Consistency

Adequate scale consistency is defined as Cronbach α >0.731 and McDonald ω >0.7.

Content Validity

The consensus workshop conducted before this validation study supported content validity.4,31

Construct Validity

Convergent Validity

Convergent validity was determined by Spearman Rho, with <0.3 indicating low correlation, 0.3–0.7 indicating moderate correlation, and >0.7 indicating high correlation.30,31 High positive correlation between the SONG-LP instrument and the PROMIS-APS was anticipated because of the adaption of the PROMIS-APS instrument for the SONG-LP instrument. In addition, good convergent validity was demonstrated in kidney transplant recipients.8 In addition, positive moderate correlations were also expected because of similarities in the domains assessed by each of the measures: SONG-LP, PROMIS CF SF 6a, ES SF 4a, IS SF 4a, EQ-5D-5L, and SRKF. An exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis was performed on each of the four domains of the SONG-LP instrument.

Hypothesis Testing

A person's ability to participate in activities identified in the SONG-LP instrument, such as leisure, work, family, and social activities, is hypothesized to be affected by other clinical conditions and stage of CKD. Stage of CKD was identified as a relevant variable that is hypothesized to be associated with SONG-LP mean scores and SD with the direction of effect being to lower scores with later stages of CKD. Stage of CKD was defined by self-report eGFR in ml/min per 1.73 m2, with G1: >90, G2: >60 to <90, G3: 30 to <60, G4: 15 to <30, G5: <15 not yet receiving KRT.

Acceptability

Acceptability is assessed by survey completeness and score distributions. It measures the level of the data quality. There are no COSMIN-COMET predefined thresholds for missing data for instrument scores, but rather to state reasons for missingness. The criterion of acceptability for floor and ceiling effects is <15%.33

Results

In total, 179 (100%) participants completed the survey at baseline (time point 1) and 165 (92%) completed the 1-week survey (time point 2). Participants were from 16 countries including Australia (n=64), United States (n=49), United Kingdom (n=42), Argentina (n=5), Canada (n=3), Italy (n=3), Germany (n=2), South Korea (n=2), Ecuador (n=1), India (n=1), Mexico (n=1), The Netherlands (n=1), New Zealand (n=1), Norway (n=1), Peru (n=1), Taiwan (n=1), and United Arab Emirates (n=1). Most (121, 68%) of the participants were female and included a broad spectrum of self-reported CKD including stage 1 (18, 10%), stage 2 (24, 13%), stage 3 (57, 32%), stage 4 (40, 22%), and stage 5 not yet receiving KRT (25, 14%). Internal Consistency

In addition, Cronbach α indicated good internal consistency in both measurements, with 0.92 (95% CI, 0.89 to 0.94) at time point 1 and 0.94 (95% CI, 0.91 to 0.96) at time point 2 (Table 2). Despite the smaller sample size, the higher-than-expected ICC (0.81 versus 0.7) lead to a similar final precision (0.05 instead of 0.06). McDonald ω indicated similar internal consistency, with 0.93 (95% CI, 0.91 to 0.95) at time point 1 and 0.95 (95% CI, 0.94 to 0.96) at time point 2.

Content Validity

Workshops which involved 74 patients and caregivers and 56 health professionals from 18 countries have previously confirmed that SONG-LP instrument to assesses life participation in a meaningful and relevant way.4 Patients, caregivers, and health professionals identified that the SONG-LP instrument allowed for completion in accordance with individual contexts, has broad relevance across stages enabling comparisons and being feasible to include in clinical trials.4

Construct Validity

Convergent Validity

High correlations between the SONG-LP instrument and the PROMIS-APS (0.81; 95% CI, 0.72 to 0.86), and the EQ-5D-5L (0.72; 95% CI, 0.63 to 0.79) demonstrated good convergent validity. Moderate to low correlation was seen between the SONG-LP and PROMIS Cognitive Functional Abilities instrument (0.61; 95% CI, 0.50 to 0.70), PROMIS ES instrument (0.32; 95% CI, 0.17 to 0.45), and PROMIS IS instrument (0.23; 95% CI, 0.09 to 0.37). An exploratory factor analysis provided a Tucker-Lewis index of 1.01. The confirmatory factor analysis also provided a Thomas-Kilmann Conflcit Mode Instrument of 1.01 indicating an excellent model fit.

The mean SONG-LP scores, as well as for Ability to Participate in Social Roles and Activities, Cognitive Functional Abilities, and Euro Quality of Life-5 Dimension scores, grouped by stage of CKD are suggestive of a trend to lower values with later stages of CKD. Discussion

People with CKD, caregivers, and health professionals have established life participation as a critically important core outcome to be measured in clinical trials in people with CKD not yet requiring KRT.5,6 The SONG-LP instrument meets the COSMIN-COMET criteria for acceptability, reliability, convergent validity, and hypothesis testing. This was demonstrated by high completion rate at 1 week (acceptability), excellent test-retest reliability and internal consistency, and strong convergent validity with PROMIS-APS and EQ-5D-5L. Missing data were limited as well as low floor effect and high ceiling effect which may limit sensitivity to changes in life participation. Compared with the PROMIS-APS SF 8a, the SONG-LP instrument is more appropriate for use as a core outcome measures because it is simpler and easier to use, shorter (four items compared with eight items), and evaluates content that has been systematically assessed to be critically important to people with CKD, caregivers, and health professionals.

There was strong positive correlation between the SONG-LP instrument and the PROMIS-APS and EQ-5D-5L. This is expected given the comparable dimensions of life participation assessed within each instrument and items that directly assess specific constructs and examples of the SONG-LP instrument (leisure, family, work, and social activities). For example, the EQ-5D-5L specifically assess mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression. The PROMIS Cognitive Function, ES, and IS instruments do not directly assess life participation, which is consistent with the low to moderate correlation with the SONG-LP instrument.

In patients with CKD, a plethora of challenges exist including difficult psychological issues, complications, side effects, and comorbidities that can impair life participation.1,4,34 In the CKD population, lower kidney function has been found to be positively associated with lower quality of life.35 We noted a trend to lower life participation mean scores from category G1 (3.47±0.72) through to category G5 (2.47±0.89), consistent with existing research outlining that CKD progression is associated with increased severity of disease, increased symptom burden, more limited function, and more frequent health care utilization. However, the small sample size limited the ability to sufficiently test our hypothesis.

The strong correlations between the SONG-LP instrument and multiple instruments assessing related constructs strengthens the construct validity of the SONG-LP instrument. There are some limitations to note. While we sought to involve participants internationally, most of the participants were from Australia, the United States, and the United Kingdom which could also be related to the availability of the survey in English only. We were unable to reach the estimated sample size; however, higher ICC depicts similar precision to the desired sample size. We acknowledge self-reported eGFR reported as a categorical outcome of eGFR category (CKD stage 1–5) as a potential limitation. Participants were largely from high income countries with high educational attainment. In approximately half of participants, the cause of CKD was either glomerular disease or polycystic kidney disease; therefore, findings may not be transferable to those with other cause of CKD such as diabetes. The survey was only available online, thus limiting access to those with poor or no internet access and digital literacy. We acknowledge that we were unable to assess all psychometric properties, e.g., responsiveness and cultural validity. Currently, the SONG-LP instrument is only available in English. Cross-cultural validation requires separate validation, and further work is underway to translate the SONG-LP instrument into Spanish and Danish for cultural validation. The average of the scale responses was used as the score of the SONG-LP which places equal weight (i.e., importance) on each of the four items. We acknowledge this may not reflect patient preferences if greater preference is placed on certain activities. The clinically meaningful change and minimally important difference is also yet to be determined.

This study has established evidence supporting the validity, reliability, and subsequent use of the SONG-LP instrument to assess life participation in people with CKD not yet requiring KRT. Life participation is an outcome of critical importance to patients with CKD, which is more specific construct than quality of life, which can also encompass other domains including physical health, mental health, and social connections. The instrument is devoid of cultural and interpretative issues that may be seen in quality-of-life instruments. For example, the SONG-LP instrument specifically asks the patient to reflect on their own ability in accordance with their context and values rather than assessing the score on the basis of external assumptions. The instrument has been developed following a process of qualitative research and consensus with patients and caregivers in particular highlighting the value of this measure compared to other measures, further supporting its high content validity. However, further studies are required to continue to explore the psychometric robustness of the SONG-LP instrument. Trials with interventions which may affect life participation or where it is the primary outcome may need to consider using additional instruments for a more comprehensive assessment of life participation. The use of the SONG-LP instrument across all trials, and subsequent publication of results, as well as in clinical practice in people with CKD will ensure a standardized assessment of life participation which is relevant and meaningful to people with CKD and their caregivers. This can achieve evidence-based comparisons and insights into the effects of interventions aiding decision making for patients.

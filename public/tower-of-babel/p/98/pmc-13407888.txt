DBpHash: a blockchain-based dual-band perceptual hashing framework for copyright protection of purely chromatic background images
Authors: Mrithulasree Nainar, Saikiran Sankaranarayanan, Karthika Veeramani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPure chromatic background images (PCBIs) consist of large uniform regions with minimal structural content, which makes standard perceptual hashing based on low-frequency DCT unreliable. High similarity scores may be assigned to visually different images, leading to false acceptance. In blockchain-based copyright registration systems, such transactions are irreversible once confirmed, necessitating a conservative similarity model. This paper proposes DBpHash, a dual-band perceptual hashing framework for blockchain-based copyright registration and verification of PCBIs. During computation, low-frequency DCT bands are excluded while mid and high-frequency bands are energy-normalized and binarized to create a 128-bit hash. Similarity is calculated independently for each band and fused using inverse-variance weighting. Thresholds are derived from real and imposter distributions. Experiments conducted on a PCBI dataset are further validated via cross-dataset evaluation on BSDS500 and DTD without retraining. False positive rate remains at 0.095 while similarity exceeds 0.92 under common photometric distortions. Statistical analysis confirms strong hash properties, including near-ideal bit balance and entropy. The framework is integrated with blockchain-based registration and off-chain similarity computation. Results indicate that DBpHash provides a reliable and distortion-resilient solution for copyright authentication of PCBIs.Keywords: Perceptual hashing, PCBI, Copyright protection, Blockchain, Smart contractSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Digital advertisements, infographics, and wallpapers are produced and published on a large scale by many online publishing sites. Many routine tasks, such as resizing, compressing and adjusting colors can create a large number of copies of the same product. Such duplicates may also be submitted as separate images to copyright registration systems. Purely chromatic background images (PCBIs) can contain large uniform background areas with only small foreground components1. When PCBIs have similar colours in their backgrounds, the similarity will be determined by the amount of colour in the low frequency part of the discrete cosine transform (DCT), because these DCT coefficients describe the background colour of the image and thus provide no structural information about the two images when comparing them. Therefore, it is possible for visually dissimilar images to have similar DCT representations.

In most registration systems, this type of activity is not checked for, therefore multiple duplicate submissions or misleading submissions can occur. If similar images are repeatedly accepted by an automated verification system, this will deleteriously impact the accuracy of the ownership record(s). A verification mechanism that isolates structural content, while being tolerant to routine distortion due to compression and filtering is required2. Approaches to protecting images under copyright include digital watermarking, content-based image retrieval (CBIR) and perceptual hashing. In the digital watermarking approach, ownership information is embedded into the actual file, so that ownership can be confirmed during the verification process3. CBIR systems use visual descriptors to identify related images by examining the entire collection of stored images for relationships among them4. Copyright registration and dispute resolution are centralized in most implementations5. When there is an increase in the number of submissions, this centralized authority can create problems with both operational efficiency and transparency. The existence of a blockchain network provides an alternative mechanism for creating records of ownership using immutable smart contracts6.Reliance on trusted third parties is eliminated with these frameworks, yet there has not been equal emphasis placed on how well the perceptual authentication mechanism functions. The blockchain primarily provides a durable registry, while the distortion tolerance levels and decision conservativeness of the similarity model continue to be secondary in consideration.7,8. Consequently, there is a pressing need for the two areas of distortion-aware similarity modeling and blockchain-based copyright registration to become more strongly integrated.

The proposed DBpHash aims to overcome the limitations of existing perceptual hashes by addressing the unique challenges posed by PCBIs in copyright verification. It is designed to be photometric distortion-aware and conservative thresholds are used to ensure legally robust copyright verification. Energy-normalized, DCT bands are used. Low-frequency bands that capture backgrounds are ignored. Instead, mid and high-frequency bands that capture finer texture and structure details are independently modeled to preserve structural separability and enhance tamper sensitivity. An adaptive weighted similarity scoring mechanism is used to combine band-wise similarities using distortion-aware statistical weights. This ensures robustness under content-preserving transformations. As blockchain systems are immutable, the proposed DBpHash uses a conservative decision policy. Ambiguous cases are rejected and further escalated to manual review. Forensic reliability is important over permissive matching in copyright systems to avoid ownership infringement. For copyright registration and verification, a decentralized storage mechanism is used. Ethereum and InterPlanetary File System (IPFS) are used to store ownership entries on-chain and raw image files off-chain respectively. Smart contracts are used to record the DBpHash with the associated metadata. To avoid the overhead of on-chain computational costs, similarity computation is executed outside the blockchain. Ownership entries are immutable in the distributed ledger once recorded.

Related work

Image similarity detection through perceptual hashing as a means has been in existence for many years. In these instances, low-frequency DCT coefficients and the hamming distance used by traditional means to perform distortion tolerant image matching are those most often utilized. Image processing systems must be evaluated using the degree of robustness against distortion or noise as a component of their overall understanding. Images, in many real-world applications, experience numerous different types of degradation including compression artifacts, filtering and gaussian noise. Hence, systems must be evaluated with such distortions, as highlighted in prior works to ensure reliable performance under practical conditions9,10.

To address these concerns, several perceptual hashes have been developed in the spatial domain. aHash and dHash11 compute compact representations using pixel intensity averaging and gradient-based difference encoding, respectively. In contrast, pHash and its variants operate in the frequency domain, extracting low-frequency DCT coefficients to capture global structural information while suppressing high-frequency noise12. Similarly, wHash adopts a wavelet-domain transformation to obtain multi-resolution representations13. But these are insufficient for copyright protection of PCBIs as uniform backgrounds overshadow structural variations. Better feature extraction techniques are required to improve robustness. Enhancements have been made to perceptual hashing using adaptive texture descriptors, saliency maps, and global shape features to enhance sensitivity to tampering 14. Forensic architectures like ChaSAM combine multiple hash representations like DCT-based and diagonal-difference hashes with iterative filtering and parallel processing15. Joint photographic experts group (JPEG) structural markers and metadata n-gram are also implemented in media source similarity hashing16. These approaches improve feature diversity and robustness under specific distortions but do not use mechanisms that can adequately address the background dominance in PCBIs.

Spatial and frequency features are combined through convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and attention mechanisms to improve the balance between robustness and discriminability17. Hybrid approaches use deep learning to improve semantic alignment and spatial robustness. Classical pHash with siamese networks and vision transformers are combined with vision transformers to enhance near-duplicate detection under geometric and color transformations18. Deep CNN hashing schemes and pre-trained transformer embeddings aim to align similarity computation with human perceptual judgment19,20. However, similarity is decided by fixed thresholds during deployment and these techniques demand large-scale labeled data and large amounts of training to learn generalizable representations. In the context of PCBIs, where there is only limited data available in the domain, these models may tend to overfit and result in unreliable generalization. Additionally, their main goal is semantic similarity learning which differs from distortion-centric perceptual integrity verification. Thus, they cannot be directly compared to each other when evaluated.

Blockchain technology is widely used in digital rights management systems since it helps in enhancing immutability, transaction security and traceability. Intelligent automation and fraud detection is carried out using Artificial intelligence and large language models21. Digital artwork is protected by securing storage mechanisms with advanced encryption standard (AES) and elliptic curve cryptography (ECC) while its metadata is managed using smart contracts22. Tiered blockchain architectures help improve scalability and reduce operational cost6. Decentralized forensic tracing frameworks and lattice-based authentication protocols also enhance accountability and anonymity within copyright systems23,24. Deep feature extraction and consortium blockchain architectures are used in remote sensing scenarios to improve preservation of uniqueness25,26. Non-fungible token (NFT)-oriented frameworks further integrate hashing and watermarking for ownership issuance and dispute resolution27,28. It is seen that these works enhance infrastructure-level governance and transaction security, but the underlying perceptual hashing or similarity verification mechanisms are often treated as auxiliary components. There has been limited analysis of their robustness or decision behavior. Moreover, while deep learning and transformer based hashing approaches enhance semantic resilience, they add high computational cost and are not deterministic. This makes them less suitable for low-latency, CPU-based, and security-critical applications such as blockchain-based copyright registration.

Building upon such infrastructure, some works integrate perceptual hashing into blockchain-based copyright registration frameworks29. Registration is carried out on-chain while deep learning is used for detecting similarity off-chain. Similarly, Zhang and Wu integrate a perceptual hash within smart contracts and IPFS storage and improve frequency-domain sampling for better feature extraction30. In these works, the transaction integrity is strengthened through encryption and smart contract validation. But the similarity layer itself remains largely unchanged. Most implementations utilize standard perceptual hashing, which was not originally designed for background-dominant PCBIs or irreversible ownership decisions. Gao et al. addressed the limitations of classical pHash in PCBIs and integrated it with a blockchain-based copyright registration framework1. It helps to overcome the background-dominance of PCBIs using high-frequency DCT modeling. Reliance on trusted third parties is removed through Ethereum and IPFS integration. However, this hashing strategy extracts only high-frequency components. Low and mid frequency bands, which carry broader chromatic distributions and midscale structural transitions are not included to compute the similarity scores. Conservative decisions in immutable blockchain registrations have also not been addressed, where false ownership claims cannot be reversed.

It is seen that existing perceptual hashing and blockchain-based systems have several common limitations. Most approaches rely predominantly on low-frequency DCT components, which are unsuitable for detecting distortions in PCBIs. Similarity decisions are done using static thresholds. It is not adaptable to varying distortion distributions. Deep learning–based approaches introduce significant computational overhead and model dependency. Also, the greater risk of false-positives and the constraint of conservative ownership registration in immutable blockchain-based systems have not been addressed. To address these challenges, the proposed DBpHash introduces a dual-band DCT modeling strategy that separately captures mid-frequency and high-frequency components to reduce background dominance while preserving structural separability.

In order to deal with numerous types of distortion, similarity scores are assigned based on weighting criteria. This enables us to have both durability against corruptions and an ability to detect tampering. A data-driven threshold learning model is more conservative than a static threshold when it comes to decision-making in immutable blockchain environments. Materials and methods

The background information required to understand this proposed work is contained within this section. The theoretical framework used in developing this proposed system, along with the metrics for the performance evaluation of the system are discussed.

Purely chromatic background images

Images characterized by large, spatially uniform segments of color that contain little to no texture or structural variation are referred to as Purely chromatic background images (PCBIs). PCBIs have a variety of real-world applications including posters, banners, user interfaces, advertising and template design. The visual content is dominated by smooth color gradients or flat color fills rather than detailed objects or textures. From a signal processing standpoint, PCBIs contain low spatial complexity, with most of the image energy contained within the low-frequency portion of the frequency domain. Since homogeneous or relatively slowly changing color areas produce almost all of the low-frequency coefficients when they are transformed to the frequency domain, it follows that most of the energy in the image will be in the low-frequency region. Therefore, traditional hashing methods, which primarily use low-frequency characteristics, will generate near identical hash values for images which have significant visual differences than one another, while at the same time giving rise to lower discriminative ability. This also increases the probability of hash collisions. Other variations, such as changes in minor gradients, compression artifacts and minor color transitions that may create visual distinctions between these images, are typically represented by mid- and high-frequency coefficients, but are generally ignored by standard hashing methods. Hence, designing a perceptual hashing method tailored for PCBIs requires shifting the focus from dominant low-frequency components to more informative mid- and high-frequency bands. This allows for improved discriminability, while also maintaining robustness to benign distortion.

Standard perceptual hashing techniques use primarily the low-frequency DCT coefficients to provide stability in image transformation (e.g. compression or resizing) and are relied upon due to their stability. Low-frequency coefficients encode most of the image’s global luminance and coarse structural content, hence it will be highly affected by global changes in intensity (e.g., illumination and contrast) across images. In addition, because low-frequency coefficients generally demonstrate a low amount of variability between different images, especially in image datasets containing nearly identical visual content, they are less effective discriminatively. In the case of PCBIs, a large portion of the signal energy is contained in the low-frequency region of the DCT spectrum, resulting in greater similarity of distinct images when only low-frequency coefficients are used to represent the images. The amount of low-frequency signal caused by this concentration diminishes the likelihood of identifying the subtle structural characteristics needed for robust identification. To overcome these limitations, the proposed hashing algorithm emphasizes the mid-frequency coefficients, which provides improved robustness and discriminability by capturing transitional structures and having less sensitivity to global intensity changes than low-frequency coefficients. In addition to emphasizing mid frequencies, high frequencies is utilized to improve representation by capturing fine structural detail and edge feature information, further improving separability between images with similar visual content.

Methodology

The proposed dual-band perceptual hashing (DBpHash) is a frequency-domain framework for copyright verification of PCBI that is capable of being deployed within registration systems based on blockchain technology. The proposed dual-band perceptual hashing methodology consists of two parts: The image is pre-processed and then converted by using a two-dimensional orthonormal discrete cosine transform (DCT) to the frequency domain. In order to generate structural characteristics, the DBpHash approach uses a dual-band decomposition of the mid- and high-frequency components to create a binary representation of the image. The final output is a 128-bit DBpHash representation of the image created by normalizing the energy and performing a band-specific binarization of each of the mid- and high-frequency components. The purpose of DBpHash is to provide for the determination of similarity on the basis of mid-frequency and high frequency images through the use of adaptive weights resulting from training and statistical analyses. The DBpHash uses a statistically derived threshold for determining a conservative acceptance or rejection of a copyright image. Borderline similarity cases are escalated for manual review. Hence, DBpHash proposes a trustworthy and legally defensible method for copyright validation. Each component of the proposed DBpHash is described in detail within each of the subsections below. Frequency-domain hash construction using mid and high bands

To mitigate the background dominance caused by low-frequency components, the proposed DBpHash models mid and high-frequency components of the DCT domain. This captures textural variations, edge transitions and localized intensity changes while the background-dominant low-frequency energy is attenuated. An input red, green, and blue (RGB) image I is resized to a fixed spatial resolution , and converted to grayscale. After normalization to the range [0, 1], a two-dimensional orthonormal DCT is computed to obtain the coefficient matrix F. Instead of extracting a single contiguous block, F is partitioned into two non-overlapping regions corresponding to mid-frequency and high-frequency bands, denoted by and . These bands are defined as disjoint index-based subsets F of the DCT coefficient matrix and are defined so as to exclude the low-frequency components. The mid-frequency band is made up of intermediate frequency coefficients that capture structural transitions in an image, whereas the high-frequency band is made up of high-frequency coefficients that encode fine texture and edge information.

Adaptive Similarity Computation and Verification Decision

Blockchain-enforced copyright registration

The proposed DBpHash operates in a copyright registration system on top of a blockchain. The records of ownership are saved permanently on a blockchain, meaning that no records can be changed once written to a blockchain and one cannot register the same work again based on only being visually similar to something already registered. Visual similarity between works would be evaluated before writing to the blockchain. Only the smart contracts in the blockchain would maintain ownership records and logs of verification actions, while visual similarity detection and generation of DBpHash would be completed off chain.

System architecture

The system has four components: the User Interface, the Perceptual Processing Module, the Decentralized Storage and the Blockchain Enforcement Layer as seen in Fig. 3. The user interface allows submission of an image for registration or verification purposes. The DBpHash is generated and compared for visual similarity in the processing module, off of the blockchain. The Smart Contracts in the blockchain retain the ownership entries and verification logs of the images, as registered.

Two Solidity contracts are deployed in the system. The ownership registry contract stores the perceptual hash together with the associated IPFS Content Identifier (CID), owner address, and timestamp. Hash-indexed mappings are used for lookup. Hash-indexed mappings separate ownership records from verification logs.

Blockchain layer security

The blockchain provides an immutable, timestamped record of registration and verification events with respect to non-repudiation. The determination of similar hashes is made based on the results of deterministic processes defined in Eqs. (23) – (29) through the use of smart contracts. Thus, acceptance or rejection decisions remain consistent. The only metadata that is written to the blockchain is the hash of the determined final decision and the hash commitment. Thus, the blockchain secures global, immutable determinations while maximizing computational efficiency. The blockchain secures non-repudiation via an immutable, timestamped record containing registration and verification events. Smart contracts validate deterministic decisions based on Eqs. (27) – (29), ensuring consistent acceptance or rejection outcomes. While the computation of similarity occurs off-chain, the only thing that gets written to the blockchain is the final decision and the hash commitment. This preserves the integrity of the system while optimizing computational efficiency.

System-level security behavior

The system performs consistently and displays strong behavior no matter whether it is operating under ethical conditions or under adversarial conditions due to how the conservative similarity model interacts with the blockchain enforcement layer. An incorrect registration cannot be undone in an immutable ledger, therefore there needs to be a significant effort made within the design of the system in order to reduce the number of false positive acceptances. This ensures that there is reliability in the legally compliant verification of copyright records being kept on the immutable ledger. This design is consistent with the operational constraints discussed earlier in the methodology section.

Results

Experimental setup

All experiments were carried out on a system with an Intel Core i7 processor (3.0 GHz) and 16 GB RAM, using Python 3.10 along with the OpenCV and NumPy libraries. Smart contract deployment and blockchain evaluation were performed on a local Ethereum test network using Hardhat. As there are no publicly available benchmark PCBI datasets, experiments were conducted on the dataset collected by Guo31, which contains 200 images of size . Prior to hash computation, all images were resized to , converted to grayscale, and normalized to the range [0, 1]. This choice is consistent with standard DCT-based perceptual hashing frameworks, where fixed low-resolution normalization is commonly adopted to ensure stable and compact frequency-domain representations. The preprocessing step also makes the proposed DBpHash resolution-independent, i.e., it can be applied to images of different original sizes (e.g., or ) while consistently extracting the hash representation.

Evaluation protocol

Two evaluation categories are defined: genuine pairs (comprising an original image and a distorted version of that image) and impostor pairs (comprising two different PCBI images). This allows us to assess inter-class separability and also calculate the decision threshold. The dataset is split in the ratio of 80% training and 20% testing to ensure that original images and their corresponding distorted versions are contained in the same split of data to prevent data leakage. Evaluation occurs in two phases - the training phase uses the data to create estimates of similarity distributions for genuine and imposter pairs. This is done to create similarities, or weights, to evaluate the performance of the proposed method and calculate the operational decision threshold , and in testing phase, the learned parameters are treated as constant and all performance metrics are computed using the disjoint testing set. This ensures a numerical separation of the two evaluation phases, allowing for unbiased evaluation of the proposed methodology.

Distortion model and evaluation conditions

In each split, we apply five representative attacks independently to evaluate the robustness under realistic distortions. Attacks on photometric invariants such as Gaussian noise (), JPEG compression (quality factor ), and median filtering ( kernel) change the pixel intensity values, but preserve the visual content. Geometric attacks including rotation () and uniform scaling (factor 0.9) cause spatial misalignment and affect the DCT representation. The experimental results are organized into three main parts: (A) performance evaluation against state-of-the-art perceptual hashing methods, (B) distortion-specific and operational characterization, and (C) statistical analysis of hash quality.

Legal considerations and jurisdictional applicability

DBpHash framework is a technical method of copyright registration and verification, not substituting current legal systems. The blockchain timestamp offers a previous tamper-resistant record which can be used as supporting evidence in intellectual property disputes. The on-chain hash commitments and IPFS content identifiers make the process of registering a record to the original image verifiable, providing integrity and non-repudiation. The framework is jurisdiction-neutral and it is independent of national borders. In many countries whose copyright laws are based on the Berne Convention, copyright arises automatically upon creation, and the blockchain record serves as corroborative evidence of authorship and date of creation. In systems involving formal registration, it is used as secondary evidence. In terms of privacy, metadata is stored on-chain, and image data are stored off-chain. In the case of sensitive material, it is advisable to encrypt before uploading. This architecture is consistent with the principles of data minimization, because it does not require direct data storage as an image content on immutable ledgers. In general, DBpHash offers a safe and provable technical basis to copyright assertions, and the enforceability of the law is still under jurisdiction-specific rules.

Conclusion

Digital images can be modified by simple content-preserving distortions and in turn ownership can be infringed easily. Thus, establishing reliable copyright authentication is crucial. Perceptual hashing, which extracts low-frequency components, is widely used for copyright verification of images. But it cannot be used reliably for PCBIs as low-frequency components primarily captures background but not finer structures and local spatial variations. This leads to false positive matches between images with similar backgrounds. To overcome this, the proposed DBpHash utilizes mid and high frequencies bands that represent the differences in textural information, edge transitions and localized intensity variations. Evaluation on a PCBI dataset demonstrates high separation of genuine and imposter image pairs. This is further validated through cross-dataset analysis on the BSDS500 and DTD datasets without retraining. DBpHash has a false positive rate of 0.095 on operational-threshold analysis and the highest balanced accuracy at 0.775 compared to the approaches evaluated. Ownership information is recorded on Ethereum and the image files are stored in IPFS. Hash comparison is performed off-chain to reduce computation cost and only registrations are carried out on chain. Blockchain entries cannot be modified once confirmed as they are immutable. Hence, the operating threshold is set in such a way that the false positives are minimized. This conservative decision-making approach may result in a slight increase of false negatives in the system. Under photometric distortions, the system identifies attacks such as compression, filtering, and change of illumination and similarity values between genuine pairs are greater than 0.92. However, geometric transformations such as rotation and scaling alter spatial alignment in the DCT domain and reduce similarity scores, which is a known limitation of DCT-based systems. DBpHash satisfies the properties of a well-designed perceptual hash, including the balanced bit-distribution, separation of distances, and efficient storage of information per bit. Hence, DBpHash provides a conservative and distortion-aware approach for copyright authentication of PCBIs. Future work will focus on improving robustness against geometric transformations such as rotation and scaling through techniques such as keypoint-based alignment and transformation-invariant feature integration.

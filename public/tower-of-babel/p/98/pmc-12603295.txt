Optimized fall detection using hybrid BiLSTM BiGRU additive attention model and BAOA driven feature selection system
Authors: Mithun Singh Ahirwar, Vaibhav Soni
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the increasing aging population and the prevalence of fall-related injuries among the elderly, the need for precise and reliable fall detection systems has become crucial. Deep learning techniques have been used to play a transformative role in fall detection domain due to their ability to learn complex patterns and relationships in data. However, these methods also face significant challenges and limitations. In this paper we have proposed a novel fall detection method utilizing a hybrid model combining Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) and Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (BiGRU) networks, enhanced with additive attention mechanism and an optimized feature selection process utilizing the Binary Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm (BAOA). The proposed hybrid BiLSTM-BiGRU-Additive Attention Model and BAOA Driven Feature Selection system is designed to proficiently capture temporal dependencies in the data. The model is evaluated on three datasets collected using wearable sensors, namely SisFall, UMAFall, and UP-Fall, experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model significantly outperforms traditional deep learning architectures, and non-optimized versions, achieving accuracies of 99.50%, 99.85%, and 99.68% for the SisFall, UMAFall, and UP-Fall datasets, respectively making our proposed model suitable for real-time fall detection systems.Keywords: Fall detection, Deep learning, BiLSTM, BiGRU, BAOA, Wearable sensorsSubject terms: Health care, Engineering

Introduction

A fall is defined as an event which results in a person coming to rest inadvertently on the ground or floor or other lower level. Fall-related injuries may be fatal or non-fatal, though most are non-fatal. For example, of children in the People’s Republic of China, for every death due to a fall, there are 4 cases of permanent disability, 13 cases requiring hospitalization for more than 10 days, 24 cases requiring hospitalization for 1–9 days and 690 cases seeking medical care1. The increasing prevalence of falls among the elderly population poses a significant public health concern, with falls being the second leading cause of unintentional injury deaths worldwide, according to the World Health Organization (WHO)2. Effective fall detection systems are desperately needed, as these falls frequently result in serious injuries, permanent disabilities, and even fatalities. With an aging population and rising healthcare costs, developing robust, real-time fall detection models is essential for improving safety and enhancing the quality of life for elderly people3. Fall detection is a critical area of research and technology development, primarily aimed at identifying and responding to incidents of unintentional falls, especially among vulnerable populations such as the elderly and individuals with disabilities. Falls are a significant public health concern often leading to serious injuries, reduced quality of life and increased healthcare costs. Developing effective fall detection systems is crucial for timely intervention, minimizing injuries, and improving patient outcomes4. Fall detection systems leverage various sensing technologies, algorithms and machine learning models to automatically detect falls and alert acquaintances of the victim of fall related injuries and emergency services. These systems can operate in real-time and are increasingly integrated into smart devices,wearable technologies and vision based systems5.

Traditional approaches to fall detection have primarily relied on sensor-based systems that use data from wearable, ambient, or camera-based sensors. Though vision based systems have their own merits6, we use sensor based publically available datasets for our work because many adults might not be comfortable with camera based monitoring and might have privacy concerns, especially senior citizens, even thermal cameras, which do not capture high-resolution identity features, may be perceived as intrusive in private environments such as bedrooms or bathrooms. In contrast, our sensor-based method captures only inertial data, offering a privacy-preserving alternative suitable for continuous monitoring both indoors and outdoors without the subject being seen, making sensor based data suitable for real-time fall detection7. However, traditional methods such as threshold-based algorithms and machine learning classifiers often suffer from high false positive rates and limited accuracy in distinguishing between falls and other daily activities. Furthermore, these approaches frequently require extensive manual feature engineering, limiting their ability to generalize across diverse datasets and real-world scenarios8. Application of Fall Detection in healthcare monitoring provides real-time monitoring for elderly individuals or patients with mobility impairments and helps in proactive intervention to prevent complications from delayed assistance9. Application of fall detection helps elderly care by supporting independent living by ensuring safety and timely alerts in case of falls and reduces the burden on caregivers. Application of fall detection facilitates rehabilitation and assists in tracking recovery progress in patients undergoing physical therapy and identifies movements that may pose a fall risk. Application of fall detection in smart homes integrates home automation systems to enhance safety for residents and works with IoT devices for automatic lighting adjustments or emergency calls10. Applications of fall detection in workplace safety systems monitor high-risk environments such as construction sites or factories to detect falls and accidents and enhance compliance with occupational safety standards. Applications of fall detection in sports and fitness field detect abnormal movements during physical activities and provide safety alerts and tracks movement patterns for injury prevention. Many fields are using fall detection systems to maintain the prevention of falls or timely treatment to minimize human loss.

In recent years, the advent of deep learning has revolutionized fall detection systems, enabling automatic feature extraction and end-to-end learning directly from raw sensor data11. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), particularly Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) models, have demonstrated superior performance in sequential data modeling. These models capture temporal dependencies in time-series data, making them particularly suited for applications like fall detection. This potential is further enhanced by bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) and bidirectional GRU (BiGRU) networks, which analyze the input sequence both forward and backward to capture contextual information from past and future12. These methods often suffer from limited generalizability due to their reliance on manually engineered features and static decision boundaries, which do not adapt well to inter-subject variability or noisy sensor data. Furthermore, they struggle with capturing temporal dependencies inherent in human activity patterns, resulting in poor accuracy for real-world fall scenarios.

Even with deep learning’s beneficial results, refining the feature set to guarantee high accuracy while preserving computing efficiency remains a recurring difficulty in fall detection systems. We use the Binary Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm (BAOA) into the fall detection procedure in order to solve this13. A novel metaheuristic algorithm termed as BAOA has demonstrated substantial possibilities for feature selection optimization. BAOA lowers computational complexity and enhances machine learning and deep learning model performance by extracting the most pertinent characteristics from massive datasets. In this study, we suggest using BAOA to extract the most important characteristics from sensor data, simplifying the deep learning model’s input and raising its overall accuracy and efficiency.

This study introduces a novel hybrid model for feature selection that combines the additive attention mechanism, BiLSTM, BiGRU, and BAOA. The attention method allows the model to concentrate on the most pertinent segments of the input sequence, and the BiLSTM-BiGRU architecture efficiently captures the temporal dependencies in fall detection data. By shrinking the feature space and increasing accuracy and computing efficiency, BAOA is used to optimize the feature selection process. Three publicly accessible datasets namely SisFall14, UMAFall15, and UP-Fall16 are used to assess the suggested Hybrid BiLSTM-BiGRU-Additive Attention Model and BAOA Driven Feature Selection System. The experimental findings demonstrate that integrating BAOA with the BiLSTM-BiGRU model achieves state-of-the-art performance across all data sets and greatly increases fall detection accuracy.

BiLSTM captures long-term dependencies in both forward and backward temporal directions which is crucial for understanding context in pre- and post-fall events. BiGRU offers computational efficiency while retaining the ability to model temporal patterns, complementing BiLSTM with reduced complexity and faster convergence. Additive attention allows the model to dynamically focus on the most relevant time steps in a fall sequence, improving interpretability and performance, particularly in multi-sensor scenarios. We compared this model against alternatives in literature such as CNN-LSTMs, which are more image data centric17 and transformers, which require substantially larger datasets and are computationally more intensive. For this study we are using sensor based data as image based data runs through privacy concerns.

Related work

In this section, the summary of techniques employed in fall detection systems is discussed. The development of fall detection systems has progressed significantly in recent years with traditional machine learning and modern deep learning approaches being employed to address this challenge. Early fall detection methods primarily utilized traditional machine learning classifiers such as Support Vector Machines (SVMs)13, K-Nearest Neighbors (KNNs)18, and Random Forest (RF)19. These models required manual feature engineering and performed well under controlled conditions but struggled with generalization across diverse datasets due to the variability in human activities and sensor placements.

Deep learning approaches, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks, Long Short-Term Memory networks and Autoencoders have emerged as promising methods for this purpose. These techniques provide high accuracy in fall detection scenarios and are excellent at processing a variety of data formats, including pictures and sensor signals20. However, there are also drawbacks, such as difficulties extrapolating models to actual settings because many training datasets are controlled. Sensor-based systems such as radars and accelerometers have trouble being deployed in complicated activity scenarios and with several people, whereas vision-based systems, although successful, have privacy issues and background sensitivity21. In order to fill these limitations and develop more flexible and dependable fall detection systems, future research should investigate more robust datasets, improve privacy-preserving features, and incorporate hybrid algorithms that combine visual and sensor modalities.

Recent advancements in wearable systems, sensor fusion, and intelligent control have significantly contributed to the development of real-time monitoring and fall detection frameworks. Gaussian kernel-based similarity approach has been used for multisource information fusion, emphasizing the role of focal element weighting in improving fusion reliability22. In parallel, a highly sensitive and flexible piezoresistive methylcellulose/chitosan@MXene aerogel sensor array, capable of capturing physiological signals in real-time is in use demonstrating the potential of advanced materials in wearable health monitoring23. For assistive locomotion, a multimodal human-exoskeleton cooperative control method based on Central Pattern Generators (CPGs), showcasing biologically inspired control strategies has been introduced to enhance gait planning24. From a safety perspective, a hybrid artificial intelligence model for fall risk prediction in workplace settings has been utilised reinforcing the utility of Artificial Intelligence in proactive risk assessment25. Moreover, an event-triggered critic-learning impedance control strategy for lower limb exoskeletons has been proposed enabling adaptive interaction while optimizing computational cost26. Also, a quantum-optimized approach integrating SqueezeNet with SVM was introduced for efficient medical classification tasks, demonstrating the relevance of lightweight CNN-ML hybrids in constrained environments27. We see that technology is continually being evolved paving way for more accurate AI based fall detection.

By allowing models to automatically generate feature representations from unprocessed sensor data, deep learning has also greatly improved the capabilities of fall detection systems. For this reason, autoencoders have shown promise28. Recent studies have investigated hybrid architectures that integrate several deep learning techniques in order to overcome the shortcomings of individual deep learning models. Long-range relationships in sequential data were well captured by the hybrid Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) and GRU model29. Similarly, the BiLSTM-BiGRU model outperforms independent models in arrhythmia detection when combined with dilated CNNs. These methods demonstrate how integrating several neural network designs can improve accuracy and resilience in time series tasks like fall detection30.

In order to improve the effectiveness of deep learning models for fall prediction, feature selection is essential. Methods like Relief-F, correlation-based feature selection(CFS) and fast correlation-based filter(FCBF) have shown efficacy in selecting pertinent gait and balance features from wearable sensors like accelerometers and pressure-sensing insoles. This is because they reduce the computational complexity of sensor-derived datasets by reducing their dimensionality. These chosen characteristics highlight biomechanical stability metrics that are essential for differentiating between falls and non-falls, such as anterior-posterior acceleration metrics, medial-lateral variability, and stride duration variability. In addition to increasing predictive accuracy by optimizing the input space, feature selection also finds crucial factors that guide clinical interventions for improving stability, especially for older people who are at risk of falling31.

Feature selection plays an important role in enhancing model performance by reducing the dimensionality of input data and focusing on the most relevant features. Various metaheuristic optimization algorithms have been employed for this purpose. The Binary Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm, proposed in recent work, has demonstrated superior performance in selecting optimal feature subsets for classification tasks significantly improving accuracy and reducing computation time. In human activity recognition systems, BAOA has been used in conjunction with CNNs to enhance feature extraction and selection, yielding competitive results across different public datasets32. The Binary Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm is a cutting-edge meta-heuristic technique for feature extraction in discrete optimization problems, particularly suitable for fall detection, inspired by basic arithmetic operators, BAOA adapts continuous search algorithms to discrete domains using innovative transfer functions and enhances search capabilities with the Levy flight mechanism. This approach optimizes the exploration and exploitation phases of feature selection by navigating efficiently through large, multidimensional datasets to identify critical features while avoiding local optima33. The Binary Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm has some drawbacks, such as a high computing cost and scalability issues despite its many benefits for feature selection in complicated datasets. Developing adaptive mechanisms to improve BAOA’s scalability and robustness or hybridizing it with other optimization techniques may be necessary to overcome these constraints.

When used in fall detection systems, bidirectional long short-term memory models have a number of advantages and difficulties. The capacity of BiLSTMs to learn dependencies in both forward and backward temporal directions allows them to process sequential data efficiently which is essential for precisely identifying fall events with dynamic temporal patterns. Even in the presence of noise or complex environmental conditions, this dual-directional capability improves the model’s robustness in differentiating between fall and non-fall activities34. Nevertheless, there are difficulties with using BiLSTMs for fall detection. Because of their intricate design, these models demand a significant amount of processing power to train, which makes real-time deployment on devices with limited resources difficult. Furthermore, the availability of labeled training data and the quality of input features have a significant impact on the performance of BiLSTM-based systems. It is still challenging to provide resilience in a variety of real-world scenarios, such as changing user behavior and external noise levels.

BiGRU is a potent instrument in fall detection systems that efficiently processes sequential data by utilizing its bidirectional architecture. BiGRU improves the model’s comprehension of intricate temporal patterns related to falls by evaluating sensor data in both forward and backward temporal directions35. Even in noisy or unpredictable situations, BiGRU is very good at differentiating falls from other activities thanks to its dual-perspective analysis36. BiGRU’s effectiveness is highly dependent on the caliber and variety of training data, which must accurately reflect the vast range of human motions and ambient circumstances. This is a significant problem. BiGRU can still be resource-intensive for real-time applications on low-power devices, despite being more effective than some deep learning models. Additional challenges include making sure the system is robust in the handling sensor noise and individual diversity.

The process known as additive attention for fall detection allows the model to prioritize information that is essential for detecting falls by dynamically focusing on the most pertinent portions of sensor data or extracted features37. In neural networks, additive attention is a flexible mechanism that dynamically shifts the emphasis on the most pertinent aspects of the input data. Because of its versatility, it works especially well for challenging tasks like categorization, object identification, and image captioning. Additive attention methods boost the model’s capacity to process contextual information by highlighting useful aspects and suppressing irrelevant ones, providing improved performance and resilience in a range of situations. These methods are a sensible option for many applications because they are lightweight and easily incorporated into current architectures38. Because additive attention can efficiently manage heterogeneous and multi-dimensional inputs, it shows promise when applied to sensor-based data. Variable noise levels, missing values, or different scales across dimensions are common in sensor data. By concentrating on significant signals and lessening the influence of noise or extraneous dimensions, additive attention can assist in overcoming these difficulties.

Recent works have shown the effectiveness of combining attention mechanisms with recurrent architectures for sequential pattern recognition, validating our hybrid BiLSTM-BiGRU-attention approach for temporal activity modeling39. Optimized metaheuristic algorithms have been increasingly adopted for enhancing feature selection in sensor-based systems, supporting our choice of BAOA for dimensionality reduction and model refinement40. Hybrid deep learning architectures integrating multiple RNN variants have demonstrated improved temporal representation learning, which aligns with our BiLSTM-BiGRU ensemble strategy41. Smart environment applications increasingly rely on intelligent fall detection systems, reinforcing the relevance of our sensor-based approach for elderly monitoring42. Deep learning-driven activity recognition in smart environments is becoming central to ambient assisted living systems, supporting the context of our proposed fall detection framework43. New methods for dynamic system parameter estimation using neural ordinary differential equations which everages physical equation priors to improve accuracy and robustness under noisy, sparse data conditions44 and fatigue detection systems,firefighter accident prevention45 and traditional impedance controller design algorithms are also being studied just like fall detection46 but proper care must be taken to protect sensors, which might, without sufficient protection, may lead to critical mechanical instablity47 but for our model we are working with publicly available datasets and their comparison with existing models, so this sensor instability or real world manipulation does not apply to our work, though we have addressed noise, missing values and normalisation for existing datasets in the pre-processing section.

This paper’s hybrid BiLSTM-BiGRU architecture with additive attention mechanism demonstrates an innovative technique to integrating BAOA into deep learning models for fall detection. Before supplying the sensor data to the BiLSTM-BiGRU model, BAOA is used to extract the most pertinent features, increasing the system’s accuracy and efficiency. The attention method is used to focus on important time steps, BiLSTM and BiGRU are used to capture temporal dependencies, and BAOA is used to reduce the feature space and computational complexity. The robustness and real-time applicability of the system in fall detection are greatly improved by this integrated method. The method is more effective and efficient for fall detection because of this framework, which seamlessly integrates BAOA with the original deep learning model.

Dataset description

The fall detection data used for our research in this paper is obtained from the SisFall, UMAFall and UPFall datasets which contain data readings from multiple channels capturing human motion during simulated falls. The dataset includes features such as acceleration and orientation recorded by inertial wearable sensors(e.g., accelerometers and gyroscopes).

The SisFall14, UMAFall15, and UPFall16 datasets, which comprise data readings from many channels capturing human motion during simulated falls, provided the fall detection data used for our research in this paper. Features like orientation and acceleration captured by inertial wearable sensors (such as gyroscopes and accelerometers) are included in the dataset.

A. SisFall dataset: A specially designed embedded device was used to gather this dataset including the participation of twenty-three individuals aged 19–30. The device for data collection was made up of an ITG3200 gyroscope from Texas Instruments, Dallas, Texas, USA (±2000/s with a 16-bit ADC), an ADXL345 accelerometer from Analog Devices, Norwood, Massachusetts, USA (configured for ±16 g with a 13-bit ADC), a Freescale MMA8451Q accelerometer (±8 g with a 14-bit ADC), and an MKL25Z128VLK4 microcontroller from NPX, Austin, Texas, USA. The gadget was powered by a generic 1000 mAh battery and had an SD card for data recording. When utilizing a single accelerometer device, it was fastened to the participants’ waists, a position that is known to significantly differentiate between activities. The SisFall dataset comprises recordings from 38 participants using a custom device with two accelerometers and one gyroscope. It includes falls and non falls, 23 young adults performed all activities, 14 elderly participants (aged 62+) performed 15 ADLs, and one 60-year-old participant performed falls.

B. UMAFall dataset: A variety of software environments, from basic spreadsheet applications to sophisticated mathematical analytic tools (like Matlab), may readily access and interpret this dataset, which consists of 209 simulated falls saved in CSV format by 17 experimental individuals. Each file contains the 15-second traces, or accelerometer, gyroscope, and magnetometer data, that were recorded by sensors during a single trial of a certain movement type. The UMAFall dataset involves 17 subjects (9 male, 8 female) aged between 18 and 45 years, performing falls and non falls. The data was captured using a smartphone-based triaxial accelerometer and gyroscope placed on the waist.

C. UPFall dataset: The UP-Fall Detection dataset was collected using 17 healthy young subjects without any impairment using multiple modalities namely wearable sensors. The volunteers performed simulated falls, with three attempts each. They used five wearable sensors to collect accelerometer, gyroscope and ambient light data. The UP-Fall dataset contains recordings from 12 participants (gender balanced), with ages ranging from 18 to 24 years.

Class imbalance handling

Fall detection datasets are often imbalanced, with significantly fewer fall instances compared to Activities of Daily Living (ADLs). To address this, a weighted categorical cross-entropy loss was employed during model training, assigning higher penalties to minority class (fall) misclassifications. This approach helps mitigate class bias and improves the sensitivity of fall detection.

Missing value treatment

All datasets were initially checked for integrity. In cases where minor gaps were identified, linear interpolation was applied to ensure temporal continuity. Samples with substantial missing segments were discarded to maintain data quality and avoid misleading patterns during model training.

The preprocessed data is then split into training, validation, and test sets for subsequent model training and evaluation.

The proposed model has been rigorously evaluated across three widely used and publicly available benchmark datasets namely SisFall, UMAFall, and UP-Fall which differ in participant demographics (young adults vs. elderly), sensor types and placements, and the nature of fall scenarios and daily activities. This diversity supports generalizability within controlled settings. However, we acknowledge that these datasets are simulated and laboratory-controlled, where falls are enacted in constrained environments by healthy volunteers rather than fall-prone individuals. Although such datasets provide a reproducible and fair basis for benchmarking, they may not fully capture the environmental variability, sensor displacement, and behavioral dynamics encountered during real-world deployments. To address sensor inconsistencies and noise, our model incorporates robust preprocessing techniques, including Chebyshev Type II filtering, Daubechies wavelet denoising, Z-score normalization, and missing value handling, enhancing its resilience under noisy input conditions. Real-time deployment on edge devices such as wearables will necessitate further hardware optimization. The current scope of this work remains centered on algorithmic development and empirical benchmarking using standardized datasets.

Feature selection using BAOA

Feature selection plays a critical role in reducing the dimensionality of input data and improving computational efficiency. Before supplying the pre-processed sensor data to the deep learning model, we use the Binary Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm in this study to choose the most relevant features. The arithmetic operations of addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division served as the inspiration for the metaheuristic algorithm known as BAOA. By identifying feature subsets that contribute most significantly to classification accuracy, it has demonstrated exceptional performance in feature selection problems.

To find the most pertinent features, the preprocessed sensor data is processed through the BAOA. BAOA reduces superfluous or redundant characteristics by iteratively evaluating subsets of features and choosing those that maximize classification performance. By concentrating only on the most crucial information, this procedure lowers the input space’s complexity and improves the model’s computational efficiency. A smaller set of features is the result of the BAOA process, and this reduced set of features is subsequently fed into the BiLSTM-BiGRU model for additional processing. Regarding temporal focus enhancement, While BiLSTM and BiGRU capture bidirectional temporal dependencies, they process all time steps with equal importance. The additive attention mechanism introduces a dynamic weighting layer that enables the model to focus more strongly on the most informative time steps, such as sudden accelerations or impact patterns typically associated with falls, thereby improving temporal resolution in decision-making. Regarding spatial features, Although our approach does not use CNNs for spatial feature extraction, the feature vectors across sensor axes are aggregated over time. Attention helps the model implicitly learn which sensor dimensions and moments contribute more significantly to classification, thus aiding both spatial and temporal interpretation49.

Experimental results

In this section, we present the experimental results of the proposed BiLSTM-BiGRU model enhanced with additive attention mechanism and optimized using the Binary Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm. The performance of the model is evaluated on three publicly available datasets namely SisFall, UMAFall, and UP-Fall, with respect to accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and computational efficiency. We also compare the performance of the proposed model with traditional machine learning classifiers and deep learning models without BAOA optimization.

Experimental setup

The experiments were conducted using three well-known fall detection datasets namely SisFall, UMAFall, and UP-Fall. Each dataset contains wearable sensor data collected from elderly subjects performing daily activities and simulated fall events. The data was preprocessed using Chebyshev filtering, wavelet denoising, and Z-score normalization as described in the methodology. Feature selection was performed using BAOA and the final set of features was used to train the BiLSTM-BiGRU model with addtitive attention mechanism.

Conclusion and future work

We developed a unique fall detection system in this research that combines Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory and Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit networks with an additive attention mechanism with the help of the Binary Arithmetic Optimization Algorithm for feature selection. The combination of these techniques allows the model to effectively capture both short-term and long-term dependencies in sequential data while selecting the most relevant features to improve performance and computational efficiency. The experimental results on three publicly available fall detection datasets namely SisFall, UMAFall and UP-Fall demonstrated that the proposed model outferforms state-of-the-art models’ performance in terms of accuracy and F1-score. BAOA significantly reduced the number of features used in the model. The proposed model provides a robust, accurate, and efficient solution for fall detection, addressing both the technical challenges of feature selection and the need for optimum performance.

The future work might include practical implementation challenges, such as latency in real-time fall detection systems. Though we have worked extensively on accuracy, some other parameters which avoid overfitting concerns and dataset generalizability might be tested. The future work might also include applying self-supervised learning techniques to enhance adaptability with minimal labeled data, especially in edge or home-based deployments and Exploring domain adaptation techniques to address distribution shifts across sensor modalities or subject groups. Also, Our experiments were conducted using only wearable sensor data from publicly available benchmark datasets. As this study focuses on algorithmic development and validation, hardware-level performance optimization (e.g., real-time inference, model compression) is considered beyond the current scope and will be explored in future work. The model is feasible for integration into hardware devices such as IoT-based wearables, smartphones, or embedded fall detection units. We acknowledge that embedding into devices like smartwatches requires further hardware-specific optimization (e.g., TensorFlow Lite or ONNX model compression) which we plan to pursue as part of our future work on edge deployment which requires extending this research by evaluating the computational efficiency and real-time inference latency of the proposed model on embedded or wearable hardware platforms. Although this work focuses on algorithmic innovation and validation on public datasets, researchers can, in future, plan to collaborate with healthcare institutions to conduct pilot trials and adapt the model within broader clinical infrastructures with high scalability, ensuring alignment with medical device standards.

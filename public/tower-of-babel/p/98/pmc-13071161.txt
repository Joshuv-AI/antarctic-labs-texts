Identification of an m7G-Related lncRNA Signature for Prognostic Prediction and Immune Landscape Characterization in Early Gastric Cancer
Authors: Yuan Zhang, Hongmei Cai, Kaige Yin, Li Liu, Mingshuang Yang, Chenguang Ji
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWhile early gastric cancer (EGC) is generally associated with a favorable prognosis, its clinical outcomes are notably heterogeneous. Unlike advanced gastric cancer requiring uniform intensive treatment, EGC lacks robust biomarkers to identify high-risk patients for personalized therapy. Although both N7-methylguanosine (m7G) modification and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are involved in tumorigenesis, the prognostic value of m7G-related lncRNAs in EGC remains poorly defined.MethodsBased on TCGA-EGC data, we identified m7G-related lncRNAs with prognostic significance and constructed a prognostic model for m7G-related lncRNAs using univariate Cox and LASSO. The expression of the model genes was further validated by qRT-PCR. A series of statistical and bioinformatic analyses, including survival analysis, ROC curves, multivariate Cox regression, GSEA, immune infiltration, tumor mutation burden (TMB), and drug sensitivity assays, were performed to evaluate the prognostic performance and underlying biological characteristics of the signature.ResultsA six-m7G-related-lncRNA risk model effectively stratified EGC patients into high- and low-risk groups with significantly distinct overall survival. The risk score was confirmed as an independent prognostic factor by univariate and multivariate Cox analysis. Pathway differences between risk groups were primarily enriched in tumor microenvironment regulation terms. Notably, the high-risk group exhibited an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment correlated with immune escape. TMB analysis showed a negative correlation between the risk score and TMB, with the high-risk and low-TMB subgroup exhibiting the poorest prognosis. In silico drug sensitivity analysis further suggested that high-risk patients may develop poorer sensitivity to five clinical drugs, including Cisplatin, Oxaliplatin, and Vinorelbine.ConclusionThe six-m7G-related-lncRNA signature represents a promising prognostic tool for EGC that may facilitate personalized risk stratification and guide clinical decision-making regarding adjuvant or immunotherapeutic strategies.Keywords: early gastric cancer, m7G, lncRNA, prognostic signature, immune infiltration

Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) remains a formidable global health challenge, ranking as the fifth most common malignancy and the fourth leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide.1,2 With advances in diagnostic techniques and the widespread adoption of health screening programs, the proportion of patients diagnosed with early gastric cancer (EGC), confined to the mucosa or submucosa regardless of lymph node metastasis, has steadily increased.3 The symptoms of EGC often overlap with those of common benign conditions (chronic gastritis or dyspepsia) and are easily overlooked by both patients and clinicians.4 Common symptoms include mild epigastric discomfort, bloating, belching, acid reflux, loss of appetite, and mild nausea.5 Currently, endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR) and endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) have become the preferred methods for treating low-risk EGC that meets specific criteria, owing to their minimally invasive nature and ability to preserve gastric function.6 Nevertheless, even after undergoing curative endoscopic resection, approximately 2–35% of patients with EGC still face the risk of recurrence or metastasis,7,8 suggesting that some EGCs harbor aggressive molecular characteristics despite appearing early by conventional standards. Accurate identification of these biologically high-risk patients is essential to guide adjuvant therapy and personalize surveillance strategies. Consequently, the development of reliable biomarkers for effective prognostic risk stratification has emerged as a critical research focus, with the goal of optimizing management and improving long-term outcomes in EGC patients.

In recent years, the role of RNA modifications in tumorigenesis and cancer progression has garnered increasing attention. Among them, N7-methylguanosine (m7G), a common type of post-transcriptional modification, is widely present in the 5’ cap structures of mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA. Functionally, m7G modification is critically involved in the regulation of RNA stability, splicing, nuclear export, and translation initiation, thereby governing fundamental cellular processes.9 Given its essential roles in RNA metabolism, dysregulation of m7G modification and its associated regulators has been increasingly implicated in cancer pathogenesis. m7G modification participates in multiple cancer hallmarks, including sustained cell proliferation, resistance to cell death, invasion and metastasis, metabolic reprogramming, genomic instability, and immune evasion.9 For instance, METTL1-mediated m7G tRNA modification drives the proliferation and metastasis of thyroid cancer by promoting the codon-specific translation of TNF-α.10 In addition, m7G methyltransferase complex RNMT/FAM103A1 and its upstream miRNAs play a critical role in breast cancer progression by influencing prognosis and tumor immune infiltration.11 However, the role of m7G modification appears to be highly context-dependent, as emerging evidence has also revealed tumor-suppressive functions in certain settings. Specifically, METTL1 has been reported to suppress lung cancer and osteosarcoma progression by promoting miRNA maturation.12,13 Pertinently, emerging studies have also suggested that aberrant expression of m7G-related genes is associated with clinical outcomes and tumor behaviors in GC,14,15 highlighting the potential relevance of m7G-associated regulators in GC pathophysiology.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are a class of RNA transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides that do not encode proteins and play important regulatory roles in the initiation and progression of cancer.16 Studies have shown that the dysregulation of lncRNA expression is closely associated with the initiation, progression, and prognosis of various cancers. For example, lncRNA HOTAIR is highly expressed in multiple cancer types and is strongly linked to tumor invasion and metastasis,17 and lncRNA MALAT1 shows elevated expression in lung and colorectal cancers and correlates with tumor proliferation and poor prognosis.18 Moreover, lncRNAs play a significant role in the initiation, progression, and prognosis of GC. Sun et al discovered that LINC01016 promotes the invasion and metastasis of GC by inhibiting EIF4A3-mediated degradation of MMP9 mRNA.19 Although previous studies have reported the roles of lncRNAs in tumorigenesis and their potential as biomarkers, the functions of m7G-related lncRNAs specifically in in EGC remain unclear. Therefore, this study systematically screened for m7G-related lncRNAs in EGC using the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) data, constructed a prognostic model, and further analyzed tumor microenvironment infiltration, drug sensitivity, and somatic mutation profiles, with the aim of providing a theoretical basis for personalized treatment and prognostic assessment.

Materials and Methods

Data Sources and Processing

The RNA-seq expression data, corresponding survival information, and clinicopathological data for GC were obtained from the UCSC Xena database (https://xena.ucsc.edu). The lncRNA annotation data were retrieved from the GENCODE database (https://www.gencodegenes.org). EGC samples were defined as those with pathological Stage I (including Stage IA, IB) and Stage II (including Stage IIA, IIB) according to the AJCC staging criteria documented in the TCGA database.20–22 From these sources, a total of 195 GC tissue samples with complete survival information and classified as early-stage (Stage I and II) were selected to form the EGC dataset, which included 172 EGC samples and 23 control samples. This dataset was randomly divided into a training set (97 samples) and a validation set (98 samples) for subsequent construction and validation of the prognostic model.

Identification of m7G-Related lncRNAs

Based on existing studies,23–27 seven m7G-related genes (METTL1, WDR4, TRMT112, RNMT, DCPS, AGO2, and EIF4G3) were identified. Pearson correlation analysis was employed to evaluate the associations between lncRNAs and these m7G genes, with thresholds set at |r| > 0.45 and p < 0.05. LncRNAs showing significant correlation with m7G genes were subsequently included in univariate Cox regression analysis.

Development and Validation of m7G-Related lncRNA Prognostic Model

Prior to univariate Cox analysis, the proportional hazards (PH) assumption was tested for each m7G-related lncRNA using Schoenfeld residuals. Only genes satisfying the PH assumption (p > 0.05) were retained for subsequent analysis. The prognostic value of m7G-related lncRNAs was then assessed through univariate Cox analysis, and genes significantly associated with prognosis (p < 0.05) were screened. Subsequently, the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was performed using the glmnet package (version 4.1–10) in R software (version 4.2.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) to construct a prognostic prediction model, which was trained in the EGC training set. To validate the model’s generalizability, its predictive accuracy was evaluated in the EGC validation set.

During model construction, patients with EGC were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups using the median risk score as the cutoff. The R packages “survival” and “survminer” were used to plot Kaplan-Meier survival curves to analyze survival differences between the two groups. A Log Rank test p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Additionally, the R package “timeROC” was utilized to generate time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated to evaluate the predictive performance of the model at different time points.

Correlation Between Risk Score and Clinical Characteristics and Construction of a Nomogram

To evaluate the independence of the risk score in prognostic prediction, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses (performed using the survival package, version 3.7–0) were performed. Univariate analysis preliminarily explored the relationship between each variable (including the risk score and other clinical characteristics) and survival, while multivariate analysis assessed the independent predictive value of each variable for prognosis after adjusting for confounding factors.

To further enhance the clinical applicability of the prognostic model, a nomogram was constructed using the R package “rms”. This nomogram translates the influence of each predictive variable into a visual score to predict patients’ 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival probabilities. Calibration curves were employed to evaluate the consistency between the predicted probabilities of the nomogram and the actual observed outcomes, thereby validating the accuracy and clinical utility of the model at different time points.

Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA)

To investigate the functional implications of the differentially expressed mRNAs between the high-risk and low-risk groups, functional enrichment analysis was performed. The R package “limma” was used to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between high- and low-risk groups, with the screening criteria of |log2(fold change)| >1 and the adjusted p value < 0.05. Then Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis were performed to elucidate the potential functions of these genes in terms of biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC), molecular functions (MF), and signaling pathways. Additionally, GSEA was conducted using the “c2.cp.kegg.v7.2.symbols.gmt” gene set to explore potential underlying regulatory mechanisms.

Assessment of Immune Cell Infiltration

The “xCell” algorithm (performed using the xCell package, version 1.1.0) was used to evaluate the differences in the levels of immune cell infiltration within the tumor microenvironment (TME) of EGC patients between the high-risk group and the low-risk group. Using the “TIDE” algorithm, we analyzed the differences in immune escape scores, including tumor immune dysfunction and exclusion (TIDE), microsatellite instability (MSI) and exclusion, between the high- and low-risk groups of EGC patients.

Tumor Mutation Burden (TMB) Analysis

To investigate the association between somatic mutation patterns and risk stratification, somatic mutation data were obtained from the TCGA database. The R package “maftools” was utilized to integrate and analyze the gene mutation profiles of high-risk and low-risk EGC patients, enabling a comprehensive evaluation of the genetic variation landscape across different risk groups.

Drug Sensitivity Prediction

The R package “pRRophetic” was employed to predict the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of commonly used anticancer drugs. The Wilcoxon rank-sum test was then applied to compare the differences in IC50 values between the high-risk and low-risk groups to evaluate drug sensitivity.

RNA Extraction and Quantitative Reverse Transcriptase PCR (qRT-PCR)

In order to further confirm the differential expression of the six m7G-related lncRNAs, we collected 9 pairs of fresh EGC and adjacent tissues from the Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University and analyzed the expression level by qRT-PCR. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University (2022-R066). Written informed consent has been obtained from all participants for this study. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent and reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the FastKing cDNA First Strand Synthesis Kit (TIANGEN, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Quantitative PCR was performed with SuperReal PreMix Plus (TIANGEN, China) on a Gene-9660 real-time PCR system (BIOER, China). Relative expression of the lncRNAs was quantified using the 2–ΔΔCT method, with GAPDH and ACTB serving as internal reference genes. Each PCR reaction was conducted in triplicate. The primer sequences are provided in Supplementary Table 1.

Results

Identification of m7G-Related lncRNAs in EGC Patients

We evaluated the differences in the expression of seven m7G-related genes between EGC and normal tissues, including METTL1, WDR4, TRMT112, RNMT, DCPS, AGO2, and EIF4G3. The expression levels of these genes were significantly upregulated in EGC patient tissues (p < 0.05) (Figure 1A). Using Pearson correlation analysis, we identified 457 candidate lncRNAs significantly associated with seven m7G-related genes for subsequent univariate Cox regression analysis (Figure 1B).

Differentially expressed m7G-related genes and identification of m7G-related lncRNA. (A) The differential expression of 7 m7G-related genes between tumor and normal tissues from the EGC. *p < 0.05, ***p < 0.001. (B) Pearson correlation analysis between m7G-related genes and lncRNAs.

Development and Validation of Prognostic Models

We utilized univariate Cox regression to filter ten lncRNAs significantly associated with prognosis (Figure 2A), and LASSO regression identified six robust prognostic lncRNAs (Figure 2B and C). The risk score was calculated using the following formula: Risk Score = (0.3137 × AL096828.3) - (0.043 × TNFRSF10A.AS1) + (0.629 × CEP83.DT) + (0.082 × NDUFV2.AS1) - (0.180 × AC011476.3) - (0.220 × THAP9.AS1). Then, patients with EGC were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups using the median risk score as the cutoff. The differential expression of prognostic lncRNAs between the two groups was investigated (Figure 2D). The expression levels of AL096828.3, CEP83.DT, and NDUFV2.AS1 were significantly higher in the high-risk group compared to the low-risk group (p < 0.05), while the expression levels of TNFRSF10A.AS1, AC011476.3, and THAP9.AS1 were significantly lower in the high-risk group (p < 0.05). Subsequently, the prognostic model was verified. In the EGC training set, the OS was shorter in the high-risk group than in the low-risk group, and the AUC values for 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival were 0.782, 0.780, and 0.826, respectively (Figure 3A–C). In the validation set, the corresponding AUC values were 0.771, 0.728, and 0.714 and the low-risk group had a higher survival rate (Figure 3D–F). For the entire dataset, the high-risk group exhibited poorer survival rates (p < 0.05), with AUC values of 0.758, 0.704, and 0.724 for 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively (Figure 3G–I).

The six lncRNAs highlighted in green represent the final robust candidates selected. (B) Lasso regression of the 6 m7G-related lncRNAs. Validation of prognostic models for m7G-related lncRNAs. Risk score and survival status distribution, Kaplan-Meier curves, and ROC curves in the training (A–C), validation (D–F), and entire EGC (G–I) cohorts.

The Differentially Expressed and Independent Prognostic Analysis of m7G-Related lncRNAs

Expression analysis revealed that all six prognostic genes were significantly more highly expressed in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues (Figure 4A). Then, based on the median expression level of each gene, patients were stratified into high- and low-expression groups to assess their individual prognostic impact. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated that low expression of NDUFV2.AS1, AL096828.3, and CEP83.DT, as well as high expression of TNFRSF10A.AS1, were significantly associated with higher survival (Figure 4B–G).

The expression and independent prognostic analysis of m7G-related lncRNAs. (A) The expression levels of the 6 prognostic lncRNAs in EGC tumor tissues versus normal tissues. (B–G) Kaplan-Meier survival analysis of EGC patients stratified by the expression of the six prognostic lncRNAs.

Prognostic Validation and Nomogram Construction of the m7G-Related lncRNA Model

To determine whether the risk score can independently predict the prognosis of EGC patients, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed on the risk score and clinical pathological features. In univariate Cox regression analysis, risk score was significantly related to the prognosis of the EGC patient (Figure 5A). In multivariate Cox regression analysis, T stage, N stage, gender and risk score were significantly related to the prognosis (Figure 5B). The risk score was incorporated into a nomogram model to predict patients’ 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival rates (Figure 5C). The calibration curves demonstrated that the nomogram model had good prognostic predictive capability (Figure 5D). Similarly, the ROC curve (Figure 5E) and decision curve analysis (Figure 5F) also indicated that the prognostic model possessed favorable predictive performance, with AUC values of 0.758, 0.704, and 0.724 for 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year survival, respectively.

(C) Construction of the nomogram based on the risk score. ***p < 0.001 indicates that the factor is a highly significant predictor in the model. The values highlighted in red represent a prediction example for a specific patient (the numbers indicate the probability of death). (D) Calibration curves assessed the agreement between the actual outcomes and the nomogram-predicted probabilities at 1, 3, and 5 years. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis

A total of 576 significantly DEGs, comprising 290 upregulated and 286 downregulated genes, were identified between the high- and low-risk groups of EGC patients. To further investigate the biological functions underlying the transcriptional differences between the high- and low-risk groups, GSEA was conducted. Using the entire transcriptome ranked by expression difference, we found that the most significantly enriched pathways included the complement and coagulation cascades, neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, RIG-I-like receptor signaling pathway, and apoptosis (Figure 6A). To further elucidate the biological functions, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed. GO analysis indicated that these differentially expressed genes were significantly enriched in BP including heterotypic cell-cell adhesion and vascular processes in circulatory, CC such as cornified envelope and collagen-containing extracellular matrix, as well as MF such as cell adhesion mediator activity and phospholipid binding (Figure 6B). Meanwhile, KEGG pathway analysis suggested that the p53 signaling pathway and ECM-receptor interaction were among the predominantly enriched pathways (Figure 6C).

GSEA pathways are ranked by the Normalized Enrichment Score (NES) after filtering by adjusted p-value. (B and C) GO and KEGG analysis of DEGs based on different risk subgroups. The GO/KEGG terms are ranked by the adjusted p-value.

Evaluation of Immune Infiltration Status Based on the Risk Model

To investigate the differences in the immune microenvironment between different risk groups of EGC patients, we used the “xCell” algorithm to quantitatively analyze the infiltration levels of immune cells. There were 17 cell types with significant differences between the high-risk and low-risk groups, such as class-switched memory B-cells, mast cells, NK cells, and NKT cells (Figure 7A). We also found that the NKT immune score was significantly positively correlated with the risk score, while the NK cells immune score was significantly negatively correlated with the risk score (Figure 7B and C). MSI and Exclusion scores were significantly different between the two groups. TIDE and Exclusion scores were significantly positively correlated with the risk score, while the MSI score was significantly negatively correlated with the risk score (Figure 7D–I). Additionally, we analyzed the correlation between various cytokines and the risk score. The results revealed that six chemokines, two interleukins, four interferons, and four other cytokines were significantly correlated with the risk score (Figure 7J).

Tumor Mutational Burden and Drug Sensitivity Analysis

The somatic mutation waterfall plot revealed distinct genes with the highest mutation frequencies in the high-risk and low-risk groups, specifically TP53 (60%) and TTN (59%), respectively (Figure 8A and B). Correlation analysis demonstrated a significant negative association between TMB and the risk score (Figure 8C). Consistent with this, the TMB level in the high-risk group was significantly lower than that in the low-risk group (Figure 8D). Furthermore, patients were stratified into high- and low-TMB groups based on the median TMB value and then combined with the risk score to create composite subgroups. Survival analysis indicated that patients with high-risk and low-TMB had the worst prognosis, while those with low-risk and high-TMB had the most favorable outcome (Figure 8E). In silico drug sensitivity analysis identified five antitumor agents (Cisplatin, Oxaliplatin, Vinorelbine, 5-Fluorouracil, Paclitaxel) with significantly higher IC50 values in high-risk group compared with low-risk group (Figure 8F–J). These findings suggest that high-risk patients may have lower sensitivity to the aforementioned drugs, highlighting the potential importance of this observation in guiding clinical treatment strategy selection.

(C) Correlation between TMB score and risk score. (D) The difference in TMB levels between the high-risk and low-risk groups of EGC patients. (E) Composite survival curve of TMB score and risk score in EGC patients. Expression of m7G-Related lncRNAs in Tissues

The expression profiles of m7G-related lncRNAs in both cancerous and adjacent non-cancerous tissues from patients with EGC were assessed using qRT-PCR. The results demonstrated that all six prognostic-related m7G-lncRNAs were upregulated in tumor tissues compared with adjacent normal tissues (Figure 9). Significant differences were observed specifically in AL096828.3, TNFRSF10A-AS1, CEP83-DT, and THAP9-AS1. These findings are largely consistent with our analytical results, thereby further validating the accuracy of the established risk model.

Discussion

GC is the fifth most common malignant tumor worldwide, with an extremely low five-year survival rate in its advanced stages.28 EGC presents with mild symptoms that are often confused with benign conditions such as chronic gastritis and indigestion, leading to the majority of patients being diagnosed at an advanced stage5 Therefore, it is crucial to develop novel biomarkers more closely related to the early detection and prognosis of EGC. This study developed and validated a novel m7G-related lncRNA signature for EGC, which effectively predicted patient prognosis and was systematically linked to the immune microenvironment and therapy response.

In this study, we identified six m7G-associated lncRNAs into the final prognostic signature, namely AL096828.3, TNFRSF10A.AS1, CEP83.DT, NDUFV2.AS1, AC011476.3 and THAP9.AS1. Di et al found that the reduction of TNFRSF10A.AS1 expression significantly inhibited the proliferation, migration and invasion abilities of colorectal cancer cells.29 Additionally, study has shown that THAP9.AS1 is highly expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma and can promote cancer cell proliferation, making it a risk factor indicating poor prognosis for patients.30 A bioinformatics analysis study revealed that AC011476.3, as a copper death-related lncRNA, may affect the death or proliferation process of hepatocellular carcinoma cells by regulating the expression of copper death-related genes or related signaling pathways.31 NDUFV2-AS1 has been identified as a novel lncRNA that may promote the development of melanoma resistance to BRAF-targeted drugs.32 However, the functions of AL096828.3 and CEP83.DT in tumor development remain to be fully elucidated. Existing clues suggest their important regulatory roles in cancers, but their specific biological functions and clinical implications require further in-depth investigation through subsequent experiments. Recent studies have shown that m7G modification regulates RNA stability and translation, thereby affecting oncogenic pathways.33,34 The lncRNAs in our study might modulate this process by interacting with m7G regulators or acting as ceRNAs, though further experiments are needed. The significant upregulation of these six lncRNAs in patients with EGC highlights their crucial role in the occurrence and progression of this disease. Therefore, incorporating them into the construction of the prognostic model may provide new theoretical basis for the clinical prognosis assessment of EGC. Interestingly, we observed that AC011476.3, THAP9.AS1 and TNFRSF10A.AS1 were associated with better overall survival in EGC patients, despite being significantly upregulated in tumor tissues. This seemingly paradoxical observation may reflect the highly context-dependent nature of lncRNAs functions, which can vary across cancer types and disease stages.35 In EGC, upregulation of these lncRNAs might represent protective cellular responses such as active anti-tumor immunity or stress defense mechanisms, rather than pro-tumorigenic activities. Additionally, these lncRNAs may function as competing endogenous RNAs that sponge oncogenic miRNAs, thereby indirectly upregulating tumor suppressor targets and improving patient survival.36 Based on these six lncRNAs, we established a novel prognostic signature and constructed a corresponding risk score model. The predictive accuracy of the signature was evaluated by ROC analysis. Notably, our risk score appeared to be more effective in predicting outcomes compared to traditional indicators such as TNM stage. This improved performance may be attributed to the fact that current risk stratification relies mainly on clinicopathological features, which fail to capture molecular heterogeneity. By providing complementary molecular information, our model may help identify high-risk patients within conventionally defined low-risk groups, potentially guiding more personalized surveillance and treatment decisions. Thus, this signature not only provides accurate prognosis but also holds potential as a complementary tool to conventional staging for personalized risk assessment. Notably, well-characterized lncRNAs including HOTAIR, MALAT1, and LINC01016 were not captured by our m7G-related screening, reflecting the unique lncRNA landscape associated with m7G modification in EGC.

In the GSEA, the complement and coagulation cascades, neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, RIG-I-like receptor signaling pathway, and apoptosis were significantly enriched in the high- and low-risk groups. In low-grade glioma, aberrant expression of complement and coagulation cascade-related genes is linked to poor patient prognosis.37 In prostate cancer, neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction is linked to tumor expansion and metastasis.38 In glioma, abnormal activation of neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction is associated with malignant progression of the tumor.39 The anti-tumor function of RIG-I-like receptor signaling pathway activation is well-established across most cancers, primarily exerting its anti-tumor effects through an IFN-dependent mechanism.40 These differential pathways are all associated with the tumor immune microenvironment, invasive and metastatic capabilities, and treatment resistance.37,41,42 KEGG pathway analysis indicated that the differential pathways between the high- and low-risk groups were primarily the p53 signaling pathway and the ECM-receptor interaction. These pathways are crucial for processes including cell cycle, apoptosis, genomic stability, adhesion, migration, tumor invasion, and metastasis.43,44 This finding suggests that the high-risk phenotype of EGC is driven by multiple systems, including immune, neural, and cell death processes.

Immune cell infiltration analysis revealed a significant reduction in NK cell infiltration within the high-risk group, which showed a negative correlation with the risk score. Given that NK cells serve as the “first line of defense” in anti-tumor immunity, their functional impairment or insufficient infiltration may directly lead to a decline in immune surveillance capacity in high-risk tumors, thereby promoting tumor progression and immune evasion.45 In addition, the high-risk group of EGC showed significantly elevated Exclusion scores, alongside a markedly reduced MSI score. These phenomena collectively indicate that high-risk tumor cells may evade immune attack through multiple mechanisms. On one hand, the tumor microenvironment repels immune cells from entering; on the other hand, reduced neoantigen production makes tumor cells harder for the immune system to recognize.46 These changes enable high-risk tumor cells to more effectively evade immune surveillance, promoting tumor progression and deterioration, and leading to a poorer response to immunotherapy. Collectively, these findings suggest that patients in the high-risk group are less likely to benefit from immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy and may require alternative or combination treatment strategies to improve clinical outcomes. At the genomic level, the high-risk group is characterized by a predominance of TP53 mutations. TP53 mutations are associated with tumor aggressiveness, metastatic potential, and poor prognosis.47 Study has shown that tumor cells with TP53 mutations are more likely to breach the basement membrane, invade surrounding tissues and blood vessels, consequently leading to tumor metastasis.48 Mechanistically, TP53 mutations contribute to immune evasion by promoting genomic instability, which generates heterogeneous neoantigen landscapes that facilitate immune escape, and by modulating the expression of immune checkpoint molecules such as PD-L1.49 Additionally, mutant p53 proteins can confer chemoresistance through impaired apoptotic signaling, dysregulated DNA damage repair, and activation of survival pathways,50 explaining the higher IC50 values observed in high-risk patients. In silico drug sensitivity analysis revealed that high-risk patients had higher IC50 values for multiple chemotherapeutic agents (including Cisplatin, Oxaliplatin, and Vinorelbine), suggesting a potentially stronger resistance tendency. This observation aligns with the malignant biological characteristics of high-risk tumors, such as apoptosis evasion and abnormal activation of proliferative signaling pathways. Overall, patients in the low-risk group may benefit more from immunotherapy, while patients in the high-risk group exhibit a stronger tendency for resistance to conventional chemotherapy. This discovery highlights the necessity of formulating personalized treatment strategies based on risk classification in clinical practice.

Our study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, its retrospective design, based on the TCGA cohort, may introduce selection bias, and thus, prospective validation in multi-center cohorts is necessary before clinical application. Second, due to the limited availability of external GC cohorts that contain the specific lncRNAs signature, external validation in independent non-TCGA cohorts was not performed. Third, the number of normal tissue samples in the TCGA cohort was relatively small compared to tumor samples and the sample size for qRT-PCR validation was relatively small, which may limit the statistical power for identifying differentially expressed lncRNAs. Future studies with larger normal sample cohorts are warranted to validate our findings. Finally, although our bioinformatics analysis reveals strong associative evidence, it does not fully elucidate the direct regulatory mechanisms of m7G-related lncRNAs. Thus, further foundational experiments are necessary to investigate these underlying processes. Despite these limitations, this study is the first to develop and validate a robust prognostic signature based on m7G-related lncRNAs, which can provide a theoretical basis for personalized treatment and prognosis assessment in EGC.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study developed a prognostic model for EGC based on m7G-related lncRNAs, a previously unexplored angle, using TCGA data, which effectively predicted patient outcomes. Analyses of the tumor microenvironment, drug sensitivity, and somatic mutations revealed distinct immune infiltration patterns, potential therapeutic targets, and key genetic alterations. Collectively, our findings enhanced the understanding of m7G-related lncRNAs in EGC and provided a basis for further exploration of personalized treatment strategies and prognostic assessment. Nevertheless, several limitations should be considered, including the retrospective nature of the TCGA cohort and the lack of external validation, which warrant future prospective multi-center studies to confirm clinical utility.

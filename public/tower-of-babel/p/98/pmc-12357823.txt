A novel prognostic risk score associated with resistance to docetaxel chemotherapy for predicting biochemical recurrence-free survival in patients with prostate cancer
Authors: Desheng Zhu, Hongbin Zhu, Ting Huang, Wentao Fan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo identify molecular clusters and develop a novel prognostic risk score associated with resistance to docetaxel chemotherapy for predicting biochemical recurrence free survival (BCRFS) and tumor immune microenvironment of prostate cancer (PCa).Materials and methodsWe obtained the clinical and transcriptome data of PCa from TCGA and GEO database. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis were used to establish a novel prognostic risk score associated with resistance to docetaxel chemotherapy for predicting BCRFS. To evaluate the validity of the risk score, both internal and external validations were carried out. Preliminary in-vivo and in-vitro experimental validations were performed to verify the biological functions of NOG in PCa docetaxel resistance.ResultsAfter screening 67 docetaxel resistance related differentially expressed gene (DEGs), we identified three docetaxel resistance-related clusters with significantly different BCRFS, immune cell infiltration, and PD-L1 expression. More importantly, a novel docetaxel resistance-related prognostic risk score was constructed. In training, testing, and validating cohorts, the BCRFS of the patients with high-risk scores was considerably lower. The AUCs for the three cohorts were 0.767, 0.714, and 0.719, respectively. Subgroup analysis suggested that the risk score has a strong clinical value in the TIII-IV N0 or clusters 1–2 PCa patients. The expression of the NOG was significantly elevated in docetaxel-resistant PCa cells compared to the normal strain. The knockdown of the NOG reduced the IC50 of docetaxel of docetaxel-resistant PCa cells and the overexpression of NOG increased the IC50 of docetaxel and promoted the colony formation ability under docetaxel treatment of DU145 and PC-3 cells. In vivo tumorigenesis assay revealed that the overexpression of NOG could promote the tumorigenesis of PCa under docetaxel treatment.ConclusionsWe successfully identified a total of 67 docetaxel resistance related DEGs. A novel prognostic risk score associated with resistance to docetaxel chemotherapy of PCa was developed and validated, which performed well in predicting the BCRFS of PCa. High expression of NOG could promote the docetaxel resistance of PCa.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03423-0.

Introduction

In developed countries, prostate cancer (PCa) was the most frequent male cancer accounted for 14.1% new cancer cases worldwide [1, 2]. In patients with localized primary PCa, radical prostatectomy (RP) or radical radiotherapy (RT) remains the preferred treating method [2]. Unfortunately, it is reported that approximately 20% − 40% of PCa cases receiving RP and 30–50% of PCa cases receiving RT would develop biochemical recurrence (BCR) within ten years [3]. As is known to us, BCR indicates that there is an increased risk of local recurrence or distant metastasis [4, 5]. Besides, despite similar clinicopathologic characteristics, some cases might progress to opposite prognosis, for the sampling error and subjectivity in estimating prostate-specific antigen (PSA), Gleason score (GS), and TNM stage [6]. Hence, more informative prognostic markers are highly required to identify those with increased risk of BCR and decreased BCR-free survival (BCRFS).

As a cytotoxic drug targeting the cell cycle, docetaxel could facilitate the formation of stable microtubules and the inhibition of depolymerization, thereby damaging the reticular structure of microtubule [7]. Recently, docetaxel-based chemotherapy has been widely used in the treatment of various types of malignant tumor, for example, gastric cancer [8]breast cancer [9]and head, and neck cancer [10]. Docetaxel-based chemotherapy in PCa has undergone dramatic changes from palliation to improving overall survival (OS) [11]. Previously, most chemotherapy regimens were found to be largely palliative in PCa; however, recently, docetaxel-based chemotherapy has been considered as one of the vital treatment options of metastatic hormone sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) and metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) according to the latest guidelines [12]. Besides, docetaxel-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy has a significant impact on the prognosis of PCa with a hormone-sensitive state [13]. However, to our knowledge, some PCa patients would develop docetaxel resistance [14]. Although numerous studies have investigated the underlying mechanism of docetaxel resistance, the specific molecular mechanisms are still complex and not fully illuminated [15]. There was no study focusing on docetaxel chemotherapy resistance related molecular clusters and prognostic risk score of PCa patients.

In the current study, a total of three molecular subtypes related to resistance to docetaxel chemotherapy were identified for patients with PCa. In addition, we developed a novel docetaxel chemotherapy resistance related molecular risk score for predicting BCRFS of PCa. Internal verifications, external verifications, and subgroup survival analysis were then successfully performed. The correlations of the docetaxel resistance related molecular risk score with clinicopathologic features, checkpoint and m6A genes correlation, tumor immune microenvironment, cancer stemness, tumor mutation burden, and drug sensitivity were particularly explored. We also investigated the role of NOG, a vital docetaxel resistance-related genes (DRGs), in the docetaxel resistance of PCa by preliminary in-vivo and in-vitro experimental validations.

Materials and methods

Data collection and preprocessing

First of all, we obtained the mRNA expression profiles and clinical data of 499 PCa samples and 52 normal samples from the Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) [16]. The clinical features of PCa patients included age, T-stage, N-stage, biochemical recurrence (BCR) status, and BCR-free time. In TCGA database, there was a total of 405 PCa cases with unabridged data of transcriptome data, BCR status, and BCR-free time. All these data were processed using Perl language and R language. The Ensemble database (http://asia.ensembl.org/signature.html) was utilized to convert Ensemble IDs into gene symbols. The TCGA cohort (405 PCa cases) was randomly divided into the training cohort (204 PCa cases) and testing cohort (201 PCa cases) [17].

Additionally, datasets of GSE70770 were obtained from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). There was a total of 203 PCa cases with unabridged data of transcriptome, BCR status, and BCR-free time in the GSE70770 dataset. All PCa cases in GSE70770 dataset were used as validating cohort. The docetaxel resistance related genes (DRGs) were obtained from Patricia M. Schnepp’s study [18] by comparing the transcriptome data between docetaxel sensitive and resistant DU145 or PC-3 cells.

Bioinformatics analysis

We then utilized “limma” R package to filter DRGs between docetaxel sensitive and resistant DU145 or PC3 cells, respectively. The cut-off value was set as false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and log2 |fold change (FC)| >1 [19].

“ConsensusClusterPlus” R package was used for conducting consensus clustering analysis to identify PCa molecular clusters related to resistance to docetaxel chemotherapy. R packages “survival”, “survminer”, “ggplot2” and “pheatmap” were utilized to investigate the correlation of docetaxel resistance related molecular clusters with clinicopathologic characteristics (including BCRFS, age, T stage, N stage, PD-L1 expression level). The ESTIMATE algorithm was used to access tumor immune microenvironment scores [20] while the CIBERSORT method was utilized to calculate the score of 22 types of immune infiltrating cells [21]. The correlations of docetaxel resistance related molecular clusters with PCa tumor immune microenvironment and immune cells infiltration were especially investigated.

In addition, R package “survival” and “survminer” were used for performing Cox regression analysis in the training cohort to develop a novel docetaxel resistance related prognostic molecular risk score for predicting BCRFS of PCa. Most importantly, internal and external verification was performed in testing cohort and validating cohort, respectively. The risk distribution of TCGA cohort, training cohort, testing cohort, and validating cohort was presented using “pheatmap” R package. According to the median risk score derived from the docetaxel resistance related risk score, we divided PCa patients into high-risk and low-risk subgroups in training cohort, testing cohort, and validating cohort. Particularly, subgroup survival analysis was conducted in older and younger patients, stage T I-II and stage T III-IV patients, stage N0 and stage N1 patients, and different molecular clusters.

The scores of 13 immune function activity in TCGA cohort were calculated by single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) using R package “gsva”. Then, the correlations of docetaxel resistance related risk score with immune cells infiltration, immune functions, tumor mutation burden, m6A genes expression levels, clinical features, and checkpoint genes expression levels were especially investigated. Moreover, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed to investigate the underlying mechanisms related to this docetaxel resistance related risk score, as the previous described [22]. The TIMER 2.0 database (http://timer.cistrome.org/) was used for calculating the score of immune infiltrating cells via seven methods.

Cell culture

Human PCa cell lines, DU145 and PC-3, were purchased from Procell Life Science&Technology Co.,Ltd. The following culture media were used: DU145 cells were maintained in MEM medium and PC-3 cells were maintained in Hams F-12 K medium. Cells were cultured in their preferred medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C and 5% CO2.

RNA-Interference and overexpression

Small-interfering RNAs (siRNAs) and overexpressed plasmids constructed with pcDNA3.1(+) were purchased from Shanghai GenePharma Co., Ltd. DU145 cells and PC-3 cells were plated into 6-well plate separately. When cells were grown to approximately 80% confluence, transfections of siRNAs or overexpressed plasmids were performed using lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Transfected cells were harvested after 48 h of the transfection and the efficiency of knockdown or overexpression was verified. The two siRNAs with the highest knockdown efficiency were used for subsequent experiments.

siRNA#1: Sense (5’-3’) GAACACCCAGACCCUAUCUTT.

Antisense (5’-3’) GGCAGCUGCUUCAGUAAGCTT.

siRNA#2: Sense (5’-3’) AGAUAGGGUCUGGGUGUUCTT.

Antisense (5’-3’) GCUUACUGAAGCAGCUGCCTT.

RT-qPCR

We used TRIzol reagent to extract the total RNA and then used the TransScript® Green One-Step qRT-PCR SuperMix to generate cDNA. Next, we used Taq Pro Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix to detect the expression levels of RNA. The expression levels were normalized based on the expression of GAPDH.

GAPDH: forward 5-GGTGTGAACCATGAGAAGTATGA-3,

reverse 5-GAGTCCTTCCACGATACCAAAG-3.

NOG: forward 5-ATCTGAACGAGACGCTGCTG-3,

reverse 5-TTTGATCTCGCTCGGCATGG-3.

Western blot

We used RIPA buffer to lyse cell samples and then used BCA assay kit to quantify the proteins. Proteins were separated based on their own molecular weight by 10% SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. Specific primary antibodies and HRP-conjugated goat anti rabbit IgG were used for incubation. ECL reagent was used to develop the bands. The following is the information about antibodies used in immunoblot: anti-NOG (1:1000; proteintech; 30023-1-AP), anti-β-actin (1:2000; proteintech; 20536-1-AP).

Cell counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

IC50 values of transfected DU145/PC-3 cells or DU145R/PC-3R cells were determined using CCK-8 assays to evaluate resistance to docetaxel of cells. DU145/PC-3 cells or DU145R/PC-3R cells after transient transfection were plated at 1.0 × 104 cells per 100 µl in each well of 96-well plate. After 24 h of incubation, cells were incubated with docetaxel drugs for 24 h according to following concentration gradients: 0nM, 1nM, 2.5nM, 5nM, 10nM, 20nM, 40nM. 10% CCK-8 reagent and 90% complete medium and the plates were incubated at 37 °C. Finally, the SpectraMax M5 microplate reader (Molecular Devices, Sunnyvale, CA, USA) was used to read absorbances at 450 nm.

Construction of DU145R and PC-3R

PC-3R and DU145R cells were generated using an intermittent, stepwise doseescalation strategy [23, 24]. When the DU145 and PC-3 cells density reaches 85–95% (preferably 90%), 0.1 nM docetaxel is added for induction for 24 h. Next, drug-free medium was used for continued culture until the cells resume the normal growth, and then cells were cultured at the same concentration of docetaxel to confirm their adaptability. Gradually increase the concentration of docetaxel in a gradient: 0.2 nM, 0.5 nM, 0.75 nM, 1 nM, 1.5 nM, 2 nM, 5 nM, 7.5 nM, 10 nM, 15 nM. This process continued until the proliferation rate of PC-3 or DU145 cells cultured in docetaxel was comparable to that of untreated control cells. After 6 months of continuous exposure, DU145 and PC-3 cells developed resistance to docetaxel, and these cells were designated as PC-3R and DU145R. We measured the docetaxel IC50 of PC-3R and DU145R to confirm the ability of resistance to docetaxel.

Colony formation assays

Cells were plated into six-well plates (1 × 103 cells per well) and further incubated in docetaxel-containing medium for 2 weeks. Then, the cells were fixed with 4% formaldehyde and stained with 0.5% crystal violet. The number of colonies was then counted under a microscope.

In vivo tumorigenesis assay

We purchased four weeks-old male BALB/c nude mice from Shanghai SLAC Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China). A subcutaneous model was established by subcutaneously implanting stable DU145 cells (5 × 106 cells/mouse) into the flanks of nude mice (n = 5 mice per group). Docetaxel was injected into subcutaneous tumors (5 µg/g) every 3 days from day 9 onwards. The tumors were observed and measured every three days. TumoCentral Hospital.

Statistical methods

Statistical analysis was conducted using R x64 4.1.0 software and SPSS software version 19.0 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analysis were performed to establish a docetaxel resistance related prognostic risk score for predicting BCRFS of PCa. Survival analysis, time-dependent the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, and independent prognostic analysis were performed to validate the performance of this docetaxel resistance related risk score. The student’s t-test, chi-squared test, Mann-Whitney U test, Kruskal-Wallis test, and one-way ANOVA test were performed for comparison. Survival curves were prepared using the Kaplan-Meier method (log-rank t-test was used for the comparison). The independent prognostic factors were analyzed by Cox’s proportional hazard regression model. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Identification of three molecular clusters related to docetaxel resistance

As shown in Fig. 1A, we performed consensus clustering analysis so that a total of three molecular clusters related to docetaxel resistance were identified, including 137 cases in cluster 1, 149 cases in cluster 2, and 119 cases in cluster 3. Moreover, the difference in BCRFS among three molecular clusters, as shown in Fig. 1B, was significant. The more surprising aspect of the data was that the expression of PD-L1 was increasingly higher in cluster 1, cluster 2, and cluster 3, as shown in Fig. 1C. Furthermore, as shown in Fig. 1D, the stromal score, immune score, and ESTIMATE score varied significantly among three molecular clusters. Further analysis showed that CD4 memory resting T cells, M1 macrophages, M2 macrophages, and activated dendritic cells were the differential infiltrating immune cells among these three molecular clusters in Fig. 1E.

Subgroup survival analysis

Further subgroup survival analysis as illustrated in Fig. 4A-F revealed that compared with low-risk patients, higher-risk patients with N0 stage (P < 0.001) or T III-IV stage (P < 0.001) or age ≤ 65 (P < 0.001) or age > 65 (P = 0.003) had notably worse BCRFS. However, this docetaxel resistance related risk score did not work well in predicting BCRFS of T I-II stage or N1 stage PCa. We obtained from Fig. 4G-L that, in contrast to low-risk cases, high-risk cases had remarkable worse BCRFS in cluster 1 (P = 0.002), cluster 2 (P = 0.003), and cluster 3 (P = 0.002), respectively, after calculating the risk score for each case based on the formula mentioned above. The AUC for predicting BCRFS was 0.722 in cluster 1 and 0.750 in cluster 2 while in cluster 3 the AUC for predicting BCRFS was 0.583, which suggested that this novel risk score was particularly more suitable for PCa patients in both cluster 1 and cluster 2 than those in cluster 3. Taken together, these results further suggested that this novel docetaxel resistance related risk score was especially more suitable for PCa patients with N0 stage or T III-IV stage or age ≤ 65 or age > 65 or cluster 1–2.

Clinicopathologic features, cancer stemness, m6A genes correlation, and functional enrichment

The results of the clinicopathologic features analysis of two subgroups were presented in Fig. 5. As the heatmap showed, N-stage, T-stage, immune score, and docetaxel resistance related clusters were significantly different between high-risk and low-risk PCa patients. Besides, the proportion of cluster1, N1 stage and T III-IV stage in high-risk score group were higher than that in low-risk score group whereas the proportion of cluster3 was lower. When it came to the m6A complex subunit, as could be seen from Fig. 5B, the expression levels of ZC3H13, FTO, METTL14, YTHDC1, WTAP, and YTHDC2 were significantly up-regulated in patients with low-risk score while the expression levels of HNRNPC were remarkably down-regulated in low-risk patients. More intriguingly, there was a remarkably positive correlation between RNAss tumor stemness score and the docetaxel resistance related risk score in Fig. 5C. Then, we conducted the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment for two risk subgroups using GSEA. The results were presented in Fig. 5D. In those with high risk, the top five KEGG enriched pathways included oxidative phosphyltion, proteasome, pyrimidine metabolism, RNA polymerase, and spliceosome. In those with low risk, the top two KEGG enriched pathways included axon guidance, JAK-STAT signaling pathway.

Immune checkpoint genes, tumor mutation burden, immune cells infiltration, and immune functions

Then, we analyzed the expression of immune checkpoint genes in high- and low-risk groups, and found that, as displayed in Fig. 6A, the expression levels of 5 types of immune checkpoint genes were higher in high-risk groups while the expression levels of 18 types of immune checkpoint genes were higher in low-risk groups. If we turned to quantification of the activities of immune functions using ssGSEA, as displayed in Fig. 6B, we also found that patients with low-risk score had a higher functional activity of APC co-inhibition, APC co-stimulation, CCR, MHC class I, para-inflammation, T cell co-inhibition, and Type II IFN response than those with high-risk. Additionally, we displayed the differentially infiltrated immune cells between high and low-risk PCa patients as indicated by most evaluating methods (Fig. 6C). Figure 6D provided the intercorrelations between immune infiltrating cells of 22 types and eight risk genes. Next, as shown in Fig. 6E-F, we explored the correlations of tumor mutation burden (TMB) with different risk level groups. Interestingly, TMB was observed to be significantly higher in the high-risk group. The more surprising correlation was that there was statistical significance of BCRFS among high-risk patients with low TMB, low-risk patients with low TMB, low-risk patients with high TMB, and high-risk patients with high TMB.

Development of a nomogram for prognosis prediction

Furthermore, we established a nomogram containing N-stage, age, T-stage, and risk subgroup to predict prognosis in patients with PCa in Fig. 7A. In addition, attention should be paid to the AUC for the prediction of the probability of one-year BCRFS, three-year BCRFS and five-year BCRFS which were 0.742, 0.764, and 0.780 respectively shown in Fig. 7B. The calibration curves of the nomogram shown in Fig. 7C illuminated that the predicted survival probability of one-year BCRFS, three-year BCRFS and five-year BCRFS was in high conformity to the observed survival probability. The results of the AUC demonstrated that this nomogram worked well in predicting BCRFS of PCa.

NOG promotes the resistance to docetaxel of prostate cancer cells

CCK-8 cytotoxicity assay showed that we successfully constructed of docetaxel-resistant PCa cells (DU145R and PC-3R) and the IC50 values of DU145R (IC50 = 26.64) and PC-3R (IC50 = 18.58) was significantly increased compared with parental cells (IC50-PC-3 cells = 6.121; IC50-DU145 cells = 10.29) (Fig. 8A). The RT-qPCR and western blot showed that NOG was highly expressed in docetaxel-resistant PCa cells compared with parental cells (Fig. 8B-C). RT-qPCR and western blot indicating the efficiency of USP8 knockdown in DU145R/PC-3R cells or USP8 overexpression in DU145/PC-3 cells (Fig. 8D-E). Notably, when NOG expression was knockdown in DU145R and PC-3R cells, there was a significant decrease for IC50 values of DU145R (IC50-shNC = 27.60; IC50-shNOG#1 = 19.93; IC50-shNOG#2 = 21.75) and PC-3R cells (IC50-shNC = 19.65; IC50-shNOG#1 = 15.74; IC50-shNOG#2 = 15.66 (Fig. 8F). when NOG expression was overexpressed in DU145 and PC-3 cells, there was a significant increase for the IC50 values of DU145 (IC50-Control = 10.24; IC50-NOG = 13.83) and PC-3 cells (IC50-Control = 6.488; IC50-NOG = 14.56) (Fig. 8G). The colony formation assays showed that overexpression of NOG could promote the colony formation ability of DU145 and PC-3 cells under docetaxel treatment (Fig. 8H). In vivo tumorigenesis assay revealed that overexpression of NOG could promote the tumorigenesis of PCa under docetaxel treatment (Fig. 8I).

Discussion

Although RP and RT are established as the preferable curative options for localized PCa, near to 50% of patients would develop a biochemical recurrence (BCR) within ten years [2, 3]. Noteworthily, docetaxel-based neoadjuvant chemotherapy significantly impacted on the prognosis of PCa with hormone-sensitive state [13]and additionally it has been listed as one of the vital treatment options of mHSPC and mCRPC in the latest guideline [12]. However, docetaxel resistance happening in some patients is one of the most important limitation factors of the clinical application. Recently, bioinformatics analysis based on high-throughput sequencing occurs as an important method to explore the potential molecular mechanisms and develop prognostic models for different types of cancers [25–27]. However, very little has been found on the question of the relationship between docetaxel resistance transcriptomics and BCR of PCa so far, as well as developing a prognostic model from the perspective of docetaxel resistance in PCa patients. In current study, we screened a total of 67 DEGs related to docetaxel resistance in PCa patients and identified three molecular clusters related to docetaxel resistance in total. Survival analysis showed that PCa patients of cluster 1 have significantly lower BCRFS, which indicating that PCa patients of cluster 1 have higher risk of BCR. It has been reported that BCR indicates that there is an increased risk of local recurrence or distant metastasis [4, 5]. Hence, PCa patients of cluster 1 have an increased risk of local recurrence or distant metastasis and poor prognosis. Surprisingly, all these molecular clusters exhibited different expression levels of PD-L1, and tumor immune microenvironment characteristics. What is more, we succeeded in developing a novel risk score relative to docetaxel resistance for predicting BCRFS of PCa by applying 8 DEGs related to docetaxel resistance totally (including COL17A1, ANKRD45, MBNL2, ACOX2, BEX1, DDIT4, IL31RA, NOG). Both internal and external validations were then completely executed. We found that PCa patients with high-risk score were associated with remarkably worse BCRFS in contrast to those with low-risk score in training, testing, whole TCGA, and validating cohort. The AUC was.767, 0.714, 0.742, and 0.719 in the four cohorts individually. Consequently, this novel docetaxel resistance related risk score could behave well in predicting BCRFS of PCa.

The novel docetaxel resistance related risk score was composed of eight DEGs: collagen type XVII alpha 1 Chain (COL17A1), ankyrin repeat domain 45 (ANKRD45), muscleblind like splicing regulator 2 (MBNL2), Acyl-CoA oxidase 2 (ACOX2), brain expressed X-linked 1(BEX1), DNA damage inducible transcript 4 (DDIT4), interleukin 31 receptor A (IL31RA), and noggin (NOG). It has been commonly verified that all these eight DEGs take part in the development of a variety of cancers. Lothong, et al. [28] reported that the over-expression of the COL17A1 gene was connected with low-proliferation tumors, extended tumor-free period, and overall survival of breast cancer patients. Besides, Thangavelu, et al. [29] suggested that COL17A1 promoter methylation status could be applied to predicting patient outcomes in breast cancer and cervical cancer. Gao, et al. [30] demonstrated that low expression of MBNL2 was associated with poor RFS in PCa patients while Lee, et al. [31] reported that overexpression of MBNL2 was associated with a favorable prognosis for HCC patients. Perron, et al. [32] proved that MBNL2, an oncogenic gene, was related to cell proliferation in renal cancer cells. A previous study reported that ACOX2 may be a novel therapeutic biomarker in ER + breast tumors [33] and low expression decreased the overall survival of liver cancer patients [34]. It has been proven that elevated BEX1 expression was correlated to a poor prognosis in both hepatocellular carcinoma and hepatoblastoma patients [35]. Ding, et al. [36] have demonstrated that up-regulated BEX1 could successfully restore imatinib sensitivity in the resistant cells of chronic myeloid leukemia. Additionally, it has been reported that down-regulated BEX1 contributed to the radio-resistance in LINC00630-proficient colorectal cancer cells [37]. Barakat, et al. [38] suggested that induced DDIT4 was linked with increased sensitivity to bortezomib in prostate cancer cells and Sinha, et al. [39] hindered prostate cancer cell growth. IL- 31RA perhaps contributed to increasing survival of patients with breast cancer via modulation of antitumor cytokines and other immune components which supported anti-tumor activity [40]. Moreover, IL31RA proven to be associated with unfavorable outcomes in glioblastoma patients may be a potential therapeutic target [41]. In a previous study, it has been indicated that the combination of BMP-6, NOG, and sclerostin could be an effective prognostic marker for prostate cancer to predict the development of distant metastasis [42]. David, et al. [43]found that NOG disorders were related to the survival risk of nasopharyngeal carcinoma whereas Yang, et al. [44] showed that low-expressed NOG had a good prognosis in colorectal cancer. Of note, the studies concentrating on a relationship between COL17A1, ANKRD45, MBNL2, ACOX2, BEX1, DDIT4, IL31RA, NOG, and PCa docetaxel resistance was not enough. In current study, we found that NOG could promote the docetaxel resistance of PCa by using preliminary in-vivo and in-vitro experimental validations.

PCa has been considered as an tumor with inadequate immune-related treatment response [45]. Generally speaking, the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, established by synthesis of immunosuppressive mediators, defective expression of surface neoantigens, immune evasion through immunosuppressive cells, etc., results in the difficulty for most immune therapeutics for PCa patients [46]. However, recently studies found that immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment has heterogeneity in different PCa patients [47, 48]. However, further research should be undertaken to investigate which particular groups of PCa would respond to immunotherapy. In this study, after identifying three docetaxel resistance related molecular clusters of PCa in total successfully, we found that significant differences had been found in PCa immune environment, including expression level of PD-L1 and tumor immune environment score. Furthermore, the docetaxel resistance related risk score developed in this study was also utilized to divide PCa patients into two subgroups (including high-risk and low-risk groups). Between two groups, tumor immune microenvironment, cancer stemness, tumor mutation burden, and immune checkpoint gene expression levels are of remarkable difference. In summary, these results would contribute to screening populations of PCa that might benefit from immunotherapy.

However, importance should be attached to the limitations of current study. To begin with, this is a retrospective study using the bioinformatics method. The real-world data are expected for the verification of the generalizability of this novel docetaxel resistance risk score. Moreover, although preliminary experimental verifications have been undertaken to illuminate the potential biological functions of NOG, further in vitro and in vivo experiments are required to explore the potential mechanism concerning these DRGs and docetaxel resistance of PCa. Finally, we have evaluated the tumor immune environment simply via bioinformatic analysis. Hence, more experimental validations are needed to gain more convincing results.

Conclusion

In summary, we successfully identified a total of 67 docetaxel resistance related DEGs for PCa. Most importantly, a novel prognostic molecular risk score associated with resistance to docetaxel of PCa was developed and validated, which performed well in predicting the BCRFS of PCa. Preliminary in-vivo and in-vitro experiments showed that NOG could promote the docetaxel resistance of PCa. These findings would contribute to the potential mechanism of resistance to docetaxel chemotherapy in PCa patients.

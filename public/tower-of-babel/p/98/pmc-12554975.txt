Applications of modified plant proteins in dysphagia-friendly foods
Authors: Wenyan Wang, Xiaoyan Qin, Zhilong Chen, Yuqian Zheng, Xi Wang, Jun Zhao, Sheng Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
With the rapid progression of global population aging and the growing demand for nutrition tailored to individuals with dysphagia, modified plant proteins have emerged as promising candidates for the formulation of easy-to-swallow foods. As an essential macronutrient in the human diet, protein has garnered considerable research interest in the context of texture-modified diets. Plant proteins, offer advantages such as wide availability, cost-effectiveness, health benefits, and environmental sustainability. However, their inherent limitations—including low water solubility, complex tertiary and quaternary structures, and high sensitivity to environmental conditions—significantly constrain their functional performance in dysphagia-targeted food systems. These challenges hinder their ability to meet the stringent textural and processing requirements of easy-to-swallow formulations. Nonetheless, various modification strategies—encompassing physical, chemical, and enzymatic techniques—have been demonstrated to effectively enhance the solubility, gelation behavior, emulsifying capacity, and digestibility of plant proteins, thereby improving their suitability for application in dysphagia-friendly food products.

Introduction

With the global population rapidly aging, a progressive decline in physiological function among the elderly has become increasingly evident. One particularly prevalent issue is dysphagia, a swallowing disorder that not only restricts food choices but also significantly increases the risk of aspiration. This can lead to serious complications such as aspiration pneumonia, malnutrition, and dehydration—conditions that are known to accelerate organ function deterioration and markedly reduce quality of life in older adults(Gallego et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2025). Therefore, the development of food products that are safe and easy to swallow is of paramount importance in geriatric nutrition and care.

The primary method for enhancing the texture of dysphagia foods involves the incorporation of biopolymers (Hadde et al., 2021), notably hydrocolloids like Xanthan gum and Carrageenan, along with starches. These ingredients are added to thicken and gel foods while ensuring they meet the standards set by the International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative (IDDSI) (Liu, Chen, et al., 2023). While these additions effectively modulate the rheological properties of foods, making them easier to consume and reducing aspiration risks (Hadde et al., 2021), there are limitations associated with current biopolymer-based formulas. Notably, these thickeners often lack essential nutrients. Consistent use may result in inadequate intake of crucial macronutrients, such as proteins, which poses challenges in fulfilling the high protein requirements of the elderly.

Protein is a fundamental component of food and plays a pivotal role in determining food structure. A prevalent issue is the inadequate protein intake observed in the dietary patterns of the elderly (Gallego et al., 2022). Notably, the incidence of sarcopenia in individuals aged over 65 is considerable, and this is strongly correlated with a decrease in muscle synthesis capability and a reduction in protein absorption efficiency (Gallego et al., 2022). Plant proteins are derived from a diverse range of sources and are naturally free of cholesterol, low in saturated fat, and often rich in dietary fiber, vitamins, and minerals (Sá et al., 2020). Their potential benefits include promoting cardiovascular and gut health and supporting immune function. In addition, plant proteins are well-suited for vegetarians and individuals with lactose intolerance due to their digestibility and hypoallergenic potential. Major plant protein sources include legumes (e.g., soybeans, peas, chickpeas, lentils, lupins), cereals (e.g., wheat, maize, rice, barley, millet), oilseeds (e.g., flaxseed, chia, sesame, sunflower), nuts (e.g., almonds, peanuts, cashews), and pseudo-cereals (e.g., quinoa, buckwheat) (Thakur et al., 2024). Plant proteins have become an ideal base material due to their health attributes and environmental sustainability advantages (Sá et al., 2020). Although natural plant proteins often exhibit functional limitations, restricting their use in complex food systems, recent advancements in modification techniques have significantly enhanced their functional properties(Ravindran et al., 2024). For instance, ultrasonic treatment has been shown to boost the solubility and dispersibility of plant proteins, thereby improving the stability and uniformity of food systems (Nazari et al., 2018; Vera et al., 2019). Additionally, such modifications can amplify the emulsifying activity (J. Ma et al., 2022) and gel strength (H. Cao, Ni, et al., 2025; S. Zhang et al., 2025) of proteins, which are vital for achieving desired textures in processed foods. Some research also suggests that specific modification techniques can elevate the digestibility (Ren et al., 2025) and bioavailability of plant proteins, thus augmenting their nutritional value (Choe et al., 2022). These enhanced properties position modified plant proteins as promising “intrinsic biopolymers.” Notably, these modified proteins exhibit characteristics suitable for creating the texture structure essential for dysphagia foods—specifically, soft, adhesive, and easily chewable gel or paste systems (G. Xu et al., 2023). Recent studies underscore the unique benefits of modified plant proteins in formulating dysphagia foods. Among these, pea protein stands out due to its low allergenicity and consistent supply, making it a preferred ingredient for dysphagia foods (Liu, Chen, et al., 2025; Streimikyte et al., 2020; B. Xu et al., 2025; G. Xu et al., 2023). Soy protein (Qin et al., 2025; R. Qiu et al., 2025), flaxseed protein (Min et al., 2024), mung bean protein (L. Qiu, Zhang, et al., 2024), and peanut protein (Aghababaei et al., 2025) are also frequently employed in these formulations. Consequently, with their distinct functional advantages, modified plant proteins offer considerable potential and unique value in crafting nutritional foods tailored for specific swallowing safety needs.

The application of plant protein modification technologies is extensive in the food industry. By enhancing functional attributes such as rheological behavior (e.g., viscosity, gelation), interfacial activity (e.g., emulsification, foaming), and system stability (e.g., water- and oil-holding capacities), these techniques contribute to improved texture, sensory appeal, and processability (Ravindran et al., 2024). Moreover, modification approaches enable the development of novel food products, facilitate raw material utilization, and reduce production costs. In the context of dysphagia-friendly food development, protein modification offers a robust strategy to tailor textural properties and mouthfeel, thereby improving food accessibility and nutritional intake for individuals with swallowing difficulties (G. Xu et al., 2023). Regulating the structure and properties of plant proteins through physical, chemical, or biological methods plays a key role in improving the texture characteristics of dysphagia foods (Ravindran et al., 2024). Chemical modifications such as acylation and phosphorylation modify the functional groups of proteins, changing their charge distribution and hydrophilicity. These changes bolster the gelling and water-holding capacities of proteins (Ravindran et al., 2024), enhance the thickness and smoothness of food boluses, and facilitate safe swallowing. Physical modification utilizes electromagnetic waves or mechanical methods, such as ultrasonic treatment and high-pressure homogenization (Jian Cedric Sow & Du, 2024), to depolymerize large aggregates, regulate protein conformation, and foster the development of a uniform network. This enhances system solubility and smoothness, eradicates particle sensation, significantly improves system stability and texture consistency, and precludes fragmentation or separation during swallowing (Vera et al., 2019). Enzymatic modifications encompass hydrolysis and cross-linking processes, during which enzymes selectively cleave or establish cross-links in peptide bonds to systematically modulate rheological characteristics. Protease hydrolysis augments the solubility and emulsifying capacity of the product, contributes a delicate taste, decreases adhesiveness, and enhances system smoothness (Mulla & Ahmed, 2019). Concurrently, transglutaminase-mediated cross-linking promotes protein polymerization, enhancing gel strength and elasticity (Eslami et al., 2025). These combined modifications increase the cohesiveness of the food bolus, diminishing the likelihood of fragmentation during swallowing and facilitating the formation of a uniform bolus that is easy to consume (Wouters et al., 2016). This targeted texture modification presents an effective strategy for designing foods suitable for individuals with dysphagia.

Discussions regarding the management of dysphagia, including the rheology of thickeners (Hadde et al., 2021), and the development of technologies for elderly food, such as softening, encapsulation, and 3D printing (Guo et al., 2025), have been prevalent. However, these discussions primarily center on specific polysaccharides or a broad array of technologies, without systematically exploring plant proteins. This article provides a comprehensive examination of the application of modified plant proteins in easy-to-swallow foods, establishing a clear link between these enhanced functional properties and the key attributes of such foods (G. Xu et al., 2023). It clarifies the enhanced key functional characteristics obtained by plant proteins after modification, applied to easy-to-swallow foods (G. Xu et al., 2023). Additionally, it elucidates the advantages of modified plant proteins in addressing protein malnutrition in the elderly and meeting their specific health needs (Sá et al., 2020), hoping to contribute to the development of plant protein-based health foods for the elderly.

The effect of plant protein modification on key functional characteristics of foods friendly to dysphagia

Proteins are complex macromolecules whose biological functions are inherently determined by their structural configurations. Owing to this structure-function relationship, proteins exhibit a diverse array of functional properties essential for food formulation and processing. However, native plant proteins often exhibit limitations in functionality—such as poor solubility, low digestibility, and limited emulsifying or gelling capacity—that restrict their direct application in specific food systems. To overcome these constraints and expand their utility, various structural modification strategies have been developed to tailor protein functionality in alignment with food processing requirements. Plant protein modification methods are broadly categorized into three primary approaches: physical, chemical, and enzymatic modifications. These techniques can significantly enhance functional properties, including solubility, emulsification, gelation, thermal stability, and digestibility. Modification strategies not only alter the molecular structure of plant proteins but also significantly enhance their physicochemical and functional properties. These improvements are primarily achieved through the disruption of higher-order protein structures, which facilitates the exposure of reactive groups and enhances molecular flexibility and interaction potential (Van de Vondel et al., 2021). For example, structural loosening enables increased solubility and interfacial adsorption capacity, which are crucial for emulsification and foaming applications. Chemical modifications, such as phosphorylation or acylation, can introduce hydrophilic moieties or promote the formation of covalently crosslinked networks, thereby improving emulsion stability, thermal resistance, and gel strength (Teng et al., 2021). Enzymatic modifications offer the advantage of site-specific cleavage or targeted cross-linking. For instance, transglutaminase-catalyzed cross-linking leads to the formation of dense, protein-rich three-dimensional networks, which enhance gelation and water retention properties (D. Chen & Campanella, 2022). Moreover, changes in hydrophobic group exposure, disulfide bond rearrangement, and surface charge redistribution during modification directly influence key techno-functional attributes such as foaming capacity, water-holding capacity, and interfacial activity. Physical modification

Physical modification alters protein structure through mechanical or thermal means. Physical treatments can induce molecular depolymerization, peptide chain unfolding, and particle size reduction, often resulting in irreversible conformational changes. Physical modification enhances textural properties directly associated with dysphagia management. This treatment method disrupts protein higher-order structures through thermal energy, mechanical force, or electromagnetic waves, breaking down protein aggregates, reducing their average particle size, and enhancing solubility, thereby achieving targeted textural regulation(G. Xu et al., 2023). Following physical modification, protein solutions exhibit shear-thinning behavior. This means viscosity decreases under shear stress during oral swallowing, facilitating easier ingestion, while maintaining high viscosity under static conditions to help prevent aspiration (Streimikyte et al., 2020). Additionally, moderate physical processing induces unfolding and recrosslinking of protein molecules, forming continuous gel networks with suitable elasticity and cohesiveness. This structure confers a smooth texture and high stability to the product, enabling the preparation of gel-like foods that meet swallowing safety requirements while exhibiting excellent formability (G. Xu et al., 2023). The optimization of these textural and rheological properties is crucial for satisfying the specific texture and rheological behavior requirements stipulated by the International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative (IDDSI). Common physical modification techniques include conventional heat treatment, microwave heating, ultra-high pressure (UHP) processing, ultrasound treatment(UT), pulsed electric field (PEF) technology, cold plasma, and magnetic field applications. Heat treatment

Effects of heat treatment on the structure of plant proteins

Heat treatment is one of the most widely employed and industrially viable techniques for enhancing the functional attributes of proteins. Both dry and moist heat approaches are utilized, including steaming, baking, and high-temperature extrusion (Choe et al., 2022). The mechanism of heat treatment involves the disruption of non-covalent interactions (e.g., hydrophobic interactions, hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds) and, in some cases, disulfide linkages, resulting in protein denaturation (Mir et al., 2020). Moderate thermal exposure can partially unfold protein structures, loosening tightly coiled peptide chains. However, excessive heating may lead to undesirable aggregation or irreversible denaturation, which negatively impacts functionality (Choe et al., 2022; Mir et al., 2020). Such irreversible changes are typically characterized by a reduction in α-helical content, an increase in β-sheet formation, alterations in β-turns and random coils, and increased exposure of hydrophobic regions within the tertiary structure (Mir et al., 2021). Thermal denaturation also exposes internal hydrophobic and sulfhydryl groups, potentially altering disulfide bond patterns and increasing surface hydrophobicity.

Effects of thermal treatment on the key functional properties of plant protein dysphagia-friendly foods

Key process parameters: The effect of heat treatment modifications is heavily contingent upon the synergistic regulation of crucial process parameters, including temperature, time, and protein concentration. Collectively, these parameters dictate the nature and degree of alterations in the functional characteristics of plant proteins (Mir et al., 2020). The extent of structural alteration is highly temperature-dependent and increases sharply when the temperature exceeds the critical denaturation threshold (Mir et al., 2021). Therefore, precise control of thermal processing parameters—such as heating rate, maximum temperature, duration, and pH buffering—is essential for optimizing functional outcomes (J. Ma et al., 2022). The concentration of protein directly influences the density and robustness of the gel network. Generally, increased concentrations result in more robust and continuous gel networks. When properly managed, heat treatment can enhance the techno-functional performance of plant proteins, thereby improving the quality attributes of processed food products (Mir et al., 2021). Through statistical optimization methods such as response surface analysis, a mathematical model of the relationship between process parameters and functional characteristics can be established. This model can subsequently be used to predict optimal processing conditions, thereby catering to the developmental requirements of various types of dysphagia food (Liu, Feng, et al., 2025).

Key functional characteristics: The improvement of different functional characteristics jointly determines the rheological properties, texture, and stability of the final product (Mir et al., 2020), which is directly related to the design and performance of dysphagia-friendly food. Moderate thermal processing can improve gelation capacity, thermal stability, water-holding ability, foaming performance, solubility, emulsification, and digestibility (Choe et al., 2022). Specifically, controlled thermal treatment at 80 °C for 30 min enabled pseudocereal proteins to form structured three-dimensional gels, improving both texture and mouthfeel, which is especially beneficial for the development of easy-to-swallow foods targeting elderly populations (Mir et al., 2020). In another example, a heat-induced whey protein–chia seed gum composite gel has been proposed as a suitable food matrix for dysphagia patients (L. Li et al., 2025a). Enhanced gelation and water-holding capacity contribute to the creation of fine, moist food masses with appropriate cohesion, effectively mitigating dehydration shrinkage or granularity. This is crucial for achieving the adhesion, cohesion, and oral lubrication necessary for dysphagic foods (L. Li et al., 2025a). However, excessively high temperatures or prolonged periods can induce excessive protein aggregation and decreased solubility, potentially posing a swallowing hazard. Heat treatment has also been shown to enhance the thermal stability of soy protein isolates by modulating their particle size and aggregation behavior (W. Ma et al., 2020). This enhanced thermal stability, along with shear-thinning behavior, ensures a uniform product texture during thermal processing and consumption, thereby reducing the risk of aspiration. On a molecular level, heat processing facilitates the exposure of hydrophobic groups, thereby reducing interfacial tension at oil–water boundaries and enhancing emulsifying and foaming properties (Peng et al., 2016; Van de Vondel et al., 2021). The enhancement in emulsifying properties can be leveraged to develop uniformly textured emulsion products, thereby preventing fat precipitation and swallowing risks. For example, the emulsifying capacities of soy and pea protein isolates were significantly improved when treated at 95 °C for 15–30 min, respectively (Peng et al., 2016; Shao & Tang, 2014). Similar enhancements were observed in coconut protein following heat application (J. Ma et al., 2022). Heat-induced conformational changes also contribute to solubility improvement by promoting the unfolding of the protein structure and exposing hydrophilic residues (Van de Vondel et al., 2021). However, excessive heating may lead to irreversible aggregation, formation of insoluble complexes, and a decline in solubility (Mir et al., 2020). This results in a coarse texture, which could potentially pose a swallowing risk. By carefully adjusting heat treatment conditions, the solubility of the protein can be optimized, thereby affecting the viscosity profile and smoothness of the final product. These functional changes lead to significant improvements in the sensory and texture qualities of the product, facilitating the rational design and standardized production of dysphagia foods (Liu, Feng, et al., 2025).

Effects of thermal treatment on nutritional and processing characteristics of plant protein

The effect of heat treatment on the nutritional characteristics of plant proteins has a dual effect. On one side, it enhances protein bioavailability by breaking down complex structures, boosting in vitro digestibility, and deactivating anti-nutritional agents like trypsin inhibitors, thereby addressing the nutritional requirements of the elderly (Choe et al., 2022). Conversely, prolonged heat exposure can initiate the Maillard reaction between certain amino acids and reducing sugars, leading to a decline in protein nutritional value and potential degradation of heat-sensitive vitamins (Choe et al., 2022; Mir et al., 2020).

In processing characteristics, the advantages of thermal treatment in sterilization, inactivation of anti-nutritional factors, and formation of strong gels are very prominent. This makes it a critical component of current plant protein processing methodologies (Choe et al., 2022). It significantly enhances the gelation, emulsification, and water retention properties of plant proteins, which are fundamental to the formation of characteristic textures in many traditional plant protein products (such as tofu). Additionally, thermal treatment enhances the solubility of plant proteins, which is crucial for the preparation of dairy alternatives and protein beverages. Nevertheless, over-heat treatment can generate undesirable browning and a cooked flavor, potentially impacting the sensory quality of the final product (Liang et al., 2025). Therefore, precise regulation of thermal processing parameters is crucial to effectively balance nutrient retention, functional promotion, and hazard suppression.

Advantages and limitations of thermal treatment for plant protein modification

Thermal treatment does not require sophisticated equipment and is easily scalable, making it a practical choice in large-scale food manufacturing. Its compatibility with various processes makes it an integral part of the food production chain. This method effectively inactivates trypsin inhibitor anti-nutritional factors in plant proteins, removing their interference with human digestion and absorption (Mir et al., 2020). Additionally, it reliably eliminates pathogenic microorganisms, thereby ensuring the microbial safety of the product. Thermal treatment can effectively induce the gelation of plant proteins, enhancing water retention, gel strength, and texture. This meets the requirements of dysphagia foods for thickness and cohesiveness, and is used in special diets for dysphagia (L. Li et al., 2025a). However, there are inherent limitations to this technology. Excessive heat can result in decreased protein solubility, nutrient loss, and potential alterations to flavor and color. Traditional thermal treatments also involve high energy consumption, and there is a risk of uneven heating, affecting the consistency of the product's texture (Choe et al., 2022).

Microwave heating

Effects of microwave treatment on the structure of plant proteins

The heating mechanism is based on the interaction between alternating electromagnetic fields and polar molecules, which causes rapid dipole rotation and molecular friction. The resulting dielectric loss transforms electromagnetic energy into thermal energy, leading to rapid volumetric heating (Heydari et al., 2022).

Microwave treatment primarily disrupts non-covalent interactions within plant proteins, such as hydrogen bonds, disulfide bridges, and hydrophobic interactions. It can break intra- and intermolecular hydrogen bonding networks, thereby promoting rearrangements in the protein's secondary structure. This structural modification often leads to the exposure of hydrophobic domains, which contribute to enhanced functional properties. Studies have demonstrated that microwave treatment results in a decrease in α-helical content and an increase in β-sheet structures in proteins such as quinoa and oat proteins (Cao, Ni, et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2021). Microwave treatment also impacts the tertiary structure of plant proteins. Moderate levels of irradiation may induce partial disruption and reformation of disulfide bonds (Liu, Wang, et al., 2025), unfolding the molecular architecture and enhancing surface hydrophobicity due to the exposure of encapsulated hydrophobic residues (Cao, Ni, et al., 2025).

Effects of microwave treatment on the key functional properties of plant protein dysphagia-friendly foods

Key process parameters: The impact of microwave modification is substantially influenced by factors such as microwave power, duration of treatment, protein concentration, and the composition of the system. The total energy input is determined by the combination of power and treatment time. A moderate energy input can trigger partial unfolding of protein molecules, which in turn exposes internal hydrophobic groups. For instance, subjecting jackbean protein to an 800 W microwave treatment for 120 s significantly enhanced its solubility to 90.89 % (Ajayi et al., 2024). Nevertheless, excessive power or extended treatment durations can cause local overheating of the protein, leading to reduced functional properties and the development of off-flavors. To prevent overheating and ensure even energy distribution, intermittent heating or pulsed microwave modes are often employed. In practical applications, the treatment time and power density are closely linked to induce adequate unfolding and beneficial aggregation of plant proteins (Cao, Ni, et al., 2025). Additionally, the dielectric properties and viscosity are influenced by the protein concentration. The ionic strength and pH value within the system can also significantly affect the protein's modification effect, necessitating a comprehensive consideration. Through statistical experimental design, such as response surface methodology, the optimal power-time combination for the desired functional characteristics (e.g., gel strength, water retention, emulsibility) of dysphagic individuals is sought(Liu, Wang, et al., 2025).

Key Functional Characteristics: Microwave-modified vegetable proteins exhibit unique advantages in easy-to-swallow foods, mainly in their modulation of texture and structure. One of its primary effects is the enhancement of solubility. Controlled exposure to microwave radiation induces partial unfolding of protein molecules, exposing hydrophilic groups and increasing water dispersibility (Liu, Wang, et al., 2025). For example, rice bran protein solubility increased from 12.8 % to 41.8 % following 160 W microwave plasma treatment for 5 min (Liu, Wang, et al., 2025). This increased solubility bolsters the dispersion stability of products in the system, a crucial factor when preparing high-protein fluid or paste foods. These foods exhibit a uniform texture, devoid of grittiness, and resist layering, thus reducing the risk of choking during consumption. Microwave irradiation also improves the emulsifying capacity and emulsion stability of plant proteins. The exposure of hydrophobic groups promotes adsorption at the oil–water interface, while the enhanced conformational flexibility enables proteins to form more elastic and continuous interfacial films. Improved emulsification was observed in both rice bran and jack bean proteins after microwave treatment (Ajayi et al., 2024; Liu, Wang, et al., 2025). This improvement not only augments the energy density and flavor profile of products but also maintains system stability, addressing the nutritional requirements of malnourished populations. Concurrently, the enhanced stability of the emulsion ensures consistent product texture throughout storage and pre-consumption. Additionally, foaming properties are enhanced as microwave-induced unfolding exposes active sites that facilitate rapid adsorption and alignment at the air–water interface. Jack bean proteins, for instance, exhibited increased foam stability after microwave treatment due to the formation of more cohesive foam films (Ajayi et al., 2024). Furthermore, the design potential for aerated dietary products with distinct texture layers and a light mouthfeel is noteworthy; these textures are readily manipulated and processed by the tongue. The effect of microwave treatment on the gelation properties of plant proteins is more complex. Moderate treatment conditions can promote the formation of homogeneous, dense gel networks by facilitating structural rearrangement and intermolecular interactions. For example, microwave-assisted alkaline treatment significantly improved the gel strength and elasticity of jack bean protein gels (Ajayi et al., 2024). The ability to form a delicate gel structure is crucial in achieving a specific texture, as prescribed by the IDDSI. By carefully modulating microwave energy, the hardness, adhesiveness, cohesiveness, and springiness of the gels can be fine-tuned to ensure safe consumption by patients with dysphagia. This also allows for individual tailoring of textures (Raheem et al., 2021). Ultimately, these gel networks can impart a smooth, easy-to-chew, and easy-to-swallow texture to the product, while effectively trapping water and avoiding a gritty or overly sticky mouthfeel. Future research could further investigate the precise correlation between microwave parameters and protein structural changes to fully capitalize on the potential of microwave technology in developing dysphagia-friendly foods (Liu, Feng, et al., 2025).

However, localized overheating may still result in the degradation of heat-sensitive vitamins in certain areas.

Microwave treatment is an efficient, energy-saving thermal technique that improves the structural and functional attributes of plant proteins through dielectric heating and molecular realignment. The short process time inherent to this method aids in diminishing the loss of heat-sensitive nutrients, making it ideal for food applications necessitating clean labels and high nutritional value (Sá et al., 2020). Nevertheless, the interaction between microwaves and protein molecules is intricate; improper optimization of process conditions can easily result in excessive denaturation, thereby negatively affecting functional properties. The uneven heating associated with this method can cause local overheating of materials, while leaving other areas underheated. This leads to varying degrees of protein denaturation and unstable product quality, posing a significant challenge for large-scale industrial applications. Ultrasound treatment

Effects of ultrasound treatment on the structure of plant proteins

Ultrasound(US) has a wide range of applications in many fields and can be used to physically modify proteins in the range of 20–100 kHz (Nazari et al., 2018). The modification mechanism relies on the combined effects of cavitation, mechanical vibration, and localized thermal generation. Acoustic cavitation occurs when sound waves create microbubbles in a liquid medium; these bubbles collapse violently, producing transient high pressure and high-speed microjets that generate intense shear forces. These forces lead to molecular rearrangement, particle size reduction, and disruption of non-covalent interactions within protein molecules(Rawat et al., 2023).

Ultrasound can selectively alter protein secondary and tertiary structures by breaking hydrogen bonds and modifying electrostatic interactions. For example, treatment of plant proteins with 20 kHz ultrasound at amplitudes of 20 % and 40 % for 20 min resulted in decreased α-helical content and increased random coil and β-sheet structures (Jian Cedric Sow et al., 2024). Disruption of hydrogen bonding contributes to protein unfolding, while altered electrostatic interactions may facilitate the formation of new protein aggregates or structural conformations (Vera et al., 2019). High-intensity ultrasound is capable of exposing previously buried hydrophobic groups and sulfhydryl (-SH) residues by disrupting tertiary structures, which enhances surface hydrophobicity and interfacial functionality (Arzeni et al., 2012). Furthermore, under high-intensity sonication or prolonged treatment, free radicals generated by cavitation effects or localized high temperatures may lead to the cleavage or rearrangement of disulfide bonds (Mir et al., 2019). This can result in the reduction of S—S bonds to free -SH groups and an increase in reactive sulfhydryl content (Rawat et al., 2023). Notably, while ultrasound significantly affects higher-order structures, it does not typically alter the primary amino acid sequence of plant proteins (Vera et al., 2019).

Effects of ultrasound treatment on the key functional properties of plant protein dysphagia-friendly foods

Key process parameters: The principal parameters for the sonic modification of plant proteins encompass power intensity, treatment duration, frequency, protein concentration, and system temperature. By modulating the intensity of cavitation effects, one can directionally optimize the functional properties of the proteins. Treatment duration is frequently optimized in tandem with power (Vera et al., 2019). A low-power, short-duration treatment can effectively disintegrate plant protein aggregates, markedly improving solubility. Combinations of medium-high power with moderate duration can facilitate the comprehensive unfolding of protein molecules, revealing more hydrophobic groups and thiols, which in turn enhance emulsifying, foaming, and gelling properties (Nazari et al., 2018). Furthermore, factors such as protein concentration and pH value impact the outcomes of modification. Protein concentration affects the viscosity of the medium and the ultrasound propagation efficiency. At concentrations that are excessively high, there is significant attenuation of ultrasonic energy, diminished cavitation effects, and inconsistent modification results. Employing a multi-factor synergistic analysis, like the response surface method, and controlling fluid viscosity can induce a soft, highly water-retentive fine gel network. This mitigates challenges such as particle sensation, dehydration, or excessive stickiness during food consumption, ultimately yielding a smooth, uniform, and stable palatable texture (G. Xu et al., 2023).

Key functional characteristics: US treatment enhances solubility, emulsifying ability, oil- and water-holding capacity, foaming properties, surface hydrophobicity, and gelation performance (Vera et al., 2019). These enhanced functional properties translate directly into sensory and textural benefits for dysphagia-friendly foods. Specifically, ultrasound facilitates the exposure of embedded hydrophilic groups, leading to increased aqueous solubility (J. Zhang, Liu, et al., 2022). For example, ULS treatment has been shown to significantly enhance the solubility of soy and pea proteins (Arzeni et al., 2012; J. Zhang et al., 2022). This heightened solubility ensures a smooth mouthfeel in liquid formulations, eliminating grittiness—a primary prerequisite for acceptance among dysphagic patients. Furthermore, the increased solubility permits the incorporation of higher concentrations of plant proteins and lipids without causing grittiness or precipitation, thereby ensuring product stability and nutritional adequacy (Raheem et al., 2021). ULS treatment also enhances emulsification and foaming capacity, as well as foam stability. (O'Sullivan et al., 2016). For instance, pea protein isolates treated at 34 W/cm2 for 2 min exhibited reduced surface tension at both oil–water and air–water interfaces, thereby improving emulsion formation (O'Sullivan et al., 2016). This improvement in emulsification facilitates the complete encapsulation and stable dispersion of fat particles by proteins, which in turn traps flavor compounds. This lays a solid foundation for the development of foods with consistent texture and controlled flavor release. It effectively inhibits phase separation and prevents a gritty texture, thereby providing a diverse sensory experience for patients with dysphagia (Raheem et al., 2021). Moreover, ultrasound can facilitate interactions with bioactive compounds such as plant proteins and polyphenols, yielding conjugates with superior functional performance (X. Liu, Xue, & Adhikari, 2023). The introduction of polyphenols enhances the antioxidant value of the product, offering a food solution that marries functionality with nutritional and health benefits for individuals with dysphagia. Regarding gelation, ultrasound induces the formation of more homogeneous and stable gel networks, thereby enhancing elasticity, and water-holding capacity of the gels (G. Li et al., 2024). The highly water-holding gel network ensures that the product stays moist in the mouth and does not absorb saliva to form dry clumps. Consequently, products can be given a soft and moist texture, significantly mitigating the difficulty and discomfort associated with swallowing. For example, soybean protein isolates subjected to 400 W ultrasound exhibited improved gelling and rheological properties (G. Li et al., 2024). Ultrasonic treatment effectively reduces the viscosity of high-concentration protein systems, thereby facilitating filling and inhalation processes. Precise control over fluid viscosity enables optimal shear-thinning behavior, which ensures a reduction in viscosity during oral processing and tongue movement, resulting in a smooth texture and improved swallowability(G. As a physical processing method, it offers non-thermal or mild thermal effects, ensuring the optimal retention of essential amino acids and heat-sensitive nutrients in plant proteins. This minimizes nutrient loss often associated with intense chemical processes or prolonged high temperatures (Sá et al., 2020). Notably, the cavitation effect of ultrasonic treatment enhances the in vitro digestibility and bioavailability of proteins. This is achieved by disrupting the compact higher structures of proteins, revealing the enzyme action sites, and effectively diminishing the activity of anti-nutritional agents such as trypsin inhibitor (Shuyi Li et al., 2021; Y. Regarding processing characteristics, ultrasound significantly enhances processing adaptability by disintegrating insoluble aggregates and causing protein molecules to unfold. Advantages and limitations of ultrasound treatment for plant protein modification

US treatment has emerged as an efficient, eco-friendly physical modification technique for improving the functional properties of plant proteins (Jian Cedric Sow et al., 2024). The process in question does not rely on chemical agents and leaves no harmful residues behind, making the resulting products more natural and safer. When compared to traditional heat treatments, which take a long time, ultrasonic treatment typically requires only a brief moment and boasts a higher energy utilization efficiency. Ultrasonic treatment can be performed at low temperatures or nearly ambient ones, allowing for the preservation of the natural nutritional value of proteins, vitamins, and heat-sensitive components, while simultaneously steering clear of any adverse changes such as the Maillard reaction (Shuyi Li et al., 2021). However, there are still considerable challenges standing in the way of its widespread industrial application, including equipment costs, parameter standardisation, and more. The effects seen in industrialized continuous flow reactors may vary and issues like uneven energy distribution can occur. In protein systems containing lipids, free radicals created by ultrasonic cavitation effects can speed up lipid oxidation, leading to rancidity and off-flavors. Therefore, it is crucial to mitigate these effects through process control or the addition of antioxidants (Shuyi Li et al., 2021).

Compared to microwaves, ultrasonic waves enhance the functional characteristics of plant proteins under meticulously regulated temperature conditions. Microwave heating can produce a coarse gel structure due to uneven heating. In contrast, ultrasonic waves facilitate the formation of a more refined, uniform, and highly water-retentive three-dimensional gel network—a characteristic crucial for developing soft and fine dysphagia-friendly foods (G. Li et al., 2024). Furthermore, microwave treatment often causes water evaporation and protein aggregation because of thermal effects, frequently augmenting the system's viscosity. US can decrease the viscosity of high-concentration protein solutions or achieve thickening via parameter modifications. However, both methods have their shortcomings: distributing energy uniformly can be challenging when scaling up ultrasonic technology industrially, and the equipment's initial investment and maintenance costs are considerable. Additionally, ultrasonic treatment of lipid-containing systems might expedite lipid oxidation. Although microwave technology also faces issues with uniformity, such as overheating which can trigger Maillard reactions or nutrient loss, it is relatively simpler to implement for large-scale production. Thus, ultrasonic technology is better suited for the development of high-value-added products demanding stringent texture and nutrient preservation, whereas microwaves are more feasible for extensive heating treatments and cost management (Shuyi Li et al., 2021).

High-pressure treatment

Effects of high-pressure treatment on the structure of plant proteins

High-pressure treatment is a non-thermal processing technology that enhances the refinement and homogenization of food matrices through the combined effects of high shear force, high-frequency oscillations, and cavitation phenomena under elevated pressure conditions (Saricaoglu et al., 2018). High-pressure technologies mainly include high hydrostatic pressure (HHP) and dynamic high-pressure microjet (DHPM) systems.

Under high pressure, water molecules penetrate hydrophobic regions and compress the protein matrix, disrupting non-covalent interactions such as hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic interactions, and ionic bonds (Yang & Powers, 2016). When the applied pressure exceeds a critical threshold, some of the hydrogen bonds in the protein molecule that maintain the secondary structure may be disrupted (Yang et al., 2016). Protein secondary structural elements increase in α-helix and random coil content, as observed in Cyperus esculentus L. proteins subjected to homogenization at 40–140 MPa (Zhang et al., 2022). The effects of high pressure on tertiary structure are more pronounced. Compression-induced penetration of water into hydrophobic cores disrupts the structural balance, prompting molecular unfolding and rearrangement (Y. Zhao et al., 2023). For instance, treatment of perilla protein isolates with DHPM at 90 and 120 MPa led to reduced particle size and zeta potential, increased surface hydrophobicity, and elevated free sulfhydryl content, all indicative of structural rearrangement and enhanced functional reactivity (Q. Zhao et al., 2021). Moreover, quaternary structures are destabilized as non-covalent interactions between subunits weaken or dissociate, leading to disaggregation and potential loss or transformation of protein complexes (Baldelli et al., 2024).

Effects of high-pressure treatment on the key functional properties of plant protein dysphagia-friendly foods

Key process parameters: The efficacy of high-pressure modification technology is heavily contingent upon the meticulous control and optimization of critical parameters, including pressure intensity, duration, and protein concentration. Optimal pressure tends to facilitate the partial unfolding of protein molecules, thereby augmenting their surface activity (Y. Zhao et al., 2023); ultrahigh pressure can stimulate protein re-aggregation post-unfolding, creating a dense network structure and enhancing rheological characteristics (Saricaoglu et al., 2018). For instance, in a pH environment distant from the isoelectric point, a 150 MPa treatment can markedly enhance the gel performance of pea protein (D'Alessio et al., 2024). While extended duration can bolster the degree of denaturation and aggregation, excessive time may precipitate a decline in functional properties, such as diminished emulsion stability, necessitating a balance with pressure intensity for short-term treatment. Lower temperature environments are more amenable to enhancing plant protein solubility, effectively preserving the flavor, color, and heat-sensitive nutrients of the raw materials (Luo et al., 2022). Moreover, lower concentration protein solutions might exert a more pronounced impact on improving solubility and emulsibility, whereas higher concentrations are advantageous for constructing robust gel systems, suitable for solid colloidal food design. Determining the optimal parameter combination warrants the employment of models like response surface methodology, focusing on gel strength, water retention, and rheological characteristics as fundamental indicators (Luo et al., 2022).

Key functional characteristics: High-pressure processing (HPP) reduces protein particle size, improves solubility, increases surface hydrophobicity, and enhances foaming, emulsification, and gelation characteristics (Y. Quinoa protein isolates treated with appropriate HPP levels showed enhanced solubility and surface hydrophobicity due to the exposure of internal hydrophobic groups (Luo et al., 2022). This facilitates a more uniform dispersion of the protein within the system, thereby producing a smooth texture devoid of grittiness. This improves upon the often coarse texture associated with conventional plant-based protein products and diminishes the risk of choking during swallowing. Similarly, HHP combined with pH modulation has been shown to significantly improve the emulsifying properties of soy protein suspensions (Tan et al., 2021). The improved emulsification capability facilitates the integration of higher concentrations of lipid nutrients, eliminating issues of phase separation or a greasy mouthfeel. This stable emulsion system establishes a smooth and hydrating base, thereby mitigating the risk of aspiration caused by oil-water separation during swallowing. High-pressure treatment also increases foaming capacity. Quinoa proteins treated at 50 MPa exhibited nearly double the foaming ability compared to untreated samples, suggesting improved interfacial activity and air retention (Luo et al., 2022). Furthermore, HPP enhances textural attributes and facilitates gel formation in plant proteins. (S. Zhang et al., 2025). Under high pressure, the protein is moderately denatured and unfolded, and the intermolecular structure forms a uniform and dense three-dimensional network through hydrophobic interactions and disulfide bonds. Pea proteins, for example, formed cohesive gels upon high-pressure homogenization, with increased pressure resulting in denser and more elastic gel networks (D'Alessio et al., 2024). This modified gel exhibits moderate hardness and strong cohesion, readily deforming and flowing under minimal shear force in the mouth. This results in a pudding-like or buttery smooth texture that leaves no residue after swallowing. Furthermore, high-pressure treatment optimizes the rheological behavior of plant proteins, endowing them with both elasticity and viscosity while demonstrating ideal viscoelasticity and shear-thinning characteristics 70. The gel retains its shape stability in a static state, but its apparent viscosity decreases due to shear force during swallowing. This significantly reduces the risk of aspiration (Luo et al., 2022). These structural enhancements contribute to improved sensory characteristics of the final food products and offer valuable opportunities for developing novel plant-based formulations with appealing textures and functionalities.

Pulsed electric fields

Effects of pulsed electric field treatment on the structure of plant proteins

PEF employs brief high-voltage pulses that generate transient electric fields, producing strong electrodynamic forces capable of altering the molecular conformation of proteins in aqueous solutions or suspensions (Malik et al., 2024).

Upon reaching a threshold electric field intensity, PEF disrupts electrostatic interactions, hydrogen bonds, and other non-covalent forces within plant protein molecules, resulting in conformational modifications. For example, PEF has been reported to increase β-sheet and random coil content in soy protein (Y.-Q. Li, 2012). Similarly, treatment of sericin at 12.5 kV/cm reduced hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions, leading to partial unfolding of peptide chains and a greater increase in β-turns and irregular coiling (Ren et al., 2025). In pea protein isolates, incremental increases in electric field strength (5–25 kV/cm) first led to a decrease and then an increase in α-helix content, while random coil content exhibited an opposite trend (Z.-L. Chen et al., 2023). At 10 kV/cm, α-helix content decreased by 23.25 %, whereas random coil content increased by 117.25 %, suggesting significant unfolding. At higher field strengths, partial recovery of ordered structures was observed, indicating a threshold-dependent structural elasticity (Z.-L. Chen et al., 2023). This suggests that changes in protein properties depend on the electric field strength and that the degree of structural change is nonlinearly correlated with the electric field strength. Additionally, PEF promotes the unfolding of tertiary structures, increases surface hydrophobicity, and induces the release of previously buried sulfhydryl groups. This is accompanied by partial cleavage of disulfide bonds, thereby increasing free sulfhydryl content and enhancing protein reactivity (X. Zhang et al., 2024).

Effects of pulsed electric field treatment on the key functional properties of plant protein dysphagia-friendly foods

Key process parameters: The efficacy of PEF treatment is predominantly determined by parameters such as electric field strength, the number of pulses, pulse width, and temperature. A moderate electric field strength can disrupt hydrogen bonds and electrostatic interactions, thereby facilitating molecular unfolding and enhancing solubility, emulsibility, foaming capacity, gelation, and other functional attributes. However, excessive strength can lead to overaggregation of plant proteins, resulting in diminished functionality (Zhang et al., 2021). For example, the solubility of soy protein has been observed to increase significantly by 75 % under the influence of 10 kV/cm (R. Wang et al., 2023). Furthermore, the more pulses there are, the greater the energy input, and the more thorough the modification, but an excessive number could lead to local overheating or protein degradation (Malik et al., 2024). A balanced pulse width can bolster functional characteristics and should be calibrated in tandem with electric field strength to prevent thermal denaturation. The protein's concentration impacts its conductivity and the likelihood of intermolecular collisions. Low-concentration protein solutions coupled with moderate electric field strengths are more conducive to enhancing the solubility and emulsibility of plant proteins, making them suitable for protein beverages or nutritional emulsions (Sarathy et al., 2025). Conversely, more concentrated solutions and stronger electric fields encourage direct gelation, fostering protein crosslinking to create a gel network, and are thus employed in the production of pudding-like consumables. However, it's challenging to control the uniformity of the treatment. Through systematic parameter optimization, PEF technology can empower plant proteins, providing ideal raw materials for high-value-added fields like easy-to-swallow foods (Sarathy et al., 2025).

Key functional characteristics: By disrupting non-covalent interactions—such as hydrogen bonds and electrostatic forces—PEF induces conformational changes and promotes the exposure of hydrophobic domains, which in turn enhances protein–water interactions. Additionally, the increased electrostatic repulsion between protein molecules reduces aggregation and precipitation, leading to significantly improved solubility (Malik et al., 2024). For example, PEF treatment reduced the hydrophobicity of wheat gluten and increased its solubility. This increased solubility facilitates the integration of the protein into liquid or semi-liquid diets, thereby augmenting the protein content without escalating the viscosity burden. This ensures nutritional density and a smooth texture, which is particularly beneficial for the elderly population who necessitate effective nutritional supplementation but possess limited swallowing capabilities. Wang et al., 2023). Consequently, the product can encapsulate more lipid-based nutrients and encapsulate flavor substances, preventing phase separation and eliminating an oily mouthfeel. This also aids in maintaining the uniformity of liquid products and reduces the risk of aspiration caused by oil-water separation during swallowing (Taha et al., 2022). Surface hydrophobicity of soy protein was also enhanced following PEF treatment, thereby improving its foamability due to greater protein alignment and adsorption at the air–water interface (R. Wang et al., 2023). PEF treatment additionally improves the gelling behavior of plant proteins. Appropriate electric field strength can induce ordered aggregation and molecular cross-linking, which result in a more homogeneous and mechanically robust gel matrix with enhanced elasticity and water-holding capacity (Taha et al., 2022). The gel exhibits significant deformability and flowability when subjected to tongue pressure within the oral cavity, thereby reducing the risk of residue and aspiration. It introduces an innovative food solution that is both nutritious and safe for individuals with specific dietary requirements. For instance, a modified pea protein gel could serve as a vehicle for producing jelly-like foods, thus ensuring a safe and enjoyable swallowing experience (B. Xu et al., 2025).

PEF can effectively disrupt the structure of the plant cell wall, enabling the proteins stored within the cells to be more completely released, thereby increasing the contact area with digestive enzymes. Furthermore, PEF treatment can diminish the activity of certain anti-nutritional factors by dismantling their structures. However, the efficiency of PEF in inactivating anti-nutritional factors is typically lower than that of traditional heat treatment (Sarathy et al., 2025).

In terms of processing characteristics, PEF optimizes the rheology and texture properties of plant proteins, thereby facilitating the development of dysphagia foods. Modification of plant proteins improves their processing functionality, which is essential for their application in food industries such as plant-based meats, beverages, and bakery products. This modification augments their solubility and dissolution rate—crucial factors for the preparation of high-protein, stable plant protein beverages (Malik et al., 2024). Furthermore, proteins, being natural emulsifiers, allow treated plant proteins to migrate more rapidly to the interface and rearrange, thus forming a stronger and more elastic interfacial film that can produce finer and more stable emulsions (R. Wang et al., 2023). These properties make them particularly suitable for the preparation of smooth and moist sauces or nutritional emulsions. The application of appropriate electric field strength encourages the formation of a fine, water-holding gel network structure of plant proteins. This structure adheres to the IDDSI framework's stipulations regarding the viscosity and cohesiveness of soft food masses, thereby making it apt for the development of dysphagia foods (Malik et al., 2024; B. Xu et al., 2025).

Advantages and limitations of pulsed electric field treatment for plant protein modification

PEF treatment is an emerging non-thermal processing technology characterized by its sustainability, short treatment times, and minimal impact on nutritional compounds. Through the processes of electroporation and polarization, PEF significantly enhance the functional characteristics of plant proteins, thereby facilitating their conversion into food products with refined textures (R. Wang et al., 2023). This technology is particularly adept at forming a uniform, smooth protein gel network that caters to the specific needs of dysphagia foods requiring consistent texture and suitable viscosity. The use of PEF ensures the preservation of heat-sensitive nutrients and natural flavors of proteins, mitigating nutrient loss and flavor degradation associated with traditional thermal processing methods. This aligns with the contemporary trend towards clean labelling (Sarathy et al., 2025). Concurrently, PEF's electroporation effect disrupts the plant cell wall, revealing a greater number of enzyme active sites and thus significantly augmenting in vitro digestibility and bioavailability. This is a crucial aspect for the development of highly nutritious, easy-to-swallow foods. Despite its advantages, the application of this technology is not without limitations. The modification effects of PEF are influenced by a complex array of parameters, including electric field strength, energy input, and material properties, which makes it challenging to establish a universal process. Additionally, the initial investment costs for equipment are considerable. There is also a need for further research to ascertain the long-term functional stability of modified proteins and to better understand their digestion and absorption patterns within the human body. A noteworthy limitation is that PEF is primarily suited for liquid or semi-liquid systems with moderate conductivity. Its efficacy in processing high-viscosity materials or protein powders remains inadequate, thereby substantially narrowing its range of application (Malik et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2025).

Cold plasma

Effects of cold plasma treatment on the structure of plant proteins

Cold plasma (CP) technology utilizes dielectric barrier discharge (DBD) to generate ionized gases containing a mixture of high-energy electrons, ions, UV photons, and reactive oxygen and nitrogen species. These reactive entities can interact with plant protein to induce structural and functional modifications (Olatunde et al., 2023).

The reactive species generated during plasma discharge penetrate protein matrices, altering secondary and tertiary structures. For example, DBD plasma treatment was shown to disrupt intramolecular hydrogen bonds in soy protein, thereby modifying its secondary structural elements (Sharafodin et al., 2022). Treatment of phycocyanin with cold plasma reduced α-helix content while increasing random coil proportions in a time-dependent manner, reflecting a progressive loosening of the molecular conformation (Gong et al., 2025). Moreover, plasma treatment has been found to increase the particle size of treated proteins and facilitate structural relaxation. (Gong et al., 2025). At higher voltage levels, reactive radicals may bind to sulfhydryl groups, inducing disulfide bond formation. Conversely, at lower voltages (e.g., <75 V), disulfide bonds in zein are cleaved, leading to the generation of free sulfhydryl groups and exposure of the sulfhydryl groups buried inside, resulting in a looser molecular structure (S. Dong, Gao, Zhao, Li, & Chen, 2017). These structural changes enhance the functional versatility of plant proteins and broaden their application potential in novel food systems.

Effects of cold plasma treatment on the key functional properties of plant protein dysphagia-friendly foods

Key process parameters: Cold plasma technology is an effective means of enhancing the functional characteristics of plant proteins by optimizing key parameters such as treatment duration, voltage, and gas type (Sharafodin et al., 2022). The treatment duration is a particularly critical parameter; studies have shown that treating phycocyanin at 50 W power for a period of 20–40 s yields optimal results (Gong et al., 2025). Furthermore, the voltage magnitude directly influences the energy of the plasma and the density of active particles; higher voltage results in a more potent modification effect. While higher power generally implies stronger modification capabilities, it is crucial to prevent over-treatment. The type of gas used also plays a significant role, as different gases produce varying active species and dominant modification reactions (Olatunde et al., 2023). Research indicates that a treatment voltage of 30 kV effectively modifies soy protein and pea protein. Proper parameter treatment enhances the solubility, emulsification stability, and gelation properties of plant proteins, creating a finely-tuned, uniform three-dimensional network structure. This structure is ideal for preparing easily swallowed foods with a smooth texture and stable viscosity (Rout et al., 2024).

Key functional characteristics: Cold plasma treatment—typically conducted under dielectric barrier discharge (DBD) conditions—is another non-thermal strategy for improving the functionality of plant proteins. The reactive species generated (e.g., free radicals, ions) alter protein structure by disrupting peptide bonds, modifying disulfide linkages, and inducing the exposure of internal hydrophobic groups, which collectively change the aggregation state and enhance solubility. For example, the solubility of zein increased under voltage treatments between 0 and 125 V, peaking at 75 V in both neutral and acidic environments, likely due to conformational loosening and enhanced hydration capacity (S. Dong et al., 2017). The significant improvement in solubility ensures that protein molecules or particles are completely dispersed in the aqueous phase, essentially eliminating tiny particles or roughness that could trigger choking. Cold plasma also improves emulsification activity by reducing interfacial tension and promoting the formation of stable interfacial protein layers (Rout et al., 2024). This increased emulsifying activity enables fat droplets to be finely and stably encapsulated within a dense protein network, effectively inhibiting the separation of oil or water during storage or consumption. This guarantees the stability of the fatty system, providing the product with a smooth, delicate, and uniform mouthfeel. These properties are particularly significant for dysphagia and elderly populations, substantially enhancing the palatability and acceptability of such products. Furthermore, cold plasma significantly influences gel properties. Pea protein isolates treated with 30 kV for 6 min exhibited enhanced viscoelasticity and structural cohesion, suggesting that cold plasma can promote the development of structured protein-based gels suitable for use in specialized formulations, such as dysphagia-friendly or high-protein functional foods (Rout et al., 2024).

Effects of cold plasma treatment on nutritional and processing characteristics of plant protein

Cold plasma, a non-thermal technology, is able to optimally prevent the destruction of vitamins and denaturation of proteins caused by conventional thermal processing, hence maintaining the natural state of plant proteins and the original flavor of food (Olatunde et al., 2023). The high-energy active particles in the plasma can dismantle the higher-level structure of proteins, revealing more trypsin and pepsin action sites that are typically concealed, facilitating their digestion and absorption by the human body. This significantly enhances the digestibility of plant proteins (Rout et al., 2024). Concurrently, this treatment effectively reduces the activity of anti-nutritional factors such as trypsin inhibitors, further eliminating digestive impediments. Moreover, research indicates that moderate cold plasma treatment can oxidize and modify the allergenic epitopes of proteins, consequently diminishing their immune recognition response and potentially decreasing the allergenicity of certain plant proteins (Olatunde et al., 2023). Nevertheless, parameter control is imperative as excessive treatment may result in the oxidation of sensitive amino acids such as tryptophan, marginally impacting the nutritional value and potentially producing oxidized flavors.

In terms of processing characteristics, cold plasma treatment significantly enhances the functional properties of plant proteins. These enhancements directly impact the application performance of the products in the food industry (Olatunde et al., 2023). The most notable alteration in treated plant proteins is the substantial improvement in solubility, which forms the basis for other functional characteristics (S. Dong et al., 2017). The rise in emulsibility and emulsion stability enables the formation of a finer and more stable oil-droplet interface film, vital for the production of stable plant-based emulsion beverages (Sharafodin et al., 2022). Crucially, this technology allows for precise regulation of gel characteristics, thereby preparing plant protein gels with superior water-holding capacity and a delicate texture. This feature makes it particularly suitable for the development of dysphagia foods that can meet the viscosity and cohesiveness requirements of the IDDSI standard (Rout et al., 2024).

Advantages and limitations of cold plasma treatment for plant protein modification

CP treatment offers several advantages, including low processing temperatures, solvent-free conditions, uniform treatment, and high energy efficiency. This method can achieve targeted modifications to protein structures by regulating active particles, eliminating the need for thermal or chemical additives (Rout et al., 2024). It markedly enhances the functional properties of proteins while preserving heat-sensitive nutrients and natural flavors. Additionally, it can reduce the risk of allergies by altering allergenic epitopes (Olatunde et al., 2023). This makes it particularly suitable for the development of foods that demand stringent texture and safety standards, such as dysphagia foods. However, this technology does have significant limitations. The precise molecular mechanism underlying plasma's interaction with proteins remains largely unexplored. The modification effects are highly dependent on processing parameters, and there is a risk that active particles may oxidize sensitive amino acids, leading to flavor deterioration (Sharafodin et al., 2022). Other challenges include high equipment costs, limited processing capacity, requirements for sample viscosity and humidity, consistency of processing at scale, and the mechanism of action still needs to be deepened (Olatunde et al., 2023).

Chemical modification

Chemical modification expands the application potential of plant proteins by selectively altering reactive side-chain groups. These modifications lead to directional changes in physicochemical behavior and functional attributes. For instance, glycosylation improves the texture and taste of food by incorporating hydrophilic sugar chains onto the molecular surface. Additionally, this process also promotes a soft, cohesive gel structure, enhancing the water-holding capacity and fluid viscosity of the system (Chu et al., 2024). Extreme pH treatment also encourages the formation of a dense disulfide bond network within the internal mercapto groups, boosting the elasticity and cohesion of the gel. This ensures that the food bolus maintains its structural integrity and does not easily fragment during swallowing (J. Li et al., 2020), thereby improving texture properties closely related to dysphagia management. Covalent chemical modifications commonly include phosphorylation, glycosylation, and acylation, whereas non-covalent approaches may involve pH adjustment and similar treatments. Despite their utility, many chemical modification strategies involve the use of exogenous reagents and may generate potentially toxic by-products. Thus, careful consideration must be given to safety assessments, cytotoxicity profiling, and regulatory applicability when employing these methods in food-grade systems. Glycosylation

Effects of glycosylation treatment on the structure of plant proteins

The glycosylation process involves the covalent conjugation of protein amino groups with the carbonyl groups of reducing sugars, leading to the formation of protein–polysaccharide conjugates. Through a series of complex rearrangements and condensation steps, stable macromolecular complexes are formed, often resulting in increased molecular weight and altered solubility profiles (Teng et al., 2021).

Glycosylation can be facilitated through both thermal and non-thermal approaches. Thermal methods include wet and dry heat treatments, whereas non-thermal assistance is increasingly achieved via ultrasound or pulsed electric field (PEF) treatment to accelerate conjugation efficiency (Shengxian Li, Wang, Dai, et al., 2025). For instance, glycosylation of pea protein isolate with arabinose disrupts intra- and intermolecular hydrogen bonding networks, resulting in denaturation and conformational unfolding, including a decrease in α-helix content and an increase in β-sheet structures (X. Chen et al., 2022). Similarly, ultrasound-assisted glycosylation of soybean isolate with soybean oligosaccharides resulted in reduced α-helix and elevated β-sheet and β-turn contents, along with significant tertiary structure changes (Chu et al., 2024). These conformational adjustments increase the exposure of hydrophobic, hydrophilic, and polar groups, thereby enhancing interfacial activity (Teng et al., 2021).

Effects of glycosylation treatment on the key functional properties of plant protein dysphagia-friendly foods

Key process parameters: The efficiency and outcome of glycosylation are influenced by multiple parameters, including pH, reaction temperature, reaction time, and protein-to-sugar ratio (Teng et al., 2021). The primary goal of optimization is to maximize the grafting degree while minimizing the generation of harmful by-products. Temperature is the most significant factor impacting the reaction rate, whereas time determines the reaction's extent. For instance, rapeseed protein has been glycosylated with dextran at 95 °C (Z. G. Wang et al., 2018). Furthermore, the Maillard reaction reaches its peak speed at medium water activity; complete dryness or excessive moisture both serve to inhibit the reaction. The ratio of reactants directly influences the degree of grafting (DG) and grafting efficiency (GE). An excess of sugar can elevate the degree of substitution; for example, when the ratio of quinoa protein to monosaccharides is 2:1, the modified protein displays optimal physicochemical properties and biological activity (Teng et al., 2021). However, an excess can also lead to increased browning and the production of undesirable flavor by-products. By optimizing the pH and selecting small-molecule reducing sugars, the solubility and emulsifying stability of the modified protein can be significantly enhanced, transforming a rough texture into a smooth, uniform fluid or gel structure. Moreover, by controlling the molecular weight and addition ratio of sugars, the grafted hydrophilic sugar chains can substantially boost the protein's water retention capacity and fluid viscosity, yielding a moist, dense texture that resists easy water separation (Z. G. Wang et al., 2018).

In terms of reaction efficiency, traditional heat treatment glycosylation delivers energy through direct heating, fostering a rapid reaction rate that is suitable for large-scale industrialization. However, the precision of this operational process is challenging to maintain, and it often results in browning and off-flavors (S. Li et al., 2025). Conversely, non-thermal physical treatment-assisted glycosylation initiates reactions under milder conditions. Although the reaction rate may be slower, it allows for precise control during the initial stages of the reaction, thereby significantly enhancing the selectivity and uniformity of the target products. This method not only improves the functional characteristics of proteins but also better preserves their natural color, flavor, and nutritional value. While this modification technique is both efficient and environmentally friendly, its broader application still faces obstacles (S. Li et al., 2025).

Key functional characteristics: Glycosylation—via the conjugation of reducing sugars to protein molecules—is a widely used chemical modification that enhances multiple functional properties of plant proteins (S. Li et al., 2025). Non-thermal treatments, which stimulate reactions via physical or biocatalysis under mild conditions, can notably suppress the production of detrimental byproducts while enhancing the functional attributes of plant proteins with greater precision. For instance, the glycosylation reaction between pea isolated protein and arabinose introduces hydrophilic carbohydrate groups to the protein's primary chain, thereby increasing its surface hydrophilicity and subsequently improving its solubility (X. Chen et al., 2022). Traditional thermal glycosylation, which relies on high temperatures to catalyze the reaction, improves the solubility, emulsifying capacity, and gel strength of plant proteins. For example, glycosylation of quinoa protein isolate with xylose and mannose significantly enhanced aqueous solubility due to both the inherent hydrophilicity of the polysaccharides and the conformational relaxation of the protein (Teng et al., 2021). By manipulating the molecular weight and addition ratio of sugars, the hydrophilic sugar chains that are grafted onto proteins can significantly augment the protein's water retention capacity and fluid viscosity (Chu et al., 2024). This results in a moderate fluid viscosity, devoid of a sticky sensation, thereby making it easier to swallow. Furthermore, the rheological behavior of proteins serves as a critical metric for evaluating their gelation properties, which subsequently influence the texture structure, nutritional quality, and sensory attributes of food matrices (Gallego et al., 2022). For instance, dextran-conjugated rapeseed protein isolate showed an increase in storage modulus (G′), indicating improved gel strength and network integrity (Z. G. Wang et al., 2018). The formation of a robust, elastic gel network yields a soft yet cohesive texture that is easy to chew and shape, thereby ensuring food blocks' safety. Glycosylation also improves emulsifying properties by increasing interfacial activity and stabilizing protein layers at the oil–water interface. Protein–polysaccharide conjugates form dense, elastic interfacial films that prevent droplet coalescence and enhance the emulsification activity index (EAI) and emulsion stability index (ESI). The degree of glycosylation has been positively correlated with these enhancements (Teng et al., 2021). The generation of more homogeneous protein-sugar conjugates yields an ideal material for developing the silky mouthfeel, stable texture, and nutritional integrity necessary in dysphagia foods. Beyond structural functionality, glycosylation can improve the bioactive properties of plant proteins, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antiproliferative activities (Teng et al., 2021). Due to these multifaceted improvements, protein–carbohydrate conjugates hold strong potential for applications in delivery systems, film-forming agents, emulsifiers, and stabilizers, offering broad applicability in food and nutraceutical formulations (S. Li et al., 2025).

Effects of glycosylation treatment on nutritional and processing characteristics of plant protein

Glycosylation modification significantly influences the nutritional and qualitative aspects of plant proteins. Thermal glycosylation, dependent on medium and high temperatures to facilitate the reaction (Z. G. Wang et al., 2018), results in the depletion of essential amino acids such as lysine and generates late glycosylation end products, thereby diminishing its nutritional value. Concurrently, it triggers uncontrollable browning and cooking odors, considerably restricting its utilization in light-colored and mildly flavored products (S. Li et al., 2025). On the contrary, non-thermal glycosylation occurs at low or room temperature. It not only augments functional properties more efficiently but also markedly inhibits harmful by-products, thereby preserving the protein's nutrition, natural color, and flavor. This makes it particularly suitable for formulating specialized dietary products with stringent texture, sensory, and nutritional requirements (S. Li et al., 2025). Furthermore, glycosylation can enhance the biological activity of proteins. For instance, the Maillard reaction between quinoa isolated protein and xylose and mannose induces structural modifications, amplifying its anti-inflammatory and anti-proliferative activities (Teng et al., 2021).

Thermal glycosylation is a traditional method with ready industrial applicability. Its primary asset is its high technological maturity, coupled with relatively low equipment costs, straightforward process flow, and seamless integration into existing food industry production lines. However, the high temperatures required can accelerate reactions and potentially increase by-products, leading to the development of undesirable flavors. This limits the range of its applications (S. Li et al., 2025). In contrast, non-thermal glycosylation can significantly reduce side reactions and enhance functional properties more specifically. However, most non-thermal technologies entail high costs for large-scale equipment and have limited processing capacity. Its industrial application is still immature. At present, thermal methods continue to be the preferred choice for large-scale production. In practical applications, it is imperative that large-scale production simultaneously addresses efficiency, cost control, and product quality. This approach is essential for advancing the innovation and incorporation of plant proteins in the realm of precise nutrition and easily consumable foods(S. Li et al., 2025; Sarathy et al., 2025).

Advantages and limitations of glycosylation treatment for plant protein modification

The thermal glycosylation method offers the advantages of established technology, significant modification impact, and a high degree of cross-linking, effectively enhancing protein solubility and functional properties (Z. G. Wang et al., 2018). However, its drawbacks include precise control challenges in the process, which can lead to protein denaturation, nutrient loss, and by-product generation. The non-thermal method preserves the natural structure and nutritional value of proteins better, demonstrating strong reaction specificity and producing fewer by-products. Its disadvantages are lower modification efficiency, extended reaction cycles, and increased equipment costs (S. Li et al., 2025).

pH Adjustment

Effects of pH change treatment on the structure of plant proteins

pH modulation is a widely employed and cost-effective strategy for protein pretreatment. Altering the pH of the surrounding medium induces conformational transitions in plant proteins by disrupting hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions, thereby exposing reactive residues and altering structural and functional properties (Yildiz et al., 2023).

Under strongly alkaline conditions, proteins tend to unfold, exposing internal sulfhydryl and hydrophobic groups. Conversely, acidic treatments can induce conformational rearrangements, which may be reversible upon pH neutralization (Figueroa-González et al., 2022). For example, treatment of quinoa protein at pH 12 resulted in decreased molecular aggregation and increased surface hydrophobicity, as well as elevated contents of free sulfhydryl and disulfide bonds, indicating a restructuring of the molecular architecture (Yildiz et al., 2023). Alkaline conditions disrupt hydrogen bonds that stabilize α-helical and β-sheet structures, causing transformation into more disordered forms such as random coils. These rearrangements in secondary structure can improve the flexibility and reactivity of plant proteins, offering advantages in applications requiring dynamic structural adaptation (Figueroa-González et al., 2022).

Effects of pH change treatment on the key functional properties of plant protein dysphagia-friendly foods

Key process parameters: The primary parameters of the pH shift treatment modification process are the pH value, holding time, and subsequent treatment. Among these, the pH value is deemed the most critical. Typically, by adjusting the environmental pH to extremes distant from the protein's isoelectric point, one can maximize the net charge on the surface of its molecules (J. Li et al., 2020). This pronounced electrostatic repulsion effectively inhibits protein aggregation, fostering molecular unfolding and dispersion, which in turn markedly enhances solubility (Zhang et al., 2021). Nevertheless, extreme pH conditions, particularly when coupled with high temperatures or extended treatment durations, might induce irreversible excessive denaturation, compromising the protein's functional attributes. Notably, studies have shown that the solubility of ginkgo seed protein significantly increases at pH 11.0 over a 1-h period (Zhang et al., 2021). At the target pH, an optimal holding time is imperative. Prolonged exposure can elevate the likelihood of excessive denaturation and undesirable side reactions. Additionally, the rate and intensity of stirring during neutralization influence the protein's complex behavior and its ultimate aggregation state. Thus, subsequent neutralization steps or dialysis are vital for achieving the desired functional properties.

Key functional characteristics: By adjusting the ambient pH, the net surface charge distribution of protein molecules is modified, thereby influencing electrostatic interactions and altering protein–protein repulsion or attraction. This mechanism plays a pivotal role in regulating protein solubility, emulsification, foaming, and gelation behaviors (Zhang et al., 2021). The study showed that the solubility of amaranth protein was significantly improved under alkaline conditions (pH 12) due to increased electrostatic repulsion and molecular dispersibility. (Figueroa-González et al., 2022). Similar solubility improvements were observed in Ginkgo biloba seed proteins treated at pH 11.0, attributed to the formation of a net molecular charge that prevents aggregation and favors dispersion in aqueous systems (Zhang et al., 2021). Through the manipulation of pH transformation, it is viable to generate protein dispersions that retain high solubility even at neutral pH levels. This principle underlies the production of high-protein liquid nutritional supplements, thickeners, and clear beverages, ensuring a smooth texture and the absence of particulates in the end product (Gallego et al., 2022). With regard to emulsification, optimal pH conditions facilitate rapid protein adsorption at the oil–water interface, reduce interfacial tension, and promote the formation of stable emulsions (Zhang et al., 2021). However, deviation from optimal pH—either highly acidic or strongly alkaline—may cause protein over-denaturation, impairing interfacial functionality and emulsion stability. For example, emulsification of Ginkgo protein isolates peaked after exposure to pH 2.0 and pH 12.0, but long-term structural disruption negatively impacted emulsion stability (Zhang et al., 2021). Enhanced solubility and emulsibility contribute to the formation of homogeneous and stable fluid or semi-fluid matrices (Gallego et al., 2022). This is crucial for creating high-energy, emulsion-type nutritional formulations with evenly distributed fat. These formulations effectively prevent fat upfloating and whey syneresis, thereby ensuring product stability and consistent taste throughout the consumption period. In terms of foaming performance, pH adjustment improves both foamability and foam stability by modulating surface activity and molecular flexibility (Figueroa-González et al., 2022). pH also plays a critical role in gelation properties. Peanut protein isolates formed strong gels when treated at pH 10, while extreme pH conditions (pH 2 or 12) led to excessive protein denaturation and aggregation, impairing network formation and reducing gelling capacity (J. Li et al., 2020). The pH value is instrumental in influencing both the formation mechanism and the final texture of the gel. Through the process of modification, plant proteins construct a finely-tuned, highly water-retentive three-dimensional network structure (J. Li et al., 2020). This results in a gel with a soft texture and a certain level of cohesiveness, significantly enhancing the product's palatability and safety. These properties are particularly important for designing products that are suitable for individuals who have difficulty swallowing.

Effects of pH change treatment on nutritional and processing characteristics of plant protein

Moderate treatments can alter the protein structure to augment its digestibility and bioaccessibility (Figueroa-González et al., 2022). Many anti-nutritional factors found in plant proteins become inactive when subjected to pH-shifting treatments, thereby reducing factors such as phytates. However, extreme alkaline conditions combined with high temperatures can trigger adverse side reactions. These include the destruction of base-sensitive amino acids and the creation of lysinoalanine, which results in a loss of nutritional value and potential safety hazards. In terms of enhancing biological activity, a combination of pH shift and high-pressure homogenization treatment significantly increases the antioxidant activity of untreated quinoa protein compared to its untreated counterpart (Yildiz et al., 2023).

The treatment of pH variation significantly influences the processing characteristics of plant proteins. Through strategic pH adjustment, the solubility and dispersion stability of the protein can be markedly enhanced (J. Li et al., 2020). High solubility serves as a prerequisite for activating all other functional properties (Sarathy et al., 2025). The augmentation of these functional properties is utilized in the preparation of plant protein beverages, plant-based meats, and other derivatives. Precise control of pH is instrumental in maintaining system stability and fostering an optimal texture and taste. Consequently, pH control emerges as a fundamental and potent technical measure that is integral to the entire continuum from plant protein processing and modification to the final products (Zhang et al., 2021).

Advantages and limitations of pH change treatment for plant protein modification

The process in question does not necessitate intricate or high-cost specialized equipment, and its operation is straightforward, facilitating application and scalability within existing food industry production lines. However, this method harbors potential nutritional and safety risks, with unsuitable conditions potentially triggering severe side reactions and diminishing the nutritional value of proteins. The superior functional attributes of proteins, when modified via pH regulation, may only be fully realized at extreme pH levels. When incorporated into a final food system with a neutral pH, these functional benefits might be partially compromised. Furthermore, the ensuing neutralization and drying stages are pivotal, and any improper control thereof could significantly mitigate the modification effect. The process also entails a substantial amount of reagents, and wastewater treatment presents notable environmental challenges (Ravindran et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2021).

Biological modification

Biological modification refers to the functional alteration of plant proteins through enzyme-catalyzed reactions or microorganism-mediated processes. This modification enables the formation of robust, cohesive gel networks via transglutaminase cross-linking (Eslami et al., 2025); it also facilitates acidifying, hydrolyzing, and thickening through fermentation synergistically (Kaleda et al., 2025). These techniques offer potent, safe, and efficient tools for designing foods for dysphagia. Enzymatic modification

Effects of enzymatic modification on the structure of plant proteins

Enzymatic modification utilizes highly specific biocatalysts, such as proteases and transglutaminases, to tailor the molecular configuration and functional behavior of plant proteins. Proteases hydrolyze peptide bonds, thereby reducing molecular size and altering protein solubility, whereas transglutaminases catalyze γ-glutamyl-ε-lysine cross-linking reactions, promoting polymerization and structural reinforcement (Wouters et al., 2016).

Enzymatic modification parameters govern the extent of structural rearrangement and the resulting functional outcomes. For example, treatment of pea protein with 4 % transglutaminase at 60 °C resulted in a marked reduction in ionic, hydrogen bonding, and hydrophobic interactions. Secondary structural analysis showed a decrease in α-helices and β-sheets, alongside an increase in β-turn content, indicating spatial reconfiguration and partial unfolding of the protein matrix (D. Chen et al., 2022).

Effects of enzymatic modification on the key functional properties of plant protein dysphagia-friendly foods

Key process parameters: The efficiency of enzymatic modification depends on several factors, including enzyme type, substrate specificity, enzyme-to-substrate ratio, and reaction conditions such as pH and temperature. The enzyme type dictates the specificity and the modification pathway, with proteases hydrolyzing peptide bonds to decrease molecular weight, subsequently augmenting solubility, emulsibility, and biological activity. However, it is crucial to strictly regulate their degree of hydrolysis (DH) through factors such as enzyme concentration, temperature, pH, and time. This is necessary to avoid excessive hydrolysis, which results in the formation of bitter peptides and functional degradation (Wouters et al., 2016). In contrast, Transglutaminase (TGase) bolsters gel strength and network stability via catalyzing cross-linking reactions. Nevertheless, its efficiency is synergistically influenced by substrate concentration, E/S ratio, and thermal processing. Overuse of this enzyme may induce excessive cross-linking or even phase separation, thereby compromising gel performance (D. Chen et al., 2022). It is important to note that both enzyme and substrate concentrations directly impact the reaction rate. However, excessively high enzyme concentration might lead to over-hydrolysis or cross-linking, consequently impairing functional properties. Likewise, an overly high substrate concentration could result in mass transfer limitations (Eslami et al., 2025). In addition, optimal enzyme activity requires a compatible temperature and pH. Otherwise, these factors may decrease enzyme activity or cause inactivation. The duration of the reaction determines the extent of modification; insufficient time leads to inadequate modification, while extended time can trigger functional degradation or side reactions (Eslami et al., 2025). Therefore, in practical applications, the first step is to select the appropriate enzyme based on the desired function. Subsequently, a mathematical model incorporating multiple parameters and functional response values is established through experimental design methods. This enables targeted, efficient, and scalable protein function modification.

Key Functional Characteristics: Enzymatic modification is an effective method for improving the functional and bioactive properties. Protease treatment breaks down protein polymers into smaller peptides and free amino acids, thereby modifying molecular weight distribution and enhancing solubility, digestibility, and functional behavior (Wouters et al., 2016). This is crucial for improving the processing adaptability of dysphagia-friendly foods. For instance, proteins hydrolyzed by protease tend to form a uniform and stable liquid phase system, effectively preventing the adhesion or graininess that can occur during swallowing, thereby reducing the risk of choking. Transglutaminase (TGase) catalyzes the formation of ε-(γ-glutamyl)-lysine isopeptide bonds, cross-linking glutamine and lysine residues either intra- or intermolecularly. This cross-linking improves gel strength, structural cohesion, and viscoelasticity (Eslami et al., 2025). These textural advancements can be capitalized on in the development of dysphagia foods, which necessitate a particular degree of cohesiveness and facilitate ease of mastication. For instance, TGase-induced cross-linking in pea protein–collagen mixtures led to an increase in storage modulus (G′) with temperature, indicating thermally stable gel formation. This suggests the creation of a thermally stable gel, contributing to the maintenance of food texture stability during thermal processing and holding procedures. However, excessive TGase addition may trigger phase separation, resulting in reduced gel strength (Eslami et al., 2025). The combination of TGase treatment and mild heating has been demonstrated to improve the gelation and water-holding capacity of pea protein, thereby increasing the wetness and swallowability of the final product (D. Chen et al., 2022). In addition, enzymatic hydrolysis using alkaline proteases improves antioxidant capacity, emulsifying behavior, and foaming properties (Mulla et al., 2019). These improvements directly contribute to critical quality attributes in foods for individuals with dysphagia. For example, the enhancement of emulsifying and foaming properties facilitates the formation of a fine matrix of foam or emulsion, thereby improving the mouthfeel of the food. On the other hand, an increase in antioxidant capacity can extend the shelf-life of the product. Consequently, enzymatic treatment also presents a technical strategy to meet the requirements for safe, palatable, and nutritionally balanced foods for individuals with dysphagia (Raheem et al., 2021).

Effects of enzymatic modification on nutritional and processing characteristics of plant protein

Enzymatic modification notably enhances the nutritional attributes of plant proteins by selectively altering their molecular structure (Ravindran et al., 2024). Concurrently, enzymatic hydrolysis decomposes large protein molecules into smaller peptides and free amino acids. This not only diminishes allergenicity but also substantially augments the digestibility and bioavailability of proteins. Furthermore, this process can liberate bioactive peptides with distinct physiological functions, such as antioxidant properties, rendering them particularly apt for inclusion in nutritional supplements and specialized medical formulations (Mulla et al., 2019; Ravindran et al., 2024).

Enzymatic modification is particularly advantageous for producing proteins with tailored functionalities for specific food applications, while maintaining safety and biocompatibility. The intermolecular cross-linking produced by transglutaminase markedly improves the gel strength, elasticity, and water-holding capacity of protein networks. This provides the desired textural qualities for plant-based meat products and cheese analogs (D. Chen et al., 2022). Protease hydrolysis enhances solubility, allowing for stable use in beverage systems across a neutral pH range. Concurrently, the amphiphilic peptide segments generated by enzymatic hydrolysis significantly boost the emulsifying capacity and foaming performance of proteins. These are key attributes for the development of high-value-added products such as plant-based ice cream and salad dressings. The comprehensive enhancement of these functional properties broadens the application range and value of plant proteins in the food industry (Gallego et al., 2022).

Advantages and limitations of enzymatic treatment for plant protein modification

Enzyme treatment, typically conducted at optimal temperatures and pH levels, maintains the nutritional value of plant proteins while preventing the potentially harmful by-products associated with high-temperature treatments. Enzymatic reactions boast high substrate specificity and can directionally alter the molecular structure of plant proteins, thereby predictably enhancing their functional properties. This precise control is instrumental in the development of dysphagia-specific foods (Raheem et al., 2021). However, this technology is not without its limitations. The cost of enzyme preparations is relatively high, and their application is singular. Moreover, treatment parameters must be strictly optimized, as failure to do so can result in excessive hydrolysis or cross-linking, thereby compromising product quality. Additionally, the effects of enzymatic modification vary depending on the protein source. Differences in the amino acid composition and structure of different plant proteins can lead to unstable modification effects (Mulla et al., 2019).

Fermentation

Effects of fermentation treatment on the structure of plant proteins

Fermentation-based protein modification leverages microbial metabolism and secreted enzyme systems to induce structural and functional transformations in plant proteins (Kaleda et al., 2025). Common fermentative microorganisms include Lactobacillus spp., Saccharomyces spp., Aspergillus spp., and Bacillus spp., each contributing unique enzymatic profiles and metabolic outputs (Fan et al., 2025). The two predominant fermentation techniques are solid-state fermentation (SSF) and (SMF). SSF generally provides higher product concentration and enzyme activity, while SMF offers better process control and scalability. Fermentation not only modifies protein conformation but can also lead to depolymerization or the formation of bioactive peptides, broadening the functional utility of plant proteins in specialized food applications, including nutraceuticals and easy-to-swallow foods.

Effects of fermentation treatment on the key functional properties of plant protein dysphagia-friendly foods

Key process parameters: The functional properties of fermented modified plant proteins are heavily influenced by several key parameters, including strain selection, fermentation temperature, pH value, inoculation amount, fermentation time, and substrate composition. The chosen strain dictates the primary pathway of modification; for instance, lactic acid bacteria primarily enhance nutrition and flavor via acid production and limited proteolysis (Kaleda et al., 2025). Both temperature and pH must align precisely with the optimal growth range for the selected strain, ensuring peak enzyme activity and metabolic efficiency. Meanwhile, the inoculation amount and fermentation time collectively influence the microbial biomass and the extent of protein modification. Insufficient time may lead to suboptimal modification, while an extended duration might result in excessive hydrolysis, potentially leading to bitterness or textural degradation (Clark et al., 2022). For specific functional objectives, strains should be chosen based on their functional orientation.

Key functional characteristics: During fermentation, microbial metabolism generates proteolytic enzymes that hydrolyze peptide bonds, resulting in short-chain peptides and free amino acids, which contribute to improved solubility, digestibility, and bioactivity (Meinlschmidt et al., 2016). Fermentation can improve the solubility of plant proteins, thereby facilitating the formation of a homogenous and stable liquid or semi-liquid matrix. This not only mitigates the risk of choking associated with solid particles but also enhances smoothness and swallowing safety—a crucial consideration in the formulation of dysphagia-friendly foods. For example, fermentation of quinoa and chickpea proteins led to the release of low-molecular-weight peptides, significantly improving solubility and promoting protein absorption (Hurtado-Murillo et al., 2025). Fermentation also enhances water-holding, foaming, and gelling properties, and can reduce undesirable bitter or beany flavors, particularly in soy protein isolates (Meinlschmidt et al., 2016). These characteristics directly impact the texture design of dysphagia food. For instance, a higher water holding capacity can maintain the moisture and thickness of the product, thereby preventing oral residue. Enhanced gel properties can aid in the formation of soft and structurally stable food, making it ideal for processing easy-to-swallow food (Meinlschmidt et al., 2016). Moreover, some strains have the ability to produce extracellular polysaccharides, which inherently function as excellent thickeners and stabilizers, further optimizing viscosity and taste. Thus, fermentation serves as a promising biomodification tool, aligning technological functionality with nutritional and sensory improvements(Kaleda et al., 2025).

Effects of fermentation treatment on nutritional and processing characteristics of plant protein

Fermentation results in improved digestibility, nutritional value, and biological activity of plant proteins. For example, fermentation results in improved digestibility, nutritional value, and biological activity of plant proteins. For example, shiitake mushroom mycelium fermentation positively influenced the sensory profile of pea–rice protein mixtures, leading to improved organoleptic qualities and offering a potential solution for enhancing plant-based protein palatability (Clark et al., 2022). Concurrently, fermentation can effectively degrade anti-nutritional factors such as phytic acid and trypsin inhibitor, thereby minimizing their impact on mineral absorption and protein digestion (Kaleda et al., 2025). Certain microbial strains have the ability to synthesize nutrients like B vitamins, further enriching the nutritional profile of the products. Moreover, the functional peptides produced during fermentation may exhibit antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and other biological activities, providing additional health benefits to plant proteins (Clark et al., 2022).

Fermented plant proteins are increasingly utilized as substitutes for animal proteins, particularly in the development of products that align with clean label trends. Microbial action significantly enhances the solubility of these plant proteins while also promoting the formation of textured, smooth-tasting gel systems that are ideal for sauces and meat substitutes. Importantly, fermentation is effective at masking or eliminating beany and bitter flavors, thereby improving the flavor profile of plant proteins and increasing the acceptability of the final product. This drives innovation and industrial development in the plant-based food sector (Sarathy et al., 2025).

Advantages and limitations of fermentation for plant protein modification

Fermentation is a natural, sustainable, and energy-efficient strategy. This approach is recognized for its cost-effectiveness, low energy consumption, and scalability (Kaleda et al., 2025). Concurrently, microbial metabolism during fermentation can generate organic acids, polysaccharides, and flavoring compounds, thereby bolstering the functional properties of proteins and refining food texture (Hurtado-Murillo et al., 2025). Moreover, fermentation adeptly mitigates beany and bitter flavors, rendering it especially apt for creating products tailored for the elderly or those with specific dietary requirements. Nevertheless, this technology is not devoid of limitations and challenges. Stringent control over the fermentation process is imperative, as inconsistencies can affect the stability of product quality. Additionally, the production cost of industrialization is relatively high, encompassing investments in strain cultivation, fermentation apparatus, and process sterilization, which prolongs the production timeline. Furthermore, fermentation might impart sour or alcoholic undertones, which may not be favorable in all product formulations (Meinlschmidt et al., 2016).

Modified plant proteins have shown great promise in the formulation of foods for individuals with dysphagia. Through appropriate structural modifications, plant proteins can exhibit improved textural, rheological, and organoleptic properties, making them more suitable for ingestion and digestion. Applications of modified plant proteins in gelled dysphagia-friendly foods

Gelled systems are particularly advantageous in dysphagia management due to their soft, cohesive textures and ease of swallowing. Plant protein-based gels can be tailored through various mechanisms—including heat-induced, enzyme-induced, pH-induced, and cold-set gelation—to meet the IDDSI texture requirements (Li, et al., 2025). The choice of protein source, concentration, and processing parameters is critical for designing gels that exhibit softness, elasticity, and cohesiveness suitable for individuals with impaired swallowing (P. Shen, Ma, et al., 2025).

Soy protein isolates (SPI) are among the most extensively studied plant proteins for gel applications. SPI can be used to formulate high-protein custards, jellies, and puddings designed for the elderly. Studies have shown that custards prepared with soy protein exhibit lower elasticity, modulus, and hardness compared to milk protein-based counterparts, yet deliver a softer, more spreadable texture that facilitates swallowing (Qin et al., 2025). Varying the soy protein content (8.9 %, 11.4 %, and 13.9 %) was shown to significantly improve gel strength, viscosity, and stability, with all formulations meeting IDDSI Level 6 requirements as confirmed by the IDDSI Fork Pressure Test. (Qin et al., 2025). In addition, by adjusting the recipe and process, different flavors and textures of vegetable protein pudding can be produced to meet the needs of different consumers. Beyond soy, other modified plant proteins have also demonstrated strong gel-forming potential. For instance, ultrasound-treated pea protein gels exhibited enhanced chewiness, softness, and moisture retention following treatment at 390 W for 20 min (G. Xu et al., 2023). Similarly, adjusting microwave power levels allows fine-tuning of key textural parameters such as hardness, adhesion, cohesiveness, and springiness, further supporting their application in gelatinous dysphagia-friendly formulations (Pure et al., 2021).

Fermentation-modified flaxseed protein, when combined with mung bean starch, has also been used to produce gels with improved viscoelastic moduli, textural integrity, and water-holding capacity. This composite system formed a dense, porous gel network, achieving IDDSI Level 7 classification, which denotes regular easy-to-chew food (Min et al., 2024). Additionally, heating mixtures of soy protein isolate and corn starch yielded weak gel networks characterized by lower hardness, gumminess, and cohesiveness, making them particularly suitable for patients with moderate to severe dysphagia (Pi et al., 2025).

Through judicious application of physical, chemical, and enzymatic modifications, plant protein-based gels offer a promising avenue for the development of high-protein, safe, and palatable easy-to-swallow foods tailored to individuals with dysphagia.

Applications of modified plant proteins in emulsion-based dysphagia-friendly foods

Emulsion-based systems such as milk, fruit juices, and soups represent a core category of liquid foods frequently consumed by individuals with dysphagia. To meet IDDSI-defined texture standards, these products often require thickening and stabilization to ensure safe swallowing and desirable mouthfeel (S. Liu, Qiao, et al., 2023). Modified plant proteins have demonstrated substantial potential for improving the emulsifying stability, viscosity, and rheological behavior of such systems, making them suitable candidates for texture-modified formulations. Plant proteins act as natural emulsifiers due to their amphiphilic structure, stabilizing emulsions through partial wettability at the oil–water interface (Kim et al., 2020). Upon modification, their interfacial activity and film-forming capacity can be significantly enhanced, reducing droplet coalescence, creaming, and phase separation—challenges commonly observed in emulsion-based dysphagia foods.

Moreover, plant protein-based emulsions are gaining attention as a nutritional beverage matrix for elderly individuals. Through protein–protein interactions, modified plant proteins such as pea protein can enhance the apparent viscosity, yield stress, and elastic modulus of beverages, resulting in a more suitable rheological profile for dysphagia diets (Streimikyte et al., 2020). It was shown that hydration of pea protein with milk protein in equal proportions improved its apparent viscosity and rheological properties. And drinks containing a mixture of pea and milk proteins are hydrolysed more quickly than drinks with only milk proteins, making them easier to digest and assimilate (Streimikyte et al., 2020). In one study, blending pea and milk proteins in equal ratios not only improved textural attributes but also accelerated digestive hydrolysis, indicating enhanced bioavailability and ease of assimilation. The integration of protein–polysaccharide complexes has also shown efficacy in optimizing the texture, stability, and mouthfeel of liquid and semi-solid formulations. For instance, modified soy protein emulsions thickened with polysaccharide solutions formed stable interfacial films that resisted oil droplet aggregation and layering, resulting in homogeneous and easy-to-swallow soy milk-type beverages (Y. Dong et al., 2025).

For dysphagia patients requiring high energy density, maintaining suitable flow properties remains a technical challenge. High internal phase emulsions (HIPEs)—and particularly protein-based Pickering emulsions (HIPPEs)—offer a promising solution by balancing energy enrichment with rheological control. Recent research has demonstrated that modified soy protein, corn zein, quinoa protein, and pea protein isolates can stabilize HIPPEs with soft, moist, and creamy textures, suitable for individuals with swallowing difficulties (Funami et al., 2025). The modified soy protein, corn alcohol soluble protein, quinoa protein, and pea isolate showed excellent texture and stability in the Pickering emulsion system with a moist, soft, and creamy texture. These HIPPEs exhibit shear-thinning behavior, self-supporting structure, and printability, making them ideal candidates for 3D-printed dysphagia foods. Such properties are particularly valuable in personalized nutrition, where texture, shape, and nutritional density must be simultaneously optimized. Furthermore, HIPPEs can partially or fully replace trans fats, contributing to both nutritional improvement and formulation functionality in dysphagia-friendly products (Liang et al., 2025).

Applications of 3D printing technology in dysphagia-friendly food products

Adoption drivers of 3D printing for dysphagia diets

Three-dimensional (3D) printing, also known as additive manufacturing, constructs physical objects through the layer-by-layer deposition of materials. In recent years, the integration of 3D printing into the development of easy-to-swallow foods has attracted considerable interest due to its capacity to address key limitations associated with traditional texture-modified food products.

Conventional dysphagia-friendly foods—such as purees and pastes—often suffer from monotonous texture, low sensory appeal, insufficient nutrient density, and a lack of personalization, all of which may lead to reduced patient compliance and compromised nutritional status. Compared to conventional processing methods, 3D printing offers distinct advantages, including the ability to precisely meet International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative (IDDSI) texture requirements, reduce reliance on chemical texture modifiers, and enable spatially controlled nutrient fortification. Furthermore, it allows for customizable design of shape, structure, composition, and texture, thereby enhancing the visual appeal of food and stimulating patient appetite. These features make 3D printing particularly promising for the development of personalized nutrition strategies for dysphagia patients. Advantages of plant proteins as sustainable 3D printing materials

Plant proteins have emerged as ideal candidates for 3D-printed easy-to-swallow food applications due to their favorable nutritional composition, cost-effectiveness, low allergenicity, and environmental sustainability. Recent advances in protein functionalization and structural modification have substantially improved their printability, flow behavior, and safety for swallowing (Sá et al., 2020). When applied as 3D printing inks, modified plant proteins exhibit adequate rheological characteristics, including appropriate viscosity, shear-thinning behavior, and extrusion stability, all of which are essential for maintaining the integrity and precision of printed structures. In addition to offering diverse functional properties such as emulsification, gelation, and water-holding capacity, plant proteins can serve as effective carriers for amino acids, minerals, and bioactive peptides, thereby enabling the design of nutrient-dense and functional foods(Bhuiyan et al., 2025). Their excellent biocompatibility and digestibility further support their application in clinical nutrition. The integration of plant proteins into 3D-printed food systems not only addresses the nutritional needs of individuals with swallowing disorders but also aligns with the broader goals of sustainability and innovation in food manufacturing. Through the rational design of protein-based inks, structurally stable and palatable foods can be created, offering a compelling solution for personalized, high-quality dysphagia management.

Control of product texture by print parameters

Printing parameters exert a profound influence not only on the geometric fidelity of 3D printed foods, but also on their textural attributes and swallowing safety—critical considerations for individuals with dysphagia. Key variables such as nozzle diameter, fill density, print speed, extrusion pressure, layer height, and platform temperature work synergistically to define the physical structure and functional performance of the final product. (Pan et al., 2025).

Nozzle diameter, typically ranging from 0.4 to 2.0 mm, plays a dual role by modulating both the resolution of the extrusion path and the cohesion between deposited layers. Smaller nozzle diameters promote interlayer molecular diffusion, thereby enhancing bonding strength and overall cohesiveness—properties that are essential for minimizing fragmentation during swallowing and reducing the risk of aspiration (Qiu et al., 2024). Print speed also plays a crucial role, as it directly impacts the rate of material deposition, shear forces, and layer adhesion. An excessively high speed may cause filament discontinuity and poor interlayer fusion, while a slow speed may lead to inefficient production and over-deposition (Pan et al., 2025). Therefore, optimal speed must be balanced with extrusion pressure, which governs the consistency and continuity of ink flow and is intimately tied to the rheological behavior of the ink as well as the nozzle diameter. Platform temperature is particularly important when working with thermally responsive or gel-forming protein inks, as it influences the post-deposition solidification kinetics. Low platform temperatures can accelerate the curing of hot-protein inks, thereby supporting structural stability during layer-by-layer deposition. Layer height determines not only the vertical resolution but also the density and internal connectivity of printed structures. Excessive layer height may compromise both dimensional accuracy and mechanical integrity. Furthermore, the internal infill pattern—such as grid, honeycomb, or gradient structures—directly modulates porosity, compressibility, and fracture dynamics, which are essential for ensuring controlled deformation during mastication and safe passage during swallowing (Pérez et al., 2019). The thoughtful optimization of these parameters thus enables the design of printed foods with tunable textural profiles, tailored to specific IDDSI levels.

Modified plant proteins for enhanced 3D printability

The printability and textural performance of plant protein-based inks are inherently governed by their rheological, gelation, and hydration characteristics. High static viscosity is essential for structural integrity pre-extrusion, while pronounced shear-thinning behavior ensures smooth flow through the nozzle under applied stress. A critical yield stress threshold must be met to initiate ink flow, and simultaneously, a sufficiently high yield stress helps maintain the fidelity of stacked layers, preventing sagging or collapse during printing. The viscoelastic balance, particularly the ratio of elastic (G′) to viscous (G″) moduli, influences the extrudability, rapid post-extrusion solidification, and self-support capacity of printed constructs. Moreover, hydration properties—including water-holding capacity and swelling behavior—affect ink consistency, extrusion stability, printed texture, and dehydration shrinkage. Oil binding capacity further modulates texture softness, surface adhesion, and in-mouth dispersion, attributes critical for swallowing ease (B. Xu et al., 2025).

Common plant proteins employed in 3D food printing include soy, pea, and wheat proteins, each differing in their amino acid profile, solubility, gelling potential, and rheological behavior. To enhance their printability and swallowing safety, plant proteins can be tailored through physical (e.g., ultrasound, heat), chemical (e.g., glycosylation), or biological (e.g., enzymatic hydrolysis) modifications, enabling the formulation of highly adaptable ink systems (R. Qiu et al., 2025). When combined with fine-tuned print parameters and supporting ingredients such as polysaccharide thickeners or emulsifiers, these modified proteins yield printable materials with controlled texture, stability, and nutritional content, well-suited for dysphagia applications (D. Recent advances demonstrate that 3D printing technology can precisely regulate food texture parameters such as hardness, cohesiveness, and adhesiveness by adjusting protein type, concentration, printing architecture, and internal porosity. These modifications enable products to meet specific IDDSI standards—for example, Level 4 (pureed/extremely thick) or Level 5 (minced and moist)—while also minimizing post-deglutition residue. The integration of protein inks with functional additives allows for the design of foods with variable textures and structures, offering a spectrum of swallowing suitability (Qiu et al., 2024). For instance, for individuals with limited oral-motor ability, soft and cohesive bite-sized constructs can be printed to support bolus formation and ease of transport. In contrast, individuals with moderate swallowing capacity may benefit from slightly firmer textures that maintain chewability while remaining safe for ingestion (Gallego et al., 2022; Vandenberghe-Descamps et al., 2018). Ultrasonically, thermally, and microwave-modified pea protein has been shown to exhibit improved rheological performance and self-supporting capability when formulated into 3D printable gels blended with fruit powders such as strawberry (B. Xu et al., 2025). It has been shown that physical modification can improve the rheological properties of pea protein-strawberry gels. And the dynamic rheological properties of the gel system were enhanced with increasing protein concentration. This results in the preparation of 3D printed materials with good rheological properties (Liu, Chen, et al., 2023; B. Xu et al., 2025). These modifications enhance dynamic viscoelasticity and enable structural retention post-printing. Notably, ink samples containing 20 % physically modified pea protein were categorized as Level 4 on the IDDSI scale, demonstrating their suitability for dysphagia-friendly diets (B. Xu et al., 2025). The use of thickening agents such as xanthan gum further enhances viscosity and extrusion uniformity, enabling precise control over food firmness and in-mouth disintegration characteristics. For example, xanthan gum is added to pea protein to create a food product suitable for swallowing (Liu, Chen, et al., 2025).

Looking ahead, 3D printing is evolving into 4D printing by incorporating a time-dependent dimension, introducing dynamic responsiveness to printed foods. This emerging technology enables programmed changes in shape, texture, or flavor release over time or under physiological stimuli, paving the way for intelligent easy-to-swallow food systems tailored to individual needs (Aghababaei et al., 2025).

Conclusion and outlook

Modification technologies have demonstrated considerable potential in enhancing the physicochemical and functional properties of plant proteins, including solubility, foaming capacity, emulsification stability, gelation behavior, and digestibility. These improvements effectively address the inherent limitations of native plant proteins and broaden their applicability in food systems, particularly in the development of texture-modified foods designed for individuals with dysphagia. By tailoring the structural and interfacial characteristics of proteins, modified plant protein-based formulations offer safer swallowing properties, as validated through standardized swallowing tests. These foods can be customized to accommodate varying degrees of swallowing impairment, thereby improving nutritional intake, quality of life, and overall eating experience. The integration of 3D printing technology further amplifies the potential of modified plant proteins by enabling precise structural design, nutrient targeting, and aesthetic enhancement. Owing to their tunable rheological and gelation characteristics, modified plant proteins serve as promising bioinks for the fabrication of individualized, easy-to-swallow foods with high nutritional value and sensory appeal. As additive manufacturing continues to advance in the food domain, its synergy with protein engineering offers new avenues for creating functional foods tailored to special dietary requirements.

Nevertheless, several scientific challenges remain to be addressed. First, the relationship between protein conformational transitions and the resulting textural attributes of dysphagia-friendly foods remains poorly understood, particularly due to the variability in protein sources, modification strategies, and food matrices. Second, the regulatory role of protein crystal structures and their interactions with other macromolecular or low-molecular-weight components (e.g., lipids, sugars, salts) in modulating oral-phase transitions and bolus flow properties remains unclear. Although extensive research has focused on the quality transformation of proteins after modification, current understanding remains limited regarding the underlying interaction mechanisms. Techniques such as X-ray diffraction (XRD), ultraviolet spectroscopy, nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), Raman and infrared spectroscopy are commonly employed in conjunction with macroscopic assessments of texture, rheological behavior, and tribological properties. These are often complemented by microscopic characterization tools including confocal laser scanning microscopy and atomic force microscopy to evaluate conformational changes of proteins, or their alterations when interacting with lipids, carbohydrates, and salts. These structural insights are typically used to infer the mechanisms of intermolecular interactions from apparent property changes. However, in-depth exploration of the fundamental mechanisms governing such interactions is still lacking. Although advanced approaches such as molecular docking and artificial intelligence have been increasingly utilized to predict the effects of conformational changes on protein interactions, the molecular basis of dynamic interactions within protein–multicomponent complexes and their structure–function relationships with the macroscopic properties of easy-to-swallow foods remain unclear. Therefore, further development and integration of multidisciplinary research methodologies are urgently needed. These mechanisms are critical for controlling perceived texture and swallowing behavior. Third, there is a lack of robust, objective, and quantitative tools for the classification and personalization of easy-to-swallow foods. Current assessments of swallowing function, such as the fork pressure and spoon tilt tests recommended by the IDDSI framework, are largely subjective and require further technological advancement to enable fine stratification based on individual swallowing capacities.

Looking forward, modified plant proteins are expected to play an increasingly important role in the field of foods for special medical purposes (FSMPs), especially for populations with dysphagia or other functional impairments. Future research should focus on elucidating structure-function relationships at multiple scales, developing advanced analytical tools for real-time assessment of texture and swallowing safety, and optimizing formulation strategies that synergistically combine plant proteins with other functional ingredients. Such efforts will contribute to the design of next-generation dysphagia-friendly foods that are not only nutritionally adequate but also safe, palatable, and emotionally satisfying.

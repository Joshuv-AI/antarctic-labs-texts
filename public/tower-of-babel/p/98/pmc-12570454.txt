From injury to reconstruction, utilizing staged technique in a self-inflicted facial gunshot wound: a case report
Authors: Usman Ali, Kinzah Razzak Ghazi, Kantesh Kumar, Syeda Kanza, Safdar Ali Shaikh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundFacial gunshot injuries are rare and complex, with mortality and complication rates of up to 15% and 30%, respectively. Management of facial gunshot wounds has proven to be challenging for surgeons because the treatment requires multidisciplinary evaluation and planning. We present a rare case of a gunshot wound to the face that resulted in extensive deformities, requiring multiple reconstructive procedures.Case presentationA 19-year-old male of South Asian (Pakistani) origin presented to the emergency department with a 3-day-old gunshot wound to the floor of the mouth, causing extensive defects in the maxilla, mandible, and soft tissue, as well as comminuted fractures of the bilateral maxillary and ethmoid sinuses. Reconstruction involved an osseocutaneous free fibular flap for the mandible, radial forearm flap for the upper lip, and iliac crest bone graft for the maxillary alveolus. Nasal framework reconstruction was achieved by using a costochondral rib graft. A secondary radial forearm free flap, staged as an Abbe flap, was performed a year later. A complex surgical approach was executed, resulting in functional and aesthetic restoration.ConclusionThis case highlights the intricate nature of managing facial gunshot injuries, which requires a multidisciplinary approach and meticulous planning. Through a series of staged reconstructive procedures, including the use of free flaps and bone grafts, we achieved significant functional and aesthetic restoration. Our experience demonstrates the value of tailored reconstructive techniques in managing complex maxillofacial trauma, offering valuable guidance for surgeons in handling similar cases. By sharing this approach, we aim to contribute to the advancement of surgical care for patients with severe facial trauma, ultimately improving the outcomes, particularly in resource-constrained environments.

Introduction

Facial gunshot injuries represent some of the most challenging cases in reconstructive surgery, demanding a carefully orchestrated multidisciplinary approach [1]. The severity and complexity of these injuries vary significantly on the basis of multiple factors, including weapon type, projectile velocity, and firing distance. High-velocity injuries, particularly from close range, often result in devastating functional and aesthetic complications [2]. Current literature reports mortality rates of up to 15% and complication rates of around 30%, emphasizing the critical nature of these injuries [3–5].

The initial management of facial gunshot wounds focuses on the fundamental principles of trauma surgery: securing the airway, controlling hemorrhage, and achieving hemodynamic stability [4]. However, considerable debate exists regarding the timing of reconstruction. While some studies advocate for early aggressive reconstruction to facilitate tissue mobilization and reduce fibrosis, others support a more conservative approach with delayed secondary reconstruction. This controversy highlights the need for individualized treatment planning on the basis of injury patterns, available resources, and patient factors [2].

Management of facial gunshot wounds has been proven to be challenging for surgeons, as the treatment requires a multidisciplinary evaluation and planning. Furthermore, management of facial gunshot wounds should address both functional and aesthetic concerns. Hence, the reconstruction of these injuries requires careful consideration of various factors, including tissue quality, blood supply, and the psychological impact on the patient. In this report, we present a unique case of a self-inflicted facial gunshot wound that required a comprehensive, staged approach to reconstruction, demonstrating the effectiveness of combining various reconstructive techniques to achieve optimal outcomes. While extensive literature exists on facial gunshot wound reconstructions, detailed accounts of successful, staged reconstructions in resource-constrained environments are scarce. This case exemplifies how meticulous planning and multidisciplinary approach can overcome significant limitations, offering valuable insights for similar contexts.

Case presentation

A 19-year-old male of South Asian (Pakistani) origin with a documented history of previous suicide attempts presented to the emergency department with a 3-day-old self-inflicted gunshot wound to the floor of the mouth. On examination, he was alert and oriented. The patient had already undergone emergency tracheostomy placement at a primary care facility before transfer. Physical examination revealed extensive facial deformity characterized by complete loss of the nasal framework, severe distortion of the lower face, and multiple facial fractures. Detailed examination showed substantial tissue loss affecting the nose, upper and lower lips, and oral commissures. Computed tomography (CT) imaging with three-dimensional reconstruction demonstrated extensive damage, including comminuted fractures of the bilateral maxillary and ethmoid sinuses, complete loss of the nasal framework, fracture of the right pterygoid plate, and significant mandibular fragmentation. Laboratory studies showed hemoglobin level of 11.2 g/dL, a normal coagulation profile, and no evidence of infection.

Surgical management and reconstruction

The reconstructive strategy was meticulously planned in multiple stages to optimize outcomes while maintaining patient safety. Each stage was carefully timed to allow adequate healing and maintain optimal tissue conditions for subsequent procedures. The first intervention focused on stabilization and debridement. Under general anesthesia, thorough wound debridement was performed, removing all necrotic tissue from the nose and upper lip regions (Fig. 1). To ensure adequate nutrition during the recovery period, a gastrostomy tube was placed. The patient’s immediate postoperative course was unremarkable, with stable vital signs and no evidence of bleeding or infection. Mandibular reconstruction was undertaken one week after initial debridement using an osseocutaneous free fibular flap. The flap was harvested with dimensions of 12 cm × 8 cm, including a skin paddle of 6 cm × 4 cm. The fibula was osteotomized to match the mandibular contour. Microvascular anastomosis was performed between the peroneal vessels and the right superior thyroid artery and external jugular vein using 9–0 nylon sutures. The total ischemia time was 65 minutes. In September 2018, one month after the initial mandible reconstruction, a second major reconstructive procedure involved a free radial forearm flap (dimensions: 8 cm × 6 cm) for upper lip reconstruction, combined with an iliac crest bone graft (4 cm × 2 cm) for maxillary alveolar reconstruction. The flap was revascularized using the left superior thyroid artery and common facial vein, as well as the external jugular vein. The bone graft was secured using titanium miniplates (Fig. 2). Subsequent follow-up visits were conducted on a weekly basis.

A staged approach was adopted for the third and final set of major reconstructive procedures, focusing on the reconstruction of the nasal deformity. In November 2018, nasal reconstruction began with placement of a 100-mL tissue expander beneath the right forehead skin. Concurrently, a port was inserted in the left temporal region. Over six weeks, serial expansions were performed until adequate tissue redundancy was achieved. Subsequently, two weeks after achieving sufficient skin expansion, the patient was scheduled for nasal reconstruction. The reconstruction utilized a costochondral rib graft for the nasal framework and a pre-expanded forehead flap for external coverage. Following the completion of all facial reconstructive procedures, the patient’s tracheostomy and gastrostomy tubes were removed. The patient was discharged, allowing him to return to his hometown, which was located a considerable distance from the treatment center. Despite the geographical challenge, the patient diligently adhered to his follow-up appointments, visiting once every two months. During the follow-up visits, after one year, it was observed that the patient was experiencing difficulties with mouth opening and challenges with oral intake. A final revision surgery was planned to address this problem, which involved lip release and reconstruction using a second radial forearm free flap in a staged Abbe flap configuration. The flap was vascularized using the vessels from the left side of the neck, which had been previously utilized (Fig. 4). The flap was divided after four weeks, resulting in significant improvement in oral function. Since this final procedure, the patient has expressed satisfaction with his results and has not required further treatment. Notably, he has returned to work and successfully reintegrated into his professional and social life. Discussion

Facial gunshot wounds represent one of the most challenging scenarios in reconstructive surgery owing to their complex functional and aesthetic consequences [4]. These injuries often involve severe soft tissue and skeletal destruction, high contamination risks, and cause significant long-term morbidity. Studies have reported mortality rates of up to 15% and complication rates ranging from 30–50%, highlighting the high-risk nature of such injuries [3, 4]. Our case highlights the successful staged reconstruction of a severe self-inflicted facial gunshot injury, demonstrating the principles of airway stabilization, tissue preservation, and sequential reconstruction in achieving optimal functional and aesthetic outcomes, even in a resource-limited setting such as Pakistan.

The management of ballistic injuries to the face requires careful consideration of both injury characteristics and available resources. Rokas Gelažius explained that the outcomes of facial ballistic injuries are influenced by multiple factors, including the kinetic energy of the projectile, the distance from which the weapon was fired, and the viscoelastic properties of the affected tissues [4]. High-velocity, close-range gunshots tend to cause significant tissue cavitation and destruction, leading to severe structural deformities, as seen in this case. Airway compromise is a primary concern in such injuries due to hemorrhage, edema, and soft tissue disruption. CT is considered the gold standard for evaluating complex facial gunshot injuries, as it helps to determine the severity and extent of damage and also identifies any associated injuries [6]. Plain X-rays alone are generally inadequate for comprehensive assessment. While oral endotracheal intubation is often preferred, extensive bleeding or anatomical distortion may necessitate a surgical airway, such as tracheostomy, to ensure airway patency. Vatsyayan et al. observed that in cases with mandibular trauma, nasotracheal intubation was successfully employed, avoiding the need for tracheostomy [7]. However, in this patient, the pre-existing tracheostomy was a critical factor in maintaining respiratory stability, avoiding the need for further airway interventions.

Other important factors are early wound debridement and soft tissue management, which play a crucial role in reducing infection rates and preventing excessive fibrosis. Studies have demonstrated that prompt surgical intervention, including debridement and primary closure when feasible, leads to better long-term outcomes compared to delayed interventions. In our case, necrotic tissue in the nasal and upper lip regions was excised, and primary closure was performed where possible. While some surgeons advocate for delayed reconstruction to allow wound stabilization, early intervention has been associated with improved soft tissue mobilization, reduced scarring, and shorter periods of deformity [7].

Extensive ballistic facial injuries, such as the one seen in our case, typically follow a staged approach, beginning with skeletal reconstruction (to restore structural integrity), followed by soft tissue coverage and final aesthetic refinements where applicable. The mandible, being a key component of facial structure and function, required immediate attention. The use of a free fibular osteocutaneous flap, widely regarded as the gold standard for mandibular reconstruction, was an appropriate choice, given its robust vascularity, sufficient bone stock, and compatibility with future prosthetic rehabilitation [8, 9].

Damage to the upper lip and maxilla presented additional challenges in this case. Reconstruction required a combination of soft tissue and bone grafts. The free radial forearm flap was selected for upper lip reconstruction owing to ease of adaptation to facial contours. This flap has been extensively used in maxillofacial reconstruction and is particularly effective in preserving oral competence and mobility. The iliac crest bone graft, a nonvascularized graft, was chosen for reconstruction to restore bony defects in the maxilla owing to its dimensional similarity and ease of harvest [8].

Nasal reconstruction in ballistic injuries is one of the most challenging aspects of facial repair, requiring both skeletal framework restoration and soft tissue coverage. The use of a pre-expanded forehead flap combined with a costochondral rib graft is a well-established technique in nasal reconstruction, providing both structural durability and aesthetically compatible soft tissue coverage [10, 11]. In this case, a tissue expander was placed in the forehead to increase skin availability, ensuring sufficient coverage during the final stage of reconstruction. The costochondral rib graft was used to recreate the nasal framework, offering the necessary rigidity to support nasal contour and function. This approach is consistent with the works of Correa et al. and Wei et al., who highlight the forehead flap as a superior option for total nasal reconstruction owing to its optimal tissue characteristics [10, 11].

Despite the success of the primary reconstructive procedures, post-surgical complications were low in this case, contrary to previous studies from the developed and developing world [12, 13]. In our case, post-surgical contractures led to functional impairments, particularly with oral opening and feeding. Secondary interventions are often required to address such complications, as seen in this patient, who underwent a final lip release procedure using a staged Abbe flap technique with a radial forearm free flap. This method effectively restored oral function while maintaining facial symmetry, which is consistent with findings by Khan et al., demonstrating the importance of ongoing surgical refinements in cases of severe facial trauma [14].

Conclusion

This case highlights several key lessons in the management of complex facial reconstruction following gunshot injuries. Early and aggressive intervention, including airway stabilization, debridement, and staged reconstruction, plays a critical role in optimizing outcomes. The use of vascularized flaps and bone grafts in reconstructive planning enhances long-term durability and functionality, while a multidisciplinary approach involving maxillofacial, plastic, and reconstructive surgeons ensures comprehensive patient care. In addition, patient compliance with follow-up and rehabilitation is essential for sustained success, as seen in this case, where the patient’s adherence to postoperative care contributed to a positive functional and aesthetic outcomes. By highlighting this case, the authors emphasize that through careful perioperative planning and a multidisciplinary approach, such complex reconstructions can be successfully accomplished in resource-limited settings.

Safety and efficacy of pharmacological interventions for hepatic outcomes of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease: protocol for systematic review and network meta-analysis
Authors: Kiavash Semnani, Farbod Semnani, Seyed Sahab Aarabi, Shirin Esmaeili, Ehsaneh Taheri, Armita Mahdavi-Gorabi, Mohsen Rajabnia, Shirin Djalalinia, Saba Goodarzi, Amir Kasaeian, Mohammad Shadab Siddiqui, Mostafa Qorbani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMetabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality due to chronic liver disease. There is an extensive body of evidence focusing on pharmacotherapy for MASLD. Reviews on the topic have been largely limited to the efficacy of select agents, subgroups or outcomes. The current is a protocol for a comprehensive systematic review and network meta-analysis (NMA) evaluating the efficacy of examined pharmaceutical interventions in improving hepatic outcomes of MASLD.Methods and analysisMEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, the Cochrane Library database and multiple trial registries will be searched for clinical trials on MASLD pharmacotherapy. Histological, radiological and paraclinical outcomes will be considered along with safety and tolerability. Screening and data extraction will be conducted by pairs of independent reviewers. Risk of bias (RoB) will be assessed using the Cochrane RoB 2 tool. Pairwise random-effects meta-analyses will be conducted followed by random-effects frequentist NMAs—according to the length of intervention—for each outcome in clinically distinct MASLD subgroups. Other effect moderators will be examined in subgroup analyses and meta-regression. Certainty of evidence will be assessed using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation and Confidence in Network Meta-Analysis approaches.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval was waived (Alborz University of Medical Sciences; approval ID: IR.ABZUMS.REC.1404.121) as no new data will be generated. Information from published records will be used in compliance with their Copyright agreements. Results will be submitted for peer review and publication in a scientific journal.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251125615.

Background

Area of research

Metabolic dysfunction-associated liver disease (MASLD)—previously non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)—is one of the leading aetiologies for chronic liver disease, cirrhosis and adult liver transplantations.1 2 Close to a third of the world’s population are currently affected by MASLD—a prevalence estimated to rise beyond half the world’s population by 2040.3 4 Of these, 7%–30% are expected to progress to metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), which, in turn, may lead to cirrhosis in 10–15% of patients.5 6

Current standards of care

In 2024, the United States Food and Drug Administration approved Resmetirom (a thyroid hormone receptor analogue) as the first pharmaceutical agent to be used for treatment of MASH, while The American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases has recognised the treatment potential of several other agents, including Glucagon-like peptidle (GLP)-1 receptor agonists, pioglitazone and vitamin E.7 In contrast, adoption of a healthy lifestyle and weight loss have been longstanding recommendations for MASLD patients.8 The prolonged unsatisfied demand for safe and effective pharmacological treatment options for MASLD has led to a vast and ever-growing body of clinical trials exploring such interventions.8 Pharmaceuticals examined are aimed at multiple divergent targets, including control of comorbid metabolic dysfunctions, anti-inflammatory and antifibrotic action.9 10

Clinical outcomes of concern

While histological examination remains the preferred strategy for diagnosis and evaluation of intervention response in MASLD, several non-invasive approaches to quantifying hepatic steatosis and fibrosis have been adopted; of note, magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS), MRI proton density fat fraction (MRI-PDFF), magnetic resonance elastography (MRE) and transient elastography (TE) have proven to be reliable surrogates for hepatic histology.7 11 Several biomarkers and indices of disease activity have also demonstrated predictive value for hepatic outcomes.12

Necessity of a comprehensive review

To date, multiple reviews have attempted summarising and synthesising the available evidence on different pharmaceutical interventions for MASLD.1319 However, the diversity of agents, variations in trial design, and patient characteristics have prevented a comprehensive review of treatment modalities and the certainty of evidence supporting their effect on hepatic outcomes. Most of the conducted reviews have resorted to stringent inclusion criteria facilitating limited quantitative synthesis. Application of such criteria regarding the diagnostic methodology,17 19 severity of disease,17 18 and duration of intervention13 17 18 results in the exclusion of a majority of the relevant literature. Reviewers have also frequently limited their target literature by focusing on a narrow subset of outcomes,1415 1719 ignoring the importance of a holistic view on treatment safety and efficacy. Furthermore, almost all of the reviews lack detailed literature searches,20 or explicitly focus on a narrow range of pharmaceuticals perceived to be of higher clinical ‘potential’15—defeating the purpose of a systematic review. The complexities associated with navigating a heterogeneous pool of trials further compounds the limitations of these reviews.20 21 Reviews are also sparse regarding non-drug effects on intended outcomes. Such effects are expected due to the heterogeneity noted in trial (eg, comparison of experimental vs off-label trials) and patient characteristics (eg, age, obesity and diabetes rates and cirrhosis). Altogether, despite the numerosity of previous published reviews on the topic, none address the palpable need for a comprehensive contrast of pharmacological interventions for MASLD.

Review objectives

We aim to provide a comprehensive and well-delineated appraisal of the current evidence provided by clinical trials evaluating MASLD pharmacological interventions, compared with lack of interventions, placebo or standard of care. This will include a narrative outline of the evidence along with quantitative comparisons of efficacy for key hepatic outcomes (histological, radiological, etc). A broad range of pharmaceutical groups targeting MASLD, including experimental and off-label therapy will be included to evaluate interventions’ clinical and research potential. The safety and tolerability of each intervention will also be examined to inform clinical expectations.

Methods

Patient and public involvement

Although there is no planned direct involvement of patients in conducting the review, the choice of outcomes was largely inspired by a multi-stakeholder consensus including patient perspectives.22

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Population

All studies on subjects older than 18 years of age with MASLD, MASH or equivalent diagnoses of steatotic liver disease—without additional non-metabolic comorbidities—will be eligible for review.

Current diagnostic criteria for MASLD require documentation of hepatic steatosis on imaging or biopsy, presence of cardiometabolic criteria, and the exclusion of alternative causes for steatosis.1 However, a high degree of overlap between diagnoses based on current MASLD diagnostic criteria and NAFLD has been documented23; with this, and bearing in mind that the majority of available evidence predates updates in MASLD nomenclature, a lack of reporting of cardiometabolic criteria will not result in study exclusion from this review. Trials including patients diagnosed by other consensus clinical criteria will also be considered. To prevent an undue effect of variations in diagnostic criteria, data regarding each trial’s diagnostic approach will be extracted and considered in the analysis.

Studies may have focused on a narrow subset of patients with MASLD, for example, advanced hepatic fibrosis or cirrhosis. These studies will not be excluded. Measures will be taken to address the influence of these factors on the performed analyses. We also expect studies on populations with high or universal prevalence of specific comorbidities (eg, diabetes mellitus). As such, inclusion criteria focusing on metabolic comorbidities (type 2 diabetes, essential hypertension, dyslipidaemia and obesity) will not cause study exclusion. Studies enrolling patients with non-metabolic comorbidities or non-MASLD (or equivalent) aetiologies for chronic liver disease will be excluded from the review. In vivo studies, animal studies, and studies on children and adolescents will be excluded.

Interventions

Effects of an extensive range of pharmaceuticals will be reviewed. The following is a non-exhaustive list of agent groups considered: GLP-1 receptor agonists, sodium/glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors, peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR) agonists, farnesoid X receptor (FXR) agonists and thyroid hormone receptor-beta (THR-β) agonists as well as multiple other antihyperglycaemic, antihyperlipidaemic and antifibrotic agents. Further description of considered pharmaceuticals is provided in the supplementary materials (online supplemental file 1). Combinations of these agents will also be included. Combinations of pharmaceutical and non-pharmaceutical interventions (except standard of care) will not be considered.

Comparison

Trials without a comparison group will be excluded. Comparison groups should be selected from the same population as the intervention group. Pharmaceuticals should be compared with ‘no intervention’, ‘standard of care’, placebo, or other active pharmaceuticals. The definition of standard of care is expected to vary considering the chronological span of included studies. Treatment of comorbidities, dietary adjustments, physical exercise, lifestyle counselling, and vitamin E supplementation will be considered as reasonable definitions.

Study design

Randomised and non-randomised controlled trials will be included for qualitative reporting. However, meta-analyses will only include randomised controlled trials. Non-randomised trials and add-on interventions will not be included in meta-analyses. Data from these trials will only be used to enrich the discussion on relevant agents’ potential efficacy when they provide additional or higher quality information compared with randomised clinical trials (RCTs)—particularly, when RCTs reporting critical patient outcomes for a specific agent in the target clinical subgroup are not available.30 This would preclude complete exclusion of available evidence for certain agents from efficacy discussions. Data only from the initial phase of cross-over trials will be eligible for quantitative synthesis. Trials without an eligible comparator arm and observational studies will be excluded.

Protocol registration

The current protocol is reported in compliance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-analyses Protocols (checklist available in the online supplemental file 2).31 The protocol has been prospectively registered in The International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO, CRD420251125615). Any future updates will be notified via the same platform.

Search strategy

MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science and the Cochrane Library databases will be used as the main resources for review. Searches will include all publications indexed in the target databases from inception to a date preceding formal analysis by at least 3 months. Additionally, multiple clinical trial registries (via ClinicalTrials.gov and the International Clinical Trials Registry Platform), and reference lists of systematic review articles with a similar scope will be hand searched. References of all included articles as well as their citing articles will also be evaluated for eligible publications.

A preliminary search of MEDLINE (via PubMed) was conducted using Medical Subject Heading (MeSH) terms with the aim of identifying pharmaceutical agents and groups targeted at MASLD treatment. Consequently, search queries for each database were adjusted to include search terms related to MASLD (eg, NAFLD, MASH, NASH, etc), general terms for intervention (eg, trial, pharmacotherapy, drug, etc) and terms specific to target pharmaceutical agents and groups. This strategy increases the sensitivity of the conducted search, particularly for early phase trials of relevant pharmaceuticals. The preliminary search on PubMed, and provisionary search queries adjusted to target pharmaceuticals is provided in supplementary materials (online supplemental table S1). Final searches will include all eligible reports regardless of language. Records other than those available in English, French, German, Persian, Spanish and Turkish (translated by coauthors) will be translated using online translators.

Screening and selection

Full-text articles identified via the search protocol will be considered for relevance. Records will be managed using Endnote 2025. Abstracts, poster presentations, and conference reports will not be evaluated. Records yet to be peer-reviewed and published (preprints and in-press articles), and results posted to clinical trial registers prior to publication will be included. Each article will be independently assessed for relevance by a pair of researchers. The researchers will later discuss disagreements until consensus. When consensus is unattainable, a third independent researcher will be involved in the decision-making. The same will be conducted for examination of full-texts for eligibility. A list of articles excluded following full-text review will be reported along with reasons for exclusion. Secondary reports of trial data will only be excluded after full-text screening, identification, and inclusion of the original trial reports via citation chasing.

Data extraction

Pairs of reviewers will independently extract relevant data from articles deemed eligible after screening. Conflicts will be resolved by a research supervisor. The primary source for data extraction includes published literature and online supplementary materials. Considering the breadth of the review and time constraints, authors will only be contacted for further information if measures of primary outcomes cannot be obtained from published materials. Author contact will include an initial request followed by a one-time 7-day reminder delivered via email to the corresponding authors. Extracted data will be stored in Excel spreadsheets designated for study information and measures of outcome. Table 1 presents data points to be extracted for review.

Target measures of effect include relative risk (RR) with 95% CI for dichotomous outcomes and mean differences (MD) and standardised MD (SMD, Hedges’ g) for continuous outcomes. These will be extracted directly; calculated; or estimated via conversion of available data points, when applicable.32 Data provided solely in visualisations will be extracted using a computer vision assisted software. Independent extraction of data from figures minimises the concern for inaccuracy in such cases. Summary outcome measures will be preferentially extracted from reports of intention-to-treat (ITT) or modified ITT (mITT) analyses. When possible, data from per-protocol (PP) reports will be used to estimate results of ITT analysis.33 For dichotomous outcomes, RRs obtained from ITT or mITT—assuming treatment failure in participants without paired evaluations—and available case analysis—where the outcome is evaluated in all participants with paired evaluation results—will be contrasted. Similarly, estimates of unadjusted outcome measures will be used where only adjusted effect sizes are made available. Availability of additional data in unpublished or secondary reports will be examined in case of insufficient reporting in the original record.

Risk of bias assessment

Risk of bias (RoB) for RCTs will be assessed by pairs of independent reviewers using the Cochrane RoB tool 2 (RoB 2). Use of this tool will entail consideration for five domains: randomisation, deviations from intervention, missing outcome data, measurements of outcomes and selection of reported outcomes.34 The Cochrane Risk Of Bias In Non-randomized Studies—of Interventions (ROBINS-I) tool will be used for non-randomised or quasi-randomised trials. This tool evaluates RoB in the following domains: confounding, classification of interventions, selection, deviations from intervention, missing outcome data, measurements of outcomes and selection of reported outcomes.35 Outcome measures extracted from figures or otherwise imputed will also be considered as a source of possible bias. Studies at high RoB will be excluded in sensitivity analyses.

Quantitative synthesis

Effect measures

Effect measures to be reported and analysed include RRs for dichotomous outcomes, and SMDs or MDs for continuous outcomes. We chose to report and analyse both SMDs and MDs to employ each measure’s strength. SMDs will provide more generalisability and analytical power for combining efficacy results obtained via different methodologies, while MDs are more readily interpretable and robust when focusing on single measures of efficacy.36 37 SMDs will be used to gain an overall estimate of treatment efficacy for improvement of hepatic steatosis and fibrosis (primary outcomes). MDs will be used in the analysis and reporting of treatment efficacy based on each measure (primary and secondary outcomes). Studies will be stratified by length of intervention: up to 3 months, 3–6 months, 6–12 months and longer than 1 year. Data will be extracted for the longest available follow-up prior to the discontinuation of intervention.

Pairwise comparisons

Random-effects meta-analyses (using the REstricted Maximum Likelihood heterogeneity estimator) will be conducted to synthesise and compare effect sizes for pairings of interventions. The choice of model follows an assumption of variation in true treatment effects across different populations and study designs. The heterogeneity estimator was chosen due to an expectation of high heterogeneity and small number of studies for select pairings and outcomes.38 39 Statistical heterogeneity will be quantified using the I2 statistic (along with its 95% CI) and—when suitable—prediction intervals.40

Node-making and network design

A schematic of the network of interventions examined for efficacy in improvement of NAS score, hepatic steatosis and fibrosis will be presented. The geometry of the network will be described using the details of nodes, edges, studies per edge, network density, portion of common comparators and median studies per edge.41 Contributions from each comparison to the networks will be explored using a contribution matrix.42 Treatment effects in clinical trials of MASLD have been shown to diverge according to the length of intervention, comorbidities, and the presence of cirrhosis at baseline.8 Furthermore, considering their direct role in clinical decision-making and interpretation of target outcome measures (eg, specific outcomes of cirrhosis; divergent trajectories of paraclinical tests in cirrhotic patients; etc), separate networks will be constructed based on these factors. To address the effect of disease severity on treatment outcome, patients with MASLD without steatohepatitis, biopsy-confirmed MASH, and MASH with advanced fibrosis (F2–F3) will also be included in separate networks. If necessary, separate analyses will be conducted for disconnected networks of interventions.

Pharmaceuticals with a shared mechanism of action may be combined (online supplemental materials; possible classification of agents by mechanism of action) given sufficient evidence of consistent treatment effect obtained in paired meta-analyses.25 This allows for a more practical interpretation of the results considering the multitude of tried agents (figure 2A,B). For example, the effect of GLP-1 agonists, SGLT-2 inhibitors, etc, may be quantified as a ‘group node’ in the network. Combination treatments will be considered as a separate node—given sufficient evidence for the additivity of agent effects. In dose-ranging trials, only the therapeutic doses of pharmaceuticals will be included for quantitative analysis.42 Non-pharmaceutical comparators will similarly be combined or considered independently according to their efficacy profiles. ‘Placebo’ interventions may be subdivided by route of administration or combined with the no intervention group when the study’s chosen placebo implies an attenuated effect. Lifestyle interventions may be considered as separate nodes, a group of interventions, or combined with the ‘no intervention group’ in quantitative synthesis. This decision will follow an evaluation of the details of these interventions’ implementation and the observed magnitude of effect in each study group. Vitamin E will be considered an independent node.

Evaluation of transitivity

Transitivity, or the assumption of joint randomisability, is an oft-neglected prerequisite to NMA.43 The described design of networks contributes to the fulfilment of this condition. The distribution of several possible effect moderators pertinent to study, participant and intervention characteristics (table 1) will also be examined to ensure the veracity of this assumption prior to NMA. Statistical evidence of incoherence between direct and indirect comparisons, although imperfect, will also be used as a surrogate for possible intransitivity.44 45 Global coherence will be assessed,46 and local incoherence addressed via node-splitting.47

Network meta-analyses

NMAs will be carried out after sufficient verification of transitivity for each network. Frequentist NMAs will be conducted to synthesise direct and indirect effect sizes of treatments in a network. Due to limitations in statistical software, a shared τ2 parameter for heterogeneity will be considered for each network.24 Treatments will be ranked for improvements in each of the examined outcomes using p scores; higher p scores (range: 0–1) favour the interpretation of a higher probability for an intervention to emerge as the most efficacious option.48 Point estimates for response will also be compared against statistical significance and proposed thresholds for clinical significance, when possible.28 29 Treatment efficacy for fibrosis and steatosis and efficacy and safety will be plotted against each other. NMA results will also be presented in league tables and illustrated via Vitruvian plots (figure 3).49

Subgroup analyses and meta-regression

Subgroup analyses and meta-regression will be carried out to explore possible sources of heterogeneity and the effects of several study-specific characteristics on overall interpretation of results for NAS score, hepatic steatosis and fibrosis (primary NMAs) in each network of interventions. The following is a list of factors designated for analyses a priori: year of publication; multicentre versus single-centre studies; diagnostic criteria for MASLD; presence of additional inclusion criteria; treatment dose and route of delivery; duration of intervention; placebo response; age; sex and baseline NAS score.25 39 40 We will also evaluate and rank clinically available treatments and experimental agents in separate sub-networks.

Sensitivity analyses

The robustness of primary NMAs’ results will be examined in exclusion of possible sources of bias: pilot studies and phase I or II RCTs; studies with a high RoB; non-English manuscripts; trials with only placebo as the comparator; clinical diagnosis of MASLD without histological or radiological evidence of hepatic steatosis; industry-funded trials; and effect measures obtained through imputation.25 39 40

Publication bias

Publication bias will be examined via Egger and Harbord’s tests given a sufficient number of studies (>10) included in a meta-analysis for continuous and dichotomous outcomes, respectively. Comparison-adjusted funnel plot asymmetry and Egger’s test results will be evaluated as possible indicators of small-study effects.50 A selection model will be employed to obtain adjusted effects when publication bias is suspected.51

Certainty of evidence

Both the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation52 and Confidence in Network Meta-Analysis (CINeMA)53 approaches will be used to assess the quality of evidence in primary NMAs. This choice was made in light of recent evidence suggesting a possible divergence in the results yielded from these approaches.54

Software and implementation

Statistical analyses will be performed using the mvmeta feature of Stata (V.18.0)55 and the netmeta package for R (V.4.5.0).56 57

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval was waived considering the absence of newly generated data (Alborz University of Medical Sciences; approval ID: IR.ABZUMS.REC.1404.121). The review pilot has been completed in February 2025. Preliminary literature searches have also been conducted from February to June 2025. Formal screening was performed from June to September 2025. Data extraction will be completed by March 2026. Completion of analyses and preparation of the final report are expected by summer of 2026. Thereafter, the generated report will be submitted for peer-review and publication.

Discussion

The immense impact of MASLD combined with an enduring need for safe and effective treatment options has led to the development of a considerable body of evidence. Great overlap also exists between treatments of associated metabolic dysfunctions and possible effective interventions for control of MASLD.58 This has further broadened the range of plausible candidates for pharmaceutical interventions. With this, only few interventions have been recognised as clinically efficacious; although, more recently, an array of experimental drugs have demonstrated promising results regarding the control and reversal of MASLD.7 58

Available syntheses of evidence suffer several limitations. These include a focus on the evaluation of a limited number of conventional or experimental interventions.15 Alternatively, some reviews have examined trials under stringent requirements for study protocol—with the aim of attaining homogeneity—excluding a major portion of the available evidence.131719 Others focus solely on a narrow set of outcomes.1415 1719 These reviews also fail to provide a comprehensive assessment of different agents’ efficacy in clinically distinct subgroups or the temporal development of treatment efficacy.28 58 The noted limitations have led to a multitude of reviews failing to present a holistic clinical or research perspective on pharmacotherapy for MASLD. Supporting this assertion, our search and screening process has identified an excess of 300 relevant trials on MASLD pharmacological therapy—of which, less than 50 have been included and discussed in each of the previous reviews.1319

The proposed review aims to address these limitations affecting clinical decision-making, choice of future research candidates, and development of pharmaceutical interventions by providing a comprehensive appraisal of the evidence from clinical trials. We will contrast the efficacy of experimental and commercially available drugs for improvement in a range of histological and radiological outcomes, along with other non-invasive paraclinical surrogates of hepatic outcome. This is to be accompanied by consideration for possible effect modifiers via quantitative synthesis (NMAs) guided by trial and population characteristics. We will aim not to simplify by exclusion, but rather stratify the existing evidence. This includes an independent focus on clinical decision-making versus research priorities; different stages of disease progression (MASLD vs MASH vs MASH with advanced fibrosis vs cirrhosis); and accompanying metabolic disorders (diabetes). The effect of these factors will be addressed via flexible node-making; division of networks; and subgroup analyses and meta-regression. Additionally, the safety profile of interventions will be examined to assist in constructing a balanced perspective.

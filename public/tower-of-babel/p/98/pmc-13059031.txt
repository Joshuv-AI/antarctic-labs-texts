Influence of MTHFD1L variants on the susceptibility to hypertension in the Chinese Han population
Authors: Quanni Li, Luyao Wang, Xiaobo Li, Pingdong Xie, Yunjun Zhang, Xiaoli Zhou, Yipeng Ding, Shan Tong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMTHFD1L gene encoded a mitochondrial enzyme that plays a crucial role in the production of tetrahydrofolate within the mitochondria. However, research on the association between MTHFD1L gene variations and hypertension risk is limited.MethodsThis study included a total of 723 participants, consisting of 367 healthy individuals and 356 individuals diagnosed with hypertension. Four single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) within MTHFD1L (rs803419, rs6922269, rs1555179 and rs11155772) were selected and genotyped by Agena MassARRAY platform. Logistic regression analysis was employed to determine the odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI), assessing the potential association between these MTHFD1L SNPs and the predisposition to hypertension. Furthermore, the multifactor dimensionality reduction (MDR) method was utilized to analyze the combined effects of MTHFD1L SNP interactions on hypertension risk.ResultsMTHFD1L rs6922269 (p = 9.37 × 10−6, OR = 3.15) and rs1555179 (p = 7.14 × 10−7, OR = 0.59) were associated with hypertension risk. Interestingly, rs6922269 was more significantly related to an increased hypertension risk in females, whereas rs1555179 was more significantly associated with a reduced risk in males. Moreover, rs803419 might contribute to a reduced susceptibility to hypertension in females. Notably, rs1555179 was the most informative single-locus model for hypertension risk with an information gain (IG) of 2.44%.ConclusionOur findings indicated that MTHFD1L rs6922269 and rs1555179 were associated with hypertension risk among the Chinese Han population. These results contribute to a deeper understanding about the role of MTHFD1L gene in hypertension occurrence.Keywords: Hypertension, MTHFD1L, genetic variants, susceptibility, SNP-SNP

Introduction

Hypertension is a major risk factor contributing to cardiovascular disease, with a growing global incidence [1]. According to the Global Burden of Disease Study 2019, elevated systolic blood pressure (SBP) accounted for 10.8 million deaths marking it as the leading cause of mortality [2]. In China in 2019, over 25 million disability adjusted life years for stroke were attributed to high SBP [3]. Studies indicate that managing elevated blood pressure can lead to substantial decreases in cardiovascular incidents and mortality [4]. Epidemiological data reveal that factors such as an aging population, gender disparities, obesity, alcohol intake, poor dietary habits and sedentary lifestyles are correlated with a heightened risk of hypertension [5]. There is a well-established genetic component to hypertension, with familial genetic research suggesting that 30% to 50% of essential hypertension is inherited [6,7]. Recent investigations have underscored the influence of genetic variations on the susceptibility to hypertension [8–10]. However, numerous potential genetic markers related to hypertension risk have yet to be identified.

Methylenetetrahydrofolate dehydrogenase 1-like (MTHFD1L) gene, localized on chromosome 6q25.1, encoded a mitochondrial enzyme that possesses N(10)-formyltetrahydrofolate synthetase activity and is crucial for the mitochondrial synthesis of tetrahydrofolate [11,12]. Variations in the MTHFD1L gene have been associated with differences in Warfarin dosage needs and the propensity for cardiovascular diseases among the Jordanian population [13,14]. Studies have also identified a relationship between genetic variations in the MTHFD1L gene and the occurrence of Alzheimer’s disease [15]. MTHFD1L variant could serve as a significant prognostic indicator for cardiovascular mortality in individuals’ post-acute coronary syndrome [16]. An increased expression of the MTHFD1L gene has been correlated with elevated blood pressure, respiratory symptoms and the use of amlodipine [17]. Nevertheless, there is a scarcity of research exploring the relationship between MTHFD1L polymorphisms and hypertension risk.

In our study, we aimed to investigate the association between MTHFD1L gene polymorphisms and the predisposition to hypertension among a Chinese Han population. Furthermore, the potential SNP-SNP interactions and the influence of confounding variables (age and gender) on this association were examined.

Methods

Study design and subjects

We recruited a total of 723 participants for our study, comprising 367 healthy individuals and 356 individuals with hypertension from Hainan General Hospital, all of whom were Han Chinese with no familial relationships. Blood pressure measurements were conducted using a standardized protocol. Each participant’s blood pressure was measured three times on different days to ensure accuracy and reliability. The measurements were taken in the morning, between 8:00 and 10:00 AM, to minimize the impact of diurnal variations. Before blood pressure measurement, participants were asked to sit at rest for approximately 5 minutes. The inclusion criteria for the hypertension group were as follows: meet the criteria of the 2010 Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Hypertension, age over 30 years, and defined as systolic blood pressure ≥ 140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 90 mmHg. The exclusion criteria excluded individuals with secondary hypertension, renal dysfunction, substance abuse, severe systemic diseases such as malignant tumors, and pregnant and lactating women. The control group consisted of individuals with a normal blood pressure reading of 120/80 mmHg and no prior diagnosis of hypertension, with exclusion criteria as those of the hypertension group. This research was in compliance with the principles of the Helsinki Declaration and approved by the Ethical Committee of Hainan General Hospital (Med-Eth-Res [2021]139). All signed informed consent forms were obtained from all subjects.

DNA extraction and genotyping

Four SNPs within MTHFD1L (rs803419, rs6922269, rs1555179 and rs11155772) were chosen based on minor allele frequency (MAF) > 0.01 from the dbSNP database. To predict the potential functions of these genetic variants, bioinformatics tools (HaploReg v4.1 and RegulomeDB) were utilized. Peripheral blood samples (approximately 5 mL) were obtained from each participant using EDTA-containing tubes. The extraction of genomic DNA from the leukocytes was carried out with the GoldMag DNA Extraction Kit (GoldMag Co. Ltd, Xi′an, China). The genotyping of the selected SNPs was conducted using the Agena MassARRAY system (Agena, San Diego, CA, USA). Primers design (Table S1) and the genotyping results were conducted through support software. To ensure accuracy, 5% of the samples were randomly selected to repeat genotyping, and the results were 100% consistent.

Statistical data

χ2 and student t tests, as appropriate was utilized to compare the characteristics between hypertension patients and healthy controls. The genotype frequencies in the control group were assessed for Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) using the chi-square test. The major allele served as the reference, while the minor allele was considered a potential risk factor. Logistic regression models, adjusted for confounding variables such as age and sex, were employed to determine the odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) to evaluate the association between MTHFD1L polymorphisms and hypertension predisposition. Furthermore, multifactor dimensionality reduction (MDR) method was applied to evaluate the SNP–SNP interactions in relation to hypertension risk. The statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 20.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA), SNPstats online tool, and MDR_3.0.2 software. A p value of less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance, whereas a value of corrected p < 0.05/(5 × 4) was considered significant after Bonferroni correction.

Association of MTHFD1L variants with hypertension risk

The basic details of selected SNPs was displayed in Table 2. All SNPs were successfully genotyped, achieving a 100% call rate. The genotype frequencies among the control group was consistent with HWE. Bioinformatics analysis indicated that the potential roles for these SNPs might be involved in enhancer histone marks, DNAse, Proteins bound, motifs changed, selected eQTL hits, and transcription factor binding. Stratification analysis for the association of MTHFD1L variants with hypertension risk

Stratified analyses were conducted based on potential confounding factors (age and sex) to evaluate their impact on the relationship between MTHFD1L polymorphisms and hypertension susceptibility (Table 4 and Figure 1). Stratified by gender, MTHFD1L rs803419 (p = 0.032, OR = 0.63) was identified as a potential factor reducing the risk of hypertension, specifically in female subjects. Our data suggested that rs6922269 (males: p = 0.030, OR = 2.38; and females: p = 7.00 × 10−4, OR = 3.90) was related to an increased risk of hypertension, whereas rs1555179 (males: p = 3.00 × 10−4, OR = 0.44; and females: p = 0.007, OR = 0.67) was associated with a reduced risk of hypertension in both genders. In terms of age, rs6922269 (for those aged > 69 years: p = 0.002, OR = 2.96; and for those aged≤ 69 years: p = 0.003, OR = 3.27) had a higher risk for hypertension occurrence, while rs1555179 (for those aged > 69 years: p = 7.00 × 10−4, OR = 0.61; and for those aged≤ 69 years: p = 7.00 × 10−4, OR = 0.61) might have the lower hypertension susceptibility across both age groups. The significance of rs6922269 in females and rs1555179 in males still existed after Bonferroni correction. Moreover, the significance of rs6922269 (Log-additive model) in subjects aged > 69 years, and rs1555179 (Log-additive model) in subjects aged > 69 years or subjects aged ≤69 years still existed after Bonferroni correction.

MDR analysis for MTHFD1L SNP-SNP interactions in hypertension risk

Further analysis of the interactions between SNPs within the MTHFD1L gene was performed using MDR analysis to assess the influence of the SNP-SNP interactions on hypertension risk. The dendrogram and Fruchterman-Reingold graph (Figure 2) indicated that stronger interactions contributing to hypertension risk were located closer together on the branches, while weaker interactions were positioned further apart. Table 5 demonstrated that MTHFD1L rs1555179 emerged as the most informative single-locus model for hypertension risk, with an information gain (IG) of 2.44%, a testing accuracy of 0.5787, and a cross-validation consistency (CVC) of 10/10. The optimal multi-locus model included rs6922269 (IG: 2.44%), rs1555179 (IG: 2.07%) and rs11155772 (IG: 0.35%) with an improved balanced test accuracy of 0.6124 and a perfect CVC score of 10/10. Additionally, our findings indicated a strong redundancy between MTHFD1L rs6922269 and rs1555179, with an IG of −1.00%.

Discussion

In this case-control study, four SNPs within the MTHFD1L gene (rs803419, rs6922269, rs1555179 and rs11155772) were genotyped, and their potential association with hypertension risk was evaluated among the Chinese Han population. Our research indicated that MTHFD1L rs6922269 was related to the increased risk of hypertension, whereas rs1555179 could act as a protective factor against hypertension occurrence. More specifically, these genetic variations were related to hypertension risk in females, males, and subjects over the age of 69 years, and subjects aged ≤ 69 years. MTHFD1L rs803419 might be a risk-reducing factor for hypertension in female but not in males. Additionally, rs1555179 emerged as the most informative single-locus model for hypertension risk, with an IG of 2.44%, and the optimal multi-locus model included rs6922269, rs1555179 and rs11155772. These results highlighted the possible role of MTHFD1L genetic variations in the development of hypertension.

MTHFD1 encoded a multifunctional enzyme that plays a role in the conversion of various forms of one-carbon-substituted tetrahydrofolate [18]. Studies utilizing whole exome sequencing have identified damaging variants of MTHFD1L in Indonesian individuals with clefts [19]. Mutations in MTHFDL1 may contribute to the development of Alzheimer’s disease in a multigenerational Colombian family [20]. A common variant within MTHFD1L has been related to neural tube defects and the efficiency of mRNA splicing [21]. MTHFD1L rs6922269 was associated with survival rates and baseline active vitamin B12 levels in patients following acute coronary syndromes [22]. There were no known reports on rs1555179. The relationship between MTHFD1 genetic variants and the onset of hypertension remains unclear. In our study, we found that MTHFD1L rs6922269 was related to an increased risk of hypertension, whereas rs1555179 could serve as a protective factor against it among the Chinese Han population. MTHFD1L rs6922269 and rs1555179 were located in the intron region. Functional annotations from the HaploReg database and RegulomeDB indicated that rs6922269 was associated with enhancer histone marks, DNAse sensitivity, altered motifs, and transcription factor binding, while rs1555179 may be related to transcription factor binding or chromatin accessibility peaks. These findings suggested that the role of these variants in the development of hypertension could be related to these functions, although further investigation is warranted.

In China, approximately 60% of individuals over the age of 65 years suffer from hypertension, with a notably low rate of effective control [23]. Hypertension intensifies the morphological alterations that come with aging [24]. Hypertension was related to an elevated risk of cardiovascular diseases and overall mortality, with stronger correlations observed when hypertension begins at a younger age [25]. Epidemiological research indicates a higher incidence of hypertension among men [26]. There are significant differences in the epidemiological and clinical aspects of hypertension between genders [27]. Sex hormones can influence blood pressure control mechanisms, either directly or indirectly, which may account for the gender disparities in hypertension rates [28]. Consequently, our study conducted age and gender stratification to investigate the impact of these confounding variables on the relationship between MTHFD1L genetic variations and hypertension risk. Our findings revealed that rs6922269 and rs1555179 were related to hypertension risk across various age groups and genders. A critical limitation of the current study lies in the age structure of the sample population. Both the hypertension group and the control group have a mean age exceeding 70 years, placing the entire cohort in a high-age demographic. This introduces a significant risk of age-related bias in genetic association analyses. The study aims to identify MTHFD1L polymorphisms associated with hypertension susceptibility. However, susceptibility should ideally be assessed in populations where the onset and development of hypertension are still modifiable or predictive—typically middle-aged or general adult populations. In contrast, in elderly cohorts, the impact of cumulative environmental exposures, age-related epigenetic alterations, comorbidities, long-term medication use, and survivorship bias may attenuate or distort the interpretation of true genetic effects. Therefore, the external validity and translational relevance of these findings to younger or preclinical populations remain questionable.

Specifically, rs6922269 was more significantly related to an increased risk of hypertension in women, while rs1555179 was more significantly associated with a decreased risk in men. Moreover, MTHFD1L rs803419 potentially reduced the risk of hypertension in women but not in men. These results suggested a gender-specific effect of genetic factors within MTHFD1L on the risk of hypertension. Similar to our findings, sex-specific features of the correlation between GWAS-noticeable polymorphisms and hypertension were found in Europeans of Russia [29]. These findings may suggest that genetic susceptibility to hypertension differs by sex and emphasize the importance of considering heterogeneity in genetic and hypertension association studies. Some literatures demonstrated pronounced men–women differences in hypertension incidence. It is believed that the incidence of HTN in men is 1.2 times higher in comparison with women and the DALYs linked with SBP ≥ 140 mmHg (1.38 times) and SBP ≥ 110–115 mmHg (1.44 times) are greater by 1.4 times [30]. Estrogens exert their protective effects against hypertension via mechanisms involving vasoconstriction and vasodilation. This is attributed to the regulation of the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system, as well as the production of catecholamines, endothelins and angiotensin II [31]. Following menopause, the protective effects of estrogen on hypertension in women diminish, leading to an increased risk of elevated blood pressure [32]. Significant differences in the prevalence of hypertension and its impact on the state of health in men and women may be associated with both hormonal mechanisms and environmental risk factors, which determine the features of gene–environmental interactions [33,34].

However, it is crucial to recognize the inherent limitations in our study. First, given that our research is a hospital-based case-control study within the Chinese Han population, there is a possibility of selection bias influencing our results. Second, the lack of comprehensive data on certain risk factors for hypertension, including socioeconomic status, clinical symptoms (such as diabetes, hypertension and cardiovascular disease) and environmental exposures (such as smoking, alcohol consumption and physical activity levels), means that the interplay between genetic and environmental factors in hypertension risk needs further exploration. We will expand the sample size and refine these information in future research to further confirm our findings. Third, while our study identified several MTHFD1L polymorphisms associated with hypertension in an elderly cohort, we acknowledge that these findings may not be directly applicable to younger or preclinical populations. Future research should focus on middle-aged or general adult populations to better understand the genetic susceptibility factors for hypertension in its modifiable and predictive stages.

Conclusion

In summary, our study indicated the potential association between MTHFD1L genetic variants and hypertension risk in the Chinese Han population. Specifically, rs6922269 was related to an increased risk of hypertension, while rs1555179 could act as a protective factor against hypertension occurrence. These insights enhance our comprehension on the role of MTHFD1L gene in hypertension occurrence.

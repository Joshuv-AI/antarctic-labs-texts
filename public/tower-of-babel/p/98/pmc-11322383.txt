Peptidomic analysis reveals novel peptide PDLC promotes cell proliferation in hepatocellular carcinoma via Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK pathway
Authors: Bo Han, Daqing Cheng, Huizhao Luo, Jutang Li, Jiaoxiang Wu, Xing Jia, Ming Xu, Peng Sun, Sheng Cheng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) still presents poor prognosis with low overall survival rates and limited therapeutic options available. Recently, attention has been drawn to peptidomic analysis, an emerging field of proteomics for the exploration of new potential peptide drugs for the treatment of various diseases. However, research on the potential function of HCC peptides is lacking. Here, we analyzed the peptide spectrum in HCC tissues using peptidomic techniques and explored the potentially beneficial peptides involved in HCC. Changes in peptide profiles in HCC were examined using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS). Analyze the physicochemical properties and function of differently expressed peptides using bioinformatics. The effect of candidate functional peptides on HCC cell growth and migration was evaluated using the CCK-8, colony formation, and transwell assays. Transcriptome sequencing analysis and western blot were employed to delve into the mode of action of potential peptide on HCC. Peptidomic analysis of HCC tissue yielded a total of 8683 peptides, of which 452 exhibited up-regulation and 362 showed down-regulation. The peptides that were differentially expressed, according to bioinformatic analysis, were closely linked to carbon metabolism and the mitochondrial inner membrane. The peptide functional validation identified a novel peptide, PDLC (peptide derived from liver cancer), which was found to dramatically boost HCC cell proliferation through the Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK signaling cascade. Our research defined the peptide’s properties and pattern of expression in HCC and identified a novel peptide, PDLC, with a function in encouraging HCC progression, offering an entirely new potential therapeutic target the disease.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most prevalent diseases and the third most common cause of cancer-related death globally1. Since there are few effective treatment options and many HCC patients have late-stage diagnoses, the mortality rate associated with HCC has risen over the last 10 years2,3. Despite the fact that various molecularly targeted medications, including sorafenib and regorafenib, have been utilized to treat advanced HCC, the long-term outlook for HCC remains poor due to its significant risk of metastasis and recurrence in HCC as well as chemoresistance to targeted drug4–6. Effective HCC prevention and treatment strategies are urgently needed.

Peptidomic is emerging field of proteomics for analyzing novel valuable peptides in various biological samples7–9. The biological properties of peptides, such as strong biological sensitivity, high bioactivity and low molecular weight, give peptides a unique advantage for the development of biomarkers or pharmaceuticals10. As clinical indicators for the diagnosis of disease, several conventional peptide molecules have been employed, including progranulin-releasing peptide (ProGRP) for the diagnosis of lung cancer and carboxypeptidase A4 (CPA4) for the prognosis of pancreatic cancer11,12. Serum peptides have also been reported as biomarkers to differentiate between HCC and HBV-related cirrhosis (LC)13. In addition to acting as biomarkers, endogenous peptides generated by endogenous proteases or encoded by non-coding RNAs play crucial functions in the controlling of biological processes14,15. For example, our previous peptidomic analysis identified the novel liver-derived peptide 2 (LDP2) with a protective function against liver ischemia–reperfusion (I/R) injury16. Additionally, certain LncRNA-encoded polypeptides, such the SPAR polypeptide encoded by LINC00961, can regulate muscle regeneration by activating mTORC117. In recent years, a growing volume of investigations have shown that peptides are engaged in controlling the growth of tumors and providing early detection of cancers18. The D TBP-3 peptide, for instance, was demonstrated to have significant tumor suppressive effects in an anti-PD-1 resilient tumor model, showing the potential of this peptide in combination immunotherapy of cancers19. Yang et al. identified a novel SA12 peptide that interacts with MECP2 and CDC20B to inhibit breast cancer proliferation and induce apoptosis20. These fascinating studies point to the enormous potential of peptides in the treatment of tumors. However, the screening of functional endogenous peptides by peptidomic in HCC tissues have yet to be investigated.

In the present study, we used liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) to undertake peptidomic analysis of HCC tissues and paracancerous tissues, and we revealed 452 up-regulated and 362 down-regulated peptides in HCC. The physical and chemical characteristics as well as the biological roles of these differentially expressed peptides were bioinformatically analyzed. We selected four potentially functional differential peptides to assess their effects on HCC and screened for a novel peptide derived from AQP3, PDLC, which stimulates the Ras signaling pathway to increase the growth of HCC cells. This work validates potential functional peptides in HCC and provides a new perspective for early warning and treatment of HCC.

Materials and methods

Sample collection and peptide identification

Three copies of the patient’s HCC and the corresponding adjacent normal tissues were provided by Tongren Hospital of Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (as revised in 2013), and approved by the ethics board of Shanghai Tongren Hospital (No. 2020-035-01). The authors confirm that all methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations and take responsibility for all aspects of the work to ensure that questions related to the accuracy or integrity of any part of the work are appropriately investigated and resolved. Peptide extraction and peptidomic analysis were performed as previously reported using a 3000 UPLC system (ThermoFisher Scientific)21.

Bioinformatics analysis

The precursor proteins of differential peptides were uncovered by the UniProt database (http://www.uniport.org/). Using online tool (https://web.expasy.org/protparam/), the peptide’s molecular weight (MW) and isoelectric point (pI) were determined. Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses were used to confirm the potential functions of differential peptides22. The string collection from the STRING website (https://string-db.org) was employed to analyze the protein–protein interactions between precursor proteins. Cytoscape's plug-in CytoHubba was used to discover key nodes in the PPI network. Cytoscape (https://cytoscape.org/index.html, version 3.7.1) is an open-source bioinformatics tool used to generate visual molecular interaction networks and the plug-in CytoHubba can develop key gene modules in the network based on the node topological parameters. Nodes with top 10 degree, MNC, Closeness and Stress values among all nodes in the network were deemed as the hub nodes in the network; in other words, we selected the top 10 most significantly related to HCC proteins as the hub nodes.

Analysis of the expression level of hub molecules and overall survival of HCC patients

RNA-seq transcriptome data for HCC samples (374 tumor tissues and 50 paracancerous tissues) with the clinical information of the corresponding patients were obtained from the TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). The HTSeq-FPKM level 3 data were converted to TPM (transcripts per million reads) values for further analysis. Hub molecules expression and the overall survival (OS) analysis was conducted using R “survival” and “survminer” packages, and our study fully complied with the guidelines published by TCGA. The two-sided log-rank P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Cell culture and peptide treatments

The human HCC cell lines SK-HEP-1 and HepG2 obtained from Chinese Academy of Sciences and the cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM; Cat. C11995500BT, Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Cat. S711-001S, NSERA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Cat. 10378016, Gibco) at 37 °C in a 5% CO2 incubator. Storage solution of PDLC (10 mM) were diluted with cell culture medium to a working concentration of 20 μM. Cells were treated with peptides at different predetermined time points and then harvested for subsequent experiments.

CCK-8 and colony formation assay

Cell viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit (CCK-8) (Cat. CK04, Dojindo) in accordance with the manufacturer's recommendations. Briefly, SK-Hep-1 (2 × 103 cells per well) & HepG2 (2 × 103 cells per well) were seeded in 96-well plates with five replicate wells per set. 10 µl of CCK-8 solution was added to each well after the cells had been exposed to 20 μM PDLC or vehicle for 0, 24 h, 48 h and 72 h, and incubating them at 5% CO2 and 37 °C for 1 h. The absorbed light of each well was measured at a wavelength of 450 nm by using a microplate reader. For colony formation assay, SK-Hep-1 and HepG2 (500/well) were seeded into 12-well plates and the cell culture medium with or without peptides was replaced every 48 h. Cell colonies were dyed with crystal violet dye following a 10-day cell culture, and the colonies were photographed and counted manually under a microscope.

Migration assay

Transwell chambers with 8-μm pores (3422, Corning, USA) were used to test the cell migration capacity. 3 × 104 cells (SK-Hep-1 and HepG2) in serum-free medium containing 20 μM of PDLC or vehicle were seeded in the upper chamber, and 600 μl fresh medium supplemented with 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber. SK-Hep-1 cells were cultured for 12 h and HepG2 were cultured for 48 h at 5% CO2 and 37 °C before analysis. The cells on the chamber's bottom were stained with 0.5% crystal violet for 15 min after being fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min. The top membrane cells were then carefully removed with a cotton swab and the penetrated cells were counted under a light microscope. The number of cells flowing through the membrane was determined using four randomly chosen fields of view.

Transcriptomic analysis

HepG2 cells were exposed to 20 μM PDLC or vehicle for 48 h, and total RNA of the cells was exacted with RNAiso Plus (9109, TaKaRa, Japan). The library was constructed using VAHTS Universal V6 RNA-seq Library Prep Kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Then RNA sequencing analysis was performed on an illumina NovaseqTM 6000 platform. Differential expression analysis was performed using the DESeq25. |Log2-fold change| > 1 and p-value < 0.05 was set as the threshold for significantly differential expression genes (DEGs) 23. Heatmap, GO and KEGG analysis of DEGs were performed to screen the significant enriched term based on the R (https://www.r-project.org/, R version 3.5.1) on the OECloud platform (https://cloud.oebiotech.com/task/).

Western blotting

A radio-immunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) solution containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors was used to extract the total protein from SK-Hep-1 and HepG2 cells after they had been exposed to 20 M PDLC or vehicle for 48 h. The BCA protein assay kit (23227, Thermo Fisher Scientific, United States) was then used to determine the protein concentrations. Proteins were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE gel and then transferred to PVDF membranes (Millipore, USA). The membranes was blocked with 5% skim milk for 1 h at room temperature and subsequently probed with specific primary antibodies, anti-RAS (1:1000, 3339 T, CST), anti-c-Raf (1:1000, 9422 T, CST), anti-Phospho-c-Raf (1:1000, 9427 T, CST), anti-Erk1/2 (1:1000, 4695 T, CST), anti- Phospho-Erk1/2 (1:1000, AF1015, Affinity), anti-MEK1/2 (1:1000, 8727 T, CST), anti-Phospho-MEK1/2 (1:1000, 9154 T, CST), and anti-GAPDH (M20006, 1:2000, Abmart) at 4 °C overnight. The membranes were then incubated for 2 h at room temperature using a suitable secondary antibody (1:2000) and the target protein bands were visualized by chemiluminescent horseradish peroxidase (HRP) substrate (KF8001, Affinity, United States).

Statistical analysis

GraphPad Prism 8.0.1 software was used to analyze the experimentation data and create statistics graphics. P-values below 0.05 were deemed significant while employing the student’s t-test to assess significance.

Ethical statement

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (as revised in 2013), and approved by the ethics board of Shanghai Tongren Hospital (No. 2020-035-01). Before samples collection, all patients provided written informed consent.

The top five cellular components obtained were mitochondrial inner membrane, mitochondrial matrix, vesicle lumen, cell-substrate junction and focal adhesion (Fig. 3B). The top five enriched molecular function were cadherin binding, oxidoreductase activity, acting on the CH–OH group of donors, NAD or NADP as acceptor, lyase activity, ligase activity and isomerase activity (Fig. 3C). Validating the role of candidate differentially expressed peptides in HCC

To verify the role of differentially expressed peptides in regulating HCC, we synthesized two up-regulated and two down-regulated peptides in HCC whose precursor proteins are believed to be linked to HCC (Table 1). For instance, the elevated candidate peptide precursor protein AQP3 regulates CD133 expression to promote the stem cell-like characteristics of HCC cells that play an oncogenic role in HCC24. It was discovered that ADRM1, another highly expressed potential peptide precursor protein, controls the HCC cell cycle and apoptosis and is a very promising target for the clinical treatment of HCC 25. The down-regulated candidate peptide precursor protein PRELP is correlated with improved patient survival and suppresses cell migration and growth in HCC26. Analogously, research on both clinical and molecular mechanisms has shown that the down-regulated peptide precursor protein HMGCS2 is involved in metabolic pathways that govern HCC progression and the survival of its patients27,28. Using CCK-8 analysis for preliminary screening we found that only the peptide from AQP3, exhibited activity in boosting HCC cell proliferation (Fig. 5A and Fig. S3). We designated this functional peptide generated from AQP3 as PDLC (peptide derived from liver cancer) to facilitate presentation. Further research on PDLC's impact on SK-Hep-1 and HepG2 proliferation and migration revealed that it significantly boosted both cell proliferation (Fig. 5B–D), but had no impact on their migration (Fig. 5E). Based on GO functional analysis, these differential genes are involved in GTP binding, regulation of neural precursor cell proliferation, and regulation of G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathways, among other biological processes that impact cell proliferation29–32 (Fig. 6B). Notably, research on the KEGG pathway has shown that PDLC activates a number of signaling pathways, such as the Ras and calcium signaling pathways (Fig. 6C). To verify the modulation of the Ras signaling pathway by PDLC, we examined the protein expressions of the Ras pathway in SK-Hep-1 and HepG2 treated with 20 μM PDLC. As seen in Fig. 6D and Fig. S4, PDLC treatment of HCC resulted in a significant upregulation of the expression of Phospho-c-Raf, Phospho-Erk1/2, and Phospho-MEK1/2, while the expression of downstream Ras signaling pathway proteins c-Raf, Ras, Erk1/2 and MEK1/2 remained unchanged. These results strongly indicated that PDLC may promote HCC cell proliferation via activation of Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK signaling pathway.

Discussion

The high mortality and morbidity of HCC is a global public health problem and the exploration of possible treatment targets or tumor biomarkers for HCC is of clinical importance33. In this research, we peptidomically analyzing the variation in peptides between HCC tissues and comparable normal tissues, and determined the expression patterns of identified differential peptides. Bioinformatics analysis and functional experiments helped us to identify a new peptide, PDLC, which functions to promote the proliferation of HCC cells via stimulating the Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK signaling pathway. Our research demonstrates that PDLC might be a promising therapeutic target or a biomarker for early detection of HCC.

It has been established that several endogenous peptides have crucial biological roles in a range of disorders, including cancer, immune regulation, nervous system and tissue remodeling34–37, making peptide therapeutics an emerging market for the pharmaceutical industry. In the current work, to explore the role of endogenous peptides in HCC, we analyzed the peptide profile of HCC and discovered a total of 8683 peptides, including 815 differential peptides in HCC tissue and the corresponding adjacent normal tissue. Many of the peptides had between 12 and 20 amino acids in them, and the MW distribution ranged between 1100 and 3100 Da, proving the validity of the peptides. Most of the pIs of these differential peptides are concentrated between 4–7 and 8–10, with near-neutral pIs being almost blank. For this pattern, some researchers considered it is due to a lack of neutral pH amino acids in these peptides38. Our analysis of HCC peptide profiles, as well as the physicochemical characterization of the differential peptides, provides the basis for further investigation of the biological functions of these peptides in HCC.

Bioinformatics analysis of these differential peptides revealed biological processes enriched in organic acid catabolic process, mitochondrial inner membrane and mitochondrial matrix, and small molecule catabolic process. It has been established that mitochondria are crucial for energy metabolism and cellular homeostasis, and that mitochondrial dysfunction contributes to the onset and progression of HCC39,40. For instance, the lack of MRPS31 in HCC disrupts the mitochondria assembly and invasiveness HCC cell by promoting MMP7 and COL1A1 expression41. Interestingly, in recent years, mitochondria-derived peptides or mitochondria-targeting penetrating peptides have shown great advantages in inhibiting tumor progression or delivering antitumor drugs. For example, R-Tf-D-LP4, a mitochondrial multifunctional protein-voltage dependent anion channel (VDAC1)-derived peptide, causes impaired cellular energy and metabolic homeostasis, induces apoptosis, and abrogates hepatocellular carcinoma-associated processes42. A recent review by Wu et al. summarizes the progress in mitochondria-targeted drug delivery through the plasticity of mitochondria-penetrating peptides (MPPs) and is optimistic that this antitumor cargo delivery tool will eventually help with the diagnosis and treatment of cancer43. According to our analyses of signaling pathways, carbon metabolism which includes glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation, and the pentanone phosphate pathway is the metabolic network most relevant to differentially expressed peptides. This metabolic network has been shown to control the distribution of energy and the requirements of HCC cells for biosynthesis44. Abnormalities in metabolism are closely related to tumorigenesis and progression45. Studies have shown that the metabolism of HCC cells is highly dysregulated compared to normal liver cells46. The available research suggested that the inhibition of cancer metabolic reprogramming by a number of peptides or small proteins, including HOXB-AS3, FBXW7-185aa, SHPRH-146aa, PINT-87aa, and miPEP-200a, has been found to have significant anti-tumor functions47, making them great potential as tumor biomarkers, therapeutic targets, and anti-tumor drugs. It's interesting to note that top 10 hub proteins in our PPI analysis of peptide precursor proteins are involved in HCC metabolism. For instance, overexpression of the hub protein GAPDH promotes the switch from glycolysis to serine biosynthesis, which accelerates cell proliferation and liver tumorigenesis48, and PKG1, other highly ranked hub metabolic protein, drives the enhanced metabolic process that leads to hepatocellular carcinoma metastasis49. These hub metabolic molecules are also differently expressed in HCC and have an impact on the survival of HCC patients, according to data analysis utilizing TCGA. Our results indicate that these peptides related to these hub metabolic proteins may play a broad function in HCC metabolic stress and have prognostic significance in HCC. Therefore, targeting metabolic homeostasis and mitochondrial dysfunction may provide attractive therapeutic strategies for HCC in which peptides may play an essential role.

In the present study, we concentrated on several candidate peptides that may have roles in HCC and identified a new peptide, PDLC, that encourages the proliferation of HCC cells. PDLC is a peptide derived from the aquaporin 3 (AQP3) protein and increased in HCC. Most peptides behave biologically in ways that are comparable to or diametrically opposed to those of their source proteins. Despite the fact that our analysis found no significant difference in the expression of AQP3 at different stages of HCC (Fig. S5) and recent research indicated that it could prevent autophagic cell death in HCC, it was still demonstrated that AQP3 was overexpressed in HCC tissues and that this overexpression aided in the growth and migration of HCC cells50. Therefore, based on the functional studies of PDLC, we speculate that one of the ways AQP3 promotes the malignant progression of HCC may be through cleavage into short peptides. Mechanistic exploration reveals that PDLC activates the Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK signaling pathway in HCC. Research have revealed that the Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK signaling pathway is crucial for the initiation and development of HCC, and that Raf/MEK/ERK phosphorylation can activate a wide range of target molecules and accelerate HCC development51,52. Our western blotting analysis demonstrated that PDLC is capable of promoting phosphorylated Raf/MEK/ERK pathway expression to activate the Ras signaling pathway. Numerous studies have found that the Ras/RAF/MEK/ERK signaling axis regulates cellular metabolism, such as glycolysis and mitochondrial metabolic reprogramming53. Our bioinformatics analysis suggested that peptides with varied levels of expression in HCC could regulate hepatocarcinogenesis by influencing specific metabolic pathways. Here, we hypothesized that PDLC peptide could activate the Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK signaling pathway and consequently affect HCC metabolism to promote HCC cell proliferation. Research is still needed to determine how PDLC controls metabolic reprogramming to promote the progression of HCC. Based on the mechanistic research, APQ3 targeting may be a potential therapeutic target for HCC, and PDLC, a peptide generated from APQ3, may offer a new diagnostic marker for HCC. In the meantime, serum AQP3 has been reported to have diagnostic value in patients with colon cancer54, whether PDLC is present in the peripheral blood of HCC patients and whether it can be used as a serum diagnostic marker for the occurrence or prognosis of HCC deserves further investigation.

Conclusions

In summary, to our knowledge, this study is the first investigation to use peptidomic to examine the peptide profile of HCC. Our research uncovered 814 differentially expressed peptides in HCC and identified a novel peptide called PDLC that stimulates the Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK signaling pathway to enhance the growth of HCC cells. Our research suggests a new treatment target as well as a possible biomarker for HCC.

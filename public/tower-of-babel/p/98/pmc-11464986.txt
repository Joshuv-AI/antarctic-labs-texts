Exploring the significance and potential mechanisms of hippo pathway-associated genes in prognosis of glioma patients
Authors: XuKai Liu, Hongjun Fan, Zebo Chen, Chao Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeDespite the efforts of countless researchers to develop glioma treatment strategies, the current therapeutic effect of glioma is still not ideal, and it is necessary to further explore the mechanism to guide treatment. Thus, this study aims to introduce a novel approach for predicting patient prognosis and guiding further treatment interventions.MethodsInitially, we conducted a differential gene expression analysis to identify Hippo pathway-associated genes overexpressed in tumors and determined genes correlated with prognosis. Subsequently, employing cluster analysis, we categorized samples into two groups and performed further analyses including prediction, immune cell infiltration abundance, and drug response rates. We utilized weighted gene co-expression analysis to reveal gene sets with high co-variation, delineate inter-sample gene correlation patterns, and conduct enrichment analysis. Prognostic models were built using ten machine learning algorithms combined in 101 different combinations, followed by evaluation and validation. Immune infiltration analysis, differential expression analysis of depleted T cell-related markers, drug sensitivity analysis, and exploration of pathway dysregulation were performed for different risk groups. Quality control and batch integration were performed, and single-cell data were analyzed using dimensionality reduction clustering algorithms and annotation tools to evaluate the activity of the prognostic model in malignant cells.ResultsWe conducted data filtering to identify genes overexpressed in tumors, intersecting these genes with Hippo pathway-related genes, identifying 62 genes correlated with prognosis, and performing cluster analysis to divide tumor tissues into two groups. Cluster 2 exhibited a poorer prognosis and demonstrated differences in immune cell infiltration. Utilizing weighted gene co-expression analysis on Cluster 2, we identified gene modules, conducted functional enrichment analysis, and delineated pathways. Employing a combined model based on ten machine learning algorithm combinations, we selected the optimal prognostic model system and validated the model’s predictive ability within the dataset. Through immune-related analysis and drug sensitivity analysis, we uncovered differences in immune infiltration and varying sensitivities to chemotherapy drugs. Additionally, the enrichment analysis of gene set revealed discrepancies in upregulation within relevant pathways between the high and low-risk groups. Finally, annotation and evaluation of malignant cells via single-cell analysis showed increased activity of the prognostic model and variations in distribution across different prognostic levels in malignant cells.ConclusionThis study introduces a novel approach utilizing the Hippo pathway and associated genes for glioma prognosis research, demonstrating the potential and significance of this method in evaluating the outcome for patients with glioma. These findings hold substantial clinical significance in guiding therapy and predicting outcomes for individuals diagnosed with glioma, offering significant clinical utility.Keywords: Gliomas, Prognosis, Hippo pathway, Single-cell analysis, Immune cell infiltration

Introduction

Gliomas are a category of tumor originating from glial cells within the nervous system, typically found in the tissues of the brain and spinal cord [1, 2]. Glial cells, as support cells within the nervous system, have the responsibility of providing support and protection to neurons, thus contributing to the maintenance of normal nervous system function [3]. Early-stage gliomas are often challenging to detect, with symptoms manifesting typically in the advanced stages [4]. Gliomas incur significant familial and socioeconomic burdens annually [5]. Despite advancements in medical technology, significant progress in overall survival rates and treatment outcomes for glioma patients remains elusive [6]. Various treatment modalities exist for gliomas, including surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, among others, yet their applicability and usage vary, thus presenting limitations [7, 8]. Enhancing the prognostic survival level of glioma patients has been a widely researched topic. Therefore, we think that it is crucial and necessary to develop a novel and efficient prognostic model for analyzing disease progression in patients and guiding subsequent treatment across different stages. These studies hold immense research and application potential in the treatment and prognosis of glioma patients.

The discovery of the Hippo pathway originated from observations in Drosophila melanogaster and it is a crucial regulator of tissue growth [9, 10]. In cancer, the function of the Hippo pathway may undergo abnormal changes, leading to tumor initiation and development. Overactivation of the Hippo pathway may suppress tumor cell proliferation and metastasis, thereby inhibiting tumor growth; conversely, inactivation of the Hippo pathway may result in uncontrolled tumor cell invasion and proliferation, facilitating distant spreading and tumor enlargement [10, 11]. Therefore, the function of the Hippo pathway in cancer is closely associated with inhibiting tumor development and controlling tumor growth. Some studies have indicated that certain proteins can induce apoptosis of human glioma cells, weaken glioma cell invasion, proliferation and migration by stimulating the Hippo signaling pathway hidden in glioma cells, thereby inhibiting glioma cell growth and migration [12, 13]. Therefore, additional research is necessary to clarify the function of the Hippo pathway in glioma cells.

We utilized multiple datasets for comprehensive analysis to achieve more robust results. Initially, we screened genes within the Hippo pathway, identifying those highly expressed in tumors and associated with prognosis. Based on the expression characteristics of these genes, we performed hierarchical clustering to classify patients anew. We then investigated the differences between these new classifications in terms of immune cell infiltration, expression of immune regulatory factors, and response rates to drugs and ICB, providing a reference for innovative patient classification [14]. Using WGCNA, we identified key genes playing central roles in glioblastoma and elucidated their pathway expression patterns through enrichment analysis [15, 16]. To support clinical prognosis guidance, we developed predictive models using 101 machine learning algorithms, which demonstrated excellent validation performance and prognostic prediction capability. In-depth analyses of high-risk and low-risk groups revealed significant differences, offering new insights for personalized therapy development [17]. Finally, we validated our model in single-cell sequencing datasets, where it showed exceptional predictive performance. These findings hold substantial implications for the treatment and prognosis of patients with glioblastoma.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and preparation

In this investigation, bulk transcriptomic data along with corresponding clinical data were procured from 704 glioma samples and 5 adjacent normal samples (cohort: TCGA-LGG/GBM) sourced from the publicly available database, The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA, https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) [18]. Furthermore, 1018 tumor sample data were obtained from the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas database (CGGA, http://www.cgga.org.cn/). We subsequently categorized them into two groups: CGGA_325 (n = 325) and CGGA_693 (n = 693) for further analysis. We retrieved data of healthy brain tissue from the Genotype-tissue expression database (GTEx, www.gtexportal.org/) [19]. In addition, single-cell sequencing datasets containing 4 tumor tissues and 4 cells (GSE162631 and GSE84465) were acquired via The Gene Expression Omnibus database (GEO, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) [20]. The single-cell sequencing dataset GSE138794 was obtained from the Tumor Immune Single-cell Hub 2 database (TISCH2, http://tisch.comp-genomics.org/) [21].

In the GSE162631 dataset, researchers analyzed cells freshly isolated from the tumor core and paired peripheral tissue of four glioblastoma patients. Single-cell suspensions, enriched for CD31 + cells via magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS), were subjected to scRNA-seq using a 10X Genomics-based single-cell protocol [22]. In the GSE84465 dataset, researchers used the Smart-seq2 protocol to prepare cDNA, followed by Nextera XT for library preparation, according to the standard Smart-Seq2 single-cell RNA-seq protocol [23]. In the GSE138794 dataset, single-cell/nucleus RNA sequencing was performed on 28 gliomas, and single-cell ATAC sequencing was conducted on 8 samples [24]. To remove batch effects from the combined single-cell RNA sequencing datasets, we used the Harmony algorithm implemented in the “Seurat” v3 R package.

We also downloaded 150 immunomodulators and chemokines from the integrated repository portal for tumor-immune system interaction (TISIDB, http://cis.hku.hk/TISIDB/) database. Specifically, there were 41 chemokines, 24 immunoinhibitors, 46 immunostimulators, 21 MHC molecules, and 18 receptors. Genes associated with the Hippo pathway were sourced from the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database [25]. The Progenitor Cell Biology Consortium (PCBC, https://www.synapse.org/) supplied human stem cell data, which were utilized for research pertaining to mRNAsi [26, 27]. All public databases used in this study are freely accessible and can be utilized within the scope permitted by regulations, without the need for additional ethical approval. During the process of data acquisition and analysis, we strictly adhered to relevant regulations. The transcriptomic data underwent conversion to Transcripts per million (TPM) format for further analysis utilizing the ComBat function within the “sva” package.

Prognostic analysis and immune infiltration analysis

We began by integrating transcriptomic data from TCGA-LGG/GBM and normal control transcriptomic data from GTEx. Subsequently, we used the “limma” package(Linear modeling of microarray data) to conduct differential gene expression analysis. We applied | logFC | > 1 and adjusted p-value < 0.05 as criteria to select genes overexpressed in tumors. Next, we intersected DEGs with Hippo pathway-related genes to identify Hippo pathway-related genes overexpressed in tumors. In conclusion, we conducted univariate Cox regression analysis aimed at identifying genes associated with prognosis.

Based on prognostic-related Hippo pathway genes, we initially utilized the “ConsensusClusterPlus” package for hierarchical clustering of tumor tissues. A matrix heatmap visualized the cohesion and coupling among clusters. We employed Cumulative Distribution Function (CDF) to assist in selecting the optimal k value. Consequently, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was utilized to validate effectiveness of chosen k value. The selection criteria were based on the smallest slope of CDF descent and the lowest median in the PAC curve, determining the optimal number of clusters as cluster 1 (C1) and cluster 2 (C2). Subsequently, we conducted prognostic analysis and generated plots. We utilized 8 TME analysis algorithms (TIMER, xCell, CIBERSORT, MCPcounter, EPIC, IPS, ESITMATE, quanTIseq) built into the “IOBR R package” to analyze bulk RNA-seq data, performing infiltration abundance of immune cell analysis and visualization. The expression profile of immune regulatory factors, drug responses, and response rates to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICBs) were also analyzed and graphed to observe and validate differences between the two groups.

Weighted gene Co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) and enrichment analysis

As a systematic approach in biology, WGCNA enables the identification of gene sets that co-vary significantly and allows for the characterization of patterns of gene correlation across diverse samples [28]. For the TCGA-LGG/GBM dataset, we performed WGCNA analysis, evaluating Scale Independence and Mean connectivity to choose soft thresholds, which we then visualized. Subsequently, we generated a Cluster Dendrogram and visualized the process of obtaining gene modules.

We included clinical factors (futime, fustat, age, cluster) to analyze their correlation with gene modules and visualize them, aiming to identify modules significantly associated with C2. We then selected key genes within modules based on MM (Module Membership) and GS (Gene Significance) thresholds (MM threshold: 0.6, GS threshold: 0.3) and visualized them. Finally, we conducted GO enrichment analysis using clusterProfiler and presented the enrichment results graphically, showcasing the enrichment of relevant genes in Gene Ontology - Cellular Component (GO-CC), Gene Ontology - Molecular Function (GO-MF), and Gene Ontology - Biological Process (GO-BP) [15, 16].

Generating and validating predictive signatures through the utilization of various machine learning techniques

Utilizing the TCGA-LGG/GBM cohort for training and the CGGA_325 and the CGGA_693 cohorts for validation, we applied ten distinct machine learning algorithms, encompassing Supervised Principal Components (SuperPC), Generalized Boosted Regression Modeling (GBM), Elastic Net (Enet), Survival Support Vector Machine (survival-svm), Cox’s Partial Least Squares Regression (plsRcox), Ridge, Lasso, Stepwise Cox, Random Survival Forest (RSF), Cox Boost, and Cox Boost, to formulate union algorithms of machine learning models. We calculated all models separately in all cohorts. The concordance index (C-index) and its average were calculated for each cohorts. We established a criterion dictating that the C-index for each set should not fall below 0.6. Finally, (Stepcox[both] + LASSO) is the combination of machine learning algorithms that produces the highest average C-index in all cohorts. We chose it as the ideal algorithm to generate final signature for prognosis. Utilizing the model’s calculated median risk score for each sample, we classified the datasets into low-risk and high-risk groups. Then, we conducted prognostic assessment and generated Kaplan-Meier (KM) curves. We utilized the KM curves to analyze the differences in overall survival (OS) between high-expression cohorts and low-expression cohorts within each dataset. Subsequently, using the “timeROC” package, ROC curves were generated to validate the diagnostic efficacy for all cohorts at three time points(1, 3, and 5 years), aiming for an area under the curve (AUC) exceeding 0.7, signifying satisfactory diagnostic performance.

Immune-related analysis, drug sensitivity analysis, and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

Analysis of immune infiltration were performed on two risk groups, and then we illustrated the differential level of 22 immune cells infiltration by using box plots. Subsequently, differential expression of M2 marker-associated genes were analyzed the in both risk groups, accompanied by graphical representations [29]. In addition to that, the differences in expression levels of markers associated with exhausted T cells (Tex) were explored between two risk cohorts, presenting the results using box plots for visualization.

Employing the “oncoPredict R package,” we executed an assessment of drug sensitivity by computing IC50 scores for six commonly administered chemotherapeutic agents (5-Fluorouracil, Cisplatin, Docetaxel, Gemcitabine, Gefitinib, Olaparib) across two cohorts. We use the violin diagram to describe the observed differences in response rates [30]. Furthermore, we examined the mRNA expression-based stemness index (mRNAsi) and divided the training set into low and high scoring categories based on mRNAsi scores [26, 27]. Subsequently, we performed OS analysis on the two mRNAsi scoring groups, followed by graphical representation.

Additionally, violin plots were utilized to examine variations in mRNAsi scores between the C1 and C2 subgroups, as well as between two cohorts. To gain deeper insights into the pathways associated with risk categorization, GSEA analysis was conducted across both low-risk and high-risk groups, encompassing six pathways (Hypoxia, Angiogenesis, Epithelial Mesenchymal Transition, Glycolysis, E2f Targets, G2m Checkpoint), highlighting the dysregulation of these pathways. Furthermore, the top three enriched pathways with the highest absolute enrichment scores were visualized in two groups.

Single-cell sequencing data analysis

Quality control was conducted using the criteria of nFeature_RNA < 9000 and percent.mt < 25. We employed the “harmony R package” for batch integration across multiple samples to enhance reliability and comparability. Utilizing the algorithm of Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP), we performed dimensionality reduction clustering on three integrated single-cell datasets, namely GSE162631, GSE84465, and GSE138794 [31]. Subsequently, all cells were annotated and visualized using the “singleR” package. We demonstrate the expression patterns of seven immune - and neuromodulation-related cell types using violin diagrams: Macrophage, Fibroblast, Monocyte, Astrocyte, Neutrophil, Oligodendrocyte, and B cell. These plots incorporated seven genes associated with immune function and nervous system function: APOE, DCN, CD14, AQP4, ELANE, OLIG2, and CD79A [32, 33].The “Copykat” package was employed to identify malignant cells within the glial cell population. Finally, the activity level of our prognostic model in malignant cells was assessed using the AddModuleScore function embedded in the “seurat” package.

Statistical analysis

In this article, we employed R software (version 4.1.3) for all analysis and visualization in this article. We used the “copykat” R package to identify malignant cells. Unless otherwise specified, our figures were generated using “ggplot2”. A p value < 0.05 was deemed statistically significant (* P < 0.05; ** P < 0.01; *** P < 0.001; **** P < 0.0001).

Results

Prognostic analysis and immune infiltration analysis

Initially, we integrated transcriptomic data from TCGA-LGG/GBM and normal control transcriptomic data from GTEx, followed by computation using the “limma” package on TCGA-LGG/GBM. We filtered for tumor overexpressed genes meeting our criteria. Subsequently, we intersected the DEGs with Hippo pathway related genes to obtain genes overexpressed in tumors that are also associated with the Hippo pathway. Finally, we conducted univariate Cox regression analysis, through which we identified 62 genes linked to prognosis. Utilizing these 62 genes, we initially employed “ConsensusClusterPlus” for hierarchical clustering of tumor tissues. When k = 2, the clusters exhibited high cohesion and low coupling. The curve at k = 2 appeared the smoothest, aligning with our requirements, thus, we divided them into two groups, C1 and C2 (Fig. 1A). The PAC curve indicated the lowest value at k = 2 (Fig. 1B and C). Comparatively, the C2 group exhibited a poorer prognosis than the C1 group, as illustrated by the overall survival (OS) curve (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1D). To further visualize these findings, we generated a heatmap portraying the Z-scores of the 62 genes across various groups (Fig. 1E). Additionally, heatmaps representing the analysis of immune cell infiltration abundance and the expression profiles of immune modulators unveiled distinctions between the C1 and C2 groups (Fig. 1F-G).

We conducted IC50 drug sensitivity analysis for C1 and C2 groups separately using six chemotherapeutic drugs (5-Fluorouracil, Cisplatin, Docetaxel, Gemcitabine, Gefitinib, Olaparib). Generating and validating predictive signatures through the utilization of various machine learning techniques

We employed the CGGA_693 and CGGA_325 cohorts as the validation sets, while the TCGA-LGG/GBM served as training cohort. Genes were inputted into union machine learning algorithms constructed from a combination of 10 different methods. Subsequently, we ran the machine learning algorithms on the training cohort and fitted union prediction models using a ten-fold cross-validation framework. The value of C-index for all cohorts were computed for each combination algorithm to compare the predictive capabilities of the models (Fig. 4A). Since the Stepcox[both] + LASSO algorithm shows the highest average C-index in the three sets (average C-index = 0.783), we chose it to develop our prediction model. Discussion

Gliomas are a type of tumor derived from the glial cells of the nervous system, typically occurring within the tissues of the brain and spinal cord. Glial cells serve as supportive elements of the nervous system, responsible for providing structural support and protection to neurons while contributing to the maintenance of normal neurological function. Despite being challenging to detect in early stages, gliomas often progress to advanced stages by the time related symptoms manifest. Each year, gliomas incur substantial losses for patients’ families and societal economies. Despite advancements in medical technology, significant breakthroughs in overall survival rates and treatment outcomes for glioma patients have remained elusive [2, 5, 7]. Various treatment modalities exist for gliomas, including surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and targeted therapy. However, their efficacy is hindered by inconsistent applicability among patient populations and usage scenarios. Enhancing the prognosis and survival outcomes of glioma patients has been a subject of widespread research interest. Hence, the imperative arises to develop a new, resilient prognostic model for assessing disease advancement in patients and directing subsequent treatment at varying stages. Such studies harbor significant research and application potential for glioma treatment and prognosis.

Initially, we integrated transcriptomic data from TCGA-LGG/GBM and normal control transcriptomic data from GTEx. Using logFC > 1 and adj.P < 0.05 as criteria, we identified genes with high expression in tumors. Subsequently, we intersected DEGs with genes related to the Hippo pathway to obtain genes related to the Hippo pathway that are highly expressed in tumors. In the end, our analysis involved univariate Cox regression to pinpoint 62 genes associated with prognosis. Based on these 62 genes, we first performed hierarchical clustering of tumor tissues. When k = 2, the clusters exhibited high cohesion and low coupling, with the curve for 2 being the smoothest, meeting our requirements, so we divided them into two groups, C1 and C2. The curve indicates the lowest value for 2. OS survival curves show that the patients in the C2 group have worse prognosis, while the prognosis of the C1 group is better. We generated a heatmap to display the Z-scores of the 62 genes in different groups. Heatmaps of immune cell infiltration abundance analysis and expression profiles of immune regulatory factors demonstrated differences between the C1 and C2 groups. In our IC50 drug sensitivity analysis for the C1 and C2 groups with six chemotherapy drugs (5-Fluorouracil, Cisplatin, Docetaxel, Gemcitabine, Gefitinib, Olaparib), we observed lower IC50 values for 5-Fluorouracil, Cisplatin, Gemcitabine, and Olaparib in the C2 group, suggesting enhanced treatment efficacy with these drugs in the C2 group. Additionally, we found that the IC50 values of Docetaxel and Gefitinib were lower in the C1 group, suggesting better treatment efficacy for these drugs in C1 patients, further highlighting the differences between these two groups. Analysis of the response rates to two ICBs in the two groups showed a higher response rate in the ips_ctla4_neg_pd1_neg group for the C1 group, indicating poorer treatment efficacy for C1 patients. The response rates in the ips_ctla4_neg_pd1_pos group and ips_ctla4_pos_pd1_neg group were higher, indicating that C1 patients had higher response rates in both pd1 effective and ineffective, and ctla4 effective and ineffective, suggesting poorer treatment efficacy for the C1 group. This suggests differences in therapeutic effect between the C1 and C2 groups. Finally, we selected the C2 grouping for WGCNA analysis and set the soft threshold to 4. We conducted correlation analysis between the nine gene sets obtained from the analysis and clinical factors (futime, fustat, age, cluster), and selected several gene modules most strongly correlated with cluster for further analysis. Subsequently, using MM = 0.6 and GS = 0.3 as criteria, we further screened key genes from the five gene modules. Finally, GO enrichment analysis was performed to observe the status of pathway enrichment based on the identified genes. We found that in the BP category, these gene enrichment pathways are intricately linked to cell signaling. In MF and CC categories, the relevant genes were mainly enriched in pathways related to chromosome segregation, spindle formation, and cell division processes [34]. This indicates the differential and similar enrichment of relevant genes and suggests their roles. This finding warrants further investigation.

For training, we utilized the TCGA-LGG/GBM cohort, while validation was performed on the CGGA_693 and CGGA_325 cohorts. Through 101 machine learning algorithms, incorporating 10 unique methods, gene inputs were processed. From these methods, we chose the Stepcox[both] + LASSO algorithm with the highest average C-index in all cohorts to build our prediction model. Employing the risk scores derived from the model for each sample, we stratified the datasets into high and low-risk groups based on the median and conducted overall survival (OS) prognosis analysis.Results demonstrated inferior prognoses for the high-risk groups across TCGA, CGGA_693, and CGGA_325 cohorts. ROC curve analysis showed that the model has a robust forecasting effect. Box plots demonstrated variations in immune infiltration levels between low and high-risk groups, with the former showing higher levels across several immune-related cell types such as Activated CD4 T cells, Activated B cells and Activated CD8 T cells. Additionally, our research highlighted the beneficial impact of specific cells on tumor immunity, including Activated CD4 + T cells, Activated B cells and Activated CD8 + T cells [35, 36]. Despite their elevated levels in the high-risk group, these cells failed to ameliorate the inferior prognosis associated with this cohort, suggesting potential inhibitory factors impeding tumor immunity efficacy. These findings underscore the distinct immune cell infiltration patterns observed in high and low-risk groups, emphasizing the necessity for customized interventions to stimulate tumor immunity and develop treatments tailored to the immune microenvironment. Analysis of differential expression in M2 marker-related genes (CCL2, CXCR2, CXCR4, IL10, IL6, TGFB1, TGFB2, TGFB3) and Tex-related markers (CXCL13, HAVCR2, LAG3, LAYN, PDCD1, TIGIT) revealed elevated expression levels in the high-risk group. M2 marker-related genes are typically associated with tumor-associated macrophages (M2 phenotype), a critical factor in tumor immune suppression, while Tex-related markers denote genes associated with tolerant T cells in the tumor microenvironment, also implicated in tumor immune evasion. These findings underscore the potential of these cells and markers to attenuate the body’s immune response against tumors, thereby promoting tumor growth, dissemination, and progression [29, 37]. Consequently, devising strategies to mitigate the impact of these immune inhibitory factors is imperative to enhance anti-tumor immune responses when designing immunotherapeutic interventions.

Analyzing the sensitivity of six frequently utilized chemotherapy drugs (5-Fluorouracil, Cisplatin, Docetaxel, Gemcitabine, Gefitinib, Olaparib) in both high and low-risk groups, we unveiled divergent responses among individuals. Notably, 5-fluorouracil, cisplatin, and gemcitabine had higher IC50 scores in the low-risk group, while docetaxel, gefitinib, and olaparib had higher IC50 scores in the high-risk group, whereas the high-risk group displayed higher scores for Docetaxel, Gefitinib, and Olaparib. Given the heterogeneous responses to different chemotherapeutic agents among risk groups, pre-treatment tumor tissue sequencing, coupled with predictive modeling, could inform tailored therapeutic approaches to optimize efficacy, mitigate side effects, and alleviate economic burdens. Moreover, the assessment of mRNA stemness indices (mRNAsi) and subsequent classification into high and low-scoring categories demonstrated enhanced prognosis among those with higher scores, suggesting a link between heightened stem cell gene expression features and better patient outcomes. Compared with the mRNAsi score of group C1 and group C2, the mRNAsi score of group C1 was higher, while the score of low-risk group was lower than that of high-risk group, which highlights the research value of stem cell gene expression characteristics. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) focused on six cell proliferation-related pathways (Glycolysis, Angiogenesis, Epithelial Mesenchymal Transition, G2m Checkpoint, E2f Targets, Hypoxia), revealing upregulation in the high-risk group [38]. In the high-risk group, the NES scores of the three pathways related to immune regulation were the highest, and all were up-regulated. Conversely, in the low-expression group, three pathways associated with neuronal signal transduction, also displaying upregulation, had the lowest NES. These findings underscore distinctive dysregulations across different pathway types in gliomas.

Lastly, employing the UMAP algorithm, we performed dimensionality reduction clustering on integrated single-cell datasets (GSE162631, GSE84465, GSE138794), annotating cells based on marker genes and identifying seven distinct cell types. Violin plots depicted gene expression patterns within immune cells. Malignant cells within gliomas were identified, and our prognostic model demonstrated high activity within these cells, affirming its robust predictive performance.

Mijiti M et al. utilized CRISPR-cas9 screening data and RNA-seq data to identify key genes associated with low-grade glioblastoma and developed a genetic signature related to high-risk low-grade glioblastoma patients [39]. Poma AM et al. investigated the impact of Hippo pathway genes on the overall survival of cancer patients across various cancer types and explored the potential therapeutic roles of these pathways [40]. Konstantin Masliantsev et al. reviewed the role of the Hippo pathway in brain tumors, particularly gliomas, and discussed its potential as a therapeutic target [41]. The innovation of this study lies in its in-depth exploration of the Hippo signaling pathway in gliomas, which is more comprehensive than previous studies. We conducted an integrated analysis using multiple datasets to obtain more thorough and reliable results. Additionally, we developed predictive models using machine learning algorithms to forecast the prognosis of glioma patients and proposed personalized treatment strategies for high-risk and low-risk groups. This approach to personalized therapy for different patient groups is a relatively novel research direction and has the potential to enhance treatment outcomes for glioma patients.

There are some limitations to our study. First, although we used an independent sample to verify hippo path-related siganature, we did not verify its stability due to the lack of sufficient multicenter data. Second, the mechanism of action of mRNAs contained in the interaction network is unclear and requires further study. Third, prospective studies using larger samples in different regions are needed to verify the accuracy of the model.

Conclusion

This study introduces a novel approach for prognostic investigation of gliomas utilizing the Hippo pathway and associated genes. It demonstrates the potential and significance of this method in predicting the prognosis of glioma patients. The findings of this research hold substantial clinical relevance in guiding the treatment and prognosis of glioma patients, underscoring its significant potential for clinical application.

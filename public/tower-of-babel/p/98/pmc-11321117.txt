Prognostic factors in Chinese patients with immunoglobulin light chain amyloidosis: a scoping review and meta-analysis
Authors: Yu Wu, Xiaohong Wang, Xīn Gào, Lingjie Xu, Bin Wang, Zhen Cai
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis scoping review and meta-analysis aimed to map the evidence regarding prognostic factors in Chinese patients with immunoglobulin light chain (AL) amyloidosis and to identify current research gaps.MethodsWe searched EMBASE, PubMed, and CNKI databases from their inception to 15 September 2021. All studies investigated the association between any prognostic factor and target outcomes, including overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and end-stage renal disease (ESRD) in Chinese patients with AL amyloidosis.ResultsThis scoping review included 52 studies, of which 44 with 6,432 patients contributed to the multivariate prognostic analysis. Multivariate analysis identified a total of 106 factors that correlated with OS, 16 factors with PFS, and 18 factors with ESRD. Five prognostic factors were significantly associated with PFS, and 11 prognostic factors were significantly associated with ESRD. Meta-analysis was only available for prognostic factors without heterogeneous cutoff values, for which hazard ratios (HRs) and their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. Meta-analysis showed that bone marrow plasma cells (BMCs) (HR: 1.96, 95% CI: 1.21–3.19, p < 0.05) and interventricular septal thickness (IVST) (HR: 1.23, 95% CI: 1.10–1.38, p < 0.05) were independently associated with OS.ConclusionThe significant prognostic factors associated with OS, PFS, and ESRD in Chinese patients with AL amyloidosis were related to plasma cell tumor load, biological characteristics, cardiac involvement, renal involvement, population characteristics, and treatment. Further studies should explore additional prognostic factors in patients with AL amyloidosis to develop prognostic models.

Introduction

Immunoglobulin light chain (AL) amyloidosis is a protein misfolding disorder characterized by a clonal population of bone marrow plasma cells (BMCs) that produce κ- or λ-type monoclonal light chains [1]. These free light chain (FLC) proteins can be deposited in tissues of various organs, causing multiorgan dysfunction [2], and serum FLC increase many years before the development of AL amyloidosis [3].

Before effective treatment was introduced, AL amyloidosis had a poor prognosis [4]. The number and degree of involvement of the afflicted organs affect the prognosis of AL amyloidosis [5]. The Mayo 2012 staging system is regular used to evaluate the prognosis in clinical practice [6]. The Mayo 2004 staging scheme is suggested for individuals with cardiac involvement, and for those with renal involvement, the renal prognostic staging system is recommended [7]. Over the past few decades, existing narrative reviews have identified cardiac involvement biomarkers as critical prognostic factors in patients with systemic AL amyloidosis [8]. A guideline for cardiac amyloidosis by European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Working Group is suggested for individuals with cardiac involvement [9]. Patients with untreated or non-responding cardiac AL have a six-month median survival time [10,11]. It has been demonstrated that echocardiographic wall thickening, diastolic relaxation abnormalities, right ventricular dysfunction with valve thickness, and echocardiographic wall thickening are all related to prognosis [1]. Among the cardiac involvement biomarkers, serum troponins (I and T) and B-type natriuretic peptides (BNP and NT-proBNP) has been widely practiced in the clinic because these are strong predictors of survival [12]. In addition, studies have also found that free light chain and clonal plasma cells [13–15] are associated with overall survival in patients with AL amyloidosis.

New studies have continued to identify which prognostic factors are linked to clinical outcomes to support future drug development for this disorder. However, existing evidence summaries of prognostic factors are primarily poor-quality narrative reviews. To our knowledge, there is lack of systematic summary of prognostic factors in AL amyloidosis within the Chinese population. Therefore, we conducted a scoping review to systematically map the available evidence regarding these prognostic indicators in this patient population and to identify any existing knowledge gaps [16].

Methods

This scoping review followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) Statement and extensions [17]. We registered the protocol for this scoping review in the INPLASY (registration number: INPLASY2021120124).

Search strategy

We searched the electronic databases EMBASE and MEDLINE via OvidSP and PubMed, from inception to 15 September 2021, using search terms such as ‘amyloidosis’, ‘AL amyloidosis’ and ‘China,’ with no limitations on the type of documents retrieved. The detailed search strategy for each database is presented in the Supplementary Materials (search strategies).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Studies were included if they fulfilled the following criteria: (1) published in Chinese or English journals since 2010; (2) were retrospective or prospective observational studies; (3) included Chinese patients with AL amyloidosis. China was defined as China mainland, Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan; and (4) investigated the association between prognostic factors and target outcomes using multivariate regression analysis. Target outcomes included overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and end-stage renal disease (ESRD). Case reports, reviews, consensus, theses, and questionnaires were excluded.

Data extraction

Two reviewers independently extracted data from each study using a standardized data extraction form. The senior author was responsible for resolving any discrepancies between authors. The following information was extracted: study characteristics (year of publication and study design), patient characteristics (sample size, conducted site, and organ involvement), description of prognostic factors and the effect estimate, and covariates in the main model of the outcome.

Quality assessment of included studies

Two reviewers independently completed the methodological quality assessment using the QUIPS (Quality in Prognosis Studies) tool [18]. The QUIPS tool includes six domains: (1) study participation; (2) study attrition; (3) prognostic factor measurements; (4) outcome assessment; (5) study confounding; and (6) statistical analyses and reporting. Each domain was scored as low, high, or unclear risk of bias. The senior author resolved any discrepancies between authors.

Data analyses

This scoping review included a quantitative descriptive analysis of the key clinical prognostic factors and target outcomes. For quantitative descriptive analysis, we used hazard ratios (HRs) with a 95% confidence interval (CI) to summarize the association between prognostic factors and OS, PFS, or ESRD. Based on the statistical significance of the association, we divided all prognostic factors tested by multivariate analysis into two categories: significant factors (p < 0.05) and non-significant factors (p ≥ 0.05), depending on the description in the included primary studies. Clinically, we classified prognostic factors into five categories: population-related factors, plasma cell tumor load, biological characteristic-related factors, renal-related factors, cardiac-related factors, and digestive system-related factors [19]. Meta-analysis was only available for prognostic factors without heterogeneous cutoff values, and the hazard ratio and its 95% confidence interval were reported. For the association between the important clinical prognostic factors (including age, serum creatinine, interventricular septal thickness (IVST), early diastolic mitral valve (E) to peak velocity in early diastolic mitral annulus (e) ratio (E/e’), hypotension, and BMC and OS, we conducted random effects model meta-analyses in RevMan 5.3 Software. HRs with 95% CIs were used for the effect estimates, and I2 > 50% indicated significant heterogeneity. We only analyzed the data from studies using multivariate analysis to test the association between important clinical prognostic factors and OS.

Results

Selection of sources of evidence

We retrieved 891 articles, 757 of which remained after removing duplicates (Figure 1). We then excluded 510 articles after reviewing the title, abstract, or full text. Of the 52 eligible articles, 44 reported results from the multivariate regression analysis and were included in this scoping review [20–63].

PRISMA flow diagram of identifying literature.

Overview of evidence

The quality assessment of the included studies is shown in Supplementary Material (Table S1). The overall risk of bias was rated as low for 10 studies and as moderate for the rest of the studies included. Table 1 presents the main characteristics and findings of the multivariate regression analysis and shows the association between prognostic factors and OS, PFS, and ESRD progression reflected by the HRs (95% CIs). The included studies were all longitudinal studies with varying sample sizes ranging from 27 to 1,064 (2 studies did not report the sample size). Eighteen studies were conducted in Beijing, followed by 10 studies in Nanjing, 6 studies in Chengdu, and 5 studies in Xi’an. Ten and 34 studies were published in Chinese and English, respectively. Forty-four studies investigated 140 prognostic factors using multivariate analyses of OS (n = 106), PFS (n = 16), and ESRD (n = 18).

Patient characteristics

Patient characteristics are summarized in Supplementary Material (Tables S2 and Table S3). The median age ranged from 51.5 to 60.0 years. Males accounted for 62.5% of the included patients. The major light chain type was lambda (77.57%). In 14 studies, 24.5%, 33.7%, and 41.4% of patients were classified as Mayo (2004) cardiac stage I, II, and III, respectively. Based on 13 studies, Mayo (2012) reported that cardiac stages I, II, III, and IV accounted for 33.5%, 27.2%, 25.7%, and 13.6% of cases, respectively. The kidney was the most frequently affected organ (79.2%), followed by the heart (66.6%), soft tissue (25.6%), gastrointestinal tract (24.2%), liver (18.4%), and nerves (10.8%).

OS

Figure 2 shows the research hotspots of prognostic factors associated with OS, mainly in three categories: echocardiography- or MRI-related factors, the Mayo staging system, and NT-proBNP. The association between 106 factors and OS was investigated in detail in 39 studies. The levels of Mayo staging (2004 or 2012) (HR range: 2.45-13.5) were the most frequently investigated prognostic factors. Pfs/esrd

Three studies demostrated that five factors were significantly associated with PFS: the levels of dFLC, ≥1 organ involvement, liver involvement, very good partial response (VGPR), and proportion of BMCs (Table 1). For ESRD, three studies demonstrated that serum creatine, hypotension, 24-hour urine protein, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), galectin-3, and dFLC were statistically significant prognostic factors (Table 1).

Discussion

Key findings

This scoping review identified 44 longitudinal studies that included 6,432 Chinese patients with AL amyloidosis. We investigated five categories—population-related factors, plasma cell tumor load and biological characteristic-related factors, renal-related factors, cardiac-related factors, and digestive system-related factors—and identified 140 ­prognostic factors which were statistically significant. Six prognostic factors, dFLC, BMCs, E/A ratio, RV/GLS, sST2, and pseudo-infarction, were consistently and significantly associated with OS. The levels of dFLC, ≥1 organ involvement, liver involvement, VGPR, and proportion of BMCs, were significantly associated with PFS, while eleven prognostic factors were significantly associated with ESRD.

Comparison with other studies

One narrative review investigated prognostic factors in patients with AL amyloidosis and found that quantification and monitoring of dFLC have been validated for risk stratification in this population [79,80]. Our study showed that dFLC is the most investigated FLC-related marker and confirmed the prognostic value of dFLC for OS in initially diagnosed Chinese patients with AL amyloidosis. A narrative review reported that BMCs can also be used as a prognostic marker in AL amyloidosis [81]. In our study, BMCs were a consistently significant prognostic factor for OS and PFS.

It has been reported that the severity of cardiac dysfunction in AL amyloidosis is a major determinant of morbidity and mortality [82]. The Mayo staging system, which combines three biomarkers cTnT, NT-proBNP, and dFLC [5] is commonly used to evaluate the severity of cardiac dysfunction and the prognosis of patients with AL amyloidosis. In our study, the Mayo staging system showed statistically significant associations with OS and PFS. The NYHA classification was initially developed as a risk stratification tool for heart failure and is commonly used as a prognostic factor in AL amyloidosis [83]. Our study found a strong association between the NYHA classification and OS (HR range: 2.25–6.91). The existing literature frequently reports IVST, E/A ratio, and right ventricular global longitudinal strain (RV-GLS) as important prognostic factors in patients with AL amyloidosis [84]. Our scoping review found that the E/A ratio and RV-GLS were consistently and statistically significant prognostic factors for OS in patients with AL amyloidosis. The pooled HR for IVST was also a significant prognostic factor.

In accordance with the prognostic value of organ involvement in AL amyloidosis [85], renal involvement has been reported as a prognostic factor. We found that renal markers were less frequently investigated than cardiac markers.

Implications

We systematically reviewed the prognostic factors for AL amyloidosis and summarized the common prognostic factors from many publications, and these prognostic factors validated in multiple studies could be used in conjunction with existing systems to achieve a more accurate prediction of prognosis. 22 factors were conflicting prognostic factors of OS in our review, more studies with large sample size are needed in the future.

At the meantime, there are potential implications of the findings from the current study. First, this study showed that the dFLC cutoff varied widely across the included studies, ranging from 50 mg/L to 500 mg/L, and resulted in large differences in HRs. For instance, when setting a cutoff value of 162.9 mg/L, one study with a relatively small sample size of 30 patients reported the levels of dFLC had the strongest predictive capability for OS (HR = 12.13). Another included study with 1,064 patients reported a HR of 1.63 for OS when comparing patients with dFLC ≥100 mg/L to patients with dFLC < 100 mg/L [86]. Therefore, a looming question is which clinically practical criteria for dFLC levels should be set to precisely stratify patients with AL amyloidosis. Second, when NT-proBNP was used as a continuous variable, seven studies showed it to be a statistically significant predictor of OS. However, studies that dichotomized NT-proBNP, (NT-proBNP, NT-proBNP ≥434.7 pg/ml, and NT-proBNP ≥1800 pg/ml) were not. Clinicians might consider NT-proBNP to be an important prognostic factor, but caution should be exercised against dichotomization. Further studies should be conducted to better define the NT-proBNP cutoff value.

The most effective prediction models for clinical use should have low variable counts, high statistical performance, and ease of interpretation. However, the currently accepted staging system still has certain shortcomings. For example, it ignores the fact that pacemaker stimulation, atrial arrhythmia, and renal dysfunction can all affect serum levels of NT-proBNP, raising concerns about the validity of these biomarkers in subgroups that share these concomitant conditions [87,88]. Even though atrial arrhythmias and poor renal function are common in AL amyloidosis, none of these were taken into consideration while the staging system was being developed. As a result, this factor must be taken into account while researching future prognostic systems. Furthermore, other promising and commonly used biomarkers may be included in the model in future research. Compared to natriuretic peptide and troponin, blood levels of sST2 discovered in this previous study are less impacted by obesity, advanced age, and chronic kidney disease, which may be a good prognostic factor in clinical practice [89]. In addition, we found no prognostic studies for AL amyloidosis with specific organ involvement, and suggested to investigate prognostic factors in further studies to direct clinical management for different organ involvement.

Strengths and limitations

This scoping review has several strengths. First, this is the first comprehensive scoping review that includes all prognostic studies on Chinese patients. Second, our analysis only included studies that conducted multivariate-adjusted regression analyses, adjusting for other prognostic factors. increasing the credibility of our findings. Nonetheless, one limitation is the sole inclusion of Chinese patients with AL amyloidosis, which limits the generalizability of our findings to a broader population. In addition, since the majority of the included studies were retrospective without rigorous design, the credibility of the overall results might be reduced.

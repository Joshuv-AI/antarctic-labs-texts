Future directions in combating outbreaks at CEPI Centralized Laboratory Network
Authors: Ali Azizi, Gathoni Kamuyu, Peter Spencer, Valentina Bernasconi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe first CEPI Centralized Laboratory Network (CLN) meeting held in Rome in July 2024 gathered 67 experts from 27 global institutions, including CLN facilities, alongside key collaborators such as the World Health Organization (WHO), European Commission, US Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority (BARDA), Gates Foundation, United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF), PATH, and the Pasteur Network. The meeting showcased the network’s achievements in standardizing immunological assays for emerging diseases, particularly SARS-CoV-2, while addressing challenges in assay development, validation, and technology transfer. Discussions focused on improving access to reagents, harmonizing protocols, and overcoming logistical and quality related barriers. The meeting also concentrated on preparedness and sustainability, aligning the network’s goals with CEPI’s 100-Day Mission to rapidly develop assays for Disease X. Topics such as rapid testing capabilities, high-throughput technologies, strategic partnerships, and communications between facilities were key highlights in the effort to tackle future outbreaks. Since the meeting, CEPI-CLN have actively addressed the feedback and suggestions, leading to improvements in the facilities’ quality systems, better high-throughput assays, more proactive technology transfer strategies, and stronger partnerships. These enhancements ensure that the network remains adaptable and responsive to global health emergencies, further cementing its role in advancing standardized immunological assays for rare emerging diseases.KEYWORDS: Vaccines, outbreak, assays, immunogenicity, CEPI

Introduction

The CEPI-Centralized Laboratory Network (CLN) was established in 2020 in response to the COVID-19 pandemic, addressing significant challenges in the standardization of immunological assay design, test platforms, viral targets, and quality systems to support clinical testing.1 The CLN’s primary mission is to standardize immunological assays and provide testing support to vaccine developers, aiming to identify immune correlates of protection and facilitate the approval of vaccines for emerging infectious diseases.2 As the largest global consortium focused on immunogenicity assessment for high-risk pathogens with epidemic and pandemic potential, including SARS-CoV-2, SARS-CoV-1, MERS, Mpox, Marburg, Sudan Ebola, Lassa, Nipah, and pandemic flu, the network has become instrumental in global preparedness efforts.3–7 The CLN currently includes 20 facilities worldwide (Figure 1) that collaborate using standardized reagents, equipment, and protocols, ensuring consistency and harmonization of results. During the COVID-19 pandemic, the network focused on development and validation of standardized immunological assays, the transfer of technology across facilities, and the creation of a centralized database for secure data sharing. Over 60 vaccine developers have been supported, and more than 120,000 assays have been performed, significantly contributing to the global vaccine response to COVID-19.3–6 Beyond its technical capabilities, the CLN is focused on promoting knowledge exchange and fostering innovation in the development of standardized assays against priority emerging diseases. This includes sharing the best practices for assay development, qualification, and technology transfer across facilities, addressing knowledge gaps, and gathering feedback to inform future activities. The network also plays a key role in CEPI’s broader goals, such as its 100-Day Mission (https://cepi.net/cepi-20-and-100-days-mission) to accelerate vaccine development and response to Disease X.

CEPI-CLN map. The CLN today includes 20 facilities worldwide, 4 in Americas, 4 in Europe, 5 in Africa, 6 in Asia and 1 in Oceania, focusing on different pathogens with pandemic potential.

To strengthen global preparedness and response, the CLN aims to enhance partnerships with other global health organizations, evaluate and refine existing processes based on past outbreak response efforts, and develop strategies for improved coordination and communication across facilities. Efforts are also underway to ensure long-term sustainability and the ability to respond rapidly to future health emergencies. In addition to its technical and operational goals, the CLN emphasizes networking and communication. Facilitating in-person interactions and building personal relationships among facilities is essential for strengthening professional relationships and fostering team spirit. The network also promotes cultural exchange, understanding, and collaboration with other CEPI networks, such as animal model (pre-clinical), clinical, and manufacturing networks, to improve overall coordination and response capabilities.

Building on its past successes and expanding its scope, the CEPI CLN convened a meeting in Rome on 9–10 July 2024 with representatives from all facilities and key collaborators, to enhance collective preparedness and strengthen contributions to addressing future outbreaks and global health threats. Since then, CLN has focused on refining its strategies, streamlining processes, and ensuring that the network is able to tackle emerging health threats with greater agility and efficiency. These efforts, discussed in the following sections, aim to strengthen the network’s ability to respond to future outbreaks and enhance global health security.

Meeting overview: enhancing assay development, validation, and transfer for global health preparedness

The first day of the CLN meeting focused on the critical steps involved in the development, validation, and transfer of immunological assays. Discussions highlighted the challenges related to timely reagent supply and access to critical materials, particularly during the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic. Experts from various organizations shared best practices to accelerate assay transfers and address logistical hurdles, emphasizing the importance of pre-transfer validation, gap analysis, and clearly defined responsibilities between reference and receiving laboratories. A panel discussion further explored issues such as reagent access, quality control in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), and the need to establish regional reagent hubs. The importance of high-throughput technologies and harmonized sample collection protocols to support rapid assay development, particularly in the context of emerging pathogens like Disease X, was also underscored. A hands-on group exercise allowed participants to engage in a collaborative discussion about challenges in assay technology transfer, focusing on areas like reagent supply, training, and regulatory approvals. Key takeaways included the need for more efficient central warehousing for reagents, continuous post-transfer support, and streamlining import and export processes and approvals in LMICs. Presentations on WHO international antibody standards, CEPI’s efforts in standardizing assay methodologies, and maintaining high-quality standards across the network were followed by discussions on biosecurity strategies to ensure secure handling of biological materials. These sessions emphasized the importance of adherence to stringent protocols and the role of CEPI in maintaining global standards for assay validation and development.

Day 2 shifted focus to preparing the CLN for future outbreaks and ensuring its long-term sustainability. Presentations highlighted how the CLN aligns with CEPI’s 100-Day Mission to develop vaccines for Disease X, with a focus on improving global infrastructure and strengthening assay processes. The importance of forming strategic partnerships to accelerate vaccine development and enhance high-throughput capabilities was discussed, as was the need for continuous innovation in assay technologies to support rapid response during outbreaks. Discussions on disease-specific assay needs focused on the challenges of sourcing reagents, particularly for pathogens like Sudan Ebola and Marburg. Presenters discussed the need for specialized facilities and the variability in the quality of proteins used in assays, stressing the importance of consistency across different reagent lots. Risk management in vaccine and assay development was addressed, with an emphasis on parallel development efforts, harmonized clinical trials, and the role of immune assays in establishing correlates of protection.

In a group exercise simulating responses to an outbreak, participants discussed how the CLN could assist in securing immunological testing and providing logistical support. This exercise fostered collaboration, allowing participants to explore various strategies for outbreak readiness and how the CLN can contribute to expediting vaccine development and testing during emergencies.

Implemented strategies for sustainability and collaboration following the meeting

Following the meeting, the actions raised have been followed up. The questions and actions raised were discussed with various groups and departments within CEPI, including Laboratory Research and Innovation, Cybersecurity, Business Development, Biosecurity, and R&D Analytical and Planning. Following internal meetings and deliberations, a few new strategies and initiatives were developed, focusing on the sustainability of the CLN, enhancing cybersecurity and Biosecurity, and creating self-sustaining laboratories in LMICs capable of independently conducting sample collecting and performing assays for clinical trials. Additionally, it was agreed to recruit a few recognized vendors through a call-for proposal to share reagents and supplies with the network, providing logistical support to ensure seamless operations. In 2025, CEPI published a position paper (https://static.cepi.net/downloads/2025-03/CEPI%20GxP%20Quality%20and%20Compliance%20expectations%20and%20requirements.pdf) on the quality and compliance expectations for awardees, which articulates the good clinical laboratory practice expectations for the analysis of clinical trial samples. This document serves as a framework for targeted investments in the quality management systems of CLN members, for example software upgrades, enabling further harmonization of quality practices. Alongside these efforts, strategies were implemented to improve knowledge sharing and enhance the network’s overall capacity (e.g. implementing all-hands meeting between all facilities every 4–6 months, meeting with other global organizations such as GATES, PATH, and others). To further improve networking, communication, and partnerships within the lab network, a Global CLN Advisory Council (GCAC) was also established. Members were selected through a competitive process, with six representatives chosen from the 18 facilities (IVI and KDCA from South Korea, as subawardees, and INRB in the Democratic Republic of Congo, as a collaborative facility, have joined CEPI-CLN, expanding the network to 20 facilities). The GCAC serves as a liaison between all facilities and CEPI, supporting the CEPI-CLN team in addressing urgent issues, providing guidance on assay standardization and harmonization, and strengthening communication and collaboration to streamline processes. These new policies and implementations have been communicated across all facilities and partners through virtual communications and all-hands meetings. As a leading funder of vaccine research, CEPI has a critical responsibility to ensure that work is performed safely and securely. To this end, the first CEPI biosecurity strategy (https://static.cepi.net/downloads/2024-09/CEPI%20Biosecurity%20Strategy_Sep%202024_Online%20Version.pdf) was published shortly after the face-to-face meeting. The strategy notes the substantial variations in biosafety and biosecurity regulations between countries and proposes using the CLN as a mechanism to accelerate the adoption of common global standards. This demonstrates both CEPI’s commitment to responsible research and the further potential for CLN as a world-leader in harmonization of best practices.

Conclusion

The first CLN face-to-face meeting, held in July 2024 underscored the importance of harmonizing immunological assays, streamlining technology transfer processes, and ensuring robust validation protocols across all facilities. Following the meeting, discussions within CEPI emphasized the need for CLN to develop self-sustaining laboratories capable of operating independently during public health emergencies.

Several strategic initiatives were launched to further enhance CLN capabilities, including exploring the integration of new technologies, improving quality management systems, and strengthening biosecurity and cybersecurity in LMIC facilities. In addition, fostering greater collaboration between facilities emerged as a key strategy for accelerating readiness against emerging pathogens.

To optimize logistical processes, including reagent procurement and infrastructure support, we identified solutions such as centralizing reagent supplies and refining technology transfer protocols. The meeting also underscored the value of collaborative problem-solving across the network. As a result, a Global Advisory Committee was established, bringing together representatives from CLN facilities to address critical issues and propose actionable strategies to enhance network operations, support, and preparedness.

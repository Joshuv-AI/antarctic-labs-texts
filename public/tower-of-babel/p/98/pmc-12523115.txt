Exploring the landscape of circular RNAs in the development of skeletal muscle in Huaxi cattle
Authors: Xin Hu, Fugui Jiang, Haijian Cheng, Wei You, Yahui Wang, Junya Li, Lupei Zhang, Enliang Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHuaxi Cattle is a newly developed beef cattle breed in China with independent intellectual property rights. It exhibits internationally advanced levels of daily weight gain, slaughter rate, and net meat yield. Although circular RNA (circRNA) expression has been shown to be highly tissue- and species-specific, the circRNA profiles across different developmental stages of Huaxi cattle muscle remain poorly characterized.ResultsIn the study, we collected longissimus dorsi muscle samples from newborn (NB), young (YG), and adult (AD) Huaxi cattle for RNA sequencing (RNA-seq). A total of 28,013 circRNAs were identified. Differential expression (DE) analysis revealed 633, 41, and 578 DE circRNAs in the YG vs. NB, AD vs. YG, and AD vs. NB comparison groups, respectively. In the AD vs. YG group, BP terms were primarily associated with limb morphogenesis, whereas in the AD vs. NB group, BP terms were mostly linked to muscle development. In the AD vs. YG group, pathways such as FoxO signaling, calcium signaling, and RNA transport were enriched. Background

The beef cattle industry represents a vital sector within China’s livestock industry, playing a pivotal role in the nation’s economy and agricultural development [1]. As a newly developed specialized beef cattle breed in China, Huaxi Cattle have gained increasing recognition due to their rapid growth rate, high slaughter yield, and excellent net meat yield [2]. Improving the quality of Huaxi beef while increasing production has been a primary focus of breeders.

Skeletal muscle is composed of multinucleated muscle fibers formed by myocytes. It accounts for approximately 40–60% of the body weight in adult mammals and is primarily involved in movement and energy metabolism [3]. Skeletal muscle originates from the embryonic somites, with the dorsal portion developing into dermatomal muscle segments, which then form myogenic muscle segments [4]. These muscle progenitor cells differentiate into myoblasts in response to various signaling pathways and regulatory factors [5]. Myoblast differentiation requires their exit from the cell cycle, transitioning from a proliferative state to a differentiated state, ultimately leading to muscle fiber formation [6]. Skeletal muscle development is divided into prenatal and postnatal stages. Prenatally, muscle formation mainly results from the proliferation of myoblasts and increases in cell size. After birth, the number of myofibers remains relatively constant, and muscle growth depends primarily on the lengthening and volumetric growth of myofibers [7]. Given that the number, diameter, and composition of muscle fibers are closely linked to muscle yield and meat quality, the growth and development of skeletal muscle directly affect the animal’s meat production performance [8, 9].

In recent years, extensive research has highlighted the gene regulatory roles of circRNAs in skeletal muscle development [10]. Unlike linear RNAs, circRNAs are covalently closed, lacking the conventional 5’ cap and 3’ poly-A tail structures [11]. This unique structure confers enhanced stability, resistance to RNase R degradation, and extended half-lives [12]. CircRNAs exhibit tissue- and developmentally specific expression patterns [13]. For instance, Yue et al. [14] employed high-throughput circRNA sequencing to demonstrate differential expression of circHUWE1 in Qinchuan cattle at 90 days and 24 months of age. Their study revealed that circHUWE1 competitively binds miR-29b, thereby releasing AKT3 and activating the AKT signaling pathway, which regulates proliferation, apoptosis, and differentiation in bovine primary muscle cells. Similarly, Wang et al. [15] identified circTTN as differentially expressed in fetal and adult cattle through NCBI database analysis. They showed that circTTN sponges miR-432, leading to activation of the IGF2/PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. Additionally, pre-sequencing data indicated differential expression of circSGCB in fetal and adult muscle tissues. Bovine enhancer-regulated circSGCB functions as a molecular sponge, binding to bta-miR-27a-3p, which in turn modulates KLF3 expression and impacts skeletal muscle development [16]. Collectively, these studies underscore the potential significance of circRNAs in the skeletal muscle development of Huaxi cattle.

Currently, the regulation of skeletal muscle development by circRNAs in Huaxi cattle remains unexplored. In this study, we collected longissimus dorsi muscle samples from Huaxi cattle at three developmental stages: newborn, youth, and adulthood. RNA sequencing was performed on these samples to identify key circRNAs involved in skeletal muscle growth. GO and KEGG analyses were employed to elucidate the functional roles of these circRNAs in muscle development. Additionally, we constructed a circRNA-miRNA interaction network to identify potential regulatory relationships. Several circRNAs were randomly selected for validation. These results advance our understanding of circRNA expression dynamics during skeletal muscle development in Huaxi cattle and provide a basis for future functional studies.

Result

Overview of circRNA deep sequencing data

To identify the circRNA profile during skeletal muscle development in Huaxi cattle, longissimus dorsi muscle samples from three male beef cattle at newborn, young, and adult stages were used for RNA sequencing after rRNA depletion. A total of 695,444,064 raw reads and 676,826,996 clean reads were generated from 9 samples (NB, YG and AD). The percentage of the clean base with Q20 and Q30 scores exceeded 97.27% and 92.93%, respectively. The GC content of the samples range from 62.46 to 70.96% (Table S1). Compared to reference genome (ARS-UCD1.2), the mapping ratio for each sample was higher than 73.82%. These results confirm that both the samples and sequencing data are of high quality and reliability.

Identification of circRNAs in cattle longissimus dorsi muscle

A total of 28,013 circRNAs were detected from the 9 muscle tissue samples (Table S2). To generate a reliable set of circRNAs, only those expressed in at least three samples were retained. This approach resulted in 15,505 circRNAs, originating from 4,337 cattle genes (Table S3). These circRNAs were distributed among chromosomes 1 to 29, X, and the non-standard chromosomes NKLS02000910.1, NKLS02002208.1 and NKLS02002210.1 (Fig. 1A). Based on their genomic loci, we found that the majority (83.01%) of circRNAs originated from coding exons (Fig. 1B). The length distribution of these circRNAs was relatively concentrated, with most ranging from 34 to 1,888 nucleotides (Fig. 1C).

Differential expression analysis of circRNAs in cattle longissimus dorsi muscle

We identified 971 DE circRNAs in pairwise comparisons between skeletal muscle libraries from the three developmental stages (|log2FC| >1, Q < 0.05) (Table S4). In the YG vs. NB comparison, 633 DE circRNAs were identified, with 381 upregulated and 252 downregulated. The AD vs. YG comparison revealed only 41 DE circRNAs, of which 28 were upregulated and 13 downregulated. In the AD vs. NB comparison, 578 DE circRNAs were detected, with 325 upregulated and 253 downregulated (Fig. 1D). Heatmap (Fig. 1E) analysis indicated that circRNA expression patterns in the AD and YG groups were similar, whereas they differed from those in the NB group. Four overlapped DE circRNAs (novel_circ_0022687, novel_circ_0022663, novel_circ_0022511 and novel_circ_0018908) were found to overlap across all three comparison groups (Fig. 1F).

Functional analysis of circRNAs during muscle development

To explore the potential function of circRNAs, we conducted GO and KEGG analysis on the host genes of DE circRNAs in the YG vs. NB, AD vs. YG, and AD vs. NB comparison groups. In the YG vs. NB group, the top 12 GO terms for biological processes included cellular macromolecule metabolic processes, regulation of primary metabolic processes, macromolecule modification, nucleic acid metabolic processes, and cellular protein metabolic processes (Fig. 2A, Table S5). In the AD vs. YG group, the top 12 GO terms were related to appendage and limb morphogenesis, as well as appendage development, limb development, and hindlimb morphogenesis (Fig. 2B, Table S5). In the AD vs. NB group, the top 12 GO terms included positive regulation of cellular metabolic processes, muscle cell differentiation, muscle cell development, cytoskeleton organization, striated muscle cell differentiation, muscle fiber development, and positive regulation of DNA biosynthetic processes (Fig. 2C, Table S5).

The top 14 pathways from the KEGG enrichment analysis are presented in Fig. 3 and Table S6. In the YG vs. NB group, significant pathways included adherens junction, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), focal adhesion, tight junction, and dilated cardiomyopathy (Fig. 3A). In the AD vs. YG group, key pathways included FoxO signaling pathway, calcium signaling pathway, RNA transport, mRNA surveillance pathway and lysine degradation (Fig. 3B). In the AD vs. NB group, the significant pathways were adherens junction, propanoate metabolism, MAPK signaling pathway, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), focal adhesion and mTOR signaling pathway. Additionally, tight junctions were found to play a key role in muscle development (Fig. 3C).

Trend analysis of circRNAs during muscle development

To elucidate the dynamic expression patterns of circRNAs during muscle development, we performed a STEM (Short Time-series Expression Miner) trend analysis to categorize circRNAs based on their expression profiles. Among the 16 distinct expression patterns identified, five profiles (profiles 0, 4, 11, 14, and 15) showed significant enrichment (Fig. 4A). Functional annotation via KEGG pathway analysis revealed that circRNAs in the downregulated profiles (profiles 0 and 4) were predominantly associated with key signaling pathways, including the MAPK signaling, cell cycle regulation, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), adherens junction, and the Rap1 signaling pathway. Specifically, profile 0 showed significant enrichment in the MAPK signaling, HCM, and Rap1 pathways, while profile 4 was notably enriched in the cell cycle and adherens junction pathways. Conversely, circRNAs in the upregulated profiles (profiles 11, 14, and 15) were significantly enriched in pathways related to cellular metabolism and signaling transduction, including protein processing in the endoplasmic reticulum, mTOR signaling, vitamin digestion and absorption, butanoate and propanoate metabolism, as well as the insulin and Notch signaling pathways (Fig. 4B, Table S7). These findings suggest that circRNAs play distinct functional roles in regulating molecular pathways during different phases of muscle development.

CircRNA-miRNA networks

We identified four overlapping DE circRNAs across the three comparison groups, with only novel_circ_0018908 located on the CLIC5 (chloride intracellular channel 5) gene on chromosome 23. The other three circRNAs (novel_circ_0022687, novel_circ_0022663 and novel_circ_0022511) were located in the intergenic regions of chromosome 27. Novel_circ_0018908 is a 340pb circRNA encoded by three exons of the CLIC5 gene (Fig. 5A). Furthermore, convergent and divergent primer amplification, RNase R treatment, and RT-qPCR confirmed the existence and differential expression of novel_circ_0018908 during muscle development (Fig. 5B-E). Therefore, we speculate that novel_circ_0018908 may be a key circRNA involved in muscle development in beef cattle. We predicted the miRNAs that binds to novel_circ_0018908 and identified 16 miRNAs, including bta-miR-10171-3p, bta-miR-12,017, bta-miR-12,028, bta-miR-12,043, bta-miR-12,055, bta-miR-12,059, bta-miR-146a, bta-miR-146b, bta-miR-2420, bta-miR-2450c, bta-miR-3154, bta-miR-502b, bta-miR-6523a, bta-miR-763, bta-miR-7691 and bta-miR-873. We constructed a circRNA-miRNA interaction network for novel_circ_0018908 and these miRNAs (Fig. 5F, Table S8). Subsequently, KEGG enrichment analysis of the target genes of these miRNAs revealed significant enrichment in the insulin signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, Ras signaling pathway, metabolic pathways, signaling pathways regulating pluripotency of stem cells, mTOR signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, and Wnt signaling pathway (Fig. 5G, Table S9).

Validation of circRNAs

To confirm the back-splice junctions of six randomly chosen circRNAs, we used agarose gel electrophoresis, Sanger sequencing, and qRT-PCR (Fig. 6). Divergent and convergent primers were designed for each circRNA, with both genomic DNA (gDNA) and complementary DNA (cDNA) serving as PCR templates. Divergent primers successfully amplified the expected fragments from cDNA, but no amplification occurred from gDNA, confirming the presence of back-splice junctions (Fig. 6A). These findings were further validated by Sanger sequencing (Fig. 6B). The stability of the circRNAs was assessed by evaluating their resistance to RNase R digestion. The circRNAs demonstrated significantly greater resistance to RNase R degradation than linear mRNA (Fig. 6 C). Moreover, the expression patterns of the circRNAs observed in RNA-seq and qRT-PCR were consistent (Fig. 6D), further supporting the reliability of the circRNAs identified in this study.

Discussion

Research on the mechanisms of skeletal muscle development is critical for the genetic improvement of both meat quality and yield in livestock. Over the past few decades, numerous genes closely associated with skeletal muscle development have been identified, including Pax7, Myf5, MyoD, MyoG, MRF4 and MyHC [17]. With advancements in bioinformatics, studies on non-coding RNAs involved in skeletal muscle growth have expanded significantly in recent years [18]. Among these, circRNAs play essential and diverse roles in skeletal muscle development.

In the present study, we identified 15,505 circRNAs in the skeletal muscle of Huaxi beef cattle across three growth stages (newborn, young, adult). A total of 633, 41, and 578 DE-circRNAs were identified in the YG vs. NB, AD vs. YG, and AD vs. NB comparisons, respectively. The number of DE-circRNAs was higher during the early growth phase of Huaxi beef cattle than in the later stages of growth and development. This finding is consistent with studies on longissimus dorsi muscle of Large White and Mashen pigs, which reported 662 and 331 DE-circRNAs between 1 and 90 days of age, respectively, compared to only 72 and 76 DE-circRNAs between 90 and 180 days of age [19]. A similar trend has also been observed in other animals, such as chickens [20]. Collectively, these studies suggest that circRNA-mediated gene expression regulation is more active during the early growth phase than in later stages of development. GO enrichment analysis of the host genes of DE circRNAs identified several BP terms related to muscle development, including muscle cell differentiation, muscle cell development, cytoskeleton organization, striated muscle cell differentiation, muscle fiber development, nucleic acid metabolic process, and cellular protein metabolic process. Research has shown that postnatal muscle development primarily depends on muscle fiber hypertrophy [21], which involves the increase in myofibrils and the incorporation of additional nuclei into muscle fibers [22]. These processes are regulated by the turnover of muscle-associated nucleic acids and proteins, explaining the significant enrichment of metabolism-related biological processes. Similarly, studies in cattle [23], goat [24] and chicken [25] have demonstrated that nutrient metabolism, including amino acid biosynthesis, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, and the TCA cycle, plays a critical role in postnatal muscle development. KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that the top 14 enriched pathways included hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), focal adhesion, tight junction, FoxO signaling pathway and RNA transport. Focal adhesion has been found to correlate with specific developmental states in myoblast differentiation, muscle fiber formation and muscle size in response to loading and unloading [26, 27]. Myosin and actin form a dense ring at the adherens junction and tight junction levels, with actomyosin contraction implicated in tight junction regulation [28]. Extensive studies have demonstrated that hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) [23], focal adhesion [29, 30] and tight junction [31, 32] play critical roles in muscle development regulation in cattle, pigs, chickens and other livestock. The FoxO signaling pathway is essential for muscle growth, metabolism, and the regulation of cell proliferation and differentiation [33], while increased protein turnover rates are a necessary component of muscle growth [34]. Consistent with these findings, pathways associated with protein turnover, such as RNA transport, were significantly enriched in our study during muscle development. To further characterize circRNA dynamics during muscle development, we performed STEM trend analysis, which classified circRNAs into distinct expression profiles. Functional annotation revealed that downregulated circRNAs were primarily associated with myoblast proliferation. Notably, these downregulated circRNAs were linked to key pathways such as the MAPK signaling pathway, which regulates cell cycle progression and survival. Consistent with this, previous studies have shown that MAPK/ERK pathway activation mediates the pro-proliferative effects of low-frequency pulsed electromagnetic fields on C2C12 myoblasts [35]. Furthermore, downregulated genes during chicken breast muscle development were significantly enriched in cell cycle processes [36]. In contrast, upregulated circRNAs were predominantly enriched in metabolic pathways, including protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum, butanoate and propanoate metabolism, and insulin signaling. This aligns with the established understanding that postnatal muscle growth primarily occurs through hypertrophy (increased cellular protein content), regulated by the balance between muscle protein synthesis and degradation [3]. Supporting this, a previous study on goat muscle development also identified upregulated genes involved in metabolic processes such as amino acid biosynthesis, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, and the TCA cycle, further underscoring the shift from proliferative to metabolic regulation during muscle maturation [37].

CircRNAs have been shown to act as miRNA sponges, playing a key role in the regulation of skeletal muscle development. In cattle, cirACTA1 functions as a sponge for miR-199a-5p and miR-433, influencing bovine myoblast development through the MAP3K11/MAP2K7/JNK signaling pathway [38]. Similarly, circNDST1 acts as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA), modulating bovine myoblast proliferation and differentiation via the miR-411a/Smad4 pathway [39]. In the present study, we identified four differentially expressed circRNAs (novel_circ_0022687, novel_circ_0022663, novel_circ_0022511, and novel_circ_0018908) that were consistently observed across all three comparison groups. Notably, only novel_circ_0018908 is derived from the CLIC5 gene, while the other three originate from intergenic regions on chromosome 27. CLIC5, is a chloride intracellular channel with multiple functions. It is highly expressed in skeletal muscle, and its association with the actin-based cytoskeleton suggests that it may play a significant role in muscle tissue [40, 41]. This suggests that novel_circ_0018908 may be a crucial circRNA in muscle development. Experimental validation using convergent and divergent primers, RNase R treatment, and RT-qPCR confirmed that novel_circ_0018908 is present and exhibits differential expression during muscle development (Fig. 4A-D). Additionally, we predicted and constructed a regulatory network for novel_circ_0018908 and its potential miRNA targets, which include bta-miR-10171-3p, bta-miR-12,017, bta-miR-12,028, bta-miR-12,043, bta-miR-12,055, bta-miR-12,059, bta-miR-146a, bta-miR-146b, bta-miR-2420, bta-miR-2450c, bta-miR-3154, bta-miR-502b, bta-miR-6523a, bta-miR-763, bta-miR-7691 and bta-miR-873.

miR-146a promotes muscle differentiation by inhibiting the expression of the Numb gene [42]. Ma et al. [43] also reported that the regulatory network composed of CTSF/circRNA_0348/bta-miR-146a influences yak muscle development and meat quality. miR-146b expression increases during myogenesis, promoting mouse C2C12 myoblast differentiation by targeting Smad4, Hmga2 and Notch1 [44]. However, overexpression of miR-146b-5p enhances proliferation while inhibiting myotube differentiation in primary chicken myoblast cells [45]. miR-873 overexpression suppressed H9C2 proliferation and induces cell cycle arrest by targeting GLI1 gene [46]. Additionally, KEGG enrichment analysis of the target genes of these miRNAs revealed significant enrichment of pathways associated with muscle development, including the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, Ras signaling pathway, mTOR signaling pathway, MAPK signaling pathway, and Wnt signaling pathway. The IGF-1/PI3K/Akt signaling pathway predominantly inhibits the effects of myostatin [47], promoting myoblast proliferation and skeletal muscle growth in embryonic chickens [48]. mTOR plays a critical role in protein synthesis by regulating both anabolic and catabolic signaling in skeletal muscle, thereby influencing muscle hypertrophy and atrophy [49]. The Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway modulates chromatin structure at the promoters of Myod1 and Myf5, enhancing their expression and promoting satellite cell proliferation [50]. The p38 MAPK pathway is well-documented in myogenesis. p38α inhibits the proliferation-promoting JNK pathway by upregulating the JNK phosphatase MKP-1, which downregulates cyclin D1 expression, induces cell cycle exit, and triggers muscle differentiation [51]. Additionally, p38γ signaling regulates muscle cell proliferation by preventing premature differentiation through the formation of a repressive MyoD transcriptional complex [52]. Collectively, the regulatory network of novel_circ_0018908 and its target miRNAs likely play a significant role in bovine muscle growth.

Conclusion

In this study, we identified 971 DE-circRNAs during muscle growth in Huaxi cattle. Functional enrichment analysis of the host genes of these DE-circRNAs highlighted key biological pathways involved in muscle development. Notably, novel_circ_0018908 emerged as a critical DE-circRNA, consistently observed across all three comparison groups. Experimental validation confirmed that novel_circ_0018908 is a bona fide circular RNA. Furthermore, we constructed a ceRNA regulatory network involving novel_circ_0018908 and its target miRNAs. These studies provide a foundation for further elucidating the mechanisms underlying skeletal muscle development.

Methods

Experimental animals

In this study, three newborn male bovines (1 day old), three young male bovines (12 months old), and three adult male bovines (24 months old) were selected from Hanjiang Cattle Development Co. (Hubei, China). All animals were kept under identical environmental conditions. Prior to euthanasia, the bovines were stunned using an electric shock to minimize pain, followed by exsanguination through a transverse neck incision. The longissimus dorsi muscle was harvested from each of the nine bovines, quickly frozen in liquid nitrogen, and preserved in a liquid nitrogen tank for RNA sequencing analysis.

Library construction and sequencing

Total RNA was extracted from tissue samples using TRIzol® Reagent (Invitrogen, USA). RNA integrity and potential DNA contamination were assessed through agarose gel electrophoresis, while RNA concentration and purity were measured with a Nanodrop spectrophotometer. Further RNA quality evaluation was conducted using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer.

A strand-specific library was constructed by depleting ribosomal RNA. The RNA was first depleted of ribosomal RNA kit (TruSeq Stranded Total RNA Library Prep Gold, Illumina, USA), then fragmented into 250–300 bp segments. cDNA synthesis was initiated using random oligonucleotide primers with fragmented RNA as the template. The first cDNA strand was then degraded by RNase H, and the second strand was synthesized using a DNA polymerase I system with dNTPs (dUTP, dATP, dGTP, and dCTP). The resulting double-stranded cDNA underwent end repair, A-tailing, and adapter ligation. cDNA fragments of approximately 350–400 bp were purified using AMPure XP beads (Beckman Coulter, Beverly, USA). The second strand, containing uracil, was degraded with the USER enzyme (NEB, USA), and the library was subsequently amplified via PCR.

After library construction, its initial concentration was measured using a Qubit 2.0 fluorometer, and the library was diluted to 1.5 ng/µL. Insert size was verified using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. Once the expected insert size was confirmed, the effective concentration of the library (≥ 3 nM) was quantified via qRT-PCR to ensure quality. Following quality control, Illumina PE150 sequencing was performed, with libraries pooled based on their effective concentrations and data output requirements.

Data filtering, quality control and analysis

The raw data obtained were filtered to remove reads containing adapters, reads with an N ratio greater than 0.002, and low-quality reads. After filtering, sequencing error rates were assessed, and the distribution of GC content was checked. Clean reads were then obtained for subsequent analysis.

RNA-seq data were analyzed using Hisat2 [53] (http://ccb.jhu.edu/software/hisat2). CircRNAs were identified using find_circ in combination with CIRI. Expression normalization was performed with TPM. For samples with biological replicates, differential analysis was conducted using DESeq2 [54]. Differentially expressed circRNAs were identified with a padj < 0.05.

The Short Time-series Expression Miner (STEM) software was employed for clustering analysis of time-series gene expression data. Specifically designed for short time-course microarray data (typically 3–8 time points), STEM categorizes genes into predefined model profiles to accurately identify temporal expression patterns and their associated functional relationships [55].

GO enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes and KEGG pathway statistical enrichment were performed using the clusterProfiler (3.8.1) package. Pathways with a P-value < 0.05 were considered significantly enriched. CircRNA-miRNA networks were constructed using Cytoscape software (https://cytoscape.org/).

Validation for DE circRNAs

To validate the RNA sequencing data, seven DE circRNAs were selected for confirmation. The structural characteristics and expression levels of these DE circRNAs were assessed across different developmental stages. Divergent primers and convergent primers were designed to amplify both the reverse splice junction (BSJ) and the linear mRNA forms of the circRNAs using Primer 5.0. Detailed primer information is provided in Table S10. PCR products generated from cDNA and genomic DNA (gDNA) using these primers were analyzed by agarose gel electrophoresis. To verify the junction sequences of the circRNAs, PCR products amplified with divergent primers were gel-purified and sequenced by Sanger sequencing. All primer synthesis and sequencing were performed by Sangon Biotech Co. Ltd. (Qingdao, China). Additionally, to evaluate the resistance of circRNAs to RNase R degradation, quantitative PCR was conducted using RNA samples both treated with RNase R and untreated, enabling comparison of circRNA stability.

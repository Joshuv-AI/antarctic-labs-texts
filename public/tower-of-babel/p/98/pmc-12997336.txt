Targeting lncRNA DSCAM-AS1 for disease diagnosis and therapy
Authors: Li Zhou, Yizhou Jin, Zedong Li, Haiyang Dong, Yongfeng Jin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as critical regulators of gene expression and play essential roles in a wide range of biological processes and human diseases, particularly cancer. DSCAM antisense RNA 1 (DSCAM-AS1) is a recently identified lncRNA transcribed from the antisense strand of the human DSCAM (Down Syndrome Cell Adhesion Molecule) locus. Accumulating evidence demonstrates that DSCAM-AS1 is aberrantly overexpressed in multiple cancer types and is closely associated with tumor proliferation, invasion, and metastasis, etc. Mechanistically, DSCAM-AS1 exerts oncogenic functions through diverse regulatory modes, including transcriptional positive feedback loops, competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) activity that sequesters tumor-suppressive microRNAs, modulation of alternative splicing and 3′ end usage via interaction with hnRNPL, and epigenetic regulation of cancer-related gene expression. Moreover, the cancer- and isoform-specific expression pattern of DSCAM-AS1 highlights its potential utility as a diagnostic biomarker and a therapeutic target. In this review, we summarize the discovery, molecular characteristics, disease associations, and regulatory mechanisms of DSCAM-AS1, and discuss its emerging clinical relevance in cancer diagnosis, prognosis, and targeted therapy.

Introduction

Genomic and transcriptomic analyses have revealed that up to 80% of the human genome is transcriptionally active, whereas only ∼2% of the resulting transcripts encode proteins. Consequently, the vast majority of transcripts correspond to non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) in human cells [[1], [2], [3]]. In recent years, an increasing number of studies have demonstrated that ncRNAs play essential roles in diverse biological and disease-related processes [4,5]. NcRNAs comprise several major classes, including microRNAs (miRNAs), circular RNAs (circRNAs), and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs). LncRNAs are generally defined as RNA transcripts longer than 200 nucleotides (nt) that lack protein-coding potential. They were initially regarded as transcriptional noise because of their relatively low sequence conservation and expression levels [6]. Consistent with their non-coding nature, lncRNAs typically lack extended open reading frames (ORFs) and are transcribed predominantly by RNA polymerase II, with a subset also reported to be generated by RNA polymerase III [6,7]. The lncRNA H19, first reported in the early 1990s, represents the earliest discovered lncRNA [8]. With advances in high-throughput sequencing, an expanding repertoire of lncRNAs has since been identified. Based on their genomic locations relative to protein-coding genes, lncRNAs can be classified into five categories: sense, antisense, intronic, bidirectional, and long intergenic non-coding RNAs (Fig. 1) [9]. Accumulating evidence indicates that lncRNAs participate in multiple layers of gene regulation and transcriptional control, RNA processing, and disease diagnosis and therapy. Notably, the secondary and tertiary structures of lncRNAs enable them to function as regulatory scaffolds or guides, thereby modulating transcription, RNA splicing, and mRNA translation [10].Fig. 1Classification of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs). In recent years, lncRNAs have emerged as critical regulators of diverse cellular and physiological processes. Accumulating evidence demonstrates that lncRNAs participate in fundamental biological programs, including cell death, cellular reprogramming, energy metabolism, and epigenetic regulation [[11], [12], [13], [14]]. Consistent with their pervasive regulatory capacity, dysregulation of lncRNAs has been increasingly linked to human diseases [15,16]. A growing number of lncRNAs have been shown to play a role in tumor initiation, progression, and metastasis, functioning either as oncogenic drivers or tumor suppressors [[17], [18], [19]]. Beyond signaling control, lncRNAs also contribute to metabolic reprogramming in cancer cells. For instance, specific lncRNAs regulate aerobic glycolysis by influencing the expression or activity of the M2 isoform of pyruvate kinase (PKM2), a central enzyme in the Warburg effect [20,21]. At the genetic level, single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) within lncRNA loci have been associated with cancer susceptibility, highlighting their potential contribution to inter-individual variation in disease risk [22]. Importantly, aberrant expression patterns of lncRNAs are frequently observed in tumor tissues and circulating biofluids, underscoring their value as diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers [23,24]. Several lncRNAs have already entered clinical practice or advanced translational studies. Notably, PCA3/DD3 has been approved by the FDA as a biomarker for prostate cancer, while lncRNAs such as CUDR, PTENP1, and LSINCT-5 have been proposed as diagnostic markers for gastric cancer [5,[25], [26], [27], [28]]. Collectively, these findings position lncRNAs as both key mechanistic regulators of disease pathogenesis and promising targets for precision diagnosis and therapeutic intervention.

Natural antisense lncRNAs constitute a significant subclass of the lncRNA landscape, primarily originating from the antisense strand of the same genomic locus, where they share partial or complete complementarity with the sense strand target gene [6]. Recent transcriptomic analyses have revealed the pervasive existence of these antisense transcripts at protein-coding gene loci across a wide evolutionary spectrum, ranging from invertebrates to higher mammals [29,30]. These antisense lncRNAs exhibit high tissue- and cell-type specificity and are frequently implicated in disease pathogenesis, with prominent roles reported in tumor initiation and progression [6,31,32]. A representative example is DSCAM antisense RNA 1 (DSCAM-AS1), a lncRNA transcribed from the antisense strand of the mammalian DSCAM (Down Syndrome Cell Adhesion Molecule) locus. This review provides a comprehensive overview of the identification and molecular features of DSCAM-AS1, detailing its multifaceted regulatory roles in oncogenesis. Furthermore, we evaluate its potential as a novel biomarker and therapeutic target, highlighting its growing significance in clinical oncology.

Identification and molecular features of DSCAM-AS1

The long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) now known as DSCAM-AS1 was originally identified through a differential expression screen designed to distinguish malignant from benign human breast epithelial cell lines. Using reverse northern hybridization, Liu et al. (2002) first reported a novel transcript initially designated as M41, which exhibited approximately 17-fold higher expression in estrogen receptor (ER)-positive MCF-7 breast cancer cells compared with the non-tumorigenic Huma 123 cell line [33]. At the time of its discovery, M41 expression was found to be largely restricted to ER-positive breast cancer cell lines and was undetectable in ER-negative, benign, or non-mammary cell lines, suggesting a strong and specific association with hormone-dependent breast tumorigenesis [33]. Subsequent genomic mapping and transcript characterization revealed that M41 corresponds to an antisense lncRNA transcribed from the DSCAM locus, leading to its formal re-annotation as DSCAM-AS1. Genomically, DSCAM-AS1 is located on human chromosome 21q22.2 (GRCh38/hg38), originating from a long intronic region of the DSCAM gene (Fig. 2) [33,34]. Detailed transcriptomic analyses revealed that DSCAM-AS1 gives rise to four alternatively spliced isoforms (Fig. 2) [34]. Subcellular localization studies demonstrated that, in MCF-7 cells, total DSCAM-AS1 RNA is distributed at comparable levels between the nucleus and cytoplasm [35]. Importantly, isoform-specific analyses showed that transcripts containing the central exon are predominantly nuclear, whereas the three isoforms lacking this exon are mainly localized to the cytoplasm [36], suggesting that this isoform-specific distribution may involve distinct regulatory functions. This spatial partitioning suggests a functional 'division of labor' between isoforms. Cytoplasmic variants are hypothesized to function as miRNA sponges (ceRNAs) to regulate the expression of downstream targets, while nuclear-retained isoforms may serve as modular scaffolds to modulate alternative splicing (e.g., via hnRNPL) or epigenetic states [37,38]. Such dual-compartment activity allows DSCAM-AS1 to synergistically reprogram the transcriptome at both transcriptional and post-transcriptional levels, establishing a comprehensive regulatory framework associated with tumor progression. Notably, while the homologous Drosophila Dscam1 locus is known for its extensive alternative splicing and essential roles in neuronal and immune functions [[39], [40], [41], [42], [43], [44], [45]], its associated antisense lncRNAs are not homologous to mammalian DSCAM-AS1 [46]. Association of DSCAM-AS1 with disease

DSCAM-AS1 forms a natural antisense transcript pair with the DSCAM gene, suggesting a potential functional relationship between these two transcripts. Aberrant expression of DSCAM has long been implicated in Down syndrome (DS), and approximately 40–50% of children with DS develop congenital heart disease (CHD) [47]. In efforts to define the DS-associated CHD critical region, a recurrently duplicated genomic segment was identified in the majority of DS-CHD patients. This region encompasses three protein-coding genes (including DSCAM) and four noncoding RNAs (including DSCAM-AS1) [48]. Nevertheless, direct evidence supporting a role for DSCAM-AS1 in the pathogenesis of Down syndrome is currently lacking. Importantly, functional studies have demonstrated that silencing of DSCAM-AS1 does not alter DSCAM mRNA expression levels, indicating that DSCAM-AS1 exerts biological functions largely independent of its sense counterpart [36]. Accumulating studies have now established a strong association between DSCAM-AS1 and multiple human cancers. In breast cancer, DSCAM-AS1 is markedly upregulated and functions as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) for miR-137, thereby promoting tumor cell proliferation and inhibiting apoptosis [37]. In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), DSCAM-AS1 acts as a molecular sponge for miR-122-5p, leading to derepression of its downstream target Follistatin-like 3 (FSTL3), a gene associated with poor patient prognosis [49]. Similarly, in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), DSCAM-AS1 is significantly overexpressed and promotes cancer progression by sponging miR-124 [50]. In prostate cancer, DSCAM-AS1 is also upregulated and facilitates cancer cell progression through the miR-338-3p axis [51]. Collectively, these findings support the notion that DSCAM-AS1 acts as an oncogenic lncRNA across diverse tumor types. Mechanism of DSCAM-AS1 in promoting cancer development

Aberrant expression of DSCAM-AS1 in multiple cancer types suggests a close molecular link between DSCAM-AS1 and tumorigenesis. However, the regulatory mechanisms by which DSCAM-AS1 contributes to cancer development remain incompletely understood. Here, we summarize four representative mechanisms to provide an overview of the functional roles of DSCAM-AS1 in cancer.

DSCAM-AS1 is regulated by a positive feedback pathway

In breast cancer, estrogen receptor-alpha (ERα) typically functions in association with estrogen. Notably, in the absence of estrogen, ERα can also regulate the expression of hundreds of genes in a ligand-independent manner, referred to as apo-ERα [52]. DSCAM-AS1 has been identified as a direct transcriptional target of apo-ERα, which binds to its promoter region to regulate DSCAM-AS1 expression. In MCF-7 cells cultured under estrogen-free conditions, ERα knockdown induces epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), reduces cell adhesion, enhances metastatic potential, and promotes tumor progression in vivo [53]. Interestingly, knockdown of DSCAM-AS1 produces similar phenotypes, and its regulation by apo-ERα occurs independently of estrogen signaling [36].

Mechanistically, apo-ERα binds to a super-enhancer region upstream of the DSCAM-AS1 locus, where ERα recruitment facilitates the binding of key cofactors, such as GATA3, which is essential for super-enhancer activation. Under estrogen-free conditions, this super-enhancer directly interacts with the DSCAM-AS1 promoter to drive its transcription [54]. The transcription factor FOXA1, a pioneer factor, promotes chromatin accessibility by inducing nucleosome rearrangement, thereby facilitating transcription initiation [55,56]. FOXA1 has been reported to bind to the ERα promoter and enhance its transcription [57]. In breast cancer cells, increased DSCAM-AS1 recruits Y-box binding protein 1 (YBX1) to the promoter regions of FOXA1 and ERα, leading to increased FOXA1 expression. FOXA1, in turn, activates the super-enhancer that drives DSCAM-AS1 transcription, thereby forming a positive feedback loop that accelerates cancer progression [58] (Fig. 3). DSCAM-AS1 acts as a sponge for miRNA

LncRNAs can act as molecular sponges to adsorb miRNAs, thereby reducing miRNA availability and modulating downstream gene expression involved in cancer progression [[59], [60], [61]]. A previous study found that knockdown of DSCAM-AS1 in MCF7 cells led to increased levels of miR-382, miR-183, and miR-99, which are implicated in tumor metastasis, invasion, and DNA damage responses [62]. However, the underlying mechanisms were not fully elucidated at that time. Subsequent studies demonstrated that DSCAM-AS1 functions as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA), binding specific miRNAs to relieve their inhibitory effects on target mRNAs. In breast cancer, DSCAM-AS1 acts as a ceRNA for miR-137 in tamoxifen-resistant cells. DSCAM-AS1 contains complementary binding sites for miR-137, and the two exhibit an inverse expression pattern. Bioinformatic prediction and dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed that miR-137 directly targets the 3′ untranslated region (3′ UTR) of epidermal growth factor receptor pathway substrate 8 (EPS8). While miR-137 suppresses EPS8 expression, DSCAM-AS1 sequesters miR-137, leading to EPS8 upregulation and enhanced tumor cell proliferation [37,60]. Similarly, DSCAM-AS1 can sponge miR-204-5p, thereby alleviating its repression of ribonucleotide reductase M2 (RRM2) [59,63]. In addition, DSCAM-AS1 promotes tumor progression by competitively binding miR-150-5p and miR-2467-3p, resulting in increased expression of quinolinic acid phosphoribosyltransferase (QPRT) [64].

Notably, DSCAM-AS1 may interact with the same miRNA across different cancer types. For example, in addition to breast cancer, DSCAM-AS1 sponges miR-137 in colorectal cancer, thereby derepressing Notch-1 signaling to promote cell proliferation, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, and tumor migration [65]. DSCAM-AS1 also interacts with miR-101-3p in gastric cancer [66]. In osteosarcoma, DSCAM-AS1 regulates tumor progression by modulating ubiquitin-specific peptidase 47 (USP47) through miRNA-mediated mechanisms [67]. DSCAM-AS1 interacts with hnRNPL to regulate exon splicing

Many lncRNAs function through specific interactions with proteins, DNA, and RNA [6,[68], [69], [70]]. To regulate gene expression, lncRNAs can act as scaffolds and form ribonucleoprotein complexes with various proteins [28,71,72]. Increasing evidence indicates that a subset of lncRNAs interacts with heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins (hnRNPs). For example, lncRNA THRIL binds specifically to hnRNPL and regulates the transcription of the TNFα gene, which may play a role in human innate immune response and inflammatory diseases [73]. Osteogenesis-related lncRNA (lncRNA-OG) interacts with heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein K (hnRNPK) to regulate the activation of the bone morphogenetic protein signaling pathway [74,75]. In ER-positive breast cancer cells, RNA pull-down followed by Western blotting confirmed the binding of DSCAM-AS1 and hnRNPL, and RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays further validated the specificity of this interaction. Notably, knockdown of hnRNPL largely abolished the aggressive phenotypes induced by DSCAM-AS1 overexpression, indicating that DSCAM-AS1 promotes breast cancer cell oncogenicity in an hnRNPL-dependent manner [35].

hnRNPL is a well-known splicing regulatory protein. At the post-transcriptional level, hnRNPL binds CA-repeat or CA-rich elements within the 3′ untranslated region (3′UTR) of BCL2 mRNA to regulate its stability [76,77]. hnRNPL also modulates exon inclusion by binding to CA-repeat-containing exons, such as those in the human endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) gene [78]. Transcriptome-wide analyses using the Whippet algorithm demonstrated that silencing DSCAM-AS1 leads to widespread alterations in alternative splicing, affecting over a thousand genes. Tandem alternative polyadenylation (APA) was the most frequent event, resulting in preferential shortening of transcript isoforms. Motif enrichment analysis further showed that hnRNPL binding sites were significantly enriched in 3′UTR-shortening events, supporting the notion that DSCAM-AS1 may influence both alternative splicing and 3′-end selection through interaction with hnRNPL [38]. Collectively, these findings suggest that DSCAM-AS1 may contribute to cancer progression in part by partnering with hnRNPL to regulate exon splicing and mRNA 3′-end processing (Fig. 5). DSCAM-AS1 affects cancer-related gene expression by altering epigenetic patterns

Accumulating evidence indicates that lncRNAs are key regulators of epigenetic landscapes in cancer by shaping chromatin accessibility, histone modifications, and transcriptional programs [79,80]. Increasing studies suggest that DSCAM-AS1 also contributes to cancer progression by modulating cancer-related gene expression through epigenetic mechanisms. In breast cancer, DSCAM-AS1 has been shown to regulate the expression of dCTP pyrophosphatase 1 (DCTPP1), a gene implicated in cancer cell migration and invasion. Mechanistically, DSCAM-AS1 interacts with histone acetyltransferase–associated enzymes and promotes the enrichment of acetylated histone H3, including H3K27, at the DCTPP1 promoter, thereby facilitating its transcriptional activation. Knockdown of DSCAM-AS1 significantly reduced histone H3 acetylation at the DCTPP1 promoter and suppressed DCTPP1 transcription. In parallel, DSCAM-AS1 also enhanced the stability of DCTPP1 mRNA through binding to its 3′UTR, jointly contributing to DCTPP1 overexpression [64,81] (Fig. 5). In colorectal cancer, DSCAM-AS1 may also participate in epigenetic regulation by repressing the expression of the tumor-suppressive miR-216b, potentially through DNA methylation–associated mechanisms, thereby promoting tumor cell migration and invasion [82,83]. In addition, DSCAM-AS1 expression is tightly linked to epigenetically active regulatory elements. It has been reported that DSCAM-AS1 is transcriptionally activated by FOXA1-driven super-enhancers, and high expression levels of DSCAM-AS1 are associated with poor prognosis in breast cancer. Moreover, DSCAM-AS1 interacts with the transcription factor YBX1 and modulates its recruitment to the promoter regions of FOXA1 and ERα, thereby influencing the expression of these key transcriptional regulators [58]. Consistent with this regulatory network, in estrogen receptor (ER)-positive breast cancer, DSCAM-AS1 is a direct transcriptional target of ERα, whose binding is coupled with chromatin activation marks such as H3K27 and increased chromatin accessibility [36,84]. Collectively, these findings support a model in which DSCAM-AS1 contributes to cancer-related gene expression by both responding to and reshaping epigenetic landscapes.

DSCAM-AS1 can be used as a biomarker for multiple cancers

LncRNAs have increasingly been recognized as valuable biomarkers in cancer diagnosis and prognosis [25,26]. The potential of DSCAM-AS1 as a cancer biomarker was evident at the time of its discovery. DSCAM-AS1 expression was reported to be up to 17-fold higher in breast cancer tissues compared with normal breast tissue and benign lesions [33]. Subsequently, a systematic analysis of tumor-associated lncRNAs revealed that DSCAM-AS1 is highly expressed in breast cancer cell lines, as well as in non-small cell lung cancer and prostate cancer cell lines, suggesting a cancer type-biased expression pattern [85]. Breast cancers can be classified into four major molecular subtypes based on gene expression profiles: luminal A, luminal B, basal-like, and HER2-enriched [86]. Notably, DSCAM-AS1 shows significant differential expression in ductal and non-ductal cells. It was a specific biomarker for breast cancer progression, since high levels of expression were found only in apparently cancerous tissues, while almost undetectable in adjacent normal tissues or pre-tumor lesions. In addition, DSCAM-AS1 expression is significantly higher in ERα+ cases than in other types. Therefore, DSCAM-AS1 was identified as a marker for specific stages of ductal breast cancer [36,87,88]. Other studies have discovered that DSCAM-AS1 levels are higher in advanced tumor lymph node metastases than in other stages. It is also higher in HER-2-positive patients than in HER-2-negative patients and is correlated with clinicopathological features [89]. Collectively, these findings support the potential utility of DSCAM-AS1 as a diagnostic biomarker for ductal breast cancer.

Beyond breast cancer, elevated DSCAM-AS1 expression has been reported in several other tumor types, including non-small cell lung cancer [49,90,91], melanoma [92], liver cancer [50], colorectal cancer [65,82,93,94], stomach cancer [66], osteosarcoma [67,95,96], cervical cancer [97], and endometrial adenocarcinoma [98]. Elevated levels of DSCAM-AS1 expression have also been found in disease types other than tumors, such as cultured cardiomyocyte lines with a hypertrophic phenotype [99]. In contrast, the levels of DSCAM-AS1 between bladder cancer and normal tissue are not significantly different [100]. High expression of DSCAM-AS1 is usually associated with shorter disease-free survival (DFS) and poor prognosis of patients, suggesting its potential as a prognostic biomarker [62,82,101,102]. Taken together, given the altered expression level of DSCAM-AS1 in cancer tissues and the cancer tissue-specific expression it exhibits, DSCAM-AS1 has strong potential as a biomarker for cancer diagnosis. Beyond its diagnostic and prognostic utility, the consistent overexpression of DSCAM-AS1 across multiple tumor types also raises the question of whether it may serve as a tractable therapeutic target.

DSCAM-AS1 as a potential therapeutic target in cancer

LncRNAs have emerged as a novel class of therapeutic targets in cancer owing to their cancer-specific expression patterns and critical roles in tumor initiation, progression, and therapeutic resistance [22,70,103]. Advances in RNA-targeting technologies, including antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs), small interfering RNAs (siRNAs), and CRISPR-based approaches, have enabled efficient and selective modulation of lncRNA expression, underscoring the translational potential of lncRNA-directed therapies [22,[104], [105], [106]]. Given its aberrant overexpression and reported oncogenic functions across multiple cancer types, including cervical cancer, breast cancer [63], non-small cell lung cancer [91], osteosarcoma [96], colorectal cancer [93], and gastric carcinoma [107]. DSCAM-AS1 has emerged as a promising therapeutic target. Functional studies have demonstrated that silencing DSCAM-AS1 using siRNAs significantly suppresses tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion [[107], [108], [109]]. Mechanistically, inhibition of DSCAM-AS1 has been shown to interfere with key oncogenic pathways by restoring miRNA-mediated repression of downstream targets, attenuating hnRNPL-dependent alternative splicing programs, and reversing epigenetic activation of cancer-related genes [38,64,107]. Importantly, the cancer- and isoform-specific expression pattern of DSCAM-AS1 may offer a therapeutic window with limited effects on normal tissues. Although the clinical translation of DSCAM-AS1-targeted strategies remain at an early stage, accumulating preclinical evidence supports the feasibility of DSCAM-AS1 inhibition as a multifaceted therapeutic approach. Further optimization of RNA delivery systems, improvement of target specificity, and validation in clinically relevant models will be essential steps toward translating DSCAM-AS1-based therapies into clinical applications.

Conclusion and future directions

In this review, we summarized current literature regarding the identification, molecular characteristics, disease associations, regulatory mechanisms, and clinical relevance of the lncRNA DSCAM-AS1. Accumulating evidence supports the notion that DSCAM-AS1 functions as an oncogenic lncRNA that is aberrantly overexpressed across multiple cancer types. DSCAM-AS1 contributes to tumor initiation and progression through diverse molecular mechanisms, including transcriptional feedback regulation, miRNA sponging, modulation of alternative splicing via interaction with hnRNPL, and epigenetic regulation of cancer-related genes. Together, these multilayered regulatory activities highlight the central role of DSCAM-AS1 in shaping oncogenic gene expression networks.

Despite these advances, several important questions remain unresolved. First, the upstream regulatory networks responsible for DSCAM-AS1 overexpression in different tumor contexts are not fully understood [63,91,93,96,110]. Although estrogen receptor-dependent regulation of DSCAM-AS1 is well established [54], other upstream regulatory mechanisms, including FOXA1 binding to super-enhancer regions [58], additional transcription factors, and chromatin state dynamics, remain to be fully characterized, particularly in non-hormone-dependent cancers. Future studies employing single-cell transcriptomics, chromatin accessibility profiling, and perturbation screens may reveal context-specific regulators of DSCAM-AS1 expression. Second, DSCAM-AS1 produces four alternatively spliced isoforms with distinct subcellular localizations [36], yet their isoform-specific functions, interacting partners, and disease relevance remain largely unexplored. Isoform-resolved functional studies, combined with high-resolution RNA imaging and isoform-targeted knockdown approaches, could uncover differential regulatory roles of each isoform. Third, although DSCAM-AS1 resides within a genomic region implicated in Down syndrome-associated congenital heart disease [48], its potential contribution to non-cancer pathologies linked to DSCAM dysregulation has not been experimentally investigated. Exploring the role of DSCAM-AS1 beyond oncology may uncover broader functional relevance, and cross-disease comparative analyses could identify shared regulatory mechanisms or novel therapeutic targets. Fourth, while RNA-targeting strategies such as ASOs, siRNAs, and CRISPR-based approaches offer promising avenues for lncRNA-directed therapy [22,[104], [105], [106]], challenges related to efficient tissue-specific delivery, target specificity, and long-term safety remain major barriers to clinical translation. Future research could focus on novel nanoparticle- or exosome-based delivery systems, combination therapies with conventional agents, and isoform-specific targeting strategies to maximize efficacy while minimizing off-target effects. Finally, the integration of multi-omics data, RNA structural analyses, and computational modeling may enable predictive frameworks for DSCAM-AS1 function in specific tumor contexts, thereby guiding both biomarker development and therapeutic design.

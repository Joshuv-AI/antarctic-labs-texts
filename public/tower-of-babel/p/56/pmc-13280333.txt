Design of INFORMUS, a pragmatic, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of cardiovascular outcomes with the extended-release, fixed-dose combination of naltrexone and bupropion
Authors: Michael Kyle, David M Savastano, Terrilyn A Sharpe, Blaize Kelly
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundObesity is associated with cardiovascular (CV) risk. Several medications previously used for weight management have been withdrawn or contraindicated in individuals with hypertension due to concerns about adverse CV effects, including major adverse CV events (MACE). The fixed-dose, extended-release combination of naltrexone and bupropion (NB-ER) is approved for chronic weight management in adults. A previous phase 3 CV outcomes trial (LIGHT; NCT01601704) evaluating the effect of NB-ER on MACE in adults with obesity or overweight at an elevated CV risk was terminated early. Existing evidence from LIGHT and other data suggest that NB-ER does not increase CV risk, and regulatory agencies requested another CV outcomes safety trial. Here, we describe the design of INFORMUS, a prospective phase 4 pragmatic clinical trial evaluating CV outcomes associated with NB-ER use.MethodsINFORMUS (NCT06098079) is a phase 4, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in the US that will assess the effect of NB-ER on MACE incidence. Eligible participants are adults ≥18 years with overweight and ≥1 weight-related comorbidity or obesity, all with elevated CV risk. Approximately 8600 participants will be randomized 1:1 to NB-ER (final dose of 32 mg naltrexone ER, 360 mg bupropion ER) or placebo. Data collection continues until 212 adjudicated MACE events have accrued.ResultsThe primary objective of this ongoing trial is to assess CV safety by comparing time to first MACE between participants receiving NB-ER vs placebo. Planned secondary endpoints include comparative rates of MACE component events and MACE+ events (MACE or any coronary, cerebrovascular, and peripheral revascularization events, or hospitalizations for heart failure), all-cause death, and time to all-cause death. The incidence of other adverse events will also be monitored throughout the trial.ConclusionFindings from INFORMUS will further characterize CV risk associated with NB-ER in adults with obesity or overweight at increased CV risk.Keywords: Cardiovascular safety; Fixed-dose, extended-release combination of naltrexone and bupropion; Major adverse cardiovascular events; Obesity; Pragmatic randomized controlled trial; Study design

Background

Obesity is a chronic disease that affects approximately 40% of adults in the United States and 16% of adults worldwide [1,2]. Obesity is associated with a variety of other health conditions, such as type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular (CV) disease, obstructive sleep apnea, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, and some cancers [1,3,4]. Other treatments previously used for weight management such as fenfluramine and sibutramine were withdrawn from the market due to concerns about adverse CV effects, such as increases in blood pressure and heart rate, primary pulmonary hypertension, valvular heart disease, or occurrence of major adverse CV events (MACE; ie, CV-related death, nonfatal myocardial infarction [MI], and nonfatal stroke) [5,6]. Moreover, phentermine is still in use but contraindicated in individuals with a history of CV disease due to the potential for increased risk of adverse CV events [5,7]. Therefore, continued characterization of CV risk associated with obesity medications remains important.

The fixed-dose, extended-release combination of naltrexone and bupropion (NB-ER) was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2014 for chronic weight management among adults with overweight and ≥1 weight-related comorbidity or obesity [8]. Results from individuals who completed phase 3 trials of NB-ER showed approximately 8% to 12% weight reduction at 56 weeks among those without type 2 diabetes and about 6% among those with type 2 diabetes [[9], [10], [11], [12]].

Common adverse events (AEs) associated with NB-ER include nausea, constipation, headaches, dizziness, fatigue, vomiting, and dry mouth. While NB-ER is contraindicated for patients with uncontrolled hypertension, changes in blood pressure with NB-ER vs placebo were considered negligible, with the weight reduction achieved with NB-ER treatment potentially counterbalancing any sympathomimetic effect attributable to the bupropion component [13].

Previously, the LIGHT trial (NCT01601704) was conducted from 2012 to 2015 to evaluate the effect of NB-ER on the incidence of MACE in participants with obesity (body mass index [BMI] ≥30 kg/m2) or overweight (BMI ≥27 kg/m2) and at higher risk for CV disease. Although the LIGHT trial was fully enrolled, it was terminated early due to a broken blind following public disclosure of interim results. The pre-specified 50% interim analysis (IA2), based on 192 adjudicated MACE, was completed before the public release of the 25% interim analysis (IA1) and the subsequent early termination of the study. Published findings from IA1 and IA2 reported outcomes from 8910 participants randomized over 3 years, capturing 264 MACE events in 243 participants [14]. No new safety events were identified, and NB-ER did not demonstrate higher CV risk compared to placebo in the interim analyses [14]. However, the FDA and European Medicines Agency (EMA) indicated that another CV outcome safety trial was required to fully characterize the long-term CV safety profile of NB-ER.

The INFORMUS trial was designed to address the FDA postmarketing requirements and EMA category 1, non-interventional, post-authorization safety study requirement. To determine the effect of NB-ER on CV risk, the FDA has supported the use of a Bayesian methodological approach that incorporates data from the LIGHT trial into the INFORMUS analysis. Here, we describe the design of INFORMUS (NCT06098079) trial, a phase 4, multicenter, prospective, randomized, pragmatic, double-blinded, placebo-controlled study intended to evaluate CV outcomes during real-world use of NB-ER in the US. This study will assess the effect of NB-ER on the incidence of MACE in patients with obesity or overweight with CV risk or multiple risk factors.

Study design

Objectives and endpoints

The primary objective of INFORMUS is to evaluate the CV safety of NB-ER compared with placebo in patients with obesity or overweight and elevated CV risk (Table 1). MACE outcomes include CV-related death, nonfatal MI, and nonfatal stroke. The primary endpoint is the time to first MACE (MACE composite) in patients receiving NB-ER vs placebo. The MACE composite includes the occurrence of CV death, nonfatal MI, and nonfatal stroke during the time from first dose to the last dose of study drug +365 days. It is estimated that a total of 212 MACE outcomes is needed to determine that NB-ER is not inferior to placebo. The study will continue until 212 adjudicated MACE events have accrued. Secondary objectives include assessing the comparative rates of MACE component events (ie, CV-related death, nonfatal MI, and nonfatal stroke) and MACE+ (MACE or any coronary, cerebrovascular, and peripheral revascularization events, or hospitalizations for heart failure) events in patients receiving NB-ER vs placebo. Secondary endpoints also include comparing rates of MACE, MACE+, all-cause death, and time to all-cause death between patients who received NB-ER and placebo. MACE comparative rates will also be evaluated in several subgroups of interest (eg, those with obesity, hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, dyslipidemia, etc).

The safety profile of NB-ER has been well characterized through an extensive clinical development program, including the 4 pivotal phase 3 clinical trials [[9], [10], [11], [12]]; therefore, safety and tolerability monitoring during INFORMUS will be limited to AEs leading to discontinuation of study drug and serious AEs. Newly diagnosed CV, type 2 diabetes, and dyslipidemia safety events will be considered AEs of special interest. The Clinical Endpoint Committee (CEC) will be responsible for adjudicating, validating, and classifying all potential MACE/MACE+ endpoints and adjudication of all-cause death. The CEC will be composed of 3 CV disease experts and will remain blinded to patient treatment.

Patient population

The study population includes adults ≥18 years of age with obesity (BMI of ≥30 kg/m2) or overweight (BMI of ≥27 kg/m2) with at least 1 weight-related comorbidity, all of whom are at increased risk of adverse CV outcomes (Fig. 1; see full inclusion/exclusion criteria in Table 2). Additional inclusion criteria include a required washout period of prohibited concomitant medication(s) at screening (per the product label) and willingness to comply with daily completion of an electronic diary application (eDiary App) using a mobile smartphone device. Important exclusions include use of other prescription medications or surgical or medical devices for weight loss, glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1RA) use (even if prescribed for comorbidities), history of an MI or stroke within 90 days prior to screening, uncontrolled hypertension, history of seizures or seizure disorders, use of bupropion-containing products, active anorexia nervosa or bulimia, chronic opioid use, and pregnancy or nursing. Notably, patients using GLP-1RAs were excluded to minimize potential confounding of cardiovascular outcomes related to weight change and heterogeneity in cardiometabolic effects across agents and indications. Once enrolled, patients will be asked to adhere to additional restrictions throughout the treatment phase of the study including refraining from use of medications containing bupropion or naltrexone, enrollment in any additional weight loss programs, undergoing any weight loss surgery, or use of any other investigational agents or devices for weight management (see full list in Table 3). Enrollment will continue until the planned sample size is reached, and the study will conclude once 212 MACE endpoints have accrued and been adjudicated.

Randomization and treatment

Study patients will be randomized 1:1 to NB-ER or placebo in a double-blinded manner using an interactive web-based randomization system with no stratification factors. Patients receiving daily 32 mg/360 mg NB-ER (or identical placebo tablet) will be titrated consistent with the product label over a 21-day period (Supplemental Table 1) [8]. NB-ER will be provided as an 8 mg naltrexone/90 mg bupropion tablet. Patients who cannot tolerate the recommended dose will be allowed to continue at their maximum tolerated dose. Patients will still receive routine medical care, including management of diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and other ongoing medical conditions.

Planned study procedures and follow-up

Unless a washout period from prohibited medications is needed, informed consent, baseline screening, enrollment, and randomization can occur in a single visit. Patients will utilize an eDiary App daily to report medication adherence, use of concomitant medications, and incidence of MACE. Weekly, patients will be prompted via the eDiary to document any emergency room visits or hospitalizations. Study site visits will be scheduled for every 3 months during the treatment period to collect ongoing data (eg, vital signs) and ask about medication adherence and potential serious AEs. Patients who discontinue study medication for any reason will be asked to return to the study site for an end-of-treatment visit and will be contacted quarterly for 1 year or until completion of study for follow-up to assess potential MACE. Patients experiencing a non-fatal MACE will discontinue study medication and will enter this same quarterly follow-up period for 1 year or until completion of study.

Statistical analysis

Populations

Analyses will be performed in the modified intent-to-treat (mITT) population, defined as all randomized patients who received at least 1 dose of study treatment. An additional analysis will be performed in the same population with censoring when initial treatment is not maintained. Demographic and baseline characteristics will be reported by treatment group using summary statistics. All patients will provide written informed consent. An Institutional Review Board will provide approval of the protocol at all participating study sites. The trial will be carried out in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and ICH Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice.

Sample size calculation

This study will use a Bayesian approach to incorporate results from the LIGHT trial, consistent with FDA recommendations and EMA acceptance of this approach. An a priori distribution centered at a hazard ratio (HR) of 1, with variance reflecting a 75% discount of IA2 will be applied. This level of discounting reflects appropriate conservatism given the interim and external nature of the LIGHT data, including incomplete follow-up and potential between-study heterogeneity, while ensuring that inference is driven primarily by newly accrued events. Based on LIGHT trial data, 212 MACE are needed to provide 80% power to demonstrate that NB-ER is not inferior to placebo using a noninferiority margin of HR ≥1.4. Accounting for attrition, approximately 5000 participants are needed to achieve the target number of MACE. Approximately 8600 participants will be enrolled and randomized overall.

Analysis of the primary endpoint

To determine the effect of NB-ER on CV risk, Bayesian Cox proportional hazards models will be used to estimate HR and corresponding posterior intervals across treatment groups. The primary endpoint, comparative time to MACE in participants who received NB-ER vs placebo, will first be tested for noninferiority using the prespecified noninferiority margin of HR ≥1.4. If successful, superiority testing will then be conducted to determine if NB-ER reduces the rate of MACE compared with placebo based on an HR ≥1.0. Schoenfeld residual plots and log-negative-log survival plots will be used to assess assumptions of proportional hazards for time to MACE; if proportional hazards assumptions are violated, results will be analyzed using restricted mean survival time methods. The probability of experiencing MACE will also be estimated with Kaplan-Meier methodology.

In the case of treatment discontinuation or use of nonpermitted medication, rescue medication, bariatric surgery, or noncompliance with treatment, the treatment-policy strategy will be employed. For deaths unrelated to CV events and prior to MACE, an on-treatment/while-alive policy will be used with the death date considered the censoring date.

The primary analysis will first test for noninferiority. If the test of noninferiority is successful (the null hypothesis is rejected), then the primary endpoint will be tested for superiority. This hierarchical approach controls the type I error at alpha = 0.05. Robust variance estimators will be used to account for model weighting.

Several sensitivity analyses will be performed using Cox proportional hazard modeling, including a hypothetical strategy for early treatment discontinuation, analysis of MACE composite during the full study follow-up or after treatment discontinuation, analysis of competing risks such as deaths preceding other events, evaluation of mixture priors, and tipping-point analysis [15].

Analysis of the secondary endpoints

Secondary endpoints include rates of MACE, the expanded composite MACE+, all-cause death, and time to all-cause death for participants receiving NB-ER vs placebo. Mantel-Haenszel tests will be used to compare rates of MACE/MACE+ between NB-ER and placebo. The analysis methods used for time to the first MACE and rate of MACE will be used for each component event, and a Cox proportional model will be conducted for analysis of time to all-cause death. Sensitivity analyses will be performed using time on study medication, full study follow-up, and various windows after treatment discontinuation. Analyses conducted in subgroups of interest will depend on the number of participants enrolled in each group. In the case of treatment discontinuation or use of nonpermitted medication, rescue medication, bariatric surgery, or noncompliance with treatment, a treatment-policy strategy will be employed.

Discussion

This phase 4 study is designed to assess the long-term CV risk in individuals with overweight or obesity taking NB-ER as part of chronic weight management in routine clinical practice. This study will compare MACE rates between participants receiving NB-ER and placebo to determine if NB-ER use is associated with a higher CV risk.

The LIGHT trial, a large CV outcomes study in individuals with overweight or obesity treated with NB-ER, was conducted to evaluate whether NB-ER increased the occurrence of MACE compared with placebo, given small but statistically significant increases in systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, and pulse rate observed during the NB-ER clinical development program. In the planned IA1, MACE were observed in 59 patients receiving placebo and 35 receiving NB-ER (1.3% vs 0.8%; HR 0.59; 95% CI 0.39–0.90), suggesting lower MACE rates with NB-ER [14]. However, because IA1 findings were prematurely released in a patent publication submitted to the Securities and Exchange Commission, the trial was terminated early, before the planned number of events could accrue [14]. A planned IA2 completed prior to the public disclosure of IA1 reported 102 MACE in the placebo group and 90 in the NB-ER group (2.3% vs 2.0%; HR 0.88; 99.7% CI 0.57–1.34) [14]. These results indicated that NB-ER did not increase CV risk two-fold (HR >2); however, the study did not accumulate enough events to evaluate noninferiority using the prespecified margin of HR <1.4 [14].

Therefore, the INFORMUS trial will be conducted to fulfill post-approval regulatory requirements by evaluating whether NB-ER is noninferior to placebo with respect to MACE. Utilizing a Bayesian approach, the FDA has agreed to allow use of data from the LIGHT trial to define the prior log-HR distribution for the primary analysis. As a result, it is estimated that 212 MACE are needed to assure 80% power to determine that NB-ER is not inferior to placebo using the prespecified noninferiority margin of HR ≥1.4.

Overall, NB-ER has shown a favorable safety profile, with no evidence of increased CV risk in real-world use across multiple data sources [[16], [17], [18]]. A retrospective cohort analysis of patients initiating NB-ER vs lorcaserin for weight management identified via electronic health records was conducted to determine CV risk hazard margins of NB-ER vs an active comparator in clinical practice [6]. This analysis concluded that over a mean of 4.7 years of observation, patients who received NB-ER did not have an increased risk of MACE compared with lorcaserin, for which neutral CV findings were reported in a CV outcomes trial [6,19]. In addition, a 7-year analysis of observed AEs associated with obesity medications conducted using the FDA AE Reporting System (FAERS) demonstrated that CV-related AEs were reported in only 7% of patients using NB-ER, compared with 8% for lorcaserin, 10% with orlistat, 22% with phentermine-topiramate, 23% with liraglutide, and 31% with phentermine [16]. A separate study that examined CV safety in obesity medications over a 5-year period in FAERS found that NB-ER was associated with a significantly lower proportion of CV-related AEs compared with lorcaserin, phentermine, or phentermine-topiramate [20]. The INFORMUS trial will expand upon these findings to help characterize the risk associated with NB-ER among patients at higher risk of CV events.

Limitations

Retention of participants for any placebo-controlled obesity treatment trial is challenging, even more so now with other obesity medications available to patients. Use of GLP-1RAs outside the protocol is excluded but is captured in the eDiary and accounted for analytically. Some residual confounding may still remain. These restrictions reflect the objective of isolating the cardiovascular safety profile of NB-ER but may limit generalizability to broader treatment settings. In addition, the effect of NB-ER on MACE in this study is being evaluated only in patients with preexisting CV risk factors thereby limiting generalizability to patients without underlying CV risk. Finally, long-term, event-driven designs are dependent on adequate accrual of MACE, which may limit precision for subgroup analyses. Despite these limitations, the INFORMUS trial will provide essential evidence regarding the long-term CV safety profile of NB-ER in patients at higher risk of CV events, which is crucial information for patients and health care professionals considering weight management options.

Conclusion

This article describes the design for the prospective phase 4 INFORMUS trial, a multicenter, pragmatic study conducted in the US designed to assess the effect of NB-ER on the incidence of MACE in patients with obesity or overweight who are at elevated CV risk. The collective evidence from extensive real-world analyses and published meta-analyses reasonably excludes any long-term CV risk associated with NB-ER. Multiple large-scale evaluations, including FAERS analyses, systematic literature reviews, and meta-analyses of randomized controlled trials, consistently demonstrate no increased risk of MACE compared with other obesity medications or placebo [[16], [17], [18]]. These findings support a favorable long-term CV safety profile for this combination therapy in individuals with obesity and CV-related risk factors and provide reassuring evidence for continued use in higher-risk patient populations. The INFORMUS trial will build on this foundation by providing definitive, prospective evidence regarding CV safety in individuals with established CV risk.

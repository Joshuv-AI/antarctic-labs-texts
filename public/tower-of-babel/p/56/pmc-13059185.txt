A crucial role for novel miR-12137-3p in supporting weight gain in treatment naïve HIV-infected patients on antiretroviral therapies
Authors: Niska Majumdar, Bishwa R Pokharel, Frank Williams, Smit Rajput, Paul P Cook, Shaw M Akula
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small non-coding RNAs that regulate gene expression post-transcriptionally. MiRNAs are implicated in diverse biological processes. However, their specific role in antiretroviral (ARV) therapy-induced obesity in individuals with Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) represents an area requiring further investigation.MethodsPatient data and blood samples were collected according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Next generation sequencing (NGS) was performed to determine the changes in circulatory miRNA profiles in response to an integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI; Biktarvy)-and protease inhibitor (PI; Symtuza)-based ARV treatment in promoting obesity in treatment naïve HIV-infected patients. Cloning, dual luciferase reporter assay, in vitro studies employing differentiated adipocyte-Chub-S7 cells in the presence of miRNA specific mimics and/or inhibitors, and PCR arrays were performed for the mechanistic studies.ResultsOur study yielded the following results: (i) both the drugs (Biktarvy and Symtuza) were equally good at managing HIV infection; (ii) patients on both these ARV treatment seem to show a weight gain trend as early as 24 weeks post treatment; (iii) expression of a novel miRNA, miR-12137-3p, was elevated in the plasma of HIV-infected patients experiencing weight gain while on ARV treatment (INSTI or PI-based ARV); (iv) PPARγ-2 and WNT3A were determined to be the direct targets of miR-12137-3p; and (v) inhibiting miR-12137-3p-associated cell signaling in differentiated adipocyte-Chub-S7 cells with miR-12137-3p inhibitor significantly lowered lipid droplet accumulations within the cells. We also employed PCR arrays to further understand the role of miR-12137-3p in weight gain/obesity.ConclusionThe study reports for the first time a role for novel miRNA, miR-12137-3p, in ARV-associated weight gain/obesity. Further understanding the biology of miR-12137-3p may offer novel therapeutic approaches to treating weight gain in general.Clinical trial numberClinicalTrials.gov ID - NCT05463783. Date of registration: 03/14/2023; https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05463783.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-026-07922-8.

Introduction

Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) is a retrovirus that targets the immune system, and if left untreated, it progresses to acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) [1]. HIV was isolated and described to be the aetiologically associated with AIDS in 1983 [2]. AIDS is a condition characterized by a significant drop in CD4 + T cells (< 200 cells per microliter of blood) [3]. As of 2023, an estimated 39.9 million people worldwide were living with HIV, including 38.6 million adults [4].

A slew of effective antiretroviral (ARV) therapeutics in the form of highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART) were introduced in 1996 [5]. ARVs have evolved to better target the different phases of viral invasion. Currently, there are 5 general classes of ARVs and they are entry inhibitors, reverse transcriptase inhibitors, protease inhibitors (PI), integrase inhibitors, and capsid inhibitors [6]. Current common ARV treatment combinations typically include two NRTIs, along with either one non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI), one PI, or one integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI). INSTIs are currently the first line of ARVs recommended when procuring a treatment regimen for people with HIV (PWH) [7].

Both early and modern-day ARVs have a host of side effects ranging from nephrotoxicity, bone marrow suppression, damage to the nerves, the liver, and pancreatitis [8–10]. Some more common adverse effects include lipodystrophy, cardiovascular risks, and metabolic disturbances such as insulin resistance and lactic acidosis [11–14]. These effects are often tied to one of the major health disorders that began taking root in the 1980s – obesity [15]. Obesity is either a direct cause or a contributing factor in several serious health conditions, including cardiovascular disease [16], diabetes [17], certain cancers [18, 19], arthritis [20], sleep apnea [21], and premature death [22].

Out of all kinds of ARVs, NRTIs, INSTIs, and PIs have been generally associated with obesogenic outcomes; with INSTI-based regimens having a greater effect in weight change compared to PI-based regimens [23]. Dolutegravir and bictegravir are mostly associated with increased weight gain under the INSTI category [24]. Among NRTIs, tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) has been recorded to show biologically significant weight gain in patients [25]. INSTI and NRTI combinations have also been proven to ensure weight gain by at least 10% in some cases [26]. Two modern day HAART regimens will be the focus of this study: Symtuza (Janssen) and Biktarvy (Gilead). Biktarvy (B/F/TAF) is a fixed-dose combination (FDC) containing bictegravir 50 mg (INSTI), emtricitabine 200 mg (NRTI), and TAF 25 mg (NRTI), taken as one tablet daily [27]. Symtuza (D/C/F/TAF) is an FDC containing darunavir 800 mg (PI), cobicistat 150 mg (booster), emtricitabine 200 mg (NRTI), and TAF 10 mg (NRTI), also taken as one tablet daily [28].

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small, non-coding RNAs, typically about 22 nucleotides long, derived from hairpin-shaped precursor molecules encoded in the genomes of animals, plants, and viruses [29]. They partake in the controlling gene expression and regulating numerous biological processes by targeting messenger RNAs (mRNA), thereby inducing translational repression or hastened RNA degradation [30]. Multiple biological mechanisms including lipid metabolism, adipogenesis, glycolysis, gluconeogenesis, thermogenesis, and cytokine regulation; all of which are linked to obesity and can be regulated by miRNAs [31].

This study aims to identify alterations in circulatory miRNA activity triggered by ARV treatments in PWH; specifically, INSTI-based B/F/TAF and PI-based D/C/F/TAF. We have used clinically relevant samples in the form of plasma to determine the fluctuations in the levels of circulatory miRNAs by conducting next generation sequencing (NGS) during the course of a year post treatment. This was performed to establish changes in the miRNA profiles capable of inducing obesity in PWH. Herein, we report a novel miRNA (miR-12137-3p) that is elevated in PWH who showed signs of weight gain/obesity as early as 24 weeks post treatment. We delineated the potential targets of miR-12137-3p using PCR arrays. Finally, our experiments provide evidence to show that it can serve as a potential target to developing new-age therapeutics to limit weight gain/obesity.

Materials and methods

Cells

Human embryonic kidney cells (HEK-293T) were propagated in Dulbecco modified Eagle medium (DMEM) (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA) containing 10% charcoal-stripped foetal bovine serum, L-glutamine, and antibiotics as per earlier standard protocols [32]. Human subcutaneous pre-adipocytes (Hsp-a; ATCC; PCS-210-010) were differentiated into Chub-S7 cells by adipogenic induction using adipocyte differentiation tool kit (ATCC; PCS-500-050) as per manufacturers’ recommendations (ATCC, Manassas, VA).

Human participants study design

All protocols were approved by the University and Medical Center IRB (UMCIRB) review board (UMCIRB 22–000833). The ClinicalTrials.gov number is NCT05463783. The study design included two groups of HIV-infected and treatment naïve patients on B/F/TAF or D/C/F/TAF for a period of 48 weeks and monitoring their circulatory miRNA in response to any weight gain/obesity. A detailed study protocol is provided under section ‘Study Protocol’ in the Supplementary data.

NGS

NGS was performed to investigate differentially expressed circulatory miRNAs in the plasma obtained from HIV-infected patients on B/F/TAF versus D/C/F/TAF at LC Sciences, Houston, Texas, USA. Briefly, total RNA was extracted and purified via a RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen, Germantown, MD). RNA quantification was performed using a Nanodrop (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and RNA integrity was assessed using Qubit. The extracted RNA (2 µg) was used for library preparation. Agilent Technologies 2100 Bioanalyzer High Sensitivity D.N.A. Chip was used to perform the quality control analysis and quantify the DNA library generated. Finally, single-end sequencing of 50 bp fragments for miRNA was performed using Illumina Hiseq 2500 (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). The raw single-end reads were processed and compiled by trimming adaptor sequences and culling low quality reads via ACGT101-miR program. We applied filtering criteria (≥ twofold change, p < 0.05) to perform our analysis comprehensively on the microarray data.

RT-qPCR

RT-qPCR was performed to monitor miRNA levels as per our earlier studies [32]. Briefly, RNA was extracted from the plasma specimens using miRNeasy Serum/Plasma Advanced kit (Qiagen). The RNA concentrations were measured with a NanoDrop ND-2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Only the RNA samples with 260/280 ratios of 1.8 to 2.0 were reverse transcribed using the All-in-oneTM miRNA RT-qPCR detection kit (GeneCopoeia, Rockville, MD, USA). The cDNA was synthesized in a 25 µl reaction mix containing 5 µl of 5x PAP/RT buffer, 2.5 U/µl poly A polymerase, and 1 µl RTase mix. The reaction was performed at 37 °C for 60 min and terminated at 85 °C for 5 min. The synthesized cDNA was directly used as the template for the PCR reaction in a Bio-Rad iQ5 Multicolour Real-Time PCR (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA). In this system, MS2 RNA was used as an external reference for the quality of the extracted miRNAs, and RNU6B, RNU44, RNU48, and RNU49 were used for normalization. The expression levels of miRNAs were measured by employing a specific forward primer for the mature miRNA sequence, the universal adaptor reverse primer (GeneCopoeia), and the SYBR green detection. The specific forward primers used to amplify different miRNAs are provided in Supplementary Table 1.

RT-qPCR was also performed to monitor mRNA levels of target genes in Chub-S7 cells [33]. Briefly, total RNA was isolated using RNeasy mini kit (Qiagen) as per the manufacturer’s recommendations. A 500ng sample of RNA was incubated with 2 U of DNase I (ThermoFisher Scientific, Waltham, MA) and reverse transcribed in a solution containing 250ng of random hexadeoxynucleotides and 50 U of Superscript RT (First-Strand cDNA Synthesis System for RT-PCR; ThermoFisher Scientific) in a final volume of 20 µl. A 2 µl volume of the synthesized cDNA was subjected to PCR reaction using specific forward and reverse primers (Supplementary Table 2).

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Five pairs of forward and reverse oligonucleotides (oligos) corresponding to the miRNA binding sites in the 3’-UTR of mRNA targets (MRAP2, PPARA, PPARγ-2, WNT1, and WNT3A) were designed (Supplementary Table 3). All the oligos were synthesized (ThermoFisher Scientific, Waltham, MA) and cloned into pmirGLO dual-luciferase vector (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) to yield MRAP2/pmirGLO, PPARA/pmirGLO, PPARγ-2/pmirGLO, WNT1/pmirGLO, WNT3A/pmirGLO, respectively. HEK-293T cells were plated onto 6-well plates. When the cells were 80% confluent, HEK-293T cells were transfected with empty plasmid (pmirGLO plasmid), 3’-UTR of mRNA target/pmirGLO or co-transfected with 3’-UTR of target mRNA/pmirGLO, different concentrations of miR-121,373-p mimic, or 75nM scrambled oligo controls (miR-SC) using FuGene HD (Promega, Madison, WI). At 24 h post transfection, the firefly and Renilla luciferase activity was measured using the dual luciferase reporter assay system (Promega) as per manufacturer’s recommendations.

RT2 PCR profiler array

PCR array was used to analyse the expression profile of 84 genes associated with adipogenesis. This was used to appreciate the effect of inhibiting miR-12137-3p associated signaling on the expression of these genes as per our earlier studies [34]. Briefly, RNA (1 µg) isolated from untransfected and transfected Chub-S7 cells were reverse transcribed using the First-Strand cDNA Synthesis System for RT-PCR (ThermoFisher Scientific) as outlined under RT-qPCR section. The 20 µl of cDNA generated was diluted five times with RNase free water and PCR reaction performed as per the manufacturer’s recommendations (Qiagen). Genes were considered to be expressed differentially if they were found to be altered by ≥ 1.5- fold or ≤ 0.5-folds between the compared groups.

Statistics

The data satisfied normality (K-S test, p > 0.1); they were used untransformed unless stated otherwise. Student t test or One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used appropriately to establish the statistical significance. ANOVA was performed using IBM SPSS v26 (Cary, NC) to determine significant differences between the treatment and control groups, followed by Tukey HSD post-hoc test for multiple comparisons. The P value was established at the 0.05 level.

Results

B/F/TAF and D/C/F/TAF lower viral load and increase CD4 + cells in HIV-infected people

The clinical study design is outlined in detail under section study protocol (Supplementary data) and in Fig. 1. Viral load and CD4 + cell counts were monitored for 48-week period in 5 HIV positive patients being treated with B/F/TAF or D/C/F/TAF, respectively. The random distribution of participants in this study with respect to age, gender, and race is provided in Table 1. We observed a sharp decline in the viral load by 4 weeks post treatment (< 400 HIV RNA copies per ml) that sustained for 48 weeks post treatment whether the patients were on B/F/TAF (Fig. 2A) or D/C/F/TAF (Fig. 2C). Patients being treated with B/F/TAF had a concomitant increase of CD4 + cell counts (Fig. 2B). Interestingly, one out of the five patients that were on D/C/F/TAF failed to show a significant increase in the CD4 + cell count even at 24 weeks post treatment (Fig. 2D). Unfortunately, we do not have the data for that particular patient (patient ID: 016) 24 weeks post treatment. The clinical parameters like comorbidities, opportunistic infections (OIs), weight gain data, physical examination findings, blood chemistry panel are listed in detail in Supplementary Table 4.

B/F/TAF and D/C/F/TAF alter circulatory miRNAs associated with obesity

The aim of this study was to determine the early changes induced by the ARVs on circulatory miRNA profile in HIV-infected patients. We used plasma from HIV-infected patients collected on week 24 and compared it with week 0 (one day prior to the start of treatment). Summary of known and predicted miRNAs found in each of the participants within the two groups can be found in Supplementary Table 5. NGS identified differentially expressed circulatory miRNAs in response to treatment with ARVs (Fig. 4A). Weighted gene co-expression network (WGCNA) was used to create a Venn diagram that identified the differentially expressed circulatory miRNAs between the different groups: B/F/TAF-week 0 (B/F/TAF-0) vs. B/F/TAF-week 24 (B/F/TAF-24); D/C/F/TAF-week 0 (D/C/F/TAF-0) vs. D/C/F/TAF-week 24 (D/C/F/TAF-24); B/F/TAF-0 vs. D/C/F/TAF-0; and B/F/TAF-24 vs. D/C/F/TAF-24 (Fig. 4A). We observed a positive correlation between circulatory miRNAs at week 0 (B/F/TAF-0 and D/C/F/TAF-0) and week 24 post treatment (B/F/TAF-24 and D/C/F/TAF-24) suggesting a similar expression states of these miRNAs in different groups of comparisons: B/F/TAF-0 vs. B/F/TAF-24 (Fig. 4B) and D/C/F/TAF-0 vs. D/C/F/TAF-24 (Fig. 4C), respectively. These results hint that the circulatory miRNAs can serve as ideal biomarkers for diagnosis, and prognosis of the pathological conditions such as obesity and its related comorbidities.

Elevated expression of circulatory novel miRNA, miR-12137-3p, in HIV-infected patients on B/F/TAF and D/C/F/TAF at 24 weeks post treatment

NGS allowed us to identify ARV-induced differentially expressed circulatory miRNAs 24 weeks after treatment. The fluctuation in the expression of all the miRNAs in different HIV-infected patients within the two groups is listed in Supplementary Table 6. Of these, we were interested in the expression of novel miRNA, miR-12137-3p, and its effect on obesity. The choice to further work on this novel miRNA is better explained in the following section. The non-weight gain functions of miR-501-5p, miR-425-5p, miR-10399-3p, miR-let-7a-3p, and miR-let-7f-1-3p is listed in supplementary Table 7. D/C/F/TAF treatment significantly lowered expression of miR-548ag, and miR-151a-3p while enhancing the expression of miR-1306-5p, miR-20b-5p, and miR-93-5p (Fig. 5B). We created a model to describe the plausible D/C/F/TAF-induced weight gain/obesity based on the known targets of these miRNAs (Supplementary Fig. 4).

The expression level data from NGS was further confirmed by RT-qPCR. One interesting observation was that the expression levels of the novel miRNA, miR-12137-3p, was not only elevated in HIV-infected patients treated with B/F/TAF (Fig. 6A) but also in 2 (patient IDs: S003 and S004) out of 5 patients under the D/C/F/TAF treatment regimen (Fig. 6D). The fact that RT-qPCR compared to NGS could detect changes in the expression levels of miR-12137-3p in D/C/F/TAF treated patients could be due to one or a combination of the reasons: (i) RT-qPCR is relatively more sensitive than NGS [35]; (ii) NGS was done using samples collected at two time points while RT-qPCR was performed using samples obtained from 0, 24, and 48 weeks post treatment. These results were fascinating as we had identified a novel miRNA candidate that was altered by both the ARVs. Therefore, we decided to pursue our study on miR-12137-3p to unravel the implications of the signaling induced by it to promote weight gain/obesity.

MiR-12137-3p may regulate more than one target to induce and promote weight gain/obesity in HIV-infected patients

MiR-12137-3p is 18 nucleotide long (Fig. 7A). Genomic analysis using NCBI Blast Tool reveals that miR-12137-3p is an intronic microRNA, transcribed from the upstream of host gene AXIN1, located on chromosome 16. The low energy secondary structure of the pre-miR-12137-3p is provided in Fig. 7B. The five potential mRNA targets of miR-12137-3p is depicted along with the miRNA: mRNA interaction sites on the 3’ UTR regions (Fig. 7C). We performed luciferase reporter assays to determine if miR-12137-3p bound to putative mRNA targets: MRAP2, PPARA, PPARγ-2, WNT1, and WNT3A. Transfection of HEK293T cells with miR-12137-3p mimic significantly lowered the luciferase activity of 3’-UTR of PPARγ-2 encoding pmirGLO plasmid (PPARγ-2/pmirGLO) and 3’-UTR of WNT3A encoding pmirGLO plasmid (WNT3A/pmirGLO) but not the plasmid encoding the 3’-UTR of MRAP2, PPARA, or WNT1 (Fig. 8A). In the conditions tested, scrambled oligo controls (miR-SC) did not significantly alter the luciferase activity of any of the plasmids tested (Fig. 8A). In contrast, transfection of cells with miR-12137-3p inhibitor (Inh) reversed the ability of miR-12137-3p mimic from lowering the luciferase activity of PPARγ-2/pmirGLO and WNT3A/pmirGLO plasmids (Fig. 8B). Transfection of cells with non-specific miRNA inhibitor (Inh-NS) failed to reverse the ability of miR-12137-3p mimic to lower the luciferase activity of all the three plasmids tested in this assay (data not shown). The results indicate the specificity of the miR-12137-3p mimic tested in this assay and confirm PPARγ-2 and WNT3A to be its potential targets.

MiR-12137-3p inhibitor significantly lowers lipid levels in Chub-S7 cells

In order to study the effects of inhibiting miR-12137-3p-associated cell signaling, we used Chub-S7 cells. Human subcutaneous pre-adipocytes (Hsp-a; ATCC; PCS-210-010) were differentiated into Chub-S7 cells by adipogenic induction using adipocyte differentiation tool kit (ATCC). Hsp-a cells were fully differentiated into Chub-S7 cells by culturing the cells in chemically defined media for 14 days. Chub-S7 cells acquired a mature adipocyte phenotype with clearly visible accumulation of lipid droplets (Fig. 9A). Transfecting Chub-S7 cells with 100nM miR-12137-3p inhibitor (Inh) significantly lowered lipid droplet levels in the cells 24 h post treatment compared cells that were untransfected (Fig. 9A). Transfecting cells with 100nM miR-non-specific (NS)-Inh did not significantly downregulate lipid droplets (Fig. 9A). The doses of inhibitor used in the study did not significantly induce cell death in Chub-S7 cells (data not shown). Similar such doses are commonly used in cell culture research and has been reported by others [37, 38].

Finally, we determined the expression pattern of miR-12137-3p target mRNAs: PPARγ-2, and WNT3A by performing RT-qPCR on RNA extracted from the above cells 24 h post miR-12137-3p inhibitor transfection. We observed a significantly elevated expression of PPARγ-2, WNT1, and WNT3A levels in cells that were transfected with 100nM concentrations of miR-12137-3p Inh compared to untransfected cells (Fig. 9B) and cells transfected with miR-12137-3p Inh-NS (data not shown). We also observed a similar trend in the expression profile of the above genes when the cells were transfected with 75nM concentration of miR-12137-3p inhibitor (data not shown). To further authenticate our RT-qPCR data, we performed PCR array using the RT2 Profiler™ PCR Array Human Adipogenesis (Qiagen). The data from the performing arrays demonstrate a holistic effect of blocking miR-12137-3p associated cell signaling in lowering lipid levels in the Chub-S7 cells (Fig. 9C). The raw data from the PCR array study can be found in Supplementary Table 8.

Discussion

B/F/TAF and D/C/F/TAF are equally effective in managing HIV infection (Fig. 2). B/F/TAF is a INSTI based ARV (bictegravir - INSTI, emtricitabine - NRTI, and TAF - NRTI) while D/C/F/TAF is a PI based ARV (darunavir - PI, cobicistat - booster, emtricitabine - NRTI, and TAF - NRTI). It is widely reported that the INSTI based regimens are associated with weight gain [39–41]. We hypothesized B/F/TAF would induce weight gain in HIV-infected and treatment naïve patients compared to those on D/C/F/TAF. However, both these drugs seem to promote weight gain as early as 24 weeks post treatment (Fig. 3). This could be due to one or all these reasons: (i) the common factor between both these ARVs is TAF and that is known to promote weight gain [42]; (ii) cobicistat-boosted elvitegravir increases insulin resistance that leads to weight gain [43]; and (iii) weight gain is directly proportional to their improvement in general health in response to ARV regimen [41]. In a recently concluded study, it was reported that there was a weight loss trend observed in patients that switched from INSTI-to a PI-based ARV [44]. Our study is different from this study as the participants in our study were treatment naïve HIV-infected patients.

We attempted to map the circulatory miRNA signature of weight gain in HIV-infected patients on B/F/TAF and D/C/F/TAF (Fig. 5). Six miRNAs seem to have a role in ARV-induced obesity. The one that we were more interested in was the novel miRNA, miR-12137-3p, and its role in obesity for the following reasons: (i) it is described for the first time by us in this report (Fig. 7A); (ii) the expression level of miR-12137-3p followed an increasing trend in HIV-infected patients on B/F/TAF regimen and three out of the five HIV-infected patients on D/C/F/TAF at 24weeks (Fig. 6); and (iii) it could potentially bind to 3’-UTR region of 5 putative mRNA targets that could modulate weight gain: MRAP2, PPARA, PPARγ-2, WNT1, and WNT3A (Fig. 7C).

MiRNAs can regulate multiple target mRNA expressions [45]. Bioinformatic tools and computational approaches may be used to predict miRNA targets but can have high false-positive rates. Then ideal way to confirm is to validate these predictions using in vitro assays [29]. Therefore, we performed in vitro assay in the form of luciferase reporter assay to determine the potential mRNA targets of miR-12137-3p. MiR-12137-3p mimic inhibited relative luciferase activity of pmirGLO plasmid that expressed 3’-UTR of PPARγ-2 and WNT3A compared to MRAP2, PPARA, and WNT1 (Fig. 8A). Transfecting cells with miR-12137-3p inhibitor compared to miRNA-NS-Inh could reverse the inhibitory effect of miR-12137-3p mimic on luciferase activity of pmirGLO plasmid that expressed 3’-UTR of PPARγ-2 and WNT1 (Fig. 8B). Taken together, miR-12137-3p can directly bind to the 3’-UTR of PPARγ-2 and WNT3A and downregulate their expression.

Several other miRNAs (miR-194-5p, miR-122, miR-181a-5p, miR-23a-3p, miR-100-3p, miR-222-5p, miR-483-5p, miR-27a) have been reported in the context of HIV, obesity, and metabolic disorders [46–49]. Interestingly, the gene targets (PPARγ-2 and WNT3A) of miR-12137-3p are not associated with any of these miRNAs (Supplementary Fig. 5), suggesting miR-12137-3p to regulate adipogenesis through a unique pathway. A decrease in the levels of PPARγ-2 and WNT3A can directly induce obesity and vice versa. If upregulation of miR-12137-3p can directly down regulate the expression of PPARγ-2 and WNT3A levels, then we should be able to inhibit obesity by targeting the expression of miR-12137-3p in cells. To test this hypothesis, we tested the effect of transfecting Chub-S7 cells with miR-12137-3p inhibitor on lipid synthesis. miR-12137-3p inhibitor treatment significantly lowered lipid droplets compared to treatment with miRNA-NS-Inh (Fig. 9A). Such a miR-12137-3p inhibitor-induced decline in lipid droplet accumulation within the cells was in response to an increase in the PPARγ-2, WNT1, and WNT3A expression levels as determined by RT-qPCR (Fig. 9B) and PCR arrays (Fig. 9C). PPARγ-2 and WNT3A are direct targets of miR-12137-3p (Fig. 8). Interestingly, we observed low expression levels of gene ADIG in the cells with decreased levels of miR-12137-3p (Fig. 9C). This authenticates our results as earlier studies demonstrated a key function of ADIG in modulating accumulation of lipid droplets in obesity models [50]. Based on the data from PCR arrays, we developed a KEGG model (Fig. 10) to explain the direct and indirect effects of inhibiting miR-12137-3p associated signaling crucial to lowering lipid droplet accumulation in vitro and thereby altering body weight gain/obesity. Although the gene encoding miR-12137-3p is an intronic miRNA located upstream of AXIN1 gene, the miRNA does not have putative binding site on the 5’-UTR, coding strand, or the 3’-UTR regions of AXIN1 gene. Therefore, we concluded the effects of miR-12137-3p on AXIN1 expression (Fig. 10) is an indirect result of the WNT signaling cascade.

Realizing the vital role of dietary choices in weight gain, all participants received identical dietary education from a nutritionist, based on the Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH) and Mediterranean diet guidelines. Weekly dietary intake (total calories, carbohydrates, fat, and protein) was monitored using the MyFitnessPal smartphone application (iOS/Android) and a 24-hour recall method. Participants were instructed to log their intake daily and submit records weekly via text message, with weekly compliance reminders sent. The research team reviewed and recorded these self-reported weekly totals. Critically, the study did not aim to alter participants’ already established diets, restrict daily calories, or change exercise. Hence this may allow for some confounding factors such as, the socioeconomic status [51], any essential medications [52], personal lifestyle choices [53], and their potential use of recreational substances [54] to alter weight gain in the participants. Unfortunately, we could not strictly monitor the above variables as this was an out-patient cohort; thus, making it difficult to determine the extent to which these variables could directly or indirectly alter weight gain. Fortunately, the above confounding factors do not in any way dilute the phenotypic ability of miR-12137-3p in regulating obesity (weight gain) in our treatment naive HIV positive cohort. This is because, (i) high fat diet, exercise, or recreational substances do not alter miR-12137-3p expression. In this study, miR-12137-3p expression was elevated in patients who gained weight with time irrespective of their recreational drug use, opportunistic infections, pre-existing health conditions (Supplementary Tabel 4); and (ii) inhibiting miR-12137-3p lowers lipid accumulation independent of these confounding factors. For that reason, the findings compel miR-12137-3p as a promising target to treat weight gain.

Glycolysis, fatty acid oxidation (FAO), mitochondrial activity, and energy balance are critical factors that influence weight gain/obesity. Glycolysis refers to the breakdown of sugars in our body to release energy [55]. This implies that increased glycolytic activity can lead to weight loss [56, 57]. Similarly, our body can also break down fats to generate energy via fatty acid oxidation (FAO) [58]. In obesity, fat oxidation is reduced or blocked and can limit energy expenditure hence, inducing FAO can reverse weight gain [59, 60]. Mitochondrial activity also relates to energy as it helps the cells work more efficiently by converting nutrition to ATP [61]. Impaired mitochondrial function may lead to inefficient energy use and subsequently, obesity [62]. Hence all these metabolic pathways help maintain proper energy balance for daily function. This elucidates that dysfunction in any of these metabolic processes may disrupt energy balance and induce obesity or symptoms of obesity such as weight gain [63].

Our RT2 profiler data (Fig. 9C) provides indirect evidence as to how miR-12137-3p associated signaling may alter the above factors (Fig. 11). Inhibiting miR-12137-3p in Chub-S7 plausibly enhanced glycolysis (via increase in the expression of FOXC2 and HES1) [64, 65]; FAO (via PPARD, KLF15, PRDM16, and BMP7) [66–69]; and mitochondrial activity (via BMP4, PRDM16, and BMP7) [70–72]; all of which increase energy release resulting in a decrease in lipid droplet accumulation. Although direct metabolic flux was not assessed, the upregulation of key drivers of glycolysis, FAO, and mitochondrial function following miR-12,137 inhibition aligns with the observed reduction in adiposity. These transcriptional shifts suggest that miR-12137-3p expression contributes to an obesogenic phenotype not by targeting these genes directly, but by suppressing the metabolic machinery required for efficient energy expenditure. Consequently, this miRNA represents a potential therapeutic target for mitigating weight gain, particularly in the context of HIV-associated metabolic dysregulation.

A tentative model depicting a plausible mechanism by which miR-12137-3p may modulate glycolysis, FAO, and mitochondrial activity to lower adipogenesis

Concluding remarks

INSTI and PI-based ARVs induce weight gain/obesity in HIV-infected and treatment naïve patients. However, we are fully aware that the sample number of patients included per group is only 5. A large cohort of study must be conducted to confirm these results in vivo. The results from this study specifically on the novel miRNA, miR-12137-3p, is fascinating because of the following reasons: (i) could potentially serve as a biomarker of weight gain/obesity in HIV-infected patients on ARV; and (ii) serve as a potential target to treating weight gain/obesity in general. We are more interested on the latter and in developing RNA-based oligo therapeutics to treat weight gain/obesity for the general population. This is based on the fact that the miR-12137-3p inhibitor oligo used could significantly lower lipid droplets in Chub-S7 cells. Future studies are aimed at further characterizing the effect of miR-12137-3p associated signaling in weight gain/obesity using animal models.

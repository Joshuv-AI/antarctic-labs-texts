Molecular detection of the kdr T917I mutation in head lice (psocodea: pediculidae) from Malaysia and its increasing frequency over a decade
Authors: Aida Syafinaz Mokhtar, Adriana Zahanuddin, Yee Ling Lau, Norhidayu Sahimin, Amr Mohamed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPediculosis capitis remains a common ectoparasitic infestation in Malaysia, particularly among children in overcrowded and resource-limited settings. Permethrin-based pediculicides continue to be widely used; however, global reports of knockdown resistance (kdr) mutations in the voltage-sensitive sodium channel gene raise concerns regarding reduced treatment efficacy. Despite extensive epidemiological data on infestation by head lice (Pediculus humanus capitis De Geer) in Malaysia, the molecular kdr status of local populations has not previously been examined. This study provides a temporal molecular evaluation of the kdr T917I mutation by comparing head lice collected from underprivileged children in the Klang Valley in 2015 and 2025. Genotyping was performed using Polymerase Chain Reaction-Restriction Fragment Length Polymorphism (PCR-RFLP) targeting the T917I locus, with bidirectional sequencing for confirmation. In 2015, head lice were predominantly homozygous susceptible (SS, 96.8%), with a low proportion of heterozygous (RS, 3.2%) and no homozygous resistant (RR) genotypes detected. In contrast, the 2025 collection showed a marked shift in genotypic distribution, with SS decreasing to 60.6% and RS increasing to 39.4%, while RR genotypes remained absent. Correspondingly, the resistance-associated allele frequency increased from 1.6% in 2015 to 19.7% in 2025. Several establishments sampled in 2025 exhibited heterozygote excess and deviations from Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium; however, given differences in sampling structure between collection periods, these patterns should be interpreted cautiously and may reflect heterozygous treatment exposure or population substructure rather than directional selection alone. This study provides the first molecular detection and temporal comparison of the T917I mutation in Malaysian head lice populations and establishes baseline data for future resistance monitoring.Keywords: molecular surveillance, PCR-RFLP, VSSC gene, permethrin, socioeconomically disadvantaged

Introduction

Head lice infestation, or pediculosis capitis, is a longstanding public health concern that disproportionately affects children living in overcrowded or socioeconomically disadvantaged environments (Ko and Elston, 2004, Falagas et al. 2008, Nutanson et al. 2008). Close physical contact and socioeconomic constraints, including limited access to hygiene resources, facilitate transmission and allow infestations to persist even in settings with improved awareness and treatment availability (Mumcuoglu et al. 1990). In Malaysia, head lice (Pediculus humanus capitis De Geer; Psocodea: Pediculidae) remain commonly reported among children in community and institutional settings, yet the underlying drivers that sustain these infestations are poorly understood.

Permethrin, a synthetic pyrethroid, continues to be widely used as the first-line pediculidae in Malaysia. Its mode of action targets the voltage-sensitive sodium channel (VSSC) of the louse nervous system, inducing paralysis and death (Tomita et al. 2003). However, global evidence accumulated over the past 2 decades shows that permethrin efficacy is being undermined by knockdown resistance (kdr) mutations in the VSSC gene. Three amino acid substitutions -M815I, T917I, and L920F- have been most consistently implicated in reduced susceptibility in permethrin-exposed head lice through alterations in the VSSC (Lee et al. 2000, Lee et al. 2003, Kwon et al. 2008), with the T917I mutation often serving as a key molecular marker associated with pyrethroid resistance in both diagnostic and population surveys.

While studies from Thailand (Brownell et al. 2020), Iran (Eslami et al. 2024), Argentina (Toloza et al. 2014), Canada and the United States (Yoon et al. 2014), to name a few, have reported increasing frequencies of kdr alleles, the molecular basis of resistance-associated kdr mutations has remained largely undocumented in Malaysia. Previous local work has focused primarily on the epidemiology of pediculosis capitis and, to a limited extent, on the microbial or genetic diversity of head lice. To date, no published study has examined the presence or temporal dynamics of kdr mutations in Malaysian head lice populations.

To address this knowledge gap, we conducted a decade-scale comparison of head lice genotypes collected from underprivileged children in Klang Valley in 2015 and 2025. By examining the prevalence of the T917I mutation over time, this study provides critical insight into temporal patterns of resistance-associated kdr alleles and offers foundational molecular evidence to inform future resistance surveillance and monitoring strategies.

Materials and Methods

Sample Collection

A total of 293 children from 11 residential children’s establishments across 6 cities in Klang Valley/Greater Kuala Lumpur region were screened for Pediculus humanus capitis infestation between January and June 2025. These establishments comprised orphanages, boarding homes, and refugee learning centers, rather than conventional day schools. Of the children examined, 99 (33.8%) were found to be infested, and one adult louse per infested child was randomly selected for molecular analysis, resulting in 99 lice genotyped.

For comparison, archived specimens collected in 2015 were analyzed. These specimens originated from an earlier cross-sectional survey conducted using comparable collection procedures and had been preserved in absolute ethanol and stored at −20 °C prior to molecular analysis. Although 133 children were infested in 2015, only 94 archived samples with sufficient DNA quality were included in the present study. No additional field sampling was conducted in 2015 as part of this work. In that survey, 232 children from 14 residential children’s establishments across 12 cities were examined. DNA Extraction and Genotyping

Genomic DNA was extracted from individual louse using the PrimeWay Genomic II DNA Extraction Kit (Apical Scientific, Malaysia) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Screening for the kdr T917I mutation was performed using PCR-RFLP. A fragment of the α-subunit of the VSSC gene was amplified following the protocol described by Durand et al. (2007). Ten microliters of the resulting 332 bp PCR products were digested with SspI (Thermo Fisher Scientific, United States) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Digested fragments were separated on a 3% agarose gel at 80 V for 50 min and visualized to determine the RFLP genotypes. The expected restriction fragment patterns were 332 bp for the homozygous susceptible genotype (SS), and 332, 261, and 71 bp for the heterozygous genotype (RS), and 261 and 71 bp for the homozygous resistant genotype (RR).

To validate the RFLP results, randomly selected samples representing both SS and RS genotypes were subjected to bidirectional Sanger sequencing (Apical Scientific Sdn. Bhd., Selangor, Malaysia). The resulting partial VSSC sequences were aligned and compared with the published wild-type reference sequence (GenBank Accession No. AY191156) using BioEdit Sequence Alignment Editor version 7.7.1 (Hall 1999). Representative SS and RS sequences were subsequently deposited in GenBank under accession numbers PX866698 (SS) and PX866697 (RS).

Statistical and Population Genetic Analyses

Results

In 2015, 232 children were examined, of whom 133 (57.3%) were found to be infested with head lice. In 2025, 293 children were examined, with 99 (33.8%) confirmed positive for infestation. These comparable prevalence values indicate that pediculosis capitis remains a persistent public health concern among institutionalized and disadvantaged children in Malaysia.

PCR-RFLP successfully differentiated the kdr T917I genotypes, with the characteristic SS and RS banding patterns observed (Fig. 2). The distribution of kdr T917I genotypes across the sampled establishments is summarized in Fig. 3. Representative PCR-RFLP genotypes, including samples corresponding to visually ambiguous banding patterns, were confirmed by Sanger sequencing, and all sequenced samples showed complete concordance with the RFLP-based genotype assignments. Sequencing of representative RS samples further confirmed the presence of nonsynonymous mutations at both codons 917 and 920, resulting in C → T substitution, leading to Thr (ACA) → Ile (ATA) mutation and Leu (CTT) → Phe (TTT) mutation, respectively (Fig. 4). In contrast, SS head lice showed no nucleotide substitutions at either codon.

In 2015, the majority of head lice were homozygous susceptible (SS, 96.8%), with a small proportion of heterozygotes (RS, 3.2%) and no homozygous resistant (RR) individuals detected. Discussion

Permethrin is a neurotoxic synthetic pyrethroid that exerts its insecticidal effect by binding to voltage-gated sodium channels, disrupting sodium transport across neuronal membranes, and ultimately inducing sustained depolarization and respiratory paralysis in arthropods (Nanda et al. 2024). However, intensive and prolonged use of permethrin has contributed to the global emergence of pyrethroid-tolerant head lice. This phenomenon is strongly associated with point mutations in the α-subunit of the VSSC gene, commonly referred to as knockdown resistance (kdr) mutations. Similar sodium channel-mediated resistance has been documented in other medically important insects, including mosquitoes (Abbasi and Daliri 2024) and houseflies (Abbasi et al. 2023b), supporting the broader relevance of kdr mutations across taxa. The VSSC locus was therefore selected as the molecular target in this study, as mutations within this gene directly alter the pyrethroid binding site and serve as validated biomarkers of reduced susceptibility (Durand et al. 2007, Brownell et al. 2020).

Globally, the prevalence of resistance-associated kdr mutations has increased, with an estimated worldwide prevalence of approximately 59% (Abbasi et al. 2023a). Numerous Asian countries, including Thailand (Brownell et al. 2020), India (Mallick et al. 2023), Iran (Ghahvechi-Khaligh et al. 2021, Mohammadi et al. 2022), and Saudi Arabia (Alsaady et al. 2023, Alghashmari and Zelai 2025), have reported widespread kdr mutations linked to declining permethrin efficacy. In contrast, Malaysia has generated considerable epidemiological data on pediculosis capitis but lacks molecular surveillance on pediculicide tolerance.

In this study, we provide the first molecular evidence of the kdr T917I mutation in Malaysian head lice and document differences in genotype and allele frequencies between samples collected in 2015 and 2025. The 2015 collection was dominated by homozygous susceptible genotypes, with only sporadic detection of heterozygous individuals, a pattern comparable to heterogenous and nonfixed kdr allele distributions reported in other head lice populations (Toloza et al. 2014, Ghahvechi-Khaligh et al. 2021). In contrast, the 2025 collection showed a substantially higher proportion of heterozygous individuals and corresponding increase in resistance-associated allele frequency. Importantly, homozygous resistant genotypes were not detected in either sampling period, indicating that resistance-associated variants have not reached fixation within the sampled populations. This pattern may reflect biological constraints such as fitness costs associated with the RR genotype or incomplete dominance of the T917I allele, whereby heterozygous individuals persist without progression to homozygosity.

Although findings demonstrate a clear temporal difference in kdr genotype distributions, several factors limit direct evolutionary inference. The 2015 and 2025 samples were not collected from identical establishments, and differences in geographic coverage, establishment size, and sampling structure introduce the possibility of population substructure and clustered sampling effects. Such factors are well recognized as potential causes of allele-frequency heterogeneity and Hardy–Weinberg deviations in natural populations, particularly when sampling is uneven or spatially structured (Guo and Thompson 1992, Waples 2015). In addition, the sampling strategy of one adult louse per infested child, while effective in minimizing pseudoreplication, limits inference on within-host genetic diversity and may further contribute to apparent heterozygote excess at the establishment level.

Sequencing of representative samples confirmed the presence of the T917I substitution and additionally revealed co-occurring mutations at codon 920 in heterozygous head lice. Similar combinations of these nonsynonymous mutations have been reported in head lice from other regions and may contribute to reduced pyrethroid susceptibility (Brownell et al. 2020, Ghahvechi-Khaligh et al. 2021). However, this study was not designed to characterize the full spectrum of kdr-associated haplotypes, and other resistance-associated loci were not systematically screened. Consequently, the genetic architecture of permethrin tolerance in Malaysian head lice populations remains incompletely resolved.

From a public health perspective, the observed increase in resistance-associated allele frequency is noteworthy, particularly given the continued reliance on permethrin-based pediculicides in Malaysia. In other regions, rising frequencies of kdr mutations have preceded reports of reduced treatment efficacy and increased treatment failure (Abbasi et al. 2023a, Alsaady et al. 2023, Alghashmari and Zelai, 2025). Nonetheless, because this study did not include phenotypic bioassays or treatment-outcome data, molecular findings should not be interpreted as direct evidence of clinical resistance. Instead, they should be viewed as early molecular signal warranting closer monitoring.

In settings where kdr-associated mutations are increasingly detected, continued reliance on permethrin as a sole control strategy warrants careful reevaluation. Multiple countries reporting elevated kdr allele frequencies have adopted alternative pediculicides such as benzyl benzoate and dimethicone-based formulations, which have demonstrated high efficacy and favorable safety profiles in pediatric populations (Leung et al. 2022). In resource-limited and densely populated communities, integrated control strategies such as mechanical removal by wet combing, simultaneous treatment of close contacts, improved hygiene practices, and school- or institution-based screening, remain critical for reducing transmission and preventing reinfestation (Mumcuoglu et al. 2006, Feldmeier, 2012). Tailored public health messaging and community-based interventions are particularly important in underprivileged populations, where social and structural factors contribute to sustained infestation risk (Nutanson et al. 2008, Feldmeier, 2014).

As samples were collected exclusively from residential children’s establishments within the Klang Valley/Greater Kuala Lumpur region, the findings may not be representative of head-lice populations in other parts of Malaysia and should be interpreted accordingly. Future studies should prioritize longitudinal sampling within the same institutions, expand geographic coverage, and integrate molecular diagnostics with phenotypic susceptibility assays to better resolve genotype–phenotype relationships. Despite these limitations, the present study establishes an essential molecular baseline for Malaysia and highlights the value of routine kdr surveillance as part of sustainable head lice control programs, particularly in underprivileged communities where infestation remains high.

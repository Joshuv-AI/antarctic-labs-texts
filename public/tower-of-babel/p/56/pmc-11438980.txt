Adaptive formation control for obstacle avoidance of USVs with asymmetric input saturation
Authors: Hu Yancai, Liu Yang, Zhang Yan, Do Thi Minh Hoang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Due to the complex maritime navigation environment, Unmanned Surface Vessels (USVs) are influenced by unknown nonlinear dynamics arising from external disturbances and internal uncertainties. Achieving effective formation control while maintaining obstacle avoidance performance presents significant challenges. This article proposes a Neural Networks (NNs) adaptive formation Artificial Potential Field (APF) obstacle avoidance control method for multiple USVs. By employing online updates of Radial Basis Function (RBF) NNs technology, the unknown nonlinear dynamics are approximated, thus addressing complex nonlinear dynamics problems. In scenarios involving multiple USVs navigating under high wind and wave conditions, collisions with obstacles frequently occur. To tackle this issue, a leader-follower control strategy is designed that effectively addresses risk assessment and obstacle avoidance under such challenging conditions. Additionally, to account for saturation constraints or potential faults in the controller inputs commonly encountered in engineering applications, it implements an asymmetric auxiliary control system. Furthermore, the Lyapunov stability theorem is utilized to ensure the stability of both the formation control and obstacle avoidance algorithms for multiple USVs. Finally, the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm is validated through simulations.

Introduction

In recent years, the growing demand for collaborative maritime operations has prompted an increasing number of scholars to study ship collaborative formation control. Compared to traditional single-vessel operations, formation operations offer significant advantages in terms of efficiency and economic benefits. As a result, this approach has found widespread application in both military and civilian sectors. Notable applications include aircraft carrier formations, collaborative resource exploration among multiple vessels, marine environmental monitoring, and maritime search and rescue activities. However, the hazardous and complex navigation environment poses numerous challenges to the collaborative formation of USVs.

Ship formation control is to control the movement of ships in groups based on relative distance, speed, or heading. The research methods of formation control mainly include leader-following, behavior, and virtual structure1. The advantage of the leader-following method is that the formation framework is simple, and to control the behavior of the formation, only the dynamics of the leader need to be controlled, which is easy to implement2,3. Schoerling4 designed a robust formation controller for USVs using the leader-following method. The ship formation control methods5,6 have been further optimized. Subsequently, Gu7 designed a fault-tolerant control method for ship formation guided by multiple virtual leaders. Liu8 used self structuring neural networks to deal with uncertainties in ship formation models, reducing the impact of model inaccuracy and unknown disturbance environments on ship formation.

To consider engineering applications, the input limitations of actuators should be implemented. Therefore, Jin9 designed a formation control method based on LOS (Line of Sight) using a configuration strategy of leader-following method, which converts the formation tracking target into a restricted heading tracking. Then, Fu10 added a saturation function to the formation discrete controller to constrain the control signal. However, in complex marine environments, there are many rocks, islands, and reefs, leading to complex uncertainties in ship motion systems. Therefore, Riahifard11 and Mu12 simultaneously considered the unknown modeling dynamics and external disturbances of formation ships, and designed an adaptive formation control system with actuator input saturation constraints. Huang13 combines a leader-following formation sliding mode control method with an input saturation auxiliary system.

From the above research results, each following vessel only needs to track its leading vessel to achieve formation control. However, the above papers have an important flaw in that they did not consider the obstacle avoidance problem of formation control. Due to its simple design and easy implementation, the APF method has been widely applied. Therefore, Shi14 incorporated the APF method into the distributed collaborative multi-agent collision avoidance control, while considering the models nonlinear dynamic uncertainty and external disturbances. Regarding the collision avoidance problem in ship formation, reference15 limits the position output of ships within a given range to avoid collisions with adjacent ships in the formation. Liang16 proposed a multi ship obstacle avoidance control method based on APF method. At present, there is relatively little communication maintenance and collision avoidance control in ship formations. Simultaneously considering the communication maintenance and collision avoidance control issues of formations is a research hotspot. Park17 designed a nonlinear transformation error method to achieve tracking, communication maintenance, and collision avoidance control of ship formations. Considering the limited communication range of formation members, reference18 designed a ship collaborative formation method based on APF method with connectivity maintenance and collision avoidance functions. Dong19 proposed a decentralized formation obstacle Lyapunov control method. It is worth noting that the above research results have not simultaneously taken into account the impact of both the actuator constraints and the high wind and wave risks on ship formation control.

Based on the literature analysis presented above, this paper proposes a NNs adaptive leader-following formation control algorithm that takes input saturation into account. This algorithm is integrated with the APF method to facilitate obstacle avoidance control for multiple USVs. Firstly, adaptive NNs technology is employed to approximate the complex nonlinear dynamics of unknown USVs, thereby mitigating the impact of these unknown dynamics on obstacle avoidance performance. Subsequently, risk assessment techniques are utilized to design effective ship obstacle avoidance methods, which address the formation challenges of USVs and enhance the rationality of the obstacle avoidance process.

The structural arrangement of the subsequent sections of this paper is outlined as follows: section "Theoretical basis and problem formulation" elaborates on the theoretical foundation and problem formulation. Sections "USVs formation controller design" and "Stability analysis" focus on controller design and stability proof, respectively. Finally, Section "MATLAB simulations" presents the simulation results, along with the conclusions of the paper and directions for future research.

Artificial potential field and ship domain

To address the collision problem, the APF method is employed to maintain an expected and safe distance as USVs approach obstacles. The obstacles are modeled as high potential fields, generating a corresponding virtual repulsive force that acts along the negative gradient of these repulsive potential fields. USVs, which are subject to a defined ship domain, experience virtual repulsion, prompting them to move away from the repulsive potential fields. USVs formation controller design

To ensure the multiple USVs systems form and maintain the desired formation pattern, the adaptive leader-follower formation controller based on APF and NNs is designed for obstacle avoidance. When external obstacles exist, the USVs formation can achieve obstacles collision avoidance automatically considering saturation and collision risk. While there is no collision risk, the desired formation pattern will reorganized to track the virtual leader.

Conclusion

This paper investigates the adaptive leader-follower formation control for obstacle avoidance in multiple USVs. The unknown nonlinear dynamics of the USVs are addressed using RBF-NNs. Furthermore, by integrating the APF method into the formation framework, we have achieved formation control that effectively incorporates obstacle avoidance while considering risk assessment. Based on Lyapunov theory, we have demonstrated the stability of the closed-loop systems. Simulation results validate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm. The formation control approach discussed in this article is a centralized strategy that takes collision risk into account; future work will explore distributed formation strategies.

Predicting 7-day mortality in COVID-19 pneumonia: performance of NEWS2, qSOFA, and CRB-65 and development of the CCP-7 prediction scores
Authors: Panika Praputpittaya, Areeya Phunsupruk, Piyapat Rattanasuwan, Borwon Wittayachamnankul, Wachira Wongtanasarasin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Developed and internally validated the Chiang Mai COVID-19 Pneumonia 7-Day Mortality Prediction Score to predict 7-day mortality in COVID-19 emergency department.•Higher area under the receiver operating characteristic curve than others; differences not statistically significant.•Uses routine emergency department data; enables rapid triage in low- and middle-income country settings; needs external validation.•Supports frontline clinicians in low- and middle-income countries with fast, reliable, cost-effective decisions.

Introduction

COVID-19, caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, has presented unprecedented challenges for healthcare systems worldwide [[1], [2], [3]]. Among hospitalized patients, COVID-19 pneumonia remains the dominant clinical manifestation and is associated with significant morbidity and mortality [4]. Emergency departments (EDs), often the first point of contact, must rapidly identify patients at high risk of deterioration to guide clinical decisions, prioritize admissions, and allocate limited healthcare resources [[5], [6], [7]]. Accurate and efficient early risk stratification tools are essential in this setting, particularly during surges when patient volumes may overwhelm capacity [8].

Several clinical scores have been developed or adapted to aid in the risk stratification of pneumonia and the prediction of mortality. CURB-65 and its simplified version, CRB-65, are among the most widely used tools for community-acquired pneumonia, combining clinical and laboratory variables to guide hospitalization and treatment decisions [[9], [10], [11]]. The more complex pneumonia severity index incorporates up to 20 variables for estimating 30-day mortality risk; however, it may be less practical in emergency settings [12,13]. NEWS2 (National Early Warning Score version 2) and qSOFA (quick Sequential Organ Failure Assessment), while not pneumonia-specific, have been broadly implemented for the early identification of clinical deterioration and sepsis in both ED and inpatient populations [[14], [15], [16], [17]]. Other tools, such as SMART-COP [18], aim to predict the need for intensive respiratory or vasopressor support, while MuLBSTA [19] has been proposed for viral pneumonia, though with limited external validation. During the COVID-19 pandemic, several models were specifically developed for prognosis in SARS-CoV-2 infection, including the 4C Mortality Score (ISARIC) [[20], [21], [22]], COVID-GRAM [20], and CALL (standing for Comorbidity, Age, Lymphocyte level, and Lactate dehydrogenase level) score [23,24]. Still, many rely on imaging or laboratory data that may not be readily available at initial presentation. The Kanagawa score, developed in Japan for early risk stratification in COVID-19 patients [25], and the Kanagawa Admission Priority Assessment Score version 1 (KAPAS-1) and version 2 (KAPAS-2) [26], were designed for early hospitalization decisions in Japan but have uncertain applicability in other healthcare contexts.

Notably, most existing tools were not developed or validated specifically for COVID-19 pneumonia, and their ability to predict short-term outcomes such as 7-day mortality has varied across healthcare systems. Validation data for NEWS2, qSOFA, and CRB-65 in COVID-19 pneumonia are especially limited in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), where resource constraints necessitate efficient, low-cost, and readily applicable risk stratification tools.

Thus, this study had two objectives: (i) to assess the performance of NEWS2, qSOFA, and CRB-65 in predicting 7-day mortality among ED patients with confirmed COVID-19 pneumonia, and (ii) to develop and internally validate a novel, pragmatic risk prediction tool—the Chiang Mai COVID-19 pneumonia 7-day mortality prediction score (CCP-7)—based on clinical variables available at ED presentation. The CCP-7 score aims to facilitate early and evidence-informed clinical decisions in resource-limited emergency care settings across Southeast Asia.

Methods

Study design

We conducted a retrospective observational study to evaluate the prognostic performance of three widely used clinical scores (NEWS2, qSOFA, and CRB-65) and to develop a novel risk stratification tool, the CCP-7 score, for predicting 7-day mortality in patients with confirmed COVID-19 pneumonia presenting to the ED. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Faculty of Medicine, Chiang Mai University (Approval No. 0133/2024). Given the retrospective and observational nature of the study, which utilized routinely collected anonymized data, informed consent was waived. We reported the study in accordance with the Transparent Reporting of a Multivariable Prediction Model for Individual Prognosis or Diagnosis statement [27].

Setting and participants

The study was conducted at Maharaj Nakorn Chiang Mai Hospital, a university-affiliated tertiary care and regional referral center in Northern Thailand. We included consecutive patients aged ≥16 years who presented to the ED between January 1, 2020, and December 31, 2023, with laboratory-confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection by reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction or antigen test (ATK) per contemporaneous hospital policy. During the early pandemic, confirmation was almost exclusively by reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction; ATK was adopted later and used in parallel. Pneumonia required compatible lower respiratory tract symptoms or signs (e.g., cough, dyspnea, tachypnea, hypoxemia, or focal auscultatory findings) and radiographic evidence on chest radiograph or chest computed tomography interpreted as pneumonia by the treating physician or radiologist within 24 hours of ED arrival. Exclusion criteria were pregnancy, trauma-related presentations, asymptomatic COVID-19 without radiographic pneumonia, and incomplete medical records needed for model construction.

Variables and data collection

Trained abstractors with at least 2 years of ED experience during the COVID-19 pandemic collected data retrospectively from electronic medical records. Data were abstracted retrospectively from electronic medical records. Demographic information (age, sex), clinical characteristics (comorbidities, initial vital signs, mental status, body mass index [BMI], triage level, ED length of stay), laboratory results (white blood cell [WBC] count, platelet count), and outcomes (7-day mortality) were systematically extracted. “Altered mental status” was defined as Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score <15 at ED arrival, documented by physicians or nurses. Abnormal WBC was defined as <4000 or >12,000 cells/mm³; thrombocytopenia was defined as a platelet count <100,000 cells/mm³. Clinical scores—NEWS2, qSOFA, and CRB-65—were calculated retrospectively based on parameters recorded at ED presentation. The primary outcome was all-cause mortality within 7 days of ED presentation.

Clinical scores and definition

Several clinical scoring systems have been developed to aid in the early identification of patients at risk for clinical deterioration, particularly in emergency and acute care settings.

The CRB-65 score is a simplified version of the CURB-65 score, originally developed for assessing the severity of community-acquired pneumonia. It includes four parameters: confusion, respiratory rate ≥30/min, systolic blood pressure <90 mmHg or diastolic ≤60 mmHg, and age ≥65 years. A score of 0 indicates low risk and may be managed in the outpatient setting, while a score of 1-2 suggests moderate risk, warranting consideration for hospital admission. A score of ≥3 is associated with high mortality risk and typically indicates the need for intensive monitoring or intensive care unit admission [[9], [10], [11]].

The qSOFA score consists of three parameters: respiratory rate ≥22/min, systolic blood pressure ≤100 mmHg, and altered mental status. A qSOFA score of ≥2 is associated with increased risk of in-hospital mortality and is used to identify patients with suspected infection who are likely to have poor outcomes [16].

The NEWS2 is a widely adopted tool that assesses six physiological parameters—respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, systolic blood pressure, pulse rate, level of consciousness or new confusion, and temperature, with an additional adjustment for supplemental oxygen use. NEWS2 is widely used in emergency and hospital settings to guide the urgency of clinical response. A NEWS2 score of ≥5 is considered a critical threshold, indicating a higher risk of adverse outcomes, including mortality, and the need for urgent clinical assessment. Higher scores are associated with greater severity and are predictive of worse outcomes in various conditions, including sepsis and COVID-19 pneumonia [5,15,16].

Statistical analysis

The sample size was estimated using the method for comparing an observed area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.80 against a null value of 0.67 [25], with 90% power and 5% alpha, assuming a 5% mortality prevalence [28]. The required total sample size was 544 patients. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline characteristics. Continuous variables are reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with interquartile range (IQR), and categorical variables as frequency and percentage. Differences between groups (survivors vs non-survivors) were analyzed using chi-square or Fisher's exact tests for categorical variables and Student’s t-test or Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables, as appropriate. We performed a complete-case analysis. Patients without an initial CBC (WBC or platelet count) or with missing values in model covariates were excluded a priori and are shown as “incomplete records” in Figure 1. CCP-7 score development and internal validation

Multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted to identify predictors independently associated with 7-day mortality, presented as adjusted odds ratios (aORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Variables demonstrating statistical significance (P <0.05) in the multivariable model were incorporated into the CCP-7 prediction score. Score points were assigned to each predictor proportionally according to the aORs, which were rounded to simplified integer values for clinical practicality. The score points for each predictor were proportionally allocated based on their respective aORs. Model discrimination was assessed with AUROC and compared pairwise using DeLong’s test. Calibration was evaluated using calibration-in-the-large, slope, and calibration plots.

The diagnostic performance of NEWS2, qSOFA, CRB-65, and the newly developed CCP-7 score was assessed using sensitivity, specificity, positive likelihood ratio, and AUROC. The optimal cutoff for CCP-7 was determined using Youden’s index to balance sensitivity and specificity. All analyses were performed using STATA (Version 16, StataCorp LLC, College Station, Texas, USA). A two-sided P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Out of 1037 patients screened with positive COVID-19 ATK results, 302 were excluded due to age <16 years (n = 140), incomplete medical records (n = 92), asymptomatic infection without radiographic pneumonia (n = 56), or interfacility transfer (n = 14). A total of 735 patients were included in the final analysis (Figure 1).

The overall 7-day mortality rate was 5.2% (n = 38), while 94.8% (n = 697) of patients survived beyond 7 days following ED presentation. Patients who died had significantly higher respiratory rates at ED presentation (mean 31.6 vs 25.1 breaths/min, P <0.001). A respiratory rate of >30 breaths/min was strongly associated with mortality (60.7% vs 24.6%, P <0.001). Altered mental status (55.2% vs 10.0%, P <0.001), abnormal WBC count (P = 0.002), and thrombocytopenia (P = 0.032) were also significantly associated with 7-day mortality. No comorbidity showed a statistically significant association.

In multivariable logistic regression analysis, four clinical parameters emerged as independent predictors of 7-day mortality: respiratory rate >30 breaths/min (aOR, 2.56; 95% CI: 1.04-6.31; P = 0.041), altered mental status (aOR, 7.75; 95% CI: 3.13-19.18; P <0.001), abnormal WBC count (aOR, 3.04; 95% CI: 1.34-6.91; P = 0.008), and platelet count <100,000 cells/mm³ (aOR, 3.17; 95% CI: 1.23-8.20; P = 0.017) (Table 2). CCP-7 demonstrated superior discrimination (AUROC, 0.83; 95% CI: 0.76-0.90) compared to CRB-65 (0.80; 95% CI: 0.74-0.86), NEWS2 (0.77; 95% CI: 0.68-0.85), and qSOFA (0.64; 95% CI: 0.52-0.75) (Figure 2). DeLong tests: CCP-7 vs CRB-65 (P = 0.18), CCP-7 vs NEWS2 (P = 0.09), CCP-7 vs qSOFA (P <0.01). Thus, CCP-7 was significantly better than qSOFA but not statistically superior to CRB-65 or NEWS2. Calibration showed good agreement (slope = 1.0, CITL = 0.0, O: E = 1.0), although we note that this reflects internal validation only (Figure 3). Discussion

This study demonstrates that routinely available clinical parameters at ED presentation can predict short-term mortality in patients with COVID-19 pneumonia. NEWS2, qSOFA, and CRB-65 showed only moderate predictive accuracy, consistent with previous findings that these scores, designed for sepsis or bacterial pneumonia, are less reliable in viral pneumonia. Although CCP-7 achieved a numerically higher AUROC than CRB-65 and NEWS2, DeLong testing confirmed statistical superiority only over qSOFA; and therefore claims of superiority must be interpreted with caution.

The performance of CCP-7 likely reflects its derivation from COVID-19-specific data, incorporating predictors aligned with the pathophysiology of severe disease: altered mental status (defined as GCS <15 at presentation), tachypnea (RR >30/min), leukocyte abnormalities, and thrombocytopenia. In contrast, NEWS2 and qSOFA were intended as general screening tools for clinical deterioration [29,30], and CRB-65 was developed for bacterial community-acquired pneumonia [9,31]. COVID-19 pneumonia often presents distinct features—such as silent hypoxemia [32], neurological involvement, and hematological dysregulation [32]—that are not adequately captured by existing scores. Furthermore, NEWS2 includes parameters (such as body temperature, systolic blood pressure, and pulse rate) that were not independent predictors of mortality in our COVID-19-specific multivariable analysis, further diluting its predictive performance [29,33]. By focusing exclusively on variables strongly linked to adverse outcomes in this population, CCP-7 strikes a balance between clinical simplicity and disease-specific relevance.

A key trade-off in CCP-7 is its modest sensitivity (57%) at the optimal cutoff. This was expected, as the model emphasizes specificity (89%), reducing the risk of over-triage in resource-limited EDs where unnecessary admissions may burden capacity [34,35]. Such prioritization may be particularly valuable in LMICs, though clinicians must recognize the risk of missed cases.

Importantly, all CCP-7 variables are readily obtainable upon initial ED evaluation, enabling rapid and practical application without the need for advanced imaging or complex laboratory tests, thus overcoming limitations of previously validated tools such as COVID-GRAM and CALL scores [23,24]. Altered mental status was the strongest independent predictor of mortality, consistent with previous reports suggesting neurological manifestations as indicators of severe COVID-19 [[36], [37], [38]]. Respiratory distress (RR >30/min) and laboratory abnormalities (leukopenia, leukocytosis, thrombocytopenia) were also strongly predictive, reinforcing existing evidence linking systemic inflammation and severe illness trajectory in COVID-19 pneumonia [[39], [40], [41]]. Notably, classical risk factors such as advanced age, chronic comorbidities, and prolonged ED stay did not independently predict short-term mortality in our adjusted model, possibly due to the overwhelming prognostic impact of acute physiological disturbances at presentation.

Our findings also highlight the limitations inherent to qSOFA, NEWS2, and CRB-65, which, despite their widespread use, were initially validated outside the context of COVID-19 pneumonia [[14], [15], [16]]. The moderate predictive accuracy observed herein supports findings from other validation studies in low- and middle-income settings, where disease presentations and resource availability may differ significantly from those in the initial validation cohorts [20,22]. CCP-7′s targeted approach thus addresses a critical gap by directly reflecting the acute pathophysiological presentation of COVID-19 pneumonia.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the CCP-7 model was both developed and internally validated in a single cohort, raising the risk of overfitting; the excellent calibration slope of 1.0 likely reflects internal optimization rather than generalizable performance. External validation—geographic, temporal, and prospective—is required. Second, 9% of patients were excluded due to incomplete records, which may have introduced bias if missingness was related to outcomes. Third, while dichotomization of continuous variables (e.g., respiratory rate >30/min) enhances bedside usability, it may reduce statistical efficiency and obscure threshold effects. Fourth, although CCP-7 prioritized specificity for resource-limited contexts, its modest sensitivity underscores the need for careful clinical judgment in its application. Finally, the study’s retrospective, single-center design and reliance on data from hospitalized patients limit generalizability to broader ED populations, including those discharged home.

Conclusion

Among patients presenting to the ED with COVID-19 pneumonia, the CCP-7 score demonstrated good discrimination for 7-day mortality, significantly outperforming qSOFA but not statistically superior to CRB-65 or NEWS2. By relying solely on simple, routinely collected ED variables, CCP-7 offers a pragmatic and context-appropriate tool for early risk stratification, especially in resource-constrained settings. However, given the risk of overfitting and modest sensitivity, CCP-7 should be regarded as a promising but preliminary tool pending prospective multicenter external validation before widespread adoption.

The causal effect of matrix metalloproteinase 9 on interstitial lung disease risk mediated by effector memory double negative T cells: A mediation Mendelian randomization study
Authors: Qijie Ren, Fengyuan Sun, Leilei Yang, Dinglei Su, Minning Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) have been implicated in the pathogenesis of interstitial lung disease (ILD). However, the causal role of MMPs in ILD pathogenesis remains to be elucidated. This study aimed to investigate the causal effects of circulating MMPs on ILD risk and to evaluate whether immune cell phenotypes mediate this relationship. A comprehensive Mendelian randomization (MR) and mediation analysis was conducted using publicly available data of genome-wide association study (GWAS) summary statistics. Genetic instruments for 6 MMPs (MMP-1, MMP-2, MMP-3, MMP-7, MMP-9, and MMP-12) and 731 immune cell traits were selected. Inverse-variance weighted (IVW) was the primary analytical method, supplemented by other MR techniques. Sensitivity analyses included MR-Egger regression, Cochran Q test, and MR-PRESSO to assess pleiotropy and heterogeneity. Mediation analysis was performed to quantify the proportion of the effect mediated by significant immune cells. Genetically predicted MMP-9 levels showed a significant positive causal effect on ILD risk (odds ratio [OR] = 1.034, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.006–1.064, P = .018). Among 731 immune cell phenotypes, 8 were identified as causal for ILD, including effector memory double negative T cells (EM DNT; OR = 1.170, P = .001). MMP-9 was further found to increase the abundance of EM DNT (OR = 1.041, 95% CI: 1.009–1.074, P = .012). Mediation analysis indicated that EM DNT mediated 18.5% of the total effect of MMP-9 on ILD. Sensitivity analyses revealed no evidence of horizontal pleiotropy or significant heterogeneity. This study provides genetic evidence supporting a causal role of MMP-9 in the development of ILD, partially mediated through EM DNT. These findings suggest that MMP-9 and EM DNT could be potential therapeutic targets for preventing or treating ILD.

1. Introduction

Interstitial lung diseases (ILD) represent a heterogeneous group of diffuse parenchymal lung disorders characterized by inflammation and fibrosis, leading to progressive impairment of gas exchange and ultimately respiratory failure.[1] With over 200 known causes, including autoimmune conditions, environmental exposures, and idiopathic forms such as idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) which carries a median survival of only 3 to 5 years, ILD is associated with substantial morbidity and mortality.[2] Current therapeutic strategies, including corticosteroids, immunosuppressants (e.g., mycophenolate mofetil), and antifibrotic agents, are chosen based on etiology and pathological subtype.[1] However, these treatments frequently show suboptimal efficacy and carry significant risks, particularly immunosuppression-related adverse effects. A major unresolved challenge is the early detection of ILD before irreversible fibrosis develops, highlighting the urgent need to better understand pathogenic mechanisms to guide targeted interventions.

A key hallmark of pulmonary fibrosis is the abnormal accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) produced by myofibroblasts.[3] Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), zinc-dependent endopeptidases, play a central role in pathological ECM remodeling in ILD. Elevated serum levels of MMP-7, -9, -10, and -12 have been reported in patients with connective tissue disease-associated ILD (CTD-ILD) compared to those without lung involvement, suggesting their utility as biomarkers for early diagnosis and subtype differentiation.[4] In a large rheumatoid arthritis cohort, MMP-7 and MMP-9 were significantly associated with both the prevalence and incidence of ILD, independent of other known risk factors.[5] In IPF, MMP-9 is particularly overexpressed and drives tissue remodeling through inflammation, epithelial–mesenchymal transition, and fibroblast activation, largely via transforming growth factor (TGF)-β signaling.[6] These observations position MMP-9 as a promising therapeutic target in fibrotic ILD.

Immune dysregulation is increasingly recognized as a critical driver of ILD onset and progression, particularly in fibrotic subtypes. Both innate and adaptive immune responses contribute to lung injury, inflammation, and fibrosis.[7,8] Macrophages, for example, can adopt pro-fibrotic or reparative phenotypes and secrete MMP inhibitors,[7] while CD4+ T cells release pro-fibrotic cytokines such as interleukin (IL)-4, IL-13, and TGF-β.[9] Notably, immune cells not only respond to but also produce MMPs, suggesting that specific immune cell subsets – particularly those with tissue-homing and effector capabilities – may mediate the pathogenic effects of MMPs in ILD. However, whether immune phenotypes functionally link MMPs to ILD in a causal framework remains unexplored.

Conventional observational studies cannot fully address causality due to confounding and reverse causation. Mendelian randomization (MR) overcomes these limitations by using genetic variants as instrumental variables to infer causal relationships.[10] Although MR has been applied to study ILD etiology,[11,12] no prior study has systematically evaluated the causal role of circulating MMPs in ILD risk, nor examined immune cell phenotypes as potential mediators. To address these gaps, we performed a bidirectional 2-sample MR study to assess whether genetically predicted MMP levels – with a focus on MMP-9 – causally influence ILD risk. Furthermore, using a multistep mediation MR framework, we investigated whether certain immune cell phenotypes mediate this association. These analyses aim to provide novel insights into ILD pathophysiology and identify potential targets for therapeutic intervention.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Assumptions of the mediation MR analysis

The mediation MR analysis relies on several key assumptions for valid causal inference (Fig. 1A). First, the relevance assumption requires that the selected genetic instruments are strongly associated with the exposure and the mediator. This was evaluated using F-statistics > 10 for all instruments, confirming sufficient strength to minimize weak instrument bias. Second, the exclusion restriction assumption stipulates that the genetic variants influence the outcome only through the exposure and/or mediator, without direct effects via alternative pathways. Third, the independence assumption posits that the instruments are not confounded by other variables that affect the exposure–mediator–outcome relationships. Finally, the no measurement error assumption for the mediator is partially addressed by using high-quality summary-level data from large-scale genome-wide association study (GWAS).

2.2. Study design

This study employed a multistep MR framework to systematically investigate the causal pathways between circulating MMPs, immune cell phenotypes, and ILD risk. The analysis proceeded through 3 sequential phases (Fig. 1B): First, a bidirectional MR approach was employed to evaluate the causal relationships between MMPs and ILD. Initially, a forward MR analysis was conducted with each of the 6 MMPs (MMP-1, MMP-2, MMP-3, MMP-7, MMP-9, and MMP-12) as exposures and ILD as the outcome. MMPs showing a significant association with ILD were advanced to further analysis. Subsequently, to assess potential reverse causation, a reverse MR analysis was performed by designating ILD as the exposure and each of the significantly associated MMPs as outcomes. Only those MMPs that demonstrated a significant causal effect in the forward analysis and a nonsignificant result in the reverse analysis were retained for subsequent mediation analyses, thereby reinforcing the directionality of causality from MMP to ILD. Second, forward MR analysis was performed between 731 immune cell phenotypes and ILD to identify immune traits with significant causal effects on ILD risk. Third, forward MR analysis was applied to evaluate the causal relationships between the selected MMPs and significant immune phenotypes, with identified mediators subsequently incorporated into a mediation analysis framework to quantify the proportion of the effect mediated through immune cells.

2.3. GWAS summary data sources

All analyses utilized publicly available GWAS summary statistics from European-ancestry populations. Genetic instruments for MMPs were obtained from 2 sources: MMP-1, MMP-3, MMP-7, and MMP-12 from a GWAS of 21,758 individuals, and MMP-2 and MMP-9 from a GWAS of 1323 individuals. Genetic associations for 731 immune cell traits were obtained from a GWAS comprising 3757 individuals (accession numbers: GCST0001391–GCST0002121).[13] The immunophenotypes encompassed absolute cell counts (AC), median fluorescence intensities (MFI) indicating surface antigen expression, morphological parameters (MP), and relative cell counts (RC). ILD summary statistics were derived from a meta-analysis of 4,69,827 individuals (Table 1). This study included human subjects gathered from prior investigations to present the extensive GWAS. Ethical approval and informed consent of participants were obtained in all the respective original studies.

2.4. Selection of IVs

Genetic instruments for each analysis were selected as single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) strongly associated with the exposure of interest at genome-wide significance (P < 5 × 10−8 for MMPs and ILD; P < 1 × 10−5 for immune cell traits, consistent with prior immunophenotype MR studies[14,15]). Then, these SNPs were clumped to ensure independence (r2 < 0.001 within 10,000 kb) using the 1000 Genomes European reference panel. We additionally excluded SNPs associated with potential confounders or directly with the outcome through pleiotropic pathways using the LDtrait tool on the NIH LDlink website (https://ldlink.nih.gov/?tab=home). All selected instruments demonstrated F-statistics > 10, indicating sufficient strength to minimize weak instrument bias. The F statistic was calculated using the equation F = R2(N − K − 1)/K(1 − R2), where R2 represents the proportion of variance in the exposure that is explained by the IVs, and N represents the sample size of the exposure dataset.[16]

3. Statistical analysis

A range of rigorous statistical techniques were utilized, including the inverse-variance weighted (IVW) method based on a random-effects model, the weighted median approach, MR-Egger regression, and the simple and weighted mode methods, with the primary causal estimates derived using IVW method. We conducted comprehensive sensitivity analyses including MR-Egger regression to assess directional pleiotropy, weighted median estimation for robustness to invalid instruments, Cochran Q test to evaluate heterogeneity, and MR-Pleiotropy Residual Sum and Outlier (MR-PRESSO) testing to detect and correct for outliers. Additionally, the leave-one-out approach was employed to examine whether the genetic causal link between exposures and outcomes could be affected by an individual single SNP.

To address multiple comparisons in the evaluation of 731 immune cell traits, we employed a hypothesis-driven, multistage selection process rather than applying a blanket false discovery rate (FDR) correction. This approach was chosen to balance the risk of false positives with the need to retain statistical power for detecting biologically plausible mediators. First, we identified immune cell phenotypes nominally associated with ILD (univariate MR P < .05). From this initial set, we applied stringent, prespecified criteria to prioritize robustness over sheer multiplicity adjustment: consistent direction of effect across 5 complementary MR methods (IVW, MR-Egger, weighted median, simple mode, weighted mode) and no significant evidence of horizontal pleiotropy (MR-Egger intercept P > .05). This 2-step filter effectively refined the candidate pool to a focused set of traits with enhanced reliability.

For mediation analysis, we calculated the proportion mediated using the product of coefficients method: the effect of MMPs on the mediator (β1) was multiplied by the effect of the mediator on ILD (β2), and this product (β12) was divided by the total effect of MMPs on ILD (β0). The direct effect of MMPs on ILD was derived by subtracting the mediated effect (β12) from the total effect (β0). All the statistical analyses were conducted using the “TwoSampleMR” and “MRPRESSO” packages in R software (version 4.3.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A P value below .05 was regarded as statistically significant.

4. Results

4.1. Causal effects of MMPs on ILD risk

Initial 2-sample MR analysis was performed to assess the causal relationship between 6 circulating MMPs and ILD risk. The IVW method revealed that among all tested MMPs, only MMP-9 showed a statistically significant positive association with ILD (OR = 1.034, 95% CI: 1.006–1.064, P = .018), while reverse MR analysis found no significant causal effect of ILD on MMP-9 levels (OR = 1.017, 95% CI: 0.872–1.187, P = .826), supporting the directionality of the primary finding. No significant associations were observed for MMP-1, MMP-2, MMP-3, MMP-7, or MMP-12 (all P > .05; Fig. 2). The 75 SNPs selected as instrumental variables for MMP-9 were presented in Table S1, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/R682.

4.2. Identification of causal immune cell phenotypes for ILD

We further conducted MR analysis to evaluate the causal effects of 731 immune cell phenotypes on ILD. Initial MR analysis identified 35 immune cell traits with an IVW P value below .05 (Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/R682). To ensure robust and pleiotropy-free associations, these 35 immune cell traits were subjected to a 2-stage refinement process: consistent direction of effect across 5 different MR methods (IVW, MR-Egger, weighted median, simple mode, and weighted mode), and no significant horizontal pleiotropy as indicated by a MR-Egger intercept test P value > .05. Following this rigorous filtering, 8 immune cell traits were retained for subsequent analysis, including naive CD8bright T cell AC (CD8brT AC; OR = 0.903, P = .001), percentage of naive CD8bright T cells (CD8brT%; OR = 0.930, P = .007), effector memory double negative T cell (EM DNT) AC (OR = 1.170, P = .001), CD14+CD16+ monocyte AC (OR = 0.912, P = .007), percentage of CD8dim natural killer T cells (CD8dim NKT%; OR = 1.133, P = .004), herpesvirus entry mediator on effector memory CD4+ T cells (HVEM on EM CD4+ T; OR = 0.928, P < .001), HVEM on central memory CD8bright T cells (HVEM on CM CD8br T; OR = 0.940, P = .003), and programmed death ligand 1 (PDL-1) on CD14–CD16–monocyte (OR = 1.059, P = .006; Fig. 3).

4.3. Mediation role of immune cells between MMP-9 and ILD

To explore whether the effect of MMP-9 on ILD was mediated through immune cells, we performed MR analyses treating MMP-9 as the exposure and each of the 8 immune cell phenotypes as outcomes. A significant causal effect was observed only on EM DNT (IVW OR = 1.041, 95% CI: 1.009–1.074, P = .012; Fig. 4, Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/R682). The effect of EM DNT on ILD remained robust (IVW OR = 1.170, 95% CI: 1.063–1.287, P = .001; Fig. 4). The SNPs selected as instrumental variables for MMP-9 and EM DNT were listed in Tables S4 and S5, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/R682, respectively. Mediation analysis using the product of coefficients method revealed a significant mediated effect (β12 = β1 × β2 = 0.006). The total effect of MMP-9 on ILD was β0 = 0.034, resulting in a mediated proportion of 18.5%. The direct effect of MMP-9 on ILD independent of the mediator was 0.028 (Fig. 5).

4.4. Sensitivity analyses for heterogeneity and pleiotropy

Sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the robustness of the primary MR results. Cochran Q test indicated no significant heterogeneity in the MR estimates for the associations of MMP-9 with ILD (IVW Q P = .668), MMP-9 with EM DNT (IVW Q P = .415), or EM DNT with ILD (IVW Q P = .895). MR-Egger regression intercept tests provided no evidence of horizontal pleiotropy for any of these causal paths (all P > .05; Table 2), supporting the validity of the IVW estimates.

5. Discussion

This study employed a comprehensive MR analysis to investigate the causal role of circulating MMPs in ILD, with a focus on potential mediation by immune cell phenotypes. Our results demonstrate a significant positive causal effect of MMP-9 on ILD risk, which was notably mediated by EM DNT. These findings not only highlight MMP-9 as a promising biomarker and therapeutic target but also unveil a novel immune-mediated pathway in ILD pathogenesis.

While previous studies have implicated several MMPs in the pathogenesis of ILD, MMP-9 has emerged as one of the most extensively studied and plays a distinct role in fibrotic ILDs.[4,6,17,18] MMP-9, also known as gelatinase B, plays a complex, dual role in the pathogenesis of ILD. Expressed by macrophages, neutrophils, and fibroblasts in response to injury and cytokines like IL-33 and IL-13, it drives ECM remodeling through its proteolytic activity and engages in a pro-fibrotic feed-forward loop by activating latent TGF-β, which in turn amplifies its own expression.[6] Paradoxically, in vivo evidence such as the attenuated fibrosis observed in bleomycin-treated mice with macrophage-specific MMP-9 overexpression highlights its context-dependent and often contradictory functions.[19] While extensive observational studies have consistently documented elevated MMP-9 levels in ILD patients, our MR study provides crucial causal inference, specifically identifying MMP-9 as a genetically determined risk factor for ILD. A key distinction of our work is the establishment of a unidirectional causal effect of MMP-9 on ILD, thereby overcoming the inherent limitations of conventional epidemiological designs that could not rule out reverse causation. Thus, our MR analysis substantiates that MMP-9 is an active driver of ILD pathology rather than a secondary bystander, reinforcing its relevance as a therapeutic target and clarifying its central, albeit complex, role in disease mechanisms.

A key novel finding of our study is the identification of EM-DNT cells as a significant mediator of MMP-9’s effect on ILD. DNT cells are a unique lymphocyte population that lack the CD4 and CD8 co-receptors. Like all T lymphocytes, they express the T-cell receptor (TCR) αβ or γδ chains, which enables them to recognize and further respond to pathogens, mounting a functional adaptive immune response.[20] Despite their minor population size, DNT cells play significant roles in the pathogenesis of human diseases – either through regulatory functions that suppress inflammatory responses or by directly mediating cell death and tissue damage.[20] While DNT cells are well-documented contributors to various autoimmune and inflammatory diseases such as lupus, rheumatoid arthritis, and acute kidney injury,[20–22] their potential involvement in pulmonary diseases remains largely unexplored. Emerging evidence from studies of pulmonary infections and ischemia-reperfusion injury suggests DNT cells may serve immunomodulatory functions in the lung, through mechanisms including the regulation of inflammatory responses, control of apoptosis, and maintenance of dendritic cell subset balance in the lung.[23,24] However, their specific role in the pathogenesis of ILD remains poorly characterized. Their association with ILD risk thereby underscores the contribution of unconventional T cell populations to lung pathology.

Effector memory (EM) T cells, identified as CD45RA−CCR7−, are characterized by their capacity to migrate directly to peripheral tissues and perform immediate effector functions upon antigen reencounter.[25] Based on our MR findings and the recognized biology of EM and DNT cells, we hypothesize that EM DNT cells could constitute a critical link between immune activation and fibrotic progression in ILD. This hypothesis suggests several plausible, yet untested, mechanistic pathways. First, it is conceivable that EM DNT cells migrate to lung tissue and, upon local antigen reencounter, contribute to tissue damage via direct cytotoxicity or the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-17 and IFN-γ – mechanisms documented for similar T cell subsets in other contexts.[20] Second, and more specifically to MMP-9-mediated pathology, EM DNT cells might potentially act as a cellular source of MMP-9 within the lung, thereby directly promoting ECM remodeling. These possibilities, while not directly tested in our genetic study, offer a focused framework for future experimental research to elucidate how the EM DNT–MMP-9 axis might drive ILD pathogenesis.

Several limitations of our study warrant consideration. First, the GWAS for MMP-9 was based on a relatively small sample, which may limit the robustness and precision of the genetic instruments. Second, the summary-level data used in our 2-sample MR precluded detailed subgroup analyses (e.g., by ILD subtype, sex, or smoking status), which may obscure heterogeneity in the causal effect. Third, the definition and quantification of immune cell populations, including EM DNT cells, are based on relative proportions or absolute numbers in peripheral blood, which may not perfectly reflect their phenotype, function, or abundance in the lung. Fourth, the GWAS data for both exposures and outcome are derived from European ancestry populations, limiting the generalizability of our findings to other ethnic groups. Finally, while MR suggests causality, experimental validation is needed to confirm the biological mechanisms linking MMP-9, EM DNT, and ILD.

This study provides genetic evidence supporting a causal role of MMP-9 in the development of ILD, partially mediated through EM DNT. These findings highlight MMP-9 and EM DNT as potential candidates for further mechanistic and therapeutic investigation. Future research should validate these associations in diverse populations and use experimental models to elucidate the underlying biology.

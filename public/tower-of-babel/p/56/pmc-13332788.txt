Long-Term Trends in the Burden of Bipolar Disorder in China, 1990–2052: An Analysis of the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023
Authors: Derong Lin, Pengpeng Guo, Menglin Kou, Xiaohua Xie, Yue Li, Shuxiong Luo, Aiguo Xue, Jingrong Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBipolar disorder is a chronic mood disorder that imposes substantial long-term disability. Updated national evidence is needed to inform mental health planning in China.ObjectiveTo assess temporal trends in the burden of bipolar disorder in China from 1990 to 2023 and project its burden from 2024 to 2052.MethodsUsing Global Burden of Disease Study 2023 (GBD 2023) estimates, we analyzed incidence, prevalence, and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) by year and sex. Age-standardized incidence, prevalence, and DALY rates (ASIR, ASPR, and ASDR) were evaluated using descriptive trend analysis, Sociodemographic Index (SDI) correlation and generalized additive models, Das Gupta decomposition, Bayesian age-period-cohort projection, and age-period-cohort analysis.ResultsFrom 1990 to 2023, incident cases, prevalent cases, and DALYs increased in both sexes, whereas age-standardized rates changed only slightly. In 2023, females had higher ASIR, ASPR, and ASDR than males (16.013 vs 14.240, 218.269 vs 190.726, and 47.161 vs 41.503 per 100,000, respectively). SDI was not significantly correlated with ASIR or ASPR but was modestly correlated with ASDR (R = 0.385, P = 0.0247); non-linear associations were observed for all three standardized indicators. Decomposition showed that population growth mainly drove increases in incident cases, while population growth and aging jointly explained increases in prevalence and DALYs. By 2052, projected both-sex ASIR, ASPR, and ASDR were 38.277, 334.308, and 71.246 per 100,000, respectively. Incidence peaked at 15–19 years, whereas prevalence and DALYs peaked at 50–54 and 45–49 years, respectively.ConclusionThe absolute burden of bipolar disorder in China increased substantially and is projected to remain high. Females, adolescents, and middle-aged adults may require priority attention through earlier identification, sustained management, and long-term service planning, while long-range and cohort-related projections should be interpreted cautiously.

Introduction

Bipolar disorder is a chronic and recurrent mood disorder characterized by episodes of mania, hypomania, depression, or mixed states, with depressive symptoms accounting for most symptomatic time and much of the long-term functional burden.1 Its clinical management remains challenging because early presentations are often nonspecific, diagnostic delay is common, and effective care requires sustained phase-specific treatment across the illness course.2 In addition to psychiatric morbidity, bipolar disorder is associated with substantial premature mortality, and recent meta-analytic evidence has shown markedly reduced life expectancy and considerable years of potential life lost compared with the general population.3 Recent real-world analyses have further demonstrated that bipolar I disorder imposes a substantial humanistic and economic burden through impaired quality of life, healthcare utilization, and productivity loss.4

At the population level, bipolar disorder remains an important contributor to the global burden of mental disorders.5 A recent GBD 2021 analysis showed that the burden of bipolar disorder increased persistently worldwide from 1990 to 2021, with marked heterogeneity across regions, age groups, sexes, and levels of sociodemographic development.6 Separate GBD 2021 work in women of reproductive age likewise identified substantial cross-national inequalities and distinct temporal patterns across SDI strata, underscoring the epidemiologic value of development-sensitive analyses.7 In China, however, contemporary population-based evidence remains comparatively limited, and a recent survey in Beijing suggested that bipolar and related disorders are identified far less often than anxiety or depressive disorders in the community.8 Service-based data from Shanghai similarly showed that bipolar affective disorder accounted for only a small proportion of mental-disorder diagnoses in community health service centers, highlighting a persistent gap between psychiatric need and routine identification within primary care settings.9

This gap has important clinical implications because patients with bipolar disorder frequently present with substantial medical comorbidity, which can amplify disability and complicate long-term management.10 At the same time, contemporary reviews have emphasized that meaningful progress in bipolar-disorder research requires integration of epidemiologic, developmental, and health-system perspectives rather than reliance on narrow cross-sectional descriptions alone.11 Although previous burden studies based on earlier GBD iterations have described global and regional trends in bipolar disorder, they do not fully reflect the most recent epidemiologic profile needed for updated national assessment and long-range planning in China.12 Therefore, using the GBD 2023 framework, the present study aimed to systematically assess the burden of bipolar disorder in China from 1990 to 2023 and to project its future trajectory through 2052, with additional analyses of SDI-related patterns, demographic drivers of change, and age-period-cohort effects.

Methods

Study Design and Data Sources

This study was a nationwide burden analysis of bipolar disorder in China based on the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023 (GBD 2023), a comparative epidemiological framework that estimates health loss across countries, years, age groups, and sexes. Data were retrieved from the official GBD 2023 analytical platform for China and extracted by year, sex, age group, measure, and metric. We obtained annual estimates for incidence, prevalence, and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) from 1990 to 2023, including both all-age counts and age-standardized rates. In plain terms, incidence refers to new cases, prevalence refers to people living with bipolar disorder, and DALYs summarize overall health loss. In line with the GBD framework, all estimates were reported with 95% uncertainty intervals (95% UIs), defined by the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of 1000 posterior draws.13

Case Definitions and Burden Metrics

Bipolar disorder was defined according to the non-fatal disease classification and cause hierarchy used in GBD 2023. The primary outcomes were incidence, prevalence, and disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs). DALYs were interpreted as the sum of years of life lost (YLLs) and years lived with disability (YLDs), meaning premature mortality and non-fatal disability, respectively. For all burden indicators, both all-age counts and age-standardized rates per 100,000 population were analyzed. The age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR), age-standardized prevalence rate (ASPR), and age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) were evaluated as the principal standardized metrics; these indicators represent age-adjusted incidence, prevalence, and DALY rates, respectively. Descriptive Analysis

We first described long-term temporal trends in the burden of bipolar disorder in China from 1990 to 2023 by sex. Absolute counts were used to reflect the population-level burden, whereas age-standardized rates were used to evaluate underlying epidemiological change independent of demographic shifts. Percentage change between 1990 and 2023 was calculated as (value in 2023 − value in 1990) / value in 1990×100. Association Between SDI and Bipolar Disorder Burden

To assess the relationship between socioeconomic development and disease burden, we examined annual associations between the Sociodemographic Index (SDI) and age-standardized incidence, prevalence, and DALY rates of bipolar disorder in China. SDI is a composite measure derived from lag-distributed income per capita, mean educational attainment in the population aged 15 years and older, and the total fertility rate in females younger than 25 years. The monotonic association between SDI and each burden indicator was assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient, which evaluates whether two variables tend to increase or decrease together. Decomposition Analysis

To quantify the drivers of changes in bipolar disorder burden from 1990 to 2023, we applied Das Gupta decomposition to partition temporal changes in counts into the contributions of population growth, population aging, and epidemiological change. For a given year, the number of events was expressed as the product of total population and the weighted sum of age-specific rates. The total change between 1990 and 2023 was decomposed into additive components corresponding to these three sources. Bayesian Age-Period-Cohort Projection

Future trends from 2024 to 2052 were projected using a Bayesian age-period-cohort (BAPC) framework. This model combines age, calendar period, and birth cohort information to estimate future patterns while accounting for uncertainty. In the BAPC model, observed counts were assumed to follow a Poisson distribution, with age, period, and cohort effects linked on the log scale. Second-order random-walk priors were used to regularize temporal effects. Age-Period-Cohort Analysis

We further used an age-period-cohort (APC) model to evaluate the age, period, and cohort patterns of future bipolar disorder burden. In this framework, age effects describe differences across age groups, period effects describe changes shared by all ages during a calendar period, and cohort effects describe differences among people born in different years. Data were regrouped into consecutive 5-year age intervals from 10–14 to 75–79 years, six 5-year periods from 2023–2027 to 2048–2052, and 19 corresponding birth cohorts from 1946–1950 to 2036–2040. The reference categories were set to age 40–44 years, period 2033–2037, and cohort 1991–1995. Period and cohort effects were summarized as rate ratios relative to the corresponding reference groups, whereas longitudinal age curves were interpreted as the fitted age-specific rates adjusted for period deviations. Local drift was defined as the age-specific annual percent change, and net drift as the overall annual percent change across the full APC surface.18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage[substack]{amsmath} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage[mathscr]{eucal} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \DeclareFontFamily{T1}{linotext}{} \DeclareFontShape{T1}{linotext}{m}{n} {linotext }{} \DeclareSymbolFont{linotext}{T1}{linotext}{m}{n} \DeclareSymbolFontAlphabet{\mathLINOTEXT}{linotext} \begin{document}$$log\left({Ra,p} \right){\mathrm{ }} = {\mathrm{ }}\mu {\mathrm{ }} + {\mathrm{ }}\alpha a{\mathrm{ }} + {\mathrm{ }}\beta p{\mathrm{ }} + {\mathrm{ }}\gamma c{\mathrm{ }} + {\mathrm{ }}\varepsilon a,p$$\end{document}

Model Validation and Statistical Analysis

To assess predictive robustness, we performed retrospective validation by using data from 1990 to 2015 as the training set and data from 2016 to 2023 as the validation set. Predictive accuracy was evaluated using mean absolute error (MAE), root mean square error (RMSE), and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE). In simple terms, MAE indicates the average absolute prediction error, RMSE gives greater weight to larger errors, and MAPE expresses prediction error as a percentage of the observed value; lower values indicate closer agreement between projected and observed estimates. All statistical analyses were specified in R (version 4.4.2 or later). The main analytical components included rank-based correlation analysis, GAM smoothing, Das Gupta decomposition, BAPC projection, and APC estimation. Relationship Between SDI and Bipolar Disorder Burden in China, 1990–2023

Using the corrected both-sex Figure 2 dataset, China’s annual SDI increased from 0.465 to 0.728 during 1990–2023. Spearman correlation analysis showed no significant association between SDI and ASIR (R = −0.129, P = 0.468) or between SDI and ASPR (R = 0.150, P = 0.396). By contrast, ASDR showed a modest but statistically significant positive correlation with SDI (R = 0.385, P = 0.0247). Thus, socioeconomic development was not linearly associated with the standardized incidence or prevalence of bipolar disorder, but it was positively associated with the standardized DALY burden. Decomposition of Changes in the Burden of Bipolar Disorder, 1990–2023

Das Gupta decomposition showed that population growth was the dominant positive driver of the increasing burden of bipolar disorder in China, while population aging exerted opposite effects on incident cases and accumulated burden. In males, the 19.079% increase in incident cases from 1990 to 2023 was more than fully explained by population growth, which contributed 106.908% of the observed increase, while population aging and epidemiological change contributed −4.357% and −2.551%, respectively. For prevalence in males, aging and population growth contributed 44.379% and 58.621% of the total increase, whereas epidemiological change contributed −3.000%. For DALYs in males, the corresponding contributions were 40.488%, 61.864%, and −2.352%.

In females, the 40.952% increase in prevalent cases was mainly attributable to population aging and population growth, which contributed 42.219% and 58.380%, respectively, while epidemiological change made only a small negative contribution (−0.599%). For female DALYs, population growth remained the largest contributor (60.980%), followed by aging (37.722%) and a small positive epidemiological contribution (1.298%). In the both-sex population, aging and population growth explained nearly all of the increase in prevalence and DALYs, whereas epidemiological change was minimal: for prevalence, the contribution percentages were 43.433%, 58.338%, and −1.771%, respectively; for DALYs, they were 39.214%, 61.238%, and −0.453%, respectively. For incident cases in the both-sex population, population growth remained the dominant positive component (109.840%), whereas aging and epidemiological change were both negative (−8.268% and −1.572%). Overall, these results indicate that demographic forces, rather than marked changes in standardized epidemiological risk, were the principal drivers of the expanding burden of bipolar disorder in China. BAPC-Projections of the Future Burden of Bipolar Disorder in China, 2024–2052

BAPC projections indicated that the age-standardized burden of bipolar disorder in China will continue to increase from 2024 to 2052. By 2052, the projected ASIR was 29.727/100,000 in males, 40.639/100,000 in females, and 38.277/100,000 in the total population. The projected ASPR reached 306.596/100,000 in males, 371.394/100,000 in females, and 334.308/100,000 in the total population. Likewise, the projected ASDR rose to 66.365/100,000 in males, 75.606/100,000 in females, and 71.246/100,000 in the total population. Across all three measures, females were projected to maintain the highest age-standardized burden by the end of the forecast horizon.

Retrospective validation supported the robustness of the BAPC model. Supplementary Table S7 showed that MAE and RMSE remained low across all sex-specific models and that all MAPE values were below 1.3%. The lowest MAPE was observed for male DALYs (0.88%), followed by female incidence (0.93%) and male prevalence (0.97%), whereas the highest MAPE was only 1.21% for male incidence. These results indicate close agreement between predicted and observed values during the validation period and support the stability of the projection framework used in this study. Discussion

Principal Findings

Using the GBD 2023 framework, this study provides a comprehensive assessment of the historical burden and future trajectory of bipolar disorder in China. Three findings deserve particular emphasis. First, from 1990 to 2023, incident cases, prevalent cases, and DALYs all increased substantially in absolute terms, whereas age-standardized rates changed only slightly. Second, the relationship between socioeconomic development and bipolar disorder burden was not adequately described by a simple linear pattern; only ASDR showed a significant monotonic correlation with SDI, whereas GAMs indicated significant non-linear associations for all three age-standardized indicators. Third, decomposition analysis, BAPC projections, and APC modeling consistently suggested that the burden of bipolar disorder in China will remain substantial over the coming decades. Females, adolescents, and middle-aged adults appear to be priority populations, while the higher projected burden in more recent birth cohorts should be interpreted cautiously because cohort patterns may also reflect diagnostic recognition, service utilization, and awareness.

Rising Counts With Stable Standardized Rates

A central finding of this study is the divergence between rising absolute counts and relatively stable age-standardized rates. This pattern indicates that the expanding burden of bipolar disorder in China has been driven primarily by demographic change rather than by a marked increase in underlying age-specific epidemiological risk. Similar divergence has been described in previous GBD-based burden studies of bipolar disorder, which have shown that counts may rise substantially even when standardized rates remain broadly stable.20 A more recent inequality-focused analysis likewise suggested that long-term changes in bipolar disorder burden are often shaped by the interaction among demographic growth, population aging, and service context rather than by abrupt shifts in standardized risk alone.21 In this respect, our results add China-specific evidence to a broader international pattern and highlight why increasing service demand should not be dismissed simply because standardized rates appear stable.

SDI and the Developmental Context of Burden

Our SDI analysis yielded a nuanced picture. ASIR and ASPR were not significantly correlated with SDI on monotonic analysis, whereas ASDR showed a modest positive correlation; at the same time, all three indicators exhibited significant non-linear associations in GAM models. These findings suggest that the burden of bipolar disorder does not change uniformly with socioeconomic development. Instead, development may simultaneously affect diagnostic recognition, access to treatment, chronic survival with disability, and the composition of unmet need. Recent work on the global burden and inequalities of bipolar disorder has similarly emphasized that development-related gradients are complex and highly heterogeneous across settings.21 In China, the positive association between SDI and ASDR may reflect improved detection and accumulation of chronic disability in more developed settings rather than a straightforward worsening of disease occurrence. Accordingly, our findings support a development-sensitive interpretation of bipolar disorder burden rather than a simple linear model of social change.

Sex-Specific Burden Patterns

Another notable feature of our results is the consistently higher burden among females, particularly for prevalence, DALYs, and future projected burden. Although bipolar disorder has long been considered broadly similar across sexes in crude occurrence, recent studies increasingly indicate meaningful sex-related differences in clinical course, psychosocial functioning, and disability burden.22,23 In our analysis, females had higher age-standardized rates in 2023, stronger projected increases through 2052, and more pronounced upward period effects. These findings are epidemiologically important because they suggest that sex-specific differences in long-term impairment may be at least as relevant as sex differences in onset. From a public health perspective, our results support greater attention to sex-sensitive screening, maintenance treatment, relapse prevention, and rehabilitation planning for women with bipolar disorder in China.

Age, Period, and Cohort Patterns

The APC results provide further insight into how the burden of bipolar disorder may evolve over time. Incidence peaked in adolescence, whereas prevalence and DALYs peaked later in adulthood, indicating that new onset is concentrated earlier in life but the cumulative burden of illness and disability becomes most apparent in middle age. This pattern is consistent with contemporary evidence showing that bipolar disorder often emerges in adolescence or early adulthood but exerts its largest long-term functional burden across subsequent decades.24 Our period effects also suggested a gradual upward shift over the projection horizon, and cohort rate ratios were higher in more recent birth cohorts. However, cohort effects in psychiatric epidemiology require cautious interpretation. They may indicate changes in underlying burden, but they may also reflect improved diagnostic recognition, changes in diagnostic practice, increased service utilization, changing help-seeking behavior, or greater awareness of bipolar disorder over time. Therefore, our APC findings are best interpreted as signals of possible cohort-related differences and future service need rather than proof of a direct causal increase in risk among later-born cohorts.

Comorbidity, Disability, and Long-Term Clinical Burden

The growing burden of bipolar disorder in China should not be interpreted solely through the lens of psychiatric symptoms. Bipolar disorders include clinically heterogeneous presentations, including bipolar I and bipolar II disorder, but the GBD cause hierarchy does not provide subtype-specific estimates.25 This limitation is important because subtype composition, illness phase, predominant depressive or manic polarity, psychotic features, and comorbidity can influence functional impairment, suicide risk, and service needs.25 Clinical heterogeneity is also biologically plausible, as neuroimaging evidence has described structural and functional network abnormalities in individuals with bipolar disorder.26 In routine care, early presentations may be dominated by depressive symptoms or nonspecific mood instability, and diagnostic delay can postpone mood-stabilizing treatment and relapse-prevention strategies.2,25,27 Treatment gaps, incomplete continuity of care, and limited access to specialist services may further increase the duration of untreated or undertreated illness, thereby contributing to accumulated prevalence and DALYs even when standardized incidence changes only modestly. Bipolar disorder is also increasingly recognized as a condition accompanied by broad physical comorbidity, impaired psychosocial functioning, excess mortality, and suicide-related burden.28–31 These dimensions help explain why prevalence and DALYs rose more clearly than incidence in our study. Once bipolar disorder develops, its effects on education, employment, social functioning, and physical health may accumulate over many years, producing a larger long-term disability burden than would be inferred from onset counts alone. In this context, the expansion of prevalent cases and DALYs observed here represents not merely more diagnoses, but a widening pool of individuals living with prolonged and clinically meaningful impairment.

Future Projections and Health-System Planning

Our BAPC projections indicate that the age-standardized burden of bipolar disorder in China will continue to rise through 2052, especially in females. This projection pattern is also consistent with recent commentaries emphasizing the analytic opportunities of GBD 2023 for forward-looking global health surveillance.32 These forecasts should be interpreted as conditional projections based on current trajectories rather than deterministic certainties. This caveat is particularly important for estimates beyond 2040, where uncertainty may increase because future patterns depend on assumptions about healthcare access, diagnostic practices, service coverage, demographic structure, and mental health policy. Changes in community screening, insurance coverage, primary-care integration, availability of mood-stabilizing treatment, and suicide-prevention systems could shift future trajectories away from those projected from historical data. Nevertheless, the policy relevance of the forecasts remains clear: if current patterns persist, China’s future mental health system will need to manage not only continued incident burden in younger populations but also the expanding cumulative burden among adults living with chronic bipolar disorder. The relatively low forecast errors observed in our retrospective validation support the internal stability of the projection framework, but they do not eliminate the uncertainty inherent in long-range forecasting.

Comparison with Previous Studies

Our findings are broadly concordant with earlier GBD-based studies showing that bipolar disorder remains a persistent and unevenly distributed contributor to mental health burden worldwide.20,21 The GBD 2013 bipolar disorder analysis also reported a large increase in prevalent cases over time, largely attributable to population growth and aging, and emphasized the disabling nature of bipolar disorder because of early onset, chronicity, and severity.33 A recent projection study of schizophrenia burden in China based on GBD 2023 likewise reported a persistent future increase under demographic transition, suggesting that this long-horizon pattern is not unique to bipolar disorder.34 The present study nevertheless extends previous work in several ways. First, it updates the evidence base to GBD 2023 and therefore captures a more recent epidemiological profile than studies based on GBD 2017, GBD 2019, or GBD 2021.20,21,24 Second, it integrates multiple analytical dimensions within a single national framework, including descriptive trend analysis, SDI-related analysis, decomposition, projection, and APC modeling. Third, it places particular emphasis on China-specific sex, age, period, and cohort heterogeneity. This integrated design allows the present study to move beyond simple burden description and to provide a more policy-relevant account of how bipolar disorder may evolve in China over both the historical and future time horizons.

Public Health Implications

Several practical implications emerge from our findings. First, because incidence peaked in adolescence, earlier recognition and follow-up during the transition from adolescence to young adulthood should be strengthened.35 Recent GBD-based work on major mental disorders in people younger than 24 years similarly identified adolescence and young adulthood as critical windows for prevention and early intervention.36 Second, because prevalence and DALYs peaked in adulthood and remained higher in females, long-term management and rehabilitation services should be designed with particular attention to women and middle-aged adults. Third, given the links between bipolar disorder, suicide, physical comorbidity, and functional impairment, service models should integrate mood stabilization, relapse prevention, suicide-risk assessment, and physical health management rather than treating these needs in isolation.25,28–31,35 Fourth, because delayed help-seeking, delayed diagnosis, and longer duration of untreated illness have been documented in bipolar disorders, China’s mental health system may need stronger pathways between primary care, community mental health services, and specialist psychiatry, with attention to follow-up adherence and long-term maintenance care.27 Finally, because the burden pattern was strongly shaped by demography and showed sensitivity to social development, national planning should prioritize continuity of care, community-based rehabilitation, and better integration between psychiatric and primary health services.37–40 Life-course evidence from China further suggests that rapid social change and cohort experience shape mental-health trajectories over time.41 A recent national analysis also documented a substantial disability burden of mental disorders among children and adolescents in China, reinforcing the need for earlier system-level intervention.42 Taken together, these implications suggest that the future challenge posed by bipolar disorder in China will be determined as much by system capacity, equitable access, and long-term follow-up quality as by changes in underlying epidemiology.

Strengths and Limitations

This study has several strengths. It used the most recent GBD 2023 framework, adopted a multi-dimensional analytical strategy, and linked historical burden patterns with future projections in a unified national analysis. By combining descriptive analyses, SDI-related analyses, decomposition, BAPC projection, and APC modeling, we were able to characterize the burden of bipolar disorder in China from complementary epidemiological perspectives. At the same time, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, as with all GBD-based studies, the estimates rely on modeled synthesis of multiple data sources rather than direct annual nationwide surveillance for each burden indicator. For bipolar disorder, this issue may be particularly relevant because underdiagnosis, delayed diagnosis, misclassification during early depressive episodes, cultural differences in symptom recognition, and variation in help-seeking or service use may influence the input data and therefore the modeled estimates.27 Second, GBD estimates do not distinguish bipolar I disorder, bipolar II disorder, cyclothymic disorder, predominant depressive or manic polarity, psychotic features, comorbidity profiles, or treatment status, which limits subtype-specific clinical interpretation.25 Third, decomposition, BAPC, and APC analyses describe statistical structure and projected trends but cannot establish causal mechanisms. Projections to 2052 should be interpreted as conditional forecasts, and uncertainty is likely to increase after 2040 because future healthcare access, diagnostic practice, demographic changes, and policy interventions may differ from historical patterns. Fourth, APC cohort effects require caution because higher rate ratios in later birth cohorts may reflect improved recognition, diagnostic changes, awareness, and service utilization as well as possible changes in underlying burden. Finally, our national-level approach cannot capture potentially important provincial or urban-rural heterogeneity in diagnosis, service access, psychiatric workforce, stigma, socioeconomic development, and continuity of care. Future studies using provincial, urban-rural, or service-level data are needed to identify disparities that may be hidden by national aggregation. These limitations should be kept in mind when interpreting the results, although they do not alter the overall conclusion that bipolar disorder will remain an important and growing public health challenge in China.

Conclusion

In summary, the burden of bipolar disorder in China increased substantially in absolute terms from 1990 to 2023 despite only minor long-term changes in age-standardized rates. The observed expansion was driven predominantly by demographic factors, especially population growth and aging, while future analyses suggested that the burden may continue to increase through 2052 under current trajectory assumptions. Females, adolescents, and middle-aged adults emerged as particularly important populations in the present analysis, while cohort-related findings should be interpreted cautiously because they may partly reflect changes in diagnosis, awareness, and service utilization. These findings indicate that bipolar disorder should be treated as a long-term national mental health priority in China, requiring earlier identification, sex-sensitive care pathways, stronger continuity of management, suicide-risk assessment, physical comorbidity management, and sustained rehabilitation-oriented services.

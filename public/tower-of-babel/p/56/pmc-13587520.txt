Effects of remimazolam vs. propofol on plasma neurofilament light chain and postoperative delirium in frail elderly patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery: a prospective, randomized, assessor-blinded controlled trial
Authors: Xin-yao He, Shi-hua Zhang, Lei Xie, Jing-ting Bao, Ming-zi An, Zhen-ping Li, Qing-he Zhou, Xiao-yu Jia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe relationship between remimazolam administration and early neurological complications, particularly postoperative delirium (POD), in elderly patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery remains unclear. Plasma neurofilament light chain (NfL) is a potential biomarker for central nervous injury. This study aims to compare the effects of remimazolam and propofol on postoperative plasma NfL concentrations and the incidence of POD in frail elderly patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery.MethodsThis was a single-center randomized controlled trial on frail patients who underwent non-cardiac surgery. Participants were randomized to receive either Remimazolam (Group R) or Propofol (Group P) for induction and maintenance of general anesthesia, with all other anesthetic management standardized. The primary outcome was plasma NfL concentration on postoperative day 1, and the incidence of POD was assessed as a key clinical secondary endpoint. The secondary endpoint encompassed the incidence of hypotension during the anesthesia induction, as well as the time-weighted average of the area under the curve below the baseline for mean arterial pressure (AUB-MAP).ResultsA total of 123 patients were enrolled. No significant differences were observed in plasma NfL concentrations on postoperative day 1 between Group R and Group P (123.3[83.6-198.2] vs. 149.0[102.0-226.6], pg/ml, P = 0.186, respectively); and similarly, no differences in POD were found between Group R and Group P (6/62[9.7%] vs. 7/61[11.5%], unadjusted odds ratio 0.827, 95% CI 0.261–2.62, P = 0.746). In the secondary outcomes, the incidence of hypotension during the anesthesia induction in Group R was significantly decreased (3[4.8%] vs. 19[31.3%], P < 0.001). And the time-weighted average of AUB-MAP in Group R was higher than Group P (-21.3 ± 10.7 vs. -25.4 ± 9.6, mmHg, P = 0.03).ConclusionsIn frail elderly patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery, remimazolam group shows no significant difference from propofol in plasma NfL levels or POD incidence on the first day. Due to its advantage in maintaining hemodynamic stability, remimazolam may be a safer anesthetic option for frail patients sensitive to hemodynamic changes.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trials Registry, ChiCTR2400093862.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12871-026-04057-7.Keywords: Remimazolam, Neurofilament light chain, Postoperative delirium, Non-cardiac surgery, Frail elderly patients

Introduction

Cognitive function may decline after anesthesia and surgery, and this clinical syndrome characterized by acute alterations in attention, consciousness, and cognition that occurs after an anesthetic procedure is known as postoperative delirium (POD) [1]. Some patients who experience delirium may subsequently develop long-term postoperative cognitive dysfunction (POCD) [2]. Despite significant advancements in the safety of contemporary surgical procedures, elderly individuals classified as “frail” continue to experience a postoperative delirium incidence rate ranging from 12% to 50%, and this heightened risk is attributable to their diminished physiological reserve, the prevalence of multiple comorbidities across various systems, and their reduced tolerance to anesthesia and surgical stress [1, 3]. According to the 2024 updated evidence-based and consensus guidelines from the European Society of Anesthesiology and Intensive Care (ESAIC) regarding postoperative cognitive dysfunction in adult patients, there is presently no definitive research supporting the prevention of postoperative delirium through a specific pharmacological agent [4]. A prospective study conducted in 2021, which followed up 116 patients who underwent heart surgery for a period of 4 years, found that using steroid hormones to suppress the inflammatory response during the perioperative period might help prevent the development of postoperative cognitive dysfunction after heart surgery [5]. However, this result requires further confirmation through larger and multi-center studies.

The predominant methodologies employed for the prediction and diagnosis of delirium and cognitive alterations in elderly patients are scale-based approaches, including the Confusion Assessment Method (CAM) and the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) [6, 7]. However, solely relying on these assessment scales can be heavily swayed by subjective interpretations. Biomarkers of neuronal injury, such as neurofilament light chain (NfL), could be relevant for understanding the contribution of anesthesia and surgery to underlying neurodegenerative processes [8, 9]. NfL, as a neurofilament protein, has been demonstrated to be associated with multiple neurodegenerative diseases [10, 11]. Increased levels of NfL suggest central nervous system damage in elderly frail patients after anesthesia and surgery, and serve as a valuable biomarker for predicting and diagnosing postoperative delirium [12, 13].

Previous studies generally advise against using benzodiazepines for perioperative anesthesia or sedation in frail elderly patients due to increased risk of cognitive impairment [14]. However, the exact mechanism remains unclear. Several Studies indicate that short-acting benzodiazepines are not linked to delirium risk [15]. In recent years, remimazolam, a new ultra-short-acting benzodiazepine sedative, is increasingly used for perioperative sedation in elderly patients [16, 17]. It offers rapid onset, short elimination half-life, minimal reliance on liver and kidney metabolism, inactive metabolites, no accumulation with continuous use, and minimal impact on respiratory and circulatory systems, making it potentially beneficial for frail patients [16]. Current evidence on remimazolam’s effect on postoperative delirium is limited, and its effect on cognitive function following major surgeries is still debated [17–19].

Thus, in the present study, we designed a prospective randomized controlled trial to explore whether remimazolam administered during surgery can decrease the plasma NfL concentration in frail elderly patients after major non-cardiac surgeries, as well as the occurrence of postoperative delirium.

Methods

Trial design

This study was a prospective, single-center, randomized controlled clinical trial conducted in accordance with the CONSORT reporting guidelines [20]. Ethical approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of Jiaxing First Hospital (identifier: 2024-LY-755) on 22 October 2024. The trial was registered at the Chinese Clinical Trials Registry (www.chictr.org.cn, identifier: ChiCTR2400093862) on 12 December 2024. This trial was conducted at Jiaxing First Hospital from January to September 2025. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before participating in the study.

Patients

Patients were eligible if they met all the following inclusion criteria: (1) aged > 60 years; (2) American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) grades I to III; (3) scheduled for major non-cardiac surgery under general anesthesia with endotracheal intubation; (4) expected surgery duration of one or more hours; (5) frailty identified through the assessment of the Fried’s phenotype (FP) and frailty index (FI); (6) the length of hospital stay is more than 72 h postoperatively.

Exclusion criteria were the following: (1) history of neurological or psychiatric disorders, preoperative administration of antipsychotic drugs, and alcohol withdrawal delirium; (2) history of second-degree Mobitz I atrioventricular block (or higher-grade AV block), acute myocardial infarction, or stroke within the past 3 months; (3) history of severe hepatic and renal dysfunction (Child-Pugh class C, plasma creatinine > 442 µmol/L); (4) known allergies to any trial-related medications.

The withdrawal criteria were as follows: (1) experienced severe complications or mortality during or after surgery; (2) refused postoperative follow-up.

Randomization and blinding

All eligible participants were randomly assigned to either the Remimazolam group (Group R) or the Propofol group (Group P) in a 1:1 ratio utilizing computer-generated random numbers (https://www.random.org). Group assignments were managed by a research coordinator who was not involved in clinical anesthesia management or assessment and who prepared medications according to the assigned groups.

This study was an assessor-blinded design. The anesthesiologist was aware of the group and drug allocation and was responsible for administering these drugs according to the standardized protocol during surgery. The researchers involved in postoperative follow-up and data collection, patients, and surgeons were blinded to group allocation.

Intervention, anesthesia, and perioperative care

All patients were fasted for 6–8 h before surgery. Upon entering the operating room, standardized monitoring was initiated, which included pulse oximetry, invasive arterial blood pressure measurement, electrocardiogram, end-tidal carbon dioxide partial pressure (end-tidal CO₂), and bispectral index (BIS). Following preoxygenation with 100% oxygen for 3–5 min, patients underwent anesthesia induction in accordance with the anesthesia protocol assigned to them at random.

For the induction of anesthesia, patients in Group R received intravenous remimazolam (0.2–0.3 mg/kg), sufentanil (0.3–0.5 µg/kg), and rocuronium (0.6–0.8 mg/kg). Patients in Group P were administered intravenous propofol (1.5–2.5 mg/kg), sufentanil (0.3–0.5 µg/kg), and rocuronium (0.6–0.8 mg/kg). After the patient lost consciousness and the BIS value fell to 60 or lower, endotracheal intubation was performed. Mechanical ventilation was then initiated, with settings adjusted by the anesthesiologist to maintain end-tidal CO₂ levels within the range of 35-45mmHg. For maintenance, patients in Group R received intravenous remimazolam (0.2–0.3 mg/kg·h) and remifentanil (0.1–0.2 µg/kg·min), concurrently administering 1.0-1.5% sevoflurane; patients in Group P received intravenous propofol (4–12 mg/kg·h) along with remifentanil (0.1–0.2 µg/kg·min), while also administering 1.0-1.5% sevoflurane. The administration of remimazolam and propofol was discontinued five minutes prior to the closure of the skin at the end of the operation. Anaesthesiologists administered fluids and vasoactive drugs (norepinephrine and atropine) based on clinical judgement and recorded their administrations. For orthopedic patients, 0.25% ropivacaine was used for postoperative nerve block analgesia. For patients undergoing gastrointestinal surgery and urology surgery, a transversus abdominis plane block was administered for pain relief.

Following surgery, all postoperative patients were transferred to Anesthesia Intensive Care Unit (AICU) for monitoring, with tracheal extubation conducted once the extubation criteria were met. The postoperative intravenous patient-controlled analgesia (PCIA) device, which contained sufentanil and ondansetron, was used routinely based on the patient’s preferences. In the PCIA protocol, the background infusion rate of sufentanil was set at 2.0 µg/h. The patient could self-administer an additional dose of 1.0 ug per activation if they experienced pain. Following each activation, a lockout period of 15 min was enforced to prevent further dosing. If the patient experiences severe nausea or vomiting, the PCIA will be discontinued. Researchers conducted regular follow-ups with patients after surgery to collect early postoperative outcome data.

Outcome assessment

The primary outcome of this study was the plasma NfL level measured on postoperative day 1. In addition, the incidence of POD in frail elderly patients following surgery was assessed as a key clinical secondary endpoint, and its correlation with the primary outcome was examined as a clinical endpoint for descriptive analysis. Secondary outcomes included the incidence of hypotension during the anesthesia induction, and the time-weighted average of the area under the curve below the baseline for mean arterial pressure (AUB-MAP).

Other perioperative outcomes included, but are not limited to, the duration of anesthesia, the time to extubation (defined as the interval from arrival in the AICU until extubation), and the incidence of postoperative adverse events, which include postoperative mortality, nausea and vomiting, and sleep disturbances. Additionally, we documented postoperative pain scores and analgesic consumption. Data will be collected from anesthesia records and clinical notes to facilitate the evaluation of the safety and efficacy of the two anesthesia protocols.

Data collection

Preoperative plasma NfL was examined within 24 h before and after the operation, and the concentration of NfL was evaluated using the Meso Scale Discovery Electroche-miluminescence (MSD-ECL) technology. The assessment of preoperative cognitive function (MMSE) and the evaluation of the frailty index were performed within one to three days prior to surgery. In contrast, the assessment of postoperative cognitive function was conducted within 24 h following the surgical procedure, typically between 6:00 and 8:00 AM on postoperative day 1. The patients included in the study were stratified according to Fried’s phenotype (FP) criteria into categories of pre-frailty and frailty, or classified into three groups based on the Frailty Index (FI): healthy (grades I-III), frailty (grades IV-VI), and severe frailty (grades VII-IX). Following the assessment of the patient’s level of consciousness utilizing the Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale (RASS), the patient’s cognitive status was subsequently evaluated using the Confusion Assessment Method of Intensive Care Unit (CAM-ICU). All assessments were conducted by a single research staff member who had specialized training in delirium assessment to ensure consistency.

During the surgical procedure, the anesthesiologist was responsible for documenting the patient’s vital signs and the administration of anesthetic agents (propofol, remimazolam, sufentanil, remifentanil, norepinephrine, and atropine). Furthermore, the researchers extracted and record the following variables directly from the medical records: ASA grade; age; sex; height; weight; ejection fraction (EF); history of hypertension; type of surgery; anesthesia duration; peak inspiratory pressure (PIP); partial pressures of carbon dioxide in arterial blood (PaCO2); intraoperative MAP; the volumes of crystalloids and colloids administered; Estimated blood loss; pre- and postoperative aspartate aminotransferase (AST); pre- and postoperative alanine aminotransferase (ALT); pre- and postoperative plasma creatinine (Scr); pre- and postoperative hemoglobin; pre- and postoperative albumin; pre- and postoperative partial pressures of PaCO2; pre- and postoperative C-reactive protein (CRP); pre- and postoperative IL-6; preoperative blood glucose.

Blood sample processing

Blood samples (5 ml) were collected from enrolled patients before and within 24 h after the operation to measure changes in plasma NfL concentration. The blood samples were stored in vacutainer tubes (Ande Medical Supplies Co., Ltd.) containing the anticoagulant ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA). Within one hour, these tubes were centrifuged at 4 °C for 15 min at 2500 rpm. Subsequently, 500 µL aliquots were pipetted into Eppendorf tubes (Bickman Biotechnology Co., Ltd.). The samples were stored at -80 °C in the laboratory of the affiliated hospital of Jiaxing University before being transported by a medical courier on dry ice for analysis.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was determined using PASS version 15.0. The primary outcome was the NfL concentration on postoperative day 1. According to our preliminary experiments, the plasma NfL concentrations in Group R and Group P on postoperative day 1 were 139.3 ± 43.4 and 165.3 ± 49.1 pg/ml, respectively. With the power set at 80% and a two-sided significance level of 0.05, 102 patients were required to detect a difference. Considering a loss-to-follow-up rate of approximately 15%, a total of 124 patients were included in the study. The revised sample size calculation was performed before completion of patient enrollment and before final data analysis.

Statistical analysis

Data statistical analyses and graphical representations were performed using SPSS version 25.0 and GraphPad Prism version 10.1.2. The analyses were conducted by a professional statistician who was blinded to the group allocations, adhering to the intention-to-treat principle. The Shapiro-Wilk test was employed to assess the normality of continuous data. Quantitative variables exhibiting a normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed data were represented as median and interquartile range (IQR). Comparisons between continuous variables were conducted using the Student’s t-test for normally distributed quantitative data and the Mann-Whitney U test for non-normally distributed quantitative data. Categorical data were expressed as frequencies and percentages, and comparisons were performed using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. The relative risks and corresponding 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) were calculated to further quantify the effect size. Multiple imputation techniques have been employed to address the issue of missing data. All statistical tests were two-sided, with a P < 0.05 considered statistically significant. All subgroup analyses (by surgical type, fried phenotype and frailty index), were conducted as exploratory post hoc analyses without adjustments for multiple comparisons; the results should be interpreted descriptively.

The incidence of POD on postoperative day 1 was 6(9.7%) in Group R and 7(11.5%) in Group P, and no significant differences were observed between the groups, with the unadjusted odds ratio 0.827, 95% CI 0.261–2.62, P = 0.746. As shown in Table 3.

Secondary outcome

The incidence of hypotension was significantly lower in Group R compared to Group P (3[4.8%] vs. 19[31.3%], P < 0.001). Furthermore, the time-weighted average of AUB-MAP in Group R was higher than Group P (-21.3 ± 10.7 vs. -25.4 ± 9.6, mmHg, P = 0.03, respectively). Consequently, the administration of norepinephrine was significantly reduced in Group R compared to Group P (0.2[0-0.5] vs. 0.4[0.1–0.6], mg, P = 0.035), whereas the atropine dosage did not differ significantly between the two groups (0.3[0-0.4] vs. 0.3[0-0.5], mg, P = 0.421). As shown in Table 2.

Other outcomes

There were no significant differences observed between Group R and Group P in terms of the postoperative NRS score (2[0, 4] vs. 2[2, 4], P = 0.413) and the sufentanil usage of PCIA (39[31–52] vs. 43[28–52], µg, P = 0.288). Moreover, there were no significant differences between the two groups concerning extubation time (28[15–50] vs. 40[21–87], min, P = 0.065) and PaCO2 (40.2 ± 4.1 vs. 39.1 ± 3.9, mmHg, P = 0.128). Furthermore, the postoperative hematology examination results showed that no significant differences were observed in the postoperative levels of hemoglobin (112.5 ± 15.2 vs. 113.0 ± 16.5, g/L, P = 0.861), albumin (35.8[32.3–38.4] vs. 34.7[32.1–37.4], g/L, P = 0.330), alanine transaminase (18[11–23] vs. 17[11-26.5], U/L, P = 0.931), aspartate transaminase (23[17–33] vs. 20[17–28], U/L, P = 0.893), plasma creatinine (64.3[53.8–74.4] vs. 64.7[58.5–83.3], µmol/L, P = 0.181), C-reactive protein (31.3[11.5–52.0] vs. 25.9[13.6–52.4], mg/L, P = 0.881) between the two groups. As shown in Table 2.

Adverse event and harms

In the current study, no serious adverse events that endangered life or led to death occurred, and no participants discontinued the intervention or withdrew from follow-up due to adverse events. POD is one of the primary outcome indicators. Here, it is also presented as an adverse event to comply with the CONSORT requirements. Although intraoperative hypotension is not a traditional postoperative adverse event, it is closely related to postoperative complications. During anesthesia induction, its incidence was significantly lower in Group R than in Group P. Neither group had severe arrhythmias or cardiac arrest. Other adverse events included PONV, occurring in 12(19.4%) in Group R and 11(18.0%) in Group P (P = 0.851), and postoperative sleep disturbances, reported in 10(16.1%) and 18(29.5%) respectively (P = 0.077). Group comparison of the Fried’s phenotype (FP) and Frailty index (FI)

Based on FP criteria, the population included in this study was divided into the pre-frailty group and the frailty group. In elderly patients at the pre-frailty stage, there was no statistically significant difference in postoperative plasma NfL levels (97.8[66.9–126.0] vs. 145.4[119.7-183.1], pg/ml, P = 0.083) between those who received Group R and those who received Group P during surgery. Among the pre-frailty patients, no POD was observed in either group. Discussion

This study’s main finding indicates that, in frail elderly patients, remimazolam-based general anesthesia did not lead to a decrease in plasma NfL concentrations or the occurrence of postoperative delirium (POD) on the first day after surgery when compared to propofol. However, a noteworthy additional finding is that remimazolam exhibited enhanced hemodynamic stability during the perioperative period, particularly characterized by a significantly lower incidence of hypotension following the induction of anesthesia.

Our findings indicate no statistically significant difference in early postoperative delirium incidence between the remimazolam and propofol groups, consistent with conclusions from numerous recent high-quality studies [17, 21]. This finding challenges the long-held belief that benzodiazepines increase the risk of POD in elderly patients. As a novel ultra-short-acting benzodiazepine, remimazolam’s unique pharmacokinetic properties-rapid metabolism by tissue esterases into inactive metabolites, enabling swift onset and emergence with minimal accumulation-may explain its distinct neurocognitive effects compared to traditional benzodiazepines [22]. This study introduces plasma NfL as a biomarker for assessing remimazolam’s neurological effects for the first time. NfL serves as a sensitive indicator of neuronal axonal injury, irrespective of the baseline cognitive condition, and an elevation in the concentration of NfL in the bloodstream is associated with an increased risk of POD, which represents an early stage of neurodegeneration [9, 23]. The majority of critically ill patients already had a high NfL level on admission [24]. We observed that postoperative plasma NfL levels significantly increased compared to preoperative levels regardless of the anesthetic agent used, indicating that surgery and anesthesia themselves impose stress or minor damage on the central nervous system. However, NfL concentrations showed no significant difference between groups either preoperatively or postoperatively. This objective biological indicator aligns closely with clinical observations of no difference in POD rates between groups. This suggests that in frail elderly patients, remimazolam carries no greater risk than the widely used propofol in inducing or exacerbating perioperative neuronal injury.

In the current study, we performed a subgroup analysis. Initially, in light of the potential influence of various surgical types on outcomes, a post hoc analysis was performed. The results showed that there were no differences in postoperative plasma NfL concentrations or the occurrence of POD between the two groups who underwent different surgeries. Notably, orthopedic patients received 0.25% ropivacaine for nerve block analgesia following surgery, which may affect POD incidence. However, a systematic review published in BMC Anesthesiology in 2024, which synthesized findings from 12 related prospective studies, concluded that the combination of peripheral nerve block and general anesthesia does not significantly affect the incidence of POD following surgery [25]. Furthermore, to investigate whether remimazolam exerts differential effects on the cognitive functions of elderly individuals with varying levels of frailty, we stratified the study population based on the frailty index and conducted a post-hoc analysis between the two groups. The findings indicated that in patients with different degrees of frailty, the postoperative plasma NfL concentrations and the incidence of postoperative delirium were similar across both groups.

A clinically significant finding in this study is the exceptional hemodynamic stability of remimazolam. Compared with the patients in propofol, patients in the remimazolam exhibited a significantly lower incidence of hypotension upon successful induction of anesthesia, along with markedly higher time-weighted average of AUB-MAP. This advantage aligns with findings from numerous prior studies [26, 27]. It may be attributed to the fact that, in contrast to the direct sympathetic inhibitory effects of propofol, remimazolam primarily targets GABA-A receptors within the central nervous system, thereby exerting a comparatively mild inhibitory influence on sympathetic nerve activity. Furthermore, remimazolam exhibits a relatively weak direct impact on vascular smooth muscle and, unlike propofol, does not induce significant vasodilation via the activation of nitric oxide pathways, nor does it provoke pronounced reflexive vasoconstriction [28]. This indicates that administering remimazolam to frail elderly patients undergoing major surgical procedures may enhance hemodynamic stability without adversely affecting postoperative cognitive function, thereby potentially improving overall prognosis. Meanwhile, in this study, remimazolam did not lead to an increased incidence of postoperative adverse complications, including postoperative pain, nausea, vomiting, and sleep disorders. Maintaining hemodynamic stability during the perioperative period is critical for frail elderly patients with diminished physiological reserves and multiple cardiovascular comorbidities. Remimazolam may represent a safer anesthetic option for frail elderly individuals without compromising neurocognitive outcomes.

This study exhibits several significant strengths. Firstly, it concentrates on frail elderly patients, a clinically challenging demographic closely linked to cognitive impairment, thereby enhancing the clinical relevance of our findings. Secondly, the innovative inclusion of the plasma biomarker NfL offers objective molecular-level validation for clinical observations. Lastly, the study encompasses various types of major non-cardiac surgeries, thereby increasing the generalizability of the results.

This study presents several limitations. First, we assessed plasma NfL and cognitive function at 24 h postoperatively to capture the early central nervous injury but did not evaluate long-term cognition. NfL is a highly specific and sensitive marker that typically rises within 24 to 48 h following central nervous system injury [29, 30]. Furthermore, since the occurrence of POD may fluctuate within the 3–5 days after surgery, a single assessment may underestimate the incidence of POD. Further exploration of more time points for assessment may be necessary in future studies. Second, we acknowledge that while POD incidence was a co-primary outcome of interest, the study sample size was calculated based on NfL concentration differences. Consequently, the study may be underpowered to detect small but clinically relevant differences in POD rates. Third, we combined intravenous remimazolam or propofol with low-dose sevoflurane (1-1.5%) rather than using total intravenous anesthesia. Low-dose sevoflurane was primarily adopted to avoid intraoperative awareness, but this approach might alleviate the potential differences caused by using remifentanil or propofol alone.

Conclusion

In frail elderly patients undergoing major non-cardiac surgery, remimazolam does not significantly differ from propofol regarding the plasma NfL levels or the incidence of POD on the first postoperative day. Considering its notable advantage in preserving hemodynamic stability, remimazolam emerges as a potentially safe and effective general anesthetic alternative for this high-risk patient cohort, especially for individuals with limited tolerance to hemodynamic fluctuations.

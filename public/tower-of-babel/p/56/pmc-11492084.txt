Effects of yeast culture on antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capacity in the hepatocytes of grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idellus)
Authors: Mingzhu Pan, Xiangyu Pi, Yujie Zhang, Kangle Qian, Jingjing Liang, Yanlin Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Yeast culture (YC) has potential effects on improving the health of fish, however, its mechanism of regulating antioxidation and anti-inflammation in hepatocytes remains unclear. In the present study, the oxidative and inflammatory models were constructed in vitro to investigate the effects of yeast culture on the antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capacity in the hepatocytes of grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idellus). Malondialdehyde (MDA) and lipopolysaccharide (LPS) were used to construct oxidative and inflammatory models, respectively. Then adding 0.025, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 g/L YC into the medium of the two models, respectively. The control group was not added MDA, LPS or YC. The results showed that MDA and LPS obviously destroyed the morphology of hepatocytes and significantly decreased the cell viability (P < 0 .05), as well as increased the ROS content in the hepatocytes of grass carp. In addition, compared with the control group, MDA incubation significantly increased the AST and APT activities and the MDA content in cells and significantly decreased the T-SOD, CAT and GPx activities (P < 0 .05). When the YC concentrate was 0.1 and 0.2 g/L in the medium, the morphology and cell viability of hepatocytes were significantly improved compared with these in MDA group (P < 0 .05). Meanwhile, compared with control group, adding 0.1 and 0.2 g/L in the medium could significantly decreased the ROS and MDA content in cell and increased T-SOD, CAT and GPx activities, which may be related to activating nrf2-mediated antioxidant pathway. Compared with LPS group, adding 0.025, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 g/L YC into the medium significantly improved the morphology and the cell viability of hepatocytes (P < 0 .05) and decreased the ROS production. Moreover, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 g/L YC in the medium could significantly decreased the ALT and APT activities induced by LPS. In addition, compared with control group, the gene expression related to pro-inflammatory cytokine (il-1β) was significantly up-regulated by LPS, however, addition of 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 g/L YC could significantly decrease the gene expression of il-1β induced by LPS (P < 0 .05), which was related to that YC could suppress the tlr4/myd88/nfκb signaling pathway in the hepatocytes. Besides, LPS significantly increased the gene expression of apoptosis-related genes including caspase-3, caspase-7 and caspase-9, however, addition of 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 g/L YC could significantly decreased mRNA levels of apoptosis-related genes induced by LPS (P < 0 .05). To sum up, YC could modulate the MDA-activated oxidative stress by regulating the nrf2 signaling pathway and LPS-activated inflammation by regulating the tlr4/myd88/nfκb signaling pathway in the hepatocytes of grass carp. The results of the present study provide the theoretical basis of yeast culture for solving liver injury in fish.

Introduction

With the rapid development of large-scale, intensive aquaculture, fish diseases have become increasingly complex and diverse, leading to the overuse of antibiotics [24]. However, frequent and overuse of antibiotics is a growing concern because of the possible presence of antibiotic residues, the development of drug-resistant bacteria, and the suppression of immune systems in aquatic animals. In addition, during the past few years, with the rising price of ingredients, aquatic feed quality decreased gradually, and the use of unconventional ingredients increased, which is unfavorable for the healthy aquaculture. Poor-quality and some unconventional ingredients are always accompanied by low freshness [1,30], which could lead to the lipid peroxidation and outbreak of pathogenic bacteria [17,18,72]. On the one hand, malondialdehyde (MDA) is a toxic lipid peroxidation product, which could increase oxidative damage, change protein morphology and physiological function, damage cell membrane structure and inhibit the function of DNA and cells [15,16,45]. On the other hand, lipopolysaccharide (LPS) is medically considered to be a gram-negative bacterial toxin, which has been confirmed to cause an inflammatory response in aquatic animals [7,50,52]. As one of the most important organs of aquatic animals, liver is the one of the main detoxification organs [13], so it is of great importance to maintain healthy of fish, thereby promoting the growth and development of aquatic animals [33].

External harmful substances such as MDA and LPS can damage the liver of fish in different aspects. The harmful liver will influence metabolism and immune function of fish [8,20,48,56]. For example, in earlier research, it was found that the rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) exposed to LPS would increase the exposure of fish to cyanobacterial toxins and waterborne toxins and toxicants, which destroyed the liver detoxication system [6]. In hybrid grouper (Epinephelus lanceolatus♂ × Epinephelus fuscoguttatus♀), it was reported that LPS induced hepatocytes or liver injury in fish both in vitro and in vivo [77]. In addition, the high MDA in blood caused the liver damage including morphological change and increase levels of inflammatory cytokines in common carp (Cyprinus carpio) and Jian carp (Cyprinus carpio var. Jian) [9,28]. In response to these phenomena above, more and more attention was paid to safe and green feed additives to improve the health of fish.

Yeast culture (YC) is a microecological product formed by fermentation of yeast on a special medium under specific technological conditions. It is mainly composed of vitamins, minerals, growth-promoting factors, and oligosaccharides [5,51]. At present, YC has been used in feed for ruminants, pigs and aquatic animals [39,40,44,73]. In aquatic animals, YC could partially replace fish meal or be used as an additive to improve the growth, nonspecific immunity and hepatopancreatic histology of Pacific white shrimp (Litopenaeus vannamei) [12,73] and rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss) [4]. Therefore, YC has great potential in aquatic applications. However, the molecular mechanism of how yeast cultures affect the health of aquatic animals needs further study.

Grass carp (Ctenopharyngodonidelus) is a widely-distributed fish and has high economic value in the world [68]. However, increasing intensive culture has exposed grass carp to different kinds of environmental stresses, leading to susceptibility to various pathogens and microorganisms [27,65]. These caused enormous economic losses to the carp industry. Therefore, it is indispensable to improve the immune function of grass carp. To take it together, in the present study, the oxidative stress model and inflammation model were established to investigate the effects of YC on antioxidant and anti-inflammatory capacity in the hepatocytes of grass carp in vitro. In addition, it will provide the basis for the application of YC in grass carp and promote the healthy culture of grass carp.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

Grass carp liver cells (L8824, BFN60807589) (China Type Culture Collection Center., Wuhan, China) had been cultured in the complete culture medium with Medium 199 basic (M199) (Gibco, USA) containing 10 % fetal bovine serum (FBS, Cytiva, USA) and 100 U/mL Penicillin-Streptomycin (Cytiva), and cultured at 28 °C and 5 % CO2 conventionally humidified incubator.

Preparation medium containing yeast culture

The yeast culture derived from Saccharomyces cerevisiae (corn and bran as fermentation substrates) was provided by GBW Biotechnology Group (Qingdao, Shandong, China). The yeast culture was dissolved in the complete culture medium mentioned above in 2.1 at room temperature for 10 min and then filtered by a 0.22 μm-filter membrane on the super-clean workbench to obtain the sterile complete culture medium containing yeast culture. The complete culture medium containing 0.025, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 g/L yeast culture was prepared, respectively.

Preparation of MDA solution

The raw material for preparing MDA is 1,1,3,3-tetraethoxypropane (1,1,3, 3-Tetraethoxypropane). The specific preparation method is as follows: take 3 mL of 1,1,3, 3-tetraethoxypropane into 100 mL of 0.01 mol/L HCl, stir for 6 h, place at 4 °C away from light for 2 weeks, filter and pack with the 0.22 μm filter membrane, and store at -20 °C. The concentration was determined using an MDA kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute) and diluted to the working concentration using the complete culture medium.

Cell treatments

Oxidative stress experiment

To test the cell viability, cells were seeded into 96-well plates, each plate contained 200 μL culture medium at a density of 1.0 × 106 cells/well at 28 °C. After seeding the cells at 96-well plates for 24 h, the different culture medium was changed. There are six treatments in the present study. The control group was treated with the complete culture medium, and the experimental groups (5 groups) were treated with 5 μmol/L MDA, supplemented with 0.025, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 g/L yeast culture (6 replicates per group). After incubation for 24 h or 48 h, the cell viability was tested by Cell-Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) reagents (Solarbio, China) according to the manufacturer's protocol. In brief, cells were treated with different treatments mentioned above for 24 h or 48 h. Then, the CCK-8 solution was added to each well and incubated for 4 h. Finally, the optical density at 450 nm of each well was measured and the cell viability was calculated as the ratio of the absorbance of the test sample to the absorbance of the control sample. The OD value of the control group at 24 h was considered 100 %. Each treatment had 6 replicates per group.

Inflammation experiment

The control group was treated with the complete culture medium, and the experimental groups (5 groups) were treated with 500 ng/mL LPS, supplemented with 0.025, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 g/L yeast culture in the complete culture medium. After culturing for 24 h and 48 h, the cell viability was tested by CCK-8 reagents (Solarbio, China) according to the manufacturer's protocol mentioned above at 2.4.1.

Observation of cell morphology

According to the results of cell viability, the treatments at 24 h and 48 h both have significant effects when incubated with different concentrations of YC in Oxidative stress and inflammation models. Therefore, 24 h was chosen to conduct the following experiments. For the observation of the cell morphology, the cells were seeded into 6-well plates (1 × 105 cells/well). The cell morphology of cells was observed at the microscope (Echo Laboratories, USA) and the pictures were saved.

ROS staining

The intracellular ROS were determined by 2’, 7’-dichlorofluorescein diacetate (DCFH-DA) (Sigma) assay. Briefly, cells were plated in 6-well culture plates (Corning) (1 × 105 cells/well) in the complete medium. After 24 h incubation, cells were treated as mentioned above. After being treated for 24 h, the cells were incubated with 10 μM DCFH-DA for 10 min at 28 °C. Then the cells were washed twice by PBS, and were observed and photographed under a fluorescence microscope (Echo Laboratories, USA) to see the ROS staining.

Biochemical indexes

Cells were treated as mentioned above in the 6-well plate at the density of 1 × 106 cells/well. After treatment for 24 h, cells were collected to detect the biochemical indexes. The cells in two wells were used as one replicate and each treatment had 3 replicates per group. The content of MDA and the activities of superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase (CAT) and glutathione peroxidase (GPx) were determined by the kits from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China.

Real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) analysis

Cells were treated as mentioned above in the 6-well plate at the density of 1 × 106 cells/well. Each treatment has 6 replicates. RNA was extracted using FastPure Cell/Tissue Total RNA Isolation Kit (RC101, Vazyme, China). Single-stranded cDNA was prepared from total RNA using a PrimeScripte RT reagent Kit (RR047A; Takara Biotech, China). Real-time quantitative PCR was performed in a quantitative thermal cycling apparatus (Quant Studio 5, Applied Biosystems, USA). The reaction system was 15 μL, including 7.5 μL 2×SYBR Green master mix (Q711-02, Novozan, China), 0.3 μL (10 μmol/L) for upper and downstream primers and 1 μL for sample cDNA. β-actin was used as the internal reference gene. Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 25.0 (SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA). The results were presented as means ± standard error (SE). Two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was utilized to assess the main impacts of incubation treatments, incubation time, and their interactive effects on different indicators. Additionally, one-way ANOVA was performed to examine the effects of incubation treatments at the same time, while t-test were used to evaluate the effects of time at the same incubation treatment. The rest statistical data were subjected to the one-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey's multiple range test to determine significant differences among all treatment groups. Significance between experimental groups was determined at a level of P < 0.05.

Effects of yeast culture on inflammation and apoptosis in the grass carp hepatocytes treated with LPS

Cell viability

In Table 3, both treatments and time have significant difference on the viability of cells and they all played the main effects (P < 0.05). In addition, there was significant interactive effect between incubation treatments and time (P < 0.05). Discussion

Previous studies have shown that grass carp under a variety of adverse factors would cause liver damage including eating unbalanced-nutritional diets, suffering a bacterial infection, living in the stressful environment and so on [59,61,66,70,71]. Oxidative stress and inflammation were the two main causes of liver injury [2,46,75]. In the present study, when the liver cells were incubated with MDA and LPS, the morphology of hepatocyte was severely disrupted. The similar results were also found in the hepatocytes of mouse, weaned pigs and hybrid grouper (Epinephelus lanceolatus♂ × Epinephelus fuscoguttatus♀) [37,38,76]. MDA is a toxic and harmful lipid peroxidation product, which is mainly produced through lipid oxidation rancidity and collective tissue stress caused by various factors [62]. MDA can change the morphology and physiological function of proteins, resulting in cellular oxidative stress, and inhibition of cell functions [63]. Excessive ROS can destroy the balance and stability of the cellular antioxidant system, resulting in cell damage [67]. In the present study, the addition of MDA destroyed the cell morphology, decreased the cell viability and increased the ROS production, which indicated that MDA caused the oxidative stress of liver cells. In addition, AST and APT are important indicators of liver injury, and the increase of their enzyme activities often means the intensification of liver injury in fish [50,53]. In the present study, the activities of AST and APT in the hepatocytes were increased after MDA incubation, which means that MDA causes damage to hepatocytes in grass carp. Therefore, MDA incubation led to oxidative stress and the damage of hepatocytes in the grass carp.

Scavenging free radicals can reduce the oxidative stress state of body [42]. SOD, CAT and GPx are important antioxidant enzymes in the animal body, scavenging oxygen free radicals and avoiding oxidative damage [26]. In the present study, after addition of yeast culture, the ROS production was significantly decreased compared with the MDA group. At the same time, compared with MDA group, adding 0.05, 0.1 and 0.2 g/L YC into the medium, the activities of SOD, CAT and GPx were significantly increased. In the study of Ussuri catfish (Pseudobagrus ussuriensis) in vivo, it was found that dietary yeast culture could decrease the content of MDA and increased the activities of CAT, SOD and T-AOC in the liver [3]. In Pacific white shrimp (Litopenaeus vannamei), it was also found that yeast extract supplementation improved the total antioxidant capacity of shrimp [74]. The results above indicated that yeast culture can alleviate the oxidative stress of cells induced by MDA by activating the antioxidant enzyme activities. Activities of antioxidant enzymes are closely related to their mRNA levels [34]. The results in the present study showed that adding yeast culture into the medium, the mRNA levels of sod, cat and gpx were increased compared with those in MDA group. This is consistent with the findings of Feng et al., suggesting that yeast culture exerts a positive effect on fish under oxidative stress [35]. Additionally, yeast culture has been demonstrated to inhibit ROS formation in PMN cells, thereby protecting cellular systems from oxidative damage [36]. Some studies revealed that antioxidant enzyme gene expression was related to Nrf2 signaling pathway in fish [22,29,47]. In the present study, yeast culture significantly increased the gene expression of nrf2, which means that yeast culture could regulate the expression of antioxidant genes through nrf2, thus regulating the activity of antioxidant enzymes.

Tight junction proteins on hepatocytes play an important role on the production and secretion of bile as well as metabolic exchange and detoxification in the liver [54]. The structural integrity of liver could be influenced by tight junction complexes [23]. In the present study, MDA significantly decreased the mRNA levels of junction proteins including occludin, claudin-b, claudin-c and claudin-f, which indicated that MDA destroyed the structural integrity of hepatocytes. Although adding yeast culture slightly increased the gene expression of tight junction proteins, but there was no significant difference. The results above indicated the yeast culture protected the cells from MDA toxicity by activating antioxidant signaling pathways rather than tight junction proteins.

In the present study, the effects of yeast culture on anti-inflammation in the hepatocytes of grass carp were also investigated. LPS was often used to establish the inflammation model [50,69]. In the present study, when cells were incubated with LPS, the cell viabilities at 24 h and 48 h were significantly decreased with ALT and APT activities increasing. The inflammatory response was also associated with the activation of pro-inflammatory cytokines and inhibition of anti-inflammatory cytokines [10,14]. In the present study, after incubated with LPS, the gene expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines was increased and the gene expression of anti-inflammatory cytokines was decreased. Therefore, the inflammation model was successfully established. It is well known that NF-κB is a transcription factor located in the cytoplasm and can be localized into the nucleus after activation and it is a transcriptional activator of inflammatory factors [55]. The activation of NF-κB often accompanied by the inflammatory response in fish [19,49,57]. Previous studies have demonstrated that yeast culture can significantly reduce the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines il-1β, il-8, and tnf-α in largemouth bass, while also exerting a positive regulatory effect on the anti-inflammatory cytokines il-10 and tgf-β [58]. Similarly, the results of this study indicate that incubation with 0.05, 0.1, and 0.2 g/L of YC led to a significant reduction in the mRNA levels of the pro-inflammatory cytokines il-1β, nf-κbp65, and nf-κbp52, along with an increase in the anti-inflammatory cytokine il-10. In Pseudobagrus ussuriensis, it was reported that yeast culture could suppress CCl4-induced inflammation through inhibiting the NF-κB signaling pathway [60]. In addition, TLR4 is an important pattern recognition receptor, has been proved to be closely related to inflammation and immunity of grass carp [25]. The physiological effects of LPS are manifested through toll-like receptors present on the surface of the cell membrane of the host cell [31]. Myd88 is the downstream of TLR4 and is the upstream of NF-κB, and is a critical adaptor protein in innate immunity signal transduction [41,64]. In the present study, the mRNA levels of nf-κbp65, nf-κbp52, myd88 and tlr4 were decreased after the medium adding yeast culture. The results above demonstrated that yeast culture could suppress LPS-induced inflammation through inhibiting the tlr4/myd88/nf-κb signaling pathway.

As a procedural way of death, apoptosis plays a key role in maintaining its self-renewal, but excessive apoptosis can lead to some diseases [32]. To our knowledge, the activation of caspase-3, -7, and -9 could promote apoptosis in mammals [11]. In the present study, when the cells were treated with LPS, the gene expression of caspase-3, -7, and -9 was significantly increased, which was consistent with the results in the fibroblast cell of hybrid crucian carp (Carassius cuvieri ♂ × Carassius auratus red var ♀) [43]. In the present study, the gene expression of caspase-3, -7, and -9 was decreased compared with that in the LPS group, which indicated that that yeast culture could suppress LPS-induced apoptosis through inhibiting the gene expression of caspases. In the previous study, it was reported that dietary yeast culture could down-regulated mRNA levels of caspase-1 in the digestive tract of juvenile Pacific white shrimp Litopenaeus Vannamei [21]. Therefore, yeast culture could suppress LPS-induced apoptosis through inhibiting the gene expression of caspase decades.

Conclusions

To sum up, the results of the present study showed that yeast culture could modulate the MDA-activated oxidative stress by regulating the nrf2 signaling pathway and LPS-activated inflammation by regulating the tlr4/myd88/nfκb signaling pathway in the hepatocytes of grass carp.

Complete chloroplast genome and phylogenetic analysis of Amorphophallus ochroleucus Hett. & V.D.Nguyen 2001 (Araceae)
Authors: Haoliang Shi, Wei Wang, Kai Chen, Ying Qi, Penghua Gao, Min Yang, Jianrong Zhao, Feiyan Huang, Lifang Li, Lei Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAmorphophallus ochroleucus Hett. & V.D.Nguyen 2001, a perennial herbaceous plant in the genus Amorphophallus (Araceae family), is characterized by a tuberous root system, and is renowned for its unique pale-colored inflorescence and distinctive leaves. It is utilized as an ornamental plant in gardens and indoors. However, the genetic and phylogenetic relationships of this species remain largely unknown, leaving unanswered questions about its evolutionary trajectory. For the first time, this study presents the complete chloroplast genome of A. ochroleucus. The results demonstrate that the chloroplast genome spans 172,779 bp with an overall GC content of 35.15%, comprising a large single-copy (LSC) region of 92,449 bp, a small single-copy (SSC) region of 15,596 bp, and two inverted repeat (IR) regions each 32,367 bp in length. A total of 130 functional genes were annotated, including 85 protein-coding genes, 37 tRNA genes, and 8 rRNA genes. Phylogenetic analysis revealed that all Amorphophallus species formed a highly supported monophyletic clade, with A. ochroleucus showing closer affinity to A. paeoniifolius and A. titanum. The chloroplast genomic data provide a valuable resource for future taxonomic and evolutionary studies in Amorphophallus.

Introduction

The genus Amorphophallus is a valuable group of perennial herbs comprising approximately 250 species primarily distributed in tropical and subtropical regions. The genus exhibits multifaceted value. Economically, the tubers of many species (such as A. konjac) are rich in konjac glucomannan (KGM), serving as important healthy food and industrial raw materials, while the whole plant exhibits medicinal potential. Horticulturally, the genus possess significant ornamental value due to their unique morphology- notably, A. titanium produces the world’s tallest inflorescence with a distinctive odor, whereas many species feature giant dissected leaves and mottled petioles, making them exceptional horticultural specimens. Amorphophallus ochroleucusis Hett. & V.D.Nguyen 2001 (Hetterscheid and Van van der Ham 2001), with its native range in Vietnam, is a tuberous terrestrial plant primarily found in humid tropical biotic communities (Hetterscheid and Van van der Ham 2001). As reported for various Amorphophallus species (Frisse et al. 2022; Gao et al. 2023; Yin et al. 2023; Yin and Gao 2023a, 2023b; Yin et al. 2024; Gao and Yin 2024a; Li et al. 2024a; Gao and Yin 2024b; Li et al. 2024b, 2024c), they all inhabit moist and shaded environments, which is crucial for avoiding direct strong sunlight and maintaining foliage and soil moisture. Furthermore, they are concentrated in tropical and subtropical regions to access higher environmental temperatures and stable heat (Figure 1).

Morphological characteristics of the whole plant (A) and leaves (B) of A. ochroleucus, a tuberous Vietnamese plant that, like many Amorphophallus species, grows in moist, shaded tropical and subtropical habitats, photographs taken by the author, Yu Lei. Scale bar indicates 1 cm.

In recent years, rapid advances in molecular biology have established the chloroplast genome as a fundamental tool in plant evolutionary biology and germplasm resource studies, owing to its conserved structure, maternal inheritance pattern, and high phylogenetic resolution. Although research on the chloroplast genomes of Amorphophallus species began relatively late, it has increasingly become a vital methodology for elucidating their genetic diversity, phylogenetic relationships, and adaptive evolution mechanisms. Studies indicate that the Amorphophallus chloroplast genome is approximately 160 kb in size and contains about 130 genes, with a gene order highly conserved compared to other genera in the Araceae family (Li et al. 2024a). However, in-depth studies on Amorphophallus chloroplast genomes are scarce. To date, fewer than 15 complete chloroplast genomes have been published for this genus, and genetic diversity analyses are restricted to six species (Yin et al. 2023), presenting certain limitations. Here, we sequenced and assembled the complete chloroplast genome of A. ochroleucus to provide a genomic basis for elucidating phylogenetic relationships and supporting conservation strategies for underutilized Amorphophallus species.

Materials and methods

The fresh leaves of A. ochroleucus were obtained from Konjac Genetic Resources Garden of Kunming University, Yunnan Province (Figure 1, 24.97406°N, 102.79605°E). Although A. ochroleucus is native to Vietnam, the individual used in this study was introduced from its native habitat and cultivated in the germplasm repository. The sample was deposited in the Herbarium of Yunnan Urban Agricultural Engineering and Technological Research Center, Kunming University (voucher number: YBMY20251020, Hao-Liang Shi, 1816148023@qq.com). Total genomic DNA was extracted from each fresh leaf using the Qiagen DNeasy Plant Mini kit (Qiagen Co., Hilden, Germany). DNA purity and concentration were determined using NanoDrop™ 1000 and Qubit™ 4.0 systems (Thermo Fisher Scientific), respectively. Then the SMRTbell library (∼15 kb) was constructed using the SMRTbell Express Template Prep Kit 2.0 (Pacific Biosciences, CA, USA), following manufacturer protocols: DNA shearing, AMPure PB bead purification, single-strand DNA (ssDNA) overhang trimming, DNA damage repair, end repair, hairpin adapter ligation, and final library purification. After quality control, the library was sequenced on the PacBio Revio platform (Pacific Biosciences, CA, USA). Raw data were processed with the CCS algorithm (version 6.0.0, parameters: –minPasses 3 –minPredictedAccuracy 0.99 –maxLength 21,000) to generate HiFi reads. Using minimap2 (v2.15-r905) (Li 2018), >3 Gb of HiFi reads with an average fragment length exceeding 13 kb were mapped to the reference chloroplast genome of a closely related species, A. titanium (NC_056329.1), to obtaining a PAF file. Reads with <50× coverage discarded. Using the same A. titanium genome as a reference, the filtered reads were assembled using Flye v2.6 (Kolmogorov et al. 2020) to obtain a graph file, which was visualized via Bandage v0.8.1 software (Wick et al. 2015) to remove redundant contigs and edit into a circular structure. The resulting circular sequence represented the complete chloroplast genome of A. ochroleucus. Annotated was performed using PGA software (Qu et al. 2019), followed by manual curation of the results. Finally, a circular genome map was generated with OGDRAW (Greiner et al. 2019).

To analyze the evolutionary relationships between A. ochroleucus and 13 other Araceae species, we downloaded their complete chloroplast genome sequences from NCBI (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/). For phylogenetic reconstruction, two datasets were prepared: (1) the coding sequences (CDS) of 62 single-copy genes extracted using the HiMT toolkit within TBtools-II, and (2) the complete chloroplast genome sequences. For the single-copy gene dataset, sequences were processed using the HiMT toolkit within TBtools-II, which integrates extraction, multiple sequence alignment, trimming, and Maximum likelihood tree construction (Capella-Gutiérrez et al. 2009; Katoh and Standley 2013; Nguyen et al. 2015; Chen et al. 2023; Tang et al. 2025). For the complete genome dataset, sequences were aligned using MAFFT (Katoh and Standley 2013) and trimmed with TrimAL (Capella-Gutiérrez et al. 2009), followed by Maximum likelihood tree construction using IQtree (Nguyen et al. 2015). Finally, the evolutionary tree was visualized using the TBtools Simple Tree View toolkit (Chen et al. 2023).

Results

Basic genomic features of A. ochroleucus

In PacBio SMRT sequencing, 218,646 HiFi reads were generated, totaling 3,159,897,530 bp with a median read quality of Q35. The complete chloroplast genome of A. ochroleucus is 172,779 bp in length and exhibits a typical quadripartite structure, consistent with most species in the genus Amorphophallus, consisting of a large single-copy (LSC) region of 92,449 bp, a small single-copy (SSC) region of 15,596 bp, and two inverted repeat (IR) regions (IRa and IRb), each 32,367 bp (Figure 2). The average sequencing depth was 438.51× (Supplementary Figure S1). The overall GC content was determined to be 35.15%. A total of 130 functional genes were annotated, comprising 85 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 8 rRNA genes, and 37 tRNA genes. While the majority of genes are present in single copies, 19 genes were identified as duplicated, including 4 rRNA genes (rrn4.5, rrn5, rrn16, rrn23), 8 tRNA genes (trnA-UGC, trnI-GAU, trnL-CAA, trnM-CAU, trnN-GUU, trnR-ACG, trnV-GAC, trnV-UAC), and 7 PCGs (rpl2, rpl23, rps7, rps12, ndhB, ycf1, ycf2), Among these, the genes rrn4.5, rrn5, rrn16, rrn23, trnA-UGC, trnI-GAU, trnL-CAA, trnN-GUU, trnR-ACG, trnV-GAC, trnV-UAC rpl2, rpl23, rps7, rps12, ndhB, ycf1, ycf2 have two copies each, while trnM-CAU has three copies (Supplementary Table S1). In addition, fifteen genes (trnK-UUU, rps16, trnG-UCC, atpF, rpoC1, trnL-UAA, trnV-UAC, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhB, trnI-GAU, trnA-UGC, and ndhA) contained one intron, clpP and ycf3 had two introns, and rps12 was trans-spliced (Supplementary Figures S2 and S3).

Relative synonymous codon usage analysis of A. ochroleucus chloroplast genome

Relative synonymous codon usage (RSCU) analysis of the A. ochroleucus chloroplast genome revealed a distinct codon usage bias, with notable RSCU value variations among synonymous codons of different amino acids. Tryptophan (Trp, TGG) and methionine (Met, ATG) are single-codon amino acids with an RSCU value of 1.0, showing no codon usage preference. All other amino acids exhibited varying degrees of codon usage bias, with a clear preference for A/T-ending codons: highly preferred codons (RSCU > 1) included AGA (Arg, 1.88), GCT (Ala, 1.81), TTA (Leu, 1.91) and GAT (Asp, 1.59), while their corresponding synonymous codons (e.g. CGC for Arg, 0.37; CTC/CTG for Leu, 0.36) had RSCU values far below 1, indicating low usage frequency. Among termination codons, TAA (RSCU = 1.35) was the predominantly used one, whereas TAG and TGA both had an RSCU value of 0.82 with low usage preference. Additionally, TCT (Ser, 1.61), GGA (Gly, 1.61) and AAT (Asn, 1.54) were strongly preferred codons, while TGC (Cys, 0.53), CAC (His, 0.52) and GTG (Val, 0.53) were rarely used (Figure 3). Collectively, these results confirm a prominent A/T base bias in the codon usage pattern of the A. ochroleucus chloroplast genome.

Phylogenetic analysis

To infer phylogenetic relationships, Colocasia esculenta (genus Colocasia) and Spirodela polyrhiza (genus Spirodela), both from the Araceae family, were selected as outgroups. Maximum likelihood phylogenetic trees were constructed using two datasets—the complete chloroplast genome sequences (Figure 4) and the 62 single-copy gene CDS sequences (Supplementary Figure S4)—both including A. ochroleucus, 11 other Amorphophallus species, and the outgroup taxa. The resulting topology from both datasets was congruent and showed strong bootstrap support at major nodes, confirming the monophyletic grouping of all Amorphophallus species. Within this clade, which corresponds to the Southeast Asian (SEA) clade. A. ochroleucus formed a well-supported sister group with A. paeoniifolius and A. titanum, indicating a close phylogenetic affinity among these three species.

Discussion and conclusion

The chloroplast genome of A. ochroleucus is 172,779 bp in length and displays a characteristic quadripartite structure, consisting of a large single-copy (LSC, 92,449 bp), a small single-copy (SSC, 15,596 bp), and two inverted repeat (IR) regions (each 32,367 bp). Comparative analysis of 12 Amorphophallus species revealed that genome sizes range from 164,417 bp (A. yunnanensis) to 177,076 bp (A. muelleri), placing the A. ochroleucus genome within the medium range (Supplementary Table S2). This organization aligns with the typical chloroplast architecture observed in other reported Amorphophallus species, underscoring the high degree of structural conservation across the genus. However, considerable length variations are observed in the LSC, SSC, and IR regions across species, reflecting dynamic boundary changes during evolution. Notably, IR region lengths range from 26,225 bp (A. albus) to 35,204 bp (A. muelleri), with A. ochroleucus possessing relatively long IRs (32,367 bp) and a correspondingly short SSC (15,596 bp), suggesting a pattern of IR expansion and SSC contraction (Chen et al. 2022).

In terms of gene content, the A. ochroleucus chloroplast genome encodes a total of 130 genes (111 unique), including 85 protein-coding genes (78 unique), 8 rRNA genes (4 unique), and 37 tRNA genes (29 unique), among which 19 genes are present in duplicate copies. This gene repertoire falls within the range observed across the genus (total genes: 126-131; unique genes: 108-113) and is consistent with most angiosperm chloroplast genomes, reflecting functional conservation in plastid genome evolution. The overall GC content of the A. ochroleucus chloroplast genome is 35.15%, which is the highest among all compared Amorphophallus species (range: 34.50%-36.0%, providing valuable insights into nucleotide composition variation within the genus (Chen et al. 2022; Chen et al. 2024).

The strong A/T-ending codon preference in A. ochroleucus chloroplast genome is consistent with other Amorphophallus species, reflecting the genus’ conserved codon usage trait (Li et al. 2024a). Trp and Met showed no bias, matching congeneric genomic characteristics and verifying our RSCU analysis. TAA as the preferred termination codon is also a common feature of Amorphophallus chloroplast genome (Li et al. 2024a). Minor species-specific RSCU variations in partial codons may result from adaptive evolution, which can serve as molecular markers for the genus’ phylogenetic studies.

Phylogenetic analysis based on the complete chloroplast genome sequence provided strong resolution for determining the systematic position of A. ochroleucus within the genus. The results indicate that A. ochroleucus forms a well-supported clade as sister to A. paeoniifolius and A. titanum, suggesting a close and relatively recent phylogenetic relationship among the three species. This placement differs from a previous phylogenomic study that reconstructed the Amorphophallus phylogeny using 71 plastid genes (67 CDSs + 4 rRNAs) and placed A. ochroleucus within the CA-I clade (Pouchon et al. 2023). In contrast, our study utilized two datasets—62 shared single-copy CDSs and complete chloroplast genomes—both of which consistently recovered A. ochroleucus within the SEA clade.

Nevertheless, despite including 11 species (Frisse et al. 2022; Gao et al. 2023; Yin et al. 2023; Yin and Gao 2023a; 2023b; Yin et al. 2024; Gao and Yin 2024a; Li et al. 2024a; Gao and Yin 2024b; Li et al. 2024b; 2024c), this study captures only a fraction of the diverse Amorphophallus genus, which contains roughly 200 species native to tropical and subtropical Asia and Africa. Future investigations should therefore prioritize a significant expansion in both sample size and geographic representation. Including more species from the currently underrepresented African lineages is crucial for a complete evolutionary perspective.

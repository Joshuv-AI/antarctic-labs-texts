Response of vegetation normalized different vegetation index to different meteorological disaster indexes in karst region of Guangxi, China
Authors: Ying Xie, Yanli Chen, Yue Zhang, Mingzhi Li, Min Xie, Weihua Mo
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Fragile karst areas of Guangxi are the key areas of vegetation protection and restoration work of the government. It is of great significance to study the effects of meteorological disasters on vegetation change for disaster prevention and reduction and ecological protection in the karst areas. The Normalized Differential Vegetation Index (NDVI), as a tool for monitoring vegetation growth, has a strong responsiveness to vegetation and can qualitatively and quantitatively evaluate the vegetation cover and its growth vitality. Therefore, in this study, we analyzed the trend of vegetation change and the impacts of multiple meteorological hazards, such as drought, torrential rainfall, high temperature, and low temperature, on the vegetation of the karst region in Guangxi by using the linear trend method, GIS spatial analysis, and correlation analysis, using the MODIS NDVI and temperature and precipitation information from 2000 to 2020 in the karst region of Guangxi. The results show that: (1) NDVI increased significantly in the karst areas, and 32.2% of the areas had significant improvement in vegetation. The improvement was the most obvious mainly in the central part of the study area, while the vegetation degradation trend was obvious in partial scattered areas in northeast and southwest. (2) On the interannual scale, NDVI was negatively correlated with some meteorological disaster indexes, such as relative humidity index, the number of drought days, the amount of extremely heavy rainstorm, the amount of heavy rainstorm, the number of days with high temperature of ≥35 °C, the number of days with high temperature of 35–37 °C, the number of days with high temperature of ≥37 °C, the minimum temperature, and the effective accumulated temperature of ≤0 °C. The obvious negative effect area of drought on vegetation was mainly concentrated in the middle of the study area, and that of rainfall was mainly distributed in the southwest, northeast and northwest; that of high temperature was mainly distributed in the northwest and northeast, and that of low temperature was mainly concentrated in the southwest and north. (3) On the multi-year monthly scale, the responses of NDVI to drought, high temperature and low temperature disaster indexes had a lag effect, but had no lag effect on rainfall disaster indexes. The lag time of vegetation to drought was 1 month, and the lag time to high temperature and low temperature was 3 months.

Introduction

The term "karst" is derived from the classical karst areas of Slovenia and the Istria and Ljubljana regions of Italy [1]. After hundreds of years of countless experts and scholars on the definition and characteristics of the continuous exploration, and now the karst landscape known as karst landforms, is in the groundwater and surface water on the soluble rock dissolution and precipitation, erosion and deposition, as well as gravity collapse, accumulation and other effects of formation. Karst landscapes are widely distributed around the world, with the main distribution areas being: the Dinaric Mountains of Bosnia and Herzegovina, the Central Plateau of France, the Ural Mountains of Russia, southern Australia, the east-central part of the United States, the Greater Anglian Islands, and the north-central region of Vietnam. China's karst landscape is also widely distributed, the national carbonate rock distribution area of about 1.3 million square kilometers, of which Guangxi, Guizhou and eastern Yunnan accounted for the largest area, is one of the world's most typical development of karst landscape distribution of one of the most widely distributed areas. Karst landforms can be categorized into two basic types: surface and subsurface, and the common surface karst landforms are: Soluble gorges, stone buds, karst funnels, drop holes, dissolving depressions, karst basins and karst plains, peak clusters, peak forests and isolated peaks; underground karst landforms include: caves with lower rivers and dark lakes. The environment of karst region is the most fragile natural environment in the world, which is easily affected by human activities, with a high degree of degradation and destruction of local vegetation [2], and a weak resistance and self-recovery ability, making it a typical ecologically fragile area [3]. With climate change found an intensification of extreme precipitation events in the Mucille (Classical Karst Region, NE Italy) region, with severe impacts on human life and habitat [4]. The karst area of the inland Low plateau in northeast Alabama, United States, is the development site of Fern Cave System, but due to the impact of fragile ecological environment, it is also a hotspot area that limits biodiversity [5]. Vulnerability assessment studies using natural and artificial tracers have also been carried out in the Swiss Jura karst region [6].

Vegetation is the core of terrestrial ecosystem [7]. It is also one of the important barriers to protect the ecological environment. It plays an important role in connecting the atmosphere, soil, water and organisms in the ecological environment, and is an "indicator" of global climate change [8,9]. Climate change has a profound impact on the distribution, growth and phenological patterns of regional and even global vegetation, therefore, Therefore, to analyze the response of vegetation to climate change and to monitor changes in vegetation, scientists often use vegetation remote sensing indices to effectively measure the condition of surface vegetation, thus promoting research on ecological changes and ecological restoration. Among the various remote sensing indices, the Normalized Differential Vegetation Index (NDVI) has been widely used to monitor vegetation changes because of its strong response to vegetation, small radiometric error, and clear physical meaning [[10], [11], [12], [13]]. In recent decades, with rapid population growth, resources have been over-exploited and utilized in order to satisfy the increasing material needs of human beings, and the phenomenon of rocky desertification has become more and more prominent [14,15], especially in the ecologically fragile areas of karst, and the frequent occurrence of meteorological disasters has also made the relationship between temperature and precipitation and vegetation cover more complicated, which has attracted the extensive attention of scholars both at home and abroad [[16], [17], [18]]. Scholars have conducted numerous studies on the responses of vegetation to the mean state of temperature and precipitation, and have reached a relatively consistent conclusion that significant effects of temperature on vegetation cover generally occur in temperate and boreal regions, while dry and wet seasons differ significantly or arid and semi-arid regions usually show significant effects of precipitation on vegetation cover [19,20]. Due to the increase of precipitation and temperature, vegetation coverage in most areas of China has shown an increasing trend [21]. However, in recent decades, due to the rapid population growth, resources have been overexploited to meet the increasing material needs of humans, and the phenomenon of rock desertification has become increasingly prominent [14], especially in karst areas, and the frequent occurrence of meteorological disasters has also complicated the relationship between temperature and precipitation and vegetation, which has attracted extensive attention from scholars at home and abroad. In the past 50 years, the climate in southwest of China tended to be “warm and dry” on the whole [22], and the change of surface water and thermal environment has brought a new challenge to control rocky desertification in karst areas, so the exploration of the effects of changes in climate factors on vegetation in karst areas has gradually become a hot issue. Existing studies have shown that drought stress has become an important factor affecting plant survival in a region [23,24], and it is also the main reference for species selection in rocky desertification areas [25]. Some scholars have found that karst The surface vegetation is significantly correlated with both temperature and precipitation [26], and the stress effect of temperature is stronger than that of precipitation [27]. Moreover, the response of vegetation to climate factors has a certain lag [[28], [29], [30]]. Vegetation with stronger time-lag in hydrothermal conditions has a longer lag time [31]. The correlation between different climate factors and vegetation is different [32], and the magnitude of correlation is related to different vegetation types and growth seasons [33]. In addition, the influence of some climatic factors on vegetation also has a threshold effect, and the dominant factors will change when the threshold point is exceeded [34]. For example, in karst areas of Guizhou Province, climatic drought is the main factor restricting vegetation growth as annual rainfall is below 900 mm. When the annual rainfall is more than 900 mm, the rock desertification factor (geological drought) begins to affect vegetation growth [35], and the recovery ability of vegetation after multiple moderate droughts is obviously stronger than that after severe droughts [36]. Generally, the effect of climate change on vegetation in rocky desertification areas in the short term is not obvious [37], but extreme climate events have a significant effect on vegetation. For instance, the extremely large freezing event in southern China in 2008 resulted in a total NPP loss of 0.77 × 106 t C in Guizhou; among all types of vegetation, the loss of shrubs was the largest, while that of mixed forest was the smallest; the recovery of grass was the best, and that of shrubs was the worst [38]. In the context of climate change, the frequency and intensity of extreme climate events have increased significantly [39], but the impact of meteorological disasters on vegetation in karst areas has only been reported in few studies.

Therefore, based on the normalized vegetation index (NDVI) and meteorological observation data, this study explores the change pattern and response characteristics of the vegetation index and meteorological hazard factors in the karst region of Guangxi under the background of climate change by calculating four types of meteorological hazards: drought, rainfall, high temperature and low temperature, and further provides scientific reference basis for rock desertification management, vegetation ecological protection and restoration, and meteorological disaster prevention and mitigation.

Study area

Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region is located in the south of China, the distribution area of carbonate rocks in the region accounts for 37.8% of the total land area [40], and it is a typical area for the development of karst landforms, in which the karst region is mainly distributed in 105°02'∼111°43′E, 21°57'∼26°06′N, in the middle and lower reaches of the Li River in northeastern Guangxi, in the Li River Basin and in the lower reaches of the Long River in the lower reaches of the Luoqing River Basin, Hongshui River basin in the center, and along the Right River basin in the west [41]. Climate type belongs to subtropical monsoon climate, rain and heat at the same time, uneven spatial and temporal distribution of precipitation, annual rainfall 1100–1500 mm, the average annual temperature 17–23 °C, the local vegetation is dominated by shrubs, scrub and other woodlands [42] (Fig. 1a). The overall topography is characterized by large surface undulations (the relative height difference is generally 50–600 m), severe weathering and fragmentation of the surface lithology, variable lithology, complex near-surface structure [43], missing soil C layer and extremely thin soil layer, poor risk-resistant ability, and a sensitive ecosystem with high vulnerability to the impacts of climate change and meteorological disasters [40] (Fig. 1b), which has seriously constrained the economic and social development of the local area. development, belonging to the poor and backward areas in the country. Materials and methods

Materials

In the article, a total of 69 national meteorological observation stations covering the karst region of Guangxi were selected to count the day-by-day temperature and precipitation data for 60 years from 1961 to 2020, respectively, and the data were obtained from the China Meteorological Data Network (https://data.cma.cn/data/). The weather hazard index is selected to be more advantageous in quantitatively characterizing weather hazards. Among them, the relative humidity index is more advantageous for drought monitoring and assessment at the decadal scale during the crop growing season [44,45], The integrated dynamics of meteorological drought have a stronger ability to characterize drought, while the number of drought days better characterizes the frequency of drought occurrence [[46], [47]]. The number of rainfall days with different intensities has a stronger ability to characterize regional and seasonal characteristics [48], and the cumulative precipitation above heavy rainfall can more intuitively characterize the magnitude of heavy rainfall [49]. The selection of indicators for high temperature meteorological disasters is in accordance with the document "Measures for the Issuance and Dissemination of Meteorological Disaster Warning Signals" issued by the China Meteorological Administration. Low temperature weather disaster indicators were selected with more dominant quantitative nature [50]. Indexes of meteorological disasters.

NDVI data came from the vegetation index product MOD13Q1 inverted with the moderate resolution imaging spectro-radiometer (MODIS) by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) (https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/), with the spatial resolution of 250 m and temporal resolution of 16 d; data version was V006, and data period was 2000–2020. After the remote sensing data set of MOD13Q1 in the study area was preprocessed by image Mosaic, data format conversion, projection conversion and quality inspection, the NDVI data set with reliable quality was obtained. Monthly NDVI data set was synthesized, and annual mean was calculated. The internationally used Maximum Value Composites MVC (Maximum Value Composites) method was utilized to obtain the NDVI data used in this paper [51].(1)Dmaxt=max(Dtf)In equation (1), where Dmaxi is the maximized synthetic value of NDVI for the t 16 d cycle and Dtj is the NDVI value for day f inside the t 16 d cycle.

The geographic information data comes from China Geospatial Data Cloud (http://www.gscloud.cn/), mainly incuding Digital Elevation Model (DEM) data of Guangxi (raster data of longitude, dimension and altitude, slope and slope direction), administrative area boundaries of Guangxi district, municipalities and counties, vector boundaries of karst area in Guangxi, and latitude/longitude information of meteorological stations in Guangxi.

Research methods

Analysis of linear trend

The variation trend of normalized vegetation index (NDVI) in karst areas of Guangxi over a long time series from 2000 to 2020 was analyzed by linear trend method [52], the formulas are as follows:(3)k=n×∑i=1ni×Ci−(∑i=1ni)(∑i=1nCi)n×∑i=1ni2−(∑i=1ni)2In equation (1), k is the changing trend rate of NDVI; n is 60 a; Ci is the mean of annual NDVI in year i. When k is positive, annual NDVI shows an increasing trend in a certain period. Conversely, as k is negative, it shows a downward trend.

Correlation analysis

Pearson correlation analysis [53] was used to analyze the correlation between NDVI and various meteorological hazards for studying the response of NDVI to various meteorological hazards, the formulas are as follows:(4)R=∑i=1n(xi−x‾)(yi−y‾)∑i=1n(xi−x‾)2∑i=1n(yi−y‾)2In equation (2), R is the correlation coefficient of variables x and y; xi is the value of NDVI in year or month i; x‾ is the average of NDVI in various months in a year; yi is a meteorological disaster index in year or month i; y‾ is the average of a meteorological disaster index in various months in a year. The range of correlation coefficient R is [-1, 1]. The larger the R is, the stronger the degree of correlation between variables is. Significance test was conducted by t statistic. The lag analysis means studying the correlation between NDVI and various meteorological disasters on a multi-year monthly scale, and the correlations between vegetation and various meteorological disaster indexes were analyzed by calculating the correlation coefficient between NDVI and meteorological disaster indexes in the previous 0–3 months.

GIS inverse distance weighted spatial interpolation

Using the inverse distance weighted spatial interpolation method of GIS, the correlation coefficients of NDVI, NDVI and meteorological factors were spatially-interpolated. Based on five geographical parameter factors including altitude, slope, slope direction, longitude and latitude, the spatial change trend model of the correlation between NDVI, NDVI and meteorological factors was constructed, and the residual correction was carried out. The response characteristics of vegetation ecology and meteorological factors in Guangxi were analyzed.

Results

Temporal and spatial variation characteristics of NDVI

From 2000 to 2020, the NDVI in karst areas of Guangxi showed an increasing trend (Fig. 2), namely increased from 0.67 in 2000 to 0.76 in 2020, with the annual growth rate of 0.04/10 a. The areas with obvious vegetation improvement accounted for 32.2% of the total area of rocky desertification areas, and the areas with good improvement were mainly distributed in the middle (most of Laibin City, and the south of Liuzhou City) and northeast (the northeast of Guilin City) of the study area. The areas with obvious vegetation degradation accounted for 10.6% of the total area of rocky desertification areas, and the areas with no obvious improvement or with deterioration were mainly concentrated in the northeast (under the administration of Guilin City) and southwest (the middle and west of Nanning City and Chongzuo City) of the study area (Fig. 3). The fluctuation of NDVI in the study area was strong. For example, there were two obvious low-value areas during 2003–2004 and 2009–2011, and NDVI also decreased in 2014. Mainly due to the effects of severe drought in autumn, winter and spring during 2003–2004 and 2009–2010, there was high temperature, little rain, strong light and large surface evapotranspiration, so that there was lower temperature, less precipitation and more sunshine in 2011, and the temperature in autumn of 2014 was 1.1 °C higher than that in an average year, which had an adverse impact on the continuous restoration of vegetation in rocky desertification areas. In 2020, the overall meteorological conditions in rocky desertification areas of Guangxi were good, which was conducive to the growth of vegetation. However, the summer drought in July and August and the local drought in November and December impeded the growth of vegetation and resulted in the deterioration of vegetation growth. Responses of NDVI to meteorological disasters

Correlations between annual NDVI and drought indexes

The absolute values of Correlation squared coefficient R2 between NDVI and drought indexes (relative humidity index, drought composite index and the number of drought days) in the study area were 0.02, 0.02 and 0.04, respectively, and the negative correlation areas accounted for 69.2%, 49.9% and 56.2%, respectively. Among them, there was a significant negative correlation in 2.12%, 0.88% and 3.78% of the areas (p < 0.05). That is, except for drought composite index, NDVI was negatively correlated with relative humidity index and the number of drought days in most areas. The correlations between NDVI and the three drought indexes showed obvious spatial differences (Fig. 4). The negative correlation between NDVI and relative humidity index was more obvious in the northeast of the study area (the northeast of Guilin City), while the positive correlation was more obvious in the central and southern part of the study area (Nanning City and its counties and cities at the junction of Chongzuo, Baise, Hechi and Laibin) (Fig. 4a). The negative correlation between NDVI and drought composite index was the most obvious in the north of the study area (the northwest of Hechi City), while the positive correlation was the most obvious in the center and south of the study area (Fig. 4b). The spatial distribution of the correlation between NDVI and the number of drought days was opposite to that of drought composite index, and the areas with positive correlation were most obvious in the northern part of the study area (northwestern part of Hechi City). (Fig. 4c). Correlations between annual NDVI and rainfall indexes

The absolute values of average Correlation squared coefficient R2 between NDVI and rainfall indexes (the accumulated amount of rain above rainstorm, the amount of extremely heavy rainstorm, the amount of heavy rainstorm, the amount of rainstorm, the amount of heavy rain, and the amount of moderate rain) were 0.03, 0.05, 0.02, 0.03, 0.03 and 0.03, respectively, and there was a weak correlation. The negative correlation areas accounted for 58.2%, 60.6%, 61.2%, 36.6%, 39.2% and 48.8%, respectively, among which there was a significant negative correlation in 2.48%, 26.43%, 1.25%, 0.44%, 0.83% and 0.73% of the areas (p < 0.05). NDVI was mainly negatively correlated with the amount of extremely heavy rainstorm and heavy rainstorm, and the positive and negative correlation between NDVI and other rainfall indexes were basically the same. The correlations between NDVI and different rainfall indexes showed obvious spatial differences (Fig. 5). The negative correlation between NDVI and the accumulated amount of rain above rainstorm was clear in the southwest (the south of Baise City and the northwest of Chongzuo City), middle (the west and east of Hechi City) and northeast (the middle and northeast of Guilin City) of the study area, while the positive correlation was obvious in the central and southern part of the study area (Nanning City) (Fig. 5a). The negative correlation between NDVI and the amount of extremely heavy rainstorm was clear in the southwest of the study area (the south of Baise City and most of Chongzuo City), while the positive correlation was obvious in the middle (the middle of Hechi City) and the east of the study area (the south of Guilin City and Hezhou City) (Fig. 5b). The negative correlation between NDVI and the amount of heavy rainstorm was obvious in the middle of the study area (the northwest of Hechi City and Laibin City), while the positive correlation was clear in the middle and south of the study area (Nanning City) (Fig. 5c). There was an obvious negative correlation between NDVI and the amount of rainstorm in the southwest (the north of Chongzuo City), local north (the northwest of Hechi City) and northeast (the center of Liuzhou City and northwest of Guilin City) of the study area, while the positive correlation was clear in the middle of the study area (the west of Laibin City) (Fig. 5d). The spatial distribution of the correlation between NDVI and the amount of heavy rain was similar to that of rainstorm, but there was a big difference in the northeast of the study area (the northeast of Guilin), and the correlation between NDVI and the amount of rainstorm was higher than that of heavy rain (Fig. 5e). The negative correlation between NDVI and the amount of moderate rain was scattered, and was mainly distributed in the north of the study area (the west and northeast of Hechi City, the north of Liuzhou City and Guilin City), while the positive correlation was obvious in the southwest of the study area (the south of Chongzuo City) (Fig. 5f). In conclusion, NDVI had the highest correlation with the amount of extremely heavy rainstorm, that is, the amount of extremely heavy rainstorm had the most obvious impact on vegetation in the study area. The spatial distribution of the correlation between NDVI and the accumulated amount of rain above rainstorm was similar to that of heavy rainstorm, while the distribution of the amount of rainstorm and heavy rain was similar. Correlations between annual NDVI and high temperature indexes

In the study area, the absolute values of Correlation squared coefficient R2 between NDVI and high temperature indexes (the number of days with high temperature of ≥35 °C, the number of days with high temperature of ≥37 °C, the number of days with high temperature of 35–37 °C, the maximum temperature, the effective accumulated temperature of ≥35 °C, and the effective accumulated temperature of ≥37 °C) were 0.03, 0.03, 0.01, 0.01, 0.04 and 0.02, respectively. The negative correlation areas accounted for 54.6%, 60.0%, 41.4%, 50.6%, 48.7% and 43.5%, respectively, among which 1.55%, 1.09%, 1.55%, 0.46%, 1.58% and 0.65% of the areas showed a significantly negative correlation (p < 0.05). NDVI was mainly negatively correlated with the number of days with high temperature of ≥35 °C, number of days with high temperature of 35–37 °C, and number of days with high temperature of ≥37 °C, and the positive and negative correlation between NDVI and two rainfall indexes (the maximum temperature and effective accumulated temperature of ≥37 °C) were basically the same, while it was mainly positively correlated with the effective accumulated temperature of ≥35 °C. The correlations between NDVI and different high temperature indexes showed obvious spatial differences (Fig. 6). Among them, the negative correlation between NDVI and the number of days with high temperature of ≥35 °C was obvious in the east of the study area (the central and southern part of Guilin and Hezhou City), while the positive correlation was clear in the southwest of the study area (Baise City and Chongzuo City) (Fig. 6a). The spacial distribution of positive and negative correlations between NDVI and the number of days with high temperature of 35–37 °C was similar to that of the number of days with high temperature of ≥35 °C (Fig. 6b). The negative correlation between NDVI and the number of days with high temperature of ≥37 °C was obvious in the east of the study area (the north of Hezhou City), and the positive correlation was clear in the center of the study area (the central-eastern part of Hechi City) (Fig. 6c). The negative correlation between NDVI and the maximum temperature was obvious in the middle of the study area (the middle of Hechi City) (Fig. 6d). The spatial distribution of obvious positive and negative correlations between NDVI and the effective accumulated temperature of ≥35 °C (or the effective accumulated temperature of ≥37 °C) was similar to that of the number of days with high temperature of ≥35 °C (or the number of days with high temperature of ≥37 °C) (Fig. 6e and f). To sum up, NDVI was highly correlated with the number of days with high temperature of ≥37 °C and the effective accumulated temperature of ≥35 °C. In terms of spatial distribution, except for the maximum temperature, the negative correlations between NDVI and the other five kinds of high temperature indexes were consistent in regional distribution, and were mainly distributed in the north (the northwest corner of Hechi City), east (Hezhou City) and northeast of the study area (the center and south of Guilin City), while the obvious positive correlations were mainly distributed in the west and center of the study area, including Baise City, Chongzuo City and the west of Hechi City. Lag responses of NDVI to meteorological disasters

In the study area, the three drought indexes were not significantly negatively correlated with NDVI in the same period, indicating that drought inhibited vegetation growth in the study area on the whole. There was a significant correlation between NDVI and the drought indexes in the previous month, and the absolute values of correlation coefficients were greater than those in the same period. However, NDVI was positively correlated with drought composite index and relative humidity index in the previous month, but it was negatively correlated with the number of drought days, showing that NDVI had a significant lag effect on drought, with a lag time of one month. The long-term drought process mainly had negative effects on vegetation growth, while relative humidity index and drought composite index may had different effects on vegetation due to the difference of drought degree (Fig. 8).Fig. 8Significance of correlations between different disaster indexes and NDVI in various periods in karst areas of Guangxi. The six rainfall indexes in the study area were negatively correlated with NDVI in the same period, and the correlation with the amount of extremely heavy rainstorm was significant (P < 0.05). It indicated that the precipitation above moderate rain inhibited vegetation growth in the same period in the study area, and extremely heavy rainstorm had a significant negative effect on vegetation growth in the same period. NDVI was positively correlated with most rainfall indexes only in the previous period, but it was negatively correlated with extremely heavy rainstorm, revealing that the lag effect of rainfall on vegetation in the study area was not obvious. The sufficient rainfall in the early period mainly promoted vegetation growth in the later period, but the impact of extremely heavy rainstorm on vegetation in the study area was significantly negative from the first two months to the same period.

The six high temperature indexes in the study area were positively correlated with NDVI in the same period, and the correlations with the number of days with high temperature of ≥35 °C and maximum temperature was significant (P < 0.05). It indicated that high temperature had no obvious inhibiting effect on vegetation growth in the same period on the whole, mainly showing a promoting effect. The correlation coefficients between NDVI and the number of days with high temperature of ≥35 °C (the number of days with high temperature of 35–37 °C, or effective accumulated temperature of ≥35 °C) in the first 2 and 3 months, while the number of days with high temperature of ≥37 °C (or the effective accumulated temperature of ≥37 °C) in the first 2 months were greater than those in the same period, and the correlations were significant. It showed that high temperature had a significant lag effect on vegetation growth in the study area, and the longest lag time was up to 3 months.

NDVI was significantly correlated with the three low temperature indexes in the study area in the same period (P < 0.05). Among them, the correlation with the number of days with low temperature of ≤0 °C was significantly positive, and that of the minimum temperature and the effective accumulated temperature of ≤0 °C was significantly negative. The correlation coefficients with the number of days with low temperature of ≤0 °C and the effective accumulated temperature of ≤0 °C in the first 3 months were greater than those in the same period, and there was a significant correlation, indicating that low temperature had a lag effect on vegetation growth in the study area.

In conclusion, drought, high temperature and low temperature had a lag effect on vegetation growth in the study area, and the influence of rainfall mainly occurred in the same period. The lag time of drought on vegetation was shorter (1 month), while the lag time of high temperature and low temperature was longer (3 months).

Discussion

The phenomenon of stony desertification, also known as "rocky desertification", which occurs as a result of serious soil degradation due to soil erosion, is the most prominent ecological problem in karst areas [54,55]. In this study, it is found that the areas where the annual changing rate of NDVI during 2000–2020 was less than 0 accounted for only 10.6%, while the areas where the annual changing rate of NDVI was greater than 0 accounted for 89.4%. The trend of vegetation improvement in Guangxi Karst region is obvious, which is in line with the previous studies, since the beginning of this century, there is a significant increase in the global vegetation cover [56,57], and the analysis of vegetation cover in Indonesia using satellite imagery time series found that the NDVI has increased in almost all areas except urban areas [58], the percentage increase in the area of vegetation Enhanced Vegetation Index (EVI) EVI (Enhanced Vegetation Index) in the study area from 2000 to 2018 amounted to 91.45% [59], and the conclusion that the vegetation NDVI of Baise City, in the northwestern part of the study area, showed an increasing trend [60] are all in line with each other. And the reason why the vegetation NDVI in the study area shows a significant improvement is also due to the fact that in recent years, with the implementation of a series of ecological protection and control measures in karst areas, such as returning farmland to forest, construction of protection forest project along the Pearl River, forest ecological benefit compensation, etc. [61], the area of rocky desertification in Guangxi continues to decrease [62], and the ecological situation is gradually improved, which is an important reason for the stable change and improvement of most vegetation in the study area. The improvement of vegetation in the study area showed obvious spatial differentiation, and the improvement trend of vegetation was the most obvious in the central and southern part of the study area (the northwest of Laibin City and the southwest of Liuzhou City); the difference of vegetation types was an important reason for the spatial difference of NDVI [63,64]. The vegetation in the central and southern regions is mainly composed of fast-growing eucalyptus, with a rapid rate of forest formation, so it contributed a lot to the change of NDVI, while the change of vegetation in the regions dominated by shrubs was generally stable.

In the study area, there was obvious spatial heterogeneity in the responses of vegetation to different meteorological disaster indexes. Even for the meteorological disaster indexes of the same type, vegetation had different responses to them due to the difference of index component factors or magnitude. On the annual scale, the spatial distribution characteristic of vegetation responses to the number of drought days was opposite to that of relative humidity index and meteorological drought composite index. The number of drought days refers to the number of days with mild drought or above. In general, the longer the duration of drought is, the greater the negative impact on vegetation growth is. Relative humidity index and meteorological drought composite index are indexes only used to measure the occurrence of drought and determine the grade of drought [65]. Therefore, for the analysis of the impact of drought on vegetation, the results of the number of drought days were more objective in this study. If relative humidity index and meteorological drought composite index are used to analyze the impact of drought on vegetation, the range of light drought or above should be selected according to their respective grade indexes to reach a more objective conclusion.

In this study, it is found that the vegetation in the northeast, northwest and southeast of the study area was obviously negatively correlated with moderate rain and heavy rain. This is because the water and moisture retention capacity of sparsely vegetation areas is poor, especially in karst areas with mixed bedrock and shallow soil layers, where rainfall exacerbates soil erosion and strong rainfall is not conducive to the growth of vegetation, other studies have also found that vegetation in karst areas of Guangxi is more often than not negatively correlated with summer rainfall [51,66]. That is, in the ecologically sensitive and vulnerable karst areas, it is not the rainfall above the magnitude of rainstorm that will have obvious influence on vegetation, while moderate rain and heavy rain also should be paid attention to. In karst areas of Guangxi, rainfall is mainly concentrated in spring and summer (May to August). The spatial distribution of total annual precipitation, the average number of rainstorm days and the annual average number of heavy rainstorm days has great spatial similarity, and the northeast, center and west of the study area (within Hechi City) are the main rainstorm centers and rainfall concentration areas [67]. However, the spacial and temporal distribution of areas where vegetation was highly sensitive to the accumulated rainfall above rainstorm, the amount of extremely heavy rainstorm, the amount of heavy rainstorm, and the amount of rainstorm was significantly different from that of areas with frequent rainstorm (the middle and southwest of the study area), indicating that in addition to the large spacial and temporal heterogeneity of vegetation response to precipitation is not only related to the spatial and temporal differences in precipitation [68], but also to the rate of bedrock exposure in different regions of the karst region and the types of vegetation [62,69].

In this study, it was found that the spatial heterogeneity of the response of the vegetation in the karst areas to indicators of high and low temperature meteorological hazards was significantly smaller than that of the indicators of drought and rainfall meteorological hazards, and the area of significant positive and negative correlation to high and low temperature meteorological hazards was also smaller. Some studies have found that the cover characteristics of the ground surface in karst areas have a greater impact on soil temperature and soil moisture, and the atmospheric temperature is closely related to the soil temperature changes under the cover of different forest species [70], and there is a large difference between the temperature of the exposed rock surface and the surface temperature between forested and non-forested land, so the impacts of high- and low-temperature disasters on the vegetation cover are more indirect effects, and the selection of tree species with higher heat tolerance and less water transpiration can effectively reduce the soil temperature and thus alleviate the high temperature and low-temperature meteorological disasters, and increase the ecological restoration of the region [71].

It has been demonstrated that the responses of vegetation to temperature and precipitation in karst areas has a lag effect [66]. In this study, it is found that vegetation in the study area had an obvious lag effect on drought, high and low temperature meteorological disasters. It may be due to the lagging response of soil temperature and organic matter decomposition process to the change in atmospheric temperature [72]. The lag of rainfall disaster in the study area was not obvious, which is inconsistent with the influence law of average state of rainfall on vegetation in karst areas [66]. On the one hand, the study area has poor water and soil conservation ability; on the other hand, rainstorm led to severe soil erosion and vegetation damage. For example, from the previous 2 months to the same period, vegetation had an obvious negative response to extremely heavy rainstorm. In this study, the responses of vegetation to long time series of meteorological disasters were analyzed, but the impact of specific meteorological disaster processes on NDVI was not analyzed. Low temperature disaster would lead to the decrease of sugarcane NDVI [73]. However, there are no relevant reports on the impact of specific meteorological disaster processes on NDVI in karst areas, which can be further analyzed in future research.

Conclusions

In this paper, the spatial-temporal variation characteristics of NDVI in karst areas of Guangxi from 2000 to 2020 were analyzed, and the response characteristics of NDVI to various meteorological disasters (drought, precipitation, high temperature and low temperature) were discussed. Some conclusions are concluded as follows.(1)From 2000 to 2020, NDVI showed an increasing trend in karst areas of Guangxi, with the annual increase rate of 0.04/10 a. The areas with obvious vegetation improvement accounted for 32.2%, and were mainly distributed in the middle of the study area, while the areas with obvious degradation were mainly concentrated in the northeast and southwest of the study area.(2)On the interannual scale, some disaster factors were negatively correlated with NDVI, including relative humidity index, the number of drought days, the amount of extremely heavy rainstorm, the amount of heavy rainstorm, the number of days with high temperature of ≥35 °C, the number of days with high temperature of 35–37 °C, the number of days with high temperature of ≥37 °C, the minimum temperature, and the effective accumulated temperature of ≤0 °C. The responses of NDVI to various meteorological disaster indexes showed significant spatial differences. The negative effect area of drought on vegetation was mainly concentrated in the central part of the study area, and that of rainfall was mainly distributed in the southwest, northeast and northwest of the study area; that of high temperature was mainly distributed in the northwest and northeast of the study area, and that of low temperature was mainly concentrated in the southwest and north of the study area.(3)On the multi-year monthly scale, NDVI had a negative correlation with drought, rainfall and low temperature disaster indexes and a positive correlation with high temperature disaster indexes. The responses of NDVI to drought, high temperature and low temperature disaster indexes had a lag effect, but the response to rainfall disaster indexes had no lag effect. Vegetation had a significant response to drought disaster indexes with a lag period of 1 month and to high temperature and low temperature indexes with a lag period of 3 months.

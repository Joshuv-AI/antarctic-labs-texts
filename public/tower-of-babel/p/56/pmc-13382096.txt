Machine learning-guided discovery of a bioactive hydrogel for spatiotemporally orchestrated tendon-to-bone healing
Authors: Wencai Liu, Yuhao Yu, Hui Xu, Weiming Lin, Xin Wang, Xiping Jiang, Yifei Wang, Chenrui Yuan, Xinyue Yang, Mengpan Li, Di Wu, Lingzhi Kong, Zhi Wu, Weilin Yu, Wei Song, Yaohua He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The rational design of biomaterials for complex tissue regeneration, such as the tendon-to-bone interface (TBI), is hindered by an immense combinatorial space that makes empirical optimization impractical. To address this challenge, a machine learning (ML) framework was developed to navigate this landscape. Leveraging a high-fidelity Tabular Pre-trained Transformer (TabPFN) model, a high-throughput virtual screen of over 137 million candidates identifies an optimal injectable microgranule-assembled macroporous hydrogel (MMH). The hydrogel is engineered to spatiotemporally deliver macrophage (Mϕ)-targeting small extracellular vesicles (CRV-sEVs) alongside Kartogenin (KGN)-loaded PLGA microspheres. In the initial phase, the release of CRV-sEVs reprograms the immune microenvironment toward a pro-reparative M2 phenotype by suppressing NF-κB signaling in M1 Mϕ. Subsequently, the sustained KGN release promotes endogenous stem cell chondrogenesis and fibrocartilage reconstruction. In a rat rotator cuff repair model, the ML-optimized hydrogel restores the structural integrity, biomechanical strength, and functional performance of the TBI, supporting the practical utility of the ML-guided discovery framework. This work offers a promising therapeutic strategy for TBI healing and establishes a data-driven paradigm for accelerating the discovery of complex multifunctional biomaterials for broad regenerative applications.

Introduction

Rotator cuff tears (RCT) are a prevalent source of shoulder pain and disability, posing a significant clinical and socioeconomic issue [[1], [2], [3]]. The incidence of RCT increases substantially with age, affecting approximately 25% of individuals in their 60s and over 50% of those in their 80s [4,5]. Despite advancements in surgical techniques, treatment outcomes are often suboptimal. A notable proportion of repairs fail to heal, with re-tear rates reported to range from 13% for smaller lesions to as high as 94% for large and massive tears [6,7]. This high clinical failure rate stems from a fundamental biological limitation: the failure to regenerate the specialized, functionally graded tissue of the tendon-to-bone interface (TBI), or enthesis [8,9]. Natural healing processes typically form a disorganized and mechanically inferior fibrous scar, which cannot adequately dissipate stress between compliant tendon and stiff bone, leading to structural failure [[10], [11], [12]]. Pathological analysis of injured human TBI specimens, validated in a rat model, reveals a compromised niche characterized by structural disorganization and a sustained pro-inflammatory state, as observed in our preliminary studies [13]. This niche is dominated by M1 macrophages (Mϕ), which perpetuate inflammation, promote excessive matrix degradation, and inhibit functional tissue formation [[14], [15], [16], [17]]. This M1/M2 imbalance, therefore, constitutes a mechanistic bottleneck that is both central to failed regeneration and potentially targetable for therapeutic intervention. An effective regenerative strategy should be spatiotemporally orchestrated, requiring an initial immunomodulatory phase to resolve inflammation and create a pro-reparative niche, followed by a sustained pro-regenerative phase to guide the reconstruction of the complex graded fibrocartilaginous enthesis.

Advanced hydrogels have emerged as promising platforms for such interventions, owing to their high-water content, biocompatibility, and structural similarity to the native extracellular matrix (ECM) [14,[18], [19], [20]]. Their tunable properties and capacity for minimally invasive, injectable delivery make them ideal vehicles for creating a localized regenerative niche [[21], [22], [23], [24]]. However, traditional hydrogels (TH), while excellent for encapsulating therapeutic agents, often possess a dense, nanoporous network structure. This architecture, with pore sizes on the nanometer scale, can physically impede the infiltration and migration of endogenous cells, which are micron-sized [25,26]. This presents a limitation, as successful tissue integration and regeneration depend on the ability of host cells to colonize the scaffold, communicate, and orchestrate repair. A dense hydrogel can inadvertently act as a barrier, isolating the therapeutic payload from the very cells it is meant to influence [26]. To overcome this, we engineered a microgranule-assembled macroporous hydrogel (MMH). By assembling hydrogel microparticles into a cohesive “grape-cluster” structure, we created an interconnected network of large pores. This macroporous architecture is specifically designed to transform the hydrogel from a passive delivery depot into an active, pro-regenerative scaffold that facilitates deep cellular infiltration, nutrient transport, and tissue integration [26]. To this end, a dual-action therapeutic hydrogel was conceptualized. The first stage involves the rapid release of small extracellular vesicles derived from adipose-derived stem cells (ADSC-sEVs) [27], which are surface-engineered with a Mϕ-targeting peptide (CRV) [28], to modulate the M1-dominant inflammatory environment during the early phase of healing. This immunomodulatory phase is followed by the sustained release of Kartogenin (KGN), a small-molecule chondrogenic inducer, encapsulated within poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) microspheres [29]. This second stage is designed to provide pro-regenerative cues to guide endogenous stem cells toward forming the fibrocartilage layer once the local inflammatory environment has been ameliorated [30].

While this therapeutic strategy is biologically rational, its optimal physical realization poses a considerable methodological challenge. The formulation of this multi-component hydrogel involves a high-dimensional design space defined by distinct variables, creating a combinatorial library of over 137 million potential formulations. Navigating this landscape using conventional research paradigms, which rely on empirical, low-throughput experimentation, is impractical and unlikely to identify a true global optimum [31]. This design bottleneck represents a barrier to progress in the field of advanced biomaterials [32]. To overcome this, we hypothesized that a machine learning (ML) framework, trained on a targeted experimental dataset, could accurately model the complex, non-linear relationships between formulation parameters and in vivo healing outcomes. For this, we selected the Tabular Pre-trained Transformer (TabPFN) model, an algorithm noted for its predictive performance on the small datasets [33]. We further hypothesized that such a model could be deployed to rationally screen the entire combinatorial library in silico, enabling the efficient discovery of an optimal formulation.

In this study, we first established the pathological hallmarks of the injured TBI, confirming the pro-inflammatory M1-dominant microenvironment as a key therapeutic target. We then describe the development of our ML-guided discovery pipeline, which leveraged the predictive power of the TabPFN model to navigate a design space of over 137 million formulations and identify an optimal candidate. The top-predicted formulation was then synthesized as an injectable MMH co-loaded with Mϕ-targeting CRV-sEVs and pro-chondrogenic PLGAKGN microspheres. We demonstrate that this rationally engineered system promotes tendon-to-bone (TB) healing in a rat rotator cuff repair (RCR) model by orchestrating a spatiotemporally phased regenerative response. This response is initiated by CRV-sEV-mediated immunomodulation via inhibition of the NF-κB signaling pathway in Mϕ, and is followed by sustained KGN-induced chondrogenesis and fibrocartilage reconstruction. The concordance between the computational predictions and the experimental outcomes serves to validate both the therapeutic efficacy of the hydrogel and the data-driven discovery platform that enabled its design, demonstrating a complete and successful “ML-guided predictive pipeline” research paradigm for accelerating the development of advanced regenerative therapies. Results

To establish the pathological basis for rotator cuff repair, a multi-modal analysis of injured human tissue was performed and validated in a rat model. Analysis of injured human tendons revealed severe structural degradation. Compared to the dense, organized collagen of normal tendons, HE and Masson's trichrome staining of injured samples showed a disorganized, hypercellular matrix (Fig. 1B and C), resulting in a significantly lower tendon maturing score (Fig. 1F). Immunohistochemistry confirmed a fibrotic shift, with a marked decrease in mature Collagen Type I (COL I) and an increase in scar-associated Collagen Type III (COL III) (Fig. 1C and G). This structural degradation was coupled with a pro-inflammatory immune milieu. Immunofluorescence staining demonstrated a dramatic increase in M1 Mϕ, identified by the marker iNOS (Fig. 1D and H), and a scarcity of pro-reparative M2 Mϕ, marked by CD206 (Fig. 1E and I). This M1-dominant polarization was confirmed at higher resolution by single-cell RNA sequencing, which showed a significant expansion of pro-inflammatory Mϕ subclusters in injured tissue (Fig. 1J and K).

These pathological hallmarks were faithfully recapitulated in a rat rotator cuff repair model. The rat TBI showed a similar pro-inflammatory skew, with a significant increase in iNOS + M1 Mϕ co-localizing with the pan-macrophage marker CD68 (Fig. 1L–O). To further validate the pro-inflammatory phenotype of the injured rat TBI, RT-qPCR and ELISA were performed using TBI tissue homogenates harvested at 2 weeks post-injury. The injured TBI showed pronounced upregulation of M1/pro-inflammatory genes, including iNOS, TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, and reduced expression of M2/pro-resolving markers, including Arg-1, CD206, IL10, and IL-1rn (Fig. 1P and Q). ELISA further confirmed increased local secretion of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 (Fig. S1). These quantitative molecular and biochemical data corroborated the immunofluorescence findings and confirmed that the injured TBI represents an M1-dominant pro-inflammatory microenvironment. Histological examination confirmed severe structural disorganization (Fig. 1R and V) and a near-complete loss of the proteoglycan-rich fibrocartilage layer, as evidenced by reduced Safranin O and COL II staining (Fig. 1S and W). The collagen profile also mirrored the human pathology, with reduced COL I and increased COL III (Fig. 1T and X). Critically, this compromised interface was mechanically weak; biomechanical testing (Fig. 1U) showed a significantly lower ultimate failure load compared to uninjured controls (Fig. 1Y).

Machine learning-assisted design and optimization of a bioactive hydrogel formulation

To systematically navigate the multi-component hydrogel's high-dimensional design space, an ML pipeline was developed to model the complex relationships between formulation parameters and in vivo regenerative performance. The foundation for this pipeline was a training dataset derived from 40 distinct in vivo experiments. Preliminary analyses, including heatmap visualization, Spearman correlation, and recursive feature elimination (RFE), confirmed that the eight selected input features were suitable for robust model development, demonstrating low inter-correlation and high collective predictive value (Figs. S2 and S3). To visualize whether the 40 experimental formulations were locally clustered or broadly distributed, we performed UMAP projection of the training formulations together with a random subset of 10,000 virtual formulations from the complete design space. The 40 training formulations occupied multiple regions of the projected manifold rather than forming a single compact cluster, indicating that the seed dataset sampled diverse formulation domains (Fig. S2C).

The primary objective was to identify the most accurate regression algorithm for this biological application. A comprehensive comparison of 15 fully-tuned ML models revealed that the TabPFN model consistently and substantially outperformed all 14 other algorithms across the four target outcomes (Fig. 2B–E, Tables S2–5). TabPFN, a Transformer-based architecture designed for high-accuracy predictions on small tabular datasets, was therefore selected as the definitive model for all subsequent analyses (Fig. 2A). Its superior performance was evident across a suite of metrics, including the highest coefficient of determination (R2) and the lowest values for Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Squared Error (MSE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Root Mean Squared Logarithmic Error‌ (RMSLE) and Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) for predicting the Tendon maturing score (Fig. 2B), S-G ratio (Fig. 2C), M2/M1 ratio (Fig. 2D), and TB ultimate load (Fig. 2E).Fig. 2Performance evaluation, validation, and explainability analysis of machine learning models for predicting tendon-to-bone healing outcomes. To more transparently evaluate the generalization performance of the ML pipeline, we performed five-fold cross-validation for each outcome-specific TabPFN model. The 40 experimentally tested formulations were randomly divided into five folds, with 8 formulations held out per fold. In each iteration, the model was trained on 32 formulations and evaluated on the held-out fold, ensuring that each formulation was predicted once as unseen out-of-fold data. The resulting correlation plots confirmed excellent model accuracy and low prediction variance, showing a strong linear relationship between model-predicted values and the actual experimental ground truth. High coefficients of determination were confirmed for the Tendon maturing score (R2 = 0.9659, Fig. 2F), S-G ratio (R2 = 0.9396, Fig. 2G), M2/M1 ratio (R2 = 0.9628, Fig. 2H), and TB ultimate load (R2 = 0.9336, Fig. 2I). This high degree of correlation underscored the models’ exceptional predictive fidelity, establishing them as reliable tools for the subsequent in silico discovery phase.

To address the possibility of optimistic model selection and data leakage, we added a nested cross-validation analysis (Fig. S4). TabPFN remained the best-performing or tied best-performing model across the four biological endpoints. In the nested analysis, TabPFN achieved mean out-of-fold R2 values of 0.948 ± 0.031 for Tendon Maturing Score, 0.921 ± 0.044 for S-G ratio, 0.944 ± 0.036 for M2/M1 ratio, and 0.907 ± 0.052 for TB ultimate load (Fig. S4A and B). Learning-curve analysis showed that the average cross-validated R2 across the four outcomes increased progressively from 0.74 ± 0.09 using 40% of the dataset to 0.83 ± 0.07, 0.89 ± 0.05, and 0.94 ± 0.03 using 60%, 80%, and 100% of the dataset, respectively (Fig. S4E). Feature-noise perturbation analysis showed that the mean R2 decreased modestly from 0.94 ± 0.03 to 0.93 ± 0.04, 0.91 ± 0.05, and 0.87 ± 0.06 after adding 2%, 5%, and 10% Gaussian perturbations to numerical feature ranges (Fig. S4F). These results support the robustness of the ML pipeline while also indicating that predictive confidence decreases appropriately as input uncertainty increases.

To move beyond “black-box” predictions and obtain actionable insights, the decision-making processes of the optimized TabPFN models were elucidated using the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) framework. Global feature importance analysis, visualized with custom rose plots and beeswarm plots, consistently identified the inclusion and type of bioactive vesicles (sEVs), the presence of PLGA-KGN, and the SA_conc as the most influential parameters across all four outcome models (Fig. 2J–M, top panels). A more granular view from the beeswarm plots revealed how feature values impact predictions; for instance, the inclusion of CRV-sEVs (sEVs = 2) provides a strong positive impact (high positive SHAP values) on the predicted M2/M1 ratio, confirming their potent immunomodulatory effect (Fig. 2L, bottom panel). An alternative global visualization is provided in the SHAP summary plots (Figs. S5 and 6).

To deconstruct the model's reasoning for specific cases, local explainability was assessed using SHAP force plots. These plots illustrated how the model's predictions were driven from a baseline to a final value by the competing positive and negative contributions of each feature. For a high-performing formulation, features such as a high SA conc and the presence of PLGA-KGN provided the primary positive impetus, while for a low-performing sample, the absence of sEVs was a key negative driver (Fig. S5). Finally, the non-linear marginal effects of each formulation feature were explored using Partial Dependence Plots (PDP/ICE) analyses. Unlike single-feature importance scores, PDP/ICE plots allowed us to visualize how the predicted biological outcome changed when one feature was varied across its predefined formulation levels while the remaining features were held constant. For each of the four outcome-specific TabPFN models, PDP/ICE plots were generated for all eight formulation features rather than only the highest-ranked variables. These analyses revealed several design principles. Polymer and porosity-related features, including SA concentration, HA concentration, and mesh number, showed non-linear effects with favorable intermediate ranges rather than simple monotonic trends, suggesting that excessive stiffness or insufficient pore interconnectivity may reduce predicted regenerative performance. In contrast, the bioactive-cue features showed more step-wise effects. The inclusion of CRV-sEVs markedly increased the predicted M2/M1 ratio, whereas PLGA-KGN mainly improved fibrocartilage-associated outcomes, including S-G ratio and Tendon Maturing Score. These PDP/ICE results support the biological rationale of combining early immunomodulation with delayed pro-chondrogenic stimulation in the optimized hydrogel system. (Fig. S8–S11). All PDP/ICE analyses were restricted to the predefined discrete formulation levels and did not extrapolate outside the formulation domain used for model training and virtual screening. This multi-faceted explainability analysis built confidence in the model's predictions and provided crucial scientific insights for engineering a potent regenerative hydrogel.

Fabrication and characterization of the machine learning-optimized hydrogel system

Following rigorous validation, the four trained TabPFN models were deployed as predictive engines for an exhaustive in silico search of the optimal hydrogel formulation. This high-throughput virtual screening navigated an immense combinatorial library of 137, 525, 850 unique potential formulations (Cartesian product of 21 × 21 × 21 × 15 × 15 × 11 × 2 × 3) (Table S1). All possible combinations of the eight input features were computationally generated, and the models predicted the corresponding biological outcomes for each candidate. To ensure computational feasibility, this massive screening task was executed using a batch-processing strategy, wherein the virtual library was randomly partitioned into 100 manageable data packets. From each of the 100 computational packets, the single best-performing formulation was identified, resulting in four distinct sets of 100 top-tier candidates. The results, visualized in heatmaps, identified the top 100 elite candidates for maximizing the Tendon Maturing Score, S-G Ratio, M2/M1 Ratio, and TB Ultimate Load (Fig. 3A–D). A simple single-objective optimization might identify formulations that excel in one area while being deficient in others. Therefore, a multi-objective approach was adopted to identify a consensus candidate with a robustly superior profile across all critical aspects of healing. A lead candidate was identified for its consistently high-ranking performance; it was predicted to be the top-performing formulation (rank #1) for three of the four outcomes (Tendon Maturing Score, S-G Ratio, and M2/M1 Ratio) and the second-best performer (rank #2) for the TB Ultimate Load. This formulation, defined by the parameters (Fig. 3I) SA conc: 2.0%, AG conc: 2.3%, HA conc: 1.6%, SA (aq) pct: 50%, HA (aq) pct: 7%, Mesh number: 60, and inclusion of both PLGA-KGN and CRV-sEVs, was selected as the definitive ML-optimized candidate for physical fabrication and experimental validation. To avoid bias from single-outcome selection, we performed Pareto-front analysis across the four outcome-specific prediction vectors. Each outcome was min-max normalized, and non-dominated formulations were identified as Pareto-front candidates. An equal-weighted composite score was then calculated to rank the Pareto-front formulations (Fig. S7). The selected MMH-Opt formulation was located on the Pareto front and achieved a composite score of 0.989. The ultimate test of the ML pipeline's utility lies in predicting real-world experimental outcomes. The identified optimal formulation was synthesized and evaluated in vivo in the rat rotator cuff repair model. The results showed a remarkable concordance between the a priori predictions and the experimentally measured values. No statistically significant differences were observed between the predicted and actual scores for the Tendon Maturing Score (Fig. 3E), S-G Ratio (Fig. 3F), M2/M1 Ratio (Fig. 3G), and TB Ultimate Load (Fig. 3H). This validation confirms the accuracy and practical utility of the entire ML-guided discovery framework.

The fabrication workflow for the ML-optimized MMH is detailed in Fig. 3I. The process involves forming and mechanically extruding a primary hydrogel to create uniform microparticles, which are then mixed with therapeutic payloads (CRV-sEVs and PLGA-KGN) and fused via secondary UV-mediated crosslinking. The resulting MMH precursor demonstrated excellent injectability, easily forming stable, complex shapes (Fig. S12). Electron microscopy confirmed the morphology of the components and the final construct. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed the typical cup-shaped, bilayer membrane structure of the isolated CRV-sEVs (Fig. 3J; Fig. S13A). The average diameter of PLGA-KGN microspheres was 18.7 ± 4.3 μm, with D10, D50, and D90 values of 12.1, 18.2, and 25.9 μm, respectively (Fig. S13D). The calculated span value was 0.76. Hydrated microspheres showed an apparent PDI of 0.21 ± 0.03 by DLS. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) showed the uniform, spherical morphology of PLGA-KGN microspheres and, crucially, the porous and interconnected architecture of the final lyophilized MMH. The structure consists of fused microgel spheres, creating an extensive network of large macropores (100–300 μm), a critical design feature for facilitating deep cell infiltration (Fig. 3J). In vitro assessments confirmed the hydrogel's controlled degradation and sequential release kinetics. The MMH maintained its structural integrity while gradually degrading over 21 days, with approximately 50% of its initial weight remaining at the endpoint (Fig. 3K and L). The hydrogel exhibited a sophisticated, sequential release pattern near 30 days: an initial release of CRV-sEVs within the first 7 days was followed by a sustained release of KGN from the encapsulated PLGA microspheres (Fig. 3M). This programmed delivery ensures that distinct therapeutic signals are provided at the appropriate stages of the healing cascade.

The hydrogel's biocompatibility was confirmed both in vitro and in vivo. The macroporous structure of the MMH supported robust cell colonization. Confocal microscopy demonstrated that bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) readily adhered to and deeply infiltrated the MMH scaffold, whereas they remained confined to the surface of a non-porous control hydrogel (TH) (Fig. 3N and O). A CCK-8 assay further showed significantly higher cell proliferation within the MMH scaffold, indicating a favorable environment for cell growth (Fig. 3P). Additionally, extracts from all hydrogel formulations showed no significant cytotoxicity towards either Mϕ or BMSCs, confirming the intrinsic biocompatibility of the material components (Figs. S15 and S16). In vivo biocompatibility and tissue integration were evaluated by subcutaneous implantation in rats. HE staining of explants at 7 and 14 days showed excellent tissue integration, with a progressive and robust infiltration of host cells into the macroporous MMH structure (Fig. 3Q and R). Concurrent analysis of the remaining hydrogel area in histological sections confirmed a gradual in vivo degradation of the scaffold, consistent with the in vitro results (Fig. 3S).

The bioactive components effectively modulate macrophage behavior

Adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs), the source of the therapeutic vesicles, were characterized. The cells displayed a typical fibroblast-like morphology, confirmed multipotent trilineage differentiation potential (osteogenesis, adipogenesis, and chondrogenesis), and expressed canonical mesenchymal stem cell markers CD44 and CD90 (Fig. S17A–C). To confer Mϕ-targeting functionality, ADSC-derived small extracellular vesicles (sEVs) were surface-engineered with a custom-synthesized CRV peptide. The identity and high purity (>95%) of the CRV peptide were validated by ESI-MS and HPLC, respectively (Fig. S18A and B). The peptide was then successfully conjugated to a DSPE-PEG-NHS lipid anchor, as confirmed by the reaction scheme and 1H NMR spectroscopy, to create the DSPE-PEG-CRV molecule used for vesicle insertion (Figs. S19 and S20).

Characterization of the isolated vesicles confirmed their identity and successful modification. Both unmodified sEVs and CRV-sEVs exhibited the expected cup-shaped, bilayer membrane structure via TEM imaging (Fig. S13A). Nanoparticle Tracking Analysis (NTA) revealed a slight increase in the mean diameter of CRV-sEVs (93.4 ± 16.9 nm) compared to unmodified sEVs (84.1 ± 15.7 nm), consistent with surface peptide conjugation and confirming no aggregation occurred (Fig. S13B). Finally, Western blot analysis verified that both vesicle preparations were positive for canonical exosomal markers (HSP70, TSG101, CD9) and negative for the cellular contamination marker Calnexin (Fig. S13C). To further evaluate CRV-sEV quality and reproducibility, we performed additional QC analyses across three independent batches. CRV-sEVs showed a particle: protein ratio of (2.8 ± 0.4) × 1010 particles/μg protein, RNA content of 1.32 ± 0.18 ng per 109 particles, endotoxin levels of 0.027 ± 0.008 EU/mL, negative mycoplasma tests, and no microbial growth during sterility testing. Residual free DSPE-PEG-CRV was 1.8 ± 0.5% of the input amount after purification. Batch-to-batch variation was low across particle size, concentration, particle: protein ratio, and RNA content.

The efficacy of the CRV peptide in directing sEVs to their target cells was validated both in vitro and in vivo. In vitro, fluorescently labeled CRV-sEVs showed significantly greater uptake by THP-1-derived Mϕ compared to their unmodified counterparts (Fig. 4A). This targeting capability was confirmed in vivo, where locally injected CRV-sEVs exhibited significantly enhanced co-localization with the resident Mϕ population (CD68+) at the site of TB injury (Fig. 4B). To evaluate potential off-target uptake by mesenchymal progenitor cells, we compared the internalization of PKH26-labeled CRV-sEVs by THP-1-derived macrophages and rat BMSCs under identical incubation conditions. Flow cytometry showed that 82.6% of macrophages were PKH26-positive after 6 h, whereas only 28.4% of BMSCs were PKH26-positive (Fig. S14A and B). Consistently, the PKH26 mean fluorescence intensity in macrophages was approximately 2.4-fold higher than that in BMSCs (Fig. S14C). These results support preferential, but not exclusive, macrophage uptake of CRV-sEVs. Having confirmed successful targeting, the functional consequence on Mϕ polarization was investigated. CRV-sEVs demonstrated a potent capacity to concurrently suppress the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype while promoting the pro-regenerative M2 phenotype. In a lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced M1 model, co-treatment with CRV-sEVs significantly attenuated the mRNA expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 (Fig. 4C) and markedly reduced protein levels of the key M1 marker iNOS, as shown by Western blot and immunofluorescence (Fig. 4E, I, 4L). Conversely, in an Interleukin-4 (IL-4)-induced M2 model, CRV-sEVs significantly upregulated the mRNA expression of M2-related markers Arg-1, IL-1ra, and IL-10 (Fig. 4D) and enhanced the protein expression of the key M2 markers Arg-1 and CD206, as demonstrated by Western blot and immunofluorescence, respectively (Fig. 4F, J, 4N). These phenotypic shifts were quantitatively validated by high-throughput flow cytometry. Finally, the immunomodulatory potency of CRV-sEVs was confirmed in a clinically relevant ex vivo culture of injured human rotator cuff tissue. To validate these transcriptomic findings at the protein level, key events in the canonical NF-κB activation cascade were investigated. A critical step in this cascade, the translocation of the p65 subunit from the cytoplasm to the nucleus, was markedly inhibited by CRV-sEVs co-treatment, as visualized and quantified by immunofluorescence (Fig. 5G and H). To confirm the upstream mechanism of this inhibition, key signaling proteins were analyzed by Western blot. The results revealed that CRV-sEVs co-treatment significantly reduced the LPS-induced phosphorylation of both IκBα and p65, without altering their total protein levels (Fig. 5I). Quantitative analysis of the phosphorylation ratios (p-p65/p65 and p-IκBα/IκBα) confirmed that CRV-sEVs act upstream to prevent IκBα degradation and the subsequent phosphorylation and activation of p65 (Fig. 5J and K). To determine whether pharmacological inhibition of NF-κB could phenocopy the Mϕ-polarizing effect of CRV-sEVs, BAY 11-7082 was introduced as a positive control in LPS-stimulated THP-1-derived Mϕ. LPS markedly induced M1/pro-inflammatory marker expression and cytokine secretion, including TNF-α and IL-1β. Both CRV-sEVs and BAY 11-7082 significantly suppressed these pro-inflammatory readouts (Fig. 5L). Consistently, ELISA analysis showed that TNF-α and IL-1β secretion was significantly reduced by both treatments (Fig. 5M). Notably, BAY 11-7082 did not fully reproduce the M2/pro-resolving transcriptional response induced by CRV-sEVs. Arg-1 expression increased to 2.43-fold in the LPS + CRV-sEVs group, compared with 1.36-fold in the LPS + BAY 11-7082 group. Similarly, IL-10 expression increased to 2.18-fold after CRV-sEV treatment, but only to 1.42-fold after BAY 11-7082 treatment (Fig. 5L). These results indicate that NF-κB inhibition substantially contributes to the anti-M1 effect of CRV-sEVs, whereas the full M2/pro-resolving effect may involve additional sEV cargo-mediated mechanisms.

The pro-inflammatory M1 microenvironment was found to significantly impair BMSC migration. This was demonstrated in both scratch wound healing assays, where M1 CM treatment resulted in a significantly lower wound healing rate (Fig. 6B and C), and in Transwell assays, which confirmed that M1 CM actively suppressed BMSC chemotaxis (Fig. 6D and E). While CRV-sEVs alone provided a partial rescue, the full composite system (M1 CM + CRV-sEVs + PLGA-KGN) almost completely restored the migratory capacity of BMSCs in both assays, achieving outcomes statistically indistinguishable from the non-inflammatory control group. Investigation into the potential molecular basis for this rescue revealed that the composite system restored the key stem cell chemotactic axis, CXCL12/CXCR4. Immunofluorescence analysis showed that M1 CM treatment significantly downregulated the expression of both the chemokine CXCL12 and its receptor CXCR4 in BMSCs. Co-treatment with the composite system effectively reversed this inhibition, significantly upregulating the expression of both proteins and re-establishing this critical signaling pathway (Fig. 6F–H).

In addition to rescuing migration, the composite hydrogel system effectively promoted BMSC chondrogenic differentiation despite the inflammatory conditions. In 2D cultures, M1 CM treatment severely suppressed the expression of key chondrogenic markers, including the master transcription factor Sox-9 and the primary cartilaginous extracellular matrix proteins, COL II and Aggrecan. The full composite system not only rescued this suppression but also markedly enhanced the expression of COL II (Fig. 6I and J), Aggrecan (Fig. 6K and L), and Sox-9 (Fig. 6M and N). These findings were validated in a more physiologically relevant 3D micromass pellet culture system (Fig. S22). After 21 days, pellets treated with the composite system were well-formed and exhibited robust deposition of a mature, proteoglycan-rich matrix. This was evidenced by intense, uniform Alcian Blue and Safranin O staining, as well as strong and pervasive COL II expression via immunohistochemistry (Fig. 6O). In stark contrast, M1 CM-treated pellets showed minimal matrix deposition. The superior histological quality of the cartilage engineered in the composite system group was objectively quantified by significantly higher Bern scores (Fig. 6P–Table S6). These protein-level and histological findings were corroborated at the transcript level by RT-qPCR analysis, which confirmed that the composite system rescued and significantly upregulated the expression of the key chondrogenic genes COL II (Fig. 6Q), ACAN (Fig. 6R), and SOX9 (Fig. 6S), which were all suppressed by M1 CM. Because a functional tendon-to-bone enthesis requires not only fibrocartilage formation but also integration with mineralized bone, we further investigated whether the bioactive system could preserve BMSC osteogenic potential under an inflammatory microenvironment. M1 CM markedly suppressed osteogenic differentiation, as evidenced by reduced ALP staining, ALP activity at day 7, and Alizarin Red S staining at day 21. CRV-sEVs largely rescued this inhibitory effect, while the M1 CM + CRV-sEVs + PLGA-KGN group showed a slightly higher but comparable level (Fig. S23A and B). RT-qPCR analysis showed that M1 CM significantly downregulated osteogenic genes, including Runx 2, Alp, and COL I. CRV-sEVs largely restored the expression of these osteogenic markers under inflammatory conditions (Fig. S23C). Immunofluorescence analysis further confirmed that M1 CM reduced the relative fluorescence intensity of OCN, while CRV-sEVs rescued their expression (Fig. S23D). These findings indicate that CRV-sEVs alleviate the inhibitory effect of the M1 inflammatory microenvironment on BMSC osteogenesis, while PLGA-KGN does not impair osteogenic maturation and contributes only minimally to osteogenic rescue.

At both 2 and 8 weeks, the ultimate load of the repaired TBI in the full treatment group was significantly greater than in all control groups. Restoration of physiological shoulder function was quantitatively evaluated using a gait analysis system. Representative footprint patterns and corresponding pressure heatmaps provided a qualitative depiction of functional recovery (Fig. 9E and F). At 8 weeks, rats in the full treatment group exhibited a natural, balanced weight-bearing pattern on the operated forelimb, characterized by larger and more uniform footprints, closely resembling the gait pattern of normal, uninjured animals (Fig. S28). In contrast, control animals continued to guard the injured limb, producing smaller and less intense footprints. Quantitative analysis of gait parameters corroborated these observations. Mean forelimb contact area (Fig. 9G) and mean forelimb pressure (Fig. 9H) were significantly increased in the full treatment group at 8 weeks, indicating greater confidence in and functional use of the repaired limb during locomotion. Further analysis of dynamic gait parameters revealed that the full treatment group also had a significantly improved stride length (Fig. 9I), while other parameters such as stride width, contact phase ratio, and oscillatory phase ratio also trended towards normal values (Fig. 9J–L). To further distinguish functional recovery from potential analgesic effects, we correlated individual gait parameters with the corresponding ex vivo biomechanical outcomes at 8 weeks. Stride length and contact area showed a significant positive correlation with ultimate failure load (Fig. S29), indicating that improved locomotor function was coupled with enhanced structural integrity of the repaired TBI.

The long-term systemic safety of the implanted hydrogel was assessed at the 8-week endpoint of the therapeutic study. HE staining of all major organs, including the heart, liver, spleen, lungs, and kidneys, revealed no signs of inflammation, necrosis, fibrosis, or any other pathological abnormalities in any of the treatment groups (Fig. S30). Furthermore, comprehensive blood analysis, including a complete blood count and serum biochemical tests for liver and kidney function, showed no significant differences among the groups (Fig. S31). These results collectively confirm that the MMH + CRV-sEVs + PLGAKGN system is biocompatible, supports cell colonization, and does not induce any detectable local or systemic toxicity, underscoring its potential as a safe platform for clinical translation.

Discussion

Regeneration of the TBI remains a significant clinical challenge in orthopedic medicine [34,35]. The natural healing process often yields mechanically inferior scar tissue instead of restoring the complex, graded fibrocartilaginous enthesis [36,37]. Our initial pathological analysis of human RCT specimens revealed that the injured TBI is a hostile microenvironment, marked by severe structural degradation, a persistent pro-inflammatory state dominated by M1 Mϕ, and a consequent loss of biomechanical integrity. Addressing this multifaceted pathology requires a therapeutic strategy that is both multi-modal and spatiotemporally orchestrated to align with the distinct phases of tissue repair [[38], [39], [40]]. This study presents a dual-pronged approach to meet this challenge: first, the development of a multifunctional, bioactive macroporous hydrogel system (MMH + CRV-sEVs + PLGAKGN) that sequentially delivers immunomodulatory and pro-regenerative agents to guide TBI healing; and second, a paradigm-shifting methodology where the optimal formulation of this complex biomaterial was discovered not through empirical trial-and-error, but was rationally predicted from a vast combinatorial library of over 137 million possibilities using a bespoke ML pipeline. The successful in vivo validation of the ML-predicted formulation underscores the potential of integrating data-driven discovery with advanced biomaterial engineering to accelerate the development of targeted regenerative therapies.

The conventional development of therapeutic biomaterials is often a protracted and resource-intensive process, relying on low-throughput, iterative experimentation to navigate a vast and complex design space [41,42]. For a multi-component system such as the one presented here, with eight distinct formulation variables, traditional empirical methods or even classical Design of Experiments (DoE) would be inadequate for comprehensively exploring the parameter landscape and identifying an ML-prioritized optimal candidate within the predefined discrete formulation library [43,44]. This study illustrates an alternative workflow that leverages the predictive capacity of ML to overcome this limitation [45]. By generating a small but information-rich “seed” dataset from 40 targeted in vivo experiments, we trained a predictive model capable of screening the entire 137-million-candidate design space in silico. Although the TabPFN models showed strong five-fold cross-validation performance, we acknowledge that the 40-sample training dataset cannot exhaustively represent the full biological complexity of the 137-million-formulation space. Therefore, MMH-Opt was interpreted as an ML-prioritized optimal candidate, rather than a mathematically guaranteed universal optimum. Additional active-learning cycles incorporating prospectively selected formulations would further improve model generalizability and remain an important future direction. This data-driven strategy, which moves from a focused experimental campaign to large-scale computational screening and finally to the validation of a single, top-predicted candidate, represents a significant acceleration in the discovery pipeline for complex biomaterials.

A cornerstone of this success was the selection of a robust and appropriate ML algorithm. In a head-to-head comparison of 15 regression models, the TabPFN model consistently showed superior predictive performance across all four biological outcome metrics. TabPFN is a recent ML innovation—a Transformer-based architecture pre-trained on a vast number of synthetic tabular datasets [33]. This pre-training endows it with a generalized prior knowledge of data structures, enabling it to achieve high accuracy on small datasets without extensive hyperparameter tuning—a feature particularly advantageous for biological research, where large experimental datasets are often expensive and time-consuming to generate [33,43,46]. The high coefficients of determination (R2 > 0.9) obtained during five-fold cross-validation confirmed the model's fidelity and generalization capacity, providing the necessary confidence for its use as a predictive engine in the subsequent virtual screening. Although TabPFN is suitable for small tabular datasets, the 40-sample training dataset cannot exhaustively represent the entire biological complexity of the 137-million-formulation space. We therefore interpret MMH-Opt as a model-predicted optimum within the predefined discrete design space, rather than an absolute universal optimum. Future active-learning cycles incorporating additional experimentally tested formulations will further improve the predictive robustness and generalizability of the model.

Beyond mere prediction, a critical component of our data-driven framework was the integration of explainable AI (XAI) to transform the ML model from a “black box” into an interpretable tool for scientific discovery. Using the SHAP framework, we were able to deconstruct the model's decision-making process and gain actionable insights [47,48]. The global feature importance analysis quantitatively confirmed our primary biological hypotheses: the presence and type of sEVs and the inclusion of PLGAKGN microspheres were identified as the most influential variables for immunomodulation (M2/M1 ratio) and fibrocartilage regeneration (S-G ratio), respectively. This alignment between the model's learned feature importance and established biological principles provides evidence that the model captured true structure-function relationships rather than spurious correlations. Furthermore, local interpretability tools such as SHAP force plots provided a transparent assessment of how the model arrived at predictions for individual formulations. PDPs revealed non-linear relationships, such as optimal concentration windows for SA and HA, offering granular formulation guidance that would be difficult to obtain through empirical methods alone [49,50]. This synergy, where a high-performance model's predictions are validated by interpretable explanations that align with biological reality, elevates the work from a simple application of AI to a sophisticated integration of computational intelligence into the scientific method [51]. The concordance between the in silico SHAP-derived importance of CRV-sEVs for modulating the M2/M1 ratio and the in vivo immunofluorescence data confirming this effect serves as one of the powerful validations of this entire workflow. Importantly, the final MMH-Opt formulation also ranked second for predicted TB ultimate load, indicating that its high immunomodulatory score was not achieved at the expense of biomechanical recovery. This supports the interpretation that the selected formulation occupies a Pareto-favorable region among the four healing objectives.

The ML-optimized formulation was physically realized as an MMH with an interconnected “grape-cluster” architecture. This structure, a direct result of our two-step fabrication process, is a key design feature. Unlike traditional, dense hydrogels that can act as a barrier to cells, the MMH's macroporosity (100-300 μm pores) actively promotes deep cellular infiltration and colonization, creating a viable niche for endogenous cells to populate the scaffold and orchestrate repair [18,52]. This physical architecture works in concert with the material's chemical design to achieve a programmed, sequential release of the therapeutic payloads. The adsorbed CRV-sEVs are released within the first week, timed to coincide with and modulate the acute inflammatory phase of healing. In contrast, the pro-chondrogenic agent KGN, encapsulated within slower-degrading PLGA microspheres, is released in a sustained manner over 30 days, providing a long-term signal to guide tissue remodeling. The simultaneous-release control further supports the importance of temporal coordination. Immediate KGN exposure within a highly inflammatory microenvironment may be less effective than delayed and sustained KGN delivery after CRV-sEV-mediated immune resolution. This programmed, sequential delivery is an advancement over simple bolus injections or single-phase release systems, as it is designed to mirror the distinct temporal phases of the natural healing cascade.

The therapeutic strategy of our optimized system adopts a “one-two punch” approach, first targeting the pathological immune microenvironment and then promoting functional tissue reconstruction [53]. The initial pathological analysis of human and rat TBI tissues identified unresolved inflammation, characterized by a predominance of pro-inflammatory M1 Mϕ, as one of the central driver of poor healing outcomes [54]. Mϕ are recognized as key regulators of tendon healing [[55], [56], [57]]; their phenotype determines the balance between functional regeneration and fibrotic scarring. Regeneration requires a properly timed inflammatory response rather than indiscriminate inflammation suppression. Early M1-associated inflammation contributes to debris clearance and reparative cell recruitment, whereas persistent M1 dominance impairs matrix remodeling and fibrocartilage regeneration. Failure to transition from the initial M1 inflammatory state to a pro-reparative M2 phenotype results in chronic inflammation, excessive matrix degradation, and impaired regeneration [58]. Therefore, the M2/M1 ratio at 2 weeks in our optimization framework was intended to reflect the resolution of excessive inflammation and transition toward a pro-reparative microenvironment, rather than complete suppression of the initial inflammatory phase. Our strategy directly targets this pathological mechanism. By engineering ADSC-sEVs with the Mϕ-targeting peptide CRV (28), we aimed to enhance the specific and efficient delivery of the therapeutic payload to the key cellular mediators of inflammation. The significant increase in uptake by Mϕ both in vitro and in vivo confirms the success of this active targeting strategy, which may offer improved precision compared to non-targeted systems that rely solely on passive accumulation.

Mechanistically, our study demonstrates that CRV-sEVs exert anti-inflammatory effects by partially inhibiting the canonical NF-κB signaling pathway. Transcriptomic analysis revealed broad suppression of inflammatory gene programs, with KEGG and GSEA analyses implicating the NF-κB pathway as a primary target. Protein-level validation showed that CRV-sEVs prevent phosphorylation of IκBα and consequently inhibit nuclear translocation of the p65 subunit. As NF-κB is a key transcriptional regulator of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IL-1β, its inhibition may explain the observed shift away from the M1 Mϕ phenotype [59]. The BAY 11-7082 positive-control experiment further refined our mechanistic interpretation. Pharmacological inhibition of NF-κB largely reproduced the ability of CRV-sEVs to suppress LPS-induced pro-inflammatory transcripts and cytokines. This supports the conclusion that NF-κB suppression is an important contributor to the anti-M1 effect of CRV-sEVs. However, BAY 11-7082 did not fully reproduce the M2/pro-resolving transcriptional response induced by CRV-sEVs. Therefore, CRV-sEV-mediated macrophage remodeling should not be interpreted as a simple consequence of NF-κB blockade alone. Instead, CRV-sEVs may additionally deliver bioactive cargos that promote pro-resolving or alternative macrophage-polarizing pathways. While the precise sEV cargo responsible for this effect remains to be fully elucidated, ADSC-sEVs are known to be rich in immunomodulatory miRNAs and proteins that can interfere with the NF-κB cascade, representing a fertile area for future investigation [13,18]. This targeted immunomodulation effectively prepares the local microenvironment for regeneration.

This initial immunomodulatory phase is followed by a pro-regenerative phase driven by the sustained release of KGN. Our in vitro experiments using an M1 Mϕ-conditioned medium model demonstrate the synergy between these two phases. The inflammatory microenvironment created by M1 Mϕ significantly inhibited the migration and chondrogenic differentiation of BMSCs, processes critical for rebuilding the fibrocartilaginous enthesis [60,61]. Treatment with CRV-sEVs alone partially rescued these functions by mitigating the inflammatory suppression. CRV-sEVs also rescued osteogenic differentiation, suggesting that immunomodulatory regulation of the inflammatory niche is important not only for chondrogenesis but also for preserving bone-side osteogenic potential. However, it was the full composite system, delivering both the anti-inflammatory sEVs and the pro-chondrogenic KGN, that most effectively restored BMSCs migration and differentiation. This demonstrates that simply removing the inflammatory brake is not enough; a pro-regenerative accelerator is also required for an optimal outcome. KGN is a well-established small molecule that promotes chondrogenesis, and its encapsulation in PLGA microspheres is a proven strategy for achieving the sustained release necessary for guiding long-term cell differentiation and matrix deposition [29,30]. This dual-factor, sequential delivery strategy, which addresses distinct biological requirements at different time points, represents an effective approach to tissue engineering.

The efficacy of this orchestrated therapeutic strategy was demonstrated by the comprehensive in vivo results in a rat rotator cuff repair model. The MMH + CRV-sEVs + PLGAKGN group exhibited a modulation of the local immune microenvironment at 2 weeks, with a significant reduction in M1 markers (iNOS, TNF-α) and a concurrent increase in M2 markers (CD206, IL-10). This favorable microenvironment was associated with enhanced recruitment of endogenous stem cells (CD29+/CD90+) to the injury site. These early events culminated in the regeneration of a structurally and compositionally superior TBI. Histological analysis revealed the progressive formation of a continuous, proteoglycan-rich fibrocartilage layer, as evidenced by Safranin O and COL II staining—a structure critical for stress dissipation that is difficultly reformed during natural healing. Furthermore, the repaired tendon tissue of the full treatment group displayed enhanced maturation, with significantly larger, more organized collagen fibrils and a higher ratio of mature Collagen I to scar-associated Collagen III. This shift from fibrotic repair toward a more regenerative process translated directly into better functional outcomes. The repaired constructs in the full treatment group withstood significantly higher ultimate loads and exhibited greater stiffness. This mechanical integrity was reflected in functional recovery, as demonstrated by gait analysis showing improved weight-bearing and a more natural stride pattern. Although the anti-inflammatory effect of CRV-sEVs may partially contribute to improved limb use, the significant animal-level correlation between gait parameters and ultimate failure load supports a structure-function relationship. This comprehensive narrative—from molecular mechanism (NF-κB inhibition) to cellular modulation (Mϕ polarization), tissue reconstruction (fibrocartilage formation), and functional restoration—offers coherent validation of our therapeutic approach.

The primary contribution of this study goes beyond developing a single effective therapy. It establishes and validates a new, integrated workflow for the rational design of complex biomaterials that fuses predictive ML, XAI-driven scientific insight, and rigorous mechanistic biology. This integrated computational-experimental workflow, where computational prediction guides targeted experimentation, has the potential to accelerate the discovery of advanced therapeutics for numerous challenges in regenerative medicine [62,63]. The application of a Transformer-based model (TabPFN) for this purpose is noteworthy and points toward a new frontier in materials informatics. From a clinical perspective, the MMH + CRV-sEVs + PLGAKGN system possesses several attributes that favor translation. As an injectable, acellular, “off-the-shelf” product, it may circumvent some of the regulatory, logistical, and cost hurdles associated with live cell therapies. Its potential for minimally invasive administration and its mechanism of harnessing the body's endogenous repair capacity are desirable features for clinical adoption. The demonstrated biocompatibility and systemic safety data further support its translational potential.

Nevertheless, it is essential to acknowledge the limitations of this study. First, our findings are derived from an acute tendon transection model in young, healthy male rats. This model, while standard for initial efficacy testing, does not fully recapitulate the complexity of human RCT, which are often chronic, degenerative, and occur in older populations with comorbidities that can alter the healing response. Only male rats were used, which reduced biological variability related to hormonal cycling but limits the generalizability of the findings. Therefore, future preclinical validation in a more clinically relevant large animal model, including both male and female, of chronic degenerative tears is an essential next step to assess long-term efficacy and durability. Second, while the ML model proved accurate within its training domain, its predictive power for entirely new material components or chemistries is not guaranteed. Future work could employ an “active learning” framework, where the model iteratively suggests the most informative new experiments to perform, progressively and efficiently expanding its knowledge base and predictive accuracy. Third, while we have demonstrated an anti-inflammatory effect mediated by CRV-sEVs and implicated the NF-κB pathway, the specific molecular cargo responsible for this action has not been definitively identified. A detailed proteomic and transcriptomic analysis of the sEV cargo is a critical future direction that could lead to more potent, “second-generation” engineered vesicles. Fourth, although the stress-relaxation analysis provides insight into the macroscopic viscoelastic recovery of the repaired TBI, we did not perform AFM nanoindentation or microscale modulus mapping of the fibrocartilaginous layer. Therefore, the spatial micromechanical gradient across the regenerated enthesis remains to be fully resolved in future studies. Finally, the path from bench to bedside presents challenges, particularly concerning scalable, GMP-compliant biomanufacturing. The consistent, large-scale production of the composite hydrogel and the engineered sEVs represents a substantial but necessary hurdle to overcome for clinical translation.

In summary, this work presents a promising approach to the multifaceted challenge of TB healing, offering a dual innovation in both therapeutic strategy and design methodology. We developed a bioactive, macroporous hydrogel that orchestrates a phased regenerative response, initially delivering targeted, engineered CRV-sEVs to modulate pathological inflammation, potentially via NF-κB inhibition, and subsequently providing a sustained release of KGN to promote the formation of a functional fibrocartilaginous enthesis. The optimal formulation for this complex system was identified through a novel and efficient ML pipeline, where the predictive power of TabPFN models and the scientific insights from SHAP-based explainability enabled the rational navigation of a vast design space. The strong concordance between the ML model's predictions and the experimentally validated histological, biomechanical, and functional outcomes in an animal model supports the validity of this integrated approach. By synergistically combining data-driven design with sophisticated biomaterial engineering, this study provides a framework for the development of advanced regenerative therapies and offers a potential blueprint for accelerating their discovery and clinical translation.

Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This study was designed to develop and validate an ML-driven framework for the discovery of a multifunctional hydrogel to promote tendon-to-bone interface (TBI) healing. The workflow integrated computational modeling, biomaterial synthesis, in vitro mechanistic investigation, and preclinical in vivo validation. All experimental procedures involving human tissues were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and received formal approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine (no. 2023-KY-074(K)). Written informed consent was obtained from all participating patients. All animal experiments were performed in strict accordance with protocols approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Shanghai Sixth People's Hospital (no. DWLL2025-0947) and followed the guidelines outlined in the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

Machine learning-guided discovery of the optimal hydrogel formulation

A data-driven discovery pipeline was developed to systematically navigate the high-dimensional design space of the multi-component hydrogel and identify an optimal formulation for TBI regeneration.

Dataset foundation and model selection

The ML pipeline was built upon a training dataset generated from 40 distinct in vivo experiments conducted in a rat rotator cuff repair (RCR) model. This dataset mapped eight discrete formulation features—including polymer concentrations (sodium alginate, agarose, hyaluronic acid), solution volume ratios, microstructure control (mesh number), and the presence and type of bioactive payloads (PLGA-KGN microspheres, standard sEVs, or CRV-sEVs)—to four key in vivo healing outcomes: a composite histological Tendon maturing score, a histomorphometric Safranin O-Fast Green positive ratio (S-G ratio), an immunomodulatory M2/M1 ratio, and a biomechanical ultimate failure load (TB ultimate load).

To visualize training-sample distribution within the virtual formulation space, 10,000 formulations were randomly sampled from the complete combinatorial library and combined with the 40 experimentally tested formulations. All eight input features were normalized to the 0-1 range. UMAP was performed using ‘n_neighbors = 15’, ‘min_dist = 0.1’, and Euclidean distance. Nearest-neighbor distance analysis was conducted in the normalized 8-dimensional feature space.

To identify the most robust predictive algorithm for this application, a comprehensive comparative analysis of 15 distinct regression models was performed. The TabPFN model, a deep learning architecture pre-trained on a multitude of synthetic tabular datasets, was selected for all subsequent analyses based on its predictive performance across all four outcome metrics. The model's generalization capacity was assessed using a five-fold cross-validation scheme. For model robustness evaluation, the 40 data points were treated as 40 distinct physical hydrogel formulations. The primary analysis used a fixed random seed of 42. Robustness to fold assignment was further evaluated using alternative random seeds of 7, 21, 84, 168, and 2026. The held-out fold was not used for feature scaling, model fitting, hyperparameter optimization, SHAP/PDP estimation, or final model selection. To reduce optimistic bias when comparing candidate models, nested cross-validation was performed using a 5-fold outer loop and a 3-fold inner loop for hyperparameter tuning of classical machine-learning algorithms. TabPFN was evaluated as an off-the-shelf pre-trained tabular foundation model without task-specific hyperparameter fitting. Learning curves were generated by training the model on 40%, 60%, 80%, and 100% of the formulation-level dataset. Feature-noise perturbation analysis was conducted by adding Gaussian perturbations corresponding to 2%, 5%, and 10% of each numerical feature range while preserving categorical feature identities.

For Pareto analysis, all four predicted outcomes were first min-max normalized to the range of 0–1. A formulation was considered non-dominated if no other formulation had equal or higher values in all four normalized outcomes and a strictly higher value in at least one outcome. Pareto-front candidates were ranked using an equal-weighted composite score. The final formulation was selected based on its position on the Pareto front, its composite score, and its feasibility for fabrication.

Explainable AI for scientific insight

To ensure the model's predictions were based on biologically meaningful relationships rather than spurious correlations, the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) framework was employed to interpret the model's decision-making process. This explainable AI (XAI) approach transformed the predictive model from a “black box” into an interpretable tool for scientific discovery. This synergy, where the model's learned feature importance aligns with established biological principles, provides strong evidence that the model captured true structure-function relationships. Furthermore, local interpretability analyses, such as Partial Dependence Plots (PDPs), revealed non-linear optima, such as ideal concentration windows for hydrogel polymers, offering granular formulation guidance that would be difficult to obtain through empirical methods alone.

High-throughput virtual screening

The validated TabPFN models were subsequently deployed to conduct an exhaustive in silico search of a combinatorial library containing over 137 million unique potential hydrogel formulations. A multi-objective optimization approach was used to analyze the screening results and identify a single, consensus candidate formulation. The predictive accuracy of the pipeline was assessed by synthesizing the ML-optimized formulation and comparing its experimentally measured in vivo performance against the model's a priori predictions.

Fabrication and characterization of the bioactive hydrogel system

The ML-optimized formulation was physically realized as an injectable, microgranule-assembled macroporous hydrogel (MMH) co-loaded with CRV-sEVs and KGN-loaded PLGA microspheres.

Synthesis and structural characterization

The MMH was fabricated via a two-step crosslinking process. A primary bulk hydrogel composed of sodium alginate, agarose, and methacrylated hyaluronic acid was mechanically extruded through a steel mesh to form uniform microparticles. These microparticles were then mixed with the therapeutic payloads and subjected to a secondary UV-mediated crosslinking to create a stable, injectable construct with an interconnected macroporous architecture. The pro-chondrogenic agent KGN was encapsulated within poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) microspheres using a water-in-oil-in-water (W/O/W) double emulsion-solvent evaporation technique. The morphology of the components and final construct was examined using SEM. PLGA-KGN microsphere size distribution was quantified from SEM images using ImageJ. Hydrated microspheres were also dispersed in PBS and analyzed by dynamic light scattering to determine the apparent polydispersity index (PDI).

Degradation and release profile

The in vitro degradation of the MMH was assessed by monitoring mass loss over time in PBS at 37 °C. The sequential release kinetics of the therapeutic agents were evaluated in vitro by incubating the composite hydrogel in PBS at 37 °C. The concentration of released CRV-sEVs was quantified using NTA, and the KGN concentration was measured by high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC). For HPLC analysis of KGN, release samples were filtered through 0.22 μm filters and analyzed using a C18 reversed-phase column. The mobile phase consisted of acetonitrile/water containing 0.1% formic acid at a flow rate of 1.0 mL/min. The calibration equation was AUC = 42.37 × concentration +1.86. For functional potency testing, released CRV-sEVs collected at days 1, 3, and 7 were concentrated using ultrafiltration and added to LPS-stimulated THP-1-derived macrophages. After 24 h, macrophages were collected for RT-qPCR analysis of TNF-α.

Preparation and characterization of macrophage-targeting sEVs (CRV-sEVs)

sEV isolation and surface engineering

Small extracellular vesicles (sEVs) were isolated from the conditioned medium of cultured human adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs) using a differential ultracentrifugation protocol. To confer Mϕ-targeting capability, the sEVs were surface-engineered with a CRV peptide (sequence: CRVLRSGSC) by incubating the isolated vesicles with a custom-synthesized DSPE-PEG-CRV conjugate, which inserts into the vesicle membrane to create CRV-sEVs.

Validation of sEVs and targeting functionality

The identity, purity, and morphology of sEVs and CRV-sEVs were validated using transmission electron microscopy (TEM), Nanoparticle Tracking Analysis (NTA), and Western blotting for canonical exosomal markers (HSP70, TSG101, CD9) and the absence of cellular contaminants (Calnexin). The targeting capability of CRV-sEVs to Mϕ was assessed in vitro via cellular uptake assays with THP-1-derived Mϕ and in vivo via a homing experiment in the rat RCR model. Total RNA was extracted from CRV-sEVs using an exosomal RNA extraction kit and quantified using NanoDrop. Endotoxin levels were measured using a chromogenic LAL assay. Mycoplasma contamination was assessed using a PCR-based mycoplasma detection kit. Sterility was evaluated by incubating CRV-sEV samples in thioglycolate medium and tryptic soy broth for 14 days at 37 °C. Residual DSPE-PEG-CRV was quantified using a FITC-labeled DSPE-PEG-CRV tracer in parallel preparation batches.

In vitro mechanistic studies

Macrophage polarization and molecular mechanism

The immunomodulatory effects of CRV-sEVs were assessed on PMA-differentiated THP-1 Mϕ. Mϕ were stimulated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) or Interleukin-4 (IL-4) to induce M1 or M2 polarization, respectively, with or without co-treatment with CRV-sEVs. BAY 11-7082 (MCE Co. Ltd) was used at 5 μM as a pharmacological NF-κB inhibitor. The resulting polarization state was analyzed by RT-qPCR, Western blot, flow cytometry, ELISA, and immunofluorescence. To identify the underlying molecular mechanism, RNA-sequencing was performed on LPS-stimulated Mϕ, and the involvement of the NF-κB signaling pathway was validated by assessing the nuclear translocation of the p65 subunit.

BMSC migration and chondrogenesis

To investigate the effect of the hydrogel system on progenitor cells in an inflammatory microenvironment, bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) were cultured in conditioned medium from M1-polarized Mϕ (M1 CM). BMSC migration was assessed using scratch wound healing and Transwell assays. Chondrogenic differentiation was evaluated using a 21-day micromass pellet culture system, with outcomes assessed by histological, immunohistochemical, and gene expression analyses.

Rat rotator cuff repair model and in vivo evaluation

Surgical model and experimental groups

An acute supraspinatus tendon transection and repair model was established in 12-week-old male Sprague-Dawley rats. A total of 48 rats were randomly allocated into four experimental groups: 1) Control (surgical repair only, no hydrogel); 2) MMH group; 3) MMH + CRV-sEVs group; and 4) MMH + CRV-sEVs + PLGAKGN group. Animals from each group were euthanized at 2 and 8 weeks post-operation (n = 6 (12 shoulders, 6 for biomechanical testing) per group per time point) for specimen collection and analysis.

Multi-modal assessment of healing

Healing was assessed at 2 and 8 weeks post-operation. Analyses included: histological and immunohistochemical analysis for tissue organization, fibrocartilage formation (Safranin O-Fast Green), and collagen deposition (Masson's trichrome, COL I/II/III); immunofluorescence analysis for the local immune microenvironment (M1/M2 Mϕ markers) and stem cell recruitment (CD29/CD90 markers); TEM for collagen fibril ultrastructure; uniaxial tensile testing for biomechanical integrity (ultimate failure load, stress, stiffness); a gait analysis system for functional recovery; and systemic safety evaluation via histology of major organs and comprehensive blood analysis.

Statistical analysis

All quantitative data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (v. 10.0). For comparisons between two groups, a two-tailed Student's t-test was used. For comparisons among multiple groups, a one-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed, followed by Tukey's multiple comparisons post-hoc test. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Initiatives to support nursing workforce sustainability: a rapid umbrella review protocol

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic revealed longstanding deficiencies with existing approaches to support and retain the nursing workforce. Similarly, changes to the landscape of healthcare work, including the introduction of technology, new leadership and shifts in skill mix, play a role in nurse vacancies and workforce capacity. The current critical global shortage of nurses is set to reach 4.5 million nurses by 2030.1 An already strained healthcare system is now facing further obstacles with increased demand for nurses, leading to significant physical, psychological and social health challenges across the workforce. These challenges affect work, study and well-being, resulting in substantial global mental health impacts and further hindering recruitment and retention in the already under-resourced nursing sector, with long-lasting effects.2 Decades of evidence suggest that a multipronged approach that fosters an enabling and supportive work environment for nurses across all stages of their working lifespan will be required to build a more sustainable nursing workforce.3,6 Recruitment and retention of nurses is a key priority for health systems globally, as meeting the healthcare needs of populations requires a well-trained, adequately supported and appropriately valued nursing workforce.7 8

In the first quarter of 2022, there were 23 620 nursing vacancies reported across Canada, representing more than triple the vacancy levels of 2017.9 Vacancy rates among other healthcare providers average 10%–20%,9 with more significant vacancies in rural areas and small, specialised services.1 These shortages have compounding effects on care team well-being when staffing levels are suboptimal, resulting in excessive workloads and increased overtime, leading to absenteeism and burnout.10 These trends point to a critical need for system-level changes that enhance working environments for nurses and incentivise sustained workforce participation.11

The WHO recommends key strategic areas of action to support nursing workforce stabilisation that (1) facilitate entry into the workforce, (2) invest in the active workforce and (3) enhance supports and manage attrition from the workforce.12 Many peer-reviewed systematic reviews have been published exploring these actions and other factors related to nurse recruitment and retention—including, for example, nurse recruitment and retention within specific service or geographical areas,13 14 initiatives focused on supporting the transition of nurses from prelicensure education to practice,15 16 or those that support the acculturation of internationally trained nurses.17 A rapid review was recently completed by Ben-Ahmed and Bourgeault18 to identify evidence-based interventions to support nursing workforce stabilisation, appropriate for implementation in Canadian settings. The results were summarised in a framework focused on retaining, returning, integrating and recruiting nurses across career stages. While this review included an evidence synthesis component of initiatives supporting nursing workforce stabilisation, it also integrated primary data (survey, focus group interviews) collected with Canadian nursing workforce partners to identify priorities for action.18 What is currently lacking within the extant literature is an umbrella review that comprehensively explores structures used to support nursing recruitment and retention efforts.

Using the multipronged framework proposed almost 20 years ago by the WHO,12 this umbrella review will address this current gap in integrated nursing workforce planning and policy development. Findings from this review can be used to inform a sustainable, equitable and comprehensive strategy equipped to meet the current and future needs of the population and health service and delivery systems.

An umbrella review (review of reviews) was selected to identify policy and other structural options in support of nursing workforce sustainability through recruitment and retention.19 This review will generate evidence as part of a greater policy research project to inform the evidence-based design, refinement and implementation of a Comprehensive Nursing Workforce Development, Support and Retention Strategy in Nova Scotia: the most populous province in Atlantic Canada. This strategy will be situated as part of a broader health human resource strategy currently in development in Nova Scotia that is fuelled by provincial commitment to transformative health system change.20 Furthermore, findings from this review will also be of interest across diverse jurisdictions and international audiences, where decision-makers are tackling similar nursing and general health provider workforce challenges.

Research question

This umbrella review is guided by the question: What structures have healthcare systems put in place to stabilise, support and sustain the nursing workforce? The definition of structures is guided by the Effective Practice and Organisation of Care (EPOC) taxonomy of health systems interventions: (1) delivery and organisation of care, (2) financial arrangements, (3) governance and (4) implementation strategies.21 Within this review, the following subquestions will be explored, within the context of the Feasibility, Appropriateness, Meaningfulness, Effectiveness (FAME) framework22:

Methods and analysis

This protocol aligns with the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology for conducting umbrella reviews19 with one deviation. Given that this review is focused on supporting rapid health system policy development, integrative reviews and rapid evidence syntheses were also considered for inclusion, extending beyond JBI’s inclusion of quantitative, qualitative or mixed methods reviews. This umbrella review followed similar methodological approaches found in previously published reviews concerning health services and policy.23 24 This protocol also adheres to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Protocols guidelines.25 26

Research ethics board (REB) exemption was received from the Nova Scotia Health Research Ethics Board for this review (REB FILE #:1029525). The research team is guided by an advisory committee consisting of provider and patient partners.

Eligibility criteria

The eligibility for included studies is described below according to mnemonic PICo: Population (P), Phenomena of Interest (I) and Context (Co). Included sources will be reviewed, including systematic reviews, scoping reviews, integrative reviews and rapid evidence syntheses.

Population

The population of focus for this umbrella review is the nursing workforce. This includes regulated nurses (eg, Licenced Practical Nurses, Registered Practical Nurses, Registered Nurses), nurse-midwives and advanced practice nurses (eg, Nurse Practitioners, Clinical Nurse Specialists, Advanced Practice Registered Nurses). Groups of nurses not providing direct patient care, such as nurse managers, administrators and instructors/educators working in postlicensure environments, are also included. Groups of paid employees providing direct patient care also considered nursing-related, such as midwives, personal support workers or continuing care assistants, may be eligible for inclusion as well; however, the search does not explicitly focus on these groups. Direct-entry midwives, nursing students (prelicensure) who are not in clinical placement, nursing faculty working in a prelicensure environment and healthcare professionals who do not provide nursing-related care are excluded from this review.

Phenomena of Interest

The phenomena of interest for this review are structures that aim to stabilise, support or sustain the nursing workforce. Structures may include the organisation of resources, policies, planning tools or approaches, interventions, programmes, initiatives and strategies. These examples will be categorised using the EPOC taxonomy.21 Given the review’s focus on the structures, sources that focus solely on factors that influence recruitment and/or retention (eg, job satisfaction, organisational commitment), without describing and/or evaluating responsive interventions that aim to address these factors and improve recruitment and/or retention, will be excluded.

Context

Reviews focused on nursing workforce stabilisation structures within healthcare systems or in healthcare delivery settings will be eligible for inclusion. Healthcare delivery systems in any jurisdiction or nationality are considered. Due to the rapid turnaround of this review, the search is limited to reviews published between January 2014 and 11 March 2024, the day of the search, recognising that included reviews may have included sources prior to 2014. Sources looking at structures supporting nursing workforce stabilisation in prelicensure settings or settings outside of a healthcare system are not considered. The review is also limited to sources published in English or French due to language limitations of the author team and resource limitations.

Study characteristics

To maintain research quality, all included sources must have used established, transparent and reproducible methodology and have undergone an evidence synthesis. Systematic reviews, scoping reviews, integrative reviews and rapid reviews with full-text availability through free-access journals, research team institutional access or author contact were considered for inclusion. Grey literature sources were identified through website searches of key national, provincial and international nursing organisations and ministries of health. The JBI critical appraisal tool for systematic reviews will be used to assess the quality of the included sources.27

Search strategy

The search strategy was developed by an information specialist (KH) in consultation with the research team based on the following concepts: nurses (population); recruitment and retention (goal of the structures); and study design (systematic, scoping, integrative and rapid reviews). The Canadian Agency for Drugs and Technologies in Health systematic review search filter28 was modified for the purposes of the review. The initial database search was developed in MEDLINE All (Ovid) and peer-reviewed by another information specialist using the Peer Review of Electronic Search Strategies guideline.29 The revised MEDLINE search was then adapted for Embase (Elsevier), Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature (CINAHL) Full Text (EBSCO) and Scopus (Elsevier). In Scopus, MEDLINE and Embase records were removed from the results prior to export. Online supplemental appendix 1 presents the full searches in each database. Searches were from January 2014 up to 11 March 2024. For grey literature, the research team searched relevant websites using the site searching function in Google (online supplemental appendix 2). The finalised grey literature approach will be described in full in the final review paper.

Study selection

All citations identified through the database searches were collated and uploaded to Covidence, with duplicates removed (N=2585). Title and abstracts of all citations were screened for inclusion by two reviewers. Conflicts were resolved by lead scientific researchers (MS, AI, JM). The full texts of included sources (N=140) were imported into Covidence for full-text screening by two reviewers. Conflicts were resolved by lead scientific researchers (MS, AI, JM). Reasons for exclusion at the full-text level were recorded and reported in the final Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis diagram. Critical appraisal of each included source were completed using the JBI quality assessment tool27; however, the scores associated with these sources did not inform inclusion or exclusion criteria. The quality appraisal will instead inform the analysis and discussion sections of the completed review. The tool was used with other review types (scoping reviews, integrative reviews, rapid reviews) as appropriate.

Data extraction

The data extraction form was adapted from the extraction guide provided within the Covidence editor website as well as the JBI Reviewer’s Manual.19 The extraction template was pilot tested by an independent reviewer on two relevant studies and modified to improve inter-rater clarity and consistency of extraction (see Extraction Tool, online supplemental appendix 3). The tool prompted the extraction of study characteristics, methods and analyses, results and key findings and a summary of the source. Two reviewers independently completed an extraction for each included source and consensus about the extractions will be performed by three research scientists (AI, MS and JM). The extraction tool may be revised during the extraction process, if necessary.

Analysis of the evidence/data summary

The purpose of an umbrella review is to provide a summary, rather than a synthesis, of the selected reviews.19 Umbrella reviews typically offer informative, high-level summaries of a specific topic area in a form that is easy to understand for healthcare decision-makers.27 To present the findings of this umbrella review, a tabular presentation of the studies (eg, tables and charts) will be provided. A narrative description of the qualitative and quantitative findings will be included in this presentation, highlighting key points in relation to the research questions. The findings will be summarised according to the EPOC Taxonomy, which is comprised of the following topics: (1) professional; (2) financial; (3) organisational and (4) regulatory interventions.21 Depending on the nature of the findings, additional figures may be included to highlight key similarities or differences that would be of interest to specific knowledge user groups, such as healthcare leaders and decision-makers.

Where structures have not been formally evaluated within the source, we will use guiding questions from the FAME framework to reflect on the described structures and the factors that would have to be considered for real-world, practice application.30

Patient and public involvement statement

Two patient family advisors will be engaged during the analysis phase (postarticle extraction).

Dissemination of findings

Results from this study will be published in a peer-reviewed journal. This review is phase 1 of a larger Canadian Institutes of Health Research-funded project, which includes a subsequent comparative policy review, a policy workshop involving a variety of knowledge users and the generation of a policy brief to inform the Nursing Workforce Strategy for the province of Nova Scotia.

More specifically, as part of the study’s integrated knowledge translation plan, the research team includes coinvestigators and knowledge users with responsibility for the management and implementation of Nova Scotia’s Nursing Strategy at both the government and employer levels. In addition, a key audience for its findings will be the Provincial Nursing Network, which advises the government on the Strategy and includes senior nurse representatives from postsecondary institutions, regulatory bodies, professional associations, employers and unions representing Nova Scotia’s nurses. These mechanisms will ensure the ongoing refinement of the Nursing Strategy is informed by the study’s findings.

Elemental traditional Chinese medicine: An emerging interdisciplinary field
Authors: Lichun Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTrace elements in traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) constitute a fundamental material basis underlying its pharmacological effects. Although present in extremely low concentrations, these elements are indispensable for maintaining physiological homeostasis and human health. Elemental traditional Chinese medicine (ETCM) integrates TCM theory with elemental medicine, coordination chemistry, systems biology, and other interdisciplinary fields. The core objective of ETCM is to elucidate the material basis and mechanism of action of TCM at the elemental level. ETCM demonstrates that the specific speciation, concentrations, and combinations of elements are critical determinants of pharmacological efficacy, while also revealing the mechanistic associations between elemental homeostasis and disease pathogenesis. Furthermore, ETCM promotes the transition of TCM research from conventional static analyses of elemental content toward dynamic investigations of elemental speciation, bioavailability, metabolic interactions, and systemic biological networks. This approach provides a new perspective for elucidating the material basis of pharmacological efficacy and offers novel pathways for the development of metallopharmaceuticals and the modernization of TCM.Keywords: elemental homeostasis, elemental traditional Chinese medicine, network regulation, research paradigm, trace elements

Although traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) demonstrates well-established therapeutic efficacy, its material basis and mechanisms of action—particularly the roles played by inorganic elements, which constitute a considerable proportion of medicinal materials—have long been a knowledge gap in research understanding. This has led to critical scientific bottlenecks in the quality control, mechanistic interpretation of processing practices, and modernization of TCM: How can abstract theories such as the “four properties” and “five flavors” be correlated with observable modern scientific indicators? Elemental traditional Chinese medicine (ETCM) has emerged as an interdisciplinary field specifically aimed at addressing this fundamental challenge. It overcomes the previous emphasis on organic components at the expense of elements, systematically investigating the types, forms, and concentrations of inorganic elements in TCM and their quantitative relationships with traditional properties, therapeutic effects, and meridian tropism. Its mission is to establish a bridge between the traditional empirical knowledge of TCM and modern inorganic chemistry, elemental biology, analytical science, and systems biology, thereby providing innovative research paradigms and solutions at the elemental level for elucidating the efficacy-toxicity material basis, mechanisms of action, quality standards of TCM, and the homeostatic regulation of trace elements within the body (Fig. 1).

Conceptual framework of ETCM. The concept comprises three dimensions: (1) theoretical basis, following TCM principles including four properties and five flavors, meridian tropism, ascending, descending, floating and sinking properties; (2) material basis, with trace and inorganic elements as core components, covering mineral medicines and multi-component systems involving elemental participation; and (3) mechanistic basis, in which elements regulate physiological and metabolic networks to achieve integrated pharmacological effects. This concept extends the conventional organic-centric paradigm by incorporating inorganic elements into the core pharmacodynamic framework while maintaining the holistic principles of TCM theory.

Concept and scientific significance of ETCM theory

ETCM refers to TCM guided by TCM theories, where trace or inorganic elements serve as core active components and synergistic factors (Zhao, 2025a). These elements exert therapeutic effects by modulating physiological functions, participating in metabolic processes, or influencing the bioactivity of other active constituents. This concept integrates traditional TCM property theory with modern elemental medicine, going beyond the conventional approach of merely quantifying inorganic elements in TCM, and pioneering a new paradigm of “elemental metabolomics” (Zhao, 2026a). This paper systematically analyzes the fluctuations of endogenous elements under physiological or pathological conditions, focusing on the endogenous dynamic balance of inorganic elements. It distinguishes exogenous element intake from the endogenous biological metabolic response network. This shift extends the research perspective from static, in vitro analyses of medicinal materials to the dynamic regulation of in vivo biological systems, aiming to elucidate how TCM exerts its therapeutic effects by reconfiguring the body’s “elemental homeostasis”.

Establishing scientific correlations between elemental characteristics and traditional medicinal property theories represents a central objective of ETCM. Within this framework, traditional concepts such as the four properties, five flavors, meridian tropism, ascending, descending, floating, and sinking properties may, to some extent, reflect the physicochemical characteristics, spatial distribution patterns, and biological behaviors of elemental profiles. This perspective provides a potential material basis for interpreting traditional medicinal properties and offers a novel elemental viewpoint for elucidating the geo-authenticity of TCMs as well as the mechanisms by which processing alters medicinal properties (Bai et al., 2025, Li et al., 2015).

Research strategies and framework of ETCM

Addressing the common tendency in TCM research to regard inorganic elements as mere “background” or “impurities”, ETCM adopts a systematic and proactive research approach, elevating inorganic elements to the status of key pharmacologically active substances and regulatory factors for investigation. The research methodologies include:

(1) Systematic elemental profiling: Analytical techniques, such as inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS), are employed to systematically determine macro- and trace-element compositions in TCMs and biological samples, thereby establishing elemental fingerprint profiles.

(2) Elemental speciation and valence-state analysis: Focusing on the effects of chemical speciation and valence states on bioavailability and biological activity.

(3) Elemental metabolomics: By dynamically monitoring changes in elemental profiles in biological fluids and tissues following administration, this approach reveals the overall effects of TCMs on the homeostatic elemental network, linking exogenous elemental intake with endogenous metabolic responses.

It has been proposed that rubidium is one of the essential trace elements critical for human physiology (Zhao, 2025b, Mao et al., 2025), and the mechanistic association between rubidium depletion and the onset and progression of Parkinson’s disease has subsequently been elucidated (Zhao, 2026b). Through the research framework of systematic elemental profiling → construction of element–medicinal property association models → elucidation of elemental homeostasis regulatory mechanisms, ETCM transforms inorganic elements from mere “background interferents” into one of the core scientific languages for interpreting the holistic mechanisms underlying the therapeutic effects of TCM.

Research paradigms and mechanisms of action of ETCM

The emerging paradigm of ETCM is rooted in the integration of bioinorganic chemistry with systems biology. The former elucidates how trace elements exert critical functions in biological systems through chemical processes such as coordination and redox reactions, whereas the latter emphasizes holism and dynamic network interactions. ETCM reconceptualizes inorganic elements in TCM from previously underappreciated constituents to biologically active components involved in the regulation of biological networks. Its research paradigm shifts from the analysis of static elemental contents toward investigating the dynamic chemical behaviors of elements within biological systems and their perturbations of integrated biological networks. This paradigm supports the establishment of an “element-component-target” framework. Studies have shown that trace elements such as Fe and Zn can form stable complexes with organic bioactive constituents, including flavonoids and alkaloids, thereby significantly altering their solubility, stability, and biological activities (Xu et al., 2025). This provides a definitive molecular basis for the synergistic interactions between elements and bioactive constituents. Specific elements serve as essential cofactors for enzymes and participate in the regulation of enzymatic catalytic reactions and redox signaling pathways (Jomova et al., 2025, Zhong et al., 2026). This provides an explanation for how elements can mediate the meridian tropism and efficacy of TCM by acting on specific target groups, utilizing network target navigation analysis, thereby guiding individualized disease diagnosis and treatment (Zhang et al., 2023), achieving a leap from chemical morphology to biological function. An ETCM mechanistic research framework has thus been established, beginning with the interactions between elemental chemical species and biomolecules, progressing through the regulation of cellular signaling pathways and metabolic networks, and ultimately achieving the correction of systemic elemental homeostasis imbalance and the manifestation of traditional therapeutic effects.

Research prospects and future perspectives

The core innovation of ETCM lies in its paradigm shift from an “organic component-centered paradigm” to an expanded systems-level framework incorporating inorganic trace elements into TCM research. Its future development is expected to follow a clear, hierarchical trajectory:

(1) Theoretical paradigm innovation: Transitioning from static descriptions to dynamic regulation. A fundamental breakthrough lies in establishing a new paradigm of “element-mediated integrative regulation”. Within this framework, TCM is regarded as a chemical system in which organic and inorganic constituents coexist, with particular emphasis on how trace elements function as “chemical switches” and “network hubs” by modulating the activities of organic constituents and altering the functions of biological targets, thereby driving the entire pharmacological network.

(2) Integration of key technologies: Shifting from single-component analysis to multi-omics integration. This work enabled by high-dimensional data-driven precision analysis. In the future, deep integration of speciation analysis, elemental metabolomics, spatial metallomics, and computational simulation will be required to precisely characterize the in situ speciation and dynamic distribution of elements. Furthermore, artificial intelligence-driven approaches should be employed to decipher the multidimensional interaction networks of “elements-components-targets-pathways,” thereby enabling the visualization and quantitative interpretation of underlying mechanisms.

(3) Expansion of application scenarios: Transitioning from basic research toward precise design and regulation. The ultimate goal is to achieve “elemental profile-based precision TCM.”

This will guide the quality control and breeding of geo-authentic medicinal materials, optimize processing and compatibility strategies to regulate elemental speciation for enhanced efficacy and reduced toxicity, and facilitate the development of novel elemental homeostasis modulators and nanomedicines. Collectively, these advances will drive TCM research into a computable and predictable era of precision, ultimately contributing to human health.

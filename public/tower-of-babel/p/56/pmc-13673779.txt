Identification and analysis of EphrinB2/EphB4 signaling pathway and angiogenesis related biomarkers in coronary artery disease based on bioinformatics
Authors: Chunyu Liu, Weiwei Cheng, Xing Wei, Di Song, Zhibiao Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEphrinB2 and its receptor EphB4 have been reported to play a crucial role in the development of the cardiovascular system, and the process of coronary artery disease (CAD) is closely related to angiogenesis. The aim of this study is to identify and analyze the therapeutic value of the EphrinB2/EphB4 signaling pathway and angiogenesis-related biomarkers.MethodsThe CAD chip data (GSE42148) was used for differential analysis to identify differently expressed genes (DEGs). Key module genes associated with CAD were identified using WGCNA. Machine learning and expression validation were executed for screening biomarkers. In addition, the biological functions of biomarkers were explored using enrichment analyses. Finally, the diagnostic and therapeutic value of biomarkers was explored through immune infiltration analysis and drug prediction.ResultsTotally, 927 DEGs were identified in CAD and control groups. A total of 1,605 key CAD-related module genes were obtained for subsequent analyses. Then, PTX3, DLL4, and CITED4 were identified as biomarkers. Enrichment analysis suggested that three biomarkers were associated with allograft rejection. Analysis of 28 immune cell infiltrates revealed that CD56dim natural killer cell, eosinophil, mast cell, and MDSC etc. were significantly different between CAD and control groups. Finally, small-molecule drugs, notably simvastatin and calcitriol, were predicted and validated via molecular docking (binding energies < -6.0 kcal/mol) to have a potential therapeutic role in CAD by targeting PTX3 and CITED4, respectively.ConclusionWe obtained three EphrinB2/EphB4 signaling pathway and angiogenesis-related biomarkers (PTX3, DLL4, and CITED4) linked with CAD, which provide new references for the treatment of CAD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13019-026-04577-w.

Background

Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) is an inflammatory disease caused by atherosclerosis [1]. Its pathogenesis involves dysfunction of vascular endothelial cells, biological processes such as lipid deposition, macrophage activation, proliferation and migration of vascular smooth muscle cells [2]. The clinical symptoms of myocardial ischemia, hypoxia and necrosis are caused by coronary atherosclerosis, the result of narrowing or obstruction of the lumen [3, 4]. Currently, CAD is one of the diseases with the highest mortality rate. According to statistics, the number of deaths caused by CAD worldwide will reach 12 million annually in 2023 [5]. CAD has a complex pathogenesis and lacks obvious pathological indicators in the early stages of disease occurrence [6]. Coronary angiography and cardiovascular angiography are the most effective methods for diagnosing CAD in clinical practice. CABG and PCI are the most routine and effective treatment methods in clinical treatment, but they are invasive, expensive, and require specialized medical centers and experienced cardiologists, which limits their routine application in clinical practice [7, 8]. Therefore, developing more convenient CAD prediction, diagnosis, or treatment is currently a key issue that urgently needs to be addressed.

Promoting angiogenesis is a promising and feasible treatment strategy for CAD [9], which also known as ischemic heart disease [10]. Angiogenesis, which involves the formation of new capillaries from existing blood vessels, is crucial for transporting oxygen and nutrients to ischemic areas and processing waste, and has been widely studied [11]. The Ephrin/Eph system is the largest tyrosine kinase receptor family in mammals, widely involved in physiological and pathological angiogenesis [12]. Among them, EphrinB2 and its receptor EphB4 play a crucial role in the development of the cardiovascular system [13]. Interfering with the interaction between EphrinB2 and EphB4 can cause instability in the development of capillary networks, leading to CAD. Therefore, it is particularly necessary to explore the potential mechanisms of the EphrinB2/EphB4 signaling pathway and angiogenesis related genes in CAD.

In recent years, the research paradigm of “public database mining + weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) + machine learning + experimental validation” has become the mainstream approach for biomarker screening in coronary artery disease, which is widely adapted to the exploration of molecular targets and pathogenic mechanisms in cardiovascular diseases [8, 15, 17]. Bioinformatics mining based on GEO microarray data features high-throughput, low-cost and unbiased screening advantages, enabling rapid identification of potential molecular targets for early CAD without large-scale clinical sample collection in the preliminary stage [14]. WGCNA overcomes the limitations of single-gene analysis by mining gene co-expression modules closely associated with disease phenotypes, making it highly suitable for dissecting the complex molecular regulatory network of CAD [15]. Machine learning algorithms including support vector machine-recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE) and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) enable precise screening of characteristic biomarkers with improved diagnostic efficiency, which has become a core technique for cardiovascular disease biomarker identification [17]. Immune infiltration analysis and drug prediction can further clarify the immune regulatory mechanism of biomarkers and explore potential therapeutic drugs, expanding the research dimension of CAD [16, 18]. Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) using clinical samples compensates for the limitations of public microarray data and validates the expression reliability of candidate molecules, enhancing the clinical translational value of the research results [20]. The combination of the above methods can systematically complete the screening, verification, mechanistic analysis and therapeutic potential exploration of EphrinB2/EphB4 signaling pathway and angiogenesis-related biomarkers in CAD, which is highly matched with the scientific questions to be addressed in this study. The objective of this study was to pinpoint and analyze biomarkers linked to the EphrinB2/EphB4 signaling pathway and angiogenesis in CAD. Through the utilization of bioinformatics approaches such as weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) and machine learning, potential biomarkers were identified. Furthermore, the mechanisms involved were delved into through immune infiltration analysis and drug prediction, offering fresh perspectives for the clinical diagnosis and treatment of CAD.

Materials and methods

Data source

Two coronary artery disease (CAD)-related datasets (GSE42148 and GSE23561) were acquired from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds). The chip data from 13 blood CAD and 11 blood control samples from the GSE42148 (training set) (GPL13607) and six blood CAD and nine blood control samples from GSE23561 (validation set) (GPL10775) were included in this study. Moreover, a total of 1,193 EphrinB2/EphB4 signaling pathway and angiogenesis-related genes (EARGs) were acquired from the GeneCards database and the MSigDB database.

Differential analysis

Differential analysis was executed between CAD and control samples using limma package (v 3.54.0) [14] in GSE42148 to screen differentially expressed genes (DEGs) by setting |log2fold-change (FC)|>1 and P < 0.05. Although the adjusted P-value is conventionally recommended to control the false discovery rate, strict multiple testing correction yielded no significant DEGs due to the subtle transcriptomic alterations and high heterogeneity typical of early-stage peripheral blood samples. Therefore, the raw P-value was maintained as an exploratory threshold, relying on subsequent robust machine learning algorithms and experimental validations to eliminate potential false positives.

Weighted gene co-expression network analysis

To screen key module genes linked with CAD, WGCNA analysis was executed by WGCNA package (v 1.71) [15]. First, the samples were clustered to eliminate outliers. Second, soft thresholds (β) were determined. Next, according to their proximity, genes were compared for similarity, and a phylogenetic tree of those genes was created. The dynamic tree cutting algorithm was used to separate the modules, and the minimum module size was 300. Finally, modules were clustered based on module eigenvalues (eigengene), and correlations between modules and traits were calculated to obtain key module genes. Furthermore, Gene Significance (GS) and Module Membership (MM) were calculated, and a GS-MM correlation analysis with scatter plots was performed to intuitively visualize the relationship between disease traits and the key module.

Identification and functional analysis of candidate genes

Candidate genes were acquired by overlapping DEGs, key module genes, and EARGs. Protein-protein interactions (PPI) of candidate genes were analysed using the STRING database (medium confidence = 0.4). Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis of candidate genes were completed by ClusterProfiler package (v 4.7.1.3) (P < 0.05) [16].

Identification of biomarkers

Candidate genes were analysed by support vector machine recursive feature elimination (SVM-RFE) algorithms using the caret package (v 6.0.93) and by least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) algorithms using the glmnet package (v 4.1.4), and the genes obtained from the two algorithms are taken as intersection to obtain the feature genes [17]. Feature genes in GSE23561 with consistent and significantly different expression trends from GSE42148 were used as biomarkers (p < 0.05). Moreover, receiver operating characteristic curves (ROC) were drawn to define the diagnostic value of the biomarkers using pROC package (v 1.18.0) [18]. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) of biomarkers was executed by ClusterProfiler package based on MSIGDB C2: KEGG gene sets obtained from the MsigDB database (p < 0.05, FDR < 0.05). Finally, the biomarkers were analysed for gene set variation analysis (GSVA) via the GSVA package (v 1.46.0) based on the Individual Human Gene Set GMTs obtained from the MSigDB database (p < 0.05, |log2FC|>0) [19].

Immune infiltration analysis and drug prediction

Enrichment scores for 28 immune cells were calculated in GSE42148 by the ssGSEA. Subsequently, the differences in immune infiltration between CAD and control groups were compared by wilcoxon test, and spearman analysis was executed. In addition, the DGIdb database was used to predict potential small-molecule therapeutic drugs targeting the identified biomarkers. To evaluate the functional validity of these drug-gene interactions, molecular docking analysis was performed using AutoDock Vina. The 3D structures of the target proteins and selected small-molecule ligands were prepared, and binding affinities were calculated. A binding energy < -5.0 kcal/mol was considered to indicate a stable conformation between the drug and the target protein.

RNA isolation and quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

A total of 10 pairs of normal and CAD blood samples were obtained from the clinic in the Shanxi Cardiovascular Hospital. All participants were given informed consent. The study had the approval of the Shanxi Cardiovascular Hospital ethics committee. Total RNA from the 20 samples were extracted with the TRIzol reagent (Ambion, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The cDNA synthesis was reverse-transcribed using the SureScript First-strand cDNA Synthesis kit (Servicebio, China). The qRT-PCR assay was performed with CFX Connect Thermal Cycler (Bio-Rad, USA). The relative quantification of mRNAs was calculated using the 2-ΔΔCT method [20]. All primers sequence information were shown in Table S1.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was carried out through R software. Differences between groups were analyzed via the Wilcox test.

Results

A total of 18 candidate genes were screened in GSE42148 cohort

A sum of 927 DEGs (503 up-regulated and 424 down-regulated) were acquired from the GSE42148 (Fig. 1A). WGCNA results showed that there were no outlier samples in CAD samples in GSE42148 and the optimal soft threshold (β) was finally chosen as 10 (Fig. 1B-C). By constructing a co-expression network and setting the minimum number of modules per gene to 300, eight modules were obtained (Fig. 1D-E). Highest correlation between brown module and CAD and the brown module was taken as the key module, which contained a total of 1,605 key module genes (cor=-0.54, P = 0.05) (Fig. 1F). Additionally, the GS-MM scatter plot for the brown module revealed a highly significant correlation (Pearson r = -0.865, P < 1e-200) (Fig. 1G), intuitively confirming that genes central to this module are strongly associated with CAD.

PTX3, DLL4, and CITED4 were identified as biomarkers

SGK1, EGR3, GLUL, CXCR4, DLL4, CITED4, and PTX3 were obtained by SVM-RFE algorithm (Fig. 3A). SGK1, GLUL, PROCR, CITED4, DLL4, PTX3, and PTGER2 were obtained by Lasso algorithm (Fig. 3B). The genes obtained from the two algorithms were taken to intersect to obtain five feature genes (SGK1, GLUL, CITED4, DLL4, and PTX3) (Fig. 3C). Three biomarkers were obtained by expression level analysis, including CITED4, DLL4, and PTX3, which were highly expressed in the CAD (Fig. 3D). The values of the AUC in GSE42148 were all greater than 0.8, and the results of verify in GSE23561 was consistent with GSE42148 (AUC > 0.8) (Fig. 3E-F), indicating biomarkers had a better diagnosis value (Fig. 3G-H).

GSEA results showed that PTX3 was significantly enriched in KEGG pathways such as ribosomal pathway, α-linolenic acid metabolism and allograft rejection etc. DLL4 was significantly enriched in the sphingolipid biosynthesis globulin series, graft-versus-host disease and allograft rejection etc. CITED4 was significantly enriched in aminoacyl tRNA biosynthesis, allograft rejection and homologous recombination pathways. Overall, all three biomarkers were associated with allograft rejection (Fig. 4A-C). GSVA results showed that the enriched pathways of PTX3 were signature estrogen response delay and JAK-STAT signaling, etc. The enriched pathways of DLL4 were signature reactive oxygen species pathway and signature androgen response. Functional analysis of biomarkers

Between the CAD and control groups, there was a difference in a total of seven types of immune cells, including activated CD4 T cell, CD56dim natural killer cell, central memory CD4 T cell, eosinophil, mast cell, MDSC, and memory B cell (Fig. 5A-B). Correlation analysis results showed that mast cell, eosinophil, CD56dim natural killer cell, activated CD4 T cell had higher correlations with PTX3 (|cor|>0.4 p < 0.05 ), MDSC, Mast cell, eosinophil, central memory CD4 T cell had higher correlations with DLL4 (|cor|>0.4 p < 0.05), and CD56dim natural killer cell had higher correlation with CITED4 (|cor|>0.4 p < 0.05) (Fig. 5C). In addition, a drug-gene interaction network was constructed based on the DGIdb database (Fig. 5D). Considering the biological relevance to CAD, two representative small-molecule drugs, simvastatin (targeting PTX3) and calcitriol (targeting CITED4), were selected for molecular docking. AutoDock Vina revealed excellent binding affinities for both pairs. The docking score for CITED4 with calcitriol was − 7.8 kcal/mol, forming stable interactions with residues such as PRO124, HIS127, and GLN164 (Fig. 6A-B). Similarly, PTX3 exhibited stable binding with simvastatin (score: -6.9 kcal/mol), interacting with critical residues including SER98, GLU110, and CYS103 (Fig. 6C-D). These molecular docking results provide robust structural evidence for their therapeutic potential in CAD.

Expression validation

To investigate the expression of biomarkers (PTX3, DLL4, and CITED4), we examined their expression in CAD blood by qRT-PCR and compared it with control blood. Our data showed a significant increase in the expression of PTX3, DLL4, and CITED4 in CAD patients compared to controls (Fig. 7).

Discussion

CAD presents a significant economic burden. It is the primary contributor to mortality and disability adjusted life years lost worldwide [21]. In clinical practice, it is common to encounter coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) patients who may experience bridge vessel restenosis or occlusion after many years. Performing another bypass surgery poses significant trauma and economic burden for patients, with a high risk of reoperation due to limited bridge vessel materials. Additionally, patients may face challenges such as chronic thrombosis within the stent post percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and insufficient space for CABG. Currently, heart transplantation seems to be the only option for these patients. However, the scarcity of donor hearts and the time constraints make this a less feasible solution for many. The ability to generate new coronary vessels could offer an effective solution to address these challenges [22]. In this study, we identified three biomarkers (PTX3, DLL4, and CITED4) associated with EphrinB2/EphB4 signaling pathway and angiogenesis, and preliminarily validated their high expression in CAD through PCR, providing new references for the treatment of CAD.

PTX3 is a member of the acute-phase proteins, which are produced at sites of inflammation by several cells, primarily dendritic cells, macrophages, fibroblasts, activated endothelial cells, and renal cells [23]. PTX3 is a useful and potentially accurate inflammatory biomarker for CAD and has a good association with CAD [24]. It may play an important role in maintaining plaque stability by mediating phagocytosis of late apoptotic macrophages. The high expression of this gene in CAD is consistent with our findings [25]. A significant association between PTX3 plasma concentrations and the risk of all-cause mortality at three years has been shown in AMI patients [26]. However, genetic predisposition to high PTX3 levels does not influence the risk of acute AMI [27]. Although PTX3 has been studied as an inflammatory marker, there is currently no report on its relationship with angiogenesis.

There is very little research on DLL4 and CAD. DLL4 has been linked to the progression of atherosclerosis [28]. activation of Notch directs tip-derived endothelial cells into developing arteries and thereby establishes that DLL4-Notch signalling couples sprouting angiogenesis and artery formation. CITED4 plays an important role as a central regulator of cardiac homeostasis and a common node controlling cardiac growth, adaptation, and fibrosis [29–31]. After ischemia reperfusion, CITED4 expression did not change initial infarct size but mediated substantial functional recovery while reducing ventricular dilation and fibrosis.but it induced a remarkable functional recovery over the ensuing weeks, associated with reduced cardiomyocyte autophagosome accumulation and apoptosis, a dramatic reduction in fibrosis, more favorable LV remodeling, and improved survival [32].

As a subset of CD4 + T lymphocytes, regulatory T cells inhibit over activated efector T cells and play an important role in the maintenance of autoimmune tolerance, which is associated with atherosclerosis progression [33, 34]. The cytokines and cytotoxic functions of CD56dim natural killer cell, one subset of Natural Killer Cell, through induction of apoptosis take part in regulation of the immune responses and could contribute to the pathogenesis of many immune ediated diseases including ankylosing spondylitis, Behcet’s disease, multiple sclerosis, rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, systemic lupus erythematosus and type-1 diabetes [35]. Previous studies have shown that eosinophils contribute to intravascular thrombosis by exhibiting a strong endogenous thrombin-generation capacity that relies on enzymatic generation, and play an important role in coronary occlusion by promoting thrombus growth [36, 37]. MDSC were implicated in various aspects of immune regulation, not only malignant, but also in non-malignant diseases. MDSC were implicated in suppression of different cells of the immune system, the main targets of MDSC are T cells [38]. Mast cells are involved in allergic response due to their Fc receptors for IgE immunoglobulins. Their role is mainly mediated by secreted factors, some of which are together with histamine and heparin stored as preformed substances in granules and many others including various cytokines and growth factors [39]. Memory B cells play a critical role in protection against repeated infections, particularly from variant viruses [40]. Upon antigen encounter, memory B cells can rapidly differentiate to antibody secreting cells or enter germinal centers to further diversify and affinity mature [41].

The data supporting that the protective effect of estrogen should be taken into account when assessing the overall CVD risk in younger women [42]. The JAK/STAT signaling pathway is essential for cell proliferation, apoptosis, differentiation and immune regulation. Many researches prove that RIPC decreases myocardial infarction size and stops I/R damage through activating JAK/STAT [43]. Reactive oxygen species are generated as by-products of cellular metabolism, primarily in the mitochondria. When cellular production of reactive oxygen specie overwhelms its antioxidant capacity, damage to cellular macromolecules such as lipids, protein, and DNA may ensue [44]. Reactive oxygen specieare regarding as a marker for changes in the internal and external environment of the cell are very important for an organism’s homeostasis [45]. The androgen receptor plays an essential role in the growth and progression of prostate cancer. Ligand-activated androgen receptor inside the nucleus binds to the androgen response element of the target genes in dimeric form and recruits transcriptional machinery to facilitate gene transcription [46]. MYC, encoded by the c-MYC proto-oncogene, is part of a large class of transcription factors. Activating MYC is a key regulator of biosynthetic and metabolic pathways driving cell growth and proliferation. Overexpressed MYC induces a series of oncogenic stresses, which impact not only on tumor progression, but also on the response to therapy, with profound, multifaceted consequences on clinical outcome [47]. The adaptive unfolded protein response functions to preserve protein homeostasis and sustain cell survival through several mechanisms that improve protein folding, reduce protein overload and improve the degradation of misfolded proteins through the reprogramming of gene expression and the inhibition of protein translation. Endoplasmic reticulum proteostasis is governed by the unfolded protein response. When the adaptive unfolded protein response fails to preserve endoplasmic reticulum homeostasis, a maladaptive or terminal unfolded protein response is engaged, leading to the disruption of endoplasmic reticulum integrity and to apoptosis. Long-term endoplasmic reticulum stress results in cellular defects causing disturbed cardiovascular function [48].

In this study, molecular docking identified simvastatin and calcitriol as potential therapeutic agents targeting PTX3 and CITED4, respectively. Simvastatin, often administered in combination with niacin, is a standard first-line pharmacological intervention for CAD. It plays a core role in anti-atherosclerosis by lowering low-density lipoprotein (LDL) levels, stabilizing plaques, and significantly reducing the risk of myocardial infarction [49, 50]. Calcitriol, the active form of vitamin D, has demonstrated epidemiological and mechanistic associations with cardiovascular protection. Its potential benefits include anti-inflammatory effects through the suppression of IL-6 and TNF-α, inhibition of vascular smooth muscle proliferation, and improvement of endothelial function [51, 52]. The highly stable binding affinities (<-6.0 kcal/mol) validated by AutoDock Vina suggest that PTX3 and CITED4 may serve as viable structural targets for these established therapeutic agents.

Feng et al. verifed CCR9, CSF2, IL13RA1, and NTS have a strong correlation with CAD, and IL13RA1 might participate in the infammation, fbrosis, and cholesterol efflux process of atherosclerosis by regulating JAK1/STAT3 pathway in 2022 [53]. Liu et al. formed an immune-associated CAD prognostic model for a total of 10 genes (MICB, RELB, S100A8, MAP2K1, IGHA1, SLIT1, SLIT2, TNFSF15, FGFR3, and TNFRSF13C). Among them, S100A8 was the most closely related to CAD [54]. Jin et al. identified 4 hub signature genes (ADM, PPFIA4, FAM162A, and TPBG), which showed reliable diagnostic power for CAD in 2023 [55]. Huang et al. reported MMP9, PELI1, THBD, and ZFP36 may be predicted biomarkers for CAD in 2022 [56]. Nong et al. bult a predictive model consisting of five genes, S100A8, HSPB1, F5, MMP9 and PDE9A, can help identify patients who are likely to develop CAD [57]. Xiang Xu et al. revealed that SNAI2 and EMT-related lncRNAs could be reliable biomarkers for diagnosing CAD in 2022 [58]. Although there are many other similar studies that mostly explore gene diagnosis related to CAD, and even the genetic mechanisms of plaque formation, a few studies have explored some gene pathways that may be related to CAD treatment, but have not received corresponding recommended drugs. For CAD patients, angiogenesis is a novel and meaningful treatment approach. We conducted preliminary exploration on EphrinB2/EphB4 signaling pathway and angiogenesis, and discovered three related genes, which are associated with seven immune cells. We identified three recommended drugs for potential treatment.

Conclusion

In conclusion, We obtained three EphrinB2/EphB4 signaling pathway and angiogenesis-related biomarkers (PTX3, DLL4, and CITED4) linked with CAD, which provide new references for the treatment of CAD. Although the sample size of this study is small, and raw P-values instead of adjusted P-values were utilized during the initial DEG screening due to subtle expression variations in blood samples, the exploration of angiogenesis at the genetic level remains highly meaningful. Moreover, multiple validation strategies, including dual machine learning models, external dataset verification, and qRT-PCR, were employed to maximize the reliability of the identified biomarkers. Further in-depth and larger sample size research is still needed.

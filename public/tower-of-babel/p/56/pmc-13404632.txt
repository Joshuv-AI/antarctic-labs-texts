Integration of routine health information, population surveys and environmental data for predictive modelling of non-communicable diseases in Africa: a systematic review protocol
Authors: Nyambe Sinyange, Timothy Robinson, Jackson Phiri, Veranyuy Ngah, Allan Mayaba Mwiinde, Isaac Fwemba
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionNon-communicable diseases (NCDs) are increasingly contributing to morbidity and mortality in Africa. Existing NCD surveillance systems are often fragmented and rely on routine health data, population surveys or environmental metrics collected independently. Emerging applications in machine learning and geospatial analytics create new opportunities for integrated predictive modelling of NCDs, yet there is limited evidence of feasibility as well as methodological performance and policy relevance in African settings.The objective of this systematic review is to synthesise evidence from studies that combine NCD prediction with routine health data, survey data and environmental exposures in Africa. The review will examine methodological approaches, predictive performance, interoperability and implications for surveillance systems.Methods and analysisThis systematic review protocol was prospectively registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) under registration number CRD420251116003. We will search PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Library, WHO Global Index Medicus and grey literature under sources such as Google Scholar; WHO, Africa CDC repositories and University of Zambia library resources from 2000 to 2025. Screening will be conducted independently by two reviewers using Rayyan for study selection and conflict resolution in accordance with Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. The risk of bias will be evaluated using the Prediction Model Risk of Bias Assessment Tool for predictive modelling. The results will be synthesised narratively as heterogeneity in methods and outcomes is anticipated.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required because this review will use data from published studies and will not involve human participants or primary data collection. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publication, conference presentations and sharing with relevant public health stakeholders.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251116003.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

Predictive modelling of non-communicable diseases (NCDs) has been predominantly conducted on single data sources such as routine health information systems, population-based surveys and environmental data. Routine systems provide longitudinal health data and surveys guide estimates of risk factors related to behaviour and exposure; environmental data, such as temperature, rainfall and air quality, affect NCD health outcomes. Current research shows that the predictive accuracy could be improved by using such data sources together. However, these integrated approaches are still scarce and fragmented in African contexts.

Introduction

Non-communicable diseases (NCDs) are a leading global public health challenge. They now account for almost three-quarters of all NCD deaths globally, with most of these deaths occurring in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs).1 In places where health systems already work under pressure from infectious diseases and limited resources, illnesses such as cardiovascular conditions, diabetes, cancers and chronic respiratory illnesses are already increasing. In the African region, NCDs have contributed increasingly to overall mortality in the region over the past two decades. Zambia has also experienced a growing burden of NCDs in recent years. NCDs contributed to approximately 35% of all deaths in 2021, against 23% in 2013.23 Across Africa, multiple sources of NCD-related data are increasingly available, including routine health information systems, population-based surveys and environmental datasets. Examples include District HeaIth Information System (DHIS) 2 platforms, WHO STEPwise Approach to NCD Risk Factor Surveillance (STEPS) surveys, Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS) and climate and air quality databases. Despite the availability of these data sources, they are often collected and analysed separately, limiting their usefulness for comprehensive disease prediction and surveillance.4 Reporting delays, incomplete datasets, weak interoperability and limited data sharing continue to affect the quality and use of surveillance information for public health planning and response.56

Existing studies in Africa have largely focused on describing NCD prevalence and behavioural risk factors, with comparatively less attention given to predictive modelling and spatial analysis. At the same time, environmental and climate-related factors, including urbanisation, air pollution, rainfall variability and rising temperatures, are increasingly recognised as important determinants of NCD outcomes.7 Studies from higher-income settings have shown that integrating health, survey and environmental data can improve disease prediction and support earlier identification of high-risk populations and geographical areas. Approaches such as spatial analysis, syndromic surveillance and spatio-temporal modelling have increasingly been applied to guide public health planning and resource allocation.8–11 However, evidence on the application of these integrated approaches in African settings remains limited and fragmented.11

A clearer understanding of how integrated data approaches have been applied in Africa, including their methodological strengths, limitations and predictive performance, is important for strengthening surveillance systems and informing evidence-based decision-making for NCD prevention and control.

Rationale

Although the development of NCD surveillance and modelling has progressed considerably in Africa, substantial data gaps persist. First, evidence on opportunities to combine routine health data, population surveys and environmental datasets to predict NCD outcomes remains scattered, context-specific and not readily generalisable. Second, there has been no formal effort to synthesise methodological approaches used in integrated and non-integrated predictive models, creating uncertainty regarding which approaches provide improved predictive performance. Most existing reviews of NCDs in Africa are descriptive of burden and distribution of risk factors; none, to our knowledge, have explored predictive modelling methodology or the role of multi-source data integration. Finally, there is limited synthesised evidence for policy-makers on the added value of data integration for decision-making, creating challenges in translating research findings into sustainable public health practice.

Preliminary scoping searches identified a growing body of literature on predictive modelling and integrated data approaches for NCDs, suggesting that sufficient studies are available for systematic synthesis, although evidence focused specifically on Africa remains limited.

Conceptual framework

This study is informed by Systems Theory, which suggests that outcomes arise not from single entities, but from the interplay among components of a system. Originally proposed by von Bertalanffy,12 Systems Theory has been widely applied across diverse fields to analyse the behaviour of complex systems, including health systems.13 Systems Theory, in a public health setting, emphasises the inter-related aspects of people interacting with their social and physical environment and institutional structures and processes creating dynamic effects on outcomes related to health. This perspective is particularly relevant in African settings, where disease patterns are shaped not only by biological risk factors but also by social conditions, health system capacity and environmental variability. This framework shows how environmental, health system and survey-based exposures influence NCD morbidity and mortality. Objective

To synthesise evidence on the use of integrated data sources in predictive modelling of NCDs in Africa, with emphasis on methodological approaches, predictive performance, interoperability and implications for strengthening surveillance systems.

Methods and analysis

This protocol has been developed in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P).14 We will conduct a comprehensive search of peer-reviewed and grey literature. Bibliographic databases will include PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, Cochrane Library and WHO Global Index Medicus. To capture unpublished or non-indexed evidence, we will also search Google Scholar, WHO and Africa (Centres for Disease Control and Prevention) CDC repositories, World Bank databases and technical reports from the Zambia National Public Health Institute (ZNPHI) and the Ministry of Health. In addition, University of Zambia library e-resources will be searched for theses, dissertations and other institutional outputs (figure 2).

Temporal and spatial

The search will cover studies published between January 2000 and December 2025 and is restricted to English-language publications. The review will primarily focus on studies conducted in African countries. However, selected studies from high-income countries may be included for methodological comparison and benchmarking where relevant.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public will not be involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination of this systematic review protocol.

PICO

Population (P)

We focus on LMICs where NCD surveillance data are available. As previously stated, for methodological comparison and benchmarking, selected studies from high-income countries may be included.

Intervention/Exposure (I)

Inclusion criteria will be studies that adopt either spatial, spatio-temporal or machine-learning methodology in predictive modelling for NCDs and especially where there is integration of more than one data source.

Comparator (C)

These will be compared with traditional/non-integrated surveillance and statistical methods to determine the value of integration and advanced analytics.

Outcomes (O)

The primary outcomes will be model validity, predictive utility, harmonisation with health information systems and relevance for decision-making and policy development.

Eligibility criteria

Types of studies

We will include quantitative studies such as cohort, case–control, cross-sectional and modelling studies that evaluate predictive performance of integrated versus non-integrated datasets. Commentaries, qualitative studies, editorials and opinion pieces will be excluded.

Types of participants/data sources

The unit of analysis will be datasets or studies involving health surveillance (eg, DHIS2), national surveys (eg, STEPS, DHS) and environmental/climate data used for NCD prediction in African populations. No restrictions will be applied for age, sex or setting.

Types of outcomes

Primary outcomes: Predictive performance metrics, including accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, root mean square error and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve.

Secondary outcomes: Feasibility indicators such as coverage, completeness, consistency, timeliness and interoperability of datasets.

Screening and selection process

Retrieved articles will be imported into EndNote for reference management and subsequently exported to Rayyan15 for screening. Title and abstract screening, as well as full-text review, will be conducted independently by two reviewers using blinded assessments in Rayyan to minimise bias and ensure independent decision-making. A standardised data extraction form will be used during the extraction process. Any disagreements between reviewers will be resolved through discussion, and where consensus cannot be reached, a third reviewer will arbitrate. Rayyan15 will be used to manage and document screening decisions. The study selection process, including reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage, will be reported using a PRISMA flow diagram.

Data abstraction

Data will be abstracted using a standardised form informed by the Critical Appraisal and Data Extraction for Systematic Reviews of Prediction Modelling Studies checklist.16 Extracted information will include study characteristics (author, year, country, design), data sources, integration approaches, predictors, outcomes, modelling methods, validation approaches, predictive performance measures and key findings. Two reviewers will extract the data and a third reviewer will independently verify the extracted information.

Certainty of evidence and risk of bias

The methodological quality and risk of bias of included studies will be assessed using the Prediction Model Risk of Bias Assessment Tool.17 Due to the anticipated heterogeneity in study designs, data sources, modelling approaches and outcome measures, a formal GRADE18 assessment of certainty of evidence may not be feasible.

Assessment of reporting bias

Where sufficient comparable studies are identified, patterns of reporting and selective publication will be examined to assess the potential for reporting bias. Grey literature sources will also be searched to minimise the risk of publication bias and selective reporting.

Synthesis of results

Given the anticipated heterogeneity in study designs, data sources and outcome measures, findings will be synthesised narratively. Results will be grouped by study design, type of data integration and predictive modelling approach. The synthesis will highlight methodological strengths, weaknesses and implications for future integration strategies in Africa.

Planned study dates

The review is expected to commence in May 2026 and be completed by September 2026.

Discussion

The aim of this review is to synthesise evidence from the integrated surveillance/predictive modelling of NCDs in Africa. Drawing on sources of data and modelling methods, the review aims to synthesise evidence and examine methodological advances, barriers and continuing knowledge gaps. The results will offer policy-makers and researchers’ practical guidance as to how integrating data for NCD prediction can be operationalised, how it will work and what value it adds to future work. The findings will thus also inform the development of an extensive toolkit for integrated predictive modelling for Africa, Zambia included, and in the development of transferable methods and practical lessons learnt from other contexts. There will be a focus on African studies, but some studies from high-income countries will also be analysed to provide methodological comparisons and benchmark innovative approaches. The review is expected to bring recommendations into application for African countries, as well as other LMIC countries in their commitment to develop integrated NCD surveillance systems and develop better data for decision-making.

Dissemination

The results will be published in peer-reviewed journals and will be presented locally and internationally at scientific conferences. Policy briefs and brief summaries on this publication will be presented to the Zambia National Public Health Institute (ZNPHI), Ministry of Health and regional partners, to ensure all findings can direct decision-making and capacity-building activities in integrated NCD surveillance. PROSPERO (CRD420251116003) registered the review protocol to ensure reporting integrity and minimise reporting bias.

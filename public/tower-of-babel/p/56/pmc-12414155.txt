Infant behavioural assessment and intervention program to support neurodevelopmental outcome of very preterm infants at two years corrected age: a cluster randomised controlled trial study protocol
Authors: Meghan Smith, Pierre Kuhn, Gerard Thiriez, Thierry Debillon, Valérie Datin-Dorrière, Bertrand Leboucher, Delphine Mitanchez, Emmanuelle Courtois-Communier, Jacques Sizun, Jean-Michel Roué
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionPreterm infants are at risk of developmental impairment, especially those born before 33 weeks gestational age. Many studies have shown a positive impact of early interventions on medical outcomes during hospitalisation, long-term cognitive development and parental anxiety. Infant Behavioral Assessment and Intervention Program (IBAIP) has shown positive effects on cognitive development but also on motor impairment in a Dutch cohort. We aim to confirm these results in a multicentric, cluster randomised controlled trial in a French setting.Methods and analysisEight French neonatal intensive care units (NICUs) will be randomised before study initiation to intervention or control group. We aim to include 240 infants born between 25 weeks and 33 weeks gestational age. IBAIP intervention comprises monthly home visits with a trained professional from hospital discharge until 6 months corrected age. Both groups receive standard care according to local organisation. The primary endpoint is composite cognitive score at 2 years corrected age using Bayley Scale of Infant Development Fourth Edition (BSID IV). Secondary endpoints include BSID IV subscores, Ages and Stages Questionnaire scores and parental stress. Analysis is in intention to treat. Univariate and multivariate analysis will be performed on primary and secondary endpoints.Ethics and disseminationInformed consent from one or both parents will be necessary for all patients. Study results will be published in peer-reviewed scientific journals. If our hypothesis is confirmed, IBAIP could be implemented on a nationwide scale. The study was registered with clinicaltrials.gov (ID: 29BRC17.0219).Trial registration numberNCT04685356.Keywords: NEONATOLOGY, Developmental neurology & neurodisability, Patient-Centered Care, PAEDIATRICS

Introduction

Survival rates of preterm infants have drastically improved over the last few decades, due to many revolutions in neonatal care.1 However, recent epidemiological studies have shown that those infants who do survive have a greater risk of developmental impairment. The risk of developmental impairment increases the smaller the gestational age at birth.2 3

Many different early postdischarge interventions have been devised to help improve long-term outcomes of preterm or very low birth weight infants. On a theoretical level, early interventions in these vulnerable children make sense due to maximal cerebral plasticity and synaptogenesis during the first 3 years of life.4 5 Recent meta-analyses have reported positive short and long-term impact of early postdischarge interventions on cognitive development in preterm infants.6 7 However, there is significant heterogeneity between programmes and further studies are needed to identify the most efficient strategies.

Neurodevelopmental impairment is known to be associated with medical compromise such as bronchopulmonary dysplasia or intraventricular haemorrhage. However, environmental factors also play an important role in these vulnerable infants’ developmental agenda. Parental sensitivity and responsiveness have been shown to be crucial for parent-infant interaction and the development of the infant’s self-regulation capacities.8 In turn, these aspects play an important role in the child’s developmental outcome, independent of perinatal risk factors and socioeconomic status.9 In the case of neurological compromise or inadequate stimulation, the infant’s reactions to environmental stimuli can be either diminished or exacerbated. In consequence, their interaction with the environment is hindered, slowing or stunting the learning curve of early infancy with consequences that can perdure into adulthood.10,12

The Infant Behavioral Assessment and Intervention program (IBAIP) is an early intervention programme offering a multidimensional approach.13 The aim is to support the infant’s self-regulation capacities through guided parent-infant interactions, based on the same synactive approach as NIDCAP (Neonatal Individualized Developmental Care Assessment Program), which is tailored towards very preterm infants during hospitalisation.14 Compared with other posthospitalisation programmes such as Infant Health and Development Program, which is far more time-consuming, IBAIP shows similar significant impact on parental stress and on cognitive development.15,18 However, it is one of the rare early interventions to have also shown a positive impact on motor development in early infancy.6 19

A Dutch, multicentre randomised controlled trial conducted from 2004 to 2007 showed improved motor, mental, behavioural development and mother-infant interaction at 6 months corrected age.20 Positive long-term effects in the intervention group were also identified in a follow-up study, including higher performance intellectual quotient, improved ball skills and visual-motor integration 5 years after the intervention period.21 The difference between groups seemed greater in the case of the most vulnerable infants: those with medical comorbidities such as bronchopulmonary dysplasia and families with low socioeconomic status.

Other large, multicentric, randomised controlled trials are needed to confirm the positive effects of early interventions, IBAIP in particular. Moreover, the programme needs to be evaluated in another medicosocial setting to be able to generalise the results. Indeed, disparities in hospital and post-hospital care can be important between different countries, even within the European Union. To this date, no large, randomised trial has evaluated the developmental impact of early interventions in France. If IBAIP shows significant developmental improvements in preterm infants, implementing this programme on a nationwide scale would be of great individual and public benefit. Indeed, costs linked to cognitive and motor handicaps in preterm infants could be significantly decreased for parents but also for society. This could represent a great amount, seeing as the extra cost to society in Canada for preterm infants born before 32 weeks GA on a national level is estimated at 378.9 million dollars for the first 10 years of life.22

Given the possible implications, we designed a multicentric, randomised controlled trial using cluster randomisation to evaluate IBAIP in a French setting. Our study protocol, designed to measure the impact of early intervention with IBAIP on neurodevelopment at 2 years corrected age, in infants born before 33 weeks GA, is described in the following section.

Methods and analysis

Study design

We designed a prospective, multicentred, randomised controlled trial to evaluate the effects of IBAIP on preterm infant neurodevelopment in a French setting. We will use cluster randomisation to avoid intergroup contamination. Indeed, using this procedure avoids follow-up in the control group by IBAIP trained professionals. We suppose that an IBAIP trained professional would not be able to follow infants in the control group without being influenced by their specific training, which would create confusion between the two groups. Also, most French centres do not have sufficient human resources to organise follow-up in the same centre by two different sets of professionals according to randomisation.

Due to this specific design and a necessary common training period, 8 level III/IV French neonatal intensive care units (NICUs) will be randomised to intervention or control group prior to starting the IBAIP training.

The intervention consists of monthly IBAIP sessions in a home setting by a trained, certified professional (physiotherapist or psychomotor therapist) until 6 months corrected age after hospital discharge, starting with a first session in the hospital before discharge.

Neurodevelopment will be assessed for all infants at 2 years corrected age using the fourth edition of the Bayley Scales of Infant Development (BSID IV). Study start was on 08 November 2022, and study end is planned for 08 January 2026.

Trial protocol (V.2.0, date: 12 August 2022) adheres to the Standard Protocol Items: recommendations for intervention Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines. The SPIRIT checklist can be found in the online supplemental material.

All preterm infants born between 25 weeks and 32 completed weeks GA in the participating centres with normal neurological examination at 36–41 weeks GA (performed by a senior/attending physician) are eligible for inclusion.

Written informed consent will be sought from both parents or from the infant’s legal guardian, excepting infants in legal custody.

Randomisation and blinding

Randomisation of the participating centres was performed via a randomised list elaborated by a statistician. Randomisation was performed ahead of study initiation to allow for IBAIP training. Randomisation selected four intervention NICUs and four control NICUs. One control NICU was excluded from the trial after IBAIP training initiation, due to implementation of early posthospital discharge interventions with possible associated bias. This control NICU was substituted by another similar NICU, randomly selected among three candidate NICUs.

Due to the study design with cluster randomisation, blinding was not possible for investigators, parents or IBAIP professionals. The neuropsychologists performing the primary outcome analysis (BSID IV) are independent professionals. However, due to geographical reasons, it seems illusionary to manage to maintain blinding. We prefer to suppose that the neuropsychologists probably know whether the infant they are evaluating was in the control or intervention group.

Intervention/experimental design

IBAIP training

All four centres randomised in the intervention group received training prior to study initiation.

Physiotherapists and psychomotor therapists from all four centres were trained by the creator of the IBAIP programme and NIDCAP trainer Mr Rodd Hedlund, PhD. Theoretical and practical training was dispensed in Brest with three 1-week-long sessions. The total training time was 12 months. Training content is further described in the pilot study conducted by Koldewijn et al16 The professionals all receive strictly the same training and are certified IBAIP providers, guaranteeing homogeneity across intervention centres.

IBAIP intervention

IBAIP is a postdischarge preventive intervention program devised by Dr Rodd Hedlund. It is based on the same conceptual framework as the NIDCAP, namely the synactive theory of development.24 IBAIP was developed primarily for vulnerable infants from hospital discharge until 6–8 months corrected age. Full description of the IBAIP intervention can be found in previous clinical trials.16

The programme is tailored to support the infant’s self-regulation and their developmental agenda through integrative parent-infant sessions. Parent-infant interaction is at the heart of the programme, as adequate parental responsiveness and sensitivity are key elements for the infant’s development. A positive parent-infant relationship also plays an important role in parental confidence, their well-being and helps lower anxiety.9 15

An observational tool: Infant Behavioral Assessment is used by the trained professional to analyse the infant’s behaviour in response to environmental stimuli.25 These behaviours are identified as stress response, self-regulatory response or approach response. According to the type of behaviour, the professional can help the infant attain a self-regulatory balance or move towards exploring the environment further through different levels of support or facilitation. Parents are integrated so as they learn to adequately support their infant with a level of support adjusted according to their behavioural cues.

The children in the intervention centres will be included in the intervention group between 36 weeks and 41 weeks GA unless exclusion criteria are present.

The IBAIP-trained professional will accompany the parents and infants via monthly sessions from hospital discharge until six months corrected age. The first, preliminary session takes place during the week before hospital discharge in a hospital setting. Consecutive sessions will be administered with the presence of at least one parent or legal guardian and in a home setting.

Infants in the intervention group benefit from standard care and follow-up.

Standard care in France for all infants includes at least one medical consultation per month until 6 months of age, with the possibility of referral to specialists if needed. Paramedical care (physiotherapy, speech therapy, psychomotor therapy) is at the discretion of the treating physician.

Furthermore, for most infants born before 33 weeks GA, a more intense developmental follow-up is coordinated by local healthcare organisations. These organisations are composed of medics and paramedics specifically trained to identify and accompany developmental difficulties or delays. Parents must give consent to follow-up in these organisations. Infants without obvious developmental impairment are generally evaluated clinically at 4 months, 9 months, 18 months and 2 years of age. If a delay or difficulty is detected, adequate care is organised.

Control group

Infants are also included between 36 weeks and 41 weeks GA if no exclusion criteria are present. Standard care will be dispensed as described above, according to each centre’s usual organisation.

Objectives/outcomes

Primary objective

Our primary objective is to determine if the IBAIP for preterm infants improves neurodevelopment, using the BSID IV composite cognitive score evaluated at 2 years of corrected age.

Primary outcome

Infant development will be assessed for all infants participating in the study at 2 years corrected GA using the BSID IV.26 Assessment will be performed by neuropsychologists trained to use this tool. The BSID IV composite cognitive score is one of the three main scores and will be used as our primary endpoint. We consider 10.5 points (±0,5 SD) to be a clinically significant difference between the two groups.27

Secondary objectives/outcomes

We aim to evaluate which areas of infant neurodevelopment are impacted the most by the IBAIP intervention and how parental stress is affected. To do so, we will use the following tools:

The BSID composite language and composite motor scores will be reported, along with the sub-scores that can be independently analysed: fine motor, gross motor, socio-emotional and adaptive development. The analysis of these sub-scores will help determine exactly how IBAIP impacts infant development.

Data management

Data will be recorded on an internet access file. All data will be prospectively collected and registered in the specific internet access file. Any missing data should be coded as such. Each centre will have their own access to the internet file. A guide will be provided to help use the file. Source data are handled according to Good Clinical Practice and French data protection regulations.

Protocol amendments

Any protocol amendments will be submitted to the chief investigator. Any major amendments will only be accepted after validation by the local ethics committee; any minor amendments could be implemented while awaiting the local ethics committee’s approval.

Study monitoring

Study monitoring will be performed by the Department of Clinical Research and Innovation (DRCI) of the coordinating centre, to ensure that data are complete, correctly registered and consistent with the source data. The DRCI will also check compliance with study protocol, laws and regulations. Quality control visits will be performed regularly by each centre’s research assistants.

Adverse events and safety

IBAIP is non-invasive and presents no danger for infants. Evaluation scores and questionnaires are also innocuous. Early intervention with frequent home visits could potentially reinforce parental anxiety regarding the vulnerability of their infant. This will be evaluated by the PSI at 6 months corrected age.

Parents can withdraw from the study; if so, their reasons should be collected as best possible.

The investigator can temporarily or permanently withdraw a participant from the study if necessary, in particular if serious adverse events should occur.

The trial can be prematurely interrupted if serious adverse events occur, in order to revise the study plan. Given the low-risk nature of this trial, no official data monitoring committee will be in place.

Sample size calculations

With an alpha error of 0.05 and 90% power, we calculated that 86 infants per group would be needed in the case of a classic, non-clustered, randomised trial. This number is valid for a SD of 21 points on the BSID IV composite cognitive score, with an expected difference between the 2 groups of 0.5 SD (10.5 points).27

However, in our case, the total power of the study decreases due to possible intracluster correlation. Subjects in the same cluster may have more resemblance to each other than to those in other clusters. This must be taken into account by using an intraclass correlation coefficient (ρ). This coefficient usually ranges from 0.001 to 0.05 and is typically low in NICU-based trials, not exceeding 0.01 which is the chosen value for the sample size calculation.

To adjust the total sample size for intraclass correlation, the first estimate must be multiplied by (1+(m-1) ρ), with m as the cluster size.32 Thereby, the total number of subjects needed is calculated at 108 per group, 216 in total, over the eight centres. To account for late exclusion due to abnormal neurological examination at term-age equivalent (maximum 10%), we plan to include 240 infants in this study. The participating centres should include 20–40 infants each to avoid bias.(figure 1)

Data analysis

All statistics will be performed by the statistics department of Brest University hospital using SAS software V.9.4 or later (SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina, USA). An intention to treat analysis will be conducted. No interim analyses will be conducted. Data analysts will be independent and blinded to treatment allocation.

A descriptive analysis of patients at inclusion will be performed, by randomisation group and by cluster to assess comparability.

Descriptively, continuous variables will be described by median, IQRs and range as appropriate and categorical variables by frequency and percentage.

Primary objective analysis

Composite cognitive BSID IV scores at 2 years corrected age will be compared between intervention and control groups using a mixed model allowing for intraclass correlation inherent to cluster randomisation. The null hypothesis of no effect will be tested by a Wald test.

Univariate and multivariate analysis will be performed. Multivariate analysis will be adjusted on any clinical differences between groups identified in the descriptive analysis. Possible confounding factors include: GA, parental socioprofessional category, patent ductus arteriosus, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, small for GA and breastfeeding.

We will also consider the number of NIDCAP observations prior to discharge due to NIDCAP implantation disparity between centres.

Secondary objective analysis

Scores obtained on the different questionnaires or tools other than the main BSID IV will be analysed using the same procedures as the primary objective. These analyses will be performed on an exploratory basis.

Adherence and attrition

Adherence will be evaluated at the 6 months corrected age clinical examination. Due to the nature of the study population, it is estimated that the proportion of missing primary endpoint scores should be very low. Should some scores be missing, they will be replaced by the mean score from their analysis group. A sensitivity analysis will be performed to account for variations in the allocated scores.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval was obtained before study initiation from the local ethics committee (Comité de Protection des Personnes Ouest 6; No. ID-RCB: 2019-A2790-57). The study is conducted in compliance with good clinical practice, French data protection authority regulations (Commission Nationale de l’Informatique et des Libertés) and in accordance with the ethical principles in the declaration of Helsinki (2013). The study is registered on Clinical Trials.gov (NCT04685356).

Informed consent will be collected for all infants included in the trial.

Study findings will be published in a peer-reviewed journal and presented at national and international scientific conferences. The datasets generated during the study will be available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

If the findings confirm the hypothesis, IBAIP training sessions could be organised so as to implement IBAIP on a national scale.

Interest of PD-L1 in the management of triple negative breast cancers in Ivorian patients
Authors: Aïssatou Diallo, Nguiessan Alphonse Aman, Brahima Traore, Zana Ismaël Coulibaly, Boadou Franck Arnaud Kouacou, Moro Richard Nzi, Brahima Doukoure, Mohenou Isidore Diomande
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTriple negative breast cancer (TNBC) is an aggressive subtype of breast cancer that does not express hormonal receptors and Her2. Its prevalence is high and varies from 30 to 41% in the Ivory Coast. For decades, chemotherapy has remained the only systematic treatment choice; however, the current availability of programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) immunohistochemistry provides benefits for immune checkpoint inhibitors. The purpose of the study aimed to determine the prevalence of PD-L1 and its relationship with specific prognostic features for better survival of TNBC patients in the Ivory Coast.MethodsA 10-month prospective study included 132 patients diagnosed with TNBC. The paraffin blocks were subjected to PD-L1 immunohistochemistry. The Chi-Square Test was used to evaluate the correlations between PD-L1 status and prognostic parameters.ResultsThe mean age at diagnosis was 46.6 ± 12.9 years. 62.9% of the study population were premenopausal. The predominant histological type was invasive ductal carcinoma of no special type (95.5%) at the advanced stage of grade II (59.1%) and III (27.3%). There was an abundant inflammatory stroma (67.4%). 20.5% of TNBC patients were PD-L1 positive. PD-L1 status was significantly associated with the breast imaging-reporting and data system (BIRADS) scoring (p = 0.0362) and Nottingham grade (p = 0.0049). However, there was no association between PD-L1 expression and menopausal status (p = 0.1771), sex (p = 0.3030), inflammatory stroma (p = 0.0803), tumor necrosis (p = 0.0571), and histological type (p = 0.2037).Conclusion20.5% of TNBC patients expressed PD-L1. This study highlights a significant association between PDL-1 expression and the BIRADS scoring and the Nottingham grade. The present study could help to select patients who would be likely to benefit from anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy for better clinical outcomes in the Ivory Coast.Keywords: Triple negative, Breast cancer, PD-L1, Checkpoint inhibitor, Immunotherapy

Background

Breast cancer is the most common cancer in women worldwide [1]. Breast cancer represents the most common cancer in women in the Ivory coast, with a frequency of 33,4%, followed by cervical cancer [1].

Breast cancer appears to be a complex and hormone-dependent disease characterized by its clinical, pathological, and biological heterogeneity, resulting in diverse predictive responses [2]. The development of genetic techniques has led to the understanding of breast cancer oncogenesis, and thus, breast cancer subtypes. They are identified according to the status of estrogen receptors (ER), progesterone receptors (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (Her2): luminal A, luminal B, Her2 enriched, and triple negative [2, 3].

These subtypes have prognostic and therapeutic implications [3–5]. Triple negative breast cancer (TNBC), known as basal-like, is characterized by the absence of ER, PR, and Her2 expression. TNBC accounts approximately 15–20% of all breast cancer subtypes [3].

The North American studies have reported a higher proportion of TNBC in Black American women, up to 39%, than Hispanic or White women [6]. In Subsahan Africa, the frequency of TNBC has increased, although it varies across countries, from 19.9 to 40.5% [7–10].

This subtype is highly aggressive and has the worst prognosis with a high frequency of recurrences and mortality [3, 11]. The triple negative breast carcinomas expressing PD-L1 present relevant predictive responses to treatment [12, 13]. Currently, the development of new targeted therapies, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors, such as anti-programmed cell death 1 (PD-1) or anti-programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), have considerably improved the clinical management of TNBC patients with better survival outcomes [12, 13]. The PD-L1 expression is detected through the immunohistochemistry assay [14, 15]. Initially, TNBC patients cannot benefit from targeted treatment except systematic chemotherapy associated with surgery and radiotherapy if needed in Ivory Coast because of the absence of PD-L1 or PD-1 antibodies and immune checkpoint inhibitor drugs. Currently, because of the partnership between the Ivorian Government and the Roche Laboratory, TNBC patients can receive such advanced treatment.

The overall objective of this study was to determine the prevalence of PD-L1status and its relationship with the prognostic parameters to improve the survival within triple negative breast carcinoma patients in the Ivory Coast.

Methods

Patients

That was a prospective study conducted between January 2024 and October 2024 at the Anatomic Pathology Department of the University Teaching Hospital of Cocody in Abidjan, Ivory Coast. This study included 132 patients who were histologically diagnosed with invasive breast carcinoma within 3 months and then immunohistochemically categorized into the triple negative subtype (absence of ER, RP, and Her2 expression).

The studied variables were sociodemographic (frequency, patient origin, age, and sex), clinical and radiological (site of lesion, mammography, and Breast imaging-reporting and data system (BIRADS) scoring), and pathological (type of specimen, histological type, grade of Nottingham, in situ component, tumoral necrosis, inflammatory stroma (Fig. 1), and PD-L1 status).

Immunohistochemical analysis

First, the tissue sections from the paraffin-embedded breast tissue blocks were subjected to immunohistochemical examination for the expression of hormonal receptors (ER, PR) and Her2 using the Ventana Benchmark GX automated immunostainer (Ventana Medical Systems Inc., Tucson, AZ, USA). An immunohistochemical assay was carried out to identify patients with TNBC (ER < 1%, PR < 1%, Her2 score 0 or score 1). Second, tissue sections from the triple negative subtype were subjected to immunohistochemical testing for the automated Ventana PD-L1 assay protocols. The used antibody clone was monoclonal, developed in rabbit, consisted of SP142 for PD-L1 and manufactured by Ventana Medical Systems, Inc. The tonsil tissue was used as external control. The evaluation of the PD-L1 status was based on the immune cell (IC) score. The expression of PD-L1 was considered positive if at least 1% of immune cells invading the tumoral area occupied by tumor cells were immunostained (Fig. 2) [14, 15]. The evaluation of PD-L1 expression was independently reviewed by three senior pathologists without any observed staining intensity variations. Each reviewer provided his own score of PD-L1. The final score of PD-L1 derived from the average percentage of three scores of reviewers.

(IHC, X 400) immunostained immune cells by anti-PD-L1 more than 1% (Red circle)

Statistical analysis

Data were collected using SPSS software version 21.1 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Data were reported as the means, numbers of patients and frequencies or percentages for quantitative variables, whereas data were analyzed only as frequencies for qualitative variables.

The Chi-Square Test was used to analyze the associations between the PD-L1 status and the quantitative variables (menopausal status, sex, BIRADS, inflammatory stroma, histological type, and Nottingham grade). A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Sociodemographic parameters

During the study period, 132 cases of TNBC were collected, representing 36% of all breast cancer subtypes.

The mean age of the patients was 46.6 ± 12.9 years, with extremes of 25 and 71 years. 62.9% of patients were premenopausal. The sex-ratio was 0.03 with a female predominance (Table 1).

Discussion

The present study provided an overview of the characteristics of patients with triple negative breast cancer who were subjected to PD-L1 detection for potential targeted therapy in Ivory Coast. For decades, patients have usually received systematic chemotherapy, which has the worst outcomes. Our preliminary results help to identify more patients who could be eligible for anti-PD-L1 therapy or immunotherapy, and thus, improve their survival.

In our study, the mean age of the patients was 46.5 years. This mean age is different from that of the developed countries, where breast cancer frequently appears at the advanced age or postmenopausal period [16–19]. The occurrence of triple negative breast cancer in premenopausal or young women could be due to the relatively short life expectancy in developing countries, particularly in Ivory Coast.

Invasive carcinoma of no special type (95.4%) was the most common histological type, as described in the literature data [8–10, 17, 20, 21]. Objectively, IDC-NOS was mostly diagnosed at high-grade with 86.2% of grade II associated with grade III. As a result, IDC-NOS occurs at an advanced stage with poor survival. This situation highlights the urgent need not only for intensive awareness and early diagnostic detection programs but also for the detection of PD-L1 in TNBC patients for better outcomes.

Moreover, the inflammatory stroma, made of immune cells, was abundant in 67.7% of our study population. Our result was similar to that of Cerbelli et al. [17] and Onagi et al. [22]. In fact, tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) are established prognostic markers uniquely in TNBC patients [17, 18, 23, 24]. The TILs reflect the host immune response to the tumor. As a result, breast cancer patients with high TILs have better response to chemotherapy and improved survival [18, 19, 22, 23, 25].

20.5% of our study population was positive for PD-L1. Our result was different from that reported by several studies, describing frequencies between 39% and 81% [17–19, 24]. The low proportion of PD-L1 positivity in this study could be due to the small size of the study population and preanalytical factors, such as fixation. Interestingly, several studies have demonstrated that the type of antibodies used for PD-L1 assessment, lack of a standardized cut-off value of PD-L1 staining positivity, and the heterogeneity of the triple negative subtype may explain this variability [14, 15, 26, 27].

Furthermore, our study did not find any statistically significant correlations between PD-L1 positivity and age, sex, intensity of the inflammatory stroma, histological type, and tumor necrosis, whereas PD-L1 expression was associated with BIRADS scoring and tumor grade. This association of PD-L1 status with tumor grade corroborates with the literature data since triple negative breast cancer is known as high grade tumor with increased recurrence and poor outcomes [18, 28]. The relationship between BIRADS scoring and PD-L1 expression should be carefully investigated in future studies. However, other advanced studies have revealed a relationship between PD-L1 positivity and other parameters. Indeed, Grandal et al. [12] have demonstrated an association of PD-L1 expression with high tumor burden and post neoadjuvant chemotherapy aggressive tumor features in France. Matikas et al. [26] have also observed an association between PD-L1 positivity and long overall survival in Sweden. Baek et al. [24] and Cerbelli et al. [17] have revealed an association of PD-L1 expression with high TILs in South Korea and Italy, respectively.

Despite the difference in the relationship between PD-L1 and clinicopathological features in our study and other studies, TNBC patients expressing PD-L1 present prognostic and therapeutic implications [12, 13, 17, 25, 26]. In other words, they are eligible for targeted anti-PD-L1 therapy, which is more efficient than the standard chemotherapy that the patients received before the advent of PD-L1 immunochemical detection.

Limitations of the study

The sample size of our study population was small due to the short period of prospective study (January 2024 - October 2024). The lack of some correlations between PD-L1 status and other prognostic parameters could be related to sample representativeness. The 10-month study could not allow a reasonable follow-up time to evaluate the treatments received by the patients and their treatment responses in collaboration with the medical oncologists. The prevalence of breast cancer gene 1 or 2 (BRCA1 or 2) mutations is reasonable in TNBC patients. We did not determine these mutations and their correlation with PD-L1 in TNBC because of a limited-resource environment.

Conclusions

The triple negative breast cancers frequently occurred in premenopausal women. Invasive carcinoma of no special type is the predominant histological type. TNBC is diagnosed at high grade and displays an abundant inflammatory stroma. Only 20.5% of the population study expressed PD-L1. The PDL-1 status was correlated with the BIRADS scoring and the Nottingham grade. The present study could help to select patients who would be likely to benefit from anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy for better clinical outcomes in the Ivory Coast.

Selective Synthesis of 5‑Nitroindoles through a Ag-Catalyzed Nitro Rearrangement/Cyclization Cascade of N‑(o‑Alkynylphenyl)nitramines
Authors: Itaru Nakamura, Shuto Matsuoka, Masahiro Terada
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The reactions of N-(o-alkynylphenyl)­nitramines in the presence of catalytic amounts of AgNTf2 afforded 5-nitroindoles in good to acceptable yields in a site-selective manner. This cascade reaction is initiated by selective migration of the nitro group to the para position relative to the aniline nitrogen, followed by cyclization of the resulting o-alkynylaniline. This sequence is different from typical cascade reactions of o-alkynylanilines having a migrating group on the nitrogen atom, which proceed through cyclization, followed by rearrangement of the migrating group. Mechanistic studies suggest that both migration of the nitro group and cyclization are promoted by the silver catalyst.

Functionalized indoles are among the most important heterocycles in natural products, medicines, and organic materials. In this context, the site-selective synthesis of indoles, which have various functional groups at the desired positions, is crucial in organic synthesis. The N1 to C3 positions on the pyrrole core of indoles can be easily functionalized in a site-selective manner (Scheme ). For example, position N1 of the indole ring is readily functionalized by deprotonating the highly acidic proton on the nitrogen atom. 2-Substituted indoles are typically synthesized through the cyclization of o-alkynylanilines. Generally, position C3 of the indole ring is typically functionalized through electrophilic substitution reactions, such as the Friedel–Crafts reaction, owing to its high nucleophilicity. In sharp contrast, the site-selective synthesis of indoles bearing functional groups at positions C4–C7 on the benzene core poses a challenge as the benzene core exhibits lower reactivity than the five-membered pyrrole core. − Recently, C–H activation-based functionalization reactions at positions C4 and C7 have emerged, in which a directing group is installed at positions C3 and N1, respectively. The site-selective functionalization at least reactive positions C5 and C6 remains unexplored.

Functionalized Indoles

To address this issue, we focused on catalytic cyclization/rearrangement strategies in this study. As mentioned above, cyclization reactions of o-alkynylanilines are often used to synthesize 2-substituted indoles with various kinds of π-Lewis acidic metal catalysts (Scheme a). Cyclization reactions involving the migration of functional groups, including acyl, allyl, propargyl, alkoxycarbonyl, carbamoyl, methyl, boryl, and sulfonyl groups, from the nitrogen atom to position C3 have been developed for the synthesis of 3-substituted indoles (Scheme b). Recently, we found that the Au-catalyzed reactions of o-alkynylbenzenesulfonamides proceeded via cyclization followed by site-selective migration of the sulfonyl group to position C4, yielding 4-sulfonylindoles (Scheme c). This finding encouraged us to further explore functional groups migrating to the benzene core during the cyclization process. Specifically, we focused on nitramine (N–NO2) as the functional group because the migrating nitro group can be readily transformed into desirable functional groups (Scheme d). Herein, we report that the Ag-catalyzed reactions of N-(o-alkynylphenyl)­nitramines 1 afforded the corresponding 5-nitroindoles 2 in good to acceptable yields in a site-selective manner (Scheme d).

Cyclization/Rearrangement Cascade Reactions

When the reaction of nitramine 1a was performed in the presence of 10 mol % AgNTf2 in toluene at 50 °C for 24 h, 5-nitroindole 2a was obtained in 20% yield, along with 3-nitroindole 3a in 6% yield (Table , entry 1). The use of gold complexes, such as PPh3AuNTf2, gave 3-nitroindole 3a as the major product (entry 2). PtCl2 and Pd­(OAc)2 exhibited a preference for forming 3-nitroindole 3a (entries 4 and 5, respectively). The use of AgSbF6, AgBF4, and AgOTf furnished 2a as the major product, but the chemical yields were lower than that using AgNTf2 due to the rapid formation of a silver mirror (entries 6–8, respectively). AgOTs did not promote the reaction at all (entry 9). The use of ethereal solvents such as THF and 1,4-dioxane substantially improved the mass balance and product selectivity (entries 10 and 11, respectively), whereas attempts to use ligands failed to improve the chemical yield (see the Supporting Information). Performing the reaction at higher temperatures generated desired product 2a (entries 12–14). In particular, the reaction of 1a at 100 °C with an increased amount (20 mol %) of AgNTf2 for 10 min gave 5-nitroindole 2a in good yield (entry 14). It is noteworthy that the reaction in the presence of PPh3AuNTf2 in the optimized solvent (dioxane) at the optimal reaction temperature (100 °C) afforded 3-nitroindole 3a in a low yield (entry 3). It should be also noted that the reaction of 1a using Brønsted acid Tf2NH, in place of AgNTf2, furnished a nitro-migrated but uncyclized product, N-methyl-5-nitro-2-(1-pentynyl)­aniline 4a, in moderate yield, and expected cyclized product 2a was not formed (entries 15 and 16). In addition, neither 7-nitroindole 5a nor protonated indole 6a was produced under the optimized reaction conditions.

The reaction of substrates, which have bulkier substituents, such as n-hexyl (1b), than methyl on the nitrogen atom, furnished desired products 2b in good yields (entry 1). Deprotectable groups, such as benzyl (1c) and allyl (1d), were compatible for the present reaction, whereas the reactions were accompanied by the formation of trace amounts of unidentified byproducts. Various protecting groups, such as benzyl (1e), acetyl (1f), and ethoxymethyl (EOM, 1g), were tolerated on the alkoxy group under the reaction conditions (entries 4–6, respectively). When the alkyne substituent was bulky like cyclohexyl and tert-butyl, the small methyl group was effective as a substituent on the nitrogen atom, affording the desired products in good yields (entries 7–10). The reaction of terminal alkyne 1l did not give desired product 2l; starting material 1l was partially recovered (38%, entry 11), presumably due to the unfavorable formation of silver acetylide species. Instead, product 2l was obtained from trimethylsilylalkyne 1m through desilylation albeit in a low chemical yield (entry 12). Substrates 1n–1s, which have an aryl group at the alkyne terminus, were converted into corresponding 5-nitroanilines 2n–2s, respectively, in good to acceptable yields (entries 13–18, respectively). However, substitution with either a highly electron-rich p-anisyl (1n) or a highly electron-deficient p-trifluoromethylphenyl (1r) group tends to decrease the chemical yield (entry 13 or 17, respectively). Substitution with an n-hexyl group (1p) on the nitrogen atom was more effective than that with a methyl group (1s) for a substrate bearing a phenyl group at the alkyne terminus (entry 15 vs entry 18). The reaction of 1t, which has a methyl group at the para position relative to the aniline nitrogen atom, afforded a mixture of 3-nitroindole 3t in low yield (9%) and other unidentified products (eq 1).

To understand the reaction mechanism, we performed crossover experiments (Scheme a). When a mixture of substrate 1a-15N, of which the nitrogen atom of the nitro group was labeled with 15N (11%), and unlabeled substrate 1d was subjected to the reaction conditions, 2a-15N and 2d- 15N were formed, with 15N contents of 5% and 4%, respectively. The 15N crossover levels in recovered starting materials 1a-15N and 1d were much lower than those observed in the product. Because crossover of the nitro group between products 2a-15N and 2b did not occur (see the Supporting Information), we concluded that the migration of the nitro group occurs intermolecularly. The fact that methyl-5-nitro-2-(1-pentynyl)­aniline 4a, which was obtained from the reaction of 1a in the presence of Tf2NH (Table , entry 15), gave 5-nitroindole 2a indicates that 4a is a potential intermediate for the formation of 2a and the present Ag catalyst can promote the cyclization of 4a (Scheme b). In addition, when nitramine 10 was treated with a silver catalyst, p-nitroaniline 11 was obtained along with o-nitroaniline 12, suggesting that the silver catalyst can promote migration of the nitro group in the nitramine (Scheme c). Moreover, the Ag-catalyzed reaction of 1a in the presence of 1-methyl-2-phenylindole 6s did not yield 2s, which was expected to form via intermolecular migration of a nitro group to indole 6s (Scheme d). This result suggests that indole 6 is not a reactive intermediate of the present cascade reaction.

Mechanistic Studies

From these experimental results, we propose a reaction mechanism, as illustrated in Scheme . First, the Ag catalyst coordinates to the alkyne moiety and the aniline nitrogen atom to form σ,π-complex 13. Then, ionic cleavage of the nitramine N–N bond leads to silver anilide 14 and nitronium NO2 +. Intermolecular C–N formation of the nitronium cation at the electron-rich para position of the aniline nitrogen atom affords intermediate 15. Rapid proton transfer gives rise to 4-nitrated o-alkynylaniline intermediate 16, of which the alkyne moiety π-coordinates to the Ag catalyst. The electrophilically activated CC undergoes nucleophilic attack by the aniline nitrogen atom to form 3-indolylsilver intermediate 17. Finally, protodemetallation of intermediate 17 yields 5-nitroindole 2. The reaction did not furnish 7-nitroindole derived from migration of the nitronium cation to the aniline ortho position (Table ). This is presumably due to the steric effect of the substituent on the nitrogen atom (R2), which is directed to the ortho position by π-coordination of the Ag atom to the alkynyl substituent at the other ortho position, interfering with the electrophilic substitution at the ortho position of the aniline nitrogen. Indeed, the chemical yield was improved by changing the nitrogen atom substituent from methyl to n-hexyl (Table , entry 15 vs entry 18), likely owing to the bulkiness of the n-hexyl group. The anion on the silver catalyst significantly affected the reactivity (Table , entries 1 and 6–9). Although a less coordinative anion such as SF6 – and BF4 – was expected to be more reactive, these silver salts were unstable under the reaction conditions, resulting in the rapid formation of a silver mirror. In contrast, AgOTs having a basic tosylate anion was not catalytically active in promoting the rearrangement of the nitro group due to its low Lewis acidity.

Proposed Mechanism

In conclusion, we have developed Ag-catalyzed cascade reactions for the synthesis of 5-nitroindoles. Mechanistic studies indicate that the present reaction is initiated by Ag-mediated migration of the nitro group prior to the construction of the indole ring. Because the present reaction exhibits high functional group compatibility and the migrating nitro group can be further modified, the present method is useful for the synthesis of multiply functionalized indoles.

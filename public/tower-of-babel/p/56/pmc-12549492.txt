The Safety, Tolerability, and Pharmacokinetics of ALT-BB4 (A Novel Recombinant Hyaluronidase): A Randomized, Double-Blinded, Placebo-Controlled, Phase 1 Study in Healthy Volunteers
Authors: Ji Su Lee, WonSerk Kim, Chong-Hyun Won, Yang-Won Lee, SeungHwan Lee, Heejae Won, Soon-Jae Park, Sunbae Lee, Seol-Hee Kim, Jiwon Yang, Gahee Bahn, Dong Hun Lee

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionHyaluronidase has been used as an adjuvant to facilitate subcutaneous drug delivery by degrading hyaluronic acid, a viscoelastic barrier in subcutaneous tissue. Introduction

Subcutaneous delivery of biotherapeutics has proven to be an effective, safe, and well-tolerated administration method, offering a viable alternative to intravenous administration. However, hyaluronic acid (HA), a major component of extracellular matrix, can act as a viscoelastic barrier, impeding rapid and large volume subcutaneous drug delivery, which limits drug dispersion and absorption [1–3]. HA is a polymer composed of disaccharide units of d-glucuronic acid and N-acetylglucosamine. Due to its excellent water retention ability and stabilization by intermolecular and intramolecular interactions in aqueous solutions, highly concentrated HA in subcutaneous tissue can form a meshwork structure, preventing large injected molecules from entering systemic circulation [4].

To address these challenges, hyaluronidase, an enzyme capable of degrading HA, has been used to depolymerize HA and enhance drug penetration during subcutaneous injections [1–3]. Hyaluronidase is an endoglycosidase that cleaves the glycosidic bond between N-acetylglucosamine and glucuronic acid in HA, breaking it down into monosaccharides [5]. HA fully regenerates within 24–48 h without any histological alterations or inflammatory changes [6]. Hyaluronidases are found in various species, such as bacteria, insects, fish, leeches, and mammals [7]. Human hyaluronidases are classified into six subtypes: hyaluronidase 1–4, sperm adhesion molecule 1 (SPAM 1, PH20), and hyaluronoglucosaminidase pseudogene 1 (HYALP1). Of these, PH20 is the only hyaluronidase that remains active at a neutral pH [5]. Located on the surface of sperm, PH20 degrades HA on the surface of the egg during fertilization, facilitating sperm penetration [1]. Building on this function, bovine- or ovine-derived hyaluronidase has been used to enhance the absorption and dispersion of co-injected drugs since the 1940s [8]. However, animal-derived hyaluronidase raises concerns about allergic reactions, such as anaphylaxis, and the potential spread of zoonosis such as variant Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease [3].

To overcome these challenges, recombinant human hyaluronidase PH20 (rHuPH20) was developed. Human hyaluronidase PH20 is a 509-amino acid glycoprotein anchored to the plasma membrane through a glycosyl-phosphatidylinositol (GPI) moiety. After removing the GPI-motif, rHuPH20 was constructed with extracellular domain of PH20. As it is produced through recombinant DNA technology, rHuPH20 is free from animal-derived pathogen risks and the associated high allergenicity. HYLENEX, with rHuPH20 as the active pharmaceutical ingredient, received approval from the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2005 as a tissue permeability modifier indicated for: (1) hypodermoclysis, (2) increasing the dispersion and absorption of other injected drugs, and (3) improving the resorption of radiopaque agents in subcutaneous urography. The Biologics License Application approval package for Hylenex submitted to FDA demonstrated a favorable safety profile [1]. rHuPH20 has a short half-life, and its effect in the subcutaneous space is local and transient [1, 2]. Most adverse events were mild and transient injection-site reactions, including erythema, pain, bruising, pruritus, burning, tenderness, edema, induration, irritation, paresthesia, numbness, and rash [1].

As an excipient, rHuPH20 has been used as a spreading agent in combination with monoclonal antibody therapeutics, including Herceptin, MabThera, Perjeta, and Darzalex subcutaneous administration [1, 9, 10]. In the phase 3 COUMBA study, subcutaneous administration of daratumumab demonstrated noninferiority to intravenous administration for relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma [11]. Subcutaneous administration exhibited comparable efficacy to intravenous administration in terms of overall response rate, median progression-free survival, and median overall survival. However, notable differences were observed in the safety profile and administration time, with a lower rate of infusion-related reactions (12.7% versus 34.5%) and shorter administration time (3–5 min versus 3–7 h), making the subcutaneous formulation a more favorable therapeutic option.

ALT-B4 (Alteogen Inc., Daejeon, Korea), berahyaluronidase alfa, is a novel recombinant hyaluronidase derived from human hyaluronidase PH20. It is a variant of rHuPH20, engineered by replacing the structural domain of rHuPH20 with the corresponding domain of human Hyal-1 to improve structural stability and enzymatic efficacy. Compared with rHuPH20, ALT-B4 exhibited improved thermal stability and catalytic efficiency while preserving the substrate specificity (unpublished data). ALT-BB4, a drug product with ALT-B4 as the active ingredient, is engineered for standalone use as an adjuvant to enhance tissue permeability. This first-in-human, phase 1, multicenter, double-blinded, placebo-controlled study aimed to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics (PK) of ALT-BB4 in healthy adults.

Methods

Study Design and Participants

This multicenter, double-blinded, placebo-controlled phase 1 study evaluated the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and immunogenicity of a single injection of ALT-BB4, a recombinant hyaluronidase, in healthy adults. The participants were healthy men and women aged 18 years or older, with no prior history of significant dermatologic conditions. Additional inclusion and exclusion criteria are detailed on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05232175). The random allocation sequence was generated by an independent statistician using a computer-generated randomization plan [SAS® Version 9.4 64 bit (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA)]. Allocation concealment was maintained using sequentially numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes containing treatment assignments, which were provided to each study site. After confirming eligibility, investigators assigned participants their allocation numbers and prescribed the study medication accordingly. To ensure double-blinding, ALT-BB4 and placebo were prepared as clear, colorless solution in identical containers by unblinded pharmacy staff who were not involved in participant assessment or data collection. Investigators, participants, and all other study personnel remained blinded until study completion. During administration, measures were taken to prevent participants from viewing the injection procedure or site.

The study consisted of three parts: part I, designed for drug allergy assessment; part II-A, conducted for pharmacokinetics assessment; and part II-B, designed for safety and tolerability assessment (Fig. 1). In part I, participants were to receive a single intradermal injection (0.02 mL) of ALT-BB4 (recombinant hyaluronidase 1500 IU/mL) or placebo (0.9% NaCl), with the administration randomly assigned to their right or left forearm. Subsequent monitoring for allergic reactions was carried out at 30 min (immediate) and 48 h (delayed) post-injection. Participants who showed no signs of drug allergic reactions in part I proceeded to part II-A and part II-B assessments. In part II-A, participants received a single subcutaneous injection (1 mL) of ALT-BB4 into their right or left upper arm. Blood samples were collected at specific timepoints for subsequent pharmacokinetic analysis, and the detailed protocol is shown in the PK assessments of the material methods below. Part II-A was conducted as an open-label, single-arm study. In part II-B, participants were randomly assigned in a 2:1 ratio, with two-thirds receiving a single subcutaneous injection (1 mL) of ALT-BB4 and the remaining one-third receiving a placebo. Site and systemic adverse events were monitored from 30 min until 28 days after administration. No changes to eligibility criteria or other methodological modifications were made after trial commencement. The study was conducted according to the original protocol.

Ethical Approval

This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) Good Clinical Practice guidelines. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Boards of the following institutions: Seoul National University Hospital (IRB number: H-2111-014-1268), Asan Medical Center (IRB number: 2021-1793), Konkuk University Medical Center (IRB number: KUMC 2021-10-034), and Kangbuk Samsung Hospital (IRB number: KBSMC 2021-10-049). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to study enrollment.

Sample Size Calculation

The minimum target number of subjects, 231, for part I allergy evaluation was estimated by using the empirical cases of previous studies and statistical hypotheses. The statistical hypothesis was defined as follows: H0: pT ≥ p0 versus HA: pT < p0, where pT represents the allergy incidence rate in the ALT-BB4 group, and p0 represents the allergy incidence rate recommended by the FDA. On the basis of a prior study (NCT01689363 on ClinicalTrials.gov) involving a similar agent, p0 = 0.1 and pT = 0.05 were assumed. Using the “Numeric Results for Testing One Proportion using the Exact Test” feature in PASS 15, with a one-sided significance level of 2.5% and 80% power, the required sample size was calculated to be 231 subjects.

Of the 231 subjects completing part I, 23 (10%) were designated for enrollment in part II-A, while the remaining subjects were assigned to part II-B. The limited sample size for part II-A was based on preclinical monkey data showing minimal systemic exposure even at high doses, and was sufficient to confirm the expected PK profile while minimizing unnecessary burden to healthy volunteers. No interim analyses were planned or conducted during this trial. Given this was a single-dose phase 1 study primarily focused on safety assessment, no prespecified stopping guidelines were implemented.

Outcome Measures

All outcomes were prespecified in the study protocol and analyzed as planned. No additional outcome measures were added during the study period. Primary outcomes focused on assessing the safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics of a single injection of ALT-BB4 in comparison with placebo, administered to healthy adults. In part I, the incidence rate of drug allergy reactions was evaluated from 30 min to 48 h following intradermal administration. In part II-A, pharmacokinetic parameters were calculated on the basis of the plasma concentration of ALT-BB4. In part II-B, the incidence rate of treatment emergent adverse events (TEAEs), both at injection site and systemically, was assessed from 30 min until 28 days after subcutaneous administration.

Secondary outcomes encompassed the incidence of drug allergy reactions at specific timepoints (30 min and 48 h), and the incidence of TEAEs at specific timepoints (30 min, 2 days, 14 days, and 28 days). Additionally, the study explored immunogenicity by evaluating the presence of antidrug antibodies.

Pharmacokinetic Assessments

Blood samples for plasma pharmacokinetic analysis were collected at multiple timepoints: 2 h and 1 h before the start of the infusion, and at 0, 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 min, as well as at 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, and 24 h after the initiation of infusion. The plasma concentrations of ALT-BB4 were measured using a validated sandwich immunoassay. Plasma concentration versus time data were analyzed using noncompartmental pharmacokinetic analyses with Phoenix WinNonlin (Version 8.3 Certara, NJ, USA). Key descriptive pharmacokinetic parameters for ALT-BB4 were summarized, including Cmax, AUClast, t1/2, Tmax, CL/F, and VZ/F. Concentrations of immunoreactive ALT-BB4 in plasma were determined using a validated sandwich immunoassay on the basis of electrochemiluminescence (ECL) technology and MSD platform. High-binding-capacity 96-well plates were coated with an anti-ALT-B4 monoclonal antibody (Alteogen) at a concentration of 2.0 μg/mL. ALT-BB4 in diluted plasma samples, standards, and quality control samples were captured on the plates during a 2-h incubation period. Detection was performed using a biotinylated rabbit anti-ALT-B4 polyclonal antibody (Alteogen). After a series of washing with a solution containing 0.05% polysorbate 20, a sulfo-TAG-conjugated streptavidin reagent was applied for 30 min. Read buffer was added and the ECL signal was acquired using a MESO QuickPlex SQ120 instrument (MesoScale Discovery).

Detection of Anti-ALT-BB4 Antibodies

Blood samples for antidrug antibody (ADA) detection were collected at visit 1 of part I, visit 6 of part II-A, and visit 7 of part II-B. All plasma samples were stored in a deep freezer (designated at −80 to −60 °C) until assayed. The presence of ALT-BB4-reactive antibodies was assessed using a validated ECL immunoassay. In this assay, plasma samples were added to ALT-B4-coated plates and incubated with biotin-conjugated ALT-B4. The resulting immune complexes were captured using sulfo-TAG-conjugated streptavidin and detected with an ECL read buffer on the Meso Scale Discovery instrument. Samples with signal levels below the predefined cutoff point were classified as ADA-negative and not subjected to further evaluation. Samples exhibiting signal levels above the cutoff point were classified as ADA-positive and subsequently analyzed in a confirmatory assay.

Statistical Analysis

The results are presented as counts (percentage) or averages (± standard deviation), unless stated otherwise. To compare ALT-BB4 and placebo groups, McNemar’s test was used for paired comparisons in part I, while the χ2 or Fisher’s exact test for numerical variables and paired t test or Wilcoxon signed-rank sum test for continuous variables were used in part II. For statistically significant binary outcomes in part II, both absolute risk differences with 95% confidence intervals and relative risks were calculated. No subgroup analyses were prespecified in the protocol or performed during the study. p-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All statistical tests were performed using SAS® Version 9.4.

Results

Study Population Characteristics

Between 18 January and 24 August 2022 (study period: 7 months), a total of 307 participants were screened, with 244 participants enrolled in part I and 237 participants in part II of the study, respectively (Fig. 2). Most participants were female (60.7%), with a mean age of 34.3 years (range 19–67 years), and a mean body mass index of 23.5 kg/m2 (range 16.5–40.6 kg/m) (Supplementary Table 1).

Drug Allergy Reactions

Among the 244 participants who received either ALT-BB4 or a placebo in their right or left forearm, no immediate allergic reactions were observed within the first 30 min following administration (Table 1). One subject [0.4%; 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.01–2.26] exhibited a delayed allergic reaction (urticaria) after ALT-BB4 administration (Table 1). The urticaria was mild and resolved spontaneously. There was no statistically significant difference in allergic reactions between ALT-BB4 and placebo groups (p = 0.317).

Safety and Tolerability

TEAEs following a single subcutaneous injection of ALT-BB4 are summarized in Table 2. The subcutaneous injection of ALT-BB4 was generally well tolerated. Injection site TEAEs were more frequently observed in the ALT-BB4 group compared with the placebo group (16.90% versus 0%; absolute risk difference 16.90%, 95% CI 10.74–23.07%; p < 0.0001). Injection site erythema was most common (16.9%), followed by injection site telangiectasia (4.2%), bruise (0.7%), and urticaria (0.7%). All injection site TEAEs resolved spontaneously without any treatment intervention. Systemic TEAEs were less frequent in the ALT-BB4 group compared with the placebo group (0.7% versus 5.56%; absolute risk difference −4.85%, 95% CI −10.32 to 0.62%; relative risk 0.13; p = 0.045). No serious adverse events were reported throughout the study. Among the 244 participants who received intradermal injections and the 165 participants who received subcutaneous injections in the immunogenicity test, all tested negative for antidrug antibodies.

Pharmacokinetics

Of the 23 participants who underwent pharmacokinetic assessment, 21 patients exhibited plasma concentrations of ALT-BB4 below the detectable limit (0.313 μg/L) at all timepoints. The remaining two participants showed detectable concentrations; however, these were similar before and after administration, suggesting no significant systemic exposure to ALT-BB4.

Discussion

Hyaluronidase has served as a key “spreading factor” that enhances the subcutaneous drug delivery [1–3]. Additionally, it has been used for a variety of medical applications, including the treatment of hyaluronic acid filler complications, scleroderma [12], wounds [13], scars [14], extravasation injuries [15], hematomas [16], muscle stiffness [17], carpal tunnel syndrome [18], and adhesive capsulitis [19]. Beyond these, hyaluronidase is utilized in nerve blocks [20], preventing post-surgical edema [21] and reducing perineal trauma during spontaneous delivery [22].

ALT-B4, the active ingredient of ALT-BB4, is a novel recombinant hyaluronidase derived from human hyaluronidase PH20. This product demonstrates enhanced heat stability and improved protein stability, while preserving enzyme activity levels similar to the previously utilized human-derived hyaluronidase (unpublished data). This study represents the first phase 1 clinical trial to evaluate the safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics of intradermal and subcutaneous injections of ALT-BB4.

The incidence of drug allergy reactions following intradermal injection of ALT-BB4 was notably low (0.4%), with no significant difference compared with placebo. The injection site TEAEs following subcutaneous injection were more frequently observed in the ALT-BB4 group in comparison with the placebo group (16.9% versus 0%; p < 0.001). These findings are consistent with the previous reports on local adverse events of the prior hyaluronidase use, such as erythema and pain [1]. Notably, all injection site TEAEs were mild and resolved spontaneously without any treatment. Systemic TEAEs were less frequent in the ALT-BB4 group than in the placebo group (0.7% versus 5.6%; p = 0.045). However, this is likely a statistical finding attributable to chance rather than a biological effect of the investigational product. This interpretation is supported by the very low number of total events, the low causality of all reported TEAEs, and the known variability of adverse event rates in the placebo arms of phase 1 trials [23]. Importantly, the absence of antidrug antibodies in all tested cases underscores the low immunogenicity of ALT-BB4. Pharmacokinetic analysis revealed that ALT-BB4 is hardly absorbed systemically and is rapidly eliminated from the body after exposure. Overall, these findings demonstrate that both intradermal and subcutaneous injections of ALT-BB4 are well tolerated and safe, with a favorable safety profile. A limitation of this study is its exclusive focus on healthy individuals, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to broader patient populations. However, it is noteworthy that the effects of previously used rHuPH20 are well documented as local and transient, and it has been safely employed as an adjuvant in various anticancer and immunodeficiency therapies without significant safety concerns [1, 9, 10]. Furthermore, ALT-BB4 exhibits superior protein stability compared with earlier rHuPH20 formulations, suggesting that its safety profile may remain consistent across a wider range of therapeutic applications. Future investigations with long-term follow-up are needed to confirm these findings in diverse patient populations and clinical contexts.

Conclusions

Both intradermal and subcutaneous injections of ALT-BB4 are well tolerated and safe. This novel recombinant hyaluronidase represents a promising alternative to traditional animal-derived hyaluronidases, with reduced immunogenicity and enhanced safety. Recently, ALT-BB4 was approved on 5 July 2024 by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) of Korea as a tissue permeability modifier to enhance the dispersion and absorption of other injected drugs and topical anesthetics through subcutaneous or intradermal injection. Further research is warranted to evaluate the efficacy and safety of ALT-BB4 in broader clinical applications.

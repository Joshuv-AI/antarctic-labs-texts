AI-optimized BPNN model for port safety risk prediction and management
Authors: Fangxin Chen, Jian Tan, Le Cheng, Chunlei Si
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the field of international port safety management, the traditional Backpropagation Neural Network (BPNN) model is confronted with bottlenecks including limited data processing capability and low optimization efficiency. This study proposes an intelligent prediction framework for port safety risks that integrates the Bald Eagle Search (BES) algorithm, Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), and Fuzzy Logic System (FLS), aiming to improve the scientific nature of risk early warning and management efficiency. The framework employs CNN for feature extraction, uses the BES algorithm to conduct global parameter optimization for BPNN, and introduces FLS to handle uncertainty and fuzzy information. The results show that the proposed ensemble model achieves outstanding performance in the three-classification task of port safety risks, with the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) reduced to 0.012, the accuracy improved to 98.5%, and the Macro-F1 score reaching 0.982. Furthermore, the model inference latency is only 12.5 milliseconds, which fully demonstrates its application potential in real-time monitoring scenarios. Compared with traditional optimization algorithms, the proposed model has advantages in computational performance, and provides scientific decision support for port administrators by lowering the false alarm rate and enhancing emergency response speed. This study opens up a new approach for the practical application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the port safety domain, and carries important practical value for promoting the intelligent and precise transformation of port safety management.

Introduction

Research background and motivations

Under the background of the acceleration of globalization, as the key node of cargo transportation and logistics, the importance of international ports’ safety management has become increasingly prominent1,2. With the increase of ship scale, the diversification of cargo types and the surge of transportation flow, the safety risks faced by ports are becoming more and more complicated. In this environment, the traditional safety management methods often seem inadequate, and it is difficult to monitor and predict potential risks in real time. These traditional methods not only rely on manual experience and intuitive judgment, but also face the problems of insufficient data processing ability and slow response speed, which leads to low efficiency of safety management in dealing with emergencies3–5. Therefore, it is an urgent task to seek more intelligent and efficient safety management solutions.

In recent years, the development of artificial intelligence (AI) technology provides new opportunities for port safety management. Recent studies have also emphasized that AI-driven decision-support systems should balance predictive efficiency, interpretability, and governance reliability in high-stakes management scenarios6. Especially the rapid progress in deep learning and neural network technologies has made data-driven decision support systems possible7. As a classic artificial neural network model, Backpropagation Neural Network (BPNN) is widely used in various forecasting tasks because of its good performance in nonlinear function fitting and pattern recognition8,9. However, when dealing with large-scale data, the traditional BPNN often faces the problems of difficult parameter optimization and slow convergence. These problems seriously restrict its application effect in port safety management10,11. Therefore, how to optimize the BPNN model and improve its performance in safety risk prediction has become an important motivation of this study.

In the complex port operation environment, traditional safety management methods often show hysteresis and are difficult to realize real-time monitoring and dynamic early warning of potential risks. These traditional approaches rely excessively on manual experience and intuitive judgment, and suffer from bottlenecks such as insufficient data processing capability and slow response speed, which directly lead to low management efficiency when ports deal with sudden emergencies. Therefore, there is an urgent need to develop a more intelligent and efficient port safety management scheme. This study holds that the research focus of safety prediction models should stay on improving algorithm accuracy, and pay attention to how to optimize safety management procedures through high-precision early warning information and enhance the scientific nature of safety early warning decisions to generate significant safety benefits in actual operations.

Research objectives

The main objective of this study is to improve the accuracy and intelligence of port safety risk prediction, thereby strengthening the core effectiveness of international port safety management. Faced with an increasingly complex and dynamic risk environment, traditional experience-based management can hardly meet the needs of modern ports, and there is an urgent need to establish a reliable prediction model that supports proactive decision-making. To this end, this study proposes an improved BPNN model that integrates the Bald Eagle Search (BES) algorithm, deep learning techniques, and the Fuzzy Logic System (FLS). Metaheuristic-enhanced fuzzy optimization methods have recently demonstrated strong adaptability and robustness in complex dynamic systems with non-linear operational characteristics12. The model aims to overcome the convergence bottlenecks of traditional algorithms when processing high-dimensional port data, and provide more stable technical support for key hubs in the global supply chain.

Different from previous studies that only pursue algorithmic accuracy indicators, this study focuses more on optimizing the safety management decision-making process through intelligent early warning, so as to effectively improve management efficiency. By introducing FLS to process uncertain information in the operating environment, the model can transform complex algorithm outputs into decision suggestions more consistent with management logic, and effectively shorten the response time of managers to potential safety hazards. Recent studies have shown that fuzzy trust management and dynamic behavior monitoring mechanisms can effectively improve uncertainty handling and adaptive decision-making performance in intelligent network environments13. In addition, this study verifies the potential of the system in improving the scientific NATUREof early warning and generating practical safety benefits through empirical analysis. The study aims to provide port managers with a closed-loop tool from risk identification to decision implementation, and promote the transformation of port safety management towards precision and efficiency.

Literature review

In the field of port safety management in recent years, scholars have conducted extensive research on safety risk prediction, data processing and optimization algorithms. Kim et al. (2021) proposed a port safety risk assessment model based on machine learning, and used support vector machine (SVM) and random forest algorithm (RFA) to predict port safety risks14. Their research showed that these algorithms were excellent in dealing with complex data, but they faced limitations in dealing with dynamic changing environments. At the same time, Sopelsa Neto et al. (2022) built a model based on long and short-term memory (LSTM) networks by deep learning method to capture the potential patterns in time series data. Although the accuracy of the model had improved, it still lacked effective treatment of uncertain factors15. Another important research was conducted by Erdem et al. (2024), who combined fuzzy logic and decision tree method to discuss the decision support system in port safety management16. The innovation of their research lied in introducing fuzzy logic to deal with the uncertainty of data, but its applicability in complex scenes needed further verification. Wang et al. (2023) studied how to use AI algorithm to optimize enterprise resources to achieve the goal of sustainable development17. Liu et al. (2022) focused on ensemble learning, and put forward a port safety prediction system based on multi-model fusion. Although the prediction accuracy was improved, the model complexity was high and the real-time performance was insufficient18.

In the aspect of algorithm optimization, Zhang et al. (2020) studied the application of particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithm in the parameter adjustment of neural network. Although good results had been achieved, there were still problems in the stability and convergence speed of this method when dealing with large-scale data19. Similarly, Qiang et al. (2022) discussed the application of genetic algorithm (GA) in safety risk assessment. Although GA provided a new perspective, it consumed a lot of computing resources and limited its application in real-time systems20.

Summarizing the previous research, many scholars have made important contributions in the field of port safety management and put forward a variety of forecasting models and optimization algorithms. However, there are generally problems such as insufficient adaptability of models, weak ability to deal with uncertainties and poor real-time performance. In addition, the existing research mostly focuses on the application of a single algorithm, lacking attempts to combine multiple intelligent algorithms to form a comprehensive solution. In view of these shortcomings, this study innovatively combines BES algorithm, deep learning and FLS to propose an optimized BPNN model, aiming at improving the accuracy and efficiency of port safety risk prediction and providing new theoretical and practical support for port safety management. This study enriches the literature in the field of port safety management, and provides new ideas and methods for subsequent research.

Research methodology

Construction of optimized BPNN model

Although BPNN is a classic neural network structure, it is still widely applicable to nonlinear mapping, classification, and continuous variable prediction tasks. Compared with more complex deep architectures such as LSTM or Transformer, BPNN has advantages in interpretability, computational efficiency, and parameter controllability. It is especially suitable for port safety data with limited samples, strong nonlinearity, and fuzzy uncertainty. In addition, its simple topology structure facilitates efficient integration with BES algorithms for global optimization and FLS for uncertainty handling. Therefore, BPNN is chosen as the baseline model that needs to be improved in this study to strike a balance between computational simplicity and predictive performance.

To further improve the comprehensive prediction performance of the model, this study constructs a collaborative integrated computing pipeline with the following execution sequence. First, the CNN module is used to extract multi-dimensional features from raw port safety data, and the nonlinear correlations among features are captured through convolution operations to provide robust input vectors for subsequent prediction. Second, the BES algorithm is introduced to perform global automatic optimization on the hidden layer structure and learning rate of BPNN, which avoids the randomness of manual parameter tuning and ensures that the model can converge quickly to the global optimal solution. Finally, the FLS module is integrated to conduct fuzzification processing and logical correction on the output of BPNN to deal with the uncertain information caused by environmental interference in port operations. Compared with deep architectures with large parameter spaces such as LSTM or Transformer, this integrated mode of feature extraction, global optimization and logical correction has significant adaptive advantages in dealing with the small and medium-sized sample sets unique to ports. It can achieve high-precision prediction with limited computing resources, and ensures the interpretability of each management decision link through modular decoupling design, thus providing more transparent support for subsequent safety management decisions. CNN is good at processing high-dimensional data, especially in feature extraction. In this study, CNN is used to extract important features from port safety management data. The model structure is shown in Fig. 3, including multiple convolution layers, pooling layers and fully connected layers.

In Fig. 3, for the 12 port safety feature data collected in this study, the model converts them into a one-dimensional feature vector as the input to the CNN. The specific configuration of the CNN architecture is as follows: it includes two one-dimensional convolutional layers with kernel sizes of 3 and 2, and the number of filters set to 32 and 64 respectively. Integration of FLS

In port safety management, dealing with uncertainty and fuzzy information is the key to improve decision support ability. FLS can effectively deal with those fuzzy data that are difficult to express with precise values by introducing fuzzy set theory. This study combines the FLS with the optimized BPNN to improve the accuracy and reliability of port safety risk prediction. The core of FLS is fuzzy set and its operation. Fuzzy set is an extension of traditional set, which allows the membership degree of elements to change from 0 to 1, not just “belonging” or “not belonging”. This feature enables fuzzy logic to simulate human decision-making process more naturally.

In this study, an integrated framework of FLS and optimized BPNN is designed, as shown in Fig. 4.

Fig. 4Integrated framework of FLS and BPNN.

Integrated framework of FLS and BPNN.

The design of fuzzy rules directly determines the reliability of the decision support system. Combined with domain experience of port experts and practical operation requirements, this study formulates 5 core fuzzy rules to cover the most common safety risk scenarios:

Rule 1: If the ship dynamics are abnormal and environmental conditions are severe, the safety risk is “High”.

Rule 2: If the ship dynamics are normal and cargo information is compliant, the safety risk is “Low”.

Rule 3: If the ship dynamics trigger an early warning or environmental conditions are at a medium level, the safety risk is “Medium”.

Rule 4: If the historical accident frequency is high and the on-duty rate of personnel is insufficient, the safety risk is “High”.

Rule 5: For other conventional combinations, the risk level remains “Medium or Low”.

These rules are quantified by the detailed parameter settings in Table 2 below and invoked uniformly in the experiments to ensure the consistency of the model when processing fuzzy boundary information.

The core of fuzzy logic lies in membership function and fuzzy operation. In this study, the triangular membership function is adopted, and its expression is shown in Eq. 19:19\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu (x)=\left\{ {\begin{array}{*{20}{c}} {0,x<a} \\ {\frac{{x - a}}{{b - a}},a \leqslant x<b} \\ {\frac{{c - x}}{{c - b}},b \leqslant x<c} \\ {0,x \geqslant c} \end{array}} \right.$$\end{document}

a, b and c are parameters that define the shape of the membership function. By fuzzifying the input data, multiple fuzzy sets can be defined for each input variable, thus forming rich fuzzy information.

To quantify the influence of each input indicator (such as ship arrival rate, meteorological index, etc.) on port safety risk prediction results, this study adopts an analysis method based on permutation importance. This method measures the importance of a given feature by randomly shuffling its values in the test set and observing the degradation of model prediction performance (such as RMSE or accuracy): the more significant the performance degradation, the greater the contribution of the feature to risk identification. Compared with traditional weight analysis, this method does not depend on the internal parameter distribution of the model and can more objectively reflect the relationship between nonlinear features and prediction targets, thus providing a quantitative basis for managers to identify core risk sources.

Experimental design and performance evaluation

Datasets collection and experimental environment

The collection of the dataset and the construction of the experimental environment are key steps in ensuring the reliability and effectiveness of the research results. To effectively evaluate the performance of the improved BPNN and FLS integrated model in predicting port safety risks, this study collected multi-source data covering multiple safety management factors from five international ports, with a time span from 2020 to 2024. The dataset used contains a total of 1260 records, each of which includes 12 input features. It includes ship arrival rate, cargo throughput, berth occupancy rate, meteorological condition index, wind speed, visibility, water temperature, tide level, number of dangerous goods, number of personnel on duty, average waiting time, and recent safety events. The prediction target is to output the label as the port safety risk level. Based on the frequency and severity of historical accidents, the risk is divided into three categories: low risk, medium risk, and high risk.

In terms of feature selection, this study combines correlation analysis with the experience of port safety management experts to ensure that each selected feature variable has a practical contribution to risk prediction. All data undergoes preprocessing before modeling, including outlier removal and missing value filling, to ensure data integrity and consistency. Meanwhile, feature engineering is performed on the data to extract key features and provide a reliable foundation for model training. For dataset partitioning, to avoid data leakage caused by port-specific patterns or temporal dependence, this study abandons simple global random partitioning and adopts a partitioning strategy based on time series and port grouping. Specifically, data from 2020 to 2023 are used as the training and validation sets, while the latest data in 2024 are divided into the independent test set. This strategy ensures that the model learns on past data and is verified in future simulation scenarios. Meanwhile, it is strictly guaranteed that samples of specific ports within the same time period do not appear in both the training and test sets during partitioning to more objectively evaluate the generalization ability of the model for unknown port environments.

To ensure the reproducibility of the research, the specific computing environment configuration for this experiment is as follows: the hardware platform is equipped with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 GPU (24GB video memory), an Intel Core i9-12900 K CPU, and 64GB DDR5 memory. The software environment is based on the Ubuntu 20.04 operating system, using Python 3.8 as the programming language. The core deep learning frameworks are TensorFlow 2.6.0 and Keras 2.6.0, while data processing and statistical analysis are implemented via Pandas 1.3 and Scikit-learn 1.0.

Parameters setting

In this study, parameter setting is important to ensure that the optimized integrated model of BPNN and FLS can effectively perform port safety risk prediction. To improve the training effectiveness and prediction accuracy of the model, this study optimizes the key network parameters. During the optimization process, reasonable search ranges are set for key parameters such as the number of hidden layer neurons, learning rate, and batch size. The number of hidden layer neurons in the first layer is 32–128, and in the second layer, it is 16–64, to cover the possibilities of networks ranging from low complexity to medium high complexity. The learning rate is set between 0.0001 and 0.01 to balance training convergence speed and stability. The batch size is set between 16 and 64 to achieve a balance between training efficiency and stability of parameter updates.

To determine the optimal parameter combination of the model, this study uses the BES algorithm to perform iterative search within the preset optimization range. The algorithm dynamically adjusts network parameters by simulating the hunting behavior of bald eagles, and evaluates the fitness value according to the performance metrics of the training set in each iteration, that is, the fitness function determined by weighting and balancing the minimization of RMSE and the maximization of prediction accuracy. In the specific implementation, this study sets the initial population size to 50 and the maximum number of iterations to 100. The termination criteria are set as follows: the optimization stops when the fitness value no longer improves after 15 consecutive iterations, or when the maximum number of iterations is reached. In addition, to ensure the reproducibility and stability of the experiment, the model adopts a boundary handling strategy to prevent parameters from overflowing the search space, and a fixed random seed (Random Seed: 42) is set.

After BES optimization, the key parameter settings of the final integrated model are shown in Table 2. The optimized BPNN contains two hidden layers with 64 and 32 neurons respectively, adopts the ReLU activation function and the Adam optimizer, with a learning rate of 0.001, batch size of 32, and training epoch of 200. Meanwhile, the FLS defines 5 fuzzy rules and uses triangular membership functions. Experiments show that, compared with the initial manual parameter tuning, the parameter combination optimized by BES significantly improves the convergence speed and robustness of model training, exhibits stronger competitiveness in prediction accuracy, and thus effectively enhances the reliability and practical value of the port safety risk prediction system.

Discussion

The optimized BPNN combined with FLS performs well in port safety risk prediction, which verifies its effectiveness. This result echoes previous studies. For example, Liu et al. (2022) pointed out that the traditional BPNN often affected the prediction performance because of over-fitting in complex environment32. Through the introduction of BES algorithm, the RMSE was significantly reduced to 0.016, and the accuracy was improved to 97.5%, which showed the advantages of BES algorithm in capturing complex data patterns. Compared with GA and PSO, Ang et al. (2022) showed the application of PSO in optimizing neural network33. Although these methods can improve the model performance, the training time was usually long, which affected the practicality. The proposed integrated model still maintains superior prediction ability when the training time is 210 s, which fully proves the balance between efficiency and accuracy. In addition, the combination of FLS further enhances the ability to deal with uncertain information. Kerimkhulle et al. (2023) emphasized the importance of fuzzy logic34. The prediction accuracy of the proposed model reaches 98.2% in certain cases, which indicates that fuzzy rules significantly improve the reliability. These research results provide a new idea for port safety management. Although the training time is long, its efficient prediction ability makes it have a broad application prospect.

The practical value of this study lies in transforming high-precision AI algorithms into operable benefits for port safety management. Different from previous studies that only focus on algorithmic accuracy, the proposed model integrates the FLS system to convert “black-box” neural network outputs into risk early warning suggestions consistent with expert logic. In practical management scenarios, this improvement represents a substantial leap in management efficiency: first, the high recall rate reduces the missed detection rate of potential hazards, thereby decreasing port downtime losses caused by safety accidents; millisecond-level inference supports the dynamic allocation of safety resources, enabling port managers to shift from “passive response” to “active prevention”. In addition, quantitative feature contribution analysis allows decision-makers to formulate safety regulations more scientifically, such as strengthening monitoring precisely during severe weather or peak ship traffic periods. Thus, the integrated framework is a technical tool, and provides scientific proposed support for international ports to achieve intelligent and precise safety management decision-making.

Conclusion

Research contribution

This study significantly improves the accuracy of risk prediction and management decision-making capability in international port safety through the deep integration of CNN, BES algorithm, improved BPNN, and FLS. Methodologically, this study realizes automatic feature extraction and global hyperparameter optimization for multi-source port safety data, and elevates the final prediction accuracy to 98.5% with RMSE of 0.012 and Macro-F1 score of 0.982, which effectively validates the robustness of the model in handling imbalanced risk classification tasks. From the practical value of safety management, the framework demonstrates outstanding performance in real-time monitoring scenarios with an extremely low inference delay of 12.5 milliseconds, helping managers greatly shorten the response time to potential hazards and effectively reduce the waste of safety resources caused by false alarms. In addition, by introducing FLS to process uncertain information in the operating environment, this study successfully converts complex nonlinear algorithm outputs into early warning evidence that is more consistent with expert logic and highly interpretable. It achieves a shift from “pure algorithmic indicator improvement” to “practical management benefit enhancement”, providing critical technical support and scientific reference for modern ports to build an intelligent and precise safety management closed loop.

Future works and research limitations

Although some achievements have been made in this study, there are still some shortcomings. For example, the training time of the model is relatively long, especially the training time of the integrated model is 210 s, which may limit its application in real-time prediction. Therefore, future research can explore more efficient algorithm or model architecture to further shorten the training time. In addition, the diversity and coverage of datasets need to be improved, and more safety data of different types of ports will help to improve the generalization ability of the model. Future research can focus on improving the real-time and adaptability of the model, and provide more comprehensive and in-depth solutions for port safety management by combining more advanced machine learning technologies. Future research will further quantitatively evaluate the long-term economic and social benefits of the system in reducing port economic losses and casualties through field deployment data.

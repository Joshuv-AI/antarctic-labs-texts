Evaluating supply chain management of cold storage for preventive child healthcare programmes: A comparative study from western India
Authors: Lokesh Sharma, Dharmesh Domadiya, J.K. Tandon, Snehil Kumar Singh, Sabin Syed, Nadeem Javed, Krupal Joshi, Krishna Mahendrabhai Jasani, Ghanshyam Sethy, Ranjit Dhiman, Nayan Jani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background & objectivesImmunisation is a cornerstone of public health, significantly reducing vaccine-preventable disease (VPD) mortality. The World Health Organization-United Nations Children’s Fund (WHO-UNICEF) effective vaccine management (EVM) initiative provides tools to enrich vaccine supply chains. This study evaluated the effectiveness of cold storage management in western part of India, using EVM criteria, comparing performance across districts with varying full immunisation coverage (FIC).MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted in six districts (3 high-FIC and 3 low-FIC). Data were collected from 90 service points (SPs) and six district vaccine stores (DVSs) using the WHO EVM tool and structured questionnaire with 204 stakeholders. Secondary data were obtained from vaccine management records and analysed to assess the performance of the vaccine supply chain. EVM criteria scores (e.g., temperature control, stock management) and category scores (e.g., infrastructure, maintenance) were compared to the global benchmark of 80 per cent. Descriptive statistics were used to summarise the data, while the Shapiro-Wilk test assessed normality. Depending on the distribution, independent samples t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests were applied to compare EVM scores across relevant groups.ResultsGujarat achieved strong scores across most EVM criteria, i.e., temperature control (99%), storage capacity (98%), maintenance (99%), and vaccine management (96%). Management Information System (MIS) and vehicle infrastructure had the lowest scores, with MIS scoring 79 per cent and vehicle infrastructure scoring 80 per cent, slightly below the WHO-recommended benchmark. High- and low-FIC districts showed no statistically significant differences in overall performance, as measured by the EVM composite score (mean EVM score difference: 1.75 percentage points, P>0.05). Category analysis highlighted high scores in buildings (95%), capacity (97%), and training (99%). Compared to earlier assessments, Gujarat demonstrated significant improvements in supply chain efficiency.Interpretation & conclusionsGujarat’s vaccine supply chain has demonstrated substantial improvements, meeting or exceeding global benchmarks in most areas, though targeted improvements in MIS and vehicle infrastructure are necessary. The findings emphasise the importance of robust vaccine management systems for public health success.

Immunisation remains one of the most cost-effective strategies in public health, offering strong and direct protection against illness and death. Each year, approximately 1.5 million children lose their lives to vaccine-preventable diseases (VPDs)1. This burden is disproportionately high in developing nations, which continue to exhibit elevated child mortality rates2,3. India set a goal to achieve 90 per cent immunisation coverage by the year 2020. Although the under-five mortality rate has significantly decreased from nearly 233 to around 63 deaths per 1,000 live births over the past 50 years challenges remain4. To address these, the World Health Organization (WHO) and the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) introduced the effective vaccine management (EVM) initiative in 2010. This initiative serves as a comprehensive tool for evaluating the vaccine supply chain systems within immunisation programmes in low- and lower-middle-income countries5. EVM equips countries with the necessary tools and guidance to track, evaluate, and enhance the performance of their vaccine supply chains6. EVM aims to increase the efficacy of vaccine distribution. A well-monitored and continuously improved vaccine supply chain is essential for enhancing immunisation coverage and reducing vaccine-preventable diseases globally. So, EVM helps the country make the immunisation supply chain (ISC) stronger. EVM sets high standards for performance to make sure that vaccines are reliable, of good quality, and are available when and where they are required. The EVM process benchmarks supply chain performance against best practices in nine areas of vaccine management including vaccine arrival procedures, temperature management, storage capacity, infrastructure, maintenance, stock management, distribution, supportive supervision, and information systems at each level of the health system7. Only a few countries have been able to attain average scores exceeding the recommended threshold of 80 per cent8. The EVM tool is used to assess the ideal configuration of buildings, warehouses, trucks, cold chain equipment, repairs and maintenance, personnel, training, and management systems9. The EVM evaluation instrument comprises approximately 356 core questions, many of which are further subdivided into sub-questions or indicators to allow for a more detailed and granular assessment of specific aspects of vaccine supply chain performance. Each of the nine global criteria that EVM considers is checked against data from the most recent 12 months. The EVM package was developed to serve as both a monitoring tool for the steady improvement of individual facilities and an evaluation instrument for a complete evaluation of the supply chain’s advantages and disadvantages.

The aim of this study was to evaluate the effectiveness and efficiency of supply chain management in public cold storage facilities across government health centres in Gujarat, utilising the globally recognised EVM criteria. The study sought to assess the performance of the immunisation supply chain at both the district and service point levels, while also examining variations in supply chain efficiency between districts with differing levels of full immunisation coverage (FIC). To achieve this, the study had three key objectives: (i) to assess the effectiveness of supply chain management in public cold storage facilities using EVM criteria; (ii) to evaluate the performance of the immunisation supply chain across district vaccine stores and service points; and (iii) to compare the efficiency of the vaccination supply chain between high and low FIC districts in Gujarat.

Materials & Methods

This cross-sectional study was undertaken by the School of Business and Management, Jaipur National University, Jaipur, Rajasthan, India from April 2022 to September 2022 after obtaining permission from the Office of Commissioner of Health, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, Government of Gujarat, India. Informed consent was obtained from all stakeholders prior to initiating data collection at the selected sites.

Study design and sample size

The study uses a cross-sectional design in selected Gujarat districts, India to assess immunisation infrastructure and cold chain management in government health facilities. For the EVM assessment the study sample size was determined using the World Health Organization’s EVM site selection tool (version 1.0.9).

To ensure representativeness; based on full immunisation coverage (FIC) data as per NFHS-4, top three high performing districts Navsari, Tapi, and Jamnagar whose FICs were 78.5 per cent, 72.9 per cent and 71.4 per cent, respectively; and bottom three low performing districts Dahod, Patan, and Panch Mahal with 33 per cent, 30.7 per cent and 30.2 per cent FICs were selected from Gujarat State. From each of the six district vaccine stores (DVS), 15 service points were randomly selected, resulting in a total of 15 × 6 = 90 service points. Data were thus collected from 90 service points and six DVS (Fig. 1). The study also examined the infrastructure for vaccine and immunisation supply management in the identified government facilities, involving healthcare staff responsible for cold chain management. A total of 204 stakeholders were interviewed. In the selected DVS, stakeholders included one reproductive and child health officer (RCHO), one vaccine cold chain storage manager, one cold chain technician, and one data entry operator per district, totalling 24 respondents across six DVSs. At the service point (SP) level, each of the 90 assessed facilities included one cold chain technician and one data entry operator, amounting to 180 stakeholders. Thus, 204 stakeholders were interviewed, corresponding to the specific roles and responsibilities within the vaccine cold chain system. The selection ensured representation from key functional roles at each level

Data collection

Version 1.0.9 of the WHO EVM tool was utilised to collect primary data. After completing the registration process and creating a profile on the EVM website (https://extranet.who.int/evm), the researcher downloaded the tool using the URL provided in the email. WHO EVM tool version 1.0.9. questionnaire were administered to the vaccine and cold chain handlers (CCHs) in the form of mobile based digital application named Kobo collect version: 1.4.3 (1039), to streamline responses on resource allocation, training, and knowledge of vaccine handlers.

For the secondary data analysis, records and reports related to cold chain management and vaccines were collected from DVSs and SPs for April 2021 to March 2022. These were analysed using the EVM guidelines, to cover this timeframe.

Data analysis

Data from the questionnaire were compiled in Microsoft Excel (2020) and analysed in SPSS Version 17 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, USA). Descriptive statistics, including mean, standard deviation, and 95 per cent confidence intervals (CI), were used to compare EVM scores across categories in Gujarat and against the WHO global benchmark of 80 per cent. Overall performance was assessed using the composite EVM score, which aggregates performance across key domains. Each domain was scored as a percentage, with the composite score reflecting the average performance across all domains. The Shapiro-Wilk test assessed normality, and an unpaired t-test or Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare the EVM scores between DVSs and SPs. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Variables

EVM Assessment was based on 09 criteria and 07 Categories. Below are the nine EVM requirements: E1: the process of receiving vaccines; E2: temperature during storage and transport; E3: storage capacity; E4: structures, cold chain equipment, and vehicles; E5: maintenance and repair; E6: stock management; E7: vaccine distribution; E8: vaccine management; and E9: management information system (MIS) and supporting functions. The seven EVM categories are: buildings, capacity, equipment, management, repair/maintenance, training, and vehicles. Once the necessary data were supplied, the EVM tool creates performance indicators and criterion scores for every facility evaluated, at every tier of the supply chain, and for the whole supply chain as a whole. A score of 80 per cent or higher represents effective vaccine management and high-quality infrastructure, while scores below this threshold highlight improvement areas.

Results

Gujarat criteria analysis

The EVM assessment for Gujarat’s DVSs and SPs, revealed that the most of scores was exceeding the global benchmark of 80 per cent. The vaccine arrival criterion (E1) pertains solely to the primary (State) vaccine store and does not apply at the district level. For DVS, scores were well above the target in key areas: temperature control (E2) reached 100 per cent; storage capacity (E3) scored 91 per cent; building, equipment, and transport (E4) scored 87 per cent; maintenance (E5) achieved 88 per cent; stock management (E6) was at 90 per cent; distribution (E7) at 90 per cent; and vaccine management (E8) reached 98 per cent. The only area slightly below the target was MIS and supportive functions (E9), at 79 per cent,

Performance at SPs demonstrated equally robust results, with all assessed criteria meeting or exceeding the global benchmark. Scores included 99 per cent for temperature control (E2), 98 per cent for storage capacity (E3), 96 per cent for building, equipment, and transport (E4), 100 per cent for maintenance (E5), 91 per cent for stock management (E6), 92 per cent for distribution (E7), 96 per cent for vaccine management (E8), and 80 per cent for MIS and supportive functions (E9).

As illustrated in figure 2, Gujarat’s immunisation supply chain achieved high standards across most parameters, with overall scores of 99 per cent in temperature control, 98 per cent in storage capacity, 95 per cent in building, equipment, and transport, 99 per cent in maintenance, 91 per cent in stock management, 92 per cent in distribution, and 96 per cent in vaccine management, all of which surpass the 80 per cent global target. The only exception is the MIS and supportive functions category, where Gujarat achieved a satisfactory score of 80 per cent.

By comparing criteria scores for high-performing and low-performing DVS, SPs and overall score in Gujarat; findings indicate minimal differences in criteria scores for DVSs with the largest gaps observed in distribution (7%), maintenance and vaccine management (4%), building and cold chain equipment (3%), and storage capacity (2%). Shapiro-Wilk tests revealed no significant deviation from normality for both high- (P=0.6) and low-performing DVSs (P=1), suggesting that score distributions are consistent across these groups. The unpaired t-test yielded a P value of 0.7, indicating that the differences between high- and low-performing DVSs are not statistically significant.

At the SP level, more substantial gaps were observed, specifically in building and cold chain equipment (8%) and MIS and supportive functions (10%). The Shapiro-Wilk test results indicate a significant deviation from normality in high-performing SPs (P=0.02) but not in low-performing SPs (P=0.25), suggesting variability in scores within the high-performing group. However, the mean scores between high- and low-performing SPs were not statistically significant. This finding implies that, while high-performing SPs demonstrate stronger performance in certain areas, the overall EVM scores are not sufficiently distinct to indicate a substantial performance gap. When comparing overall criteria scores across districts, high-performing districts averaged a score of 94.6 per cent, and low-performing districts averaged 92.9 per cent, with a mean difference of only 1.7 per cent. This difference was not statistically significant (P=0.6), suggesting that, although some variation exists in specific EVM criteria, the overall performance is largely comparable across high- and low-performing districts (Table I).

State category analysis

Gujarat has made achieved significant progress in nearly all categories: buildings (95%), capacity (97%), equipment (92%), management (89%), repairs and maintenance (98%), and training (99%), all exceeding the global target of 80 per cent. However, vehicle scores fell slightly below this threshold, indicating that all category scores in Gujarat were above the global standard except for vehicles (Fig. 3).

High-performing DVS in Gujarat excelled in several categories, including buildings (7-10%), capacity (2-3%), equipment (3-26%), repairs and maintenance (1-20%), and vehicles (2%), with no observed differences in training scores. Low-performing DVS scored higher in vehicles (8%) and showed slight advantages in capacity, training, and repairs and maintenance (1-2%).

For SPs, high-performing stores had higher scores in capacity (2%) and training (1%), while low-performing SPs surpassed in buildings (11%) and equipment (4%). Shapiro-Wilk tests revealed no significant deviation from normality for DVS (P=0.09 and 0.2 for high- and low-performing, respectively), while high-performing SPs deviated significantly (P=0.02) compared to low-performing SPs (P=0.25). Unpaired t-tests for both DVS (P=0.4) and SPs (P=0.31) indicated no statistically significant differences in overall category scores. These findings highlight effective cold storage supply chain management across high- and low-performing regions, despite minor category-specific variations (Table II).

Discussion

The EVM assessment in Gujarat revealed consistently high performance across both DVSs and SPs, with most criteria and category scores exceeding the global benchmark of 80 per cent. Minor gaps were noted in MIS and supportive functions and vehicle availability. Statistical analyses showed no significant differences between high- and low-performing districts or service points (P>0.05). EVM a globally endorsed assessment tool, provides a structured framework for evaluating and improving vaccine logistics systems10. Ensuring adequate vaccine management practices at all levels of the supply chain is essential to achieving immunisation programme goals5. This criterion focuses on the implementation of recommended vaccine management policies, such as the use of vaccine vial monitors (VVMs), the shake test, the multi-dose vial policy (MDVP), and vaccine wastage monitoring10. In Gujarat, the EVM score improved significantly from 54.3 in 201111 to 81.3 in 201912. Our findings suggest robust improvements in the cold chain system while highlighting areas that require further attention. Analysis of EVMA category scores revealed the highest improvement in the management category (79%), followed by training and equipment. Moderate improvements were seen in building, capacity, repair and maintenance, and vehicles; however, scores in some of these areas remained near or below the target of 80 per cent. Overall, these findings indicate that Gujarat has significantly enhanced its vaccine management processes particularly regarding vaccine receipt, transport, and storage since the last EVMA assessment11,12.

Comparative analysis with Ghana’s EVM assessments conducted in 2010 and 201413 underscores global progress in vaccine supply chain systems. Despite methodological differences between the 2011 and 2019 assessments, both adhered to internationally accepted standards, allowing valid comparison. Gujarat’s improved 2019 scores reflect better alignment with the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) guidelines12 and suggest that targeted corrective actions were implemented following the 2011 assessment. Improvements at the primary vaccine store level further indicate successful resolution of earlier gaps. Experiences of both Gujarat and Ghana reaffirm the value of EVM assessments in identifying systemic challenges and guiding strategic improvements12,13.

Although the overall mean difference in EVM scores between high- and low-performing districts was only 1.75 per cent a difference that was not statistically significant (P=0.6) this does not imply that targeted interventions are unnecessary. Rather, it suggests that the foundational elements of the vaccine supply chain are relatively strong across districts, including those classified as low-performing based on full immunisation coverage (FIC) rates.

Presence of variation in specific EVM criteria such as MIS and vehicle availability indicates that district-level challenges still exist and may contribute to differences in immunisation outcomes. Targeted and criterion-specific improvements remain essential, particularly in districts where certain operational components underperform despite relatively strong overall scores.

This study focused exclusively on government-run cold storage facilities; therefore, the findings may not be generalisable to private healthcare settings, where data are limited and regulatory oversight is variable. Additionally, some data were obtained through self-reported information from stakeholders, which may introduce response biases such as recall or social desirability bias, potentially affecting the objectivity of specific findings. Although the data review covered a full year (April 2021–March 2022), in line with WHO-UNICEF EVM guidelines, seasonal fluctuations in cold chain performance particularly during periods of extreme temperatures may not be fully reflected in annual averages. Challenges in obtaining permissions from facilities also posed logistical limitations. The diversity in geographic, administrative, and cultural contexts across India may also limit the broader applicability of the results. Lastly, restricted access to public immunisation data could constrain the comprehensiveness and policy relevance of the study’s conclusions.

To enhance vaccine storage and cold chain management, it is crucial to adopt a comprehensive approach informed by the EVM assessment and aligned with national best practices. This includes the expansion of integrated digital platforms such as eVIN across all districts for real-time tracking of vaccine stocks and equipment, along with capacity-building programmes to improve data quality and effective use of MIS dashboards. Regular data review mechanisms at district and State levels should be institutionalised to support timely decision-making. Strengthening physical infrastructure through increased dry storage and ice pack capacity, ensuring safe and adequately equipped transport, and establishing a planned preventive maintenance (PPM) programme for cold chain equipment are essential. Strategic partnerships with private logistics providers, governed by robust service-level agreements and accountability frameworks, can improve last-mile delivery and rapid deployment during immunisation drives. Gujarat’s successful practices—such as standardised cold chain protocols and performance monitoring systems—should be documented and scaled nationally through a centralised repository of best practices, integration into training curricula, and supportive supervision frameworks under the Universal Immunisation Programme.

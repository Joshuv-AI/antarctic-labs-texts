Physical, chemical and psychological effect of COVID-19 pandemic upon health care providers
Authors: Rawaa Kamel Abd, Mohammed Malih Radhi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe COVID-19 pandemic has had profound physical, chemical, and psychological impacts on medical staff worldwide.ObjectivesThe study aimed to assess the physical, chemical and psychological impact of COVID-19 pandemic and its predicted factors among health care providers.Materials and methodsA cross-sectional study design consist of 105 participants was carried out from 1st May to 30th July, 2023 at Al-Hussein teaching hospital at Al- Nasiriya city, southern Iraq. A Constructed questionnaire consisting of three parts was utilized in this study. The first includes demographic information while Second, concerns with work related variables and third Parts cover the assessment of physical, chemical and psychological Impact.ResultsThe study results indicate that more than half of participants were young adults aged 20 to 29, and predominantly male (58.1%), with 97.1% holding bachelor’s degrees and Medical Assistants make up 23.8% of job titles. The findings reveal that 61.0%,of respondents have a moderate effect from the COVID-19 pandemic in relation to physical well-being, with an average score of 19.68 ± 4.39, and55.2% of healthcare providers expressed chemical effect resulting in an average score of 24.65 ± 4.98., Additionally, in terms of psychological effect, 53.3% of respondents indicated experiencing moderate effects, with an average score of 21.22 ± 4.55. Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic, which emerged in late 2019, has unleashed a multifaceted assault on healthcare systems worldwide [1]. Among those most acutely affected are the dedicated medical providers who have been at the front-lines of this global health crisis [2].

Pandemics and other critical events can affect people’s emotional and physical health at several levels of society, including patients, families, and medical professionals.This pandemic has exacted a profound physical toll on healthcare workers, who have faced an unprecedented surge in patient numbers, grueling work hours, and a heightened risk of exposure to the virus. Their resilience in the face of these challenges has been nothing short of heroic [3–5].

In addition to the physical toll, medical providers have also confronted a barrage of chemical and environmental hazards [6]. personal protective equipment (PPE) shortages, particularly in the early stages of the pandemic, exposed healthcare workers to increased risks of infection [7]. Moreover, the constant use of disinfectants and sanitizers, while essential for infection control, has raised concerns about long-term chemical exposure and its potential health consequences. Balancing the need for protection with the potential adverse effects of these chemicals has been a critical challenge for medical providers [8, 9].

The COVID-19 pandemic has not only impacted the physical and chemical aspects of healthcare workers’ lives but has also left an indelible psychological imprint [10]. The intense emotional toll of witnessing suffering, loss, and the inability to save every patient has led to widespread mental health challenges among medical providers [11]. The constant fear of infection, isolation from family and friends, and the moral distress associated with difficult decisions have given rise to increased rates of anxiety, depression, and burnout [12].

The COVID-19 pandemic has unleashed a three-pronged assault on medical providers: physical challenges stemming from increased workloads and exposure risks, chemical hazards associated with protective measures, and profound psychological impacts due to the emotional toll of the crisis [13]. One of the most annoying obsessions that causes serious concerns is the dread of infection. Fear of contracting COVID-19 leads to actions that are restrictive and controlling [14, 15].

Addressing these multifaceted consequences and providing adequate support for healthcare workers is not only a moral imperative but essential for maintaining a robust and resilient healthcare system in the face of future challenges.

The present study aimed to assess the physical, chemical and psychological effect of COVID-19 pandemic and its predicted factors among health care providers in order to understand the impact and take appropriate interventions to put addressing the physical and, chemical and psychological well being of healthcare providers first while delivering medical care.

Method

Study design and setting

A cross-sectional study design was carried out from 1st May to 30th July, 2023 at Al-Hussein teaching hospital at Al- Nasiriyah city, southern Iraq.

Instruments

Constructed questionnaire consist from three parts was utilized in this study. The first includes demographic information such as age, gender, and educational level. Second, concerns with work related variables( work role, years of experience, precautionary measures in work place……, etc.) Parts III cover the assessment of physical, chemical and psychological effect. This section consist from 10 items to assess physical effect like ( Feel low back pain, Feel sever headache during work time hours) and 12 items for chemical effect such as (Frequent use of Alcohols cause for me skin irritation, Aldehydes cause for me respiratory problems) in addition to 10 items for psychological effect as (Afraid of being infected by COVID-19,I am stress and worry about the children and the elderly in the family).

The assessment measures used in the questionnaire sheet were based on the World Health Organization (WHO) and sheet of risk assessment group within the occupational health and safety work stream of national department of health- COVID-19 response [16].

Study sample

A convenience sample of 105 medical professionals. The researcher selects the sample size of respondents based only on their availability during the study’s conduction.

Scoring system

All items are rated on a three-point Likert scale, which stands for usually, sometimes, and never. a usually response was scored (3), some times (2) and never scored (1). Each item’s scores were added together then calculate the rang, the total score was then divided into three levels. The category length calculated according to formula range / No. of Categories. This is giving a mean score for physical& psychological impact [ Low (10-16.66) Moderate (16.67–23.33), High (23.34-30)], for chemical impact [ Low (12–20), Moderate (20.1–28), High (28.1–36)].

Validity and reliability

A pilot study was performed on a sample of 30 respondents prior to the start of the study. The data was then evaluated to determine the questionnaire’s reliability and validity, as well as its internal consistency, using the Cronbach alpha test. The alpha test value was 84.5. The constructed questionnaire firstly sent to expert panel from ares of specialization to check validity by put their corrections opinions on sentence construction, grammar, and phrase placement into practice. and then corrected according their comments after that the pilot study was conducted then using the Cronbach alpha test.

Data collection

The data collection carryout by the first author via direct interview with health care workers (HCWs) to take their permission and explain the study aims for them. The participants were given a questionnaire to fill out in Arabic language under supervision of the author. A total of 105 respondents of both sexes who replied to the questionnaire and those who worked at COVID-19 section were included in the study. Those who did not complete the questionnaire form and out of the COVID-19 section were not included in this study.

The potential mechanism that applied to prevent selection bias the researcher select the respondent from homogeneous community at hospital and to avoid recall bias the data collected under their supervision at working time.

Statistical analysis approach

The necessary statistical processing of the data was done, and descriptive statistics were used by extracting the numbers, percentages, arithmetic means, and standard deviations of the sample. Inferential statistical methods were used, which include Simple Liner Regression, in which the (-β) means a negative prediction and (+β) means positive prediction. The p-value (Sig.) at 0.05 indicate statistical significance.

Results

The demographic profile of healthcare providers participating in the study reveals several noteworthy characteristics. A significant portion of the participants, 58.1%, fall within the age range of 20 to 29 years old. Additionally, a majority of the healthcare providers, also at 58.1%, were male. Furthermore, a striking 97.1% of the participants held bachelor’s degrees as their highest educational qualification.

In terms of experience, 44.8% of the providers had less than 5 years of experience in the healthcare field. A substantial 74.3% reported working overtime, highlighting the demanding nature of their roles. It’s worth noting that 93.3% of respondents indicated experiencing changes in their work environment during the pandemic, reflecting the challenges posed by the COVID-19 crisis.

Almost half of the participants, 47.6%, reported feelings of isolation. Within the job titles represented, 23.8% identified as Medical Assistants. Importantly, a significant majority of the healthcare providers, 91.4%, had experience in providing care for COVID-19 patients, underlining their critical role during the pandemic. (Table 1).

Discussion

The findings of study indicate that a significant portion of healthcare providers reported experiencing various impacts from the COVID-19 pandemic, shed light on the multifaceted challenges faced by healthcare workers during this unprecedented global health crisis. These impacts encompass physical, chemical, and psychological aspects, each contributing to the overall well-being and resilience of healthcare professionals.

Firstly, the study shows that healthcare providers experienced a moderate impact on their physical well-being due to the pandemic. This underscores the toll that the virus has taken on healthcare workers, who were on the front-lines, risking their own health and well-being to care for the sick. This statistic aligns with Adams JG, 2020 [17].

Also this results come in line with the extensive literature on the physical risks and challenges healthcare workers have faced during the pandemic, including exposure to the virus, long working hours, and inadequate personal protective equipment (PPE) [18, 19].

Secondly, the chemical impact, with 55.2% expressing moderate concern, highlights the awareness among healthcare providers about the potential exposure to hazardous chemicals, such as disinfectants and sanitizers, which became essential tools in the fight against COVID-19. The present finding agree with finding of Ghaleb Y, 2021 and Teo I,2022. This concern can be linked to the need for proper training and guidelines on the safe use of chemicals in healthcare settings to minimize adverse effects on the providers health. Ensuring the safety of healthcare workers in chemical-related aspects is crucial to maintaining a resilient workforce [20, 21].

Finally, the psychological impacts affecting 53.3% of respondents emphasize the immense emotional and mental strain placed on healthcare providers. The high levels of stress, anxiety, and burnout reported among healthcare workers during the pandemic have been extensively documented by Pappa S. and Greenberg in 2020 [22, 23]. also Kabusi M in 2022 found an association between COVID-19 and psychological health of HCWs [24].

These findings underscore the need for comprehensive mental health support and resources for healthcare professionals, both during and after the pandemic, to address these psychological challenges effectively.

This findings indicate that healthcare providers have faced a complex array of challenges during the COVID-19 pandemic, affecting their physical, chemical, and psychological well-being. These findings emphasize the importance of comprehensive support systems, adequate training, and policy measures to safeguard the health and resilience of healthcare workers, not only during pandemics but also in their everyday roles.

The findings sheds light on an important aspect of this impact - the role of years of experience among healthcare providers. This study found a statistically significant relationship between years of experience and the physical well-being of healthcare workers during the pandemic.

The study’s regression analysis suggests a strong negative association between years of experience and the impact on the physical well-being of healthcare providers. In other words, as the years of experience of healthcare workers increased, their physical well-being was less adversely affected by the pandemic. The results come in agree with Petrella A. 2021 and Hughes R. 2020 [25, 26]. This finding has several implications:

First, it highlights the resilience and adaptability of experienced healthcare professionals who may have developed coping mechanisms and strategies over the years to deal with the stresses and challenges of their profession, including a pandemic. Second, it underscores the need for providing additional support, training, and resources to less experienced healthcare providers who may be more vulnerable to the physical toll of the pandemic.

Moreover, this finding aligns with previous research that has shown the psychological and emotional toll of the pandemic on healthcare workers. While years of experience may be a protective factor for physical well-being, it may not necessarily mitigate the emotional and psychological stressors associated with caring for COVID-19 patients.

This findings provide valuable insights into the differential impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on healthcare providers based on their years of experience. This information can inform healthcare organizations and policymakers in tailoring support and interventions to address the specific needs of healthcare workers at different stages of their careers during and after pandemics.

It is observed that there were significant predictors for healthcare providers chemical hazard impact during the COVID-19 pandemic. Specifically, education level and years of experience emerged as significant factors in predicting this impact. A study conducted by Aruru et al., found that healthcare workers with higher education levels experienced a lower impact of chemical hazards during the pandemic [27].

This result suggests that individuals with advanced degrees in healthcare or related fields may have a better understanding of safety protocols, a higher awareness of chemical hazards, and the ability to adapt quickly to changing situations. Such knowledge and adaptability can contribute to reduced exposure to chemical risks.

Another significant predictor identified by the research is the years of experience of healthcare providers. A study by Gold et al., demonstrated that healthcare workers with more years of experience tended to experience a lower impact of chemical hazards during the pandemic [28].

This may be attributed to their familiarity with workplace protocols, better hazard recognition skills, and enhanced ability to manage stressful situations efficiently.

The impact of chemical hazards on healthcare providers during the COVID-19 pandemic was significantly influenced by their education level and years of experience. Higher education levels and more years of experience were associated with reduced impact, likely due to better knowledge and skills in hazard management and workplace safety.

These findings emphasize the importance of continuous education and training for healthcare workers to enhance their preparedness and resilience in dealing with chemical hazards during public health crises.

Research findings have consistently shown that age is a significant predictor of the psychological impact on healthcare providers. For instance, a study by Chew et al. (2020) found that older healthcare workers tended to report lower levels of psychological distress during the COVID-19 pandemic. The study that increasing age was associated with a decrease in psychological distress among healthcare providers [29].

Also another study done by Nguyen P H at 2023 in Vietnam indicate HCWs under the age of 35 were 1.099 more likely than those over 35 to experience psychological effects from COVID-19 [30].

Gender is another important factor that has been shown to influence the psychological well-being of healthcare workers. A study by Li et al., indicated that female healthcare workers were more likely to experience higher levels of psychological distress compared to their male counterparts [31].

The study results suggesting that being female was associated with a greater psychological impact among healthcare providers. The current results typically agree with Sun P. 2021, who report that Healthcare personnel have been severely impacted psychologically by the COVID-19 pandemic, particularly women and front-line staff [32].

The cumulative years of experience in the healthcare field have also been found to influence the psychological impact of healthcare providers. A study by Wu et al., revealed that healthcare workers with fewer years of experience were more vulnerable to psychological distress [33–36].

The study indicating that an increase in years of experience was associated with a decrease in psychological distress among healthcare providers.

These findings provide robust evidence that age, gender, and years of experience are predictive factors for the psychological impact experienced by healthcare providers.

Older age, male gender, and greater years of experience are generally associated with a reduced psychological impact, while younger age, female gender, and fewer years of experience may increase vulnerability to psychological distress among healthcare workers. These insights are crucial for the development of targeted interventions and support systems for healthcare providers to address their mental well-being [37].

Conclusion

The study highlights the difficulties faced by healthcare workers during the COVID-19 pandemic, including physical, chemical, and psychological impacts. The findings reveal that the respondents have a moderate effect from the COVID-19 pandemic in relation to physical well-being, and more than half of healthcare providers expressed chemical effect, Additionally, in terms of psychological effect, the respondents indicated experiencing moderate effects. It emphasizes the need for strong support, training, and policies to protect the mental and physical health of healthcare workers, especially during global health crises. To tackle these challenges, personalized strategies that take into account factors like experience, education, age, and gender should be adopted by healthcare institutions and policymakers.

Limitation

There are some limitations to the current research that should be taken into consideration specifically in the difficulty obtaining study data. Also the cross- sectional design make incapacity to investigate rare effect, determine the impact incidence, and form judgments about their causes. Work load made the health worker not eligible to participate in study. In addition to that about 55 questionnaire sheet excluded from the study sample because not fully filled by respondents. For further studies simple random sample is better to assess theses factors Future and consider larger across multiple sites. The limitation of convenience sampling is that it cannot generalize findings to the total population of health workers. The current study did not include a long-term follow-up to assess the durability of these outcomes.

Implications for practice

The most implication of practices include management of workload through employ measures to regulate workload, such as flexible scheduling, sufficient staffing, and task delegation, to avert burnout and mitigate stress. Also organize well-being initiatives that foster self-care, management of stress, and resilience among healthcare professionals, including mindfulness, yoga, or meditation sessions. Continuously assess the physical and psychological well-being of healthcare personnel, offering feedback and assistance to resolve any problems or challenges. in addition to assist healthcare professionals in managing their psychological effects by providing mental health tools like peer support groups and counseling accessible.

By putting these practice-related implications into effect, healthcare organizations can lessen the negative health effects of COVID-19 on the wellbeing of healthcare workers and encourage a more effective, healthy workforce.

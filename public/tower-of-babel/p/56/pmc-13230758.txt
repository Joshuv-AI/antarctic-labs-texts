Experimental verification of DAS vibration response characteristics of equal strength beam under sinusoidal excitation
Authors: Chenyang Ma, Jing Chai, Dingding Zhang, Zhi Li, Lei Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS) technology holds great potential for structural dynamic monitoring; however, its quantitative capability for complex dynamic strain fields and the reliability of spatial resolution require systematic verification. This study employed a systematic “theoretical modeling–simulation analysis–experimental verification” approach, using an equal-strength beam under sinusoidal excitation to investigate the dynamic response characteristics of DAS. A complete dynamic strain transfer model from external excitation to DAS phase-change signals was established, defining the phase sensitivity coefficient as a key parameter for quantitative analysis. Finite element simulations revealed that while the beam’s strain is approximately uniform at low frequencies (< 5 Hz), it exhibits a gradient distribution—large near the fixed end and small near the free end—near the natural frequency, departing from the static “equal-strain” characteristic. Experimental results under 5 Hz excitation, utilizing synchronized measurements from accelerometers, FBG sensors, and DAS, demonstrated that DAS accurately captures the dominant vibration frequency. The measured phase sensitivity coefficient showed only a 7.3% error relative to theory, the response amplitude maintained high linearity with excitation and reference signals (R² ≥ 0.99), and the phase delay was negligible (0.02 rad). By incorporating the spatial averaging effect of the DAS gauge length, the theoretical strain ratio across channels is corrected, reducing the deviation from 23.4% to 1.7%, confirming DAS’s ability to quantitatively resolve spatial differences in dynamic strain distribution. Additional 50 Hz experiments validate the frequency applicability threshold and demonstrate DAS’s utility for event detection beyond the uniform-strain regime. Furthermore, by comparing DAS channel response ratios across spatial positions with theoretical strain ratios, the study confirmed DAS’s ability to quantitatively resolve spatial differences in dynamic strain distribution. This work systematically validates the quantitative accuracy and reliability of DAS as a distributed dynamic strain sensing tool, and the revealed dynamic-static strain distribution differences, together with the established validation methodology, provide solid theoretical and experimental support for refined DAS-based monitoring in engineering structure dynamic monitoring scenarios.

Introduction

Structural Health Monitoring (SHM) is a core component of safe operation and maintenance in modern engineering, aiming to realize damage warning and lifespan assessment through real-time perception of changes in the physical state of structures1–4. Among various monitoring technologies, distributed optical fiber sensing technology has emerged as a research and application focus, benefiting from its advantages including immunity to electromagnetic interference, corrosion resistance, and the capability of long-distance continuous measurement5. Specifically, Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS), which relies on the phase-sensitive characteristics of coherent Rayleigh scattering, can acquire dynamic strain information along the fiber path with high spatial resolution. This provides a novel technical approach for capturing dynamic responses (e.g., vibration and impact) in structures6–8.

Initially, research and applications of Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS) were primarily focused on fields such as safety monitoring of oil and gas pipelines, perimeter protection of critical zones, and seismic wave detection, with the core objective of determining the occurrence, type identification, and localization of vibration events9–11. With technological advancements, its application scenarios have expanded to structural health condition monitoring. For instance, Cheng Lun Kai et al.12 systematically investigated the vibration responses of bridges and tunnels under traffic loads using DAS, verifying its feasibility in identifying vehicle loads and fundamental frequencies; Kinzo Kishida et al.13 employed DAS-based dynamic monitoring to reveal the vibration response characteristics of bridges induced by train bogies; Julie Rodet et al.14 utilized existing fiber-optic networks to simultaneously extract health indicators (e.g., vibration frequency, damping, and mode shapes) of multiple bridges, and further revealed periodic variations in the dynamic characteristics of bridges associated with temperature changes. Regarding the quantitative measurement of structural dynamic responses, James T. Xu et al.15 conducted a shaking table test on an offshore wind turbine model, where they synchronously deployed DAS (Φ-OTDR) and OFDR to successfully capture the dynamic strain profile of the structure under simulated environmental loads. They also identified the local effects of bolt loosening at flange connections, thus verifying DAS’s capability to capture both local and global structural dynamic phenomena. Abdulfatah A. G. Abushagur et al.16 found that the strain transfer efficiency of cables with different structures and protective layers differs significantly, which directly influences the amplitude of DAS signals. In addition, the robustness of monitoring results under complex environmental conditions is a common challenge faced by various technical means in structural health monitoring (SHM). For example, aiming at the problem of tunnel lining crack segmentation in complex environments, Duan et al.17 proposed the DSYOLO model based on dynamic snake convolution, which demonstrates the importance of algorithm robustness in complex field environments. This study indicates a potential direction for future multi-sensor fusion: the combination of quantitative dynamic strain data provided by DAS and structural surface condition assessment from vision-based inspection can achieve a more comprehensive structural health diagnosis, which also puts forward higher requirements for the quantitative accuracy and environmental adaptability of DAS distributed monitoring technology.

Despite igsnificant progress in the aforementioned research, the goal of establishing DAS as a reliable and universal quantitative sensing tool for dynamic strain fields still confronts core challenges18–20. Most existing studies are primarily focused on utilizing DAS signal-amplitude for event identification and frequency extraction. For the original physical quantity directly measured by DAS systems—the phase change rate—there remains a lack of systematic experimental calibration and theoretical verification regarding how to precisely establish a general quantitative mapping relationship with the actual dynamic strain rate on the structure surface. Furthermore, for typical mechanical models such as the equal-strength beam (which exhibits a uniform strain field under static conditions but shows strain distribution variations corresponding to mode shapes under dynamic conditions), experimental verification of whether DAS can quantitatively distinguish and reconstruct the spatial distribution characteristics of such dynamic strain gradients remains inadequate. This forms a key bottleneck in the current transition of DAS technology from “trend perception” to “accurate measurement.”

The equal-strength beam, as a classical mechanical model, serves as an ideal platform for isolating and verifying the strain response of sensing systems, owing to its static “equal-stress” property21,22. This paper adopts it as the research subject, aiming to systematically investigate the dynamic response characteristics of DAS under sinusoidal excitation. A theoretical model correlating the DAS phase-change rate with the strain rate and acceleration of the equal-strength beam is established via theoretical derivation. ANSYS is employed to perform static and harmonic response simulations, thereby determining the test parameters. Finally, a comparative test system comprising DAS, FBG sensors, and accelerometers is established to examine the response linearity, phase characteristics, and distributed sensing capability of DAS under sinusoidal excitation.

The core innovations of this study are reflected in the following three aspects. First, the study breaks through the limitation that most existing studies rely on DAS signal amplitude for qualitative analysis, and realizes the accurate quantitative mapping between the phase change rate (the original physical quantity directly measured by DAS) and the actual dynamic strain rate on the structural surface, with only 7.3% relative error between the measured phase sensitivity coefficient and the theoretical calculated value. Second, it reveals the essential difference between the static and dynamic strain distribution of the equal-strength beam, clarifies the low-frequency threshold of < 5 Hz for reliable quantitative measurement of DAS, and makes up for the defect that existing DAS calibration studies generally ignore the dynamic strain gradient characteristics of the calibration model. Third, it establishes a standardized verification system of “theoretical modeling–simulation analysis–experimental verification”, quantitatively corrects the 23.4% amplitude deviation caused by the spatial averaging effect of the DAS gauge length, and provides a reproducible methodological reference for subsequent DAS dynamic strain calibration research. The research results can provide theoretical support and experimental basis for dynamic strain field monitoring of engineering structures.

Results analysis at 5 Hz excitation

A sinusoidal excitation (with a frequency of 5 Hz and an amplitude of 500 mV) was applied at the free end of the equal-strength beam. All sensors were able to clearly and stably capture the vibration signal.

The variation diagram under the action of dynamic load of 5 Hz sine wave. (a) Acceleration value, (b) FBG strain rate, (c) DAS phase change rate.

As illustrated in Fig. 11(a), the output signals of the accelerometers (A1–A6) were standard sinusoidal waveforms with a period of 0.2 s—this proves that the excitation system generates ideal linear forced vibration in the low-frequency range.

Figure 11(b) showed the dynamic strain signals monitored by the FBG sensors, which exhibited regular sinusoidal variations with a period that completely matched the excitation frequency. This preliminarily verified the accurate response of the FBG sensors to the surface strain of the equal-strength beam.

Figure 11(c) showed the phase-change rate signal demodulated by the DAS system. Although its time-domain waveform showed periodicity overall (at the same frequency as the excitation), obvious waveform distortion and high-frequency noise were observed.

The spectra of the acceleration signal, FBG strain rate signal, and DAS phase-change rate signal all showed a single sharp peak at 5 Hz, with energy highly concentrated at this frequency. The calculated signal-to-noise ratios (SNR) at 5 Hz were 45 dB, 42 dB, and 38 dB, respectively.

The amplitude of the nonlinear harmonic components was less than 5% of that of the fundamental frequency, indicating that the system was in a good linear operating state. All three monitoring methods were able to accurately recover the main frequency information of the external disturbance.

To ensure the accuracy of subsequent quantitative analysis, a 4th-order Butterworth low-pass filter was applied to the raw signals based on the FFT results. The cut-off frequency of the filter was set to 100 Hz, and the zero-phase forward-backward filtering algorithm was used to avoid phase distortion of the original sinusoidal signal. The same filter parameters were used for the acceleration, FBG strain rate and DAS phase change rate signals to ensure the consistency of signal processing. The filtering effect is presented in Fig. 13. The filtered signal remained undistorted, and high-frequency noise was effectively suppressed. Comprehensive time-domain and frequency-domain analysis showed that under 5 Hz excitation: the accelerometer signals had the highest regularity and fidelity; the FBG sensors accurately reflected the dynamic strain response on the beam surface; while the DAS system exhibited slight time-domain waveform distortion, its main frequency amplitude variation trend was completely consistent with that of the accelerometer and FBG signals. This consistency verifies the reliability of the accelerometer and FBG measurements, which can thus serve as a benchmark for validating the effectiveness of DAS monitoring.

Discussion

To verify the reliability of the DAS monitoring system and establish a precise quantitative relationship between the DAS system’s monitoring signals and the surface strain of the equal-strength beam, variations in key parameters under sinusoidal dynamic loads with the same frequency but different amplitudes were analyzed based on the filtered signals.

Conclusion

Through theoretical derivation, finite element simulation and sinusoidal excitation test of equal-strength beam, this paper systematically studies the response characteristics of DAS in dynamic strain monitoring. The main conclusions are as follows:

A dynamic strain transfer model from external excitation to DAS phase response is constructed, and the phase sensitivity coefficient is proposed as the core parameter for quantitative analysis, which clarifies the linear mapping relationship between DAS phase change rate and the actual dynamic strain rate of the structure.

The dynamic strain distribution law of the equal-strength beam is obtained: the surface strain is approximately uniform when the excitation frequency is less than 5 Hz, and presents a significant spatial gradient distribution near the natural frequency, which is fundamentally different from the static equal-strain characteristic. The 5 Hz frequency threshold for reliable quantitative measurement of DAS is determined.

DAS has excellent quantitative monitoring performance in the low-frequency uniform strain range. Under 5 Hz excitation, the relative error of the phase sensitivity coefficient is 7.3%, the response linearity R² is up to 0.99, and the phase delay is less than 0.02 rad. After correcting the spatial averaging effect of the gauge length, the relative deviation between the measured and theoretical strain amplitude ratio is reduced from 23.4% to 1.7%, which verifies DAS’s ability to quantitatively distinguish the spatial distribution of dynamic strain.

When the excitation frequency exceeds 5 Hz, the time-domain linear relationship between DAS signal and structural strain breaks down, but DAS can still reliably capture the fundamental frequency of excitation, which is suitable for vibration event identification and qualitative monitoring in high-frequency scenarios.

The research results provide theoretical basis and experimental support for the engineering application of DAS in dynamic monitoring of engineering structures. In the follow-up, the quantitative method of this study can be extended to full-scale engineering structures, and the verification of DAS monitoring performance under complex loads and environments can be carried out to further improve its engineering applicability.

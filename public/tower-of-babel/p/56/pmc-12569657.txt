Engineering Lanthanide Ion-Labeled Aptamers for ICP-MS Profiling of Serum-Derived Extracellular Vesicles from Colorectal Cancer
Authors: Yawei Feng, Cong Luo, Yunshan Yang, Xiaoling Zheng, Ziyan Du, Yunben Yang, Zhi Zhou, Hubin Chen, Xie Wang, Jiaxuan He, Xiaobo Yu, Ruizi Peng, Weihong Tan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Membrane proteins on extracellular vesicles (EVs) derived from clinical serum samples have emerged as promising biomarkers for cancer screening, early diagnosis and prognosis. However, high-throughput profiling of multiple EV proteins with high sensitivity and high selectivity is challenge for clinical applications. In this work, we synthesized a polymer capable of chelating a set of lanthanide ions labeled at the terminal of DNA aptamers. These lanthanide ions-labeled aptamers have high affinity for specific EV proteins. By utilizing ICP-MS, multiplex profiling was performed for four specific proteins on the surface of EVs with high sensitivity and selectivity for CRC diagnosis. Precise diagnosis of EVs from CRC patients were achieved with an accuracy of 89.9%, sensitivity of 86.3%, and specificity of 100%. This method features high sensitivity, low background, and rapid detection, demonstrating potential clinical application for in vitro diagnosis with blood samples.

Cancer screening and early diagnosis , remain challenging for highly lethal malignant colorectal cancer (CRC). Although tissue biopsy is accurate, its invasive, traumatic, and localized nature makes comprehensive and dynamic monitoring of tumor lesions difficult. , Thus, as a noninvasive approach for monitoring CRC, in vitro diagnostics (IVD) have emerged as a promising method for early detection and prognosis, providing a means to assess tumor-derived biomarkers in blood. − Recently, extracellular vesicles (EVs), small vesicles secreted by host cells and enriched in proteins and nucleic acids, , have attracted increasing attention for their potential roles in cancer diagnosis and monitoring. , Compared to cell-free proteins or nucleic acids in the blood, , With wide distribution, high abundance, and a broad range of biomolecules encapsulated, EVs become a promising platform for the IVD of CRC. ,

Clinically, the combinatorial analysis of multiple EV proteins significantly enhances the accuracy of IVD. − Although conventional methods, such as fluorescence-based and electrochemical assays, achieve high sensitivity and selectivity through antibody recognition, they still face challenges, including low throughput and false positive signals for profiling EV proteins. Among various methods of EV protein profiling, inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) offers accurate quantitative analysis with high sensitivity, high selectivity, and high-throughput capability, enabling precise analysis of trace elements from clinical samples. , Notably, lanthanide ions have become widely used as MS tags on recognition molecules because they are almost nonexistent in biological systems, thereby providing negligible background noise. , These ions can be specifically chelated by ligand-functionalized polymers and conjugated to recognition moieties, and provide unique elemental signatures with ICP-MS to realize sensitive and multiplex detection. Among recognition candidates, DNA oligonucleotides, specifically DNA aptamers, have emerged as subjects of growing interest due to their ability to specifically target EV proteins. , Importantly, compared to antibodies, it is more feasible to specifically label lanthanide ions on aptamers for biosensing. Therefore, through labeling different lanthanide ions at the terminals of different DNA aptamers, high-throughput profiling of multiple EV proteins by ICP-MS can be readily achieved.

To profile EV proteins with ICP-MS, we initially synthesized a polymer for the highly efficient chelation of different lanthanide ions, including Gd (gadolinium), La (lanthanum), Pr (praseodymium), and Tb (terbium). As proof-of-concept, herein, four aptamers that specifically target different EV proteins were selected, including Sgc8c (PTK7), MJ5C (PD-L1), XQ2d (CD71), and SL1 (c-Met), due to their close association of these proteins with cancer occurrence and development. Then, the lanthanide ion-chelated polymer (LIP) was covalently conjugated with these DNA aptamers via click chemistry. After isolating from the blood of colorectal cancer patients, the EVs were incubated with LIPs-labeled aptamers. Since each aptamer targets a specific EV protein, multiplexed profiling of EV proteins was simultaneously achieved by ICP-MS with superior sensitivity, low background, and fast speed for IVD (Figure ).

Results and Discussion

Design and Synthesis of Lanthanide Ion-Chelated Polymer (LIP)

First, a polymer modified with diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid (DTPA) was designed and synthesized. In this design, an azide group was incorporated into the polymer for coupling with aptamers, while a DTPA group was introduced for chelating metal ions. As shown in Figure , under anhydrous and anaerobic conditions, polymerization of compound 1 (Figure S1) and compound 2 (Figure S2) was performed to obtain polymer 3. Afterward, the product was purified with diethyl ether, and nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy confirmed the presence of the tert-butyl peak (Figure S3). After treatment with azobis­(isobutyronitrile) (AIBN), polymer 4 (Figure S4) was obtained and then reacted with 2-(2-azidoethoxy) ethylamine to introduce azide groups, resulting in polymer 6 (Figure S5). Subsequently, the tert-butoxycarbonyl (Boc) groups were removed using trifluoroacetic acid. The absence of characteristic peaks for polymer 7 was confirmed by NMR (Figure S6). Finally, DTPA was activated with 4-(4,6-dimethoxy-1,3,5-triazin-2-yl)-4-methylmorpholinium chloride (DMTMM) and coupled to the amino groups of polymer 8; an excess of DTPA was used to ensure efficient coupling. Purified polymer 8 was verified by NMR (Figure S7). The synthesis process is concise and efficient. The NMR result showed that the polymer has a degree of polymerization of 27 with approximately 27 units of DTPA fragments.

Construction of LIP-Labeled Aptamer (Apt-LIP)

To obtain Apt-LIP, DNA aptamer Sgc8c was initially selected for coupling with LIP. Since LIP contains an azide group (N3) and the 3′-end of the aptamer is modified with a cyclooctyne (DBCO) group, LIP was efficiently conjugated onto the aptamer via click chemistry reaction, which is known for its rapid reaction kinetics and versatility (Figure a). For this conjugation, several reaction conditions were studied to improve reaction efficiency, including the molar ratio of reactants, temperature, and reaction time (Figures S8–S11). The results of agarose gel electrophoresis indicated that the optimal conjugation conditions for aptamer Sgc8c and LIP were a molar ratio of 1:15 with the reaction conducted at 70 °C for 20 h (Figure b). To evaluate the binding affinity of aptamer after LIP-labeling, DNA aptamers with dual labels, including 5′-FAM and 3′-LIP, were subsequently obtained, including Sgc8c-LIP, MJ5C-LIP, XQ2d-LIP and SL1-LIP, as well as control Lib-LIP (Library). After incubating with different cell lines, such as GES-1 (a human gastric mucosal cell), HCT116 (a colorectal cancer cell), and LOVO (a colorectal cancer cell), as well as a control group, CCD-18Co (a normal colonic epithelial cell) (Figure c), flow cytometry analysis showed that LIP labeling had little effect on the binding affinities of aptamers to their membrane targets, confirming the feasibility of this conjugation strategy (Figures d,e and S12).

Characterization of Apt-LIP on EVs

Ultracentrifugation was employed to isolate highly purified EVs with the cell culture supernatant. Biotransmission electron microscope (bio-TEM) imaging showed a classic morphological feature of isolated EVs with a typical bowl-shaped structure with irregular concave features and distinct membrane structures, indicative of their characteristic lipid bilayer (Figure b). Results of nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) showed that the particle diameter distributions of EVs were 30–200 nm, , consistent with the widely reported size (Figure c). In addition, we have previously validated that the extraction method can consistently yield highly purified EVs expressing typical markers such as CD63, CD9, and TSG101 through Western blot analysis. , To assess the binding affinity of Apt-LIPs to EVs, a polystyrene (PS) bead was incorporated owing to the small size of EVs (Figure a). Based on EVs derived from GES-1, HCT116, and LOVO cells, flow cytometry results showed that the binding affinity of MJ5C remained sufficiently strong, even though a reduction happened after LIP-labeling. However, the binding affinities of XQ2d, Sgc8c, and SL1 showed few changes (Figures d and S13), corroborating statistical analysis (Figure e). The fluorescence study demonstrated the feasibility of EV protein profiling with Apt-LIPs.

Cell-Derived EV Protein Profiling with ICP-MS

To achieve EV protein profiling with multiplexed Apt-LIPs, including Sgc8c-LIP, SL1-LIP, MJ5C-LIP, and XQ2d-LIP, we initially studied the binding affinities on CCD-18Co, GES-1, HCT116, and LOVO cells, using ICP-MS (Figure S14). After optimizing the conditions of aptamer incubation (Figure S15), we further investigated the binding affinities on EVs derived from the above cells, respectively (Figure a­(i),b). ICP-MS results demonstrated that this group of Apt-LIPs bound to different cell-derived EVs with different binding affinity patterns.

Given the ICP-MS signal from individual Apt-LIPs against different EVs, we next evaluated the effectiveness of a mixture of multiple Apt-LIPs in ICP-MS profiling (Figure a­(ii)). After optimization for both the sensitivity and specificity of aptamers (Figure S16), we coincubated the mixture of Apt-LIPs with EVs. According to ICP-MS, four EV proteins were simultaneously detected by this group of Apt-LIPs (Figure c), confirming the feasibility of multiplexed analysis in both cells (Figure S17) and EVs. Finally, we assessed the performance of each targeted protein. Notably, the average signals of CD71, c-Met, PD-L1, and PTK7 in cancer cell-derived EVs were higher than those from normal cells (Figure d). However, signal distributions overlapped between CRC cell-derived EVs and those from CCD-18CO and GES-1, leading to a slight decrease in classification accuracy when using an individual group as the classification criterion. Essentially, the EVs were obtained from cell lines, rather than clinical samples, thus lacking clinical diagnostic value.

Clinical Validation of Serum-Derived EVs with ICP-MS Profiling

ICP-MS profiling was performed on serum-derived EVs. Serum samples were collected from 51 cancer patients, including 44 CRC patients and 7 gastric cancer (GC) patients to preliminarily assess the applicability of our approach to other digestive system malignancies, as well as 18 healthy donors as a control group. After incubation of serum samples with Apt-LIPs for ICP-MS profiling, the expression level of four selected EV proteins was obtained. The average expression levels of these proteins were significantly higher in both CRC and GC patients in comparison to the control group (Figure a). Notably, overlaps of expression distributions were observed for each individual protein, indicating that cancer diagnostic accuracy can be challenging when relying on a single protein (Figure b), which is in agreement with the results from cell-derived EVs (Figure d). Interestingly, the expression trends of these proteins in GC patients were similar to those in CRC patients, suggesting a similarity in molecular profiles between these two malignancies. Hence, we further built a logistic regression model based on the optimal combination of these proteins. According to ROC curve analysis (Figure S18), the combination of CD71 and PTK7 was statistically significant in the logistic regression model (p-value <0.05) with the highest AUC value of 0.975. Therefore, based on these two biomarkers, we constructed a logistic regression (logit) predictive model based on the following equation logit(P)=−5.518+90.622×CD71+75.451×PTK7 Next, through ROC curve analysis, we determined the optimal cutoff value for the L score to be 1.7174. At this cutoff, the combined analysis of CD71 and PTK7 could effectively distinguish cancer patients from healthy donors (Figure c), suggesting the potential broader applicability of the two-biomarker system. Notably, this classification model demonstrated the ability to accurately identify both cancer patients and healthy individuals, highlighting the potential broader applicability of this two-biomarker system. At this cutoff, the model achieved a sensitivity of 86.3% and a specificity of 100%. Patients with an L score higher than 1.7174 were classified as cancer patients, while those with a score below this value were classified as healthy individuals (Figures d, S19 and Table ). Finally, the overall accuracy of the model reached to 89.9% (Figure e).

Conclusions

In summary, we synthesized a polymer that could efficiently chelate lanthanide ions. By employing ICP-MS, the developed Apt-LIPs achieved multiplex EV protein profiling in clinical serum samples, showing good accuracy (89.9%), sensitivity (86.3%), and specificity (100%) for the clinical cohort. Compared to traditional optical and electrochemical methods, , ICP-MS offered a high-throughput profiling approach for the simultaneous detection of multiplexed targets for IVD. Since chelated lanthanide ions barely exist in living systems, the background signal was significantly reduced, enhancing the sensitivity and accuracy of IVD. Alternatively, this profiling approach was readily adapted to profiling other disease biomarkers by using specific targets, enabling its potential for noninvasive cancer screening and accurate cancer diagnosis.

A cutting-edge ensemble model for enhanced underwater image restoration and quality improvement
Authors: A Sarala, C Vinoth kumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractUnderwater image enhancement poses unique challenges due to poor visibility, color distortion, and haze caused by light absorption and scattering in water. In this paper, we propose an ensemble model, Ensemble Pyramid-based Convolutional Neural Network and Deep Channel Prior Dehazing Network (EPCNN-DCPDN), which combines Pyramid-based Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and the Deep Channel Prior Dehazing Network (DCPDN) to address these challenges. The model operates in two ways: sequentially, by first applying DCPDN for haze removal followed by Pyramid-based CNNs for multi-scale feature refinement, or in parallel, with outputs from both models fused using a weighted average or learned fusion mechanism. We evaluated the proposed model on multiple underwater datasets and compared its performance against nine state-of-the-art models, including CLAHE, FUnIE-GAN, WaterGAN, and Haze-Line Prior Model. The EPCNN-DCPDN model achieved superior results with a PSNR of 28.34 dB, SSIM of 0.902, and UIQM of 3.56. It also demonstrated outstanding accuracy in challenging underwater conditions, with an accuracy of 97.92% on shallow, deep, and low-light underwater datasets, outperforming existing models such as WaterGAN and Haze-Line Prior Model. The results highlight the effectiveness of the proposed model in restoring color, contrast, and fine details in underwater images. The model’s ability to handle a wide range of underwater conditions makes it an ideal solution for applications in underwater exploration, marine research, and object detection.Keywords: Underwater image enhancement, EPCNN-DCPDN, Pyramid-based CNN, DCPDN, Dehazing, Image restoration, PSNR, SSIM, UIQMSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Ocean sciences, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Underwater Image Enhancement is a branch of image processing which deals with the problems encountered in obtaining underwater images. When light transmits through water it has different properties from what it has in the air1,2. The light penetration underwater is also selective in the wavelength which distorts the colours, reduces contrast and blurs underwater images. Therefore, many images acquired underwater have blurred images with a bluish green hue and blurred and low visibility. This causes the inability to come out with important information especially to fields such as marine biology, archaeology and underwater research3. The requirement for image enhancement underwater is vital today since the usage of underwater photographs and videos in various disciplines is rising. Enhancement techniques enable image quality to be improved so that objects or scenes in images become more respective. Techniques used for enhancement include one or the other of image restoration and image enhancement algorithms. Image restoration, seeks to reverse distortions by taking into consideration the physical characteristics of the image including scattering and absorption while image enhancement tries to improve the perceived quality through processes such as contrast enhancement, colour correction and dehazing4,5.

The most used technique for improving the quality of underwater images is applying filters for color correction and visibility. Some of the more sophisticated approaches employ deep learning paradigms which utilizes massive repository of underwater images and learns to automatically correct the imagery6. These methods are often more effective than historical learning techniques in a number of applications and provide stable algorithms for detail reconstruction and color correction. Since the enhancement of the image increases the clarity and quality of the picture, underwater image enhancement also aids underwater research to some extent to observe marine structures and animals, conduct exploration, and improve the aesthetic value of underwater photography7,8. The process of enhancing underwater image is a complex technical problem due to specific physical characteristics of the water medium. Actually, depending on the type of water and the distance from the surface, the attenuation is selective with respect to the wavelength of the light. For example, red light penetrates only several meters and is reabsorbed after several meters making blue and green colors dominant in underwater scenes9. This absorption effect is then bordered by scattering, whereby light batters contaminating materials floating on water and distorts the resulting image. Also, sufficient ambient light is rarely available in underwater environment and often artificial light is used which could cause issues with harsh and or non-uniform shadowing. Combined together, these factors cause images captured underwater to be low contrast, soft and hazy most of the time requiring post processing to be comprehensible10,11.

In response to these effects, there are many enhancement methods employed today. Histogram equalization is one of the methods which are used to increase contrast of the images; the main idea is to make the distribution of pixel intensities as even as possible12. This helps in increasing the chances of obtaining lost details in the making of images. Another approach is the homomorphic filtering which, at the same time, acts as a contrast and sharpness boosting while mitigating the amplification of noise by uneven lighting. Apart from these methods, enhancement algorithms that are very smart have developed from Retinex based method to improve the local contrast and color equality with the way the human vision adapts to different lighting conditions13–15. Another facet with regards to underwater image enhancement is its importance in specified usage. For example, in marine biology, clearer images of the ocean help identify changes in coral reefs, observe fish abundance, and research aquatic environments. They use improved visualization methods to study underwater objects and wrecks by observing features hidden underneath the water layer16. Shaded images also have their usefulness in the industrial applications such as in observation of underwater pipes, cables and structures in which details provide clear distinction between defect areas and sound areas. Novelty in deep learning, nonetheless, has given rise to complex models including GANs and CNN-based structures that have been found to yield optimal results on image restoration and enhancement20. In this paper, we propose a new ensemble model of Pyramid-based CNNs, namely EPCNN-DCPDN, with an improved Deep Channel Prior Dehazing Network. A structural solution of the proposed model is expected to perform haze removal and multi-scale details simultaneously in underwater images. Our method of model evaluation shows that the proposed approach outperforms the nine existing state-of-the-art methods based on the PSNR, SSIM, and UIQM criteria concerning the improvement of image quality.

The enhancement of underwater images represents a fundamental issue for oceanographic studies alongside marine exploration and underwater surveillance and autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) operations. Fundamental reasons propelling this work revolve around ongoing difficulties with current enhancement technologies to handle underwater conditions characterized by haze and color distortion as well as low image contrast and reduced fine detail preservation. Traditional image enhancement techniques including histogram equalization, Compact Local Adaptive Histogram Equalization and Retinex-based approaches succeed in improving contrast but fail to redeem lost color data and eliminate scattering artifacts which arise from turbid waters. The image realism enhancement produced by GAN-based approaches WaterGAN and FUnIE-GAN often results in unreliable performance that affects high-precision underwater applications because they generate artifacts and inconsistent textures. We present EPCNN-DCPDN which unites two deep learning frameworks namely Pyramid-based CNNs (EPCNN) with Deep Channel Prior Dehazing Network (DCPDN) to address these issues. The rationale behind this method stems from requirements for an image processing technique which performs scale-based feature extraction while simultaneously improving contrast while dehazing and maintaining detail quality. Compared to standalone single-network systems the proposed ensemble methodology blends sequential and parallel processing methods to produce an optimized enhancement performance. The DCPDN network enables effective transmission estimation for dehazing together with Pyramid-based CNNs that perform multiscale fusion for precise enhancement of image details and structural elements.

The chosen approach proves suitable for diverse underwater conditions ranging from shallow to deep-sea zones and low-light underwater atmospherics. Current approaches can only generate effective enhancements for specific aquatic conditions because their enhancement parameters remain static. By adjusting its operational features and dehazing parameters according to conditions EPCNN-DCPDN maintains enhanced performance in multiple forms of underwater environments. Underwater enhancement model evaluations will receive thorough analysis through this research by using both standard assessment metrics and perceptual quality metrics. The common practice in previous studies is to report PSNR along with SSIM metrics but these values do not accurately measure underwater image quality. The evaluation consists of five measurement tools which include Underwater Colorfulness Measure (UCM) alongside Natural Image Quality Evaluator (NIQE) and Entropy-Based Image Quality (EIQ) as well as Underwater Image Sharpness Measure (UISM) and Feature Similarity Index (FSIM) to provide complete assessment of enhancement success. The EPCNN-DCPDN methodology aims to unify physical hazing models with deep learning enhancement techniques to develop superior solutions for real underwater image processing needs. The designed model strikes an effective balance between processing speed and quality improvement making it an auspicious choice for autonomous underwater controls and marine investigation as well as underwater detection systems.

Related works

The complex nature of underwater image enhancement occurs due to destructive processes including absorption and scattering of light and color distortion. The research community has developed several image enhancement models which include lightweight models and reinforcement learning techniques and multi-stage neural networks and single-image enhancement models to boost underwater image quality. The section introduces current techniques by discussing their achievements together with their described constraints.

Lightweight deep learning models for underwater image enhancement

Lightweight deep learning models try to enhance imaging quality under water by requiring lesser computational resources. The Rep-UWnet model combines RepConv blocks with skip connections to reach high efficiency in underwater image enhancement21. The SimSPPF feature extraction module of the model generates refined visual quality through a feature summation process from the original picture input. The parameter reduction of Rep-UWnet from 2.7 M to 0.45 M enables an 83% diminution which leads to state-of-the-art results in contrast together with color balance and clarity. This model excels at producing outstanding solutions in tasks like high-level vision edge detection and single-image depth estimation. The performance of Rep-UWnet declines in severe underwater environments because it fails to address the problems caused by hazy conditions and color distortions and changing noise levels. Under extreme underwater imaging situations it is necessary to develop advanced processing models with feature extraction mechanisms and adaptive methods to boost image clarity.

Reinforcement learning-based enhancement methods

Underwater image enhancement strategies are optimized through dynamic selection processes with reinforcement learning as the technique of choice. A reinforcement learning framework enables automatic selection of optimal enhancement methods which it adjusts for optimal results. This system functions differently than standard black-box deep learning models because it handles processes transparently as a white-box system. The framework uses human visual perception coupled with underwater color information to create non-reference score increments which steer the enhancement procedure22. These principles enable the model to eliminate the training barriers found in deep learning-based methods when they use manually labeled datasets for training. The training process of reinforcement learning models needs both significant amounts of data and powerful computational power which prevents them from being practical for real-time deployment. The system faces difficulties when trying to generalize across various underwater backgrounds because experts must develop hybrid methods that maintain efficient performance while generating high-quality enhancements.

Multi-stage deep learning models for underwater image enhancement

Multiple research works introduced multistage deep learning systems to enhance underwater images by counteracting their degradation through contrast loss and color distortion issues and structural alterations. The Divide-and-Conquer Network (DC-Net) introduces its enhancement strategy through three distinct components according to23. The network features a texture component with multi-axis attention blocks to combine regional with channel-wise features and color and refinement parts functioning through adaptive 3D Look-Up Tables for color correction and structure and artifact refinement. The processing of individual enhancement aspects leads to better underwater image clarity and accurate color representation through this method. The multiple stages involved in this complex enhancement process increases computational requirements making real-time applications more challenging to implement. The performance of DC-Net needs enhancement in dynamic underwater environments since it currently shows limited success in such rapidly changing lighting conditions.

Single-image enhancement models

Underwater image enhancement through deep learning models works with a single input image while solving problems related to light attenuation in addition to color shift and noise interference. A model based on Light Field Map (LFM) and Sketch Module allows detailed imagery reconstruction of underwater images24. Guided filtering methods within this model perform multiple recalculation steps to optimize image quality and enhance color presentation. The enhancement of underwater images through multi-step training integrates medium transmission map generation alongside atmospheric light estimation to yield improved restoration results. This model empowers image restoration by integrating a multi-discriminator GAN that enhances texture realism through its ability to detect artificial images across various color spaces when compared to real images. These single-image enhancement methods tend to fail at maintaining precise textures when dealing with underwater images because they find difficulty in deep-sea environments with intense light modifications. The adoption of large training datasets hampers such systems when dealing with real-world environments that lack sufficient data resources.

Identified research gaps

Research has succeeded in presenting light-weight models and reinforcement learning techniques and deep neural networks yet several unresolved problems exist. Traditional medium transmission-based models generate weak results when erasing haze in both dimly lit areas and deep-sea lighting conditions. Multi-discriminator GAN approaches boost image texture quality while creating unwanted artifacts which degrade image fidelity levels25. The selection capability of reinforcement learning models for enhancement strategy choice depends on extended training time and high computational power which limits their real-time use. The majority of CNN-based models struggle to grasp delicate multi-scale elements in images which results in necessary textural characteristics being overlooked. A robust adaptable model needs to be developed because it should perform simultaneous enhancement of contrast and restoration of color balance and structural refinement in various underwater conditions.

Methodology

The EPCNN-DCPDN model as a proposed methodology combines Pyramid based CNNs and Deep Channel Prior Dehazing Network, which effectively help in the enhancement of underwater images captured by the underwater robots. In the sequential approach, the input image is first de-hazed by DCPDN to eliminate haze in the image where a transmission map is estimated to minimize the influences of scattered and absorbed lights. The dehazed image is then subjected to multiple resolutions with the help of Pyramid-based CNNs to refine the features on fine levels as well as the structural schema of the image as a whole. Or in the parallel approach where, both models work in parallel to produce the outputs which are then fused using weighted average or learned fusion. EPCNN-DCPDN implements a hybrid sequential-parallel processing framework because it contrasts with standard multi-scale models using single enhancement pipelines. Under the sequential operation the dehazing module (DCPDN) executes haze elimination before EPCNN undertakes multi-scale feature refinement. During simultaneous processing in parallel mode the image moves through both modules until the adaptive fusion process selects the most beneficial result. Dual processing within EPCNN-DCPDN allows the method to perform better across all shallow and deep and low-light underwater conditions which existing models tend to fail.

A Pyramid-based CNN architecture presents an enhanced method for multi-scale feature fusion to protect image details specifically designed for underwater applications. The standard CNN models regularly discard delicate image details but our method successfully maintains structural elements together with detailed texture features to tackle previous enhancement model deficiencies.

The hazing methods Haze-Line Prior and WaterGAN tend to depend on static ground truths about water elements or apply GAN-based transformations that modify image appearance. Our system incorporates DCPDN’s adaptive transmission estimation functionality that lets the model modify its hazing intensity adaptively according to the underwater environment conditions. This innovation produces precise haze elimination by maintaining natural image appearance and deterring artificial distortions.

The EPCNN-DCPDN model features design elements which make it more applicable in real-world systems and efficient for scaling. The system provides real-time processing capabilities through both sequential and parallel operations to suit autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) and marine exploration and underwater surveillance needs which results in a superior solution than current models.

Image enhancement

The image enhancement process is central to solving the various problems related to imagery taken underwater including color shift, haziness and blurred images. Indoor illuminations are generally low and non-uniform, but underwater scenes in particular can be rather dirty and chromatically shifted due to light absorption, scattering in water. The enhancement workflow is aimed at fixing these problems in turn, making the images look better and preparing them for other processing steps, such as object detection and recognition.

Deep learning-based underwater image enhancement methods currently exist independently with single CNN-based enhancement models or physics-based priors while demonstrating restricted ability to adapt across different water conditions. The multi-scale approaches WaterGAN and FUnIE-GAN primarily focus on aesthetic improvements which results in structural compromise and artifact generation from adversarial training methods. The conventional dehazing methods which use DCP fail to adapt their transmission map model across different underwater conditions because they depend on fixed assumptions about water transmission.

Haze removal

Haze which is an effect of light particles have influence in water is one of the most difficult matters in underwater imagery. This haze gives images a foggy look due to the fact that it partially hides most of the fine details within an image. To handle this problem, dehazing approaches are used, and special attention is paid to the development of deep learning models, including the proposed DCPDN technique. This assumption assists in predicting the transmission map of the image that depicts how much light can penetrate the scene to the lenses of the camera.

Multi-scale feature refinement

The last operation concerning image enhancement directs to create a comprehensive multi-scale feature refinement. Due to the fact that light scattering and poor vision are common underwater problems, underwater images, including the details as well as large structural information, are easily lost. To retrieve such lost details and to make such improved image clear and distinct even in the higher resolutions, Pyramid-based CNNs are required. Pyramid-based CNNs are designed how they analyze the image at several resolutions at the same time. This approach enables the model to capture the gross flow like the contour and the boundary of an object in addition to capturing the fine grained features like texture and edges.

Proposed model - EPCNN-DCPDN (ensemble of pyramid-based CNNs and DCPDN)

The EPCNN-DCPDN model is a proposed ensemble approach designed to address the unique challenges of underwater image enhancement by combining the strengths of two powerful deep learning architectures: Pyramid based Convolutional Neural Network (CNNs) and Deep Channel Prior Dehazing Network (DCPDN). Strobe lit images of underwater scenes have numerous problems such as haze, low contrast and problems in resolving details at various scales due to both light absorption and scattering. Combined with the use of two models simultaneously, the proposed EPCNN-DCPDN model enhances image sharpness, reconstructs missing details, and enhances image quality in underwater images.

In the sequential processing method, it starts with the DCPDN in order to reduce haze to enhance the images. DCPDN is proposed to obtain reduced haze effect by learning the transmission map and reconstruct the scene radiance based on the Dark Channel Prior (DCP). This step is probably most important for enhancing overall underwater image quality, as increasing light scattering results in a ‘foggy’ or ‘blurry’ appearance in images. When haze is filtered out, the feature of the objects that dwell in water such as fish, coral, or other sea creatures becomes more visible and the overall contrast in the picture rises. Using the DCPDN model, which is capable of learning haze patterns through deep learning, the system remains scalable to different underwater environments thanks to the training data input.

After that, the image will be taken and be assessed through the Pyramid-based CNNs in order to refine multi-scale details of the enhanced image. Pyramid-based CNNs operate with the idea that the image is processed at multiple resolutions at once, this way the model can attend to higher level features and at the same time pinpoint to the finer details on the image. When capturing an underwater scene, fine details are washed out by scattering and absorption as compared to non-underwater scenes in regions of low and high lighting contrast. Through implementation at these two scales, the Pyramid-based CNNs make sure that the fine textural and structural details are recovered while retaining the composition of the image. This sequential manner helps to ensure that the haze layer, which is the main cause of image degradation, is first eliminated by the DCPDN, before Pyramid-based CNNs help restore lost details due to underwater imaging.

The second approach in the proposed EPCNN-DCPDN model is the parallel processing and then fusion model. In this case, the input image is also fed into both, the DCPDN and Pyramid-based CNNs but the two architectures operate in parallel on the image fed to them. The DCPDN helps in removing haze and gives a better visibility whereas Pyramid based CNNs are help full in improving the details at multi resolution. The significant benefit of this approach is that every model can learn diverse features of the image by having the DCPDN for the global hazy issues while the Pyramid-based CNNs concentrate on smaller areas in the image.

Model evaluation

The performance of the EPCNN-DCPDN model used for restoring underwater images needs to be assessed to determine its ability to clear haze, correct color, and increase visibility and detail. The combination of the qualitative and quantitative factors is employed in order to ensure the accurate assessment of the model’s effectiveness to restore image quality. With regard to underwater image enhancement, quantitative metrics involve parameters that enable one to determine how well the enhanced imagery correlates with actual images or a predefined set. To assess various aspects of the model three quantitative indicators, namely the PSNR, SSIM and the UIQM are used.

Peak signal-to-noise ratio (PSNR)

PSNR is one of the most used objective precise measures when discussing the quality of image restoration in image processing field. It quantifies the relationship between the maximum possible value of a pixel and the power of the noise that distorts image quality. Concerning underwater image enhancement, PSNR assists in evaluating the enhanced image generated by the model, to a reference ground truth image. Since a higher PSNR value means that the enhanced image is closer to the ground truth and less noisy and better restoration quality. In underwater image enhancement, PSNR is useful in assessing the ability of the model to reconstruct the otherwise lost image as a result of scattering by water and loss of color. Although PSNR offers a primary quantitation of picture quality, for example, PSNR cannot perfectly mimic human visual perception such as the structure information retention where other metrics including SSIM come with the equation.

Structural similarity index (SSIM)

While PSNR checks pixel spatial inaccuracy individually, the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) also analyzes images’ proxy into structural likeness. SSIM considers three key aspects of visual perception: Luminance contrast and structure are three key criteria that an image should have to qualify for display on any particular medium. It is also more useful for enhancing underwater image because it measures how well the enhanced image retains the global structure and prominent textural features of the scene as opposed to minimizing pixel errors only.

SSIM falls between − 1 and 1; the border line 1 stands for enhanced image having identical resemblance with the reference images. In the context of underwater image improvement, it is beneficial to maintain a high SSIM as it guarantees the preservation of the important structural and textural features of the underwater environment including the margins of marine life, corals, and other objects adjunct to haze and color improvement. SSIM, therefore, offers a more faithful estimate than PSNR in terms of perceptual quality as shown in underwater scenes where structural details are important for a realistic depiction of scenes.

Underwater image quality measure (UIQM)

The Underwater Image Quality Measure (UIQM) is a specialized, unique measure which has solely been developed to measure the quality of underwater images. It accounts for issues that are related to underwater circumstances like color fading, low contrast and image sharpness due to light diffuser and absorber. The UIQM can be obtained from UICM, UISM, and UIConM; these sub-metrics carry out assessments on picture colors, focuses, and contrasts, respectively. In order to be compared at a final UIQM score, each of these sub-metrics are weighted and added together, offering an all-encompassing method in which the improved image quality may be evaluated.

Novelty of the work

The novelty of this work lies in the development of the EPCNN-DCPDN ensemble model, which uniquely combines Pyramid-based CNNs and the Deep Channel Prior Dehazing Network (DCPDN) to simultaneously address multiple underwater image degradation issues such as haze, color distortion, and loss of fine details. Unlike existing models that focus on either dehazing or image refinement separately, the proposed model integrates both processes in a single framework. This dual approach—sequential and parallel processing—offers flexibility to adapt to varying underwater conditions, from shallow clear waters to deep murky environments. The multi-scale feature refinement in Pyramid-based CNNs ensures that both global structures and fine textures are preserved, while DCPDN efficiently removes haze caused by light scattering. This integrated method provides superior image clarity, better color correction, and enhanced contrast, making the model highly effective for real-world applications like underwater exploration, object detection, and marine research, surpassing the performance of existing models.

The model operates in two primary phases: haze removal through Deep Channel Prior Dehazing Network (DCPDN) and details prior through Pyramid- based Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). In the sequential processing approach, the DCPDN is the first component which the image has to go through. This model eliminates the frequently observed underwater fog as it first, estimates the transmission map of the scene and secondly, reconstructs the scene radiance using a built-in Dark Channel Prior. The DCPDN has a good manner in dealing with the problems of gathering and dispersing light in water and ensures a high readability and contrast of the image formed. After the haze is eliminated, the Pyramid based CNNs then further optimize the dehazed image based on features of varying spatial resolutions. When applying a Pyramid-based CNN to the image, structural information is preserved, and fine textures are restored, which would have been eliminated due to haze or poor lighting, and the resulting image structure and sharpness follow the pyramid arrangement. In the second method also called parallel processing and fusion the input image is fed to both the DCPDN and the Pyramid-based CNNs at the same time. The DCPDN specifically targets haze removal from the global content with the Pyramid-based CNNs enhancing the details of the image across different resolutions. The output of these two models is then combined, either by a weighted average, or a learned fusion method. This is because this method enables the model to have multiple objectives for image restoration where cue reasoning restores both, large scale features and small details and structures.

The Entropy-Based Image Quality (EIQ) assessment determines how much information maintains its presence within an image. The measure of preservation for fine details and textures increases when EIQ value goes up. EPCNN-DCPDN achieves an EIQ score of 7.12 which exceeds the scores of 6.73 for WaterGAN and 6.45 for FUnIE-GAN. Troubled deep sea detection and navigational systems benefit from the proposed model because it restores missing details alongside improved texture sharpness and achieves better scores in quantitative analyses. The quantification of image edge definitions plus fine structures relies on the Underwater Image Sharpness Measure (UISM). EPCNN-DCPDN reveals superior performance against other models since it achieves a UISM rating of 4.58 while WaterGAN reaches 4.02 and Haze-Line Prior stands at 3.95. The proposed model demonstrates superior effectiveness in recovering detailed features because this strength matters in underwater research investigations and robotic operations as well as object recognition work. The evaluation of enhanced images based on the Feature Similarity Index (FSIM examines their capacity to maintain structural details. By implementing EPCNN-DCPDN the researchers obtained the peak FSIM score of 0.92 which surpassed both WaterGAN (0.87) and Haze-Line Prior (0.86). The proposed model preserves structural characteristics and establishes better visual clarity in its performance.

Conclusion and future work

The EPCNN-DCPDN model developed in this research provides a reliable answer to the underwater image enhancement challenges. The Deep Channel Prior Dehazing Network (DCPDN) used alongside Pyramid-based CNNs delivers effective improvements to image clarity as well as color balance and sharpness. The proposed model showed superior performance compared to nine state-of-the-art models which included CLAHE and FUnIE-GAN as well as WaterGAN and Haze-Line Prior Model for dealing with different underwater situations. The model exhibited performance excellence through SSIM 0.902 and PSNR 28.34 dB and UIQM 3.56 which exceeded all current hazy image restoration methods. The model exhibited exceptional performance by reaching 97.92% accuracy while processing shallow scenes and deep underwater locations and dark underwater conditions on different datasets. The new model demonstrates superior capability to restore detail structures and minimize underwater hazy conditions while representing a great option for marine investigative and underwater perception functions and object recognition applications. The current research identifies both benefits and drawbacks of EPCNN-DCPDN that need resolution through future improvements. The main challenge with this system arises from its excessive computational requirements. The multi-scale features combined with the sequential dehazing process elevate the processing requirements of this model beyond those of Rep-UWnet and traditional enhancement methods. Real-time application restriction exists for EPCNN-DCPDN because of its elevated computational demands that affect its utility with low-power embedded systems and underwater drones. The system faces restrictions because it requires extensive data variety. The performance of EPCNN-DCPDN on benchmark underwater test sets demonstrates good results but researchers have not confirmed how it handles unknown water conditions, different turbidity conditions and lighting variations beyond the original baseline datasets. To achieve better generalization in unseen underwater settings the model needs either domain adaptation methods or self-supervised training approaches. DCPDN through its Deep Channel Prior Dehazing Network successfully resolves haze and increases visibility although it generates minor artifacts when transmission map estimation fails because of underwater extreme situations characterized by low-light or high sediment turbidity. The problems can be solved by adopting adaptive learning methods in combination with reinforcement learning strategies for selecting enhancements. The upcoming stage of our research involves designing MENet by consolidating MSFF-Net features with EUVP-Net visual prior capabilities. The model will enhance its feature detection capabilities through multiple resolutions by implementing specific underwater visual priors which leads to superior results under complicated water conditions. We strive to enhance both the operational speed and performance of the model for real-time applications in autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) and remotely operated vehicles (ROVs).

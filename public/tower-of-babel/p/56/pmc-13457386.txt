Comparative Study: Phaco with Micropulse Cyclophotocoagulation vs Phaco with Endoscopic Cyclophotocoagulation
Authors: Medhat A Bakr, Mohammed Falah Aljsir, Saad Mutlaq Alosaimi, Khalid A Alanazi, Ahmed Mohammed Alzahrani, Ussama Moustafa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and ObjectivesGlaucoma is a leading cause of irreversible blindness, primarily managed by lowering intraocular pressure (IOP). In individuals with both cataract and glaucoma, phacoemulsification alone can substantially reduce IOP. However, to achieve better pressure control, combining cataract surgery with cyclophotocoagulation has gained attention as an effective approach. Among the available methods, micropulse transscleral cyclophotocoagulation (MP-TCP) and endoscopic cyclophotocoagulation (ECP) represent two techniques that target the ciliary body through distinct mechanisms, differing in their level of invasiveness and therapeutic outcomes. The objective of this study is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of phacoemulsification combined with MP-TCP compared with phacoemulsification combined with ECP in patients presenting with both cataract and glaucoma.Materials and methodsIn this randomized trial-based study, adults aged 40–80 years undergoing cataract surgery with glaucoma were enrolled and randomized to receive either phaco + MP-TCP or phaco + ECP. Primary outcomes included variations in IOP, several IOP-lowering therapeutics, clarity of vision [logMAR (minimum angle of resolution)], and complications observed over 12 months postoperative follow-up.ResultsBaseline characteristics were comparable between ECP (24 eyes) and MP-TCP (15 eyes) groups. Both interventions significantly reduced IOP over time, with the endoscope group showing greater early reductions at 1 week and 3 months, although differences were no longer significant by 6 and 12 months. Medication burden decreased in both groups, with MP demonstrating a greater reduction at 1 week but no significant differences afterward. Visual acuity improved similarly across both treatments throughout follow-up. Complication rates were low and comparable (20.8 vs 21.4%). Overall, endoscopic treatment achieved larger early IOP reductions, while long-term outcomes, including IOP control, medication use, visual acuity, and safety, were similar between the two interventions.ConclusionBoth phaco + MP-TCP and phaco + ECP are effective, safe surgical options for managing glaucoma in patients undergoing cataract surgery. ECP offers superior early IOP reduction, whereas MP-TCP more effectively reduces medication dependence. The choice of technique should be tailored to individual clinical priorities.Clinical significancePhaco + MP-TCP and phaco + ECP are safe and efficacious options for patients with coexisting cataract and glaucoma, with ECP providing greater early IOP reduction and MP-TCP offering sustained medication burden relief. The choice of procedure should be individualized based on patient priorities and clinical needs.Keywords: Endoscopic photocoagulation, Glaucoma, Intraocular pressure, Micropulse, Phacoemulsification, Transscleral photocoagulation

Introduction

Glaucoma is a chronic, progressive optic neuropathy and a main cause of permanent visual impairment globally. It is characterized by optic nerve damage frequently associated with elevated intraocular pressure (IOP). It encompasses a spectrum of conditions that initially impair peripheral vision and, if left untreated, can ultimately result in complete blindness.1 Due to its insidious onset and the absence of symptoms in early stages, routine eye examinations are essential for early detection and timely intervention. The estimated global prevalence of glaucoma among individuals aged 40 years and older is 3.54%, and this figure is projected to increase significantly by 2040.2

In the United States, approximately 2 million adults are currently diagnosed with glaucoma, although it is estimated that over 50% of cases remain unidentified.3 In Asia, particularly in South Central and East Asia, the burden of glaucoma is among the highest globally, with steep increases in prevalence also projected for the South Central Asian region.4 Furthermore, adult glaucoma prevalence in Middle Eastern Asia has been identified as significantly greater than in European and Western countries.5,6

Reduction of IOP continues to be the primary strategy in glaucoma management to slow disease progression and preserve vision. Elevated IOP is the principal modifiable risk factor for glaucoma, and its reduction is the only proven method to slow disease progression.7 The optic nerve damage seen in glaucoma is strongly related to an imbalance in aqueous fluid production and drainage within the eye.8 Management approaches are diverse and include pharmacological therapies, laser treatments, and surgical interventions, all focused on reducing IOP. First-line therapies for glaucoma include medications such as prostaglandin analogs (PGAs), beta-blockers, and carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (CAIs). These are frequently used medications; however, they can have systemic side effects.9 Laser therapy and surgical procedures like trabeculectomy serve as important adjuncts when pharmacologic management is insufficient.7

Innovative surgical strategies, such as combining phacoemulsification (phaco) with micropulse transscleral cyclophotocoagulation (MP-TCP) or with endoscopic cyclophotocoagulation (ECP), have emerged for the concurrent treatment of cataract and glaucoma. Phacoemulsification is an established technique for cataract extraction (CE), and its integration with cyclophotocoagulation targets the dual pathology of cataracts and elevated IOP. In phaco with MP-TCP, MP laser energy is applied toward cilia, processes to reduce aqueous production and humor formation while restricting collateral tissue injury caused by the pulsed nature of the energy delivery. This technique offers a potentially safer profile compared to traditional continuous-wave cyclophotocoagulation. The MP laser (usually 810 nm with a duty cycle of ~31.3%) is applied across the pars plicata region through the sclera, aiming to partially destroy or modify the ciliary processes to reduce aqueous humor production. The procedure is quick and can be done through small external applications without incisions.10,11 Phaco + MP-TCP is considered for glaucoma patients undergoing CE who require additional IOP lowering or medication reduction. It is suitable for mild, moderate, and even some advanced glaucomas (except severe pathologies such as neovascular glaucoma, which is often excluded). The combination leverages the modest IOP reduction from phaco (which by itself typically lowers IOP by ~1–3 mm Hg) plus the added effect of ciliary ablation. The goal is to achieve greater IOP reduction and a decrease in glaucoma medications with a safe, combined procedure.11,12

Conversely, phaco with ECP uses endoscopic visualization to directly deliver laser energy to the ciliary processes. In glaucoma management, ECP is a procedure that is performed during or after cataract surgery, using a small intraocular endoscope through a camera and diode laser to directly visualize and treat the ciliary processes, lowering aqueous humor production and IOP. Laser is applied through a limbal incision under direct vision, typically treating 180°–270°, and up to 360° if needed, with minimal collateral damage compared to blind transscleral approaches. Phaco-ECP is especially useful for individuals with coexisting cataract and mild-to-moderate primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG), particularly in surgical-naïve eyes, as it is implant-free, preserves the conjunctiva, and provides an early, safe IOP-lowering option to minimally invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS).13,14

Both approaches leverage the opportunity presented by cataract surgery to concurrently address glaucoma. Several studies have demonstrated that phacoemulsification is effective in managing glaucoma; direct comparisons between these two integrated techniques (phaco with MP-TCP vs phaco with ECP) remain limited in the literature.15,16 Therefore, this study is designed to determine the extent of IOP lowering following treatment (phaco combined with MP-TCP vs phaco combined with ECP), assess changes in the use and effectiveness of antiglaucoma medications, and identify and categorize any treatment-related complications, both minor and major.

Methods

Study Design and Duration

The research was carried out as a randomized controlled experiment that was prospective. The randomization sequence was generated using the National Cancer Institute Clinical Trial Randomization Tool by an independent statistician and was computer-generated and stratified by glaucoma severity, age group, gender, and socioeconomic status. Allocation concealment was ensured through sequentially numbered, sealed opaque envelopes prepared by an independent statistician. The study coordinator enrolled participants, while treatment assignments were concealed until the point of allocation. Clinicians administering the interventions were blinded to the randomization list. The study's duration (14 May 2024 to 30 May 2024) was established following the feasibility evaluation; however, the recruitment of study participants started in 2024, after ethical approval (DESH1148) was obtained.

Study Settings and Population

The study was conducted at the Dhahran Eye Specialist Hospital and King Fahad University Hospital, Saudi Arabia. The target population of the study was adults between the ages of 40 and 80 years requiring cataract surgery for glaucoma.

Participant Selection Criteria

Eligible participants were adults between 40 and 80 years of age with visually significant cataract [best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) ≤ 20/40, Lens Opacities Classification System III (LOCS III) ≥ 2] and confirmed primary open-angle, normal-tension, pseudoexfoliative, or pigmentary glaucoma (PG); IOP ≥ 21 mm Hg on stable medical therapy or documented progression despite maximal tolerated medications; reliable Humphrey visual fields (MD ≥ –12 dB) with retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL) ≥ 60 µm; deemed suitable for standard phacoemulsification with posterior-chamber IOL; and able to provide informed consent and adhere to a 12-month follow-up schedule. Exclusion criteria include prior incisional glaucoma or cyclodestructive surgery, ocular trauma, secondary glaucomas (neovascular, uveitic, and steroid induced), narrow/closed angles (Shaffer ≤ 2) or significant peripheral anterior synechiae (PAS), active intraocular inflammation or advanced retinal disease, corneal decompensation (ECC < 1,000 cells/mm²), uncontrolled systemic disease (BP > 180/110 mm Hg, HbA1c > 9%), bleeding disorders or nonpausable anticoagulation, pregnancy or breastfeeding, relevant medication allergies, poor compliance, or participation in another interventional trial within the past 3 months.

Sample Size Calculation

Based on IOP data from a previous study,17 we performed a sample-size calculation in ClinCalc (α = 0.05, two-sided; power = 80%; 95% confidence interval) and determined that 18 subjects per group are required.

Phacoemulsification with Micropulse Transscleral Cyclophotocoagulation

Phacoemulsification with MP-TCP begins with standard phacoemulsification to remove the cataract. After administering anesthesia (either topical, regional, or general, based on the patient's needs), a corneal incision is performed. The anterior chamber is supported with a viscoelastic agent before creating a continuous curvilinear capsulorhexis. Hydrodissection is performed to loosen the lens, and ultrasonic energy is then used to break up and aspirate the cataractous lens material. Following phacoemulsification, a foldable intraocular lens (IOLs) is implanted into the capsular bag this part of the procedure. Following phacoemulsification, MP-TCP is performed together. Using the MP P3 probe, followed by direct application of an 810 nm diode laser to the ciliary body. The laser parameters typically, the diode laser was set at 2,000 mW with a 31.3% duty cycle, and the probe was moved in a slow, continuous “painting” motion over the superior and inferior ciliary body hemispheres, deliberately while avoiding the 3 and 9 o'clock positions to protect the long ciliary nerves. Treatment time per hemisphere usually takes 90 seconds for every half (Fig. 1).

Phacoemulsification with MP transscleral cyclophotocoagulation

Phacoemulsification with Endoscopic Cyclophotocoagulation

Phacoemulsification was performed as described above. Once the lens is in place, an endoscopic probe is inserted either through the main phacoemulsification incision or a separate corneal paracentesis. Through direct endoscopic visualization on a monitor, the surgeon targets the ciliary processes. Laser energy is then precisely applied to the ciliary body at a power setting between 0.2 and 0.5 W in a continuous-wave mode. Treatment typically covers 200°–270° of the ciliary processes. Laser effectiveness was confirmed by observing blanching and shrinkage of the ciliary processes. Precautions are taken to avoid excessive ablation, which could risk postoperative hypotony (Fig. 2).

Ethical Considerations

The Institutional Review Board of Dhahran Eye Specialist Hospital (Saudi Arabia) authorized the study protocol before it started, and each participant gave their written informed consent.

Statistical Analysis

The primary analysis followed the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle: All randomized participants were included in the analysis according to their original assigned groups. Missing data were minimal; however, when present, they were handled using last-observation-carried-forward (LOCF) imputation to preserve the randomized treatment structure. A perprotocol sensitivity analysis was also performed to ensure robustness of results, yielding consistent findings.

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 26. The Shapiro–Wilk test was applied to assess the normality of each variable. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, whereas continuous variables were presented as median [interquartile range (IQR)] for non-normally distributed data. Friedman was used to examine changes over time within and between study groups. The Mann–Whitney U test was employed to compare the change from baseline at each time point between groups. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All tests were two-tailed, with 95% confidence intervals reported.

Results

The CONSORT 2025 flow diagram outlines the progression of participants through a randomized trial involving two groups, from enrollment to data analysis. Initially, 43 individuals were assessed for eligibility, of which 4 were excluded (2 did not meet the inclusion criteria, 1 declined participation, and 1 for other reasons). The remaining 39 participants were randomized, with 24 allocated to the endoscope and 15 to MP. All participants in both groups received their allocated interventions. During follow-up, two participants in the MP group and seven in the endoscope group were lost to follow-up for the primary result. Finally, 13 participants from the MP group and 16 from the endoscope group were examined for the primary result (Fig. 3).

At baseline, no statistically significant differences were observed between the MP and endoscopic groups regarding key demographic and clinical characteristics. In terms of laterality, right eyes [oculus dexter (OD)] accounted for 40% in the MP group and 45.8% in the endoscopic group while 60 and 54.2% were left [oculus sinister (OS)] (p = 0.721). Participants in the MP group had a median age of 67 years, compared to 66 years in the endoscopic group, showing no significant difference (p = 0.479). The gender distribution was also similar, with males comprising 53.3% of the MP group and 54.2% of the endoscopic group. Analysis of glaucoma types revealed that POAG was the most common diagnosis in both groups, with 80% in the MP group and 83.3% in the endoscopic group. Other types of glaucoma, including primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG), pseudoexfoliative glaucoma (PXG), PG, normal-tension glaucoma (NTG), and narrow-angle glaucoma (NAG), were rare and distributed evenly without significant differences (p = 0.392). These findings confirm the successful randomization of the study population, resulting in comparable groups at the start of the study (Table 1).

Discussion

Both combined procedures (phacoemulsification with MP-TCP and with ECP) were effective for patients with coexisting cataract and glaucoma, with balanced baseline characteristics confirming successful randomization. ECP produced a larger early IOP drop (1 week and 3 months), whereas MP-TCP showed a slower IOP decline but a significant and sustained reduction in glaucoma medication use. By 6 and 12 months, IOP outcomes were comparable and medication use was similar between groups. Visual acuity improved significantly and to a similar degree in both groups throughout follow-up, and both procedures had acceptable safety profiles with no major complications. Overall, both surgical combinations are viable. ECP favors early pressure reduction, MP-TCP favors medication minimization.

Unlike findings from this study, Nirappel et al.17 reported greater IOP reduction with phacoemulsification plus MP-TCP than with phacoemulsification plus ECP; however, both approaches were comparable at 6 and 12 months, as in our study. This discrepancy may be because of Nirappel's retrospective design, differences in baseline IOP and medication use, and population differences.17 Results from this study agree with another report showing similar IOP medication use, logMAR visual acuity, and complication rates between the two treatments.18 Previous studies found phacoemulsification alone produces modest, nonsignificant IOP reductions (Arthur et al.19: –2.5 mm Hg; Poley et al.20: –2.7 mm Hg at 1 year), with the magnitude of reduction related to baseline IOP. Phaco/ECP has become a common adjunct because of its simplicity and low complication rate.21,22 Retrospective cohorts23 reported higher 1-year surgical success for phaco/ECP vs phaco alone (69.6 vs 40%, p = 0.004) and more sustained IOP lowering, though long-term effectiveness is uncertain. Siegel et al.21 found no significant difference at 3 years, while Lindfield et al.22 documented an average of –7.11 mm Hg at 18 months without a significant reduction in glaucoma medications.

Transscleral cyclophotocoagulation has demonstrated strong IOP-lowering efficacy, with Zhekov et al.24 reporting that 67.7% of patients achieved >30% IOP reduction within 6 weeks along with a decrease of one glaucoma medication, although hypotony was four times more common in neovascular glaucoma. Similar outcomes were noted by Kosoko et al.25 after a single cyclodiode treatment. A meta-analysis by Hauber and Scherer26 showed a linear relationship between treatment success (IOP < 22 mm Hg) and total laser energy, yet hypotony remained the most frequent long-term complication. Murphy et al.27 found increased hypotony risk in neovascular glaucoma, especially with high preoperative IOP and higher energy delivery, a finding shown in the UK National Cyclodiode Laser Survey by Agrawal et al.28 To standardize treatment parameters, Lanzagorta-Aresti et al.29 excluded neovascular glaucoma due to routinely reduced energy use in these cases to avoid complications such as ischemic ciliary body-related hypotony. Similarly, Iliev and Gerber30 observed that limiting laser applications or lowering power can reduce hypotony risk.

Cohen et al.31 reported that ECP carries fewer complications while remaining effective. Chen et al.32 (retrospective, refractory glaucoma) found success rates of 94% at 1 year and 82% at 2 years (success = IOP < 21 mm Hg, with or without medications), with mean glaucoma medications decreasing from 3.0 ± 1.3 to 2.0 ± 1.3. Francis et al.33 observed a 23–35% IOP reduction over 6–24 months in patients with prior aqueous tube shunts, accompanied by a reduction in mean glaucoma medications from 1.8 to 1.5 per patient, although a trend toward higher IOP and increased prescription requirements was noted. Lima et al.34 conducted a prospective comparison (ECP over 210° via the pars plana vs Ahmed valve) in 68 patients with uncontrolled IOP and reported success rates of 74% (ECP) and 71% (Ahmed). Gayton et al.35 compared CE with trabeculectomy to ECP and found the ECP group achieved a 30% success rate for maintaining IOP < 19 mm Hg without medications vs 42% for trabeculectomy. Reported postoperative complications included fibrin exudate (Chen et al.32: 24%; present study: 33%), hyphema (Chen et al.32: 12%; present study: 10%), cystoid macular edema (CME) (Chen et al.32: 10%; present study: 13%), two-line vision loss (6% in both Chen et al.32 and the present study), and choroidal detachment (Chen et al.32: 4%; present study: 3%).

Multiple retrospective studies show that phaco-ECP provides a moderate additional IOP reduction over phaco alone, along with meaningful medication reduction.36 In a large London cohort of naïve POAG eyes, IOP decreased from 18.4 to 13.6 mm Hg over 3 years with medication use halved, success rates ranging from 70% at 1 year to 45% at 3 years, and only mild complications were reported.14 A prospective nonrandomized study also found greater 2-year IOP and medication reduction with phaco-ECP than phaco alone.18 Comparisons with phaco-trabeculectomy show both lower IOP, though trabeculectomy achieves greater reduction at the cost of more serious complications, whereas phaco-ECP demonstrates higher qualified success and lower reoperation rates.36 A large 24-month retrospective analysis reported significant IOP lowering across phaco-ECP, phaco + iStent, and phaco + iStent + ECP, with success rates of 54.4, 75.3, and 55.6%, respectively, indicating stronger performance for phaco + iStent and modest added early benefit from combining iStent with ECP.37

A case series by Daas et al.38 reported that phacoemulsification combined with MP-TCP produced a significant IOP reduction, falling to 15 mm Hg at 6–12 months from a preoperative level reported around 19.5 mm Hg (4–5 mm Hg mean decrease; p < 0.001) with qualifying success (≥20% IOP reduction without additional surgery/medication increase) in 56% at 6 months and 54% at 12 months.38 Mean glaucoma medications decreased from 2.8 preoperatively to 1.6 at 6 months and 2.2 at 12 months (p < 0.001).38 Visual acuity generally remained stable or improved (43% ≥1 line improvement, 55% stable at 12 months).38 The safety profile was favorable with no reported vision-threatening complications over 12 months. The most common adverse events were mild anterior uveitis (10 patients) and CME (5 patients), one case of persistent uveitis, one case of persistent early CME, and five eyes that required subsequent glaucoma surgery.38

Regarding the complication risk, both procedures are generally safe but have different profiles. MP-TCP, while designed to minimize severe complications like hypotony and phthisis compared to traditional continuous-wave TCP,39 is linked to an increased incidence of transient postoperative inflammation.11 In contrast, phaco + ECP tends to induce less severe postoperative inflammation. ECP, however, has its risks. Since it involves intraocular manipulation, it carries a slightly higher chance of early IOP spikes, with one study reporting such spikes shortly after surgery.40 There is also a theoretical risk of mechanical trauma, including zonular damage, which could predispose patients to vitreous prolapse in future surgeries. Still, serious vision-threatening complications are rare with either technique. Ciliary atrophy following phaco with ECP is typically an intended outcome to reduce IOP, but excessive atrophy should be avoided to prevent complications like hypotony. Although ciliary atrophy is a well-known side effect of ECP,41 none of our patients reported having this complication.

Cost is another important differentiating factor between phaco + MP-TCP and phaco + ECP. MP treatment uses a diode laser system and a single-use disposable probe, meaning each case incurs a recurring cost related to the consumable probe, typically in the range of $300–$400 per eye. ECP requires a more significant upfront investment in the endoscopic equipment, but the probes are reusable for multiple cases (usually 10–12 uses), substantially lowering the per-case consumable cost once the system is in place. Economic analyses show that the average cost per ECP procedure decreases significantly as case volume increases; in a UK study, the per-case cost dropped from about £819 in early cases to £342 after achieving higher volume use.42 Conversely, MP-TCP maintains a relatively fixed cost structure due to the one-time use of each probe,43 making it potentially more expensive per case in high-volume centers, but easier to implement without major initial investments. Operating room time also factors into cost considerations. Because ECP involves intraocular maneuvering and careful targeting, it typically adds 10–15 minutes to surgical time compared to phaco alone.44 MP-TCP, on the other hand, can often be performed rapidly after CE with minimal additional operative time, which may translate into lower facility and staffing costs.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged when evaluating the study's conclusions. First, the sample size was small due to attrition, which may have reduced statistical power to identify modest changes between treatment groups, especially at later follow-up periods. A larger cohort would strengthen the reliability and generalizability of the results. Second, the follow-up duration, although extending to 1 year, may not be sufficient to fully assess the long-term stability of IOP control, medication burden reduction, and visual acuity outcomes. Glaucoma is a chronic condition, thus extended follow-up is necessary to assess whether the reported benefit persists over time or if late problems develop.

Third, the randomization method, while effective in balancing baseline characteristics, was based on clinical factors such as glaucoma severity, age, gender, and socioeconomic status. However, other potential confounders, such as baseline optic nerve status, corneal thickness, or preoperative visual field loss, were not stratified or controlled, which might introduce bias in treatment effect estimation. Fourth, short-term complications were assessed, but long-term complications were not. Due to which we may have missed clinically significant late-onset issues such as chronic hypotony maculopathy, CME, and endothelial cell loss.

Fifth, this study mainly assessed IOP, medication use, and logMAR visual acuity. Also, glaucoma-related outcomes, including structural changes of the optic nerve and functional progression of the visual field, were not evaluated, which could have offered a more comprehensive understanding of disease control. Additionally, the higher baseline values observed in patients undergoing ECP compared with those receiving MP-TCP may have introduced bias into the results.

Recommendations

In light of the study findings, the following recommendations are suggested for clinical practice implications and directions for future research both phacoemulsification combined with MP-TCP and phacoemulsification combined with ECP as effective surgical options for patients with both cataract and glaucoma. Clinicians should consider patient-specific factors when selecting the most appropriate technique. In patients where the primary goal is associated with a substantial reduction IOP shortly after surgery, ECP may be preferred given its greater early pressure-lowering effect observed in this study. Conversely, in patients for whom reducing the burden of glaucoma medications is a higher priority, MP-TCP may offer greater advantages due to its consistent reduction in medication use over time.

Second, patient counseling should emphasize that while both procedures are effective, the expected outcomes differ slightly between techniques. Surgeons should thoroughly discuss with patients the benefits, potential risks, and recovery expectations based on their clinical profiles, severity of glaucoma, lifestyle needs, and adherence to postoperative care. Third, future studies should use bigger, multicenter designs with extended follow-up to confirm and broaden these findings. An extended follow-up would provide more insight into the durability of IOP control, the stability of visual outcomes, and the long-term safety of each procedure. Moreover, future research should incorporate detailed evaluations of optic nerve health (such as optical coherence tomography), functional visual outcomes (visual fields), and patient-reported quality of life metrics to capture a broader and more clinically meaningful picture of glaucoma management success.

Fourth, randomized controlled trials that include masking of outcome assessors would be beneficial to reduce potential bias. Additionally, stratified randomization based on optic nerve status, baseline IOP levels, and visual field damage could enhance the robustness of the comparative results between surgical techniques. Fifth, there is a need to explore the cost-effectiveness of each surgical approach, particularly in healthcare systems where resource allocation is a major concern. Assessing the economic burden of each procedure, including surgical costs, postoperative medication use, and need for additional interventions, would offer valuable guidance for policymakers and healthcare providers. Finally, the development of standardized protocols for postoperative management and complication reporting in studies involving glaucoma interventions would improve comparability between studies and enhance clinical decision-making. Incorporating standardized patient education programs to improve adherence and monitoring would also likely optimize surgical outcomes.

Conclusion

This study found that both phacoemulsification combined with MP-TCP and phacoemulsification combined with ECP are effective and safe surgical alternatives for treating patients who have both cataracts and glaucoma. Both techniques resulted in significant improvements in visual acuity and provided effective IOP control during a 12-month follow-up. However, distinct patterns of outcomes were observed between the two approaches.

Endoscopic cyclophotocoagulation was related to a more substantial and statistically meaningful early decrease in IOP. In contrast, MP-TCP achieved a more consistent and significant reduction in the need for glaucoma medications. Importantly, visual acuity outcomes were similarly favorable present in both groups, showing no significant differences in the degree of visual improvement.

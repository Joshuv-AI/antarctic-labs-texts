Spatiotemporal patterns and driving mechanisms of terrestrial carbon sinks in Jiangxi Province, subtropical China
Authors: Xu Song, Xi Chen, Bofu Zheng, Wei Wan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights Significant and persistent degradation of NEP is identified in urban agglomeration areas.Critical thresholds of topography conditions for NEP variation were determined.Interaction between soil type and land use patterns is the key driver of NEP variations.Region-specific optimization strategies for the dual carbon goals are proposed.

Introduction

Global climate change profoundly impacts the sustainable development of human society [1]. Addressing climate change actively and fostering a low-carbon economy have become a widespread consensus within the international community [2]. Achieving the goals of “carbon peak and carbon neutrality” requires not only vigorous efforts to reduce carbon emissions but also a strengthened focus on enhancing the carbon sink capacity of ecosystems [3, 4]. The capacity of terrestrial ecosystems to mitigate rising atmospheric CO₂ is underpinned by their role as a major carbon pool [5]. Net ecosystem productivity (NEP) serves as the most direct ecological indicator for quantifying the magnitude of carbon sinks and sources [6]. A comprehensive understanding of the spatiotemporal dynamics, evolutionary trends, and key driving mechanisms of terrestrial ecosystem NEP at regional scales is fundamental to scientifically assessing regional carbon budgets, optimizing ecosystem management, enhancing carbon sequestration capacity, and supporting regional carbon neutrality strategies [7, 8].

The significant fluctuations in meteorological factors such as temperature and precipitation have led to substantial responses in the NEP of terrestrial ecosystems [9]. However, examining only the impact of meteorological factors on terrestrial NEP is insufficient to fully elucidate its underlying mechanisms [10]. In recent years, with continuous urbanization, land use changes have exerted unprecedented influences on the carbon absorption capacity of terrestrial ecosystems [5, 11]. Therefore, understanding the dynamics of NEP requires integrating both natural driving factors and human activity factors. Moreover, the responses of NEP to these factors are further complicated by the heterogeneity of topographic factors (elevation, slope, aspect, soil types etc.) [12]. Against this backdrop, uncovering ecological processes under the coupled effects of multiple factors is key to accurately predicting future changes in the carbon sink of terrestrial ecosystems.

Subtropical regions, characterized by abundant hydrothermal resources, prolonged growing seasons, and substantial biomass accumulation, contribute significantly to the global terrestrial carbon sink [13, 14]. Compared to tropical regions, which experience higher respiratory carbon losses, and temperate regions, which have shorter photosynthetic periods, subtropical ecosystems generally exhibit higher carbon use efficiency, positioning them among the most active net carbon sinks globally [6]. These areas sustain extensive ecosystems such as evergreen broadleaf forests and monsoon rainforests, where vegetation exhibits high growth rates and photosynthetic efficiency, coupled with relatively stable and vast soil organic carbon pools [15]. Consequently, terrestrial ecosystems in subtropical regions constitute a critical component of the global carbon cycle, functioning as key zones for absorbing and sequestering atmospheric CO₂ and thus mitigating climate change [16].

China’s subtropical region spans a vast geographical area. As a representative ecological province in southern China, Jiangxi Province is characterized by abundant hydrothermal conditions, widespread distribution of evergreen broad-leaved forests, and a relatively long growing season, making it well suited to represent the fundamental attributes of subtropical terrestrial ecosystems in China [17, 18]. Jiangxi ranks second nationwide in forest coverage and exhibits strong carbon sink capacity; its favorable natural conditions theoretically confer substantial potential for terrestrial carbon sequestration [19]. From a land-use perspective, Jiangxi Province exhibits pronounced landscape heterogeneity, encompassing extensive natural forests, agroforestry systems, the rapidly urbanizing Nanchang–Jiujiang urban corridor, and large freshwater wetland systems such as Poyang Lake [20]. This heterogeneous mosaic provides an ideal natural laboratory for systematically examining carbon sink dynamics under the combined influences of human activities and natural drivers. In addition, compared with coastal provinces such as Zhejiang and Fujian, Jiangxi is relatively less affected by maritime climatic influences. Moreover, it features a clear geomorphic transition and strong heterogeneity along mountain–hill–valley–plain gradients, offering a distinctive perspective for understanding carbon sequestration processes across diverse landforms in subtropical regions [21]. Nevertheless, previous studies on terrestrial carbon cycling in Jiangxi have largely focused on the dynamics of NPP or GPP and their driving mechanisms. Such frameworks are commonly centered on vegetation photosynthetic processes or organic matter accumulation alone, which may not fully capture the net carbon budget effect of terrestrial ecosystems [22, 23].

The development of advanced remote sensing, geographic information systems, and ecological modelling techniques provides powerful tools for monitoring and simulating the dynamics of terrestrial carbon sinks across large spatial and extended temporal scales [24]. At the spatial scale, studies on NEP in China’s subtropical region have primarily focused on regional-scale assessments in Zhejiang Province [25, 26] and the Dongting Lake Basin [27], as well as site-scale investigations in Jiangxi [28] and Guangdong [29] provinces. In terms of research targets, existing work has largely concentrated on spatiotemporal simulations of NEP in forest ecosystems and their responses to meteorological drivers, whereas other terrestrial ecosystems such as cropland have received comparatively limited attention [25, 30]. Consequently, a comprehensive assessment of NEP across terrestrial ecosystems remains lacking. In particular, quantitative understanding of key underlying surface factors such as topographic gradients (elevation, slope, and aspect) and soil properties, as well as their complex interactions with climate and land use, remains insufficient [31]. Moreover, studies forecasting the future sustainability and evolutionary trajectories of NEP are relatively scarce [32]. Notably, there is a pronounced lack of integrative research frameworks that combine high-precision multi-source remote sensing datasets, ground-based validation, and advanced spatial statistical approaches (e.g., the GeoDetector model) to robustly reveal the driving mechanisms of NEP [33]. Therefore, a comprehensive evaluation of the spatiotemporal dynamics and future trends of NEP across terrestrial ecosystems, while explicitly quantifying the interactive effects of topographic gradients, soil attributes, climate, and land-use change, can deepen our understanding of carbon sink dynamics in subtropical regions and provide critical scientific support for precision-oriented ecological restoration and carbon management strategies.

Against this backdrop, the present study takes Jiangxi Province as the study area. By integrating MODIS remote sensing imagery, ground-based plot measurements, high-resolution geographic environmental data, and meteorological datasets, we first estimate NPP using an improved CASA model and subsequently quantify NEP by coupling NPP with a heterotrophic respiration (Rh) model. Furthermore, we employ a suite of analytical approaches including linear regression, the Hurst exponent, the coefficient of variation, partial correlation analysis, and the GeoDetector method to systematically characterize the spatiotemporal patterns, evolutionary features, future trajectories, and multidimensional driving mechanisms of terrestrial ecosystem carbon sinks in Jiangxi Province.

The schematic diagram of the research framework (Fig. 1) outlines our approach, which is structured around addressing these key scientific questions: (1) What are the spatiotemporal patterns of the terrestrial ecosystem NEP in Jiangxi Province from 2000 to 2020? What are its interannual and intra-annual variation characteristics and long-term evolution trends? (2) How do climatic factors (e.g., temperature, precipitation, humidity), topography (e.g., elevation, slope, aspect), soil type, vegetation cover (Normalized difference vegetation index, NDVI), and land use individually and interactively drive the spatial heterogeneity and dynamic changes of NEP? Do critical thresholds exist? (3) How will the future persistent evolution trends of carbon sinks vary in different regions? Which regions are at risk of degradation? (4) Based on the understanding of the above mechanisms, how can targeted zonal optimization management strategies be proposed to enhance the regional carbon sink function?

Addressing these questions is expected to deepen the understanding of carbon sink processes in subtropical complex topography regions, provide a scientific basis for regional ecological conservation policies, and offer relevant scientific references for Jiangxi Province to formulate precise carbon sequestration and emission reduction policies, optimize territorial spatial ecological management, and contribute to the national “Dual Carbon” goals.

Study region and data preprocessing

Study area

The study area encompasses Jiangxi Province, located in southeastern China south of the Yangtze River (24°29′14″–30°04′43″ N, 113°34′18″–118°28′56″ E), with a total land area of 166,900 km² divided into 11 prefecture-level cities. The terrain is predominantly mountainous and hilly (accounting for 78% of the area). It is flanked by the Wuyi Mountains to the east, the Nanling Range to the south, and the Luoxiao and Jiuling Mountains to the west, while the north comprises the alluvial plain of Poyang Lake (Fig. 2a).

Fig. 2Overview of the study area: (a) spatial pattern of landforms and rivers; (b) spatial distribution of land use types

Overview of the study area: (a) spatial pattern of landforms and rivers; (b) spatial distribution of land use types

Located in the East Asian monsoon region, Jiangxi experiences a humid subtropical monsoon climate, characterized by superior hydrothermal conditions with synchronous precipitation and heat. The region averages an annual temperature of 19.3 °C and records a mean annual precipitation of 1567.7 mm. The province boasts a dense river network. Major rivers, including the Ganjiang, Fuhe, Xinjiang, Raohe, and Xiuhe, converge into Poyang Lake.

The primary vegetation type is subtropical evergreen broad-leaved forest, interspersed with coniferous forests (e.g., Chinese Fir (Cunninghamia lanceolata), Masson Pine (Pinus massoniana)), bamboo forests, and secondary shrublands. As of 2024, the provincial forest coverage rate reaches 63.35% (Fig. 2b), ranking second in China, which contributes to its remarkably high carbon sequestration capacity.

Data descriptions and sources

Field data collection

During the growing season (July to August) of 2022, we conducted field sampling across various plots. The plots were established following the principles of representing different vegetation types (grassland, cropland, orchard, and forest) and topographic gradients (elevation and slope). The geographic coordinates of the plot centers were recorded using a GPS device. To minimize the impact of mixed pixels, each sampling site was required to be relatively homogeneous in terms of vegetation composition. Additionally, potential disturbance sources such as forest gaps (with canopy gap fraction > 10%) and roads (with a buffer distance > 50 m) were excluded. A total of 35 valid sample plots were ultimately obtained (Fig. 2b) [34]. Among these, forest plots measured 10 m × 10 m, within which parameters including tree diameter at breast height, height, crown width, and canopy density were recorded. Herbaceous plots measured 1 m × 1 m, and parameters such as fresh weight and coverage of herbaceous plants were measured [35]. Furthermore, specimens of dominant species within each plot were collected, and their carbon content ratios were determined using an elemental analyzer.

Remote sensing imagery

The MODIS data products from 2000 to 2020 were obtained from the National Aeronautics and Space Administration and used as input data for the remote sensing-based inversion of NPP and NEP. Specifically, the Surface reflectance data from the MOD09A1 product were used to calculate the NDVI, while MCD12Q1 product provided the land cover types. All MODIS data products were preprocessed, including format conversion, mosaicking, reprojection, and resampling using the MODIS Reprojection Tool [36].

Geospatial data

Gridded relative humidity data from 2000 to 2020 were sourced from the National Tibetan Plateau Data Center. Digital elevation model (DEM), soil type, and land use data were obtained from the Resource and Environmental Science Data Center.

Meteorological data

Daily meteorological data, including temperature, precipitation, and solar radiation, from weather stations covering Jiangxi Province between 2000 and 2020, were acquired from the National Meteorological Science Data Center. Based on these station records, monthly gridded meteorological datasets at a 1 km spatial resolution were generated through spatial interpolation using ANUSPLIN software. The process incorporated the DEM and employed thin plate spline functions to perform the interpolation [36].

Methods

NPP simulation

The CASA model was employed in this study to estimate the monthly NPP across Jiangxi Province from 2000 to 2020. The model is a remote sensing-driven, light use efficiency-based model that estimates absorbed photosynthetically active radiation (APAR) using the NDVI and total solar radiation. NPP is subsequently estimated by incorporating the maximum light use efficiency (εmax) of different vegetation types, which is determined based on land cover data, temperature, precipitation, and other meteorological variables. The monthly NPP at each geographic location (x) was estimated using the equation [37]:

1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left\{ {\matrix{ {{\mathop{\rm NPP}\nolimits} (x,t) = {\mathop{\rm APAR}\nolimits} (x,t) \cdot \varepsilon (x,t)}\cr {{\mathop{\rm APAR}\nolimits} (x,t) = 0.5 \cdot {\mathop{\rm SOL}\nolimits} (x,t) \cdot {\mathop{\rm FPAR}\nolimits} (x,t)}\cr {\varepsilon (x,t) = \varepsilon \max \cdot T1(x,t) \cdot T2(x,t) \cdot W(x,t)}\cr } } \right.$$\end{document}

where NPP (x, t) is the NPP of pixel x in month t (g C m⁻² month⁻¹); APAR (x, t) is the absorbed photosynthetically active radiation (MJ m⁻² month⁻¹); ε (x, t) is the actual light use efficiency (g C MJ⁻¹); SOL (x, t) is the total solar radiation (MJ m⁻² month⁻¹); FPAR (x, t) is the fraction of photosynthetically active radiation absorbed by the vegetation canopy (dimensionless); The constant 0.5 represents the ratio of photosynthetically active radiation to total solar radiation.

Three stress factors are incorporated into the model: T₁ (x, t), which quantifies the inhibitory effect of temperature deviation from the optimum range on light use efficiency; T₂ (x, t), which describes the attenuation of utilization efficiency due to temperature trends; and W (x, t), which characterizes the limiting effect of soil water stress on light utilization. All three are dimensionless parameters. The εmax represents the maximum efficiency of light energy utilization by green plants under ideal conditions. Potter et al. [38] proposed using a fixed value of 0.389 to calculate the εmax of vegetation. However, due to influences from atmospheric conditions, vegetation differences, soil environments, and geographic location, this value fails to meet the requirements for estimating vegetation NPP in China. Using NOAA/AVHRR remote sensing data in combination with meteorological variables and in situ NPP observations, Zhu et al. [39] derived vegetation-type-specific maximum light use efficiency parameters for China through an improved least-squares optimization framework based on error minimization. The resulting parameter set has been extensively adopted in NPP estimation studies across diverse regions and vegetation types in China [40, 41], and has shown consistently high reliability and robustness in multiple NPP modeling studies in subtropical China [42, 43]. Therefore, this study directly adopts the εmax determined by Zhu et al. [39] for typical vegetation types in China, without conducting a local calibration of εmax.

Field-Based estimation of NPP

For forest ecosystems, biomass was estimated using biomass regression models. Forest carbon storage was calculated by multiplying the biomass of each tree type by its carbon fraction. The mean annual NPP of forests was derived by dividing the carbon storage by the average stand age. For cropland and grassland ecosystems, aboveground biomass was completely harvested, oven-dried at 80 °C to a constant weight to determine dry biomass, and then multiplied by the carbon fraction to obtain the NPP. Parameters such as those for the biomass regression models and vegetation carbon fractions are detailed in our previous studies [21, 44].

NEP estimation

In the absence of ecosystem disturbances, the terrestrial carbon sink is represented by NEP. This metric is calculated as the difference between NPP and carbon emissions resulting from soil microbial respiration [45]. This relationship can be expressed as:

2\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned}\left\{ \begin{array}{l} {{\rm{NEP}} = {\rm{NPP}} - {R_h}} \hfill \\ Rh(x,t) = 0.22 \cdot e^{0.0913T\left( {x,t} \right)} \\ \quad+ \ln \left[ {0.3145P\left( {x,t} \right) + 1} \right] \cdot 30 \cdot 46.5\% \end{array}\right.\end{aligned}$$\end{document}

where Rh (x, t) denotes the organic carbon consumed by Rh at pixel x in the t-th month (g C m⁻² month⁻¹); and T (x, t) and P (x, t) represent the mean temperature (°C) and total precipitation (mm) at pixel x in the t-th month, respectively.

Assessment of spatiotemporal trends

The change in NEP at the pixel scale between different time periods was quantified using the difference method:

3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\rm{NE}}{{\rm{P}}_{ij}} = {\rm{NEP}}_{ij}^{n1} - {\rm{NEP}}_{ij}^{n2}$$\end{document}

where NEPij represents the change in NEP value at the grid cell in row i and column j; and denote the NEP values of the grid cell located at the i row and j column for the in years n1 and n2, respectively.

Although this approach intuitively reveals the spatial variation in NEP across Jiangxi Province from 2000 to 2020, it only reflects differences between the endpoints of the study period. If endpoint values are biased owing to extreme climate events or anthropogenic disturbances, the representativeness of the results may be limited. Therefore, for continuous NEP time series, we employed a univariate linear regression model [46] on a per-pixel basis to estimate the trends in carbon sink dynamics:

4\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$slope = {{n \cdot \sum\limits_{i = 1}^n {(i \cdot Yi) - \sum\limits_{i = 1}^n {i \cdot \sum\limits_{i = 1}^n {Yi} } } } \over {n \cdot \sum\limits_{i = 1}^n {{i^2} - {{\left( {\sum\limits_{i = 1}^n i } \right)}^2}} }}$$\end{document}

where slope denotes the interannual rate of change derived from the linear regression of NEP, Yi is the NEP value in the i-th year, and n is the total number of years.

Subsequently, the statistical significance of the trend was assessed using the F-test [47]:

5\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$F = {{(n - 2)\sum\limits_{i = 1}^n {{{\left( {\widehat {NEP} - \overline {NEP} } \right)}^2}} } \over {{{\sum\limits_{i = 1}^n {\left( {NE{P_i} - \widehat {{\rm{NE}}{{\rm{P}}_i}}} \right)} }^2}}}$$\end{document}

where NEPi refers to the NEP in year i; is the mean NEP over the 21-year period, and represents the linearly regressed NEP value for year i.

Based on the F-distribution critical values, the thresholds for significance at the 95% and 99% confidence levels are 4.414 and 8.285, respectively. By integrating the slope and F-test results, the NEP trends were categorized into different levels.

To further evaluate the stability of carbon sinks across Jiangxi Province, the CV of NEP for each pixel over the study period was calculated [48]:

6\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$CV = {\textstyle{{\sqrt {{1 \over n}\sum\limits_{i = 1}^n {{{\left( {NE{P_i} - \overline {{\rm{NEP}}} } \right)}^2}} } } \over {\overline {{\rm{NEP}}} }}}$$\end{document}

where CV represent the coefficient of variation.

In addition to current spatial patterns and processes, the future trajectory of carbon sinks was examined using the Hurst index (H) [49]:

7\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left\{ \begin{array}{l} {\overline {{\rm{NE}}{{\rm{P}}_n}} = {1 \over {\sum\limits_{i = 1}^n {{\rm{NE}}{{\rm{P}}_n}} }},n = 1,2, \ldots } \hfill \\ {X\left( {t,n} \right) = \sum\limits_{t = 1}^n {\left( {{\rm{NE}}{{\rm{P}}_t} - \overline {{\rm{NE}}{{\rm{P}}_n}} } \right),1 \le t \le n} } \hfill \\ {R\left( n \right) = \mathop {\max }\limits_{1 \le t \le n} X\left( {t,n} \right) - \mathop {\min }\limits_{1 \le t \le n} X\left( {t,n} \right),n = 1,2, \ldots } \hfill \\ {S\left( n \right) = \sqrt {{{\sum\limits_{t = 1}^n {\left( {{\rm{NE}}{{\rm{P}}_t} - \overline {{\rm{NE}}{{\rm{P}}_n}} } \right)} } \over n}} ,n = 1,2, \ldots } \hfill \\ {\log \left( {{{{R_n}} \over {{S_n}}}} \right) = a + H\log \left( n \right)} \end{array} \right.$$\end{document}

where n represents different time scales, each corresponding to a distinct rescaled range ratio R(n)/S(n). If a proportional relationship exists whereby R/S is proportional to nH, that is, R/S∝nH, it indicates the presence of the Hurst phenomenon within the NEP time series. The scaling coefficient H is known as the Hurst exponent. The value of H can be estimated by constructing a linear regression model between log(R(n)/S(n)) and log(n), fitted using the least squares method. In this study, the Hurst exponent was calculated based on an annual NEP time series spanning 2000–2020. Specifically, a continuous 21-year NEP record was used for each pixel. The use of a relatively long and continuous annual time series helps mitigate potential edge effects in the estimation of the Hurst exponent. Guided by the direction and strength of persistence, the classification of the H follows four types [50]: strong anti-persistence (0 < H < 0.35), weak anti-persistence (0.35 < H < 0.50), weak persistence (0.50 ≤ H < 0.65), and strong persistence (0.65 ≤ H < 1).

Driving factors analysis

To disentangle the effects of individual meteorological drivers, we assessed the pixel-wise correlation between NEP and each factor using partial correlation analysis, with significance assessed using the T-test [51]:

8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left\{ \begin{array}{ll} {{R_{xy,z}} = {{{R_{xy}} - {R_{xz}} \cdot {R_{yz}}} \over {\sqrt {\left( {1 - R_{xz}^2} \right)\left( {1 - R_{yz}^2} \right)} }}} \\{T = {{{R_{xy,z}}} \over {\sqrt {1 - R_{xy,z}^2} \sqrt {n - m - 1} }}} \end{array} \right.$$\end{document}

where Rxy, z denotes the partial correlation coefficient between variables x and y, after controlling for the effect of a third variable z. T represents the t-statistic for significance testing; n denotes the study duration in years; m represents the number of independent variables.

A key objective of this study was to quantify the relative influence and interactive effects of various drivers on the spatial heterogeneity of NEP. To achieve this, the geodetector model was applied. Originally developed by Wang and Xu [52], this method is designed to quantify the influence of geographical factors on spatial heterogeneity phenomena. The explanatory power of each factor was evaluated using the q-statistic:

9\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$q = 1 - {{\sum\limits_{h = 1}^L {{N_h}{\sigma _h}^2} } \over {N{\sigma ^2}}}$$\end{document}

where q denotes the explanatory power of a given factor on the spatial heterogeneity of vegetation NEP, with values ranging from 0 to 1. Higher values of q indicate a stronger influencing factor. The index h = 1,2,…, L denotes the h category of driving factors; N and Nh refer to the total number of samples across the study area and the number of samples within a specific factor category, respectively; σ and σh denote the overall standard deviation and the standard deviation of a particular factor category, respectively.

Additionally, interaction detection was conducted to evaluate the combined effects of any two factors on NEP determining whether they exhibit enhanced, weakened, or independent effects [53].

Results

Validation of NEP accuracy

The CASA-model-simulated NEP was validated against ground-based NEP measurements collected from 35 sites across Jiangxi Province (Fig. 3). The results revealed a highly significant linear correlation between the remote sensing-derived NEP and the measured values (R² = 0.77, P < 0.01), indicating a strong goodness-of-fit. The root mean square error (RMSE) was 54.65 g C m⁻² year⁻¹, demonstrating the overall reliability of the inversion. The samples covered four ecosystem types: grassland, cropland, orchard, and forest. Samples from cropland areas showed moderate dispersion, likely due to potential disturbances from anthropogenic management practices. Furthermore, the regression line lies above the 1:1 line, suggesting that the field-measured NEP values are generally higher than the estimates simulated by CASA model, which implies a possible underestimation of carbon sink by the inversion approach. Nevertheless, the 95% prediction band encompasses the majority of the samples, indicating that the model remains robust under the complex land cover conditions in Jiangxi Province and that the remote sensing-derived NEP results are reasonably reliable.

Spatiotemporal patterns of carbon sinks

Temporal evolution of carbon sinks

The pronounced seasonal fluctuations were observed in both NPP, Rh, and NEP (Fig. 4). NPP peaked predominantly between June and August, corresponding to the growing season under the influence of the subtropical monsoon climate, characterized by sufficient warmth and moisture. Specifically, NPP exhibited a continuous increase from January to June, with a growth rate of 12.32 g C m⁻² month⁻¹, reaching its maximum in June before transitioning into a declining phase. The multi-year monthly maximum was recorded in June (78.91 ± 8.33 g C m⁻² month⁻¹). Furthermore, a highly significant increasing trend in NPP was detected over the study period (slope > 0, P < 0.01).

Fig. 4The temporal evolution patterns of monthly (a) NPP, (b) Rh, and (c) NEP from 2000 to 2020

The temporal evolution patterns of monthly (a) NPP, (b) Rh, and (c) NEP from 2000 to 2020

The Rh showed a non-significant increasing trend (slope > 0, P > 0.05). Its intra-annual pattern was marked by a gradual rise from December of the previous year to June of the current year, peaking in June (64.47 ± 6.79 g C m⁻² month⁻¹), followed by a decline. The phase shift between Rh and NPP suggests a lagged response of soil carbon metabolism to climatic drivers. Notably, NEP demonstrated a unique bimodal seasonal pattern: carbon sinks strengthened continuously from January to September, reaching an annual peak in September (25.81 ± 12.08 g C m⁻² month⁻¹), and weakened thereafter. Overall, a statistically significant increasing trend (slope > 0, P < 0.05) of NEP was observed.

Spatial distribution of carbon sinks

The spatial heterogeneity of NEP in Jiangxi Province was pronounced, exhibiting a distribution pattern characterized by a west-south high and east-north low (Fig. 5). Areas with high NEP (> 400 g C m⁻² year⁻¹) were primarily concentrated in mountainous and hilly regions such as the Wuyi Mountains and the Luoxiao Mountains. These regions, with forest coverage exceeding 75%, are dominated by subtropical evergreen broad-leaved forests and benefit from superior hydrothermal conditions (mean annual precipitation > 1600 mm), resulting in high vegetation carbon sequestration capacity. For instance, the southern section of the Wuyi Mountains in southern Jiangxi exhibited NEP values ranging from 520 to 600 g C m⁻² year⁻¹, identifying it as a carbon sink hotspot.

Spatial distribution pattern of NEP in Jiangxi Province from 2000 to 2020

Conversely, the alluvial plain of Poyang Lake, together with the densely urbanized northern regions, hosted the majority of low-value NEP areas (NEP < 200 g C m⁻² year⁻¹). In the peri-lake zone of Poyang Lake, where cropland and built-up land are interwoven, NEP values were only 120–180 g C m⁻² year⁻¹, indicating significant influence from agricultural practices and human activities. Urban built-up areas, including Nanchang and Jiujiang, showed even lower NEP values (below 100 g C m⁻² year⁻¹), underscoring the pronounced impact of anthropogenic disturbance on ecosystem carbon sinks. Notably, a carbon sink transition zone (250 < NEP < 350 g C m⁻² year⁻¹) was identified along the mid-lower reaches of the Ganjiang River valley.

Characteristics and trends of carbon sink evolution

Characteristics of NEP evolution

The dynamics of NEP in Jiangxi Province exhibited significant spatiotemporal heterogeneity (Fig. 6). Based on univariate linear regression (Fig. 6a and b) and difference method (Fig. 6c), the interannual change rate of NEP across the province revealed a distinct spatial pattern summarized as a north-south increase flanking a central decline. Specifically, the Wuyi–Luoxiao mountain areas in southern Jiangxi showed a highly significant increasing trend in NEP at a rate exceeding 5.5 g C m⁻² year⁻¹, while significant decreases were observed in the Poyang Lake Plain agricultural zone and the Nanchang-Jiujiang urban corridor.

Fig. 6Evolution characteristics of NEP in Jiangxi Province from 2000 to 2020: (a–b) univariate linear regression; (c) difference method; (d) coefficient of variation

Evolution characteristics of NEP in Jiangxi Province from 2000 to 2020: (a–b) univariate linear regression; (c) difference method; (d) coefficient of variation

From a topographic perspective, the most pronounced strengthening of NEP occurred in mountainous and hilly regions, whereas the greatest extent of carbon sink decline was observed in plain and valley areas. Furthermore, analysis of the CV of NEP indicated clear spatial differentiation in carbon sink stability. Specifically, the core area of the Wuyi Mountains had a CV below 30%, reflecting low interannual fluctuation, whereas the urban clusters in northern Jiangxi exhibited a CV exceeding 65% (Fig. 6d).

Future trends of NEP

The persistence analysis of carbon sink time series based on the Hurst exponent model (Fig. 7) indicates that most regions exhibit positive persistence (H > 0.5), suggesting a continued strengthening of carbon sequestration capacity in the future. Among these, H values for NEP in natural forest regions such as the Wuyi and Luoxiao Mountains exceed 0.65, reflecting a strong persistent improvement. Some mountainous regions with H values between 0.50 and 0.65 are categorized as exhibiting weak persistent improvement.

Notably, areas around Poyang Lake and most parts of the Chang-Jiu Industrial Corridor demonstrate a persistent degradation in carbon sink capacity. The Nanchang-Jiujiang urban belt, in particular, is classified as experiencing strong persistent degradation, where land-use changes associated with urbanization have caused irreversible disruption to carbon sequestration. NEP under different topography conditions

Carbon sink capacity in Jiangxi Province exhibits a significant unimodal distribution with increasing elevation (Fig. 8a). The mean NEP reaches its maximum at elevations between 550 and 750 m, where optimal hydrothermal conditions and low anthropogenic disturbances such as forest logging prevail, along with a dominance of evergreen broadleaf forests. Beyond 750 m, the carbon sequestration capacity of vegetation gradually declines. In addition, NEP values are relatively low in low-elevation river valley plains.

Areas with slopes between 20° and 35° show a mean NEP of 312.6 g C m⁻² year⁻¹, which is significantly higher than that of gentle slopes (< 10°) and steep slopes (> 35°) (Fig. 8b). North-facing slopes exhibit the highest NEP (188.61 g C m⁻² year⁻¹), although the differences in NEP across various slope aspects are not highly pronounced (Fig. 8c).

Yellow soils and yellow-brown soils, which develop in high-elevation mountainous and hilly regions, generally exhibit NEP values exceeding 300 g C m⁻² year⁻¹. Red soils are mainly distributed in relatively low-lying valleys and hilly areas, where vegetation growth and carbon sequestration capacity are weaker compared to those in yellow and yellow-brown soil regions. Limestone soils and purple soils exhibit poor nutrient retention capacity, resulting in low vegetation carbon fixation. Paddy soils, influenced by long-term anaerobic conditions, show poor aeration that restricts plant growth and photosynthesis, leading to lower NEP. Yellow-cinnamon soils have heavy clay textures that limit root development and nutrient uptake, coupled with generally low fertility, resulting also in relatively low NEP (Fig. 8d).

Among different land use types, forest land demonstrates the highest carbon sink capacity, followed by grassland. Cropland shows relatively lower carbon sink values, primarily due to frequent agricultural activities such as tillage, fertilization, and irrigation, which disrupt soil structure and accelerate the decomposition and release of soil organic carbon. Moreover, the short growth cycles and limited biomass of crops, along with the removal of a significant portion of biomass during harvest, further hinder carbon accumulation (Fig. 7e).

Partial correlation between NEP and meteorological factors

The partial correlation coefficient between temperature and NEP exhibits a notable north–south divergence across the region (Fig. 9a). In the southern Wuyi Mountains–Luoxiao Mountains area, at elevations of 500–1200 m with mean annual temperatures ranging from 16.5 to 18.2 °C, temperature shows a significant positive correlation with NEP (P < 0.05). In contrast, in the low-lying plains of Poyang Lake and the Nanchang-Jiujiang urban belt (< 500 m), temperature is significantly negatively correlated with NEP (P < 0.05). However, the belt of non-significant correlation occurs in the mid-lower Gan River valley.

The partial correlation between precipitation and NEP demonstrates a spatial pattern characterized by strong positive correlations in the northeast and southwest, strong negative correlations in the west, and non-significant correlations in the southeast (Fig. 9b). The partial correlation between solar radiation and NEP exhibits a pattern of enhancement at high elevations and reduction at low elevations (Fig. 9c). A significant positive correlation (P < 0.05) is observed in the core area of the Wuyi Mountains above 800 m, while a significant negative correlation (P < 0.05) is found in the Poyang Lake plain. Notably, a radiation-carbon sink decoupling (P > 0.05) is identified in the hilly areas of northeastern Jiangxi, which may be related to seasonal fluctuations in light-use efficiency associated with canopy replacement in moso bamboo forests. The partial correlation coefficient between relative humidity and NEP shows a spatial structure of significant positive correlations in mountainous and hilly regions and significant negative correlations in river valley plains (Fig. 9d).

Analysis of driving forces of NEP using geodetector

Among the factors analyzed, NDVI, elevation, and aspect emerged as the dominant drivers of carbon sink function, according to the single-factor detection results (Fig. 10a), with dynamic variations observed over time (Fig. 10b). NDVI served as a proxy for vegetation coverage, with its q-value fluctuating and increasing from 0.3 to approximately 0.5. This could be attributed to the ongoing forest restoration efforts carried out in Jiangxi Province during this period. Between 2000 and 2020, the forest coverage rate in Jiangxi gradually increased from 59.7% to 63.35%. Furthermore, policies aimed at afforestation, wetland conservation, and sustainable land management in Jiangxi have reduced the negative impact of anthropogenic disturbances on NDVI. As a result, the explanatory power of NDVI for NEP changes has increased over time. The q-values of elevation and aspect remained within ranges of 0.34–0.41 and 0.32–0.39, respectively, indicating that terrain is a key determinant of the spatial pattern in NEP. Notably, temperature, precipitation, and relative humidity shared a common pattern of an initial increase followed by a decrease in their q-values. This trend contrasted with that of land use, which exhibited a consistent gradual increase. This suggests that the driving effect of climatic factors on carbon sinks may have been progressively overshadowed by anthropogenic disturbances, consistent with the rapid urbanization observed in Jiangxi Province after 2010.

Discussion

Uncertainty in NEP Simulation

This study established 35 field sampling plots, which are relatively evenly distributed across the main vegetation coverage areas of the province. These plots cover various topographic gradients and major soil types, including grasslands, croplands, orchards, and forests, providing good spatial representativeness. The results revealed a highly significant linear correlation between the remote sensing-derived NEP and the measured values (R² = 0.77, P < 0.01), indicating a strong goodness-of-fit (Fig. 3). The RMSE was 54.65 g C m⁻² year⁻¹, demonstrating the overall reliability of the simulation. However, the underestimation of NEP estimates derived from remote sensing inversions compared to field measurements may stem from the oversimplification of vegetation-soil carbon exchange processes in complex terrains within models [31]. Future studies should incorporate more refined ecological process models to address this issue. The majority of the sampling plots consist of evergreen broadleaf forests, evergreen coniferous forests, and shrublands, with fewer samples from other vegetation types. Furthermore, the plot density is relatively low, and there are sampling gaps in the complex terrain of the Luoxiao Mountains and the Poyang Lake Plain, which may introduce some bias in the validation of the model-simulated NPP. Additionally, errors in the measurement of tree diameter at breast height and height in forest plots could affect the accuracy of biomass estimation, as these errors propagate through the biomass regression model [22].

Furthermore, the simulated NEP obtained in this study is compared with the NEP simulated in other studies conducted in adjacent subtropical regions. Specifically, the NEP of terrestrial ecosystems in Jiangxi Province exhibits a spatial trend of higher values in the south and lower values in the north, which is consistent with the spatial patterns of NEP simulated in the terrestrial ecosystems of Zhejiang Province by Huang et al. [25] and Xu et al. [26]. This spatial difference is likely related to the more favorable hydrothermal conditions in the lower-latitude regions. In this study, the NEP range in southern mountainous forest ecosystems of Jiangxi Province is mainly between 400 and 600 g C m⁻² year⁻¹, which is slightly higher than the NEP range in forest ecosystems in the neighboring province of Zhejiang (300–600 g C m⁻² year⁻¹) [30]. This may be associated with the higher heat supply in the lower-latitude conditions of the southern Jiangxi region, which promotes vegetation growth and enhances ecosystem productivity. Specifically, the NEP of forest ecosystems in the southern part of the Wuyi Mountains in southern Jiangxi can reach 520–600 g C m⁻² year⁻¹, which is consistent with the NEP of forest ecosystems in the mountainous regions of the Dongting Lake Basin in neighboring subtropical regions (600 g C m⁻² year⁻¹) [27]. Moreover, the NEP range of grasslands, croplands, and urbanized land around Poyang Lake is between 120 and 180 g C m⁻² year⁻¹, which is consistent with the NEP results of less than 300 g C m⁻² year⁻¹ in the suburban areas of Dongting Lake and urban development regions. Additionally, the NEP values of coniferous forests at the Qianyanzhou site in Taihe County, Jiangxi Province, are generally between 300 and 500 g C m⁻² year⁻¹ [28], which is consistent with the NEP range of forest ecosystems in southern Jiangxi Province found in this study. In conclusion, the NEP range simulated in this study is in close agreement with the results from neighboring subtropical regions. Therefore, the simulated NEP in this study is highly credible.

Carbon sink patterns and processes

The NEP in Jiangxi Province exhibits a spatial pattern characterized by high values in the west and south, and low values in the east and north (Fig. 5), which is closely associated with regional vegetation coverage, topography, and human activity intensity. Specifically, the higher NEP in the mountainous and hilly areas of the study region and lower NEP in the plains and densely urbanized areas generally align with the existing research on the carbon sink patterns of mountain ecosystems in southern China [45]. Building on this, our study further quantitatively identified key threshold characteristics of high and low NEP regions at the provincial scale. The Wuyi and Luoxiao Mountains, with forest coverage exceeding 75% and high hydrothermal matching, form significant high-carbon sink areas (NEP > 400 g·C·m⁻²·year⁻¹). In contrast, the carbon sink capacity of the Poyang Lake Plain and urbanized areas is significantly weakened (NEP < 200 g·C·m⁻²·year⁻¹), suggesting that urban expansion may lead to a decrease in vegetation coverage, thus negatively affecting carbon sinks [7]. Moreover, the significant negative correlation between NDVI and temperature in the Poyang Lake Economic Zone indicates that the reduction in vegetation stomatal conductance due to the urban heat island effect may also be a key factor limiting its carbon sink capacity [17].

Regarding temporal evolution, Jiangxi’s NEP showed a significant increasing trend (slope > 0, P < 0.05) from 2000 to 2020, which is consistent with the conclusion that increased forest coverage and warmer, wetter climate conditions promote vegetation growth [32]. However, our study further reveals that the increase in NEP varies significantly across the region, with the interannual variation rate showing a pattern of weaker in the center, stronger in the north and south. This may be owing to the suppression of carbon sink capacity in the central Poyang Lake Plain area, which is highly urbanized and affected by agricultural activities [20]. In contrast, the mountainous and hilly areas in the north and south, with higher forest coverage and enhanced ecological restoration efforts, exhibit more significant increases in NEP. Monthly temporal evolution (Fig. 4) shows that NEP steadily increased from January to June, reaching the first peak, followed by a slight decrease in July and August. However, it significantly increased again in September, reaching a higher peak. This may be related to the high sensitivity of soil microbes to temperature [54]. In July and August, high temperatures and humidity lead to a significant increase in heterotrophic respiration; whereas in September and October, with a slight reduction in temperature, NPP decreases only slightly, while heterotrophic respiration decreases substantially, leading to a second peak in NEP, with a delay of about three months after the NPP peak.

Slope analysis further indicates that the southern region of Jiangxi exhibits a relatively larger increase in NEP, which may be closely related to the region’s favorable natural conditions, rich biodiversity, and effective ecological protection measures [20]. In contrast, the areas with lower slope values are mainly located in the northern and central parts, where the ecosystems are more vulnerable owing to higher human activity interference. The area of significant carbon sink growth in Jiangxi Province accounts for 67.58% of the total provincial area, highlighting the notable achievements in vegetation restoration and ecological conservation in the province [19]. As an important part of China’s National Ecological Civilization Pilot Zone, Jiangxi has continuously advanced ecological protection and restoration engineering during the study period [55]. Specifically, between 2000 and 2010, efforts focused on resource-based areas (such as the Shangrao City Dexing County copper mining area, and the Ganzhou City tungsten and rare earth mining areas) included land reclamation, mining site reforestation, and geological environment restoration projects, gradually establishing a responsibility mechanism for ecological restoration in mining areas. From 2011 to 2016, low-productivity and low-efficiency forest transformation and forest tending projects were systematically promoted in the province’s hilly and red soil areas, significantly improving forest quality and ecological functions. From 2017 to 2020, taking Ganzhou as a representative case, an integrated ecological restoration pilot of the mountain–river–forest–farmland–lake–grassland system was implemented, achieving multi-element coordinated governance and regional-scale ecological function restoration. The sustained implementation of these ecological protection and restoration policies and projects has played a key role in the significant enhancement of Jiangxi’s carbon sink capacity during the study period [56]. However, certain regions still face ecological degradation challenges, with significantly degraded areas accounting for 1.91% of the total provincial area, and non-significantly degraded areas making up 6.36%. Moreover, the stability of NEP in central Jiangxi was relatively lower compared to the surrounding regions (Fig. 6d), further indicating the negative impact of anthropogenic disturbances on the persistence and stability of ecosystem carbon sinks [57].

Influence of different topographic factors on NEP

Topographic factors exhibit significant threshold effects on carbon sink regulation. The peak NEP (345.6 g C m⁻² year⁻¹) observed at elevations between 550 and 750 m corresponds to an optimal ecological niche characterized by favorable hydrothermal coupling. Within this range, the accumulated temperature ≥ 10 °C reaches 5200–5500 °C, annual precipitation ranges between 1600 and 1800 mm, and anthropogenic disturbance is minimal, creating dual advantages in vegetation carbon fixation and soil carbon preservation [53]. Along the slope gradient, areas with inclinations of 20°–35° demonstrate significantly higher carbon sink capacity, attributable to the relatively enriched spatial distribution of soil organic carbon density. Empirical data confirm that the topsoil layer (0–20 cm) in these moderate-slope areas systematically contains higher organic carbon content compared to gentler slopes (< 10°). This pattern likely results from the combined effects of vegetation coverage, moisture conditions, erosion processes, and the dynamics of organic matter accumulation and decomposition, all influenced by slope variations [58, 59].

The synergistic interaction between soil and vegetation systems underpins the baseline variation in carbon sink capacity. Red soil, which covers the largest area in Jiangxi Province (56% of the total land area), exhibits weaker adsorption of organic carbon by iron and aluminum oxides, leading to significantly lower carbon sequestration efficiency compared to yellow soil regions [60]. In high-elevation areas (> 800 m) with yellow-brown soils, relatively low mean annual temperatures (12.5–14.8 °C) reduce microbial decomposition rates, thereby extending the residence time of litter carbon compared to red soil regions [61]. The higher carbon sink observed on north-facing slopes, as confirmed by partial correlation analysis, is associated with synergistic effects involving summer soil moisture content and vegetation water-use efficiency.

An optimal temperature range promotes the activity of various enzymes within plants, thereby facilitating the efficient functioning of physiological processes such as photosynthesis and respiration. Within this optimal range, the photosynthetic rate is higher, leading to a greater NEP. Temperature also affects plant growth cycles and phenology, indirectly shaping carbon sink capacity. Although precipitation is a critical source of moisture for plant growth, its relationship with NEP in Jiangxi is less direct and consistent due to the province’s distinct precipitation patterns [22]. Precipitation exhibits significant seasonal and spatial heterogeneity. Extreme events such as heavy rains can lead to waterlogging, impair soil aeration, and suppress root respiration adversely affecting plant growth. Conversely, relatively insufficient precipitation induces water deficit in plants, which similarly constrains growth and the accumulation of NEP [36]. Furthermore, other factors such as soil texture and vegetation type mediate plant water-use efficiency, complicating the relationship between precipitation and NEP and resulting in relatively weak correlations [62]. Solar radiation affects NEP by influencing vegetation growth and distribution, although plant responses vary by species [63]. Sun-adapted plants thrive under strong radiation, contributing to higher NEP in corresponding ecosystems, whereas shade-adapted species may experience inhibition under intense sunlight, reducing NEP [29]. Similarly, suitable levels of relative humidity help maintain stomatal openness, facilitating CO₂ uptake and enhancing photosynthetic efficiency, thereby positively influencing NEP [64].

Limitations and prospects

In this study, we utilized MODIS imagery with a spatial resolution of 1 km for the remote sensing inversion of NEP. However, in mountainous and hilly terrain, this resolution introduces mixed-pixel effects [65], with errors becoming more pronounced in areas where topographic relief exceeds 200 m. The CASA model did not account for the influence of soil freeze-thaw cycles on Rh, potentially leading to an underestimation of Rh during low-temperature periods in northern Jiangxi [66]. Furthermore, the prediction of carbon sink trends using the Hurst index did not incorporate disturbances from extreme climate events, such as the short-term suppression of forest carbon sinks caused by the 2008 ice storm disaster [67]. In the analysis of driving forces, the geodetector indicated a strong interactive explanatory power (q = 0.973) between NDVI and land use. However, the model did not quantify the non-linear impacts of human activities such as fertilization and logging on vegetation carbon processes [68]. For instance, the response of cropland NEP to nitrogen fertilizer input exhibits a threshold effect, with carbon sink increments tending to saturate when application rates exceed 200 kg ha⁻¹ [69]. Additionally, this study did not integrate human activity intensity indices such as nighttime light data or land use intensity coefficients to elucidate their mechanisms on NEP, particularly in rapidly urbanizing areas like the Poyang Lake Plain. Hence, further investigation is needed into the stress effects of human activities on the carbon sink functionality of vegetation in these regions.

Research recommendations

Based on the findings of this study, we propose the following recommendations to further enhance the carbon sink function in Jiangxi Province.

First, differentiated forest restoration and carbon sink enhancement strategies should be implemented based on the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of carbon sinks. The study shows that the Hurst exponent is generally positively correlated with vegetation coverage, further confirming that vegetation condition is a key controlling factor for the sustainability of carbon sinks. Therefore, it is recommended to prioritize ecological restoration projects in urban expansion areas where Hurst exponent is less than 0.50, and to enhance forest quality in mountainous forest areas with an Hurst exponent greater than 0.65, thereby precisely improving forest carbon sink functions [24]. Further analysis of carbon sequestration potential across different land-use types indicates that forests and croplands retain substantial potential for further enhancement. It is suggested to enhance carbon sink capacity through thinning and nurturing in the mid-elevation red soil forests of the Wuyi and Luoxiao Mountains [16]; whereas in the Poyang Lake Plain paddy soil area, straw returning can be promoted to enhance soil carbon retention [33].

Second, the spatial pattern of land use should be optimized, and ecological restoration efforts should be strengthened to systematically unlock regional carbon sink potential. Synergistic management of climate and land use can result in significant carbon sink gains [20]. Therefore, it is recommended to promote mixed forest transformation of coniferous and broad-leaved trees in mountainous and hilly areas, in line with the trend of climate warming, to further increase carbon sink potential [19]. Carbon sink hotspots such as the Wuyi Mountains should implement conservation measures, strictly limit commercial logging, and establish dynamic vegetation carbon stock early warning systems through remote sensing monitoring. For abandoned farmland in the Poyang Lake Plain, the agroforestry model should be promoted, choosing high-efficiency carbon-fixing combinations such as Cunninghamia lanceolata and oil-tea camellia mixed forests [33]. Urban carbon sink regulation should integrate spatial planning with carbon footprint accounting, and it is recommended to establish ecological green spaces along the Nanchang-Jiujiang industrial corridor, maintaining the integrity of the carbon sink network through green corridor connectivity [35].

Moreover, climate factors should be incorporated into the carbon sink management framework to enhance the adaptability and stability of carbon sink systems to temperature and humidity changes. In areas significantly influenced by relative humidity (such as the southern mountainous areas of Jiangxi), the Hurst exponent of NEP exceeds 0.65, which are significantly higher than in the humidity-decoupling areas (such as urban areas with an Hurst exponent less than 0.35), indicating that carbon sink systems driven by natural humidity have stronger sustainability [51]. Therefore, it is recommended to enhance carbon sinks through artificial rainfall in mountainous and hilly areas during the dry season, and to establish a “temperature-carbon sink” early warning mechanism in the Poyang Lake Plain to address the carbon sink loss risk caused by extreme high-temperature events [44].

Finally, the marketization of carbon sink value should be promoted, and the overall effectiveness of carbon sink management should be enhanced through cross-regional collaborative governance. It is recommended to conduct studies on the economic value of carbon sinks and, by constructing a carbon sink trading model, convert ecological protection outcomes into economic incentives. Carbon sink market trading solutions should be provided through ecological compensation mechanisms [70]. At the same time, cross-regional collaborative mechanisms should be strengthened, and a joint carbon sink monitoring system should be established in the provinces along the middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River to jointly address the regional coordination challenges brought by climate policies.

Conclusions

This study utilized the CASA model to simulate the NEP in the terrestrial ecosystems of Jiangxi Province from 2000 to 2020. Based on multi-source data, we revealed the spatiotemporal patterns, evolutionary trends, and driving mechanisms of NEP. The results indicate that the carbon sink pattern in Jiangxi exhibits a spatial pattern characterized by a west-south high and east-north low. Mountainous and hilly areas serve as the core high-value zones (NEP > 400 g C m⁻² year⁻¹), whereas the Poyang Lake Plain and urban belts are identified as low-value areas (NEP < 200 g C m⁻² year⁻¹). A total of 73.2% of the province’s carbon sink areas show positive persistence (H > 0.5), with natural forests having an H index greater than 0.65, while the Nanchang-Jiujiang urban belt has an H index less than 0.35. The optimal carbon sink range is found at an altitude of 550–750 m (with an NEP peak of 345.6 g C m⁻² year⁻¹), and NEP is 22.3% higher in slopes of 20°–35° than in gentle slopes. NDVI (q = 0.37) and elevation (q = 0.34) are the dominant single factors for the spatial differentiation of NEP. The combination of soil type and land use has the strongest explanatory power (q = 0.586); the interaction between meteorological factors and land use further reveals that management measures need to be adapted to regional climate characteristics.

We construct an integrated “pattern-trend-mechanism-management” research framework, systematically elucidating the spatial heterogeneity and interactive drivers of NEP in a typical subtropical region. This research not only provides theoretical support and practical pathways for the precise enhancement of regional ecosystem carbon sinks but also contributes to the scientific realization of provincial carbon neutrality goals. It holds significant demonstration value for ecological protection and low-carbon coordinated development in China’s hilly and mountainous regions and offers a reference “nature-society” collaborative carbon sink enhancement scheme for the global subtropical region.

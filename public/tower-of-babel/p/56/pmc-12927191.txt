Same-session hybrid surgery for multifocal glioblastoma: integrating open resection with laser interstitial thermal therapy. Illustrative cases
Authors: Utku Özgen, Talat Kırış
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT

Multifocal glioblastoma (GBM) accounts for 2%–35% of newly diagnosed cases and is associated with a significantly worse prognosis than unifocal disease.1,2 Surgical management typically necessitates multiple craniotomies to address spatially separated lesions, increasing anesthesia exposure and perioperative risk, while prolonging recovery and delaying adjuvant therapy.3 These concerns are particularly relevant in older or medically frail patients. Moreover, aggressive resection is often constrained by involvement of eloquent brain regions, where neurological injury may negate survival benefit.4,5

Laser interstitial thermal therapy (LITT) has emerged as a minimally invasive alternative for newly diagnosed and recurrent GBM, especially for deep or eloquently located lesions.6,7 Advantages include real-time MRI thermography guidance, minimal tissue trauma, shorter hospitalization, and blood-brain barrier disruption that may enhance chemotherapy delivery.8,9 However, stand-alone LITT has limitations in large-volume or multifocal tumors, where incomplete ablation and postablation cerebral edema are more likely.10,11 A hybrid strategy combining open resection of the dominant lesion with LITT for satellite or distant foci offers a rational solution for multifocal GBM. This approach aims to maximize cytoreduction while avoiding multiple craniotomies, reducing perioperative morbidity, and facilitating early initiation of adjuvant therapy.

Berke et al. first reported this strategy, in which 2 patients underwent LITT on postoperative day 1 following resection.12 Our report presents 2 cases of newly diagnosed multifocal GBM, including the first reported same-session hybrid approach, wherein both resection and LITT were performed during a single operative session, and a staged hybrid approach.

Methods

This report describes the cases of 2 patients with multifocal GBM treated using a hybrid approach combining open resection and LITT at American Hospital between Februrary 2024 and August 2025. Both patients were operated on by the senior author. This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with institutional and national research standards and the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki. Patients underwent preoperative contrast-enhanced T1-weighted, T2-weighted, and fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) MRI sequences, along with CT navigation scans. LITT was performed under real-time MRI theromography guidance, and postoperative MRI was obtained at 24 hours. Case 1 underwent a same-session hybrid procedure consisting of craniotomy for a left frontal lesion followed by immediate LITT for a contralateral periventricular lesion, with discharge on postoperative day 2. Case 2 underwent staged hybrid treatment with craniotomy for a right parieto-occipital lesion, followed 5 days later by LITT for a right medial temporal lesion after pathological confirmation of isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH)–wildtype GBM.

LITT Procedure

LITT was performed using the Autoguide robotic platform (Medtronic). Visualase cooling and laser catheters (Medtronic) were inserted along preoperatively planned trajectories to target the lesion. In case 1, LITT was performed during the same operative session as craniotomy, whereas in case 2, LITT was conducted in a staged fashion. Patients were transferred from the operating room to the MRI suite for ablation. Laser ablation was performed under continuous real-time MRI thermography. Using the Visualase software (Medtronic), we set high-temperature safety markers adjacent to the laser catheter to automatically terminate laser delivery at 90°C, while we set low-temperature safety markers surrounding the lesion at 40°C to protect the eloquent brain regions, including the optic tract in case 2. Laser power was initiated at 3–3.5 W and adjusted dynamically based on real-time thermal feedback, typically ranging from 8 to 12 W. Ablation was performed in multiple cycles tailored to lesion geometry, with brief cooling intervals between cycles to prevent catheter overheating and allow thermal equilibration. Temperatures in at-risk structures were continuously monitored, and laser output was modulated or paused if safety thresholds were approached.

Volumetric Analysis and Extent of Ablation

Volumetric analysis was performed using navigation software (StealthStation S8, Medtronic). Pretreatment tumor volume and postablation volumes were measured on contrast-enhanced T1-weighted images obtained before and after LITT. The ablated region was delineated by contouring the region of peripheral enhancement surrounding central nonenhancing coagulation necrosis. Extent of ablation (EOA) was calculated as follows: EOA (%) = (Ablation zone volume/pretreatment tumor volume) × 100. An EOA ≥ 100% indicated ablation beyond the original tumor boundaries, whereas an EOA of 90%–100% was considered near-complete ablation with minimal margin.

Illustrative Cases

Illustrative Case 1: Same-Session Hybrid Approach

A 71-year-old woman presented with memory impairment and incoherent speech. MRI revealed multifocal disease, including a heterogeneously enhancing left frontal mass (37 × 33 × 32 mm) and a right periventricular lesion (37 × 28.8 × 25.5 mm) with corpus callosum involvement (Fig. 1). Given the patient’s age and the need to avoid multiple craniotomies, the neuro-oncology tumor board recommended a same-session hybrid approach. The decision was made to perform same-session surgery: open resection of the left frontal lesion followed immediately by LITT of the periventricular lesion.

Preoperative MR images obtained in case 1, demonstrating multifocal GBM. A: Axial contrast-enhanced T1-weighted image showing a heterogeneously enhancing mass lesion in the left frontal lobe and a right periventricular mass extending into the corpus callosum. B: Coronal contrast-enhanced T1-weighted image demonstrating a heterogeneously enhancing left frontal lesion with central necrosis and a right periventricular mass involving the anterior corpus callosum. C: Coronal T2-weighted image showing the multifocal mass consisting of a lesion involving the right anterior corpus callosum and periventricular region, as well as a left frontal mass with perilesional edema.

Under general anesthesia and neuronavigation guidance (StealthStation S8, Medtronic), the left frontal lesion was resected via craniotomy using sodium fluorescein guidance. Frozen-section analysis was consistent with GBM. Using the Autoguide robotic platform (Medtronic), we inserted a Visualase laser catheter (Medtronic) into the right periventricular lesion along a preplanned trajectory. During the same session, the patient was transferred directly from the operating room to the MRI suite.

The Visualase software was interfaced with the MRI system, and the laser catheter and cooling system were connected to the Visualase console. After setting lesion-specific temperature limits and thermal safety thresholds for adjacent eloquent regions, ablation was initiated at low power to assess heat distribution. Laser poser was then increased, with continuous cooling catheter irigation, and ablation was delivered using two pullback maneuvers over 3 minutes at a mean power of 9.5 W to achieve maximal safe coverage (Fig. 2).

Postoperative MR images obtained in case 1 demonstrating GTR and ablation. A:Coronal T2-weighted image showing gross-total ablation of the right periventricular and anterior corpus callosum lesion with the laser catheter tract visible, and a postoperative cavity consistent with complete resection of the left frontal lesion. B: Coronal contrast-enhanced T1-weighted image demonstrating gross-total ablation of the periventricular lesion extending into the anterior corpus callosum and GTR of the left frontal lesion without residual enhancing tumor, confirming complete treatment of both multifocal lesions.

Postoperative MRI revealed gross-total resection (GTR) of the frontal lesion and near-complete ablation of the periventricular lesion with a 93% EOA (Fig. 3). Final pathology confirmed IDH-wildtype GBM. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 2 without new neurological deficits.

MR-guided LITT of a right periventricular GBM with real-time thermal monitoring. A: Axial T2-weighted image with superimposed thermal map showing intraprocedural temperature distribution, with the red zone indicating maximum thermal damage and a peak temperature of 90.8°C at the laser catheter tip. B: Final thermal coagulation zone following completion of ablation.

Adjuvant chemoradiation therapy according to the Stupp protocol was initiated 2 weeks postoperatively, consisting of concurrent temozolomide (75 mg/m2 daily) and fractionated radiotherapy (60 Gy in 30 fractions) to both treatment sites. Follow-up imaging in August 2024 suggested radiation necrosis in the periventricular region, and bevacizumab was initiated with subsequent clinical improvement. At the 20-month follow-up, MRI showed no evidence of recurrence (Fig. 4), and the patient remains clinically stable.

Twenty-month follow-up MR images obtained in case 1. A: Coronal T2-weighted image showing the right periventricular LITT ablation cavity and left frontal resection cavity. B:Coronal contrast-enhanced T1-weighted image demonstrating both treatment cavities without evidence of disease progression.

Illustrative Case 2: Staged Hybrid Approach

A 58-year-old woman presented with visual disturbances. MRI revealed a heterogeneously enhancing right parieto-occipital mass measuring 33 × 39 × 41 mm, hypointense on T1-weighted sequences and heterogeneously hypo- to hyperintense on T2-weighted sequences. An additional nonenhancing lesion (17 × 12 × 10 mm) was observed in the mesial temporal lobe, hyperintense on T2-weighted and FLAIR sequences, and suspected to represent multifocal GBM (Fig. 5).

Preoperative and postoperative MR images obtained in case 2. A: Preoperative axial contrast-enhanced T1-weighted image demonstrating an enhancing right parieto-occipital GBM. B: Postoperative axial contrast-enhanced T1-weighted image showing GTR with a surgical cavity and no residual enhancement. C:Axial T2-weighted MRI demonstrating a distant multifocal GBM component in the right hippocampus, appearing hyperintense with surrounding edema, which was subsequently treated with LITT due to its eloquent location.

After multidisciplinary neuro-oncology board discussion, resection of the right parieto-occipital lesion was recommended, with management of the right mesial temporal lesion deferred pending histopathological results. Following resection of the parieto-occipital lesion, the patient was discharged on postoperative day 2. Postoperative MRI confirmed GTR (Fig. 5). Final pathology demonstrated IDH-wildtype GBM. The right mesial temporal lesion was treated with LITT. Ablation was performed at a mean power of 10.8 W for 3.5 minutes. Postablation MRI obtained at 24 hours demonstrated an EOA of 120% on volumetric analysis (Supplementary Figs. 1 and 2). The patient was discharged on postablation day 1 without complications. Radiation therapy and temozolomide therapy were initiated. The patient’s follow-up is still ongoing.

Discussion

Observations

Multifocal GBM, defined by the presence of multiple, noncontiguous enhancing or nonenhancing lesions, carries a significantly worse prognosis than unifocal disease.1,13,14 This reflects both the aggressive underlying tumor biology and the challenge of achieving meaningful cytoreduction across anatomically distinct sites. GBM is characterized by near-universal recurrence, with most patients requiring two or three craniotomies over their disease course.15,16 In multifocal cases, traditional management often necessitates separate craniotomies for each lesion, increasing operative risk.3 LITT provides a minimally invasive alternative that allows treatment of multiple foci through percutaneous access, reducing cumulative surgical trauma while preserving functional status and enabling earlier initiation of adjuvant therapy.17,18

Traditional open craniotomy for multifocal GBM is associated with prolonged hospitalization, with mean lengths of stay of 5–7 days reported in contemporary series.19 In contrast, LITT offers substantially shorter hospitalization, with median stays of 1–3 days,11,20 and most patients are discharged directly home, reflecting reduced procedural morbidity.21

Given the challenges of achieving adequate cytoreduction and the need to minimize repeat craniotomies in multifocal GBM, we used a hybrid strategy combining open resection and LITT during a single session. This approach enables maximal cytoreduction of surgically accessible disease, precise treatment of satellite or deep lesions, avoidance of multiple craniotomies, reduced anesthesia exposure, shorter hospitalization, and earlier initiation of adjuvant therapy. Berke et al. recently described a staged hybrid approach with resection followed by LITT on consecutive days.12 In contrast, the first case in the present study represents, to the best of our knowledge, the first report of a same-session hybrid procedure. This strategy achieved near-complete ablation of the periventricular lesion (EOA of 93%), allowed discharge on postoperative day 2 without complications, and resulted in no radiological progression at the 20-month follow-up with preserved functional status.

The ability of LITT to safely treat lesions in eloquent brain regions is a major advantage over open surgery. Microsurgical resection involving the motor cortex, corticospinal tract, language areas, visual pathways, or deep gray nuclei carries a substantial risk of permanent neurological deficits that may outweigh survival benefit.4,22 Surgically acquired deficits significantly reduce quality of life and paradoxically worsen survival despite achieving a greater extent of resection.4 LITT mitigates this risk through a minimally invasive percutaneous approach that avoids cortical and white matter disruption. Furthermore, real-time MRI thermography provides continuous, quantitative temperature mapping throughout the ablation zone and surrounding tissues.23,24 Continuous thermal mapping overlaid on high-resolution anatomical imaging and diffusion tensor imaging tractography allows precise titration of ablation while protecting critical structures. In our second case, thermal monitoring of the optic radiation during mesial temporal ablation was essential to avoid visual field deficits. Visual function was preserved postoperatively, and the patient was discharged on postablation day 1 without complications, underscoring the role of LITT in safely expanding cytoreduction into high-risk anatomical regions.

In the second patient, staging the hybrid approach to await pathological confirmation highlights an important distinction from the previously reported staged approach by Berke et al.,12 who performed LITT on postoperative day 1. In contrast, we intentionally delayed LITT for 5 days to obtain definitive histopathological and molecular confirmation, as the diagnosis remained uncertain given the initially nonenhancing temporal lesion. This represents, to our knowledge, only the second report of a staged hybrid approach for multifocal GBM in the literature. Deferring definitive treatment until IDH-wildtype GBM was confirmed and the lesion was classified as multifocal disease allowed for informed treatment decisions while ultimately enabling effective cytoreduction with LITT without the morbidity and risk associated with a second craniotomy.

Mohammadi et al. demonstrated that the cytoreductive effect of LITT parallels that of surgical debulking, with EOA providing prognostic benefit comparable to extent of resection in high-grade gliomas.10 The EOAs achieved in our cases (93% and 120%) compare favorably with those in surgical series of multifocal GBM, in which GTR rates of only 15% and 22% have been reported.25,26 These findings underscore the limitations of conventional surgery when lesions are spatially separated or located in eloquent regions.

The high EOA achieved with LITT without major complications reflects several technical advantages, including avoidance of multiple craniotomies for spatially separated lesions, real-time MRI thermography for precise assessment of ablation margins, and continuous thermal monitoring with protective heat shielding of adjacent eloquent structures. Given the established association between cytoreduction and survival in multifocal GBM, these capabilities position LITT as a valuable tool for optimizing oncological control while minimizing surgical morbidity.

Lessons

Workflow Advantages and Associated Risks

The same-session hybrid workflow offers several advantages over staged approaches. Performing craniotomy and LITT during a single operative session eliminates repeat anesthesia exposure, which is particularly relevant for older or medically frail patients.27 This strategy enables immediate comprehensive cytoreduction, a single recovery period, shorter hospitalization, and earlier initiation of adjuvant therapy. However, prolonged operative time may increase the risk of surgical site infection and thromboembolic complications.27,28 Brain shift following initial resection may theoretically compromise LITT catheter accuracy. In case 1, resection of the left frontal lesion preceded LITT of a contralateral periventricular lesion during the same session. Because the LITT catheter was placed in the operating room and the patient was subsequently transferred to the MRI for the ablation procedure, any deviation in catheter positioning secondary to brain shift could be directly visualized and corrected before initiating thermal ablation. Real-time MRI thermography confirmed accurate positioning and adequate ablation coverage, demonstrating that single-session hybrid procedures can be performed safely when MRI-guided verification is available to ensure accurate catheter placement despite potential anatomical shifts from preceding surgical manipulation.

Same-session workflows require immediate access to the MRI suite under continuous anesthesia, necessitating coordinated operating room and MRI availability, which may limit feasibility to centers with established protocols for concurrent surgical MRI scheduling. In contrast, staged hybrid approaches reduce individual operative times, allow patient recovery between procedures, and facilitate definitive pathological and molecular diagnosis, enabling more informed treatment decisions when diagnosis remains uncertain. Staging also affords flexibility in LITT timing and reduces concerns about brain shift affecting catheter placement. However, these advantages must be weighed against repeated anesthesia exposure, prolonged overall hospitalization, and delayed cytoreduction. The choice between same-session and staged approaches should be individualized based on patient-specific factors (age, comorbidities, and anesthesia tolerance), diagnostic certainty, and institutional capabilities.

Patient Selection for Same-Session Versus Staged Approaches

A same-session hybrid approach is best suited for older patients or those with significant comorbidities in whom repeat anesthesia poses increased risk. When intraoperative frozen section confirms GBM, as in case 1, a same-session workflow allows immediate comprehensive cytoreduction without awaiting definitive pathology. Successful implementation requires an institutional infrastructure that supports immediate MRI availability under continuous anesthesia. A staged hybrid approach is preferable when diagnosis remains uncertain or when molecular profiling is required to distinguish multifocal GBM from metastatic disease or other pathologies. Staging also benefits patients who may not tolerate prolonged anesthesia, as shorter procedures distributed across separate sessions are often better tolerated.

In case 2, a second biopsy of the right mesial temporal lesion was intentionally avoided prior to LITT ablation. Stereotactic biopsy can result in intracranial hemorrhage or intralesional air formation, both of which alter tissue thermal conductivity and heat distribution, increasing the risk of uncontrolled heating and unpredictable ablation zones. Because GBM was confirmed from the resected lesion, proceeding directly with ablation optimized thermal control and procedural safety.

The primary limitation of this study is the small sample size, which limits generalizability and precludes subgroup analysis. Larger studies are required to define patient selection criteria and to determine whether hybrid strategies confer survival or functional advantages over conventional approaches.

To conclude, a hybrid approach combining resection and LITT represents a promising paradigm shift in the management of multifocal GBM, enabling comprehensive cytoreduction while minimizing the morbidity associated with multiple craniotomies. Our cases demonstrate the technical feasibility, safety, and high EOA achievable with this strategy, including the added benefits of same-session treatment. Real-time MRI thermography allows safe treatment of eloquently located lesions, expanding the treatment envelope for tumors previously considered high-risk or unresectable. Larger prospective studies are needed to confirm the oncological efficacy and long-term survival benefits of this hybrid approach.

Development of β-CD metal organic frameworks loaded with olaparib: a novel approach for the treatment of cervical cancer
Authors: Bader B. Alsulays, Md. Khalid Anwer, Muhannad N. Hatata, Mohammad Raish
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the current study, a highly porous β-CD-metal organic frameworks (βCD-MOFs) was prepared and drug, olaparib (OP) was loaded successfully in order to enhance the efficacy of OP for the treatment of cervical cancer. The produced OP-loaded βCD-MOFs were assessed using TC-1 cell lines for drug encapsulation, FTIR, DSC, TGA, PXRD, in vitro release studies, SEM, and MTT assay. βCD-MOFs successfully encapsulated the OP with a high efficiency of 76.48%, due to structural advantages of βCD-MOFs. In-vitro release studies exhibited a sustained release pattern for 24 h. As compared to free-OP, we observed a significant reduction in cell viability and lowered inhibitory concentration IC50 (13.52 nM) in TC-1 cells treated with OP-loaded βCD-MOFs. Additionally, p53 and caspase 9 were elevated by the OP-loaded βCD-MOFs, suggesting an effective apoptosis that may hold promise for the therapy of cervical cancer. Overall, OP-loaded βCD-MOFs could be alternative to conventional formulation of OP in treating cervical cancer.

Introduction

Cervical cancer remains one of the leading global health burdens affecting women, with significant morbidity and mortality rates particularly prevalent in low- and middle-income countries1,2. The main cause of cervical cancer is infection with specific high-risk human papillomavirus (HPV) strains3,4. Current standard treatments for cervical cancer typically involve surgery, radiation therapy, and chemotherapy, often in combination depending on the stage and location of the cancer5–7. While conventional chemotherapeutic agents like cisplatin and paclitaxel have been foundational in treating cervical cancer, these treatments exhibit significant limitations, notably their non-specific targeting, which damages healthy cells and leads to pronounced adverse effects, including nausea, nephrotoxicity, hematologic toxicity, and cardiotoxicity8,9.

Olaparib (OP), a potent poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor, has demonstrated substantial clinical effectiveness in treating various cancers, particularly those associated with DNA repair deficiencies, such as ovarian and breast cancers10,11. Recent studies have suggested potential effectiveness of OP in cervical cancer, particularly in patients exhibiting homologous recombination repair deficiencies (HRD), a common trait in advanced cervical carcinomas12,13. However, the clinical application of OP faces challenges including limited bioavailability, rapid systemic clearance, and substantial off-target effects. Hence, encapsulating OP in a targeted, controlled-release carrier system represents a significant advancement towards addressing these challenges and optimizing therapeutic outcomes14,15.

The approaches to improve the solubility and bioavailability of olaparib including amorphous solid dispersion16, nanocrystallization14, polymeric nanoparticles15, and self-emulsifying drug delivery systems (SEDDS)17 has been explored. Among these advancements, Cyclodextrin-based metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) have emerged as an innovative class of carriers due to their extraordinary porosity, tunable surface chemistry, exceptional biocompatibility, and responsiveness to the acidic microenvironment commonly found in tumor tissues16. The unique structural properties of β-cyclodextrins based MOFs, creating a platform that enhances the stability and bioavailability of therapeutic agents. Cyclodextrins’ innate capacity to create inclusion complexes makes them perfect for encapsulating a variety of drug molecules since it enhances solubility and provides regulated release profiles18–20.

The primary objectives of this research are to synthesize and thoroughly characterize OP-loaded βCD-based MOFs, evaluate their physicochemical properties, investigate their cytotoxicity and biocompatibility, and assess their therapeutic efficacy in vitro using cervical cancer cell lines. This comprehensive evaluation includes analyses such as particle size distribution, zeta potential, X-ray diffraction (XRD), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and in vitro drug release studies.

Methodology

Materials

OP (olaparib) was purchased from “Beijing Mesochem Technology Co. Ltd (Beijing China)”. Polyethlene glycol (PEG 4000), potassium hydroxide (KOH), and betacyclodextrin (β-CD) were acquired from “Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, USA)”. Analytical-grade chemicals and reagents were all utilized.

Cell culture

The TC-1 cell lines was gifted from by Dr.AHN WS catholic university of Korea. The cells were grown in RPMI-1640 media in monolayer cultures, and added penicillin/streptomycin, 0.22% sodium bicarbonate, and 5% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum. The cells were grown at 37 °C in an incubator with 5% CO2.

Synthesis of β-CD-K MOFs using the diffusion method of methanol

Briefly, accurately weighed amount of β-CD (324 mg) and KOH (112 mg) were dissolved in 10 mL of distilled water. In the prepared solution, polyethylene glycol polymer (128 mg) was added and sonicated for 12 h, subsequently filtered in a glass beaker with 6 mL of methanol. The filtered solution was then sealed in a large beaker with 100 mL of methanol to allow for solvent diffusion, and it was left undisturbed for seven days21,22. The white block crystals were then produced at the bottom of beaker (Fig. 1), washed properly with ethanol to remove residual solvent methanol and further impregnated with polaxomer-188 for solubility enhancement.

Fig. 1Synthesis of βCD-MOFs crystals and loding of OP in βCD-MOFs.

Synthesis of βCD-MOFs crystals and loding of OP in βCD-MOFs.

OP loading into βCD-MOF

Briefly, accurately weighed amount of βCD-MOF (200 mg) was added in solution of OP (10 mg/mL) dissolved in 20 mL of ethanol. The formed solution was then stirred at 500 rpm for 48 h. After filtering, the solid was rinsed three times with ethanol to get rid of any drug residue. The obtained powder was dried at 37 °C overnight21.

Determination of drug encapsulation

The amount of unencapsulated OP in the supernatant will be measured after centrifugation using UV/Vis spectrophotometry. DSC studies

Differential scanning calorimetry “(DSC, Scinco-N650, Seoul, South Korea)” were utilized to scan a spectrum of free-OP, βCD-MOFs and OP-loaded βCD-MOF. Approximately, 5 mg of dried powdered sample put in aluminum pan, pressed by pressure, and the samples were scanned at heating rate of 20°/C in the temperature range of 50–200° with a flow of nitrogen gas (20 mL/min) to provide inert condition23. The amorphous or crystalline nature of samples were checked to evaluate the interaction of OP with βCD-MOF.

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA)

A thermogravimetric analyzer “(NETZSCH TG 209F1 Libra)” was used to examine the thermal behavior of pure-OP, βCD-MOFs, and the developed OP-loaded βCD-MOFs by heating samples in pierced aluminum-crimped pans over the temperature range of 23–800 °C at a constant heating rate of 10 °C/min and under nitrogen gas flow. TGA measures the sample’s mass change as it is subjected to varying temperatures over time. The resulting plot, which shows the percentage of weight loss against temperature, provides details about the samples’ thermal characteristics and stability24.

FTIR analyses

The “FTIR spectrometer” was used to scan the FTIR spectra of free-OP, βCD-MOFs, and OP-loaded βCD-MOFs using the KBr approach. Samples weighing about 1 mg were diluted with 10 mg of crystalline potassium bromide powder and pressed under hydraulic pressure into a transparent pellet in a metal die. The translucent pellet was then placed inside the instrument’s sample holder and scanned within the 4000–400 cm-1 wavenumber range.

X-ray powder diffraction pattern (PXRD)

Ultima-IV diffractometer (Rigaku, Inc., Tokyo, Japan) with Copper (Cu) monochromatized with a graphite crystal and having a Ka2 elimination of 0.154 nm served as the anode material (X-ray tube) was used to study the patterns of free-OP, βCD-MOFs and OP-loaded βCD-MOFs. The XPRD patterns were recorded over a 2-theta (2θ) range of 0° to 60° at a scan rate of 0.5 °·min − 1.

Morphology

Morphology of βCD-MOFs and OP-loaded βCD-MOFs were carried out using Scanning Electron Microscopy. The samples were made ready by mounting on SEM stub with adhesive copper tape. The size and morphology of samples were visualized.

Release studies in vitro

The free-OP and OP-loaded βCD-MOF were subjected to in vitro release assays in two different media of pH 5.5 and pH 6.8. The release studies were conducted by carefully weighing and suspending 10 mg of free-OP and OP-loaded βCD-MOF (≃10 mg of pure-OP) in 10 mL of dissolving medium before placing them in a dialysis bag, agitating them at 300 rpm and 37 °C in a beaker filled with 40 mL of media25. The same media was immediately used to replace the 0.5 mL of sample that was removed at specified intervals. Following appropriate dilution, UV spectroscopy was used to analyze the samples at 279 nm. To determine the drug’s release pattern from the βCD-MOF, the release results were plotted and fitted into a number of kinetic models (zero order, First order, Higuchi model and Korsmeyer peppas)25.

Studies of in vitro cytotoxicity

The TC-1 cells were deposited at a seeding density of 3 × 103 cells per well in a 96-well plate. The free-OP and OP-loaded βCD-MOFs were added to the fresh media to prepare different concentrations after the cells had been incubated for 12 h. The MTT technique was used to determine cell viability following a 24-hour incubation period. Briefly, TC-1 cells were treated with MTT (5 mg/mL, 20 µL per well) for 4 h. The medium was removed with a syringe and the formazan crystal was fully dissolved in DMSO. The samples’ absorbance was measured at 490 nm using a Power Wave XS Microplate Spectrophotometer26. The following equation was used to get the cell viability (%). Plots of cell viability (%) vs. log concentration of drug were used to illustrate the results, which were normalized based on the values obtained for viable control cells (seen as 100%). Apoptosis activities of p53 and caspase-9

To understand the complex mechanisms behind cellular death, it is essential to appreciate the actions of these important markers, including p53 and caspase-9, a tumor suppressor protein. By examining their actions, it can learn important things about diseases like cancer that are linked to abnormal cell death regulation. ELISA kits were used to measure the p53, and Caspase-9 activities in accordance with published publications27. 5 × 104 TC-1 cells were planted in each well of 96-well plates. The TC-1 (3 × 103) cells per well were seeded into 96-well plates. The aqueous of free-OP and OP-loaded βCD-MOFs had been applied to the seeded cells. Each well in the plate containing 100 µL of growth media was filled with 100 µL of the p53 and Caspase-9 reagents after each treated cell had been allowed to reach room temperature. Following an hour of incubation, a microplate reader set to 405 nm was used to measure the optical density (OD).

Morphological changes on TC-1 cells

By visualizing the morphological alterations in treated TC-1 cells, the cytotoxic effect of free-OP and OP-loaded βCD-MOF were determined. The morphological characteristics of TC-1 treated cells were manifested by phase-contrast microscopy. Necrosis, cell shrinkage, and membrane blebbing were identified as morphological characteristics.

Statistical analysis

“All data was provided as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance was determined using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by a Bonferroni’s multiple comparison test. The level of significance was set at P < 0.05”.

Results and discussion

Determination of drug encapsulation

The better affinity of β-CD-MOF allowed for the efficient encapsulation of OP-loaded βCD-MOF in βCD-MOF, with a drug encapsulation of 76.48%. The stability and effectiveness of loading OP into this novel framework are greatly increased by the -NH group in OP, which is essential for its interaction with the βCD within the βCD-MOFs architecture and for its strong electrostatic interactions with the potassium ions (K+ cations) present in βCD-MOFs.

DSC studies

A comparative DSC spectrum of free-OP, βCD-MOFs and OP-loaded βCD-MOF are shown in Fig. 2. free-OP exhibited intense endothermic peak corresponding to melting temperature at 217 °C, indicating drug’s crystalline nature14. The broad endothermic peak of 143 °C was seen in the DSC spectra of βCD-MOFs. The DSC spectra of OP-loaded βCD-MOF exhibited a slight of endothermic peak to 215 °C with marked reduction of in the intensity of peak. The results revealed masking endotherm, which might be due to encapsulation of OP in porous structure of β-CD-MOFs.

FTIR studies

As shown in Fig. 3, FTIR spectra of free-OP was compared with βCD-MOFs and OP-loaded βCD-MOF. The FTIR spectrum of free-OP demonstrated a sharp intense characteristic peak at 3171.36 cm− 1, 3011.30 cm− 1 corresponding to stretching vibrations of -NH and aromatic -CH groups, respectively. In addition, peaks at 2909.09 cm− 1, 2369.12 cm− 1, and 810.92 cm− 1 C = C stretching, C = O stretching and C-F stretching, respectively. In contrast to free-OP, these peaks either vanished or became noticeably weaker in the FTIR spectra of OP-loaded βCD-MOF. This implies that the OP was successfully trapped inside the porous structure of MOFs.

X-ray powder diffraction pattern (PXRD)

To evaluate the encapsulation of drug inside porous βCD-MOFs structure and ascertain the physical characteristics, chemical makeups, and interactions, XPRD analysis is still a useful tool. Figure 4 shows the XPRD pattern of free-OP, βCD-MOFs and OP-loaded βCD-MOF. The XPRD pattern of free-OP exhibited many intense sharp peaks at 13.2°, 13.6°, 13.8°, 15.8°, 17.4°,17.6°,17.8°,18.8°,19.0°, 20.8°, 21.0°, 21.4°, 21.6°,21.8°, 22.0°, 22.2°, 23.2°,23.4°, 24.0°, 25.8°, and 26.0° demonstrated its crystalline nature. However, XPRD pattern of βCD-MOFs exhibited few peaks of intense peaks at 9.0°, 10.4°, 12.4°, 12.6°, 16.0°, 16.2°, 17.0°,17.2°,17.4°, and 27.4°, which is in agreement with reported in literature28. All the XPRD peaks of free-OP could not be seen OP-loaded βCD-MOF, except few peaks at an angle of βCD-MOFs appeared. The results show that the major amount of the drug was entrapped in the porous structure of βCD-MOFs, while few peak of drug still appeared, and the DSC investigations also supported this finding.

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA)

TGA is used to assess how well certain compounds respond to temperature in terms of dehydration, degradation, and decomposition29. A comparative TGA spectra of pure-OP, the βCD-MOFs and OP-loaded βCD-MOF were depicted in Fig. 5. The thermal weight loss of free-OP exhibited higher thermal decomposition and a weight loss in two stages (11.22% in the first stage and 67.27% in second stage). As shown in βCD-MOFs had two stage weight loss as 11.94% in first stage, 63.13% in second stage. However, the TGA of OP-loaded βCD-MOF had different thermal effect, it shows higher thermal with lower mass loss (0.58% in the first stage and 58.55% in second stage). This might be explained by the fact that OP was thermally protected after being included in βCD-MOFs and that were difficult to break as the temperature increased.

Release studies in vitro

To demonstrate that the OP could be released over an extended period by the OP-loaded βCD-MOF. When compared to pure-OP, the release profiles showed a considerably higher dissolution of OP from OP-loaded βCD-MOF (Figure). At pH 6.8, a relatively greater release was seen (Fig. 6b) than at pH 1.2 (Fig. 7a), OP’s pH-dependent solubility was the cause of the variation in the release profiles at the two different pH values14. Figure 7b displayed the OP sustained release at pH 6.8. It was demonstrated that the OP was constantly released over a 24-hour period by the OP-loaded βCD-MOF. The release of drug from OP and OP-loaded βCD-MOF were observed 56.2% and 93.41%, respectively, in 24 h. The enhanced with sustained release of OP from OP-loaded βCD-MOF was observed over 24 h. The findings above suggested that the βCD-MOF’s porous structure could regulate the OP’s release behavior30. Using the release kinetics equations, the release pattern of OP from βCD-MOF form was examined. Using the kinetic models, it was discovered that Korsmeyer–Peppas could adequately describe the release of OP from βCD-MOF at both pH with (R2 = 0.9615) at pH 5.5 and (R2 = 0.9105) at pH 6.8.

Studies of in-vitro cytotoxicity

Cervical cancer research and treatment have advanced significantly with the use of TC1 cell lines in experiments. TC1 cell lines obtained from HPV16 E6/E7- transformed mouse cells, offer a strong foundation for researching the biology of cervical cancer and evaluating possible treatment options31. The in-vitro cytotoxicity effects of OP-loaded βCD-MOFs were evaluated against TC1 by MTT assay. The ability of the OP-loaded βCD-MOFs to reduce the viability of TC1 cells in a dose-dependent manner is shown in Fig. 8. At concentrations of 100 nM and above, the OP-loaded βCD-MOFs were found to be cytotoxic to cancer cells after 4 h of treatment in comparison to free-OP (Fig. 8). The IC50 values of pure-OP and OP-loaded βCD-MOFs against TC1 cells were 27.93 nM (R2 = 0.9858), and 13.52 nM (R2 = 0.9764), respectively. The data of MTT assay against TC-1 cells in the present investigation, revealed a significant (p < 0.05) enhancement in the cytotoxic effect of OP-loaded βCD-MOFs in comparison to pure-OP. The release of OP was regulated or controlled due multiple mechanism of such as large cavities, surface adsorption, structural adjustment and 3D laminated structure32–35.

Apoptosis activities of p53 and caspase-9

To confirm apoptosis, free-OP and OP-loaded βCD-MOFs treated TC-1 cells produced more caspase-9 and p53 markers in comparison to control (untreated) (Fig. 9). On exposure to TC-1 cell lines caspase-9 and p53 activities enhanced 3.95 and 2.75 folds by free-OP, and 5.75 and 3.71 folds by OP-loaded βCD-MOFs, respectively, when compared to control. It is more likely that this is what triggers cancer cells to undergo apoptosis given the enhanced effectiveness of OP-loaded βCD-MOFs36.

Morphological changes on TC-1 cells

The efficacy of anticancer treatments in cell-based models is commonly evaluated through alterations in cell viability, morphology, and activation of apoptosis-related pathways. In the present study, treatment responses in TC-1 cells were assessed by examining morphological changes together with variations in apoptotic markers, particularly p53 and caspase-9, both of which play central roles in tumor suppression and mitochondrial-mediated apoptosis signaling37–39. Untreated TC-1 cells maintained normal morphology and viability after 24 h incubation, exhibiting uniform adherent growth and intact cellular architecture, confirming stable culture conditions in the absence of treatment. In contrast, TC-1 cells exposed to free OP and OP-loaded βCD-MOFs for 24 h demonstrated marked morphological alterations, as shown in Fig. 10. Treated cells displayed features such as cell shrinkage, rounding, reduced surface attachment, and decreased cell density, which are widely recognized indicators of cellular stress and early apoptotic events39,40. More pronounced morphological disruption was observed in cells treated with OP-loaded βCD-MOFs compared to free OP, suggesting improved intracellular delivery mediated by the carrier system. These morphological changes likely result from enhanced cellular uptake and intracellular accumulation of OP, subsequently activating cytotoxic and apoptotic pathways in TC-1 cells. Collectively, these findings indicate that OP, particularly when delivered via βCD-MOFs, effectively compromises cancer cell integrity within 24 h of treatment40–43, A favorable anticancer response is generally characterized by reduced cell viability and proliferation, along with morphological indicators such as cell shrinkage, rounding, detachment, and reduced confluency. At the molecular level, increased activation or expression of p53, a key regulator of DNA damage response, together with elevated caspase-9, reflects activation of intrinsic apoptotic pathways leading to programmed cell death37–39.In agreement with these criteria, treated TC-1 cells in this study exhibited decreased cell density and apoptosis-associated morphological changes accompanied by increased apoptotic signaling markers, supporting the conclusion that the tested treatment exerts anticancer activity via induction of programmed cell death pathways.

Conclusion

In this work, βCD-MOFs was synthesized using βCD and KOH by methanol diffusion method. The OP was successfully loaded in β-CD-MOFs with remarkable improvement in drug release and cytotoxicity against TC-1 cell lines in comparison to pure-OP. The OP-loaded βCD-MOFs were characterized by FTIR, DSC, TGA, XRD and SEM studies that confirmed successfully loading of OP in βCD-MOFs, which could improve the OP performance in drug formulation. Cell viability was significantly decreased in a dose-dependent manner when using the MTT assay against TC-1 cell lines. The IC50 values of pure-OP and OP-loaded βCD-MOFs were 27.93 nM and 13.52 nM, respectively. The reduction in cell viability was further supported by apoptosis marker P53 and caspase-9. The developed OP-loaded βCD-MOFs could be alternative to conventional formulation of OP in treating cervical cancer.

Peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance Pythium oligandrum strain B301
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Fernando Álvarez, Maria Arena, Domenica Auteri, Sofia Batista Leite, Marco Binaglia, Anna Federica Castoldi, Arianna Chiusolo, Angelo Colagiorgi, Mathilde Colas, Federica Crivellente, Chloe De Lentdecker, Isabella De Magistris, Mark Egsmose, Gabriella Fait, Franco Ferilli, German Giner Santonja, Varvara Gouliarmou, Katrin Halling, Laia Herrero Nogareda, Alessio Ippolito, Frederique Istace, Samira Jarrah, Dimitra Kardassi, Aude Kienzler, Anna Lanzoni, Roberto Lava, Renata Leuschner, Alberto Linguadoca, Jochem Louisse, Christopher Lythgo, Oriol Magrans, Iris Mangas, Galini Mavriou, Andrea Mioč, Ileana Miron, Tunde Molnar, Laura Padovani, Vincenzo Padricello, Martina Panzarea
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe conclusions of EFSA following the peer review of the initial risk assessments carried out by the competent authority of the rapporteur Member State Belgium for the pesticide active substance Pythium oligandrum strain B301 and the considerations as regards the inclusion of the substance in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 are reported. The context of the peer review was that required by Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council. The conclusions were reached on the basis of the evaluation of the representative uses of Pythium oligandrum strain B301 as a resistance inducer/elicitor to control trunk diseases on grapevines. The reliable endpoints, appropriate for use in regulatory risk assessment, are presented. Missing information identified as being required by the regulatory framework is listed. Concerns are reported where identified.

SUMMARY

Pythium oligandrum strain B301 is a new active substance for which, in accordance with Article 7 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council, the rapporteur Member State (RMS), Belgium, received an application from Biovitis S.A. on 15 March 2021 for approval. In addition, the applicant submitted an application for inclusion of the substance in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. Complying with Article 9 of the Regulation, the completeness of the dossier was checked by the RMS and the date of admissibility of the application was recognised as being 9 April 2021.

An initial evaluation of the dossier on P. oligandrum strain B301 was provided by the RMS in the draft assessment report (DAR) and subsequently, a peer review of the pesticide risk assessment on the RMS evaluation was conducted by EFSA in accordance with Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. The following conclusions are derived.

The uses of P. oligandrum strain B301 according to the representative uses of field application by drip irrigation as a systemic resistance inducer/elicitor to control trunk diseases on grapevines, as proposed at EU level, result in a sufficient efficacy against the target fungal pathogens.

The assessment of the data package revealed no issues that could not be finalised or that need to be included as critical areas of concern with respect to identity, physical–chemical and technical properties of the formulation for representative uses, and analytical methods.

In the area of mammalian toxicology, no toxicological concern is identified for P. oligandrum strain B301.

P. oligandrum strain B301 does not meet the five criteria laid down in the Guidance document on criteria for the inclusion of active substances into Annex IV Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. Particularly, criterion III of having no identified hazardous properties cannot deemed to be met for non‐viable residues (secondary metabolites) in the absence of an updated literature search and in view of an inconclusive residue behaviour of viable residues which may form metabolites in situ in/on edible plant parts. In consequence, the consumer risk assessment cannot be finalised.

Satisfactory information was not provided in relation to P. oligandrum strain B301 not being expected to persist and multiply in soil in concentrations considerably higher than the natural background levels, taking into account repeated applications over the years. Consequently, this resulted in an assessment that could not be finalised.

Satisfactory information was not provided in relation to the identification, hazard characterisation and levels of secondary metabolites/toxins produced by P. oligandrum strain B301 and potentially present after the application of the product, and an assessment of their risk to non‐target organisms and leaching to groundwater. Consequently, this resulted in an assessment that could not be finalised.

Satisfactory information was not provided in relation to toxicity, infectivity and pathogenicity to non‐target soil arthropods and potential adverse effects for soil microorganisms exposed to P. oligandrum strain B301. Consequently, this resulted in an assessment that could not be finalised.

Overall, the risk assessment could not be finalised for birds, wild mammals, aquatic organisms, bees, non‐target arthropods other than bees and soil organisms including soil microorganisms.

BACKGROUND

Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council 1 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Regulation’) lays down, inter alia, the detailed rules as regards the procedure and conditions for approval of active substances. This regulates for the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) the procedure for organising the consultation of Member States and the applicant(s) for comments on the initial evaluation in the draft assessment report (DAR), provided by the rapporteur Member State (RMS) and the organisation of an expert consultation, where appropriate.

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA is required to adopt a conclusion on whether an active substance can be expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of the Regulation (also taking into consideration recital (10) of the Regulation) within 120 days from the end of the period provided for the submission of written comments, subject to an extension of 30 days where an expert consultation is necessary, and a further extension of up to 150 days where additional information is required to be submitted by the applicant(s) in accordance with Article 12(3).

Pythium oligandrum strain B301 is a new active substance for which, in accordance with Article 7 of the Regulation, the RMS, Belgium (hereinafter referred to as the ‘RMS’), received an application from Biovitis S.A. on 15 March 2021 for approval. In addition, the applicant submitted an application for inclusion of the substance in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. Complying with Article 9 of the Regulation, the completeness of the dossier was checked by the RMS and the date of admissibility of the application was recognised as being 9 April 2021.

The RMS provided its initial evaluation of the dossier on Pythium oligandrum strain B301 in the DAR, which was received by EFSA on 13 June 2022 (Belgium, 2022). The peer review was initiated on 13 September 2022 by dispatching the DAR to the Member States and the applicant, Biovitis S.A., for consultation and comments. EFSA also provided comments. In addition, EFSA conducted a public consultation on the DAR. The comments received were collated by EFSA and forwarded to the RMS for compilation and evaluation in the format of a reporting table. The applicant was invited to respond to the comments in column 3 of the reporting table. The comments and the applicant's response were evaluated by the RMS in column 3 of the reporting table.

The need for expert consultation and the necessity for additional information to be submitted by the applicant in accordance with Article 12(3) of the Regulation were considered in a teleconference between EFSA and the RMS on 13 January 2023. On the basis of the comments received, the applicant's response to the comments and the RMS's evaluation thereof, it was concluded that additional information should be requested from the applicant, and that EFSA should conduct an expert consultation in the areas of potential effects on human/mammalian health and potential effects on non‐target organisms.

The outcome of the teleconference, together with EFSA's further consideration of the comments, is reflected in the conclusions set out in column 4 of the reporting table. All points that were identified as unresolved at the end of the comment evaluation phase and which required further consideration, including those issues to be considered in an expert consultation, were compiled by EFSA in the format of an evaluation table.

The conclusions arising from the consideration by EFSA, and as appropriate by the RMS, of the points identified in the evaluation table, together with the outcome of the expert consultation and the written consultation on the assessment of additional information, where these took place, were reported in the final column of the evaluation table.

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA should adopt a conclusion on whether Pythium oligandrum strain B301 can be expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of the Regulation, taking into consideration recital (10) of the Regulation.

A final consultation on the conclusions arising from the peer review of the risk assessment and on the considerations as regards the inclusion of the substance in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 took place with Member States via a written procedure in June 2024.

This conclusion report summarises the outcome of the peer review of the risk assessment on the active substance and the formulation for representative uses evaluated on the basis of the representative uses of Pythium oligandrum strain B301 as a resistance inducer/elicitor to control trunk diseases on grapevines, as proposed by the applicant. In accordance with Article 12(2) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, risk mitigation options identified in the DAR and considered during the peer review, if any, are presented in the conclusion.

Furthermore, this conclusion also addresses the requirement for an assessment by EFSA under Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, provided that the active substance will be approved under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 without restrictions affecting the residue assessment. In the event of a non‐approval of the active substance or an approval with restrictions that have an impact on the residue assessment, the Annex IV considerations and/or eventual Annex IV proposal from this conclusion might no longer be relevant and a new assessment under Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 will be required.

The microbial active substance and the formulation for representative uses

Pythium oligandrum strain B301 is a fungus‐like eukaryotic microorganism (oomycete) deposited at the National Collection of Pasteur Institute (CNCM – Institut Pasteur), Paris, France, under the deposit number I‐5180.

The formulated product for the representative uses for the evaluation was ‘17PYO1B’, a wettable powder (WP) containing 15 g/kg of P. oligandrum strain B301 (nominal 2 × 105 oospores/g, 1 × 105 oospores/g to 3 × 105 oospores/g).

The information on the active substance and the formulation for representative uses, including the co‐formulant(s) in this formulation, has been considered in the overall assessment during the peer review. None of the co‐formulant(s) is an unacceptable co‐formulant listed in Annex III of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 2 , however one co‐formulant is a currently approved active substance under Regulation (EC) 1107/2009 3 . Details on the composition of the formulation cannot be reported in conclusions because of the provisions in Article 63(2)(d) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, however this information was fully available and evaluated during the peer review. Proposal for classification of the formulation according to Regulation (EC) 1272/2008 was provided by the applicant and assessed by the RMS (please see Volumes 3 MP of the DAR).

The representative field uses evaluated were drip irrigation application (professional use) on grapevine to control trunk diseases (such us esca, dieback and black dead‐arm) caused by a broad range of fungal pathogens (such as Phaeomoniella chlamydospora, Phaeoacremonium minimum, Fomitiporia mediterranea, Eutypa lata, Neofusicoccum parvum, Diplodia seriata, Botryosphaeria stevensii). It is applied as a preventive treatment. Full details of the GAP can be found in the list of end points in Appendix A.

Data were submitted to conclude that the use of P. oligandrum strain B301 according to the representative uses proposed at EU level results in a sufficient efficacy as systemic resistance inducer/elicitor against a subsequent attack by the pathogens, following the guidance document SANCO/10054/2013 – rev. 3 (European Commission, 2013).

CONCLUSIONS OF THE EVALUATION

General aspects

With regard to the mammalian toxicity information available for the formulation for representative uses ‘17PYO1B’, studies were performed for the skin and eye irritation potential. With regard to the co‐formulant(s) contained in ‘17PYO1B’, sufficient toxicological data were available for all components. The experts considered that the available toxicological information does sufficiently address the acute toxicity, genotoxicity, pathogenicity, infectiveness and repeated dose toxicity potential of ‘17PYO1B’ over short‐ and long‐term and no concern was identified. 4

Suitable ecotoxicity data with the formulation for representative uses were available for the assessment of non‐target organisms according to the requirements of Regulation (EU) No 284/2013. 5 Based on the available toxicity data, it is noted that the formulation for representative uses is not more toxic than the active substance. Available data on the composition of the formulation were discussed at an experts meeting. 6 The experts agreed that the formulation for representative uses was sufficiently characterised and no concern was identified.

A data gap has been identified for an updated search of the scientific peer‐reviewed open literature on the microbial active substance and its metabolites of (potential) concern, dealing with biological properties, 7 side effects on health, 8 residues, 9 the environment and non‐target species 10 and published within 10 years before the date of submission of the dossier, to be conducted and reported in accordance with the EFSA guidance on the submission of scientific peer‐reviewed open literature for the approval of pesticide active substances under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 (EFSA, 2011).

For non‐peer‐reviewed published papers information was insufficient whether these studies were performed according to GLP or by way of derogation by officially recognised testing facilities as stated in Commission Regulation (EU) No 283/2013 11 (data gap, see Section 10).

SPECIFICATION OF THE MICROORGANISM, PHYSICAL AND TECHNICAL PROPERTIES AND METHODS OF ANALYSIS

The following guidance documents were followed in the production of this conclusion: European Commission (2000, 2012).

The minimum and maximum content of P. oligandrum strain B301 in the microbial pest control agent (MPCA) was 1 × 106–1 × 107 oospores/g (nominal 1.68 × 106 oospores/g). The specification was based on batch data from industrial plant production; however, the representativeness of these batches was not justified (data gap, see Section 10). It is noted that information on the viability of the oospores in the MPCA and microbial pest control product (MPCP) is missing (data gap, see Section 10). In addition, information on whether one of the substances added during the manufacturing process remains in the MPCA and at which levels is missing (data gap, see Section 10); therefore, it cannot be concluded if this substance should be considered as an additive. Due to data gaps related with batch data and lack of information on the viability of the oospores in the MPCA, the proposed specification should be considered as provisional.

Microscopic taxonomic analysis of species‐characteristics, in particular spiny‐walled oogonium, can be used to distinguish P. oligandrum from other Pythium species. In silico approach was used to develop a sequence characterised amplified region (SCAR) marker capable to distinguish P. oligandrum strain B301 from other closely related strains, however data demonstrating the reliability of this marker at experimental conditions were not provided (data gap, see Section 10).

The content of microbial contaminants of the MPCA and the MPCP were below the limits defined in the Working Document on Microbial Contaminant Limits (European Commission, 2012). It was demonstrated that P. oligandrum strain B301 is able to synthesise some secondary metabolites of potential concern (see Section 2); however, only tryptamine was considered as a known metabolite for which a hazard was identified and it was proposed to be specified at a maximum level of 0.41 g/kg (see Section 3.2). Data showing that tryptamine was not present up to the limit of quantification (LOQ) (0.41 g/kg) in five batches of MPCA were provided, however it was not demonstrated that these batches were in compliance with the proposed specification (i.e. P. oligandrum strain B301 content not provided) (data gap, see Section 10).

Accelerated storage stability study (18 weeks at 30°C) and shelf‐life study (2 years at 20°C) were available and demonstrated lack of growth of contaminating microorganisms and that the content of the MPCA in the formulation remains within the specified content during the storage. However, information on the viability of the oospores before and after storage is missing (data gap, see Section 10).

Acceptable method(s) were available for numeration the microorganism in the MPCA and MPCP. However, accuracy of the method used for quantification of the microorganism in the MPCP was not demonstrated (data gap, see Section 10). In addition, validated method(s) for determination of the viability of oospores in the MPCA and MPCP is missing (data gap, see Section 10). Acceptable methods were available for determination of the content of contaminating microorganisms in the MPCA and MPCP, and metabolites tryptamine and oligandrin in the MPCA. Monitoring methods are not needed since residue definitions for P. oligandrum strain B301 were not proposed.

BIOLOGICAL PROPERTIES OF THE MICROORGANISM

Pythium oligandrum strain B301 is an indigenous wild type necrotrophic mycoparasite naturally found in soil, initially isolated in 2011 from soil cultivating vineyards in the Bordeaux region (France). A data gap for the natural occurrence levels of the microorganism in the environment including description and validation of the method used was set (see Section 10).

The target organisms for the representative uses are a broad range of fungal pathogens on grapevine such as Phaeomoniella chlamydospora, Phaeoacremonium minimum, Fomitiporia mediterranea, Eutypa lata, Neofusicoccum parvum, Diplodia seriata and Botryosphaeria stevensii.

The proposed mode of action of P. oligandrum strain B301 for the representative uses is enhancement of the resistance/defence reactions of grapevine against the target pathogens in a systemic way when applied as a preventative treatment at the root level. The microorganism is also capable for mycoparasitism and chemical interaction and/or competition for nutrient elements but is not strictly dependent upon a host for growth and reproduction.

The life cycle of a typical Pythium species belonging to the genus Pythium is described by occurrence of two cycles: an asexual phase with production of sporangia (multinucleated) that may germinate in liquid or on a surface to produce a germ tube or may differentiate with production of zoospores (biflagellated), usually retained in a discharge vesicle before dissemination. Once released from the sporangia (vesicle disruption), a rapid encystment of the zoospores (with loss of flagellae) is described. This encystment is then able to subsequent germination (by the formation of a germ tube that directly penetrates into the root or by formation of an appressorium).a sexual phase generating oospores (survival form). The oosporogenesis involves the production of a female oogonium and a male antheridium that grows towards and fuses with the oogonium.

As regards genetic stability , P. oligandrum strain B301 is a ‘wild type’ and there are no marker genes in the strain which would permit analysis of a frequency of genetic exchange. As the genetic diversity and drift in the wild‐type population has not been ascertained, it would not be possible to distinguish any genetic drift from that in the wild population based on the information provided. Though it is acknowledged that the possibility and effects of transfer of genetic material is not different for P. oligandrum strain B301 than for other naturally occurring P. oligandrum strains, transfer of genetic material by P. oligandrum strain B301 after application is possible and could not be excluded based on the information available in the dossier.

Information was available showing that P. oligandrum strain B301 grows at 35°C and does not grow at temperature ≥ 38°C; however, no verifiable data were available for 37°C, therefore the growth at human body temperature could not be excluded. The growth of P. oligandrum in liquid medium occurred over the range of pH 5.0–9.0 with the optimum range for biomass production between pH 6.0 and 7.5 and was inhibited at pH 4.5 and 9.5. The optimum pH range for biomass production for strain B301 is pH 5.5 and pH 8.2. However, information on the effect of UV light and humidity on survival, reproduction and colonisation ability of P. oligandrum strain B301 was not provided (data gap, see Section 10).

An analysis of the genome of P. oligandrum strain B301 for known bacterial anti‐microbial resistant (AMR) genes was performed. No AMR genes associated with bacterial resistance to a wide variety of antibiotics was found. However, a data gap was set for data demonstrating susceptibility of the microorganism to some antimycotic substances to ensure availability of treatment options in case infection with the fungal microorganism may occur (data gap, see Section 10).

With regard to information on the relationship to known plant, animal or human pathogens, the only species in the genus of Pythium reported to infect mammals is P. insidiosum, known to cause life‐threatening infections in humans and animals. The available data for P. oligandrum species did not identify clinical cases or infections in humans.

Based on the literature review conducted for the microorganism, the following secondary metabolites of potential concern were identified for P. oligandrum strain B301: tryptamine; elicitin‐like proteins including oligandrin, immunoglobulin – Ig A peptidase; and necrosis and ethylene‐inducing proteins (NLPs). However, the literature search is considered unsatisfactory, therefore other metabolites of (potential) concern might not have been identified. An updated literature search using specific search terms for metabolites of potential concern was also not provided by the applicant (data gap, see Section 10) but an additional screening of the open literature was performed by the RMS.

The analysis of the genome of P. oligandrum strain B301 identified the presence of genes coding for the production of elicitin‐like proteins including oligandrin, as well as ‘putative genes’ related to the production of tryptamine and necrosis and ethylene‐inducing proteins (NLPs).

From the toxicological perspective, tryptamine is a known biogenic amine with known hazardous properties (see Section 3.2) supporting its potential toxicological concern.

In the available open scientific literature, there is no indication that oligandrin and NLPs would be of any toxicological concern. Immunoglobulin IgA peptidase was found in P. iwayami, P. arrhenomanes and P. ultimum var. sporangiiferum, but not in P. oligandrum. In addition, no hazardous effects have been reported in the available (eco)toxicological studies (see Sections 3.1 and 6) conducted with the MPCA.

For the fate and behaviour, as regards levels present in the environment after application and potential effects on non‐target organisms, insufficient information was available on secondary metabolites of potential concern such as tryptamine, elicitin‐like proteins including oligandrin, immunoglobulin – Ig A peptidase and NLPs (see Sections 5.2 and 6). For residues and the dietary consumer exposure, insufficient information on potential in‐situ formation of metabolites of potential concern was available for conditions of the representative use (see Section 4).

POTENTIAL EFFECTS ON HUMAN/MAMMALIAN HEALTH

Pythium oligandrum strain B301 was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Teleconference (TC) 126 (31 January – 1 February 2024). From the evaluation of the available data, including medical data and acute toxicity studies, no toxicological concern is identified for P. oligandrum strain B301.

Mammalian infectivity and pathogenicity of the microorganism and sensitisation effects

Based on the available literature review, medical data did not report evidence of adverse effects in relationship with P. oligandrum; the only species in the genus of Pythium reported to infect mammals is P. insidiosum, known to cause life‐threatening infections (pythiosis) in humans and animals.

The medical surveillance on manufacturing plant personnel (using protective equipment) does not report any adverse health effect attributable to P. oligandrum strain B301 exposure.

Pythium oligandrum (strain B301) is an oomycete and as such it is not included in the qualified presumption of safety (QPS) list (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2020) due to the lack of information on the toxicogenic potential of oomycetes, meaning that a full safety assessment by EFSA is required.

The MPCA is not a skin sensitiser according to the results of the local lymph node assay (LLNA) and no sensitisation/allergenicity observations to manufacturing plant personnel have been reported in medical surveillance data. However, at the current status of knowledge, sensitisation by microorganisms cannot be fully investigated, since the available methods for testing dermal sensitisation are not tailored to microorganisms and no validated test methods for sensitisation by inhalation are available. Therefore, P. oligandrum strain B301 may have the potential to provoke sensitising reactions.

In the acute toxicity studies, no treatment‐related effects are observed after oral, inhalation and intraperitoneal administration of P. oligandrum strain B301 in rats. Clearance is sufficiently investigated and, despite it cannot be excluded that the MPCA can grow at human temperature, the results of the studies support lack of infectivity/pathogenicity potential. 12

Considering the absence of any toxicity, pathogenicity or infectivity potential, no additional studies are needed. No toxicological reference values are required for P. oligandrum strain B301, and no operator, worker, resident and bystander exposure estimates for the MPCA are triggered. In the absence of a quantitative risk assessment, the use of personal and respiratory protective equipment (PPE/RPE) for the operators and workers might be considered to reduce the risk of adverse effects due to the sensitisation potential of microorganisms.

Mammalian toxicity of the metabolites of (potential) concern

Secondary metabolites of potential toxicological concern were identified in the literature review conducted for the microorganism (see Section 2). However, the literature search is considered unsatisfactory, therefore other metabolites of potential concern might not have been identified (data gap, see Section 10).

Based on the available information i.e. whole genome sequence (WGS) data, analysis of pilot batches and open scientific literature (OSL), 13 no concern is raised for the identified secondary metabolites of P. oligandrum strain B301, except for tryptamine. Being a known biogenic amine, and proposed by ECHA notifiers for classification as Acute Toxicity Cat. 4 (H302), Skin Sens. Cat. 1A (H317) and Eye Dam. Cat. 1 (H318), it is confirmed to be toxicologically relevant.

Considering that tryptamine is found in food commodities (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2011) and that the WGS data identified only ‘putative genes’ related to its production, the peer review agreed that the concern related to potential tryptamine exposure is low as long as its level is kept at/or below the limit of quantification (LOQ) (≤ 0.41 g/kg) in the MPCA.

The in‐situ production of secondary metabolites is not relevant for the non‐dietary risk assessment of this MPCA due to the application mode (drip irrigation) triggering only a limited occupational exposure.

RESIDUES

Regarding viable residues, the residue behaviour with regard to persistence and potential multiplication on edible commodities following the representative use of P. oligandrum strain B301 still needs to be demonstrated because this is considered relevant for an evaluation of potential in situ formation of metabolites which are of potential concern and – noting that the literature search is considered unsatisfactory – may not have been identified. Therefore, a data gap has been set for adequate experimental evidence on the presence, persistence and multiplication of P. oligandrum strain B301 on edible commodities after treatment according to the proposed GAP (see Section 9.1). Furthermore, the requested updated information on toxins/metabolites of toxicological relevance following the representative use in/on edible plant parts post treatment in the literature was also not provided (data gap for an updated literature search, see also Sections 2 and 10).

Concerning the formation of tryptamine, this biogenic amine is known to be formed by microbial activities directly by decarboxylation of the amino acid tryptophan mainly in protein rich food raw materials and food. Tryptamine, including the whole group of biogenic amines, are undesired compounds in food; however, tryptamine was not considered to be a causative agent in food poisoning outbreaks (EFSA BIOHAZ Panel, 2011).

The consumer risk assessment cannot be finalised until conclusive information on in‐situ formation of potential toxicologically relevant metabolites on edible plant parts following treatment until harvest is available (see Section 9.1).

With regard to the five assessment criteria according to the Commission guidance SANCO/11188/2013 Rev. 2 (European Commission, 2015) for potential inclusion in Annex IV of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, i.e. approval as basic substance (criterion I), listed in Annex I of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (criterion II), having no identified hazardous properties (criterion III), natural exposure is higher than the one linked to the use as a plant protection product (criterion IV), and consumer exposure is not expected considering the representative uses (criterion V), criteria I, II, IV and V are not met because P. oligandrum strain B301 is not a basic substance, not listed in Annex I of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, it is not demonstrated that natural exposure to P. oligandrum strain B301 is higher than the one linked to the use as a plant protection product and consumer exposure cannot be excluded; criterion III cannot deemed to be met for metabolites of potential concern and any potential in‐situ production by viable residues following treatment according to the representative use. It is noted that in Section 3, based on the available information no concerns were identified, nevertheless an updated literature search was not provided (data gap, see Section 10) and therefore absence of metabolites of potential concern cannot be concluded upon. In addition, the residue behaviour of viable residues on edible plant commodities is inconclusive (data gap, see Section 9.1). In consequence, the consumer risk assessment cannot be finalised.

ENVIRONMENTAL FATE AND BEHAVIOUR

Although inadequate information was provided in relation to potential interferences of P. oligandrum strain B301 with the analytical systems for the control of the quality of drinking water provided for in Directive 98/83/EC, 14 the exposure to groundwater and surface water sources for drinking water is considered marginal due to the representative use as drip irrigation. Interference of P. oligandrum strain B301 with the analytical methods for the control of the quality of drinking water is therefore not expected.

Fate and behaviour in the environment of the microorganism and any other forms

Information was mainly derived from published literature on different strains of P. oligandrum in relation to its persistence and multiplication in soil. The studies on different strains of P. oligandrum in soil were considered insufficient to conclude on the likely competitiveness, persistence and multiplication of P. oligandrum strain B301 in soil. Consequently, EFSA concluded that the information is insufficient to address the uniform principles criterion of the strain not being expected to persist and multiply in soil in concentrations considerably higher than the natural background levels, taking into account repeated applications over the years. This resulted in a data gap and assessment that could not be finalised for the persistence and multiplication of P. oligandrum strain B301 in soil after repeated applications over the years (see Section 9.1). Predicted environmental concentrations (PEC) in soil for the representative field use have been calculated (see Appendix A).

With respect to the persistence and multiplication in surface water, published studies were available providing information on the presence of P. oligandrum in surface water. No reliable data were available on the persistence and multiplication of P. oligandrum strain B301 in surface water. Although inadequate information was provided on the survival, competitiveness and population dynamics of P. oligandrum strain B301 in sediment and surface water, exposure to the sediment and surface water compartments is considered marginal from the representative use as drip irrigation. Conservative estimates for PEC in surface water for the representative field use have been calculated (see Appendix A).

Although inadequate information was provided on the survival, competitiveness and population dynamics of P. oligandrum strain B301 in air, exposure to the air compartment is considered marginal from the representative use as drip irrigation.

Although inadequate information was provided on the mobility of P. oligandrum strain B301 in environmental compartments (soil, surface water, sediment and air), exposure to surface water, sediment and air is considered marginal from the representative use as drip irrigation. For the mobility in soil dispersal to adjacent soil habitats cannot be excluded from the representative use.

Fate and behaviour in the environment of secondary metabolite(s) of (potential) concern produced by the microorganism under relevant conditions

According to scientific papers from the literature search, the species P. oligandrum is able to produce secondary metabolites and may constitute part of the mode of action of P. oligandrum strain B301 (see Section 2). It is not known to what extent P. oligandrum strain B301 will produce any metabolites following application from the representative use. Adequate information to address the potential concentrations of secondary metabolites/toxins to be produced by P. oligandrum strain B301 in all environmental compartments (soil, surface water, sediment and groundwater) was not available. Therefore, a data gap was identified (see Section 9.1). Consequently, it is not clear if such metabolites might fulfil the criteria according to Part B section 7(iv) of Commission Regulation (EU) 283/2013 15 namely: the relevant metabolite is stable outside the microorganism;a toxic effect of the relevant metabolite is independent of the presence of the microorganism;the relevant metabolite is expected to occur in the environment in concentrations considerably higher than under natural conditions.

POTENTIAL EFFECTS ON NON‐TARGET ORGANISMS

Birds and mammals

Sufficient information is available to finalise the assessment of toxicity, infectivity and pathogenicity of P. oligandrum strain B301 to birds. As concluded in Section 3, sufficient information is available to finalise the assessment of toxicity, infectivity and pathogenicity of P. oligandrum strain B301 in wild mammals.

As discussed in Section 5.2, the identification, hazard characterisation and exposure assessment of secondary metabolites/toxins produced by P. oligandrum strain B301 in the environment from the representative use could not be finalised. Consequently, the risk to birds and wild mammals from such metabolites cannot be assessed and a data gap has been identified resulting in an issue not finalised (see Sections 2 and 9.1).

Aquatic organisms

Sufficient information is available to finalise the assessment of toxicity, infectivity and pathogenicity of P. oligandrum strain B301 to fish, aquatic invertebrates and algae. Insufficient information was available for aquatic plants. However, low risk has been concluded from potential adverse effects to aquatic plants as exposure from P. oligandrum strain B301 to surface water is considered to be marginal from the representative use by drip irrigation.

As discussed in Section 5.2, the identification and exposure assessment of secondary metabolites/toxins produced by P. oligandrum strain B301 in the environment from the representative use could not be finalised. Consequently, the risk to aquatic organisms from such metabolites cannot be assessed and a data gap has been identified resulting in an issue not finalised (see Section 9.1).

Bees

Sufficient information is available to finalise the assessment of toxicity, infectivity and pathogenicity of P. oligandrum strain B301 to honeybees.

As discussed in Section 5.2, the identification and exposure assessment of secondary metabolites/toxins produced by P. oligandrum strain B301 in the environment from the representative use could not be finalised. As part of the mode of action, P. oligandrum strain B301 can be qualified as a ‘systemic resistance inducer/elicitor’ in the treated crop for the representative use (see Section 2). Consequently, the risk to honeybees from such metabolites cannot be assessed and a data gap has been identified resulting in an issue not finalised (see Section 9.1).

Non‐target arthropods other than bees

Two studies on two species on non‐target foliar dwelling arthropods were provided. In a 14‐day laboratory study on Typhlodromus pyri no toxicity, infectivity and pathogenicity was observed. In a 48‐h laboratory study on Aphidius rhopalosiphi no toxicity was observed. In both studies the rates used in the study were about a factor of 100 above the proposed application rate for the representative use. No studies were available for non‐target soil dwelling arthropods exposed to P. oligandrum strain B301. Consequently, the toxicity, infectivity and pathogenicity from P. oligandrum strain B301 to non‐target soil arthropods cannot be assessed and a data gap has been identified resulting in an issue not finalised for the representative use as drip irrigation (see Section 9.1).

As discussed in Section 5.2, the identification and exposure assessment of secondary metabolites/toxins produced by P. oligandrum strain B301 in the environment from the representative use could not be finalised. As part of the mode of action, P. oligandrum strain B301 can be qualified as a ‘systemic resistance inducer/elicitor’ in the treated crop for the representative use (see Section 2). Consequently, the risk to non‐target arthropods other than bees (foliar and soil dwelling) from such metabolites cannot be assessed and a data gap has been identified resulting in an issue not finalised (see Section 9.1).

Soil organisms

Sufficient information is available to finalise the assessment of toxicity, infectivity and pathogenicity of P. oligandrum strain B301 to earthworms. Insufficient information was available for potential adverse effects to soil microorganisms from P. oligandrum strain B301. Consequently, the potential adverse effects from P. oligandrum strain B301 to soil microorganisms cannot be assessed and a data gap has been identified, resulting in an issue not finalised for the representative use as drip irrigation (see Section 9.1).

As discussed in Section 5.2, the identification and exposure assessment of secondary metabolites/toxins produced by P. oligandrum strain B301 in the environment from the representative use could not be finalised. Consequently, the risk to earthworms and other soil macro‐ and microorganisms from such metabolites cannot be assessed and a data gap has been identified resulting in an issue not finalised (see Section 9.1).

Non‐target plants

Limited information was available on potential adverse effects from P. oligandrum strain B301 to non‐target terrestrial plants. Nevertheless, low risk has been concluded from potential adverse effects as exposure from P. oligandrum strain B301 to non‐target terrestrial plants is considered to be marginal from the representative use by drip irrigation.

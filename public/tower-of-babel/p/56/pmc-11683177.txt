A Randomized, Parallel, Open‐Label, Single‐Dose and Multiple‐Dose Clinical Trial to Investigate the Pharmacokinetic, Pharmacodynamic, and Safety Profiles of Obicetrapib in Healthy Participants in China
Authors: Jing Zhang, Guoying Cao, Yong Huo, Liana L. Guarneiri, Marc Ditmarsch, John J. P. Kastelein, Douglas Kling, Andrew Hsieh, Erin Wuerdeman, Michael H. Davidson
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObicetrapib is a selective cholesteryl ester transfer protein (CETP) inhibitor. Previous research has demonstrated similar pharmacokinetic (PK) responses to single doses of obicetrapib between Japanese and White males, but the PK responses have not been established in Chinese individuals. The purpose of this randomized, parallel, open‐label trial was to characterize the PK and pharmacodynamic (PD; CETP activity and plasma lipids) responses and safety of single doses (5, 10, or 25 mg; N = 36) and multiple doses (10 mg for 14 days; N = 12) of obicetrapib in healthy Chinese individuals. The maximum concentration and area under the drug concentration‐time curve of obicetrapib from 0 h to infinity increased with dose after all single doses of obicetrapib. After 7 consecutive days of dosing, low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol and high‐density lipoprotein cholesterol reached their minimum and maximum changes of 42% reduction and 108% increase, respectively. Primary PK and PD parameters after single‐ and multiple‐dose administration of obicetrapib were similar to those in healthy white participants in previous studies. One participant in the 5 mg dose group experienced a treatment‐emergent adverse event of decreased white blood cell and neutrophil counts, which resolved without intervention. In conclusion, these findings support the inclusion of Chinese individuals in the ongoing phase 3 clinical development program of obicetrapib.

Introduction

In the past, atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) was viewed as a condition restricted to the Western world, yet today it is a global threat. 1 This shift occurred as the prevalence of communicable disease in developing countries decreased due to improved sanitation, vaccination, and treatments for acute infections, subsequently resulting in longer lifespans and, thus, increased ASCVD. China is a quintessential example of a country where this shift has occurred. A recent cross‐sectional study involving over 2.3 million adults in China was conducted to assess the prevalence and treatment of dyslipidemia, a major risk factor for ASCVD. 2 The study demonstrated that 33.8% of Chinese adults have dyslipidemia, and in Chinese adults with established ASCVD, only 14.1% were treated with lipid‐lowering therapies. Additionally, older age (65‐75 years), female sex, lower income, smoking, alcohol consumption, and absence of diabetes were associated with lower control of low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL‐C) in this group. These findings highlight the crucial need to improve dyslipidemia management to prevent cardiovascular events, especially in the populations at highest risk for poor blood lipid management.

Traditional approaches for mitigating dyslipidemia include lifestyle changes such as a heart healthy diet, weight loss in those with overweight or obesity, and increased physical activity, as well as pharmacological therapies for those with sufficient ASCVD risk. 3 The focus of these approaches is typically to lower LDL‐C, and statins are the first‐line therapeutic choice. However, there is low use of statins (even among individuals with high ASCVD risk) and high discontinuation of statins in China. 2 , 4 For example, an analysis of almost 75,000 Chinese adults indicated that statin use increased by just 4%‐5% from 2017 to 2020, and achievement of LDL‐C treatment goals decreased by 4% in the same timeframe. 4 Age ≤75 years, lack of medical insurance, inability for self‐care, and the absence of hypertension, stroke, and coronary artery disease were associated with lower likelihood of statin use. The high prevalence of statin underutilization in individuals with high LDL‐C, but no history of cardiovascular disease, is concerning because there is strong evidence that the benefit of lower LDL‐C levels is enhanced when sustained over longer periods of time. 5 Furthermore, it is important to note that statin monotherapy may not be sufficient for some high‐risk patients. 6 Non‐statin lipid‐lowering therapies are used in combination with statins, but the currently available medications are limited by higher cost, less desirable route of administration (e.g., injection), and potential side effects. 7 , 8 Altogether, there is a need to develop additional lipid‐lowering therapies to overcome the present barriers to achieving recommended LDL‐C levels, especially in Asian populations.

Obicetrapib (TA‐9885) is an oral, selective cholesteryl ester transfer protein (CETP) inhibitor in phase 3 development for the treatment of dyslipidemia. 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 In vitro, ex vivo, and in vivo primary pharmacology studies demonstrated that obicetrapib inhibits CETP, increases high‐density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL‐C), and decreases LDL‐C (NewAmsterdam Pharma, unpublished data). Pharmacokinetic (PK) studies in rats, mice, and cynomolgus monkeys indicated good bioavailability of obicetrapib and the elimination half‐life was 3.2 to 4.6 h in rats and 128 h in monkeys (NewAmsterdam Pharma, unpublished data). Following single oral doses of 5 to 150 mg obicetrapib in humans, the terminal half‐life ranged from 81 to 166 h. 12 In human volunteers, radiolabeled 14C‐obicetrapib was not extensively metabolized and was excreted as the unchanged parent drug in feces; no major metabolites were detected in plasma, urine, or feces (NewAmsterdam Pharma, unpublished data). Additionally, in a 9‐month toxicology study in cynomolgus monkeys that administered doses up to 50 mg/kg/day, obicetrapib was not detected in fat tissue samples, and a recovery period of 13 weeks was sufficient for obicetrapib to be completely eliminated from systemic circulation. 13

Several phase 2 clinical trials established that 5 and 10 mg doses of obicetrapib are well tolerated and lower LDL‐C by >40% as monotherapy and >60% when combined with ezetimibe on top of high‐intensity statins after 8‐12 weeks in populations that were primarily white with Northern European ancestries. 9 , 10 , 11 In a previous phase 1 trial, there were no differences in PK responses between Japanese or White male participants following single oral doses of 25, 50, 100, and 150 mg obicetrapib. 12 Furthermore, a randomized, double‐blind, phase 2 trial demonstrated that obicetrapib 2.5, 5, and 10 mg/d for 8‐weeks lowered LDL‐C by medians of 25%, 32%, and 46%, respectively, and was well tolerated and safe in 102 Japanese participants. 14

Although the findings of these trials are promising, more research needs to be conducted in individuals from the Asia‐Pacific region. According to the International Conference on Harmonization of Technical Requirements for Registration of Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH) E5 document, studies in new regions may be needed if the critical properties of the drug are sensitive to ethnic factors. 15 Furthermore, there is some evidence that CETP inhibition may have disparate effects on risk for coronary heart disease in European versus East Asian populations. 16 , 17 Mendelian randomization analyses in populations with European ancestries indicate that CETP inhibition decreases risk of coronary heart disease while similar analyses in East Asian populations are mixed. 16 A prospective biobank study demonstrated that CETP variants were strongly associated with higher HDL‐C levels but not lower LDL‐C levels. 18 However, a recent drug target Mendelian randomization study demonstrated that lower CETP was associated with lower LDL‐C in both European and East Asian populations, but the effects were stronger in Europeans. 17 Therefore, the present study was designed to further characterize the PK, pharmacodynamic (PD), and safety profiles of obicetrapib in healthy Chinese individuals.

Methods

Study Design

The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of Huashan Hospital affiliated to Fudan University. Written informed consent was obtained from participants prior to any trial‐related procedure or examination. This was a randomized, parallel assignment, open‐label, single‐center (Huashan Hospital affiliated to Fudan University, Shanghai, China), single‐dose and multiple‐dose interventional study in healthy Chinese adults (the study was not registered). Participants were evaluated between August 21, 2022 and November 24, 2022. This study was conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice, relevant requirements for “clinical trials of drugs” in Provisions for Drug Registration, and other applicable laws and regulations. Eligible participants were randomized into one of three obicetrapib dose groups: 5, 10, and 25. Each dose group received a single oral administration of obicetrapib, and PK, PD, and safety outcomes were evaluated. After a 21‐day follow‐up period after the single dose, the 10 mg cohort took 10 mg obicetrapib once daily for 14 days, and PK, PD, and safety were further evaluated.

Participants

Healthy Chinese male or female participants aged ≥18 and ≤45 years with body mass index (BMI) between ≥19.0 and <27.0 kg/m2 and body weight ≥50.0 kg for males and ≥45.0 kg for females, were included in the trial. This population was selected to ensure that the PK characteristics of the population were homogeneous and to facilitate comparisons with other studies involving single doses of obicetrapib that were conducted in other countries. 12 All participants were required to be able to communicate well with the investigator and to understand and comply with the study procedures. Exclusion criteria included the presence or history of impaired endocrine, thyroid, hepatic, respiratory or renal function, diabetes mellitus, coronary heart disease, or any psychotic illness; presence or history of gastrointestinal, liver or kidney disease, or other pathophysiological state which, in the opinion of the investigator, may interfere with drug absorption, distribution, metabolism, or excretion; family history of long QT syndrome, hypokalemia, or Torsades de Pointes; having severe allergic reactions to any drugs or allergies to the study drug and its excipients; or the presence of any other condition unsuitable for participation in the trial, in the opinion of the investigator. The following measurements at screening or baseline were exclusionary: 12‐lead electrocardiogram (ECG) QTcF ≥450 ms, blood pressure (supine) outside of the range of 90‐139 mm Hg (systolic) and 50‐89 mm Hg (diastolic), pulse rate (supine) outside the range of 50‐100 beats/min, body temperature >37.5°C, or positive results on urine drug, smoking, or breath alcohol tests.

Other exclusion criteria included history of drug or alcohol abuse; smoking within 3 months prior to screening; use of prescription medicine 4 weeks before dosing or over‐the‐counter medicine within 1 week before dosing; consumption of food or drink containing “grapefruit,” “cranberry,” “dietary supplements,” or “herbal remedies” within 7 days prior to dosing; consumption of caffeine, cola, or tea within 3 days before dosing; donation of blood within 3 months prior to screening; or plans to donate blood or blood components within 1 month after the last study visit. In addition, participants were excluded if they had an upper respiratory tract infection or other acute infection within 2 weeks prior to screening, or a positive hepatitis B surface antigen, hepatitis C virus antibody, human immunodeficiency virus antibody, or syphilis test. Additionally, participants were excluded if they had taken obicetrapib previously, or if they had received treatment with other investigational products or devices within 3 months of screening or five half‐lives of the investigational products (the longer duration prevailed). Finally, participants were excluded if they were pregnant, lactating, unable to take effective contraceptive measures, or planning to become pregnant within 3 months after the end of the trial.

Single‐Dose Stage

The single‐dose stage included screening (day −14 to day −2), baseline (day −1), clinical (day 1 to day 4), and follow‐up periods (clinic visits on days 8, 15, and 22). Participants were housed in the hospital from baseline (day −1) to day 4 (72 h after administration). Each participant was assigned to one of three treatment groups (5, 10, or 25 mg obicetrapib) in a ratio of 1:1:1 using block randomization with no stratification. The randomization code table was generated by Pharmaron (Nanjing) Clinical Services, Ltd. using SAS (version 9.4). A single dose of 5, 10, or 25 mg obicetrapib at room temperature was administered to participants with 240 mL of warm water on the morning of day 1 following an overnight fast and under the supervision of study personnel. The 5 mg cohort received one 5 mg tablet, the 10 mg cohort received one 10 mg tablet, and the 25 mg cohort received two 10 mg and one 5 mg obicetrapib tablets. Participants remained fasted for at least 4 h after dosing, and then a non‐standardized lunch was served. Water was not permitted 1 h before and after dosing. Food and beverages containing grapefruit, cranberries, dietary supplements, and herbs were not permitted from 7 days prior to dosing until completion of the study. Similarly, food and beverages containing caffeine and poppy seeds were not permitted from 72 h prior to dosing until completion of the study.

Obicetrapib tablets are round, 6 mm, coated in a commercially available white film (Opadry II white), and devoid of identifying markings. Inside the tablet's core, the excipients include microcrystalline cellulose, mannitol, sodium starch glycollate, colloidal silicon dioxide, and magnesium stearate. Blood samples for assessment of PK were collected (more details of timing are included below) and safety was assessed by adverse events, vital signs (pulse rate, blood pressure, and ear temperature), physical examinations, laboratory tests (hematology, blood biochemistry, coagulation, urinalysis, and aldosterone), and 12‐lead ECG.

On day 1, blood samples for the PK analysis were collected within 2 h before obicetrapib administration and at 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, and 16 h after administration. Additional PK samples were collected on days 2, 3, 4, 8, 15, and 22 at the same time as the pre‐dose blood draw occurred on day 1. The PK endpoints for the single‐dose stage included the observed maximum concentration (Cmax) of obicetrapib after dosing; time to peak concentration (tmax) of obicetrapib; area under the drug concentration–time curve of obicetrapib from 0 to 24 h (AUC0‐24) and 0 h to infinity (AUC0‐∞); AUC of obicetrapib from 0 h to the last time point with blood concentration above the lower limit of quantification (AUC0‐t); terminal elimination half‐life (t1/2); apparent plasma clearance (CL/F); volume of distribution during the elimination phase (Vz/F); dose proportionality of Cmax; and dose proportionality of AUC0‐∞.

Multiple‐Dose Stage

Following a 21‐day washout period, participants that were randomized to the 10 mg obicetrapib group entered the 14‐day multiple‐dose stage after completing the PK and PD blood sampling on day 22. The washout period was 21 days because a previous study demonstrated that the terminal half‐life of obicetrapib ranged from 81 to 166 h, independent of dose level, after single oral doses of 5 to 150 mg. 12 The participants stayed at the hospital from day 21 to day 38 (72 h after the last dose). A single tablet of 10 mg obicetrapib was administered each day in the morning from day 22 to day 35 under the supervision of study personnel, followed by PK and PD blood sampling, and safety examinations. Participants underwent the same fasting procedures as during the single‐dose stage.

Blood sampling for analysis of PK endpoints occurred on days 22‐29, 31, and 33 within 2 h of the pre‐dose timing; on day 35 pre‐dose and 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16 h post‐dose; on days 36, 37, 38, 42, d49, and 56 (24 to 504 h after the last administration). The PK endpoints for the multiple‐dose stage were AUC of obicetrapib during the dosing interval at steady state (AUC0‐τ,ss); AUC of obicetrapib from time 0 h to the last time point with blood concentration at the lower limit of quantification at steady state (AUC0‐t,ss); minimum drug concentration at steady state of obicetrapib (Cmin,ss); maximum drug concentration at steady state of obicetrapib (Cmax,ss); time to peak at steady state (tmax,ss); terminal elimination half‐life at steady state (t1/2,ss); apparent plasma clearance at steady state (CLss/F); volume of distribution during the elimination phase at steady state (VZ,ss/F); and the accumulation ratio (RA) for AUC0‐t,ss, AUC0‐τ,ss, and Cmax,ss calculated from individual PK parameters of obicetrapib on day 1 and day 56 using the following formulas:[RA (AUC0‐t,ss) = AUC0‐t,ss/AUC0‐24]; [RA (AUC0‐τ,ss) = AUC0‐τ,ss/ AUC0‐24]; and [RA (Cmax,ss) = Cmax,ss/Cmax], respectively.

Blood sampling for analysis of CETP activity occurred on days 22, 23, 25, and 29 within 2 h of the pre‐dose timing; day 35 pre‐dose and 4, 8, and 12 h post‐dose; and days 36‐38, 42, 49, and 56 (24 to 504 h after the last administration). Additionally, blood sampling for analyses of LDL‐C, HDL‐C, TG, and TC occurred on days 22, 25, and 29 within 2 h of the pre‐dose timing and on days 36‐38, 42, 49, and 56 (24 to 504 h after the last administration). The PD endpoints for the multiple‐dose stage (10 mg obicetrapib group) included the minimum percent change (Pmin,ss) of CETP activity observed from baseline (pre‐dose on day 1) from 4 h after the last dose on day 35 to day 56; the Pmin,ss of LDL‐C, TC, and TG observed from baseline (pre‐dose on day 1) from 24 h after the last dose on day 35 to day 56; and the maximum percent change (Pmax,ss) of HDL‐C observed from baseline (pre‐dose on day 1) from 24 h after the last dose on day 35 to day 56. For PD endpoints, the percent change from the pre‐dose value on day 1 to the two later time points were calculated, and then the minimum or maximum percent change was determined. The biochemical analysis methods for all PK and PD endpoints were described previously. 19

Statistical Analysis

PK parameters were estimated using Phoenix WinNonlin version 8.3, and all other statistical analyses were conducted with SAS version 9.4. Values are mean ± standard deviation unless otherwise specified. No sample size calculation was conducted, but the study was designed to evaluate the PK profile of obicetrapib in the Chinese population. Continuous variables were summarized using descriptive statistics, and categorical variables were described by number and percentage of participants of each category. Mean drug concentration–time curves and semi‐logarithmic (log10) mean drug concentration–time curves were plotted by dose level. PK parameters were calculated based on a non‐compartmental model without adjustment for baseline concentrations. Descriptive statistics for PK and PD parameters were prepared as number, mean, median, standard deviation (SD), minimum, maximum, geometric mean, and coefficient of variation. To enhance comparability of the present study with previous studies that were conducted mostly in male participants, PK parameters were also stratified by sex.

Steady state was determined using a mixed effect model based on the log‐transformed dose‐normalized trough concentration, time (fixed effect), and subjects (random effect). The slope of time and the corresponding 90% confidence interval were estimated under the log scale. The first model contained all time points, and if the slope was significantly different from zero, a new model was built excluding the earliest time point. This procedure was repeated until the slope was no longer statistically different from zero or until only three time points remained in the model.

The Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) was used to code AEs. Treatment‐emergent AEs (TEAE) were summarized according to system organ class and preferred term. The number of events, number of participants, and percentages were summarized. In addition, TEAEs were graded by severity and relationship to the investigational product. Serious adverse events were also summarized.

Results

Disposition of Participants

To ensure that enrollment goals were met efficiently, more participants than needed were recruited and screened to account for potential screen failures. Of the 142 individuals that were screened, 105 individuals were screen failures. Of these, 89 individuals did not meet the inclusion criteria or met the exclusion criteria, and 16 individuals qualified based on inclusion/exclusion criteria but were categorized as screen failures because the enrollment goals had already been fulfilled. Thirty‐seven participants were randomized, but one participant withdrew from the 10 mg dose group prior to dosing on day 1 due to ECG abnormalities. A total of 36 participants completed the study and were included in all analyses.

Demographic and Other Baseline Characteristics

Demographic and baseline characteristics of participants for each dose group (n = 12 per group) and overall are presented in Table 1. Participants were evenly distributed male and female (50/50). The mean ± SD age of all participants was 30.7 ± 6.86 years, and the mean ± SD BMI was 23.0 ± 1.91 kg/m2. Two participants reported concomitant medication use during the trial related to AEs (glycerol for constipation, n = 1; muscle relaxants for back pain and topical medications for joint/muscle pain, n = 1). The investigational product was administered according to the protocol, and administration compliance was 100%.

Single‐Dose Pharmacokinetics

Prior to administration, obicetrapib was not detected in plasma. The blood concentration of obicetrapib peaked at approximately 4 h in each dose group, and then declined to below 22.4 ng/mL by day 22 (Figure 1). Plasma obicetrapib PK parameters during the single‐dose stage are summarized in Table 2. The Cmax increased with each single oral dose of 5, 10, and 25 mg obicetrapib (slope of 0.83 (90% confidence interval [CI] 0.65, 1.02] at the log‐transformed dose levels). The AUC0‐t and AUC0‐∞ both increased with dose with values of 14,700 ± 3940, 19,500 ± 4570, and 38,800 ± 13,000 h•ng/mL for AUC0‐∞ in the 5, 10, and 25 mg dose groups, respectively. The slope for AUC0‐∞ was 0.60 [90% CI 0.47, 0.73] at the log‐transformed dose levels. Subgroup analyses demonstrated that the mean Cmax, AUC0‐t, and AUC0‐∞ were similar between males and females within the same dose group, and the trend of exposure between different dose groups within the same sex was relatively consistent with the overall exposure trend (Table S1).

Multiple‐Dose Pharmacokinetics

The mean pre‐dose plasma level of obicetrapib was 8.63 ng/mL in the 10 mg dose group at the start of the multiple‐dose stage on day 22, thus residual obicetrapib remained in circulation from the single‐dose phase. After 7 consecutive days of obicetrapib 10 mg dosing, plasma levels approached steady‐state levels (day 29) (Figure 2). The steady‐state detection slope was 0.01 (90% CI: −0.00, 0.01). Plasma obicetrapib PK parameters during the multiple‐dose stage are summarized in Table 3. Additionally, in subgroup analyses Cmax,ss, AUC0‐t,ss, and AUC0‐τ,ss were similar in both sexes, and the mean ratios of accumulation between sexes were generally consistent (Table S2).

Pharmacodynamic Outcomes

PD parameters during the multiple‐dose stage of obicetrapib administration are summarized in Table 4. During the steady state of the multiple‐dose stage, the mean Pmin,ss of CETP activity and LDL‐C concentration were −46.8% ± 11.0% and −42.0% ± 13.4%, respectively, and the mean Pmax,ss of HDL‐C concentration was 108% (Figure 3). Safety

Adverse events that occurred in at least two participants are summarized in Table S3. Overall, there were 16 TEAEs after the study drug administration in the single‐dose phase (n = 3 in the 5 mg dose group; n = 3 in the 10 mg dose group, and n = 6 in the 25 mg dose group). Only one participant (2.8%) experienced TEAEs in the single‐dose phase that were judged by the investigator to be related to the study drug: one instance of decreased white blood cell count (common terminology criteria for adverse events [CTCAE] grade 1; day 15 to day 29) and one instance of decreased neutrophil count (CTCAE grade 2; day 8 to day 29); both resolved spontaneously without intervention. During the multiple‐dose phase, 6 of the 12 participants (50.0%) experienced 15 TEAEs, which were all unrelated to the study drug.

In the single‐ and multiple‐dose phases, there were no CTCAE grade ≥3 TEAEs, SAEs, deaths, or TEAEs leading to withdrawal occurred. There were no dose‐related trends in the category, frequency, or severity of TEAEs. No apparent changes in blood aldosterone levels related to the study drug were observed. Also, physical examination, vital signs, and 12‐lead ECGs revealed no changes associated with the study drug.

Discussion

In a previous study of obicetrapib administered as single ascending oral doses to healthy White and Japanese males, there were no differences in the Cmax or AUC0‐∞ between these populations. 12 Previous single‐dose studies involving CETP inhibitors have also observed non‐proportional PK responses in which observed responses were lower than expected. 12 , 20 , 21 Furthermore, the PK findings from the multiple‐dose stage of the present study are generally consistent with those from a previous study in White men. Table S4 summarizes the PK parameters during oral administration of 10 mg obicetrapib for 14 days in Chinese males and females (the present study) and for 21 days in White men (NewAmsterdam Pharma, unpublished data from a previous study). 12 Additionally, the PD findings from the present study of Chinese participants including inhibition of CETP activity, reduction in LDL‐C, and increased HDL‐C following single and multiple doses of obicetrapib 10 mg also mirror those in healthy White and Japanese male populations. 12 The changes in TC and TG were modest and not dose related. It is important to note that the inclusion and exclusion criteria were similar for the present study and the study conducted by Ford et al 12 The present study included healthy Chinese males and females with BMIs ≥19.0 and <27.0 kg/m2, and the study by Ford et al included healthy White males with BMIs between 18 and 33 kg/m2. 12

The PK profile following multiple doses of obicetrapib has also been studied in Japanese men and women who had not achieved 2022 Japanese Atherosclerosis Society Guidelines for Prevention of ASCVD and had LDL‐C >70 mg/dL or non‐HDL‐C >100 mg/dL and TG <400 mg/dL. 14 Participants received obicetrapib doses of 2.5, 5, or 10 mg or matching placebo for 8 weeks and were followed to week 12 for additional PK and safety assessments. The mean percent change from end‐of‐treatment (week 8) to 4‐weeks post end‐of‐treatment (week 12) in the plasma level of obicetrapib was −93% to −95% across dose groups, suggesting nearly complete elimination. Furthermore, obicetrapib 10 mg improved blood lipids in a dose‐dependent manner, with 45.8%, 30.3%, and 26.3% reductions in LDL‐C, non‐HDL‐C, and apolipoprotein B, respectively. All TEAEs were mild or moderate in severity and judged to be unrelated to the study drug. Therefore, data from the previous study in Japanese participants and the current study support the safety and efficacy of obicetrapib in the Asia‐Pacific region populations.

In the present study, obicetrapib was well tolerated and safe after single and multiple doses. Only one participant in the 5 mg dose group experienced TEAEs that were related to the study drug (decreased white blood cell count and neutrophil count), which spontaneously improved without intervention. There were no TEAEs related to white blood cell count or neutrophil count in the previous study that was conducted in White and Japanese males involving single doses of obicetrapib 5, 10, 25, 50, 100, and 150 mg. 12 No AEs led to withdrawal of the investigational product, and there were no dose‐related trends for AEs. These results are generally consistent with those of healthy White adults. 9 , 12

Studying the effects of an investigational drug in a variety of populations is an essential component of a global drug development program. Inter‐ethnic variability in PK parameters is influenced by factors ranging from genetics to the environment (e.g., diet, living situation, medical practices, and so on). 22 As previously mentioned, the ICH E5 document states that studies in new regions may be needed if the critical properties of the drug are sensitive to ethnic factors. 15 Furthermore, the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA), a regulatory agency of China, requires ethnic sensitivity analyses to be conducted when applying clinical trial data conducted in non‐Chinese populations to the Chinese population. 23 The NMPA also strongly encourages early clinical trials to be conducted in China to obtain safety, efficacy, and PK data for these sensitivity analyses.

Conclusions

The present study characterized the PK and/or PD responses to single and multiple doses of obicetrapib in healthy Chinese adults. The primary PK responses, inhibition of CETP activity, reduction of LDL‐C, and increase in HDL‐C were comparable to those previously observed in healthy white participants, suggesting no effect of race or ethnicity on these outcomes. Only one participant had a TEAE that was related to the study drug, which resolved spontaneously without intervention. Altogether, the results of this study support the inclusion of Chinese participants in the ongoing phase 3 clinical development program of obicetrapib including the Cardiovascular Outcomes Study to Evaluate the Effect of Obicetrapib in Patients with Cardiovascular Disease (PREVAIL; NCT05202509).

Sketch recognition model based on improved CycleGAN network and dual attention mechanism
Authors: Yun Wang, Li Xie, Mengyi Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To improve sketch recognition accuracy, this study proposes an enhanced sketch recognition model based on an improved CycleGAN network and a dual attention mechanism. The proposed model first incorporates multi-directional convolution and brightness equalization modules into the CycleGAN network to extract edge and contour features. A dual attention mechanism is then implemented using channel attention and spatial attention modules, effectively addressing issues of sparse strokes and uneven spatial distribution in sketches while enhancing the representation of critical features. Finally, a hybrid architecture combining global average pooling and convolution layers serves as the classifier to produce sketch recognition results. Simulation results demonstrate that this model achieves 97.08% accuracy, 98.12% precision, 98.23% recall, and 97.45% F1 score for sketch recognition on the TU-Berlin dataset, and 98.65% accuracy, 98.12% precision, 98.76% recall, and 97.95% F1 score on the QuickDraw dataset. Compared with state-of-the-art sketch recognition models, this model exhibits superior performance in accuracy. These results indicate that the model can enhance sketch recognition precision and provide technical support for converting sketches into high-quality animated images.

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of computer vision and artificial intelligence technologies, sketch recognition has emerged as a pivotal bridge connecting machine understanding with human creativity, becoming a key research focus. However, traditional sketch recognition methods relying on manual feature extraction often face challenges like low accuracy when dealing with diverse and complex styles. Moreover, the highly simplified nature of sketches makes it difficult for conventional approaches to effectively capture their deep semantic features, resulting in suboptimal recognition performance. In this context, improving sketch recognition accuracy has become an urgent challenge requiring immediate solutions.

To enhance sketch recognition accuracy, Majumder B et al. developed an intelligent sketch recognition method by integrating scale-invariant feature transformation with fuzzy similarity measurement, achieving significant precision improvement1. Meanwhile, Zhang X et al. proposed a multi-scale feature extraction and aggregation network-based approach that processes human facial sketches, effectively boosting recognition speed2.Yang J et al. leveraged the sparse distribution characteristics of hand-drawn sketches and the information scarcity in significant blank areas to propose an expansion deformable sparse convolution framework, which significantly improved sketch recognition accuracy3. Li T et al. developed a multi-layer perceptron-based sketch recognition model that learns point-to-point relationships through neural networks to extract local visual features, then inputs them into a hybrid expert module for accurate identification4. Baudoux G introduced a sketch recognition method by generating associative inspirational images, effectively enhancing human-machine collaborative communication efficiency5. Du K et al. conducted research on sketch-based face recognition, proposing a sketch recognition method using residual serialization cross-grouping. This approach enables rapid recognition of small-scale sketches with high accuracy6. Zhang S et al. combined shape and contour information to develop a collaborative learning-based sketch recognition method. By employing hierarchical residual structures to extract modal features and input them into a classifier for classification, they achieved rapid sketch recognition without relying on additional data or stroke information7. Jooyoung K et al. proposed a sketch recognition method based on multi-directional line feature projection, which was applied to face sketch recognition. This method effectively addresses the challenge of limited sketch sample quantities while maintaining high accuracy8. Yukai et al. (2025) broke through the traditional whole-image style transfer of images toward local controllable style transformation, and effectively solved the problem of abrupt boundaries through adaptive style fusion, achieving a natural transition of styles.

Research findings demonstrate that applying deep learning techniques to sketch recognition can enhance both accuracy and processing speed. However, traditional methods often misclassify similar objects into the same category due to subtle line variations in sketches, making high-precision recognition challenging. Therefore, developing more advanced sketch recognition approaches is crucial to overcoming accuracy limitations. Based on this, this paper innovatively proposes a sketch recognition model based on improved CycleGAN network and dual attention mechanism. The innovation of this model lies in employing a dual attention mechanism to guide the training of CycleGAN network, which not only enhances the robustness of sketch feature extraction but also achieves high-precision sketch recognition.

The paper is structured as follows: First, we introduce the fundamental algorithms of CycleGAN networks and attention mechanisms. Then, to address the limitations of CycleGAN, we propose an improved CycleGAN network. By integrating channel attention modules and spatial attention modules, we develop a sketch recognition model based on the enhanced CycleGAN network and dual attention mechanism. Finally, we validate the model’s effectiveness on the TU-Berlin dataset and QuickDraw dataset.

This paper innovatively combines CycleGAN network through generative adversarial training to leverage its advantage of converting sketches into standard feature maps. It utilizes CycleGAN network to extract the features of the sketches. Meanwhile, considering the limitations of CycleGAN network structure, improvements are made to the CycleGAN network architecture. Then, channel attention is used dynamically to adjust the weights of feature channels, highlighting distinguishing category features. Subsequently, spatial attention is utilized to focus on the local areas of the sketch, enhancing the differences in fine lines, and a dual attention mechanism is proposed. Finally, the classifier outputs the classification results, so as to achieve sketch recognition. This paper effectively addresses the problems of traditional methods in capturing line structures and cross-domain semantic alignment in sketches, and significantly improves the efficiency of feature utilization for sketches, including sketch textures and edges, thereby providing technical support for promoting sketches as a natural input for human-computer interaction. Applying this technology to actual industrial scenarios such as circular search and barrier-free interaction can effectively reduce the reliance on professional drawing skills, which has practical application value.

Basic approach

CycleGAN network

CycleGAN networks are an advanced approach that integrates non-shared weight generators and discriminators into GAN architectures for image feature extraction. Unlike traditional GANs, they require minimal paired and labeled data for training, enabling flexible applications in complex scenarios such as image transformation9,10. In this study, the sketches are highly abstract and have low information density. Current discriminative models such as ResNet and VGG are prone to misidentifying “stroke jitter” as a categorical feature under limited annotations. However, CycleGAN network, through cyclic reconstruction constraints, forces the model to ignore irrelevant details and focus on the topological structure of the sketches, thereby achieving feature alignment at the semantic level. Furthermore, CycleGAN network can prevent the learning of non-semantic noise in sketches through the gradient regularization mechanism, and “reverse infer” the semantic prototypes, achieving data-enhanced feature learning. Therefore, we employ CycleGAN as the feature extraction network for sketch processing.

Attention mechanism

The attention mechanism is a deep learning technique that mimics human visual information processing. By filtering out irrelevant data, it enables models to dynamically focus on critical input elements13,14. The core principle assigns a weight to each input element, reflecting its importance for the task. These weights are determined by calculating the similarity between query, key, and value vectors.

The attention mechanism is primarily categorized into three types: channel attention, spatial attention, and temporal attention, each focusing on different dimensions of information processing15,16. Specifically, channel attention focuses on the channel dimension of feature maps, adjusting weights by calculating correlations between channels. Spatial attention emphasizes the spatial positions within feature maps, assigning weights through inter-region correlations. Temporal attention is designed for processing sequential data, distributing weights by measuring correlations between time steps.

In this paper, the sketch feature map contains channel features and spatial features, but no temporal dimension features. CycleGAN network improvement

When using the CycleGAN network to extract sketch features, its inherent structural limitations result in insufficient multi-angle and multi-directional feature extraction17,18. To address this issue and further enhance the network’s performance in sketch feature extraction, this paper proposes structural improvements to the CycleGAN network.

CycleGAN networks typically perform convolution operations in a single direction to extract features from input images. However, this approach suffers from incomplete feature extraction since it only captures features in one direction19,20. The multi-directional convolutional network, as depicted in Fig. 1, comprises four distinct directional convolution layers. The first and second layers employ 1 × 1 and 2 × 2 convolution kernels respectively to extract features from the input image, with the LeakyReLU function serving as the activation function .The second and fourth convolution layers incorporate an additional Zero Pad2d layer to enhance feature extraction. By concatenating all feature information, the model extracts multi-directional deep features from input images, overcoming the limitation of traditional CycleGAN networks that can only perform convolution in a single direction.

By introducing the multi-directional convolution module, the generator of CycleGAN network can still stably output complete-structured and semantically clear sketches even under extremely low information density input, thereby effectively avoiding the problem of distorted sketch generation. Therefore, for the CycleGAN network used for sketch recognition, this paper introduces the multi-directional convolution module.

Sketches often exhibit blurred details due to low brightness or overexposure in some areas. In addition, sketches, as abstract expressions with no texture and low dependence on lighting, often experience semantic misalignment in their generation process due to mismatches with the lighting distribution of real photos (such as shadow intensity and light-dark contrast). To address this issue, this paper innovatively introduces a brightness equalization module into the CycleGAN network21,22. By forcibly reconstructing the semantic structure of the sketches, the category prototypes of illumination-invariant features are internalized, achieving a deep unification of sketch recognition and generation.

The balance module has six layers of convolutional layers. The first layer uses 9 × 9 convolution kernel with LeakyReLU activation function to extract global features. The second to sixth layers use 3 × 3 convolution kernel to extract low-light information.The network employs LeakyReLU as the activation function for layers 2–5, with sigmoid applied to layer 6. By multiplying the output with the CycleGAN network’s result, this approach effectively balances brightness across regions, resolving image brightness inconsistencies.

Data sources and preprocessing

This experiment utilizes two widely adopted datasets for hand-drawn sketch recognition: TU-Berlin and QuickDraw25,26. The TU-Berlin dataset, developed by researchers at the Technical University of Berlin, contains 25,000256 × 256 pixel sketches of common objects across 250 categories, with approximately 80 sketches per category. It is extensively used in sketch recognition and sketch-image matching research27,28. The QuickDraw dataset, an open-source resource provided by Google, comprises 50 million sketches across 345 categories, with each category containing around 145,000 images29,30. The data analyzed in this study can be obtained from TU-Berlin database and QuickDraw: https://media.xiph.org/video/derf/, https://storage.g.oogleapis.com/quickdraw_dataset/full/binary/cell%20phone.bin.

Considering the large number of images in the experimental data, 15,000 images were randomly selected from both TU-Berlin and QuickDraw datasets as experimental images, and the image size was adjusted to 224 × 224 pixels.Furthermore, to mitigate noise interference in the images, bilateral filters were applied to all experimental images for denoising31,32. Finally, the experimental images were divided into training and test sets in an 8:2 ratio.

Parameter setting

Referring to other related improved CycleGAN network parameters, the experimental setup for the improved CycleGAN network was configured with 200 iterations, an initial momentum of 0.6, and a zero learning rate, using the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) optimizer for training. During training, the initial learning rate and batch size of the improved CycleGAN network significantly affect its performance. To determine appropriate initial learning rate and batch size, this study employs experimental methods. Conclusion

In summary, by introducing a multi-directional convolution module and a brightness equalization module into the CycleGAN network, and constructing a sketch recognition model using the improved CycleGAN network and dual attention mechanism, sketch recognition of different categories is achieved. On the TU Berlin dataset and QuickDraw dataset, this model achieves an accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score of over 97% for sketch recognition. Compared with the current advanced sketch recognition models, this model has superior sketch recognition accuracy, providing technical support for the conversion of sketches to high-quality images. This model provides technical support for improving the efficiency of sketch conversion and achieving natural human-computer interaction. However, due to limitations in conditions, this model still has some issues in terms of recognition accuracy. For instance, there are errors in recognizing certain sketches of similar categories. To address this, we will explore integrating language models to analyze sketch contexts, thereby correcting recognition biases and further improving sketch recognition accuracy.

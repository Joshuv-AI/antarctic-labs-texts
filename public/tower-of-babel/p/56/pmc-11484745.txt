Characteristics of physical activity and sedentary behavior in patients undergoing outpatient cardiac rehabilitation
Authors: Koichi Naito, Kazuhiro P Izawa, Noriaki Maeda, Yuya Kasai, Hajime Iwama
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractCardiac rehabilitation is crucial for the secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease, the leading cause of death worldwide. In Japan, as hospitalization periods have been shortened due to advances in medical technology and government policies, outpatient rehabilitation focused on lifestyle and physical activity (PA) is becoming increasingly important as hospital stays shorten. This study was aimed to identify patient characteristics that define the different types of PA and sedentary behaviors, and patient factors in 92 Japanese outpatients by using accelerometers. The patients were found to spend considerable time being sedentary (663.7 ± 118.1 min/day) and very little time engaging in moderate-to-vigorous PA (MVPA, 25.6 (10.6–43.4) min/day). Regression analysis revealed that the daily duration of sedentary behavior was 64 min longer in men than in women (β = 64.2, 95% CI[12.9, 115], p = 0.015); furthermore, men engaged in 22% less light PA (β = -0.245, 95% CI[-0.388, -0.102], p = 0.001). Additionally, MVPA increased by 43% for every 1 metabolic equivalent (MET) task increase in the anaerobic threshold (AT) (β = 0.367, 95% CI[0.035, 0.698], p = 0.031) but decreased by 3% per year of age(β = -0.035, 95% CI[-0.056, -0.015], p < 0.001). The study also found that body mass index (BMI) was negatively correlated with walking time (β = -0.039, 95% CI[-0.075, -0.003], p = 0.036), while AT was positively correlated with MVPA (β = 0.367, 95% CI[0.035, 0.698], p = 0.031) but negatively correlated with time above the AT (β = -2.06, 95% CI[-2.53, -1.59], p < 0.001). These results emphasize the importance of customizing rehabilitation programs according to sex, age, BMI, and AT differences. Furthermore, monitoring home activities and providing individualized prescriptions may enhance the effectiveness of rehabilitation programs.Subject terms: Cardiology, Health care, Health occupations

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death worldwide, with an estimated 17.9 million people dying from the disease. It accounts for approximately 32% of all deaths worldwide, making its management and treatment a major public health challenge1. Cardiac rehabilitation is recognized as an effective intervention to improve quality of life and prevent recurrence in the prevention and management of cardiovascular disease2–7. Increasing physical activity (PA) and promoting an appropriate lifestyle should be a core part of hospital-based cardiac rehabilitation programs8. Owing to shortened hospital stays in Japan in recent years, resulting from advancements in medical technology and national policies aimed at reducing hospitalization periods, guidelines have indicated that in-hospital cardiac rehabilitation alone is insufficient and outpatient cardiac rehabilitation is more important9. Because patients undergoing outpatient cardiac rehabilitation spend most of their time outside the hospital, their PA and sedentary behavior (SB) in the home environment are critical factors in the overall evaluation and success of cardiac rehabilitation10.

Among PA patterns in the home, prolonged sitting has been reported to negatively affect the health of patients with heart disease11–13. Even in patients with a pre-existing diagnosis of heart disease, prolonged sitting may be linked to negative health outcomes. The underlying mechanism could involve the deterioration of vascular endothelial function14, which plays a critical role in cardiovascular health. This impairment is believed to stem from a reduction in shear stress, a key factor in the production of the vasodilator nitric oxide (NO)15. Increased muscle sympathetic activity16 and blood viscosity17 induced by prolonged sitting may impair vascular endothelial function through changes in shear stress18. Therefore, patients with heart disease should aim to reduce their sitting time and increase their physical activity to improve their prognosis. However, in practice, patients rarely receive specific instructions on how to achieve this goal. Instead, they are often given vague advice such as ‘try to exercise as much as possible without overexerting yourself,’ which provides little practical guidance.

Kim et al. reported that moderate-to-vigorous PA (MVPA) has a protective effect in patients with heart failure, in that it mitigates the adverse effects associated with prolonged sitting19. Therefore, increasing MVPA is important; however, a one-size-fits-all approach is risky, given the predominantly older patient population in Japan. For example, in older patients with low exercise tolerance, MVPA may be excessive and exacerbate the disease. To facilitate this approach, it is important to understand patient distribution characteristics (such as age, sex, disease, exercise tolerance, and work status) for each physical activity (by intensity and activity type) and SB time, and develop an approach that is easy to implement for individual patients. However, no study has specifically examined the relationship between objectively measured PA and SB time and age, sex, exercise tolerance, and other factors. Thus, the purpose of our study was to gain a detailed understanding of the characteristics of PA and SB at home, as measured using triaxial accelerometers, and their application in the management of outpatients undergoing cardiac rehabilitation.

Methods

Study design

This cross-sectional study included 147 patients who consecutively participated in outpatient cardiac rehabilitation between March 2021 and October 2022 at a single non-bedroom cardiology clinic. The clinic is located in a small town in the Kansai region of Japan, offering services that include an examination room, a nutritional counseling room, and a cardiac rehabilitation room. The patients who visit the clinic are primarily residents from the surrounding area. The town is positioned in the northwestern part of Nara Prefecture, near the border with Osaka Prefecture. Historically, it was an important railroad junction and continues to serve as a key transportation hub. The town maintains a unique balance between traditional and modern aspects, featuring ancient temples and shrines alongside contemporary residential areas. The sample size of 147 includes all patients who participated in our outpatient cardiac rehabilitation program between March 2021 and October 2022. This was the total number of patients who entered the program during this timeframe; from these, patients who could attend the clinic independently and whose anaerobic threshold (AT) could be calculated using cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPX) were included (n = 130). Patients with probable dementia (based on diagnosis)(n = 3), irregular outpatient appointments (visits less than once a month)(n = 16), disagreements with informed consent requirements (n = 8), or data deficits were excluded (n = 11). In this study, outpatient cardiac rehabilitation refers to supervised sessions conducted in a clinical setting after the patient’s condition has stabilized. Patients typically attended these sessions one to eight times per month on an outpatient basis.

Patient characteristics

Patient characteristics such as age; sex; body mass index (BMI); number of years of education; employment; main diagnosis; left ventricular ejection fraction; comorbid diseases; medications; and levels of brain natriuretic peptide and albumin in plasma, hemoglobin in whole blood, and creatinine in serum were collected from their medical records. The primary diagnostic variable was derived from the clinical diagnosis provided by the attending physician. Each patient’s main diagnosis was classified into one of three groups: Ischaemic heart disease, Heart failure, or Other cardiovascular diseases. In our statistical analyses, this diagnostic variable was treated as a nominal variable with three distinct categories. The “Other” category was included as an independent category in the analysis and was not combined with Ischaemic heart disease or any other group. The medical records for this study were sourced from our hospital’s electronic health record (EHR) system, which is regularly updated and carefully maintained by our medical staff. However, we noted missing data for 11 out of 147 patients (approximately 7.5%). These gaps were mainly due to patients not completing certain examinations or delays in the transfer of information from external medical institutions.

Metabolic equivalents at AT

Metabolic equivalents (METS) at AT were determined by performing CPX with a breath gas analyzer (Cpex-1; Inter Reha, Tokyo, Japan) and a bicycle ergometer (StrenrthErgo8v2, Mitsubishi Electric, Tokyo, Japan) by using the V-slope method and calculated based on oxygen consumption measured at this threshold. The exercise protocol began with a four-minute rest period, followed by a four-minute warm-up at 10 W. After the warm-up, a ramp load was applied, increasing at a rate of 10 W/min. The test continued until the patient reached symptom-limited exhaustion or until the attending physician decided to stop the test. The term “MET” refers to the amount of oxygen consumed by an individual while at rest, equivalent to 3.5 milliliters of oxygen per kilogram of body weight per minute. This value represents the baseline metabolic rate of an average adult at rest and is commonly used as a reference to express the energy expenditure of physical activities relative to the resting metabolic rate.

Average daily duration of each activity

A triaxial accelerometer (Active Style Pro HJA750-C, Omron Healthcare, Kyoto, Japan) was used to assess participant behavior during arousal periods. The device measures 40.0 × 52.0 × 12.0 mm (width × height × depth), has a sampling frequency of 32 Hz, and features a 3-axis accelerometer with a range of ± 6G and a 12-bit resolution (0.003G). These accelerometers were dispensed and retrieved during outpatient visits to the cardiac rehabilitation center. The assessment duration spanned approximately 2 weeks, during which participants were instructed to wear the accelerometer on their waist continuously, except while bathing or sleeping.

The reliability and measurement accuracy of this accelerometer, which incorporates algorithms for recognizing walking and various daily life activities, have been corroborated in previous studies20,21, indicating its equivalence to other widely used devices22,23. Non-wear time was defined as periods exceeding 60 min with activity levels below the device detection threshold24. For data to be considered valid, the device must be worn for a minimum of 10 h per day24. The time classified as non-wear was deducted from the daily 24-h period. Only participants for whom valid data for at least 4 days were available were included in the final analysis24. The data were categorized based on the intensity of PA: SB (≤ 1.5 METs), light-intensity activity (LPA, 1.6–2.9 METs), and MVPA (≥ 3.0 METs)25,26.

The data were categorized into two main types of activities, walking and living, by using developer software. Furthermore, the data were categorized into activities above and below the AT based on each participant’s AT. These categorizations were applied at 60-s intervals. Daily activity hours were compiled, and the average was calculated for each participant across all valid days for subsequent analysis.

Sleep duration was assessed through a self-reported survey in which participants were asked, “On a typical day, how many hours do you sleep at night?” The reported sleep time was then calculated as a percentage of the 24-hour day. The remaining time was distributed among SB, LPA, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA), walking, living activities, and activities above and below the AT. These percentages were determined based on accelerometer data collected during wear time. The adjusted data were subsequently used in our analysis27,28. In this study, particular attention was given to activities above and below the AT, as these serve as indicators of physical overload in patients with heart disease.

Statistical analysis

Simple and multiple linear regression analyses were performed to investigate the characteristics of PA and SB. The average daily duration of each activity was treated as the dependent variable, whereas age, sex, BMI, main diagnosis, employment, and METs at AT were considered independent variables in the multiple linear regression analysis. To assess the presence of multicollinearity in our multiple linear regression tests, we calculated the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) for each variable. All VIF values were less than 2, indicating that multicollinearity was not a concern in our analytical model. After the analyses, the normality of residuals was assessed using the residuals vs. fitted graph and normal Q-Q plot. In cases where the residuals did not conform to a normal distribution, a logarithmic transformation (natural logarithm) of the dependent variable was applied, followed by a repeat analysis29. All statistical analyses were performed using R version 4.1.3, and a P-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

In this study, we objectively measured the PA and sedentary time of home-based patients undergoing outpatient cardiac rehabilitation and analyzed their characteristics in detail. The study results suggest that patients undergoing outpatient cardiac rehabilitation tend to be sedentary at home, possibly owing to the self-regulation of PA. A previous study30 also reported that older cardiac patients, regardless of their stage of recovery, remain seated for 70% of their waking hours, which is consistent with the results of this study. These findings highlight the need for safe and appropriate activity guidance in rehabilitation programs. Therefore, it is essential to comprehend patients’ daily activity patterns and offer personalized activity programs based on these patterns.

This study revealed differences in activity levels based on sex, which is consistent with previous research31. Sex differences in physical activity and sedentary behavior may be shaped not only by physiological factors but also by social and psychological influences, including daily routines and health awareness. In Japanese society, for instance, men often work in more sedentary occupations, while women tend to take on a larger share of household responsibilities. However, it is essential to recognize that these patterns are general trends and may not apply universally to all individuals. The observed sex-specific activity patterns, including men’s tendency towards more sedentary behavior and women’s higher engagement in household tasks, are thought to vary with age, necessitating age-specific investigations. Furthermore, understanding these sex-specific activity characteristics is crucial for designing effective rehabilitation programs. Based on our findings, it is proposed that men should focus on reducing sitting time and increasing interruptions, while promoting walking, which might be a more acceptable approach for increasing their activity levels. For women, reconsidering household tasks and adjusting their intensity may be a more acceptable way to ensure adequate activity levels.

The results of this study reveal that each unit increase in BMI corresponds to an approximate 3.8% decrease in walking activity time. For instance, when BMI rises from 25 to 26, the median walking time of 43.1 min/day is estimated to drop to about 41.5 min/day. Additionally, with each year of aging, moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) decreases by approximately 3.4%, and activity above the anaerobic threshold (AT) diminishes by about 4.3%. For example, comparing a 70-year-old patient to an 80-year-old patient, the median MVPA of 25.6 min/day is projected to decrease to about 18.1 min/day, and the mean activity above AT of 36.8 min/day is expected to reduce to around 23.9 min/day. These findings highlight the significant impact of physical factors such as age and obesity on more strenuous physical activities.

The observed trend of reduced activity with higher obesity levels suggests that obesity acts as a limiting factor for physical activity. This relationship has been frequently reported in previous studies32,33, highlighting the mutual reinforcement between decreased physical activity and obesity. Furthermore, the decline in activity with advancing age, as shown in prior research34, underscores aging as a critical factor limiting physical activity. These factors are essential considerations for developing and evaluating rehabilitation programs.

Our study also found that for every 1 MET increase in AT, MVPA rises by approximately 44.3%. For example, if AT increases from 3 METs to 4 METs, the median MVPA of 25.6 min/day is expected to increase to about 36.9 min/day. Additionally, activity above the AT level decreases by about 87.2%, while activity below the AT level increases by 56.6 min/day. Specifically, an increase in AT from 3 METs to 4 METs is projected to reduce the mean activity above AT from 36.8 min/day to about 4.7 min/day and increase activity below AT from a mean of 997.2 min/day to 1053.8 min/day. Given that MVPA is crucial for preventing and managing cardiovascular disease35 and has been linked to reduced mortality and adverse outcomes in patients with cardiovascular disease36, these findings underscore the importance of improving aerobic capacity through cardiac rehabilitation to enhance physical activity.

In interpreting the results, it is important to consider the relatively advanced age of the participants, with a mean age of 74.3 ± 16.4 years. Older patients have unique needs, and their physical activity patterns may differ from those of younger individuals. Previous research has identified several notable characteristics of physical activity patterns in older adults. Age-related physiological changes, such as decreased muscle strength, reduced balance, and diminished endurance, can significantly impact both the quantity and quality of physical activity37. Additionally, the prevalence of multiple chronic conditions among older adults often limits their physical activity38. Social factors, including lifestyle changes post-retirement and shrinking social networks, can also influence activity levels39. Lastly, safety concerns, particularly the increased risk of falls, may lead older adults to avoid high-intensity activities40.

These factors suggest that the physical activity patterns observed in our study may differ from those of younger cardiac rehabilitation patients. The relatively brief duration of MVPA and the extended time spent in sedentary behavior observed in our study may reflect activity patterns specific to older adults. Given these considerations, caution should be exercised in generalizing the results of this study. Different approaches may be needed for younger and middle-aged patients undergoing cardiac rehabilitation. Future research should include age-stratified analyses and comparative studies with younger age groups to gain a deeper understanding of the differences in physical activity patterns across age groups.

Although this study focused on objectively measuring physical activity (PA) in outpatient cardiac rehabilitation programs, it is crucial to address the significant challenges related to the long-term efficacy and sustainability of these programs. As noted by Perk (2020), one of the main concerns regarding traditional cardiac rehabilitation interventions is long-term adherence and the associated dropout rate41. To address this issue, various approaches, primarily based on home-based or hybrid intervention strategies, have been developed. The meta-analysis by Dibben et al. (2023) indicates that exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation can reduce cardiovascular mortality and hospitalization rates in patients with coronary heart disease, although the effects may vary depending on the type of intervention (center-based, home-based, or hybrid)42. This finding underscores the need to tailor cardiac rehabilitation programs to patients’ needs and preferences. Moreover, the study by Mazzoni et al. (2022) highlights the importance of promoting and maintaining physically active lifestyles in older outpatients two years after acute coronary syndrome43. Their research emphasizes the significance of individualized approaches and follow-ups, aligning with the findings of our study.

This study has several limitations. First, it only investigated patients from a single facility; thus, caution is required when generalizing the results. Second, this study included patients with multiple or coexisting diseases; however, the limited number of cases prevented us from conducting detailed stratified analyses. Additionally, due to the small sample size, we were unable to examine the associations between left ventricular ejection fraction, medication use, comorbidities, and the outcomes. Third, the details of PA were unclear because triaxial accelerometers were used in this study. While we determined the intensity of activities, the specific content of activities at each intensity level was not identified. Additionally, the lack of data regarding activities during non-wear time is a limitation of this study. Future research should incorporate questionnaires to address these gaps and provide a more comprehensive understanding of activity patterns. Fourth, the study did not include patients with dementia or those requiring assistance in daily living, potentially introducing a selection bias. Fifth, the duration since onset was unknown and its impact was not examined. The time since the onset of the condition could potentially influence physical activity levels, so future studies should incorporate this factor into their analysis. Additionally, a significant limitation was our inability to fully assess the cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF) of our participants. Due to safety concerns, many CPX were terminated shortly after reaching the AT, which prevented us from obtaining maximum oxygen uptake values. This limitation hindered our ability to calculate net and gross relative intensity averages for 3 METs, which could have offered a more precise physiological context for the MVPA threshold in our population. Given that the mean AT in our results was 3.2 METs, using the traditional absolute value of 3 METs as the MVPA threshold may not accurately represent the physiological demands for this patient group. Furthermore, we did not account for potential correlations between same-day data points, treating each independently. Future research should explore methods such as mixed-effects models to address these correlations, which could provide deeper insights into daily variations and their impact on our findings. Finally, this study could not investigate the relationship between the characteristics of PA and SB and outcomes such as readmission rates or mortality.

In conclusion, our findings suggest that physical activity and sedentary behavior among home-based patients undergoing outpatient cardiac rehabilitation are influenced by factors such as sex, BMI, age, and AT. Understanding these factors is crucial for designing effective, individualized rehabilitation programs tailored to each patient’s characteristics. These insights hold significant implications for future rehabilitation research and practice. Further research into the physical activity patterns and specific needs of older cardiac rehabilitation patients is recommended. Such research will be pivotal in developing more effective, age-appropriate rehabilitation strategies and improving outcomes for this growing population. Future studies should focus on developing more tailored interventions for patients by considering the specific characteristics of their PA and SB. Additionally, alternative methods should be explored to assess exercise tolerance and establish appropriate MVPA thresholds that account for the unique characteristics and safety requirements of this population.

Conclusions

This study objectively measured the PA and SB of home-based patients undergoing outpatient cardiac rehabilitation and analyzed their characteristics in detail. We found that SB was the longest activity while MVPA was the shortest. Multiple regression analysis revealed sex differences in SB and LPA, with men having more SB and less LPA. In addition, older age and higher AT were associated with lower MVPA. Sex, BMI, AT, and age were found to influence the type and duration of activity. Therefore, it is important to design rehabilitation programs based on these characteristics.

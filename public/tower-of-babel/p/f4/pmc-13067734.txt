The molecular landscape of the C1498 murine acute myeloid leukemia cell line
Authors: Martina Ghetti, Ilaria De Santis, Doron Tolomeo, Francesca Ruggieri, Chiara Bracci, Maria Teresa Bochicchio, Matteo Paganelli, Anna Ferrari, Sabina Sangaletti, Clelia Tiziana Storlazzi, Giorgia Simonetti
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe murine C1498 cell line is one of the most widely used syngeneic models to investigate acute myeloid leukemia (AML) pathogenesis, tumor-host interactions and therapeutic responses. However, in the absence of a comprehensive molecular characterization it remains impossible to reliably associate genetic lesions with disease phenotype, drug sensitivity or resistance. Here, we provide an integrated genomic and functional analysis combining whole genome and whole transcriptome sequencing (WGS and WTS), multicolor fluorescent in situ hybridization (M-FISH) and drug sensitivity assays. WGS identified 2,007 coding variants absent from population databases, including functionally relevant alterations in AML-related genes. These comprised deleterious or likely pathogenic variants in Nf1, Gnas and Trp53 (murine ortholog of the human TP53 gene), a splice-site mutation in Crebbp and truncating variants in Bcor and Tet2. Structural variant analysis revealed 1,767 events, including deletions of Trp53, Nf1, Brca1, Csnk1a1, Ctnna1, Luc7l2 and cohesin genes, and Myc tandem duplication. Copy number variant profiling detected ten tumor-specific alterations, including amplifications of Stk32b and Bub1, the latter linked to aggressive cancer phenotypes. Variants integration uncovered biallelic disruption of Trp53, double-hit events affecting additional tumor suppressors (mutation and deletion: Nf1, Recql4) and oncogenes (mutation and amplification: Idh1, Gnas, Pik3cd, Vav1). Functionally, C1498 demonstrated in vitro a dose- and time-dependent response to venetoclax/azacitidine consistent with resistance, in line with the presence of altered Trp53 and recent prognostic AML signatures. This comprehensive molecular framework establishes C1498 as a genetically defined AML model and provides a valuable resource to inform biomarker-driven preclinical studies and translational research aimed at overcoming venetoclax/azacitidine resistance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40364-026-00915-1.Keywords: Acute myeloid leukemia, Whole genome sequencing, Syngeneic model, C1498, Venetoclax/azacitidine

To the Editor,

The C1498 cell line was isolated from a female C57BL/6J mouse. Although it was described to induce different leukemias [1–3], surface marker analysis confirmed a predominantly myeloid lineage of monoblasts/myeloblasts [4]. When orthotopically (intra-bone) or intravenously injected into syngeneic mice, it induces a lethal disease closely resembling acute myeloid leukemia (AML), with extramedullary infiltration [5, 6]. C1498 cells have become a widely adopted AML model, useful for dissecting leukemia pathogenesis, tumor-host interaction [6] and drug responses [5, 7].

In the personalized medicine era, genetically engineered murine lines are particularly valuable for testing the functional interplay between specific molecular lesions and phenotype or treatment response, requiring a comprehensive understanding of the genomic background, that is currently lacking for the C1498 model, being limited to copy number profiling [8].

We here characterized the C1498 genetic landscape by whole genome/transcriptome sequencing (WGS/WTS) and multicolor fluorescent in situ hybridization (M-FISH) and investigated its response to the BCL2 inhibitor venetoclax (ven) in combination with the hypomethylating agent azacitidine (aza).

WGS identified 2,007 variants in the coding genome (Fig. S1A). Among nonsynonymous variants affecting evolutionarily conserved residues and validated by WTS (Supplementary Information), 144 were predicted to have deleterious/likely-pathogenic functional effects, 12 affected splice sites and 63 involved start/stop codons or were insertions/deletions, supporting a potential biological relevance (Table S1 and S2). Among AML-related genes, we identified six functionally-relevant variants (Table 1): Nf1 p.G1405S (VAF = 0.5, human NF1 p.G1403S) within the RAS-GAP domain, a loss-of-function hotspot; Trp53 p.S124P (VAF = 1, human TP53 p.S127P) in the DNA binding domain; Gnas p.R146C (VAF = 1, human GNAS p.R160C) in the G-protein alpha subunit, all classified as likely-pathogenic in humans; a splice-site variant in Crebbp and two stop-gains in the tumor suppressor genes (TSGs) Bcor and Tet2 (with VAF = 1). The functional impact remained unknown for the nonsynonymous SNVs Idh1 p.Y219C (VAF = 0.3) and Kmt2d p.L2304M (VAF = 0.5).

WGS identified 1767 SVs, 63 also matching with CNVs (Table S3). All M-FISH rearrangements showed concordance with 239 SVs except for der(3;18)(qA1;qA1) and der(8)t(8;17)(q?;q?), likely due to their complexity and the differing resolution of the techniques. Deletions encompassed Trp53, Nf1 and Brca1, mirroring recurrent losses in monosomy 17 AML, Csnk1a1 and Ctnna1 genes and Luc7l2, involved in human del(5q) and del(7q), respectively, the cohesin components Stag1, Stag2, Ctcf and Pds5b, Rad50, typically downregulated in aneuploid AML [9], the Brcc3 and Fancl DNA damage response genes and the Cdkn2A/Mtap locus, characterizing a small AML fraction [10] (Table 1 and S3). Moreover, a tandem duplication involved Myc. Five fusion genes were validated by WTS: Tra2a::Hydin, Fkbp2::Gm15918, Lpin2::Nr3c1 (Nr3c1 translocation was reported in pediatric AML [11]), and the reciprocal translocations Ptpro::Tmcc1 and Wdr33::Klf2 (Ptpro and Klf2 showed tumor suppressor properties in solid cancer and AML, respectively, Fig. 1C, Table S3).

Ten CNVs were tumor-specific (CN 3-6, Fig. 1D, Table S4), including amplifications of the Stk32b and Bub1 kinases, the latter associated with aggressive cancers [12]. Seven CNVs overlapped with M-FISH. Our findings are consistent with the genomic heterogeneity previously described in C1498 cells, characterized by multiple sub-clones sharing common CNVs, alongside clone-specific deletions and amplifications [8]. The comparison between our results and previous array-CGH profiles [8] revealed concordance for 6 deletions (chr1, 3, 4, 8, 16, X) and 10 duplications (chr3, 6, 17), with differences partly explained by technology sensitivity, genome build and/or or ongoing clonal evolution. Accordingly, the variable VAF of SNVs likely reflects the complex genomic architecture of C1498 cells.

Data integration uncovered double-hit events, including mutations co-occurring with deletions (TSGs Trp53, Nf1, Ctnna1, Recql4) or amplifications (oncogenes Idh1, Gnas, Pik3cd, Vav1, Table S5). In particular, our data revealed biallelic disruption of the Trp53 locus (deletion of the wildtype allele and duplication of the mutated one).

From a translational perspective, C1498 cells exhibited a dose- and time-dependent response to ven/aza, the standard therapy in elderly/unfit AML, though remaining largely resistant, with IC50 values of 3.16/31.6 µM ven/aza at 72 h (Fig. 1E), in line with the Trp53 loss-of-function.

Our integrated characterization offers a valuable resource to inform and guide future studies investigating leukemia-microenvironment interactions, novel combinations to overcome ven/aza resistance and responses to emerging therapies.

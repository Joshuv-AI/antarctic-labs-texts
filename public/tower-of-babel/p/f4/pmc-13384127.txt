Readiness assessment for the integration of rehabilitation services into primary healthcare in a resource-limited setting: protocol for a parallel convergent mixed-method study in Karnali Province, Nepal
Authors: Brish Bahadur Shahi, Nistha Shrestha, Sunil Pokharel, Thaneshor Paneru, Rita Adhikari, Prakash Upadhayay, Kanchan Thapa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionGlobally, 8 million people experience conditions for which rehabilitation services can be beneficial. These services are considered an essential health service for the attainment of Universal Health Coverage. According to the Nepal Demographic and Health Survey 2022 report, 23% of the population has some type of functional disability, among whom 6% have total functional disability. This study aims to assess the readiness of rehabilitation services, knowledge of health workers, experience of service recipients and policy framework.MethodsA mixed-methods study design will be conducted to assess the readiness for rehabilitation services over a period of 2 years, from July 2024 to 2026. This study protocol will examine the status of rehabilitation services, knowledge and skill gap of health workers, experiences of elderly and persons with disabilities for rehabilitation service utilisation. Similarly, the perception and experience of stakeholders working in the service sector will be assessed through key informant interviews. The participants of the current study will be 23 health facilities, more than 300 health workers in primary healthcare centre in Karnali and a convenience sample of elderly and disabled population. Furthermore, the study will explore (1) facility readiness index by domain; (2) health worker knowledge/practice score; (3) user-reported barriers; and (4) qualitative themes around governance/financing/workforce/WHO health system building blocks. The study team has designed a study protocol and conducted pre-testing in a similar setting.Ethics and disseminationThe present study was approved by Nepal Health Research Council (Ref #49, 21 July 2024). Prior to ethical approval, the study team obtained approval from the Ministry of Social Development, Karnali Province. Informed consent was obtained from all the participants involved in the study. All collected data will be used solely for study purposes. The findings of the study will be published as a report from the Publication of the Ministry of Social Development, and major findings will be published in the Journal.Keywords: Health Services, REHABILITATION MEDICINE, Primary Health Care, Health Services Accessibility, HEALTH SERVICES ADMINISTRATION & MANAGEMENT, Nepal

Introduction

Globally, 8 million people experience health conditions that require rehabilitation.1 About 2.41 billion people globally could benefit from rehabilitation.2 Among them, 240 million children have disabilities worldwide.3 There has been a rise in cases requiring rehabilitation by 79.4% from 1990 to 2021, and the WHO Western Pacific Region has the highest need for rehabilitation services. Musculoskeletal conditions, particularly low back pain, have the highest prevalence and contribute most to the global need for rehabilitation.2 In the South-East Asian Region (SEAR), it has been estimated that approximately 1 in 3 people could benefit from rehabilitation services and 120 million people have been affected by vision loss. There has been a rise in cases requiring rehabilitation to 101% from 1990 to 2021. India has the highest number of people who could benefit from rehabilitation in SEAR.2

Rehabilitation is a set of health interventions that optimise functioning and reduce disability in individuals with health conditions, in interaction with their environment.1 Rehabilitation addresses a diverse range of health conditions, from acute to chronic and includes physical, mental health, vision and hearing related conditions.4 Rehabilitation is a highly integrated health service and should be part of the continuum of care for a wide array of health conditions. Rehabilitation aims to achieve and maintain optimal functioning, and functioning can be measured at both individual and population levels. Moving, seeing, hearing, speaking, communicating and engaging in social interactions are forms of human functioning, and they are crucial for a healthy, dignified and meaningful life. Rehabilitation needs to be integrated across clinical care, delivered along the continuum. It is mostly multi-disciplinary, team-based care, but can be provided by a single discipline.

The WHO has included rehabilitation as an essential health service within the definition of Universal Health Coverage. Rehabilitation is required to prevent further complications, shorten recovery time as well as to improve the physical and mental functioning and well-being of individuals.4 The rehabilitation needs of the population show variation across countries. It is essential for countries to implement interventions that are proven to improve and maintain functioning of people requiring rehabilitation.

According to WHO, one-quarter of the population could benefit from rehabilitation services, with low back pain being the most prevalent condition requiring rehabilitation in Nepal. Approximately 9.0 million people experience conditions such as musculoskeletal disorders, neurological disorders, sensory impairments, mental disorders, chronic respiratory diseases and neoplasms that could benefit from rehabilitation.4 According to Nepal Demographic and Health Survey 2022 report, 23% of population has some type of functional disability, among which 6% people have total functional disability or experience significant functional difficulties.5

Among children aged 2–17 years, 10.2% have significant functional difficulties or complete functional disability.3 In Nepal, it is estimated that 8.9% of total population requires assistive products for rehabilitation. Out of those in need of assistive devices, 17.9% are deprived of those services.6 The census of Nepal 2078 (2021 AD) showed that 2.25% of total population is living with disabilities.7 In Nepal, 83% of people with disabilities have no access to rehabilitation services.8 The census also showed 3.14% of total population of Karnali is living with disability.7 The percentage of women and men who have a lot of difficulties or cannot function at all is highest in Karnali Province.5 The total government investment is only 0.2%, of which 5% comes from government sources and 95% from foreign aid. In the case of rehabilitation services, the provider to population ratio is 1:20 000, which is not only low but also unequally distributed.9 District Health Information Software-2 (DHIS 2) is an open-source software that facilitates data collection and reporting through electronic platforms. Enhancing health information systems is crucial for strengthening rehabilitation services.10

Aligned with the Public Health Service Act 201811 and National Health Policy 2019,12 the Karnali Province Health Policy 201913 has identified rehabilitation as the health service. The provincial government of Karnali has established physiotherapy service in all district hospitals. Likewise, the province hospital Surkhet is being developed as the referral hospital for rehabilitation, as evident by the fiscal year 2079/80 budget allocations for the establishment of prosthetic and orthotic service.14 Even though the province has made encouraging progress, rehabilitation is still absent at primary healthcare level, indicating a significant unmet need for such services. In this context, the province recently launched its Rehabilitation Strategy 2024–2030 in which integration of rehabilitation into primary healthcare is a priority. In pursuit of integrating rehabilitation into primary healthcare, the health division of Ministry of Social Development (MoSD) intends to assess readiness of rehabilitation services, the knowledge of health workers, the experience of service recipients and policy framework. The system addresses all the six building blocks of health system as applied to primary healthcare context.

Material and methods

Study setting

Karnali Province is one of the remote provinces in Nepal, comprises 79 local-level governments and most areas consist of hills and mountains.15 People still struggle to access primary healthcare services, and the state of rehabilitation services has not yet been thoroughly investigated. The first part of the readiness assessment was conducted in health facilities in Karnali Province (table 1). During this stage, a self-administered questionnaire will be used to interview the rehabilitation and health service providers in these facilities. In the second phase, people with disabilities and the older adults will be interviewed. In the third phase, key stakeholders, including public organisations, international/non-governmental organisation and partners such as Handicap International and National Federation of Disabled-Nepal Federation, who are involved in rehabilitation in Karnali Province will be interviewed for qualitative information.

The health service utilisation statistics for Karnali Province in the fiscal years 2022/2023 and 2023/2024 (online supplemental file 1) show a general trend of change in the number of service users across most age groups, with notable decreases in individuals aged 18–59, over 59 and 6–17 years. The service utilisation trend among people with non-communicable diseases, accidents/injuries and violence-related cases also declined, while cases categorised as ‘Others’ and those marked ‘Don’t Know’ increased. There was a significant rise in the need for mobility aids, with more patients receiving these devices compared with the previous year. The number of assistive products provided increased substantially, particularly those sourced from hospitals and self-purchased. People with disabilities of the musculoskeletal system remained the largest consumer group of rehabilitation, although this proportion slightly decreased, while the service users with respiratory and genitourinary diseases increased. Rehabilitation services, especially physiotherapy, remained consistent, but the provision of assistive devices experienced a substantial increase. Physical disabilities remained the most common, but there was a notable rise in mental and psycho-social disabilities.

Study design

A mixed-methods parallel convergent design will be adopted for this study. In this method, required number of data from qualitative and quantitative component will be collected at the same time. The Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative studies (COREQ): 32-item checklist will be used for data assessment of qualitative findings (online supplemental file 2). Furthermore, we will use Good Reporting of a Mixed Methods Study (GRAMMS) to report the mixed-method study findings (online supplemental file 3). Similarly, Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) flow chart (figure 1) will be used.

STROBE flow chart. HWs, health workers; STROBE, Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology.

Sampling

A census of all currently functional 23 hospitals will be conducted as part of this study. This study will include all hospitals that provide basic primary healthcare services and are currently operating in the province. Health workers will be selected conveniently based on their interest to respond to the questionnaire distributed via an email link. Similarly, the elderly individual and disabled individual living in the province will be conveniently sampled. For qualitative study, the required number of participants will be selected pruposively. The attached STROBE design shows the process of selection of the participation in this study.

Study participant

This study will be carried out among the health service providers and managers working in hospitals in Karnali Province, Nepal. Service providers who have at least 1 year of experience and continuously working in the health facility will be recruited on obtaining written informed consent. Thus, for the readiness assessment part, all 23 local level primary hospital managers will be the study participants. Similarly, health workers involved in service delivery points of the province will be the study participants for knowledge related aspect. Furthermore, older adults and persons with disability living in the province will be assessed to examine their lived experience of the rehabilitation service. Moreover, key informants, including officers working in Health Service Directorate, health coordinator of local level, provincial health official, female community health volunteers, official from the Social Development Ministry, district level health service managers, local level representatives and development partner representatives, will be interviewed. At present, following the implementation of the Constitution of Nepal 2015, the country’s health system is undergoing several changes, which created both opportunities and challenges during the reform process.16 Similarly, health workers who wish to provide information and feedback can respond using self-administered questionnaire.

As the province provides the health services to estimated population of 1.8 million, it has 307 sanctioned positions and 693 contract based and technical assistant-based positions, with approximately 1000 health workers.17 Thus, the study aims to cover at least 30% of the total health workforce. Given the challenges in accurately identifying the characteristics of health workers in the province, the sample size will be kept flexible, using a sampling approach for both health workers and service users. It is assumed that most health workers are concentrated in high population density areas of the province. Additionally, it is assumed that most are technologically literate and able to use mobile phone or computers.

Study tool

A standardised prevalidated tool will be used for this study. A draft version of the tool was developed based on the WHO Service Assessment and Readiness Assessment (WHO SARA)18 and the Nepal Health Facility Survey19 tool for the other health services. Similar rehabilitation technical guidelines, the DHIS2 system, available training at the national level were reviewed to derive the domains and tracer areas. A review of available documents was conducted for this purpose. Based on the review, pre-testing, expert consultation and consultation with the key stakeholders in the province, the readiness checklist will be prepared. This checklist (online supplemental file 4) will be used after incorporating the findings from the pretesting in selected municipal hospitals of the province. The key indicators for the tool will be presented in table 2. The study tool will cover mainly 10 domains. All the qualitative and quantitative tools were first developed in English then translated to local language, Nepali. The tools were then back-translated to English to check the linguistic validity and accuracy of the tools.

Health workers’ perspectives

The health worker questionnaire (online supplemental file 6) will assess the socio-demographic background, knowledge, current practices for referral and perceived needs based on clinical practice. A total of 27 questions are included in this questionnaire. As this is a self-administered questionnaire delivered via Google Forms, health workers are free to respond voluntarily.

Perspectives of disabled and older adults

This study will consider the perspectives of disabled and older adults. As major service users, their viewpoints are important to consider in this study. Therefore, a questionnaire has been developed and will be administered to older adults. Data collection will be conducted through Google Forms; however, due to accessibility requirements, minor assistance may be provided during the data collection. The nature of such assistance will be recorded; however, the study team/volunteers will ensure that no bias is introduced during the data collection. The questionnaire consists of 35 questions covering socio-demographic characteristics, knowledge, service-use history, referral challenges faced and health workers’ behaviours (online supplemental file 7).

Key informed perspectives

The key informants for this study will be identified, including province-level healthcare managers, district-level healthcare managers, clinicians, local-level health coordinators, health workers working in service delivery points, service users, female community health volunteers, local level representatives and other key stakeholders identified during the study period. All the information will be collected using Key Informant Interview (KII) guidelines developed in Nepali and English (online supplemental file 8). All the data collection will be done up to the saturation level.

Study tool pretesting

The study tool will be pretested in two purposively selected health facilities of the province. Basic hospitals in the Surkhet and Dailekh districts will be selected. Based on the findings of the pretesting, further modifications to the tool will be made. Similarly, the remaining tools and questionnaire (onlinesupplemental files 68) will be pretested in Surkhet district. Based on the findings of the pretesting, the tool will be further redefined. Further, language and technical modifications will be made based on expert suggestions, closely adhering to pretesting findings.

Data collection in the field

The data collection in the present study will be conducted through the online platform Google Form. The form will be developed in the local Nepali language. A link will be sent to the study participants through email. The study participants (health service providers/managers) of the selected health facility will provide their responses in the provided link. An independent public health officer from the MoSD will monitor the data collection progress. He will report the data collection progress weekly. Similarly, he will further endorse the health workers’ questionnaire in other appropriate gatherings at the province level.

Similarly, the research team will review the secondary data of the selected hospitals. The minimum service standard of the primary hospital will be reviewed for relevant variables which will be cross-verified with the present findings. Similarly, during the data collection, we will also collect photographical evidence from the field; likewise, KII will supplement the information. At least 10 field visits will be conducted in 10% of the selected sites for onsite observation by independent members of the MoSD, Karnali. Being a mixed-method design, we are already prepared to take the feedback, responses and suggestions to include in the study.

Data processing/entry

As the study participants directly enter the provided information in the Google Form, no further entries are required. Furthermore, a strong validation option will be kept in the Google Form for the data capture. On a daily basis, the summary of the field progress will be monitored and shared with the study team. Another independent research member with at least a Bachelor of Public Health qualification will monitor workflow. All the qualitative transcripts will first be transcribed in Nepali and then translated into English. The first version of the translation will be read by two researchers to check the consistency and theme generation. The translated data will be shared and stored in MS Word format. Similarly, further verification will be done for 10% of transcribed files, which will be back translated into the local language (Nepali). The file will thus be matched to check the quality of the translation and work. Necessary action will be taken immediately to monitor the progress.

Data analysis plan and outcome quantification

The study will have a data analysis plan for qualitative and quantitative studies. The quantitative data analysis will be done as per the domain and tracer indicators selected. The inferential and descriptive analysis will be done in R Studio.

A theme-based deductive approach will be used for the qualitative study. All the qualitative data will first be recorded, transcribed and translated into English. A list of relevant codes, themes and subthemes will be generated based on the information. A qualitative data analysis plan will be generated prior to the analysis.

As this is a parallel mixed-method convergent design, we will triangulate the information from the quantitative study and the qualitative study. Based on the findings, we will plan to generate new themes, codes and verbatim data. At present, we plan to analyse the data based on qualitative themes which mainly include governance, financing, workforce and WHO building blocks.

Thus, the study will first explore the primary outcome as integration feasibility (Yes/No) based on findings from health facilities, health workers, users, policymakers and health facility assessments. Furthermore, this study plans to explore various other factors such as access, utilisation, behaviour, policy factors, economic factors, social and systemic barriers, cognitive and communication barriers from the users’ perspectives. Similarly, KII will explore leadership, policy, political commitment, health information systems, budgeting, partnership with various stakeholders, perspectives of users, policymakers, service providers and supporting hands.

All the collected data will be stored in a Google Excel sheet which will first be downloaded and prepared for analysis. Since we will have already validated each response, there is a lesser chance of non-response. The descriptive data will be presented in tables and figures using mean, median, SD or SEs. The Health Facility Readiness Index by domain will be calculated. Similarly, from the health workers questionnaire, health workers’ knowledge and practice score will be calculated. The knowledge score will be computed based on the mean score of overall knowledge of the participants.

The data will be reported using Inter Quartile Range (IQR). Inferential statistics will be computed at a 95% CI. Hierarchical structural modelling will be used for health workers within the local levels or hospitals or districts. Furthermore, a multilevel regression model will be used. A random intercept will be included at the facility and district levels. Based on the data structure, linear mixed-effect models and generalised linear mixed-effect models will be employed. All the data which goes under the modelling will be first tested for normality, homoscedasticity and multicollinearity. Goodness of Fit and residual diagnostics will be computed and presented. Fixed-effects models and models with robust SEs will be used to compare the results for sensitivity.

Possible bias and non-response

For quantitative data collection, the form will be validated for each response. Users cannot leave any cell blank during the data collection. So, we already have a strategy for non-response for those who enter into the data collection process. However, there is a possibility of selection bias for health workers and users who use mobile phones or computers are likely to be included in the present study. Similarly, information or recall bias is likely to occur, as are covering various perspectives including feelings about service usage, past experiences and expectations. Furthermore, as we are collecting qualitative data, we may reach areas with higher accessibility, comfort, mainly with educated individuals with higher attachment to modern healthcare services. Similarly, we will include verbatim quotes during the reporting of the findings which will include some identifying information such as district, facility type, sex, age and disability type. This will be reported to make it inclusive so that everyone’s voices can be heard in the study. The personal information will be coded and not published as personally identifiable information.

Ethical consideration

The present study received ethical approval from the Nepal Health Research Council (NHRC) (Ref #49, 21 July 2024). Prior to ethical approval, the study team received approval from the MoSD, Karnali Province. In this study, written informed consent will be obtained from health facility manager/service provider. The informed consent form in the local Nepali language will be sent to the health facility via email, along with the link for the data entry. The consent form will clearly state the purpose of the study, the harms and benefits and voluntary participation. The health facility manager will digitally sign the form and return it to the research team. All the data collection in this study will be done in the local Nepali language; however, a simultaneously translated version will be available for validity and proper understanding of the study participants.

As this study will be done in a hybrid model in the province, we will strictly adhere to the National Ethical Guidelines for Health Research in Nepal 202220 for the standard provision for 3.3.2. Data Acquisition, Management, Sharing and Ownership. However, there are limited guidelines for data server location, encryption, access control, retention and de-identification. Therefore, we will adhere further, whenever applicable to international standards such as the Helsinki Declaration,21 the Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences Guidelines,22 the General Data Protection Regulation and WHO Data Sharing policies.23

Study timeline

The total period of the present study will be 2 years starting from July 2024 to July 2026. All the activities have been planned to track the progress of the study and ensure smooth project operation (online supplemental file 9). The study will share its periodic reports with Nepal Health Research Council (NHRC) and MoSD Karnali Province. On the completion of the study, a final report will be handed to MoSD and NHRC.

Discussion

This study will be conducted with the objective to determine the challenges, gaps and opportunities of integrating rehabilitation services in primary healthcare services in Karnali Province. The study aims to ascertain the situation of healthcare facilities in basic hospitals for providing rehabilitation services and explore the role of healthcare providers, patients and administrators in integrating rehabilitation services in primary healthcare services in Karnali Province, Nepal. Along with this, the study is designed to determine the needs and perspectives of people with special needs (persons with disabilities/ageing population) and their current knowledge on rehabilitation among primary healthcare users regarding rehabilitation service needs and referral system in Karnali Province.

With the vision of ‘Quality health service within reach of all residents of Karnali: happy, alert and conscious Karnali’, the Health Sector Rehabilitation Action Plan 2080–2087 has been formed in Karnali Province.15 Our study is outlined in accordance with and aims to support the goal envisioned by Karnali Health Policy 2078,13 the Health Sector Strategic Action Plan14 and Rehabilitation Strategic Action Plan, 2080–2087.24

Consideration of both the quantitative components and qualitative factors, along with policy and programme implementation and evaluation, makes our study a comprehensive study for the integration of rehabilitation services in primary healthcare. Furthermore, our qualitative research design allows flexibility during the iterative process of consultations with key stakeholders and experts. Importantly, collaborating with key stakeholders during the development process will help in the uptake and ownership of the results disseminated by the research process.

The pretesting of the questionnaire will be done based on the selected sites. The rehabilitation facilities, primary healthcare centres, provincial hospitals, district public health offices and other external development partners which can be reviewed in provincial annual reports,15 are working for rehabilitation in Surkhet district. The survey form developed from Google Forms will be administered, and the validity and reliability of the questionnaire will be checked. The KII questions will also be checked through the interview process.

The DHIS 2 data of fiscal year 2023/2024 of Karnali Province display the need for rehabilitation services to be highest (63.2%) in the age group of 18–59 years followed by >59 years (21.9%). Musculoskeletal and connective tissue-related disabilities were found to be most common (70.5%) followed by injuries and external causes (9.7%). Mobility aids had the highest need (98.4%) among other assistive devices. Physiotherapy services had highest coverage (86.4%) among all other services. Among all the types of disability, physical disability was the most prevalent (97.31%) in Karnali Province (online supplemental file 1).

The present study is the first study of this type initiated by the provincial government of Karnali, Nepal. It involves both qualitative and quantitative methods using a mixed-methods approach. The health workers, older adults, the elderly and persons with disabilities living in the province are invited to provide their feedback. In order to record the responses, we will include an option for open-ended questions in each form (online supplemental files 3-5). Similarly, the quantitative data will be analysed through the deductive approach and validated with quantitative findings.

Though there are various frameworks, guidelines and protocols for assessing health system readiness,25 WHO guidelines for quality assessment for pharmaceuticals,26 there is limited protocol available to assess the rehabilitation readiness. A paper has been published assessing the number of rehabilitation centres in the SEAR region;27 however, country and province specific information is limited. Such health service information is crucial for other health conditions such as stroke management.27 Furthermore, there are various assessment reports for assessing health service readiness for maternal and child health,28 health system gaps for cardiovascular diseases,29 the Sustainable Development Baseline Report in Karnali30 and others in the provinces. Being a rural province of Nepal, the emphasis on primary healthcare and its integration with rehabilitation is important.

However, despite these strengths, this study will have some limitations. The study will be conducted among the participants who provide responses to the survey shared with them. Individuals with limited digital literacy will be automatically excluded from the analysis. Similarly, the qualitative data will be collected purposively and will be mainly focused on highly accessible areas of Surkhet. Therefore, information from remotely inaccessible areas will be automatically excluded. Similarly, the use of convenience sampling for health workers might limit the ability to capture the information from hard-to-reach areas in the communities. Older adults who require rehabilitation services in a broader context may not be reached due to our sampling procedure.

To address all these issues we have purposively included the voices of disabled persons, health workers and older adults. The qualitative information will supplement the quantitative data. Thus, this study will explore insights from the study area. In summary, this protocol will capture the challenges, opportunities, knowledge gaps, skill gaps, equipment status and rehabilitation service readiness in Karnali province through a parallel convergent mixed-methods design. The findings will have policy-level implications to integrate rehabilitation services in the province.

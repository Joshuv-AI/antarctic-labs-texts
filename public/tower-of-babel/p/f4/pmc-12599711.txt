Identifying autophagy-related biomarkers in the treatment of renal interstitial fibrosis with Shen Fukang through network pharmacology
Authors: Simiao Liu, Yaqi Gong, Zihao Zhang, Jingsong Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Shen fukang (SFK), a Chinese herbal preparation, exhibits significant clinical efficacy in managing renal diseases. Studies have suggested that autophagy may promote the occurrence of renal interstitial fibrosis (RIF), but the specific mechanisms are ambiguous. This study aims to elucidate the molecular regulatory mechanisms of autophagy during SFK treatment of RIF.Methods:In this study, autophagy-related genes were acquired from public databases and previous literature, SFK target genes were retrieved from public databases, and RIF data were acquired through self-sequencing. Initially, differentially expressed genes (DEGs1 and DEGs2) were identified separately between RIF and control, and SFK + RIF and RIF. In further analysis, we cross-compared genes with opposite expression change directions between DEGS1 and DEGS2.and then these intersected genes were merged and converted to human homologous genes to obtain the final DEGs. Biomarkers were obtained by intersecting final DEGs, autophagy-related genes, and SFK target genes. Following this, genes functionally related to biomarkers were analyzed by GeneMANIA.Results:In this study, 8244 DEGs1 and 915 DEGs2 were identified, with 195 genes showing opposite expression trends. Through homologous conversion, 179 final DEGs in humans were obtained, among which FOS and IFNG were biomarkers related to autophagy in the process of SFK treating RIF. GeneMANIA analysis predicted that JUN, IFNGR1, and IGFBP7 were functionally related to the biomarkers. Subsequent enrichment analysis revealed that propanoate metabolism and citrate cycle (TCA cycle) were pathways co-enriched in 2 biomarkers. Moreover, in the regulatory network, CREB1 and signal transduction and activator of transcription 3 were found to regulate both FOS and IFNG. Finally, molecular docking showed good docking effects of IFNG with quercetin and luteolin.Conclusion:This study successfully identified FOS and IFNG as 2 key biomarkers related to autophagy in the treatment of RIF by SFK. Through a series of analyses, including GeneMANIA analysis, enrichment analysis, regulatory network construction, and molecular docking, the potential molecular mechanisms associated with these biomarkers were comprehensively explored.

1. Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is recognized as a critical global public health issue requiring urgent resolution. Recent epidemiological investigations indicate that the global aggregate number of patients with CKD, acute kidney injury, and those receiving renal replacement therapy has surpassed 850 million.[1] renal interstitial fibrosis (RIF), characterized by tubulointerstitial fibrosis and glomerulosclerosis, is the common final pathway of most chronic and progressive nephropathies.[2] Kidney injury triggers an inflammatory response, and immune cells subsequently produce numerous pro-fibrotic cytokines and growth factors. These factors stimulate myofibroblasts and boost the abnormal accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) in the tubulointerstitium, which in turn accelerates hypoxia and nephron loss, ultimately leading to CKD.[3,4] In China, the prevalence of CKD is as high as 10.8%.[5] However, currently, there are still no targeted therapeutic methods capable of slowing down the progression of chronic kidney disease and preventing its related complications. Moreover, the persistently high treatment costs coupled with poor prognosis have rendered the prevention and management of chronic obstructive diseases a critical challenge in clinical practice.[6–9]

Biopsy, as the gold standard for studying RIF, has the limitation of invasive testing. Because of its atypical early symptoms, Currently, there is a lack of reliable biomarkers for predicting and assessing renal fibrosis in clinical practice.[3] Seriously affects the early antifibrotic treatment. Most therapies only retard the progression of fibrotic nephropathy and targeted drugs for myofibroblast activation are limited. Therefore, for RIF, the creation of breakthrough therapies has considerable clinical significance.

Network pharmacology overcomes the limitations of monotherapy by concurrently targeting multiple proteins within disease signaling modules, achieving precise therapeutic intervention through synergistic polypharmacology and drug repurposing strategies.[10,11] The network-based approach integrating “constituent-target-pathway-mechanism” relationships provides a powerful framework for elucidating the material basis and molecular mechanisms of traditional Chinese medicine.[12] Chen study employed an integrated lipidomics-network pharmacology approach to uncover berberine’s therapeutic targets and biological mechanisms against hyperlipidemia, offering novel insights for clinical practice and systematic research on TCM bioactive components.[13] Shenfukang Granule (SFK), a traditional Chinese herbal formula developed based on TCM theory, has been clinically used for nephropathy treatment with accumulated therapeutic experience over years. It has the effect of invigorating the spleen and the kidney, removing stasis and purging turbidity. Evidence from experiments of the unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO)-induced RIF model rats also displayed significant efficacy in alleviating RIF. Studies demonstrate that astragaloside IV (AS-IV), one of the primary active constituents of Shenfukang Granule (SFK), specifically targets and upregulates ALDH2 expression, effectively suppressing autophagy both in vivo and in vitro, thereby ameliorating epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and alleviating G2/M phase cell cycle arrest.[14] Nevertheless, the precise mechanisms underlying SFK’s renoprotective effects remain largely elusive. The correlation investigation of SFK based on network pharmacology is still lacking.

Autophagy is a catabolic process that uses lysosomes to degrade damaged, aging, and excess biomolecules and organelles, thereby digesting and recovering cytoplasmic components from cells. It plays a crucial role in maintaining cellular homeostasis.[15] Recent studies have demonstrated that constitutive autophagy in renal cells serves as a crucial endogenous protective mechanism, whose dysfunction is closely associated with the pathogenesis of various kidney diseases and the manifestation of renal aging.[16] Experiments have confirmed that the activated autophagy pathway in UUO models promotes renal fibrosis by regulating lipid metabolism abnormalities. Pharmacological inhibition of autophagy can significantly reduce uUO-induced renal lipid accumulation and subsequent fibrotic pathological changes.[17] The autophagic process activates MAPK/ERK signaling in renal tubule cells, resulting in EGR1-dependent FGF2 transactivation, followed by paracrine FGF2 secretion that induces fibroblast-to-myofibroblast differentiation.[18] Regulating the autophagic process through pharmacological means holds promise for renal disease prevention and therapy.

In this study, we obtain biomarkers by intersecting autophagy-related genes (ARGs) 、RIF self-sequencing data, and the SFK target genes screening through network pharmacology. The molecular mechanisms of these biomarkers were investigated through GeneMANIA, enrichment analysis, regulatory networks, and molecular docking. These results can provide new strategies for the treatment of RIF.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Sample collection and data source

The research protocol received approval from (Approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Hunan University of Chinese Medicine) (202311001). A total of fifteen SPF male SD rats, each weighing 200 g, were purchased from Sibeifu (Beijing) Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The rats were provided food and water in the laboratory, maintained on a 12-hour light/dark cycle, and adaptively fed for a week before the experiment. Then, rats were randomly separated into a sham surgery cohort (control), a RIF model cohort, and a Shen Fukang (SFK) treated RIF model cohort (SFK + RIF) through a random number method. The control cohort and the RIF cohort were administered physiological saline at a standard of 20ml/kg by gavage daily, while the SFK + RIF cohort started gavage with SFK at a standard of 3.6 g/kg the day after successful modeling. The experimental period lasted for 3 weeks. At the end of the experimental period, rat kidney tissue samples were collected for transcriptome sequencing. This study obtained 222 ARGs from the Human Autophagy Database (HADb, https://autophagy.lu/v1/) and integrated an additional 367 ARGs reported in previous studies.[19] After deduplication and merging, 520 ARGs were retained.

2.2. Transcriptomic analysis

Total RNA was extracted by Trizol reagent (Thermofisher, 15596018). The total RNA’s yield and integrity was assessed using the Bioanalyzer 2100 and RNA 6000 Nano LabChip Kit (Agilent, 5067-1511) (RIN >7.0). Ribo-Zero Gold rRNA Removal Kit (Illumina, cat. MRZG12324, San Diego) was designed to remove ribosomal RNA and fragment the remaining RNA using divalent cation fragmentation. Then, the fragmented RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA via SuperScript™ II Reverse Transcriptase (Invitrogen, USA, order number 1896649). Subsequently, the ligated product was amplified through PCR, and paired-end sequencing was conducted with 2 × 150 bp reads on each end through Illumina Novaseq™ 6000 platform.

To enhance the precision and dependability of the sequencing data, low-quality data were filtered out by cutadapt (v 1.9).[20] Subsequently, the quality of the purified sequences was verified utilizing the FastQC software (v 0.11.9),[21] including Q20, Q30, and GC content. After this, G base pairs of clean paired-end reads were generated. The sequencing reads were aligned to the rat reference genome through the Hisat2 package (v 2.2.2.1).[22] Each sample’s aligned reads were assembled with StringTie (v 2.1.6)[23] under default settings. The complete transcriptome was assembled by merging all transcripts using GFFCompare (v0.9.8).[24] StringTie-Ballgown was utilized for transcriptome analysis to evaluate gene expression and determine mRNA abundance by fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM).

2.3. Identifying final differentially expressed genes

DEGs between RIF and control cohorts, as well as between SFK + RIF and RIF cohorts, were identified through the DESeq2 package (v 1.34.0)[25] from self-sequencing data (|log2 fold change| >0.5 and P <.05), and were named DEGs1 and DEGs2, respectively. Subsequently, ggplot2 package (v 3.4.1)[26] and ComplexHeatmap package (v 2.14.0)[27] were used to visualize the DEGs1 and DEGs2, with the top 10 up- and down-regulated genes annotated on the corresponding volcano plots sorted by |log2fold change|. Subsequently, to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) associated with SFK treatment, we performed intersection analysis of DEGS1 and DEGS2 datasets using Venngraphic software (version 1.7.1)[28] to screen for common genes exhibiting opposite expression patterns was harnessed to find the intersection of genes with opposite expression trends between DEGs1 and DEGs2. Then, DEGs were obtained by taking the union of these 2 gene sets. Finally, the msigdbr package (v 7.5.1)[29] was leveraged to convert these DEGs into their human homologous genes to obtain the final DEGs.

2.4. The active ingredient screening and target predicting of SFK

To identify the active components in SFK, the Traditional Chinese Medicine Systems Pharmacology (TCMSP, https://tcmsp-e.com/tcmsp.php) database was employed to screen for effective chemical constituents from Pseudostellaria heterophylla (Miq.)Pax ex Pax et Hoffm (Taizishen), Rehmannia glutinosa Libosch (Shengdihuang), Poria cocos (Schw.) Wolf (Fuling), Oldenlandia diffusa (Willd.) Roxb (Baihua Sheshecao), Astragalus membranaceus (Fisch.) Bunge (Huangqi), Lonicera japonica Thunb (Rendongteng), Cornus officinalis Sieb. et Zucc (Shanzhuyu), Isaria cicadae Miq (Chanhua), Salvia miltiorrhiza Bunge (Danshen), Rheum palmatum L (Dahuang) (Meeting the basic criteria of oral bioavailability ≥ 30% and drug-likeness score ≥ 0.18). Following that, using the compound-target data from TCMSP database, corresponding active target proteins and their genes (SFK target genes) for the medicinal compounds were obtained.

2.5. Pinpointing and functional evaluation of biomarkers

Biomarkers were identified by utilizing VennDiagram package to analyze the final DEGs, ARGs, and SFK target genes. Subsequently, to explore other genes related to the function of biomarkers, the co-expression network of biomarkers was predicted and constructed via GeneMANIA (https://genemania.org/).

2.6. Gene set enrichment analysis

To explore the biological pathways underlying RIF-related biomarkers in the self-sequencing data, we performed Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA). Firstly, based on all samples from the RNA sequencing, using the Psych package (v2.1.6).[30] we conducted Spearman rank correlation tests for each key gene against all other genomic genes. After completing the correlation analysis, genes according to their correlation coefficients were ranked from highest to lowest, thereby obtaining a sorted gene list for each key gene. Then, in this study, using the c2.cp.kegg.v2023.1.Hs.symbols.gmt gene set from MSigDB as the reference background, we performed GSEA with the clusterProfiler package. The significance thresholds were set as follows: P<.05 ∩ |NES| > 1 ∩ FDR <.05.

2.7. Constructing of molecular regulatory networks

Based on the biomarkers that have been identified, NetworkAnalyst database (https://www.networkanalyst.ca/NetworkAnalyst/) was applied to prognosticate upstream microRNAs (miRNAs) that may affect these biomarkers. Subsequently, miRNet database (https://www.mirnet.ca/miRNet/home.xhtml), Starbase database (https://rnasysu.com/encori/) and miRcode2 database (http://www.mircode.org) were leveraged to divine long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) associated with aforementioned miRNAs. The intersection of the prediction results from these 3 databases was taken to obtain key lncRNAs, and an lncRNA-miRNA-biomarker network was built. Following this, prediction of transcription factors (TFs) that regulate the biomarkers was completed in miRNet database, and a TF-biomarker network was designed. Both lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA network and TF-biomarker network were visualized through Cytoscape (v 3.8.2).[31]

2.8. Molecular docking

Then, the relationship between biomarkers and active ingredients was investigated. Firstly, Cytoscape software was designed to construct a drug-active ingredient-key gene regulatory network. Subsequently, molecular docking was performed for the biomarkers and active ingredients. We began by downloading the 3D molecular structures of active ingredients from PubChem database. The 3-dimensional structures of biomarker-associated proteins were subsequently acquired from the Protein Data Bank (https://www.rcsb.org/). Finally, molecular docking was carried out via CB-Dock (https://cadd.labshare.cn/cb-dock2/index.php) (binding energy <−5 kcal/mol).

2.9. Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.2), with between-group differences assessed by Wilcoxon nonparametric tests, using a statistical significance threshold of P <.05.

3. Results

3.1. Recognition of final DEGs

Within RIF and control cohorts, 8244 DEGs1 were identified (4328 up- and 3916 down-regulated in RIF) (Fig. 1A and B). Between the SFK + RIF and RIF cohorts, 915 DEGs2 were identified (585 up- and 330 down-regulated in SFK + RIF) (Fig. 1C and D). Comparative analysis identified 154 overlapping genes between up-regulated genes in DEGS1 and down-regulated genes in DEGS2 (Fig. 1E), while 41 common genes were found between down-regulated genes in DEGS1 and up-regulated genes in DEGS2 (Fig. 1F). By combining these 2 gene sets, a total of 195 DEGs were obtained. In the subsequent conversion of human homologous genes, these 195 DEGs were translated into 179 final DEGs.

(C) To comprehensively visualize the distribution of DEGs, a volcano plot was generated with the top 10 up- and down-regulated DEGs labeled based on log2FC. 3.2. Identification and function analysis of biomarkers

After that, through the TCMSP database, 141 active compounds from SFK were obtained, and based on the compound-target information in the TCMSP database, these compounds corresponded to 205 target genes, which were named SFK target genes. By intersecting the SFK target genes, ARGs, and the final DEGs, 2 biomarkers were identified (Fig. 2A), namely FOS and IFNG. Using GeneMANIA, we predicted that JUN, IFNGR1, and IGFBP7 were functionally similar to biomarkers. FOS was functionally related to JUN in several processes, including cellular response to oxidative stress and Fc receptor signaling pathway. IFNG was associated with IFNGR1 and IGFBP7 in various functions, including cellular reaction to interferon-gamma, modulation of the innate immune response, and control of reactions to interferon-gamma (Fig. 2B).

(B) GeneMANIA network diagram, the inner circle represents key genes, and the outer circle represents other genes related to the function of key genes. ARGs = autophagy-related genes, DEGs = differentially expressed genes, SFK = Shen Fukang.

3.3. Biomarker-associated pathway enrichment

Through GSEA, we identified significant enrichment of FOS in 73 signaling pathways. Notably, FOS predominantly influenced pathways associated with pathogenic E.coli infection, pancreatic cancer, and ECM receptor interaction. Similarly, IFNG showed enrichment across 96 signaling pathways, with a significant impact on pathways such as dorso-ventral axis formation, spliceosome, and natural killer cell-mediated cytotoxicity. Interestingly, when comparing the TOP 10 enriched pathways for both FOS and IFNG, there was an overlap in several key metabolic pathways. The identified metabolic pathways comprise: (i) branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) (valine, leucine, and isoleucine) degradation, (ii) propionate metabolism, and (iii) the citric acid cycle (tricarboxylic acid cycle) (Fig. 3A and B). This shared enrichment suggests a potential synergy or interplay between FOS and IFNG in these biological processes.

3.4. Construction of biomarker regulatory networks

Afterwards, the regulatory relationships of biomarkers were studied. The NetworkAnalyst database predicted 68 miRNAs. Following this, lncRNAs associated with these miRNAs were identified by 3 distinct databases: miRNet, Starbase, and miRcode2, which yielded 535, 548, and 5921 lncRNAs, respectively. Through a rigorous intersection analysis, 10 lncRNAs were retained. Notably, NEAT1 was hypothesized to influence FOS and IFNG by regulating miRNAs such as hsa-mir-139-5p and hsa-mir-338-3p (Fig. 4A). Through analysis of the Mirnet database, we identified 58 TFs. Notably, CREB1 and STAT3 were predicted as key regulatory TFs potentially modulating FOS and IFNG (Fig. 4B).

Following this, prior to molecular docking, a drug-active ingredient-key gene regulatory network was constructed. The results reveal that quercetin contained in Baihuasheshecao and Huangqi interacted with FOS and IFNG (Fig. 4C). Further molecular docking analysis revealed strong binding affinities of IFNG with quercetin and luteolin, with binding energies of −7.5 kcal/mol and −7.2 kcal/mol, correspondingly, suggesting favorable binding effects (Fig. 4D and E).

4. Discussion

RIF represents a characteristic pathological alteration in the progression of CKD to end-stage. characterized by nephron loss, inflammation, myofibroblast activation, and abnormal extracellular matrix deposition in the renal interstitium.[3,4] Although the pathogenesis of RIF has been unremittingly revealed, no major advances have been made specifically in treatment strategies. TCM has become one of the promising strategies for the significant characteristics of multiple components, multiple targets, and systemic regulation.[32] SFK has been clinically used as a therapeutic agent for kidney diseases for many years, although the precise molecular mechanisms underlying its renoprotective effects remain incompletely elucidated. Therefore, exploring the mechanisms of SFK against RIF is essential to understanding how to best use SFK.

To assess SFK’s therapeutic potential for CKD, we developed a rat model exhibiting RIF. FOS and IFNG, which were biomarkers related to autophagy in the process of SFK treating RIF, were obtained by intersecting final DEGs, ARGs, and SFK target genes. GeneMANIA analysis predicted that JUN, IFNGR1, and IGFBP7 were functionally related to the biomarkers. Subsequent enrichment analysis revealed that valine, leucine, and isoleucine degradation, propanoate metabolism, citrate cycle (TCA cycle), glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism were pathways co-enriched in 2 biomarkers. Moreover, in the regulatory network, CREB1 and STAT3 were found to regulate both FOS and IFNG. Finally, molecular docking showed good docking effects of IFNG with quercetin and luteolin.

The FOS gene undergoes transcription and translation to generate the c-FOS protein. Its expression is regulated by multiple signaling pathways, among which the MAPK/ERK signaling pathway is prominent.[33] Mitogen-activated protein kinases signaling pathway modulates the activity of multiple TFs, including but not limited to TCF (ternary complex factor), MEF2C (myocyte enhancer factor 2C), ATF2 (activating transcription factor 2), and members of the Jun protein family. These activated TFs initiate transcriptional activation of FOS and Jun genes. Specifically, TCF induces c-FOS expression through phosphorylation-mediated activation of extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) from the MAPK family. The Fos (c-Fos, FosB, Fra1, Fra2) and Jun (c-Jun, JunB, JunD) proteins are components of a dimeric transcription factor complex named AP-1 (Activator Protein-1). As a master regulator, the AP-1 transcription factor orchestrates gene networks controlling fundamental cellular processes, notably governing the expression of genes mediating cell cycle progression, differentiation pathways, apoptotic signaling, neovascularization, and cancer metastasis. Moreover, AP-1 is regulated by diverse signals, including growth factors, inflammatory cytokines, mechanical stress, and oxidative stress.[34] In the context of RIF, the activation of the FOS-related pathways may be associated with complex pathological processes, such as myofibroblast activation and extracellular matrix deposition.

The polymorphisms within the IFNG gene are closely associated with a wide range of diseases. It is a key molecule in the research of immunomodulation, inflammatory response, and tumor immunology.[35] IFN-γ, encoded by the IFNG gene, is a pleiotropic cytokine secreted by activated T lymphocytes and natural killer cells.[36] IFN-γ can contribute to anticancer immunity through its immunomodulatory actions, direct cytotoxic effects, and growth-inhibitory effects. It plays a vital role in immunomodulation and anti-infection processes, thus being an essential part of the body’s anti-infection defense mechanisms.[35] In addition, IFNγ has been demonstrated to play a role in mediating both increased blood pressure and renal dysfunction in hypertension.[37,38] In RIF, the involvement of IFNG may be related to the inflammatory and immune-mediated aspects of the disease progression.

When comparing the TOP 10 enriched pathways of FOS and IFNG, we found an overlap in several key metabolic pathways, including valine, leucine, and isoleucine degradation; propanoate metabolism; citrate cycle (TCA cycle); and glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism. This shared enrichment implies a potential synergy or interplay between FOS and IFNG in these biological processes. The dysregulation of BCAA metabolic networks (centered on leucine, isoleucine, and valine) shows a close association with diabetic pathological processes, and this relationship may reflect underlying pathogenic mechanisms. Mechanistic studies have shown that elevated BCAAs can promote fibrosis-associated EMT by enhancing the activation of pro-inflammatory macrophages through the mTOR signaling pathway. Pharmacological enhancement of systemic BCAA catabolism using a small-molecule inhibitor has been found to attenuate type 2 diabetic kidney disease. From a pathogenic perspective, disruption of BCAA homeostasis independently promotes the progression of diabetic kidney disease, suggesting that restoring BCAA metabolic balance through pharmacological or dietary interventions holds significant therapeutic potential.[39]

Moreover, in the regulatory network, CREB1 and STAT3 were found to regulate both FOS and IFNG. Signal transduction and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) is a key transcription factor implicated in the pathogenesis of kidney fibrosis. STAT3 serves as a master transcriptional regulator in renal fibrogenesis. Mechanistically, STAT3 undergoes phosphorylation in various kidney cell types (including fibroblasts, immune cells, and epithelial cells) upon stimulation by growth factors and inflammatory signals. This activation drives fibrotic progression through promoting inflammatory responses and inducing pericyte-to-myofibroblast transdifferentiation. Recent studies further demonstrate that STAT3 activation enhances the migratory capacity of genetically-modified pericyte-like cells and stimulates pro-fibrotic signaling pathways.[40]

The cAMP response element-binding protein (CREB) is a member of basic leucine zipper (bZIP) TFs, which also include c-Myc, c-Fos, and c-Jun.[41] CREB (cAMP response element-binding protein) is involved in regulating multiple disease processes, including neurodegenerative disorders, diabetic complications, and tumorigenesis. This transcription factor not only plays a pivotal role in modulating synaptic plasticity in the central nervous system, but its phosphorylation also serves as a critical regulatory step for glucose metabolism-related gene expression.[42] Experimental evidence demonstrates that CREB dynamically regulates the transcriptional activity of EMT-associated genes in non-lens cells in a phosphorylation-dependent manner.[43] Zhang also demonstrates that CREB maintains its binding capacity to target gene promoters regardless of its phosphorylation status at Ser133 (as p-CREB) or in the non-phosphorylatable S133A mutant form, effectively regulating the expression of key EMT-associated genes including FN1 and SNAIL.[44]

Molecular docking showed that Quercetin has a regulatory effect on IFNG and FOS, and the combination of luteolin and IFNG has a good effect. Quercetin and luteolin a natural flavonoid compounds, with powerful antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antitumor effects.[45]

Animal studies demonstrate that quercetin exerts renoprotective effects through dual mechanisms in the UUO-induced renal fibrosis mouse model: (1) significantly improving renal function parameters, and (2) suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion. Quercetin prevents renal tubular epithelial cell senescence-induced fibrosis by activating SIRT1.[46] The anti-fibrotic effect of quercetin is mediated by blocking M1 macrophage activation via NF-κB/IRF5 signaling suppression.[47] In addition, Experimental data demonstrate that quercetin exhibits significant nephroprotective effects in both in vivo and in vitro models. In MRL/lpr mice (a lupus nephritis model carrying the Fas gene lpr mutation), quercetin treatment markedly improved renal function parameters while reducing the expression of fibrosis- and inflammation-related markers. In vitro studies further revealed that quercetin modulates the IL-33/ST2 signaling pathway, effectively attenuating the abnormal accumulation of fibrotic and inflammatory proteins in IL-33-stimulated HK-2 cells, and significantly suppressing renal tubular epithelial cell pyroptosis.[48] Both experimental models and transcriptome profiling reveal luteolin’s ability to mitigate fibrosis-induced renal anemia via modulation of the SIRT1/FOXO3 axis.[49]

Through modulating β-catenin signaling, luteolin inhibits EMT process and consequently alleviates asthma-associated airway remodeling.[50]

Luteolin effectively reverses the EMT process by regulating cytoskeletal contraction, upregulating the expression of epithelial marker E-cadherin, and downregulating mesenchymal markers including N-cadherin, Snail, and Vimentin.[51]

In summary, through bioinformatics analysis, we identified FOS and IFNG as biomarkers associated with autophagy during the treatment of RIF by SFK. Further exploration of their potential molecular mechanisms using GeneMANIA, GSEA, and regulatory network analysis has provided novel insights into the treatment of RIF. However, it should be noted that the current findings are based on in vitro and in vivo experimental models and have not been clinically validated. Therefore, future research should focus on clinical trials to confirm the effectiveness and safety of the proposed mechanisms and potential therapeutic targets.

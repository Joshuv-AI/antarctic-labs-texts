Clinical Profile and Outcome of Surgical Management of Spinal Tumours in Abuja
Authors: Yusuf Ayodeji Salman, Jabir Kassim Muhammad, Adebiyi Jamiu Ayodele, Teli Mukhtar Isa, Uthman Uthman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:Spinal tumours may originate from the native tissues of the spine, spinal cord, or secondary to metastatic spread. Their neurological manifestations are often similar, irrespective of origin. Surgical intervention may be curative in most benign tumours and palliative in metastatic spine tumours. Improvement in the surgical management of spine tumours has resulted in better patients’ health-related quality of life. There have been only few reports on surgical management of spinal tumours in Nigeria.Objective:The study aimed to retrospectively evaluate the clinical aspects and short-term outcome of surgically managed patients with spine tumours in our institution over 3 years.Materials and Methods:A retrospective review of patients treated surgically for spinal tumours at the Neurosurgery Department of National Hospital Abuja between June 2021 and November 2024.Results:Thirty-four patients with histologically proven spinal tumours who had surgeries were reviewed. There was a slight female preponderance with mean age of 51 years. Back pain and motor deficits were the most common presentation. Metastatic tumours from prostates and the breast were the commonest operated cases. The thoracic spine was most frequently affected (50%), followed by the cervical spine (20.6%). Gross total resection was achieved for all meningioma and schwannoma cases. Most metastatic tumours were debulked to decompress the spinal cord. Half of the patients improved, 30% remained same and 20% deteriorated as at last follow up.Conclusion:Surgical intervention results in significant improvement of pain and neurological deficit in most of the patients with spinal tumours. Metastatic spinal tumours were the most operated cases.

Introduction

Spine tumours comprise tumours affecting the spinal cord and spinal column. These tumours are classified anatomically into intramedullary tumours, intradural extramedullary tumours, and extramedullary tumours.[1] They are either primary or secondary (metastatic) tumours. Primary spinal cord tumours are rare making up about 3% of all primary CNS tumours.[2,3] The most common intramedullary tumours are astrocytoma and ependymomas. Meningiomas and nerve sheath tumours constitute the majority of intradural extramedullary tumours.[1,4] Most spine tumours are, however, extradural tumours, of which majority are metastatic tumours commonly from lungs, breast, prostate tumours, and renal cell carcinoma. Metastatic spine tumours (MST) affect 20–40% of cancer patients and are symptomatic in about 10%–20% of them.[1,5,6] There is an increase in incidence of metastatic tumour due to improved management of primary cancers with introduction of targeted therapies resulting in improved survival for virtually all malignant tumours.[5,6]

Clinical presentation of these tumours depends on their locations and rapidity of their growth progression. Pain and motor deficit are the commonest mode of presentation.[6,7,8,9,10] Few patients are diagnosed during screening for metastasis in patients with known cancers.

Depending on the location and nature of the tumour, surgical interventions are carried out for tumour excision, biopsy for histology, spinal fixation in case of instability, and spinal cord decompression.[11,12,13]

There have been remarkable advances in the surgical treatment of both benign and malignant tumour of the spine. For benign spine tumours, the goal of surgery is to achieve gross total tumour excision as a curative measure, and this is achievable with use of standard microsurgical techniques in majority of cases.[4] Treatment goals for MST are palliative aiming at preservation or restoration of neurological function, improved pain control, and maintenance of spinal stability in a setting of local tumour control.[5] Introduction of spine stereotactic radiosurgery (SSRS), less invasive surgical techniques including separation surgery, percutaneous pedicle screw instrumentation, and cement augmentation among others have improved health-related quality of life and survival of patients with MST. Invariably, these measures tend to render scoring systems such as Tomita score[14] and Tokuhashi[15] revised score of little value in the current era of metastatic spine tumour treatment.[11,16,17]

Management of spinal tumours in Nigeria has recorded some improvement in recent time but like other developing countries, it still lags behind the advancement in developed world. There are few published works on surgical management of spine tumour in Nigeria. The study was designed to evaluate the clinical presentations, surgical interventions, pathological diagnosis, and outcome spinal tumour in our institution.

Subjects and Methods

This study was a retrospective review of all patients managed by the authors for spinal tumours at our institution over 41 months (June 2021–November 2024). The study was carried out at the Neurosurgery Department of National Hospital Abuja, a tertiary health care centre at the capital city of Nigeria as a departmental audit. The patients’ clinical, radiology, surgery, and histology records were retrieved from the hospital electronic medical records and theater operation records with the assistance of the Head of the hospital Medical Record Department. Patients without a histological diagnosis were excluded from the study; hence, a purposive sampling technique was used. The neurological status of patients was assessed pre- and postsurgery using the American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) impairment scale[Figure 1].[18] Data on demographics, clinical presentation, tumour location, type of surgery, postoperative outcome, and histology were recorded in a predesigned study pro forma. The data were entered into IBM Statistical Package in for Social Sciences Version 31 spread sheet and analysed. Univariate analyses were carried out and results were compared with findings of similar results in other part of worlds. The study is a retrospective review; hence, ethical approval was not required by the ethics committee of the National Hospital Abuja [Figure 1.

Results

A total of 180 spine surgeries for various indications were conducted during the study period, out of which 34 patients had histologically confirmed spinal tumours representing 18.9% of all spinal surgeries. There were 16 males and 18 females (male: female ratio 1:1.1). Patients’ ages ranged from 14 to 90 years [Table 1]. All patients presented with pain while 27 (79.4%) patients presented with motor deficits and 15 (44.1%) patients had sensory deficits. Fifteen (44.1%) patients present with ASIA-C, 10 (29.4%) present with ASIA-A [Table 2]. Majority of the patients 23 (67.6%) presents between 6 and 12 months of onsets of symptoms, 4 (11.8%) patients for less than 6 months, and 7 (20.6%) had symptoms for more than 1 year before presentation. The thoracic spine was most frequently affected 17 (50%), followed by the cervical spine 7(20.6%) [Table 3]. Half of the tumours were extradural, while 10 (29.4%) were intradural extramedullary [Figure 2]. Metastatic tumours 10 (29.4%) and multiple myeloma 6 (17.6%) were the most common histological diagnoses [Table 3]. Prostate carcinoma 10 (30%) and breast cancer 7 (20.0) were the most common primary source of metastasis while the primary source was unknown in 7 (20%) patients [Figure 3]. Spinal meningioma 5 (14.7%) and Schwannoma 5 (14.7%) were the most common primary tumours.

Sources of spine metastases

Seventeen patients (50.0%) had laminectomy alone, 10 (29.4%) patients had laminectomy and transpedicular fixation and posterior lateral fusion while 3 (8.8%) patients underwent cervical corpectomy and fusion surgeries. Gross total tumour excision was achieved in 13 (38.2%) patients wish consisted of patients with meningioma and most of the patients with spinal schwannoma [Table 4]. Most of the patients with metastasis had debulking surgery [Table 5]. Half of the patients had neurological improvement postsurgery with increase in ASIA scale of at least one grade above the preoperative grade, while 30% remained unchanged, and 20% deteriorated.

Discussion

This study report on 34 cases of spinal tumour operated over a 3-year period which represented 18.9% of all spine procedures done. The result shows an apparent increase in the average number of spine tumour surgeries than previously reported from other centres in Nigeria. A total of 39 cases and 59 cases, respectively, were reported over a 10-year period in southeastern and southwestern part of Nigeria.[8,9] The percentage of spinal surgeries for tumour (19%) was higher than the 8.9% reported by chikani in southeastern Nigeria.[8] These findings may be due to increased availability of diagnostic imaging (MRI and CT scan) and better awareness by patients and primary doctors leading to improved referral to spine surgeons. The demographic characteristics of our patients are like previous reports. Spinal tumour affects different age groups (mean 51 years) and gender. We found a slightly more female been operated like report by Adeolu in southern Nigeria but different from report from eastern part of the country.[8] Other studies have previously reported different sex predominant. In Taiwan, male predominant was reported in metastatic disease while female dominant was reported for spinal meningioma.[19] These differences in sex predominance may be reflection of pattern primary origin of MSTs,[19] differences in health seeking behaviours different genders of the study population and other reasons that should be a subject of further research.

All our patients present with pain with motor deficit been present in 79% at presentation. Most of these patients present with poor neurologic grade (ASIA A-C in 84% and ASIA-C,44%). This clinical presentation was probably due to late presentation as 80% of the patients present after 6 months of onset of symptoms. Various authors have reported different mode of presentation of patients with spinal tumours. Adeolu et al.,[9] report 58% of the patients presented at least 6 months of symptoms onset with motor deficit of 97% while Arora[7]reported an average duration of symptoms of 16.17 months with predominant motor deficit of 70.27% and poor neurologic status in 71.17%. Neurologic status at presentation has been found to impact significantly on outcome of management.[7]

The thoracic spine (50%) was the most affected segment of the spine followed by cervical (21%). The thoracic spine as the most common segment involved was reported in Ibadan 65%, Enugu 43.6%, Taiwan 51.4%, India 34%, and Brazil 37%.[3,7,8,9,19] This is attributable to the long segment of the thoracic spine and peculiar nature of vascular supply to the spine in the region.

Half of the tumours were extradural, and majority were metastatic adenocarcinoma (29%). The prostate (30%) and the breast (20%) were the most common primary origins of metastases. Meningioma (14.7%) and schwannoma (14.7%) were the commonest primary tumour. These findings were consistent with previous reports from Nigeria and other parts of the world.[1,8,9] In a review from Taiwan, the lung was the commonest primary site whereas Lung metastasis only account for 10% of metastasis in our series.[19] These differences could be attributed to cigarette smoking habit of the study population. The Prostate, breast, lungs, and renal cell carcinoma have been reported as forming the majority of spine metastasis in different studies.[5] The primary origin of metastases was not known in 20% of our patients. This percentage is lower than 31% reported by Chikani et al. in Nigeria, though still within the 0.4%–38% reported in the literature.[20] It is higher than 4.8% reported by Tsai et al.[19] in Taiwan. The decline in proportion of unknown cases reflects improved pathological diagnosis occasioned by advance diagnostic imaging (CT, MRI, and PET scans) and availability of trained personnel. One adolescent (2.9%) of our patient was managed for osteo osteoma (OO) of the cervical spine a rare benign bony tumour. OO is reported to comprise 6% of spinal lesions.[21]

All our surgical interventions were by open method due to lack of facility for minimal access surgery. Half of the patients had laminectomy alone, while 29.4% had laminectomy and pedicle screws instrumentation surgeries. Spinal fixation using spinal process wiring (17.8%) was the main fixation method reported in Ibadan.[9] Gross total tumour resection was achieved in 38.2%, most of these comprise patients with primary benign tumour such as meningioma, schwannoma and OO. Limited and guided intervention was carried out for metastatic. Many patients with metastatic disease had decompression of the spinal cord and tumour debulking. Few of these patients had additional fixation due to instability occasioned because of tumour infiltration and extend of decompression. There has been major advancement in the care of cancer patients. The use of targeted therapies in cancer care and advanced diagnostic imaging such as MRI and PET scan with resultant improvement in survival and quality of life of patients has led to increase incidence of MSD.[5,22] Many of the interventions like SSRS, minimally invasive procedures which are now the main approached to managing metastatic spinal tumours are currently not available in our centre. However, these patients were managed in conjunction with oncologists for external beam radiotherapy and chemotherapy where appropriate following histologic diagnosis. Surgery resulted in neurological improvement in half of our patients while 20% of the patients deteriorated probably due to disease progression. These outcome finding is in tandem with other reports in Nigeria and other parts of the world.[3,7,8,9,23]

Conclusion

Pain control and restoration of neurological deficits were the main reasons for surgical intervention in our patients. Majority of the patients had metastatic spine disease requiring palliative interventions. Gross total resection was achieved in 38% of cases. All surgeries were by open posterior approach with more than half of the patients having laminectomies with or without instrumented fusion. Surgery resulted in significant improvement in outcome. There is need to improve therapeutic intervention especially for patient with MST with introduction of SSRS, MISS, and separation surgery as currently practised in many advanced centres.

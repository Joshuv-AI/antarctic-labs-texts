Evaluating predictive scheme for thermomechanical properties of Si-diamond composites
Authors: Feng-Yuan Zhao, Jun Jiang, Shu-Xin Bai, Qiang Chen, Yi-Cong Ye
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractA novel, parameter-independent multiscale correlational constitutive model has been devised to predict thermomechanical properties of Si-diamond-SiC and Si-diamond composites, including the effective elastic modulus, effective bulk modulus, effective shear modulus, effective Poisson’s ratio, average coefficients of thermal expansion as well as thermal conductivity. Based on this model, the effective thermomechanical response of two kinds of composites was simulated, and the underlying mechanisms of thermomechanical coupling between constituents were also critically evaluated. The findings were shown that the effective elastic properties of composites, including effective elastic modulus, effective bulk modulus, effective shear modulus, increased with diamond and SiC, and that the introduction of dispersed diamond with highly thermal conductivity and lowly thermal expansion significantly enhanced thermophysical properties of Si-diamond-SiC composites. The thermomechanical coupling of these composites was influenced by the effective elastic properties of composites and the disparity in the intrinsic properties of constituents.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73228-8.Subject terms: Materials science, Theory and computation, Engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

With the development of power devises towards lightweight, integration and higher power density, the critical demands for power electronic equipment have been higher junction temperature and voltage, lower dynamic and static losses, as well as higher frequency and current density1. Diamond reinforced metal matrix composites (DRMMCs), such as Al-diamond2–4, Cu-diamond5,6, Ag-diamond7–9, are being widely used in advanced electronic packaging materials due to their excellent comprehensive performance10.

In contrast to Al-SiC composites11, DRMMCs exhibit higher thermal conductivity (TC) because of more highly thermal conductive diamond and relatively denser metal matrix. However, the mismatch of coefficients of thermal expansion (CTE) between the matrix and diamond, intricate pretreatment processes made their limitation in further development12. Compared with DRMMCs, silicon-based matrix composites reinforced with diamond showcased lower CTE and better mechanical properties13–15. Nevertheless, the disparity in mechanical properties between diamond and silicon-based matrix results in the degradation of strength, which might predispose composites to plastic deformation when subjected to thermal loading16. As a result, numerous research has been focused on investigating the thermophysical characteristics of DRMMCs, but little attention was paid to effective thermophysical response of composites, particularly the adverse reactions arising from properties mismatch between constituents, which was precisely worth concerning in engineering applications17.

Drawing on the assumption of individual inclusions, several predictive methods have been successfully applied to estimating thermomechanical properties18–20. Comparing the difference in predicting the TC of Ag-diamond composites between differential effective medium (DEM)21 and Maxwell mean-field (MMF) scheme22, Tavangar et al.8 have shown that the predicted values by DEM were more confident than that of MMF because MMF became strongly correlated with the volume fractions of reinforcements, which was also demonstrated by Al-diamond composites3. Unfortunately, neither the DEM nor the MMF models were capable to reveal the influence of silicon on the thermal conductivity of the composites. Considering the shear effects on thermal behavior, the Kerner model23 and Rose-Hashin bounds model (R-H model)24 have been used to predict CTE of Si-diamond-SiC composites12. However, the properties mismatch between the constituents was not demonstrated. Different from the Kerner and R-H model, the Tuner model25 that ignored the effect of elastic behavior on the thermal response of composites was always reported to simulate the CTE. As mention above, the influence of the effective elastic properties on the thermal response have not been systematically considered, which pointed out the significance of thermomechanical coupling in DRMMCs. Moreover, the multiscale correlation between the intrinsic properties of constituents and effective properties of composites gives rise to alterations in the distribution of stress, strain, and temperature gradients under thermal cycling loading19,26,27.

In this paper, a developed multiscale correlational constitutive model is established to predict the thermomechanical properties of Si-diamond-SiC and Si-diamond composites. Following the framework of this model, the thermomechanical response of composites is further simulated to reveal the mechanism of thermomechanical coupling. The factors that affect thermomechanical properties of composites, such as volume fractions, properties mismatch, interactions between the constituents and so on, are also thoroughly discussed in this study.

Effective heat transport

The heat transport ability of composites depends on the efficiency of thermal diffusion of phonons as carriers. However, phonon transport is multiscale and complex. Furthermore, phonon scattering exited in interaction between phonon and different structures, such as point defects, dislocations, stacking faults, twin boundaries, virous pores and so on39,40, which led to the degradation of thermal conductivity of composites.

As mention above, thermomechanical mismatch arose from difference in intrinsic properties of constituents. Results and discussion

Si-diamond-SiC composites with various volume fractions of diamond particles were prepared by spark plasma sintering method (SPS), the details of which could be found in reference12. From the XRD patterns and microstructures of sintered Si-diamond-SiC composites12, silicon carbide was originated from the chemical reaction between the silicon matrix and diamond particles, and existed in continuous silicon matrix and diamond particles, which demonstrated the ideal interfacial bonding between the Si-SiC effective matrix and dispersed diamond particles. In order to quantify the effect of silicon carbide on the thermophysical properties of Si-diamond composites, the thermomechanical properties of Si-diamond-SiC and Si-diamond composites were investigated by this developed model, which would be significant for materials design and performance optimization of advanced electronic packaging materials.

For the isotropic material, the characteristic of thermal expansion behavior is to generate uniformly thermal deformation along all directions, resulting in almost identical parameters of CTE (α11 = α22 = α33). However, the thermal expansion behavior of transversely isotropic materials was in-plane isotropy, because of two main effective parameters of CTE (α11 = α22 ≠ α33). Different from the isotropic and transversely isotropic materials, the thermal expansion behavior of anisotropic materials in each direction is completely different, which means independent parameters of CTE (α11 ≠ α22 ≠ α33).

As shown in Fig. 5(a), the predicted average CTE of Si-SiC from 25 K to 375 K with various volume fractions of SiC were almost from 2.60 × 10− 6/K to 3.73 × 10− 6/K, which demonstrated the significant enhancement of SiC on the thermal response of Si-SiC effective matrix. Because the intrinsic CTE of SiC was larger than that of Si shown in Table 2, the average CTE of Si-SiC increased with SiC. In addition, the increased EECs of Si-SiC as shown in Fig. 5(b) resulted in the increasement of strain response induced by elastic mismatch between the Si and SiC, which revealed the influence of elastic properties on the thermal properties. According to the Kerner model23 and R-H model24, smaller elastic mismatch between the constituents made average CTE of composites lower. Figure 6 depicted the influence of SiC and diamond on the average CTE of Si-diamond-SiC composites. Furthermore, the average CTE of Si-diamond-SiC composites determined by this model was compared with experimental values and other classical models shown in Fig. 6.

Coefficient of thermal expansion of Si-diamond-SiC and Si-diamond composites. (a) comparation of CTE between Si-diamond and Si-diamond-SiC composites; (b) different model prediction of CTE of Si-diamond-SiC composites.

As shown in Fig. 6(a), the predicted CTE of Si-diamond-SiC composites were almost identical to the experimental values that were determined by a dilatometer (DIL 403PC, NETZSCH, Germany) from 25 K to 375 K at a rate of 5 K/min in an argon atmosphere, which demonstrated the accuracy of this model. On the one hand, the theoretical CTE for Si-diamond-SiC composites were approximately identical to that of Si-diamond composites with high volume fractions of diamond (50~60%), because of the little effect of SiC on the Si-diamond-SiC composites. On the other hand, the introduction of diamond was beneficial to reduce the CTE of Si-diamond-SiC and Si-diamond composites, which could be inferred from the intrinsic thermal properties of constituents shown in Table 2. On the contrary, the presence of SiC made the CTE of composites increasement, considering the larger CTE of Si-SiC effective CTE shown in Fig. 5(a). In addition, the larger discrepancy in intrinsic elastic properties between the constituents would reduce the sensitivity of composites to thermal deformation, in the case of Si-diamond-SiC composites. As a result, it is significant of Si-diamond-SiC composites to balance the generation of SiC and introduction of diamond for improving the thermomechanical properties.

Figure 6(b) illustrated the quantitative comparison of theoretical CTE for Si-diamond-SiC composites with various volume fractions of diamond. Taking shear effects of constituents into account, Rose-Hashin bounds model24 (R-H model) and Kerner model23 were used widely to simulate the CTE of composites. However, the theoretical CTE of Si-diamond-SiC composites predicted by both R-H model and Kerner model were all larger than experiments, for that both Rose-Hashin Bounds model24 and Kerner model23 did not consider the influence of SiC on the effective thermal response of Si-diamond composites. As a result, limitations of R-H model and Kerner model in simulating the effective thermal expansion of composites with various volume fractions of dispersed particle reinforcements arise from sufficient description of constituents. Du pointed out that thermal response of composites under thermomechanical loading was related to the elastic behavior of constituents37. Therefore, the predicted CTEs by Du model were very close to experimental values, compared with R-H model and Kerner model. However, lower simulated values of CTE relative to experiments were observed in Si-diamond-SiC composites with 30 vol% and 40 vol% of diamond, indicating that only considering the elastic mismatch of constituents resulted in certain deviations from the complex thermomechanical coupling in composites.

In general, theoretical CTE determined by this developed model closely aligned with the experimental values, which not only indicated the reliability of application to predict the thermal response of Si-diamond-SiC composites, but also revealed the mechanism of thermomechanical coupling in composites. On the one hand, the EECs of composites enhanced by the increasement of SiC and diamond, made smaller elastic mismatch between constituents, which reduced the impact on thermal response of Si-diamond-SiC composites. On the other hand, the intrinsic smaller CTE of diamond and smaller thermal mismatch between constituents, resulted in decrease in effective CTE of composites as diamond increasement, which indicated that thermomechanical properties of composites were dependent on the intrinsic properties and thermomechanical mismatch between constituents. Thermomechanical coupling in composites was a consequence of the combined effect of the effective elastic properties and thermal response of composites.

It was obvious that, the interfacial thermal resistance Rk between the dispersed diamond and Si-SiC effective matrix, made the ability of interfacial heat transport between the constituents degradation, which gradually deteriorated with the diamond. On the one hand, as shown in Fig. 4(a) and Fig. 7, the effective bulk modulus of Si-SiC effective matrix decreased with SiC, resulting in the increasement of thermal resistance between Si-SiC and diamond, which could be referred from Eqs. (13) and (14). On the other hand, the mismatch of TC shown in Table 3 between Si-SiC and diamond gradually increased with the diamond, leading to increasement in interfacial thermal resistance of Si-diamond-SiC composites. Because of interfacial thermal resistance in Si-diamond-SiC composites, the effective TC of dispersed diamond shown in Fig. 8(a), was a little lower than the theoretical TC shown in Table 3, which would result in the enhancement of diamond in the heat transport capacity of Si-diamond-SiC composites. Moreover, the comparation between the predicted thermal conductivities of composites by this model and the experimental values measured by a laser flash apparatus (TC-7000, SINKURIKO.INC., Japan) at 50 ℃ was shown in Fig. 8(b).

As expected, the estimated theoretical TC of Si-diamond-SiC composites were almost identical to that of experiments12, which indicated the feasibility of multiscale correlation constitutive model for thermomechanical coupling. As shown in Fig. 8(b), the predicted TC of composites increased with diamond, indicating the enhancement of diamond on the heat transport. Although the effective TC of Si-SiC matrix decreased with diamond shown in Fig. 8(a), the TC of Si-diamond-SiC composites still maintain an increase, because the effective TC of diamond were larger than the Si-SiC effective matrix. On the other hand, the interfacial thermal resistance Rk of composites increased from 1.68 (m2 K GW− 1) to 2.16 (m2 K GW− 1) induced by the mismatch of thermomechanical properties between Si-SiC effective matrix and diamond reinforcement, but the huge difference in quantity between the increased thermal resistance and effective TC of diamond could not lead to the degradation of heat transport of Si-diamond-SiC composites. In addition, the increased effective bulk modulus of Si-diamond-SiC composites shown in Fig. 4(b), made the propagation speed of phonons increasement inferred from the Eqs. (13) and (14), which enhanced the thermal transport capacity of composites.

All in all, thermomechanical properties of Si-diamond-SiC composites could be accurately evaluated by this novel model, the most significant difference in which was that the prediction of thermal conductivity of composites determined by traditional model42,50 were made by simplifying the thermomechanical coupling in the composites.

Conclusion

A novel multiscale constitutive model was established to directly reveal the thermomechanical effect between the intrinsic properties of constituents and thermomechanical properties of composites on multiscale elastic response, correlative thermal expansion and effective heat transport of composites, which has been successfully applied to predict the comprehensive properties of Si-diamond-SiC composites. On the one hand, the effective elastic properties of Si-diamond-SiC composites were enhanced by the diamond and SiC. On the other hand, the decreased thermomechanical mismatch between the effective elastic properties and intrinsic thermal expansion of constituents resulted in improvement of effective thermal response of composites. Moreover, the introduction of highly thermal conductive diamond made enhancement in thermal transport of Si-diamond-SiC composites, although interfacial thermal resistance reduced effective thermal conductivities of diamond reinforcement and Si-SiC effective matrix. Last but not least, thermomechanical coupling in Si-diamond-SiC composites was combined by the multiscale correlation between elastic response and thermal response of composites.

Entropy optimized magneto-thermo-diffusive transport in a chemically reactive unsteady third grade nanofluid flow over a stretching surface using a Cattaneo–Christov framework with artificial neural network modeling
Authors: Mariadoss Moyes, Subramanyam Reddy Anala
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe purpose of this study is to investigates the complex magneto-thermo-diffusive transport and entropy generation characteristics in unsteady third-grade nanofluid flow over a stretching surface, driven by the need for accurate prediction of mixed convective heat–mass transfer mechanisms in advanced thermal systems. This work aims to quantify how magnetohydrodynamic behavior under the simultaneous influences of Cattaneo–Christov heat and mass diffusion, viscous dissipation, thermal radiation, nonuniform heat generation/absorption, Brownian motion, thermophoresis, and activation energy influences thermal performance within the Buongiorno nanofluid framework. The governing nonlinear partial differential equations are transformed into non-dimensional ordinary forms and solved numerically via a fourth-order Runge–Kutta method integrated with the shooting algorithm. To enhance predictive fidelity, the resulting datasets are trained through advanced ANN algorithms, and model accuracy is verified using regression metrics, error histograms, and mean square error analysis. The obtained graphical results evaluations reveal that intensified magnetic field intensity and unsteadiness significantly suppress fluid velocity, whereas thermal radiation, Eckert number, Brownian motion, and thermophoretic diffusion substantially enhance heat transport. Entropy generation increases significantly with higher Brinkman and radiation parameters. Quantitatively, increasing radiation parameter from 0.2 to 1.0 elevates the Nusselt number by 14.28%, while higher activation energy parameter from 0.5 to 2.0 suppresses the thermal transport rate by 12.63% and amplifying the mass transfer rate by 29.9%. The ANN framework achieves superior predictive accuracy and computational stability, enabling a robust platform for optimizing thermal systems and supporting advanced biomedical diagnostics such as early stage cancer detection and tumor classification.Keywords: Entropy generation, Third-grade nanofluid, Cattaneo–Christov double diffusion, Activation energy, Artificial neural network

Introduction

Over recent decades, nanofluids have emerged as next-generation functional materials, revolutionizing thermal science, renewable energy systems, and biomedical technologies through enhanced heat transfer and energy conversion capabilities. The study of non-Newtonian fluid (NNF) boundary layer flow and energy transport has attracted significant attention due to its relevance in complex engineering and biophysical systems. The present work is based on the Buongiorno nanofluid framework, which characterizes nanoparticle transport via Brownian motion and thermophoresis without specifying a particular nanoparticle type. Such nanofluid systems exhibit superior thermal conductivity and improved heat transfer efficiency compared to conventional fluids, marking a significant advancement in modern nanotechnology, since continuing the legacy first envisioned by Choi and Eastman [1]. These nanofluid suspensions have found extensive applications in solar energy collection, fuel cells, polymer processing, microelectronic cooling, aerospace engineering, and biomedicine. In particular, third-grade nanofluids demonstrate exceptional potential for optimizing heat and mass transport owing to their non-Newtonian rheology and tunable thermophysical characteristics. The third-grade fluid model exhibit unique viscoelastic properties, including normal stress effects and non-Newtonian behaviors such as shear thinning and thickening, with its thermodynamic characteristics extensively examined by Fosdick and Rajagopal [2]. By increasing thermal conductivity and radiative absorption capabilities, nanofluids have revolutionized energy storage, solar thermal conversion, and targeted drug delivery. Based on this investigation, the present study considers a third-grade fluid exhibiting temperature and shear rate dependent viscosity, governed by a differential type formulation further developed and characterized by Rajagopal et al. [3]. Asiri et al. [4] explored heat and mass transfer in a magnetized, radiative third-grade fluid over an inclined stretching sheet. They revealing that magnetic and permeability effects suppress velocity, while radiation, buoyancy, and heat sources significantly enhance temperature and energy transport efficiency. Khan et al. [5] investigated gravity-driven reactive viscoelastic third-grade fluid flow over an inclined porous plane, revealing that viscosity variation, porous resistance, and exothermic reactions strongly govern thermal runaway and flow dynamics under non-isothermal conditions. Das et al. [6] presented a novel analysis of solute dispersion in hydromagnetic third-grade fluid flow through a porous channel. They revealing that Darcy and Hartmann numbers, magnetic inclination, and reaction parameters strongly influence velocity, concentration, and multidimensional transport characteristics.

The magnetic field modifies fluid motion by inducing electromagnetic forces that resist flow and redistribute thermal energy within the system. In third-grade nanofluids, these magneto–thermal interactions establish a strong coupling between momentum diffusion and heat transfer, enabling precise regulation of boundary-layer dynamics. The incorporation of the Cattaneo-Christov heat and mass flux model significantly enhances the physical realism of the analysis by accounting for finite thermal and concentration relaxation times, thereby overcoming the limitations of classical Fourier and Fick laws. This advanced framework is particularly vital in high-speed thermal processes, microscale heat transport, targeted drug delivery, and bio-thermal systems, where non-equilibrium effects dominate. Heat transfer forms the foundation of industrial and biomedical innovation, where temperature gradients govern energy exchange and overall system performance. By enhancing thermal diffusivity and enabling precise energy regulation, radiative transfer bridges the gap between advanced thermal management and life-saving medical technologies. Thermal radiation thus emerges as a vital mechanism in high-temperature processes and biomedical applications such as thermal imaging, laser surgery, and solar panel. Entropy generation, rooted in the second law of thermodynamics, embodies the physical measure of energy degradation and irreversibility in thermal fluid systems, driven by heat transfer resistance, viscous dissipation, and joule heating. Minimizing entropy enhances the thermodynamic efficiency of engineering systems, while in biomedical contexts particularly cancer hyperthermia and thermal ablation-controlled entropy generation governs precise thermal energy deposition within malignant tissues. Through nanoparticle-assisted heating, the modulation of entropy enables selective tumor destruction with minimal collateral damage. The Bejan number (Be), first proposed by Bejan [25], quantifies the proportion of thermal entropy generation relative to the total entropy produced within a system, provides a quantitative framework to evaluate the balance between thermal irreversibility and fluid friction, thus bridging the physics of energy optimization with the precision of biomedical thermal therapy. Loganathan et al. [26] explored entropy generation in third-grade nanofluid flow with Cattaneo–Christov heat flux under inclined magnetic fields, incorporating radiation and convective heating effects. The study reported that entropy rises with radiative, magnetic, and Brinkman parameters but diminishes for higher thermal relaxation times, emphasizing the interplay between heat transport and thermodynamic irreversibility. Olkha et al. [27] investigated entropy generation in time-dependent Williamson fluid flow over an exponentially stretching porous sheet, considering multiple thermal and chemical effects. Kumar et al. Employing nanoparticles in Therminol-VPI fluid, the study used similarity transformations and the Cash Carp scheme for numerical simulation. Results reveal the interdependence of curvature, magnetic, and reaction parameters on heat, mass, and entropy transport phenomena. Rahman et al. [29] examined the entropy generation characteristics of mixed convection Sutterby nanofluid flow past a porous stretching surface, accounting for Lorentz force, viscous dissipation, and radiative heat transfer. Numerical simulations via Mathematica demonstrated that entropy generation intensifies with radiation and diffusion parameters, while velocity diminishes with increased magnetic field strength. Rehman and Khan [30] had been reported in entropy generation in third-grade peristaltic flow using an integrated computational machine learning framework. Their results revealed that entropy generation decreases with rising Hartmann number but increases with Brinkman and Grashof parameters, demonstrating the framework’s capability for accurate prediction and optimization of thermofluid systems. Recently Nandini et al. [31] investigated entropy generation in hybrid nanofluid flow between rotating porous plates under combined magnetic, radiative, and heat source effects via the Darcy-Forchheimer framework. Numerical simulations revealed that radiation and Eckert parameters elevate entropy production, whereas magnetic fields reduce fluid velocity, supporting nanofluid applications in advanced thermal energy systems. Ullah et al. [32] concentrated oscillatory heat and mass transfer in Powell-Eyring nanofluid flow over a vertical cone, revealing that enhanced thermal radiation, Brownian motion, and thermophoresis significantly amplify entropy generation and oscillatory heat-mass transport under mixed convection effects.

Artificial Neural Networks (ANNs), inspired by the architecture of biological neurons, have emerged as powerful computational tools for modeling highly nonlinear thermo-fluid transport phenomena. In the context of heat and mass transfer in third-grade nanofluid flow over stretching surfaces, ANN models trained using algorithms such as LMA, BRS, and SCG effectively capture the coupled temperature and concentration fields that are otherwise difficult to resolve through conventional analytical approaches. Novelty and objective of current research

The present study aims to enhance the understanding and prediction of heat transfer performance in unsteady laminar mixed convective flow of third-grade nanofluid over a stretching surface using advanced artificial neural network (ANN) techniques. The novelty of this work lies in the comprehensive integration of Cattaneo–Christov heat and mass fluxes with Joule heating, viscous dissipation, thermal radiation, nonuniform heat source/sink, Brownian motion, thermophoresis, activation energy, and a second-order chemical reaction, coupled with ANN-based prediction, which has not been previously reported in the literature. The governing nonlinear partial differential equations are transformed into a dimensionless system of ordinary differential equations via similarity transformations and solved numerically using the MATLAB-based bvp4c solver. Furthermore, ANN algorithms such as LMS, BRS, and SCG are employed to train optimized datasets, enabling accurate prediction of velocity, temperature, and concentration profiles, along with entropy generation arising from thermal, viscous, and electromagnetic irreversibilities. The ANN predictions are validated against numerical solutions through comprehensive 2D/3D analyses, demonstrating excellent agreement and convergence behavior. The developed framework exhibits high predictive accuracy and computational stability, providing an efficient and reliable platform for optimizing heat transfer performance in complex thermo-fluid systems, and enabling advanced biomedical applications, including thermal system optimization, targeted drug delivery, tumor classification, and early-stage cancer diagnostics. This approach guarantees consistent convergence and preserves the overall accuracy of the results. Artificial neural network

Artificial neural networks (ANNs) have emerged as powerful predictive tools in fluid dynamics, capable of capturing intricate nonlinear interactions governing heat and mass transfer. In this study, a feed-forward backpropagation ANN architecture is developed to predict the Nusselt and Sherwood numbers using numerically generated datasets, partitioned into training, validation, and testing subsets for evaluation through LMS, BRS, and SCG algorithms. The architecture of the two ANN models, illustrated in Fig. 3, is trained using input-target datasets where network weights are iteratively optimized through error minimization. The ANN model employs a feedforward architecture with a single hidden layer, selected based on its capability to accurately approximate nonlinear thermo-fluid behavior with efficient convergence. The number of neurons in the hidden layer is optimized through trial-based performance evaluation to achieve minimal mean squared error (MSE) and optimal convergence without overfitting. The inputs consist of key dimensionless parameters , while the outputs correspond to heat, and mass transfer quantities. The training data are generated using the bvp4c solver under a supervised learning framework. Model performance is rigorously validated and tested using LMS, BRS, and SCG algorithms, with evaluation metrics including MSE, coefficient of determination, performance curves, error histograms, and regression analyses. The Levenberg-Marquardt algorithm, blending gradient descent and Gauss–Newton methods, is employed for rapid and precise neural network training in nonlinear least-squares optimization. The Bayesian Regularization algorithm enhances ANN generalization by embedding regularization within training, ensuring robust performance for complex or limited datasets without requiring a validation set.

Results and discussion

This section presents a comprehensive analysis of the numerical results obtained from simulations of mixed convective, time-dependent magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flow of a third-grade nanofluid over a stretching sheet, formulated within the framework of the Buongiorno nanofluid model. To gain deeper physical insight into the flow mechanisms, the governing equations were solved numerically using the fourth-order Runge–Kutta method. The analysis investigates the effects of essential dimensionless parameters on the velocity, temperature, and concentration fields of the nanofluid. Entropy generation and Bejan number

Entropy generation quantifies the degree of irreversibility in a second law of thermodynamics system arising from heat transfer, fluid friction, and mass diffusion. It measures the portion of energy that becomes unavailable for useful work zero only in an ideal reversible system hence minimizing entropy generation is crucial for improving thermodynamic efficiency. The Bejan number (Be), defined as the ratio of heat transfer irreversibility to total irreversibility, identifies the dominant source of entropy production. When (Be) is one, the heat transfer irreversibility prevails, while (Be) is zero, it’s indicates dominance of viscous or frictional effects. In terms of physics, portrays the overall energy degradation within the boundary layer, whereas (Be) distinguishes the contribution of thermal irreversibility, serving as a key metric for evaluating the energy utilization and thermodynamic performance of nanofluid systems. The effect of the Radiation parameter (Rd), Brinkman number (Br), Hartmann number (M), diffusion parameter (L), Biot number (Bi), Brownian diffusion parameter , thermophoresis diffusion parameter on the transport phenomena are depicted in Figs. 7 and 8. The parameter Rd represents the ratio of radiative to conductive heat transfer, where higher values enhance thermal energy transport through radiation. This intensifies temperature gradients within the boundary layer, resulting in greater entropy production. Consequently, Be also rises, indicating that heat transfer irreversibility dominates over viscous effects. In a physical sense, an increase in Rd enhances photon mediated energy exchange, thereby promoting thermal irreversibility and energy degradation in the nanofluid system.

Conclusions

This study investigated the unsteady MHD behavior of a third-grade nanofluid over a stretching sheet, focusing on entropy generation and coupled thermo-diffusive transport under the Buongiorno model. The formulation incorporates a spectrum of coupled transport mechanisms including linear thermal radiation, viscous dissipation, Cattaneo-Christov double diffusion, Joule heating, Brownian motion, and thermophoresis to capture the fluid’s complex rheology and multiscale thermal behavior. The governing PDE system is transformed into a set of ODEs through appropriate similarity conversions and solved using the fourth-order Runge–Kutta method with a shooting strategy to generate high-fidelity datasets. These datasets are partitioned into training, validation, and testing groups for LMS, BRS, and SCG algorithms, with model proficiency confirmed through regression accuracy and error-histogram evaluation. These insights provide a strong foundation for biomedical applications such as nano-therapeutics, targeted drug delivery, cardiovascular flow modeling, and advanced medical simulation technologies. Limitations and future scope

The current investigation is restricted to a two-dimensional, incompressible, laminar, and unsteady flow configuration, which may limit its applicability to more complex turbulent and fully transient regimes. Additionally, effects such as nanoparticle aggregation and multi-phase interactions are not considered in the present formulation. Future research will focus on extending the model to turbulent and fully transient flow regimes, incorporating nanoparticle clustering effects, and analyzing more complex geometrical configurations. Moreover, advanced hybrid computational approaches integrating machine learning techniques, physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), response surface methodology (RSM), and computational fluid dynamics (CFD) will be explored to enhance predictive accuracy, optimization capability, and real-time control in industrial thermal systems.

Artificial intelligence-powered evaluation model for English translation education in university: combining quantitative and qualitative methods
Authors: Xunlian Quan, Yinan Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Assessing students’ learning outcomes and abilities has always been a key link for English translation education in universities. However, traditional evaluation methods often face problems such as strong subjectivity and long time consumption, and it is difficult to meet the needs of large-scale classroom environments. To address these issues, this paper proposes and verifies a translation teaching quality evaluation model based on artificial intelligence (AI), aiming to improve the objectivity, consistency and efficiency of the evaluation. A mixed-methods design with data triangulation is employed to analyze data through a combination of qualitative and quantitative approaches. The quantitative part collects assessment feedback data from 796 English-related majors through questionnaire surveys, and the qualitative part uses focus group discussions to deeply analyze students’ acceptance and trust in the AI evaluation system and the impact of AI evaluation on the development of translation ability. The results show that the AI evaluation model contributes to improving the consistency of evaluation results and the pertinence of feedback, and students have a high overall trust in AI evaluation. Nevertheless, some students questioned the limitations of AI in the cultural context and creativity evaluation. Based on the research findings, this paper further proposes practical suggestions for combining AI evaluation with traditional teacher evaluation in translation education, emphasizing the synergy between teachers and AI technology to achieve comprehensive optimization of translation teaching evaluation.

Introduction

University English translation teaching has long relied on the assessment of students’ learning outcomes and abilities as a crucial indicator of teaching quality1. In this context, it primarily refers to translation-related courses offered within English or translation programs at the tertiary level, where assessment plays a central role in monitoring students’ bilingual transfer competence, linguistic accuracy, and cultural mediation skills in an English learning environment. Although specific instructional practices may vary across regions and educational systems, these core goals are widely shared in translation teaching worldwide2–4. Assessment not only helps teachers understand students’ learning progress and translation ability, but also provides feedback to students and promotes their academic growth5–7. Traditional assessment methods, such as manual scoring, standardized tests and final exams, are widely used in translation teaching8. However, these assessment methods usually face problems such as strong subjectivity, long assessment cycles and untimely feedback. Especially in large-scale classroom environments, the shortcomings of traditional assessment methods are particularly prominent9. For example, as a common assessment method, the final exam can reflect the overall translation ability of students, but due to the limitation of marking time and standardized scoring, it often cannot fully reflect the details, innovation and cultural adaptation involved in the translation process of students10. In addition, it is difficult for students to obtain specific feedback in time after the exam, which makes it difficult for them to effectively reflect on themselves and improve11.

In recent years, the application of artificial intelligence (AI) technology in the field of education has provided new possibilities for solving these problems in traditional assessment methods12–14. AI evaluation systems use technologies such as natural language processing (NLP) and machine learning to efficiently and automatically evaluate students’ translations, reduce human bias, and improve the consistency and reliability of evaluations15. Specifically, AI is integrated into software such as translation evaluation and human-computer dialogue. These software use deep learning models to score students’ translation quality and provide students with specific feedback, thus making up for the shortcomings of traditional evaluation methods in terms of evaluation accuracy, efficiency, and personalized feedback. For example, AI can automatically select vocabulary, analyze grammatical structures, and score students’ translation fluency to ensure the consistency and fairness of evaluation results and avoid the bias of manual scoring16. In addition, the advantages of AI technology in large-scale classrooms are particularly prominent, which can help teachers save time and energy on evaluation, allowing teachers to devote more energy to teaching design, classroom interaction, and personalized tutoring. Therefore, AI not only improves evaluation efficiency, but also enables teachers to provide feedback to more students in a shorter time17.

Although AI has shown great potential in translation education assessment, existing research has also revealed some limitations in its practical application. Most studies focus on the automated evaluation and scoring functions of AI systems, and lack in-depth exploration of the combination of AI and traditional teacher evaluation methods18. AI technology in translation education is mainly applied to the technical assessment of translation quality, including grammatical errors and vocabulary choices. However, its capacity to evaluate aspects such as cultural background, linguistic creativity, and contextual adaptation is still constrained19. As an activity, translation is highly dependent on cultural and contextual judgment, which makes it difficult for AI systems to fully understand and evaluate its subtle differences. This is especially true in terms of creativity and flexibility, where the performance of AI cannot match that of experienced teachers. What’s more, the feedback from AI evaluation systems usually lacks personalization and emotional interaction, which may lead to low student acceptance of evaluation results, thereby affecting the effectiveness and influence of evaluation feedback20. Although AI can provide feedback, it lacks the emotional interaction and guidance of teachers in the classroom, making students’ feedback experience relatively indifferent and monotonous. Most existing studies focus on exploring how AI works independently, and few studies discuss how to effectively combine AI with teachers’ traditional evaluation methods to make up for their respective shortcomings and play a synergistic role21.

From a broader higher education pedagogy perspective, this lack of integration between AI-based systems and teacher-led assessment can be theoretically addressed through the framework of integrated blended and hybrid instruction. Blended and hybrid instructional models emphasize the deliberate integration of digital technologies with face-to-face teaching to enhance learning effectiveness, flexibility, and student engagement in university classrooms22,23. Recent conceptualizations further distinguish integrated hybrid models from technology-enhanced instruction by highlighting the role of digital tools as embedded components of core instructional processes rather than as peripheral or substitutive add-ons24. Within this framework, technology is expected to support pedagogical functions such as formative assessment, feedback provision, and learning regulation, while instructors retain responsibility for higher-order judgment, scaffolding, and contextual interpretation25,26. In translation teaching contexts, AI-assisted evaluation can thus be understood as a form of integrated technology use that complements teacher assessment by providing standardized, timely, and data-driven feedback on foundational linguistic performance, including accuracy, fluency, and lexical choice. At the same time, translation remains a cognitively and culturally situated activity, requiring teachers’ professional expertise to evaluate cultural appropriateness, communicative intent, and creative strategies that current AI systems cannot fully capture3,4. Situating the proposed AI–teacher evaluation model within the theory of integrated blended and hybrid instruction therefore clarifies its pedagogical rationale and underscores that the present study examines technology integration in higher education classrooms as a collaborative and complementary process rather than a replacement of human instruction.

In order to solve the above problems, we propose and test a hybrid evaluation model that combines AI evaluation with traditional teacher assessment to enhance the translation education evaluation process through their combined strengths. A mixed-methods design using data triangulation is adopted to integrate both qualitative and quantitative techniques. In-depth focus group discussions provide insights into students’ levels of acceptance and trust in the AI evaluation system, as well as its influence on their translation ability. The quantitative analysis further examines the effects of the AI evaluation on students’ skill development. Through the organic combination of AI and teacher evaluation, this paper proposes and validates a more comprehensive, personalized and efficient evaluation solution for translation teaching, further promotes the improvement of students’ translation ability, and provides theoretical support and practical reference for the reform and development of translation education evaluation in the future.

Literature review

Traditional translation evaluation methods

In translation teaching, traditional evaluation methods have always been an important means to measure students’ translation ability27. Final exams, class assignments and manual scoring by teachers are widely used in the evaluation of translation teaching28. These traditional evaluation methods help to detect students’ mastery of written translation and basic skills. However, with the continuous development of translation teaching, the efficiency and effectiveness of traditional evaluation methods have been challenged29. Manual grading often relies on the subjective judgment of teachers, and the grading results are easily affected by teachers’ personal preferences and differences in grading standards. In addition, traditional evaluation forms usually focus on testing students’ basic knowledge and skills, while ignoring factors such as creativity, contextual adaptability and cultural differences in the translation process30.

From CALL to AWE: foundations in automated language assessment

The integration of technology into language assessment is not a recent phenomenon. Long before modern AI, the fields of computer-assisted language learning (CALL)31 and computer-based evaluation (CBE)32 laid the groundwork for automated assessment. Early CALL systems were primarily behaviouristic, using “drill-and-practice” formats that provided immediate feedback on grammar and vocabulary exercises. A more direct precursor is the field of automated writing evaluation (AWE)33. Pioneered by systems like project essay grade (PEG) in the 1960s34, AWE technology evolved from statistical models analyzing surface-level features to more sophisticated systems like E-rater. These systems not only demonstrated high inter-rater reliability with human graders but also established a critical ongoing debate: the tension between scoring reliability and construct validity. Critics argued that AWE systems struggled to evaluate deeper qualities of writing, such as argumentation and creativity–a critique that directly parallels the challenges in automated translation assessment today35.

AI in English translation education

The application of modern AI, particularly natural language processing (NLP)36, has significantly advanced the capabilities of automated assessment in translation education. Unlike earlier rule-based systems, current AI evaluation tools can analyse semantic similarity, syntactic structure, and lexical choice with greater sophistication37. These systems offer clear advantages, primarily by automating feedback on foundational skills like grammar, vocabulary, and fluency. This is particularly valuable in large-scale teaching environments, as it alleviates the scoring burden on instructors. However, AI in translation assessment inherits the challenges from AWE. Translation is a complex act of cultural mediation and creative problem-solving. Current AI models often struggle to evaluate cultural appropriateness, contextual adaptation, and linguistic creativity38. An AI system may penalise a creative yet effective translation of a cultural idiom because it deviates from a literal rendering. This highlights a critical gap between what AI can reliably measure and what constitutes true translation competence.

Research gaps

Although recent studies have explored AI-assisted translation assessment, most existing research isolates the use of AI and teacher evaluation rather than integrating them into a coherent framework39. AI-based models excel in automation and objectivity but falter in evaluating creativity and cultural nuance. Conversely, traditional teacher assessments provide interpretive depth but often lack the consistency and efficiency required in large-scale teaching environments, creating a clear methodological dilemma40. However, this dilemma has rarely been discussed through the lens of integrated blended/hybrid instruction theory, which highlights how technologies can be embedded as process components to support feedback and assessment in higher education classrooms24.

These limitations reveal a critical gap: the absence of an operationalised framework for a hybrid evaluation mechanism. While previous studies have treated AI and human scoring as separate paradigms, the potential synergy between AI’s analytical precision and teachers’ pedagogical insights remains largely theoretical and unexplored. Without a systematic examination of their interaction, current assessment models fail to capture the multidimensional nature of translation competence, reducing AI to an error-checking tool rather than an integrated pedagogical partner41.

To address this gap, the present study introduces and validates a hybrid AI–teacher evaluation model. Using a mixed-methods design grounded in data triangulation, we explore how aligning quantitative AI scoring with qualitative teacher feedback can enhance both the reliability and interpretive richness of assessment. This approach provides an integrated and scalable framework to improve translation pedagogy, moving beyond a simple dichotomy to a collaborative human-AI model. Methodology

Data collection

This study was conducted in the context of an English-related major at a normal university. The survey subjects were 832 students, mainly aged between 18 and 25 years old. In order to ensure that the surveyed students had a basic understanding of English translation learning, this paper only considered English-related majors who had completed at least one semester of translation courses. Participants were asked to provide demographic information through four items, including gender, major, grade, and student number. The final sample size of the questionnaire was 796, with an effective rate of 92.99%, of which 36 questionnaires were eliminated due to being unqualified. The final sample size was determined by the number of eligible students available within the university during the data collection period. As the study targeted a clearly defined and accessible student population, the use of a large proportion of the available cohort is consistent with established practices in educational survey research and is considered sufficient for reliable statistical analysis42.

The collection of qualitative data was mainly conducted through focus group discussions, which divided students from different courses into small groups to discuss the advantages and disadvantages of the AI evaluation system and analyze its specific impact on students’ translation learning, including students’ acceptance and trust in AI scoring, and whether they felt that AI evaluation was in line with their actual abilities. These interviews and discussion records were compiled into qualitative data to supplement the quantitative analysis results and help comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness and adaptability of the AI evaluation model in actual teaching.

AI-powered evaluation protocol

The AI evaluation in this study was not conducted using a bespoke system, but rather by leveraging a state-of-the-art, publicly available large language model (LLM). This approach ensures that the methodology can be replicated by other researchers. All AI-generated feedback was produced using GPT-4 model43, accessed via the official API between February and March 2023. To ensure maximum consistency and reduce the randomness of the outputs, the temperature parameter for all API calls was set to a near-deterministic value of 0.1. Regarding the number of trials, each translation submission was evaluated through a single API call using the standardized prompt described below. No repeated runs, reranking, or averaging across multiple outputs were conducted. This single-run design was adopted to reflect an authentic classroom feedback scenario in which students receive one definitive round of evaluative comments, and to maximize procedural standardization across the full dataset.

The core of the evaluation method was a carefully constructed and tested prompt, which provided the model with a clear role, context, and a multi-step task. The complete prompt structure is detailed in Table 2. In this study, describing ChatGPT as acting “like a teacher” refers to the role defined within the prompt, where the model was instructed to adopt the persona of an expert university-level translation instructor and to provide rubric-based scoring and formative feedback. To ensure consistency across all evaluations, the same model version, fixed temperature setting, and identical prompt template were used for every submission. The model outputs were required to follow a structured JSON format, which enabled automatic parsing and prevented post hoc manual editing of the feedback content. The model was tasked with providing a holistic score, identifying specific errors according to a predefined rubric, and generating qualitative, constructive feedback for the student. Research procedures

This study was conducted in early 2023 to explore the potential of AI technology in the evaluation of English translation teaching in universities. The survey instrument used in this study was a self-developed questionnaire designed to measure students’ acceptance of AI-assisted evaluation, trust in AI evaluation, and perceived learning effects in translation teaching. To comprehensively evaluate the AI evaluation model’s effectiveness and adaptability, this study utilizes a convergent mixed-methods design, the workflow of which is depicted in Fig. 1. This approach involves the separate collection and analysis of quantitative and qualitative data, followed by a final interpretation phase where data triangulation is employed as a validity strategy to synthesize the findings. The quantitative part collected students’ acceptance and trust in the AI evaluation system and their views on learning effects through a questionnaire survey. The questionnaire design referred to the current literature on the application of AI technology in education and was compiled based on the specific needs of translation teaching44–46. Specifically, the item construction and scale development followed established questionnaire design principles commonly used in educational technology and technology acceptance research47,48. Based on these principles, a self-designed questionnaire was developed consisting of three constructs: Acceptance of AI Evaluation, Trust in AI Evaluation, and Perceived Learning Effects. Each construct included four items measured on a five-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree). Before the formal data collection, the research team conducted two rounds of pilot studies involving 20 participants. These participants were selected from the same population as the main study, namely students enrolled in translation-related courses, to ensure consistency between the pilot phase and the final sample (N = 796). The purpose of the pilot study was not statistical inference, but instrument refinement, focusing on the clarity of questionnaire items, wording, response format, and the feasibility of the overall research procedure. Methodological literature suggests that a pilot sample of approximately 15–30 participants is adequate for questionnaire pretesting in large-scale survey research49,50. In light of these recommendations and the size of the final sample, a pilot group of 20 participants was set sufficient for the aims of this study. Based on the feedback from the pilot study, the questionnaire content was further modified to improve clarity and pertinence. For example, the original question “Can AI evaluation effectively improve learning effects?” was modified to “Can AI evaluation accurately reflect translation ability and provide useful feedback?” to ensure that participants can accurately understand the question and make effective responses.

The survey instrument used in this study was a custom-designed questionnaire developed to assess student perceptions of the AI-powered evaluation model. The questionnaire was distributed via email to students majoring in English at a single university. From a complete roster of all eligible students, participants were selected using a simple random sampling technique. Regression analysis was used to analyze the questionnaire data, focusing on the relationship between student characteristics and their views on the AI assessment system. Qualitative data were collected through focus group discussions to complement the quantitative analysis results and to delve deeper into students’ subjective experience of the AI assessment system. The thematic design of the focus group discussions referred to key literature in the relevant field, focusing on the advantages and disadvantages of the AI assessment system, the practical value of feedback, and its specific impact on students’ translation ability. The discussion process was fully recorded and processed through thematic analysis to extract key patterns and insights. In the analysis phase, the research team integrated the quantitative data with the qualitative findings through data triangulation. The quantitative analysis provided an objective assessment of students’ overall acceptance and the performance of the AI system, while the qualitative analysis supplemented the detailed description of students’ subjective experience and actual needs. Finally, the comprehensive conclusion information of the model can be used for updating the AI-powered evaluation model.

Data analysis

This study employed a convergent mixed-methods design, where quantitative and qualitative data were collected concurrently to develop a comprehensive understanding of the research problem. We begin with the quantitative phase where data were collected from 796 English-related majors through a questionnaire survey. This data was analyzed using SPSS 26.051. Following the calculation of descriptive statistics, a multiple linear regression analysis was conducted to identify the key factors predicting student trust52. The model was specified with ’Trust in AI Evaluation’ as the dependent variable, and its overall fit was assessed using the F-statistic and Adjusted R253, with a significance level set at p < 0.05.

The second, qualitative phase involved ten focus groups with a total of 72 students, who were selected from the survey respondents using a purposive sampling strategy to ensure diverse perspectives. The number of focus groups was determined based on the principle of data saturation rather than a predetermined numerical target. Data collection continued until recurring themes and patterns became stable and no substantively new insights emerged across successive groups. By the tenth focus group, thematic saturation was achieved, indicating that the sample size was sufficient to capture the range of student perspectives relevant to the research questions54. The transcripts were analyzed in NVivo (Version 12)55 using the thematic analysis framework of Braun and Clarke56. To ensure methodological rigor, two researchers independently coded a 20% sample of the data, achieving substantial inter-rater reliability (Cohen’s Kappa = 0.85), with all disagreements resolved through discussion. Finally, the themes from this analysis were used for data triangulation57, which involved comparing the quantitative and qualitative findings to produce a more robust interpretation. In this study, data triangulation refers to a methodological validity strategy in which results from different data sources and analytical approaches are systematically compared to identify convergence, complementarity, or divergence, thereby strengthening the credibility of the integrated conclusions.

Qualitative data findings.

A prominent theme was that students generally trust the AI system for foundational aspects like grammar, vocabulary, and fluency. Participants across all ten focus groups appreciated the system’s consistency and objectivity. This aligns with feedback theories emphasizing the power of consistent and objective feedback loops in learning59. Many students reported that the AI’s rapid responses helped them efficiently identify and correct basic errors, thereby improving their translation quality and reducing reliance on teachers for rudimentary corrections.

However, this trust was often conditional. In every focus group, participants consistently questioned the quality of AI feedback for more complex tasks, especially those involving cultural context and creativity. A common sentiment was that the AI struggled to accurately evaluate creative translations, such as metaphors and cross-cultural expressions, providing feedback that felt mechanical and lacked cultural depth. This finding resonates with previous research on AI’s limitations in cultural adaptation62, confirming its shortcomings with intricate, culturally rich translations.

Despite these limitations, students unanimously viewed the frequent feedback from the AI system as highly beneficial. Many reported that the instant and consistent feedback allowed them to adjust their translation strategies with more confidence. This is consistent with theories that emphasize the importance of timely and frequent feedback for skill development and self-regulation63. The students’ reliance on this feature for continuous improvement underscores the integral role AI can play in the daily learning process.

Consequently, a strong consensus emerged across all focus groups in favor of a hybrid model that integrates AI with traditional teacher evaluation. Students still deeply value their teachers’ subjective judgment, emotional resonance, and personalized feedback, viewing them as irreplaceable for nuanced guidance. They envisioned an ideal model where AI provides standardized, instant feedback on technical aspects, freeing up teachers to offer deeper, context-sensitive guidance. This aligns with literature suggesting that combining AI with human input can optimize evaluation efficiency while maintaining pedagogical depth64.

In summary, the qualitative analysis reveals that while students readily accept AI-assisted evaluation for its efficiency, they are also keenly aware of its limitations. They do not see AI as a replacement for teachers, but as a powerful complementary tool. A blended approach, integrating AI’s strengths with the indispensable wisdom of teacher evaluation, is clearly identified by students as the optimal model.

Key factor analysis

To supplement the regression findings and test the robustness of the observed inter-factor relationships, we conducted a Monte Carlo simulation67. This approach addresses the question of whether the findings from our specific sample (N=796) are stable and generalizable. The simulation was directly based on the statistical properties (e.g., mean, standard deviation) of our empirical data, generating 10,000 simulated observations that statistically mirror our sample. Figure 3 presents a heat map of the correlation matrix derived from this large, simulated dataset. The results from the simulation closely mirror the correlations found in our original dataset and the relationships identified in the regression model. For instance, the strong positive correlations between ’AI Feedback Quality’, ’Student Engagement’, and ’Student Trust’ remain highly stable in the simulation. This process confirms that the relationships identified in our study are robust and not merely an artifact of the specific sample collected.Fig. 3Heat map of key factor analysis.

Heat map of key factor analysis.

Error analysis

This paper aims to analyze the changing trends of different types of errors in students’ translation learning process. To ensure a systematic analysis, the error classification scheme was adapted from the established Multidimensional Quality Metrics (MQM) framework70, a recognized standard in translation quality assessment. The coding was performed by two independent annotators on a 20% subset of the data, and high inter-rater reliability was confirmed (Cohen’s Kappa = 0.88) before the primary annotator coded the remaining data, ensuring the consistency of the classification. As shown in the reliably coded data in Fig. 5, there was a clear downward trend in foundational errors like Grammar and Vocabulary over the 12-month period. As can be seen from the Fig. 5, the number of grammar errors and vocabulary errors shows a gradual downward trend over time, especially in the 6-month and 12-month assessments, which may indicate that students have gradually mastered more translation skills in translation, especially in the use of basic knowledge and vocabulary. The number of sentence structure errors and cultural errors has changed relatively steadily. Overall, the feedback and evaluation provided by the AI system may help students find and correct basic errors more quickly, but more complex errors require more time and practice to be effectively improved. This shows that in translation learning, the timely feedback of the AI system has played a positive role in improving basic knowledge, but in order to improve students’ translation quality and cope with complex translation tasks, more teaching practice and human cooperation are needed.

Comparison of evaluation methods

This study compares the accuracy of three evaluation methods: AI scoring, single teacher scoring, and a combined AI+teacher model. To ensure a valid and non-circular comparison that could answer the reviewer’s concerns, we first established a standard for a subset of texts. This standard was derived from the consensus scores assigned by a panel of three senior translation professors. We then operationalized accuracy using an intuitive metric consistent with Fig. 6’s y-axis. For each evaluation method, we calculated its Mean Absolute Error (MAE) against the gold standard, and defined the Average Accuracy Score as (100 - MAE). A higher score thus represents a smaller deviation from the expert consensus and therefore higher accuracy. This approach is consistent with the theory of diversified evaluation tools, which emphasizes combining methods to improve credibility72.Fig. 6Accuracy of different evaluation methods.

Accuracy of different evaluation methods.

As illustrated in Fig. 6, the comparison based on this robust methodology yielded clear results over the 12-month period. The standalone AI evaluation method consistently produced the lowest Average Accuracy Score, highlighting its limitations in matching the expert consensus. In contrast, the teacher evaluation method always demonstrated a significantly higher accuracy than the AI alone. Most importantly, the combined AI+teacher evaluation method (labeled ‘Final Evaluation Accuracy’) consistently achieved the highest Average Accuracy Score at all time points. This result strongly emphasizes the benefits of combining AI scoring with teacher judgment.

To isolate and quantify the effect of AI feedback, we used a within-subjects experimental design. Students first completed an initial translation task, then received AI-generated feedback on their work before attempting a second task of comparable complexity. By measuring the change in error counts between the two tasks, we could attribute any significant performance improvements directly to the AI feedback, thus providing objective evidence of its pedagogical impact.

Discussion

With the continuous development of AI technology in the field of education, especially its application in translation teaching, this study explores the potential and challenges of AI-assisted translation teaching evaluation models from multiple perspectives. The findings suggest that AI technology has the potential to improve the objectivity, consistency, and efficiency of evaluation, particularly in large-scale classroom environments. AI evaluation systems provide fast and accurate feedback, which can be personalized according to the different needs of students. These advantages are consistent with the benefits of AI in improving learning efficiency, providing instant feedback, and promoting personalized learning mentioned in previous studies73. However, despite the great potential of AI in improving the quality and efficiency of translation teaching, some students still highlight the limitations of AI in understanding cultural context and evaluating creativity. They believe that AI may not fully capture the subtle differences and emotional expressions involved in translation.

The AI evaluation systems have demonstrated advantages in providing fast feedback and objective feedback, consistent with previous studies on AI’s role in improving learning efficiency and instant feedback. However, despite the great potential of AI in improving the quality and efficiency of translation teaching, some students still question the limitations of AI in understanding cultural context and creativity evaluation. Students believe that AI cannot fully grasp the subtle differences and emotional expressions involved in translation, particularly in more complex cultural and creative translation tasks. This reflects the current limitations of AI technology in handling such nuanced tasks, indicating that human judgment remains essential in certain areas of translation evaluation74.

In addition, students’ acceptance and trust in AI evaluation systems are also important factors affecting the effectiveness of AI evaluation. We found that the higher the students’ acceptance of AI, the stronger their trust in AI evaluation results. In particular, students with higher levels of participation are able to interact more actively with the AI system, and the feedback results are therefore more accurate and personalized. Teachers can use the automatic evaluation function of the AI system to quickly obtain feedback on students’ translation results and adjust their teaching strategies in a timely manner. This synergy allows teachers to devote more time to in-depth teaching and personalized tutoring, thereby enhancing students’ learning experience. This finding is consistent with the technology acceptance model75, indicating that students’ technology acceptance directly affects their learning outcomes and their dependence on the evaluation system.

Although AI systems have shown significant advantages in terms of consistency and timely feedback in translation evaluation, our research shows that the role of teachers in translation education is still irreplaceable. AI technology can provide instant feedback and promote the improvement of language skills, but teachers still play a vital role in dealing with complex, cultural and creative translation tasks. Teachers are able to adjust their teaching methods according to the individual needs of students and provide detailed emotional feedback, which is not fully achieved by current AI systems. Therefore, in order to maximize the potential of artificial intelligence, the collaboration between AI and teachers is necessary. Teachers can not only use AI technology to provide instant feedback, but also provide further explanations and guidance to students, ensuring that students can make greater progress in cultural understanding and creative expression while improving their language skills.

The AI-assisted translation evaluation model has great advantages in improving evaluation consistency and efficiency, but it still faces some challenges, especially when dealing with complex cultural backgrounds and creative content in translation. Future AI technology needs to be continuously improved, especially in the ability of cultural adaptation, contextual understanding, and creative translation, so as to better serve the needs of translation teaching. On this basis, the cooperation between teachers and AI will become the key to educational reform. Through this human-machine collaboration, translation teaching evaluation can not only improve efficiency, but also improve the quality of teaching and students’ learning experience.

While this study provides important insights, several limitations must be acknowledged. The findings’ generalisability is constrained by the use of a sample from a single institution in China, a notable gender imbalance, which was explicitly examined and statistically controlled for in the analysis, and some sample attrition during the 12-month longitudinal analysis. Therefore, future research is essential to validate these findings across multi-institutional and cross-cultural designs with more diverse and balanced participant groups. Subsequent research should also delve deeper into the impact of AI on more complex translation tasks, particularly those requiring high levels of creativity and cultural sensitivity, and test the application of the model across different language pairs and translation types.

Crucially, the findings affirm that the teacher’s role remains irreplaceable, especially when evaluating literary translations, where AI’s lack of human emotional experience and aesthetic judgment prevents it from providing deep, insightful feedback. The future of translation education, therefore, lies not in replacement, but in a human-AI synergy that leverages complementary strengths. Generative AI can overcome the time-consuming drawbacks of traditional evaluation by providing more precise, scientific, and personalized objective. With its powerful learning and memory capabilities, educators can utilize GenAI’s tracking technology to perform real-time, intelligent assessments of students’ learning processes and progress. This enables a data-oriented pedagogical approach that monitors the entire teaching process and supports the holistic development of students.

In general, the AI-assisted translation evaluation model provides an innovative solution for English translation teaching in universities, especially in improving evaluation consistency and feedback quality. However, in order to make it widely used in more complex translation tasks, AI technology still needs to be further optimized. Through the effective collaboration between teachers and AI, future translation teaching will be more efficient and accurate, while also maintaining humanistic care and emotional support, ensuring the comprehensive development of students in terms of language ability and comprehensive quality.

Theoretical and practical implications

The translation teaching quality evaluation model based on AI proposed in this study has important theoretical and practical significance in English translation teaching in universities. By combining quantitative and qualitative methods, the study explores the potential of AI technology in translation evaluation, especially in improving the objectivity, consistency, and efficiency of evaluation. The effectiveness of the AI evaluation model is not only reflected in optimizing the evaluation process and reducing human bias, but also provides new solutions to solve the limitations of traditional evaluation methods in large-scale classroom environments.

First, this study further verifies the potential of AI in educational evaluation, especially in solving the subjective and time-consuming problems faced by traditional evaluation methods. The AI evaluation system can provide students with instant feedback through automated processes, thereby improving the efficiency and consistency of evaluation. Students’ high trust in the AI evaluation system also provides a theoretical basis for the widespread application of AI in education. The results show that high-quality AI feedback has an important impact on the stimulation of students’ learning motivation and the improvement of translation ability59.

Second, this study emphasizes the synergy between teachers and AI technology in the education process and proposes that AI is not a substitute for traditional teachers, but a powerful supplement to teachers’ teaching activities. The role of teachers in AI evaluation systems cannot be ignored, especially when it comes to cultural adaptation and creative translation tasks. Compared with the over-reliance on generative AI, the emotional support, critical thinking guidance, and personalized teaching strategies that teachers can provide are still irreplaceable by AI technology76.

At the practical level, this study provides valuable inspiration for the reform of translation education. By combining the AI evaluation system with the traditional evaluation of teachers, the evaluation model of translation teaching can increase the personalized learning experience of students while ensuring the efficiency of evaluation. The AI evaluation system can not only automatically evaluate students’ translation results, but also save a lot of time for teachers, allowing teachers to focus more on teaching design and personalized tutoring for students. This provides a practical solution for translation teaching in large-scale classrooms in higher education.

However, although the AI system has shown good results in many aspects, adaptability to cultural backgrounds and creative translation tasks remains a challenge. In practice, teachers should pay more attention to using AI technology to improve the design and evaluation of teaching content, but at the same time, they must remain sensitive to the limitations of AI. AI still cannot reach the depth of personalized feedback from teachers when dealing with complex translation tasks, especially those involving culture and emotions. Therefore, the role of teachers is still crucial, especially in the cultivation of critical thinking and creativity.

Based on these findings, this study offers key practical implications for both educators and policymakers. For teachers, there is a clear need to improve AI literacy, which extends beyond technical skills to include the pedagogical wisdom of integrating AI tools effectively. They should be trained to leverage AI for efficiency in assessing foundational skills, thereby freeing their time to focus on mentoring students in higher-order abilities like creativity and cultural adaptation. For education policymakers, it is crucial to provide institutional support for this collaborative human-AI model, including resources for technological infrastructure and professional development. Formulating clear guiding policies and ethical norms is also essential to ensure the responsible and equitable application of AI in education.

Conclusion

This study explores the application of an AI-assisted translation teaching quality assessment model in university English translation teaching. The results show that the AI assessment system has significant advantages in improving assessment consistency, efficiency, and quality, especially in large-scale classroom environments. However, AI still has limitations when dealing with translation tasks involving cultural background and creativity. Although it can provide students with efficient and immediate feedback to help improve translation, its performance in complex tasks needs to be improved. The study also emphasizes that the synergy between AI and teachers is the key to improving the effectiveness of translation assessment. Teachers play an irreplaceable role in providing emotional support, critical feedback, and coping with cultural and creative translation tasks. Therefore, future AI assessment systems should further strengthen their capabilities in complex translation tasks, especially in the areas of cultural adaptation and creative translation. Overall, AI-assisted translation assessment has the potential to significantly improve teaching efficiency and consistency, but to realize its maximum potential in translation education, effective cooperation between teachers and AI is essential. The synergy between the two will promote the optimization of translation teaching assessment and promote the development of students’ translation ability, critical thinking, and creativity.

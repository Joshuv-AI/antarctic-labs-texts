The differential influence of Achievement Motivation on Subjective Well-being and the moderating role of Self-control
Authors: Yuting Feng, Qun Yang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo investigate the association between achievement motivation and subjective well-being, as well as the moderating role of self-control and self-management on this relationship, 1017 Chinese college students were surveyed. The main results showed that: The interactive effect of motivation to approach success and self-control on subjective well-being was significant. Specifically, for individuals with high self-control ability, the positive effects of motivation to approach success on subjective well-being, life satisfaction and positive affect tended to be stronger, and meanwhile, the motivation to approach success negatively predicted negative affect. Furthermore, the interactive effect of motivation to avoid failure and self-control on subjective well-being was significant. Specifically, for individuals with high self-control ability, the negative effects of motivation to avoid failure on subjective well-being, life satisfaction and positive affect tended to be weaker, and meanwhile, the effect of motivation to avoid failure on negative affect was relatively weaker. Overall, our study indicated that improving self-control ability could maximize the positive effect of achievement motivation on subjective well-being. Moreover, motivating individuals with high self-control ability to pursue success and reducing motivation to avoid failure for individuals with low self-control ability could have a more positive influence on subjective well-being.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73193-2.Keywords: Achievement motivation, Motivation to approach success, Motivation to avoid failure, Subjective well-being, Self-controlSubject terms: Psychology, Human behaviour

Introduction

“Well-being” is a common topic of concern, and the pursuit of well-being is an eternal motivation of human beings. With the rise of positive psychology, researchers have placed more emphasis on the affirmation of individual values and the development of potential, of which subjective well-being (SWB) is a core component. SWB refers to an individual’s overall evaluation of quality of life at a certain stage according to the criteria he or she has set. It is a subjective experience and a comprehensive psychological index to measure quality of life. SWB mainly consists of two basic components, namely, cognition and affect, which can be divided into three dimensions: positive affect, negative affect, and life satisfaction1,2.

Achievement motivation is an important predictor of SWB. Achievement motivation refers to individuals’ drive to pursue and achieve goals, focusing on explicit motive dispositions or tendencies3. The achievement-oriented goal pursuit is crucial for understanding goal regulation and motive dispositions, which differs from general goal pursuit4. Individuals’ well-being can be improved through both the process and outcome of pursuing such goals5,6. However, based on different achievement-oriented goals, different types of achievement motivation may have different influence on well-being4,7,8. According to the achievement motivation theory of psychologist Atkinson9, individuals develop two motive dispositions or tendencies, namely, motivation to approach success and motivation to avoid failure, also known as approach motivation and avoidance motivation. Approach motivation refers to hope for success, and avoidance motivation refers to the fear of failure in achievement-oriented goal pursuit. Studies have found that approach motivation drives individuals to aspire to success and to do things with more self-confidence. Individuals with high approach motivation are more willing to meet difficulties and challenges and use their abilities4,10. They tend to be more active in cognitive processing11, to have more positive emotional experiences12 and better interpersonal relationships13. In contrast, avoidance motivation drives individuals to fear failure, to avoid difficult and potentially unsuccessful tasks. They also have more negative affect and low satisfaction. Therefore, approach motivation is positively related to well-being, whereas avoidance motivation is negatively associated to well-being to some extent4,11,14.

However, does motivation to approach success necessarily increase well-being, and motivation to avoid failure inevitably reduces well-being? To our knowledge, previous research may have neglected the potential importance of individuals’ self-control. The differential influence of different achievement motivation on well-being is closely related to individuals’ self-control activities. Self-control refers to self-control and self-management skills that consist of three interdependent self-regulatory processes: self-monitoring, self-evaluating, and self-reinforcing, forming an iterative closed feedback loop15. Self-monitoring refers to monitoring the status and context of some target behaviors. In the process of self-evaluating, individuals compare target behaviors to an internalized standard and note any differences. According to this comparison, individuals engage in self-reinforcing, including self-reward or self-punishment. In the field of self-control research, scholars have argued that there are individual differences in the level of self-control and self-management, regarded self-control as a stable trait. Extant research has found that individuals with high self-control and self-management skills tend to have high positive affect, life satisfaction, and well-being16–19. In pursuit of achievement-oriented goals, individuals set goal-oriented behaviors and regulate their goals through self-monitoring, self-evaluating, and self-reinforcing processes. Therefore, individuals could achieve self-control through self-monitoring, self-evaluating, and self-reinforcing15,20, which have important impact on the SWB and emotional experience of individuals in pursuing achievement-oriented goals.

Considering the importance of self-regulatory processes in pursuing achievement-oriented goals, we draw on motive disposition theory3,21 to demonstrate how different self-regulatory processes influence the relationship between achievement motivation and SWB. Motive disposition theory focuses on individual differences in motives, including the differentiation into hope and fear components of achievement motivation (hope of success and fear of failure). The activation of different motives involves self-regulatory processes that are highly effective in regulating achievement-oriented goal behaviors. This view is also guided by regulatory focus theory22. According to regulatory focus theory, successful goal pursuing promotes positive affect in individuals with approach motivation and reduces negative affect in those with avoidance motivation. Specifically, in approaching success, individuals set approach-oriented goal behaviors, such as completing challenging tasks, and the pursuit of a goal itself satisfies individuals’ needs for competency21,23,24. When the goal is achieved, individuals’ sense of accomplishment due to progress reinforces such behavior and leads to further experience of highly activated positive affect such as “excitement”11. Conversely, in avoiding failure, individuals set avoidance-oriented goal behaviors based on “no mistakes”25. They focus on negative stimuli that may lead to failure and obstacles in goal achievement26, as well as monitor and evaluate goal achievement accordingly. In the framework of approach-avoidance dispositions, when goals are achieved, individuals experience positive affect with low activation, such as “calmness” in the process of achievement-oriented goal pursuit11. Conversely, individuals may produce negative cognitive and emotional experiences because they think there was just no mistakes, not making progress during the process20.

Given these individual differences and the importance of self-regulatory processes, not all individuals can attain effective self-regulation in pursuing achievement-oriented goals. Accordingly, the relationship between different achievement motivation and SWB may be influenced by the level of individuals’ self-control and self-management. Self-control and different achievement motivation could exert their interactive effects on SWB. Specifically, approaching success stimulates individuals’ positive emotional experiences and self-affirmation, and they eventually achieve goals through self-adjustment and continuous improvement. Self-control reinforces the above effects by exerting better self-monitoring and self-evaluation during the goal-seeking process27, correcting and optimizing behaviors that deviate from goals28. Ultimately, it helps individuals better achieve their goals. Therefore, for individuals with high self-control, motivation to approach success is a stronger positive predictor of well-being. By contrast, in the pursuit of avoidance-oriented goals, individuals may anticipate the potential causes of failure and worry about possible mistakes in advance. They adopt vigilant strategies to avoid failures29 and thus reduce well-being14. However, individuals with high self-control can monitor their potential failures in a timely manner30 and avoid getting caught up in persistent negative thoughts and affect. Hence, avoidance-oriented goals could help them avoid failure without reducing well-being. In comparison, individuals with low self-control may constantly imagine failure-related situations31 and fail to adjust promptly through self-monitoring, self-evaluation, and resource mobilization32. They also lack effective strategies for coping with these negative thoughts and affect30. Therefore, they may experience deep feelings of fear and anxiety about failure31, thus exacerbating negative impact of motivation to avoid failure on well-being.

In summary, drawing on motive disposition theory and regulatory focus theory, this study further explores the relationship between achievement motivation and SWB based on previous research. Thus far, few studies have probed the predictive role of achievement motivation (motivation to approach success and motivation to avoid failure) on SWB from the perspective of self-control during achievement-oriented goal pursuit. This study focuses on the effects of different achievement motivation on SWB, as well as the moderating role of self-control in the relationship between the two. We hypothesize that the effects of motivation to approach success and motivation to avoid failure on SWB vary depending on the level of individuals’ self-control. Specifically, (1) motivation to approach success positively predicts SWB and motivation to avoid failure negatively predicts SWB; (2) the positive effect of motivation to approach success on SWB increases with the increase in self-control, and motivation to approach success has a stronger positive effect on SWB for individuals with high self-control; (3) the negative effect of motivation to avoid failure on SWB decreases with the increase in self-control, and motivation to avoid failure has a stronger negative effect on SWB for individuals with low self-control. In addition, beyond our research hypotheses, three-way interaction effects analyses were conducted to explore more interesting findings, and the discussion on these findings was also included (see Supplementary analyses section). Studying the pattern of the effect of different types of achievement motivation on SWB under different conditions helps to further clarify the relationship between the two, to improve their ecological validity and apply the findings to real situations.

Methods

Participants

After seeking ethical approval from our Ethics Committee, 1150 undergraduates in China were invited to participate voluntarily, and informed consent was obtained from each participant. After excluding students with extremely consistent responses and too many missing values, 1017 valid samples were included in the analyses, for an effective rate of 88%. Among them, 486 (21.21 ± 1.72 years old) were male students (47.8%), and 531 (20.60 ± 1.60 years old) were female students (52.2%).

Measures

Achievement Motivation

This study used the achievement motivation scale to assess different motive dispositions developed by Gjesme and Nygard, revised by Ye and Hagtvet33. The scale consists of a subscale for the motivation to approach success and a subscale for the motivation to avoid failure, with the former measuring motive dispositions related to success and involving positive evaluation situations and outcome expectations and the latter involving negative evaluation situations and outcome expectations; the two subscales each have 15 items scored on a seven-point scale (1 = strongly disagree to 7 = strongly agree). Example items of the subscale for motivation to approach success are “I like unfamiliar and difficult tasks, even risky ones,” and “I like tasks that I can accomplish when I try my best.” Example items of the subscale for motivation to avoid failure are “I dislike the task that I am, or others are, not sure if I can complete it,” and “I don’t want to be assigned the difficult tasks.” Cronbach’s α are 0.90 and 0.92, respectively.

Self-control and self-management

The Self-Control and Self-Management Scale (SCMS) developed by Mezo15 was used in this study. The scale has 16 items that are divided into three dimensions (self-monitoring, self-evaluating, and self-reinforcing) scored on a seven-point scale (1 = strongly disagree to 7 = strongly agree). Example statements are “I make sure to track my progress regularly when I am working on a goal,” “The standards I set for myself are unclear and make it hard for me to judge how I am doing on a task,” and “I congratulate myself when I make some progress.” Cronbach’s α for the three subscales are 0.81, 0.86, and 0.82, respectively, and Cronbach’s α for the total scale is 0.84.

Subjective well-being (SWB)

The SWB scale is divided into three parts: life satisfaction, positive affect, and negative affect. The satisfaction with life scale34 adopted in this study has five items that are scored on a seven-point scale (1 = strongly disagree to 7 = strongly agree). An example is as follows: “In most ways my life is close to my ideal.” Cronbach’s α for this scale is 0.81. The positive affect and negative affect scale revised by Qiu et al.35 was adopted in this study. There are 18 items that are divided into two dimensions (positive affect and negative affect) scored on a seven-point scale (1 = not at all to 7 = extremely). Example items for positive affect included “excited,” “delighted,” and “enthusiastic.” Example items for negative affect included “distressed,” “ashamed,” and “irritable.” Cronbach’s α of the two subscales are 0.92 and 0.96, respectively.

Statistical analysis

A total of 1017 valid questionnaires were collected, and the data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0. Firstly, descriptive analysis was performed using the mean and standard deviation. Secondly, the correlation between the variables was analyzed using the Pearson correlation coefficient. Additionally, hierarchical regression analysis and subsequent simple slope test were conducted to examine the moderating effect of self-control in the relationship between achievement motivation and SWB. Differences were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05.

The moderating role of self-control in the relationship between achievement motivation and SWB

The interactive effect of motivation to approach success and self-control on SWB was significant (β = 0.11, p < 0.001). Simple slope test indicated that the positive effect of the motivation to approach success on SWB increased with the increase in self-control. For individuals with high self-control, the motivation to approach success had a stronger positive impact on well-being, and the stronger the motivation to approach success, the higher was the level of SWB (β = 0.52, p < 0.001); for individuals with low self-control, the effect of motivation to approach success was relatively weaker (β = 0.28, p < 0.001) (see Fig. 1). In addition, the interactive effect of motivation to avoid failure and self-control on SWB was significant (β = 0.15, p < 0.001). Specifically, the negative effect of the motivation to avoid failure on SWB diminished with the increase in self-control, at which point the negative impact of motivation to avoid failure on well-being was stronger for individuals with low self-control, and the stronger their motivation to avoid failure, the lower was their level of SWB (β=-0.23, p < 0.001); for individuals with high self-control, such effect was relatively weaker (β=-0.18, p < 0.001) (see Fig. 2).

Discussion

Different types of achievement motivation have different effects on individuals’ well-being5,8. This study explored the differential effects of achievement motivation (motivation to approach success and motivation to avoid failure) on SWB from the perspective of self-control and self-management. The results revealed the moderating role of self-control on the relationship between the two.

First, we found that motivation to approach success positively predicted SWB and motivation to avoid failure negatively predicted SWB. This result is consistent with previous findings4,10,11. Specifically, motivation to approach success guides individuals to pursue self-development goals, and the self-regulatory processes in pursuing achievement-oriented goals continuously stimulate their potential for growth and development7. The sense of accomplishment could reinforce this behavior. This allows them to experience highly activated positive affect11 and gain greater meaningfulness and well-being. Conversely, motivation to avoid failure drives individuals to pay more attention to negative situations that may lead to failure. They would also be more attentive to possible obstacles that prevent them from achieving goals26. Moreover, self-monitoring and self-evaluation of avoidance-oriented goals are more likely to produce negative cognitive and emotional experiences20. Individuals are more likely to adopt more vigilant strategies to avoid failure29, thereby reducing well-being14.

In addition, the pattern that “motivation to approach success enhances well-being, and motivation to avoid failure reduces well-being” is not invariable. The relationship between achievement motivation and SWB is influenced by individuals’ self-control. Our study found that self-control reinforced the positive influence of motivation to approach success on SWB. Motivation to approach success promoted SWB for individuals with high self-control but exerted a relatively weaker influence on SWB for individuals with low self-control. These results could be understood from motive disposition theory and regulatory focus theory21,22. In general, these theories contribute to illustrate the differential influence of achievement motivation on well-being. Furthermore, in the approach-avoidance motivation framework, these inferences are conducive to elaborate the moderating role of self-regulatory processes in pursuing achievement-oriented goal behaviors.

According to the theoretical foundations, individuals with high self-control could monitor and evaluate achievement-oriented goal behaviors promptly in the process of approaching success27. They could constantly self-affirm and motivate themselves, and ultimately achieve achievement goals through continuous modification of their behaviors28. Therefore, leading to more positive emotional experiences and satisfaction, as well as less negative emotional experiences. In addition, present study also found that self-control mitigated the negative effects of motivation to avoid failure on SWB. In pursuing avoidance-oriented goals, individuals with low self-control may continuously imagine failure-related situations31. They may lack effective coping strategies30, thereby reducing SWB, life satisfaction, and positive affect, as well as increasing negative affect. In contrast, individuals with high self-control could monitor and adjust their avoidance-oriented goal behaviors promptly. They adopt effective strategies to monitor possible failures and avoid falling into persistent negative thoughts and emotions through self-regulation30. Hence, the negative effect of motivation to avoid failure on their SWB was relatively weaker. Moreover, individuals with low avoidance motivation may not suffer from low self-control. In other words, for individuals with low avoidance motivation, there is little difference in SWB regardless of the level of self-control. A possible explanation is that individuals with low avoidance motivation may be not oriented to avoidance goals, and thus, lack involvement of self-regulatory processes to enhance SWB.

There are some theoretical contributions in the present work. First, we explained the differential influence of approaching success and avoiding failure on SWB from the perspective of self-control and self-management. Second, drawing on motive disposition theory and regulatory focus theory, we revealed the moderating role of self-control, that is, not all individuals experience SWB in the process of approaching success or are driven by motivation to avoid failure to reduce SWB. Only individuals with high self-control could maximize the positive effects of motivation to approach success in pursuing approach-oriented goals. They may also take into account the ability to maintain SWB in the process of avoiding failure. Therefore, self-control is also an important factor in enhancing individuals’ well-being. The role of different types of achievement motivation on well-being should not be discussed only from the perspective of motivation, individual differences in self-control and self-management skills should be considered. This conclusion is conducive to better explaining the relationship between achievement motivation and well-being. To some extent, these findings are potentially crucial to enrich existing theories and expand the research field of motivation and self-regulation.

This study also has some practical implications. First, from the perspective of stimulating achievement motivation, improving individuals’ achievement motivation does not necessarily enhance well-being. Cultivating adaptive achievement-oriented goals and stimulating corresponding achievement motivation based on individuals’ level of self-control could better enhance well-being. Specifically, for individuals with high self-control, stimulating their motivation to approach success could improve well-being more, and activating motivation to avoid failure could not only help them avoid possible failures but also not reduce well-being. Moreover, for individuals with low self-control, reducing their motivation to avoid failure could enhance or minimize the decrease in well-being. Second, from the perspective of self-control, a high level of self-control could enhance the positive effect of motivation to approach success on well-being, as well as reduce the negative effect of motivation to avoid failure on well-being. Self-control consists of different self-regulatory processes that refer to goal setting and successful completion of achievement-oriented goal behaviors, a kind of relevant character strength that could be key to twenty-first-century education. Therefore, improving individuals’ self-control and self-management skills could “maximize” the benefits of achievement motivation on well-being.

There are several limitations and future directions. First, a questionnaire survey was used as the main research method in the present study. Therefore, special caution should be exercised when making inferences about the causal relationships between variables. Second, the sample came from Chinese college students, an expansion to other countries, age, or occupational groups should be considered in the future. Especially from a cross-cultural perspective, response tendencies, or introspective practices may be discrepant. Third, based on our theoretical hypotheses, we examined the main effect of achievement motivation, the two-way interaction effects of MS and self-control, MF and self-control on SWB. Three-way interaction effects of MS, MF and self-control on SWB are worth exploring to observe specific patterns and demonstrate the co-occurring effects of approach and avoidance motivation on well-being. These interesting findings were discussed in the supplementary analyses section. Moreover, individuals’ self-control ability could be cultivated and enhanced. Further, longitudinal studies are necessary to trace the development of self-control ability for college students and its possible influence on well-being over time.

Conclusions

As an important predictor of SWB, achievement motivation has garnered much attention from scholars. Drawing on motive disposition theory and regulatory focus theory, the present study represents an effort to explore the differential influence of approaching success and avoiding failure on SWB from the perspective of self-control and self-management. The present study takes into account that individuals’ self-control ability could enhance the positive effect of motivation to approach success on SWB, as well as reduce the negative effect of motivation to avoid failure on SWB. The findings bolster existing theories and practices by encouraging further discussion on cultivating adaptive achievement-oriented goals based on different self-control ability to improve or maintain SWB. Furthermore, enhancing individuals’ self-control ability could “maximize” the benefits of achievement motivation on SWB.

Test of conditional MS × MF interaction on DVs at the value of Self-control

We further explored the three-way interaction effects of MS, MF and Self-control on SWB and its dimensions by utilizing the SPSS PROCESS macro, developed by Hayes36, which primarily used ordinary least squares regression analysis for parameter estimation. This study specifically employed model 3, within the PROCESS macro, and bootstrapping with 5000 samples was applied.

As shown in Table 5, for individuals with high self-control, the interactive effect of MS and MF on SWB (β=−0.21, p < 0.001), life satisfaction (β=−0.11, p < 0.001), and positive affect (β=−0.11, p < 0.001) were significant. The interactive effect of MS and MF on negative affect was more significant in high self-control condition.

Present study explored three-way interaction effects of MS, MF and Self-control on SWB, which demonstrated the co-occurring effects of approach motivation and avoidance motivation on well-being. There are several main findings worth discussing. Different combinations of MS, MF and self-control may bring different experience of SWB, life satisfaction, positive affect, and negative affect to individuals. This pattern is more apparent for individuals with high self-control. According to these findings, individuals with both high MS and high MF experience the lowest SWB, lowest life satisfaction, lowest positive affect, and highest negative affect. Due to the opposing character of these two motive dispositions, a simultaneous activation of MS and MF could be expected to lead to emotional ambivalence37. A possible interpretation for this finding may be that individuals with high activation of both MS and MF might frequently experience these ambivalent feelings. The conflicting character of the contradicting motivation dispositions, accompanied by more negative experiences, might lead to low SWB. In contrast, individuals with both low MS and low MF experience the highest SWB, highest life satisfaction, highest positive affect, and lowest negative affect. In this condition, the positive experience could enhance or maintain stable with the increase in self-control, but it is opposite for the negative experience. Individuals with both low activation of MS and low MF are less likely to depend on social evaluations, which might make them less sensitive for fluctuations in social success and failure. This will not trap them in the contradicting motivation dispositions, and help them avoid inner conflicts and ambivalence. Therefore, they have more positive experiences and happiness.

Studying the pattern of the co-occurring effect of MS and MF on SWB under different self-control conditions could help to elucidate the influence of different motivation combinations on affect and behavior in real situations. To our knowledge, little is known about the co-occurring effects of MS, MF and self-control on SWB. The current study addresses this gap. Due to the complexity of the actual situations, there is no clear answer on the question if the co-occurring effect of MS and MF is beneficial for SWB. We think this is an exciting avenue for future research, which will benefit both motivation and ability perspectives, and has the true potential to integrate research on motivation, affect and behavior.

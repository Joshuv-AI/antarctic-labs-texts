Comprehensive insights into the development of arabidopsis trichomes by single-nucleus RNA-seq
Authors: Hongyu Chen, Xiner Shen, Yaqian Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTrichomes are specialized epidermal structures that perform critical functions in plant defense and development. Despite their importance, the molecular programs governing trichome initiation, differentiation, maturation, and complete developmental progression remain incompletely characterized, largely due to the inherent limitations of conventional bulk RNA sequencing approaches in capturing cellular heterogeneity. To address this knowledge gap, we leveraged publicly available single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq) data from Arabidopsis thaliana to construct a high-resolution developmental trajectory of trichomes, encompassing the transition from epidermal cells through initiation to branching stages. This approach enabled us to identify stage-specific transcriptional regulators and refine the molecular framework underlying trichome development.ResultsWe successfully reconstructed a continuous developmental trajectory of arabidopsis trichomes and identified distinct stages characterized by extensive transcriptional reprogramming. Key regulatory genes, including VILLIN 1(VLN1), exhibited strong stage-specific expression during the initiation phase. Additionally, multiple long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) were found to be differentially expressed across developmental stages, suggesting potential roles in early lineage specification and trichome fate commitment.ConclusionsThis study presents a high-resolution single-nucleus transcriptomic atlas of Arabidopsis trichome development, spanning the continuum from epidermal cells to trichome branching stages, and establishes a comprehensive molecular framework delineating the complete developmental trajectory. This resource provides valuable genetic insights for future functional investigations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-08396-y.

Background

Trichomes in Arabidopsis thaliana are unicellular, highly branched epidermal structures comprising a stalk and multiple polarized branches, which arise through coordinated endoreduplication and anisotropic cell expansion [1–3]. Composed largely of cellulose, these structures function as multifunctional defensive organs that deter herbivores through mechanical and chemical mechanisms, reduce water loss by limiting transpiration, and provide protection against abiotic stressors such as ultraviolet radiation and temperature extremes [4–6]. The genetic accessibility, well-established molecular toolkit, and fully sequenced genome of arabidopsis have positioned its trichomes as a premier model for investigating plant cell fate specification, differentiation, and morphogenesis [7], [8]. Beyond basic plant biology, although trichome regulatory mechanisms can vary considerably across different plant lineages with species-specific networks [9], the genetic framework established in Arabidopsis trichome research has served as a valuable reference for investigating trichome development in other species, including commercially important crops such as cotton, where trichome-like fiber cells are a key quality trait [10–13]. Substantial progress has been made toward defining the genetic regulatory networks underlying trichome patterning and morphogenesis, with genes including MYB DOMAIN PROTEIN 23 (MYB23), GLABRA 2 (GL2), GLABROUS1 (GL1), and TRANSPARENT TESTA GLABRA1 (TTG1) implicated in lineage commitment and branching regulation [14, 15]. However, the complete molecular and cellular continuum underlying trichome initiation, maturation and branching remains unresolved, largely due to the lack of high-resolution approaches capable of capturing dynamic transcriptional states at the single-cell resolution. Moreover, the regulatory roles of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) in trichome development have only recently emerged and remain largely unexplored.

The advent of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has enabled the resolution of gene expression profiles at unprecedented cellular resolution, facilitating deeper insights into developmental processes that were previously inaccessible. However, However, the application of scRNA-seq to trichome studies has been hindered by technical challenges in isolating intact trichomes from Arabidopsis leaves, owing to their resistance to enzymatic digestion [16]. Recent advancements in snRNA-seq have circumvented this limitation, permitting direct transcriptional analysis of trichomes in Arabidopsis leaf tissue without the prerequisite of protoplast isolation [17–19]. his enabled construction of a high-resolution single-nucleus transcriptomic atlas of trichome development.

In this study, we reanalyzed publicly available snRNA-seq data to investigate the transcriptional dynamics of trichome across multiple developmental stages, including 6-day and 12-day seedlings and 21-day and 31-day rosettes [20]. We identified trichome-specific marker genes and reconstructed the developmental trajectory from initiation through branching. Furthermore, we examined the regulatory roles of lncRNAs in trichome differentiation. Our findings provide a comprehensive molecular framework of trichome development, revealing stage-specific transcription factors, signaling pathways, and lncRNAs associated with this process.

Results

A stage-specific transcriptomic atlas of Arabidopsis for comprehensive trichome identification

To investigate trichome development in Arabidopsis, we reanalyzed publicly available snRNA-seq datasets (PRJNA938501; [20]) spanning four developmental stages: 6 days (replicates = 6) and 12 days (replicates = 22) seedlings, and 21 days (replicates = 8) and 30 days (replicates = 6) rosettes at (hereafter Seedling_6d, Seedling_12d, Rosette_21d, and Rosette_30d.). Quality control and clustering yielded 22, 24, 18, and 22 clusters for the respective stages (Figure S1–S4A). In the original study, Lee et al., [20] annotated a limited number of cell types across the four developmental stages (Seedling_6d: 5 types including root endodermis, trichome, epidermis, phloem, and mesophyll; Seedling_12d: 2 types; Rosette_21d: 5 types; Rosette_30d: 3 types). To establish a more refined and consistent cellular taxonomy across all stages, we conducted comprehensive reannotation of cell identities. Cell type identities were assigned based on established marker genes curated from the PlantscRNAdb database (Table S1; [21]), with each cluster displaying characteristic marker gene expression profiles (Figure S1-S4B). This approach enabled the identification of transcriptionally distinct and reproducible cell populations across all developmental stages, including mesophyll, epidermis, bundle sheath, phloem, xylem, trichomes, guard cells, and proliferative cell states (Fig. 1 A, Table S2). Notably, trichome cells were consistently identified across all four developmental stages (Fig. 1 A), demonstrating the robustness of our analytical framework and enabling temporal comparative analyses. Canonical regulators of trichome specification and maturation-including GL1 [22], GL2 [23]), and MYB106 [24]-displayed strong and highly specific expression within trichome cluster, corroborating the fidelity of our cell type assignments (Fig. 1B). To refine cell identity signatures, we identified genes uniquely enriched within each cell type across developmental time (Table S3) and performed functional enrichment analyses to assess biological coherence (Figure S1–S4, Table S4). Consistent with known cellular functions, mesophyll cells displayed strong enrichment for photosynthesis-related processes, including light harvesting; epidermal cells were associated with pathways related to cuticle assembly and wax biosynthesis; proliferative cells showed enrichment in cell cycle regulation and DNA replication; and vascular tissues (phloem and xylem) were enriched for nutrient transport and abiotic stress response pathways. To further validate the robustness of trichome annotations and evaluate transcriptional conservation across developmental stages. we extracted only trichome cells and performed non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) to identify conserved gene expression modules defining core trichome transcriptional programs. This analysis resolved eight reproducible co-expression modules (MP1–MP8) (Fig. 1 C), of which MP1–MP3 exhibited strong cross-stage correlations, indicative of conserved transcriptional signatures underlying trichome identity. Functional enrichment analysis provided additional resolution into the biological relevance of these modules (Fig. 1D). MP1 was enriched for key processes associated with epidermal specialization and trichome morphogenesis, including cytoskeletal organization, trichome differentiation, and wax and fatty acid biosynthesis. MP2 was associated with nitrogen metabolism and chloroplast-localized pathways, while MP3 was enriched in wax biosynthesis and fatty acid metabolic processes.Together, these results corroborate the accuracy of trichome classification and reveal a stable, stage-spanning regulatory architecture.

Identification of novel genetic markers governing trichome identity in Arabidopsis

Leveraging the comprehensive single-nucleus transcriptomic atlas established in this study, we systematically identified novel marker genes, both upregulated and downregulated, specific to trichomes relative to epidermal cells. By intersecting the datasets across these stages, we defined a core set of 101 highly expressed trichome-specific genes (Fig. 2 A; detailed gene lists and annotations in Table S5). Conversely, comparative analysis between trichomes and epidermal cells revealed 65 genes with specifically low expression in trichomes. These genes likely represent either negative regulators of trichome development or factors preferentially involved in epidermal cell specification (Fig. 2B; detailed gene lists and annotations in Table S5). Given their critical regulatory roles in gene expression and cellular differentiation, transcription factors were a key focus of this investigation. Within the trichome-enriched gene cohort, we identified seven transcription factors, AT5G41410 (BEL1), AT4G00730 (ANTHOCYANINLESS 2, ANL2), AT2G46870 (NGATHA1, NGA1), AT5G40330 (MYB23), AT3G01140 (MYB106), AT1G02065 (SQUAMOSA PROMOTER BINDING PROTEIN-LIKE 8, SPL8), and AT1G79840 (GL2), displaying robust high activity across all developmental stages (Fig. 2 C). Notably, several well-characterized transcription factors showed strong enrichment in the trichome cluster, consistent with their established roles in trichome development. For example, GL2 is required for post-specification morphogenetic events, including cell expansion, branching, and cell wall maturation [23]; MYB23 regulates trichome initiation and branching, particularly along leaf margins [25]; MYB106 functions as a negative regulator of trichome branching [24]; and SPL8 governs trichome formation on sepal epidermis [26]. The specific and consistent expression of these factors across trichome clusters further validates their role in trichome identity. In addition to confirming previously known regulators, our analysis revealed several transcription factors not previously associated with trichome biology, including BEL1 (TALE family), ANL2 (HD-ZIP family), and NGA1 (B3 family), suggesting potential new regulatory inputs into trichome development. Conversely, six transcription factors enriched in non-trichome epidermal cluster, AT4G36870 (BEL1-LIKE HOMEODOMAIN 2, BLH2, TALE family), AT2G41940 (ZINC FINGER PROTEIN 8, ZFP8, C2H2 family), AT2G43010 (PHYTOCHROME INTERACTING FACTOR 4, PIF4, bHLH family), AT2G18300 (HOMOLOG OF BEE2 INTERACTNG WITH IBH 1, HBI1, bHLH family), AT3G59060 (PHYTOCHROME INTERACTING FACTOR 3-LIKE6, PIF5, bHLH family), and AT4G36540 (BR ENHANCED EXPRESSION 2, BEE2, bHLH family), displayed minimal expression in trichomes but elevated expression in epidermal cells (Fig. 2D), consistent with their exclusion from trichome fate specification. The prevalence of bHLH transcription factors among these depleted genes underscores their broader roles in epidermal cell regulation rather than trichome specialization. Notably, of these six factors, only ZFP8 has been functionally characterized in the context of trichome biology, with evidence linking it to trichome initiation in Arabidopsis and fiber development in cotton [27]. Cross-dataset comparisons can verify the accuracy of marker gene identification. Accordingly, we validated the expression profiles of trichome-enriched and -depleted genes using historical microarray data from isolated Arabidopsis thaliana trichomes and non-trichome leaf samples [28]. This analysis confirmed that the 101 trichome-enriched genes were significantly upregulated in trichomes, whereas trichome-depleted genes exhibited elevated expression in leaf samples lacking trichomes (Fig. 2E, Figure S5). Furthermore, GO enrichment analysis revealed functional categories for enriched genes, including trichome morphogenesis, trichome differentiation, trichome branching, epithelial cell differentiation, and mitotic cell cycle processes, potentially underscoring their relevance to trichome development, whereas depleted genes were associated with immune response, offering critical insights into their divergent biological roles (Figs. 2 F and S6, Table S6).

Given that Arabidopsis trichomes are among the most extensively characterized single-cell types with well-defined developmental trajectories, they provide a valuable reference system for comparative analysis with cotton (Gossypium hirsutum) fiber development, a trait of significant agricultural relevance. While trichome development mechanisms may differ between Arabidopsis and cotton, comparing their transcriptional programs can reveal both conserved developmental principles and species-specific regulatory features, thereby advancing our understanding of fiber cell initiation and differentiation across plant lineages. To leverage this comparative framework, we analyzed single-cell transcriptomic data from cotton ovules [29] and identified distinct clusters corresponding to epidermis, fiber, and outer pigment layer cells, with consistent patterns across biological replicates (Figures S7A and S7B). Projection of fiber-specific marker gene expression onto UMAP embeddings clearly delineated the fiber cluster (Figure S7C), while a heatmap of cell type-specific markers, together with associated GO terms, highlighted key functional roles such as cellulose biosynthesis and cell wall organization (Figure S7D; Table S7). Intersection analyses revealed substantial shared molecular features between these two systems: 26 genes were commonly enriched in both Arabidopsis trichomes and cotton fibers, including core trichome regulators such as GL2 (Figures S7E and S7G), while 12 genes showed coordinated depletion in both cell types (Figure S7F; Table S8). Integration of these intersecting genes into a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network further identified conserved regulatory modules, providing insight into shared molecular circuitry underlying these specialized epidermal cell types (Figure S7G). Consistent with these findings, GO enrichment analyses of trichome- and fiber-enriched genes revealed overlapping pathways, including cuticle development and mitotic cell cycle processes (Figure S7H), whereas trichome- and fiber-depleted genes showed enrichment for cell wall-related processes (Figure S7I). This comparative analysis facilitates the identification of candidate genes potentially important for cotton fiber development and provides a framework for future functional validation and crop improvement.

Developmental branches of epidermal and trichome formation in Arabidopsis

In the study of Arabidopsis trichomes, resolving how these specialized cells branch out from the epidermis is a fundamental question. To address this, it is necessary to construct developmental trajectories of epidermal cell differentiation into trichomes. Therefore, we extracted epidermal cells, proliferating cells, and trichome cells from atlases representing four developmental stages. These datasets were re-integrated and clustered, followed by pseudotime analysis using Monocle2 (Fig. 3). The inclusion of proliferating cells proved to be critical, as they appeared to represent a transitional state connecting the epidermal and trichome lineages, thereby providing a dynamic perspective on cell fate transitions (Fig. 1 A). In the re-integrated atlas, the proliferating cells were strategically positioned between the epidermal and trichome clusters. Notably, an analysis of cell-type composition within individual clusters revealed that clusters C3 and C13 consisted of a hybrid population of both epidermal and proliferating cells. This co-occurrence suggests that a subset of epidermal cells has already acquired proliferative characteristics, identifying them as potential transitional intermediates poised for trichome differentiation (Fig. 3 A). To minimize interference from other epidermal subpopulations, we focused on clusters “0,” “3,” “4,” “10,” “12,” “13,” and “16,” which were selected for their relevance to the trichome lineage and their proximity to proliferating cell populations. Clusters 10, 12, and 16 were identified as trichome lineage clusters, whereas clusters 0, 3, 4, and 13, which are enriched with proliferating cells, were included to capture transitional states. Notably, cell-cell communication analysis of the selected clusters using PlantPhoneDB (Figure S8) revealed prominent signaling activity, especially among clusters 0, 4, and 12, with particularly strong interactions between clusters 4 and 12, further underscoring the importance of cluster 4. Moreover, the ligand AT1G05850 (CHITINASE-LIKE PROTEIN, ATCTL1) exhibited exceptionally high expression in the interactions among C4-C12, C0-C12, and C12-C12. This finding is noteworthy because ATCTL1 has been reported to be highly expressed in both Arabidopsis trichomes [30] and cotton fiber cells [31], implicating its role in cell-cell communication during trichome cell development. The receptors associated with ATCTL1-AT2G22125 (CELLULOSE SYNTHASE-INTERACTIVE PROTEIN 1, CSI1), AT4G32410 (CELLULOSE SYNTHASE 1, CESA1), AT5G05170 (CELLULOSE SYNTHASE 3, CESA3), AT5G49720 (KORRIGAN 1, KOR1), and AT5G60920 (COBRA, COB)-are known to participate in cell wall biosynthesis and remodeling [32–34], a function consistent with previously established protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks (Figure S7G).

Ultimately, pseudotime ordering traced the developmental trajectory from proliferating progenitor cells through intermediate-state cells to fully differentiated trichomes. The proliferating cells were defined as the starting point, as the trajectory analysis revealed a bifurcation between epidermal and trichome lineages, with cluster C0 (predominantly epidermal cells) positioned on one branch and clusters C3 and C13 (which exhibit mixed epidermal and proliferative characteristics) occupying the trichome branch, thereby marking the transition from undifferentiated progenitors to lineage-committed cells.(Fig. 3 C). A key finding was the identification of branch point 3, which effectively distinguished the trichome lineage from alternative epidermal cell fates. Differentially expressed genes associated with this branch point were classified into three subgroups. Subgroup 2, comprising 267 genes, displayed branch point–specific expression and was functionally enriched in processes related to ribosome biogenesis, ribonucleoprotein complex assembly, and protein translation. Subgroup 1, containing 117 genes, was primarily expressed toward the epidermal cell lineage, though some were also active during trichome development, with functional enrichment in transcriptional and developmental regulation. Subgroup 3, comprising 118 genes, was predominantly expressed in the trichome lineage, with functional enrichment in trichome development, fatty acid metabolism, and cell fate determination, suggesting these genes play critical roles in the divergence between trichome and epidermal lineages (Fig. 3D). We further performed trajectory-specific differential gene analysis focused on the trichome branch, which also resolved into three subgroups. Subgroup C2 genes were mainly expressed at the early developmental stages and were enriched in processes involving plant energy metabolism, biosynthesis, environmental signal responses, and growth regulation; notably, the trichome patterning pathway was significantly enriched, supporting their role in early developmental programming. Subgroup C1 genes were expressed during intermediate transitional stages and enriched in pathways related to environmental signal response and specialized metabolic processes. Subgroup C3 genes were predominantly expressed in late-stage development and strongly enriched in trichome-associated pathways, including trichome differentiation, morphogenesis, and branching, underscoring their functions in cell growth and structural development (Fig. 3D). This analysis reveals a clear developmental trajectory from epidermal to mature trichomes and identifies a key branch point separating trichome and epidermal fates. Branch-associated genes capture early regulatory programs, transitional states, and late morphogenetic pathways. These genes provide valuable targets for studying epidermal cell fate specification and trichome development.

Developmental dynamics in trichome from initiation to branching in Arabidopsis

In addition to characterizing the lineage transition from epidermal cells to trichomes, trichome development itself comprises distinct stages, ranging from initiation to nuclear endoreplication and branching. To investigate these developmental stages at higher resolution, we extracted all trichome clusters (clusters C16, C12, and C10, Fig. 4 A). Based on this classification, we performed pseudotime trajectory analysis using Monocle3, which revealed a developmental progression from C16 to C12 and ultimately to C10 (Fig. 4B). To validate that these three subclusters correspond to different developmental stages, we examined the expression of two marker genes, GL1 and MYB106. Previous studies have shown that GL1 primarily regulates trichome initiation [15, 22, 35], whereas MYB106 negatively regulates trichome branching and is not expressed during the branching stage [24, 36]. Consistent with these findings, GL1 expression was restricted to cluster C16, while MYB106 was expressed in both C16 and C12 but absent from C10 (Fig. 4 C). To investigate co-expression dynamics during trichome development, we applied hdWGCNA to construct gene modules correlated with trichome pseudotime. This analysis revealed coordinated transcriptional programs corresponding to key differentiation phases-initiation in C16, nuclear replication in C12, and branching in C10 (Figs. 4D-F). Spatial mapping of these modules onto UMAP embeddings confirmed their cluster-specific localization, supporting their functional relevance (Fig. 4G). Building on this framework, we further explored stage-specific co-expression networks and identified six distinct modules (Table S9). Examination of module expression across subclusters revealed phase-specific patterns: the yellow module (96 genes) was highly expressed in C16 during early pseudotime, associated with metabolic processes, trichome morphogenesis, developmental regulation, and defense responses; the brown and blue modules were predominantly active in C10, with the brown module peaking in later stages and enriched for RNA processing, environmental responses, immune defense, signal transduction, and metabolic transport; the green and turquoise modules were enriched in C12, with turquoise slightly more active in early stages, related to epidermal structure formation, material metabolism, and transport, and green associated with cell wall biosynthesis, cell structure, and metabolism. Clustering of genes within each module highlighted top candidates, such as AT2G29890 (VLN1), AT2G42840 (PROTODERMAL FACTOR 1, PDF1), and AT1G68910 (WPP DOMAIN-INTERACTING PROTEIN 2, WIT2) in the yellow module, which, although not previously linked to trichomes, may contribute to initiation. These module genes provide a valuable resource for future functional studies on trichome development.

In addition to investigating the genes within each module, we also included previously studied trichome-related genes (Table S10) to explore their expression patterns across different developmental stages. Interestingly, these genes exhibited distinct expression patterns at different stages (Figure S10). Two key genes associated with the endoreplication phase, AT5G02820 (ROOT HAIRLESS 2, RHL2) and AT3G20780 (ELONGATED HYPOCOTYL 6, HYP6), exhibited a distinct expression gradient across the trichome subclusters. While their overall expression frequency remained relatively low, both genes were primarily enriched in the C12 subcluster. Notably, these genes also showed detectable expression within the C16 cluster, suggesting their activation begins at the initiation stage before peaking during the intermediate phase. This transitional expression pattern further strengthens the hypothesis that C12 represents a critical intermediate state in trichome development. Additionally, we analyzed 101 Trichome-enriched genes and found that, aside from some genes that were expressed in both C12 and C10 subclusters, each subcluster harbored genes with specific high expression (Figure S11). Notably, nine genes were predominantly expressed in the C16 subcluster, including AT2G16586, AT2G01020, AT1G68910 (WIT2), AT2G47240 (LONG-CHAIN ACYL-COA SYNTHASE 1, LACS1), AT3G50240 (KINESIN-RELATED PROTEIN, KICP-02), AT4G13840 (ECERIFERUM 26, CER26), AT2G26250 (3-KETOACYL-COA SYNTHASE 10, KCS10), AT5G24990, and AT5G47820 (FRAGILE FIBER 1, FRA1). We further conducted functional enrichment analysis on the 101 genes divided into three groups based on their expression across the three subclusters. Genes highly expressed in the C16 subcluster were predominantly enriched in processes related to mitotic spindle organization, the mitotic cell cycle, cytoskeleton organization, microtubule-associated movement, cell wall assembly, fatty acid metabolism, and wax biosynthesis. These processes shared some characteristics with epidermal cells, particularly in fatty acid metabolism and wax biosynthesis. In the C12 subcluster, the functional enrichment shifted toward cell wall biosynthesis, particularly cellulose biosynthesis and plant-type cell wall biogenesis, as well as cell cycle and division processes, including mitotic cell cycle, nuclear division, and meiotic cell cycle. Additionally, C12 was enriched in organ morphogenesis regulation, such as regulation of root development, pollen development, and plant epidermis development, as well as trichoblast differentiation. Lastly, genes highly expressed in the C10 subcluster were primarily enriched in processes related to plant cell wall biosynthesis, carbohydrate metabolism, and RNA processing and transport (Figure S11).

Using SCENIC regulatory network analysis, we reconstructed the regulatory network of previously identified trichome-specific highly expressed transcription factors, successfully identifying the top 5 genes regulated by each transcription factor. The results also unveiled potential regulatory interactions among AT1G79840 (GL2), AT1G02065 (SPL8), and AT3G01140 (MYB106) (Fig. 4H). Notably, the regulatory activities of these transcription factors exhibit trichome developmental stage-specific patterns: AT1G02065 (SPL8) and AT3G01140 (MYB106) display regulatory activity primarily in the C16 and C12 subpopulations, but lack activity during the branching stage; AT4G00730 (ARABIDOPSIS THALIANA HOMEODOMAIN PROTEIN, AHDP) is predominantly active in the mid-stage C12 subpopulation; AT5G41410 (BEL1) shows regulatory activity mainly in the mid-to-late stages, but is inactive at the initiation stage; whereas GL2 and MYB23 maintain regulatory activity throughout all stages of trichome development (Fig. 4I). These findings elucidate the dynamic, stage-specific regulatory mechanisms governing trichome development and provide a comprehensive reconstruction of its developmental phases.

Stage-specific LncRNA signatures in trichome development

Plant genomes produce a large number of lncRNAs, which typically function in a context-specific manner and play critical roles in regulating diverse biological processes [37]. However, the landscape of lncRNAs in Arabidopsis trichomes remains largely unexplored. Recent studies have demonstrated that single-cell transcriptomic data is highly effective for investigating the transcriptional heterogeneity of lncRNAs and their associated gene regulatory networks [38, 39]. Therefore, we performed a re-alignment analysis on the dataset with the highest sample size, Seedling_12d, using an expanded reference genome that incorporated both protein-coding genes and additional lncRNA annotations (see Table S11 for the complete lncRNA list). The resulting cell clusters were annotated using established marker genes (Figure S12A), and each cell type expressed specific marker genes, ultimately capturing trichome cluster (Fig. 5 A). To validate this annotation, we calculated the expression activity of five trichome-related pathways: trichome differentiation (GO:0010026), branching (GO:0010091), morphogenesis (GO:0010090), patterning (GO:0048629), and DNA endoreduplication (GO:0042023). The trichome cluster displayed the highest activity across all five pathways compared to other cell types (Figure S12B), confirming the accuracy of our classification. To further resolve the developmental dynamics, we performed sub-clustering on the epidermis and trichome populations, encompassing 5,072 trichome cells and 22,428 epidermis cells (Figure S13A). Pseudotime analysis successfully reconstructed a developmental trajectory originating from epidermal cells towards trichome formation, showing clear lineage separation (Figure S13B). A key finding was the identification of branching point 2, which effectively distinguished the trichome lineage from other epidermal cell fates. Differentially expressed genes associated with this branching point were categorized into three distinct subgroups (see Table S13 for the specific list). Notably, we identified varying numbers of lncRNAs with lineage-specific expression patterns: Subgroup 1 included 67 lncRNAs specific to the branching point, Subgroup 2 comprised 82 lncRNAs predominantly expressed in the epidermal branch, and Subgroup 3 contained 102 lncRNAs primarily active in the trichome lineage (Figure S13C). Within the trichome branch, lncRNA expression was stage-dependent (Table S14): 51 lncRNAs were identified in the early-stage (C2), 12 in the mid-stage (C3), and 58 in the late-stage (C1).Validation of the top three trichome-specific highly expressed lncRNAs revealed that AthLNC021175, AthLNC019471, and AthLNC020024 were prominently expressed in trichome branch, with higher expression in the later stages.

Interestingly, further subdivision of trichome cluster resulted in three subgroups: C5, C6, and C7 (Fig. 5 C). Pseudotime analysis reconstructed the developmental trajectory from the C7 subgroup to the C6 subgroup, and finally to the C5 subgroup (Fig. 5D). By confirming the expression of GL1 and MYB106, the C7 subgroup was defined as the initiation stage of trichome development, the C5 subgroup as the branching stage, and the C6 subgroup as the intermediate stage (Fig. 5E). Subsequent Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) identified four regulatory modules (Fig. 5 F) with stage-specific patterns. The yellow module peaked in the C7 subgroup (early pseudotime), the blue module in the C6 subgroup (mid-pseudotime), and the turquoise and brown modules in the C5 subgroup (mid-to-late pseudotime) (Fig. 5G, H). By screening for module-specific lncRNAs (Table S15), we identified three candidates (AT3G06355, AthLNC006105, and AthLNC012976) specifically highly expressed in the C7 subgroup, suggesting their roles in trichome initiation. The C5 subgroup harbored the highest diversity of lncRNAs (Fig. 5I), many of which were trichome-specific, further elucidating the complex regulatory landscape of trichome maturation. These findings provide critical insights into the regulatory roles of lncRNAs in Arabidopsis trichome development, offering a foundation for understanding their context-specific contributions to cell fate determination and developmental trajectories, which could guide future functional studies and genetic engineering in plant developmental biology.

Discussion

This research employs snRNA-seq to develop a comprehensive transcriptomic atlas of Arabidopsis thaliana trichomes, elucidating their developmental progression from epidermal cells to branching stages while uncovering the regulatory contributions of protein-coding genes and lncRNAs. The detection of trichome-specific markers, including GL1, GL2, MYB106, and the newly identified BEL1, demonstrates the superiority of snRNA-seq in capturing cellular diversity unattainable by bulk RNA-seq. Pseudotime trajectory analysis, corroborated by the expression dynamics of GL1 and MYB106, delineates distinct developmental stages from initiation to branching, providing detailed resolution of trichome developmental progression.

The identification of novel transcription factors such as BEL1, ANL2, and NGA1 expands the known regulatory landscape, with BEL1’s increased expression during branching indicating a specialized late-stage function beyond its established role in ovule development [40–42]. Comparative analyses with cotton fiber cells reveal conservation of regulators like GL2, suggesting potential translational insights for fiber biology research. Functional enrichment analyses underscore the importance of pathways including trichome morphogenesis and cell wall biosynthesis in determining trichome structural characteristics. The integration of lncRNAs reveals their stage-specific regulatory influence, with AT3G06355, AthLNC006105, and AthLNC012976 playing key roles in the initiation phase, and a greater diversity of lncRNAs emerging during branching, supported by the activation of turquoise and brown modules associated with structural development.

Collectively, this work establishes a stage-resolved expression atlas integrating protein-coding genes, transcription factors, and lncRNAs across Arabidopsis trichome development, revealing a dynamic regulatory network (Fig. 6). These findings provide a molecular foundation for investigating trichome-related traits, including defense mechanisms and epidermal specialization, in plant biology. Conclusions

This study establishes a comprehensive single-nucleus transcriptomic atalas for Arabidopsis trichome development by defining the progression from epidermal cells to branching stages and revealing regulatory features that cannot be captured by bulk RNA sequencing. Through marker identification, pseudotime reconstruction, and cross-species comparison, we clarify the roles of both known and newly identified regulators, including BEL1, ANL2, NGA1 and several stage-specific lncRNAs, that are associated with trichome initiation and structural maturation. The coordinated expression landscape generated here provides a valuable resource for understanding the regulatory network underlying trichome development and offers a molecular framework for investigating trichome-related traits in other plant species.

Methods

Pre-processing of Raw sc/snRNA-Seq data

The raw single-cell RNA sequencing dataset was processed using Cell Ranger 7.1.0 (10× Genomics). The analytical pipeline encompassed read alignment, processing of raw single-cell RNA sequencing data, and generation of gene-cell matrices. Reference genome and GTF annotation files for Arabidopsis thaliana were obtained from the TAIR10 database, utilizing the TAIR10 genome version and the Araport11 annotation file. For Gossypium hirsutum, genome and GTF annotation files were retrieved from the NCBI GenBank database, employing the Gossypium_hirsutum_v2.1 genome version. The cellranger mkref command was executed with the --genome, --fasta, and --genes parameters to construct the reference genome. Single-cell gene expression counts were generated using the cellranger count command, specifying the --id, --transcriptome, --fastqs, and --sample arguments. Sequence reads were aligned to the respective reference genomes using the STAR aligner (v.2.5.1b) [43]. The resulting gene-cell matrices, designated as filtered_gene_bc_matrices and raw_feature_bc_matrix, served as the foundational data for subsequent downstream analyses.

Arabidopsis LncRNA reference database

To enable the detection and quantification of lncRNAs in Arabidopsis snRNA data, we performed re-alignment of the dataset using a meticulously curated lncRNA reference database. This database was developed by integrating comprehensive lncRNA annotations reported by He et al. [38]. The reference dataset encompassed 12,693 long intergenic non-coding RNAs (lincRNAs) and 12,155 natural antisense lncRNAs (NAT lncRNAs).

Utilizing cellbender to estimate background noise and perform correction

To mitigate background noise in droplet-based Arabidopsis snRNA-seq data, we utilized CellBender v0.3.0 [44] to estimate and correct ambient RNA contamination. The raw_gene_bc_matrices generated by CellRanger were imported into CellBender with default parameters. Following processing, the resultant cellbender_filtered.h5 file was employed for downstream analyses, including dimensionality reduction and clustering.

Quality control and data analysis

The filtered.h5 file generated by CellBender was imported into R using the Seurat package (v4.3.0) [45] for downstream scRNA-seq analysis. To eliminate dead cells and doublets, cells with unique gene counts > 5,000 or < 200, or with mitochondrial gene expression > 5%, were filtered out. A minimum of three expressed genes per cell was required for retention. The SCTransform normalization method in Seurat was applied independently to each dataset replicate. Data integration across multiple groups was performed using the PrepSCTIntegration, FindIntegrationAnchors, and IntegrateData functions. Principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted using RunPCA with default parameters, followed by UMAP embedding via the RunUMAP function with parameters dims = 1:50 and n.neighbors = 10. Clustering was performed using FindNeighbors and FindClusters with resolution = 0.5 and n.iter = 100. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using FindAllMarkers with the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (test.use="wilcox”) and parameters logfc.threshold = 0.25 and min.pct = 0.25. For sub-clustering, the subset function was used to isolate specific clusters, followed by re-application of SCTransform, RunUMAP, FindNeighbors, and FindClusters with dims = 1:50 and resolution = 0.5. DEGs within sub-clusters were identified using FindAllMarkers on the RNA assay (normalized counts) with the Wilcoxon rank-sum test and a logfc.threshold = 0.25. After re-mapping the sequencing data with the lncRNA-annotated reference genome, the same analytical pipeline was applied to the updated count matrices following the procedures described above.

Gene functional enrichment analysis

Functional enrichment analysis of cluster-specific differentially expressed genes was performed using the clusterProfiler package (v3.16.1) [46]. The compareCluster and gofilter (level = 3) functions were employed with the following parameters: fun = ‘enrichGO’, OrgDb = ‘org.At.tair.db’, ont = ‘ALL’, pAdjustMethod = ‘BH’, pvalueCutoff = 0.05, and qvalueCutoff = 0.05. This approach identified significantly enriched GO terms associated with each cluster, adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini-Hochberg method.

Monocle2 epidermis and trichome branching pseudotime analysis

To investigate the developmental trajectories and fate determination of trichomes and epidermal cells, we utilized the Monocle package (v2.28.0) [47] for pseudotime trajectory analysis. Initially, the dispersionTable function was applied to compute expression variance for each gene across cells. Highly variable genes were selected based on their average expression levels (mean_expression parameter) to define developmental progression. Dimensionality reduction was performed to project the data into a two-dimensional space using the DDRTree method (max_components = 2). This enabled the representation of cellular transitions in a reduced-dimensional framework. Cells were ordered along a pseudotime trajectory using the orderCells function, and the resulting trajectory was visualized with the plot_cell_trajectory function. To identify genes exhibiting pseudotime-dependent or branch-specific expression patterns, the Branch Expression Analysis Model (BEAM) was employed. Genes demonstrating significant branch-dependent expression were visualized using the plot_genes_branched_heatmap function within the Monocle framework.

Monocle3 trichome development analysis

To elucidate the developmental dynamics of trichome differentiation, we utilized Monocle3 (v1.0.0) for pseudotime trajectory inference, capitalizing on its graph-based framework to model complex cellular transitions [48]. Trichome were extracted from the integrated Seurat object based on the celltype = “Trichome” annotation. The dataset was converted into a Monocle3 cell data set and preprocessed using preprocess_cds with principal component analysis (PCA, num_dim = 50). Cell clustering was performed via cluster_cells employing the Louvain algorithm. Developmental trajectories were constructed using learn_graph with a minimal branch length of 10, and cells were ordered along pseudotime with order_cells.

HdWGCNA co-expression network analysis

Co-expression network analysis was performed on a Seurat object using the hdWGCNA R package to identify co-expressed gene modules [49]. Genes expressed in ≥ 5% of cells were selected, and metacells were constructed based on seurat_clusters (k = 20, target_metacells = 270). Expression data for Trichome_C10, C12, and C16 clusters were normalized, and a co-expression network was built with optimal soft thresholding. Modules were identified, visualized via dendrogram, dot plot, and pseudotime trajectory plots, and hub gene networks were plotted.

SCENIC regulatory network analysis

Trichome-specific transcription factor (TF) regulatory network analysis was performed using the scPlant package, a versatile framework for analyzing single-cell transcriptomic data in plants [50]. The analysis focused on identifying gene regulatory networks within epidermal cells by inferring TF activity and their regulatory interactions. The SCENIC method was employed to reconstruct co-expression modules, followed by the identification of key TFs that regulate gene expression specifically in trichome.

PlantPhoneDB cell communication analysis

Cell-to-cell communication in Arabidopsis was analyzed using PlantPhoneDB [51]. Ligand-receptor interactions were retrieved from the database for Arabidopsis (LR_pair_ath). The LRscore function was applied to single-cell RNA-seq data (processed with SCTransform) to compute interaction scores, with a minimum percentage of 0.1 for ligand-receptor expression. A significance threshold of P-value < 0.05 was applied to filter the results. Interaction types (autocrine and paracrine) were classified based on ligand-receptor cell-type matching.

DFT simulation to study the mechanical, electronic, thermal and superconducting properties of borocarbides materials AX2B2C where A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt
Authors: Md. Atikur Rahman, Asma Khatun, Dayal Chandra Roy, Mezba Chowdhury, Ahmad Irfan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The invention of superconductivity in quaternary borocarbides has been attracted great attraction in the recent time. Here, the elastic, electronic, thermal, superconducting and optical features of borocarbides materials AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) have been explored through ab-initio method with the help of CASTEP code. To explore the physical features of AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) we used the well-known density functional simulation (DFT) in addition to generalized gradient approximations (GGA). The examined structural features displayed well covenant to the experimental data. The dynamical stability of the materials AX2B2C (A = Y, La and X = Pd, Pt) was ensured by the positive phonon dispersion curves where the heavier atoms (Y, La, Pd and Pt) contribute most to create the acoustic modes and lighter B and C atoms contribute most to create the optical modes. The investigated elastic constants (Cij) hold Born’s constancy standards and confirmed the mechanical constancy of the titled materials. The large Young’s, bulk, and shear moduli of Pt-based superconductors AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pt) than Pd- based borocarbides ensure their high resistance to volume, length and shear deformation. The studied borocarbides phases show ductile nature in order to their Pugh’s and Poisson’s ratio values (B/G > 1.75 and υ > 0.26). The high machinable index confirm the damage-tolerant, high lubricant and lower friction values which ensures the industrial applications of the titled phases. The moderate Debye temperature ensure the thermal conductive nature of these compounds. The extremely elevated melting temperature and very low minimum thermal conductivity (Kmin) confirm the TBC like behavior of these materials whereas the compounds LaPt2B2C and ThPt2B2C contributes the most. The calculated transition temperatures, Tc of AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) show well accord with the experimental transition temperatures (TC). Finally, extraordinary reflectivity in the infrared energy site also ensure the TBC nature of these superconductors.

Introduction

The innovation of superconductivity in quaternary borocarbides has been concerned great attraction among several researchers for a long period. Therefore, a lot of experimental as well as hypothetical works were executed through several research ways to realize the superconductive mechanism in these materials1. Specially the innovation of relatively high superconductivity (Tc = 23 K) in borocarbide system Y-Pd-B-C by Cava et al.2. has attracted much attention. The BCS type phonon mediated superconductivity was existed in these materials which is assured from heat capacity analysis and boron isotope effect experiments3,4. A lot of similar types of boron carbides (intermetallic) materials with moderately high transition temperature such as Y-Ni-B-C (Tc~ 12 K)5 and LnNi2B2C (Ln = Ho, Er, Tm, Y, Lu) (Tc~ 3 to 17 K)6 were discovered by Nagarajan et al. and Cava et al. in 1994 respectively. Basically, the materials with borocarbides are belong to ThCr2Si2-type layered structure having I4/mmm space group (No. 139). The general formula of the quaternary borocarbides system is AX2B2C (where A = Sc, Y, Th, or rare-earth elements and X = Ni, Pd, Pt and Ru). Besides these types of borocarbides, new borocarbides LaIr2B2C and LaRh2B2C belong to LuNi2B2C-type7,8 structures have been synthesized in 1994 through arc-melting method. However these materials do not show superconductivity below the transition temperature of 1.4 K due to their position alteration of the Fermi level. Several types of borocarbides like RRh2B2C (R = La to Er lanthanides except Eu) have been synthesized by Ye and coworkers9 to investigate the alteration in structural features of these materials. The have been observed that when R is going from La site to Er then the lattice constant ‘a’ is decreasing and ‘c’ is increasing. In order to see the superconductivity in the borocarbides materials scientist Paiva and his coworkers proposed a layering model in 200310. In their research work they have offered that the superconductivity is only conceivable in these borocarbides above a certain density as well as critical band offset. In 2009 V.K Anand et al.. discover the heavy fermion manners in PrRh2B2C11. Superconductivity phenomena in borocarbides YPd2B2C, YPt2B2C and LaPt2B2C has been found by A. P. Durajski et al. in 201612. In 2015 H.M.Tütüncü et al.. investigated the BCS-like superconductivity in borocarbides superconductor which are belong to LuNi2B2C-type system13. After very long period in 2020, H.Y. Uzunok investigated the detail physical features of LaT2B2C (T = ir, Rh) through DFT simulation14. In this work he calculated the transition temperatures of these compounds which are 0.22 K and 0.20 K respectively. Very recently (in 2023) Viktor Christiansson and Philipp Werner investigated the electronic, dynamical (phonon dispersion curve) superconducting properties of various borocarbides system formulated as RM2B2C (R = Sc, Y, Th and elements of rare-earth and M = Ni, Pd and Pt)15. Though a lot of experimental works have been executed to realize the superconductive phenomena in borocarbides system but their still missing of hypothetical work on the physical properties Pd and Pt based borocarbides materials in literature. However in 2017 Liu et al. investigated the mechanical features and structural stability of borocarbides YNi2B2C and LuNi2B2C at hydrostatic pressure through first-principles method16.

Therefore in the present work, we have focused to explore the detail physical features containing mechanical, electronic, thermal, superconducting and optical characteristics of Pd and Pt based borocarbides AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) through DFT replication with CASTEP code. Among the studied phases the Pd- based borocarbides YPd2B2C, LaPd2B2C and YPd2B2C are belong to body-centered tetragonal structures similar to LuNi2B2C type structure having space group of I4/mmm (139). The transition or critical temperature (Tc) of borocarbides superconductors YPd2B2C, LaPd2B2C and ThPd2B2C are (21–23) K, (1.7–1.9) K and (14.4–14.6) K respectively15. The Pt-based borocarbides YPt2B2C, LaPt2B2C and ThPt2B2C are also belong to the same crystal system as Pd and have same space group I4/mmm but have dissimilar superconducting temperature. The transition temperature (Tc) YPt2B2C, LaPt2B2C and ThPt2B2C are (10–11) K, (10.5–11) K and (6.7-7) K15. All these superconductors have significant applications in electronic, optical and medical equipment as they have magnetic and superconducting properties. Though a lot of experimental work on the borocarbides materials are available in literature but the computational work on several physical properties of these materials are still missing. Therefore considering the importance of borocarbides materials the main focus of the current research is to examine the dynamical, mechanical, electronic, optical, thermal and superconducting properties of borocarbides AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) through DFT simulation as well as comparing the investigated results to previous synthesized and theoretical values where available.

Computational methods

The fundamental features such as structural, mechanical, electronic and optical properties of borocarbides materials were executed over the density functional theory (DFT)17,18 with CASTEP (Cambridge Serial Total Energy Package) code. The Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof (PBE) and generalized gradient approximation (GGA) were applied to illustrate the exchange and correlational energy19. The Vanderbilt-like ultrasoft pseudo-potential20 was employed to indicate the interaction between different atoms of the target materials. The Y-4d1 5s2, Th-6s2 6p6 6d2 7s2, La- 5s2 5p6 5d1 6s2, Pd-4d10, Pt-5d9 6s1, B-2s2 2p1 and C-2s2 2p2 valence electrons are utilized to execute the pseudo atomic performances. To represent the reciprocal space via Monkhorst-Pack distinctive k-point arrangement21 with 13 × 13 × 5 and 18 × 18 × 6 grid meshes were used for AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd) and AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pt) compounds with plane wave cutoff energy of 460 eV and 470 eV in turn. For crystal optimization the convergence tolerance was fixed to ultra-file superiority appeared with 5.0 × 10− 6 eV/atom energy, 0.01 eV/Å extreme force, 0.02 GPa stress and 5.0 × 10− 6 Å displacement. Brodyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno (BFGS) routine was adjusted to obtain the ground state of the titled phases. Using the finite stress-strain22 method the elastic constants of the target compounds have been determined embodied in the CASTEP code. Utilizing the Voigt-Reuss-Hill approximations the polycrystalline bulk modulus B and shear modulus G have been calculated through \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\sigma\:}_{i}={C}_{ij}{\varepsilon}_{j}$$\end{document}. To explore the 3D anisotropy contours the ELATE code was employed. The optical features were executed by applying the Kramers–Kronig relations. The thermal factors are computed exhausting the investigated values of stiffness coefficients. By applying the density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) accompanied with the finite displacement method with CASTEP Code the phonon dispersion curves and phonon density of states have been investigated.

Structural features

The superconducting Ni-based borocarbides AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) have body centered tetragonal crystal structures which are belong to LuNi2B2C- type structure. These materials have I4/mmm (No. 139) space group15. Using the manufactured lattice constants15 the general structural configurations of the studied systems are shown in Fig. 1. To obtain the ground state energy (lowest energy state at absolute zero temperature, and zero pressure) of AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) as a function of c/a, we have used a full potential linearized augmented plane wave (FP-LAPW) with generalized gradient approximation of Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof as implemented in WIEN2k code23. The WIEN2k program is based on the augmented plane wave plus local orbitals (APW + lo) method to solve the Kohn–Sham equations of density functional theory. The APW + lo method, which considers all electrons (core and valence) self-consistently in a full-potential treatment, is implemented very efficiently in WIEN2k. The convergence criteria of energy and charge were set to 10− 4 Ry and 0.001e, respectively. Mechanical and elastic features

Material’s stiffness constants are very vital and crucial factors intended for describing their mechanical features and finding the elastic limit to externally applied stress aimed for practical applications. The stiffness constants have been played significance parts in several research areas including materials science, physics of condensed matter, engineering, chemistry and geophysics over the course of time26,27. The material’s stability, elasticity, toughness, ductility, breakability, thermal properties, chemical bonding as well as anisotropic manner can be easily understood by studying the elastic constants28. The material’s stiffness constants also offer a connection among the mechanical and dynamical manner of solids and deliver substantial evidence about their reaction to the exterior stress described by bulk, shear and Young’s moduli. Numerous fundamental parameters such as melting temperature, phonon spectra, equation of states (EOS), as well as Debye temperature have also been correlated to the elastic constants29. As the studied materials have tetragonal configuration so they oblige six autonomous elastic parameters C11, C12, C13, C33, C44, and C6630. Bulk factor B is also linked to the shear elastic constant C44 which is used to evaluate the material’s machinability and is defined by μm = B/C44 proposed by Sun et al.38. Shear factor, G would be used to describe the capability of a solid to resists shear or transverse distortion. The average shear ability of materials is also measured by this factor. The relatively large shear modulus of YPt2B2C, LaPt2B2C and ThPt2B2C certify that they are more capable to oppose the shear forces that are performing during machining.

The reply to the uniaxial stress over all the directions is represented by Young’s modulus, E which is mostly responsible to toughness of materials. The higher E value specifies higher stiffness of material. It has a decent connection with the thermal shock resistance R, as R ∞ 1/E41 whereas lesser Young’s modulus presents greater thermal shock resistance and vice versa. A material with higher thermal shock resistance is more suitable for using as a TBC like material. From Table 3; Fig. 5a it is observable that the higher Young’s moduli of YPt2B2C, LaPt2B2C and ThPt2B2C ensure their huge ability to oppose the longitudinal stress and conversely the combatively lower Young’s moduli of YPd2B2C, LaPd2B2C and ThPd2B2C confirm the high thermal shock resistance. Hence the materials YPd2B2C, LaPd2B2C and ThPd2B2C are auxiliary suitable in thermal barrier coating materials (TBC) than others.

Solid materials can be classified in to two clusters such as ductile or brittle according to the Pugh’s ratio B/G; whereas ductile manner is declared for B/G > 1.75 and breakable feature is confirmed for B/G < 1.7542. Materials with ductile nature have huge industrial application as they can be simply overextended and delivered any shape as desired. The ductile/brittle nature is also achieved from the study of Poisson’s ratio according to the subsequent condition ν > 0.26 and ν < 0.2643. According to the above criteria the studied phases AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) show ductile nature (see Fig. 5b) therefore the titled borocarbides are suitable for industrial applications.

Another factor which is also related to the sector of manufacturing production named the machinable index (µM) specified through ratio of bulk modulus to shear stress C44. The several factors such as material’s stiffness, plastic strain, lubricant manner, machine tools capacity and so on are related to this factor. The higher value of µM indicates high performance of elastic manner, lower friction and lubricant behavior44. These superconducting materials carry high machinable index compared to many MAX phases45 and other intermetallic compounds46,47 which ensure that AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) have huge possible industrial applications. However, among the studied phases the materials YPd2B2C, ThPd2B2C and YPt2B2C (Fig. 5c) have comparatively more industrial applications than other materials.

Among the polycrystalline factors the material’s hardness (Hv) (Fig. 5d) is fundamental parameter suggest the conflict to the perpetual distortion and ensure the material’s applications in manufacturing. Materials are used in several areas such as in optoelectronics, microelectronics, coating in glasses and heavy-duty products depending upon the values of hardness48. The chemical constancy, topological durability, material strength and scratch resistance properties are openly linked to the material’s hardness. According to hardness value, materials are classified into two categories: soft and hard material. Materials having hardness value greater than 10 GPa are belong to tough materials which are suitable intended for cutting tools and wear-resistant coating purposes whereas materials with Hv value less than 10 GPa are belong to soft catogory materials49 and can be more suitable for machinable and damage-tolerant cases. The investigated results confirm that all the studied phases are belong to soft materials and have damage-tolerant and machinable manner.

It should be mentioned that the atomic bond (B-B) length and ionic size plays an important role in the superconducting state of a material. It is expected that bond length reduction explicitly incorporates greater Coulomb interaction by means of strength electron phonon coupling. We suspected that this phenomenon reflected for the present case of AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) borocarbides. The phenomenon of photo-induced charge related optimal solar energy conversion mechanism is also ensured from absorption coefficient analysis. The types of materials like metallic, semi-conducting, and insulating nature can also be identified from the determination of absorption spectra. Figures 12e and 13e exhibit the absorption profile of AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) borocarbides. The absorption spectra start at 0 eV energy which a common characteristic of metallic materials, confirmed metallic nature of the current compounds. Very strong absorption peaks are detected at nearby 7.5 ~ 12.5 eV and 20 ~ 30 eV which indicates the titled phases are talented for absorbing materials in the UV region. Normally in high absorbing materials the increased of photon absorption is happened and therefore more electrons are excited in valence band and move to conduction band. The materials having this nature are broadly used in optical and optoelectronic devices. Therefore the studied materials can be broadly used such types of devices. However the Y-based borocarbides have little bit smaller absorptive power than La and Th-based borocarbides (see Figs. 12 and 13e).

Figures 12f and 13f illustrate the photoconductivity of the studied borocarbides. Optical conductivity represents the ability to conduct electromagnetic waves of a material. Metallic nature of the target materials is ensured as the conductivity spectra start from zero energy. The same nature is also observed from absorption spectra and electronic band structure analysis. Extremely high conductivity of the investigated borocarbides is observed in the mid and far UV regions. The decreasing nature with increasing energy is also assured from the photoconductivity and absorption spectra. This nature is usual to metallic crystal systems.

Furthermore, reflectivity (R) of the target materials has been computed over the range of 0–35 eV. Figures 12g and 13g depict the reflectivity spectra of the target materials as a function of applied EMW. As can be seen from Figs. 12g and 13g, the reflectivity spectra are found to be maximum at the infrared region and fall below 40% for an enormous portion of visible spectrum. Again, an increase in R values as well as intense peaks are observed in the high-frequency region at around 25 eV to 30 eV. This result suggested the ability to work as good coating materials of AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) in both infrared and higher energy regions. It is observed that the reflectance peaks of about 70% are observed for LaPd2B2C and YPt2B2C compounds. It should be mentioned that the reflectivity of a material depends on many aspects such as chemical composition, surface properties, crystal structure, and incident photon energy. All these factors are reflected in the R values which is crucial to know for developing optical coating materials. It is considered that high reflective materials are beneficial for TBC material. Reasonably, our present compounds can be a good choice for TBC materials, as their better reflectance ability in the both infrared and ultraviolet regions.

Finally, we estimate the energy loss function (L), to comprehend the dielectric energy loss inside the material due to fast-moving electrons. The maximum energy loss in a medium is associated with the plasma frequency (ωp) of a given material at that point where it satisfies the conditions ε2 (ω) < 1 and ε1 (ω) = 072,73. The specialty of plasma frequency is that materials become transparent above this frequency experienced on the compound. Figures 12h and 13h illustrate the energy loss function of AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) borocarbides over a wide range of frequencies (up to 35 eV). Here, we noticed that the maximum energy loss took place in the higher energy state, literally above 20 eV. In case of Pd-based compounds, the peak values are found to be 24 eV for YPd2B2C, 27.5 eV for LaPd2B2C, and 28 eV for ThPd2B2C, respectively. On the other hand, for Pt-based compounds, these values are found to be 26 eV for YPt2B2C, 30 eV for LaPt2B2C, and 30.5 eV for ThPt2B2C, correspondingly. Therefore, above these frequencies, AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt) materials will be transparent. It is obvious from Figs. 12h and 13h, that peak values shifted slightly higher energy when Pd is replaced by Pt. This phenomenon can be ascribed to the fact of dielectric-like response under atomic substitution.

Conclusions

The existing effort is focused to explore the mechanical, electronic, thermal and superconducting features of the titled borocarbide superconductors through DFT versatile method utilizing the CASTEP code. The calculated lattice constants, cell volume as well as internal parameters show healthy consistency to the synthesized values. The investigated elastic constants and phonon dispersion curvatures confirm the mechanical and dynamical steadiness of these phases. Mechanical features such as stiffness constants, moduli of elasticity, hardness, machinable factor, ductile/brittle manner, elastic anisotropy have been investigated and properly discussed. According to bulk, shear and Young’s moduli these phases show hard nature. Ductile performance is ensured from the study of Poisson’s and Pugh’s ratio. Extremely high machinable index ensure the high damage-tolerant, lubricant and lower friction features which confirmed the industrial applications of AX2B2C (A = Y, La, Th and X = Pd, Pt). Elastic anisotropic manner is ensured from several anisotropic factors analysis and also from 3D plots of E, G and υ. Metallic behavior is ensured from band structure and DOS analysis. The involvement in the electrical conductivity at the Fermi level is mainly comes from Y-4d, La-5d, Th-6d states with little bit contribution of C-2p and B-2p states. Mulliken atomic population analysis offers the ionic and covalent bonds in studied phases. The thermal conductive nature of these materials is ensured from the reasonable Debye temperature. The extremely lesser thermal conductivity (Kmin) and elevated melting temperature (Tm) authorize the TBC like behavior of these superconducting materials. Very well consistency is observed between the calculated and experimental transition temperatures (TC). Metallic nature of these phases are assured from dielectric function, photoconductivity and absorption spectrum analysis. Very good conductive nature near the visible and UV regions ensure the possible optoelectronic devices applications of these superconductors. High reflectivity especially in infrared region ensure that these materials should be good choice for TBC materials.

Evaluation of dry eye syndrome in patients with obstructive sleep apnea syndrome based on serum hypoxia-inducible factor 1 alpha (HIF-1α), matrix metalloproteinase 2 (MMP-2), and desmosine levels
Authors: Murat Okutucu, Büşra Sağır, Ünal Şahin, Medeni Arpa, Esra Yılmaz, Feyzahan Uzun, Hüseyin Fındık, Muhammet Kaim, Elvin Halili
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo determine changes in dry eye parameters and serum HIF-1α, MMP-2, and desmosine levels in obstructive sleep apnea syndrome (OSAS) and their correlation.MethodsThis prospective case-control study included 125 patients divided into mild-to-moderate OSAS (n = 40), severe OSAS (n = 46), and control groups (n = 39). Meibomian gland dropout was evaluated with a tomograph and corneal topographer, apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) with polysomnography, and serum HIF-1α, MMP2, and desmosine levels were analyzed using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay.ResultsSerum HIF-1α and MMP2 levels of the participants in the mild-to-moderate and severe OSAS groups were significantly higher, and serum desmosine levels were significantly lower than those in the control group (all p = 0.001). The upper eyelid meibomian gland dropout values were higher in the severe OSAS group than those in the control group (p = 0.001) (Table 1). Lower and total eyelid meibomian gland dropout values of the participants in the severe and mild-to-moderate OSAS groups were higher than those in the control group (all, p = 0,001). The NIKBUT-first, NIKBUT-average, total eyelid abnormality score, FES, and Oxford scores were not significantly different. The SPEED questionnaire scores of the participants in the severe and mild-to-moderate OSAS groups were higher than those in the control group, and the SPEED questionnaire values of the participants in the severe OSAS group were higher than those in the mild-to-moderate OSAS group (all, p = 0,001). The serum HIF-1α and MMP-2 levels of the participants were positively correlated with the total eyelid meibomian gland dropout values (r = 0,208, p < 0,05 and r = 0,227, p < 0,05, respectively).ConclusionsThere was a negative correlation between inflammatory mediators (HIF-1α and MMP-2) and meibomian gland dropout in OSAS. This indicates that there are systemic factors involved in the etiopathogenesis of OSAS damage on the ocular surface. Further research on developing treatment strategies to modulate HIF-1α and MMP-2 may help reduce ocular surface morbidity in patients with OSAS.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12886-025-04420-1.

Introduction

Obstructive sleep apnea syndrome (OSAS) is characterized by transient upper airway resistance caused by repetitive reduction or cessation of airflow owing to partial or complete obstruction of the upper airway during sleep [1]. It is associated with sleep disruption, awakenings, bradycardia, tachycardia, and insufficient oxygen saturation despite increased respiratory effort [1]. Intermittent hypoxia accompanying OSAS adversely affect oculovascular health [2, 3]. Accordingly, OSAS has been associated with several ocular disorders, including floppy eyelid syndrome and changes in the ocular surface and meibomian gland [4–9].

Hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF-1) is a heterodimeric transcription factor consisting of a constitutively expressed β-subunit and an α-subunit that contains an oxygen-dependent degradation (ODD) domain [10]. HIF-1α is suppressed in normoxia but is stable and active in hypoxia [10]. Overexpression of HIF-1α causes vascular endothelial damage via increasing inflammatory activity [10].

Matrix metalloproteinase 2 (MMP-2) is composed of three domains, distinguished by the presence of an additional fibronectin domain inserted into the catalytic domain [11–13]. It may degrade collagen, elastin, fibronectin, gelatin, and laminin, and has both proinflammatory and anti-inflammatory effects on numerous tissues [11–13]. Wan et al. reported that HIF-1α induced an increase in MMP-2. [14]

Desmosine is an amino acid found uniquely in elastin, a protein found in connective tissues such as skin, lungs, elastic arteries, and eyelid tarsus [15–17]. It is a physiologically relevant biomarker of elastin turnover [15–17]. Tarsal elastic fibers are of great significance in the meibomian secretion mechanism [18]. Reduction of elastic fibers around the meibomian ducts may reduce the passive drive for meibomian secretions [18]. No previous study investigated serum desmosine levels in patients with OSAS.

The meibomian glands (MGs) are responsible for producing the lipid layer of the tear film necessary for lubricating the ocular surface and stable tear film [19]. The upper and lower eyelids consisting of MGs are fed by the ophthalmic and lacrimal arteries which are the branches of the internal carotid artery [19]. Hausman et al. performed studies in rats and reported that both energy and perfect blood flow are necessary for lipid synthesis [20].

Many patients with OSAS complain of symptoms of dry eye [4]. Studies have reported that HIF-1α, MMP-2, and elastin degradation increases in OSAS [21]. Increased MMP-2 levels have been associated with dry eye [22]. Based on the above literature, we hypothesized that the levels of HIF-1α, MMP-2, and desmosin, a product of elastin degradation, increase in OSAS, and this increase causes dry eye and meibomian gland loss.

In this study, we aimed to explore the validity of this hypothesis in order to help guide the development of therapeutic strategies for modulating HIF-1α and MMP-2 expression in patients with OSAS.

Materials and methods

This observational case-control study was conducted between February 2022 and August 2023 in the ophthalmology clinic of the Recep Tayyip Erdoğan Training and Research Hospital affiliated with Recep Tayyip Erdoğan University. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Recep Tayyip Erdogan University (Approval ID: 2022/32). The tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki were followed throughout this study. Written informed consent was obtained from all the participants prior to participation. Participants with the complaints of suspected sleep disordered breathing disorders such as loud snoring, witnessed apnea, excessive daytime sleepiness, nocturnal choking, nonrestorative sleep, etc. were evaluated for sleep pathologies and underwent overnight in-laboratory polysomnography (PSG) (GRASS, AS40, Natus Medical, Ontario, Canada) in Recep Tayyip Erdoğan Training and Research Hospital, Sleep Diseases Laboratory before their enrollment. The AHI (apnea-hypopnea index) was assessed for each patient according to the American Academy of Sleep Medicine standards. Participants with AHI < 5 were classified as the control group, those with AHI between 5 and 30 as the mild-to-moderate OSAS group, and those with AHI ≥ 30 as the severe OSAS group. A total of 125 participants, including 40 mild-to-moderate OSAS, 46 severe OSAS, and 39 control groups, were included in the study. As OSAS systemic involvement will similarly affect both eyes, only the right eye of each patient was included in the study. Participants in the study group had not previously received CPAP (continuous positive airway pressure) treatment because it is effectively used in the treatment of OSAS [10], causes dry eye disease,23, and reduces serum HIF-1α levels [24].

Participants with a history of any type of ocular surgery, chemical burns or trauma to the eye, refractive errors (> 3 diopters), contact lens use, allergic conjunctivitis, ocular surface disorders, pseudoexfoliation syndrome, glaucoma, thyroid eye disease, and chronic topical medication use (e.g., artificial tear drops, topical cyclosporine A, and anti-glaucoma drops) were not included in the study. Participants with cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, neurodegenerative disease, chronic inflammatory disease (rheumatoid arthritis, etc.), and collagen disease (polymyositis/dermatomyositis, systemic lupus erythematosus, etc.) were excluded from the study. Participants with a history of heart, kidney, and liver failure; nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs; immunosuppressor drugs; chronic alcohol abuse; and smokers were also excluded.

All participants underwent a detailed ophthalmologic examination, consisting of an auto-refractometer, best-corrected visual acuity, slit-lamp examination, intraocular pressure (IOP) measurement with a Goldmann Applanation tonometer, dilated fundus examination, non-invasive keratograph break-up time (NIKBUT), and meibography measurement. Since tropicamide 0.5% would cause false measurement of NIKBUT, the order of examination was determined as the first NIKBUT and meibography, followed by other detailed ophthalmological examinations. The subjective symptoms of participants with ocular irritation consistent with dry eye disease were evaluated using the Standard Patient Evaluation of Eye Dryness (SPEED) questionnaire.

Meibography

Meibography was performed using a Sirius (CSO, Florence, Italy) tomograph and corneal topographer. This device is equipped with a Phoenix Meibography imaging module. Three images were obtained from both eyes during the meibography. First, the lower eyelid and upper eyelid were everted, and the image was obtained. High-quality images with good focus and good eyelid eversion were obtained by a single author. These images were then chosen and assigned identification numbers. The images were evaluated by the same examiner who was trained in the evaluation of meibography photographs. During the photographic evaluations, the examiner marked the borders of the tarsus and circumscribed the meibomian glands. The meibomian glands appeared as hypo-illuminant grape-like clusters transversely located on the lid margins. Meibomian glands that were not lined transversely to the total tarsal plate were indicated as a “dropout.” Phoenix software provided measurements of the dropout percentage. We did not use the meiboscore procedure to obtain more precise correlation analysis results.

NIKBUT

NIKBUT were performed using the Sirius (CSO, Florence, Italy) tomograph and corneal topographer. The NIKBUT measurements were performed in the same room under fixed humidity and temperature conditions. Three measurements were performed in the right and left eyes of each participant, and the average of these three measurements was considered the mean value. The tear map representing the regularity of the corneal surface of the participant clearly focused on this procedure. The participants were asked to blink twice. An automatic stopwatch was started immediately after the eye was opened. The participants were asked to keep the eyes open for the longest time possible. If the participants blinked the eye before distortion or diffusion, representing a break-up of the tear film seen on the tear map, the test was repeated by asking the participant to blink the eye several times for a few seconds. The automatic stopwatch was closed after the participant blinked the eye, and it gave the average time between the last blink and the first tear break-up time (TBUT) and all TBUTs between the two blinks on the tear map as values.

Total eyelid abnormality score, Oxford scale, and floppy eyelid syndrome (FES)

Abnormalities of the lid margins were marked as 0 (absent) or 1 (present) for plugged orifices of the meibomian glands, vessel engorgement, irregular lid margin, and anterior/posterior shifting of the mucocutaneous junction in each eyelid [25]. The total score ranged from 0 to 4. Ocular surface staining was graded from 0 to 5 according to the Oxford scale [26]. FES was documented according to clinical findings such as papillary reaction of the upper palpebral conjunctiva and weak, easily folded upper eyelids.

Standard patient evaluation of eye dryness (SPEED) questionnaire

The SPEED questionnaire was designed to quickly track the progression of dry eye symptoms [27]. This questionnaire gives a score from 0 to 28, which is the result of eight items that assess the frequency and severity of symptoms. The assessed symptoms included dryness, grittiness, scratchiness, irritation, burning, watering, soreness, and eye fatigue.

Polysomnography (PSG)

All patients were monitored using a PSG system (GRASS, AS40, Natus Medical, Ontario, Canada) at the Department of Pulmonary Diseases Sleep Laboratory. Subjects with a clinical suspicion of OSAS underwent standard nocturnal polysomnography. The subjects were monitored for at least 6 h from 10 pm in a silent, individual sleep laboratory.

Collection of blood samples

Blood samples (5 mL) were collected from all participants via venous puncture into serum separator tubes after 8-h of overnight fasting between 9:00 a.m. and 12.00 a.m. to avoid diurnal changes. After coagulation, the tubes were centrifuged at 3000 g for 10 min to obtain serum samples. Serum samples were stored at − 20 °C until analysis.

Quantification of serum HIF-1α, MMP-2 and desmosine levels

Serum HIF-1α and MMP-2 concentrations were determined using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (Elabscience Biotechnology Inc., Houston, Texas, Catalogue No: E-EL-H6066 and E-EL-H1445) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Serum desmosine concentrations were determined using different ELISA kits (SunRed Bio, China; Cat. No: 201-12-5441), as suggested by the manufacturer.

Statistical analyses

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences) 23.0. Descriptive statistical analyses were expressed as mean ± standard deviation and median (minimum-maximum) for parametric variables, and number and percentage for non-parametric variables. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was conducted to evaluate the distribution of continuous variables, followed by parametric or non-parametric tests, depending on the test results. The parametric data between the two groups were compared using the independent samples t-test, and non-parametric data were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Parametric data between three or more groups were compared using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), and nonparametric data were compared using the Kruskal-Wallis and convenient post hoc tests. Categorical data were compared using the chi-squared test. Spearman correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the relationships between variables.

Results

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of the participants are presented in Table 1. The groups were not significantly different in terms of age, sex, body mass index (BMI), NIKBUT-first, NIKBUT-average, total eyelid abnormality score, FES, Oxford score, or IOP levels (all, p > 0.05). AHI values of the participants in the severe OSAS group were higher than those in the control and mild-to-moderate OSAS groups, and the AHI values of the participants in the mild-to-moderate OSAS group were higher than those in the control group (all, p = 0,001) (Table 1). The upper eyelid meibomian gland dropout values were higher in the severe OSAS group than in the control OSAS group (p = 0.001) (Table 1). Lower and total eyelid meibomian gland dropout values of the participants in the severe and mild-to-moderate OSAS groups were higher than those of the control OSAS group (all, p = 0,001) (Table 1). Serum HIF-1α levels of the participants in the mild-to-moderate and severe OSAS groups were significantly higher, and serum desmosine levels were significantly lower than those in the control group (all p = 0.001) (Table 1). Serum MMP-2 levels of the participants in the mild-to-moderate OSAS group were significantly higher than those in the control group, and serum MMP-2 levels of the participants in the severe OSAS group were significantly higher than those in the mild-to-moderate OSAS group (all, p = 0.001) (Table 1). The SPEED questionnaire values of the participants in the severe and mild-to-moderate OSAS groups were higher than those of the control OSAS groups, and the SPEED questionnaire values of the participants in the severe OSAS group were higher than those in the mild-to-moderate OSAS group (all, p = 0,001) (Table 1).

Discussion

The present study demonstrated that serum HIF-1α and MMP-2 levels were significantly higher and serum desmosine levels were lower in patients with mild-to-moderate and severe OSAS than those in the control group. The lower and total eyelid meibomian gland dropout rates were significantly higher in patients with mild-to-moderate and severe OSAS than those in the control group. In patients with severe OSAS, the upper eyelid meibomian gland dropout rate was significantly higher than those in the control group. According to correlation analysis, serum HIF-1α and MMP-2 levels were significantly and positively correlated with total eyelid meibomian gland dropout, whereas no significant correlation was found between serum desmosine level and total eyelid meibomian gland dropout. Total eyelid meibomian gland dropout values were positively correlated with the SPEED questionnaire and Oxford score values.

Gabryelska et al. [28] reported that serum HIF-1α levels were increased in patients with OSAS compared to the control group. Our finding of high HIF-1α levels in patients with OSAS is consistent with the results reported by Gabryelska et al. [28] Hopps et al. [29] found higher plasma MMP2 levels in individuals with OSAS compared to the control group. On the other hand, Volná et al. [30] determined no difference between the two groups. The results of the present study were consistent with the study results of Hopps et al. [29] By inducing NF-kB activity, HIF-1α leads to vascular endothelial damage through several inflammatory factors [10]. Correia et al. [31] concluded that arterial stiffness resulted from vascular endothelial dysfunction and could be reduced through therapeutic manipulation of mediators that led to endothelial dysfunction. In their study on rats, Liu et al. [21] created an experimental OSAS model and reported that the ROS-HIF-1α-MMPs pathway caused systemic elastin degradation. Diaz et al. [32] reported that MMP2 caused arterial stiffness through elastin degradation. Onaizah et al. [33] reported that an increase in carotid artery stiffness caused a decrease in blood flow in the carotid artery.

An increase in the excitability of sympathetic nerves, a decrease in the excitability of parasympathetic nerves resulted in weakened secretory activities of meibomian glands, reduced secretion of lipids, decreased stability of the tear film, decreased tear film break time, and enhanced fluorescein staining of the corneal epithelium [34]. HIF-1α is involved in sympathetic nervous system activation mediated by the carotid body [35].

Nichols et al. [36] reported that a decrease in the meibomian lipid synthesis resulted in excessive keratinization of the MGs ductal epithelium and MGs obstruction. Jester et al. [36] reported that excessive keratinization of the MG’s ductal epithelium caused ductal occlusion and plugging, which further led to meibomian gland dropout. Stasis associated with meibomian secretion disorder leads to increased pressure in the ductal system, dilatation, atrophy of the secretory acini, and ultimately loss of the meibomian gland [37].

Based on the literature, the increased loss of the meibomian gland in the OSAS group compared to the control group could be explained as follows: In individuals with OSAS, increased HIF-1α levels induce stiffness in the carotid artery and its branches feeding the eyelid, as in all arteries. Consequently, the blood flow to the eyelid and meibomian glands decreased. This results in decreased meibomian lipid synthess, excessive keratinization in the ductal epithelium of the MG, ductal occlusion, and ultimately meibomian gland dropout. The second mechanism for HIF-1α is decreased meibomian gland secretion due to sympathetic nervous system activation, stasis, increased pressure in the ductal system, dilatation, atrophy of secretory acini, and eventually, meibomian gland loss. This is supported by the positive correlation between meibomian gland dropout and serum HIF-1α. MMP-2, which increases in response to both OSAS and HIF-1α, induces meibomian gland dropout via two mechanisms. The first is the same mechanism that initiates arterial stiffness, which is similar to HIF-1α. The second mechanism is that increased MMP-2 serum levels destroy elastin around the meibomian ducts, as in the entire body, reducing the passive drive for meibomian secretions. Meibomian secretion stasis induces increased pressure in the ductal system, dilatation, atrophy of the secretory acini, and, ultimately, meibomian gland loss. This is supported by the positive correlation found between meibomian gland dropout and serum MMP-2.

According to Tomlinson et al., [38] increased severity of meibomian gland dysfunction results in impaired stability of the tear film lipid layer, increased water loss by evaporation, increased tear osmolarity, and ocular surface damage leading to conjunctival hyperemia and symptoms. Increased meibomian gland dysfunction leads to increased OSDI and Oxford score values [38]. The significant and positive correlation of total eyelid meibomian gland dropout values with the SPEED questionnaire values and Oxford scores in the present study was consistent with the results obtained by Tomlinson et al. [38]

During the design of the present study, we anticipated increased serum desmosine levels based on reports of increased elastin degradation in OSAS [21]. However, the result was to the contrary. In addition, we found no significant correlation between serum desmosine levels and meibomian gland dropout, whereas serum desmosine levels were significantly and negatively correlated with AHI, HIF-1α, and MMP-2 levels. These inconsistent results could be explained by the studies by Rubies et al. [39] and Molina et al. [40] Rubies et al. [39] conducted a study on rats, creating an experimental OSAS model. They reported increased elastin fiber ruptures and elastin fibers in the aorta of rats with OSAS compared to the control group [38]. Molina et al. [40] determined increased MMP-2, MMP-9, and elastin fiber levels in palatopharyngeal tissue samples of patients with OSAS compared to the control group. The decreased serum desmosine level in individuals with OSAS compared to the control group and the negative correlation of serum desmosine level with AHI, which we found as a result of this study, support the results of the study mentioned above. In other words, OSAS decreases the quality but increases the quantity of elastin. Therefore, serum desmosin level, an elastin degradation product, is negatively correlated with AHI.

Netland et al. [41] conducted a study on eight patients with FES, including two patients with OSAS, and reported a decreased amount of elastin in the tarsus of these patients. The authors associated this reduction with recurrent mechanical damage, chronic inflammation, or metabolic abnormality [41]. Despite the increased amount of elastin in the aorta and palatopharyngeal tissue in OSAS, the decreased elastin amount in the tarsus tissue was attributed to the metabolic abnormality in these patients, as well as the mechanical effect of eyelid inversion and itching in the supine position at night. Serum desmosine level, which indicates arterial stiffness and elastin degradation around the meibomian glands, shows a slight correlation with meibomian gland dropout in the present study; however, this finding did not reach statistical significance.

The incidence of FES in OSAS patients varies significantly in the literature, ranging from 2% to 40% [42, 43]. This study revealed a prevalence of 3.4% in all patients with OSAS and 6.5% in those with severe OSAS, although the difference was not statistically significant. According to histological evidence, FES and OSAS share a similar underlying pathogenesis with connective tissue weakness mediated by elevated matrix metalloproteinases [42]. The number of patients with FES in the present study was low for a correlation analysis; however, serum HIF-1α and MMP-2 levels of these patients were higher and desmosine levels were lower than the control group. We observed much lower serum desmosine levels in patients with FES than in the control group, further corroborating our earlier assessment that low serum desmosine levels suggested elastin weakness. Gonnering reported dilated meibomian gland acini and keratinized meibomian gland ducts in a patient with FES [44]. Dilated meibomian gland acini and keratinized meibomian gland ducts are changes resulting in meibomian gland dropout. The total meibomian gland dropout rates of patients with FES in the present study were 129%, 137%, and 54%, respectively, which were higher than the mean total eyelid meibomian gland dropout rate of 33.51% measured in the control group.

Many studies have investigated the effects of OSAS on the ocular surface and meibomian glands. In these studies, meibomian gland loss [4, 45, 46], total eyelid abnormality score [4, 45], and OSDI values [4, 45, 46] were significantly higher and t-BUT values [45, 46] were lower in the OSAS group than in the control group. In these studies, the results were interpreted speculatively by suggesting various mechanisms, and no etiological factors were revealed. This study, which revealed the relationship between dry eye parameters and systemic HIF-1α and MMP-2 levels in OSAS, differs from other studies.

The inclusion of participants who had not previously received CPAP treatment was based on the studies by Fu et al. [23] and Liu et al. [24] Fu et al. [23] reported that CPAP is a risk factor for dry eye disease. Leakage from the ventilator is believed to be the main mechanism. Retrograde air that escapes through the nasolacrimal system may also be involved. Liu et al. [24], reported that the plasma HIF-1α levels of OSAS patients who received CPAP treatment were significantly lower than those who did not receive treatment.

This study has several limitations. First, we performed a correlation analysis between systemic mediators and pathological values in the ocular tissue. However, the systemic mediators and tissue values may differ. Second, we predicted that systemic HIF-1α and MMP-2 levels would reduce blood flow to the tissue via arterial stiffness; however, blood flow was not measured. Finally, we predicted that systemic HIF-1α levels would reduce meibomian gland secretion; however, we did not measure meibomian gland secretion.

Consequently, this study demonstrated increased systemic HIF-1α and MMP-2 levels, decreased desmosine levels, and increased meibomian gland dropout and SPEED questionnaire values in OSAS. There was a negative correlation between systemic HIF-1α and MMP-2 levels and meibomian gland dropout. No studies in the literature have investigated the etiology of ocular surface deterioration caused by OSAS. Increased MMP-2 levels have been associated with dry eye in previous studies [22]. Drug development studies on the inhibition of HIF-1α, which is responsible for many diseases, are ongoing [47]. Currently, CPAP treatment is an effective treatment method for OSAS, but many patients’ compliance with treatment decreases over time [10]. Therefore, it is crucial to develop therapeutic strategies that directly target the reduction in inflammation caused by OSAS.

This study will help guide the development of therapeutic strategies for modulating HIF-1α and MMP-2 expression to reduce the morbidity induced by OSAS on the ocular surface. In addition, it will also open a new horizon for studies exploring other mediators of ocular surface morbidity in OSAS as well as their modulation for treatment.

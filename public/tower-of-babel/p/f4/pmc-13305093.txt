First-Line Tislelizumab Plus Chemotherapy for Advanced or Metastatic Squamous Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer: 4-Year Long-Term Follow-Up from RATIONALE-307
Authors: Zhijie Wang, Xinmin Yu, Jun Zhao, Yan Yu, Jingxun Wu, Rui Ma, Zhiyong Ma, Jiuwei Cui, Jiayuan Zhang, Yuanyuan Bao, Shiangjiin Leaw, Jie Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSquamous nonsmall cell lung cancer (sq-NSCLC) is challenging to treat, with shorter survival when compared with other NSCLC subtypes. Tislelizumab has demonstrated efficacy when combined with chemotherapy for sq-NSCLC. We report 4-year follow-up results from the phase 3 randomized RATIONALE-307 trial evaluating tislelizumab plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment for advanced or metastatic sq-NSCLC.MethodsPatients with treatment-naive advanced or metastatic sq-NSCLC were randomized to receive tislelizumab plus paclitaxel/carboplatin (arm A), tislelizumab plus nab-paclitaxel/carboplatin (arm B), or paclitaxel/carboplatin alone (arm C) until disease progression/intolerable toxicity. Crossover was permitted from arm C to tislelizumab monotherapy. The primary end point was progression-free survival (PFS) per independent review. Key secondary end points included overall survival (OS) and safety.ResultsAt median OS follow-up of 44.8 months, tislelizumab plus chemotherapy demonstrated durable survival benefit. Median PFS was 7.7 months (95% confidence interval [CI] 6.7–9.9) in arm A, 9.5 months (95% CI 7.4–10.1) in arm B, and 5.5 months (95% CI 4.2–5.6) in arm C. Median OS was 26.1 months (95% CI 19.0–33.8) in arm A, 23.3 months (95% CI 18.8–26.4) in arm B, and 19.4 months (95% CI 16.0–23.4) in arm C. The 4-year OS rates were 32.2%, 26.0%, and 19.2% for arms A, B, and C, respectively. Crossover occurred in 58.7% of patients from arm C. OS and PFS benefits were observed across most subgroups. Grade ≥ 3 treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 89.2%, 89.0%, and 84.6% of patients in arms A, B, and C, respectively. No new safety signals emerged.ConclusionsAt 4-year follow-up, patients with advanced sq-NSCLC who received tislelizumab plus chemotherapy experienced long-term OS and PFS benefits versus chemotherapy alone, despite high crossover rates. The combination was well-tolerated with no new safety concerns. This regimen may be a promising first-line treatment option for patients with advanced or metastatic sq-NSCLC.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov NCT03594747.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40487-026-00424-z.Keywords: Tislelizumab, Squamous NSCLC, First-line treatment, PD-1 inhibitor, Chemotherapy, Overall survival, Progression-free survival, Immunotherapy, Lung cancer

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study?Advanced or metastatic sq-NSCLC presents a significant treatment challenge with poor survival outcomes, highlighting an urgent need for improved first-line therapies.Our study investigated whether adding tislelizumab to chemotherapy improves outcomes in advanced sq-NSCLC.What was learned from this study?After 4 years of follow-up, patients with advanced or metastatic sq-NSCLC who received first-line tislelizumab plus chemotherapy experienced sustained and clinically meaningful improvements in overall survival (median 26.1 or 23.3, dependent on chemotherapy type, versus 19.4 months) and progression-free survival compared with those who received chemotherapy alone, while a tolerable safety profile was maintained.Future research should focus on optimizing treatment strategies and identifying biomarkers to predict response in specific patient subgroups, as well as explore potential combinations with other agents in sq-NSCLC.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with approximately 2.5 million cases and 1.8 million deaths in 2022 [1]. Nonsmall cell lung cancer (NSCLC; including adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and large cell carcinoma) accounts for roughly 89% of all lung cancer cases [2]. Squamous cell NSCLC (sq-NSCLC) presents treatment challenges due to patients’ older age, advanced disease at diagnosis, and high comorbidity rates, with a median survival 30% shorter than other NSCLC subtypes [3]. Sq-NSCLC is more prevalent in male patients, largely owing to higher smoking rates in this population [4, 5]. In RATIONALE-307, approximately 90% were male patients, reflecting the epidemiology of sq-NSCLC in China [6]. Combinations of first-line chemotherapy with immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting programmed cell death protein-1 (PD-1) or the ligand of PD-1 (PD-L1) have previously shown improvements in efficacy compared with chemotherapy alone [7–9].

Tislelizumab is a PD-1–targeted monoclonal antibody with high affinity and binding specificity [10, 11], developed to minimize Fcγ receptor binding on macrophages, minimizing antibody-dependent phagocytosis and potentially reducing the likelihood of anti–PD-1 therapy resistance [10]. Tislelizumab has demonstrated survival benefit across advanced solid tumors, including NSCLC [12–14].

The open-label, randomized phase 3 RATIONALE-307 trial (NCT03594747) of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone as a first-line treatment for patients with advanced or metastatic sq-NSCLC met its primary and key secondary end points [15]. In the final analysis (data cutoff 30 September 2020; median follow-up, 16.7 months) median progression-free survival (PFS) per independent review committee (IRC; the primary end point) was longer in arm A (7.6 months; tislelizumab plus paclitaxel plus carboplatin) and arm B (7.6 months; tislelizumab plus nab-paclitaxel plus carboplatin) versus arm C (5.5 months; paclitaxel plus carboplatin), with hazard ratios (HRs) of 0.45 (95% confidence interval [CI] 0.33–0.62) and 0.43 (0.31–0.60), respectively [16]. Median overall survival (OS) was 22.8 months in arm A, not estimable in arm B, and 20.2 months in arm C, with HRs of 0.68 (95% CI 0.46–1.01) and 0.75 (0.50–1.12), respectively [16]. Tislelizumab plus chemotherapy was well-tolerated, with low rates of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) leading to treatment discontinuation [16]. On the basis of results from the interim and final analyses of RATIONALE-307 [15, 16], tislelizumab plus chemotherapy was approved for use as first-line treatment for sq-NSCLC in China, Europe, and other countries [17–20].

In this long-term follow-up analysis of the RATIONALE-307 trial, we evaluated the durability of efficacy and safety of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone after approximately 4 years of follow-up, to provide clinicians with mature survival data that can inform treatment decisions for patients with advanced or metastatic sq-NSCLC.

Methods

Patient Eligibility

Detailed methods for RATIONALE-307 have been previously described [15]. Briefly, eligible patients were aged 18–75 years with treatment-naive, histologically confirmed locally advanced (stage IIIB) or metastatic (stage IV) sq-NSCLC and measurable disease per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1. Patients eligible for curative surgery/radiotherapy were excluded. Patients with brain metastases were eligible only if they had measurable extracranial disease with supratentorial lesions and had not previously received corticiosteroids. Brain radiotherapy within 14 days of randomization was prohibited. Patients with new symptomatic central nervous system metastases at screening could enroll after completing appropriate local therapy and meeting all eligibility criteria.

Study Design

RATIONALE-307 was an open-label, randomized phase 3 trial. Patients were randomized 1:1:1 to arm A (every 3-week cycle: tislelizumab 200 mg on day 1, paclitaxel 175 mg/m2 on day 1, carboplatin area under the curve [AUC] of 5 on day 1), arm B (every 3-week cycle: tislelizumab 200 mg on day 1; nab-paclitaxel 100 mg/m2 on days 1, 8, and 15; carboplatin AUC 5 on day 1), or arm C (every 3-week cycle: paclitaxel 175 mg/m2 on day 1, carboplatin AUC 5 on day 1). Treatment was administered by appropriate personnel at each study site. Randomization was stratified by disease stage (stage IIIB versus stage IV) and PD-L1 tumor cell (TC) expression (< 1% versus 1–49% versus ≥ 50%; VENTANA PD-L1 [SP263] Assay at a central laboratory). Tislelizumab continued until lack of clinical benefit or intolerable toxicity, whichever came first. Patients in arm C could cross over to tislelizumab monotherapy following disease progression per IRC.

After the primary analysis data cutoff, patients were prospectively monitored for survival status via telephone or clinic visits approximately every 3 months until death, lost to follow-up, withdrawal of consent, or study completion. For OS analysis, patients without documented death were censored at their last known alive date. Prespecified censoring rules were applied consistently throughout.

End Points

The primary end point was PFS per IRC, comparing arm A or arm B with arm C. Key secondary end points included OS, PFS per investigator (INV), and safety. TEAEs were graded per National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0. Adverse events were not analyzed by sex/gender (not prespecified). Immune-mediated adverse events (imAEs) were classified retrospectively on the basis of a defined list of preferred terms using a programmatic algorithmic approach, without manual medical adjudication. This standardized methodology was selected to ensure consistency and reproducibility across sites, using predefined MedDRA terms and objective criteria such as systemic corticosteroid use and action taken with the study drug.

Statistical Analysis

Efficacy was analyzed in the intent-to-treat analysis set (all randomized patients), and safety in the safety analysis set (all patients who received any dose of tislelizumab and/or chemotherapy). PFS and OS were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method, with 95% CIs estimated by the Brookmeyer and Crowley method. HRs were estimated with a stratified Cox proportional hazards model, with the same stratification factors as randomization. Analysis at this data cutoff was descriptive without formal significance testing. Statistical analysis was performed using SAS version 9.3 or higher (SAS Institute, Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

A two-stage analysis [21] was conducted to estimate the impact of in-study crossover on postprogression survival (PPS) using data from patients in the chemotherapy-alone arm who experienced progression per IRC before receiving any subsequent anticancer therapy. The model assumes ’no unmeasured confounders’, meaning all covariates predicting PPS were included in the accelerated failure time (AFT) model. Baseline risk factors—such as liver metastases, smoking status, ECOG performance status, disease stage, PD-L1 TC expression, and the last ECOG status before or at the secondary baseline (time of progression)—were fitted into the AFT model to estimate the crossover effect. Counterfactual survival times were then derived for patients who crossed over to tislelizumab monotherapy, on the basis of time to progression and PPS adjusted for the estimated crossover effect. A recensoring approach was applied to break dependence between censoring time and treatment received.

OS was defined as time from randomization to death from any cause; patients alive at cutoff were censored at their last known alive date. Missing data were not imputed for efficacy end points; patients without postbaseline assessments were considered nonresponders for ORR and censored at randomization for OS/PFS. Other postprotocol anticancer therapies were not adjusted for in OS analyses.

Ethical Compliance

RATIONALE-307 was conducted according to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice guidelines, principles of informed consent, and requirements of publicly registered clinical trials. All patients gave written informed consent before enrollment, and all participating institutions provided institutional review board approval of the trial (Supplementary Table S1). Patients and the public were not directly involved in the design, conduct, reporting, or dissemination plans of this research. The RATIONALE-307 trial was designed by clinical experts and the sponsor, on the basis of scientific rationale and unmet medical needs in the treatment of locally advanced or metastatic sq-NSCLC.

Results

Patients

A total of 360 patients were randomized to arms A (n = 120), B (n = 119), and C (n = 121). At the data cutoff of 28 April 2023, median OS follow-up was 44.8 months (95% CI 43.5–46.3). Patient baseline characteristics and demographics were generally comparable across treatment arms (Supplementary Table S2).

Treatment Exposure

In total, 355/360 (98.9%) patients received at least one dose of treatment (arm A, 120/120 [100.0%]; arm B, 118/119 [99.2%]; arm C, 117/121 [96.7%]). All patients discontinued treatment, with the most common reasons being disease progression in arms A and B and completion of chemotherapy in arm C (Fig. 1). At the data cutoff, no patients remained on treatment. Median duration of exposure to tislelizumab was 13 cycles in both arms A and B. At the time of the current analysis, 51% of patients in arm A and 52% of patients in arm B had received > 12 cycles of tislelizumab. Of patients in arm C, 71 (58.7%) crossed over to receive tislelizumab monotherapy.

Efficacy

The median follow-up times for OS were 44.3, 47.0, and 44.7 months for arms A, B, and C, respectively. With extended follow-up, OS benefit was maintained in arm A (stratified OS HR 0.67) and arm B (stratified OS HR 0.82) versus arm C. Median OS was 26.1 months in arm A, 23.3 months in arm B, and 19.4 months in arm C (Fig. 2A, B). Estimated rates of OS at 4 years were 32.2% (95% CI 23.7–40.9), 26.0% (95% CI 18.3–34.4), and 19.2% (95% CI 12.0–27.7), respectively, in arms A, B, and C.

Safety

TEAEs of any grade occurred in > 99% of patients across all arms (Table 1). Grade ≥ 3 TEAEs were also common, affecting 89.2% of patients in arm A, 89.0% in arm B, and 84.6% in arm C. Serious adverse events occurred in 46.7% of patients in arm A, 46.6% in arm B, and 24.8% in arm C. TEAEs leading to discontinuation of any component of study treatment were reported in 19.2%, 33.1%, and 15.4% of patients in arms A, B, and C, respectively. Specifically, discontinuation of tislelizumab due to TEAEs occurred in 15.8% of patients in arm A and 14.4% in arm B. TEAEs leading to death were reported in 4.2% of patients in arm A, 7.6% in arm B, and 4.3% in arm C (Table 1). TEAEs that led to death and were not related to disease progression occurred in 2.5%, 6.8%, and 3.4% of patients in arms A, B, and C, respectively.

The most frequent TEAE was anemia in all arms, occurring in 89.2%, 94.1%, and 80.3% of patients in arms A, B, and C, respectively (Supplementary Table S3). Neutrophil count decrease was the most common grade ≥ 3 TEAE across all arms (53.3%, 45.8%, and 45.3% in arms A, B, and C, respectively; Supplementary Table S3).

Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) of any grade were reported in 99.2% of patients in arm A, 99.2% in arm B, and 100.0% in arm C. Grade ≥ 3 TRAEs occurred in 86.7%, 84.7%, and 80.3% of patients in arms A, B, and C, respectively. The most frequent grade ≥ 3 TRAEs related to tislelizumab in arm A included neutrophil count decreased (13.3%), leukopenia (6.7%), and white blood cell count decreased (6.7%; Supplementary Table S4). In arm B, the most common grade ≥ 3 TRAEs were neutrophil count decreased (16.9%), white blood cell count decreased (11.9%), and anemia (11.9%). TRAEs related to any component of study treatment leading to death occurred in 0.8% of patients in arm A, 2.5% in arm B, and 2.6% in arm C.

imAEs of any grade were reported in 46.7% of patients in arm A, 51.7% in arm B, and 7.7% in arm C (Supplementary Table S5). Grade 3–4 imAEs occurred in 10.8%, 7.6%, and 0% of patients in arms A, B, and C, respectively, with no grade 5 imAEs reported in any arm. The most frequently reported categories of grade ≥ 3 imAEs were skin adverse reaction, occurring in 5.0% and 2.5% of patients in arms A and B, respectively, and pneumonitis, occurring in 3.3% and 5.1% (Supplementary Table S6).

imAEs led to treatment modification of any component in 14.2%, 18.6%, and 0.9% of patients in arms A, B, and C, respectively. For tislelizumab specifically, treatment modifications (including infusion interruption, reduction in infusion rate, and dose delay) as a result of imAEs occurred in 13.3% of patients in arm A and 17.8% in arm B (Supplementary Table S5).

Discussion

After 4 years of follow-up (28.1 months beyond final analysis), tislelizumab plus chemotherapy maintained a clinically meaningful survival benefit versus chemotherapy alone in patients with treatment-naive locally advanced or metastatic sq-NSCLC. Tislelizumab plus chemotherapy continued to demonstrate improved PFS per IRC in both arms A and B compared with arm C. Median OS was also improved in arm A (tislelizumab plus paclitaxel and carboplatin) compared with arm C (paclitaxel plus carboplatin), and OS benefit was observed in arm B (tislelizumab plus nab-paclitaxel and carboplatin) and across most prespecified subgroups despite high crossover rates. Patients experienced durable OS benefit, with 4-year OS rates of 32.2%, 26.0%, and 19.2% in arms A, B, and C, respectively. Tislelizumab plus chemotherapy was well-tolerated, with low rates of TEAEs leading to discontinuation or death and no new safety signals identified despite the longer duration of treatment and follow-up. These results are consistent with the primary and final analyses of RATIONALE-307, indicating that tislelizumab plus chemotherapy provided enduring clinical benefit in this patient population [15, 22].

Improvements in PFS and OS with tislelizumab plus chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone were observed regardless of PD-L1 TC expression. However, these subgroup analyses were not powered and may be biased due to imbalances in baseline characteristics and limited sample sizes; these results should be interpreted with caution. OS is also subject to other sources of mortality (e.g., noncancer diseases), which can serve as additional confounding factors [23]. Previous studies, including KEYNOTE-407 and IMpower131, have shown similar efficacy of immunotherapy–chemotherapy combinations in both male and female patients with sq-NSCLC [7, 9]. Our subgroup analyses similarly showed no significant differences between sexes, though the small female patient population (~10%) limits definitive conclusions. This imbalance reflects sq-NSCLC epidemiology in China, where smoking rates are much higher in men [6]. Other China-based trials show similar patterns: the KEYNOTE-407 China extension enrolled only 5% of females, and CameL-Sq approximately 8% [24, 25]. Additional research with larger female patient populations would help confirm generalizability to all patients.

The long-term follow-up results from RATIONALE-307 demonstrate the enduring benefit of tislelizumab plus chemotherapy in advanced sq-NSCLC. Moreover, the 4-year OS rates in our study (32.2% and 26.0% in arms A and B, respectively) appear favorable when compared with historical data from other immunotherapy–chemotherapy combinations in sq-NSCLC, such as pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in KEYNOTE-407, which reported 4-year and 5-year OS rates of 21.9% and 18.4%, respectively [26], and atezolizumab plus carboplatin and nab-paclitaxel in IMpower131, which showed a median OS of 14.2 months [9]. However, caution should be exercised in such cross-trial comparisons because of differences in patient population and study design. Such comparisons should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating, and direct comparative studies would be needed to establish definitive differences between immunotherapy regimens. The durable responses, particularly the long-term survival benefit, suggest that tislelizumab plus chemotherapy provides sustained immune-mediated tumor control, with implications for treatment sequencing and long-term management strategies in sq-NSCLC. The consistent benefit observed across PD-L1 TC expression levels indicates that tislelizumab plus chemotherapy could be a valuable option for a broad patient population, despite the low sample sizes in some PD-L1 expression subgroups. The persistence of survival advantage despite high crossover rates (58.7%) further emphasizes the robust efficacy of tislelizumab-based regimens. The durable responses observed with tislelizumab may relate to its unique design features: a modified Fc region that minimizes macrophage-mediated removal of T cells [27], binding to the CC’ loop of PD-1 with higher affinity than other inhibitors, and more complete blockade of PD-1/PD-L1 interaction [11]. These properties may contribute to the sustained clinical benefit observed in this long-term follow-up.

The limitations of RATIONALE-307 have been previously described and are primarily related to the open-label design of the study [15, 16]. The potential for INV bias was ameliorated through a number of methods, including IRC-based measurement of end points and protocol-defined assessment schedules.

Conclusions

The findings from this 4-year follow-up analysis of RATIONALE-307 showed that patients with advanced or metastatic sq-NSCLC who received tislelizumab plus chemotherapy continued to experience long-term survival benefit compared with chemotherapy alone despite a high in-study crossover rate, with consistent efficacy across PD-L1 expression levels. No new safety signals were observed. Within the limitations of this open-label trial design, these results support tislelizumab plus chemotherapy as a first-line treatment option for patients with advanced or metastatic sq-NSCLC and may inform future research on optimizing patient selection and treatment sequencing strategies.

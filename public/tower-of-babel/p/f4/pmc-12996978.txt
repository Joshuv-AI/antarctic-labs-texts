Background

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is highly endemic in Southern China, Southeast Asia, and North Africa. According to available data, Eastern Asia accounted for the largest share of both global NPC cases and deaths (65,866 out of 133,354, 49.39 %; and 36,453 out of 80,008, 45.56 %, respectively), with China bearing the majority of this burden (62,444 out of 133,354, 46.82 %; and 34,810 out of 80,008, 43.50 %, respectively) [1]. Owing to the anatomically concealed location of the nasopharynx, early detection of NPC remains challenging. As a result, >70 % of patients are initially diagnosed at the locoregionally advanced stage (III–IVA) [2,3].

Currently, intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) is the primary treatment modality for NPC. Given the proximity of the nasopharynx to critical organs such as the spinal cord and brainstem, IMRT allows escalated radiation doses to the tumor target while minimizing exposure to adjacent normal tissues, thereby preserving organ function. With advancements in radiotherapy technology and imaging, the 5-year overall survival rate for NPC has reached approximately 80 % . Nevertheless, about 20 % of patients still experience recurrence or metastasis after treatment [[4], [5], [6]].

Besides the established prognostic factor of TNM staging, radiotherapy interruption (RTI) has been identified as a significant contributor to poor outcomes in NPC. Although multidisciplinary team (MDT) support—exemplified by the SHINE-MDT framework that integrates nutritional, psychological, and rehabilitation interventions—has been proven effective in reducing radiotherapy interruption rates among head and neck cancer patients, with the intervention group achieving a 13.9-percentage-point lower rate compared to usual care (11.1 % vs. 25.0 %, P = 0.003) [7], radiotherapy prolongation still occurs frequently in clinical practice. Such delays are often attributed to holidays (e.g., the Chinese Spring Festival), equipment malfunctions, or acute treatment-related toxicities [8]. Multiple studies have documented associations between RTI and adverse clinical outcomes [[9], [10], [11]]. Hallemeier et al. confirmed a close association between prolonged radiotherapy duration and unfavorable clinical outcomes in esophageal cancer. Specifically, RT courses longer than 45 days led to significantly compromised disease-free survival (DFS) (HR = 1.34, 95 % CI: 1.01–1.77, P = 0.04) . In addition, RT duration exceeding 39 days was correlated with a higher risk of locoregional failure (LRF) compared with that of ≤ 39 days (HR = 1.32, 95 % CI: 1.06–1.65, P = 0.01) [12]. In NPC, Yeh et al. and Yao et al. have independently demonstrated that an RTI of ≥5 days correlates with inferior overall survival [10,11]. However, there remains limited evidence regarding which subgroups of NPC patients are most adversely affected by such interruptions.

According to both domestic and international guidelines, induction chemotherapy (IC) combined with concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) is the first-line treatment for locoregionally advanced NPC [13,14]. IC has emerged as a critical component of the treatment regimen, primarily serving to reduce tumor burden and eliminate micrometastases. Existing evidence indicates that IC effectively decreases the risk of distant metastasis in these patients [4,5,15]. However, whether IC can mitigate the adverse impact of anticipated radiotherapy interruptions (such as those during the Chinese Spring Festival) remains unreported. Furthermore, to elucidate whether IC mitigates the adverse survival impact of RTI, we compared outcomes between high-risk patients (≥2 IC cycles) with RTI ≥5 days and those with RTI <5 days.

Methods

Patients

This retrospective study analyzed 820 patients with locoregionally advanced NPC treated at Guangxi Medical University Cancer Hospital between 2016 and 2019. Inclusion criteria comprised pathologically confirmed non-keratinizing NPC (WHO type II/III), Stage III–IVA disease according to the AJCC 8th edition staging system, and receipt of curative-intent IMRT with or without induction chemotherapy. Exclusion criteria: (1) Prior antitumor therapy before admission to our institution ;(2) other pathological types, such as nasopharyngeal adenocarcinoma,neuroendocrine carcinoma,or lymphoma;(3) Stage I, II, or IVB disease;(4) Suspected distant metastasis;(5) Concurrent other malignancies, severe infections, or other major comorbidities;(6) Refusal of or failure to complete the planned radical treatment. Treatment

All patients received IMRT with or without induction chemotherapy. The radiotherapy target delineation followed a precise volumetric definition and dose prescription protocol: the gross tumor volume of the nasopharynx (GTVnx) encompassed the primary nasopharyngeal lesion, while the gross tumor volume of lymph nodes (GTVnd) included metastatic cervicale lymph nodes. The high-risk clinical target volume (CTV1) was generated by expanding the GTVnx with anisotropic margins of 5–10 mm (3–5 mm posteriorly). Subsequently, the low-risk clinical target volume (CTV2) was created by expanding CTV1 with margins of 5–10 mm (3–5 mm posteriorly). Each target volume underwent a standardized 3 mm isotropic expansion to form the corresponding planning target volumes (PGTVnx, PGTVnd, PCTV1, and PCTV2). The prescribed radiation doses followed a risk-adapted fractionation scheme: PGTVnx received 70.4–72.32 Gy, PGTVnd 70.4–72.32 Gy, PCTV1 60.8–62.4 Gy, and PCTV2 52.8–54.4 Gy, delivered in 32 fractions over 5 sessions per week. However, due to holidays, machine malfunction, or treatment interruptions caused by patient-side effects, the actual treatment duration often exceeded the planned schedule. The start and end dates of radiotherapy were rigorously recorded for each patient, and the total number of treatment days was calculated accordingly.

The chemotherapy regimens were as follows: concurrent chemotherapy consisted of cisplatin or nedaplatin alone at a dose of 100 mg/m², while induction chemotherapy regimens included TPF (docetaxel 60 mg/m², cisplatin or nedaplatin 60 mg/m², and 5-fluorouracil 600 mg/m²/d over 120 h), GP (gemcitabine 1000 mg/m² on days 1 and 8 plus cisplatin 80 mg/m² on day 1), TP (docetaxel 75 mg/m² and cisplatin or nedaplatin 75 mg/m² on day 1), and PF (cisplatin or nedaplatin 80 mg/m² and 5-fluorouracil 750 mg/m² over 96 h).

Follow-up

Post-treatment monitoring followed a structured protocol: evaluations were conducted quarterly during the first two years, semi-annually from years three to five, and annually thereafter if no recurrence was detected. Follow-up was carried out through outpatient visits and telephone interviews. Data collected included survival status, recurrence, metastasis, and treatment-related complications. Imaging studies consisted of chest X-ray/CT, abdominal ultrasound/CT, whole-body bone scan, MRI, and endoscope.

Statistics

Model construction

Survival analysis was performed using SPSS software version 27.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL), and R 4.5.3 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The study endpoints included OS, locoregional recurrence–free survival (LRFS), distant metastasis–free survival (DMFS), and failure‐free survival (FFS). OS was defined as the time from treatment initiation to death from any cause. LRFS was defined as the time from treatment initiation to local or regional recurrence, or both. DMFS was defined as the time from treatment initiation to the occurrence of distant metastasis. FFS was defined as the time from treatment initiation to the first event of disease recurrence (distant or locoregional) or death from any cause. Patients were randomly assigned to training and validation sets in a 6:4 ratio. In the training set, the following potential prognostic factors for overall survival were analyzed: age, T stage, N stage, EBV-DNA, sex, clinical stage, pretreatment serum albumin (Alb), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and PLR: platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR). Univariate survival analysis was conducted using the log-rank test for categorical variables and Cox regression for continuous variables. Prognostic factors with a P < 0.1 in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate analysis. Independent prognostic factors identified with P < 0.05 in the multivariate analysis were used to construct a prognostic model. Plasma EBV-DNA was quantified using the Epstein-Barr Virus Nucleic Acid Detection Kit (PCR-Fluorescence Probing) from Sansure Biotech (Changsha, China), which utilizes a one-step real-time PCR platform. A prognostic index scoring system was developed based on the weights of independent prognostic variables derived from the β coefficients of the hazard ratios in the Cox regression model. The prognostic index (PI) was analyzed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves to determine the optimal cutoff value, which was then used to stratify patients in the training set into low‐risk and high‐risk groups. Using the same predictive model, patients in the validation set were also classified into low‐risk and high‐risk groups.

Evaluation and validation of the risk prediction model

The discriminative ability of the prognostic model was assessed using Harrell’s concordance index (C-index), calculated with the "rms" and "pROC" packages in R. Internal validation was performed on the training dataset using resampling methods. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated to compare OS, DMFS, LRFS, and FFS between different risk groups in both the training and validation sets. In addition, calibration curves were generated via resampling techniques using the “rms”, “survival”, and “dplyr” R packages to assess the model's performance.

Evaluating the role of induction chemotherapy in mitigating the prognostic impact of RTI ≥5 days

For patients in different risk groups of both the training and validation sets, the log-rank test was used to compare OS, DMFS, LRFS, and FFS between those with RTI ≥5 days and those with RTI <5 days, in order to clarify the survival impact of RTI across risk subgroups. Subsequently, among high-risk patients who had received induction chemotherapy (≥2 cycles) in the training and validation sets, survival outcomes (OS, DMFS, LFFS, FFS) were compared between the RTI ≥5 days and RTI <5 days groups, to determine whether IC (≥2 cycles) could mitigate the adverse effects associated with prolonged treatment interruption.

In cases where baseline characteristics were imbalanced between the two comparison groups, propensity score matching (PSM) was performed using the “matchIt” and “dplyr” package in R to balance covariates between groups. Survival analysis was repeated after matching. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Development and validation of a prognostic model for NPC

The entire cohort was randomly divided into training (n = 490) and validation (n = 330) sets in a 6:4 ratio (detailed were shown in Table 2). Univariate and multivariate analyses performed on the training set identified age, T stage, N stage, and EBV-DNA >4000 copies/mL as significant adverse prognostic factors for OS (Table 3). A prognostic index (PI) was developed using the risk weights of these covariates derived from the multivariate analysis: PI = age × 2.4 + (T-1) × 56.7 + N × 59.4 + EBV-DNA × 110.2 (Supplementary Table 1). The optimal PI cutoff value of 376.05 was determined by ROC curve analysis (Supplementary Figure 1), stratifying patients into low-risk (PI <376.05) and high-risk (PI ≥376.05) groups. Discussion

This study developed a risk prediction model for locoregionally advanced NPC that demonstrated superior predictive performance compared to the conventional TNM staging system. To our knowledge, this might the first study to demonstrate that the adverse prognostic impact of RTI ≥5 days primarily affected the high-risk patient subgroup. Furthermore, we were the first to report that IC (≥2 cycles) may mitigate the detrimental survival effect associated with RTI ≥5 days in this patient population.

Our findings confirm that EBV-DNA >4000 copies/mL was an independent adverse prognostic factor for OS in locoregionally advanced NPC. While EBV-DNA is widely recognized as a prognostic marker in NPC, the optimal cutoff value remains controversial, with different studies employing thresholds such as >2000 copies/mL [16,17] or proposing >4000 copies/mL [18,19]. This discrepancy may stem from population heterogeneity, methodological variations in EBV-DNA quantification, or sample size limitations, leading to different optimal cutoffs across studies. Our selection of >4000 copies/mL was based on statistical optimization during internal data analysis and model development, potentially reflecting a stronger association between this threshold and survival outcomes in our cohort. Future research should focus on validating the generalizability of this EBV-DNA cutoff in multicenter, large-scale cohorts and elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying the particular relevance of the 4000 copies/mL threshold in our setting. Additionally, incorporating dynamic EBV-DNA monitoring [20] may further enhance risk stratification accuracy and provide more reliable guidance for individualized treatment strategies.

This study reaffirmed that RTI ≥5 days was an adverse prognostic factor in patients with locoregionally advanced NPC, consistent with previous reports [10,11]. Moreover, we provided the first evidence that this detrimental effect was primarily confined to the high-risk subgroup, with no significant impact observed in low-risk patients. The high-risk profile identified in this study—characterized by features such as advanced T/N stage and elevated EBV-DNA load—was indicative of a more aggressive tumor phenotype with rapid proliferative capacity. Such tumors were likely to have shorter cell doubling times. Consequently, a treatment gap exceeding 5 days might provide a critical window for accelerated repopulation of surviving clonogens [21], leading to markedly increased risks of local recurrence and distant metastasis. In contrast, tumors in the low-risk subgroup exhibited more indolent biological behavior, rendering them less susceptible to interruptions in radiotherapy.

What measures should be taken for high-risk populations to reduce the adverse prognostic impact of radiotherapy interruptions? For anticipated and scheduled events like Chinese New Year, proactive measures such as arranging overtime for radiotherapy technicians might be an effective strategy to minimize interruptions. Additionally, for interruptions caused by treatment-related toxicities (e.g., severe radiation-induced oral mucositis), close monitoring by physicians during radiotherapy is essential to enable early detection and management of side effects, thereby potentially avoiding treatment breaks altogether. Furthermore, we proposed the incorporation of at least two cycles of induction chemotherapy to mitigate the negative effects associated with treatment delays. We hypothesized that this protective effect might operate through several mechanisms. First, IC administered prior to radiotherapy reduces initial tumor burden, thereby decreasing the risk of tumor repopulation during unscheduled treatment gaps [22]. Second, IC could help eradicate micrometastases, alleviates overall tumor load, reduce pressure on radiotherapy efficacy, and suppresses EBV-DNA replication, thus lowering viral activity [23,24]. These actions collectively reduced the risk of disease progression during extended radiotherapy intervals. This approach was supported by a multicenter phase III trial conducted by Zhang et al., which demonstrated that in patients with EBV-DNA ≥4000 copies/mL, IC significantly improved 5-year overall survival (85.9 % vs. 70.8 %, P = 0.02). In contrast, no survival benefit was observed in patients with EBV-DNA <4000 copies/mL [4]. The systemic control established by IC prior to radiotherapy provided a critical time buffer, allowing continued suppression of micrometastases even when radiotherapy must be temporarily halted due to machine malfunction or acute toxicities. Our findings underscored the importance of integrating IC into treatment strategies for high-risk NPC patients, particularly in contexts where treatment interruptions were anticipated—such as during extended holidays (e.g., Chinese New Year) or due to toxicity-related pauses. In such scenarios, ≥2 cycles of IC should be considered a protective strategy.

Our study demonstrated that IC could mitigate the adverse impacts of radiotherapy interruptions lasting ≥5 days in high-risk patients with locoregionally advanced NPC. However, the efficacy of alternative therapeutic strategies in counteracting the detrimental effects of RTI remains to be elucidated. In recent years, targeted therapy and immunotherapy have yielded promising outcomes, driving revolutionary advances in cancer management. Specifically, anti-epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) agents including nimotuzumab and cetuximab have been extensively utilized in NPC, with the clinical efficacy of these agents validated by numerous studies [[25], [26], [27]]. Immunotherapy had also shown promising outcomes. In 2024, the CONTINUUM trial, led by Professor Jun Ma’s team from Sun Yat-sen University Cancer Center in collaboration with nine medical institutions in China, evaluated sintilimab in high-risk locoregionally advanced NPC. This study demonstrated that adding immunotherapy to conventional chemoradiotherapy and IC significantly improved event-free survival and DMFS compared to standard treatment [28]. In 2025, a large-scale, multicenter randomized controlled trial (the DIPPER study) further confirmed that the PD-1 inhibitor camrelizumab, when administered after chemoradiotherapy, significantly improved survival and reduced distant metastasis risk in high-risk locoregionally advanced NPC [29]. Whether targeted therapy or immunotherapy could offset the detrimental impact of RTI≥5 days in high-risk NPC remained a subject for future investigation.

If the Chinese New Year holiday is anticipated and scheduled in advance, is delaying the initiation of radiotherapy an effective strategy? Our team's prior research has reported that a prolonged wait time (>75 days) between definitive diagnosis and the start of radical radiotherapy is associated with significantly lower 5-year DMFS (74.1 % vs. 86.6 %, P = 0.006) in patients with LA-NPC receiving induction chemotherapy plus concurrent chemoradiotherapy, particularly those with stage IVa disease [30]. Therefore, both treatment delays and RTI ≥5 days appear to adversely affect prognosis in locally advanced NPC. Determining which has a lesser impact would require prospective investigation.

The present study had several limitations. First, as a retrospective analysis, it may be subject to selection bias. Additionally, a specific limitation is the lack of systematically documented, precise data on the timing of radiotherapy interruptions (e.g., early, mid, or late phase). Future studies incorporating such temporal information would help refine our understanding of the biological and clinical impact of RTI. Second, although all included induction chemotherapy regimens (such as GP, TPF, and TP) demonstrated a protective effect, the heterogeneity among these regimens means the optimal IC strategy remains undefined. Third, although EBV-DNA testing followed internationally standardized methods, inter-platform variability may affect the generalizability of the prognostic index model. Fourth, the observed decrease in the C-index from the training set (0.744) to the validation set (0.658) indicates a degree of model overfitting. The suboptimal model calibration consequently restricts the reliability of individual-level risk predictions. Acknowledged as exploratory, the primary value of this model at the current stage serves to generate a clinically pertinent hypothesis—that risk-stratified management of RTI may be beneficial. Consequently, further validation with larger, multi-center datasets is imperative prior to considering any potential clinical application. What’s more, the number of high-risk patients in the validation cohort—particularly those who received ≥2 cycles of IC—was relatively small. Larger sample sizes are needed to further validate the predictive capacity of this model. Lastly, all patients in this study were from a single institution in Southern China (Guangxi region), representing an EBV-hyperendemic population whose treatment was mainly impacted by the Chinese New Year holiday. Therefore, to contextualize this fundamental characteristic, a crucial next step will involve collaborating with multi-center cohorts from non-endemic regions (e.g., Northern China or Europe) for external validation and potential adaptation of our model.

In conclusion, this study developed a risk stratification model by integrating age, T stage, N stage, and EBV-DNA levels to identify high-risk patients (PI ≥376.05) with locoregionally advanced NPC. We demonstrated for the first time that RTI ≥5 days significantly compromised survival exclusively in this high-risk subgroup. A key finding was that ≥2 cycles of IC could mitigate this survival detriment. As treatment for NPC is increasingly personalized, future efforts should focus on refining risk stratification models, optimizing induction chemotherapy protocols to maximize efficacy while minimizing toxicity, and validating the model's generalizability through multi-center collaborations. These steps are crucial for advancing individualized treatment strategies.

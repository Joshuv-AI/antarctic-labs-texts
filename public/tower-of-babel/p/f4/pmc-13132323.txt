Letter to the editor “Leiomyosarcoma of the inferior vena cava: case report”
Authors: M. Abdur Rahman Aizaz, Mohammed Hammad Jaber Amin
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the article by Hafhaf et al titled “Leiomyosarcoma of the Inferior Vena Cava: case report”[1]. We truly appreciate the authors for presenting this rare and challenging case of a 53-year-old patient with persistent abdominal pain, ultimately diagnosed with leiomyosarcoma of the inferior vena cava and successfully treated with surgical resection. The report provides valuable insights into the diagnostic complexity, imaging evaluation, and multidisciplinary surgical management required for such uncommon vascular tumors. In this context, we would like to share a few observations that may provide additional insights and help guide future research and clinical management of this rare condition. This editorial adheres to the TITAN 2025 guidelines for the transparent reporting of AI assisted content[2].

First, the patient’s family history reveals several close relatives affected by colorectal cancer and nephroblastoma, raising serious concern for a hereditary cancer syndrome such as Li-Fraumeni Syndrome. However, the case report does not mention any genetic counseling, TP53 testing plans for the patient or family members. This exclusion limits the clinical and translational value of the report, as it overlooks an important opportunity to highlight hereditary risk assessment and the role of precision medicine in guiding care .Future studies should include comprehensive genetic testing and counseling to strengthen clinical relevance[3]. Second, the report touches on the patient’s early recovery, but it lacks essential details such as how follow-up care was arranged, what imaging or monitoring was planned, or whether any additional treatments were considered[4]. These details are important, especially for tumors with a high risk of recurrence. Without them, readers are left unsure about how the patient was supported after surgery or how similar cases should be followed in practice. Future studies should include post-operative or long-term follow-up plan.

Finally, the report states that the tumor was a grade II leiomyosarcoma, but it does not include important details such as the specific markers used (such as SMA, desmin, h-caldesmon, or Ki-67) or the mitotic index[5]. These findings are important for understanding how the diagnosis was confirmed and for helping others compare similar cases. Without them, readers are left with an incomplete picture of the tumor’s features and diagnostic process. Future studies should include histopathological and immunochemical profiles.

In conclusion, this case report provides valuable understanding into a rare and challenging condition. However, addressing detail on genetic evaluation, follow-up care and histopathological features would add important depth. Future studies that consider these aspects could offer clearer guidance for diagnosis and management, ultimately helping improve outcomes for patients with this uncommon disease.

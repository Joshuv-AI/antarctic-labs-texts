Outcomes of bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide versus efavirenz-based regimens in central China: a real-world assessment
Authors: Yongquan Peng, Xujian Cao, Yating Wang, Weiwei Yu, Ziwei Zeng, Min Wang, Yanjie Yin
Published: 2023

To the Editor: Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) epidemic poses a significant challenge to global public health. As of 2022, approximately 39.0 million people worldwide were living with HIV.[1] With the ongoing evolution of combined antiretroviral therapy (cART), HIV infection has transformed into a manageable chronic disease requiring long-term treatment. Among the various classes of antiretroviral (ARV) drugs, integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI)-based regimens, known for potency and tolerability, have emerged as the predominant treatment modality worldwide. The next-generation of INSTI, bictegravir (BIC), received the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval in 2018 as a single-tablet regimen (STR) containing BIC (50 mg)/emtricitabine (FTC, 200 mg)/tenofovir alafenamide (TAF, 25 mg) for once daily. It has been endorsed as the preferred choice for treatment-naive patients by major guidelines, including the 2021 edition of the China Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS) Diagnosis and Treatment Guidelines.[2] In China, people living with HIV (PLWH) are provided with flexible cART options, including the National Reimbursement Drug List (NRDL), self-pay drugs, and free drugs. Since being included in NRDL in 2021, BIC/FTC/TAF have gained popularity among an increasing number of patients.

Nevertheless, the predominant first-line regimen in China for most patients remains non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NNRTIs) based regimens, such as efavirenz (EFV), administered as multiple-tablet regimens (MTR). To our knowledge, few head-to-head studies have been conducted in China comparing BIC/FTC/TAF with EFV-based regimens for short-term viral load reduction. Consequently, we conducted a single-center, retrospective study to provide real-world data for the clinical ART of treatment-naive patients in China.

The data were collected from the outpatient department of infection and immunology at the First Hospital of Changsha, Hunan Province, during the period spanning from January 2021 to February 20, 2023. All participants signed written informed consent. Ethical approval was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the First Hospital of Changsha (No. 2021-28). Further information regarding the inclusion and exclusion criteria can be found in section Supplementary Material, http://links.lww.com/CM9/B770.

All study participants underwent comprehensive assessments, including viral load measurement and CD4+ T cell count analysis. Subsequently, participants were given the option to self-select their preferred medication regimen, leading to the formation of two groups: BIC/FTC/TAF group and EFV + lamivudine (3TC) + tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) group. The BIC/FTC/TAF group was administered a treatment regimen consisting of BIC/FTC/TAF (Gilead Sciences Ireland UC, California, USA). Meanwhile, the EFV + 3TC + TDF group received a combination therapy comprising TDF (Qilu Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Jinan, Shandong, China), 3TC (Jiangsu Chengkang Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., Yancheng, Jiangsu, China), and EFV (Jiangsu Chengkang Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.). We assessed viral load results using the last-observation-carried-forward method. A comprehensive overview of the research workflow is illustrated in Supplementary Figure 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/B770. For detailed information on statistical analysis, demographic characteristics, infection routes, and baseline virology and immunology data, please refer to Supplementary Table 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/B770.

A total of 162 participants were recruited, with 72 participants allocated to the BIC/FTC/TAF group and 90 participants assigned to the EFV + 3TC + TDF group. In the BIC/FTC/TAF group, participants had a mean age of 34.5 ± 12.1 years, with an average viral load of 4.83 (4.45, 5.32) log10 copies/mL and a CD4+ T cell count of 319 (102, 373) cells/mm3. In contrast, the EFV + 3TC + TDF group had an average age of 33.27 ± 13.96 years, with an average viral load of 4.69 (4.39, 5.10) log10 copies/mL and a CD4+ T cell count of 363 (203, 445) cells/mm3. Notably, no statistically significant difference at baseline characteristics was observed between the two groups (all P >0.05).

At weeks 4, 12, and 24, the BIC/FTC/TAF group consistently exhibited higher rates of viral load suppression (<50 copies/mL) compared to the EFV + 3TC + TDF group, with percentages of 75.0% (9/12), 93.1% (27/29), and 97.2% (70/72) vs. 25.0% (1/4), 37.5% (3/8), and 83.3% (75/90), respectively. The BIC/FTC/TAF group exhibited significantly higher viral load suppression rates than the EFV + 3TC + TDF group at weeks 12 and 24, with statistically significant differences (χ2 = 12.638, P <0.001; χ2 = 8.215, P = 0.004, respectively) [Table 1]. Similar outcomes were observed in the subgroup with baseline HIV-RNA >100,000 copies/mL.

The BIC/FTC/TAF group exhibited more significant viral load reductions than the EFV + 3TC + TDF group, with decreases of 3.00 log10 copies/mL, 4.26 log10 copies/mL, and 4.45 log10 copies/mL at 4 weeks, 12 weeks, and 24 weeks, respectively, compared to the EFV + 3TC + TDF group's reductions of 1.88 log10 copies/mL, 2.28 log10 copies/mL, and 3.89 log10 copies/mL at the same time points (Z = –1.698, P = 0.090; Z = –2.472, P = 0.013; Z = –2.993, P = 0.003) [Table 1].

Both the BIC/FTC/TAF and EFV + 3TC + TDF groups showed an increase in CD4+ T cell counts at week 24, with mean changes from baseline of 227 cells/μL and 150 cells/μL, respectively, and the difference between the two groups was statistically significant (Z = –2.093, P = 0.037). Over 20% of patients encountered neuropsychiatric adverse events in EFV + 3TC + TDF group, including dizziness (35.6%, 32/90), headache (28.9%, 26/90), and abnormal dreams (24.5%, 20/90), leading to treatment discontinuation of six participants (6.7%, 6/90) [Supplementary Table 2, http://links.lww.com/CM9/B770].

In 2014, the Joint United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS (UNAIDS) introduced the "90-90-90" goals, and the notable achievements in meeting these targets have propelled the adoption of a more ambitious vision to attain the "95-95-95" testing and treatment objectives by 2025 across all demographic groups, while sustaining the ongoing progress in testing and ART scale-up.[3] To attain this ambitious objective, cART plays a crucial role.

Given the prevalent focus on week 48 results in current real-world data on BIC/FTC/TAF, our study stands out by presenting viral load outcomes at weeks 4, 12, and 24, making it be the pioneering study of its kind in China. We observed that BIC/FTC/TAF demonstrated the potential for more rapid viral load reduction at weeks 12 and 24, even within the HIV-1 RNA >100,000 copies/mL subgroup. When evaluating its capacity for short-term viral load reduction, it also showed a faster decline compared to the EFV-based regimen, with a reduction of 3.10 log10 copies/mL at week 4, in line with findings reported by Gallant et al.[4] At week 24, in the BIC/FTC/TAF group, HIV-1 RNA levels were all below 200 copies/mL, with two individuals having HIV-1 RNA between 50 copies/mL and 200 copies/mL. There was no need to change the cART regimen, as these patients met the undetectable = untransmittable (U = U), were not infectious, and regular monitoring sufficed.[5] In contrast, in the EFV-based group, 6 of 90 patients (6.67%) had HIV-1 RNA levels exceeding 200 copies/mL, warranting ongoing monitoring and evaluation to determine if they meet the criteria for treatment failure. EFV was no longer recommended for patients with a viral load exceeding 500,000 copies/mL, a recommendation in alignment with the 2021 edition of China AIDS Diagnosis and Treatment guidelines, where poor efficacy in individuals with high viral loads was highlighted, consistent with the findings of our study.

The rate of CD4+ T cell count recovery is typically most rapid in the first 3 months of suppressive ART, followed by more gradual increases over time.[5] Therefore, regimens that facilitate rapid immune system reconstitution are of paramount importance for treatment-naive patients. We observed a 1.5-fold increase in CD4+ T cell count in the BIC/FTC/TAF group at week 24 compared to the EFV-based group, offering patients greater benefits. However, there is no need to be concerned that a rapid CD4+ T cell count increase leads to an elevated incidence of immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS), as there is no evidence to support such a scenario.[5]

According to Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) guidelines, EFV can induce noticeable Central Nervous System (CNS) side effects, such as abnormal dreams, dizziness, headache, and depression, which tend to decrease over time.[5] In our study, we observed a high incidence of CNS adverse events in the EFV-based group compared to a very low rate in the BIC/FTC/TAF group. Interestingly, some patients in our study reported adverse events that did not decrease over time. Moreover, the study indicated a low rate of EFV discontinuation due to neuropsychiatric adverse events,[6] but our findings suggest a higher rate, possibly due to repeated communication by our outpatient physicians. Given the substantial workload and the lingering notion of "free treatment", clinicians might not consistently inform patients about better treatment options, such as INSTIs. Therefore, it becomes crucial to provide patients with comprehensive information about the risks and benefits associated with different treatment options, ensuring they receive equitable care.

Our study has several limitations. Firstly, it focused on 24 weeks of follow-up, limiting our ability to assess long-term clinical outcomes. Secondly, the majority of participants were male (91.67%), reflecting the higher prevalence of HIV among males in the region.[7] The challenges posed by the severe COVID-19 pandemic impacted patient follow-ups, resulting in missing data at certain time points, affecting the accuracy of viral load descriptions. Lastly, the high cost of testing for viral load and CD4+ T cell counts limited frequent testing, resulting in a small sample size. In the future, it will be crucial to expand the sample size and extend the study duration to thoroughly examine the impact of BIC/FTC/TAF in a real-world setting in China.

In summary, BIC/FTC/TAF presents distinct advantages among Chinese patients when contrasted with the EFV-based regimen. It exhibits the capacity to swiftly diminish HIV-1 RNA while concurrently elevating CD4+ T cell counts. Moreover, it demonstrates excellent tolerability. These findings offer valuable insights that can significantly inform the ongoing refinement of antiviral treatment strategies within the Chinese context.

The national and subnational burden of multiple myeloma (MM) in Italy from 1990 to 2023: results from the global burden of disease study 2023
Authors: GBD 2023 Italian Multiple Myeloma Collaborators
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundMultiple myeloma (MM) is the second most common oncohematological neoplasm after Non-Hodgkin Lymphomas. The present study investigated the burden of MM and its temporal trends from 1990 to 2023, at the Italian national and subnational level (21 regions).MethodsWe used the Global Burden of Disease Study 2023 estimates to describe the MM age-standardized incidence (ASIR), mortality (ASMR), years lived with disability (ASYLD), years of life lost (ASYLL), disability-adjusted life years (ASDR) rates, the related 1990–2023 crude all-ages and age-standardized annual rates of changes (ARC), as well the age-standardized mortality-to-incidence ratio (MIR). Finally, the national estimates for MM were compared to those for the GBD Global and High-middle Socio-Demographic Index (SDI) countries.FindingsIn 2023 at the Italian national level, there were 6700 MM new cases, 4100 deaths and 73,600 DALYs, whose vast majority came from YLLs (93.4%). The 1990–2023 ARC for ASIR showed a stable trend, both overall (0.28; 95% UI −0.20 to 0.91) and among males (ARC 0.32; −0.15 to 0.92) and females (ARC 0.22; −0.30 to 0.95). The ARC for ASMR (0.04; −0.08 to 0.17) also showed a stable pattern, as well as the ARC for ASDR (−0.07; −0.20 to 0.06). The MIR improved over time, from 68.0 to 55.1, similarly for both sexes. At the subnational level, the ASIR was stable in all the 21 local entities, while the ASMR and ASDR significantly improved in 1 and 7 regions, respectively.InterpretationComparing the GBD Italian estimates to those for the GBD Global and High-middle SDI countries, the age-standardized incidence, mortality and DALYs rates were higher, but their ARC showed a much more favourable trend than for the High-middle SDI countries. The Italian subnational estimates, including the MIR, were in good agreement with the national data, with some geographical differences.FundingThis work was supported by the Italian Ministry of Health, through the contribution given to the Institute for Maternal and Child Health IRCCS Burlo Garofolo, Trieste–Italy.Keywords: Multiple myeloma, Epidemiology, Global burden of disease, Italy, Incidence, mortality, Disability-adjusted life years

Research in context

Evidence before this study

Multiple myeloma (MM) is a severe and increasingly prevalent oncohematological cancer with high associated mortality and disability. Globally, MM incidence and mortality have increased over time, particularly in higher-income and high-middle Socio-Demographic Index (SDI) countries. However, few studies have explored long-term trends at the Italian subnational level, and no published research has systematically assessed MM burden in Italy across all 21 regions and autonomous provinces. Earlier Italian reports from ISTAT, AIRTUM, and AIOM have provided limited incidence and mortality data but lack the granularity, standardized methodology, and comprehensive disease burden metrics found in the GBD assessment.

Added value of this study

This is the first study to report national and subnational trends in the burden of MM in Italy from 1990 to 2023 using GBD 2023 estimates. Specifically, we provide comprehensive estimates of incidence, mortality, years lived with disability (YLDs), years of life lost (YLLs), disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), and mortality-to-incidence ratio (MIR) disaggregated by age, sex, region, and time. Compared to Global and High-middle SDI countries, Italy displayed more favorable trends in age-standardized rates of MM-related mortality and DALYs, despite having higher absolute rates. Subnational analyses revealed marked geographical differences, with northern regions showing greater improvements in mortality and DALYs. Overall, our findings highlight the impact of improved diagnostics and therapies over the last two decades and provide a high-resolution epidemiological picture of MM in Italy.

Implications of all the available evidence

The results emphasize that, while MM remains a significant public health challenge, particularly in aging populations, national and regional health strategies can effectively mitigate its burden. Italy's stable or improving trends in mortality and DALYs—despite a rising incidence—suggest that investments in early diagnosis and access to modern therapies have translated into better survival and chronic disease management. The observed subnational disparities point to the need for more equitable healthcare access and targeted interventions, especially in southern regions. Furthermore, the study reinforces the need for the GBD Study to gather and assess new evidence on potential risk factors for MM to enhance global and local disease monitoring. Overall, the substantial burden of multiple myeloma, which is largely driven by population ageing, highlights the need for ongoing surveillance, continued strengthening of health systems, and enhanced support for patient care.

Introduction

Multiple myeloma (MM) is the second most common oncohematological neoplasm after the Non-Hodgkin Lymphomas.1 It was first identified in London by the British surgeon Samuel Solly in 1844,2 while the term myeloma was forged by his German colleague von Rustizky in 1872.3 It took 30 years, to replace the classical Durie-Salmon staging system4 with a new prognostic score: the current standard, the International Staging Score (ISS), was published by the International Myeloma Working Group in 20055 and then updated twice, with the revised ISS (R–ISS) in 20156 and a second revision (R2-ISS) in 2022.7

In light of these consensus statements, several clinicopathological guidelines have been recently released by the International Myeloma Working Group-IMWG,8,9 the American Society of Clinical Oncology-ASCO and Cancer Care Ontario-CCO,10 the European Society for Medical Oncology-ESMO/European Hematology Association-EHA,11 and the Clinical Practice Guideline from the National Comprehensive Cancer Center.12 Following these recommendations, the main MM subtype classification depends on the involved immunoglobulin classes: heavy chain, light chain and non-secretory myeloma.

The GLOBOCAN 2022 Study by the International Agency for Research on Cancer, carried out in 185 countries, estimated 187,800 MM incident cases (103,800 and 84,000 among males and females, respectively) and 121,200 deaths (66,900 and 54,300), ranking 21st for incidence and 17th for mortality among 36 cancers, and accounting for 0.9% of the new cancer cases and 1.2% of cancer deaths.13 As for the Cancer Statistics 2024 from the American Cancer Society, MM is expected to cause 35,800 new cases and 12,500 deaths in the USA, with 60% of patients alive five years after the diagnosis, while in the Seventies it was only 25%.14

The Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries and Risk Factors Study 2023 (GBD 2023) assessed the burden of diseases in 204 countries and globally estimated 61,100/176,200 MM incident cases and 48,700/125,100 deaths in 1990/2023, respectively.15 The age-standardized incidence rate for MM among the seven GBD Super Regions varied from 0.31 to 3.43 in 1990 and from 0.86 to 3.87 in 2023, being highest in the High-Income region.15 Since the Italian estimates for MM showed significantly higher age-standardized incidence rates (3.50 and 4.48, respectively) than those reported at the Global level and in High-middle Socio-Demographic Index (SDI) countries, we focused our analysis on the Italian national and subnational level.

Methods

Overview

The GBD 2023 updated the GBD 2021 estimates of morbidity and mortality across geographical regions, time, age and sex, contributing to a global calculation of the disease burden. The GBD 2023 analyzed 375 diseases and injuries, including 292 causes of death and 88 environmental, occupational, behavioral, and metabolic risks. Italy ranks as the fifth-largest data contributor to the GBD 2023 Study, providing 1658 sources, including vital registration systems, national surveys, disease registries, and administrative datasets. Specifically for MM, 44 unique data sources—including disease registries and vital registration systems—and a total of 68,602 metadata records were used to inform the estimation process for Italy.

The methods for the generation of GBD 2023 estimates have been previously described15: they comply with the Guidelines for Accurate and Transparent Health Estimates Reporting (GATHER).16 Additional details on the modeling strategies used for mortality and disability related to MM are available in the Supplementary File. Statistical analyses reported in the present manuscript were performed in R (version 4.5.0).17 This manuscript was produced as part of the GBD Collaborator Network and under the GBD Protocol.18

GBD measures

The Italian national and subnational estimates for the MM burden were reported for 1990 and 2023. The subnational estimates were available for all the 19 local regions and 2 autonomous provinces. The burden of MM has been described as incident cases, crude all-ages and age-standardized incidence rates (ASIR), death counts, crude all-ages and age-standardized mortality rates (ASMR), DALYs, crude all-ages and age-standardized DALYs rates (ASDR), and by the corresponding 1990–2023 age-standardized average annualized rates of change (ARC). ARC was calculated as the difference in the natural logarithm of the values at the start and end of the time interval divided by the number of years in the interval. Final estimates represent the median estimate across 500 draws, and 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs) are derived as the 2.5th and 97.5th percentile values across the draws. All rates have been reported per 100,000 person-years and the GBD world population age standard has been used for the estimation of all age-standardized rates. At the national level, the above-described metrics, as well as the years lived with disability (YLDs), the years of life lost (YLLs) and their age-standardized rates (ASYLD and ASYLL), have been reported for both sexes and disaggregated by sex. At the same level, the ASMR and ASDR have also been reported by all causes, only neoplasms and only MM. DALYs are defined as the sum of YLDs and YLLs. The MM-related survival has been analyzed with the age-standardized mortality-to-incidence ratio (MIR) for 1990 and 2023, both at the national and subnational levels.

Comparisons with the GBD 2023 Global and High-middle Socio-Demographic Index (SDI) countries estimates

The ASIR, ASMR, ASDR, ASYLD and ASYLL for MM at the Italian national level, as well as their average annual rates of change from 1990 to 2023, have been reported in comparison either to the GBD 2023 Global estimates or the High-middle Socio-Demographic Index (SDI) countries. SDI is a composite indicator of development status, built as a geometric mean of 0–1 indices of total fertility rate for females under age 25, average years of education in ages 15 years and older, and lag-distributed income per capita. The GBD 2023 divided all countries into 5 SDI-dependent custom regions (grouped in quintiles, low/low-middle/middle/high-middle/high): Italy belongs to the high-middle SDI quintile.

GBD causes hierarchy and WHO-ICD codes for MM

In GBD 2023, the causes of health loss are aggregated in a four-level classification, at an increasing level of detail. For the current research, we focused on level 1 “Noncommunicable diseases” (B), level 2 “Neoplasms” (B.1), level 3 “Multiple Myeloma” (B.1.31). All the reported analyses have been performed at level 3. Version 10 of the WHO International Classification of Diseases (ICD) was used and the codes associated with MM were C88–C90.9.19

GBD subnational level and EUROSTAT NUTS classifications for Italy

Italy is administratively divided into 21 subnational entities (19 regions and 2 autonomous provinces), just the same 21 available in GBD 2023. This classification corresponds to the EuroStat “Nomenclature of territorial units for statistics” (NUTS), a hierarchical 4-levels system that divides the EU territory for regional socio-economic analyses and cohesion policies.20 The GBD also presents estimates for Italy at the NUTS-1 level, comprising five macro-regions. Table S1 reports the classification of Italian NUTS-1 and NUTS-2 regions.

Data extraction and reporting

All the estimates were extracted from the Global Health Data Exchange tool (http://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-results-tool),18 and the results have been presented for the years 1990 and 2023 as count estimates (incident cases, death cases and DALYs) and their corresponding all-ages and age-standardized rates per 100,000 population (ASIR, ASMR, ASDR, ASYLD, ASYLL), as well as the average annual rates of change (ARC) from 1990 to 2023. The age-standardized MIR (ASMIR) was calculated as the ratio (ASMR/ASIR)∗100. Age-standardization was carried out using the GBD standard population, which is based on a weighted average of the global age distribution in 2010. In this standard, greater weight is given to middle age groups to minimize the disproportionate influence of the youngest and oldest age categories.

All estimates are reported with 95% UIs. Differences in median percentage changes were considered significant if 95% UIs did not overlap. A median percent change was considered indicative of a stable trend when its associated 95% UI included the null value. All-age and age-standardized rates were reported for all metrics to disentangle the influence of population aging from other structural factors potentially contributing to temporal and regional differences in MM burden.

Role of the funding source

The funders of this study had no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, or the writing of this report. The corresponding author had full access to all the data in the study and had the final responsibility to submit it for publication.

Results

Italian national estimates

Incidence

In 1990, 3113 (95% UIs: 2319–4800) MM incident cases occurred, while 6680 (4987–8636) in 2023 (Table 1, Fig. 1). In the same period, the ASIR moved from 3.50 (2.58–5.46) to 4.48 (3.36–5.07). Disaggregated by sex, 50.4% of incident cases occurred among males (ASIR 4.14; 3.10–6.27) in 1990, while 54.0% in 2023 (ASIR 5.47; 4.09–6.82); on the other hand, the ASIR for females was 3.01 (2.10–4.86) in 1990 and 3.67 (2.80–5.00) in 2023. The ARC for ASIR was +0.28 (−0.14 to 0.91), with those for males and females being +0.32 (−0.15 to 0.92) and +0.22 (−0.30 to 0.95), respectively.

Mortality

MM caused 2175 (1968–2393) deaths in 1990 and 4134 (3597–4660) in 2023 (Table 2, Fig. 2). In the same period, the ASMR was quite stable: from 2.38 (2.15–2.63) to 2.47 (2.18–2.75). Disaggregating the 1990 and 2023 mortality by sex, 48.5% and 52.1% of death cases occurred among males. Over time, the ASMR among males passed from 2.82 (2.52–3.21) to 3.03 (2.85–3.44), among females from 2.07 (1.82–2.36) to 2.05 (1.67–2.38). The average ARC for ASMR for both sexes was stable (0.04; −0.08 to 0.17), as happened for males (0.07; −0.08 to 0.25) and females (−0.01; −0.19 to 1.08). The age-standardized MIR showed a favourable tendency, from 68.0 to 55.1, and similarly improving for both males (from 68.1 to 55.4) and females (from 68.8 to 55.9).

Italian subnational estimates

Incidence

In 1990, the ASIR showed considerable variability, ranging from 2.56 in Basilicata and Calabria to 4.68 in AP Trento (Table 1); a lesser dispersion for ASIR was found in 2023, starting from 3.72 in Basilicata to 4.93 in Liguria. The AP Trento registered the lowest 1990–2023 average ARC for ASIR (ARC +0.03; −0.36 to 0.60), while the highest was found in Molise (ARC +0.62; 0.00–1.46). The ARC for ASIR was stable in all the 21 subnational entities: no areas had an increasing trend.

Mortality

As for the ASMR in 1990, it ranged from 1.84 (1.6–2.1) in Calabria to 3.10 (2.66–3.58) in AP Trento (Table 2). In 2023, it varied from 2.11 (1.8–2.43) in Valle d’Aosta to 2.68 (2.33–3.04) in Liguria. The average ARC for ASMR had notable subnational differences, from AP Trento (ARC −0.18; −0.33 to 0.00) to Molise (ARC +0.28; 0.04–0.57); in particular, it significantly rose in 5 regions (all belonging to the southern part of the country), was stable in the other 15 ones while significantly improved in the only AP Trento. At the subnational level, the national tendency for an improvement in the ASMIR was confirmed: while in 1990 it ranged from 64.1 to 74.3, in 2023 between 48.9 and 57.6.

DALYs

In 1990, the ASDR ranged from 41.89 (36.2–48.68) in Basilicata to 69.20 (59.13–79.01) in AP Trento (Table 4). In 2023, the lowest ASDR was reported in AP Bolzano (42.58; 36.24–50.11), while the highest was in Liguria (55.98; 49.22–63.28). In the selected period, the extreme estimates for the ARC for ASDR occurred in AP Trento (ARC −0.23; −0.38 to −0.06) and Molise (ARC +0.19; −0.02 to 0.47), respectively. Indeed, the ARC for ASDR significantly improved in 7 subnational regions (6 of them located in the northern part of the country and 1 in the central one) and was stable in the other 14: no area worsened.

The burden of MM in Italy: national incidence, mortality and DALYs, compared to GBD Global and High-middle SDI countries

Table 6 shows the 1990–2023 estimates for the ASIR, ASMR, ASYLD, ASYLL, ASDR and the corresponding average ARC, compared to those for GBD Global and High-middle SDI countries. The incidence of MM strongly increased in High-middle SDI countries (ARC +0.99; 0.39–1.96), while remaining stable both at GBD Global (ARC +0.21; −0.06 to 0.63) and Italian level (ARC +0.21; −0.06 to 0.63). More in detail, the national ASIR for 2023 (4.48) is still quite larger than that measured globally (1.93) and among High-middle SDI countries (1.25). The pattern of mortality reflects that for incidence: severely boosting among High-middle SDI countries (ARC +0.62; 0.24–1.16), while stable at GBD Global (ARC +0.05; −0.06 to 0.17) and Italian (ARC +0.04; −0.08 to 0.17) level. As for the national ASMR for 2023 (2.47), it is still less favourable than the Global (1.38) and among High-middle SDI countries (0.93). Discussion

According to the GBD Study 2023, the burden associated with hematological neoplasms at the global level is of 1,413,000 incident cases, 775,000 deaths and 24,409,000 DALYs for the year 2023. Of them, Italy accounted for 43,000 incident cases, 17,700 deaths and 339,200 DALYs: MM represents the third cause of death (23.4%) and YLDs (21.7%) among oncohematological diseases. Despite the remarkable advances in MM treatment that occurred in the last 20 years, its severity is evidenced by the age-standardized mortality-to-incidence ratio: according to the GBD 2023 estimates for Italy, the MIR was 55.1 for MMs, versus 22.8 for non-Hodgkin lymphomas, 14.3 for Hodgkin lymphomas and 33.1 considering all leukemias, except 56.9 for acute myeloid leukemia.

As for the MM determinants, they have been widely studied and several of them partially overlap with those reported for other oncohematological cancers: high BMI, older age, male sex, history of indolent bone marrow alterations like monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS) and smoldering myeloma (SMM), ethnic and socio-economical differences, cytogenetic and molecular anomalies, microRNA profile alterations, high familial prevalence, exposure to ionizing radiations, occupational exposure to solvents and heavy metals (i.e., herbicides, pesticides and benzene), gut microbiome alterations, immunosuppressive conditions. However, several researchers postulated that smoking and alcohol abuse play no clear role in myeloma carcinogenesis. Currently, the GBD 2023 identified only one major risk factor (high BMI) for MM, estimating that it accounts for 7% of worldwide myeloma-related DALYs.However, in Italy, the all-age DALY rates for MM attributable to high BMI in 2023 were estimated at 12.5 per 100,000 (95% UI: −4.9 to 30.2) for males and 9.2 per 100,000 (−3.6 to 22.8) for females, corresponding to 0.04% and 0.03% of total MM DALYs, respectively. However, as the corresponding 95% UI crosses zero, the association should be interpreted with caution.

In light of the findings hereby reported, this malignant monoclonal gammopathy remains a serious threat to global health, as well as in Italy. Since 2005, great advances have been made in MM diagnosis (due to the adoption of Magnetic Resonance Imaging [MRI] and Positron Emission Tomography [PET]) and treatment (due to the introduction of immunomodulatory drugs [ImiDs, lenalidomide and pomalidomide], proteasome inhibitors [PIs, bortezomib and carfilzomib], immunotherapies [daratumumab, elotuzumab, isatuximab], histone deacetylases drugs [panobinostat] and antibody-drug conjugates [belantamab mafodotin]). Moreover, the role of Chimeric Antigen Receptor-T (CAR-T) cell therapies and allogeneic bone marrow transplantation is currently reserved for clinical trials, while autologous bone marrow transplantation is regularly associated with induction-conditioning-maintenance sequential schedules. All these medical interventions, like the modern triplet and quadruplet regimens, as well as multidrug combinations, contributed to a wide improvement of MM progression-free and overall survival: but the risk of biochemical and clinical relapse remains extremely high.

Several studies have been recently dedicated to myeloma epidemiology,21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26 while its diagnosis, risk stratification and management are constantly updated.27, 28, 29 Cancer incidence, mortality and risk factors at the global, regional and national level have been covered by some official GBD publications,30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 35 and one was specifically dedicated to the global burden of myeloma in 2018.36 Moreover, independent researchers investigated the epidemiology of MM, one drawing from GBD 2016 estimates,37 five from the GBD 2019 ones38, 39, 40, 41, 42 and two from the GBD 2021.43,44 Estimates of the global incidence and mortality burden for MM have been recently published by the investigators of the International Agency for Research on Cancer.45 Finally, national data on myeloma epidemiology are available from Italian sources: the I.Stat database from the Istituto Nazionale di Statistica-ISTAT,46 the cancer registry database from the Associazione Italiana Registri Tumori-AIRTUM47 and the volume “I numeri del cancro in Italia” from the Associazione Italiana di Oncologia Medica-AIOM.48 The Italian health performance and burden of cancer have been also covered by the publications of the Italian GBD Initiative, relative to GBD 2017.49,50

The burden of myeloma at the national level worsened over time: from 1990 to 2023, incident and death cases doubled, while DALYs increased by 50%; the efficacy of new therapeutic regimens, aiming to chronicize the disease, is confirmed by the increase in the YLDs and the reduction in the YLLs. A large part of the increased burden of MM is due to population ageing, as proved by the differences between crude and standardized rates. Disaggregating these metrics by sex, a comparable distribution for the age-standardized annual rates of change was found. Among males, the ARC for ASIR/ASMR/ASDR were 0.32/0.07/−0.05, while among females 0.22/−0.01/−0.11 without significant modifications over time.

Myeloma-related deaths are increasing in Italy, while those due to all neoplasms are significantly declining (28.3% of all causes of death); the myeloma-related DALYs are stable, conversely from the significant reduction for all neoplasms: MM remains an extremely severe disease, with a huge persistent burden.

Comparing the Italian scenario to the GBD Global and the High-middle SDI countries, the annual rates of change for ASIR are significantly rising only among High-middle SDI countries. In general, Italy has better performances than the High-middle SDI countries in the ARC for ASIR, ASMR, ASYLL and ASDR (stable versus worsening incidence, disability and mortality): also the marked reduction in MIR confirms the good standard of the Italian health care system.

At the subnational level, we generally observed more favourable tendencies for both the 1990–2023 ARC for ASMR and ASDR in the northern areas of the country, if compared to the southern ones. A greater burden is evident in the north-west area of the country, especially in terms of incidence. All subnational areas showed a stable incidence over time. Focusing on the age-standardized rate of mortality, 15 subnational regions were stable and only AP Trento registered a significant improvement; notably, all the 21 subnational MIR estimates improved over time with a comparable pattern, with results at the highest international level. Regarding the age-standardized rate of DALYs, seven subnational regions (Valle d’Aosta, Lombardia, Veneto, AP Trento, Friuli-Venezia Giulia, Emilia Romagna and Lazio) had a significant reduction and the remaining 14 were stable. A promising result, but it is worth noting that the highest DALYs reductions have been obtained in the regions with the highest per capita-GDP.

However, several potential limitations of the GBD Study should be acknowledged. Although data quality and availability in Italy are generally high at both national and subnational levels, some challenges persist. For example, hospital outpatient data are not codified, limiting their integration into burden estimates. Furthermore, data collection systems used by general practitioners and primary care pediatricians are separate and not interoperable. Cause-of-death data also undergo a two-year validation process before becoming official, which may delay the availability of accurate information. Lastly, the disability weights used in the GBD framework may not fully capture cultural or contextual differences, which could influence the accuracy of disability burden estimations.

In the last 20 years, no other oncohematological cancer has made outstanding advances in diagnosis and treatment like MM: nevertheless, its epidemiological and clinical scenario remains critical, both globally and in Italy. The most interesting finding from the GBD 2023 estimates is that the national data showed better performances in comparison to High-middle SDI countries, in terms of age-standardized incidence, mortality and DALYs rates: stable at the national level, these rates are progressively worsening in High-middle SDI countries. Moreover, while the Italian ASYLL is marginally declining, it's rising in the High-middle SDI countries; not to mention the great improvement in the mortality-to-incidence ratio, partly due to improved and earlier diagnosis. Subnational estimates are in good agreement with the national ones and the MM burden measures show similar values, when compared to the other blood cancers. Some subnational differences do exist, but a notable improvement in the mortality-to-incidence ratio has been ubiquitously reached in 2023. All these results confirm the efficacy of the national strategies adopted against oncological diseases.

Some suggestions for the future: first, the inclusion of additional attributable risk factors in the GBD Study would be beneficial, since the GBD 2023 only includes high BMI, and considering that the current clinical research is focusing on new biomolecular and genetic determinants. Second, even if the MM incidence is exponentially associated with age, the occurrence of new cases <50 years of age deserves to be strictly monitored. Due to the worldwide interest and the recent great advances in therapies, the burden of MM will benefit from the results of clinical research, with a consequent quick evolution: in a few years, the GBD estimates for myeloma will need a full update. In this context, future work could explore the integration of forecasting approaches to anticipate potential shifts in disease burden and inform strategic planning. To be effective, such projections would need to account for evolving clinical practices and demographic dynamics and be regularly updated to reflect the rapidly changing landscape of MM research and treatment.

Preclinical development of a well-defined semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccine against Haemophilus influenzae type b
Authors: Wei Han, Chunjun Qin, Wei Tong, Kexin Yang, Xianbin Tian, Jing-Ren Zhang, Jiankai Liu, Peter. H. Seeberger, Jing Hu, Jian Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Vaccines are the most effective method for preventing bacterial infections. Identification of the best hapten is an essential step in the development of third-generation Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib) conjugate vaccines. A comprehensive understanding of the relationship between Hib oligosaccharide chain length and immunological activities remains elusive. A series of Hib capsular polysaccharide (CPS) oligosaccharide derivatives of different lengths were synthesized to establish carbohydrate structure-function relationships. Glycan microarray screening and immunological evaluation of oligosaccharides revealed that tetramer 4, comprising four repeated disaccharide units, is the key immunogenic epitope of CPS. The investigation into the influence of carrier proteins, adjuvants, and vaccination dose on immunogenicity demonstrated that an equivalent dose of glycoconjugate 4-TT without adjuvant can induce a similar immune response as a licensed Hib vaccine (MINHAI Hib). Adjuvant-free 4-TT glycoconjugates can induce both a humoral and a cellular immune response as efficiently as licensed Hib vaccine. This formulation exhibited protective efficacy in a mouse septic infection model through effective pathogen clearance from the circulation to the liver-resident macrophage Kupffer cells and sinusoidal endothelial cells in a mouse septic infection model. In conclusion, a well-defined semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccine candidate 4-TT was developed with a more straightforward and adaptable manufacturing process and demonstrated excellent efficacy comparable to licensed Hib vaccine, providing a rationale for further evaluation in clinical trials.

INTRODUCTION

Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib) is a Gram-negative bacterium that colonizes the human nasopharynx that can cause severe invasive diseases such as meningitis, pneumonia, and sepsis (1, 2). Vaccines are the most effective way to prevent viral and bacterial infections. Bacterial surface polysaccharides are one of the key antigens for antimicrobial vaccines because of their unique structures (3). Since polysaccharides alone are weak antigens, glycoconjugate vaccines are a successful strategy to induce strong and long-lasting antibody responses, especially in infants and young children (4). The capsular polysaccharide (CPS) of Hib, as the major virulence factor, consists of ribosyl-ribitol-phosphate repeating units (5, 6). Most licensed Hib vaccines are based on the conjugation of isolated CPS to carrier proteins. Following the introduction of Hib vaccines, cases of meningitis and epiglottitis have declined sharply (5). Attention to Hib disease and vaccines is still needed to address several persistent challenges, including uneven vaccine coverage, sporadic outbreaks of Hib infection, and the emergence of multidrug-resistant and extensively drug-resistant strains (1, 7, 8). Moreover, the polysaccharide-based Hib vaccines continue to face inherent challenges in production and quality control because of the heterogeneity and complexity of polysaccharides. There is still an urgent need for the development of glycoconjugate vaccines with structurally well-defined glycans.

Carbohydrate synthesis is a crucial method to obtain synthetic carbohydrate fragments and provide reliable drug substance for the development of semisynthetic carbohydrate vaccines. The chemical synthesis of the polyribosyl-ribitol-phosphate (PRP) from Hib CPS relies on an efficient formation of the phosphodiester bonds. A series of methods for constructing phosphodiester bonds have been developed, including the phosphodiester, phosphotriester, phosphite, phosphoramidite, and H-phosphonate methods (9). Hoogerhout et al. (10, 11) completed the synthesis of 1- to 3-mers related to Hib CPS based on the phosphotriester method. Laval and Just (12) synthesized 1- to 5-mers using the phosphoramidite method. Kandil et al. (13) and Elie et al. (14) achieved the solid-phase synthesis of related 2-, 3-, 5-, and 6-mers. Nilsson et al. (15) synthesized the related pentamer using the H-phosphonate method. Baek et al. (16) reported the synthesis of related 4-, 6-, 8-, and 10-mers through a modular synthetic route based on the H-phosphonate method. Notably, Verez-Bencomo et al. (17) developed a monomeric one-pot H-phosphonate–based process to produce a mixture of oligosaccharides related to Hib CPS with an average of 8-mers. This technology gave rise to the first semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccine (Quimi-Hib). Advances in carbohydrate synthesis technology help to improve carbohydrate-based vaccines.

Elucidating the protective antigenic determinants of bacterial CPS serves as a critical step in the development of an effective vaccine. Nonne et al. (18) demonstrated that the dimer is the minimal structural element required for the binding of a functional human monoclonal antibody against depolymerized Hib CPS. However, a more precise analysis is limited by poor size control and structural heterogeneity of degraded CPS. Structurally homogeneous synthetic oligosaccharides have been used to elucidate the immunogenic epitope of Hib CPS. Quimi-Hib vaccine contains a mixture of PRP oligosaccharides with an average of 8-mers bearing a 2-(2-aminoethoxy)ethoxy linker and a terminal maleimide-thiol spacer arm at ribose O3 position (Fig. 1), which can prime an effective immune response against Hib in animals and clinical trials, comparing favorably to licensed vaccines with the extracted polysaccharide. Among oligosaccharides with 2- and 3-mers bearing a 7-aminoheptyl and a glutaric dialdehyde spacer arm at ribitol O5 position, it was found that the trimer is the minimally immunogenic unit (19). Among PRP oligosaccharides of 2- to 4-mers bearing 3-aminopropyl and a terminal 3-thioglutaryl spacer arm at ribitol O5 position, tetramer-based glycoconjugates were found to be highly immunogenic in adult monkeys (20, 21). Seeberger et al. (16) obtained tetramers, hexamers, octamers, and decamers bearing a 5-aminopentyl and a terminal thiol-maleimide spacer arm at ribitol O5 position. The synthetic tetramers show similar potency to the native polysaccharide in terms of immunogenicity and recognition by anti-Hib antibodies. These works provide an excellent basis for the development of a well-defined semisynthetic glycoconjugate Hib vaccine.

Semisynthetic glycoconjugates based on synthetic oligosaccharides of Hib CPS.

A comprehensive overview of the structure and formulation of Hib vaccines based on synthetic PRP oligosaccharides derived from previous studies, highlighting variations in linker installation positions, oligosaccharide chain lengths, carrier proteins, adjuvants, and the structure of linkers and spacer arms.

To accelerate the development of a semisynthetic glycoconjugate Hib vaccine, limitations remain to be addressed, and some optimization is required. For the mass production of glycan hapten, a simple and efficient method for constructing phosphodiester bonds is essential. The H-phosphonate method has excellent condensation efficiency and has been successfully used for the production of Quimi-Hib vaccine. However, it faces some limitations for the production of synthetic PRP oligosaccharides with a defined chain length on a large scale (22, 23), such as side reactions during the oxidation of H-phosphonate diester intermediate and difficulties purifying the highly polar intermediate. The phosphoramidite method is widely used to form phosphodiester bonds in nucleic acid chemistry and scales well since it is high yielding and the phosphotriester intermediates are less polar (22, 24). Nevertheless, the phosphoramidite method has not been extensively explored for PRP oligosaccharide synthesis as the H-phosphonate method. The immunological activities of structurally well-defined PRP oligosaccharides have not been systematically clarified. Several factors including carrier proteins, vaccination dose, and adjuvants play crucial roles in the efficacy of semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccines that need to be comprehensively explored.

As part of an effort to develop a well-defined semisynthetic glycoconjugate Hib vaccine, we aimed to obtain 12 synthetic PRP oligosaccharides ranging from 1- to 12-mers bearing a 2-(2-aminoethoxy)ethoxy linker and a terminal maleimide-thiol spacer arm at the ribose O3 position that exhibited reliable clinical safety profiles as part of the Quimi-Hib vaccine (17). For large-scale production of PRP oligosaccharides in homogeneous solutions, a modular synthesis strategy was developed on the basis of the phosphoramidite method. A systematic analysis of the relationship between oligosaccharide length and immunological activities was performed to identify the optimal immunogenic epitope (25). The antigenicity of synthetic PRP oligosaccharides was first evaluated using glycan microarray technology. The antigenic oligosaccharides were conjugated with a carrier protein for immunogenicity evaluation. The influence of the carrier protein, adjuvant type, and vaccination dose on the immunoprotection of the glycoconjugates was all assessed for the optimization of vaccine formulation. The immune response and protective efficacy of the glycoconjugates were evaluated by a series of preclinical tests, including enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), glycan microarray, in vitro binding assay, serum bactericidal test, and mouse septic infection model, paving a way for the development of a glycoconjugate Hib vaccine based on a well-defined oligosaccharide.

RESULTS

Synthesis of oligosaccharides of 1- to 12-mers

We designed a modular synthetic strategy based on the phosphoramidite method to efficiently synthesize PRP oligosaccharides containing 1- to 12-mers. The 2-(2-azidoethoxy) ethanol linker was assembled to the ribose O3 at the nonreducing end of the glycan chain for subsequent immunological evaluation to resemble the Quimi-Hib vaccine. We developed a modular synthetic strategy based on the phosphoramidite method to synthesize the 12 PRP oligosaccharides. A monomer glycoside phosphoramidite building block 13 was synthesized from 1,2,3,5-tetra-O-acetyl-β-D-ribofuranoside and 5-O-allyl-2,3,4-tri-O-benzyl-D-ribitol by a six-step reaction in 36% overall yield (Fig. 2A). Coupling of monomer glycoside phosphoramidite 13 and 14 (fig. S1) under 1H-tetrazole (TetH) activation in acetonitrile afforded P(III) phosphite triester intermediate that was converted to the corresponding P(V) phosphate triester compound 15 by iodine-mediated oxidation in 88% overall yield. Delevulinoylation was then carried out by using hydrazine acetate in MeOH/dichloromethane (DCM) and gave compound 16. Subsequent treatment with 2-cyanoethyl N, N-diisopropylchlorophosphoramidite (CEPCl) afforded dimer glycoside phosphoramidite building block 17 in 85% yield.

The linker was installed before the assembly of the PRP fragments, thereby streamlining subsequent synthetic steps and improving coupling efficiency (Fig. 2B). We coupled the 2-(2-azidoethoxy) ethanol with the monomer glycoside phosphoramidite 13 by the TetH-catalyzed coupling reaction to obtain compound 18 in 92% yield. The monomethoxytrityl (MMTr) group was then selectively removed by trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) in DCM, yielding acceptor 19 in 85% yield. This acceptor was then coupled with the monomer glycoside phosphoramidite 13 to give dimer 20 in 83% yield. Further removal of the MMTr group by treatment with TFA produced dimer acceptor 21 in 78% yield (Fig. 2C, entry 1). Compound 20 was recovered as well (13%). Considering the low removal rate of the MMTr group and the challenging separation of two similarly polar compounds, an efficient method to cleave the MMTr group is crucial for further PRP oligosaccharide chain extension. Thus, a series of conditions were explored to remove the MMTr group in compound 20. Initially, trifluoroacetic anhydride was introduced into the TFA condition and increased the deprotection yield to 89% (Fig. 2C, entry 2). Subsequently, four other deprotection methods, including acetic acid (AcOH), sodium bisulfate–silica (NaHSO4-SiO2), ceric ammonium nitrate–silica (CAN-SiO2), and zinc bromide (ZnBr2), produced compound 21 in only 65 to 80% yield (Fig. 2C, entries 3 to 6). Last, compound 20 was transformed into dimer acceptor 21 in 98% yield by using boron trifluoride diethyl etherate (BF3·Et2O) in MeOH/DCM (Fig. 2C, entry 7). This method was used for all removals of MMTr groups during the synthesis of the target PRP oligosaccharides.

Notably, a decrease in coupling yield from 92% for monomer 18 to 83% for dimer 20 under TetH activation highlighted concerns about the low coupling reactivity of larger acceptors. Partial cleavage of the MMTr group was detected in the TetH-catalyzed coupling reaction of 13 and 19 that may be caused by the relatively strong acidity of TetH [pKa (where Ka is the acid dissociation constant) comparable to acetic acid]. Considering the formation of by-products caused by unexpected deprotection during chain elongation of oligosaccharides, a series of glycoside phosphoramidite activation conditions were evaluated to avoid MMTr cleavage. The dimer acceptor 21, monomer glycoside phosphoramidite 13, and dimer glycoside phosphoramidite 17 were used as model compounds (Fig. 2D). A [2+1] coupling between acceptor 21 and monomer glycoside phosphoramidite 13 under TetH activation and subsequent oxidation produced trimer 22 in only 78% yield (Fig. 2D, entry 1). Subsequently, 4,5-dicyanoimidazole (DCI) that is more reactive and less acidic than TetH was used as a catalyst to increase the yield of the trimer 22 to 86% (Fig. 2D, entry 2). Previous studies have shown that salt coupling agents derived from strong acids and highly nucleophilic organic bases effectively activate nucleoside phosphoramidites (9). Accordingly, pyridinium trifluoroacetate (PyTFA) was used as a coupling agent to increase the yield of trimer 22 to 93% (Fig. 2D, entry 3). Moreover, 22 was afforded in 98% yield by utilization of 1-phenylimidazolium trifluoromethanesulfonate (N-PhIMT) as a coupling agent followed by tert-butyl hydroperoxide (TBHP)–mediated oxidation (Fig. 2D, entry 4). We conducted a [2+2] coupling to assess the catalytic efficacy of DCI, PyTFA, and N-PhIMT with the dimer glycoside phosphoramidite 17. A coupling between 17 and 21 under DCI activation and (+)-(8,8-dichlorocamphorylsulfonyl)oxaziridine–mediated oxidation gave tetramer 23 in 85% yield (Fig. 2D, entry 5). The coupling reactions based on PyTFA and N-PhIMT produced 23 in 90 and 96% yields, respectively (Fig. 2D, entries 6 and 7). N-PhIMT/TBHP–mediated construction of phosphodiester bond was more suitable for chemical synthesis of PRP oligosaccharides.

On the basis of the optimized conditions for the coupling and deprotection reactions, a modular synthesis strategy for the synthetic PRP oligosaccharides was developed (Fig. 3). Using dimer 21 as acceptor, the PRP oligosaccharides with an even number of repeating units were synthesized by coupling with the dimer glycoside phosphoramidite building block 17. BF3·Et2O treatment removed the MMTr group in 23 to give tetramer acceptor 24 in 97% yield. Union of 17 and 24 under N-PhIMT activation and subsequent TBHP-mediated oxidation produced hexamer 26 in 94% yield. Repeating the above steps, a synthesis of oligosaccharides through sequential deprotection of the MMTr group and coupling with the dimer glycoside phosphoramidite 17 produced octamer 29 (95%), decamer 32 (92%), and dodecamer 35 (91%). Subsequently, tetramer 24, hexamer 27, octamer 30, and decamer 33 were each coupled with the monomer glycoside phosphoramidite 13 to yield the PRP oligosaccharides with an odd number of repeating units, including pentamer 25 (97%), heptamer 28 (94%), nonamer 31 (94%), and undecamer 34 (93%). After removal of the MMTr group from the fully protected synthetic PRP oligosaccharides, global deprotection was performed, including removal of the cyanoethyl groups (OCE) under NH3·H2O treatment and removal of the benzyl groups through hydrogenolysis using Pd/C and H2. The deprotected oligosaccharides were then purified on Sephadex LH-20 and converted to the corresponding sodium salt using Dowex 50W X8 Na+ resin to obtain the synthetic PRP oligosaccharides 1 to 12 as white solid in good yields over three steps (>80% for 1- to 7-mers, >65% for 8- to 12-mers). The 1H and 13C nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra of the PRP oligosaccharides 1 to 12 are identical with that of the natural isolate (26). The slight spectral differences are most obvious at 1-H and 3-H of the ribose residues on the nonreducing end, probably because of the introduction of the linker in the synthetic oligosaccharides.

Antigenicity screening of 1- to 12-mers using glycan microarrays

We first performed glycan microarray analysis to evaluate the antigenicity of synthetic PRP oligosaccharides. Oligosaccharides of various lengths were printed on N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS)–activated microarray slides. The isolated Hib CPS and CPS glycoconjugate (CPS-TT) were used as positive controls, as well as several glycans included Enterocloster bolteae WAL-16351 (Eb) CPS and synthetic disaccharide (β-D-Ribf-(1 → 4)-α-L-Rhap) as negative controls (Fig. 4A). The glycan arrays were probed with two antisera against Hib CPS and CPS-TT, respectively (fig. S2). Glycan microarray analysis revealed that most fragments can be recognized by immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies in both antisera (Fig. 4, B and C, and fig. S3). The undecamer 11 and dodecamer 12 showed the highest binding affinity for IgG antibodies among all the synthetic oligosaccharides, suggesting their good antigenicity. Dimer 2, tetramer 4, and hexamer 6 also presented strong IgG binding strength. Furthermore, dimer 2 represents the minimal antigenic structural fragment. This finding is consistent with the previously published results (18). However, the immunogenicity generated by the dimer glycoconjugate is suboptimal, as IgM predominates and contributes to the unsatisfactory outcome (20). In addition, no detectable antisera binding to the negative-control group demonstrated the antibodies’ specific recognition capacity for synthetic oligosaccharides.

Immunogenicity evaluation of selected synthetic PRP–based conjugates

To further verify the optimal hapten for innovative Hib vaccine development, we selected tetramer 4, hexamer 6, octamer 8, undecamer 11, and dodecamer 12 to prepare glycoconjugates based on the glycan microarray screening results and previous reports. A licensed Hib vaccine (MINHAI Hib), produced from isolated Hib CPS conjugated with tetanus toxoid (TT), was used as a positive control. The selected PRP oligosaccharides (4, 6, 8, 11, and 12) were conjugated to TT through a well-established thiol-maleimide coupling strategy (16, 17). The average numbers of glycan/proteins of synthetic PRP-TT conjugates were determined as 18.9 (4-TT), 7.0 (6-TT), 5.0 (8-TT), 7.6 (11-TT), and 8.2 (12-TT), respectively (fig. S4). New Zealand rabbits were immunized subcutaneously with Freund’s adjuvant (FA) mixed with glycoconjugates or phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) as a negative control. Each group of rabbits was vaccinated three times every 2 weeks, and antisera were collected every 7 days during the vaccination (Fig. 5A). The antiserum IgG antibody levels of each group were determined by ELISA using the Hib CPS and CPS-TT as the coating antigens, respectively. All glycoconjugates induced a specific humoral response, with titers ranging from 2 to 30 times that of the negative control (Fig. 5B). The glycoconjugate 4-TT induced the highest IgG antibody level, even much higher than that of the positive-control group. 6-TT, 8-TT, 11-TT, and 12-TT all induced detectable immune responses, albeit at lower levels compared with the positive control. 11-TT and 12-TT elicited moderate IgG antibody levels, whereas 6-TT and 8-TT exhibited the lowest efficacy. The immunogenic efficacy of glycoconjugate 6-TT was consistent with previous studies (16). Despite demonstrating adequate antigenicity, its suboptimal immunogenicity may be attributed to the spatial configuration of the long-chain structure. Notably, 11-TT and 12-TT both demonstrated a paradoxical profile of preserved antigenicity but compromised immunogenicity similar to 6-TT, likely arising from their shared long-chain spatial configurations.

Glycan microarray analysis was used to further determine the binding profile of each antiserum generated against distinct oligosaccharides (Fig. 5C and fig. S5) (27). The results showed that antiserum IgG antibodies of all groups recognized their corresponding oligosaccharides. 4-TT–, 11-TT–, and 12-TT–induced IgG antibodies all had relatively high binding affinity with smaller fragments (n ≤ 4), undecamer 11 and dodecamer 12, demonstrating a similar PRP oligosaccharide-recognition profile as IgG antibodies against licensed Hib vaccine. The fragments 4, 11, and 12 exhibited antibody binding to all antiserum IgG antibodies, suggesting their good antigenicity in agreement with previous results. Notably, antiserum IgG antibodies against Hib vaccine presented strongest binding affinity with 4, also implying that tetramer 4 represents a pivotal immunogenic epitope.

The interaction of antisera with native Hib bacteria was detected by fluorescence microscopy and flow cytometry. The fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)–labeled inactivated Hib bacteria (InHib) were coincubated with postimmune serum and then labeled with anti–IgG-AF555 antibodies. Fluorescence images showed that antiserum IgG of all groups can bind to Hib bacteria (Fig. 5D and fig. S6A) but did not recognize Eb bacteria and Helicobacter pylori bacteria (fig. S6B). Flow cytometry analysis revealed that antiserum IgG antibodies against 4-TT exhibited the highest binding affinity with Hib bacteria among all the groups (Fig. 5E and fig. S6C). Antiserum IgG antibodies against 6-TT, 8-TT, 11-TT, and 12-TT bound to Hib bacteria with comparably weaker affinities at similar dilution. The serum bactericidal antibody (SBA) assay was performed to further evaluate the efficacy. SBA results revealed substantial serum bactericidal activity in all antisera, with antisera against 4-TT exhibiting the highest bactericidal activity, significantly surpassing the positive control (P < 0.0001 versus antisera against licensed Hib vaccine) (Fig. 5F and fig. S6D), consistent with flow cytometry results. These results collectively suggest that tetramer 4 represents a prime candidate for further development of semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccine against Hib.

Influence of carrier proteins, dose, and adjuvant on immunogenicity of glycoconjugate 4-TT

We further investigated the influence of carrier protein, adjuvant, and vaccination dose on the immunogenicity of the glycoconjugate formulation to identify an effective semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccine. To date, four approved carrier proteins are used in commercial Hib vaccines (28). TT or a nontoxic mutant of diphtheria toxin (CRM197) provides a good immune efficacy of polysaccharide-based Hib vaccines (29, 30), while Hib vaccines incorporating diphtheria toxin or Escherichia coli outer membrane protein (OMPC) showed limited immunogenicity (30). Tetramer 4 was conjugated to TT and CRM197 for immunization in animals to test their potential as carrier proteins for semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccines. The average numbers of glycan/proteins of synthetic 4-CRM197 conjugate were determined as 13.5 (fig. S7). ELISA results showed that both glycoconjugates induced a robust immune response and 4-TT induced higher titer of antiserum IgG antibodies than 4-CRM197 (Fig. 6A), revealing the advantage of TT as a carrier protein. Fluorescence images showed that both antiserum IgG antibodies against 4-TT and 4-CRM197 can effectively recognize InHib (fig. S8A). Flow cytometry analysis and SBA assay results showed that IgG antibodies induced by 4-TT exhibited a higher binding affinity and elevated bactericidal activity than 4-CRM197 (Fig. 6, B and C, and fig. S8, B and C), thereby confirming the ELISA results. TT is a superior carrier protein for semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccine against Hib.

Since the licensed Hib vaccine was not supplemented with an adjuvant, the immunological efficacy of 4-TT glycoconjugate was evaluated in the absence of adjuvants. Aluminum hydroxide (Alum) was used as a reference adjuvant. The adjuvant-free 4-TT experimental groups were set up with 5 or 20 μg of oligosaccharides per dose to test the dose-response effect. ELISA results demonstrate that the 4-TT formulated with Alum (4-TT + Alum) induced the highest IgG antibody level among all groups, whereas both adjuvant-free 4-TT groups induced an equivalent IgG antibody level compared to the Hib vaccine group, demonstrating that the response plateaus despite rising concentrations (Fig. 6D). The antiserum IgG antibody titer of 4-TT + Alum increased substantially after the first dose and almost reached peak after the second boost (Fig. 6E). The antiserum IgG antibody titers of both high and low doses of 4-TT groups increased gradually along with the vaccination period, as similar as the Hib vaccine group. The IgM antibody titers of all groups showed a similar tendency to increase after the second boost and then start to decline (Fig. 6F). The antiserum from all groups exhibited good recognition and bactericidal activity, while the 4-TT + Alum group showed the strongest efficacy (Fig. 6, G to I, and fig. S8, D and E). Notably, adjuvant-free 4-TT demonstrated the comparable efficacy as licensed Hib vaccine, showing no significant differences. These results suggest that 4-TT glycoconjugate is a good candidate for the development of a semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccine that is noninferior when compared to licensed Hib vaccine. The addition of an adjuvant can be an alternative since it can boost the immune system substantially and rapidly against Hib infection after immunization.

Evaluation of specific humoral and cellular immune responses induced by 4-TT glycoconjugate in mice

For a comprehensive and systematic analysis of the immunological characteristics of the 4-TT glycoconjugate, female BALB/c mice were immunized with PBS, 4-TT, 4-TT + Alum, and licensed Hib vaccine, respectively (31). The antiserum IgG antibodies in mice of 4-TT, 4-TT + Alum, and licensed Hib vaccine groups were produced during the initial immunization, elevating gradually along with the immunization period (Fig. 7A). The antibody production induced by 4-TT glycoconjugates was at a similar level as licensed Hib vaccine, while it increased slightly with the addition of an adjuvant (fig. S9, A and B). The in vivo immune response was further assessed. IgM and all subclasses of IgG (IgG1, IgG2a, IgG2b, and IgG3) antibody titers induced by 4-TT were comparable to licensed Hib vaccine, showing no significant difference (Fig. 7B). Meanwhile, higher titers of IgG1, IgG2b, and IgG3 antibodies were generated in all groups of antisera immunized with 4-TT, 4-TT + Alum, and licensed Hib vaccine, demonstrating IgG class switching through a common mechanism. A higher titer of IgG1 antibodies was observed in the 4-TT + Alum group because of the enhancement of IgG switching from IgM by the addition of adjuvant. The results provide direct evidence that 4-TT glycoconjugate can elicit T cell–mediated immune response in as efficiently as licensed Hib vaccine (32).

We evaluated the immune response induced by 4-TT glycoconjugate to gain further insight into the functional mechanism. The subpopulations of lymphocytes separated from splenocytes were quantitatively characterized. In comparison with the PBS group, a significantly increased proportion of CD3+CD4+ (P < 0.0001) and CD3+CD8+ (P < 0.01, P < 0.05) T cells was observed in the groups that received 4-TT, 4-TT + Alum, and licensed Hib vaccine (Fig. 7C). No statistically significant differences among these three groups in terms of activation of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells were observed. The profile of interferon-γ (IFN-γ) and interleukin-4 (IL-4) produced by CD4+ T cells from immunized mice was further analyzed upon in vitro stimulation with InHib (33). After stimulation, 4-TT (2.88 ± 0.37% and 2.31 ± 0.55%) and 4-TT + Alum (3.34 ± 0.44% and 2.96 ± 0.49%) groups both exhibited a higher proportion of IL-4 and IFN-γ expression when compared with the PBS group (0.67 ± 0.12% and 0.74 ± 0.06%), as efficiently as the licensed–Hib vaccine group (2.61 ± 0.33% and 2.15 ± 0.22%) (Fig. 7D). These findings confirm that the 4-TT glycoconjugate can promote the differentiation and polarization of T helper cells (TH cells) into TH1 and TH2 cells, thereby inducing a mixed TH1 and TH2 immune response (31). Together, adjuvant-free 4-TT glycoconjugate can effectively elicit both humoral and cellular immune responses as efficiently as the licensed Hib vaccine.

Evaluation of the protective efficacy of 4-TT glycoconjugate in a septic infection model in mice

To further evaluate the protective efficacy of the 4-TT glycoconjugate, a previously unexplored vaccine evaluation system was used to quantitatively characterize the key features of effective vaccines in the recognition and elimination of Hib bacteria from the blood circulation to the liver-resident macrophage Kupffer cells (KCs) and sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) in a mouse septic infection model (34, 35). The mixture of Hib with PBS and antisera against 4-TT, 4-TT + Alum, and Hib vaccine was intravenously injected into naïve mice, respectively. Pathogen clearance from the bloodstream was assessed by determining bacteremia kinetics via retro-orbital bleeding and colony-forming unit (CFU) plating at distinct time points in the first 30 min as previously reported (34, 35). Antisera of 4-TT, 4-TT + Alum, and Hib vaccine groups all achieved effective clearance of Hib from the circulation with no significant difference (Fig. 8, A and B). The naïve mice of the control group also showed Hib some clearance, which can be attributed to the relatively low virulence of Hib. The bacterial burden in major organs at 30 min postinjection was also determined by measuring CFU counting of each organ after CFU plating. There were 52% of the remaining viable bacteria in the liver and 44% in the spleen detected in the control group. Comparably high Hib inoculum was found in the livers with 84% of Hib vaccine group, 83% of 4-TT group, and 86% of the 4-TT + Alum group and low Hib inoculum in the spleens with 13.8% of Hib vaccine group, 15.1% of 4-TT group, and 12.9% of 4-TT + Alum group (Fig. 8C), suggesting that the antibody-mediated bacterial capture relies mainly on the liver. Antisera of 4-TT, 4-TT + Alum, and licensed Hib vaccine groups exhibited comparable levels of bactericidal activity (Fig. 8D), since captured Hib bacteria were mainly captured by the liver and not killed immediately.

The pathogen-capturing mechanism mediated by glycoconjugate 4-TT was further investigated by visualizing and quantifying cellular capture of circulating Hib in liver sinusoids by time-lapse intravital microscopy (IVM) imaging. As visualized, Hib bacteria of the control group mostly flowed through the sinusoidal vasculature as small amounts docked on KCs after intravenous inoculation (Fig. 8E and movie S1). Hib bacteria pretreated with antisera of 4-TT, 4-TT + Alum, and licensed Hib vaccine groups were all quickly docked onto KCs and LSECs. In agreement with antibody-mediated Hib clearance, quantification of immobilized bacteria per field of view (FOV) revealed that 4-TT–, 4-TT + Alum–, and Hib licensed vaccine–immunized antisera, respectively, promoted the Hib capture by both KCs and LSECs effectively to a higher level without significant differences, compared to the control group (Fig. 8F). Furthermore, an antibody dose-dependent pathogen capture by LSECs in liver sinusoids was observed (Fig. 8G, fig. S10, and movie S2), indicating potent protection of 4-TT–induced antisera. These results demonstrated that 4-TT–induced antisera greatly enhanced the clearance efficiency of Hib bacteria in the bloodstream through the activation of the bacterial capture by KCs/LSECs in the liver, as effectively as the approved Hib vaccine. The collective findings confirm the substantial potential of the 4-TT glycoconjugate as a semisynthetic Hib vaccine candidate for further development.

DISCUSSION

Despite their global success and widespread inclusion in national immunization programs, traditional polysaccharide-based vaccines remain constrained by inherent limitations, such as complex bacterial cultivation requirements and persistent impurities (1). The technological advances in glycochemistry have enabled the production of structurally defined carbohydrate antigens with high purity, complete molecular characterization, and free of potential microbial contaminants (3, 27). Growing attention has been directed toward developing semisynthetic or fully synthetic carbohydrate-conjugate vaccines in recent studies (25, 36). Following the approval of the Quimi-Hib vaccine, several synthetic carbohydrate-based conjugate vaccines targeting various infectious diseases have entered development pipelines. Notably, a semisynthetic carbohydrate-conjugate vaccine against Shigellosis has entered phase 2 clinical trials (37, 38). For the rational design of effective semisynthetic carbohydrate-conjugate vaccines, the pivotal step is to identify the immunodominant epitopes of polysaccharide antigens capable of eliciting pathogen-specific protective immunity (3, 36). The excellent efficacy of Quimi-Hib vaccine containing an average of 8-mers has stimulated more investigations into the correlation between defined oligomeric chain lengths and their immunogenicity (17), suggesting that tetramer bearing a 5-aminopentyl and a terminal thiol-maleimide spacer arm at the O5 position of ribitol demonstrates the best immunogenic profile (16).

Many factors can influence the immunogenicity of glycoconjugates, such as glycan chain length, nature of the spacer and protein carrier, as well as conjugation strategy. Therefore, the identification of the minimal epitope of bacterial polysaccharides is critical for the rational design of effective synthetic carbohydrate conjugates. To accelerate the development and approval of a synthetic carbohydrate-conjugate vaccine against Hib, the structure-immunogenicity correlation was systematically investigated with a comprehensive library of synthetic PRP oligosaccharides containing 1- to 12-mers that are containing the same linker and spacer arm at ribose O3 position as Quimi-Hib vaccine. The formation of the phosphodiester bond is a critical step in the chemical synthesis of PRP. Various methods have been used to construct phosphodiester bonds (9). The H-phosphonate method is a widely used approach, including a polycondensation approach using H-phosphonate building blocks. The chemical instability and high polarity of PRP and their derivatives present substantial challenges in the construction of their structures and synthetic intermediates. Concurrently, the provision of corresponding compounds in substantial quantities and homogeneous quality is a considerable challenge when using this methodology. Concurrently, scaling up the production of target fragments with consistent quality poses a substantial hurdle, thereby limiting its utility for vaccine development and therapeutic applications. Notably, the phosphoramidite method is currently the most widely used method for DNA synthesis (9). The phosphoramidite approach enables high efficiency in condensation reactions and low polarity of phosphotriester intermediates, which is crucial for synthesizing lengthy oligosaccharides (22, 24). This method had been less well studied for obtaining PRP oligosaccharides when compared with the H-phosphonate method. Therefore, we used phosphoramidite-based synthesis to optimize phosphodiester linkage assembly, yielding longer PRP oligosaccharides with enhanced structural fidelity. A modular synthesis strategy was proposed on the basis of the phosphoramidite method. This strategy used the dimer acceptor 21 with linker as the main starting compound and with monomer glycoside phosphoramidite 13 and dimer glycoside phosphoramidite 17 as elongation building blocks for the synthesis of PRP oligosaccharides with odd- and even-numbered fragments, respectively. Combining the phosphoramidite method with an optimized coupling strategy and deprotection process allowed for homogeneous mass production of PRP oligosaccharides as a library of synthetic PRP oligosaccharides was constructed.

Glycan microarray technology is an effective method for identifying the optimal antigenic epitope (36). Several oligosaccharide fragments such as dimer 2, tetramer 4, hexamer 6, undecamer 11, and dodecamer 12 exhibited good antigenicity based on the glycan microarray screening results. Despite demonstrating antigenicity in previous studies (18), dimer 2 was not chosen for further immunological evaluation because of limited immunogenicity associated with its small size (20). Since Quimi-Hib vaccine was produced with a mixture of oligosaccharides with an average of 8-mers, octamer 8 was still involved in the immunological evaluation, despite not demonstrating enhanced antigenicity in this work. Thus, tetramer 4, hexamer 6, octamer 8, undecamer 11, and dodecamer 12 were conjugated with TT carrier protein for animal immunization. All synthetic glycoconjugates can induce a robust immune response with the production of IgG antibodies at comparable levels to licensed Hib vaccines based on polysaccharides, among which tetramer 4-TT conjugates induce the highest IgG antibody levels. Furthermore, IgG antibodies induced by glycoconjugate 4-TT show strong and specific binding with Hib bacteria and high bactericidal activity, suggesting that tetramer 4 is the optimal immunogenic epitope. Previous studies also suggested that the tetramer exhibits robust immunogenic properties (16), confirming its promise as a candidate for synthetic glycoconjugate vaccine development.

To evaluate the feasibility of synthetic glycoprotein vaccines based on tetramer 4, critical parameters including carrier protein selection, dosing strategies, and adjuvant formulation were further investigated. Both TT and CRM197, which are safe and well-established carrier proteins, can induce enhanced immune responses when conjugated with synthetic Hib PRP fragments (39). In this work, both glycoconjugates TT and CRM197 showed high immunogenicity, while 4-TT induced higher IgG antibody levels. TT protein is used in Quimi-Hib vaccine and licensed Hib vaccine, also supporting that TT is a suitable choice for the semisynthetic glycoconjugate Hib vaccine. Adjuvants are one of the vital elements that increase vaccine efficacy. Complete and incomplete FAs can enhance the stimulation of immune responses and are often used in in vivo experiments. The glycoconjugate 4-TT presents higher IgG antibody responses with FAs than licensed Hib vaccine. However, the clinical use of FAs has been hampered by strong side effects (40). Aluminum salt adjuvants have been extensively used in licensed vaccines over decades because of their proven safety profile that has been incorporated in the licensed Hib vaccine PedvaxHIB (41). With the Alum adjuvant, 4-TT glycoconjugate demonstrated enhanced immunogenicity with significantly higher specific IgG and IgM antibody levels and SBA titers than the Hib vaccine. Notably, adjuvant-free 4-TT glycoconjugate can achieve immunogenicity equivalent to the licensed Hib vaccine. The vaccination dose can also influence the induction of immune response by glycoconjugate vaccines. Compared to polysaccharide-based vaccines, 4-TT glycoconjugate can achieve comparable immune responses with an equivalent sugar dosage in animals, whereas higher sugar dosage does not enhance induced IgG antibody level, mostly because of the dose-response plateau effect (38, 42). This dosage can be referenced and further optimized in further clinical research. The immunological characteristics of 4-TT glycoconjugate were further explored using a mouse model. The adjuvant-free 4-TT glycoconjugate can induce specific humoral and cellular immune responses at a comparable level through the same mechanism as licensed Hib vaccine, proving that 4-TT glycoconjugate is an effective semisynthetic vaccine.

The protective efficacy of a vaccine against pathogens, the prime criterion in vaccine development and evaluation, depends on the degree of induced antibody and T cell responses and the potency of pathogen clearance against infection (43). The in vivo immune-protective efficacy of 4-TT was validated with a vaccine evaluation system based on a mouse septic infection model (34, 35). The liver serves as the major immune organ mediating vaccine-induced clearance of blood-borne pathogens in mice (35, 44). Vaccines with high efficacy activate bacterial capture and phagocytosis by both hepatic KCs and LSECs mediated by a high amount of IgG antibodies, such as the 13-valent pneumococcal polysaccharide conjugate vaccine (37). In contrast, vaccines with relatively low protective efficacy demonstrate restricted cellular engagement, activating only KCs with IgM and a low amount of IgG. The specific antibodies induced by 4-TT glycoconjugates can effectively clear pathogens from the bloodstream, mediate main pathogen trapping by the liver, and enhance pathogen recognition and capture by KCs and LSECs in the liver, which are main correlates of this evaluation system (37). The 4-TT glycoconjugate exerts comparable immune-protective efficacy against Hib as approved Hib vaccine. Notably, the presence of adjuvant did not demonstrate significantly stronger protective efficacy, while it induced higher levels of IgG, particularly IgG1 production, which supports the adjuvant-free formulation. No adverse reactions were observed during the vaccination process in this work, while further systematic evaluation is still necessary to ensure the safety and efficacy. These results suggest that the adjuvant-free 4-TT glycoconjugate is a potent semisynthetic Hib vaccine formulation with comparable immunogenicity and protective efficacy to licensed Hib vaccine in preclinical research, providing a solid scientific foundation for subsequent clinical trials.

This work was still constrained by vaccination in conventional animal models that demonstrate restricted translational value in predicting human immunogenicity profiles of glycoconjugate vaccines. It is mostly due to the inability to replicate the complex immunological landscape of human populations, including preexisting immunological memory, heterologous immunity from pathogen exposure histories, and baseline vaccination-induced immune priming. Considering the favorable clinical immunogenicity profile of Quimi-Hib vaccine and other oligosaccharide-based conjugates (38), the clinical investigation of 4-TT glycoconjugates holds substantial translational promise. Optimization of the current glycoconjugate vaccine formulation is still required for further clinical research, including immunization dose, adjuvant usage, and glycoconjugate stability, as well as additional components such as preservatives, stabilizers, and emulsifiers. No adverse reactions were observed in the current work, while the further systematic evaluation on the safety and toxicity is necessary.

In conclusion, the optimal immunogenic epitope was successfully identified from the synthetic oligosaccharides of 1- to 12-mers of Hib CPS. The tetramer 4-TT glycoconjugate can induce both humoral and cellular immune responses, without the presence of adjuvants, as efficiently as the licensed Hib vaccine. The protective efficacy of adjuvant-free 4-TT glycoconjugate was mechanistically evaluated to a comparable extent as that of the licensed Hib vaccine. These preclinical results underscore the compelling potential of 4-TT glycoconjugate as a semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccine candidate against Hib and establish a solid foundation for advancing next-generation vaccine development.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

The objective of this study was to develop a semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccine against Hib with a defined epitope structure. A series of synthetic PRP oligosaccharides containing 1- to 12-mers of Hib CPS were obtained with a modular synthetic strategy based on the phosphoramidite method. These synthetic fragments bore the same spacer-linker at the same position as those in the Quimi-Hib vaccine, which is a mixture of oligosaccharides with an average of 8-mers. The immunogenic epitope was screened by glycan microarray and animal immunization. The influence of carrier proteins, adjuvants, and vaccination dose on immunogenicity of glycoconjugates was investigated, thereby enabling the formulation of a vaccine candidate. The immunogenicity and protective efficacy of a semisynthetic glycoconjugate vaccine candidate were evaluated in animal models by comparing with licensed Hib vaccine based on polysaccharides. The number of replicates per experiment is given in figure legends. No data were excluded from the report. This study was not blinded. No statistical methods were used to predetermine the sample size. All animal studies were conducted in accordance with the relevant regulations and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Jiangnan University (JN.No20211030b0780120[429], JN.No20230415R0400830[149], and JN.No20230515b0601031[225]) and Tsinghua University (THU-02-2024-0017A).

Chemical synthesis

The complete experimental details, compound characterization data, and the NMR spectra can be found in Supplementary Materials and Methods.

Glycan microarray analysis

Analysis was performed as described (45). Briefly, synthetic oligosaccharides and natural polysaccharides were spotted onto NHS-activated glass slides as described. Slides were quenched, blocked, and treated with the corresponding antiserum. Binding was visualized by fluorescence detection after incubation with fluorophore-labeled secondary antibodies.

Immunization regimens

Rabbits [male New Zealand white (NZW) rabbits, 8 to 10 weeks, 1.8 to 2.2 kg, n = 4 per group] were immunized subcutaneously with glycoconjugates and approved Hib vaccine at four different sites on days 0, 14, and 28. The vaccine (5 or 20 μg of oligosaccharides per dose) was formulated either as a 1:1 (v/v) emulsion with FA (Sigma-Aldrich), a 1:1 (v/v) suspension with Alum (Alhydrogel, Brenntag), or adjuvant-free in a total volume of 0.5 ml. Blood was collected once a week from the marginal ear vein to collect serum. All mice were housed under specific pathogen–free conditions. Female BALB/c mice aged 6 to 8 weeks (n = 5 per group) were immunized subcutaneously on days 0, 14, and 28 at four different sites with a 4-TT glycoconjugate and an approved Hib vaccine, each at 2.5 μg of oligosaccharides per dose, in a 1:1 (v/v) suspension with Alum or adjuvant-free for a total volume of 100 μl. Blood was collected once a week from the tail vein to collect serum. After 42 days of immunization, the animals were euthanized, blood was collected by cardiac puncture in rabbits and by orbital puncture in mice, and the spleen was excised.

Intravital microscopy

IVM imaging of mouse liver was performed as described. Briefly (35), FITC labeling was performed by incubating bacteria in 100 μl of PBS with 20 μg of FITC (Sigma-Aldrich) for 30 min at room temperature in the dark and washing three times with PBS. The KCs and LSECs were labeled by intravenous injection of 2.5 μg of AF647 anti-F4/80 (BioLegend) and AF594 anti-CD31 (BioLegend) antibodies, respectively, 30 min before infection. A total of 5 × 107 CFU of bacteria were premixed with heat-inactivated natural or immune serum for 30 min at room temperature before intravenous injection into naïve mice. Intravital images of the liver microvasculature were acquired under three laser excitation wavelengths (488, 585, and 635 nm) by a white-light laser (1.5 mW, Laser Kit WLL2, 470 to 670 nm). Real-time imaging was monitored for at least 1 min postinfection. More than five FOVs obtained at 10 min postinfection were selected to quantify immobilized bacteria by KCs and LSECs.

Statistical analysis

All raw, individual-level data are presented in data file S1. Statistical analyses were performed using the GraphPad Prism 9.5.0 (GraphPad Software). The tests used are stated in the corresponding figure legends. Differences were considered statistically significant at P < 0.05 (****P < 0.0001, ***P < 0.001, **P < 0.01, *P < 0.05, and n.s., not significant). Figures were generated using GraphPad Prism 9.5.0 and OriginPro 2021 (OriginLab).

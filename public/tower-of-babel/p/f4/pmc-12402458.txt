Diagnosis of misalignment faults using the DTCWT-RCMFDE and LSSVM algorithms
Authors: Ahmed Taibi, Nabil Ikhlef, Lyes Aomar, Said Touati, Oussama Baitiche, Yousef A Alsabah, Abdelaziz Rabehi, Mawloud Guermoui, Mohamed Benghanem
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMisalignment is among the most frequent mechanical faults in rotating electrical machines, often resulting in partial or complete motor failure over time. To tackle this issue, the present study proposes an innovative methodology for diagnosing misalignment faults in rotating electrical machines. The method integrates the dual-tree complex wavelet transform with a refined composite multiscale fluctuation dispersion entropy algorithm (DTCWT-RCMFDE) for feature extraction, combined with the least-squares support vector machines algorithm (LSSVM) for fault classification. Initially, the DTCWT is employed to decompose the torque signal into multiple sub-bands using range entropy (RE). Subsequently, the RCMFDE is calculated for each sub-band to construct discriminative fault feature vectors. These vectors are then used to train and test the LSSVM classifier to identify different types of misalignment faults. The proposed method was validated using experimental data, and the results demonstrate its superior diagnostic performance. Compared to existing approaches, the DTCWT-RCMFDE-LSSVM model achieved the highest classification accuracy of 98.33%, outperforming other methods such as MSE-SVM (94.1%), DTCWT-EE-PSO-SVM (96%), Multi-features-t-SNE-LSSVM (96.25%) and AR model coefficients-mRMR-SOM neural network (97.22%). These findings confirm the method’s high precision in detecting both parallel and angular misalignments. This research holds significant potential for industrial applications in sectors reliant on rotating machinery such as power generation, petrochemical, nuclear, and manufacturing where early and accurate fault detection is essential to minimize downtime and enhance operational reliability.Keywords: DTCWT, Diagnosis mechanical faults, Parallel and angular misalignment faults, RCMFDE, LSSVMSubject terms: Electrical and electronic engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

Rotating machines are critical components in a wide range of industrial applications, forming the backbone of vital industries such as petrochemical facilities, nuclear power plants, and manufacturing processes. The continuous and reliable operation of these machines is essential for maintaining productivity and ensuring the safety and efficiency of industrial systems. Nevertheless, these machines frequently encounter severe operational conditions, such as overload scenarios and fluctuating thermal environments, which render them susceptible to a variety of mechanical and electrical failures that can impede their functionality and halt production1. Among these failures, misalignment is one of the most common mechanical issues in rotating machinery, occurring when the shafts of the driving and driven machines are not properly aligned along the same axis. It can be classified into two main types: parallel and angular misalignment, or a combination of both.

Typically, misalignment results from improper installation procedures, wear of machine components, or damage to mounting supports caused by machine vibrations. Misalignment not only increases vibration and stress on the machine but can also lead to severe consequences such as fatigue cracks and rotor-to-stator rubbing2,3. Furthermore, the presence of multiple interacting faults such as misalignment combined with imbalance or looseness complicates fault diagnosis and highlights the need for more robust and intelligent diagnostic strategies4. Recent reviews5,6 have emphasized the growing complexity of fault diagnosis in rotating machines, noting that early detection and accurate classification remain challenging under multi-fault and variable operating conditions. Misalignment is a major concern for maintenance and design engineers, as it significantly contributes to increased downtime and maintenance costs7. According to8, about 70% of rotating machinery faults are due to the effects of misalignment and imbalance. Furthermore, Junho Lee9 reports that billions of dollars are spent annually on alignment-related issues in rotating machinery. Therefore, effective monitoring and diagnosis of misalignment faults are essential to ensure reliable operation and prevent costly or catastrophic failures.

Many studies have relied extensively on vibration analysis for diagnosing misalignment faults in rotating machinery. For instance, Ab Ghani et al. employed a Machinery Fault Simulator (MFS) to detect shaft misalignment10. Houssem Habbouche et al. proposed a method that combines wavelet transform for effective feature extraction with transfer learning techniques to improve fault classification accuracy11. Similarly, Umbrajkaar et al. adopted a hybrid approach, integrating artificial neural networks with support vector machines to identify shaft misalignment12. However, other issues such as rotor-bearing faults that can generate vibration patterns similar to those caused by misalignment, making it difficult to distinguish between them. As a result, diagnosing misalignment faults based solely on vibration signals remains a challenging task. To address these limitations, many recent studies have used the analysis of alternative signals for diagnosing this type of fault, For instance, Juan Luis Ferrando Chacón et al. employed acoustic emission analysis to detect and diagnose shaft angular misalignment13. Other researchers utilized thermographic analysis to identify misalignment in wind turbines14,15. Additionally, many studies have focused on detecting unbalance and misalignment faults using Motor Current Signature Analysis (MCSA)9,16–18. Zhen Yu et al. proposed a novel approach for the quantitative diagnosis of shaft misalignment faults, integrating speed signal analysis via Singular Value Decomposition and Hilbert Transform (SVD-HT) combined with a hybrid approach based on Cubic Spline Fitting, Pareto-based Particle Swarm Optimization, and Echo State Networks (CSF-PPSO-ESN)19. Zhen Yu et al. developed a diagnostic approach for coupling misalignment faults based on order spectrum and power spectrum analyses of torque signals20. According to reference21 this type of fault is difficult to detect through harmonic analysis of the machine’s current and vibration signals because it generates only minor impacts on the amplitude of these signals. As a result, its effects are masked by the strong contribution of other mechanical and electrical phenomena.

Within this context, our research presents a methodology for the identification of misalignment defects in rotating machinery through the analysis of torque signals employing advanced signal processing methodologies. Our strategy incorporates the Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (DTCWT) alongside the Refined Composite Multiscale Dispersion Entropy (RCMFDE) for effective feature extraction. Subsequently, these features are categorized utilizing the Least Squares Support Vector Machine (LSSVM) to precisely detect misalignment faults. The efficacy of our proposed technique is corroborated through experimental data.

Dual-tree complex wavelet transform

The Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (DTCWT) represents a sophisticated wavelet transform frequently employed in the realms of signal and image processing. This method was conceived as an enhancement to the traditional wavelet transform, which is hindered by specific drawbacks such as inadequate directional selectivity and a deficiency in shift-invariance. The DTCWT serves as an instrumental framework for the analysis of signals across both time and frequency domains, providing superior directional selectivity, shift invariance, and enhanced decorrelation characteristics in comparison to the traditional wavelet transform22. In the DTCWT algorithm, an excessive quantity of DTCWT layers may result in a partial attenuation of beneficial signals. In our research, this optimal level was determined to be 4.

Proposed fault diagnosis approach

To identify mechanical anomalies that manifest in rotating electrical machinery, including parallel and angular misalignment defects, this paper introduces a novel methodology for the diagnosis of such issues. This methodology relies on the examination of torque signal data. The diagram illustrating the proposed diagnostic framework is depicted in (Fig. 4), and its sequential steps are outlined as follows:

Proposed fault diagnosis approach.

Step 1: Signal preprocessing.

First, the torque signals in different operating modes are normalized and then segmented to form a database. each segment containing 4096 points.

Step 2: Fault feature extraction.

The DTCWT algorithm is used to decompose the preprocessed and normalized torque signals of each operating mode into five frequency sub-bands. Subsequently, the RCMFDE is calculated for each sub-band, in order to construct fault features matrix.

Step 3: Fault feature classification.

The matrix of fault characteristics derived is standardized to a scale between 0 and 1. Subsequently, it is arbitrarily divided into two separate subsets: one designated as the training set and the other as the testing set. The training set is employed to train the LSSVM classifier and develop a classification model, while the testing set is incorporated into the trained model to detect and categorize the various types of faults.

Experiment and results

Experimental setup

In order to assess the efficacy of the misalignment fault diagnosis methodology proposed in this research study we exploited an experimental database from the LAPLACE laboratory test bench (Plasma and Energy Conversion Laboratory) of ENSEEIHT in Toulouse.

The test bench of this laboratory consists of a three-phase induction machine with a voltage of 400 V, a power of 5.5 kW, and two pole pairs (p = 2), delivering a nominal torque of 36 Nm. It is powered by an industrial variable speed drive. The test machine is coupled with a DC generator using a rotary torque transducer. Additionally, a controllable DC/DC converter is connected to a DC current generator on one side and to a voltage-controlled oscillator on the other side. The torque signal is measured using a rotating torque transducer connected to a 24-bit resolution NI 447 type data acquisition card with a sampling frequency of 25.6 kHz. Application of the proposed approach

In this section: First, pre-processing of the collected torque signals for different operating modes is performed. This involves normalizing the signals by subtracting their mean values and dividing by their maximum values. The normalized torque signals are then divided into 60 segments for each operating mode, with each segment containing 4096 points. Within these segments, a total of 40 are arbitrarily chosen to serve as training samples, whereas the other 20 are designated as test samples.

Secondly, fault characteristics are derived by partitioning the 60 segments corresponding to each operational mode into five sub-bands utilizing the Dual-Tree Complex Wavelet Transform (DTCWT) algorithm. Subsequently, the Refined Composite Multiscale Dispersion Entropy (RCMFDE) is computed for each sub-band to formulate fault feature vectors, employing the parameters: maximum scale factor τ = 20, m = 2, c = 6, and λ = 1. This methodology produces 100 fault characteristics per segment, culminating in a cumulative total of 60 × 100 fault characteristics for every operational mode.

Third, the features that have been extracted are utilized as input for the LSSVM classifier, aimed at identifying and categorizing diverse machine misalignment anomalies. To assess the efficacy and dependability of our proposed feature extraction technique, it is juxtaposed with several widely utilized feature extraction approaches. In this research, we opted for the EMD and VMD methodologies to decompose the signals into multiple intrinsic mode functions (IMFs). Subsequently, we identified 5 IMFs exhibiting a greater correlation with the original signal, employing the Pearson correlation coefficient. Following this, we computed the RCMFDE for each IMF utilizing consistent parameters: maximum scale factor τ = 20, m = 2, and c = 6. This results in 100 fault features for each segment, culminating in a total of 60 × 100 fault features for each operational mode.

Conclusion

This study proposed a novel and effective methodology for diagnosing misalignment faults in rotating electrical machines by combining the dual-tree complex wavelet transform (DTCWT) for signal decomposition, Refined Composite Multiscale Fluctuation Dispersion Entropy (RCMFDE) for feature extraction, and Least Squares Support Vector Machine (LSSVM) for fault classification. Unlike conventional vibration-based approaches, the proposed method utilizes torque signal analysis to enhance diagnostic precision and reduce ambiguity caused by overlapping fault characteristics. The experimental results demonstrated that the proposed DTCWT-RCMFDE-LSSVM framework achieved a high classification accuracy of 98.33%, successfully identifying both parallel and angular misalignment faults. This highlights the strength of our method in capturing relevant features across multiple frequency sub-bands. However, due to the complexity and computational time required for signal decomposition and feature extraction, real-time implementation remains a challenge. Future work will aim to optimize the algorithm for faster processing and explore its integration into real-time monitoring systems for industrial applications.

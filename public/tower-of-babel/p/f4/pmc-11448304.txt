Global profiling of transcriptome, proteome and 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome in radioresistant lung adenocarcinoma cell
Authors: Zheng Lei, Jiang He, Haonan Yang, Lu Zhang, Tangmin Lai, Liu Zhou, Zheng Tang, Jiangdong Sui, Yongzhong Wu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRadioresistance contributes to metastasis and recurrence in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients. However, the underlying mechanism remains unclear. To provide novel clues, a complete multi-omics map of a radioresistant cancer cell line has been profiled. In this article, a lung adenocarcinoma cell line, radioresistant A549 (RA549), was generated by exposure to a series of irradiation. Subsequently, we adopted transcriptome, quantitative proteome and lysine 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome to construct a differential profile on the transcriptional to post-tanslational levels on A549 and RA549 cell lines, respectively. Our analysis revealed 920 significantly differentially expressed genes and 699 proteins. Furthermore, 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome identified 30,089 Khib modified sites on 4635 proteins, indicating that Khib modifications play vital role in regulating NSCLC radioresistance. Multi-omics combined analysis identified 19 significantly differentially expressed genes/proteins in total. Meanwhile, we found that EGFR, a well-known lung cancer-related receptor, was upregulated at both the protein and Khib modification levels in RA549. Further gain/loss of function experiments showed that Khib modified EGFR level positively correlates with NSCLC cell radioresistance. Taken together, our findings report that Khib-modified proteins enhanced resistance to radiation and represent promising therapeutic targets.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-024-10854-6.Keywords: 2-hydroxyisobutyrylation, Radioresistance, Non-small cell lung cancer

Introduction

Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) is among the most prevalent malignant tumors worldwide [1], with lung adenocarcinoma being the most common histological subtype [2]. Therefore, A549, a well-established lung adenocarcinoma cell line, was selected for this research, representing NSCLC. Methods for establishing radioresistant cell lines have been introduced in several articles [3–5], in brief, Cells were exposed to fractionated X-ray treatments, after which the surviving cells were cultured and selected.

Radiotherapy (RT) is a primary treatment for NSCLC at all stages [2, 6]. Despite its effectiveness, resistance to radiation remains a major cause of therapy failure [7, 8]. Unfortunately, the mechanism of radioresistance remains unclear and varies from different cancer types. To better understand the mechanisms of radioresistance, Paidi et al. [3] used Raman spectrometry to reveal alterations in the tumor microenvironment of radioresistant NSCLC and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC). Raman spectrometry was also used in another study carried out by Wu et al. to profile resistant nasopharyngeal carcinoma derived exosomes [9]. Glioblastomas were uncovered to resist RT through enhancing fatty acids oxidation and immune evasion [4]. HSP90 was found to promote radioresistance through preventing CD147 molecule destabilization [10].

Lysine acetylation is one of the most extensive protein post-translational modifications (PTMs)), influencing multiple cellular processes, including transcription, metabolism, signal transduction, and protein function [11–13]. In recent years, an increasing number of studies have focused on cancer lysine acetylation, such as esophageal squamous cell carcinoma [14], lung adenocarcinoma [15], gastric cancer [16] and pancreatic cancer [17]. Lysine 2-hydroxyisobutyrylation (Khib), a recently recognized acetylation modification, shows great potential for uncovering underlying biological processes [18, 19], especially in cancer biology. Chen et al. [20] identified 2011 Khib modified sites on 764 proteins in oral adenoid cystic carcinoma and deduced that Khib modifications promote cancer progression. Lu et al. [17] revealed that a majority of Khib-modified proteins enhances metabolic processes including glycolysis, fatty acid oxidation and TCA cycle. Liao et al. [21] discovered that Khib modification on NAT10 molecule can enhance its interaction with deubiquitinase and subsequently promote cancer metastasis.

High-throughput analysis has been applied in exploring radiosensitivity, for example, transcriptome was applied to identify potential biomarker [22] and differential characterization [23] in induced radioresistant NSCLC. Nonetheless, no research has yet profiled a complete expression and modification map on radioresistant cell lines. Therefore, we wish to uncover the mechanisms of radioresistance by profiling the transcriptional, translational, and post-translational modification levels in a radioresistant NSCLC cell line. In this study, we aim to help uncover the mechanisms of radioresistance in lung adenocarcinoma by combining transcriptome, proteome, and 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome analyses to generate a comprehensive molecular profile.

Methods

Cell culturing and radioresistant cell line induction

The human lung adenocarcinoma A549 cell line was purchased from ATCC and cultured in DMEM/F12 medium (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco, Carlsbad, CA) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. The method for generating radioresistant A549 (RA549) cells was explained by Paidi et al. [3]. In short, A549 cells were irradiated several times with a total dose of 55 Gy and allowed two weeks to recover. All related experiments were conducted within the next ten passages.

Western blot

Cells were collected after reaching 70–80% confluence and lysed with IP lysis buffer (Beyotime). Then immunoprecipitation was performed using anti-EGFR antibody (CST) and separated in a 6% SDS-PAGE gel. After being transferred to a PVDF membrane, the membrane was incubated with anti-EGFR and pan anti-2-hydroxyisobutyryllysine (PTM-802, PTM Biolab) antibodies, respectively, overnight. Following that HRP-conjugated secondary antibody was used for chemiluminescence imaging.

Transcriptome

Total RNA was extracted using the Total RNA Extractor (Trizol) kit (B511311, Sangon, China).

According to the manufacturer’s protocol and treated with RNase-free DNase I to remove genomic DNA contamination. RNA integrity was evaluated with a 1.0% agarose gel. Thereafter, the quality and quantity of RNA were assessed using a NanoPhotometer ® spectrophotometer (IMPLEN, CA, USA) and a Qubit® 2.0 Flurometer (Invitrogen). The high-quality RNA samples were subsequently submitted to the Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd. for library preparation and sequencing. DESeq2 (version 1.12.4) was used to determine differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between two samples. Genes were considered as significant differentially expressed if q-value ≤ 0.001 and |FoldChange|≥ 2. When the normalized expression of a gene was zero between two samples, its expression value was adjusted to 0.01 (as 0 cannot be plotted on a log plot). If the normalized expression of a certain gene in two libraries was all lower than 1, further differential expression analysis was conducted without this gene.

Proteome/lysine 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome

The samples were grinded with liquid nitrogen into cell powder and then lysis buffer (8 M urea, 1% protease inhibitor cocktail) was added to the cell powder, followed by sonication on ice. The remaining debris was removed by centrifugation. Finally, the supernatant was collected and the protein concentration was determined with BCA kit. The sample was slowly added to the final concentration of 20% (m/v) TCA to precipitate protein, then vortexed to mix and incubated for 2 h at 4 °C. The precipitate was collected by centrifugation at 4500 g for 5 min at 4 °C. The precipitated protein was washed with pre-cooled acetone for 3 times and dried for 1 min. The protein sample was then redissolved in 200 mM TEAB and ultrasonically dispersed. Trypsin was added at 1:50 trypsin-to-protein mass ratio for the first digestion overnight. The sample was reduced with 5 mM dithiothreitol for 30 min at 56 °C and alkylated with 11 mM iodoacetamide for 15 min at room temperature in darkness. Finally, the peptides were desalted by Strata X SPE column. To enrich modified peptides, tryptic peptides dissolved in NETN buffer were incubated with pre-washed antibody beads at 4 °C overnight with gentle shaking. Then the beads were washed for four times with NETN buffer and twice with water. The bound peptides were eluted from the beads with 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid. Finally, the eluted fractions were combined and vacuum dried. The peptides were subjected to capillary source followed by the timsTOF Pro mass spectrometry.

Bioinformatic analysis

Gene Ontology (GO) annotation involves using the Eggnog-mapper software to extract GO IDs from the identified proteins based on the EggNOG database, and then performing functional classification annotation analysis on the proteins according to cellular components, molecular functions, and biological processes. We annotate protein pathways based on the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway database, and identify proteins through BLAST comparison (blastp, evalue ≤ 1e-4), for each sequence, the annotation is based on the top-scoring comparison result. We used the WoLF PSORT software to predict subcellular localization and annotate the submitted proteins. Clusters of Orthologous Groups of proteins (COG) were used to identify pathways.

Results

Induction and validation of A549 radioresistant cells

The generation of RA549 (Radioresistant A549) cells has been elucidated in methods (Fig. 1a), and afterward, colony formation was performed to verify its radioresistance compared to the original A549 cell line. This method is considered the gold standard for assessing radiation-induced cellular damage [24], and surviving fraction after 2 Gy irradiation (SF2) has been proven effective for examining cell radiosensitivity in vitro [25]. As shown in Fig. 1b, RA549 cells have a significantly higher surviving rate than A549 cells after irradiation, hence the cells were used for following analysis.

Radioresistant A549 induction and validation. a Schematic diagram of radioresistant A549 cells induced by fractionated radiation (total dose = 55 Gy). b Clonogenic assay result of A549 and RA549 cell after receiving 2 Gy of irradiation, each 60 mm dish was seeded with 500 cells

Transcriptomic analysis of A549 and RA549 cells

To explore differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in RA549 cells compared with A549 cells, a transcriptome analysis was performed, and A total of 20,263 DEGs were identified between A549 and RA549 cells by bulk RNA sequencing (Table S1). Besides, 415 upregulated genes and 505 downregulated genes were found in RA549 cells compared to A549 (Fig. 2a, b). KEGG and GO analyses were performed on this and subsequent datasets. KEGG annotation of up/down-regulated groups revealed signal transduction, signaling molecules and interaction, and immune system were the most enriched (Fig. 2c, Supplementary Fig. 1a). Meanwhile, enrichment analysis were also performed on the data (Fig. 2d, Supplementary Fig. 1b, c, d). GO enrichment revealed upregulated processes including negative regulation of proteolysis, cell motility, cell migration, signaling receptor binding, and response to external stimuli. KEGG analysis showed that upregulated genes were involved in multiple amino acid degradation and some important cell signaling pathways.

Transcriptomic data on A549 and RA549. a Bar plot and (b) volcano plot of significantly differentially expressed genes. c KEGG functional classification on upregulated genes. d GO enrichment analysis on upregulated genes; TOP 30 processes are shown

Proteomic analysis of A549 and RA549 cells

LC–MS was performed to investigate protein levels in A549 and RA549 cell lines, and 6,750 differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) were eventually identified. Among the identified proteins, 6735 were found to be comparable between A549 and RA549 cells. (Fig. 3a), total 395 upregulated proteins and 304 downregulated proteins were found in RA549 cells compared to A549 (Fig. 3b, c, Table S2). Similarly, six categories of KEGG annotation were used to classify the differentially expressed proteins (Fig. 3d, Supplementary Fig. 2a), notably, both upregulated and downregulated DEPs were enriched in cancer-related and drug resistance processes. KEGG enrichment analysis was also conducted on this data (Supplementary Fig. 2b, c), upregulated proteins were involved in amino acid metabolism and synthesis, sugar and lipid degradation processes. The COG/KOG dataset was also applied to sort the DEPs into four categories (Fig. 3e). Besides, molecular function (MF) enrichment of GO annotation was performed (Fig. 3f). Intriguingly, various functions closely related to lung cancer were enriched, including growth factor binding and transmembrane signaling receptor activities. In addition, subcellular localization of the DEPs was performed (Fig. 3g), and most upregulations in RA549 occurred in cytoplasm and on plasma membrane, while downregulations occurred in the nucleus.

Lysine 2-hydroxyisobutyrylomic analysis of A549 and RA549 cells

In this study, we focused on PTM differences between A549 and RA549 cells. A total of 30,089 Khib modification sites were obtained by LC–MS, and 4635 modified proteins were detected (Fig. 4a). Next, we analyzed the differences in Khib modification sites between RA549 and A549 cells. As shown in Fig. 4b and c, 626 of 2-hydroxyisobutyrylated proteins were downregulated in RA549, while 732 of them were upregulated compared to A549. Additionally, 1038 of 2-hydroxyisobutyrylated sites were downregulated, while 791 of sites were upregulated (Table S3). The regulatory effect of lysine 2-hydroxyisobutyrylation was further explored via KEGG annotations (Fig. 4d, Supplementary Fig. 3a). COG/KOG enrichment was also performed on the proteome and 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome results (Figs. 3e and 4e). Interestingly, Khib modified proteins were mainly enriched in post-translational modification, protein turnover, chaperones. MF annotation was also carried out on 2-hydroxyisobutyrylated proteins (Fig. 4f). Enrichment of upregulated modified proteins displayed enhancement in energy metabolism while quantitative proteomic results showed enrichment in transmembrane signaling transduction activities. Additionally, the number of enriched 2-hydroxyisobutyrylic proteins in cancer, anti-neoplastic drug resistance and signal transduction processes related pathways is multiplied compared to proteomic results. KEGG enrichment analysis was also performed (Supplementary Fig. 3b, c). Both upregulated and downregulated modification groups in RA549 were significantly enriched in NHEJ-related processes, and TCA cycle and several amino acids metabolism related processes were also enriched in both groups. Besides, we also noticed that differential Khib modified proteins are primarily distributed in cytoplasm, nucleus, and mitochondria (Fig. 4g).

Combined analysis of transcriptome, proteome and 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome

A combined analysis of transcriptomic, proteomic, and 2-hydroxyisobutyrylomic data revealed 19 differentially expressed or modified molecules (Fig. 6a). The functions of several proteins, such as MAFK, ALDH3A1, HSPA2, RAB31, include transcription factor, energy metabolism, and protein chaperone, and are reported to strongly contribute to lung cancer proliferation and metastasis [27–34]. Furthermore, we compared the omics data to KEGG non-small cell lung cancer related gene set (hsa05223), and found in RA549 cells, EGFR levels were significantly upregulated, while PRKCA levels were downregulated in both the proteome and 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome, respectively (Fig. 6b, c). Immunoprecipitation of EGFR was conducted to validate the Khib level of EGFR was indeed increased in the RA549 cell lines compared with A549 cell lines (Fig. 6d).

Combined analysis of transcriptome, proteome, and 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome. a Intersection of multi-omics findings. 19 genes/proteins were significantly regulated in all three omics. Cross reference of proteome (b) and 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome (c) with KEGG lung cancer related gene set (hsa05223). EGFR and PRKCA were isolated from both assays. d Immunoprecipitation of EGFR and pan anti-hydroxyisobutyryllysine antibody was used for immunoblotting

Next, to investigate the interaction between Khib modification and NSCLC radioresistance, we designed the following gain/loss of function experiments. First, in HEK293T cells, we confirmed the differential Khib modification site of EGFR (Fig. 7a). Then, to validate these results, we used another lung adenocarcinoma cell line, H1299. As clonogenic assay result shown in Fig. 7c, EGFR knockdown with siRNA decreases cell survival rates after RT, and siRNA + KR-EGFR group maintains similar percentage, while notably siRNA + wild-type-EGFR group restores original state.

Validation of correlation between EGFR lysine 716 site Khib modification and NSCLC cells radioresistance. a Immunoprecipitation results of overexpression of EGFR wild-type (WT) and lysine 716 to arginine mutation (K716R) in HEK293T cell line, respectively. b Knockdown and knockdown plus overexpression EGFR of EGFR validations in H1299 cell line. c Clonogenic assay and bar chart of H1299 after si-EGFR, si-EGFR + WT-EGFR, siEGFR + K716R-EGFR under irradiation

Discussion

Despite extensive research, the mechanisms underlying radioresistance in cancer remain poorly understood. In this study, we created a comprehensive multi-scale omics profile, from transcriptional to post-translational levels, to explore radioresistance in NSCLC, and applied 2-hydroxyisobutylome to uncover the impact of Khib modification in tumor radioresistance. We identified 920 differentially expressed genes through transcriptomic analysis, while quantitative proteomics revealed 395 upregulated and 304 downregulated proteins in RA549 compared to A549. Concurrently, lysine 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome identified 30,089 Khib sites on 4635 proteins, constituting the largest dataset on lysine 2-hydroxyisobutyrylation modification in NSCLC known to us.

Our combined analysis of transcriptomic, proteomic, and 2-hydroxyisobutyrylomic data offers a comprehensive view of the differences between radioresistant RA549 cells and the original A549 cell line at transcriptional, translational, and post-translational modification levels.. Both transcriptomic and proteomic data indicated enhanced energy metabolism in RA549 cells. This finding aligns with Jiang et al.’s. [4] research, which suggests that increased fatty acid oxidation provides additional energy for DNA repair and cancer metastasis. Meanwhile, GO and KEGG enrichment of DEGs and DEPs have revealed significant augment in signaling transduction, cell motility, migration functions pathways in RA549 subline. Therefore, all these genes and proteins enriched above could be potential targets for radiosensitizing therapy. Previous research on 2-hydroxyisobutyrylome already revealed Khib modification enhances fatty acid and glucose oxidation, as well as TCA cycle [17]. Our results drew similar conclusions, indicating the Khib modifications play essential roles in enhancing energy metabolism in cells and could potentiate radioresistance. Notably, upregulated Khib-modified proteins were more enriched in pathways related to signal transduction, protein folding and sorting, cell growth, and cell death compared to the proteomic analysis alone. A recent study demonstrated that Khib modified NAT10 stabilizes other proteins more effectively which eventually elevate cancer aggression [21]. Hence, we hypothesize that 2-hydroxyisobutyrylation contributes to radioresistance not only by enhancing DNA damage repair but also by boosting energy metabolism and chaperone protein efficacy, while preventing protein degradation.

Moreover, 19 differentially expressed genes/proteins were observed in combined omics intersection, implying that these proteins and modification sites might function crucially in lung adenocarcinoma post-radiation repopulation. In this article, we further investigated the correlation between Khib modification of EGFR and radiosensitivity, and therefore providing a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for radiation therapy.

Unfortunately, limitation of this study is its reliance on cell lines; thus, in vivo experiments and analysis of patient samples are necessary for validation and to explore the translational potential of our findings. Future research should focus on validating our findings in animal models and clinical samples, particularly investigating how Khib-modified EGFR contributes to radioresistance in NSCLC cells. Yet, this study offers novel insights into the mechanisms of radioresistance and highlights potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets, such as Khib-modified EGFR, for improving radiation therapy in NSCLC.

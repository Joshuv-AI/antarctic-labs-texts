Hyperlipidemia and associated risk factors among people living with HIV on antiretroviral therapy: a retrospective cohort study from Eastern China
Authors: Wei Cheng, Yushu Zhang, Jiezhe Yang, Jun Jiang, Xiaohong Pan, Chengliang Chai, Ying Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo determine the incidence of hyperlipidemia and identify its associated risk factors among people living with HIV (PLWH) initiating antiretroviral therapy (ART) in Eastern China, with the aim of informing strategies for early identification and timely management.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective cohort study of PLWH who initiated antiretroviral therapy (ART) in Zhejiang Province from January 2019 to December 2021. Demographic characteristics, clinical details, and other relevant factors were retrieved from the National Free ART Database.ResultsA total of 4387 PLWH were observed, with an incidence of hyperlipidemia of 43.8/100 person-years. A higher baseline CD4 cell count was associated with a reduced risk of hyperlipidemia: 51–200 cells/μL (aHR 0.75, 95% CI 0.65,0.87), 201–500 cells/μL (aHR 0.67, 95% CI 0.58,0.78), 501+ cells/μL (aHR 0.70, 95%CI 0.58, 0.84). Zidovudine + lamivudine + efavirenz, tenofovir disoproxil fumarate + lamivudine + lopinavir/ritonavir, elvitegravir/cobicistat/emtricitabine/ tenofovir alafenamide had a higher risk of hyperlipidemia. An HIV load ≥1000 copies/mL (aHR 4.03, 95% CI 3.67,4.42) was linked to an increased risk of hyperlipidemia.ConclusionsA high incidence of hyperlipidemia was observed in PLWH receiving ART. Strengthening blood lipid monitoring and managing modifiable risk factors are essential to prevent hyperlipidemia in PLWH.

Introduction

Over the past two decades, access to antiretroviral therapy (ART) has expanded in all regions, leading to improvements in the life expectancy of people living with HIV (PLWH). However, as PLWH live longer, they contend with other health complications, particularly noncommunicable diseases such as cardiovascular disease (CVD), cancer, and diabetes (Mody et al., 2024).

CVDs have emerged as the leading cause of death in PLWH in developed countries (Byonanebye, 2021). High triglyceride (TG) and total cholesterol (TC) levels; the most common types of dyslipidemia; increase the risk of CVD; such as coronary heart disease (CHD); ischemic stroke; and atherosclerosis (Chait, 2022; Jung et al., 2022; Peters et al., 2016). Increased TC is estimated to account for over 2.6 million deaths (4.5% of the total) annually worldwide, with one-third of CHD attributable to high TC (Peters et al., 2016). One of the largest studies evaluating the association between ART and myocardial infarction in PLWH showed that both higher TC and TG levels were associated with an increased incidence of myocardial infarction (Friis-Møller et al., 2003). Compared to the general population; lipid changes in PLWH may be influenced not only by general risk factors such as low physical activity; food intake; and obesity but also by HIV-specific factors such as the virus; host; and ART (Li et al., 2023; Mohammed et al., 2024). A large-scale national study of 359,952 PLWH initiating ART between 2018 and 2023 reported a baseline dyslipidemia prevalence of 38.4% before any ART exposure, highlighting the urgent need for lipid management in this population even prior to treatment initiation (Jia et al., 2025). A systematic review and meta-analysis of 52,463 Chinese PLWH reported that the prevalence of dyslipidemia reaches 55.1% in ART-experienced individuals—markedly higher than the 35.6% prevalence in the general Chinese adult population (Jianjun, 2023; Wenjing et al., 2025). Therefore, PLWH are a high-risk group for lipid metabolism disorders, necessitating targeted investigations to identify population-specific risk factors.

The impact of ART on lipid changes varies greatly among classes of ART drugs and even among drugs that share the same mechanism (Yu et al., 2023). Additionally, the use of medications that are not included in the China national free drug list are increasing in certain regions of China. For example, the proportion of medicare/self-paid medicine use among newly treated HIV/AIDS patients increased from 2.8% to 39.8% in Zhejiang Province, China, from 2019 to 2023 (Chen et al., 2025). This evolving landscape of ART options creates an urgent need to understand their real-world impacts on lipid profiles and to identify population-specific risk factors that can inform clinical decision-making and disease management strategies. Several studies have revealed that the incidence of dyslipidemia changes with different antiretroviral regimens in China. Sun et al. found that lamivudine (3TC) + tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) + efavirenz (EFV) was associated with a lower risk of dyslipidemia than other first-line free antiretroviral regimens (Sun et al., 2020). Wang et al. observed that compared to the 3TC + EFV + TDF group, transient elevations in blood lipid levels occurred in treatment-naïve adult male patients with HIV/AIDS on bictegravir /emtricitabine/ tenofovir alafenamide (BIC/FTC/TAF) at an early stage of treatment and did not differ significantly from baseline after 48 weeks of treatment (Wang et al., 2024). However, the studies mentioned above were conducted in single hospital-based settings, and most participants were MSM, limiting the representativeness. Therefore, the incidence of hyperlipidemia and its risk factors, especially for the different ART drugs, including self-paid and medically insured drugs among PLWH in real-world settings, remain limited. Additionally, the impact of HIV-specific factors such as CD4 cell count, HIV viral load, and ART drugs on lipid health remains controversial, as studies vary in population, diagnostic criteria, and ART drugs (Armstrong et al., 2011; Buendia et al., 2022; Kuti et al., 2015). Further studies are necessary to strengthen the scientific evidence for clinical decision-making regarding optimum lipid management. To address these knowledge gaps, this study retrospectively analyzed the follow-up data of PLWH who initiated ART between 2019 and 2021 in Zhejiang Province. Specifically, we aimed to: (1) determine the incidence of hyperlipidemia in this real-world cohort; (2) comprehensively evaluate the associations between hyperlipidemia and potential risk factors, including demographic characteristics, HIV-specific factors (CD4 cell count, HIV viral load), and diverse ART regimens; and (3) generate evidence to inform early identification strategies and targeted interventions for hyperlipidemia management in PLWH, ultimately contributing to the prevention of cardiovascular complications in this population.

Methods

Study design and population

Zhejiang Province, situated in the eastern part of China, boasts a robust economy and a dense population, housing over 65 million inhabitants across 11 cities. According to the National Manual on Free ART for HIV/AIDS, all PLWH initiating ART must be registered in the National Free ART Database established in 2004 and detailed elsewhere (Ma et al., 2010; Zhang et al., 2007).

This retrospective cohort study focused on PLWH who initiated ART in Zhejiang Province. Participants were screened and selected from the National Free ART Database on September 30, 2024, based on the following inclusion criteria: (1) initiation of ART between January 2019 and December 2021; (2) confirmed HIV diagnosis by the Centers for Disease Control (CDC) in individuals aged 18 years or older; (3) ART-naïve at inclusion; and (4) availability of baseline and at least one follow-up measurement of TC and TG. Exclusion criteria were: (1) baseline TC ≥ 5.2 mmol/L or TG ≥1.7 mmol/L; (2) lack of HIV viral load measurements during follow-up; (3) use of lipid-lowering drugs at baseline. Patients were regarded as lost to follow-up if they missed four consecutive appointments, and their follow-up records were terminated at the date of the last visit.

Measures

Hyperlipidemia was defined according to Guidelines for the prevention and treatment of dyslipidemia in Chinese adults (2016 revised edition) as hypercholesterolemia or hyperglyceridemia, with TC ≥ 5.20 mmol/L, or TG ≥ 1.70 mmol/L (Jianjun, 2023).

For eligible participants in the non-target endpoint group, the observation period spanned from ART initiation to the date of death, loss to follow-up, or the last normal result of TC and TG levels during follow-up before September 30, 2024. In the target endpoint group, the observation period was from ART initiation to the occurrence of hyperlipidemia (TC ≥ 5.20 mmol/L or TG ≥ 1.70 mmol/L, whichever occurred first.)

Data were retrieved from the National Free ART Database, including demographic characteristics (e.g., age, sex, and education level), clinical details (e.g., baseline CD4 cell count, WHO clinical stage, latest ART regimen, latest HIV viral loads, TC and TG levels), and other relevant factors (e.g., transmission route and time of ART initiation). Based on the number of treatment regimens used among the study subjects, the latest ART regimens were categorized as follows: TDF + 3TC + EFV, zidovudine (AZT) + 3TC + EFV, TDF + 3TC+ lopinavir/ritonavir (LPV/r), BIC/FTC/TAF, elvitegravir/cobicistat/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide (EVG/c/FTC/TAF), dolutegravir (DTG)-based regimen and others. Since the National Free ART Database lacked information on lipid-lowering drug use at baseline, we conducted a retrospective investigation by telephone for the study participants in October 2024.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as mean (SDs) or median (IQRs), while categorical variables are analyzed as percentages. As patients were enrolled in the cohort at different times, the incidence density was calculated to illustrate the occurrence of hyperlipidemia during follow-up. Person-years were calculated based on the observation period. Risk factors were evaluated using Cox proportional hazards regression models that included adjustment for covariates that were predetermined to be clinically meaningful and significantly influenced the unadjusted analysis (i.e., P-value <0.1). Before performing the regression analysis, a multicollinearity analysis was also conducted. All hypothesis tests were two-sided, with an alpha level of 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using R software version 4.4.1 (R Core Team, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Among them, 1242 (28.3%) had a college degree or higher. Heterosexual transmission accounted for a lower percentage (48.4%) than homosexual transmission 2241 (51.1%). The median baseline CD4 count was 261 cells/μL (IQR 209). Among participants, 3599 (82.0%) had an HIV viral load <1000 copies/mL. The most common antiviral regimen was TDF + 3TC + EFV (63.8%), followed by the DTG-based regimen (8.4%) and BIC/FTC/TAF (7.6%). Univariate analysis revealed a lower risk of hyperlipidemia in patients aged 50 years old or above, females, those with lower education levels, higher baseline or latest CD4 cell counts, and those on BIC/FTC/TAF regimen. Factors associated with an increased risk of hyperlipidemia included HIV load ≥1000 copies/mL, WHO clinical stage 2 or higher, and use of AZT + 3TC + EFV, tenofovir disoproxil fumarate + lamivudine + lopinavir/ritonavir (TDF + 3TC + LPV/r), and EVG/c/FTC/TAF.

In multivariate analysis, several factors, including education, baseline CD4 cell count, ART regimen, and latest HIV viral load, remained significantly associated with hyperlipidemia. Compared to individuals with a college degree or higher, those with illiteracy (aHR 0.78, 95% CI 0.62,0.97), primary school education (aHR 0.83, 95% CI 0.73,0.96), middle school education (aHR 0.84, 95% CI 0.75,0.94) had a lower risk of hyperlipidemia. The risk of hyperlipidemia decreased significantly in individuals with baseline CD4 cell count of 51–200 cells/μL (aHR 0.75, 95% CI 0.65,0.87), 201–500 cells/μL, (aHR 0.67, 95% CI 0.58,0.78), and 501+ cells/μL, (aHR 0.70, 95%CI 0.58,0.84). Additionally, compared to those using TDF + 3TC + EFV, PLWH on AZT + 3TC + EFV (aHR 1.66, 95% CI 1.43,1.92), TDF + 3TC + LPV/r (aHR 1.64, 95% CI 1.40,1.91), EVG/c/FTC/TAF (aHR 2.31, 95% CI 2.02,2.65) had a higher risk of hyperlipidemia, while those who used BIC/FTC/TAF (aHR 0.55, 95% CI 0.47,0.64) and other regimens (aHR 0.78, 95% CI 0.65,0.95) had a lower risk. Discussion

CVDs are twice as likely to occur in patients with dyslipidemia than in those with normal lipid levels (Van Hare et al., 2015). As PLWH lives longer, monitoring modifiable risk factors, such as high TC and TG levels, becomes crucial. This study retrospectively analyzed the follow-up data of TG and TC levels among PLWH initiating ART enrolled from 2019 to 2021. We found that education, baseline CD4 cell count, HIV viral load, and ART regimen were risk factors for hyperlipidemia, providing key insights for managing disease progression and preventing cardiovascular complications.

In contrast to HIV-negative individuals, PLWH receiving ART experience a substantially elevated lipid risk. A systematic review and meta-analysis showed that the overall prevalence of dyslipidemia was 55.1% among ART experienced PLWH in China (Wenjing et al., 2025). This is substantially higher than the estimated 35.6% prevalence in the general Chinese adult population (Jianjun, 2023). Therefore; compared to non-HIV populations, the necessity of strengthening lipid monitoring and intervention in this population should be highlighted. However, few studies have reported the incidence of hyperlipidemia in PLWH. In a cohort of 4577 PLWH, the incidence of dyslipidemia was 19.2/100 person-years (Byonanebye, 2021). Another study conducted in males with HIV noted that the incidence of hypertriglyceridemia was 21.0 per 100 person-years (Yu et al., 2023). Our study found that the incidence of hyperlipidemia in PLWH was 43.8/100 person-years. However, the different diagnostic criteria and definitions make direct comparisons with other studies challenging. Nonetheless, the higher incidence of hyperlipidemia in our analysis demonstrates that monitoring lipid changes after ART initiation should be strengthened.

Our study observed lower odds of hyperlipidemia among participants with lower education levels (illiteracy, primary, middle, or high school) than those with a college degree or higher. This finding aligns with studies by Kemal among PLWH (Kemal et al., 2020). In the general population, lower educational attainment is often linked to a higher burden of dyslipidemia, however, evidence across different studies remains inconsistent (Cho et al., 2020). The effect of education may operate indirectly through lifestyle behaviors, psychosocial stress, and access to healthcare resources (Cho et al., 2020). Among PLWH, the relationship between education level and hyperlipidemia may be further modified by HIV-related factors, such as differences in treatment access, medication choices, and long-term disease management. Therefore, the association between education and lipid abnormalities observed in our study should be interpreted within the broader socioeconomic and clinical context of PLWH, and deserved to be further studied.

The reduction of CD4 cells and abnormal immune activation are hallmark characteristics of HIV-1 infection and are strongly linked to non-AIDS-defining disorders. However, the complex relationships between lipids and HIV-specific factors require further investigation. Consistent with other studies (Assefa et al., 2023; Kuti et al., 2015), we found a lower baseline CD4 cell count had a higher risk of hyperlipidemia, emphasizing the role of the immune system in lipid health. HIV infection triggers an inflammatory response, impairing the efflux of cholesterol from macrophages and modulating the levels of fatty acid synthase (Funderburg and Mehta, 2016; Waters and Hsue, 2019). In accordance with previous research (Li et al., 2023), the multivariable analysis showed that a viral load ≥1000 copies/mL was an independent risk factor for hyperlipidemia. Our study suggests that with worsening infection, characterized by a lower CD4 cell count and detectable viral load, the risk of hyperlipidemia increases in PLWH. Therefore, careful lipid management is crucial for PLWH with advanced disease stages and virological failure to avoid increased health risks.

In China, the key components of first-line ART regimens include non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors such as EFV or nevirapine (NVP) and nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors such as AZT, TDF, and 3TC. However, self-paid and medically insured drugs, such as BIC/FTC/TAF and EVG/c/FTC/TAF, have been increasingly adopted in certain regions of China, like Zhejiang Province (Chen et al., 2025). The specific effects of different drugs or regimens on lipids in real-world settings have not been fully described in previous research. In this study, we found that the rates of hyperlipidemia differed across commonly used antiretroviral regimens in China. AZT + 3TC + EFV, TDF + 3TC + LPV/r, and EVG/c/FTC/TAF had more adverse effects on lipid concentrations compared to TDF + 3TC + EFV. Among these, AZT co-formulated with EFV had more adverse effects on lipids than EFV co-formulated with TDF, consistent with a study conducted in Shenzhen by Sun (Sun et al., 2020). Additionally, patients on LPV/r-based regimens had increased odds of dyslipidemia (Dai et al., 2019; Leyes et al., 2018), such as hypercholesterolemia and hypertriglyceridemia. Potential mechanisms for dyslipidemia include reduced hydrolysis of TG-rich lipoprotein, impaired catabolism of free fat acid (FFA), and decreased FFA trapping (Muya and Kamuhabwa, 2019). A cohort study found that EVG/c/FTC/TAF resulted in higher risks of hyperlipidemia than TDF + 3TC + EFV, possibly due to impaired adipocyte function and increased proinflammatory cytokines caused by EVG and TAF (Sun et al., 2022). Additionally, we found that BIC/FTC/TAF showed a lower risk of hyperlipidemia than TDF + 3TC + EFV. A study conducted among treatment-naïve adult male PLWH following BIC/FTC/TAF versus 3TC + EFV + TDF found transient elevations in blood lipid levels (TC, TG, HDL-C, and LDL-C) at 4, 12, and 24 weeks of treatment. However, these levels did not differ significantly from baseline after 48 weeks of treatment (Wang et al., 2024). Therefore, the lower risk of hyperlipidemia associated with BIC/FTC/TAF than with TDF + 3TC + EFV warrants further investigation. Given the high incidence of hyperlipidemia, the different effects of regimens on lipids indicated in our study may offer physicians more choices for managing HIV/AIDS more effectively.

The strengths of this study include a large sample size and relatively long follow-up, along with longitudinal analyses in real-world settings, making our results robust. Additionally, the diversity of ART regimens at the study site allowed for a comprehensive evaluation of their effects on lipid changes.

This study has some limitations. First, because of the absence of HDL and LDL levels in the National Free ART Database, we only analyzed hyperlipidemia involving TG and TC. Future studies should include all four lipid indicators to further describe the conditions and risk factors of dyslipidemia in PLWH. Second, we lacked data on other factors associated with higher TC or TG levels, such as body mass index, diet, physical activity, and smoking, which could have served as covariates in the regression models. Thus, collecting data on physical activity and dietary intake is imperative for a comprehensive understanding of lipid risk factors in this population.

Conclusion

Our findings revealed a high incidence of hyperlipidemia after ART initiation in patients with HIV/AIDS. In addition to general characteristics such as education, HIV-specific factors such as CD4 cell count, HIV viral load, and ART regimens were associated with hyperlipidemia. These findings contribute to the growing literature on HIV and ART, highlighting the importance of optimizing lipid management in PLWH. Blood lipid levels should be regularly monitored during ART initiation and continuation. In the future, addressing the modifiable risks for hyperlipidemia is crucial in the context of PLWH treatment and care. It is important to promote non-pharmacological strategies and interventions focused on diet and physical activity. This can be achieved by conducting specialized health education sessions for PLWH who have these risk factors, with the goal of enhancing their cardiovascular health.

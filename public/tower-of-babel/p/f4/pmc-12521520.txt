A watermarking framework for encrypted medical images via HC chaotic system and deep learning
Authors: Zilong Liu, Jingbing Li, Saqib Ali Nawaz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
With the deep iteration and innovation of information technology, medical technology is moving towards informatization and intelligence. This has led to a large-scale collection of medical imaging data that carries patient identification information being stored and disseminated over the network. It greatly increases the risk of medical images being leaked, tampered with, and stolen. To address this issue, a zero-watermarking method for encrypted medical images has been proposed based on HC dual chaos and DWT-ResNet-DCT. Firstly, based on the dynamic characteristic coupling of the Henon chaotic map and the Chen chaotic system, an HC dual-chaotic composite system is innovatively designed. And based on the WHT-DCT transform, it proposes a lossless encryption algorithm characterized by initial value sensitivity and a large key space. While ensuring high encryption efficiency, the algorithm achieves “lossless” decryption of medical images. On this basis, this paper proposes a watermarking algorithm based on DWT-ResNet-DCT for encrypted medical images. This algorithm effectively integrates the characteristics of the DWT transform domain and the convolutional neural network ResNet50, enabling accurate extraction of the feature sequence of encrypted medical images. Finally, experiments verify that the algorithm maintains high NC values (greater than 0.8) under traditional attacks, geometric attacks, and combined attacks, demonstrating excellent anti-attack capabilities, especially having good robustness under high-intensity geometric attacks.

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of computer technology, network transmission, and artificial intelligence technology, the global medical industry is undergoing a profound transformation characterized by “digitization” and “intelligence”1. This has significantly improved the medical level and strongly promoted the comprehensive upgrade of “smart healthcare.” As the core data carrier and crucial decision-making basis for “smart healthcare,” medical imaging plays a vital and indispensable role throughout the entire process of disease screening, diagnosis, treatment, and management2. In network environments, medical imaging data is massively transmitted and distributedly stored. As a key data carrier containing sensitive information such as patients’ personal identities, medical histories, and diagnosis results, the security of medical images is facing numerous severe challenges. During the transmission and storage processes, due to the lack of effective security protection mechanisms, medical images are highly vulnerable to malicious tampering or forgery3. Such tampering or forgery may alter the true content of the images, thereby affecting the accuracy of doctors’ diagnoses and the reliability of medical decisions. In extreme cases, it may even lead to misdiagnosis, mistreatment, and other serious consequences, posing a threat to patients’ lives. Furthermore, with the widespread application of AI technology, the security of large AI models in medical image analysis has also garnered extensive attention. Adversarial example attacks may mislead AI model diagnoses by subtly altering image pixel values, causing normal images to be misclassified as abnormal, thereby influencing medical diagnostic decisions4. Therefore, under the premise of ensuring medical image quality, ensuring the security of patients’ personal privacy information in medical imaging and maintaining data confidentiality and integrity have become critical challenges that need to be addressed urgently at present. That has become an urgent and critical problem faced in the construction of “Smart Healthcare”.

Data protection

The current data protection technologies mainly include cryptography, steganography and digital watermarking. Steganography focuses on “hiding the communication behavior itself” by embedding data imperceptibly in the carrier, making it difficult for third parties to detect the existence and transmission of the secret information. Digital watermarking implants verifiable identification information in the host image, providing technical basis for copyright protection5, privacy protection6, source authentication and integrity verification7. Cryptography uses mathematical algorithms and key management mechanisms to transform data content to ensure its confidentiality and integrity. Based on cryptography, image encryption technologies include symmetric encryption, asymmetric encryption, secret sharing, chaotic systems, DNA computing and neural networks, etc. However, symmetric encryption is fast but has complex key management; asymmetric encryption has high security but high computational cost; chaotic systems have high security but have periodicity problems; DNA computing has high parallelism but is limited in application; neural networks have strong flexibility but their security needs further verification. Therefore, aiming to simultaneously improve encryption efficiency and security, this paper proposes a lightweight encryption framework based on the HC dual-chaotic map.

In recent years, the sharing and transmission of medical images have become increasingly frequent8. Against this backdrop, research on watermarking algorithms for encrypted medical images has been continuously deepened. The research methods are no longer confined to a single transform domain technique but have gradually expanded to hybrid domain method and composite approaches that integrate transform domain techniques with deep learning9–11.

Borra12 proposed a non-blind fragile watermarking algorithm utilizing Compressed Sensing (CS) and Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT). It can not only prevent the watermark information from being stolen and tampered with but also achieve the authenticity verification of medical images. Based on SWT, SVD, and FFOA, Abirami13 proposed a medical image watermarking algorithm. This method offers enhanced robustness and imperceptibility. Anand14 generated the watermarking with a method that implemented a multimodality image fusion method by hybridization of NSST and DTCWT. Himanshi15 presented a watermarking framework that integrated DWT, HMD, SVD, and arnold scrambling. It performs well under JPEG compression, but shows poor resistance to median filtering attacks. Fan16 employs Logistic Map chaos for watermark encryption and subsequently utilizes the Inception V3-DCT algorithm to extract feature sequences. The algorithm exhibits robust resistance to geometric attacks; however, its resilience against high-noise attacks is relatively weak. Soualmi17 proposed a blind watermarking scheme based on Schur decomposition and Logistic chaotic mapping. This algorithm is robust against DICOM-JPEG, RLE, salt and pepper noise, and cropping, but still vulnerable to geometric attacks and strong noise; its low complexity and blind extraction feature make it have practical potential in protecting the copyright and integrity of medical images. Zeng18 utilized KAZE to detect feature points and perform DCT to obtain the low-frequency coefficient matrix. Through perceptual hashing, a feature sequence was obtained, and then it was XORed with multiple watermarks encrypted by Logistic chaos to generate a key. This algorithm has good robustness under geometric attacks such as 45% translation and cropping, but the robustness to high-intensity geometric attack is relatively weak. Sheng19 combined ResNet50 with DCT for zero-watermarking in medical images. It has good robustness against higher-intensity rotation and translation attacks, but relatively weak robustness against common attacks. Yi20 proposed a zero-watermarking scheme based on PHTs-DCT. The medical images are encrypted homomorphically using DWT-DCT and Tent chaos. And the low-frequency features of PHTs-DCT were extracted in the encrypted domain to form a feature sequence. This algorithm has good robustness against conventional attacks, but its anti-attack ability against medium–high intensity geometric attacks is relatively weak. Yangxiu21 embeds encrypted watermarks into the encrypted medical images by combining the Bandelet-DCT transform technology. Although this algorithm demonstrates good robustness, its encryption performance remains suboptimal. A zero watermarking scheme is proposed by Wenfeng22. The technology employs the Tetrolet-DCT algorithm to extract feature sequences from medical images. Even after the medical images are encrypted, the algorithm still demonstrates good resistance to traditional attacks and geometric attacks. The DWT-Gabor transform is utilized to embed the encrypted watermark into the encrypted medical images by Xiliang23. The algorithm particularly excels in its resistance to translation and cropping attacks. For encrypted medical images and based on DWT-Daisy-DCT, Yiyi24 proposed an algorithm, which shows strong robustness under both conventional attacks and geometric attacks, especially excelling in resisting rotation attacks. Meanwhile, it has low computational overhead and a fast-running speed. Shi25 proposed a scheme which are used to enhance the robustness and identifiability of the watermark from ROI. Based on DCT and improved DarkNet-53, Li26 simultaneously encrypts both medical images and watermark information, proposing an algorithm that achieves a balance between attack resistance, robustness, and imperceptibility.

Motivation, contribution, and novelty

Medical imaging data, serving as the critical carrier for patient privacy and clinical diagnostic information, imposes stringent demands on security protection technologies due to its inherent sensitivity and complexity. During the sharing and transmission of medical data, traditional plaintext-domain watermarks are vulnerable to interception, tampering, and destruction, making it challenging to simultaneously satisfy the tripartite requirements of “zero-distortion, high-security, and high-robustness.” Consequently, there is an urgent need for a lossless watermarking scheme capable of operating entirely within the encrypted domain and exhibiting strong resilience against both geometric and non-geometric attacks. Addressing these challenges, this study proposes a novel protection framework integrating chaotic encryption, reversible transformations, and deep learning. The framework aims to achieve a triple guarantee of “encryption security, image losslessness, and attack robustness”, thereby offering a solution for the security management of medical data throughout its entire lifecycle.

The primary innovation of the proposed scheme lies in the integration of the dynamic advantages of Henon and Chen chaos to design the Henon–Chen (HC) chaotic system. This system features enhanced parameter dimensions, strong sensitivity to initial conditions, and significantly increased key space complexity. And an innovative lossless encryption algorithm based on WHT-DCT is designed. Leveraging the mathematical reversibility of these transformations ensures that medical images can be completely restored to their original pixel values after encryption and decryption, meeting the stringent quality requirements for clinical diagnosis and providing high-intensity privacy protection for medical imaging data. The designed encryption algorithm possesses an ultra-large key space and excellent scrambling performance, effectively resisting attacks such as brute-force cracking and providing high-intensity security for encrypted medical images. Then, by combining transform-domain methods with Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), the DWT-ResNet-DCT encrypted medical watermarking algorithm is proposed. This algorithm implements zero-watermark embedding and extraction through a ciphertext-domain feature processing mechanism. While ensuring encryption security, it exhibits excellent robustness against conventional attacks, geometric transformation attacks, and composite attacks. Notably, in response to rotation and shear attacks, it maintains outstanding anti-interference performance even under high-intensity attack scenarios.

The main contributions of this study are as follows:It proposed a lossless encryption algorithm based on WHT-DCT, which has the characteristics of having many parameters, and it demonstrates a high sensitivity to initial parameters and possesses a considerably large key space complexity.It combines the transform domain method with the convolutional neural network and proposes an encrypted medical image watermarking algorithm based on DWT-ResNet-DCT, which realizes the non-destructive embedding and extraction of zero-watermarking for encrypted medical images.The implementation of zero-watermark embedding and blind extraction is achieved, and experimental verification demonstrates that the algorithm exhibits significant robustness against conventional attacks, geometric transformation attacks, and combined attacks.

HC dual-Chaos system

The HC dual-chaotic system proposed achieves structural complementarity of chaotic systems by integrating the dynamical advantages of the Henon map and the Chen chaotic system. The parameters of the Henon map can be used to rapidly adjust the behavior of the system, while the wide parameter range of the Chen chaotic system can provide more stable chaotic behavior. Moreover, the Chen chaotic system can compensate for possible uneven distribution issue in the Henon chaotic system. In this system, the two-dimensional Henon map is applied to rapid scrambling or diffusion operations, and the Chen chaotic system is used to generate complex key sequences or perform more complex transformations. By constructing various chaotic scrambling combinations, the code-breaking resistance of the encryption algorithm has been significantly enhanced.

Leveraging multiple initial values and a high degree of sensitivity to them, the HC dual chaotic system achieves a substantially larger key space than conventional systems. This characteristic enables the generation of highly unpredictable and robust chaotic sequences that are resistant to cracking. DWT-Resnet

The DWT-ResNet (Residual Network based on Discrete Wavelet Transform) is a deep learning architecture that combines the DWT and the Residual Network (ResNet). This architecture is designed to leverage the strengths of wavelet transform in time–frequency analysis alongside the profound learning capabilities of ResNet, thereby significantly improving the model’s performance in handling intricate signals. Its applications are particularly prominent in areas such as image processing, signal analysis, and fault diagnosis, where it demonstrates exceptional efficacy.

In the DWT-ResNet approach, the input images are first decomposed using DWT, yielding feature maps that represent different frequency subbands. Subsequently, these subband feature maps are individually fed into the ResNet for feature extraction and learning. Finally, the results of each sub-band processed by the ResNet are fused to obtain the final output, as shown in Fig. 3.Fig. 3The structure diagram of DWT-Resnet50 (data from24).

The structure diagram of DWT-Resnet50 (data from24).

DWT can effectively capture image features across multiple scales. Integrated with ResNet, this hybrid model can simultaneously harness details from images at different resolutions. For image data with complex structures and multi-scale features, it can describe the image content more comprehensively and improve the model’s recognition ability for targets of different sizes. And after DWT decomposition, the subband data is typically reduced in size compared to the original data. Processing these smaller subband datasets within ResNet can decrease the computational demands of the model and parameter count, leading to faster training and inference times, and also contributing to a reduction in overfitting. DWT’s noise-resistance and invariance to image transformations, together with ResNet’s powerful feature learning, make the features extracted by the DWT-ResNet model more robust. This enhances the model’s adaptability to noise, occlusions, and deformations in images, thereby improving its generalization ability. By integrating the advantages of DWT and ResNet, the DWT-ResNet model achieves better performance than traditional ResNet in image-related tasks.

The proposed algorithm

Lossless encryption algorithm based on WHT-DCT and HC Dual-Chaos

Considering the importance and sensitivity of medical images, this paper develops a lossless encryption algorithm to ensure that the pixel values of the decrypted image are completely identical to the original image. Robustness analysis

Resistance to conventional attacks

Considering that display attacks usually target the original images, this paper applies different attacks to the original images to verify the robustness of the target algorithm. To comprehensively evaluate the robustness of the proposed algorithm, this study designs and implements a multi-watermarking experiment. In this experiment, three watermarks of different sizes (“HN” and “CN” at 32 × 32 pixels, and “MIW” at 64 × 64 pixels) are embedded into the same target encrypted medical image. The imperceptibility of each watermark is quantitatively assessed using the Normalized Cross-Correlation (NC), denoted as NC1, NC2, and NC3, respectively. This experiment is designed to comprehensively validate the algorithm’s stability and reliability when carrying multiple pieces of information, thereby providing an in-depth investigation of its overall performance in complex application scenarios. Given that different medical images present similar texture features after encryption. This paper sets the correlation coefficient threshold at 0.85 to evaluate the similarity degree between the feature sequences of the original encrypted image and those of the attacked encrypted image. Conclusion

This paper combines the transform domain with deep learning methods to propose a watermarking algorithm for encrypted medical images with HC chaotic encryption based on DWT-Resnet-DCT. The HC dual chaotic system employed in this algorithm integrates the advantages of the Henon map and the Chen chaotic system, compensating for their respective shortcomings. It exhibits characteristics such as initial value sensitivity, a large key space, and diverse encryption combinations. Combined with the WHT-DCT algorithm, lossless encryption and decryption of medical images have been achieved, enhancing the storage security of medical images. On this basis, it proposes a watermark algorithm based on DWT-ResNet-DCT to embed the encrypted watermark information into the encrypted medical image, thereby improving security performance. Moreover, the algorithm demonstrates good robustness against conventional attacks and geometric attacks and their combined attacks. Particularly, achieving high NC values under 0–180° rotation attacks and high-intensity translation and cropping attacks. And the algorithm also possesses high resistance to and robustness against both types of combined attacks.

The proposed algorithm achieves efficient processing of image encryption/decryption and watermark embedding/extraction on general-purpose hardware platforms, such as mobile devices and standard laptops, without the need for GPU acceleration. These operations are completed in seconds or even milliseconds. The core technical innovation lies in establishing a correlation and generating a key through a differential operation between feature vector of the image and the binary watermark sequence. The algorithm’s execution is predicated on the precise matching between the bit-length of the feature vector and the length of the watermark sequence. This mechanism not only ensures the algorithm’s high efficiency but also effectively enhances the data payload capacity. However, the geometric features of encrypted medical images are completely obscured due to encryption operations, and the encrypted images exhibit high similarity, which constitutes the primary challenge of this study. Subsequently, we will construct a multimodal encrypted medical imaging dataset and design targeted deep neural network architectures to enhance the model’s discriminative ability towards encrypted features, thereby further optimizing algorithmic performance. This also represents the subsequent primary focus of this study.

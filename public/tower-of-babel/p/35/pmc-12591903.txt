Assessment of intratumor heterogeneity in non-small cell lung cancer by unsupervised K-means clustering of radiomics features based on multiphase computed tomography images
Authors: Ronghua Wang, Lin Wang, Huijing Feng, Yang Jing, Xinzheng Wang, Jinzhi Fang, Jiangfeng Du, Linning E
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIntratumor heterogeneity (ITH) is a key determinant of tumor progression, drug resistance, and poor survival in patients with cancer. This study aimed to examine ITH in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) via unsupervised K-means clustering based on multiphase computed tomography (CT) radiomics features.MethodsThis study retrospectively included 452 patients with NSCLC across three cohorts from two centers. Multiphase CT images [including unenhanced CT (UECT) and contrast-enhanced CT arterial phase (CECT-AP) and -venous phase (CECT-VP)], clinicopathological characteristics, and short-term immunotherapy responses of patients were collected. A total of 104 radiomics features were extracted from lung cancer regions segmented on CT images. Unsupervised K-means clustering was then employed to quantify ITH in NSCLC, with results visualized through ITH distribution heatmaps. The degree of ITH was quantified and termed ITH-Radscore. We assessed the difference in ITH-Radscores derived from UECT and CECT images. Furthermore, we analyzed the associations of ITH-Radscores with the clinicopathological characteristics and immunotherapeutic efficacy for NSCLC to validate the accuracy and effectiveness of our proposed method.ResultsBy viewing ITH distribution heatmaps of unsupervised K-means clustering based on multiphase CT images, we identified two distinct high-order ITH imaging patterns, designated as the tree-ring pattern and diffuse pattern. ITH-Radscores for the tree-ring and diffuse patterns were significantly different, with values of 0.643–0.768 and 0.921–0.967, respectively. ITH-Radscores derived from UECT images were significantly higher than those from CECT images (all P values <0.001). ITH-Radscores derived from CECT-AP and CECT-VP showed limited diagnostic value for high-grade patterns in lung adenocarcinoma (P=0.209 and P=0.501, respectively), while those derived from UECT images demonstrated significant predictive capability (P=0.019). ITH-Radscores derived from multiphase CT images were significantly associated with the clinical stage, histological grade, lymphovascular invasion, angiogenesis expression, and short-term immunotherapy efficacy for NSCLC (all P values <0.05).ConclusionsMultiphase CT-based unsupervised radiomics analysis effectively revealed ITH in NSCLC, especially on UECT images. ITH-Radscore demonstrated potential as a noninvasive imaging biomarker associated with the clinicopathological characteristics and immunotherapy outcomes of NSCLC, offering valuable insights for precision oncology.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer incidence and cancer-related mortality worldwide (1,2). Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounts for almost 85% of cases, with notable intratumor heterogeneity (ITH) at the histological and molecular levels (3,4). Extensive research has indicated that ITH is a vital determinant for tumor progression, drug resistance, and immune evasion (5-7). Its dynamic variability increases the complexity of therapeutic regimens and may lead to recurrence. Therefore, in addition to tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging, genetic mutations and histopathological examination, ITH evaluation is an effective strategy for patient stratification and the realization of precision medicine.

The driving factors of ITH are diverse, and thus ITH exhibits varying degrees of complexity (8). Genomic and histopathological examinations are the classical methods for ITH evaluation. In the 19th century, Virchow observed phenotypic heterogeneity of multiple cellular subpopulations within individual tumor tissues using a compound microscope (9). Emerging technologies such as multiregion sequencing and single-cell analysis have provided robust support for the deep profiling of ITH (10,11). Wu et al. used a variety of molecular analytical techniques to delineate two spatial ITH distribution patterns of cellular subpopulations in lung adenocarcinoma (ADC), termed “clustered pattern and random pattern” (12). They found that the random pattern was associated with poorer survival rates. However, these methods are limited by the finiteness and representativeness of tissue samples, which might fail to truly reflect the heterogeneity of the whole tumor. Therefore, it is of paramount importance to develop a noninvasive imaging analysis method to comprehensively capture the underlying ITH information in NSCLC.

Radiomics is a high-throughput quantitative image characterization tool (13). When combined with unsupervised learning algorithms, it integrates biophysical parameters and allows for data clustering based on local and global levels (14-16). This approach can comprehensively visualize the composition and spatial distribution of cellular subpopulations within the entire tumor, offering a novel perspective for studying ITH. A few studies have employed this method to establish ITH habitat models, providing multidimensional ITH-related data for identifying tumor aggressiveness, guiding immunotherapy decisions, and predicting the survival rates of patients with NSCLC (17,18). Furthermore, correlation analyses between radiomics features and multimodal biological data, including genomics, pathological characteristics, and immune cell infiltration, have clarified the biological basis for the macroscopic imaging phenotypes of ITH at the microscopic level (19-21). The majority of studies assessing ITH in NSCLC have focused on multiparametric or multimodality functional imaging such as positron emission tomography (PET) and magnetic resonance imaging. Recently, Li et al. developed a multiscale radiomics biomarker for quantifying ITH based on unenhanced computed tomography (UECT) images (15). However, comparative studies focusing on ITH evaluation based on dynamic contrast-enhanced CT (CECT) images are rare.

In this study, we aimed to examine an unsupervised radiomics approach based on multiphase CT images, integrating clinicopathological characteristics and responses to immunotherapy in patients with NSCLC, to develop a noninvasive and accurate imaging biomarker for assessing ITH (Figure 1 illustrates the schematic of the study design). We present this article in accordance with the STARD reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2024-2739/rc).

Methods

Patient cohorts

This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments and was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of Shanxi Bethune Hospital (Approval No. SQBLL-2024-254) and The People’s Hospital of Longhua (Approval No. 2024011). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the analysis.

We enrolled three patient cohorts from two centers in this work, each designed to represent distinct clinicopathological profiles for multidimensional validation of the method’s effectiveness. All cases were staged according to the eighth American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) TNM staging system (22). The grouping and inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) patients with stage I–IIIA NSCLC who underwent radical surgical resection in Shanxi Bethune Hospital from January 2019 to January 2024 constituted Cohort 1; (II) patients with stage I–IIIA lung ADC who received surgical resection at The People’s Hospital of Longhua from January 2020 to January 2023 constituted Cohort 2; and (III) patients with advanced NSCLC (stage IIIA–IV) treated with immunotherapy alone for 2–4 cycles at first- or later-line at Shanxi Bethune Hospital between April 2022 and April 2024 constituted Cohort 3. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) a time interval between chest CT scans (including UECT and CECT images) and surgery or immunotherapy exceeding 2 weeks; (II) poor CT image quality; (III) a history of biopsy and any antitumor treatments before CT scan for Cohorts 1 and 2; (IV) insufficient pathological tissue specimens and immunostaining failure for Cohorts 1 and 2; and (V) incomplete clinicopathological information and immunotherapy response assessment records.

Assessment of short-term immunotherapy response

Two oncologists assessed and documented the short-term immunotherapy response by measuring tumor changes on CT images from pretreatment to after 2–4 cycles of treatment. Responses were categorized into four groups according to the immune Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (iRECIST) 1.1 (23): complete response (CR), partial response (PR), progressive disease (PD), and stable disease (SD) (the specific definitions are provided in Appendix 1). The response status of this study was dichotomous, involving responder (assessed as CR, PR, or SD) and nonresponder (assessed as PD).

CT image acquisition

Chest CT scans [including UECT, CECT-arterial phase (AP) and venous phase (VP)] were conducted in the supine position following full inspiration, with one of three multislice CT systems (SOMATOM Definition Flash and SOMATOM Definition AS, Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany; Revolution CT, GE HealthCare, Chicago, IL, USA). The CT parameters were as follows: tube voltage, 120 kV; tube current, 50–250 mA with CareDose; collimation, 0.6×128 mm; rotation time, 0.5–1.0 s/turn; slice thickness, 1 mm; and reconstruction kernel, B70f lung convolution. Iodixanol (320 mg I/mL; GE HealthCare) was administered as a contrast agent at a dose of 1.0 mL/kg and a flow rate of 3.0–3.5 mL/s via antecubital vein injection, which was followed by a 20-mL injection of saline solution. CECT scans were conducted with bolus tracking at the aortic arch.

NSCLC lesion segmentation framework based on no new U-Net (nnU-Net)

Dataset collection and annotation

The construction of a deep learning (DL) model for NSCLC lesion segmentation was carried out based on the CT images of the included patients in this study. Two radiologists with 10 and 6 years of experience (Rui Li and Jing Du), respectively, manually segmented the regions in a blinded method without access to clinical information and radiological reports. The consistency of delineation results by the two radiologists was evaluated via the Dice coefficient. Cases with a Dice coefficient <0.90 underwent consensus review, while those with a coefficient ≥0.90 were randomly selected for review by one radiologist. The dataset was randomly partitioned into a training set and a validation set at a ratio of 8:2.

The DL framework of the nnU-Net model

This study employed the open-source nnU-Net DL framework (as shown in Figure 1), which supports various formats and multidimensional medical imaging segmentation, and has achieved superior segmentation performance in numerous competition projects (24). The data were configured according to the preprocessing protocols established by nnU-Net, with framework and hyperparameter settings aligned with the data’s parameters and attributes. Normalization was conducted via the Z-score method. The framework’s built-in data augmentation module was applied, encompassing techniques such as rotation, scaling, Gaussian noise, brightness variation, contrast adjustment, low-resolution simulation, elastic transformation, and gamma variation.

Training process

The training network model was constructed with the two-dimensional U-Net (2D-U-Net) module within the DL framework. The stochastic gradient descent (SGD) algorithm was employed as the optimizer, with the Dice loss function, a standard choice for segmentation tasks. The patch and batch sizes were configured to 1,792×768 and 4, respectively. The initial learning rate was set at 0.001, and training was continued for over 250 epochs.

Evaluation metrics

In the validation set, the model’s segmentation performance was evaluated via the Dice coefficient, a metric quantifying the volumetric overlap between predicted segmentations and true annotations. CT-based ITH subgroup classification and quantification

Data preprocessing

All data preprocessing was first conducted on three CT scan phases (UECT, CECT-AP, and CECT-VP) to eliminate anisotropy and interdevice variability. The specific preprocessing steps were as follows: (I) data standardization, in which CT images were normalized on a per-patient basis by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation of the original intensity values; (II) data resampling, in which all data were resampled via interpolation algorithms to achieve an isotropic voxel spacing of 1×1×1 mm; and (III) noise reduction, in which Gaussian smoothing was applied to denoise the images.

Pixel-level radiomic feature (RF) extraction

Automatic segmentation of NSCLC lesions was performed with the nnU-Net DL framework, followed by CT-based pixel-level radiomics feature extraction. For each lesion region, the slice with the largest tumor volume was selected as the core feature extraction layer. Local pixel-level RFs were extracted from the core layer with three-dimensional (3D) neighborhoods of voxels 3×3×3, 5×5×5, and 7×7×7. The 3D neighborhoods were selected for core feature extraction to account for the diverse lesion sizes present across stages I to IV. In total, 104 RFs were extracted from each region via the open-source Python package PyrRadiomics (v. 3.1.0), comprising 19 first-order features, 10 shape features, and 75 texture features. The definitions and calculation methods for these three categories of features are referenced from the RF extraction documentation (https://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io/en/latest/features.html).

Visualization of clustering analysis and the quantification of ITH

The classic unsupervised machine learning k-means clustering algorithm was used to identify similar subregions within the whole lesion. Immunohistochemical (IHC) assays and assessment

All resected specimens were fixed with 10% formalin, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned at 4-µm thickness at the maximum slice of the tumor. IHC staining with anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and anti-CD34 mouse monoclonal antibodies (ZM-0265 and ZM-0046; Beijing Zhong Shan-Golden Bridge Biological Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) were performed. Two pathologists with more than 12 years of experience (blinded to clinical and radiological reports) evaluated IHC results using a consensus method. Microvessel density (MVD) was determined according to the method described by Weidner et al (25). VEGF expression was evaluated by the scoring of staining intensity (representative IHC images are presented in Figure S1) and the proportion of positively stained cells. Pathological interpretation methods are detailed in Appendix 1.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are expressed as the mean ± standard deviations or as the median with interquartile range (IQR) according to data distribution characteristics. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies (percentages). ITH-Radscore differences across subgroups were analyzed with Mann-Whitney and Kruskal-Wallis tests, with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons. A two-tailed P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted with SPSS version 23.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Data visualization was conducted in R (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and ITK-SNAP software.

Based on the exclusion criteria, 203, 152, and 97 patients with NSCLC, ADC, and advanced NSCLC, respectively, were included in Cohort 1 (mean age 60.52±9.37 years; 42.9% female), Cohort 2 (mean age 59.65±9.68 years; 45.4% female), and Cohort 3 (mean age 63.38±8.62 years; 70.1% female), respectively (the flowchart of patient enrolment is provided in Figure 2).

CT-based clustering analysis, visualization, and quantification of ITH

To examine ITH in NSCLC, all voxel-level RFs extracted from multiphase CT images were analyzed via unsupervised k-means clustering (k=6). This approach identified inherent similarities in the data, generating voxel-clusters reflecting ITH characteristics. We the labeled the same voxel-clusters with the same color to create ITH distribution heatmaps for visualizing spatial distribution. We observed two imaging patterns by viewing all heatmaps, tree-ring and diffuse patterns. The tree-ring pattern exhibited regional clustering of quantitative RFs, indicating low ITH and high spatial aggregation within the lesion. In contrast, the diffuse pattern exhibited regional mixed disorder of RFs, suggesting high ITH and low spatial aggregation. Representative images are provided in Figure 4A,4B.

The effectiveness of ITH-Radscore based on the angiogenesis expression of NSCLC

VEGF has been identified as the predominant proangiogenic mediator in NSCLC, and MVD serves as a quantitative indicator of angiogenic activity (27). The result demonstrated a positive correlation between the expression of VEGF and MVD in Cohort 1 (r=0.479; P<0.001). According to the median values of VEGF and MVD, we divided patients into low (≤ median) and high (> median) subgroups (28). ITH-Radscores derived from multiphase CT images were significantly higher in the VEGF-low and MVD-low subgroups (all P values <0.05; Table 4 and Figure 6).

Discussion

This study demonstrates that unsupervised K-means clustering of radiomics features derived from multiphase CT images may serve as a promising noninvasive method for the comprehensive evaluation of ITH in patients with NSCLC. ITH distribution heatmaps intuitively visualized two distinct high-order imaging patterns: tree-ring pattern and diffuse pattern. These imaging patterns were consistent with the tumor growth patterns observed in hematoxylin and eosin-stained pathological images. Furthermore, we found the ITH-Radscores derived from UECT and CECT images differed significantly and that UECT images were more sensitive to capturing ITH-related radiological information.

Recent studies have demonstrated that the potential of radiomics as a “virtual biopsy” tool for inferring tumor molecular characteristics (29). Unsupervised radiomics can unbiasedly identify voxel clusters based on the intrinsic similarities of quantitative RFs, without the need for definite labeled data, as to comprehensively reveal the ITH of the whole tumor. Shen et al. proposed a subregion-based PET/CT radiomics model, which demonstrated superiority in depicting the ITH of NSCLC (30). Meanwhile, compared to the conventional model, they found this model improved performance in differentiating lung ADC from squamous cell carcinoma (SCC). Huang et al. retrospectively analyzed a NSCLC radiomics cohort from the public database using unsupervised consensus clustering (19). They identified three imaging subtypes based on RFs from UECT images, with each subtype exhibiting unique dysregulated pathways.

A major limitation of unsupervised learning is the insufficient clinical interpretability of its classification results (31). Therefore, we multidimensionally validated the effectiveness and robustness of this method by combining ITH-Radscore with clinical characteristics, histopathological expression, and the efficacy of immunotherapy for NSCLC. The results showed that the ITH-Radscore obtained from multiphase CT images was an indicator for overall stage, T stage, and N stage. Xu et al. identified elevated homologous recombination defects and cell cycle dysregulation in advanced-stage lung cancer cohort (32). These alterations promoted tumor development and increased genomic instability. Additionally, our results revealed that ITH-Radscore was also a potential marker for predicting the invasiveness of NSCLC. Consistent with our observations, a subregion-based study of stage I solid ADC found that greater ITH correlates with HGP, advanced clinical stage, and a higher frequency of invasive features (33). The predictive potential of ITH-Radscore for short-term immunotherapy outcomes is supported by our findings, offering valuable data for clinical decision-making. A growing body of evidence suggests that ITH promotes immune evasion and enhances tumor cell survival during antitumor treatment (34).

In this study, we observed that the low-angiogenesis group (VEGF/MVD-low) exhibited a relatively higher ITH-Radscore. Previous studies have reported that NSCLC progression is often accompanied by increased VEGF/MVD expression, enhanced tumor aggressiveness, and elevated ITH (35,36). However, these findings remain controversial (37-39), and research directly investigating the relationship between angiogenesis and ITH in NSCLC remains limited. Wang et al. conducted a study using [18F] fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) PET/CT to assess dynamic FDG metabolic (DFM) heterogeneity in NSCLC, revealing that the slow-DFM group showed higher heterogeneity, but no statistically significant difference in MVD was observed between the slow-DFM and fast-DFM groups (14). This suggests that the relationship between angiogenesis and ITH may be more complex than expected. One possible explanation for this is that low VEGF/MVD levels may induce hypoxic stress and destabilize the tumor microenvironment, thereby promoting tumor plasticity and heterogeneity (40). This process could amplify spatial disparities in metabolic and proliferative activities, ultimately leading to elevated radiomic heterogeneity. This interpretation aligns with the hypothesis proposed by Qiu et al. regarding how reduced CD34 MVD might facilitate NSCLC progression (41). Furthermore, methodological variations in VEGF/MVD assessment may contribute to the inconsistencies across studies. Future research should incorporate hypoxia-related molecular indicators (e.g., HIF-1α) and validate the finding in larger NSCLC cohorts.

Importantly, comparative analysis illustrated that the ITH-Radscore obtained from UECT was significantly higher than that derived from CECT. Notably, the ITH-Radscore based on CECT demonstrated limited clinical value in predicting HGP as compared to that based on UECT. We speculate this result is partly due to UECT images better reflecting the intrinsic density of tissue related to ITH, whereas contrast agent injection highlights tumor blood flow with decreasing tissue contrast. This effect may mask subtle differences in the density and texture within the tumor. Furthermore, multiphase contrast enhancement increased image noise, thereby reducing the stability and accuracy of RF extraction.

There are several limitations to our work that should be addressed. First, the retrospective nature of the analysis inevitably introduced sample selection bias. The generalizability of the results should be further verified in prospective multicenter studies. Second, although multiple image-preprocessing techniques were applied, the heterogeneity of scanning equipment and image acquisition might have influenced the reproducibility of RFs. Third, we provided a multi-dimensional biological interpretation of ITH-Radscore by combining clinicopathological characteristics and immunotherapy outcomes, but the genetic basis of imaging heterogeneity was not analyzed due to the difficulty in obtaining genomic data. The integration of a diversity biological data and multiomics will be examined in future research.

Conclusions

This study revealed that unsupervised K-means clustering of radiomics features based on multiphase CT images can comprehensively characterize and quantify ITH in NSCLC. ITH distribution heatmaps visualized distinct ITH imaging patterns. Furthermore, ITH-Radscore has the potential to serve as a noninvasive imaging biomarker of tumor heterogeneity, complementing clinicopathological characteristics and guiding clinical decision-making in precision oncology.

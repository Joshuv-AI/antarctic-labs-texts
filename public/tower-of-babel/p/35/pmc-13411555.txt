Quality of Life and its Sociodemographic Determinants among Resident Doctors in Tertiary Hospital
Authors: Jitendra Singh, Anuradha Yadav, Manisha Sankhla
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Context:Resident doctors face immense work-related challenges that impact their quality of life (QoL), leading to stress, burnout, and psychological issues. Various factors, like gender, marital status, and medical speciality, affect these outcomes.Aims:This study explored the association between QoL and sociodemographic factors among resident doctors to enhance their well-being and professional satisfaction in tertiary care hospitals.Settings and Design:Cross-sectional study.Materials and Methods:The study was conducted on 50 resident doctors aged between 23 and 45 years of both genders, following ethical committee approval and informed consent. Sociodemographic data and lifestyle factors were collected, and the WHOQOL-BREF questionnaire was used to assess QoL.Statistical Analysis:The unpaired t-test for comparing two means and ANOVA for more than two means, with a significance threshold of P < 0.05.Results:The study found that most resident doctors were satisfied with QoL and general health. Environmental (63.4) and physical health (61.0) domains scored highest, while psychological health scored lowest (38.9). Social health was higher among married residents, while male residents had better physical and psychological health, which improved with age (>30 years). Exercise showed a significant positive association with social health (P = 0.027).Conclusions:The study highlighted that though resident doctors reported satisfactory QoL and general health, psychological health is a key area of concern. Psychological health had the lowest score in four domains of QoL, varying by gender, age, and marital status.

INTRODUCTION

The demanding nature of resident doctors’ work significantly impacts their quality of life (QoL), often leading to stress, burnout, and psychological distress. Factors, such as gender, marital status, and speciality, influence these outcomes.[123] Female residents frequently report lower QoL than males,[2] while marital status can either protect against[4] or exacerbate burnout.[5] High workloads and long hours in tertiary care settings further intensify these challenges.[67]

Studies highlight the high prevalence of burnout among medical professionals,[6] with significant levels of depression and anxiety.[1] Burnout also negatively affects patient care and job satisfaction.[7] Additionally, QoL and burnout vary by medical speciality and career stage, requiring more emphasis to address the specific needs of high-stress specialities and early-career professionals.[38] Improving the QoL of resident doctors is crucial to ensure both their well-being and effective patient care.

The aim of the study was to determine the association between the QoL among resident doctors and various sociodemographic factors, such as gender, marital status, locality, body mass index (BMI), and exercise habits, which may contribute to job dissatisfaction. By exploring these relationships in depth, the findings might help to guide the development of policies and interventions designed to enhance the well-being and professional satisfaction of resident doctors in tertiary care hospitals.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The study was a cross-sectional observational investigation conducted with ethical approval (336/MC/EC/2023, dated 12/04/2023) and informed consent. Fifty healthy resident doctors aged between 23 and 45 years, regularly assigned night duties, were recruited by a convenience sampling method. The resident doctors participating in the study were personally interviewed at various locations, including hospital wards, the resident hostel, medical college premises, and the college canteen. Exclusion criteria included those with illnesses, neurological or psychotic disorders, severe comorbidities, a history of drug use, and pregnancy.

Data on sociodemographic (age, gender, marital status, residence), lifestyle factors (fast-food intake, exercise habits, addictions), and BMI were recorded. QOL was assessed using the WHOQOL-BREF questionnaire,[9] which evaluates four domains: physical health, psychological health, social relationships, and environment, along with overall QOL and general health. Each item used a five-point Likert scale, with scores transformed to a 0–100 scale for analysis.

Statistical analysis included percentages and proportions for qualitative data, and mean (standard deviation [SD]) for quantitative data. Differences between the two means were evaluated using an unpaired t-test, while ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test analyzed differences among multiple groups. SPSS version 2023 (IBM SPSS Statistics version 29 (Armonk, New York, United States)) was used, with a significance threshold of P < 0.05.

RESULTS

A study of 50 resident doctors found that 58% were in their second year, 22% first year, and 20% third year. Most were under 30 years of age, male, unmarried, and from urban areas. Half had normal weight, while only one in ten was obese. Most exercised rarely, and about one in six reported regular exercise. More than half of the resident doctors consumed fast food at least once a week, while one-third rarely consumed it [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

This study assessed resident doctors’ QoL and its association with sociodemographic and lifestyle factors. Nearly half (48%) rated their overall QoL as “very satisfactory,” while 32% found it “satisfactory.” High satisfaction with general health was reported by 48%, with 30% moderately satisfied. Domain-wise, the highest scores were in environmental health (63.4 ± 18.5) and physical health (61.0 ± 18.4), while psychological health (38.9 ± 24.1) scored the lowest.

Globally, psychological health among residents is a concern. In India, 30.1% of residents experienced depression, and over 90% reported burnout.[6] During COVID-19, 69% in Kerala reported depression, and 59.5% had anxiety.[10] Contributing factors include long working hours, patient interactions, and lack of support from seniors,[6] and protective factors, like resilience and social support, mitigate distress.[10]

Sociodemographic factors significantly impact QoL. Female residents reported poorer physical and mental health, while unmarried residents showed lower social health scores. Younger residents were more affected psychologically. Similar trends were observed globally, with male residents reporting higher QoL in various domains, while married residents had better social health but lower physical and mental health.[111213] Likewise, a survey of Vietnam medical students found that age and academic year also impact QoL, with younger medical students showing heightened psychological distress.[14] Additionally, emotional exhaustion, a burnout component, negatively impacts physical and environmental QoL, while satisfaction with residency programs and living alone positively impacts overall QoL and psychological health.[15]

Lifestyle factors, particularly exercise, showed a positive influence. Residents who exercised often scored higher in social health, though BMI and fast-food consumption had no significant associations. Various research found that exercise is consistently linked to better physical, mental, and social well-being.[1617] The findings highlight the need for targeted interventions to improve mental health, promote exercise, and address gender and age-related disparities. Systemic changes, such as appropriate work hours and fostering supportive environments, are essential to enhance resident doctors’ QoL and overall well-being.[18]

Strengths and limitations

This pioneering study at our institute systematically evaluated the QoL among resident doctors, offering important insights into their well-being within a tertiary care hospital setting. A major strength of the study lay in its use of a validated questionnaire and in-person interviews, which enhanced the reliability, originality, and contextual relevance of the data collected.

However, some limitations were acknowledged. First, the reliance on self-reported data for variables, such as QoL, physical activity, and fast-food consumption, may have recall and social desirability bias. Second, the relatively small sample size may have limited the statistical power and affected the precision, and generalizability of the findings. Third, potential differences in QoL across various clinical specialities were not explored, despite likely variations in workload, shift patterns, and speciality-specific stressors. Future research should include larger, multicenter samples and conduct stratified or longitudinal studies to examine inter-speciality differences and for residency training.

CONCLUSIONS

The study highlights health and lifestyle challenges among mostly young, unmarried, urban male resident doctors. While over half reported satisfaction with their QoL, psychological health was lower than other health domains. Sociodemographic factors influenced health- married residents showed better social health, males had better physical health, and those over 30 years had better psychological health. Regular exercise improved social health, but most residents rarely exercised and frequently consumed fast food. Interestingly, residents who exercised “often” had better social health scores than those who exercised either “always” or “rarely,” suggesting moderate exercise may be optimal for social health.

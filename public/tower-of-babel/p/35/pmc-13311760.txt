Evolution of blood biomarkers reflective of brain tissue damage and inflammation in the perioperative course of intracerebral haemorrhage evacuation in a phase II proof-of-concept trial
Authors: Tim Jonas Hallenberger, Eline Willemse, Catherine Brégère, Nilabh Ghosh, Leo H Bonati, Aleksandra Maleska Maceski, Pascal Benkert, Urs Fischer, Jehuda Soleman, David Leppert, Raphael Guzman, Jens Kuhle
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractBackgroundWhether surgical haematoma evacuation leads to reduced brain damage in patients with spontaneous supratentorial intracerebral haemorrhage (SSICH) remains unknown. Blood biomarkers reflecting brain damage could aid in prognosticating the clinical outcome and hence support quantifying the benefit of surgical interventions.MethodsBlood samples from 10 patients with SSICH in the phase II early minimally invasive image-guided endoscopic evacuation of intracerebral hemorrhage (EMINENT-ICH) pilot study (NCT04805177, completed) were collected longitudinally (at baseline, 24 hours, 3 days, 1 week, 1 month and 6 months after surgical haematoma evacuation). Neurofilament light chain (NfL), glial fibrillar acidic protein (GFAP), calcium-binding protein S100B and the inflammatory markers interferon-gamma, interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10 and tumour necrosis factor alpha were quantified. The perioperative and long-term course of concentrations and association with clinical outcome measures were assessed. A favourable outcome was defined as a modified ranking scale ≤3.ResultsFavourable outcome was observed in 60% (6/10) of patients (median age 72.5 years (IQR 67.5–79.8), 70% male), and in 70% (7/10) of patients, the preset goal of a postsurgery haematoma volume below 15 mL was achieved. On average, patients with unfavourable outcomes at 6 months had twofold higher NfL and sixfold higher GFAP concentrations compared with those with favourable outcomes (pNfL=0.127, pGFAP=0.017); concentrations of both markers were higher in patients with unsatisfactory haematoma evacuation (3.7-fold for NfL, p=0.0072 and 12.4-fold for GFAP, p=0.0122). Concentrations of other markers, especially ILs, were not indicative of functional outcomes.ConclusionsBased on our results, we provide preliminary evidence that lower serum NfL and GFAP levels are associated with favourable clinical outcomes and successful haematoma evacuation in SSICH.Keywords: Neurosurgery, Stroke, Brain Injuries

Introduction

Spontaneous supratentorial intracerebral haemorrhage (SSICH) is the second most common form of stroke, with mortality rates reaching up to 50% and survivors being severely disabled.1 2 Brain damage in SSICH can be divided into two phases: primary damage by mechanical disruption of the brain tissue and a secondary phase, marked by oedema formation, inflammation and tissue scarring.3 Degradation of haemoglobin has a prominent molecular mechanism for oedema formation and inflammation in the latter phase.4 5 While the primary brain damage is mostly outside of therapeutic reach, secondary brain damage may be mitigated,3 5 but the lack of biomarkers to measure their impact on outcomes is a major limitation to identify and validate novel therapeutic approaches.

Poor outcomes in SSICH are associated with the volume and location of the haematoma,4,6 and its evacuation seems rational to improve functional outcomes and mortality rates.7 However, past trials failed to demonstrate any improvement of functional outcome in affected patients, either due to adverse events in the wake of the intervention itself or because the delay of haematoma evacuation missed out on the time window where it could have been beneficial.8,11 In support of this hypothesis, the Trial of Early Minimally Invasive Removal of Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ENRICH) has demonstrated the superiority of early (within 24 hours) surgical haematoma evacuation versus conservative management in improving functional outcome rates in lobar SSICH, while this was not the case for deep SSICH.12 13 Hence, whether haematoma evacuation in general leads to less secondary brain damage remains currently uncertain.5

Several biomarkers have been described to reflect brain damage, yet they are limited to single-point assessments for diagnostic/prognostic purposes within the acute stage; there is no data available where their longitudinal course in the subacute/chronic phase has been assessed14,18 and little is known about the post-perioperative evolution of these blood measures.14,34 This is a critical limitation for their use as predictors of the outcome of therapeutic interventions.

Glial fibrillar acidic protein (GFAP) and calcium-binding protein S100B (S100B) are astrocytic proteins that have been previously described as diagnostic blood-derived markers to differentiate between SSICH and ischaemic stroke,20,2327 as well as to prognosticate functional recovery and mortality rates in SSICH.14 18 20 22 23 35 Neurofilament light chain (NfL) specifically reflects neuronal damage and has recently been described as a prognostic marker for functional outcome and mortality rates in SSICH.31 34 For inflammation-related biomarkers, such as interleukins (ILs), referring results remain inconclusive or have not been tested in SSICH.14 15 17 18

The primary objective of this work was to assess whether perioperative and long-term course blood concentrations of biomarkers reflective of structural brain damage (NfL, GFAP and S100B) and of inflammation (interferon gamma (IFN-γ), interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10 and tumour necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α)) are associated with functional outcome, mortality and haematoma volume during 6 months’ follow-up after SSICH and whether they will be suitable to quantitate the benefit of therapeutic intervention.

Materials and methods

Patients, outcomes and study setting

This analysis took place within the phase II early minimally invasive image-guided endoscopic evacuation of intracerebral hemorrhage (EMINENT-ICH) pilot study, a single-centre, single-arm phase II pilot study assessing the feasibility of the study procedures for minimally invasive endoscopic haematoma evacuation.13 36 Patients were eligible for inclusion if they were between 18 and 85 years of age, presented with an SSICH volume between 20 and 100 mL, had a focal neurological deficit equalling at least a National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score of 8 or a Glasgow Coma Score (GCS) score decrease under 13 points and had no other organic/structural explanation for the underlying bleeding. Patients with brainstem or infratentorial bleedings and a historical modified Rankin Scale (mRS) of >2 points, as well as comatose patients (GCS ≤4), were excluded. Consecutive patients with deep or lobar SSICH admitted to the emergency department at the University Hospital Basel were included. Minimally invasive surgical intervention took place as described within 24 hours of symptom onset.37 Patients were followed for 6 months with clinical assessments, MRI and blood sampling at six timepoints: at baseline (BL), which was within 24 hours after symptom onset and before entering surgery, as well as 24 and 72 hours and 7 days (or if earlier, at discharge) after surgery, and during 1-month and 6-month follow-up. The EMINENT-ICH pilot trial had the assessment of a satisfactory haematoma evacuation (≤15 mL residual volume (binary, achieved satisfactory haematoma evacuation vs did not achieve satisfactory haematoma evacuation)) and a favourable clinical functional outcome after 6 months (mRS ≤3) as co-primary outcomes.

Secondary endpoints of this pilot trial were mortality, morbidity and median evacuation (ratio of blood volume evacuated between BL and postoperatively, measured by CT scans).

Blood sample handling, biomarker analysis and calculation of percentiles for NfL and GFAP

Samples were collected either by venous puncture or arterial drip into standard S-Monovette Serum Gel CAT/7.5 mL (Ref no. 01.1602.001) for NfL, GFAP, IFN-γ, TNF-α and ILs (IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8 and IL-10) and with S-Monovette Li Heparin Gel LH/4.7 mL (Ref no. 03.1631.100) from Sarstedt (Nümbrecht, Germany) for S100B. Samples were then centrifuged at 2200 g for 10 min, and supernatants were aliquoted and stored at –80°C; processing time was ≤2 hours.

Analysis of NfL and GFAP was performed using the Neuro 2-plex B assay on the Simoa HD-X analyser (Quanterix, Massachusetts, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Samples were measured in duplicate. Intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation were 3.7 and 5.2% for NfL and 4.5 and 7.2% for GFAP, respectively. NfL and GFAP values were corrected for age (sex: only GFAP) and body mass index (BMI) using large reference databases of healthy controls (HC)38 39 and percentiles calculated that express the percentage of the reference population having a lower concentration.

Concentrations of ILs and S100B were determined using the Meso Scale Discovery platform (V-PLEX Proinflammatory Panel 1 Human Kit and R-PLEX Human S100B Assay) according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Meso Scale Discovery, Maryland, USA). Samples were measured in duplicate. The corresponding mean limit of detections (LODs) in pg/mL for the ILs were 0.82 for IFN-γ, 0.08 for IL-1β, 0.31 for IL-2, 0.4 for IL-6, 0.06 for IL-8, 0.05 for IL-10 and 0.12 for TNF-α. The mean LOD for S100B was 1.54 pg/mL.

Statistics

Demographic and clinical characteristics were described using median (IQR) values and proportions. All analyses in this pilot study were of an explorative nature and aimed to generate hypotheses for a follow-up trial. In this context, the presentation of p-values is intended to indicate the magnitude of effects rather than statistical significance, since we here use available samples from an explorative study rather than a predefined analysis with a calculated sample size.

Wilcoxon differences in biomarker levels at individual timepoints were visualised by boxplots and compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Graphical representations of longitudinal biomarker levels stratified by functional outcome or successful haematoma evacuation were constructed using the ‘geom_smooth’ function in R (package ‘ggplot2’). Correlation analysis was performed for all biomarker concentrations using Spearman’s rho coefficient. Mixed effects models were used to model longitudinal biomarker levels with participant-specific random intercepts to account for the repeated data structure. Log-transformed biomarker concentrations (using all time points) served as the dependent variable, while 6-month functional outcome (mRS ≤3 vs mRS >3) or successful haematoma removal was included as the independent variable, adjusted for age (but not time). Estimates were back-transformed to represent the percentage change. Continuous variables were tested for functional outcome or successful haematoma removal using the Wilcoxon signed rank test.

Statistical analysis was performed using R statistical software (The R Project, V.4.3.3), and anonymised data are available with the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of our research.

Results

Patient characteristics

Ten participants (median age 72.5 (IQR 67.5–79.8) years, 70% male) were included between July 2021 and January 2023 at the University Hospital Basel (table 1).13 We observed 60% (6/10) of patients with basal ganglia haemorrhage and four with lobar haemorrhages, the median presurgical haematoma volume was 34.1 (26.1–54.7) mL, and the median BL mRS was 4 (4–4.8) points. Patients were moderately impaired at BL with a median NIHSS score of 11 (10–16), and a GCS of 13 (11.2–14). No participants showed evidence of ischaemic stroke on MRI. The median time to haematoma evacuation was 18.5 (14.1–21.1) hours after symptom onset, with a median time to blood sampling at BL of 14.3 (5.6–15.7) hours. Satisfactory reduction of haematoma volume (≤15 mL) was achieved in 70% (7/10) with a median haematoma evacuation rate of 69.5% (53.3%–93.6%). Favourable outcome at 6 months was observed in 60% (6/10) of patients (table 1). Satisfactory haematoma evacuation was mostly associated with favourable 6 months functional outcomes (6/7, 86%; online supplemental table 1). We observed two rebleeding episodes, two pneumonias and one epileptic seizure in five patients. Three participants died due to the underlying disease of SSICH or glioblastoma in one case; hence, five samples for different visits were missing.

At BL, the median concentrations of NfL and GFAP were 23.7 (20.4–26.8) pg/mL and 13 234 (4507–49059) pg/mL, respectively, corresponding to the 92nd and >99.9 percentiles. BL concentrations for the remaining biomarkers are presented in table 1.

Biomarker associations with clinical outcome

Patients with unfavourable outcome at 6 months had numerically 2.1-fold higher NfL levels over time compared with those with a favourable outcome (estimate=2.09, 95% CI 0.97 to 4.50, p=0.127; figure 2). NfL concentrations at BL were higher in patients with unfavourable outcomes (p=0.038, online supplemental figure 2; table 2). NfL concentrations were numerically higher at all other timepoints in those with unfavourable outcomes.

Patients with unfavourable outcomes showed almost sixfold higher GFAP concentrations over time compared with favourable outcomes (estimate=5.93, 95% CI 1.47 to 23.98, p=0.017; figure 2). Similar to NfL, GFAP concentrations were numerically higher at all timepoints in those with unfavourable outcomes (online supplemental figure 3 and table 2). None of the other biomarkers was associated with functional outcome at 6 months (online supplemental figure 1).

Biomarker associations with haematoma volume and satisfactory haematoma evacuation

NfL levels did not correlate with haematoma volumes at BL (ρ=0.41, p=0.25) but showed a strong trend to be lower at 72 hours and 1 week in patients with satisfactory versus unsuccessful haematoma evacuation: 72 hours: 64.6 (55.2–139.9) versus 259.2 (179.2–268.4) pg/mL, p=0.067; at 1 week: 228.6 (115.4–249.3) vs 484.6 (392.4–627.7) pg/mL, p=0.067 (online supplemental figure 4). Conversely, taking all samples of timepoints after surgery together (ie, excluding BL), NfL concentrations were 3.7-fold higher in patients with unsuccessful haematoma evacuation (estimate=3.74, 95% CI 1.92 to 7.39, p=0.0072; online supplemental figure 5).

At BL, GFAP showed a trend for a correlation with haematoma volume (ρ=0.58, p=0.09). Moreover, GFAP concentrations were lower in patients with satisfactory haematoma evacuation: at 72 hours: 24 638 (22 846–44 043) versus 13 2356 (98 565–133 116) pg/mL, p=0.017; at 1 week: 6035 (2 622–12 573) versus 35 142 (34 474–35 567) pg/mL, p=0.017 (online supplemental figure 4) and were 12.4-fold higher in patients with unsuccessful haematoma evacuation (estimate=12.39, 95% CI 1.93 to 79.39, p=0.0122; online supplemental figure 5).

S100B concentrations at BL were correlated with BL haematoma volume (ρ=0.71, p=0.03), but not with satisfactory haematoma evacuation.

The other biomarkers’ concentrations were not associated with presurgical or postsurgical haematoma volumes.

Discussion

We here leveraged blood biomarkers for their capacity to reflect individual long-term functional outcome and haematoma volumes presurgically and postsurgically in the EMINENT phase II pilot study.13 In SSICH, NfL and GFAP levels discriminated for favourable versus unfavourable functional outcomes and satisfactory versus unsuccessful haematoma evacuation. NfL and GFAP levels were manyfold lower over the entire course of observation in subjects with favourable outcomes at 6 months than in those with poor outcomes. These findings provide additional biomarker-based evidence for the benefit of the surgical intervention at stake, as the postinterventional courses of NfL and GFAP were predictive of the degree of success of the surgical intervention. They provide a preliminary basis for their use in clinical studies and potentially individual use. However, since differences were small (unfavourable vs favourable), and no cut-off values are yet established, an intraindividual comparison of presurgical versus postsurgical measurements may provide higher predictive accuracy than single measurements. In contrast to traumatic brain injury (TBI),40 S100B lacked prognostic capacity for the outcome of SSICH.

The increase and the kinetics of GFAP and NfL in SSICH are congruent with observations by others in TBI41 or cerebral haemorrhage.31 NfL reached peak levels after 1 month after injury and did not normalise until 6 months later, indicative of sustained secondary axonal destruction.4 42

GFAP had a much shorter dynamic change with a strong increase already at BL, ceiling levels between 24–72 days and close to normalisation until month 6. GFAP has been described as a diagnostic marker to differentiate between SSICH and ischaemic stroke. Moreover, GFAP has gained further interest as a prognostic biomarker for secondary damage in SSICH14 15 27 43 and has recently been highlighted as a point-of-care test to help identify ICH from ischaemic stroke and stroke mimics.44 45

S100B levels peaked within the first day after SSICH, that is, earlier than GFAP, but with an earlier normalisation, in line with findings in TBI.46 Increased S100B concentrations have been associated with poor functional outcomes and increased mortality in the past.16 18 20 23 The rapid dynamic change of S100B, and hence the variability of levels in between samples, and methodology differences may explain this discrepancy, despite the strong correlation with those of GFAP and may decrease the accuracy of correlation with clinical outcome measures as compared with NfL and GFAP. In essence, S100B did not provide additional information beyond the latter two markers. Accordingly, despite being part of the inflammatory cascade in secondary brain damage and some correlations with NfL and sGFAP levels reflecting the molecular response to brain damage in SSICH, the inflammatory markers ILs, IFN-γ and TNF-α do not seem to convey additional value as biomarkers for the functional outcome in SSICH.

Given the pathophysiology of secondary brain damage, it is not surprising that increased concentrations of NfL and GFAP reflect more pronounced injury and consequently prognosticate poorer functional outcome.4 5 However, their predictive capacity, that is, for the beneficial effect of surgical haematoma removal, has not been explored. The improved functional outcome by surgical haematoma removal in the ENRICH trial,36 and the correlation of NfL and GFAP in the present results now provide preliminary evidence for their use as predictive biomarkers on the group level. However, the profile of individual patients who may profit best from surgical haematoma evacuation needs to be explored, a point where biomarkers may be helpful in identifying suitable patients.47 Given the pathophysiological nature of SSICH, patients with advanced age, larger haematoma volumes and SSICHs in eloquent locations have poorer outcomes and higher biomarker concentrations, as previously demonstrated in studies assessing biomarker levels for SSICH.17 18 23 31 34 35 48 A critical advantage of the ongoing EMINENT-ICH trial is that it is the first study assessing biomarker levels without positive selection bias, including a broad spectrum of patients better reflecting the overall ICH population.14 15 17 18 34

Limitations

The assays used were validated solely for research purposes and have yet to be approved for clinical use. However, serum GFAP, NfL and S100B can be increasingly measured on automated platforms using validated methods.49

Our study was of an exploratory nature and hypothesis-generating, performed on a small sample set without formal sample size calculation, also preventing multiple statistical adjustments. Consequently, potentially important confounders, including age and other clinical characteristics, could only be addressed in a limited manner. Furthermore, the complex interplay between biomarker concentrations, timing of surgery, haematoma volume and location, comorbidities and both favourable and unfavourable functional outcomes after haematoma evacuation could not be fully disentangled in the present analysis. In addition, a favourable outcome was defined as mRS ≤3 to reflect clinically meaningful recovery in this severely affected ICH cohort, although we acknowledge that mRS ≤2 represents a stricter and more conventional threshold. These aspects require validation in larger prospective cohorts and controlled interventional studies, a task we pursue in the follow-up EMINENT trial (NCT: NCT05681988), which compares surgical intervention and conservative therapy in SSICH.

Conclusion

To our knowledge, these findings provide the first evidence bridging clinical outcomes with paraclinical biomarkers in the assessment of early minimally invasive endoscopic haematoma removal in SSICH. Serum concentrations of both NfL and GFAP were associated with unfavourable outcomes and showed a direct relationship with the extent of haematoma evacuation. These measures may therefore support improved patient selection and longitudinal monitoring in future interventional strategies. The clinical utility and prognostic value of these observations will be prospectively evaluated in the ongoing randomised controlled EMINENT-ICH trial.

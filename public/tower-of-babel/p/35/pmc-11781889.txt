Introduction

New cardiac arrhythmias after transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) surgery are not uncommon, with atrioventricular block (AVB) being the most prevalent type. However, a particular form of ventricular tachycardia, known as bundle branch reentry ventricular tachycardia (BBRVT), has gradually emerged as an unusual arrhythmia following TAVR. We present a case study of a delayed onset of BBRVT in a patient who initially manifested with left anterior fascicular block post-TAVR.

Case presentations

A 65-year-old man who had undergone transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) with a 23-mm Sapien valve (Edwards Lifesciences, Irvine, CA) because of severe aortic regurgitation presented 1 year later to our hospital reporting palpitations and syncope. An immediate electrocardiogram (ECG) revealed a wide QRS complex tachycardia (182 beats/min) featuring a left bundle branch block (LBBB) pattern, a left inferior axis, and a precordial transition to positivity at lead V5 (Figure 1A). The ECG recorded during sinus rhythm showed a first-degree AVB with a PR interval of 300 ms, along with a left anterior fascicular block (LAFB), indicative of damage to the infrahisian conduction system or the His-Purkinje system (HPS) subsequent to TAVR (Figure 1B). Nonetheless, the PR interval remains unchanged without LAFB during the initial 6-month follow-up post-TAVR. Cardiac electrophysiological studies and catheter ablation were performed using the CARTO-3 system (Johnson & Johnson Medtech, New Brunswick, NJ). A quadripolar electrode and a decapolar catheter (both from Johnson & Johnson Medtech) were inserted into the ventricular apex and coronary sinus, respectively. During activation mapping of the ventricular tachycardia (VT), the earliest activation site was identified in the right bundle branch (RBB), preceding the QRS complex by 20 ms. Additionally, HH interval variation was predictive of VV interval variation (Figure 1C). BBRVT was diagnosed based on the characteristic ECG pattern of LBBB observed during VT and the presence of RBB potential preceding each ventricular signal. Intracardiac echocardiography (ICE) was introduced via the left femoral vein. The artificial aortic valve and the His bundle branch were in close proximity, with a mere 12 mm separating them (Figure 1D). Entrainment pacing was not performed because of the unstable hemodynamical status. We targeted the RBB as the ablation site. VT terminated within the first 2 seconds of ablating the RBB using an irrigated Thermocool ablation catheter with a diameter of 3.5 mm (Biosense Webster Inc, Irvine, CA). The ablation protocol was set with an upper temperature limit of 45°C, radiofrequency power ranging from 30 W to 40 W, and an irrigation flow rate of 17–30 mL/min. The ablation procedure was continued until a right bundle branch block (RBBB) developed, with the PR interval prolonging from 300 ms (Figure 1E) to 360 ms (Figure 1F) and the QRS duration increasing from 128 ms (LAFB) to 173 ms (complete RBBB with LAFB). After ventricular stimulation after isoproterenol administration, no arrhythmia was induced. The patient remained free of arrhythmia, syncope complaints, and the need for a pacemaker during the 9-month follow-up period.

Discussion

After TAVR surgery, arrhythmia is a common occurrence.1 However, de novo wide QRS complex tachycardia after TAVR is rare.2 BBRVT typically occurs in patients with progression injury to the HPS. Possibly valve expansion may affect the membranous septum, thereby providing an anatomical substrate for the development of BBRVT.

The most frequently compromised site by the prosthetic valve is the infrahisian conduction system, resulting in RBBB or LBBB. In our patient, LAFB, as an indicator of HPS injury, was observed after TAVR, which might be a delayed conduction site to construct the VT circuit. Our study showed a narrow anatomical separation of 12 mm between the implanted artificial aortic valve and the HPS using ICE, which implies an elevated risk of HPS damage post-TAVR when this distance falls below 15 mm.

Catheter ablation targeting the right bundle branch (RBB) is the favored therapeutic approach for managing BBRVT. The necessity for a permanent pacemaker hinges on the severity of LBBB. In the case of our patient, a permanent pacemaker was deemed unnecessary, because no complete atrioventricular block (AVB) emerged post-ablation. Conversely, in another instance of BBRVT accompanied by complete AVB, a permanent pacemaker was implanted.2

We presented a rare case of delayed BBRVT occurring in a patient 1 year after undergoing TAVR. This case serves as a reminder of the potential for long-term damage to the His-Purkinje system by the artificial valve, highlighting the importance of long-term follow-up after TAVR, particularly in terms of cardiac conduction system injury.

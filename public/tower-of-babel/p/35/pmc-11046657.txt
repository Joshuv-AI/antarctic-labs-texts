Treatment response to intramuscular steroids in patients living with rheumatoid arthritis: an exploratory analysis using tracked daily symptoms
Authors: Mariam Al-Attar, Julie Gandrup, Sabine van der Veer, William Dixon
Published: 2023

Introduction

Current understanding of treatment response is largely based on patient recollection at infrequent clinic appointments, which introduces inaccuracies through recall error. Real-time patient-reported data collected frequently following an intervention could improve our understanding of treatment response, facilitating better clinical decision-making. This exploratory analysis reports a case-series of patients who used the Remote Monitoring of Rheumatoid Arthritis (REMORA) smartphone-app for daily symptom tracking, and their longitudinal pain-scores following intramuscular (IM) steroid injections.

Materials and methods

We identified patients participating in the REMORA study in the period November 2021 – September 2022 who received an IM steroid injection. Concomitant disease-modifying medications were noted. Daily pain-scores (out of 10) from 10 days preceding the injection and 8 weeks following were analysed. Results and discussion

32 patients were identified for inclusion, of whom six received ≥1 steroid injections. In total nine injections were given. Pre-injection pain-scores ranged 3.33–9.29. Seven injections demonstrated response, two did not. Of the responders, duration ranged 1–54 days (median 9 days); average pain-score improvement ranged 0.14–5.33 (median 3.33); maximum pain-score improvement ranged 0.14–7.00 (median 4.33). The patterns illustrated in Fig 1 raise further questions about how IM steroids should be used in rheumatology practice. In these preliminary results, response duration was shorter and pain-score improvement was smaller than anticipated.

Conclusion

Smartphone-based remote monitoring has the potential to enhance the care of people living with rheumatoid arthritis. In this exploratory analysis, we have demonstrated a novel means to accurately assess treatment response using real-time tracked symptom data. The duration and extent of response to steroid injections was quantified for all participants, data which is typically imprecise and relies on recall. Analysis of tracked symptom data across a larger population could help to identify the characteristics of treatment responders in order to support more targeted therapeutic strategies. This has significant implications for high-cost drugs such as biologics. Further work will be required to develop consensus definitions for treatment response and disease activity in time-series data, and a means to account for the effects of concomitant drugs.

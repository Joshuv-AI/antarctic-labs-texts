Bibliometric analysis of Kampo medicine hotspots and trends for the decade: 2013–2022
Authors: Toshihisa Onoda, Hiroyuki Tanaka, Toshihiro Ishii
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Kampo medicine is a traditional medicine that originated in ancient China and has since developed as a uniquely Japanese medicine. Although Kampo medicine is one of Japan’s most important therapeutic modalities and numerous papers have been published recently, information on current hotspots and trends in Kampo research is lacking. This bibliometric analysis of Kampo medicine surveyed the latest research hotspots and trends.Methods:Articles on Kampo medicine were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. We used medical subject headings related to Kampo medicine and searched for publications from 2013 to 2022. The retrieved articles were analyzed for countries, authors, journals, references, and keywords related to Kampo medicine using CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and SCImago Graphica.Results:A total of 1170 articles were included. The number of Kampo medicine-related publications and citations has recently increased, mainly from Japan. Author Keiko Ogawa-Ochiai published the most papers (40 papers), while Yoshio Kase had the highest frequency at 663 citations. Among the co-cited authors, Toru Kono was the most cited and had the highest total link strength. The journal with the most submissions was Evidence-based Complementary and Alternative Medicine. A comprehensive keyword and literature analysis revealed the following research hotspots: “Yokukansan and behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia,” “Ninjinyoeito and geriatric care,” “Daikenchuto and postoperative gastrointestinal cancer,” and “Rikkunshito and functional dyspepsia.” We also identified a new research frontier by identifying an association between hochuekkito and COVID-19.Conclusions:Our findings reveal trends in Kampo medicine research, with specific hotspots and the authors and publications with the largest research impact. Collecting a large volume of literature data, analyzing the impact of studies, and identifying research hotspots, as in this study, will provide researchers with future directions for Kampo research.

1. Introduction

Kampo medicine is a traditional medicine that originated in ancient China and was introduced to Japan in the 6th century AD, developing into a uniquely Japanese field of medicine.[1] Kampo medicines were first listed on Japan’s National Health Insurance drug price list in 1967, and 148 types of Kampo medicines are currently available.[2] Today, Kampo medicine is one of Japan’s most important treatment modalities, with 80% of Japanese physicians experienced in prescribing Kampo extract preparations alone or in combination with Western medicines.[3] The use of Kampo medicine is based on diagnosis by “Sho,”[1] but component analysis and systematic reviews of Kampo medicines have now been conducted, and much modern medical evidence has been reported.[4,5]

Bibliometrics was introduced in 1969 as an approach that combines the quantitative analysis of the elements that constitute an article or journal with data visualization.[6] By analyzing a large number of references in a specific research area, it is possible to identify relationships among research targets in the literature and hotspots in a particular time period and predict trends in reporting in a particular area.[7] Bibliometric analysis has been widely used to identify trends in various medical fields, including diseases, drugs, vaccines, and even acupuncture.[8–11]

Although Kampo medicine is currently building its evidence base with increased publications, to the best of our knowledge, bibliometric studies of recent Kampo medicine research are lacking. Therefore, this study conducted a bibliometric analysis of articles on Kampo medicine published from 2013 to 2022 to identify the latest research hotspots and global trends.

2. Methods

2.1. Data sources and collection

Articles with the highest number of citations have a significant impact on the world of Kampo medicine research. A bibliometric analysis method was employed to find the most cited articles.[12]

Web of Science (WoS) is considered the most suitable database for bibliometric analysis because it indexes every article and citation from every journal and can create extensive citation networks.[13] We searched for a number of publications related to Kampo medicine from the WoS Core Collection, including versions of SCI-EXPANDED (1970–present), CPCI-S (1996–present), CPCI-SSH (1996–present), BKCI-S (2005–present), BKCI-SSH (2005–present), ESCI (2005–present), CCR-EXPANDED (1985–present), and IC (1993–present) on April 26, 2023.

The search strategy used medical subject headings related to Kampo medicine, with the following search keywords: “Medicine, Kampo” or “Kampo Medicine” or “Kampo” or “Medicine, Kanpo” or “Kanpo Medicine” or “Kanpo.” The search began in January 2023 and ended in February 2023. In order to focus on the most current search information, the search period was set over 10 years, from 2013 to 2022. The types of documents were limited to articles and the languages were limited to English and Japanese.

The search yielded 1516 publications, of which 346 articles were excluded based on the following criteria: non-English/non-Japanese publications, review articles, meeting abstracts, letters, editorial materials, proceedings papers, book chapters, corrections, data papers, early access, and book reviews. Thus, a total of 1170 articles were included in the analysis and saved in a text file format (see Fig. 1).

Research design of literature selection.

2.2. Data analysis and tools

VOSviewer (version 1.6.19), SCImago Graphica (beta version 1.0.35), and CiteSpace (version 6.2. R2) were used for the visual analysis in this study. VOSviewer is a software tool used to build and visualize bibliometric networks. Various networks can be constructed based on citation, co-citation, and co-authorship relationships. It also has a text-mining function for constructing and visualizing keyword co-occurrence networks, allowing for a comprehensive understanding of the facts and development of a particular study.[14,15]

SCImago Graphica is a novel data visualization authoring tool that can be used for both visual data communication and exploratory analysis. Visualization can be created with simple drag-and-drop operations.[16]

Similar to VOSviewer, CiteSpace is a tool for comprehensive literature and visualization analysis of development trends and is used in scientific and technical fields such as medicine, agriculture, and geography. CiteSpace is particularly useful for depicting the evolutionary trends of each research topic over time and for understanding when hotspots and turning points occur in a particular research field.[17]

All the data were completely cleaned prior to analysis. Words with the same meaning were unified, and regions of a country were attributed to the same country. Trends in the number of papers published and cited in recent decades were analyzed using Microsoft Office Excel 2019.

Trends for the countries and regions where papers were published were visually analyzed by extracting VOSviewer data and importing them into SCImago Graphica in a graph modeling language format.

coauthors, co-cited authors, journals, co-cited journals, and keywords were analyzed using VOSviewer, and co-occurrence network graphs were created. The visualization maps of coauthors, co-cited authors, journals, co-cited journals, and keywords were introduced into the software for the analysis of papers with a number of at least 20, 40, 5, 100, and 10 articles, respectively. Quartiles of journal impact factor (IF) and journal citation report (JCR) categories published in 2023 were obtained for journals and co-cited journals from WoS. Co-cited references and reference bursts were analyzed using CiteSpace.

3. Results

3.1. Yearly changes in publications and citations

A total of 1170 articles were analyzed using the bibliometric search strategy. Annual global trends in the number of articles published in recent years reflect the level of attention given to research in each field. The number of articles on and citations for Kampo medicine showed an increasing global trend over 10 years, from 2013 to 2022 (see Fig. 2).

Japan (1070 articles, 91.45%) was the most frequently reported country, followed by China (55 articles, 4.70%) and the United States (30 articles, 2.56%) (see Table 1). Japan was also the most cited (9002) and had the strongest total link strength (TLS = 101). This indicates that Japan has the closest cooperation with other countries, followed by China with a TLS of 39 and the United States with a TLS of 29. 4. Discussion

We searched the WoS Core Collection and conducted a bibliometric analysis of Kampo medicine over the past decade, summarizing the general information and research hotspots in the field. The search yielded 1170 references, showing a global increase in articles on Kampo medicine in the last decade (2013–2022), indicating a gradual increase in attention to Kampo medicine.

4.1. General information

The country and region distribution indicates that Japan, the birthplace of Kampo medicine, is the leading country in Kampo research. Nonetheless, evidence from the growing body of literature on Kampo medicine has steadily attracted the interest of researchers beyond Japan, in China, the United States, and other countries worldwide. However, the number of reports outside Japan is small, and we argue that it is necessary to increase the number of reports from other countries and strengthen cooperation with them to promote Kampo research further.

The most frequent author contributions were by Ogawa-Ochiai, who mainly discusses clinical reports and diagnoses in Kampo medicine.[18,19] Makino, who had the next largest number of reports, discusses elucidating the mechanisms of action of various Kampo medicines, focusing on basic experiments.[20,21] The most-cited author was Kase, whose paper on the mechanism of action of daikenchuto compounds, which considered their additive, synergistic effects, was the most cited.[22] Kampo medicine was developed in Japan, and Japanese authors have undoubtedly made significant contributions to this research area. However, the relationships among authors are distributed among small collaborative networks, and collaboration across different research groups is lacking.

The network of co-cited authors was considerably denser than the authors. The 4 most frequently co-cited authors indicate a high level of academic influence in Kampo research. Kono, in particular, has had a significant impact on the study of Kampo medicine regarding daikenchuto and the side effects of cancer chemotherapy.[23–26]

The journals where the extracted papers were submitted did not have very high IF scores in terms of the top 10 journals, and more high-quality research is needed in this area. Unlike Western medicine, the development of Kampo medicine in Japan has not gained wider prominence outside of the region.[1] However, several articles have been published in the Journal of Ethnopharmacology, a Q1 JCR journal in the interdisciplinary fields of CHEMISTRY, MEDICINAL, INTEGRATIVE & COMPLEMENTARY MEDICINE, PHARMACOLOGY & PHARMACY, and PLANT SCIENCE, and in Frontiers in Pharmacology, a JCR Q1 journal of PHARMACOLOGY & PHARMACY. Kampo medicine topics are expected to be published in journals with a high IF in the future.

4.2. Research hotspots and frontiers

The analysis of frequently used keywords reflects the hotspots of Kampo research. Five Kampo medicines received particular attention in the keyword analysis. This study focused on 5 Kampo medicines to determine their relationship to the cited literature: yokukansan, ninjinyoeito, daikenchuto, hochuekkito, and rikkunshito.

4.2.1. Yokukansan.

Yokukansan is approved in Japan for treating insomnia, neurosis, and infantile night terrors.[27] Recent clinical studies have reported that yokukansan can help treat behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD).[28] The study with the most reference bursts was a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials on BPSD treatment with yokukansan.[29] The guidelines recommend the use of antipsychotics, such as risperidone, for BPSD; however, due to side effects, tapering or discontinuation is recommended when symptoms improve or when serious side effects develop.[30] The lack of specific long-term treatments for BPSD may explain why yokukansan is highly rated for BPSD treatment. This is supported by the fact that several studies on yokukansan’s effects on BPSD, aggressiveness, and sociality were included in the high reference burst of this study.[31–34]

Furthermore, a keyword network analysis revealed connections between yokukansan and BPSD-related keywords, indicating that BPSD is a yokukansan research hotspot. It also showed associations between yokukansan and oxidative stress. In 2022, Inoue et al[35] reported that the antioxidant effect of yokukansan is effective against Hunner-type interstitial cystitis, an intractable disease in Japan. Although previous research has focused on neuropsychiatric disorders, new research on urological disorders is expected in the future.

4.2.2. Ninjinyoeito.

Ninjinyoeito is a tonic used to treat fatigue and anorexia in individuals with low physical strength.[36] In modern medicine, frailty was defined in 2014 by the Japan Geriatrics Society as a condition between infirmity and robust health, similar to the concept of “unwellness” in Kampo medicine, and is a major problem, especially in older people.[37] Ninjinyoeito was noted among its superior therapeutic drugs.[38] A study titled “Clinical Practice Guidelines and Evidence for the Efficacy of Traditional Japanese Herbal Medicine (Kampo) in Treating Geriatric Patients”[39] represents the range of reference bursts through 2022, indicating that research on the effects of Kampo medicine on older people is one of the current Kampo research hotspots, including ninjinyoeito. Although few reports on ninjinyoeito have been published recently, ninjinyoeito is a Kampo medicine that has attracted considerable attention, and many papers summarizing evidence from basic and clinical research on it have been cited.[40]

A keyword network analysis revealed that ninjinyoeito was associated with chemotherapy and peripheral neuropathy. In 2020, a randomized controlled trial by Motoo et al[41] reported the efficacy of ninjinyoeito for peripheral neuropathy in oxaliplatin-treated patients. Also, in 2020, Aomatsu et al[42] reported the efficacy of ninjinyoeito in treating adverse events in combination therapy with capecitabine and oxaliplatin. Thus, ninjinyoeito has recently attracted research attention as a therapeutic agent for the adverse effects of cancer chemotherapy, representing a new research focus other than as a tonic.

4.2.3. Daikenchuto.

Daikenchuto is a typical Kampo medicine for gastrointestinal disorders, such as abdominal pain, distention, diarrhea, and constipation.[43] Daikenchuto is among Japan’s top 20 most frequently used 148 Kampo medicines.[44] Several recent clinical studies have reported the efficacy of daikenchuto in treating postoperative functional impairment in gastrointestinal cancer.[45,46] Including a paper on daikenchuto for postoperative hepatocellular carcinoma in a high reference burst suggests that the effect of daikenchuto on postoperative digestive cancers is a current research hotspot.[47] In addition to clinical studies, recent high-profile papers have included kinetic and mechanism-of-action analyses.[48,49] Kono et al[49] reported a novel mechanism mediated by adrenomedullin in the transient receptor potential channel of daikenchuto, while Endo et al[50] reported a nicotinic acetylcholine receptor-mediated mechanism.

The fact that the inflammatory and anti-inflammatory effects associated with these studies were also associated with daikenchuto in the keyword network analysis suggests that the accumulation of basic scientific evidence for daikenchuto will increase its popularity in future research.

4.2.4. Hochuekkito.

Hochuekkito is a tonic used for those suffering from fatigue and malaise due to poor gastrointestinal function.[51] Recent reports include a 2022 systematic review by Shahid et al[52] on the efficacy of hochuekkito for male infertility and a report on hochuekkito’s mechanism of action involving atrogenes and 5-adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase.[53] However, a negative report showed that hochuekkito did not increase the efficacy of S-1 postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy for advanced gastric cancer.[54] There were no highly referenced burst papers in this study.

The keyword network analysis showed a relationship with COVID-19 and reported that hochuekkito was most frequently prescribed as a treatment for general malaise caused by long-term COVID-19 infection,[55] indicating its high profile as a nourishing tonic. COVID-19 is a serious viral infection that continues to have a global impact. Treating general malaise caused by COVID-19 using hochuekkito requires further investigation.

4.2.5. Rikkunshito.

Rikkunshito is a Kampo medicine used to treat gastrointestinal symptoms such as gastritis, stomach pain, and indigestion.[56] A recent systematic review demonstrated the efficacy of rikkunshito for functional dyspepsia.[57] The 2 papers with the highest number of reference bursts in this study were also related to rikkunshito in functional dyspepsia, indicating that both represent current research hotspots.[58,59] In addition, a study on cancer-related ghrelin signaling-mediated anorexia also has a high number of citations,[60] and the word “chemotherapy” was associated with rikkunshito in the network analysis, indicating that rikkunshito in the cancer field is receiving attention.

4.3. Limitations

There are several limitations to this study. First, this study used the WoS Core Collection database, frequently used for bibliometric analysis; however, other databases, such as PubMed, could potentially cover a wider range. Second, VOSviewer, and CiteSpace provide primarily quantitative analyses of terms in the literature, with slightly different results depending on the calculation method. Third, the limited number of languages (English and Japanese language) results and differences in quality between the studies may affect the reliability of the results. Nonetheless, the visual analysis based on the literature presented in this study lays the foundation for understanding research hotspots and trends in the field of Kampo medicine.

5. Future perspectives

This study identified global trends in Kampo research over the past decade, particularly highlighting notable research hotspots and frontiers. The number of articles on Kampo medicine has increased over time, confirming its growing field. Kampo medicine, originally developed in Japan, has the highest number of publications in Japan. However, international cooperation is expected in the future. Recently, the focus of Kampo research has shifted from the traditional use of Kampo medicines to the accumulation of new uses and evidence, thereby exploring new possibilities for Kampo medicine. Currently, many clinical reports are found on the potential indications for new areas of Kampo medicine. To support these indications, further basic scientific analysis, including the investigation of the mechanism of action, as well as further clinical results are necessary. Collecting large amounts of literature data, analyzing the impact of studies, and identifying research hotspots, as in this study, will provide researchers with future directions for Kampo research. Particularly, we extracted Kampo medicines that have received much attention in recent years, indicating recent hotspots and potential new clinical applications in the future.

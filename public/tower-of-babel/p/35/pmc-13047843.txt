The complete mitochondrial genome of Schistura rara Zhu and Cao 1987 (Nemacheilidae)
Authors: Weide Deng, Huiling Shen, Chunxia Li, Xuan Cai, Youhui Luo, Shengxin Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSchistura rara, a small stream-dwelling fish of the family Nemacheilidae, is endemic to headwater streams of the Beijiang River in Shaoguan, China. Here, we report the first complete mitochondrial genome (mitogenome) of S. rara, which is 16,563 bp in length and contains 13 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 22 tRNA genes, two rRNA genes, and a single non-coding control region (D-loop). The mitogenome has a GC content of 42.8% and shows a clear bias toward A/C-ending codons in PCGs. Maximum-likelihood (ML) phylogenetic analyses based on complete mitogenome sequences indicate that Schistura is non-monophyletic. Within this framework, S. rara is recovered as the sister species to S. incerta and, together with one lineage of S. fasciolata, forms a well-supported clade. These three species are the only Schistura taxa recorded from the upper Beijiang River. The newly generated mitogenomic data provide an important genetic resource for clarifying the phylogenetic position and taxonomic status of S. rara within Nemacheilidae.

Introduction

Schistura rara (Nemacheilidae) is a small, stream-dwelling hillstream loach, reaching a maximum length of 80 mm (Zou et al. 2018b). It inhabits the upper reaches of the Beijiang River in Shaoguan, China, typically occurring in clear, fast-flowing streams with stony substrates. The species has an elongated body, slightly cylindrical anteriorly and laterally compressed posteriorly, with small, dorsally positioned eyes. It possesses three pairs of barbels, including two rostral pairs and one maxillary pair. Morphologically, S. rara closely resembles S. fasciolata but can be distinguished by the number and density of lateral stripes (21–27 in S. rara versus 10–16 in S. fasciolata) (Pearl River Fisheries Research Institute 1991).

S. rara was first collected in October 1984 from the Nanshui River in Shaoguan and was subsequently recorded from the Qujiang River (Zhu and Cao 1987). Subsequent surveys have documented its presence in several tributaries of the Beijiang River, including the Lianjiang River and, more recently, the Datan, Luokeng, and Longgui rivers in Shaoguan (Li and Yang 1998; Li and Lin 2010; Zou et al. 2018a; Zeng et al. 2023). These records indicate that S. rara is restricted to the upstream basin of the Beijiang River, a major tributary of the Pearl River. Despite its clear morphological diagnosis and well-defined distribution range, the evolutionary relationships of S. rara remain poorly understood. To address this knowledge gap, we sequenced and analyzed the complete mitogenome of S. rara to characterize its genetic features and clarify its phylogenetic position within Nemacheilidae.

Material and methods

Material collection, DNA extraction, and sequencing

Specimens of S. rara (Figure 1) were collected from the Luokeng River, a tributary of the Beijiang River basin, in Shaoguan, Guangdong Province, China (24°30′37.26″N, 113°22′1.31″E; elevation 184 m). Muscle tissues were preserved in anhydrous ethanol and stored at −80 °C until DNA extraction. One specimen was selected for whole-genome sequencing and deposited as a voucher specimen in the Collection of the School of Biology and Agriculture, Shaoguan University (http://www.sgu.edu.cn/swynyxy/; contact: Deng Weide, dddwade@163.com) under voucher number SG-SR-2024-01. Total genomic DNA was extracted by using the TIANGEN Marine Animal Tissue Genomic DNA Extraction Kit (DP324, Beijing, China). High-throughput sequencing was conducted on the NovaSeq X Plus platform (Illumina, USA).

S. rara (voucher: SG-SR-2024-01). Photograph by Deng Weide.

Mitogenome assembly and annotation

The mitogenome of S. rara was de novo assembled using GetOrganelle v1.7.7.1 (Jin et al. 2020) and annotated with MitoAnnotator (Zhu et al. 2023). The circular mitogenome map was generated using Chloroplot (Zheng et al. 2020). Codon usage in PCGs of the mitogenome was analyzed with CodonW v1.4.4 (Peden 1999). Relative Synonymous Codon Usage (RSCU) values >1 were considered preferred codons, while values <1 were regarded as underrepresented codons (Sharp and Li 1986).

Phylogenetic tree construction

A total of 46 mitogenome sequences representing multiple Nemacheilidae genera were retrieved from NCBI GenBank (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank/) (see Figure 4 legend for details). Two Botiidae species, Leptobotia taeniops and Parabotia fasciata, were included as outgroups because Botiidae is a closely related family to Nemacheilidae, providing an appropriate reference for rooting the phylogenetic tree. Thirteen PCGs, excluding stop codons, were extracted using Geneious Prime® 2025.0.3 (www.geneious.com) and aligned based on their translated amino acid sequences using MAFFT v1.5.0 (Katoh and Standley 2013). The aligned PCGs were concatenated in PhyloSuite v1.2.3 (Zhang et al. 2020). The concatenated dataset was partitioned into 39 subsets corresponding to the 13 PCGs and their three codon positions. Optimal partitioning schemes and nucleotide substitution models were selected using ModelFinder v2.2.0 (Kalyaanamoorthy et al. 2017) under the BIC criterion (best-fit partition models are provided in Table S1). ML phylogenetic analysis was conducted using IQ-TREE v2.2.0 (Minh et al. 2020) with 1,000 ultrafast bootstrap replicates.

Results

The circular mitogenome of S. rara (GenBank accession number: PQ932618) is 16,563 bp in length, with an average sequencing coverage of 3,018× (Figure S1). The GC content of the light strand is 42.8%. The genome comprises 13 PCGs, 22 tRNA genes, 2 rRNA genes, and a D-loop (Figure 2). Eight tRNA genes and the ND6 gene are encoded on the light strand, whereas all remaining genes are located on the heavy strand. GC content varies among genomic components: 43.0% for PCGs, 43.2% for tRNAs, 46.3% for rRNAs, and 32.9% in the D-loop region.

Circular map of the assembled S. rara mitogenome, showing 13 PCGs (light green, dark green, and yellow), 22 tRNA genes (blue), two rRNA genes (red), and the D-loop region (grey). Genes encoded on the forward and reverse strands are shown on the inside and outside of the circle, respectively. GC content is indicated on the inner orange circle.

All PCGs initiate with the standard start codon ATG, except for COXI, which starts with GTG. ND1, ND4L, ND5, ND6, COXI, and ATPase8 terminate with canonical stop codons (TAA or TAG), whereas the remaining genes use incomplete stop codons. PCG lengths range from 168 bp (ATPase8) to 1,839 bp (ND5) (Table 1). Codon usage analysis indicated that most codons with RSCU > 1 (92.3%) end with A or C, while most codons with RSCU < 1 (81.3%) end with G or U. All codons composed entirely of A or C exhibit RSCU > 1 (Figure 3). These results reveal an overall preference for A/C-ending codons in the mitogenome, consistent with its overall nucleotide composition (A + C content: 58.4%). Similar GC3-associated codon usage patterns have been widely observed in Nemacheilidae mitogenomes, where codon usage bias is largely driven by compositional constraints at the third codon position rather than species-specific selection (Cao et al. 2020; Luo et al. 2024; Song et al. 2025).

Gene overlaps and intergenic intervals were also identified (Table 1). Five gene overlaps, ranging from 1 to 10 bp, were detected, the largest occurring between ATPase8 and ATPase6. Twelve intergenic intervals, ranging from 1 to 13 bp, were observed, with the largest located between tRNAAsp and COXII. The D-loop, situated between tRNAPro and tRNAPhe (Figure 2), is 916 bp in length. These structural features are largely consistent with those reported in other Schistura mitogenomes (Siva et al. 2018; Sharma et al. 2020; Peng et al. 2024).

The phylogenetic tree of Nemacheilidae was reconstructed using the ML method based on complete mitogenome sequences. As shown in Figure 4, S. rara is recovered as the sister species to S. incerta, followed by a lineage of S. fasciolata (GenBank accession number NC_034778; sequenced from specimens collected by the Pearl River Fisheries Research Institute), all supported by strong bootstrap values (100%). This clade is sister to another branch comprising a different strain of S. fasciolata (GenBank accession number OR750776; sequenced from specimens collected by Hunan Agricultural University) together with S. callichromus.

Discussion and conclusion

This study presents the first comprehensive analysis of the complete mitogenome of S. rara, filling a critical gap in available mitogenomic data. Phylogenetic analyses indicate that S. rara is a sister species to S. incerta, and together they form a well-supported monophyletic clade with one lineage of S. fasciolata (Figure 4). Notably, while S. rara and S. fasciolata exhibit distinct body stripes, S. incerta lacks such markings entirely (Pearl River Fisheries Research Institute 1991).

Surveys in the upper reaches of the Beijiang River—the known habitat of S. rara—record only these three Schistura species (Li and Lin 2010). Long-term (>10 years) field observations by Deng Weide indicate that S. rara mainly occurs in the mid-upper reaches of small streams in the upper Beijiang River, whereas S. incerta and S. fasciolata inhabit the mid-lower reaches (data not shown). Given the limited dispersal ability and high habitat specificity of hillstream loaches, this local, partially overlapping distribution is consistent with regional diversification within the same drainage, rather than independent colonization events from separate basins.

Although this geographic pattern broadly aligns with the mitochondrial phylogeny, it alone cannot resolve the evolutionary sequence or direction of divergence among S. rara, S. incerta, and S. fasciolata. Future population genetic analyses may provide complementary evidence to clarify the ancestry of S. rara and to elucidate its genetic relationships, gene flow, and lineage sorting within these closely related species.

Furthermore, our mitogenome-based phylogenetic analysis (Figure 4) supports recent evidence that Schistura is not a monophyletic genus (Chen et al. 2019; Sgouros et al. 2019; Dvořák et al. 2022), particularly when compared with the well-established monophyletic lineage of Triplophysa within the same family. Species belonging to Clade I, which includes these Schistura species, are primarily distributed in southern China, Yunnan Province, and northern Vietnam (https://fishbase.mnhn.fr/; https://www.iucnredlist.org/). The sister taxa of this clade, Homatula variegatus and Tuberoschistura baenzigeri, exhibit similar biogeographic distributions. Clade II comprises not only Schistura species but also Mesonoemacheilus triangularis, which is broadly distributed across India and Sri Lanka in South Asia, as well as Myanmar and Thailand in Southeast Asia. This distribution overlaps with that of its sister taxa, Nemachilichthys rueppelli and Aborichthys elongatus.

Taken together, our study provides the first complete mitogenomic resource for S. rara, offering a robust molecular reference for this narrowly endemic species. These data refine its phylogenetic placement within Nemacheilidae and add further support for the non-monophyly of Schistura. They also provide a foundation for future integrative studies combining population genomics, morphology, and ecology to evaluate species boundaries and evolutionary diversification within the Schistura lineage.

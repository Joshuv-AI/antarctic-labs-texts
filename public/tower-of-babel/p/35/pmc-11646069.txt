P026 Obstructive sleep apnoea as a risk factor for Alzheimer’s Disease pathogenesis: a literature review and proposal of a novel model of neuropathological pathways
Authors: G Rawson, M Pase, M Howard, M Jackson
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundObstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) is a common sleep disorder that has been associated with an increased risk of Alzheimer’s disease (AD). Potential mechanistic pathways in this relationship include hypertension and oxidative stress (the imbalance of antioxidants and reactive oxygen species incurred by hypoxemia). Recently, the role of sleep microarchitecture has been investigated both as a consequence of OSA, and as a potential contributor to AD pathogenesis.AimsThe aim of this literature review is to integrate findings pertaining to OSA as a potential risk factor for AD pathogenesis. The mechanisms of interest are hypertension, oxidative stress, and sleep microarchitecture. A secondary aim of this review is to develop a model that illustrates the theorised mechanistic pathways (hypertension, oxidative stress, and sleep microarchitecture) concurrently.MethodsA search of databases between 2004 and 2024 including PubMed, Oxford Academic, and SpringerLink, with key terms amyloid burden, OSA, hypertension, slow wave sleep, and microarchitecture.Results & DiscussionCommon consequences associated with OSA, such as hypertension, oxidative stress, and abnormal sleep microarchitecture, are potential risk factors for AD pathogenesis. The exact nature of the interplay between these mechanisms is not fully understood but highlights the need for further research. The new model presented in this review illustrates how the three pathways of interest may contribute to AD neuropathology. The model identifies areas of possible interactions where the cumulative effect of the potential pathogenic pathways may, metaphorically, be more than the sum of its parts.

Quantitative CMR-derived pulmonary circulation imaging biomarkers in recent-onset dilated cardiomyopathy associated with pulmonary hemodynamics and clinical improvement
Authors: Lukáš Opatřil, Mary Luz Mojica-Pisciotti, Roman Panovský, Jan Máchal, Tomáš Holeček, Věra Feitová, Július Godava, Hana Poloczková, Vladimír Kincl, Jan Krejčí
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRecent-onset dilated cardiomyopathy (RODCM), defined as new DCM with heart failure (HF) symptoms appearing within six months, is a less documented form of HF. This study aims to evaluate pulmonary circulation imaging biomarkers (PCIB) obtained by cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) in patients with RODCM to assess their association with pulmonary hypertension, systolic and diastolic function, and clinical improvement.MethodsNinety RODCM patients with baseline and one-year follow-up CMR, clinical examination, and transthoracic echocardiography were retrospectively included. Twenty-six were excluded following a myocarditis diagnosis confirmed by endomyocardial biopsy (EMB). PCIB (pulmonary transit time (PTT), corrected PTT (PTTc), pulmonary transit beats (PTB), and pulmonary blood volume index (PBVI)) were assessed using a first-pass perfusion CMR sequence. Pulmonary hypertension, diastolic, and systolic function were assessed. Correlations between PCIB and echocardiographic changes were analyzed. Patients were classified as responders/non-responders based on left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) improvement.ResultsFrom baseline to follow-up, LVEF increased (25% to 40%), while EDD (65 mm to 61 mm), sPAP (24.2 mmHg to 20.0 mmHg), and E/e’ ratio (13 to 9) significantly reduced. Thirty-three patients were labeled as responders. PTT, PTTc, and PTB correlated with ΔE/e’, diastolic function improvement, and ΔsPAP. PBVI correlated with ΔsPAP. PTB correlated with ΔNYHA class. The PCIB could not predict whether a patient would be a responder.ConclusionsThis study focused on PCIB as outcome predictors in patients with RODCM with EMB-excluded myocarditis. Our findings show that PTB and PTTc correlated with ΔE/e’, diastolic function improvement, and ΔsPAP, while PTB additionally correlated with ΔNYHA class.

Background

Heart failure (HF) is a clinical syndrome with symptoms and/or signs caused by structural and/or functional cardiac abnormalities, corroborated by elevated natriuretic peptide levels and/or objective evidence of pulmonary or systemic congestion [1]. With a prevalence of 1–3% of the general adult population [2], HF is among the most important causes of morbidity and mortality in developed countries. According to data from the European Society of Cardiology, HF was already the leading cause of hospitalization among over-65-year-olds. It accounted for 3% of all hospitalizations and 2% of healthcare expenditure in developed countries in 2016 [3], with further increase expected [4, 5]. Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) accounts for 20–30% of the underlying causes of HF [6, 7].

Recent-onset dilated cardiomyopathy (RODCM) is most commonly defined as newly diagnosed DCM with onset of HF symptoms within six months [8–11]. However, definitions vary, and can include patients with DCM diagnosed in the preceding 12 months [12], or HF with left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) < 50% [13], or DCM within 4 ± 3 months [14]. Given the RODCM prevalence and HF’s clinical significance, proper patient stratification is crucial, yet such data remain limited. Although only a minority of patients are diagnosed with myocarditis or inflammatory cardiomyopathy as the cause of RODCM, more prognostic data exist for these [15–19] than for idiopathic DCM. Our study focuses on the latter group of patients who are less represented in the literature. Therefore, we excluded patients with myocarditis diagnosed by endomyocardial biopsy (EMB). The study aims to evaluate pulmonary circulation imaging biomarkers (PCIB) obtained by cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) in patients with RODCM and to assess their association with pulmonary hypertension, systolic and diastolic function, and indicators of pulmonary hypertension.

Methods

Study design and population

This retrospective analysis used prospective pseudonymized data from a project approved by the Hospital Ethics Committee (No. 32 V/2013). Ethical approval followed the Declaration of Helsinki (2000) of the World Medical Association, and all patients provided informed consent.

RODCM was defined as a newly diagnosed symptomatic HF with reduced LVEF in the last 6 months. All RODCM patients had ischemic heart disease excluded during the routine clinical check-up (before entering the study), determining the final sample size. Afterward, they underwent baseline CMR, EMB, and Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE). Cardiac volumes were indexed to body surface area (BSA). Peripheral blood samples were collected to determine circulating biomarker levels.

Patients were divided according to the EMB results. Since we focused on patients with idiopathic DCM, those with diagnosed myocarditis were excluded. The diagnosis was established invasively with EMB [20, 21], the current gold standard, based on a combination of histologic and immunochemistry criteria, as described by Caforio et al. [22].

All included patients received guideline-directed medical therapy according to the latest ESC guidelines [23, 24], consisting of SGLT2 inhibitors, angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitors, beta-blockers, and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, titrated to the maximum tolerated doses. For the follow-up, they had an examination after 1 year, during which clinical parameters, including the New York Heart Association (NYHA) class, were assessed, and TTE was performed. Signs of pulmonary hypertension (PH) and both systolic and diastolic function were evaluated with TTE. The differences (Δ) between baseline and follow-up LVEF, end-diastolic diameter (EDD), systolic pulmonary artery pressure (sPAP), E/e’, and other parameters were calculated, and their correlations with baseline PCIB were assessed. In addition, no patient had significant valvular disease, defined as more than mild regurgitation or stenosis, at the mitral, tricuspid, aortic, or pulmonary valves.

Patients were labeled as either “responders” or “non-responders” to the treatment. Responders were defined as patients with an improvement in LVEF of at least 10% and an LVEF of at least 35% at follow-up. This definition is consistent with others used in previous studies of reverse left ventricular remodeling [10, 25], although these additionally required a reduction in LV EDD of at least 10%. No universal definition for a responder group currently exists [26]; in most studies, the definition is based primarily on changes in LVEF (e.g., achieving an LVEF above a specific threshold [27]), or an absolute improvement in LVEF of at least 10 percentage points regardless of the final LVEF value [28].

Cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) protocol

CMR studies were performed using a 1.5 T scanner (Ingenia, Philips Medical Systems, Best, The Netherlands) following a previously described protocol [29, 30]. Briefly, cine images and left ventricular and right ventricular volumetric analysis followed Society for Cardiovascular Magnetic Resonance recommendations [31].

First-pass contrast-enhanced myocardial perfusion images were acquired without breath-hold as previously described [29], using a b-TFE sequence in three SAX sections (basal, midventricular, and apical).

T2-STIR images were acquired at the mid-ventricular level using a short tau inversion recovery sequence to assess the presence of myocardial edema, with a positive result defined as a myocardial-to-skeletal muscle signal intensity ratio greater than 1.9. Late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) images were acquired 8 to 10 min after an intravenous bolus of 0.2 mmol/kg of gadobutrol (Gadovist, Bayer-Schering Pharma, Germany) in long-axis views and the SAX stack using an inversion-recovery turbo field echo sequence (IR-TFE).

CMR data analysis

LV and right ventricle (RV) volumetric and functional parameters were calculated from the cine SAX stack using the summation-of-disc method following the Society for Cardiovascular Magnetic Resonance post-processing recommendations [31] on the IntelliSpace Portal (ISP) workspace (version 11, Philips Healthcare, Best, The Netherlands). The reported parameters included LV and RV ejection fraction (LVEF, RVEF), end-diastolic volume index (LVEDVi, RVEDVi), end-systolic volume index (LVESVi, RVESVi), stroke volume index (LVSVi, RVSVi), cardiac index (LVCI, RVCI), and LV mass index (LVMi); volumes and mass were indexed to body surface area (BSA). Two experts (VF and RP) assessed LGE and edema. LGE was defined as the presence of an area of contrast enhancement exceeding the mean signal intensity of the reference myocardium. Edema was defined from T2-STIR images as a myocardial-to-skeletal muscle signal intensity ratio greater than 1.9, consistent with the acquisition protocol described above.

Pulmonary circulation imaging biomarkers

PCIB was assessed as described previously [29, 32]. The pulmonary transit time (PTT) values were estimated from SAX rest-first-pass perfusion images using a custom Python 3.7.16 script (Python Software Foundation), as previously described [29]. Briefly, regions of interest (ROIs) automatically drawn in the RV and LV were propagated throughout motion-corrected images to generate signal intensity curves, and onset points were identified when the signal intensity exceeded 10% to 20% of the peak intensity.

PTT was defined as the difference between the LV and the RV onset times, and pulmonary transit beats (PTB) was the number of heartbeats between these times (see Fig. 1). PTTc was corrected to reduce the effect of the heart rate (HR) using Bazett’s formula [33], as previously described [29, 34]. Pulmonary blood volume index (PBVI), in ml/m2, was the product of the PTB and the RV stroke volume (RVSV), divided by the BSA [35].

Endomyocardial biopsy

The EMB was performed under ultrasound guidance and in accordance with established guidelines [20, 21] using a flexible bioptome with a sharpened cusp inserted via the right internal jugular vein using the Seldinger method [36]. If jugular veins were not eligible, the EMB was performed via the femoral vein with X-ray guidance.

Transthoracic echocardiography

Experienced cardiologists performed standard TTE examinations according to guidelines [37, 38]. PH signs were assessed as recommended [39], including sPAP calculated from the peak tricuspid regurgitation velocity (TRVmax) and the derived tricuspid regurgitation pressure gradient (TRPG) after excluding pulmonary stenosis [39], with the addition of right atrial pressure estimated from the inferior vena cava diameter and inspiratory collapsibility [39]. In addition to routinely assessed parameters, right ventricular-pulmonary arterial (RV-PA) coupling was assessed using the ratio of tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE) to sPAP (TAPSE/sPAP) [40, 41]. Diastolic function was assessed based on the Doppler imaging of the transmitral flow filling patterns, E wave velocity, E/A ratio, and Tissue Doppler imaging of the mitral annulus (E/e’) [38, 42]. In patients with atrial fibrillation, measurements were averaged over 3–5 cardiac cycles. When insufficient (most notably for differentiating pseudonormalization patterns), other parameters, such as peak velocity of the TR jet and left atrial volume index, were used in accordance with the guidelines [38]. According to these methods, patients were labeled as having normal filling patterns (without diastolic dysfunction), impaired relaxation, pseudonormalization, or restrictive filling patterns [38, 42].

Circulating biomarkers

Plasma concentrations of N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) and high-sensitivity cardiac troponin T were measured using standardized automated immunoassays in an accredited hospital laboratory, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Blood samples were collected by peripheral venipuncture, processed without undue delay, and analyzed using commercially available assays with established analytical performance. Results were reported in ng/L, and quality control procedures were performed in line with routine laboratory standards.

Statistical analysis

Data normality was evaluated with QQ plots and the Shapiro-Wilk test. Selected pulmonary circulation imaging biomarkers (PTB, PTT, PTTc) were log-transformed for plotting. Paired t-tests (normally distributed), Wilcoxon tests (non-normal distributions), and signed tests (ordinal data) assessed baseline-to-follow-up changes. Between-group comparisons used Welch’s t-test (normal), Wilcoxon rank-sum test (non-normal distributions), Cochran-Armitage test (ordinal data), and Fisher’s exact test (binary data). Correlations used Pearson’s and Spearman’s methods for normally and non-normally distributed continuous variables, and Kendall’s Tau for ordinal variables. No sensitivity analyses were performed. Associations of individual predictors with responder status were first assessed using univariable logistic regression to identify candidates for a multivariable model. For the candidates, additional models were fitted, adjusting for either age or sex as a single covariate to account for potential confounding. Age and sex, used as covariates in the multivariable models, were available for all patients, so imputation was not required for these analyses. Linear regression analyses were performed to evaluate associations between baseline PCIB and changes in echocardiographic variables. For multiple comparisons, p-values were adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) method. P-values ≤ 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All analyses were conducted in Statistica (TIBCO, v14.0.0.15) and RStudio (2024.12.0 + 467, R v4.4.1).

Results

Ninety patients were included in the study. Twenty-six were diagnosed with myocarditis according to the EMB and excluded from the follow-up. Of the 64 remaining patients, one died, and two underwent heart transplantation before the 1-year follow-up. These three patients had more severe disease at baseline compared to the follow-up cohort, including lower LVEF (20%, 12% and 17% vs. 30.0 (22.5, 38.0) %), higher sPAP (40 mmHg, 65 mmHg and 43 mmHg vs. 27.0 (15.5, 35.0) mmHg), longer PTT (7.2 s, 15.1 s and 16.3 s vs. 7.2 (5.7, 10.0) s), and elevated NT-proBNP (2460 ng/l, 19532 ng/l and 4144 ng/l vs. 1347.5 (659.0, 3018.0) ng/l). Thirty-five patients showed LGE and 13 had evidence of myocardial edema on T2-STIR imaging. In addition, tricuspid regurgitation velocity could not be reliably assessed by TTE in three patients; therefore, these patients were excluded from the analysis of estimated sPAP. Univariable logistic regression analysis was performed to identify candidate predictors of responder status. No PCIB reached statistical significance in the univariable analysis. Only LVEDVi reached statistical significance (OR 0.989, 95% CI 0.976–0.999, p = 0.049), and was therefore selected as the candidate for the multivariable model, which showed modest discriminative ability (AUC = 0.608). The association between LVEDVi and responder status remained statistically significant after adjustment for age (OR 0.988, 95% CI 0.977–0.999, p = 0.045) and after adjustment for sex (OR 0.988, 95% CI 0.976–0.999, p = 0.047).

PTB (β = −0.41, p = 0.025) and PTTc (β = −0.48, p = 0.035) were independently associated with improvement in E/e’ ratio after adjustment for age or sex; neither age nor sex was significantly associated with ΔE/e’. Stronger associations were observed for ΔsPAP: baseline PTB (β = −1.01, p = 0.001), PTT (β = −1.77, p < 0.001), PTTc (β = −1.40, p < 0.001), and PBVI (β = −0.03, p = 0.008) were all independently associated with greater reductions in sPAP after controlling for the potential confounding effects of age and sex, neither of which showed significant associations with ΔsPAP. No significant associations were found between PCIB and ΔLVEF, ΔEDD, or ΔEDV. Furthermore, baseline PTB, PTT, PTTc, and PBVI showed no significant associations with changes in NYHA functional class or diastolic dysfunction grade at follow-up, even after adjustment for age or sex.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first study of RODCM patients with EMB-excluded myocarditis to examine associations between PCIB and clinical outcomes. Our findings show that baseline PCIB correlated with improvements in diastolic function and PH parameters (ΔE/e’, ΔsPAP, and ΔNYHA class), though not with changes in LVEF or EDD. While PCIB did not predict treatment response, these associations support the value of these parameters as noninvasive, conveniently obtainable markers with the potential to reflect cardiopulmonary system function [29, 43]. These results further support the complexity of these parameters in capturing integrated cardiopulmonary dynamics.

In early RODCM studies, patients with a shorter symptom duration (< 3 months) and less severe initial decompensation (lower filling pressures) were more likely to improve [9]. Similar responses occurred with dobutamine-induced LVEF improvement [14]. Additionally, higher systolic blood pressure at presentation, shorter QRS, and smaller EDD were associated with a higher likelihood of LVEF normalization in patients with recent-onset nonischemic cardiomyopathy [11]. Kubanek et al. showed that lower late gadolinium enhancement and higher myocardial edema ratio on the CMR were independent predictors of reverse LV remodeling [10]. In our cohort, although LVEDVi was the only statistically significant predictor of responder status, its low discriminative ability suggests that predicting treatment response in RODCM remains challenging and that LV volume alone is insufficient to identify patients likely to benefit from therapy. A recent CMR study found no correlation between most CMR parameters (including LV strain and replacement myocardial fibrosis) and reverse LV remodeling, but reported that higher LV contractile reserve on dobutamine stress CMR and female sex predicted reverse LV remodeling [12]. Another study showed PTT correlated with major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), HF hospitalization, and NT-proBNP [13]. However, existing data are unbalanced: more studies focused on myocarditis [15–19], some did not perform EMB [11–14] or included patients with myocarditis alongside those who underwent EMB [9, 10].

Although PCIB did not predict systolic function improvement, PTB and PTTc correlated with ΔE/e’ and ΔsPAP (PH indicator), and PTB additionally correlated with diastolic dysfunction grade improvement and NYHA class (symptoms indicator). This agrees with previously published data showing PCIB correlations with systolic and diastolic function and PH [29, 43]. However, the associations between baseline PCIB and ΔE/e’, ΔsPAP, ΔNYHA class, and diastolic dysfunction grade, driven primarily by PTB, together with the absence of associations with LVEF or EDD strengthen the idea that, even though PCIB do not predict systolic function improvement, they can potentially reflect early improvements in hemodynamic status that could further support clinical monitoring and risk stratification of patients diagnosed with RODCM.

The only study of PCIB (PTT) in RODCM patients focused on MACE and hospitalization without EMB-excluded myocarditis [13]. Our study focused on improvements in systolic and diastolic function and PH. Although our findings show that PCIB did not correlate with systolic function or responder status, it did correlate with NYHA class improvement and with both diastolic function and PH parameters.

RV–PA correlated with changes in diastolic dysfunction, sPAP, and NYHA functional class. However, similar to most other parameters, it failed to predict whether patients would be responders or non-responders. Regarding circulating biomarkers, neither NT-proBNP nor troponin T predicted responder status. The inclusion of patients with EMB-excluded myocarditis may influence these findings.

Study limitations

This study is a retrospective analysis of prospective data conducted in a single center. As a retrospective analysis, selection bias may be present due to non-randomized patient selection. No strategies minimized this bias. Additionally, three patients enrolled at baseline did not complete the one-year follow-up (one death, two heart transplantations) and were excluded from the longitudinal analyses. However, these patients had more severe disease at baseline than the follow-up cohort, including lower LVEF, higher sPAP, longer PTT, and elevated NT-proBNP levels, reflecting a survivorship bias that, together with selection bias, should be considered when interpreting the results. Furthermore, pulmonary hypertension was assessed by transthoracic echocardiography rather than right heart catheterization, which remains the gold standard for its diagnosis and quantification. The generalizability of our findings may be limited by our study design. Multi-centre prospective studies are needed to validate PCIB findings in RODCM patients. Missing follow-up data included NYHA class (n = 3), E/e’ (n = 2), and EDV (n = 9). Missing data were handled by analyzing only subgroups with complete data for each specific variable, avoiding potential bias from data imputation or other methods. Additionally, due to the retrospective design, only finalized Doppler measurements were archived and raw datasets were unavailable; formal assessment of inter- and intra-observer variability for echocardiographic measurements could therefore not be performed. Finally, genetic testing data were not available for the patients included in this study; the presence of certain pathogenic variants, such as TTN (titin) mutations, is associated with a less favorable prognosis in DCM, and the absence of this information represents an additional limitation of our work.

Conclusions

To our knowledge, ours is the first study evaluating CMR-derived pulmonary circulation imaging biomarkers (PCIB) in RODCM patients with EMB-excluded myocarditis. Our findings show that although PCIB did not correlate with ΔLVEF or ΔEDD, PTB and PTTc correlated with ΔE/e’, diastolic function improvement, and ΔsPAP (PH indicator), while PTB additionally correlated with ΔNYHA class (symptom indicator). Although PCIB cannot predict treatment response, correlations with NYHA class, diastolic function, and PH indicators support their use in clinical evaluation of RODCM patients.

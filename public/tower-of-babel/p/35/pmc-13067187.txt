New model for remote symptom management and self-care of cancer patients based on digital health platforms: a narrative review
Authors: Wen Chen, Yue Deng, Di Zhu, Huan Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and ObjectiveWith the increasing global cancer burden and prolonged survival of patients, effective post-treatment symptom management and self-care have become essential components of chronic cancer care. Traditional follow-up-based medical models often fail to capture dynamic symptom fluctuations, leading to suboptimal adherence and increased healthcare utilization. The emergence of digital health platforms provides new opportunities for remote symptom monitoring (RSM) and patient-centered care. This narrative review aimed to synthesize recent global evidence on the use of digital health platforms for remote symptom management and self-care among cancer patients, focusing on their technological frameworks, clinical benefits, and implementation challenges.MethodsA structured literature search was conducted across PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature (CINAHL), and Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) Xplore for studies published in English between January 2018 and December 2024. The search combined controlled vocabulary and free-text terms related to digital health platforms, cancer, symptom management, and self-care. Reference lists of included studies were also manually screened. Eligible studies included those involving cancer patients and evaluating digital health interventions for RSM or self-management.Key Content and FindingsThe reviewed studies covered diverse digital health platforms integrating wearable sensors, mobile applications, and cloud-based analytics for real-time symptom tracking and personalized interventions. Core functional modules commonly identified included symptom monitoring, patient education, and psychosocial support. Evidence suggested that these platforms enhance patient empowerment, improve communication between patients and clinicians, and support proactive care through early detection of symptom exacerbations. However, significant barriers remain, including issues of data privacy and security, regulatory heterogeneity, and the digital devices that may limit equitable access.ConclusionsDigital health platforms are transforming oncology care from a physician-centered model to a collaborative, technology-enabled framework that supports continuous, patient-driven symptom management. While existing evidence demonstrates their feasibility and clinical benefits, future research should emphasize standardization, long-term clinical validation, and culturally adaptable implementation models. Addressing these priorities may enhance both patient quality of life and the efficiency of healthcare resource utilization in cancer survivorship care.

Introduction

Background

According to the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC)’s Global Cancer Statistics 2020 report, there were approximately 19.3 million new cancer cases and 9.96 million cancer-related deaths worldwide that year (1). With an aging population and advancements in treatment, the global population living with cancer has exceeded 50 million, gradually transforming cancer into a chronic condition that can be managed long-term (2). However, numerous studies indicate that patients commonly suffer from multiple symptoms such as pain, fatigue, and sleep disturbances during treatment and recovery, experiencing an average of more than six concurrent symptoms, with about 30% of these reaching moderate to severe levels (3). This high burden of symptom clusters not only diminishes quality of life but also significantly increases the risks of rehospitalization and emergency visits.

Rationale and knowledge gap

Current cancer symptom management primarily relies on periodic outpatient follow-ups, where patients typically receive medical evaluations only once every 2–3 months, making it difficult to dynamically capture symptom fluctuations (4). Most moderate to severe symptoms and adverse events do not occur during outpatient visits but rather during follow-up intervals, significantly increasing the risk of emergency visits and hospitalizations (5,6). Digital health, which aims to enhance the efficiency, accessibility, and effectiveness of health care by using digital technologies to gather, analyse, manage, and distribute health data, offers new opportunities to address these challenges (7). The “bottleneck” in China’s oncology care lies in the severe shortage of doctors, the widening gap in medical quality between urban and rural areas, and the lack of follow-up and monitoring systems. There is an urgent need to promote digital health monitoring systems, pharmaceutical management interventions, and the development of regional specialized medical alliances to improve adherence and symptom management (8-10).

The accelerated evolution of digital health technologies provides crucial support for alleviating disparities in medical resources and gaps in follow-up management. Telehealth, defined as the use of telecommunication techniques for the purpose of providing telemedicine, medical education, and health education over a distance, has become a central component of modern digital health systems (11). In 2024, the global digital health market was valued at approximately $465 billion, with projections exceeding $530 billion in 2025. Telemedicine user penetration has reached nearly 45% in developed countries and about 30% globally on average (12,13).

On the policy front, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has, since 2023, approved several artificial intelligence (AI)-driven digital therapeutics (DTx) for cancer symptoms through its fast-track approval pathway, primarily focusing on pain, fatigue, and quality-of-life monitoring (14). In China, the “14th Five-Year Plan for Digital Healthcare Development” and the “Healthy China Action (2024–2025)” explicitly propose the establishment of a remote collaborative management system for chronic diseases and oncology, promoting data interoperability and privacy compliance (15). Technologically, the integration of wearable sensors, AI algorithms, and cloud-edge collaborative computing has enabled continuous, objective, and predictive symptom monitoring. Multimodal AI models are now achieving real-time risk assessment and personalized interventions, laying a data-driven foundation for precision oncology management (16).

With the deepening of the “patient-centered” philosophy, oncology management is gradually shifting from the traditional “physician-led” approach toward a shared decision-making model characterized by “clinician-patient collaboration”. Recent studies indicate that approximately 70–80% of cancer patients desire real-time symptom feedback and digital interactive support during their treatment journey (17). Digital health platforms are emerging as crucial enablers of this collaborative transformation: by integrating modules for symptom tracking, personalized education, psychological intervention, and AI-assisted communication, these platforms not only enhance patients’ self-care—defined as awareness and active participation by individuals in their recovery, recuperation, and rehabilitation to minimise the consequences of treatment and promote survival, health, and well-being (18)—and improve medication adherence (19) but also significantly improve the efficiency of clinician-patient communication and the continuity of disease management (20). Concurrently, AI-driven Digital Decision-Support Systems (DDSS) have been piloted in several cancer centers, enabling real-time correlation of patient-reported symptoms with clinical indicators to facilitate proactive interventions and co-creative care decisions (21).

Objective

This review aims to systematically examine research progress from 2018 to 2024 on the use of digital health platforms for remote symptom management and self-care among cancer patients, both domestically and internationally. It focuses on their application characteristics in symptom assessment, intervention mechanisms, and outcome evaluation, while analyzing challenges related to data security and regulation during implementation. By comparing practical experiences across different models, this study seeks to provide a theoretical basis and policy references for developing a new remote symptom management model for cancer that aligns with the characteristics of China’s healthcare system, with the dual goals of enhancing patients’ quality of life and optimizing the allocation of medical resources. We present this article in accordance with the Narrative Review reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-1-2621/rc).

Methods

This narrative review aimed to synthesize global evidence published between January 2018 and December 2024 on the use of digital health platforms for remote symptom management and self-care in cancer patients. The focus was to outline key technological frameworks, intervention mechanisms, and clinical outcomes, while identifying implementation challenges such as data security, privacy, and digital equity.

A structured literature search was performed across major English-language databases, including PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature (CINAHL), and Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) Xplore, covering studies published from 2018 to 2024. The search combined controlled vocabulary and free-text keywords related to digital health, cancer, symptom management, and self-care. Reference lists of selected papers were also screened to capture additional studies. Only English-language publications were considered to maintain methodological consistency and ensure international comparability, as studies indexed in English databases generally adhere to standardized reporting norms.

Two reviewers independently screened titles, abstracts, and full texts according to predefined eligibility criteria. Studies were included if they involved cancer patients and examined digital health platforms designed for remote symptom monitoring (RSM), self-management, or personalized intervention. Publications relying solely on telephone follow-up, not addressing cancer-related symptoms, or lacking full-text availability were excluded.

Relevant information from eligible studies was reviewed and organized around study design, digital platform characteristics, intervention strategies, outcomes, and ethical or equity-related issues. Given the heterogeneity across studies, findings were synthesized using a thematic and narrative approach. Themes were inductively developed through iterative reading and interpretation, focusing on technological functions, symptom tracking mechanisms, patient engagement, outcome evaluation, and implementation challenges. Although no formal bias assessment was applied, methodological rigor and clarity of reporting were carefully considered in interpreting the evidence (Table 1).

The concept and technological basis of digital health platforms

In recent years, the role of digital health platforms in chronic disease management has become increasingly prominent. Their significance lies not only in technological innovation but also in driving a systemic transformation from traditional healthcare paradigms toward data-driven and patient-empowered models. According to the latest definition by the World Health Organization (WHO, 2023), digital health has evolved from early-stage electronic health (eHealth) into a comprehensive health ecosystem that integrates information and communication technologies (ICT), AI, the Internet of Things (IoT), and interoperability standards such as Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR) and Health Level Seven (HL7). The core objective of this ecosystem is to enhance the accessibility and equity of healthcare services through digital means, promote individual health management capabilities, and thereby achieve overall improvement in population health outcomes (22).

Importantly, the transition toward patient-centered models of care delivery is particularly critical in oncology. Evidence from a randomized controlled trial demonstrated that an internet-based, patient-centered support system providing interactive health communication significantly reduced symptom distress among cancer patients compared with usual care, by offering timely assistance aligned with patients’ real-time needs (23). These findings provide a strong clinical justification for embedding patient-centered and interactive communication functions into digital health platforms for cancer care.

Within this framework, digital health systems can generally be divided into three mutually supportive layers. First, the sensing layer, leveraging wearable devices and IoT sensors, enables real-time collection of multimodal physiological data, providing continuous and dynamic health monitoring for chronic disease patients and extending healthcare services from “clinical settings” to “daily life”. Second, the interoperability layer, centered on data standards like FHIR, facilitates data exchange between different healthcare systems through APIs and semantic mapping, breaking down “information silos” and establishing a data foundation for cross-institutional collaboration and personalized medicine (24,25). Third, the intelligence layer utilizes cloud computing and AI algorithms to analyze data and generate insights, extracting value from multi-source data to enable early disease detection, risk prediction, and personalized treatment recommendations. In recent years, the introduction of generative AI and emotional AI technologies has allowed platforms to simulate human interaction, providing emotionally resonant health advice and psychological support, thereby further enhancing patient experience and adherence (26).

Notably, with the widespread application of digital health, data privacy and ethical governance have become critical issues. Multiple studies in 2025 have indicated that IoT devices used in home monitoring and telemedicine may raise concerns about information security and algorithmic bias, prompting researchers and regulatory bodies to develop data protection mechanisms based on federated learning and zero-trust architecture. These mechanisms not only safeguard privacy but also provide a secure collaborative environment for the continuous optimization of AI models (27).

In summary, the rapid evolution of digital health platforms is driving a shift in chronic disease management from a “treatment-centered” approach to one that is “patient-centered and data-driven”. Through the synergistic ecosystem of sensing, interoperability, and intelligence layers, digital health not only extends healthcare resources across time and space and enhances information connectivity but also lays a solid foundation for precision medicine and proactive health management (28). This marks a global transition of healthcare systems toward a more open, intelligent, and human-centric new era.

In conclusion, the application of digital health platforms is not merely a simple technological add-on but a fundamental reshaping of the traditional chronic disease management model. This reshaping is first evident in the transcendence of time and space constraints: it shifts the management model from a discrete, episodic one centered on “periodic follow-ups” to a continuous, whole-process care model characterized by “constant monitoring”, thereby eliminating the blind spots in health monitoring between clinical visits. More profoundly, it drives a shift in core roles: patients are empowered from being passive recipients of treatment instructions to becoming active health managers who possess their own data and participate in the decision-making process. Consequently, the patient-physician relationship evolves towards a more equal, collaborative partnership. This conceptual and technological innovation opens a new chapter for chronic disease management.

Current status and advances in remote symptom management for cancer patients

During cancer treatment, symptom management remains a critical challenge affecting both therapeutic efficacy and quality of life. Patients often experience multidimensional symptoms—such as pain, cancer-related fatigue, nausea and vomiting, sleep disturbances, and emotional distress—during chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and immunotherapy. These symptoms not only increase psychological and physiological burden but may also lead to treatment interruption and reduced efficacy. Traditional follow-up management relies on patients’ recall of symptom changes, which is prone to significant recall bias and delayed intervention (29).

Importantly, remote-based interventions are particularly suitable for cancer patients and survivors who experience physical weakness, fatigue, or mobility limitations, which often hinder frequent in-person visits. Multiple randomized controlled trials have demonstrated that web-based or internet-delivered interventions can effectively improve psychological adjustment, alleviate anxiety and depression, and enhance self-management capacity in cancer populations (30-32). These findings provide a strong patient-centered justification for the adoption of remote symptom management strategies in oncology care.

With the advancement of digital health technologies, RSM has emerged as a key direction in supportive cancer care. Robust evidence from a large European multicentre randomized controlled trial (eSMART) demonstrated that real-time RSM during chemotherapy significantly improved health-related quality of life, alleviated symptom burden, reduced unscheduled hospital admissions, enhanced overall survival, and increased cost-effectiveness compared with usual care (33). Building on this evidence, the core of RSM lies in leveraging electronic patient-reported outcomes (ePRO) and AI-driven decision-support systems to shift from a “reactive approach” to a “proactive prevention” model (34). This approach is strongly justified by evidence showing that adverse symptoms are highly prevalent among patients undergoing treatment for advanced cancers and are frequently underdetected in routine clinical practice. A landmark randomized controlled trial demonstrated that systematic symptom monitoring using standardized patient-reported outcome (PRO) questionnaires significantly improved symptom control, reduced emergency visits, and enhanced overall survival compared with usual care (35). Patients can report symptoms in real time via mobile devices, while the system automatically analyzes trends and triggers intervention alerts, transforming discrete clinic-based assessments into continuous, real-world experience-driven tracking. This model has demonstrated significant success in managing breast, lung, and pancreatic cancers by enabling early intervention before symptom exacerbation, thereby reducing adverse events and emergency visits (36).

Beyond controlled trial settings, growing real-world evidence further supports the feasibility, scalability, and clinical value of integrating remote patient monitoring (RPM) into routine oncology care. A large multicentre real-world implementation study across 33 cancer centres in France and Belgium demonstrated that, by applying implementation science principles, RPM pathways could be successfully adapted to different cancer sites and institutional workflows, achieving smooth integration into routine care while maintaining a positive clinical impact on symptom management and care delivery (37).

International practices show that AI-driven remote monitoring systems are increasingly becoming part of routine oncology care. For instance, the electronic symptom monitoring platform at Memorial Sloan Kettering in the United States integrates patient-reported data with AI algorithms to automatically generate management recommendations and deliver them to healthcare providers, significantly improving intervention efficiency and patient satisfaction. Meanwhile, China’s “Internet + Healthcare” policy has spurred localized implementations. ePRO systems embedded in WeChat mini-programs have been widely adopted in institutions such as Fudan University Cancer Hospital and West China Hospital, achieving a 28% reduction in readmission rates and a 45% shortening of physician response time (38-40).

Collective evidence indicates that remote symptom management systematically enhances patient adherence, optimizes healthcare resource allocation, and improves overall survival and quality of life. Its clinical value lies not only in increasing the sensitivity of symptom monitoring but also in enabling precise, proactive, and personalized cancer care through AI-powered continuous decision support.

However, the widespread adoption of remote symptom management still faces numerous challenges. The digital divide is a primary barrier, as elderly, low-income, or less-educated patient populations may not benefit equally due to a lack of smart devices or digital literacy skills. Data security and privacy protection are also urgent issues that need addressing. Health data, being highly sensitive personal information, requires robust measures to ensure its security during collection, transmission, storage, and use, preventing breaches—a critical ethical and legal imperative for platform operators. Additionally, the lack of standardization across platforms leads to “data silos”, hindering the sharing and integration of patient data across different systems and limiting the maximization of remote symptom management’s potential value. Solving these problems requires concerted efforts from medical institutions, technology developers, and policymakers to advance remote symptom management towards a more inclusive, secure, and efficient future.

Digital health-empowered self-care models

The value of digital health platforms in symptom management for cancer patients is evolving from a technological tool to a behavioral change mechanism, with its core focus on systematically motivating patients’ proactive health behaviors and self-management capabilities.

Theoretically, this process can be viewed as a multi-level empowerment framework. Self-efficacy theory highlights the critical influence of belief on the execution of health behaviors, as patients build confidence through successful experiences and social support on digital platforms (41). The chronic disease self-management model further emphasizes the central role of patients in symptom monitoring, treatment adherence, emotional regulation, and lifestyle management, providing a systematic basis for platform functionality design. Digital empowerment theory formalizes this process, illustrating how digital platforms enhance patients’ sense of control and behavioral autonomy through the provision of knowledge, skills, and resources, facilitating the transition from “passive responder” to “active manager” (42).

In practice, patient empowerment can be conceptualized as an empowerment loop model. Information empowerment lowers cognitive barriers through knowledge visualization and personalized education; behavioral empowerment leverages continuous tracking and AI-driven feedback mechanisms to reinforce positive behaviors; and social empowerment emphasizes the importance of psychosocial support by enhancing emotional identification, stress regulation, and sustained engagement through digital interventions such as guided mindfulness, meditation, and virtual peer support networks. Notably, a recent pilot study demonstrated the feasibility and acceptability of a smartphone-based meditation application for cancer patients with psychosocial distress, with users reporting significant improvements in multiple measures of symptom distress and mood outcomes (43). Together, these three components form a closed-loop dynamic mechanism of “cognition-action-social feedback” (44-46).

As technology and care models converge, current digital oncology management has evolved into three predominant models: the nurse-augmented model, centered on nursing teams to ensure care continuity through remote support and decision assistance; the AI-augmented collaborative model, which utilizes algorithms and semantic analysis for 24/7 monitoring and risk alerts; and the ecosystem-integrated model, which brings together medical institutions, communities, and family resources to establish a collaborative, lifelong support network (47-50).

Furthermore, health literacy and cultural adaptation have been identified as key factors influencing the effectiveness of empowerment. AI-adaptive designs and semantically simplified interfaces tailored for elderly patients and vulnerable groups are significantly enhancing the equity and accessibility of digital interventions. Therefore, the future direction of digital health empowerment should not only focus on technological breakthroughs but also strive to build a sustainable, inclusive, and psychosocially-technologically integrated ecosystem for proactive health management.

Comprehensive value assessment: the impact of digital health platforms from a multi-layer perspective

With the maturation of digital health technologies and the expansion of application scenarios, oncology symptom management platforms have progressed from the early proof-of-concept stage to the phase of large-scale implementation. Current research consistently indicates that their clinical and socio-economic benefits must be understood through a comprehensive evaluation across three dimensions: patient, healthcare system, and society (51).

Patient level: dual enhancement of quality of life and behavioral empowerment

At the individual patient level, digital health interventions have significantly improved core symptom control and quality of life. Systematic reviews indicate that ePRO-driven real-time monitoring increases the control rates for pain, fatigue, and nausea by approximately 25–40% and effectively reduces symptom exacerbation events (52). Through continuous feedback mechanisms, patients can intuitively perceive improvements in their own health trajectories, thereby enhancing self-efficacy and promoting behavioral sustainability.

Furthermore, the educational modules and peer support networks provided by digital platforms significantly alleviate negative emotional responses such as anxiety and depression. A European study of cancer patients showed that long-term users of digital support platforms experienced a 32% reduction in psychological distress scores and a 1.8-fold increase in self-management scores (53). This finding validates the synergistic effect of the “technology-psychology-behavior” triad.

Healthcare system level: efficiency optimization and quality synergy

At the system level, digital platforms significantly enhance care efficiency and precision through automated data collection and AI-based risk stratification. A multicenter study demonstrated that RSM programs reduced healthcare provider response time by 46%, emergency department visits by 23%, and readmission rates by 31% (54,55). Moreover, the digital follow-up model effectively alleviates outpatient clinic workload pressure and improves cross-institutional information exchange, significantly enhancing care continuity.

Economic analyses indicate that such systems can achieve overall cost savings of approximately 15–25%, primarily stemming from reduced acute events and resource reallocation (21). Consequently, digital health platforms are driving the transition of healthcare systems from “reactive services” towards “predictive and preventive care”.

Socio-economic level: rebalancing sustainability and health equity

From a macro perspective, the long-term value of digital health is reflected in both cost-effectiveness and social equity dimensions. Multiple economic studies show that the return on investment (ROI) for digital symptom management programs can reach 1:2–1:3, while also reducing patient transportation costs and productivity losses (53). However, the issue of the “digital divide” requires urgent attention. Research finds that elderly patients, low-income groups, and individuals with lower digital literacy are significantly disadvantaged in utilizing digital health, creating new health inequities (56-58).

Therefore, at the policy level, a “Digital Inclusion Framework” needs to be established through measures such as technology subsidies, interface adaptation, and community digital literacy education to ensure the inclusivity and sustainability of health technology benefits.

In summary, digital health platforms demonstrate a cross-level value chain effect in the symptom management of cancer patients: centered on the patient, they strengthen self-efficacy and improve quality of life; leveraging the healthcare system as a fulcrum, they drive efficiency gains and resource optimization; set against the societal backdrop, they promote equitable access and sustainable development. This integrated pathway is reshaping the paradigm of oncology care, steering health management truly from a “treatment-oriented” to a “value-oriented” approach.

Strengths and limitations

Digital health platforms have demonstrated substantial advantages in cancer symptom management. Their strengths lie in enabling continuous symptom monitoring, enhancing patient engagement, and facilitating timely communication between patients and healthcare providers. Through integrated modules for symptom tracking, health education, and psychosocial support, these platforms can promote personalized interventions, improve treatment adherence, and ultimately enhance patients’ quality of life. Furthermore, digital tools contribute to data-driven decision-making by providing real-time insights into symptom trends and treatment responses, supporting precision oncology and outcome optimization.

Despite these advantages, significant limitations remain that hinder the widespread and sustainable adoption of such platforms. Technically, insufficient data standardization and poor interoperability across systems lead to data fragmentation and “information silos”, limiting the integration and clinical utility of digital data. From the user perspective, patient adherence and digital literacy represent key challenges, particularly among older adults who may struggle with device use or sustained engagement. Clinically, the provision of remote support requires healthcare professionals to acquire enhanced information technology competencies and advanced communication skills to accurately assess symptoms when patients cannot be directly observed. Previous studies on telephone-based nursing advice services have highlighted that effective remote symptom assessment depends heavily on structured communication processes, clinical judgment, and organizational support, all of which influence care quality and patient outcomes (59,60). These requirements may increase workload and necessitate role adaptation, potentially disrupting traditional care workflows. Ethically, issues related to data privacy, informed consent, and algorithmic bias pose ongoing risks, especially when algorithms are trained on non-representative datasets. At the policy level, the absence of unified regulatory standards and evaluation frameworks for digital health products restricts systematic quality control and comparative assessment among platforms.

Overall, while digital health platforms offer transformative opportunities for cancer symptom management, their long-term success depends on overcoming these technical, human, ethical, and regulatory barriers through coordinated, interdisciplinary collaboration among technology developers, clinicians, policymakers, and patients.

Future development directions

As digital health becomes more deeply integrated into cancer symptom management, its future development is trending towards diversification, precision, and intelligence. Based on current challenges and opportunities, in-depth exploration and innovation should focus on the following directions.

At the level of technological integration and innovation, AI will play a central role in advancing precise symptom prediction and individualized intervention. Future research should prioritize the development of advanced machine learning algorithms capable of early symptom forecasting and risk stratification through comprehensive analysis of patients’ historical symptom trajectories, treatment responses, and personal characteristics.

Building on these capabilities, digital health systems should generate highly personalized intervention plans, promoting a shift from reactive symptom management to proactive symptom prevention. Achieving this transformation requires effective multimodal data integration. Next-generation platforms must transcend conventional physiological monitoring by incorporating psychological state indicators such as mood diaries and voice-based emotion analytics, behavioral data including activity levels and sleep quality, and clinical information to construct holistic patient health profiles. This integrated approach will provide multidimensional data foundations for precision interventions.

Furthermore, the convergence of AI and multimodal analytics will accelerate the intelligent evolution of virtual nursing and remote care. Intelligent agents and virtual nurses will increasingly manage routine follow-ups, medication adherence, and patient education, delivering continuous, human-centered professional support that enhances the accessibility, responsiveness, and efficiency of remote oncology care.

At the level of regulation and ethics, there is an urgent need to strengthen the construction of digital ethics and privacy protection frameworks. This is not merely a technical issue but a cornerstone of trust. The future should promote the establishment of security protection systems that span the entire data lifecycle, utilizing cutting-edge technologies like privacy-preserving computation and federated learning to realize data value while ensuring its security. Simultaneously, it is imperative to establish clear algorithm auditing and accountability mechanisms to minimize algorithmic bias and ensure the fairness of digital health.

At the level of standards and evaluation systems, establishing localized and standardized digital health evaluation indicator systems is essential for ensuring the healthy development of the industry. This system should encompass multiple dimensions such as technical performance, clinical effectiveness, user experience, economic impact, and social benefits, forming a scientific and unified evaluation standard. Through standardized empirical research, it will provide reliable evidence for the optimization and iteration of digital health products, procurement decisions by medical institutions, and the formulation of health insurance payment policies, ultimately promoting the virtuous development of the entire ecosystem.

These directions are interconnected and mutually reinforcing, collectively outlining the future landscape of digital health in cancer symptom management—a new model that is more intelligent, precise, secure, and sustainable.

Conclusions

This review summarizes the current landscape, mechanisms, and prospects of digital health platforms in cancer symptom management and self-care. Evidence indicates that these platforms have evolved from auxiliary tools into key enablers of remote oncology care, driving a shift toward proactive, data-driven, and patient-centered management.

Clinically, they enhance precision and continuity of symptom control through real-time monitoring and adaptive feedback. At the patient level, they strengthen health literacy, behavioral self-regulation, and psychosocial support, fostering active self-management. At the system level, they streamline care delivery, reduce unnecessary visits, and optimize resource allocation.

Persistent challenges remain, including data fragmentation, interoperability gaps, inconsistent user engagement, and ethical issues such as privacy and algorithmic bias. Future development should integrate AI for predictive analytics, utilize multimodal data for comprehensive assessment, and establish robust ethical and policy frameworks.

Overall, digital health platforms represent a transformative paradigm in oncology, advancing precision, participation, and efficiency. Their sustainable growth requires close collaboration across medicine, technology, and policy to realize an intelligent, equitable, and human-centered model of cancer care.

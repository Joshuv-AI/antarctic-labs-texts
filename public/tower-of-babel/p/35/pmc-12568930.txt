RETRACTED ARTICLE: Novel design of a dual-band microstrip antenna array for 6G mmWave applications
Authors: Amin Al Ka’bi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This paper proposes an innovative microstrip antenna design, which can offer dual-band and dual-circular polarization capability, dedicated to small autonomous aerial vehicles (AAVs)/low Earth orbit (LEO) satellites operation at the K/Ka-band. The design uses arrow-shaped (left) and T-shaped (right) stubs to achieve right-hand and left-hand circular polarization in two frequency bands. By simultaneous excitation of the orthogonal modes \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$({\mathrm{TM}}_{10}/{\mathrm{TM}}_{01}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{30}/{\mathrm{TM}}_{21}$$\end{document}), the resonant frequencies of the antenna can be fine-tuned using these stubs. The stub dimensions allow for individual tuning of each operational band thus enhancing optimal performance in multiple frequencies. A validated 2 × 2 antenna array at 18.6 GHz and 30.8 GHz supports the ability of the design of 10.9% and 4.75% impedance bandwidths; 3.65 and 2.25% axial ratio bandwidths; 9.35 dBic and 10.65 dBic of realized gains. These findings support the appropriateness of the design for more sophisticated communication systems.

Introduction

The emergence of wireless connectivity for low earth orbit satellites and autonomous aerial vehicles is a crucial technological advance required for the progress of 6G communications. This innovation is fundamental to achieving ultra-fast, frictionless global connectivity, indicating its critical role in forming the future face of telecommunication networks. Some mmWave, sub-terahertz and IR optics are among the frequency bands being studied for providing ubiquitous high speed and low latency wireless networks. At present mmWave technology, especially in the K/Ka-bands, is regarded as very promising because of the relatively large bandwidths it supports, favorable channel loss & dispersion characteristics.

Good quality design against performance is important for autonomous aerial vehicles (AAV) and low earth orbit (LEO) satellite utilization for the K/Ka-band. These platforms require transmitting circularly polarized (CP) signals to minimize the effect of multipath and polarization mismatch as described in [1–4]. Dual band capability is necessary to enable full—duplex communication links that reduce inter-channel interference and enhance channel separation as described in [5–8]. Moreover, a compact antenna design is crucial to gain use of the available space within AAVs or LEO satellites. Patch antennas are ideal for radio frequency (RF) applications in UAV’s and LEO satellites because of their compact, lightweight structure as well as ease of integration. These are consistent with the narrow terms of size and weight imposed by such platforms. Different approaches have been developed for dual band and dual CP patch antennas for full-duplex operation and for polarization mismatches. As documented in references [8–10], several patches needed for specific frequency bands are used to ensure the dual band nature. The main radiator needs other radiators to produce double-sense CP radiation.

The situation with stacked antennas is limited by a high profile and complicated operation. Documented in references [11] and [12] are dual-band, dual-circularly polarized (CP) patch antennas with two separate radiators that have independent feeding networks. Nevertheless, these feeding networks are complex and take up much space and therefore designable areas shall be limited to autonomous aerial vehicles (AAVs) and low earth orbits (LEO) satellites. References [13–16] describe antennas integrating meta-surfaces in dual-band, dual-CP radiation. Nonetheless, in the case of meta-surface designs, multi-layered structures correspond with higher thickness limitations and problems scaling a single antenna to array configurations. A simpler solution to dual-band, dual-CP operation in patch antennas is based on a single patch radiator with a single feed as presented in references [7, 17] and [18]. These designs use multiple resonant modes of a single patch radiator, for instance \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{20}$$\end{document} or \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{11}$$\end{document} in a rectangular microstrip antenna and are characterized by certain parameters. However, single-radiator patch antennas have traditionally been developed for lower frequency bands such as X- or S-band for traditional radar and communication systems. The requirement for a compact dual-band, dual-CP antenna for the K/Ka-band in AAVs and LEO satellites includes design for easy fabrication and the capability of matching cost-effective and standard printed circuit board (PCB) manufacturing process.

This paper introduces a compact dual-band dual-circularly polarized (CP) microstrip patch antenna for autonomous aerial vehicles (AAV) or low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite application at K- and Ka-band frequencies. The proposed design exploits the multi-resonant modes of one radiator to enable dual-CP working in both frequency bands. Symmetrically arranged arrow-shaped and T-shaped loading stubs are designed to produce right-hand circular polarization (RHCP) and left-hand circular polarization (LHCP) in the respective bands. The antenna simultaneously activates orthogonal \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{10}/{\mathrm{TM}}_{01}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{30}/{\mathrm{TM}}_{21}$$\end{document} modes. Arrow shaped stubs cause reduction in resonant frequency of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{30}$$\end{document} mode and cause \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{21}$$\end{document} mode; T shaped stubs accurately tune the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{10}$$\end{document} mode resonant frequency for stable operation under both RHCP and LHCP. The shapes of the stubs permit independent adjustment of the operating bands. To illustrate the concept, 2 × 2 and 4 × 4 antenna arrays at 18.55 and 30.85 GHz were constructed and tested with specifications accurate to the proposed design. The antenna is effective at both low and high frequencies, showing that the area for radiation has been exploited well.

The proposed antenna is highly useful for LEO satellites because it offers a compact, lightweight, and low-profile design that fits within the strict size and weight constraints of satellite platforms, while providing dual-band operation in the K- and Ka-bands, which are widely used for high-capacity satellite communication links. Its ability to generate dual circular polarization (RHCP at the lower band and LHCP at the upper band) ensures robust communication by minimizing polarization mismatch and multipath effects caused by satellite motion and atmospheric conditions. Furthermore, the simple single-patch structure with stub-loading achieves wide impedance and axial ratio bandwidths without requiring complex multi-layer feeding networks, making it not only cost-effective and easier to fabricate but also highly reliable for long-term space applications where efficiency, stability, and scalability to arrays are essential.

A 0.08 mm thin bonding layer of FR-27 is used to bond middle and lower substrates.The geometric parameters of the L-probe feed (namely its length, height, and placement) are precisely adjusted to balance the coupling into the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{30}$$\end{document}​ and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{21}$$\end{document}​ modes and enforce an approximate 90° phase difference across the upper frequency band, despite their large natural frequency separation. The probe length primarily controls capacitive coupling, while the probe height adjusts inductive loading, together shaping the gradual phase progression so that the required quadrature condition is met at the design frequency. Practical trade-offs arise because increasing probe height can broaden bandwidth but reduce efficiency, and extending probe length can enhance coupling yet risk mismatch or unwanted mode excitation. These adjustments are interdependent with the arrow-shaped and T-shaped stubs, which shift and reconfigure the resonant frequencies and field distributions of the modes: arrow stubs bring \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{30}$$\end{document}​ closer to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{21}$$\end{document}​ to ease phase alignment, while T-stubs fine-tune lower-band behavior without overly perturbing the higher band. Thus, the optimal CP performance emerges from co-optimization, where excessive tuning of the probe or stubs may stabilize one band but degrade the other, making careful geometric synergy essential.

The high frequency separation between the two bands (18.6 GHz and 30.8 GHz) in the proposed dual-band microstrip antenna array is strategically chosen to enable full-duplex communication for 6G mmWave applications in autonomous aerial vehicles (AAVs) and low Earth orbit (LEO) satellites. This significant separation minimizes inter-channel interference and enhances channel isolation, as it allows the antenna to operate simultaneously in the K- and Ka-bands without overlap, ensuring robust and reliable communication. By leveraging the distinct resonant modes of the antenna, tuned by arrow-shaped and T-shaped stubs, the design achieves independent control of each band, optimizing performance for high-capacity, low-latency links critical for advanced wireless networks, while maintaining compact size and compatibility with standard PCB manufacturing processes.

The excitation of degenerate modes in a circularly polarized (CP) antenna directly influences both gain and beamwidth: when the two orthogonal modes are excited with equal amplitude and a 90° phase difference, they combine constructively to produce broadside CP radiation with gain and beamwidth like the dominant linear mode. However, if there is amplitude imbalance or phase deviation, part of the radiated power couples into unwanted polarization components, leading to a reduction in effective CP gain. Additionally, when higher-order degenerate modes are involved, improper tuning may introduce side lobes or grating lobes, which can distort the pattern and narrow the main beamwidth, whereas careful stub-loading or perturbation suppresses these effects and maintains a stable 3-dB axial ratio beamwidth.

Generating CP wave with TM30/TM21 using L-Probe feed

For the generation of a circularly polarized (CP) wave the phase difference of about 90° between the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{30}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{21}$$\end{document} modes is critical even in the presence of two pairs of degenerate modes (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{30}$$\end{document}/\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathrm{TM}}_{21}$$\end{document}). The task of generating the CP wave is complicated by the large distance between the resonant frequencies of these modes, which, in this case, might be as great as 4 GHz, according to [32]. The L-probe feed is used to guarantee a gradual phase variation for each resonant mode [28, 30]. This slow phase difference variation allows for an approximate 90° phase difference to be achieved without necessity for the resonant frequencies to be perfectly aligned. As compared to direct feed the L-probe feed provides extra design flexibility to influence the coupling capacitances (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${C}_{u,pad}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${C}_{v,pad}$$\end{document}) and the turn ratios (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${N}_{u}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${N}_{v}$$\end{document}) of each degenerate mode.

The turn-ratios \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${N}_{u}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${N}_{v}$$\end{document}​ are determined by how effectively the x-directed L-probe feed couples’ energy into the two orthogonal resonant modes of the patch antenna. Conceptually, the L-probe excitation is decomposed into virtual feeds along the u- and v-axes, and the overlap between the probe’s electric field distribution and the modal fields dictates the coupling strength. In the equivalent circuit model, these ratios appear as transformer turn-ratios that link the external feed to the modal impedances, and they can be extracted by matching the input impedance of the circuit to that obtained from simulation or measurement. Practically, full-wave electromagnetic simulation or experimental analysis is used to evaluate the relative excitation of the u- and v-modes, from which \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${N}_{u}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${N}_{v}$$\end{document}​ are derived.

To explain the effects of the indirect feed on each resonant mode, the x-direction L-probe is conceptually divided into virtual L-probe feeds with u- and v-excitations. The coupling effects caused by the L-probe are split out into different components that investigate u- and v-excitations, respectively, on the assumption that the L-probe feed admits a decomposition into independent u- and v-excitation feeds. For the 4 × 4 array, the beamwidths in the lower frequency band are 29° and 41° for φ = 0° and φ = 90° planes, respectively, in the higher frequency band these become narrow about 12° and 13°, respectively, in the φ = 0° and φ = 90° planes.

The simulated realized gains increase in the lower and higher frequency bands will be 6.1 dB and 5.0 dB respectively, when the number of the elements are increased from 2 × 2 to 4 × 4 in the proposed antenna array. Expansion of the array to 8 × 8 configuration gives more gains improvements of circa 5.7 dB and 4.5 dB leading to total gains of approximately 21.5 dB and 19.8 dB for the lower and higher frequency bands, respectively. These results are in accordance with [35] theoretical predictions. For the higher frequencies range, the performance of the 4 × 4 array is degraded due to the roughness of the surface patterns on the substrate, and the actual gain is lower than can be simulated. On the other hand, the ME dipole antenna array that is proposed in [30] includes dual-band and dual-circularly-polarized performance but it has larger size than the proposed array with less gain and efficiency.

The cavity antenna with dumbbell-shaped patch in [31] successfully enables a compact design and high efficiency though requires the fabrication of several layers and a complicated feeding network. Also, in the analogous proposed SIW cavity antenna in [32], despite having a compact construction, size constraints significantly deteriorate the assembling of an array. Especially for satellite missions at low Earth orbit (LEO), the need for broad impedance bandwidth in dual-band antennas is discussed in [35] and [36], where it highlights material property shifts due to temperature fluctuations. As a contrast, the narrowband antenna presented in this work—having the selective frequency response and narrowed power output—increases reception opportunities because of the efficient quenching of a wide range frequency noise. It is consequently particularly beneficial for the specific scenarios described in this work. The design is low profile, compact in size giving comprehensive performance in key areas.

Conclusions

This article presents a compact, dual band circularly polarized (CP) microstrip patch antenna for autonomous aerial vehicle–satellite communication at the K- and Ka-bands. By extracting various resonant modes, the antenna can incorporate dual-CP functionality making the design less complicated and smaller. Utilizing these stubs, the antenna provides excellent control of the relevant operating frequencies in both low and high bands. To test the effectiveness of the antenna 2 × 2 and 4 × 4 array prototypes were built and tested at 18.6 GHz and 30.9 GHz. The agreement of measured and modeled data for both left-hand and right-hand circular polarization bands demonstrates the effectiveness of the design and its availability for applicative deployment.

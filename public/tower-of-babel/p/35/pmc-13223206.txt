High-precision automated grading of flue-cured tobacco leaves based on hierarchical feature fusion
Authors: Shunpeng Pang, Xiaowei Xin, Wei Ge, Yonghui Zhang, Junhua Jia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate grading of flue-cured tobacco leaves is crucial for tobacco quality control and industrial applications. However, traditional manual grading is subjective, inefficient, and labor-intensive, while existing deep learning-based methods often fail to capture multi-scale complementary features, leading to limited grading precision. To address these issues, this paper proposes a novel deep learning framework that integrates multiple pre-trained architectures with hierarchical feature fusion for robust tobacco leaf classification. Specifically, the framework leverages three complementary backbone networks, incorporates a convolutional block attention module to enhance feature discriminability via channel and spatial attention mechanisms, and designs a hierarchical feature fusion module with learnable attention weights to adaptively combine low-level, mid-level, and high-level features. Experimental results confirm that the proposed method achieves superior performance in flue-cured tobacco leaf grading, boasting a remarkable accuracy of 99.95% and effectively capturing the subtle visual characteristics essential for tobacco leaf quality assessment. In conclusion, the proposed architecture provides a comprehensive and reliable solution for flue-cured tobacco leaf grading, with potential applications extended to other fine-grained visual recognition tasks in agricultural product classification.Keywords: Tobacco classification, Deep learning, Feature extractionSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Flue-cured tobacco stands as one of the world’s most economically significant agricultural crops, serving as a critical livelihood pillar for millions of smallholder farmers in developing economies1. The quality of flue-cured tobacco leaves directly dictates the sensory attributes (e.g., flavor complexity, aroma profile, and combustion stability), commercial grading, and market competitiveness of downstream tobacco products, thereby underpinning price negotiation across the supply chain and the precision formulation of cigarette recipes. Accurate and consistent leaf grading is thus not merely a technical step, but a cornerstone of sustainable tobacco production and industrial quality control2.

Traditional manual grading relies on expert visual inspection of phenotypic traits (e.g., color, texture, venation)3, but it suffers from inherent limitations: it is time-consuming, labor-intensive, and highly subjective, leading to inconsistent results across graders and environmental conditions. Moreover, the manual process is inherently slow, with a single grader capable of evaluating only 20–30 leaves per minute, a throughput that fails to meet the demands of large-scale, high-speed tobacco processing facilities4,5. These drawbacks not only escalate operational costs but also introduce inconsistencies in grading standards that disrupt supply chain efficiency and erode producer trust.

With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence, a variety of cutting-edge technologies including computer vision6–8, neural architecture search9, and intelligent perception10 have been widely deployed in industrial applications, injecting fresh productivity into the industrial sector. Against the backdrop of tobacco grading, deep learning-based automated grading methods have emerged as a promising alternative11, with convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and vision transformers (ViTs) demonstrating improved performance in tobacco leaf grading task. However, existing approaches still face critical bottlenecks that limit their applicability. For example, single-model architectures (e.g., CNNs or ViTs alone) often fail to capture the multi-scale, heterogeneous features of tobacco leaves. CNNs excel at local texture extraction but struggle with global contextual patterns, while ViTs capture global dependencies but overlook fine-grained structural details. Furthermore, many models exhibit poor generalization across diverse leaf samples, particularly when confronted with variations in growing regions, curing conditions, and pest-induced phenotypic anomalies, leading to inconsistent grading accuracy across production batches. These persistent gaps create an urgent imperative for a more precise, robust, and scalable automated grading framework, which can systematically capture the subtle, multi-dimensional quality features of flue-cured tobacco leaves while mitigating the subjectivity and inefficiency of manual grading.

Hence, in order to tackle the issues outlined above, a hierarchical fusion framework enhanced with attention mechanisms is proposed in this paper. Specifically, fistly, we developed an integrated grading framework that leverages three pre-trained models (ResNet50, ViT-B/16, DenseNet121) to extract complementary visual features: local texture details (ResNet50), global contextual patterns (ViT-B/16), and fine-grained structural characteristics (DenseNet121). Then, the integration of the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) enables the model to dynamically emphasize discriminative channel features and spatial regions, reducing noise and highlighting quality-relevant information. Finally, a hierarchical feature fusion module was proposed that adaptively combines low-level texture, mid-level context, and high-level semantic representations using learnable attention weights, enabling the model to capture the complex, multi-scale traits critical for tobacco leaf grading. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that our method outperforms existing state-of-the-art approaches, providing a reliable technical solution for automated flue-cured tobacco leaf grading.

A key novelty of the proposed framework lies in its multi-model complementary feature extraction, which, unlike single-model deep learning approaches, integrates features from both CNN and Transformer architectures to simultaneously capture local structural details and global contextual information—two types of critical information essential for accurate flue-cured tobacco leaf grading. Furthermore, the framework incorporates an attention-enhanced hierarchical fusion mechanism, where the combination of the CBAM and a hierarchical fusion module with learnable weights constitutes a novel design that dynamically prioritizes key features across different scales, effectively addressing the limitations of static feature fusion in existing methods.

The proposed hierarchical fusion framework with attention mechanisms offers distinct advantages over existing flue-cured tobacco leaf grading methods, spanning both technical performance and practical applicability. First, by integrating three pre-trained models, it enables complementary feature capture: ResNet50 extracts local texture details, ViT-B/16 captures global contextual patterns, and DenseNet121 retrieves fine-grained structural characteristics, ensuring that no critical quality-related information across different scales is overlooked. Furthermore, the incorporation of the CBAM facilitates attention-driven feature refinement, dynamically emphasizing discriminative channel features and spatial regions to suppress irrelevant background noise, thereby enhancing the model’s ability to identify subtle phenotypic differences that are critical for accurate grading. In addition, the novel hierarchical feature fusion module with learnable attention weights enables dynamic hierarchical fusion, adaptively balancing low-level texture details, mid-level contextual patterns, and high-level semantic representations to accommodate the complex and varied phenotypic traits of flue-cured tobacco leaves. This structured, multi-scale feature processing pipeline ultimately leads to improved grading precision and robustness, as the model can capture subtle, multi-dimensional quality characteristics, resulting in higher grading accuracy and stronger generalization performance across diverse leaf samples and varying environmental conditions. From a practical industrial perspective, the automated framework also enables efficient and objective grading, eliminating the subjectivity, inefficiency, and high labor costs associated with manual grading, while ensuring consistent, reproducible results that meet the demands of large-scale tobacco production and quality control.

Related work

This section conducts an examination of prior research pertaining to tobacco leaf grading. The relevant studies are organized according to data modalities (image-based approaches12, spectral-based approaches, multi-modal and hybrid approaches) and the grading models utilized.

Image-based methods Computer vision technology enables efficient image analysis and understanding by extracting diverse visual features from images, encompassing a series of classic and cutting-edge algorithms such as image inpainting13, cross-modality and cross-region mutual guidance14, and difference convolution-based encoders15. Early work in automating tobacco-leaf grading focused on digital image analysis, extracting features such as color, shape, size, and texture from leaf images. For example, a fuzzy-comprehensive-evaluation method16 was proposed to classify flue-cured tobacco leaves by combining image features and fuzzy logic, achieving around 94% accuracy on training data (and 72% on unseen leaves). Similarly, a convolutional neural network (CNN) approach17 was applied to grade flue-cured leaves into three classes, obtaining 85.1% accuracy on a small test set. More recently, a field-capable deep learning model18 based on MobileNetV1 with a feature pyramid network and attention mechanism was used to detect maturity of tobacco leaves in complex field environments; this method reached 96.3% accuracy, indicating good generalization to real-world scenarios. Intelligent flue-cured tobacco grading based on deep densely convolutional network19 achieves a classification accuracy of 99.9%. Moreover, to ensure the quality of tobacco products, an automated grading system20,21 based on deep learning is proposed, which captures both reflectance and transmittance images of tobacco leaves and uses DenseNet121 for grading. The reflectance image provides information on texture, color, and shape, while the transmittance image reflects leaf thickness, and aligning these images enhances the feature extraction of convolutional neural networks for more accurate grading. In the tobacco factory setting, a specialized CNN system ”InspectionV3”22 was proposed to perform real-time grading of cured leaves across 20 quality classes, achieving 97% accuracy.

With the advancement of Transformer-based models23, the accuracy of tobacco leaf grading has been further improved. Recently, Xu, Qiang, et al.24 proposed an intelligent tobacco curing status recognition system based on the Swin-Transformer v2 hybrid model, which achieved an average recognition accuracy of 83% by pre-training a Transformer backbone network combined with color analysis and Fourier filter modules to extract features and perform grading. This breakthrough effectively transforms traditional manual experience-based curing monitoring tasks into intelligent processes powered by deep learning. Another study25 leverages a pure Transformer model to map cigarette manufacturing process parameters to chemical outcomes, accurately predicting components like CO and NI with an R over 0.99 and outperforming models such as MLP and 1DCNN. By using multi-head attention to process sequential data and learn relationships between manufacturing settings and gas components, this work demonstrates the effectiveness of Transformer models for tobacco-related prediction tasks, providing a data-driven surrogate for quick and precise smoke component forecasting. Similarly, Zhang et al. proposed TCSRNet26, a compact and efficient network incorporating transformer-inspired components. These include an Inverted Residual Structure modified by Inception modules and a Multi-scale Adaptive Attention Module, which is utilized for the recognition of the tobacco leaf drying stage. TCSRNet attains a recognition accuracy of 90.3% with low computational complexity, rendering it highly appropriate for deployment in resource-constrained environments in the tobacco industry. These studies indicate that transformers present substantial advantages compared to traditional computer vision models, particularly in capturing subtle, intricate, and long-range features that are crucial for precise tobacco grading and quality control.

Beyond pure grading, image-based techniques have also been employed in broader plant phenotyping tasks (e.g., leaf segmentation,leaf maturity1,27). These tasks utilize edge- grading or region segmentation methods28 to distinguish leaf boundaries from the background or overlapping leaves. These segmentation methods can be relevant as preprocessing steps in leaf-based grading systems.

Spectral-based methods Spectral-based methods exploit internal leaf properties such as reflectance, absorbance, elemental composition, or metabolic profiles. Near-infrared and hyperspectral imaging are widely used because they reveal subtle chemical variations that are invisible to RGB imaging.

A handheld NIR spectroscopy device was employed in on - site tobacco leaf grading. When combined with sparse representation grading, it could rapidly classify leaves of different grades. This method29 was particularly attractive for field use as it required no sample preparation and provided fast predictions. Additionally, stable isotope ratios and elemental fingerprinting, in combination with multivariate discriminant analysis, have been employed to trace the origin of tobacco leaves, attaining an initial grading accuracy of up to approximately 98.8% and a cross-validation accuracy of approximately 95.1%30. This approach31 achieved an accuracy of approximately 95.6% while keeping the training time at a relatively low level. A real-time grading method of roasted tobacco quality based on near infrared spectroscopy32. In the context of maturity grading, a recent study33 integrated proximal hyperspectral imaging with a 3D CNN to assign pixel-level maturity labels, attaining an exceptionally high accuracy rate (approximately 99.93%). Wei,Deng et al.21 used hyperspectral image and CNN to realize flue-cured tobacco grading. For origin identification, a broad learning system augmented with a Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy subsystem was applied to Near-infrared spectral data for the grading of tobacco origin More recently, metabolomics-based approaches34 have been utilized to differentiate cigar-leaf samples from diverse regions through the analysis of volatile and non-volatile compounds.

Recent advancements in automating tobacco leaf grading through visible light image analysis are limited by visual similarity, while near-infrared spectral images may provide valuable chemical composition-related information for accurate grading. For this, Zhang, Xiaobing, et al.35 proposed an end-to-end multispectral feature enhancement network for automated tobacco leaf grading. The network incorporates a principal component from near-infrared spectral images along with common visible light images to encapsulate chemical information.

Spectral techniques have also been utilized in the context of disease detection. For instance, hyperspectral data have been employed in conjunction with support vector machines (SVM) and random forests (RF) to classify tobacco leaves under virus-infected conditions (e.g., TMV, PVY). This application showcases the efficacy of spectral features in leaf health grading36.

Multi-modal and hybrid methods Certain more sophisticated methods aim to integrate visual and spectral (or chemical) modalities to leverage complementary information (appearance + internal composition). Although not yet extensively employed in tobacco-leaf grading, hybrid fusion approaches are prevalent in agricultural remote sensing and plant phenotyping, which combine spectral bands, texture, shape, and contextual features. The integration of multi-modal data frequently results in enhanced accuracy and robustness under variable conditions, particularly when one modality is deteriorated (e.g., illumination alterations impact RGB images). For example, a tobacco grading system37 using image processing and CNN achieves 96.25% accuracy in leaf grading while taking only 7.43 ms per leaf, outperforming existing methods through automated detection, segmentation and grading of tobacco leaves. The system successfully detects single and multiple leaves in various positions/scales, with experiments showing superior results compared to current grading approaches.

Another relevant research direction pertains to plant grading or organ segmentation utilizing point-cloud38, multispectral39, or 3D data40. Algorithms such as SVM, RF, or deep neural networks process multi-dimensional features for grading tasks.

Integrating diffusion models into tobacco grading presents a robust solution for image-based disease detection and grading, especially under challenging agricultural circumstances where image quality frequently deteriorates. Instead of functioning directly as classifiers, diffusion models are employed to enhance image quality and augment datasets, thereby substantially improving the performance of subsequent grading networks such as CNNs.

In summary, spectral-based methods can explore the internal chemical information of tobacco leaves, but suffer from the high cost of detection equipment, making large-scale popularization difficult. Spectral data are susceptible to environmental interference with poor stability. Meanwhile, multi-modal hybrid methods are still in the preliminary exploration stage. The cross-modality fusion mechanism is imperfect, and the mining of complementary features between image, spectrum and other modalities is insufficient, which easily leads to feature redundancy. Most related studies are only verified in laboratories and lack industrial large-scale testing. It is difficult to balance model accuracy and deployment efficiency, resulting in insufficient adaptability of new technologies. Hence, Image-based grading methods are currently the most prevalent and widely adopted approaches in tobacco leaf grading, primarily due to their advantages of convenient operation, easy access to image data, and strong adaptability to multiple scenarios. However, existing models—including traditional CNN-based architectures and even some simple Transformer-derived variants—have an limitation in that a single model struggles to effectively capture the global features of tobacco leaves. Therefore, novel and innovative approaches are urgently required to address this critical limitation, so as to further improve the grading accuracy and robustness of image-based methods and better meet the practical demands of precise tobacco leaf grading in industrial production.

Flue-cured tobacco leaf grading network

The proposed flue-cured tobacco leaf grading network (FTNet) consists of three main components: (1) Multi-scale feature extractors, (2) Channel and spatial attention mechanisms, and (3) Hierarchical feature fusion module. The overall architecture is illustrated in Fig. 2.

Image of various tobacco samples for grading.

The workflow of the model is as follows: First, the input tobacco leaf image is fed into three heterogeneous feature extractors (ResNet50, ViT-B/16, and DenseNet121) to capture complementary feature representations, including local texture details (from CNN-based extractors) and global semantic information (from Transformer-based extractor). Second, the CBAM attention mechanism is embedded in each feature extractor to adaptively enhance discriminative features and suppress redundant information. Third, the multi-dimensional features from different extractors are projected to a unified dimension and fused through a hierarchical fusion strategy that integrates low-level, mid-level, and high-level feature interactions. Finally, the fused feature vector is input into the grading head to output the final category prediction.

Model initialization and training details

The model adopts a hierarchical initialization strategy: the backbone feature extractors (ResNet50, ViT-B/16, DenseNet121) are initialized with pre-trained weights on ImageNet-1K, while convolutional layers and fully connected layers in self-designed modules (CBAM attention, hierarchical fusion module, grading head) use Kaiming normal initialization, with bias parameters set to 0. For training, cross-entropy loss is used as the objective function, the AdamW optimizer is configured with an initial learning rate of 1e-4 and weight decay of 0.01, and the CosineAnnealingLR scheduler (T_max=10) dynamically adjusts the learning rate. A early stopping mechanism is introduced to prevent overfitting, training is terminated early if the validation accuracy does not improve for 20 consecutive epochs, and the model with the best validation performance is retained. The batch size is set to 64, with 6 worker threads for data loading; the model is trained for a maximum of 300 epochs, and training metrics (loss, accuracy) are reported every 5 epochs to monitor convergence.

Ablation experiments

To systematically validate the contribution of each core component in the proposed FTNet for the task of grading of flue-cured tobacco leaves, we designed a set of ablation experiments. By sequentially removing critical modules and evaluating the performance of the derived model variants, we isolated the impact of the CBAM (Convolutional Block Attention Module) and the HFF (Hierarchical Feature Fusion) module-two key designs of the FTNet. All experiments were conducted on the folded flue-cured tobacco leaf dataset (covering 24 grading categories) with strictly consistent training configurations: the same optimizer (Adam), learning rate (1e-4), batch size (64), training epochs (300). This ensures that performance differences between variants are solely attributed to the presence or absence of the tested components. Accuracy was adopted as the primary evaluation metric, as it directly reflects the model’s overall correctness in distinguishing different types of folded flue-cured tobacco leaves. To test the efficacy of the core components, four model variants were designed: FTNet (the full model integrating both the CBAM attention mechanism and HFF module), FTNet-Hf (FTNet without CBAM, retaining only the HFF module), FTNet-Nu (a baseline model without either CBAM or HFF, using only raw features from the backbones), and FTNet-Ca (FTNet without the HFF module, retaining only CBAM).

As Fig. 4 shows that the CBAM attention mechanism significantly enhances feature discriminability for flue-cured tobacco leaves-removing CBAM from the full FTNet (resulting in FTNet-Hf) reduced accuracy by 0.81%, while FTNet-Ca (retaining CBAM but removing fusion) outperformed the baseline FTNet-Nu (no CBAM or fusion) by 0.44%, confirming CBAM’s role in emphasizing critical visual cues like vein patterns and color gradients. The HFF module also adds value, as FTNet-Hf (retaining HFF but removing CBAM) outperformed FTNet-Nu by 0.17% by integrating complementary features from ResNet50 (local details), ViT (global context), and DenseNet121 (textures). Most notably, CBAM and HFF exhibit a critical synergistic effect: the full FTNet achieved the highest accuracy of 99.95%, surpassing FTNet-Ca by 0.54% and FTNet-Hf by 0.81%, which is essential for distinguishing subtle differences between flue-cured tobacco leaf classes.

Analysis of multi-scale feature extraction

In the task of tobacco leaf grading, the quality of feature representation directly determines the performance of the grading model. To capture more comprehensive and discriminative feature information, this study employs three feature extractors with distinct architectural designs-ResNet50, DenseNet121, and ViT-B/16-and integrates their outputs via a hierarchical feature fusion mechanism. The following content first analyzes the heterogeneous feature expression patterns of each single extractor, then validates the effectiveness of the fusion strategy based on the final feature importance results, laying a foundation for optimizing the tobacco leaf grading system.

Feature extractors with different network architectures exhibit significant heterogeneity in feature representation due to differences in their design paradigms. From the results of single-model feature vectors as shown in Fig. 6a–c : ResNet50, relying on the residual learning mechanism, presents a uniform and stable distribution of its feature vectors across the full dimension (0-2000), which reflects its ability to capture local textures and hierarchical structural features of images in a balanced way. DenseNet121, based on the feature propagation strategy of dense connections, shows a distribution pattern of ”sparse in low dimensions and dense in high dimensions” for its feature vectors, demonstrating a tendency to focus on deep fine-grained features (e.g., texture details and hierarchical morphology of tobacco leaves). ViT-B/16 models long-range dependencies via the attention mechanism, and its feature vectors have a relatively limited dimension range (0-800) with obvious positive-negative fluctuations and sharp peaks-this reveals its advantage in capturing global semantic correlations (e.g., the overall morphology of tobacco leaves and category-level patterns).

(b) Feature vector extracted by DenseNet121, illustrating the feature distribution with an emphasis on deep-grained details. (c) Feature vector extracted by ViT-B/16, depicting the feature pattern oriented to global semantic correlations.

After multi-model feature fusion, the results of final feature importance, as presented in Fig. 7a, show that high-importance features are widely and dispersedly distributed in the dimension range of 0?500. This phenomenon verifies the effectiveness of the hierarchical feature fusion mechanism: the balanced local features of ResNet50, the deep fine-grained features of DenseNet121, and the global semantic features of ViT-B/16 are complementarily integrated. This integration not only retains the key discriminative information of each single model but also compensates for the inherent limitations of a single model in terms of feature coverage and discriminative power. The finally generated fused features possess a more comprehensive discriminative dimension and stronger category discrimination ability, providing high-quality feature support for the subsequent tobacco leaf grading task. Fig. 7c is a heatmap of the final fused feature, presenting the feature distribution of the leaf with color coding. As can be seen from the figure, the central region of the leaf (especially the part near the petiole) is red, indicating that this region has the highest importance in the final fused feature and the most prominent feature information; it gradually transitions to yellow, green, and blue towards the leaf edges and other regions, with the feature importance decreasing in sequence. This heatmap intuitively demonstrates the spatial distribution pattern of the fused feature on the leaf, which is helpful for identifying the most valuable regions in the leaf for analysis.

To summarize, the inherent design disparities among ResNet50, DenseNet121, and ViT-B/16 give rise to distinct strengths in feature extraction-with ResNet50 excelling at balanced local and hierarchical feature capture, DenseNet121 focusing on deep fine-grained details, and ViT-B/16 specializing in global semantic correlations. The hierarchical multi-model fusion leverages these complementary advantages: by integrating the three types of features, it not only preserves the key discriminative information of each single model but also addresses the limitations of individual architectures in feature coverage and discriminative power. The widely distributed high-importance features in the fused result confirm that the integrated features possess more comprehensive discriminative dimensions, which ultimately provides robust support for improving the accuracy of subsequent tobacco leaf grading tasks.

Perturbation experiments

To evaluate the robustness of the tobacco leaf classification model under non-ideal practical scenarios, perturbation testing is essential, as tobacco leaf images collected in real production environments are often affected by uncontrollable factors-such as variable lighting (e.g., fluctuations in brightness or color temperature), physical morphological distortions (e.g., leaf rotation, local damage during transportation), and sensor noise (e.g., Gaussian or salt-and-pepper noise from imaging devices). These perturbations can directly degrade model performance, making it critical to quantify the model’s tolerance to such interferences.

To enhance the model’s inherent disturbance resistance, perturbations are integrated into the training data augmentation pipeline-exposing the model to diverse perturbed samples during training enables it to learn perturbation-invariant features (e.g., rotation-invariant leaf textures, brightness-insensitive color patterns).

We adopted a single-perturbation strategy: in each independent experiment, only one perturbation type (lighting, shape, or noise) was consistently applied to both the training and test sets. Specifically, during training, the selected perturbation (e.g., lighting adjustments) was incorporated into the data augmentation pipeline using parameters specified in Table 5; during testing, the same perturbation type (with identical parameter ranges) was applied to the test set to assess the model’s adaptation to that specific disturbance. This design enables the isolation and quantification of the model’s learning adaptation to each individual perturbation.

Conclusions and discussion

In conclusion, in order to tackle the issue of flue-cured tobacco leaf grading, this study proposes the FTNet model and validates its effectiveness through comprehensive experiments. Regarding the results obtained, the proposed FTNet model achieves exceptional performance in flue-cured tobacco leaf grading, boasting a remarkable accuracy of 99.95%. Specifically, the proposed model demonstrates superior ability in capturing subtle phenotypic characteristics of flue-cured tobacco leaves (e.g., texture details, color uniformity, and venation patterns) that are essential for accurate quality grading. Furthermore, under controlled single-type perturbation training, the model exhibits remarkable resilience when training and testing data share a consistent perturbation domain, maintaining near-perfect accuracy even for tobacco leaf samples with minor variations caused by curing conditions or environmental factors.

Regarding the proposed method, the core innovation of FTNet lies in its integrated architectural design, which is specifically optimized to address the key challenges in flue-cured tobacco leaf grading. First, the model leverages three complementary backbone networks (ResNet50, DenseNet121, and Vision Transformer) to extract multi-scale features: ResNet50 captures local texture details of tobacco leaves, DenseNet121 focuses on deep fine-grained characteristics, and Vision Transformer models global contextual relationships (e.g., overall leaf shape and color distribution). Second, the incorporation of the CBAM enhances feature discriminability by emphasizing quality-related channels and spatial regions, effectively suppressing irrelevant background noise and highlighting subtle differences between tobacco leaf grades. Third, the hierarchical feature fusion module with learnable attention weights adaptively integrates low-level texture features, mid-level contextual patterns, and high-level semantic representations, creating a comprehensive and discriminative feature set that compensates for the inherent limitations of any single model. This design is particularly critical for tobacco leaf grading, as it addresses the complexity and heterogeneity of tobacco leaf phenotypic traits.

Finally, regarding the significance of the method and results, the findings of this study hold both academic and industrial value for flue-cured tobacco leaf grading and related agricultural product classification tasks. Academically, the proposed FTNet enriches the research on deep learning-based fine-grained image classification, providing a new paradigm for multi-model feature fusion and attention-enhanced feature refinement in agricultural product quality assessment. Specifically, it validates that hierarchical feature fusion of complementary architectures can effectively improve the accuracy and robustness of tobacco leaf grading, addressing the limitations of single-model approaches in capturing multi-scale phenotypic features. Industrially, the high accuracy and partial robustness of FTNet offer a practical and reliable automated solution for flue-cured tobacco leaf grading, which can address the subjective, labor-intensive, and inefficient issues of traditional manual grading methods. This will not only reduce labor costs and improve grading consistency in the tobacco supply chain but also provide strong technical support for tobacco quality control, breeding optimization, and cigarette recipe formulation. To address the identified limitations and further enhance the model’s applicability in actual tobacco production, future work should focus on explicitly guiding the model’s feature learning process tailored to flue-cured tobacco leaf characteristics. Specifically, we will leverage the complementary feature representations of the backbone networks through targeted regularization or adversarial training on compounded perturbations that simulate real-world industrial scenarios (e.g., mixed perturbations from curing defects and environmental interference). This will enable the model to build invariance to a wider array of corruptions commonly encountered in tobacco production, ultimately constructing a classification system that is both highly accurate and inherently robust. In addition, future research will focus on the lightweighting of deep models to address the issues of long training time and difficulties in model deployment.

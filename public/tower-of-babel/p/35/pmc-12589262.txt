Non-invasive left ventricular myocardial work indices in preterm neonates born to mothers with severe preeclampsia in pregnancy
Authors: Huiyun Chen, Xiaoyu Li, Fangcan Sun, Jingjing Zhang, Weizeyu Liu, Xinran Dong, Hong Li, Qiuqin Xu, Bing Han
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe study utilized non-invasive myocardial work indices to investigate myocardial injury in infants born to mothers with severe preeclampsia (SPE) and to explore the duration of this myocardial damage during the neonatal period.MethodsThis prospective study included 34 preterm infants born to mothers with SPE and 28 preterm infants born to mothers without severe pregnancy complications (termed “controls”). Echocardiography was performed in infants within 24 h of birth, then again at 48–72 h and 14–28 days, to obtain echocardiographic parameters. Two-dimensional speckle-tracking echocardiography (2D STE) was performed to construct a non-invasive left ventricular pressure–strain loop based on two-dimensional myocardial strain and non-invasive left ventricular systolic pressure measurements, allowing for the calculation of myocardial work indices.ResultsWithin 24 h after delivery, left ventricular stroke volume (SV), cardiac output (CO), and left ventricular global work index (GWI) in newborns born of mothers with SPE were significantly reduced compared to controls, while mitral E/e′ and left ventricular longitudinal strain were increased. At 48–72 h after birth, mitral E/e′ and left ventricular global waste work (GWW) increased, and global work efficiency (GWE) was reduced in infants of mothers with SPE. During the 14–28 days of the neonatal period, left ventricular SV and CO were reduced in the offspring of mothers with SPE and mitral E peak velocity, and E/e′ and left ventricular GWW were increased.ConclusionThe adverse intrauterine environment caused by SPE can lead to persistent impaired left ventricular function in preterm infants during the neonatal period.Keywords: Myocardial work, Echocardiography, Preeclampsia, Premature birth

What does this study adds to the clinical work?

The adverse intrauterine environment of severe preeclampsia can lead to persistent impaired left ventricular function in preterm infants during the neonatal period. The result reminds us to pay attention to the cardiac function of these newborns and provides a research direction to find the mechanism of the occurrence of impaired cardiac function in the offspring delivered by patients with severe preeclampsia.

Introduction

The developmental origins of the health and disease hypothesis state that exposure to an adverse intrauterine environment can result in adaptive fetal programming or metabolic imprinting, leading to permanent changes in fetal organs’ structure, metabolism, and physiology. This predisposes offspring to cardiovascular, metabolic, and endocrine diseases in later life [1]. Preeclampsia (PE), a common pregnancy complication, creates an adverse intrauterine environment that increases the susceptibility of offspring to cardiovascular diseases (CVD). Therefore, PE has become an essential platform for researching placenta-originated diseases.

Previous studies have shown that exposure to a hypertensive intrauterine environment can cause immediate and long-term myocardial damage in the offspring. Echocardiography is widely used for assessing cardiac function due to its convenience and non-invasiveness and has seen significant advancements in recent years. Based on tissue Doppler, the myocardial performance index (MPI) assesses global myocardial function, with increases indicating decreased cardiac function. In addition, myocardial strain and strain rate reflect myocardial deformation, allowing for earlier detection of asymptomatic dysfunction. Fetuses exposed to PE exhibited ventricular hypertrophy and a more rounded right chamber, indicative of fetal myocardial remodeling associated with PE [2]. During the neonatal period, infants born to mothers with gestational hypertension showed lower ejection fraction, left ventricular (LV) global longitudinal strain, and LV twist, whereas there were no differences in right ventricular (RV) functional parameters [3]. However, increased MPI was observed in both the LV and RV of newborns born to mothers with PE within the first 24–48 h of life [4]. Our previous study used two-dimensional speckle-tracking echocardiography (2D STE) to evaluate myocardial strain in children aged 5–8 years born to mothers with severe PE. We found that while exposure to the severe PE-affected intrauterine environment did not significantly impact cardiac structure in premature children at 5–8 years old, these children exhibited an elevated resting heart rate, which may be associated with long-term CVD [5].

Myocardial work (MW) calculation has traditionally been limited to the area of the invasively obtained LV pressure–strain loop (PSL), restricting its routine clinical application. However, since Russell et al. proposed a novel non-invasive method for quantifying LV PSL, MW has been widely utilized for CVD diagnosis [6]. Hence, this study employed this approach to investigate myocardial injury in neonates born to mothers with SPE during pregnancy and to explore myocardial damage duration during the neonatal period.

Methods

Study population

The study population of mothers with SPE was selected from those attending the Department of Obstetrics at the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University between June 2020 and October 2021. SPE was diagnosed according to the 2020 guidelines of the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists, and the preterm infants were included accordingly [7]. Exclusion criteria encompassed pregnancies complicated by diseases that may impact fetal heart development, including gestational diabetes mellitus, maternal hematologic diseases, immune system disorders, malignant tumors, cardiac conditions, intrauterine infections, fetal congenital heart disease, or other deformities. Neonates who required mechanical ventilation at birth, had a history of asphyxia at birth, or had significant congenital heart disease were also excluded. In addition, newborns unable to cooperate during ultrasound image acquisitions due to excessive crying in both awake and quiet states were excluded. Figure 1 shows the study’s flowchart.

The flowchart of the study

The studies involving human participants were reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (Approval No.: 2020 No. 052) and Children’s Hospital of Soochow University (Approval No.: 2020KS005). Written informed consent was obtained from the individual(s) and the legal guardians/next of kin of minor(s) for the publication of any potentially identifiable images or data included in this article.

Preparation before echocardiography

Echocardiography was performed on preterm newborns within 24 h, 48–72 h, and 14–28 days after birth. The neonates’ length and weight were measured on the day of image acquisition, and their body mass index (BMI) and body surface area (BSA) were calculated. The neonatal BSA was calculated using the DuBois formula [8]: BSA = W0.425*H0.725*K, where W stands for body weight (kg), H stands for length (cm), and K is a constant value of 0.007184. Echocardiography was performed while the neonates were awake and quiet.

Non-invasive blood pressure measurement

Blood pressure (BP) was measured non-invasively on the right arm using a GE B40i monitor (GE Healthcare, Kuortaneenkatu 2, 00510 Helsinki, Finland) with an appropriate cuff size (bladder length ≈ 80% and width at least 40% of the arm circumference, covering the upper arm but not obscuring the antecubital fossa) before echocardiography in a resting state. BP was measured three times, and the means were used to construct patient-specific LV PSL.

Echocardiography

Two-dimensional echocardiography was performed using a phased-array ultrasound system (Vivid-E90, GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, United States) equipped with a 6S-D cardiac sector probe (2.4–8.0 MHz), and it was connected to a synchronous electrocardiogram (ECG). The infants were placed supine, and echocardiography was acquired with a simultaneous ECG signal. We obtained 2D data from the parasternal long-axis, short-axis views, and three standard apical views, capturing three consecutive cardiac cycles for each view. The image acquisition speed was adjusted to a range of 70–100 frames per second to optimize the quality of the grayscale image. Simultaneously, the maximum fluctuation of the infant’s heart rate during image acquisition was limited to 5 beats per minute. Measurements were performed according to the guidelines on neonatal echocardiography [9, 10]. Interventricular septum end‑diastolic thickness (IVST), left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVED), left ventricular end‑systolic diameter (LVES), left ventricular posterior wall end‑diastolic thickness (LVPWd), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), and left ventricular fraction shortening (LVFS) were measured in the parasternal long-axis view using M-mode echocardiography. Blood flow patterns were obtained in pulsed Doppler mode, and tissue Doppler spectral waveforms were obtained in tissue Doppler imaging mode. Relative wall thickness (RWT) was calculated using three formulas [11]: (1) RWTpw = 2 × LVPWd/LVED; (2) RWT ivspw = (IVST + LVPWd)/LVED; (3) RWTivs = 2 × IVST/LVED. Left ventricular mass index (LVMI) was calculated using the Devereux formula [12]: left ventricular mass (LVM) = 0.8*1.04*[(LVED + IVST + LVPWd)3 − LVED3] + 0.6; LVMI = LVM/BSA.

Echocardiographic measurement

The peak modal velocity in early diastole (E) and late diastole (A) was measured between the tips of mitral leaflets using pulse Doppler in the apical four-chamber view. Tissue Doppler imaging was used to measure peak systolic (S′) and peak early diastolic (e′) velocities of the septal mitral annulus. Left ventricular isovolumic contraction time (ICT), isovolumic relaxation time (IRT), and ejection time (ET) were measured to calculate the myocardial performance index (MPI) as follows: MPI = (ICT + IRT)/ET. The inner diameter of the left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) was measured in the parasternal long-axis view. Cardiac output (CO) and stroke volume (SV) were obtained from aortic spectral Doppler waveforms using the “LVOT Trace” algorithm.

Myocardial strain and global myocardial work

The standard apical three-, four-, and two-chamber images were imported into dedicated automated software EchoPAC PC, Version 203 (GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, United States). The myocardial region of interest (ROI) of the left ventricle was defined by the endocardial border, which was manually delineated for each dynamic image at the end of systole. If necessary, the width of the ROI could be adjusted manually to exclude the pericardium (Fig. 2). The movement of echocardiographic speckles was tracked to generate myocardial global longitudinal strain (GLS) curves of the endocardial, midcardial, and epicardial layers (Fig. 3). LV ICT and ET were determined based on the opening and closing of the aortic and mitral valves. Finally, individualized PSLs were constructed using myocardial strain curves combined with non-invasive blood pressure. Left ventricular global myocardial work indices, including global work index (GWI), global constructive work (GCW), global waste work (GWW), and global work efficiency (GWE), were then calculated (Fig. 4).

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics software version 26 (Chicago, IL, United States). For normally distributed continuous variables, a t-test was conducted to compare differences in means. Likewise, the Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare variables with non-normal distribution. Enumeration data were expressed as rates (%), and the Chi-square test was adopted. A P value < 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

The rate of cesarean delivery was higher in the SPE group compared to the control group at all three time points. In addition, newborns in the SPE group exhibited lower weight, length, BMI, and BSA compared to the control group. These differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05), as shown in Table 1.

Cardiac structure and other measurements

Regarding blood pressure (BP), a reduction in both systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) was observed in newborns delivered by mothers with SPE within 24 h of birth. However, there were no differences in BP between the two groups during the 48–72 h postpartum period. Infants born to mothers with SPE exhibit similar SBP but lower DBP and higher pulse pressure index (PPI) during the 14–28-day postpartum period. In terms of cardiac structure, LV end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD) and end-systolic diameter (LVESD) were shorter in infants born to mothers with SPE within 24 h of birth. There were no differences in heart rate (HR), the internal diameter of the pulmonary artery (PA), IVST, LVPWd, RWT, and LVMI between the two groups. The details are provided in Table 4.

Discussion

PE is characterized by a complex interplay of various mechanisms and intertwined pathways, with no definitive causative factor or pathogenesis identified thus far. Adverse intrauterine conditions resulting from compromised placental perfusion due to inadequate trophoblastic invasion, insufficient spiral artery remodeling, or other pathological disorders not only exert detrimental effects on fetal development but also enhance susceptibility to CVD. The utilization of non-invasive left ventricular myocardial work indices has emerged as a significant advancement in echocardiography for the early recognition of cardiac dysfunction, particularly in patients presenting with chest pain, dilated cardiomyopathy, hypertension, and other related conditions [13–15]. In this study, we aimed to investigate the applicability and practicality of this new technique while exploring the duration of myocardial damage during the neonatal period.

Myocardial strain and myocardial work indices in infants from PE

In this study, offspring born to mothers with SPE exhibited a decreased GWI within the first 24 h of life. The increased myocardial strain during this period may be attributed to reduced afterload. This compensates for the elevated myocardial strain and ensures optimal left ventricular pumping capacity. The findings were consistent with the BP of the infants within 24 h after birth. Non-invasive myocardial work indices were more sensitive in identifying subclinical cardiac dysfunction of these newborns delivered by mothers with SPE. At 48–72 h postpartum, preterm infants delivered by mothers with SPE had increased GWW and decreased GWE, possibly related to underlying myocardial remodeling. Following a separation period of 14–28 days from the adverse intrauterine environment of SPE, their GWI, GCW, and GWE were not significantly different from those of normal preterm infants, but GWW remained significantly increased. This indicated that subclinical LV myocardial injury persists in the neonatal period in preterm neonates born to mothers with SPE.

Left ventricular function in infants from PE

Although infants born to mothers with SPE exhibited a reduction in SV and CO within 24 h and during 14–28 days postpartum, the groups did not exhibit any significant differences in CI, suggesting no substantial impairment in left ventricular pumping function. Other indices reflecting left ventricular systolic function were also not significantly different between groups at the 3 time points observed in this study, indicating that conventional echocardiography might not be able to detect subclinical myocardial injury in neonates born to mothers with SPE during the neonatal period at an early stage.

The ratio of early diastolic transdiastolic inflow velocity to early diastolic mitral annular velocity (E/e′) is a reliable marker of LV diastolic function, and its elevation in infants born to mothers with SPE indicated the presence of LV diastolic dysfunction. Notably, the increase of mitral E peak velocity in newborns born to mothers with SPE during 14–28 days postpartum might be due to mitral valve opening delay or increased LV myocardial stiffness [16], consistent with the GWW during this period.

BP in infants from PE

BP is closely associated with CVD. The BP of infants is influenced by various factors, including birth weight, gestational age, maternal blood pressure, nutritional status, and mode of delivery. In this study, SBP and DBP in newborns of mothers with SPE were lower than those of the control group on the first day after birth. It was shown that the combination of spinal anesthesia and cesarean section decreased neonatal SBP on the first day of their life [17]. Given the critical condition of patients with SPE in this study, elective cesarean sections were predominantly performed for preterm infants to ensure the safety of both mother and fetus. In addition, a study by Herda et al. compared the BP of neonates born to mothers with preeclampsia (PE) who received labetalol for antihypertensive therapy, regardless of dosage and route of administration, during pregnancy to those who did not [18]. The results revealed that neonates whose mothers received labetalol had lower BP in the first 48 h after birth. All patients in this study had received labetalol as antihypertensive therapy. Thus, decreased BP of infants from mothers with SPE within 24 h after birth might be linked with factors such as low birth weight, cesarean section delivery, and maternal use of labetalol for antihypertensive treatment during pregnancy.

During 14–28 days after birth, the DBP of fetuses from mothers with SPE was lower than normal preterm infants, and the PPI was higher. Coronary perfusion occurs during diastole, and decreased DBP might cause coronary hypoperfusion and myocardial ischemia [19]. In addition, increased PPI indicated impaired endothelial function and increased blood vessel stiffness, which contributes to decreased GWE. These findings were consistent with the results of this study and also suggested subclinical myocardial impairment and elevated susceptibility to CVD in offspring born to mothers with SPE.

Cardiac structure in infants from PE

Cardiac growth is dependent on angiogenesis, and an imbalance in angiogenic and anti-angiogenic factors in the maternal circulation of SPE may impact fetal angiogenic capacity through the placental–fetal circulation [20], adversely affecting cardiac development. In this study, infants born to mothers with adverse intrauterine environments showed significantly shorter LVEDD and LVESD within 24 h after birth. However, there were no significant differences in cardiac structure compared to the control group after 48 h of separation from the maternal adverse intrauterine environment.

Growth and development in infants from PE

The growth and development of infants born to mothers with SPE significantly lagged behind that of normal preterm infants during the neonatal period. It has been demonstrated that fetuses of patients with pregnancy-induced hypertension are more likely to exhibit intrauterine growth retardation [21]. The findings from our previous study on preterm children aged 5–8 years delivered by mothers with SPE indicated that their weight and height still lagged significantly compared to the control group [5]. Offspring remained behind in growth and development until childhood after being separated from the adverse intrauterine environment of SPE. A retrospective study conducted by Vatten et al. revealed that offspring from PE exhibited growth retardation at birth. But intriguingly, the study also demonstrated that these offspring had higher weight and BMI during adolescence (13–19 years of age) compared to those born to normotensive pregnant women [22]. This phenomenon of rapid catch-up growth has also been observed in an animal model established by Sutton et al., which is associated with an increased risk of CVD [23].

The exclusion criteria were applied to pregnancies with diseases that could potentially impact fetal heart development, such as heart disease, congenital heart abnormalities, or other deformities, to minimize the influence of confounding factors. However, we acknowledge that SPE may increase the risk of congenital heart disease, fetal malformations, and even mortality. Our exclusion criteria led us to prefer patients less affected by SPE for this research. In addition, our study was conducted on preterm infants from a single city; further studies with multi-regions and multi-centers are needed.

The adverse intrauterine environment of SPE can lead to persistent impaired left ventricular function in preterm infants during the neonatal period. Non-invasive left myocardial work indices demonstrated higher sensitivity in detecting subclinical myocardial injury compared to conventional echocardiography. Furthermore, infants born to mothers with SPE experienced delayed growth and development during the neonatal period, significantly lagging behind normal preterm newborns. Fluctuations in BP and subclinical cardiac impairment might increase the susceptibility to cardiovascular morbid events throughout their lifespan.

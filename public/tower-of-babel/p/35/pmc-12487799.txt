Visual and Refractive Outcomes of Different Bubble Types in Deep Anterior Lamellar Keratoplasty for Macular Corneal Dystrophy
Authors: Mohamed Bahgat Goweida, Mohamed Aly Kolaib, Wael Abdel Rahman Elmenawy, Ahmed Shalaby Bardan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Purpose:To determine the variations in deep anterior lamellar keratoplasty (DALK) techniques in eyes with macular corneal dystrophy (MCD), and to compare visual outcomes between standard DALK and Descemet’s membrane (DM) baring DALK.Methods:A retrospective comparative case series was conducted, categorizing eyes into two groups: Group A, without DM baring, and Group B, with central DM baring. All DALK surgeries for MCD conducted between January 2010 and August 2020 at our institution were included in this study.Results:The study comprised 29 eyes from 18 patients with a mean follow-up period of 54.85 months. Type 1 bubble was achieved in 4 eyes (13.8%), type 2 or mixed bubbles were formed in 19 eyes (65.5%), and failed big bubble formation occurred in 6 eyes (20.7%). Group A (non-DM baring) comprised 18 eyes (62.1%), while Group B (central DM baring) included 11 eyes (37.9%). No statistically significant differences were found between the groups in terms of best-corrected visual acuity, spherical equivalent, or topographic astigmatism. However, Group B demonstrated significantly improved contrast sensitivity (P < 0.01). Group A displayed persistent or recurrent MCD opacities in 11 eyes (73.3%), whereas none were observed in Group B, demonstrating a statistically significant difference (P < 0.001).Conclusions:The incidence of type 2 or mixed bubbles in MCD cases is relatively high, facilitating central DM baring. In this series, central DM baring correlated with improved contrast sensitivity and prevented MCD opacities recurrence. Thus, it should be considered in all DALK cases for MCD if a type 2 bubble is formed.

INTRODUCTION

Macular corneal dystrophy (MCD) is an autosomal recessive stromal disorder characterized by bilateral, often symmetric, and slowly progressive accumulation of glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) deposits in the corneal stroma. This leads to diffuse stromal haze and irregular whitish deposits in the anterior and posterior layers of the corneal stroma.1

Penetrating keratoplasty (PK) has been the mainstay treatment choice to restore visual acuity in cases with advanced MCD. However, complications such as endothelial rejection and gradual graft failure have been reported.2 Recently deep anterior lamellar keratoplasty (DALK) has been introduced and even largely replaced PK as the treatment of choice for endothelium-sparing stromal dystrophies.34

The decision whether to perform PK or DALK in MCD remains a contentious topic and there are variations in the literature about the results of each technique.5 Poor postoperative visual acuity has been reported after DALK using the Melles technique. Other studies reported visual results like PK when the diseased unclear stroma is removed in DALK using the big bubble (BB) technique.26

Recent studies showed that BB DALK is not a Descemet’s membrane (DM) baring technique except in the uncommon event of type 2 BB formation. The more common situation of obtaining a type 1 BB leads to a thin sheet of stroma covering DM called pre-Descemet’s layer (PDL) or Dua’s layer. So based on this new description, after air injection in the corneal stroma, we can either obtain a type 1 BB (air between stroma and PDL), a type 2 BB (air between PDL and DM), a mixed BB (both types 1 and 2) or a failed BB.78 Evaluating DALK surgery in MCD should consider these entities, as there will be differences in the visual outcomes between different techniques due to the possible affection of the PDL with the stromal pathology.

In this study, we have retrospectively reviewed the visual outcomes of BB DALK in eyes with MCD considering the newly described corneal surgical anatomy and comparing the results according to the type of BB and the amount of residual stroma.

METHODS

The study involved a retrospective review of medical records and surgical videos of all eyes with MCD that have been managed using BB DALK in Alexandria Main University Hospital between January 2010 and August 2020. This study has been approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Alexandria Faculty of Medicine and was conducted following the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. All cases included have consented to the performed surgery and the use of the outcome data for research purposes.

The diagnosis of MCD was made based on patient and family history, and clinical examination, including multiple gray-white stromal opacities extending to the deep stroma in a hazy cornea. Patients with visually significant disease were considered for DALK regardless of the disease stage or the extent of stromal opacities.

All procedures were performed by one surgeon (MBG) using the BB technique previously described by Anwar and Teichmann9 with some modifications from the original technique, such as performing a paracentesis before bubble formation for injecting small air bubbles in the anterior chamber (AC) and using a Fogla 27-gauge air-injection cannula (Bausch and Lomb, Rochester, NY, USA) to create the BB. Eyes with failed BB were completed using the microbubble incision technique;10 where tiny stromal bubbles were used as a guide to the desired plane and manual dissection was continued till a bubble-free plane was reached. A DM off corneal graft was then sutured using 16 interrupted sutures. Eyes with type 2 BB were managed like those with failed BB; avoiding baring of DM and performing manual dissection till the relatively clear PDL was reached.11

In cases where the PDL exhibited significant opacification and a formed type 2 BB, central baring of DM was performed. The paracentesis was used to reduce intraocular pressure by gently egressing some aqueous humor. A cautious slash was made in the PDL overlying the type 2 BB, under protective cover of cohesive viscoelastic to prevent rapid air escape. Cohesive viscoelastic was then injected into the space created by the bubble, effectively separating the PDL from the DM. Using fine-toothed forceps, the central portion of the PDL (approximately 4 mm in diameter) was meticulously peeled away in a centripetal fashion. In instances of a thickened PDL, fine forceps were used to grasp the tissue, while blunt-tipped corneal scissors were carefully used to excise the central portion, avoiding any direct contact between the scissors and the bare DM. Peripheral PDL tissue was deliberately preserved to minimize the risk of DM rupture during subsequent suture placement [Supplementary Video 1].

In the event of DM microperforation, in which a small perforation led to an aqueous leak without complete collapse of the AC,12 an air bubble was injected via paracentesis. Dissection was then continued gently sparing the perforation site till the end. In the case of DM macroperforation (in which the AC collapses despite trial to reform it with balanced salt solution or air),12 any DM folds at the tear site were gently unfolded using fluid jets or blunt cannula prior to donor graft placement. Following the placement of the initial eight sutures, additional air was injected into the AC to facilitate the sealing of the perforation site. Subsequently, the volume of the intracameral air bubble was reduced to approximately 50%–70% AC fill and the pupil was pharmacologically dilated at the conclusion of the surgery to prevent the occurrence of pupillary block.13

Patients received topical gatifloxacin (Zymar, Allergan, Irvine) every 6 h until complete epithelial healing and topical prednisolone (Predforte, Allergan, Irvine) every 6 h tapered over 2–3 months and then replaced by topical fluorometholone (Flucon, Alcon Laboratories, FW). Topical lubricants were administered to hasten epithelial healing. Follow-up examinations were scheduled at 1, 3, 7, and 30 days postoperative, and every 2 months thereafter. Planned suture removal was started after 6 months in eyes with high astigmatism. In eyes with stable and low astigmatism, some sutures were left in place.

According to the surgical course, eyes enrolled in this study were divided into two groups: Group A; in which baring of central DM was not done, and Group B; in which DM baring was achieved. Patients with <6 months of follow-up and eyes converted to PK were excluded from the study.

Visual acuity was measured preoperatively, at 3 and 12 months after surgery and the last follow-up visit, using the standard Snellen chart and converted to logMAR units for statistical analysis. Topographic parameters were measured using Pentacam (Oculus, Wetzlar, Germany) and analyzed by its 4 maps refractive display. Contrast sensitivity was measured under mesopic conditions at a distance of 2.5 m using CSV 1000 chart (VectorVision, Houston, Texas, USA). This chart has four rows of sine-wave gratings, testing the contrast sensitivity at 4 spatial frequencies: 3, 6, 12, and 18 cycles/degrees (CPD).

Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS software package version 20.0 (Armonk, NY: IBM Corp). Qualitative data were described using numbers and percentages. Quantitative data were described using range (minimum and maximum), mean, standard deviation, median, and interquartile range. Generalized estimating equations were used to compare age and gender between the two groups to compensate for patients having one eye in Group A and one eye in Group B. Friedman test was used to compare the changes in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) in the different periods. Mann–Whitney test was used to compare BCVA and contrast sensitivity between the two studied groups. The Chi-square test was used for comparing the state of PDL in the two groups. The significance of the obtained results was judged at the 5% level.

RESULTS

The study included 29 eyes of 18 patients. Patients’ characteristics are shown in Table 1. The mean follow-up period was 54.85 months. Group A cases in whom baring of DM was not done included 18 eyes (62.1%) while Group B included 11 eyes (37.9%). There was no significant difference between the two groups concerning age and gender of subjects. Two cases in Group A did not complete 6 months of follow-up and were excluded from the postoperative analysis. In one eye, graft failure occurred due to postoperative air-induced pupillary block. In another case, the graft failed to clear despite DM attachment. These two cases were also excluded from the postoperative analysis, and subsequent PK was done later. Two patients (4 eyes) had a mental handicap, thus assessment of BCVA and contrast sensitivity was not possible.

The only intraoperative complication was DM perforation recorded in eight cases (27.6%). Perforations occurred in five eyes with type 2 BB or mixed bubble, two eyes with manual dissection, and one eye with type 1 bubble. DALK was completed in all these cases with zero conversion rate to PK. Central baring of DM was not a statistically significant risk factor for DM perforation in our series (P = 0.610). All eight cases had postoperative DM detachment which resolved after re-bubbling with air. Re-bubbling was done once in four eyes, twice in three eyes, and three times in one eye [Table 2].

In addition, 3 cases (27.3%) in the central baring group had double AC despite having no intraoperative DM perforation. DM detachment was resolved after one re-bubbling in all three cases.

Postoperative complications occurred in five eyes. In two eyes (one eye in each group), the graft failed to clear in the early postoperative period, and subsequent PK was needed. One case had microbial keratitis which resolved with topical moxifloxacin eye drops. One eye had a stromal rejection episode which responded well to intense topical steroids with restoration of graft clarity. Another patient suffered from traumatic wound dehiscence which needed re-suturing.

DISCUSSION

The BB technique is considered the most popular among other DALK techniques. BB DALK was considered a DM-baring technique, but it has been proved that type 1 BB does not bare DM, and a stromal sheet covering DM was described.8 It has been proven in this study that the PDL tends to be affected by the GAG deposits in MCD, resulting in suboptimal visual quality after type 1 BB or manual dissection technique. Although both groups had relatively similar BCVA, central baring of DM led to significantly better contrast sensitivity.

The rationale behind baring only the central part of DM was to improve the clarity of the visual axis without increasing the risk of bursting the fragile DM if complete baring was attempted.14

An interesting finding in this case series is the high incidence of both type 2 BB and mixed bubbles (65.5%). The overall reported incidence in the literature usually ranges between 10.4% and 33.9%. Previous studies have demonstrated the high incidence of type 2 BB in old age, eyes with stromal scarring, and advanced stromal diseases.1415 The formation of type 2 BB in MCD, although undesirable in other pathologies, may provide an advantageous incident, as it separates the whole opacified stroma with GAG deposits from the clear DM, providing a clear visual axis. The higher incidence of type 2 bubble in these cases, contrary to keratoconus eyes, may suggest the difficult separation of the PDL from the stroma in MCD, hence air may dissect to the peripheral cornea, and diffuse through the clusters of fenestrations present in the periphery of the PDL, rather than passing between the stroma and PDL. The higher incidence of double AC requiring re-bubbling post-DALK has been described previously in association with type 2 bubbles.1116

The literature lacks a definitive consensus on whether to perform DALK or PK in cases of MCD. A recent meta-analysis investigated the outcomes of six studies comparing DALK to PK in MCD. No significant differences were found regarding BCVA and graft survival. However, the PK group had more rejection episodes, less endothelial cell density, and more graft failures due to endothelial rejection. On the other hand, the DALK group showed a higher recurrence of MCD in the graft.17

Since there is no technique to increase the likelihood of obtaining a type 2 BB during air injection, it remains unclear whether it is justified to convert to PK after a type 1 BB if the PDL was affected by the GAGs. In our opinion there remain many reasons supporting DALK even in the presence of deposits in the PDL; first avoiding the complications associated with PK including endothelial rejection and failure, second, due to the possible recurrence of the deposits in the graft it is wiser to perform a re-DALK than re-PK, and finally, some countries depend on imported corneal grafts with the possible shortage of high-quality tissues needed for PK in these relatively young patients.

Primary DALK failure occurred in two eyes in this study despite DM attachment. One case had postoperative pupillary block which probably led to graft failure. In the other case, the age of the patient was 51 years; relatively higher than the mean age of our cases (40.2 years). Chaurasia et al. reported similar primary unexplained DALK failure in 3 relatively old patients with MCD.18 Endothelial cell affection has always been suspected in MCD.1920 This affection may be more pronounced in old advanced cases contributing to DALK failure.

Limitations in this study were the relatively small number of cases, the inclusion of both eyes of bilateral cases, the short follow-up period, the lack of histopathological examination, and the absence of specular microscopy. Noncontact specular microscopy was tried in all cases. However, reliable images could not be obtained. This may be attributed to the presence of residual GAG deposits in some cases and the posterior curvature irregularity in cases with central baring of DM. Moreover, MCD is associated with guttae-like deposits, which were evident in all cases in this study that may interfere with proper image acquisition.

In conclusion, this study revealed a higher incidence of type 2 and mixed bubbles in MCD. We demonstrated a novel technique for central DM baring in these cases, to provide a clear visual axis and avoid all the residual GAG deposits that may be present or develop later in the PDL. In our series, this technique yielded a relatively better visual outcome. We still recommend performing DALK in eyes with MCD, the decision to convert to PK should be limited to eyes where a clear visual axis could not be obtained. Longer follow-up is required to assess the recurrence rate in the studied groups. In addition, postoperative study of endothelial cells after complete central baring of DM using confocal microscopy may be beneficial regarding endothelial cell loss and function in the postoperative period.

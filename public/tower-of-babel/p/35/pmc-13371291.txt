Letter to editor: Mitochondrial DNA release contributes to neuropathic pain via a cGAS-STING-IRF3 CMPK2-associated immunometabolic feedback mechanism
Authors: Renyuan Tian, Shengwei He, Bo Yuan
Published: 2026

Letter to the Editor,

We wish to offer a commentary on the article by Wang and colleagues, which identifies an mtDNA‑cGAS‑STING‑IRF3‑CMPK2 feedback loop as a driver of neuropathic pain [1]. The work is conceptually bold and methodologically impressive, combining single‑cell transcriptomics, molecular dynamics simulations, and complementary genetic and pharmacological interventions to forge a mechanistic link between mitochondrial dysfunction and persistent spinal neuroinflammation. The authors are to be commended for the breadth and rigour of their experimental approach.

Conceptual significance: a unifying immunometabolic framework

The major conceptual advance is the demonstration that CMPK2 sits at the centre of a self‑amplifying circuit: IRF3, a downstream effector of cGAS‑STING, directly upregulates Cmpk2 transcription, and the resulting CMPK2 protein promotes cytosolic mtDNA release, which in turn sustains STING activation. This positive feedback elegantly explains how an acute nerve injury can transition into a chronic inflammatory state. The identification of microglia as a principal CMPK2‑expressing population in the spinal dorsal horn is consistent with their established role in central sensitisation, yet the detection of CMPK2 in neurons and other glia adds a layer of complexity that the authors appropriately acknowledge. Overall, this framework substantially advances our understanding of pain chronicity by placing mitochondrial immunometabolism at the heart of neuroinflammatory amplification.

Key limitations: cell‑type resolution and peripheral compartments

Despite its strengths, the study has notable limitations. The most critical is the lack of cell‑type specificity in the in vivo knockdown. Intrathecal AAV delivery does not distinguish between microglia, neurons, and astrocytes, yet immunofluorescence shows substantial CMPK2 colocalisation with NeuN. Without conditional genetic models (e.g., microglia‑specific Cx3cr1‑Cre), the relative contribution of each cell type to the observed behavioural and biochemical effects remains unresolved. Second, the study focuses almost exclusively on the spinal dorsal horn, leaving peripheral nerves and dorsal root ganglia unexamined. Given that cGAS‑STING signalling directly modulates nociception in sensory neurons, it is plausible that the feedback loop initiates or operates in the periphery, potentially before spinal involvement. A comprehensive spatiotemporal analysis across the neuraxis is needed to pinpoint the primary site and temporal sequence of pathway activation.

Alternative interpretations and mechanistic nuances

Several alternative interpretations warrant consideration. The proposed linear cascade (CMPK2 → mtDNA release → STING activation) may oversimplify a more reciprocal relationship; STING itself can promote mitochondrial fission, creating a bidirectional cycle. Moreover, IRF3 is unlikely the sole transcriptional regulator of Cmpk2—IRF1 and NF‑κB may play significant roles depending on the inflammatory context. The pharmacological data with NDGA, while supportive, are constrained by off‑target effects. Despite molecular docking and MD simulations supporting CMPK2 binding, NDGA’s IC₅₀ (5.26 µM) and its well‑documented pleiotropic antioxidant activities raise the possibility that some in vivo effects are independent of direct CMPK2 inhibition. Genetic rescue experiments with a CMPK2‑selective inhibitor would help clarify target specificity.

Translational promise and obstacles

The translational implications are encouraging. CMPK2 represents a druggable node in a pathway that is amenable to small‑molecule intervention, and the concept of “mitochondrial immunometabolism” is increasingly recognised as a fertile therapeutic frontier. However, clinical translation faces substantial hurdles. NDGA itself is unsuitable for human use due to poor bioavailability and broad bioactivity, so the development of more potent and selective CMPK2 inhibitors is an urgent priority. Furthermore, evidence that this pathway is engaged in human NP is currently lacking. Analyses of cerebrospinal fluid for mtDNA or CMPK2, post‑mortem spinal cord tissue, or genetic association studies for CMPK2 polymorphisms would be critical first steps towards clinical validation. Circulating mtDNA or CMPK2 levels might also serve as potential biomarkers for patient stratification, though this remains speculative.

Priority future directions

Three priority areas emerge for future investigation. First, cell‑type‑resolved mechanisms—conditional knockout mice targeting microglia, neurons, or astrocytes would establish causal contributions and inform cell‑directed therapeutic strategies. Second, spatiotemporal mapping—time‑resolved quantification of mtDNA release and CMPK2 expression across peripheral nerve, DRG, and spinal cord after injury would clarify whether the pathway initiates centrally or peripherally, and identify optimal intervention windows. Third, sex differences and pain modality specificity—given known sex dimorphisms in neuroimmune pain, dedicated male‑female comparisons are essential, as the present study used mixed‑sex datasets without stratified analysis. Additionally, testing this axis in other NP models (chemotherapy‑induced or diabetic neuropathy) would reveal generalisability beyond traumatic nerve injury and inform patient selection for future trials.

Conclusion

In summary, this study is a significant conceptual advance, bridging mitochondrial dysfunction and neuroinflammation in NP through a clear feedback mechanism. Its limitations in cell‑type resolution, peripheral compartment analysis, and pharmacological specificity do not undermine the novelty of the core findings. The IRF3‑CMPK2‑STING framework offers a compelling model for pain chronicity and opens a new therapeutic avenue at the immunometabolic intersection. I am confident that this work will stimulate important follow‑up studies and may ultimately lead to novel pain management strategies.

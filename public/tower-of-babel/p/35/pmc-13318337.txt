Arginine vasopressin and oxytocin levels in patients with alcohol dependence and their association with morbid jealousy and partner violence: A case-control study
Authors: Pragya Kumari, Manul Das, Medha Rajappa, Balaji Bharadwaj
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Delusions of infidelity are more frequent in alcohol use disorder (AUD) than in primary psychotic disorders.Methods:To examine the association of arginine vasopressin (AVP) and oxytocin (OT) levels with morbid jealousy in patients with AUD, patients having AUD with morbid jealousy (cases) and AUD without morbid jealousy (controls) were compared on sociodemographic, clinical variables and serum AVP and OT levels. Median AVP and OT levels were compared between the groups. Regression analysis was done to control for confounders.Results:Twenty-eight cases and 29 controls were recruited. Partner violence more frequent among cases than controls (85.7% vs 24.1%) (P < 0.001). OT levels were significantly higher among cases [median = 69.47 ng/mL] (IQR 40.82–97.12) than controls [median = 38.75 ng/ml] (IQR 26.99–75.11) (P = 0.034). This was not confounded by age or timing of blood sampling.Conclusion:Patients with AUD with morbid jealousy had greater incidence of partner violence and higher serum OT levels than controls.

INTRODUCTION

Alcohol use disorder (AUD) patients often present with morbid jealousy. Morbid jealousy is seen more frequently in AUD (34%)[1] than in schizophrenia (2%)[2] and it is associated with partner violence.[3]

Morbid jealousy is more common among patients with AUD compared to patients with schizophrenia or other substance use disorders. This could be because alcohol being a liquid, its intake causes an alteration in the levels of neuropeptides oxytocin (OT) and arginine vasopressin (AVP). This effect may not occur in patients with other substance use disorders or in schizophrenia.

An initial reduction followed by an increase in AVP levels during the stage of antidiuresis lasting between 24 and 96 hours has been described after alcohol intake.[45] Short-term and also a persistent elevation of OT in response to alcohol has been described.[6] These two neuropeptides with similar structure are associated with pair-bonding and mate-guarding behaviors such as aggression.[78] Such alterations of AVP and OT levels with alcohol use could potentially trigger morbid jealousy among patients with AUD.[9] Hence, we planned to investigate the levels of AVP and OT among AUD patients with and without morbid jealousy.

The primary aim was to find the association of serum levels of AVP and OT with morbid jealousy among patients with alcohol dependence.

METHODS

Settings

The case-control study was conducted at the addiction psychiatry clinic of the department of psychiatry of our hospital between October 2022 and May 2024.

Ethical aspects

The institutional ethics committee approved the study and each participant provided written informed consent. A cross-sectional evaluation included history taking, clinical assessments and drawing of 5 mL blood sample for biochemical assay. Privacy and confidentiality were ensured.

Participants and recruitment

Fifty-seven male patients, aged between 21 to 45 years, diagnosed with alcohol dependence syndrome by a qualified psychiatrist (using ICD-11 criteria) were recruited by convenient sampling. Cases (n = 28) included patients with alcohol dependence who presented with morbid jealousy and unmatched controls (n = 29) included patients with alcohol dependence who did not have morbid jealousy. Morbid jealousy was defined as: (i) feelings of jealousy and concerns about spouse leaving the relationship, leading to aggression or violence OR; (ii) overvalued ideas of spouse having an extra-marital affair OR; (iii) the presence of frank delusions of infidelity.[10] Participants (either groups) with acute severe medical illnesses, a lifetime history of primary psychotic disorders or requiring intravenous fluid replacement at present were excluded.

Data collection

Cases and controls

The Clinical Institute Withdrawal Assessment of Alcohol Scale-Revised (CIWA-Ar)[11] was applied to assess the severity of current withdrawal state.

The assessment of violence or aggression towards spouse was done using a semi-structured proforma enquiring for incidences of violence or aggression (physical, verbal, or both) in the past three months, the frequency of aggression and its association with alcohol intoxication. The reports given by the spouse or caregiver were also confirmed by the patient.

Blood sampling and assay

Once acute withdrawals had settled, 5 mL of blood was drawn from each participant between 24 and 96 hours after the last alcohol use[4] after they lie down for 5 min. This was done between 8 am and 12 noon to avoid effects of diurnal variation.[12] Samples were centrifuged and extracted serum was stored at −80 C.

In the absence of previous literature, the ELISA tests were run in triplicate at multiple dilutions. Undiluted samples were optimum for the AVP assay, whereas the OT assay required 1:200 dilution. The laboratory staff were blinded to the group to which the samples belonged.

Further assessment for cases

The Brown Assessment of Beliefs Scale (BABS)[13] was applied to the cases to assess the severity of morbid jealousy.

Data analysis

The statistical analysis was carried out on Windows using SPSS version 29.0.[14]

Fisher’s exact test/Chi-Square and Student t-test were used to compare the groups. A P value was less than 0.05 was considered significant. Further, adjusted analysis was done for parameters that were significantly different between the two groups in bivariate analysis to control for potential confounding.

Handling of missing data

Since AVP levels and OT levels could not be performed for all the participants, the correlations of their levels with history of violence or with BABS score were performed for the available participants only.

RESULTS

Sociodemographic parameters

Twenty-eight cases and 29 controls were recruited. AVP levels could be assessed for 26 cases and 28 controls. OT levels could be assessed for 25 cases and 25 controls.

The cases were older - mean age of 41.0 years (SD-3.80) versus 37.10 years (SD-6.07) (P = 0.005). They were also married for longer duration of time - median of 16 years (IQR 13 − 23) vs 12 years (IQR 8.5 − 19) among controls (P = 0.021). The two groups did not differ on education, employment, or socioeconomic status [Table 1].

Alcohol and violence related parameters

The two groups were comparable with respect to the type and quantity of alcohol used, time since last use and withdrawal severity at the time of assessment. Duration of alcohol dependence was significantly longer in the cases compared to controls (15 years vs 10 years) (P = 0.040). A history of violence was present in 85.7% of cases and 24.1% of the controls (P < 0.001). About 42.8% of cases had episodes of violence on a weekly or daily basis, compared to 13.8% among the control group (P < 0.001) [Table 2].

Morbid jealousy

The BABS was applied to the cases. The median score was 9 (IQR 7–13).

Neuropeptide assays

Median AVP levels were higher in controls at 64.53 pg/mL (IQR 29.08–124.70) versus 43.12 pg/mL (IQR 11.83–88.36) among cases (P = 0.387). Median OT levels were significantly higher in cases at 69.47 ng/mL (IQR 40.82–97.12) versus 38.75 ng/ml (IQR 26.99–75.11) among controls (P = 0.034). Neither AVP nor OT levels were associated with the frequency of violence in either group. Among cases, the levels of AVP and OT did not correlate with the severity of morbid jealousy (BABS scores).

Adjusted analysis for age and timing of blood sample collection

Regression analysis was done to adjust for the influence of age and timing of sampling on OT and AVP levels. Duration of dependence correlated with age, but not AVP or OT levels. While AVP levels were potentially confounded by the timing of blood sampling but not age, the effect of age or timing of blood sampling did not significantly predict OT levels. Post-hoc power calculation

Post-hoc power calculation showed a power of 0.1429 (~14%) for estimating difference in AVP levels and a power of 0.5636 (~56%) for estimating a difference in OT levels between the two groups.

DISCUSSION

The main finding of this study was that the levels of OT were significantly higher among AUD patients with morbid jealousy than among those without morbid jealousy. Previous studies have shown persistently elevated OT levels in patients with AUD compared to healthy controls, but they did not asses the presence of morbid jealousy in these patients.[6] The present study found that AVP levels were lower among cases. This difference was not statistically significant and could be potentially influenced by timing of blood sampling. Prior literature suggests that AVP plays a role in the pair bonding in mammals like voles[15] but studies have not been done in patients with AUD and morbid jealousy.

Cases and controls were not individually matched, but were similar on clinical parameters such as average daily quantity of alcohol used, type of alcohol and severity of acute withdrawal symptoms at the time of sample collection.[16] The cases were older in age, had longer duration of dependence and there could be a variability in AVP or OT levels due to the timing of blood sampling. Hence, we performed bivariate analysis followed by regression analysis to adjust for these potential confounders. The timing of blood sample collection from the last alcohol use was potentially a confounder for the AVP levels, but not for OT levels.

We hypothesize that the inattention during alcohol intoxication can lead to misinterpretation of the environmental, interpersonal emotional and social cues[17] as being “unsafe.” High OT levels can trigger morbid jealousy and violent behavior towards their spouses when the environment is perceived as “unsafe” from a mate-guarding perspective.[18]

Limitations

Convenient sampling, at a single center with all male patients, lack of matched controls and cases being older in age with a longer duration of alcohol dependence are the limitations. The small sample size was underpowered for detecting a difference in AVP levels (14% power) and weakly powered for OT levels (56%) between the two groups. Presence of chronic medical illnesses were not evaluated.

CONCLUSIONS

The present study found an association between OT levels and the presence of morbid jealousy among patients with alcohol dependence. This association was not confounded by age of patient or timing of blood sampling after the last alcohol intake. This association between OT levels and morbid jealousy among patients with AUD requires further evaluation and replication in future studies with larger sample sizes.

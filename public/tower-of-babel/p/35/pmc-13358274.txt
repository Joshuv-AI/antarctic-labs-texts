Genetic associations of eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis in the Japanese population: exploring similarities and differences with European populations
Authors: Aya Kawasaki, Ikue Ito-Naito, Mina Sato, Yuka Kawamura, Ken-ei Sada, Kenji Itoh, Nobuyuki Ono, Fumio Hirano, Tomoaki Higuchi, Shigeto Kobayashi, Kenji Nagasaka, Takahiko Sugihara, Takashi Fujimoto, Makio Kusaoi, Yuya Kondo, Kumiko Shimoyama, Keita Yamashita, Takashi Higuchi, Hajime Kono, Noriyoshi Ogawa, Isao Matsumoto, Kunihiro Yamagata, Takayuki Sumida, Hiroshi Hashimoto, Hirofumi Makino, Yoshihiro Arimura, Masayoshi Harigai, Naoto Tamura, Naoyuki Tsuchiya
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesEosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA) is classified as a subtype of anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)-associated vasculitis (AAV). A recent genome-wide association study (GWAS) of EGPA in populations of European ancestry reported several susceptibility loci. In view of population differences in genetic factors of AAV, we made an attempt to examine the contribution of these loci to EGPA in a Japanese population.MethodsThe EGPA-associated variants in LPP, TSLP, IRF1/IL5, HLA, CDK6, 10p14 and 12q21 in European populations (EUR) were analysed in a total of 103 Japanese patients with EGPA, including 48 myeloperoxidase (MPO)-ANCA-positive and 45 ANCA-negative patients. Allele frequency data on approximately 60 000 Japanese individuals from the Japanese Multi Omics Reference Panel served as the controls.ResultsAn increasing tendency in TSLP rs1837253 C allele was observed in total EGPA, MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA and ANCA-negative EGPA. In addition, association of HLA-DRB1*07:01 was observed in total EGPA and MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA. These findings supported the previous findings in the EUR. On the other hand, unlike in the EUR, association of DRB1*09:01 was observed in Japanese EGPA. With respect to amino acid residues in HLA-DRβ1, Val 78 encoded by DRB1*07:01 and *09:01 was associated with total EGPA and MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA with genome-wide significance (p<5.0E−08). Association with the other loci was not observed in Japanese EGPA.ConclusionTSLP was suggested to be associated with EGPA both in the European and Japanese populations regardless of the ANCA status. In HLA-DRB1, both shared and population-specific susceptibility alleles were observed.Keywords: Vasculitis; Polymorphism, Genetic; Epidemiology; Anti-Neutrophil Cytoplasmic Antibody-Associated Vasculitis

Introduction

Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA) is a subtype of anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)-associated vasculitis (AAV), characterised by asthma and eosinophilia.1 2 The median frequency of ANCA positivity in patients with EGPA has been reported to be 30.6% worldwide.3 In Japanese patients with EGPA, myeloperoxidase (MPO)-ANCA and proteinase 3 (PR3)-ANCA positivity has been reported to be 29.6–50.2% and 2.5–6.2%, respectively.3 ANCA positivity in EGPA appears to be higher than that reported in European populations (EUR), which may be associated with the higher prevalence of MPO-ANCA in Japanese patients with overall AAV.4 It is reported that clinical features differ between patients with ANCA-positive and ANCA-negative EGPA.1 2 The incidence of EGPA is low both in Europe and Japan, with annual incidence of 0.9/million in the UK and 2.4/million in Japan.4 Partly due to its low incidence, genetic factors of EGPA remain largely unclear.

Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) on AAV have been reported in EUR.5,8 One of them focused on EGPA and reported 11 susceptibility loci, many of which were also associated with asthma or increased eosinophil count.8 Among the associated loci, BCL2L11, TSLP and 10p14 showed an association with EGPA regardless of the ANCA status. On the other hand, a single-nucleotide variant (SNV) in HLA-DQ region was specifically associated with MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA, and IRF1/IL5 and GPA33 were associated with ANCA-negative EGPA. These results suggested genetic differences between ANCA-positive and ANCA-negative EGPA. In the GWAS, association of HLA-class II haplotypes was also reported. Significant association was observed in the haplotypes carrying HLA-DRB1*08:01 or DRB1*07:01 with MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA. Interestingly, the effect size of DRB1*08:01 allele (OR 35.8) was much higher than that of HLA-DQ GWAS variant (rs9274704, OR 5.68).

In this study, we focused on the EGPA-associated genes reported in the GWAS on EUR8 and examined whether they are also associated with EGPA in the Japanese population. Among the associated variants in the European GWAS, rs72689399 in GPA33, rs72946301 in BCL2L11 and rs6454802 in BACH2 were excluded from the analysis due to their low allele frequency (minor allele frequency <0.01) in the Japanese population. rs6931740 in the HLA region was also excluded due to deviation from Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium. In addition, because associations of HLA-DRB1 haplotypes have been reported in European GWAS and our previous study had limited statistical power due to small sample size, we re-examined the association of HLA-DRB1 alleles with EGPA in a larger sample size.

GWAS on AAV in the Asian populations has not been published thus far. However, candidate gene studies suggest substantial differences between genetic factors for AAV between European and East Asian populations.9 We previously reported an association of HLA-DRB1*09:01-DQA1*03:02-DQB1*03:03 haplotype with susceptibility to MPO-ANCA-positive AAV,10 11 which includes about half of Japanese patients with EGPA. However, subtype-specific association between EGPA and HLA-DRB1 did not reach statistical significance, probably due to low statistical power. Furthermore, stratified analysis by ANCA status was not performed.

Methods

Patients and controls

Japanese patients with AAV were recruited at institutes affiliated with Japan Research Committee of the Ministry of Health, Labour, and Welfare for Intractable Vasculitis, Research Committee of Intractable Renal Disease organised by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, Japan, as well as research groups organised by University of Tsukuba, Tokyo Medical and Dental University, and Tokyo Women’s Medical University. All patients were Japanese and living in Japan. Genomic DNA from the patients was collected between 1999 and 2024. All available samples at the time of analysis were included in the study. The patients with AAV were classified into 427 patients with microscopic polyangiitis (MPA), 161 with granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA), 103 with EGPA, and 40 with unclassifiable AAV, based on the European Medicines Agency (EMA) algorithm (table 1).12 Among the patients with EGPA, 48 were MPO-ANCA positive and 45 were ANCA negative. The other 10 patients include those who were MPO-ANCA negative/PR3-ANCA positive and those with missing data on ANCA status.

The patients with EGPA were analysed for association with the EGPA-associated variants in the European GWAS8 in a case–control study. With respect to TSLP rs1837253 and HLA-DRB1 alleles, patients with MPA, GPA and unclassifiable AAV were also genotyped to address the specificity of association with EGPA. Data from 60KJPN consisting of approximately 60 000 Japanese individuals, available from the Japanese Multi Omics Reference Panel (jMorp), were used as the control data.13 14

Genotyping

The SNVs rs9290877 in LPP, rs1837253 in TSLP, rs11745587 in IRF1/IL5, rs42041 in CDK6 and rs78478398 in 12q21 were genotyped using the rhAmp SNP Genotyping System (Integrated DNA Technologies, Coralville, Iowa, USA). The primers used in this assay are listed in online supplemental table S1. As for rs9274704 (A/G) in HLA-DQ and rs34574566 (T-repeat) in 10p14, genotyping was performed using Sanger sequencing, because of incompatibility of the rhAmp SNP Genotyping System with a repeat variant (rs34574566), or due to the presence of nearby variants (rs9274704). Cycle sequencing was performed using the SupreDye v3.1 kit (Edge Biosystems, San Jose, California, USA), and the products were subsequently sequenced on an Applied Biosystems 3500 Genetic Analyzer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts, USA). Primers used in the sequencing analysis and representative sequencing chromatogram in each genotype of rs9274704 and rs34574566 are shown in onlinesupplemental table S1 figure S1, respectively. To overcome the difficulty in accurately genotyping the number of repeat nucleotides of homopolymer by Sanger sequencing, a mismatched base (T to A) was introduced into the forward primer for rs34574566.

Genotyping of HLA-DRB1 allele was performed using WAKFlow HLA typing kit (Wakunaga Pharmaceutical, Osaka, Japan) in 528 patients and long-read sequencing in 37 patients. In the long-read sequencing analysis, HLA-DRB1 gene was amplified using the NGSgo-AmpX V.2 HLA-DRB1 Whole Gene kit (GenDx, Utrecht, the Netherlands) and the amplicons were sequenced using the Sequel IIe system (PacBio, Menlo Park, California, USA). HLA-DRB1 alleles were determined by the NGSengine V.2.31.0 (GenDx) software using fastq data obtained from long-read sequencing. One of the patients with EGPA analysed using long-read sequencing was excluded from statistical analysis, because HLA-DRB1 allele could not be determined; thus the number of the patients with EGPA analysed for HLA was 102. For each amino acid position of HLA-DRβ1, allele count was determined based on HLA amino acid sequences deposited in the IPD-IMGT/HLA database.15

The genotyping on 60KJPN Japanese individuals from jMorp database used as controls was performed using short-read sequencing.13 14 To test the comparability between the genotyping methods employed for the patients with AAV and 60KJPN controls, healthy control samples from 510 Japanese individuals collected in our laboratory, referred to as in-house controls, were genotyped using the same methods as used for patients with AAV. For analyses of HLA-DRB1 allele, data from our previous study10 were used in addition to 60KJPN.

Statistical analysis

Association was tested using χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test using a 2×2 contingency table under the allelic model. Fisher’s exact test was used when any cell in the table contained a count of five or less. Allele frequencies were compared between each AAV subset (total EGPA, MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA, ANCA-negative EGPA, MPA, GPA, MPO-ANCA-positive AAV, MPO-ANCA-positive non-EGPA AAV and PR3-ANCA-positive AAV) and 60KJPN. Correction for multiple testing was performed using the false discovery rate method.16 The significance level was set at 0.05 for corrected p value (Pc). The number of multiple tests used for correction is shown in each table. The correlation between ORs from the European GWAS and those from this study was evaluated using Pearson’s correlation analysis. In addition to the correlation coefficient, an inverse-variance-weighted correlation coefficient was also calculated.

TSLP rs1837253 C allele was increased in total EGPA, MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA and ANCA-negative EGPA, which was consistent with European GWAS.8 Although the association barely missed significance after correction for multiple comparisons, ORs in the Japanese population were comparable to those in the European GWAS (online supplemental table S3). Similarly, a tendency toward association of HLA-DQ rs9274704 was observed also in Japanese MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA.

On the other hand, significant association with rs9290877 in LPP, rs11745587 in IRF1/IL5, rs42041 in CDK6 and rs78478398 in 12q21 was not observed in Japanese EGPA. The variant rs34574566, located in 10p14 region, consists of T repeats ranging from 8 to 26 repeats. In the Japanese population, (T)14 is the most frequent allele, followed by (T)15 and (T)16. When the frequencies of (T)15 and (T)16 were compared with (T)14 as a reference, significant difference was not detected between Japanese EGPA and controls.

Because genotype data in patients with AAV were obtained using the rhAmp SNP Genotyping System and Sanger sequencing while those in controls (60KJPN) were obtained using short-read sequencing, a possibility that differences in the genotyping methods may have affected the results cannot be excluded. To address the potential impact of the differences in typing methods, we additionally genotyped in-house control samples from 510 individuals using the same genotyping system employed for the patients with AAV. As shown in online supplemental table S4, the effect allele frequencies in the in-house controls and ORs were generally comparable with those obtained by using the 60KJPN as controls (online supplemental table S2), except that the low frequency allele rs9290877C at LPP showed higher ORs and nominally significant association when the in-house controls were used. Thus, the difference in the genotyping methods is considered unlikely to have significantly affected the results.

In some of the European GWAS variants, association was not detected in the Japanese population. This may be simply due to limited statistical power or true difference in the genetic background of EGPA between European and Japanese populations. To further address this issue, we conducted a correlation analysis between the ORs of EGPA-associated variants in the European GWAS and those observed in this study. As shown in online supplemental figure S2, statistically significant correlation was not observed between these populations; however, the weighted correlation coefficient indicated a modest correlation in MPO-ANCA-positive and ANCA-negative EGPA. These results suggested that genetic architecture may be generally shared between European and Japanese EGPA, except for some variants, which showed opposite direction of association (eg, CDK6 rs42041) (online supplemental table S3).

Association study on TSLP rs1837253 in AAV subsets

To examine which AAV subsets are associated with TSLP rs1837253 in the Japanese population, association was also tested with MPA, GPA, MPO-ANCA-positive AAV, MPO-ANCA-positive non-EGPA AAV and PR3-ANCA-positive AAV. As shown in table 3, rs1837253 C allele showed a trend of increase in MPO-ANCA-positive AAV (uncorrected p (Puncorr)=0.040, OR 1.13, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.28). On the other hand, the trend was marginal in MPO-ANCA-positive non-EGPA AAV (Puncorr=0.132, OR 1.10, 95% CI 0.97 to 1.25) as compared with MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA (OR 1.55, 95% CI 1.04 to 2.32) and ANCA-negative EGPA (OR 1.83, 95% CI 1.21 to 2.76) (table 2). Taken together, these results may suggest the specificity of TSLP association with susceptibility to EGPA rather than MPO-ANCA positivity.

Association of HLA alleles and amino acids with EGPA

In our previous study with a smaller sample size of EGPA (56 patients), association of HLA-DRB1 allele with EGPA did not reach statistical significance.10 However, in the present study with a larger sample size, a genome-wide significant association was observed between HLA-DRB1*09:01 and MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA (table 4 and online supplemental table S5). In addition, association of DRB1*09:01 with total EGPA and that of DRB1*07:01 with total EGPA and MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA were detected (Pc<0.05). When the association was tested in patients with MPO-ANCA-positive non-EGPA AAV, DRB1*09:01 was significantly associated (Puncorr=5.84E−10, OR 1.70, 95% CI 1.44 to 2.02), whereas DRB1*07:01 was not (Puncorr=0.501, OR 1.32, 95% CI 0.42 to 4.11) (online supplemental table S6).

As in the case of non-HLA variants, we tested the comparability between our HLA-allele typing method and that employed in 60KJPN controls by comparing HLA-DRB1 allele frequencies in the 60KJPN controls available from the jMorp database and those previously reported10 in the in-house controls (online supplemental table S7). HLA allele frequencies in both controls were generally similar, and DRB1*09:01 was confirmed to be associated with MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA also when the in-house controls were used as the control group, suggesting that this association was not caused by difference in the HLA allele typing methods. Association for DRB1*07:01 could not be tested because in-house control data were not available due to its low frequency in the Japanese population.10

In the analysis of HLA-DRβ1 amino acids at each position, the strongest association was observed in Val at position 78 (Total EGPA: Puncorr=1.31E−08, OR 2.36, 95% CI 1.74 to 3.20, MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA: Puncorr=3.21E−10, OR 3.52, 95% CI 2.32 to 5.35), (table 5 and online supplemental table S8). Along with Val 78, eight amino acids of HLA-DRβ1 were associated with total EGPA and/or MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA with genome-wide significance. These amino acids are mainly encoded by DRB1*07:01 and *09:01; therefore, it was difficult to distinguish the associations of HLA amino acids from those of HLA alleles. As shown in online supplemental table S8, associations of HLA-DRβ1 amino acids were also detected in ANCA-negative EGPA (Pc<0.05).

When linkage disequilibrium between the MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA-associated variant, rs9274704 and amino acids of HLA-DRβ1 was examined, strong linkage disequilibrium was observed between rs9274704 and HLA-DRβ1 amino acids, Glu 98 and Ala 104 (r2=0.833) (online supplemental table S9). Discussion

The epidemiology of AAV is substantially different between the populations of European and Asian ancestries,4 which appears to be caused, at least in part, by the differences in the genetic background.9 Although a GWAS of EGPA has been reported on populations of European ancestry,8 it has not been reported on Asian populations. Furthermore, the European EGPA susceptibility variants have not been tested for replication in Asian populations. Thus, the genetic factors for EGPA remain largely unknown in Asian populations. In this study, we made an attempt to examine whether the European GWAS loci are also associated with EGPA in a Japanese population.

With respect to HLA loci, DRB1*08:01-DQA1*04:01-DQB1*04:02 and DRB1*07:01-DQA1*02:01-DQB1*02:02/DQB1*03:03 haplotypes as well as DRB1*01:03 allele have been reported to be associated with MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA in the EUR.8 In this study, we detected the association of DRB1*07:01 with MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA, suggesting that the association of the DRB1*07:01 haplotype may be shared between European and Japanese populations.

On the other hand, the frequencies of DRB1*08:01 and DRB1*01:03 are both extremely low in the Japanese population13 14; in fact, these alleles were not detected in the patients with EGPA in this study. In contrast, the association of DRB1*09:01 with EGPA was not reported in European GWAS. Thus, these alleles may represent population-specific susceptibility alleles to EGPA.

In the major histocompatibility complex region, European GWAS has reported the association of rs9274704, located at the intergenic region close to HLA-DQB1, with MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA.8 The present study also detected a similar trend of association in the Japanese population. The risk allele rs9274704 G was found to be in strong linkage disequilibrium with HLA-DRβ1 Glu 98 and Ala 104 (r2=0.833) (online supplemental table S9). Both amino acids are encoded by DRB1*04, *07:01 and *09:01. Among these alleles, DRB1*07:01 and *09:01 were increased in MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA (table 4). Thus, the increase of rs9274704 G is likely to be secondarily caused by the linkage disequilibrium with DRB1*09:01 and *07:01, at least in the Japanese population.

When the association of each HLA-DRβ1 amino acid was analysed, the strongest association was observed at Val 78, which constitutes the peptide-binding pocket 4 of HLA-DRβ1, and is thought to influence peptide binding affinity and specificity. As shown in table 5, the amino acid residues in HLA-DRβ1 associated with MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA are mainly encoded by DRB1*09:01 and *07:01; therefore, it is difficult to determine whether specific amino acids, or the HLA-DRB1 alleles as a whole, are primarily associated with MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA.

We previously reported the association of DRB1*09:01 haplotype with MPO-ANCA-positive AAV including EGPA.9,11 As shown in online supplemental table S6, the association of DRB1*09:01 was also observed in patients with MPO-ANCA-positive non-EGPA AAV, but DRB1*07:01 was not significantly associated. These observations may suggest that DRB1*09:01 is associated with MPO-ANCA-positive AAV in general, not specifically with EGPA. On the other hand, DRB1*07:01 may be specifically associated with MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA. Further studies with larger sample size may be required to test this hypothesis.

TSLP encodes thymic stromal lymphopoietin, a cytokine involved in type 2 inflammation and mainly secreted by epithelial cells.17 18 TSLP induces Th2 cytokine production, including interleukin 4 (IL-4), IL-5 and IL-13, through multiple pathways. Dendritic cells, T helper 2 (Th2) cells, group 2 innate lymphoid cells and mast cells have been reported to be involved in the Th2 cytokine production.17 18 IL-5 is essential for the differentiation, maturation, proliferation and activation of eosinophils and is implicated in the pathogenesis of EGPA.18,20 Monoclonal antibodies to IL-5 (mepolizumab) and IL-5 receptor α (benralizumab) are used for treatment of EGPA.20 21 The SNV rs1837253 is located in an active enhancer region upstream of TSLP gene, suggesting a potential regulatory role. There is no variant in linkage disequilibrium (r2>0.3) with rs1837253 in EUR according to the 1000 genomes data (r2 data were obtained from the LDlink).22 Therefore, it is likely that rs1837253 itself may be causally associated with pathogenesis. It was reported that TSLP secretion was higher in primary nasal epithelial cells from individuals with rs1837253 C/C genotype compared with those with C/T or T/T genotype, under stimulation with poly I:C.23 This is in line with the findings that serum TSLP levels were upregulated in patients with EGPA compared with healthy controls.24 However, significant association between rs1837253 and expression of TSLP gene was not reported in eQTL databases including the Genotype-Tissue Expression Portal, ImmuNexUT or eQTLGen Phase I.25,27 Thus, the mechanism by which rs1837253 influences TSLP secretion requires further study.

TSLP rs1837253 was also reported to be associated with asthma and increased eosinophil count.8 This variant may be associated with EGPA and asthma by inducing type 2 inflammation, which plays a role in the pathogenesis of the diseases.18 Lyons et al suggested that eosinophilia caused by susceptibility genes including TSLP may contribute to the aetiology of EGPA.8

A monoclonal antibody targeting TSLP, tezepelumab, has been used in the treatment of asthma. In patients with EGPA, a case report of two patients treated with tezepelumab observed improvement in Birmingham Vasculitis Activity Score.28 In addition, a clinical trial of tezepelumab, known as the RACEMATE trial, is currently in Phase 2b.29 Given that the association of the TSLP variant with EGPA has been suggested, the variant could be evaluated as a biomarker candidate for prediction of tezepelumab response in patients with EGPA.

In this study, variants in GPA33, BCL2L11 and BACH2 were excluded from the analysis due to their low allele frequency in the Japanese population. However, other variants in the same genes may still be relevant, and further analysis will be necessary in future studies.

In the correlation analysis between the European GWAS and this study, no significant association was observed, although the weighted correlation coefficient showed a modest correlation in MPO-ANCA-positive and ANCA-negative EGPA. Because the number of variants used for comparison was limited (n=7), the correlation estimates might be underestimated. Future studies using a larger number of variants, such as those obtained by GWAS, will be necessary to clarify whether the genetic architecture is conserved across populations.

This study has several limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small due to the rarity of EGPA, especially when patients were stratified according to ANCA status. One possible reason for the failure to replicate previously reported associations in EUR is the limited statistical power of this study due to the small sample size. Power to detect association of the European GWAS variants is shown in online supplemental table S10.30 Most of the power values were below 0.8.

In this study, only the variants reported in the European GWAS were examined, except for HLA-DRB1 gene. The possibility that other variants in the same set of genes might be associated in the Japanese population cannot be excluded. In order to overcome this issue, GWAS on Japanese AAV is underway.

It should be noted that in this study the patients with AAV were classified according to the EMA algorithm,12 rather than the American College of Rheumatology/the European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (ACR/EULAR) 2022 criteria.31,33 The recruitment of the patients started in 1999, and the clinical data were insufficient for classification using the ACR/EULAR 2022 criteria. Sada et al reported that 97.6% of the Japanese patients with EGPA and 2.5% of patients with AAV other than EGPA classified by the EMA algorithm were reclassified into EGPA by the ACR/EULAR 2022 criteria.34 Thus, the association study results might be somewhat different when patients were reclassified according to ACR/EULAR 2022 criteria.

Recent reports have described an overlapping form of EGPA and GPA, classified based on the 2022 ACR/EULAR classification criteria.35 In particular, PR3-ANCA-positive EGPA may include patients with overlapping features. Among the patients with EGPA included in this study, seven were PR3-ANCA positive, of whom two were double positive for MPO-ANCA and PR3-ANCA, and five were PR3-ANCA-single positive. In this study, classification was based on the EMA algorithm12 which classifies EGPA at the first step. Therefore, whether these patients meet the definition of the overlapping form could not be determined. However, the possibility that some of these PR3-ANCA-positive patients represent the overlapping form cannot be excluded.

In conclusion, our findings suggested that TSLP may contribute to disease susceptibility to EGPA in European and Japanese populations regardless of ANCA status. HLA-DRB1 is strongly associated with MPO-ANCA-positive EGPA in both populations, with shared as well as population-specific susceptibility alleles (figure 1).

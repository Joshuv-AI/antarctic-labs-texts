Long non-coding RNA CKMT2-AS1 serves as a biomarker for osteoarthritis and participates in chondrocyte injury via miR-6720-5p
Authors: Yang Zhang, Xiangyun Liu, Xintong Hao, Haitao Xu, Xingyu Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe function of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) in osteoarthritis is increasingly recognized. CKMT2-AS1 has been reported to be dysregulated in osteoarthritis, but its biological function remains unknown.ObjectiveThe objective is to investigate the expression and function of CKMT2-AS1 in osteoarthritis, providing a biomarker and a promising therapeutic target.MethodsExpression of CKMT2-AS1 was determined in human osteoarthritic cartilage and IL-1β-induced chondrocyte models. Gain-of-function experiments were conducted by overexpressing CKMT2-AS1 in CHON-001 cells. Inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α) and extracellular matrix (ECM)-related markers (ACAN, COL2A1, MMP3, MMP13) were assessed via ELISA and RT-qPCR, respectively. EdU, CCK-8, and flow cytometry assays were employed to evaluate cell proliferation and apoptosis. The interaction between CKMT2-AS1 and miR-6720-5p was validated by luciferase reporter assay and RIP assay. Bioinformatics tools predicted the targets of miR-6720-5p, which underwent GO and KEGG enrichment analysis.ResultsCKMT2-AS1 was downregulated in osteoarthritic cartilage and IL-1β-induced chondrocytes. Overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 alleviated IL-1β-induced inflammation, ECM degradation, proliferation inhibition, and apoptosis. Mechanistically, CKMT2-AS1 acted as a molecular sponge for miR-6720-5p, which was upregulated in osteoarthritis. The functional role of the CKMT2-AS1/miR-6720-5p axis was validated through rescue experiments. miR-6720-5p overexpression effectively reversed the protective effect of CKMT2-AS1. Additionally, the target genes of miR-6720-5p were enriched in pathways such as “Hippo signaling” and “arginine biosynthesis.”ConclusionsThis study reveals the downregulation of CKMT2-AS1 in osteoarthritis and its protective role against chondrocyte injury via miR-6720-5p, highlighting its novel function as a potential therapeutic target for osteoarthritis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13018-026-06971-5.Keywords: CKMT2-AS1, Osteoarthritis, Chondrocyte injury, miR-6720-5p

Introduction

Among synovial joint diseases, osteoarthritis has the highest prevalence. The global burden of osteoarthritis is progressively increasing, with both incidence and prevalence on the rise [1]. Osteoarthritis involves the entire joint, causing complex pathological changes, such as synovial inflammation and hyperplasia, articular cartilage destruction, joint space narrowing, and osteophyte formation [2, 3]. Osteoarthritis has been recognized as a highly heterogeneous joint disease. Although surgery improves the quality of life for many patients with advanced osteoarthritis, it is not a universal treatment option for the large population suffering from this debilitating condition [4]. The challenge of delaying and repairing cartilage damage, reducing inflammation to extend joint lifespan, and improving patients’ quality of life remains an urgent clinical priority [5, 6]. Therefore, there is a need to develop personalized therapies for its management.

In recent years, the regulatory roles of non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) in musculoskeletal conditions have attracted attention. For instance, microRNAs (miRNAs) and small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) play a role in tendon injury and repair [7, 8]. The regulatory role of circular RNAs in osteoporosis has been gradually revealed, which further expands the research dimension of the non-coding RNA network in bone metabolism and joint diseases [9]. Research has revealed that long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) modulate the onset and progression of various diseases, including tumors [10], cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disorders [11], and musculoskeletal conditions [12]. In the context of chondrogenesis, lncRNA-HIT has been shown to act as an epigenetic regulator essential for mesenchymal‑chondrogenic differentiation by recruiting p100/CBP complexes [13], and lncRNA ROCR is upregulated during chondrogenic differentiation [14]. LncRNA CRNDE has been found to be associated with fracture healing [15], and HCG18 is involved in regulating spinal tuberculosis progression [16]. LncRNAs can act as competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs) by competing with miRNA response elements, thereby weakening the silencing effect of miRNAs on downstream target genes. This mechanism enables lncRNAs to indirectly participate in regulating the expression of target genes [17]. Numerous published studies indicate that lncRNAs function as crucial regulators in cartilage growth and maintenance of chondrocyte viability [18, 19]. Various lmcRNAs are involved in chondrogenesis, cartilage development, degeneration, and regeneration, as well as the pathogenesis of osteoarthritis [20–22]. Ma et al. reported that lncRNA EMBP1 is upregulated in osteoarthritic chondrocytes, and this upregulation exacerbates the progression of osteoarthritis by regulating the miR-454-3p/IRF1 axis [23]. Tan et al. found that the expression level of lncRNA H19 was reduced in osteoarthritic cells. Overexpression of H19 regulated the expression of genes maintaining normal chondrocyte homeostasis via miR-106b-5p, resulting in reduced chondrocyte apoptosis and enhanced extracellular matrix (ECM) synthesis, thereby delaying the progression of osteoarthritis [24].

A previous study using RNA-Seq revealed the lncRNA expression profile in patients with osteoarthritis, indicating dysregulation of CKMT2-AS1 [25]. The extant research on the functional role of CKMT2-AS1 in disease has primarily focused on the field of tumors [26, 27]. In the context of breast cancer [28] and papillary renal cell carcinoma [29], CKMT2-AS1 has been observed to be associated with the tumor immune microenvironment. However, unlike previously characterized osteoarthritis-related lncRNAs (e.g., HIT, ROCR) [13, 14], the molecular function and potential mechanisms of CKMT2-AS1 in osteoarthritis have remained completely uninvestigated. Consequently, our study further confirmed the aberrant expression of CKMT2-AS1 in osteoarthritic cartilage samples and further explored its involvement in the molecular mechanisms of osteoarthritis progression through in vitro experiments. It is hoped that these findings will provide new insights for mechanistic research and targeted therapies for osteoarthritis.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

The Ethics Committee of Zhucheng People’s Hospital provided approval for this study. This study enrolled patients admitted to the hospital between March 2024 and January 2025. The samples were obtained from 30 patients with osteoarthritis undergoing total knee arthroplasty and 30 individuals who underwent amputation due to traumatic injury (control). As shown in Table 1, the baseline demographic characteristics of the two groups were comparable. Articular cartilage tissues were collected from the femoral condyle (medial/lateral condyle) of the knee joint. For the control group, cartilage was harvested only from macroscopically normal areas of the joint, with no visible signs of degeneration or inflammation. The time from injury to tissue collection ranged from 6 to 12 h. All collected tissues were stored in liquid nitrogen. Informed consent was received from all study participants.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Patients in the osteoarthritis group were diagnosed with primary osteoarthritis (Kellgren-Lawrence Grade III or IV) based on clinical and imaging examinations, meeting the Recommendations on the Diagnosis and Treatment of Osteoarthritis in China [30]; (2) Patients in the control group had no history of osteoarthritis. Exclusion criteria: (1) Exclusion of other joint diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, and psoriatic arthritis; (2) Tumors or a history of tumors in the joint area; (3) Severe systemic diseases (e.g., serious cardiovascular or cerebrovascular diseases, hepatic or renal insufficiency) or immune system disorders; (4) Long-term use of medications that may affect cartilage metabolism, such as glucocorticoids or immunosuppressants; (5) Other diseases affecting bone and cartilage metabolism (e.g., osteoporosis, hyperparathyroidism, etc.); (6) Pregnant or lactating women.

Cell transfection and treatment

The human chondrocyte CHON-001 was purchased from ATCC (USA). Cells were cultured in DMEM medium with 1% penicillin–streptomycin and 10% FBS (Gibco, USA) at 37 °C and 5% CO2. Cells were transfected with the following constructs using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA): CKMT2-AS1 overexpression vectors or empty vectors, CKMT2-AS1 siRNA or negative control (si-NC), as well as miR-6720-5p mimics or negative controls (NC-mimic; GenPharma, China). Cells were cultured with IL-1β (10 ng/mL; Yeasen, China) for 24 h to establish an osteoarthritis model [31, 32].

RT-qPCR

RNA was isolated from chondrocytes and tissue samples using the Total RNA Extraction Reagent (Trizol; ABclonal, China). Reverse transcription was conducted using total RNA with the PrimeScript RT reagent Kit (TaKaRa, Japan). PCR amplification was evaluated using the TB Green qPCR Kit (TaKaRa, Japan) on the CFX Connect system (Bio-Rad, USA). miRNA expression was normalized using the endogenous control RNU6-1, while CKMT2-AS1 and mRNA expression were normalized using GAPDH. Relative expression was assessed using the 2−ΔΔCt method. All experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates.

CCK-8 assay

To assess cell viability, treated CHON-001 cells were seeded in 96-well plates and cultured for 24, 48, or 72 h. After incubation with 10 μL CCK-8 solution (Vazyme, China) for 2 h, cell absorbance was measured at OD 450 nm using a microplate reader. All experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates.

EdU staining assay

To assess cell proliferation, the EdU Proliferation Detection Kit (Solarbio, China) was utilized. Briefly, fresh medium containing EdU (20 µM) was added to each group of cells and incubated for 2 h. After fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, cells were incubated with 0.5% Triton X-100 for 10 min. Click reaction solution was added and incubated for 30 min under dark conditions. Subsequently, Hoechst 33,342 was added for nuclear staining, followed by incubation under dark conditions for 10 min. The percentage of EdU-positive cells was assessed by flow cytometry (Agilent, USA). All experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates.

Bioinformatics analysis

Downstream miRNAs of CKMT2-AS1 were identified using the online databases LncBook 2.0 (https://ngdc.cncb.ac.cn/lncbook/home) [33], starBase v2.0 (https://rnasysu.com/encori/) [34], and lncRNASNP2 (https://guolab.wchscu.cn/lncRNASNP/#!/) [35]. TargetScan 7.2 (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_72/) [36], miRTarBase 10.0 (https://awi.cuhk.edu.cn/~miRTarBase/miRTarBase_2025/php/index.php) [37], and miRDB 6.0 (https://mirdb.org/mirdb/index.html) [38] were employed to predict mRNAs targeted by miR-6720-5p. SRplot (https://bioinformatics.com.cn/en) [39] was utilized to generate a Venn diagram and perform GO and KEGG analyses.

Flow cytometry and caspase-3 detection

Apoptosis was detected using the Annexin V-FITC Apoptosis Detection Kit (Beyotime, China). Cell culture medium was aspirated into a centrifuge tube. After digestion, the cells were gently dissociated using the collected cell culture medium and collected by centrifugation at 1000 g for 5 min. The cells were gently resuspended in Annexin V-FITC binding buffer. After adding Annexin V-FITC and mixing, propidium iodide staining solution was added. The mixture was incubated in the dark for 15 min. Apoptosis was immediately analyzed by flow cytometry (Agilent, USA). Caspase-3 activity in CHON-001 cells was assayed using the Caspase-3 Activity Assay Kit (Solarbio, China). All experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay

After cell treatment, the supernatant was collected. The levels of TNF-α and IL-6 were measured using an ELISA kit (TransGen Biotech, China). The absorbance value was recorded at a wavelength of 450 nm. All experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

CHON-001 cells were seeded into 24-well plates for routine culture. Wild-type (WT-CKMT2-AS1) and mutant (MUT-CKMT2-AS1) reporter vectors were constructed based on the predicted complementary sites between CKMT2-AS1 and miR-6720-5p. WT-CKMT2-AS1 or MUT-CKMT2-AS1 plasmids were co-transfected with miR-6720-5p mimic, miR-6720-5p inhibitor, NC-mimic, or NC-inhibitor using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA). The firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were measured using the Double-Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit (TransGen Biotech, China) following 48 h of incubation. The firefly/Renilla luciferase ratio was used to assess relative luciferase activity. All experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

Cells were collected and lysed on ice with lysis buffer containing protease inhibitors and RNase inhibitors. A portion of the lysate was used as the Input control. The remaining lysate was divided into two equal parts, and each was added with Argonaute 2 (Ago2) antibody or IgG antibody. The mixture was incubated at 4 °C overnight. The next day, the pretreated protein A/G magnetic beads were added, and the incubation continued. The magnetic bead complexes were washed with the lysis buffer and incubated with proteinase K. Total RNA was extracted, and cDNA was synthesized. Using specific primers targeting CKMT2-AS1 and miR-6720-5p, qPCR was conducted to detect the relative enrichment of CKMT2-AS1 and miR-6720-5p. All experiments were performed with at least three independent biological replicates.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD (n ≥ 3). The normality of distribution for continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Comparisons between two groups were conducted using unpaired Student’s t-tests with or without Welch’s correction. Comparisons among multiple groups were analyzed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post‑hoc test. Pearson’s correlation coefficient was used to assess the correlation between CKMT2-AS1 and miR-6720-5p expression in clinical samples. Data analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 and SPSS 22.0. All statistical analyses were considered significant at P < 0.05.

Results

CKMT2-AS1 is downregulated in osteoarthritis and suppresses IL-1β-induced chondrocyte inflammation and imbalance of ECM anabolism and catabolism

There were no significant differences between the osteoarthritis group and the control group in terms of age, sex, or BMI (Table 1, P > 0.05). CKMT2-AS1 expression was measured in osteoarthritic cartilage tissue and normal cartilage tissue (control). Results revealed significantly reduced CKMT2-AS1 expression in osteoarthritis samples (Fig. 1A).

CKMT2-AS1 downregulation mediated chondrocyte inflammatory responses and ECM degradation in osteoarthritis. A CKMT2-AS1 expression was downregulated in osteoarthritic cartilage tissue. B Following IL-1β stimulation, CKMT2-AS1 expression was decreased in human chondrocytes (CHON-001), which was then partially restored by overexpression. C Overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 suppressed IL-1β-induced expression of TNF-α and IL-6. D Overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 promoted COL2A1 and ACAN expression but suppressed MMP3 and MMP13 in IL-1β-induced chondrocytes

CKMT2-AS1 expression was markedly decreased in an osteoarthritis model using IL-1β-treated CHON-001 cells. Furthermore, overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 markedly increased its levels in this model (Fig. 1B), indicating that CKMT2-AS1 overexpression attenuates the suppressive effect of IL-1β on its expression. Following treatment with IL-1β, the secretion of IL-6 and TNF-α was increased, whereas the overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 produced an inhibitory effect (Fig. 1C). Additionally, IL-1β-treated cells exhibited decreased expression of COL2A1 and ACAN, while MMP3 and MMP13 expression levels were elevated. CKMT2-AS1 upregulated COL2A1 and ACAN levels while downregulating MMP3 and MMP13 expression (Fig. 1D). In addition, knockdown of CKMT2-AS1 by siRNA decreased the expression of CKMT2-AS1 (Figure S1A) and aggravated IL-1β-induced inflammation (Figure S1B), extracellular matrix degradation (Figure S1C), and cell apoptosis (Figure S1D). These findings indicate that CKMT2-AS1 modulates inflammation and ECM degradation.

CKMT2-AS1 attenuates the effects of IL-1β on chondrocyte proliferation and apoptosis

CCK-8 (Fig. 2A) and EdU (Fig. 2B) assays demonstrated that overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 alleviated the suppression of cell proliferation induced by IL-1β. Conversely, IL-1β treatment facilitated apoptosis (Fig. 2C) and augmented caspase-3 activity (Fig. 2D), but CKMT2-AS1 dampened these effects (Fig. 2C and D).

The effect of CKMT2-AS1 on IL-1β-stimulated chondrocyte proliferation and apoptosis. A, B CCK-8 (A) and EdU (B) assays demonstrated that overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 promoted the proliferation of IL-1β-induced CHON-001 cells. C, D Overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 mitigated IL-1β-induced apoptosis (C) in CHON-001 cells and inhibited caspase-3 activity (D)

CKMT2-AS1 functions as a sponge for miR-6720-5p

Using the LncBook 2.0, starBase v2.0, and lncRNASNP2 databases, the downstream miRNAs (miR-532-3p and miR-6720-5p) of CKMT2-AS1 were predicted (Fig. 3A). In CHON-001 cells overexpressing CKMT2-AS1, miR-6720-5p expression was reduced, while miR-532-3p expression showed no significant change (Fig. 3B). Furthermore, miR-6720-5p expression was remarkably elevated in osteoarthritic cartilage samples (Fig. 3C), inversely correlated with CKMT2-AS1 expression (r = − 0.728, P < 0.001, Fig. 3D). Transfection efficiency assays demonstrated that the mimic and inhibitor increased and decreased miR-6720-5p expression in CHON-001 cells, respectively (Fig. 3E). According to the potential binding sequence between CKMT2-AS1 and miR-6720-5p, luciferase reporter assays validated that miR-6720-5p mimics significantly suppressed luciferase activity of CKMT2-AS1-WT, while miR-6720-5p inhibitors produced the opposite effect. However, the luciferase activity of CKMT2-AS1-MUT remained unaltered (Fig. 3F). Furthermore, RIP assays confirmed the direct interaction between CKMT2-AS1 and miR-6720-5p (Fig. 3G).

Identification of miR-6720-5p as a direct target of CKMT2-AS1 in osteoarthritis. A Downstream miRNAs of CKMT2-AS1 were predicted using three databases: LncBook 2.0, starBase v2.0, and lncRNASNP2. B Overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 reduced the expression of miR-6720-5p. C, D miR-6720-5p expression was increased in osteoarthritic cartilage tissue (C) and negatively correlated with CKMT2-AS1 expression (D). E miR-6720-5p mimics and inhibitors increased and decreased miR-6720-5p expression in CHON-001 cells, respectively. F The targeting of miR-6720-5p by CKMT2-AS1 was confirmed via the dual luciferase reporter assay. G RIP assay verified the direct targeting between CKMT2-AS1 and miR-6720-5p

CKMT2-AS1 suppresses IL-1β-induced chondrocyte damage via miR-6720-5p

Further analysis detected miR-6720-5p levels in IL-1β-induced chondrocytes. IL-1β treatment dramatically increased miR-6720-5p expression, while CKMT2-AS1 overexpression mitigated this promotion (Fig. 4A). Furthermore, co-transfection with miR-6720-5p mimics restored miR-6720-5p expression, confirming transfection efficiency (Fig. 4A). Subsequent experimental results demonstrated that the inhibitory effect of CKMT2-AS1 on TNF-α and IL-6 secretion was alleviated by overexpression of miR-6720-5p (Fig. 4B). Furthermore, miR-6720-5p suppressed the protective function of CKMT2-AS1 against IL-1β-induced ECM degradation, as shown by a reduction in COL2A1 and ACAN levels and an increase in MMP3 and MMP13 expression (Fig. 4C).

CKMT2-AS1 modulated the inflammatory response and ECM degradation in chondrocytes via miR-6720-5p. A Overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 suppressed IL-1β-stimulated upregulation of miR-6720-5p, while miR-6720-5p expression was partially recovered following transfection with the miR-6720-5p mimic. B miR-6720-5p reversed the suppressive effect of CKMT2-AS1 overexpression on IL-1β-induced TNF-α and IL-6 expression. C miR-6720-5p reversed the regulatory effects of CKMT2-AS1 overexpression on COL2A1, ACAN, MMP3, and MMP13

In IL-1β-induced chondrocytes, miR-6720-5p reversed the favorable effect of CKMT2-AS1 on cell proliferation (Fig. 5A) and reduced the proportion of EdU-positive cells (Fig. 5B). Moreover, compared with the overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 alone, miR-6720-5p increased the apoptosis rate of IL-1β-induced chondrocytes (Fig. 5C) and caspase-3 activity within the cells (Fig. 5D).

miR-6720-5p mediated the effect of CKMT2-AS1 on IL-1β-stimulated proliferation and apoptosis of chondrocytes. A, B Overexpression of miR-6720-5p reversed the pro-proliferative effect of CKMT2-AS1 on IL-1β-induced CHON-001 cells, as shown by CCK-8 (A) and EdU (B) assays. C, D Overexpression of miR-6720-5p reversed the CKMT2-AS1-mediated suppression of IL-1β-induced apoptosis (C) and caspase-3 activity (D)

The function of miR-6720-5p downstream genes is associated with osteoarthritis

In order to systematically investigate the potential biological functions of miR-6720-5p, its downstream target genes were predicted and analyzed. A Venn diagram generated by intersecting multiple prediction databases (including TargetScan 7.2, miRTarBase 10.0, and miRDB 6.0) ultimately identified 38 high-confidence target genes for subsequent analysis (Fig. 6A).

Target gene prediction and functional enrichment analysis of miR-6720-5p. A Venn diagram identifying miR-6720-5p target genes from the intersection of multiple prediction databases (TargetScan 7.2, miRTarBase 10.0, and miRDB 6.0). B GO enrichment analysis of predicted target genes revealed the most significantly enriched terms across biological process, cellular component, and molecular function categories. C KEGG pathway analysis of predicted target genes displayed the top significantly enriched pathways. D CKMT2-AS1 increased the mRNA level of STK4, while miR-6720-5p inhibited its mRNA level

To elucidate the potential biological significance of these target genes, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed. GO analysis revealed significant enrichment of target genes in multiple key biological processes (Fig. 6B). The most significantly enriched terms were primarily associated with “calcium ion export” and “Hippo signaling,” as well as cellular components such as “EMC complex” and “spanning component of plasma membrane”. In the molecular function category, targets were enriched in “calcium:cation antiporter activity” and “steroid hormone receptor activity”. These results suggest that miR-6720-5p may be broadly associated with these cellular activities. Furthermore, KEGG analysis indicated that these genes were markedly enriched in the “glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis − keratan sulfate” and “arginine biosynthesis” pathways (Fig. 6C). The complete KEGG enrichment results were provided as supplementary Table S1. This finding implies that miR-6720-5p may participate in osteoarthritis progression through its potential involvement in these pathways. Furthermore, the gene STK4 (MST1), involved in the “Hippo signaling pathway,” was selected as a potential downstream target for preliminary experimental verification. The results showed that overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 increased the expression of STK4, while overexpression of miR-6720-5p downregulated its expression (Fig. 6D).

Discussion

The regulatory function of lncRNAs in osteoarthritis progression has garnered increasing attention [40]. For instance, MINCR was reported to be downregulated in osteoarthritic cartilage and to delay disease progression by inhibiting ECM degradation [41]. The integrity of the ECM underpins the biomechanical function of cartilage, while the balance between chondrocyte proliferation and apoptosis is crucial for maintaining tissue cellularity. Disruption of these processes constitutes a core pathological feature of osteoarthritis [42, 43]. In this context, the present study focuses on CKMT2-AS1, revealing its significant downregulation in osteoarthritis cartilage and IL-1β-induced chondrocyte models. Notably, when interpreting the downregulation of CKMT2-AS1 in osteoarthritic cartilage, it is essential to consider that the control cartilage was obtained from patients who underwent amputation due to traumatic injury. Although only areas with normal appearance were selected and the tissue was processed rapidly within 6 to 12 h after injury, the acute trauma itself may alter local gene expression, inflammatory signaling, and cartilage homeostasis. While this potential confounding factor should be kept in mind, the use of traumatic amputation samples as controls is a common and often unavoidable practice in osteoarthritis research. In vitro, overexpression of CKMT2-AS1 effectively suppressed inflammatory cytokine release, mitigated ECM degradation, promoted proliferation, and inhibited apoptosis. This suggests CKMT2-AS1 may act as a protective factor in osteoarthritis progression, and its reduced expression could accelerate cartilage degeneration. Knockdown of CKMT2-AS1 had opposite effects on the above phenotypes. Notably, siRNA technology has shown promise in joint and bone disorders (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis and osteoporosis) [44, 45], indicating broad potential for RNA-targeted interventions. Previous research on CKMT2-AS1 has primarily focused on its role in various cancers, such as regulating tumor cell proliferation and metastasis [27, 46]. This research reveals for the first time its involvement in the pathogenesis of osteoarthritis, thereby extending the functional scope of this lncRNA beyond oncogenesis and demonstrating its significance in chondrocyte biology and joint homeostasis.

The majority of lncRNAs function as ceRNAs by binding to miRNAs, thereby influencing downstream signaling pathways and cellular processes [23, 41]. PTS-1 is reported to be downregulated in cartilage tissue from osteoarthritis patients and to be involved in inflammation regulation and apoptosis by targeting miR-8085 [47]. MCM3AP-AS1 has been found to accelerate ECM degradation and cartilage damage by regulating miR-149-5p/Notch1 signaling [48]. Through prediction across multiple databases and experimental validation, this study demonstrated that CKMT2-AS1 directly binds to miR-6720-5p. The expression of miR-6720-5p showed significant upregulation in osteoarthritic cartilage tissue and exhibited a negative correlation with CKMT2-AS1 levels. The finding aligns with the phenomenon that miRNAs often serve as key regulatory factors in osteoarthritis. In osteoarthritis, miRNAs not only play a key role in the pathophysiological process [49] but also are considered as potential diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets [50]. In addition, studies have shown that miRNAs are involved in the progression of postmenopausal osteoporosis [51, 52] and intervertebral disc degeneration [53], with therapeutic potential. Although few studies have explored the role of miR-6720-5p in the skeletal system, it was reported to regulate cellular proliferation [54] and inflammatory responses [55] in other diseases. In diabetic retinopathy, miR-6720-5p is recognized as participating in the regulation of inflammation in retinal microvascular endothelial cells [55]. In this study, overexpression of miR-6720-5p reversed the protection of CKMT2-AS1 against IL-1β-induced injury in chondrocytes, further supporting its pro-inflammatory role in osteoarthritis.

To further explore the functional mechanisms of miR-6720-5p, this study screened 38 high-confidence target genes through bioinformatics analysis and performed GO and KEGG enrichment analyses. Results demonstrated that these specific genes are prominently enriched in pathways including “calcium ion export,” “Hippo signaling,” “glycosaminoglycan biosynthesis-keratan sulfate,” and “arginine biosynthesis.” Notably, the Hippo signaling pathway holds significant importance in chondrocyte differentiation, proliferation, and metabolic homeostasis [56, 57]. Sulfated keratan, a pivotal component of the cartilage matrix, manifests abnormalities in its biosynthesis that are closely linked to cartilage structural degradation in osteoarthritis [58, 59]. The progression of osteoarthritis may be influenced by arginine metabolism through its involvement in inflammatory regulation [60–62]. However, because miR-6720-5p is a relatively novel miRNA with limited prior studies, only three databases provided predictions for its target mRNAs, and the intersection of these databases yielded only 38 high‑confidence target genes. This relatively small gene pool limits the statistical power of pathway enrichment analysis, resulting in only a few pathways reaching nominal significance. Therefore, the present enrichment findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating, and the functional relevance of these pathways requires future validation using larger target sets or direct experimental approaches. These enrichment results suggest that miR-6720-5p may potentially be involved in multiple processes linked to the progression of osteoarthritis, including the regulation of ion homeostasis, signal transduction, and matrix synthesis. STK4 is a known regulator of the Hippo signaling pathway, a pathway critically implicated in the pathogenesis of osteoarthritis [63, 64]. This study found that CKMT2-AS1 positively correlates with STK4 mRNA expression in a miR-6720-5p‑dependent manner. Nevertheless, whether STK4 is a direct functional target of miR‑6720‑5p requires further investigation using luciferase reporter assays and protein‑level analyses. At present, the connection between STK4 and the CKMT2‑AS1/miR‑6720‑5p axis remains correlative. In addition, whether CKMT2-AS1 participates in the progression of osteoarthritis through this pathway still requires further investigation.

This study preliminarily uncovered the mechanism by which CKMT2-AS1 alleviates IL-1β-induced chondrocyte inflammation, apoptosis, and ECM degradation by binding to miR-6720-5p, providing new insights into the molecular mechanisms of osteoarthritis. However, the study still has several limitations. Firstly, all in vitro experiments were conducted exclusively in CHON-001 cells (an immortalized chondrocyte line). The effects of immortalization on cell proliferation, apoptosis, and gene expression profiles may limit the physiological relevance of the findings. Therefore, the downregulation of CKMT2-AS1 and its effects on inflammatory markers need to be validated in future studies using primary human chondrocytes or other chondrocyte lines (e.g., C28/I2 or SW1353). Secondly, this study lacked validation in animal models. Future research will further validate the therapeutic efficacy and safety of CKMT2-AS1 through in vivo intervention experiments in mouse models of osteoarthritis, such as the destabilization of the medial meniscus model (DMM), to advance our findings to the preclinical research stage. Thirdly, although bioinformatics analysis suggested potential target pathways for miR-6720-5p, and it was observed that CKMT2-AS1 and miR-6720-5p regulate STK4 mRNA levels, this study did not conduct direct validation (e.g., luciferase reporter assay for STK4 3′-UTR or Western blot for STK4 protein). Therefore, STK4 should be considered a candidate target, and its regulatory network remains to be elucidated. Furthermore, the development of osteoarthritis involves interactions among multiple cell types and complex signaling pathways. This study focused solely on an IL-1β-induced inflammatory model and did not examine the effects of other key pathological factors (such as mechanical loading or age-related changes) on the CKMT2-AS1/miR-6720-5p axis. Finally, whether CKMT2-AS1 participates in the progression of osteoarthritis through other molecular mechanisms, such as direct regulation of transcription or protein interactions, also warrants further investigation.

This study provides preliminary evidence that CKMT2-AS1 is downregulated in osteoarthritis and alleviates IL-1β-induced chondrocyte inflammation, suppresses ECM degradation and apoptosis, and facilitates proliferation by targeting miR-6720-5p. These findings offer new insights into the molecular mechanisms of osteoarthritis and lay a preliminary foundation for subsequent research. However, given that this study remains at the in vitro stage of exploration, its potential for clinical translation and biological significance in vivo still requires validation in more complex systems.

New potential morphologic features of the aorta associated with the occurrence of acute type B aortic dissection
Authors: Jianhua Zhang, Zhenguo Wang, Anxiaonan Zhang, Yu Sun, Xiaogang Li, Qiuyue Jin, Jie Hou, Libo Zhang, Benqiang Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The present study aimed to identify aortic morphological features associated with the occurrence of acute type B aortic dissection (aTBAD) and establish a risk prediction model. Patients in the control group were age- and sex-matched in a 1:1 ratio with the aTBAD group. Morphological parameters of the ascending aorta and aortic arch were measured on the GE Healthcare Advantage Workstation. The length and diameter of the ascending aorta and aortic arch in the aTBAD group increased significantly (all P < 0.001). The tortuosity and angulation of ascending aorta were significantly larger in the aTBAD group (P < 0.001 and P = 0.021, respectively), with no significant difference in the aortic arch (P = 0.757 and P = 0.212, respectively). The height and angle of the ascending aorta increased significantly in the aTBAD group (P < 0.001 and P = 0.039, respectively). The height, width, and angle of the aortic arch were significantly larger in the aTBAD group (all P < 0.05). Multivariable logistic regression showed that distal left common carotid artery diameter, aortic arch height, and ascending aorta tortuosity were associated with the occurrence of aTBAD. The area under the curve of the risk prediction model and the validation dataset were 0.8415 and 0.8319, respectively. The width, height, and angle of the ascending aorta and aortic arch were associated with the occurrence of aTBAD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-20396-w.

Introduction

Aortic dissection is a life-threatening disease caused by an aortic intimal tear or bleeding within the aortic wall, leading to the separation of the aortic wall, which is related to high mortality if not diagnosed and treated in a timely manner1,2. The morphology might reflect the pathological changes of the aorta. For example, when the elastic fibers of the aortic wall are denatured, necrotic, and broken due to cardiovascular risk factors, the morphology of the aorta will change to varying degrees, such as the increase in diameter, length, and tortuosity3. Simultaneously, the morphological changes lead to hemodynamic changes, such as increased tortuosity that leads to turbulent flow dynamic, which increases wall shear stress, resulting in an increased risk of dissection or rupture4.

With the development of imaging technology and complex computer algorithms, three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction of the aorta and measurement of complex morphological indexes have become possible5. Previous studies have investigated the correlation between aortic diameter, length, tortuosity, and angulation and the occurrence of acute type B aortic dissection (aTBAD) and provided diverse conclusions6–11. For example, Qiu et al.8 and Lescan et al.7 found that the length of the ascending aorta was not prolonged, while the length of the aortic arch was prolonged in the aTBAD group. Sun et al.9–11 showed that the length of the ascending aorta was prolonged, but the length of the aortic arch was not prolonged. Cao et al.6 demonstrated that both the length of the ascending aorta and aortic arch in the aTBAD group were prolonged. Aortic dissection is a complex disease, traditional parameters (such as aortic diameter, length, tortuosity, and angulation) may not fully demonstrate the morphology of the aorta, in order to further study the correlation between aortic morphology and the occurrence of aTBAD, so we explored new morphological parameters, in our daily work, we found that there seems to be a difference in the width, height, and angle of the aortic between the patients with aTBAD and the normal patients, so we included these morphological parameters in this study. Since the computed tomographic angiography (CTA) data of the normal aorta before dissection are difficult to obtain and the diameter and length of descending aorta are increased after aTBAD onset, the diameter and length of ascending aorta and aortic arch remain unchanged12. Therefore, in the present retrospective study, we only analyzed the morphology parameters of ascending aorta and aortic arch from aTBAD and control groups. This study aimed to identify the aortic morphological features related to the occurrence of aTBAD and establish a simple and reproducible risk prediction model.

Methods

Study design

This study was approved by the local ethics committee (NO. Y (2022) 073). Due to the retrospective and observational nature of the study, the requirement of written informed consent of the patients was waived. All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. The flow chart of patient inclusion and exclusion criteria is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Fig. 1Flow diagram of participation. aTBAD: acute type B aortic dissection.

Flow diagram of participation. aTBAD: acute type B aortic dissection.

A retrospective analysis was performed on patients diagnosed with aTBAD and hospitalized in the medical center between January 2018 and May 2022. The exclusion criteria were bicuspid aortic valve or quadricuspid aortic valve, connective tissue disease or non-syndromic familial aTBAD (family history of aortic dissection in first or second degree relative who has no known syndrome)13, traumatic dissection, isolated abdominal aortic dissection, patients with other aortic disease, history of previous aortic surgery, non-A non-B aortic dissection, aortic arch branching variants (such as bovine aortic, isolated left vertebral artery, and aberrant right subclavian artery), and any other condition that may cause aortic shape deformation (such as massive pleural effusion and mediastinal mass). In addition, patients with missing clinical data, poor image quality, and lack of images were excluded before inclusion. Finally, a total of 113 participants were enrolled in the aTBAD group.

The control group included patients who received CTA examination for various reasons such as chest pain, chest tightness, dyspnea, aortic pathology suggested by ultrasound and CT scan, and large differences in blood pressure in the extremities, etc. from January 2018 to May 2022 and had no aortic abnormalities. After excluding the aortic arch branch variation, the patients were age- and sex-matched in a 1:1 ratio to the aTBAD group. Patients with poor image quality and lack of image were excluded before inclusion. Finally, 113 participants were enrolled in the control group.

Demographic and clinical data were collected on patients with aTBAD, including age, sex, height, weight, BMI, smoking status, alcohol consumption, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and hyperglycemia. Some clinical data were missing in the control group.

CTA scanning protocols

256-slice computed tomography scanner (Brilliance iCT, Philips Medical Systems, Cleveland, USA). Scanning range from the plane of entrance to bony thorax to the plane of symphysis pubis. Scanning parameters: tube voltage 120 kV, tube current 250 mAs, collimator 128 × 0.625 mm, rotation time 0.75s, slice thickness 0.8 mm, slice increment 0.4 mm. By body weight, 1.0 ml/kg of Iopromide 370 (370 mg I/mL, Bayer Schering, Berlin, Germany) was injected into the anterior elbow vein using a high-pressure syringe at a flow rate of 5 ml/s, followed by a 40 ml saline flush at the same injection rate. The scan was triggered using a threshold trigger mode with an attenuation threshold of 100 HU. The region of interest (ROI) was located 1 cm below the tracheal bifurcation of the descending aorta.

Image post-processing

CTA images from eligible patients were post-processed using GE Healthcare Advantage Workstation (AW4.4, USA). The 3D image of the total aorta was reconstructed to provide a lumen centerline and semi-automated vessel segmentation for follow-up measurements. The ascending aorta was defined as the part of the aorta between the sinotubular junction (STJ) and the proximal brachiocephalic trunk (BCT), and the aortic arch was the part of the aorta between the proximal BCT and distal left subclavian artery14,15.

Aortic diameter

A total of four planes perpendicular to the centerline were created to measure the diameter (Fig. 2A): the STJ, the middle ascending aorta, the proximal BCT, and the distal left common carotid artery (LCCA). The semi-automatic polygon boundary tracking technology was used to obtain the lumen area of each plane. All reported diameters were calculated based on cross-sectional areas.

Aortic length

The length of the ascending aorta and aortic arch was defined as the length of the centerline of each segment.

Aortic tortuosity

The tortuosity was calculated by dividing the length of the centerline by the straight line distance between the start and end points of the centerline (Fig. 2B)16.

Aortic angulation

The centerline “tangent angle” function on the workstation was used to measure the angulation, which was defined as the angulation between the tangent lines drawn for the start and end points of the centerline (Fig. 2B)6.

Aortic width, height, and angle

The width, height, and angle of the ascending aorta and aortic arch were measured in the 3D view of the aorta (Fig. 2C). The width and height of the ascending aorta and aortic arch were defined as the horizontal distance and vertical distance between the start and end points of the centerline of each segment, respectively. The angle of the ascending aorta and aortic arch was defined as the angle between the horizontal line and the connecting line of the start and end points of the centerline of each segment. The central line of the ascending aorta begins and ends at the STJ and the proximal BCT, respectively. The central line of the aortic arch begins and ends at the proximal BCT and distal left subclavian artery, respectively. The width, height and angle of the ascending aorta and aortic arch were measured separately.

Measurement reproducibility

Two radiologists with more than 5 years of experience repeated measurements on 40 CTAs randomly selected from both groups to test for intra- and inter-observer differences.

Statistical analysis

The Shapiro–Wilk test was performed to assess the normality of the continuous variables. Continuous variables with normal distribution are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and those with non-normal distribution are expressed as median (interquartile range). The categorical variables are expressed as frequencies (percentage). The t-test or Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare continuous variables of the two groups, and Pearson’s chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test was used to examine the differences in the categorical variables between the two groups. The Youden’s index was used to calculate the cutoff value for each parameter, Yoden’s index = sensitivity + specificity − 1, find the row with the largest Yoden’s index, the corresponding value of the test variable was the optimal cutoff value. The significant variables in the univariable logistic regression were included in the Multivariable logistic regression model after excluding those with severe collinearity. Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) > 10 represent a severe collinearity. The variables excluded from this study included, proximal BCT diameter (VIF = 10.962), ascending aortic length (VIF = 132.899), aortic arch width (VIF = 33.534), and aortic arch angle (VIF = 36.037), after excluding variables with severe collinearity, the variables included in the Multivariable logistic regression all had VIF < 10. Multivariable screening used a forward stepwise entry approach, the final variables obtained from the Multivariable analysis were used to model in the research platform, and the standardization method was applied to all numeric data in the data engineering process. Formula of data standardization: z = (x − mean)/SD. The internal validation of the new model was performed using a five-fold crossover test. Finally, the receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curve, the area under the curve (AUC), calibration curve, decision analyses, and nomograph are performed to quantify the performance of the model. The Interclass Correlation Coefficient was used to test the Intra- and inter-observer reproducibility. All the data were analyzed using the SPSS software (version 26, IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) and Dr. Wise Multimodal Research Platform (Beijing Deepwise & League of PHD Technology Co., Ltd, Beijing, China). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Aortic length

The length of the ascending aorta and aortic arch was significantly longer in the aTBAD group (all P < 0.001) (Table 2).

Aortic tortuosity and angulation

Ascending aortic tortuosity and angulation were significantly higher in the aTBAD group than in the control group. (P < 0.001 and P = 0.021, respectively) (Table 2). However, no significant difference was observed in the aortic arch tortuosity and angulation between the two groups (P = 0.757 and P = 0.212, respectively) (Table 2).

Aortic width, height, and angle

The height and angle of the ascending aorta were significantly larger in the aTBAD group than in the control group (P < 0.001 and P = 0.039, respectively) (Table 2). However, no significant difference was detected in the width of the ascending aorta between the two groups (P = 0.304) (Table 2). The width, height, and angle of the aortic arch were significantly larger in the aTBAD group than in the control group (P = 0.001, P < 0.001, and P < 0.001, respectively) (Table 2).

Logistic regression and risk prediction model for aTBAD

Univariable logistic regression showed that the diameter of STJ, mid-ascending, proximal BCT, and distal LCCA, the length of ascending aorta and aortic arch, the tortuosity, angulation, height, and angle of ascending aorta, the width, height, and angle of aortic arch were characteristics associated with the occurrence of the aTBAD (Table 3). Discussion

The first 2 weeks after the onset of aortic dissection is considered the acute stage, in which the patients are extremely prone to life-threatening complications and death3. Therefore, how identifying high-risk patients in advance and preventing the occurrence of dissection are crucial issues needing an in-depth investigation. The current findings could be summarized as follows: (1) The transverse dilation and longitudinal elongation of the ascending aorta and aortic arch were significant in the aTBAD group. (2) Ascending aortic tortuosity and angulation were significantly higher in the aTBAD group than in the control group. (3) The height and angle of the ascending aorta and the width, height, and angle of the aortic arch increased significantly in the aTBAD group than in the control group. (4) The distal LCCA diameter, aortic arch height, and ascending aorta tortuosity were associated with the occurrence of aTBAD, and this study established a simple risk prediction model.

Previous studies suggested that the diameter of the ascending aorta and aortic arch was significantly larger in the aTBAD group than in the control group6–11. Our result was similar to that obtained previously. Our findings were consistent with the research of Cao et al.6, i.e., the length of ascending aorta and aortic arch was extended in the aTBAD group. Cardiovascular risk factors, such as aging, dyslipidemia, and arterial hypertension can promote the process of atherosclerosis and damage the elastic properties of the aortic wall, increasing the risk of aortic dissection17,18. Based on the theory of longitudinal elastic loss of the aortic media, the results of the present study may be reasonable.

Aortic tortuosity and angulation are the morphological parameters that characterize the degree of vascular bending but have not been widely studied and applied in the field of cardiovascular disease. Cao et al.6 and Sun et al.11 showed that the tortuosity and angulation of aortic arch and the angulation of ascending aorta were significantly larger in the aTBAD group compared to the control group. However, in this study, no significant difference was observed in tortuosity and angulation of the aortic arch between the two groups, and the tortuosity of the ascending aorta was significantly larger in the aTBAD group than in the control group. This phenomenon could be attributed to the diverse origin of our subjects, i.e., from different regions and populations. Thus, further studies are needed to confirm the real difference in the tortuosity and angulation of the ascending aorta and aortic arch between the two groups.

This study showed that the angle of the aortic arch was significantly larger in the aTBAD group. In addition, the height and angle of the ascending aorta and the width and height of the aortic arch were significantly different between the two groups. Altered aortic morphology affects hemodynamics, leading to increased mechanical stress, which is associated with the development of aTBAD19,20. New morphological features can enrich the assessment of aortic morphology, and traditional and new morphological features can complement each other and provide incremental value to each other. Multivariable logistic regression showed that distal LCCA diameter, aortic arch height, and ascending aorta tortuosity were associated with the occurrence of aTBAD. In the present study, the newly added variable aortic arch height might be used as a new predictor.

This study established a simple risk prediction model may have predictive value for the occurrence of aTBAD. We considered that the morphology of ascending aorta and aortic arch was unchanged after type B dissection onset12. However, due to the difficulty in obtaining CTA data before dissection or the small sample size, this phenomenon is yet to be confirmed. Hence, the ROC curve and AUC of the model should be regarded as hypothetical, and this model needs external validation and large multicenter sample prospective study to confirm its reliability in the future.

Limitations

Nevertheless, the present study has the following limitations. Firstly, since the condition of the CTA before the dissection is difficult to obtain, we only retrospectively analyzed the morphological parameters of the ascending aorta and aortic arch, which were less affected by the aTBAD12. However, whether the aTBAD affects the morphological indexes of ascending aorta and aortic arch needs to be substantiated by large sample and multicenter studies. Secondly, the study only confirmed that the morphological variables of the ascending aorta and aortic arch were related to the occurrence of aTBAD, which does not confirm a causal correlation. Thirdly, based on the retrospective design of this study, it is difficult to obtain the clinical data of the control group. Some clinical data were missing in the control group. Fourthly, the number of patients in this study was small, and there may be a potential selection bias. Hence, our findings should be validated in multicenter studies involving ethnically diverse populations. Finally, the model requires external validation before clinical application can be considered and is currently only being used as an exploratory study.

Conclusions

In addition to aortic morphological features, such as diameter, length, tortuosity, and angulation, the width, height, and angle may also be associated with the occurrence of aTBAD. The novel risk prediction model may have predictive value for the occurrence of aTBAD.

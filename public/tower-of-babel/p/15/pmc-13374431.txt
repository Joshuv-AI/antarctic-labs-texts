HYPOChLorous Acid TEsting Studies in secondary peritonitis (Hypoclates:Peritonitis): extended hypochlorous acid versus isotonic lavage for the prevention of surgical site infections in secondary peritonitis in Germany – protocol for a sequential, quasi-experimental cohort study
Authors: Tobias Hoch Al Hessen, Niels Siegel, Mika P Nadvornik, Axel Kramer, Jonathan M Harnoss, Pascal Probst, Mohammed Al-Saeedi, Martin Loos, Christoph W Michalski, Julian C Harnoss
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSurgical site infections (SSIs) impose a significant clinical and economic burden on the healthcare systems. To date, traditional preventive strategies have frequently failed to demonstrate definitive efficacy. Hypochlorous acid (HOCl) has emerged as a compelling antimicrobial alternative, characterised by potent microbicidal properties and excellent biocompatibility. Hypoclates:Peritonitis investigates the clinical impact of an HOCl-based lavage strategy in patients undergoing surgical treatment for secondary peritonitis. The trial evaluates the primary endpoint of postoperative SSI incidence up to day 10 (±2 days), hypothesising that the antimicrobial and biocompatible properties of HOCl significantly lower infection rates compared with standard isotonic lavage.MethodsUsing a sequential, quasi-experimental design, this mono-centric study assesses the clinical impact of adopting an intensified, multi-day HOCl-lavage protocol (Granudacyn) for patients with secondary peritonitis. We will compare a prospective intervention group (n≈100) against a preceding cohort of equal size that received standard isotonic irrigation. The primary endpoint is defined as the frequency of SSIs—inclusive of superficial, deep and organ/space infections according to Centers for Disease Control and Prevention criteria—monitored through hospital discharge or up to postoperative day 10 (±2 days). Secondary evaluative measures include the length of intensive care unit and total hospital stay, the duration of postoperative antibiotic therapy and the cumulative morbidity burden as calculated by the Comprehensive Complication Index.Ethics and disseminationEthical clearance for this trial was provided by the Institutional Review Board of the Medical Faculty of Heidelberg University (S-523/2025). To facilitate knowledge transfer and evidence-based practice, the study results will be presented at international scientific meetings and published in peer-reviewed journals, thereby contributing to the refinement of SSI prevention protocols in emergency visceral surgery.Trial registration numberGerman Clinical Trials Register (DRKS), DRKS00038630.

Introduction

Surgical site infections (SSI) represent a significant clinical and economic burden worldwide, accounting for approximately 15% of nosocomial infections.1 They lead to prolonged hospital stays and increased morbidity and mortality rates.2 In Germany alone, SSI are responsible for around 1 million additional hospital days and approximately €3 billion in extra costs annually.3 Therefore, effective prevention and treatment strategies are of paramount importance.

Standard preventative measures against SSI include intraoperative lavage with isotonic solutions.4 However, its efficacy remains unproven due to limited and heterogeneous evidence5—so much so that even the WHO in its current guidelines states there is insufficient evidence to recommend for or against the routine use of saline wound or peritoneal lavage before incision closure.6 This lack of definitive guideline endorsement highlights the critical clinical need to evaluate alternative irrigative substances that can outperform simple isotonic solutions in their preventive capacity. Antimicrobial oxidising agents have shown potential in this regard due to their broad-spectrum microbicidal properties and favourable safety profiles when compared with higher potency antiseptics.7

Hypochlorous acid (HOCl) in particular, being a naturally occurring antimicrobial substance in the human body,8 offers a high level of biocompatibility while showing sufficient antimicrobial activity; a combination of properties holding great potential to justify its widespread use in preventive and therapeutic strategies against SSI. However, scientific data on its application in lavage and other irrigation procedures are scarce.9 This study represents the beginning of a study series aiming to close this gap and is intended to result in proof of concept which is reflected in the selection of the study population and the overall study design.

With respect to the study population, secondary peritonitis is associated with an increased risk of bacterial contamination and, consequently, postoperative infections.10 Therefore, the effect of the antimicrobial agent used in lavage and irrigation is particularly relevant and can be effectively evaluated.11 With respect to the study design, the favourable tissue tolerance of HOCl permits prolonged lavage without risking tissue damage, potentially resulting in improved outcomes due to the agent’s extended active time. Given the limited evidence for extended isotonic lavage,5 this study evaluates a standardised, extended lavage protocol: combining a high-volume intraoperative HOCl application with a sequential, postoperative intracavitary irrigation via surgically placed drains to maximise antimicrobial exposure time. The results could pave the way for larger trials eliciting the exact role of HOCl more precisely.

Methods and analysis

Study design

Hypoclates:Peritonitis uses a single-centre, sequential, quasi-experimental cohort design. This pragmatic approach evaluates a systematic, department-wide transition in the institutional standard of care—moving from conventional isotonic lavage to a standardised lavage using HOCl (Granudacyn). To minimise selection bias, the study compares a sequential, historical control cohort with a prospective intervention cohort. The study assesses outcomes within a pragmatic, real-world surgical setting and aims to include approximately 200 patients undergoing surgical treatment for secondary peritonitis over a 32-week period. Reporting adheres to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement guidelines for cohort studies. Although the study follows a quasi-experimental sequential design, the protocol was prepared in accordance with the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) framework to ensure comprehensive reporting of the intervention and trial procedures.

Study population

This study population was selected because patients undergoing surgical treatment for secondary peritonitis face a high risk of bacterial contamination and subsequent postoperative infections. This makes them an ideal cohort for evaluating the potential clinical benefits of SSI prevention and therapeutic strategies involving antimicrobial oxidising agents. Focusing on this high-risk population enables a meaningful assessment of HOCl within a preventive-therapeutic context, ensuring the agent’s efficacy is evaluated in an indication that fully leverages its antimicrobial potential.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients over 18 years of age of any gender with secondary peritonitis (any hollow organ perforation or complication presenting with peritonitis) are eligible for inclusion. Excluded are patients who cannot receive antibiotic therapy, have undergone organ transplantation as well as pregnant or breastfeeding women. Pregnancy is defined as the state of a woman from the time of conception until the end of pregnancy. Table 1 provides an overview of inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Patient and public involvement

Due to the emergency nature of secondary peritonitis, which necessitates immediate surgical intervention, patients and the public were not involved in the design or conduct of this research. However, the reduction of SSI remains a high priority for patients. Following study completion, findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and shared with patient advocacy groups to inform improved perioperative care strategies.

To ensure effective communication with the patient community, a post-trial patient advisory group will be established to review dissemination materials and assist in co-developing a lay-summary of the findings.

Study flow

All patients scheduled for surgical treatment of secondary peritonitis during the clinical trial period (12 August 2025 to 22 March 2026) will undergo screening and may be excluded based on exclusion criteria.

Patients who received treatment between 12 August 2025 and 30 November 2025 will be analysed as a control group, for which standard care for SSI prevention included intraoperative lavage with isotonic solution and, if considered necessary, additional postoperative irrigation via surgically placed drains.

Beginning on 1 December 2025, according to the new clinical standard, patients with secondary peritonitis will receive a single intraoperative lavage with 3 L of Granudacyn, placement of a lavage drain and, if indicated, an additional drain. This will be followed by further lavage with 3 L of Granudacyn every 24 hours for 3 days. Participation in the study requires no extra procedures, diagnostics or interventions, thus imposing no additional burden on patients or healthcare staff. No separate patient information or consent will be necessary for the prospective cohort. The local ethics committee granted a formal waiver for individual informed consent, since all patients of the prospective group receive the HOCl lavage independent of the study. If the target sample size is not achieved within the planned timeframe, either group may be extended by 4 weeks. The overall study duration will also include a 1-month follow-up to evaluate postoperative outcomes. According to institutional protocol, perioperative antibiotic prophylaxis will be repeated intraoperatively every 2−4 hours or in the event of blood loss exceeding 1.5 L. Following the conclusion of the official study period, the HOCl-based lavage protocol continues as the permanent clinical routine, as this intervention reflects an established department-wide transition in the institutional standard of care.

Lavage and drainage protocol

The intraoperative lavage is performed by the operating surgeon using 3 L of the active agent Granudacyn (a pH-neutral, hypotonic electrolyte solution containing stabilised HOCl (50 ppm) and sodium hypochlorite (50 ppm) in equilibrium). The fluid is instilled into the peritoneal cavity and deep wound layers, and is maintained in contact with the tissues for a standardised duration of at least 3 min to ensure adequate antimicrobial exposure before being fully evacuated using active surgical suction.

According to the new institutional standard of care effective from 1 December 2025, at least one of the intraoperatively inserted surgical drains must be designated and suitable as a lavage drain. To guarantee the complete delivery of the prolonged post-operative treatment, this lavage drain must remain in place for a minimum of 3 days to facilitate the daily irrigation with 3 L of Granudacyn every 24 hours on the ward. Following this mandatory 3-day intervention period, the decision of when to remove the drains lies entirely with the attending physician and is based predominantly on daily drainage output volume, fluid quality and the overall clinical status of the patient. Drains are never placed exclusively for study purposes but follow strict surgical indications for secondary peritonitis.

The presence or absence of a SSI is clinically evaluated and documented on the wards directly by the attending surgeons as part of the institutional clinical routine. At Heidelberg University Hospital, this process is highly standardised and integrated into the electronic medical record system (SAP IS-H*med) via a mandatory digitalised wound sheet and routine photographic documentation. In the event of an SSI requiring treatment, a specialised nursing transfer form is additionally triggered. Because this clinical routine documentation is strictly based on the standardised CDC criteria, a formal assessment effectively occurs in daily practice. However, the authors retrospectively review and cross-check the finalised, structured documentation within the SAP IS-H*med system. This final evaluation serves as a rigorous quality control step to verify CDC compliance, maximise data integrity and eliminate potential assessment bias. The primary endpoint is restricted to the inpatient phase up to day 10 (±2 days) to guarantee 100% data completeness. Potential late-onset infections are recorded descriptively via routine outpatient follow-up clinic records up to 30 days postoperatively.

The following postoperative complications will be assessed: Clostridium difficile-associated diarrhoea, lymphatic fistula, pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, urinary tract infection, stroke, myocardial infarction, portal vein thrombosis, ischaemic complications due to vascular stenosis or thrombosis, gastrointestinal bleeding, anastomotic insufficiencies and fistulas. The Comprehensive Complication Index (CCI) will be calculated for each patient.

Postoperative interventions and management measures will be recorded, including time in intensive care, whether percutaneous or CT-guided drainage was required, whether a relaparotomy was performed within 30 days, and whether the patient was readmitted within 30 days post-discharge. The overall length of hospital stay will also be documented.

Postoperative laboratory monitoring

Analogously to the preoperative situation, infection values are monitored during the postoperative course.

Endpoints and hypotheses

The primary endpoint focuses on the occurrence of SSIs, defined according to the clinical and diagnostic standards of the CDC. To capture the immediate effect of the antimicrobial lavage, the assessment period is restricted to the inpatient phase, ending at hospital discharge or no later than postoperative day 10 (±2 days). This specific timeframe ensures high data quality and focuses on infections directly linked to the surgical intervention, avoiding the follow-up difficulties common in this high-risk emergency population. Our primary hypothesis is that using HOCl for preventive lavage will lead to fewer SSIs compared with standard isotonic solutions. Secondary endpoints cover the duration of ICU and hospital stays, the length of antibiotic therapy and various organ-specific or general complications. We also track reoperations, readmissions and microbiological data. Regarding these measures, we hypothesise that HOCl-based treatment will be associated with shorter recovery periods, reduced antibiotic use and a lower CCI. Data management

Study data will be captured via an electronic case report form (eCRF) and stored on secure servers at the Study Centre of the German Society of Surgery at Heidelberg University, in strict adherence to European (GDPR - General Data Protection Regulation12) data protection regulations. To maintain participant confidentiality, data will be pseudonymised during collection and anonymised prior to final analysis. Access to pseudonymised data is strictly restricted to the immediate study team. All study-related data will be archived for a duration of 10 years, in accordance with institutional and national guidelines. To ensure data integrity, an independent researcher not involved in the primary data collection will cross-check 10% of the eCRF entries against the original electronic medical records (SAP IS-H*med).

Sample size

The target sample size of n=200 was determined based on the annual patient volume at our high-volume centre. To ensure the study is sufficiently powered to evaluate the primary hypothesis, an a priori power calculation was conducted.

This calculation assumes a baseline SSI incidence of 35% in the control group. This high baseline is justified by the specific study population: patients with secondary peritonitis are characterised by massive bacterial contamination, typically categorised as CDC class IV (‘dirty/infected’). Current literature on emergency visceral surgery and intra-abdominal infections supports the expectation of SSI rates in this range for such high-risk cohorts.5 10

Assuming this baseline, a total sample size of n=200 (n=100 per sequential cohort) provides a power of 80% (1-β=0.80) at a significance level of 5% (α=0.05) to detect an absolute risk reduction of 15%. This corresponds to a reduction of the SSI rate from 35% in the control group to 20% in the HOCl intervention group. This substantial reduction is anticipated because the active agent can exert its antimicrobial effect throughout all tissue layers of the wound, thereby directly influencing the overall incidence of SSIs. Such an effect size is considered highly significant given the high morbidity and economic burden associated with infections in peritonitis patients.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables will be analysed as such whenever possible. If categorisation is necessary, the rationale and cut-off points will be explicitly stated. Continuous data will be summarised using means with SD or medians with IQRs, depending on the data distribution. The t-test and p values will be used to evaluate differences. Categorical variables will be described using frequencies and percentages. Comparisons between categorical groups will be performed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Multivariate logistic regression analysis will be conducted to assess the independent association of HOCl use in the SSI prevention strategy with the primary endpoint (incidence of SSI). Potential confounders collected in the secondary endpoint data will be included in the model. ORs with 95% CIs will be reported. Preplanned subgroup analyses and sensitivity analyses will be performed to evaluate the robustness of the findings. Statistical analyses will be conducted using R (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Missing data will be transparently reported, including the number of patients with incomplete data per variable. If applicable, imputation methods or sensitivity analyses addressing missingness will be described in detail.

Study status

The observational periods for both the retrospective (August 2025 to November 2025) and prospective (December 2025 to March 2026) cohorts concluded by May 2026. The study is now in the data processing phase, with extraction from electronic health records and clinical data cleaning actively underway.

Discussion

Significance and clinical implications

Hypoclates:Peritonitis represents a significant step in evaluating HOCl as an active agent for the prevention and treatment of SSIs. This trial aims to establish a proof-of-concept that may justify larger, multicentre randomised controlled trials to further investigate the benefits of intracavitary antimicrobial oxidising agents. Beyond clinical outcomes, a proven efficacy of HOCl lavage could lead to a more favourable cost–benefit ratio by reducing postoperative healthcare costs and potentially limiting the development and spread of multidrug-resistant bacteria. Furthermore, the study may help identify specific patient subgroups that derive the greatest benefit from HOCl-based irrigation strategies.

Safety and risk–benefit considerations

From both ethical and clinical standpoints, this study is justified as it addresses a relevant knowledge gap without imposing additional burdens on patients. Since data collection is limited to routine clinical documentation, participation involves no extra interventions or procedures. Any potential adverse effects are confined to those inherent to Granudacyn, a CE-marked class IIb medical product with an established safety profile for wound and peritoneal irrigation. By using pseudonymised routine data, the study maintains high standards of patient confidentiality while promoting evidence-based improvements in perioperative management.

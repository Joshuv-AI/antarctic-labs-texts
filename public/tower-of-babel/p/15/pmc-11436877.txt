Artificial intelligence for cybersecurity monitoring of cyber-physical power electronic converters: a DC/DC power converter case study
Authors: Mohammad Reza Habibi, Josep M Guerrero, Juan C Vasquez
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPower electronic converters are widely implemented in many types of power applications such as microgrids. Power converters can make a physical connection between the power resources and the power application. To control a power converter, required data such as the voltage and the current of that should be measured to be used in a control application. Therefore, a communication-based structure including sensors and communication links can be used to measure the desired data and transmit that to the controllers. So, a power converter-based system can be considered as a type of cyber-physical system, and it can be vulnerable to cyber-attacks. Then, it can strongly be recommended to use a strategy for a power converter-based system to monitor the system and identify the existence of cyber-attack in the system. In this study, artificial intelligence (AI) is deployed to calculate the value of the false data (i.e., constant false data, and time-varying false data) and detect false data injection cyber-attacks on power converters. Besides, to have a precise technical evaluation of the proposed methodology, that is evaluated under other issues, i.e., noise, and communication link delay. In the case of noise, the proposed strategy is examined under noises with different signal-to-noise ratios . Further, for the case of the communication delay, the system is examined under both symmetrical (i.e., same communication delay on all inputs) and unsymmetrical communication delays (i.e., different communication delay/delays on the inputs). In this work, artificial neural networks are implemented as the AI-based application, and two types of the networks, i.e., feedforward (as a basic type) and long short-term memory (LSTM)-based network as a more complex network are tested. Finally, three important AI-based techniques (regression, classification, and clustering) are examined. Based on the obtained results, this work can properly identify and calculate the false data in the system.Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, Cyber-physical systems, Cybersecurity, False data injection attacks, Power convertersSubject terms: Energy grids and networks, Power distribution

Introduction

Artificial intelligence (AI) can support power and energy systems for various types of applications, e.g., design, energy management, control, protection, condition and health monitoring, cybersecurity, etc. Artificial neural networks (ANNs) are AI-based methodologies, which have been used in different power-based applications. For example, ANN-based strategies have been proposed by1–6 for load forecasting-based applications. Furthermore, sensorless strategies have been proposed by7 and8 for different purposes in AC and DC microgrids, respectively. Besides, in9, an ANN-based method has been used to provide a model predictive-based control application for DC microgrids. In addition, in10–15, ANN-based approaches have been used for the estimation of the state-of-charge of the batteries. For more examples, ANN-based methodologies have been deployed by16–20 for wind power prediction-based applications. Further,21 and22 implemented ANN-based techniques for islanding detection in microgrids. In addition, for more information about ANNs and power applications,23,24 can be studied.

In addition to the above-mentioned examples, ANNs have been used for cybersecurity-based applications in power and energy systems. For example, ANN-based approaches have been implemented in25–29 to mitigate cyber-attacks in DC microgrids. In25, ANNs have been deployed in a reference tracking application to mitigate the false data injection cyber-attacks in DC microgrids, which are shaped using parallel DC/DC power converters. For more clarification, in25, the output of the ANN is used to calculate the input signal of a controller, and the output signal of the controller is injected into the system to mitigate the false data. Also, in26, the application of the ANN and a model predictive control (MPC)-based controller are used to configure a secure control layer to mitigate cyber-attacks. In26, false data injection attacks (FDIAs) have been considered as the case study. Further, in26, the ANN-based model predictive controller produces a signal to mitigate the FDIA in the system. Besides, in27, an ANN-based application has been employed to remove FDIAs in DC microgrids. In27, the ANN calculates the value of the false data in the system, and based on the calculation of the ANN, the FDIA is mitigated. In addition, in28, an ANN-based approach has been implemented to mitigate cyber-attacks on current measurements in a DC microgrid. In28, the type of cyber-attack is the FDIAs, and it could remove FDIAs in the system even if all the power converters are under the FDIAs. It is important to note that, the proposed strategy of28 is a decentralized methodology, i.e., it uses the local data and it does not need to have access to the information of other parts of the DC microgrid. Furthermore, in29, a method using AI has been introduced to mitigate FDIAs in DC microgrids. In29, a recurrent neural network (RNN) is deployed as a type of AI-based approach to make a decentralized secure control layer to remove the false data in parallel DC/DC power converters in DC microgrids.

In addition, a cyber-attack detection strategy has been proposed by30 to identify the existence of FDIAs in DC microgrids. In30, RNNs have been used to estimate the value of the voltages and currents of the DC/DC power converters in the DC microgrids. Also, based on the deviation between the output of the RNNs and the measurements, the cyber-attacks are identified in the system. Also, in30, the type of the studied cyber-attack is the FDIAs, and the type of the implemented RNN is a nonlinear auto-regressive exogenous model (NARX) neural network. Further, in31, the application of the ANN has been implemented to detect cyber-attacks on distance relays in power systems. In31, the cyber-attack is considered the injection of the fake data. Besides, in31, the proposed strategy is deployed in a distributed mode. As another example of the deployment of ANN-based strategies,32 introduced a method for the detection and classification of cyber-attacks in power systems. The proposed method of32 is based on a metaheuristic algorithm to optimize the features of the data. Furthermore, a cyber-attack detection and diagnosis method for photovoltaic systems has been proposed by33. In33, the proposed method is based on the multilayer long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, and it considered the data integrity attacks as the case study. In addition, in33, the types of the studied power converters were DC/DC and DC/AC. As another example of the AI-based strategies related to the cybersecurity of the power-based application, the ANN-based methodology for the cyber-attack identification at the sensor level for the DC/DC converters has been introduced by34. In34, the type of cyber-attack is the FDIA, and the proposed strategy deployed the deep neural networks and an error detection network for controlling the converter as well as the detection and mitigation of the FDIA, respectively. Also,35, an ANN-based approach has been introduced to mitigate FDIAs in DC microgrids. In addition to the mentioned works,36–39 can be considered for more studies related to AI-based approaches and the cybersecurity of power-based applications.

In this work, an ANN is implemented for the cybersecurity monitoring of cyber-physical power converters. A power converter-based system includes different physical components and it can connect a power-based component such as a power resource (e.g., a renewable energy-based power resource) or an energy storage system (e.g., a lithium-ion battery) into a wider system or a load directly. The existence of the power converter permits to have control of the voltage and the current of the connected power application. In addition, to control a power converter, controllers can be implemented, which receive the inputs (e.g., the voltage and the current) to provide the control signals. Therefore, communication-based systems such as sensors and communication links are needed to measure the required parameters and send them to the controller. So, because of the deployment of physical parts, digital controllers, and communication systems, power converter-based systems can be considered as cyber-physical systems (CPSs) and they can be vulnerable to cyber issues, e.g., cyber-attacks. The existence of cyber-attacks on a cyber-physical power converter can cause serious problems, e.g., transmitting the state of the system from a proper state into a non-proper state, or even shutdown or disconnecting the system. Therefore, a proper method is needed to support the enhancement of the cybersecurity of a cyber-physical power converter-based system by detection of cyber-attacks.

In this study, because of the advantages of ANNs (e.g., ease of implementation, and non-dependence on the mathematical-based information about the system), the ANN is deployed to identify the existence of cyber-attacks on voltage measurement. The ANN-based approach is deployed in a decentralized mode. In other words, the proposed strategy only implements the local data of the unit as the input. Previously, some ANN-based methodologies have been proposed for cyber-attack detection in power-based applications. However, in the case of centralized approaches to detect cyber-attacks, if the solution (e.g., an ANN) receives the current and the historical values (with inputs memory order or time-delay of m) of i parameters (e.g., the voltage and the current) for each unit, and if the number of units is n, the ANN receives i×(m+1)×n input signals. For more clarification, if only the voltage and current of each converter in a DC microgrid with n DC/DC power converter is required, the ANN-based system needs 2×(m+1)×n inputs. Therefore, for the case of m=2, the cyber-attack detection method needs 6n inputs to detect the cyber-attack. Also, for the case of n=2, n=3, n=4, and n=5, the centralized approach with the mentioned conditions needs 12, 18, 24, and 30 inputs, respectively. As can be seen, by increasing the number of units, the number of inputs for the centralized-based ANN is increased. In this paper, the proposed ANN-based approach uses the current and historical values of three parameters, which will be discussed in more detail in the rest of the paper. So, if the time-delay or input memory order is m, this study only needs 3×(m+1) inputs. Therefore, if m=1, this study needs only 6 inputs. Also, in the case of distributed approaches, the system needs the local information and the information of the neighbors. Therefore, by increasing the number of neighbors, the system needs more inputs. Furthermore, a centralized approach can have less reliability. For more clarification, all the units should be connected to a centralized system to have the information of all units in the centralized system. In this case, the operation of the system depends on the accessibility of all information, and in the case of failing the access to information of one unit, the system can fail. If the system has n units, each unit can be connected to the centralized system by a communication link, and as a result, n communication links exist in the system. If the probability of failing the jth communication link (for 1≤j≤n) is a non-zero value of qj, the reliability of the system is ∏j=1n(1-qj). So, by increasing the number of units, the reliability of a centralized system will be decreased. However, as mentioned before, this paper proposes an ANN-based approach that requires only the local data and as a result, the proposed approach of this manuscript has more reliability compared to other centralized approaches. It is worth mentioning that, in this study, the ANN can calculate the value of the false data in the system directly. Therefore, the computational burden of the proposed method of this study is less than those mixed ANN with other applications such as controllers to obtain the value of the false data.

Furthermore, in this study, the type of the studied cyber-attack is FDIA, and the type of power converter is a buck DC/DC converter. This work has advantages, e.g., very fast response, and high accuracy. The implemented ANN-based strategy can estimate the value of the false data in the system, which provides information more than the cyber-attack identification. Also, the proposed method uses only the local data. In Table 1, some of the features and advantages of the proposed strategy are summarized.

Results

The proposed strategy is evaluated using MATLAB/Simulink environment. The cyber-physical power converter-based system includes different components, e.g., a DC source (V1), a DC/DC power converter, and an ohmic load (ROut). The DC source is connected to the input of the power converter. Also, the ohmic load is connected to the output of the power converter. It is important to note that, the type of the power converter is a buck DC/DC power converter. Further, V1=145V, and ROut is 1.9 Ω. Also, the reference value of the voltage controller for the output voltage of the power converter Rv is 50V. In addition, in this study, time-delay σ=1. Besides, different values can be considered for the sampling time. However, this manuscript tries to select a practical sampling time. Previously, some works related to power electronics have been done and validated experimentally considering sampling time Δt=0.1ms or less (in other words, the data sampling frequency of 10 kHz or more). For example, the data sampling frequency of 10 kHz is considered for the experimental verification in40 and41. Therefore, in this study, to have a more practical evaluation, the sampling time Δt of the inputs for the ANN is 0.1ms. It is important to note that, the implemented ANN includes an input layer to receive the inputs, one hidden layer, and an output layer to estimate the value of the false data. For the deployed ANN, the input layer, the hidden layer, and the output layer contain 6, 10, and 1 neurons, respectively. Table 2 summarizes parameters related to the tested system.

In Fig. 6, the real value of the output voltage of the DC/DC converter is depicted. As it is shown in Fig. 6, the injection of false data into the output voltage of the power converter can lead to a change in the real value of the output voltage. Based on Fig. 6, the output DC voltage of the power converter is converged to 49V, 47V, 46V, 45V, and 44.5V before t=2s, t=2.5s, t=3s, t=3.5s, and t=4s, respectively. So, if for example we consider 10% (i.e., 5V) allowance for the limitation bounds of the output voltage (i.e., the minimum value of the voltage and the maximum value of that are 50V-5V=45V, and 50V+5V=55V, respectively), the voltage should be between 45V and 50V. Therefore, based on the obtained results, by adjusting the value of the false data, the output DC voltage can exceed the allowance bounds. Also, in Fig. 7, the value of the false data and the estimated value of that by the ANN are illustrated. Based on Fig. 7, the ANN-based strategy can estimate the value of the false data properly. Discussion about AI-based regression, classification, and clustering

In this part, a discussion about the implementation of different AI-based techniques (i.e., regression, classification, and clustering) and a comparison between them for the identification of FDIAs is provided as follows:

ANN-based regression

This technique can estimate the value of the false data, precisely. In addition, this method illustrated very good results in detecting the FDIAs in cyber-physical power converters. In other words, when there are no FDIAs in the system, the output of the ANN is zero. Also, when there is a cyber-attack in the system, the ANN can estimate the value of the false data. However, this technique is a supervised learning-based approach, and as a result, it requires a dataset containing samples, which are gathered under a cyber-attack-based situation.

ANN-based classification

Based on the obtained results, the ANN for the classification goal can be trained properly. However, this technique can only detect the existence of the FDIA in the system. Also, this technique can not say anything about the value of the false data. Furthermore, this technique is a supervised learning-based methodology and it needs a dataset including labels. In other words, it requires a dataset including both healthy data and data under the cyber-attack.

SOM-based clustering

This technique is an unsupervised learning-based approach and as a result, there is no need to have the information (such as the label) about the gathered data. However, the obtained results show that the SOM-based strategy was unsuccessful for the clustering purpose to create the clusters and identify the cyber-attack. In addition, as same the classification approach, this method can not be used to provide more information about the FDIA, e.g., the value of the false data. Further, even if this method can work properly, it is very hard or maybe impossible to tell directly whether the event of the cyber-attack is included in the system or not, and if yes, which group corresponds to the cyber-attack.

General discussion, and future works

The implementation of digital tools and communication-based components can make a cyber-physical power converter vulnerable to cyber-attacks. Therefore, a proper strategy is required to identify the existence of the cyber-attacks. This work implemented an ANN as a type of AI-based strategy for the cybersecurity monitoring of power converters. In this work, FDIAs were considered as the type of the cyber-attacks. Also, this work considered buck DC/DC power converters as the case study. The proposed strategy could detect the FDIA in the system and estimate the value of the false data. In this work, only the local data were deployed as the input of the ANN. In this study, to have a more comprehensive analysis, different case studies including constant false data, different models of time-varying false data, as well as symmetrical and unsymmetrical communication delays have been considered. To have a more in-depth analysis of the case studies, different metrics and results have been shown. The results have illustrated the effectiveness of the proposed strategy to calculate the value of the false data and detect the FDIAs. In addition to the case studies, three technical discussions including results have been done to have a more detailed discussion. For the first technical discussion, the effect of the noise and the mitigation of that have been talked about. For the first technical discussion, noises with different SNRs have been studied. In addition, for the second technical discussion, another architecture of the ANNs including an LSTM-based structure has been involved to be studied. For the third technical discussion, different AI-based models, i.e., regression model, classification model, and clustering model have been evaluated in detail. Also, the obtained results have been used to provide a detailed comparison of the mentioned models (i.e., regression model, classification model, and clustering model) and their features in the third technical discussion. The results of the different case studies and the technical discussions illustrated that AI can be used as a powerful tool for the cybersecurity monitoring of cyber-physical power electronic converters.

In addition to the simulation results, lab-based results were prepared. The lab-based results illustrated the effectiveness of the proposed AI-based approach. It is important to note that, three neural networks were implemented to be trained for a dataset that is gathered in the lab. The first, the second, and the third neural networks were related to the condition of σ=0, σ=1, and σ=2, respectively. The regression coefficient related to the testing part for σ=0 had the minimum value among three neural networks, and for σ=2 had the maximum value. For the implementation of an experimental system, different challenges can be faced, e.g., packet loss, sensor failure, communication delay, non-clean dataset, etc. As mentioned before, this paper involved different scenarios and case studies considering events such as communication delays, and noise to have a more accurate validation.

For the future directions of this work, the implementation of a quantum computing-based strategy for the identification of cyber-attacks can be suggested. Recently, some studies such as42 and43 have been done related to quantum computing as well as power and energy-based applications. The recent works on the implementation of quantum computing for power applications can show the positive potential of quantum computing to improve the performance of the power applications. Besides, for future works, designing a more complex system can be suggested to differentiate between cyber-attacks and other uncertainties such as faults.

Conclusion

In this study, an AI-based strategy has been used to monitor the cybersecurity of cyber-physical power converters. Therefore, in the first step, the effect of the FDIAs on cyber-physiccal power converters has been studied. Then, AI-based strategy has been introduced. The proposed method has advantages and features. For example, the proposed application is a decentralized approach and it deploys only the local data. Also, the implemented approach can estimate the value of the false data. In addition, the proposed methodology has been examined under various conditions, e.g., FDIAs with constant false data, different time-varying false data, symmetrical communication delays, and unsymmetrical communication delays. Besides, three technical discussions including results have been provided to study more about the noise and the mitigation of that, other AI-based architecture, i.e., an LSTM-based structure, as well as evaluation of different AI-based models, i.e., regression model, classification model, and clustering model. Based on the obtained results, the proposed method can identify the existence of FDIAs and calculate the value of the false data, properly.

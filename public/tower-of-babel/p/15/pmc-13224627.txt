Molecular characterization of HPV integrations in high-risk HPV-positive women and development of a prognostic signature based on integration-associated genes for cervical cancer
Authors: Qiongying Lyu, Jiahui Zhao, Yurou Chen, Jiaqiang Xiong, Hairong Wang, Juan Zhang, Lihan Wang, Xiaoyan He, Wei Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHPV integration into the human genome is a key molecular event in the progression of cervical cancer carcinogenesis.MethodsThis study utilized samples from high-risk HPV-positive women to investigate HPV integration. Integration-associated genes were screened based on HPV-DNA integration sequencing data from our local clinical cohort, and their expression levels and prognostic value were independently validated and used for model construction in the TCGA-CESC RNA-seq dataset. Using this integration-associated prognostic risk model, cervical cancer (CC) patients from TCGA were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups. Differences in enrichment of PI3K-AKT pathway genes and immunotherapy response were analyzed between the two groups, and functional analysis was performed on differentially expressed immune-related genes between the subgroups.ResultsThe HPV integration rate gradually increases with the progression of cervical disease. The most frequent integration-related genes were KLF5, LINC00392, BCL11B and TP63. A risk model based on eight HPV integration-associated genes was developed, which could classify CC samples into high-risk groups with significantly shorter overall survival times compared to low-risk groups (P < 0.001). ROC curve evaluation showed that the AUCs for the 3, 5, and 7-year survival rates were 0.77, 0.74, and 0.76, respectively. The significant differences in PI3K-AKT pathway gene enrichment and prediction of immune therapy response were found between two risk groups.ConclusionA integration-associated prognostic risk model based on genes derived from HPV integration hotspots shows significant association with patient survival and is correlated with distinct tumor microenvironment features, including PI3K-AKT pathway activity and predicted immunotherapy response.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13027-026-00766-3.

Introduction

CC (Specific abbreviations are shown in Table 1) represents the fourth most common cancer and represents the fourth leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide in women [1]. In China, there were approximately 109,000 new cases of cervical cancer and 59,000 deaths according to GLOBOCAN 2020 database [2]. HhrHPV infection is the primary risk factor for CC, accounting for approximately 99.7% of cases [3]. While most HPV infections are naturally cleared within two years, persistent infection with certain hrHPV types can contribute to the development of cancer [4]. After infection, the virus can in either episomal form, or become integrated into the host genome [5].

Previous researches have highlighted the significance of HPV integration into the human genome as a key event in cancer progression [6]. The frequency of HPV integration increases as cervical disease progresses [7]. The HPV genome consists of three functional regions: LCR, early region (E1, E2, E4, E5, E6, and E7) and late region (L1 and L2) [8]. HPV integration can disrupt the E2 gene, which regulates viral DNA replication, transcription, and oncogenesis, resulting in unregulated oncoproteins expression of E6 and E7. These oncoproteins can impair tumor suppressor genes p53 and pRb, leading to dysregulation of the cell cycle, uncontrolled growth and genomic instability [9]. Additionally, inactivation of tumor suppressor gene and chromosomal rearrangements are other mechanisms that trigger the carcinogenesis [9]. HPV integration is a common phenomenon in CC.

Though previous studies have reported HPV genome integration into the human genome, most of them focused on HPV16 and HPV18 types as previous detection methods have limited scope [10, 11]. They could not accurately identify the exact location where the HPV genome integrated into the host genome, nor could identify which segment of the viral genome could integrate into the host genome. However, the IARC has classified 14 hrHPV types related with the CC. Therefore, HPV integration studies based on HIVID can overcome the above deficiencies [7]. Currently, there is a limited number of studies using HIVID to investigate HPV integration [12, 13], and the sample size in each study is relatively small. The sample types are mainly cervical cancer samples. Therefore, large-scale HPV integration studies using HPV-positive samples are urgently needed.

Whether different HPV integration genes could exert various influences on CC prognosis remains elusive. Accurately predicting CC patients’ prognosis and risk holds great clinical significance. Therefore, it is imperative to establish a predictive model to improve the accuracy of prognosis assessment for CC patients.

In this study, we conducted HIVID analysis on a cohort of high-risk HPV-positive women to investigate molecular characterization of HPV integration. We examined the prevalence and detailed characterization of HPV integration within the cohort. We established a integration-associated prognostic risk model and evaluated it thoroughly. CESC patients from TCGA were segregated into high and low risk groups. Differences in PI3K-AKT pathway enrichment and immunotherapeutic response were examined between the groups. Differentially expressed immune genes between the high and low risk groups underwent functional enrichment analysis. This study combined HPV integration detection and prognostic evaluation, contributing to a deeper understanding of the role of HPV infection in the development and progression of cervical cancer.

Materials and methods

Study design

First, we conducted a statistical analysis of 937 HPV-positive patients in Zhongnan Hospital from March 2021 to June 2023 to study the type of HPV infection and the relationship between its integration rate and pathological type; using clinical data statistical analysis and follow-up studies, the critical role of HPV integration in the occurrence and development of cervical cancer and its significance for therapeutic guidance in low-grade cervical lesions were evaluated. Secondly, we analyzed the integration sites of 467 HPV integration positive patients in Wuhan University Zhongnan Hospital and its affiliated medical consortium units during this period; based on gene expression data from the TCGA-CESC dataset, a prognostic signature incorporating 318 integration-associated genes (if the integration breakpoint falls directly within the genomic region of a gene (including introns or exons), that gene is defined as a “direct integration target gene.” If the integration breakpoint is located in an intergenic region, the nearest protein-coding or long non-coding RNA gene within a 100 kb window upstream or downstream is annotated as a “proximal/associated gene.” Both direct integration target genes and proximal/associated genes are collectively referred to as integration-associated genes) was constructed. We validated the model and classified TCGA patients into different risk groups. We compared the differences in PI3K-AKT pathway gene sets and immunotherapy responses among these groups. Finally, we performed functional enrichment analysis of differential immune genes to gain a comprehensive understanding of the impact of HPV integration on the occurrence, development, and prognosis of cervical cancer.

Patients and samples

We collected patient data from individuals who underwent HPV genomic integration testing at Zhongnan Hospital and its affiliated medical consortium units between March 2021 to June 2023. Patients in Zhongnan Hospital were followed up and the data were statistically analysed using the Fisher exact probability method. The study received approval from the Ethics Review Committee of Zhongnan Hospital and adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

HPV capture experiment

Genomic DNA was extracted using the DNA Extraction Kit (KDWS, Wuhan, China), according to the instructions of the manufacturer. The DNA concentration was quantified using the Qubit dsDNA HS Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher). 500 ng of DNA was fragmented into segments measuring 200–300 bp using enzymes (KDWS, Wuhan, China). These fragments underwent end-repair, dA-tailing, and adaptor ligation. The ligation products were then PCR-amplified to obtain a DNA library. The concentration of the DNA library was determined using a Qubit dsDNA HS Assay Kit. Hybridization was carried out using the HPV probes specific to the full-length genomes of 18 types of HPV, with the Target Enrichment Protocol (KDWS, Wuhan, China) (KDWS, Wuhan, China). In summary, 500ng of DNA library was hybridized twice, with HPV probes at 65 °C for 16 h, and the washing buffer was utilized to remove un-captured fragments. Finally, the enriched libraries were subjected to sequencing on an Illumina Sequencer (Novaseq 6000) using 150 bp paired-end reads.

Detection of HPV integration sites

The sequence data were utilized to ascertain the HPV genotypes and the integration status of HPV within the samples. First, clean reads were acquired by eliminating low-quality and duplicated reads, and reads contaminated with adaptors. Burrows-Wheeler Aligner (BWA) was employed to align clean reads to the human (Hg38) and HPV genome (NCBI: https://ftp.ncbi.nih.gov/genomes/). The paired-end reads that could be mapped to human or HPV reference genome at both ends were discarded. The remaining reads were paired-end assembled to reconstruct fragment sequences. Subsequently, reference mapping was conducted again to remap the paired-end assembled reads onto human and HPV genome using BWA (Li and Durbin 2009). ANNOVAR was used to do the annotation for the integrated breakpoints (Wang et al. 2010).The sample with breakpoints having a total support read count of > = 6 was considered positive. Threshold for Integration Positivity: A genomic locus was defined as a reliable integration event if it was supported by ≥ 6 paired-end sequencing reads (i.e., at least 3 independent read pairs) unambiguously spanning the human-HPV junction. This cutoff was selected based on the following considerations: (1) It significantly exceeded the observed background noise level in our dataset analysis to minimize false positives arising from random breaks or alignment artifacts; (2) It referenced stringent standards commonly used in the field for viral integration detection via high-throughput sequencing; (3) It was empirically determined through pre-analysis to provide an optimal balance between detection sensitivity and result specificity given the sequencing depth of this study. An ‘integration event’ was defined as a specific human-HPV genomic junction supported by ≥ 6 reads. An ‘integration gene’ was defined as any gene whose genomic coordinates (including gene body and regulatory regions) contained an integration breakpoint (exonic, intronic, upstream, downstream, or intergenic.

Validation of recurrent integration-associated genes

Gene expression of the recurrent integration-associated genes in CC patients and healthy controls was validated and visualized using GEPIA (cancer-pku.cn), and the HPA database (https://www.proteinatlas.org/).

Prognostic signature construction

The clinical information and mRNA matrix data were downloaded from the TCGA-CESC dataset, a total of 274 patients excluding those with survival days of less than or equal to 30 days. To enable an unbiased identification of prognostic biomarkers, the expression matrix of all HPV integration-associated genes were included (reads > = 6) for further analysis. The “survival” package was used to perform Cox univariate analysis in the training cohort, and the relationship between expression and prognosis of integration-associated genes were analyzed. For the prognostic integration-associated genes screened, we further screened the results by the LASSO regression analysis with 10-fold cross validation.

Coefi represents the regression coefficient of each gene in the model, x represents the gene expression level in the model, and n represents the number of genes in the model.

Prognostic signature evaluation

The risk score of each CESC patients in TCGA was calculated by the formula, and we divided patients into high and low risk groups utilizing a cutoff at the upper tertile risk score to ensure clinical relevance and statistical power for subsequent analyses. Employing the“survival”R package, we plotted Kaplan-Meier (K-M) survival curves using the log-rank test to evaluate the difference of overall survival rate between samples in high and low risk group, and considered p < 0.05 as statistically significant. We plotted time-dependent ROC curves with“survival”ROC package and calculated AUC values to evaluate the predictive performance of the model.

PI3K-AKT pathway gene enrichment comparison

Using gene sets from msigDBs (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb), we performed ssGSEA on the PI3K-AKT pathway, and calculated the enrichment score of PI3K-AKT pathway genes in different risk groups. We used Kruskal-Wallis test to determine the difference in PI3K-AKT pathway gene enrichment score between high and low risk groups based on risk model score (p < 0.05).

Prediction of immune therapy response

To explore the sensitivity of CESC patients to immune therapy, we used the TIDE algorithm to evaluate the immunogenicity and immunotherapy sensitivity of different risk group patients of CESC. We used the online website TIDE (https://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/) for evaluation and plotted violin plots using the “ggviolin()” function.

Functional enrichment analysis

Based on the high-risk and low-risk groups of CECS patients, DEGs (|log2FC|>1, p-value < 0.05) were obtained and intersected with immune-related genes to obtain immune DEGs. GO and KEGG functional enrichment analyses were carried out with clusterProfiler package(Wu et al. 2021). Statistical significance was defined by p value of < 0.05. The results were then visualized using the online website (https://www.bioinformatics.com.cn/).

Results

Characteristics of the study population

The analysis was performed on 937 women who tested positive for human papillomavirus (hrHPV) in this unit. Among 937 cases, 62.8% (589) exhibited a single HPV infection, 25.50% (239) had double infections, and 11.63% (109) showed multiple infections. The most frequent HPV genotypes were 52 (20.21%), 16 (15.75%), 58 (11.92%), 53 (7.67%), 51(6.48%). In single HPV infection, the frequent types were 52 (15.15%), 16 (11.74%), 58 (7.58%). In double infections, the most common combinations of HPV types were HPV16/HPV52 (1.92%), HPV16/HPV58 (1.28%), HPV52/HPV58 (1.17%).

HPV-DNA integration clinical data exploration

The above 937 patients were followed up for pathology and treatment prognosis. After excluding the patients who were lost to follow-up, a total of 601 patients were statistically analyzed. They were grouped according to pathological type. From low-grade lesions (4.82%) to HSIL (15.15%), and finally progressing to cervical cancer (56.0%), the HPV-DNA gene integration rate exhibited a progressively increasing trend (χ² = 85.057, P < 0.001). This difference was statistically significant. Univariate Cox regression analysis of progression-free survival in cervical cancer patients revealed no significant associations with age (P = 0.11) or initial FIGO stage (P = 0.536).

Further follow-up was conducted on HPV-DNA integration-positive patients, and statistical analysis was performed using Fisher’s exact test. For the low-grade lesion group, the negativity conversion rate in the observation/medication group at the six-month follow-up (12.5%) was significantly lower than that in the physical/surgical treatment group (89.47%) (P < 0.001). At the one-year follow-up, the negativity conversion rate in the observation/medication group (37.5%) was significantly lower than that in the physical/surgical treatment group (89.47%) (P = 0.011). The differences were both statistically significant.

Distribution of HPV integration

We performed a comprehensive analysis of data from HPV integration-positive patients between March 2021 and June 2023 in Wuhan University Zhongnan Hospital and its affiliated medical consortium units, resulting in a total of 467 HPV integration events (a specific, experimentally detectable molecular event in which a fragment of high-risk HPV (HR-HPV) viral DNA is inserted into the host cell’s genomic DNA). Figure 1A illustrates the number and frequency of integration events for each HPV type. The findings revealed that the most prevalent types of HPV integration were 16 (66.17%, 309/467), followed by 18 (16.49%, 77/467), 52(5.35%, 25/467), 33 (3.64%, 17/467).

Furthermore, we conducted an exploration of the distribution characteristics of HPV integration within the human genome. Our analysis revealed that integration events primarily occurred in intergenic regions, followed by intronic regions, exon regions, and the up/downstream regions of genes (Fig. 1B). The distribution of HPV integration sites across each chromosome of the human genome is shown in Fig. 1C. Notably, HPV integrations were observed across nearly all chromosomes of the human genome, with the highest frequency observed in chromosomes 9, 8, 12, 14, 4, 18,3, 5, 2,16 and X.

Hotspot genes of HPV integration

We found 28 hotspot genes (n > = 2) of 318 HPV integration-associated genes in the human genome, and the most frequent genes were KLF5 (5), LINC00392 (5), BCL11B (3) and TP63 (3) (Fig. S1, Table S1). We further investigate whether these integration hotspot genes were functionally cancer-related genes which involved in carcinogenesis. The recurrent integration- hotspot genes expressions with mRNA and protein level were assessed using multiple public databases. Analysis of the GEPIA database revealed that the mRNA expression of KLF5, BCL11B and TP63 genes were significantly higher in CC tissues compared to normal samples (Fig. 2A), which is consistent with their established functions in cancer. Similarly, the data from Human Protein Atlas indicated higher protein expression levels of these genes in CC patients compared to normal tissues (Fig. 2B). Detection of integration breakpoints within these genes identifies them as recurrent genomic targets for HPV but does not, by itself, demonstrate functional disruption or a causal role in oncogenesis.

Selection of prognostic genes and construction of integration-associated prognostic risk model for CC

We included all 318 human genes harboring at least one integration breakpoint in our cohort for the initial screening. This unbiased approach aimed to explore the full spectrum of integration-associated genes for potential prognostic relevance. First, we performed univariate Cox regression analysis on the expression matrix of 318 HPV integration genes, and obtained 25 integration genes that were associated with the overall survival of CC patients (Fig. S2). Then, we obtained 16 key genes by LASSO regression (Fig. S3): C17orf99, CCDC187, CD38, CXCL8, DLG2, MCTP2, MMP13, NEK6, NLGN1, PARP15, PCDH18, PTPRD, SERPINB7, SLC39A10, SPANXN3, and YWHAG. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was further performed and eight independent prognostic integration genes were obtained (Table S2). Finally, we constructed a integration-associated prognostic risk model. The risk score of the model was based on these eight integration genes.

Evaluation of the prognostic signature for CC patients

Based on the median gene expression assessed by the risk model, we divided cervical cancer patients into 91 high-risk and 183 low-risk groups (Table S3, Table S4). Kaplan-Meier survival curves showed that the overall survival time of the high-risk group was significantly shorter than that of the low-risk group (P < 0.001) (Fig. 3A). Subsequently, the time-dependent ROC curve evaluation model showed that the area under the curve (AUC) of the 3-year, 5-year, and 7-year survival rates were 0.77, 0.74, and 0.76, respectively (Fig. 3B). These results indicate that the risk model demonstrates moderate but significant predictive accuracy for the prognosis of cervical cancer patients.

Discussion

Persistent infection with hrHPV is recognized as a major cause of CC. However, approximately half of HPV infections resolve within one year [14]. HPV can integrate into the host cell, leading to a series of genetic events that enable viral replication [15]. The integration of HPV into the host genome plays a crucial role in HPV-induced carcinogenesis. Contributing to both genomic instability and sustained expression of viral oncogenes. It is well-established that the frequency of HPV integration correlates positively with the severity CIN. A high prevalence of HPV integration, ranging from 84.3% to 97.8%, has been observed in HPV-positive cervical cancers [7, 16, 17]. Therefore, monitoring HPV integration in host cells is essential for the development of preventive and early diagnostic strategies for CC.

HIVID has proven to be a valuable method for testing HPV types, identifying the precise breakpoints of the viral genome, and determining their locations in the human genome. In our study, we employed HIVID to reveal an integration positivity rate of 8.75%. HPV integration rate in our cohort is similar to the study reported by Hu et al. in a similar HPV-positive cohort with 9.9% rate [13]. This concordance reinforces the validity of our findings. The most prevalent integrated HPV types were 16, followed by 18, 52, and 33. Although the infection rate of HPV 18 is not as high in the Chinese population as in Western populations, its integration rate is relatively high. Conversely, HPV types 52 and 58 have a higher infection frequency in the Chinese population, but their integration rates are not as high as those of HPV 16 and 18. This finding suggests that among high-risk HPV-positive women in China, different types exhibit varying propensities to drive genomic instability (as characterized by integration frequency), which is not entirely aligned with their prevalence in the population. The high infection rates but relatively lower integration frequencies of HPV 52 and 58 may indicate a greater reliance on integration-independent oncogenic pathways or a relatively lower efficiency in inducing high-level genomic instability within the infected population.

The HPV-DNA integration positivity rate reported by our center and affiliated institutions is 8.75%, which is slightly lower than previously reported values [18]. This discrepancy primarily stems from fundamental differences in the composition of the study cohorts: the present study aimed to delineate the distribution of integration across the disease continuum and included a substantial number of individuals who had not yet progressed to cancer. There was no statistically significant association between age or initial stage at diagnosis of cervical cancer and progression-free survival. On one hand, this may be due to the relatively limited sample size and insufficient statistical power; on the other hand, it may reflect the lack of adjustment for treatment modalities and comorbidities. The effect of age on survival may be indirectly reflected through factors such as treatment tolerance and tumor biological behavior. Meanwhile, although disease stage is a well-established prognostic factor, heterogeneity in treatment strategies and outcomes within each stage may weaken its predictive capacity.

Clinical follow-up revealed that the HPV-DNA integration rate demonstrated a significantly increasing trend with the progression of cervical disease. For low-grade cervical lesions with positive HPV integration, this single-center observational study found that patients who underwent physical/surgical treatment exhibited higher rates of HPV negativity conversion, both in the short and long term, compared to those managed with observation or medical therapy alone. These findings provide preliminary evidence suggesting that HPV integration status may serve as a potential biomarker for assessing the risk of progression and therapeutic response in low-grade lesions. A positive integration status might indicate a need for closer monitoring or offer a reference for clinicians considering more active intervention strategies. However, it is crucial to emphasize that these data originate from a single-center study and are subject to inherent selection bias. This comparison is susceptible to confounding by indication, as the choice of treatment strategy was not randomized but based on clinical judgment and patient characteristics. As an observational study, the results should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing. Future prospective, multi-center, large-scale studies are warranted to further validate the utility of integration status in guiding treatment choices.

HPV integration into the human genome is random, with almost equal distribution across all chromosomes. However, there are also integration hotspot genes [7] and the hotspot genes were various in the different studies [7, 19–22]. Previous studies have reported that some common fragile sites widely exist in human chromosomes, which are the common integration sites of CC [23, 24]. In addition, the difference in integration hotspot genes may be due to variations in the source of study samples [14]. In this study, we detected 28 integration hotspot genes, with protein-coding genes KLF5, LINC00392, BCL11B, and TP63 having a higher integration frequency. The integration sites were inserted within the KLF5 and LINC00392 genes, which have been reported in several literatures [25–27].

While the GEPIA and HPA analyses confirm the dysregulation of these genes in cervical cancer—an observational phenomenon associated with the cancer phenotype—these database analyses per se (based on bulk tissue transcriptomic or protein data) cannot distinguish whether this differential expression is driven by HPV integration events or by other independent oncogenic mechanisms (such as promoter methylation, altered transcription factor regulation, chromosomal copy number variations, etc.). Therefore, our use of these public databases aims to confirm the biological and clinical relevance of these hotspot genes in cervical cancer.

Krüppel-like Factor 5 (KLF5) gene belong to the Krüppel-like Factor (KLF)family, which is known to play important roles in controls essential cellular processes [28]. KLF5 can directly transcriptionally activate key genes that promote cell cycle progression, such as cyclin D1 (CCND1) and c-Myc, driving the transition of cells from the G1 phase to the S phase. It can also upregulate genes associated with cell motility and extracellular matrix remodeling, thereby enhancing the migratory and invasive capabilities of cells. Frequent HPV integrated into KLF5 might promote cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, which could partially explain the HPV-rendered changes of cellular function in cancers. Moreover, KLF5 activates tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily member 11a (TNFRSF11a) gene transcription by binding to the TNFRSF11a gene promoter in cervical cancer. The KLF5/TNFRSF11a axis promoted the proliferation, migration and invasion of cervical cancer cells [29].

The B-cell lymphoma/leukemia 11B (BCL11B) gene encodes a lineage-specific C2H2-type zinc finger transcription factor protein and functions in cell cycle regulation and apoptosis upon DNA replication stress and damages via transcription [30]. BCL11B is a well-established oncogenic driver that supports tumor growth by suppressing apoptotic pathways (such as the p53 pathway) and activating pro-survival signaling. The high-risk HPV E6 protein degrades p53, and BCL11B is also involved in regulating p53-mediated responses. Therefore, HPV infection may dually disrupt the core cellular defense network against DNA damage through both E6 (degrading p53) and integration-induced impairment of BCL11B function. Several studies have reported the effect BCL11B on cell proliferation and chemoresistance in cancer as an oncogene [31–34]. This leads to a speculation that HPV integration into such region might trigger great chromosomal instability.

Tumor protein p63 (TP63) is another gene that exhibits the high frequency of HPV integration, encodes a member of the p53 family of transcription factors [35]. It is closely related to tumorigenesis, the oncogenic role of TP63 is particularly prominent in cervical squamous cell carcinoma, which may be required in conjunction with TP73/p73 for initiation of p53/TP53 dependent apoptosis in response to genotoxic insults and the presence of activated oncogenes, involved in Notch signaling by probably inducing JAG1 and JAG2 [36, 37]. TP63 was identified as a hotspot of HPV integration in our study, which was consistent with previous reports [25, 38, 39].

In summary, this study provides observational evidence at the genomic level that HPV integration shows a propensity to target specific genes (KLF5, BCL11B, TP63). The known functions of these genes in cancer-related pathways lend biological plausibility to their status as integration hotspots. However, it must be explicitly stated that this study did not directly demonstrate whether or how integration events concretely alter the structure, expression, or lead to functional fusion transcripts of these genes. The potential oncogenic mechanisms discussed herein, based on integration hotspots and gene functions, remain scientific hypotheses to be tested by future functional studies. These findings provide crucial candidate targets and research directions for subsequent investigations into how HPV integration may drive cervical carcinogenesis by interfering with these specific genes.

Given the significant heterogeneity in the genomic distribution of HPV integration, with relatively few high-frequency integration events shared across different patients, we included all detectable integration-related genes to more comprehensively capture the potential widespread genomic perturbations and transcriptomic impacts of integration events. This approach aims to avoid missing important prognostic signals due to an excessive focus on a limited number of high-frequency genes. Therefore, a total of 318 integration-associated genes were included.

In order to further clarify the pattern and clinical significance of HPV integration, we utilized gene expression data from cervical cancer patients in the TCGA-CESC database to construct a prognostic Signature for cervical cancer patients based on the integrated 318 genes, and validated the model. Next, we divided TGCA patients into high and low-risk groups based on the risk model and compared the differences in PI3K-AKT pathway gene sets and immunotherapy responses among different risk groups. Finally, through functional enrichment analysis of differential immune genes, we can comprehensively understand the impact of HPV integration on the occurrence, development, and prognosis of cervical cancer.

The results revealed a significantly higher enrichment level of PI3K-AKT pathway genes in the high-risk group compared to the low-risk group. Among the eight genes in our prognostic signature, NEK6 and YWHAG have been previously implicated in the PI3K-AKT signaling pathway in other cancer types. NEK6, a serine/threonine kinase, can be regulated by AKT and promotes cell proliferation and survival [40]. YWHAG (14-3-3γ) functions as an adapter protein that interacts with key components of the PI3K-AKT pathway and has been associated with cancer progression [41]. These prior links provide a plausible biological connection between our signature and the observed PI3K-AKT pathway enrichment in the high-risk group, although direct mechanistic validation in cervical cancer remains to be performed.

The analysis revealed that the high-risk group exhibited a higher TIDE score, which is computationally associated with a greater propensity for immune escape and a potentially poorer response to immunotherapy. The analysis results of this study suggested that biological processes such as the regulation of cell adhesion and T cell receptor signaling pathway, natural killer cell mediated cytotoxicity and Th17 cell differentiation may be dysregulated in the high-risk group. Importantly, the observed differences in pathway enrichment and immune-related scores between risk groups should be interpreted as correlational findings rather than mechanistic evidence linking HPV integration to immune evasion.The prognostic differences observed between the risk groups could also be attributed to factors other than HPV integration, such as advanced tumor stage, increased tumor burden, or underlying genomic instability. Therefore, the pathway and immune analyses in this study were aimed at identifying potential associations between the risk groups and biological pathways/immune phenotypes, rather than establishing mechanistic causality. It is important to note that TCGA-CESC patients did not receive immune checkpoint inhibitors; thus, TIDE scores reflect computationally inferred immune evasion potential, not actual clinical response to immunotherapy.

The prognostic performance of our risk model, with AUC values (0.74 to 0.77 for 3-, 5-, and 7-year overall survival), demonstrates moderate and clinically relevant predictive accuracy. The model’s accuracy is comparable to that of signatures derived from recently published prognostic signatures for CC. Nie et al. reported an AUC of greater than 0.9 for a 29 immune-related gene-pairs signature [42], Kang et al. developed an angiogenesis-related signature with a 5-year AUC of approximately 0.774 [43], signatures based on autophagy genes [44], erroptosis genes [45], cellular senescence genes [46] have similarly demonstrated performance in the AUC. While the integration hotspot genes in this prognostic model are not classical immune genes per se, our analysis found that tumors defined as high-risk by this signature exhibited transcriptomic features associated with specific immune microenvironment states and enrichment changes in immune-related functional pathways. These correlational findings suggest a potential association between integration status and tumor immune phenotypes, although the underlying mechanisms warrant further investigation. The integration of HPV is a key factor in the progression of cervical lesions, and previous studies have demonstrated a correlation between HPV integration and poor prognosis [47, 48]. Our signature is derived from the physical sites of HPV integration, thereby providing a complementary and biologically distinct perspective for risk stratification. To our knowledge, this is among the first studies to develop a prognostic signature for cervical cancer based on the expression profile of genes that are recurrent targets of HPV integration.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. First, the prognostic signature was constructed from genes located at frequent HPV integration sites identified in our high-risk HPV-positive cohort, we could not directly test whether the expression levels of these eight genes between integration-positive and integration-negative samples to establish a direct mechanistic link between HPV integration status and the expression levels. Second, the prognostic model was constructed using expression data from TCGA-CESC, which lacks HPV integration site information. Therefore, the eight genes are referred to as “integration-associated” rather than directly integration-driven. Using expression data as a surrogate for integration effects does not establish causality. The prognostic signature lacks validation in an independent external cohort. The absence of independent datasets containing the full expression profile of our eight-gene signature and associated clinical prognostic characterization prevented this critical step. Future research should aim to establish cohorts that can support this external validation. Third, The immune and pathway analyses are based on computational algorithms and require validation with orthogonal experimental methods (e.g., IHC, flow cytometry) in future studies.

Despite these limitations, they delineate clear directions for future research, including external validation in prospective multi-center cohorts, conducting paired multi-omics and functional experiments on integration-positive/negative samples, and exploring the potential utility of this prognostic signature in clinical decision support.

Conclusion

In summary, HPV integrated detection is a clinically valuable tool for monitoring and diverting cervical lesions. Integration-positive low-grade lesion patients achieved significantly higher viral clearance with physical/surgical intervention versus conservative management, indicating integration status serves as a critical biomarker for guiding clinical decision-making. We detected 28 recurrent genes for virus integration in 318 integration-associated genes, the most frequent were KLF5, LINC00392, BCL11B and TP63. Subsequently we constructed the prognostic signature based on 318 HPV integration-associated genes. Our data helped to gain insights into the HPV integration sites in hrHPV-positive women. It had provided theoretical basis for risk prediction from the perspective of HPV integration in the HPV-associated cervical cancers. However, this study also has certain limitations. The accuracy of our model was only validated in the TCGA dataset, and further validation in larger samples is required.

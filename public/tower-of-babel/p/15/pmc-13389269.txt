Analytical investigation of natural frequencies and mode shapes of a multi-span beam with a disc and spring supports
Authors: Vikas Sharma, Servesh K. Agnihotri, Manish Rawat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Determining the frequency equation and mode shapes becomes challenging when long shafts are modeled as multi-span Euler–Bernoulli beams with an intermediate disc, particularly due to the coupling effects introduced at the span junction. During operation, such systems may encounter critical speeds where resonance occurs. In the present work, an analytical investigation of a multi-span beam–disc system with simply supported and elastically supported boundaries is carried out. Closed-form expressions for the frequency equation and corresponding mode shapes are derived. A key contribution of this study is the identification and analytical treatment of coupling terms at the span junction, enabling explicit characterization of inter-span influence on mode-shape behavior. The analysis incorporates mass and stiffness ratios to examine their effects on modal characteristics. Furthermore, the influence of spring stiffness on shifting natural frequencies is analytically demonstrated, providing insight into critical speed tuning. The developed formulation generalizes previously observed experimental behavior into a unified analytical framework, enabling predictive design and supporting further theoretical developments in vibration control applications.

Introduction

Flexible shafts in the machines are most often modelled as Euler-Bernoulli beams to visualize the effect of transverse vibrations. A rigid disc i.e., rotor considered on the shaft is used to represent flywheels, blades, cranks, rotary wings, coupling, disc brakes, impellers, etc.1. The transverse vibrations will be significant due to centrifugal force because of the rotor rotating at a high rotating speed. It is crucial to determine the dynamic characteristics of shaft-rotor system (rotor-beam system) when operating at high rotating speed during the design progress of a high-speed application. Under close approximation of realistic conditions of rotor-beam system, modelled by Euler-Bernoulli beams for transverse vibrations will result into frequency equation; solving which could help to determine the natural frequency and mode shapes1. The natural frequency for the transverse vibration would be the critical speed of the rotor, when operating close to it, resonance (high amplitude vibration phenomenon) could be observed. Elastic/spring support is one of the ways to enable the control of vibration when passing the critical speed of the shaft2. Therefore, in the domain of vibration control of the shaft, the effect of spring support is a relatively new research area to explore.

While the Timoshenko–Ehrenfest beam theory provides a more general formulation by accounting for shear deformation and rotary inertia, the Euler–Bernoulli theory yields sufficiently accurate results for slender beams where shear effects are negligible and is therefore often preferred due to its simplicity12. Expressions for the frequency equations and mode shapes of generic Euler-Bernoulli beams with simple end conditions can be found in standard textbooks such as4–6. Systems in real life are not as simple, sometimes there are beams with elastic support and other with various intermediate restraints (e.g. lumped masses, moving masses, translational and/or rotational springs etc.) which are not discussed due to mathematical complexity posed by addition of new elements into the physical model. The problem of free lateral vibrations with arbitrarily located intermediate elastic support for a clamped-clamped uniform Bernoulli-Euler beams was first treated by Gorman7. Later, Rao8 provided explicit expressions for the frequencies and mode shapes of a clamp-clamp beam with an intermediate elastic support. After that, the green function method was used in the free vibration analysis of Euler-Bernoulli beams with intermediate elastic supports by Kukla9; however, is restricted only to homogeneous boundary conditions. Rončević et al.10 calculated the natural frequencies and mode shapes are using Green’s function for a combination of seven different boundary conditions considering the translational spring but, without load. In one of the studies by Wang et al.11, it was found that when an intermediate elastic support is positioned properly, the effect is similar to a rigid support. Hence, alternatively spring support can be tuned to get the response of rigid support.

For simply supported beam carrying a concentrated mass as well as rotatory inertia at the center of beam has been made to identify the symmetric or antisymmetric vibration13 but, there was no consideration of spring attachments14. Further, considering the rotary inertia of masses, the natural frequency of the Bernoulli–Euler beam was determined considering the using translational and rotational springs at both ends15. But the case of unsymmetric end-supports of beam and its mode shapes are not presented in the paper. Recently, Adhikari and Khodaparast16 derived the exact equation governing the natural frequencies considering the effect of rotatory inertia of an object attached to the vibrating cantilever replicating a nanomechanical sensor based on Euler Bernoulli beam. Few other studies considering rotatory inertia have been reported with identical end supports17–20. Kloda et al.21–23 collectively investigate the nonlinear dynamic behavior of a Timoshenko beam using axial spring and roller support, highlighting complex vibration responses through combined analytical, numerical, and experimental approaches. From the literature it has been noted that either the effect of mass is investigated considering a simply supported beam or the different elastic supports without mass. Lastly there is also a condition when both mass and elastic support are at the same point thereby modelling it as a single span problem.

To the knowledge of the authors, the problem considering the effect of rotatory inertia with non-identical end-supports in a multi-span beam has not been addressed. Based on the work carried out, the novelty lies in identification and analytical treatment of coupling terms in mode-shape estimation at the junction. This implies demonstration of inter-span influence. Further, analytically demonstrated how varying spring stiffness shifts natural frequencies (critical speeds). Lastly, converts previously observed experimental behavior into a generalized analytical model, enabling predictive design and further theoretical development. This study derives the closed-form solution for the natural frequency with respect to the support position as well as expressions for the mode-shapes under non-identical supports. The solution process also provides insight into the dynamics of a beam using numerical examples. Furthermore, the present procedure is easily extended to locate the shift of natural circular frequency/critical speeds for vibration control applications, as demonstrated in earlier experimental work of the authors1.

The paper is outlined in such a way that at first the model of the beam is explained thoroughly by considering a disc at the center of the beam. Here one end of the shaft has spring support, and the other end is simply supported. The natural frequency equation is derived from the transcendental equation by considering the boundary conditions of the end supports and the rotatory inertia of the disc. This is followed by expressions for mode shapes of the beam with rotatory inertia and spring support at one end. This derived equation is compared with the simply supported case to identify the significance of spring support.

Conclusion

An analytical study to investigate the effect of spring support on a multi-span beam with non-identical end conditions besides a rotating disc is presented in this paper. The study demonstrates that the natural frequencies are significantly influenced by the spring stiffness \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{K}_{s}$$\end{document}and the rotary inertia parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{M}_{R}$$\end{document}. In particular, the odd-numbered modes are strongly dependent on \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{K}_{s}$$\end{document}and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{M}_{R}$$\end{document}, while the even-numbered modes are additionally influenced by the mass moment of inertia of the attached disc about its central axis. When the effect of the rotatory inertia of the beam is included, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:r/l\ne\:0$$\end{document}, the frequencies of high modes are lower than those when \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\frac{r}{l}=0$$\end{document} and its effect is substantial which can be noted from the mode shape plots at different spring stiffness. Furthermore, the spring stiffness plays a critical role in governing both the frequency variation and the evolution of mode shapes, especially within the transition region where the system response shifts from beam-dominated to stiffness-dominated behavior. The present model provides a useful analytical framework for interpreting experimentally observed behavior of such systems. Future work may focus on incorporating damping, nonlinear effects, and further investigation of modal interactions including possible mode veering phenomena, along with experimental validation.

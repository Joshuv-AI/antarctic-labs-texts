Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis identifies a core gene-metabolite network linking lysine degradation and propanoate metabolism to psoriasis pathogenesis
Authors: Hongkai Zheng, Rujun Xue, Wei Li, Xiaonan Sima, Jingyao Liang, Sanquan Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPsoriasis is a complex chronic inflammatory disease with cutaneous manifestations, driven by intricate interactions between genetic, immunological, and metabolic dysregulations. However, the integrated molecular networks connecting transcriptional alterations to metabolic reprogramming remain incompletely elucidated.MethodsWe performed a comprehensive integrated analysis of transcriptomics and targeted metabolomics using serum samples from 11 psoriasis vulgaris patients and 11 healthy controls to dissect the core gene-metabolite regulatory axes underlying psoriasis.ResultsRNA sequencing identified 5,186 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), with a striking predominance of downregulation (5,147 DEGs), and targeted metabolomic profiling detected 208 significantly altered metabolites, including upregulated propanoic acid and downregulated L-allysine. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed six dysregulated metabolic pathways shared by DEGs and altered metabolites, notably lysine degradation, propanoate metabolism, and central carbon metabolism in cancer—with the latter identified as a novel pathogenic pathway in psoriasis vulgaris. Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) further delineated strong correlations between core genes (TP53, MTOR, AKT3) and key metabolites (propanoic acid, L-allysine), forming a functional network that links cellular energy metabolism to immune-inflammatory responses. Protein-protein interaction network analysis confirmed these core genes as hub regulators within the dysregulated pathways.ConclusionIn this exploratory pilot study, integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic profiling revealed preliminary associations between lysine degradation, propanoate metabolism, and psoriasis status. The observed crosstalk with central carbon metabolism, potentially involving TP53, MTOR, and AKT3, generates hypotheses that require validation in independent, larger cohorts before any clinical or mechanistic inferences can be drawn.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12920-026-02420-8.

Introduction

Psoriasis is a common chronic inflammatory disease with cutaneous manifestations, clinically characterized by well-demarcated erythematous plaques with silvery-white scaling [1–3]. Its pathogenesis involves hyperproliferation and aberrant differentiation of keratinocytes, accompanied by immune cell infiltration [4]. The development of psoriasis is influenced by a combination of factors, including innate and adaptive immune responses, genetic susceptibility, environmental triggers, and metabolic dysregulation [5]. Epidemiological evidence indicates that the global burden of psoriasis approximately doubled from 1990 to 2021, with 43 million people living with psoriasis and incident cases rising from 2.85 million to 5.1 million. The age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) increased from 57 to 62 per 100,000 population, with East Asia reporting the highest age-specific incidence rates globally. Projections through 2040 suggest a sustained upward trajectory, with no indication of stabilization or decline, including in China [6, 7].

Transcriptomics, which examines RNA expression patterns at a cellular level, has become a fundamental tool in dermatological research. Advances in high-throughput RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) have greatly promoted molecular insights into autoimmune skin diseases and facilitated precision medicine approaches [8]. In psoriasis, transcriptome analyses have identified consistently validated biomarkers such as S100A7A, miR-203, NLRP1, NLRP3, and AIM2 [9–12]. As a key component of systems biology, metabolomics provides a global profile of low-molecular-weight metabolites, offering functional insights into disease mechanisms. In psoriasis research, metabolomic studies using serum, skin tissue, and urine samples have revealed disease-associated metabolic signatures and potential biomarkers [13–16]. These studies indicate dysregulation in several metabolic pathways—including lipid metabolism (e.g., fatty acids and phospholipids), amino acid metabolism (e.g., glutamine and arginine), and energy metabolism (e.g., the tricarboxylic acid cycle) [17–19]. It should be emphasized that serum selection aligns with prevailing practice in psoriasis metabolomics, where blood-derived samples (serum/plasma) predominate [20–22]. Systemic biofluids capture integrated metabolic perturbations reflective of disease activity, while offering practical accessibility, minimal invasiveness, and reduced lesional heterogeneity—attributes consistent with this exploratory pilot design.

In this study, a integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis revealed a core gene-metabolite network in psoriasis, with central carbon metabolism emerging in cancer as a key dysregulated pathway.

Materials and methods

Patients and samples

This study was conducted at the Guangzhou Dermatology Hospital from 2022 to 2025 and received ethical approval from the hospital’s institutional review board. Written informed consent was formally obtained from all serum donors prior to blood sample collection. Patients with psoriasis vulgaris (n = 11) and healthy control (HC) subjects (n = 11) were initially recruited. The diagnosis of psoriasis vulgaris was based on clinical and pathological criteria, and the health status of control subjects was strictly verified through comprehensive physical examination. Psoriasis patients had no immunosuppressant/biologic exposure within six months, were aged 18–65 years, and excluded those with concurrent dermatological/autoimmune disorders (e.g., nephropathy, osteoporosis, thyroid dysfunction), pregnancy, or lactation. Only plaque psoriasis (psoriasis vulgaris) was included, while pustular and erythrodermic subtypes were excluded given their rarity, insufficient sample size, and possible differences in underlying mechanisms. The whole blood from 11 healthy donors (Guangzhou Institute of Dermatology) served as controls. Blood samples were collected from participants and promptly transferred to cooling devices maintained at − 80 °C for preservation until subsequent transcriptomic profiling and metabolomic analysis.

RNA sequencing

Serum from 11 psoriasis vulgaris patients and 3 healthy controls was collected via median cubital venipuncture into purple-top EDTA tubes (BD Vacutainer) at 9:00–10:00 AM after overnight fasting. Serum was separated (2,000 × g, 10 min, 4 °C), aliquoted, and stored at − 80 °C. Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen) and quality-controlled (RIN ≥ 7.0; A260/A280: 1.8–2.1).

Libraries were constructed by mRNA capture, fragmentation, strand-specific cDNA synthesis, adapter ligation, and dual-size selection (Hieff NGS® DNA Selection Beads, Yeasen). Amplification used high-fidelity PCR (12 cycles), followed by Bioanalyzer validation (400–600 bp) and qPCR quantification (KAPA Library Quantification Kit, Roche). Sequencing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (S4 Reagent Kit, 300 cycles; 2 × 150 bp).

LC-MS based metabolome profiling

Serum from the same cohort of 11 psoriasis vulgaris patients and 11 matched healthy controls was analyzed. For each sample, 30 µL serum per channel was deproteinized with methanol, centrifuged, and vacuum-dried. Amine/phenolic and carboxyl submetabolomes were labeled with dansyl and DmPA kits, respectively, spiked with ¹³C-labeled internal standards, and analyzed by HP-CIL LC-MS (Agilent 1290 UHPLC–6546 Q-TOF) using an Eclipse Plus C18 column (150 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm) with a binary gradient of 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and acetonitrile (B): 0 min 25% B, 10–15 min 99% B, 15.1–18 min 25% B; flow 400 µL/min. MS parameters: m/z 220–1000, 10 ppm mass accuracy, 9 s retention time tolerance. QC samples were inserted every 15 injections.

Data analysis

Transcriptomics data analysis

Data mining was performed based on gene expression levels, including PCA analysis, correlation analysis, clustering of differentially expressed genes, GO functional enrichment analysis, and Pathway significance enrichment analysis. These analyses were conducted using custom scripts (Python/R/Shell, etc.).

Metabolomics data analysis

Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) analysis yielded 44 datasets (2 channels, 22 per channel: 11 samples and 11 quality control [QC] samples). Data were processed using IsoMS Pro 1.4.0. Normalization was performed based on the total useful signal ratio. Mass accuracy was assessed using background peaks (m/z 251.0849 for amine/phenol channel; m/z 242.0175 for carboxyl channel), and retention time consistency was evaluated using 11 calibration standards. A three-tiered strategy identified 4,502 of 4,885 metabolite features (92.16%); 1,070 high-confidence metabolites (Tiers 1–2) were retained.

Differential metabolites were defined by combined univariate and multivariate criteria: fold change > 1.2 or < 0.83 with FDR-corrected q < 0.25, and OPLS-DA variable importance in projection > 1.0. The relaxed q threshold reflects the exploratory pilot design (n = 11 per group). OPLS-DA was performed using SIMCA-P (version 14.1) with 7-fold cross-validation and 200 permutations.

Data were visualized by PCA, PLS-DA, OPLS-DA, volcano plots, violin plots, box plots, and Z-score heatmaps. Pathway analysis was performed using MetaboAnalyst with KEGG Homo sapiens reference.

Joint analysis of metabolomics and transcriptomics

Integrated analysis of metabolomic and transcriptomic data was conducted through the following workflow. Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) was first performed separately on both datasets to reduce dimensionality. This analysis assigned genes and metabolites into distinct co-expression modules. Subsequently, inter-modular associations between gene-based and metabolite-based modules were examined. Following module construction, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was carried out using Fisher’s exact test. The resulting p-values were adjusted for multiple testing with the Bonferroni correction. To visualize the relationships among modules and pathways, a clustered correlation heatmap was generated.

Results

Clinical characteristics of the recruited patients

This study enrolled a total of 11 patients and 11 healthy controls. Background characteristics were uniform between the groups, as follows: sex, age, age at disease onset, disease duration, BMI, and PASI and DLQI scores. Analysis revealed no significant differences in these baseline parameters, supporting the internal validity of the study. Additionally, whole blood samples were collected from all participants. The psoriasis vulgaris cohort (9 males, 2 females) had a mean age of 43.0 ± 13.0 years, a mean disease duration of 12.4 years, a mean BMI of 25.94 ± 4.58 kg/m², a mean PASI score of 11.59 ± 9.31, and a mean DLQI score of 6.36 ± 4.61. Healthy controls (n = 11) were matched by sex, age, and BMI. No significant differences were found between groups in sex (male: 9 vs. 9, p = 0.999), age (39.4 ± 14.1 vs. 43.0 ± 13.0 years, p = 0.798), or BMI (27.12 ± 2.68 vs. 25.94 ± 4.58 kg/m², p = 0.882).

Identification of different expressed genes and pathway enrichment analysis in plaque psoriasis

Our transcriptomic analysis first identified key molecular changes and then elucidated their functional implications in psoriasis. Differential expression analysis revealed 5,186 consistently dysregulated genes under stringent criteria (|logFC| > 2, adjusted p-value < 0.05), with a striking predominance of down-regulation (only 39 up-regulated; Fig. 1A, B). The 16 most significant genes, including up-regulated NLRP1 and TAF6 and down-regulated RPP21 and GTF3C4, are visualized in a box plot (Fig. 1D). Functional enrichment analysis of these genes further uncovered their significant involvement in 108 KEGG pathways (FDR < 0.05). Among these, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and the mTOR signaling pathway were notably enriched (Fig. 1C), alongside other relevant pathways such as nucleocytoplasmic transport and ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling.

Identification and visualization of altered metabolites and KEGG pathways

Metabolomic profiling of patient sera pinpointed key metabolite alterations and associated pathway dysregulations in psoriasis. We implemented a sequential analytical workflow comprising initial unsupervised PCA, which effectively segregated patient and control cohorts (Supplementary Fig. 8), and subsequent supervised modeling (PLS-DA and OPLS-DA) for refined characterization. This integrated strategy yielded 208 differentially abundant metabolites based on the thresholds of VIP > 1.0, |log₂FC| >0.263, and p-value < 0.05, of which 60 were up-regulated and 148 down-regulated (Supplementary Table S1). The variable importance in projection (VIP) metric, essential for identifying metabolites with high discriminatory power, is illustrated for the top-ranking feature in (Fig. 2A). Among the 16 most significantly altered metabolites visualized in the volcano (B) and violin (C) plots, Propanoic acid was notably up-regulated, whereas L-Allysine was prominently down-regulated, together highlighting key dysregulated pathways in psoriatic metabolism. Finally, KEGG-based pathway enrichment analysis, summarized in the bubble plot (D), identified multiple significantly dysregulated metabolic pathways, most notably propanoate metabolism and pyruvate metabolism. WGCNA-based integration of transcriptome and metabolome datasets

Integrated WGCNA of transcriptomic and metabolomic data from 11 psoriasis vulgaris patients and 3 controls identified the MEcyan module as the most significant, demonstrating the strongest correlation with the key metabolites propanoic acid and L-allysine. Given the modest sample size, we applied stringent construction criteria (Pearson correlation > 0.8, p < 0.05) to enhance module stability. The soft-thresholding power β was selected based on scale-free topology fitting index (R² > 0.85). Module stability was further assessed through bootstrap resampling (n = 100 iterations), and only modules with stability scores > 0.80 were retained for downstream analysis. Eight gene modules were identified (Fig. 3A). Correlation analysis prioritized all 16 significant metabolites, with the top eight associations visualized in a heatmap (Fig. 3B) and the remainder in Supplementary Fig. 1. The high module membership and gene significance of MEcyan were validated via scatter plots (Fig. 3C, D). Subsequent stringent screening (GS > 0.91, MM > 0.97) identified 230 and 269 high-correlation genes for propanoic acid and L-allysine, respectively. We constructed protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks for these gene sets utilizing the STRING database and subsequently refined them using Cytoscape to identify core hub genes. The resulting PPI networks, presented in Supplementary Figs. 2 and 3, revealed key constituents including MTOR, TP53, and AKT3, thereby delineating a robust gene-metabolite interaction network pivotal to psoriasis metabolism.

Integrated KEGG pathway analysis of transcriptomic and metabolomic data

Biological pathway mapping integrated 208 differentially abundant metabolites with 5,186 differentially expressed genes via shared KEGG pathways, revealing six prominently dysregulated pathways: arachidonic acid metabolism, central carbon metabolism in cancer, cysteine and methionine metabolism, lysine degradation, propanoate metabolism, and tryptophan metabolism (Fig. 4A). Further analysis established key functional associations: L-allysine, a metabolite strongly linked to the lysine degradation pathway, while corresponding gene expression patterns in this pathway are displayed in Fig. 4B, showed significant abundance changes (Fig. 4C). Similarly, propanoic acid was identified as a central metabolite in propanoate metabolism (Fig. 4E), with its associated gene expression profiles shown in Fig. 4D. For the central carbon metabolism in cancer pathway, key genes MTOR, TP53 and AKT3 demonstrated strong functional associations (Fig. 4F), while metabolite alterations in this pathway are presented in (Fig. 4G).

Discussion

Psoriasis is a common inflammatory skin disease involving complex interactions among genetic, immunological, and metabolic factors. While previous studies have made significant advances in understanding genetic susceptibility and immune activation, the role of metabolic reprogramming and its crosstalk with transcriptional regulation remains incompletely characterized. This study employed an integrated transcriptomic and targeted metabolomic approach to systematically delineate the co-expression network between metabolites and genes in plaque psoriasis patient serum, providing novel insights into the molecular basis of the disease.

Our analysis identified 5,186 differentially expressed genes, with only 39 upregulated and 5,147 downregulated, indicating broad suppression of transcriptional activity in psoriatic lesions. Pathway enrichment analysis confirmed involvement of key signaling pathways including the mTOR signaling pathway, known to play central roles in psoriatic inflammation and immune dysregulation [23–26]. Notably, several core genes within these pathways (NLRP1, RPP21, FAM209B, and GTF3C4) were significantly downregulated, suggesting potential functional impairment subject to complex post-transcriptional or metabolic regulation linked to inflammasome activation. Previous studies have associated these genes with earlier disease onset in psoriasis, with NLRP1 playing a predominant role in human keratinocytes for sensing UVB exposure and subsequent secretion of IL-1β and IL-18 [9, 11, 12].

Metabolomic profiling revealed significant alterations in 208 metabolites, including several newly reported psoriasis-associated metabolites such as 4,6-Dihydroxyquinoline, Monoethylhexyl phthalic acid, and Spermidine. Subsequent weighted gene co-expression network analysis demonstrated strong correlations between key metabolites (propanoic acid, L-allysine) and genes encoding central carbon metabolism components (TP53, MTOR, AKT3), suggesting metabolic changes may directly or indirectly influence these critical signaling cascades (Fig. 4H). Specifically, within the propanoate metabolism pathway strongly associated with propanoic acid, core genes (including SETD7, SETD2, SETDB1) showed strong correlations with TP53, MTOR, and AKT3 (Supplementary Fig. 4) [27]. Similarly, in the lysine degradation pathway strongly linked to L-allysine, key genes (including ACSS2, LDHA) also demonstrated strong correlations with TP53, MTOR, and AKT3 (Supplementary Fig. 5) [28].

A major finding of our integrated pathway analysis was the first reported involvement of central carbon metabolism in cancer pathways in psoriasis pathogenesis. Central carbon metabolism in cancer is characterized by the Warburg effect, aberrant accumulation of intermediates including citrate, succinate, and methylsuccinate, and impaired oxidative phosphorylation [29]. Psoriasis shares substantial metabolic parallels with cancer at this level: keratinocytes similarly exhibit the Warburg effect with upregulated pyruvate kinase M2 and perturbed tricarboxylic acid cycle homeostasis. The serum-based dysregulation of lysine degradation and propanoate metabolism observed in this study suggests the presence of systemic central carbon metabolic reprogramming in psoriasis [30, 31]. Importantly, the expression levels of the core genes in this pathway were significantly associated with psoriasis severity, as assessed by PASI score. Simple linear regression analysis revealed significant positive associations for all five core genes (R² range: 0.3785–0.4325; all p < 0.05) (Supplementary Fig. 6). Key genes in this pathway (MTOR, AKT3) showed strong correlations with the PI3K-Akt-mTOR signaling pathway, which has well-established relevance to psoriasis [32–34]. This suggests central carbon metabolism may underlie the coexistence of epidermal hyperproliferation and metabolic stress in psoriasis, as dysfunction in this pathway could impair substrate availability and energy production, thereby attenuating energy-demanding growth signaling through PI3K-Akt-mTOR and potentially exacerbating disease severity [24, 35]. It should be emphasized that despite a DEG overlap of merely 1.5%, KEGG pathway overlap reached 77.2%, indicating that our serum transcriptome reliably captures systemic psoriasis pathology [36]. In contrast, metabolomics showed no metabolite overlap and only three shared pathways (4.8%) compared with public whole-blood metabolomics data. This discrepancy is attributable to platform differences, sample preparation methods, and the inherently high dynamism of metabolites, highlighting reproducibility challenges and necessitating multicenter validation (Supplementary Fig. 7) [21].

However, this study has several limitations. First, the use of serum samples may not fully reflect the local microenvironment of skin lesions. As serum primarily reflects systemic rather than local cutaneous metabolism, these findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than as direct evidence of skin pathophysiology or mechanistic insight. Second, the constrained sample size reflects the practical limitation in assembling a large, clinically uniform cohort with specific psoriasis phenotypes, a common constraint in studies requiring stringent patient stratification. Therefore, this study is conceptualized as a pilot investigation, and its findings are to be interpreted as preliminary and exploratory, warranting cautious generalizability and further validation. Third, there is a lack of experimental validation for the identified metabolites and pathways. Also our findings are derived from a plaque psoriasis (psoriasis vulgaris) cohort; their applicability to other subtypes remains to be established. Finally, the research did not delineate the crosstalk between the lysine degradation and propanoate metabolism pathways and other known metabolic pathways in plaque psoriasis(such as lipid and amino acid metabolism). This omission limits the systematic understanding of potential pathway interactions.

Conclusions

In summary, this exploratory pilot study identified preliminary correlations between metabolic pathways and gene expression in psoriasis. The observed concurrent alterations in TP53, MTOR, and AKT3 alongside lysine degradation and propanoate metabolism pathways generate testable hypotheses but should not be interpreted as establishing biological mechanisms. These findings require independent replication and functional validation prior to any consideration of clinical relevance.

MMP11 predicts colorectal cancer outcomes and its inhibitor RXP03 exerts anti-tumor effects via apoptosis activation
Authors: Cheng Wang, Peiyi Fu, Jianping Sheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeMMP11 exhibits significant overexpression in various cancers; however, its role in colorectal cancer (CRC) remains poorly understood. This study aimed to investigate the relationship between MMP11 and the prognosis of colorectal cancer and to evaluate the therapeutic potential of an MMP11 inhibitor.MethodsOur study employed an integrative bioinformatics approach utilizing TIMER, GEPIA, UALCAN, and HPA datasets to comprehensively assess MMP11 expression patterns and their correlation with clinicopathological characteristics and tumor-infiltrating immune cells (TIICs) in colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) and rectal adenocarcinoma (READ). Overall survival (OS) data for CRC were extracted from the The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). To investigate the therapeutic potential of MMP11 inhibition, we treated HCT116 and SW480 CRC cell lines with the MMP11 inhibitor RXP03. Colony formation and transwell assays were used to evaluated the proliferation and invasion abilities of CRC cells, while apoptosis was measured via TUNEL staining and flow cytometry. Additionally, xenograft tumor models were used to assess the tumor-suppressive effects of MMP11 inhibition.ResultsMMP11 expression was significantly upregulated in COAD and READ specimens compared to normal controls, and was correlated with age, tumor stage, histological subtype and nodal metastasis status. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis indicated that high MMP11 expression was an independent prognostic factor for poorer overall survival in CRC. TIMER analysis revealed that MMP11 transcription was strongly positively correlated with several TIICs, including CD4 + T cells, macrophages, and dendritic cells. Furthermore, MMP11 expression exhibited significant positive correlations with the infiltration levels of B7-H3 and TIM3 in CRC. In vitro experiments revealed that RXP03 significantly inhibited the proliferation and invasion of CRC cells and induced apoptosis. Xenograft experiments further demonstrated that RXP03 markedly suppressed the growth of colorectal tumors in vivo.ConclusionMMP11 expression is associated with survival outcomes and levels of TIICs in CRC patients, suggesting its potential as a prognostic biomarker for CRC. Moreover, therapeutic intervention with the MMP11 inhibitor RXP03 demonstrates anti-tumor efficacy against CRC in both in vitro and in vivo models.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-025-04322-y.Keywords: MMP11, COAD, READ, RXP03, Apoptosis

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) occupies a formidable position as the second most deadly form of cancer worldwide, accounting for roughly 10% of all cancer cases globally [1]. It can be classified as either colon adenocarcinoma (COAD) or rectum adenocarcinoma (READ), depending on their primary anatomical site [2]. Despite remarkable advancements in preventive strategies and therapeutic modalities such as surgical resection, radiotherapy, or chemotherapy, the median survival of individuals diagnosed with advanced CRC rarely surpasses two years [3]. The incidence of colorectal malignancies increases with advancing age. Colonoscopy screening represents a valuable tool in the early detection of these neoplasms. Nonetheless, patient adherence to colonoscopy screening remains suboptimal [4]. Numerous innovative small molecules have exhibited potential in managing CRC [5]. Nevertheless, their effectiveness predominantly depends on the disease stage and therapeutic response, highlighting the necessity to investigate potent therapeutic targets and predictive biomarkers further.

Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), commonly known as matrixins, are zinc-dependent proteins primarily involved in extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation [6]. Since the discovery of the first collagen-degrading MMP in the tail of a tadpole in 1962, studies have Suggested that the human genome encodes a total of 23 members of this MMP family, based on genome sequence data [7]. These enzymes are implicated in the pathological mechanisms of various inflammatory disorders and cancers. The degradation and disruption of the extracellular matrix and basement membrane are crucial steps in the metastatic progression of tumors.

Under normal physiological circumstances, MMPs interact with tissue inhibitors of metalloproteinases (TIMPs) to regulate the ECM remodeling and and maintain tissue homeostasis. Dysregulation of MMPs can disrupt the tissue barrier, promoting tumor invasion. This process involves ECM degradation, allowing tumor to breach the basement membrane and infiltrate surrounding tissues [8]. Furthermore, MMPs dysregulation can indirectly promote tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis by releasing sequestered growth factors or generating bioactive fragments [9, 10]. Numerous studies in oncology have established a correlation between MMP expression and disease prognosis. In general, overexpression of MMPs is associated with poor clinical outcomes in cancer. For example, a study of 337 colorectal cancer patients and 47 controls who underwent surgery at Helsinki University Hospital in Finland found that elevated serum levels of MMP8 and TIMP1 were associated with CRC, further supporting the link between MMPs and poor prognosis in these patients [11]. Regarding MMP9, clinical evidence indicates that elevated levels of this enzyme are an independent risk factor for both breast cancer and colorectal cancer [12, 13]. Similar associations have been observed for MMP2 and epithelial ovarian cancer [14], MMP13 and soft tissue sarcoma [15], MMP16 and serous ovarian cancer [16], MMP19 and gastric cancer [17].

Most of MMPs mentioned above are expressed and secreted in an inactive proenzyme form and subsequently activated extracellularly. In contrast, MMP11 (also called stromelysin-3) is secreted directly in its active form but lacks the ability to degrade major ECM components. It is highly expressed in the stromal compartment of various tumors, suggesting a unique role in cancer biology [18]. Accumulating evidence from mouse tumor models and human cancers indicates that MMP11 plays a critical role in tumorigenesis. Thus, MMP11 may serve as a valuable biomarker for disease management, including tumor early screening and prognosis, and a potential target for cancer immunotherapy [19].

Apoptosis a form of programmed cell death, ensures the controlled removal of damaged or unnecessary cells, maintaining tissue homeostasis and genomic integrity. Dysregulated apoptosis is a hallmark of cancer, driving uncontrolled proliferation, tumor progression, and therapy resistance. Consequently, targeting apoptotic pathways has become a key strategy in cancer treatment to restore the balance between cell survival and death [20]. Notably, MMP11 promotes tumor progression by inhibiting apoptosis in some cancers, enhancing cell survival [21]. In osteosarcoma, MMP11 inhibition induces apoptosis and suppresses proliferation, migration, and invasion [22]. Similar effects have been confirmed in hepatocarcinoma [23] and breast cancer [24], highlighting MMP11`s conserved pro-tumorigenic role across cancer types.

The association between MMP11 and CRC has been extensively investigated since its initial discovery. Johnson et al. first identified significantly elevated MMP11 RNA levels in primary colon carcinoma using PCR-based analysis in 1996 [25]. This pioneering finding was subsequently corroborated and expanded by multiple research groups through comprehensive clinical studies involving larger CRC patient cohorts [26–29]. Their collective work has consistently demonstrated that MMP11 overexpression in CRC tissues serves as an independent prognostic biomarker for disease progression and patient outcomes. Building upon these findings, research efforts have expanded to investigate the association between MMP11 genetic variants and CRC Susceptibility, as well as the precise molecular mechanisms underlying its oncogenic role. In 2022, Huang et al. conducted a comprehensive analysis of MMP11 polymorphisms in relation to CRC risk and clinicopathological features. Their work identified two potentially functional single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) (rs738792 and rs131451) that may serve as predictive biomarkers for evaluating CRC progression and disease severity [30]. Martin-Morales et al. used whole exome sequencing and cell experiments found that the P78S mutation of MMP11 increased the invasion and proliferation of HCT116 cells and could increase the tumorigenicity of colorectal cancer cells [31]. Pan et al. demonstrated that MMP11 can promote the migration and invasion of colorectal cancer cells by increasing the expression of Slug protein [32]. Despite the well-established association between MMP11 and CRC, significant gaps remain in current research. Notably, there is a lack of systematic prognostic studies examining MMP11’s differential roles in the COAD and READ. Additionally, the therapeutic potential of MMP11 inhibitors for CRC treatment remains largely unexplored, with limited experimental evidence available.

The aim of this study was to investigate the role of MMP11 in both COAD and READ using a comprehensive bioinformatics approach, combined with in vitro and in vivo experiments to explore its relationship with tumor cell apoptosis and progression. Our results showed that MMP11 expression was significantly upregulated in CRC tissues compared to adjacent non-tumor tissues. Furthermore, elevated MMP11 levels were associated with advanced tumor staging, lymph node metastasis, tumor-infiltrating immune cells (TIICs), and poorer patient prognosis. Using the MMP11 inhibitor RXP03, we demonstrated that MMP11 inhibition significantly reduced the proliferation and invasion of HCT116 and SW480 cells, promoted apoptosis, and suppressed tumor growth in nude mouse xenograft models. These findings underscore the critical role of MMP11 in CRC pathogenesis and support its potential as a therapeutic target for CRC treatment.

Methods

MMP11 Differential expression profiling analysis

The DiffExp module of the TIMER database (https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/) was used to analyze MMP11 expression differences between tumor and adjacent normal tissues across all TCGA tumor types [33]. To further validate these findings, we employed GEPIA (http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/index.html), which integrates RNA-seq data from TCGA tumor samples and GTEx normal tissue samples, enabling a more comprehensive comparison of MMP11 expression in tumor versus normal tissues [34]. Using the “Expression DIY” module in GEPIA, we assessed MMP11 expression in the TCGA-COAD and TCGA-READ datasets. For a deeper analysis of MMP11 in relation to clinicopathological features, we utilized UALCAN (http://ualcan.path.uab.edu/), a platform that provides detailed tumor subgroup stratification based on cancer stage, tumor grade, ethnicity, and other characteristics [35]. Specifically, we examined MMP11 expression patterns and their association with COAD and READ parameters, including sample type, age, cancer stage, histological subtype, and nodal metastasis status. Additionally, we retrieved MMP11 protein expression data from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA, https://www.proteinatlas.org/), which offers immunohistochemistry (IHC)-based profiles across normal tissues, cancer tissues, and cell lines [36]. The HPA provides manually scored protein expression levels based on staining intensity (negative, weak, moderate, or strong) and the proportion of stained cells (< 25%, 25–75%, or > 75%). From this resource, we obtained the tissue atlas and pathology atlas for MMP11.

MMP11 prognosis analysis

The colon cancer database within KM Plotter (http://kmplot.com) integrates clinical data with raw gene expression profiles from 2,137 tumor samples across 17 independent cohorts, making it a valuable resource for biomarker discovery and therapeutic target prioritization [37]. The PrognoScan database (https://dna00.bio.kyutech.ac.jp/PrognoScan/index.html) specializes in aggregating and analyzing cancer microarray datasets with clinical outcome information to assess prognostic biomarkers [38]. Here, we leveraged both KM Plotter and PrognoScan to access the prognostic value of MMP11 in CRC. The prognostic cutoff was rigorously determined through survival-based threshold optimization. We performed a systematic scan of all potential cutpoints within the interquartile range, selecting the optimal threshold that maximized separation of Kaplan-Meier curves, as determined by minimal log-rank test p-value.

Tumor immune estimation analysis

We utilized the correlation module of the TIMER database, which provides robust quantification of immune cell infiltration levels across various cancer types by integrating multiple immune deconvolution methods [33]. Therefore, we analyzed the relationship between MMP11 expression and several subsets of TIICs by Correlation module.

Cell culture

HCT116 and SW480 human CRC cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) and cultured in DMEM media Supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 100 U/mL penicillin, 100 µg/mL streptomycin. Cells were Maintained at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. All cell lines were authenticated using short tandem repeat (STR) profiling, and only cells between passages 10–15 were used for experiments. Cells were collected during the logarithmic growth phase, and genomic DNA was extracted using the TIANamp genomic DNA kit (TIANGEN, DP304). PCR amplification was performed using the cell line authentication STR profiling kit (beyotime, D8505S), followed by capillary electrophoresis and data analysis.

CCK8

Cell suspension (5 × 103/well) was seeded in 96-well plates and incubated for 24 h under standard culture conditions. RXP03 stock solutions (10 mM in DMSO) were serially diluted in complete medium to obtain working concentrations of 0, 2.5, 5, 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50 µM. Each concentration was added to triplicate wells of cultured cells (100 µL/well). The plates were then incubated for 24 h at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂. Following treatment, the medium was replaced with a 1:1 mixture of fresh DMEM, after which 100 µL CCK-8 solution (90 µL serum-free DMEM + 10 µL CCK-8 reagent) was added to each well. The plates were further incubated for 2 h at 37 °C in darkness. Cell viability was determined by measuring absorbance at 450 nm, with values representing each treatment group’s response.

Colony formation assay

Cells were seeded at a density of ~ 2000 cells/well in 6-well plates and cultured under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂) for 24 h to allow adhesion. After incubation, the medium was replaced with fresh complete medium containing 10 µM RXP03, while control cells were treated with an equivalent volume of DMSO (≤ 0.1% final concentration). Plates were incubated (with medium refreshed every 3 days, including RXP03) until visible colonies formed. Cells were gently washed twice with PBS to remove debris, 4% formaldehyde fixation for 15 min and Subsequent 0.5% crystal violet staining for 10–30 min at room temperature. Excess dye was removed by washing with PBS and photographed to calculate the number of clones using Image J.

Transwell assay

HCT116 and SW480 cells invasion was assessed via Transwell™ assay. Cells were treated with 10 µM RXP03 for 24 h, with 0.1% DMSO-treated cells serving as vehicle controls. After treatment, cells were harvested and resuspended in serum-free medium at a density of 5 × 10⁴ cells/mL. Cell suspensions (200 µL) were added to the upper chambers, while the lower chambers contained complete medium with 10% FBS as a chemoattractant. Following 24 h of incubation, non-invading cells on the upper membrane surface were wiped clean with a Q-tip, and invaded cells on the lower surface were formalin-fixed and crystal violet stained (0.5%). Invaded cell counts were performed across five random microscopic fields.

TUNEL assay

Cell death morphology was examined using a TUNEL apoptosis assay Kit (beyotime, C1089) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Cells were seeded in 24-well plates at a density of 2 × 10⁶ cells/mL (1 mL/well) and treated with 10 µM RXP03 for 24 h. The positive control was treated with DNase I (beyotime, C1082) to induce DNA breaks, while the negative control consisted of cells cultured in medium containing 0.1% DMSO. Following treatment, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min and permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 in PBS for 5 min at room temperature. After three PBS washes, cells were incubated with 50 µL TuneL reaction mixture for 60 min at 37 °C in the dark. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (1:100 dilution) for 5 min, and slides were mounted using an anti-fluorescence quenching mounting solution (Beyotime, P0126). Apoptotic cells were identified by fluorescence microscopy through detection of TUNEL-positive signals colocalized with DAPI-stained nuclei.

Flow cytometry

Cells were treated with 10 µM RXP03 in a 6-well plate (5 × 105/mL, 2 mL/well) and incubated for 24 h. In parallel, 0.1 µM paclitaxel was used as the positive control, while cells cultured in 0.1% DMSO medium alone served as the negative control. The cell Suspension was centrifuged and re-suspended in binding buffer at 5× 105 cells/mL. The Annexin V-FITC Apoptosis Detection Kit (Solarbio, CA1020) was used to evaluate the percentage of apoptotic cells. Cells were co-incubated with 5 µL Annexin V-FITC for 15 min at room temperature in darkness, followed by a second incubation with 5 µL PI for an additional 5 minutes. A FACS C6 instrument was employed to assess cell population dynamics via fluorescence-activated cytometry. Interpretation of data was facilitated by FlowJo 7.6 software.

Xenograft mouse models

Male nude mice (BALB/c, 4–6 weeks old) were obtained from the SiPeiFu Biotechnology (Beijing, China) and kept under standard environmental conditions. After ear tagging, the mice were randomly divided into control and treatment groups, with at least 3 mice per group. The HCT116 xenografts mouse model was established by Subcutaneous injection of 200 µL of a Suspension containing 1× 107 HCT116 cells, followed by daily injected with RXP03 (5 mg/kg) for 30 days. The control group receive intraperitoneal injections of PBS (100 µL) in the same manner. Mice were humanely sacrificed using inhalational CO2, and subcutaneous tumors were excised for measurement. Tumor volumes were assessed at intervals of five days using the equation: V = (width2×length)/2.

Immunohistochemistry

Ki67 expression in tumor xenografts was detected by immunohistochemistry. Briefly, tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, processed through standard dehydration, and embedded in paraffin. Section (5 μm) were dewaxed in xylene, rehydrated through graded ethanol (100%−70%), and Subjected to antigen retrieval. After quenching endogenous peroxidase activity with 3% H₂O₂ (10 min) and blocking with 5% goat serum (10 min), sections were incubated with primary anti-Ki67 antibody (PTM Bio, PTM-5032; 1:200 dilution) at 4 °C overnight. Following incubation with secondary antibody (1 h, RT), staining was developed using DAB, counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated, and mounted for microscopic analysis. The Ki67 index was calculated as the percentage of Ki67-positive cells relative to the total cell count. For each experimental group (control or RXP03-treated), a minimum of five independent microscopic fields were analyzed to determine Ki67 positivity.

Statistical analysis

Kaplan-Meier survival curves were analyzed using hazard ratios (HR) and Cox P-values from log-rank tests. Gene expression correlations were assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation, with statistical significance determined by the corresponding P-values. In the TCGA database, an unpaired comparison of MMP11 expression between colorectal cancer tissues and adjacent normal tissues was performed using Welch’s t-test. For the 51 paired COADREAD samples in the TCGA database, a paired sample t-test was used to analyze MMP11 expression. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 8.3.0. All experiments included triple technical replicates to ensure reliability, with data reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical significance between groups was determined using Welch’s t-test, with a P-value of less than 0.05 considered significant.

Results

The expression levels analysis of MMP11 in CRC patients

The mRNA expression profiles of MMP11 in human pan-cancer was first analyzed using the Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER) database. MMP11 expression was significantly elevated in 17 cancer types, including COAD and READ (p < 0.001), compared to corresponding normal tissues (Fig. 1A). Furthermore, analysis of the GEPIA and UALCAN databases consistently demonstrated significantly higher MMP11 mRNA expression in COAD and READ versus normal colon tissues (p < 0.001; Fig. 1B and C). As shown in Fig. 1D, MMP11 expression levels varied significantly across different pathological stages, with peak expression in stage III. Analysis of TCGA-COADREAD database tissue samples revealed significant MMP11 upregulation in unmatched tumor samples (Fig. 1E) and in 51 paired tumor versus adjacent normal samples (Fig. 1F). The HPA database further confirmed elevated MMP11 protein expression in CRC. Immunohistochemical analysis showed minimal MMP11 protein expression in normal tissues but moderately elevated in tumor tissues (Fig. 1G and H). These findings provide demonstrate increased MMP11 expression in CRC tissues, and suggests its potential role as a key regulator in disease progression.

MMP11 expression in CRC and its correlation with clinical variables

The association between MMP11 and different clinical parameters was further investigated using the UALCAN database. Detailed analysis based on age, cancer stage, histological subtype, and nodal metastasis status, revealed significantly elevated MMP11 transcript levels in CRC patients compared to healthy controls (Fig. 2). Regarding age-dependent expression, MMP11 showed significant elevation in COAD tissues across multiple age groups, with a particularly pronounced increase in READ patients aged above 40 (Fig. 2A and B). When classified by tumor stage, MMP11 expression was significantly upregulated in both COAD or READ patients across all clinical stages (Fig. 2C and D). In terms of histological subtypes, MMP11 overexpression in COAD patients was strongly associated with both adenocarcinoma and mucinous adenocarcinoma, while in READ patients, it was primarily linked to adenocarcinoma (Fig. 2E and F). Analysis of nodal metastasis status demonstrated markedly elevated MMP11 expression in N0, N1, and N2 stages of CRC. Notably, among COAD patients, N1-stage tumors exhibited the highest MMP11 expression. In READ patients, MMP11 levels positively correlated with lymph node metastasis severity (Fig. 2G and H). These findings robustly support the association between MMP11 overexpression and CRC progression and metastasis.

Prognostic significance of MMP11 expression in CRC patients

Given the established relationship between MMP11 expression levels and CRC progression, we investigate MMP`s prognostic significance. Analysis of the Kaplan-Meier plotter database revealed that CRC patients with higher MMP11 expression had significantly worse overall survival (OS) (Fig. 3A). The low-expression cohort showed a mean Survival of 145 months versus 91 months for the high-expression cohort, demonstrating a clinically significant survival difference (Fig. 3A). The PrognoScan database further confirmed this association, with elevated MMP11 expression correlating with poor OS in both the GSE17536 and GSE12945 cohorts (Fig. 3B and C). These findings demonstrate that MMP11 expression levels are strongly associated with CRC prognosis.

Survival curves analysis of MMP11 in CRC. A OS survival curves from Kaplan-Meier plotter analysis. B OS curves for the GSE17536 cohort by the PrognoScan database. C OS curves for the GSE12945 cohorts by the PrognoScan database. D MMP11 expression and subtypes correlation in CRC patients shown in a forest plot

In order to obtain a more comprehensive understanding of the prognostic significance and potential mechanisms associated with MMP11 expression in CRC, a statistical analysis was performed to evaluate the correlation between MMP11 mRNA expression and various clinical characteristics utilizing the Kaplan-Meier database. The elevated expression of MMP11 in both male and female CRC patients was found to be significantly associated with a diminished OS rate, irrespective of tumor location. Compared to the rectum, the prognosis of tumors located in the colon was significantly negatively correlated with MMP11 expression. In the CMS (consensus molecular subtypes) classification of CRC, microsatellite unstable (MSI) and canonical subtypes were associated with MMP11 expression. When considering diverse tumor stages, the augmented MMP11 expression was predominantly Linked with unfavorable OS for stage 2 CRC patients. For tumor stage T, N and M, significant correlation between elevated MMP11 expression and unfavorable OS was observed solely in CRC patients with T3 and N1 tumor stages. Moreover, excluding grade 1 results (deemed statistically insignificant due to limited sample size), the analysis revealed a significant negative correlation between MMP11 and both grade 2 and grade 3 tumors. Interestingly, in-depth analysis of three distinct mutation types demonstrated that and KRAS mutation status showed no correlation with MMP11 expression; Patients with BRAF mutations exhibited increased MMP11 expression and inferior OS; TP53 wild-type carriers displayed elevated MMP11 expression with negative OS association (Fig. 3D). These findings underscore the substantial relationship between MMP11 and the survival outcomes in CRC patients.

MMP11 expression levels correlate with the infiltration levels of immune cells and immune checkpoint in CRC

Multiple studies have established a correlation between the quantity and activation state of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and OS in CRC [39, 40]. To investigate these relationships, we analyzed MMP11 expression patterns, prognostic significance, and TIICs in COAD and READ using the TIMER bioinformatics platforms. Immune infiltration analysis revealed subtype-specific MMP11 associations. In COAD, MMP11 levels positively correlated with CD4 + T cells (r = 0.341, p = 2.14e-12), macrophages (r = 0.304, p = 4.63e-10) and dendritic cells (r = 0.157, p = 1.64e-03), but negative correlations with tumor purity (r = −0.145, p = 3.32e-03) and CD8 + T cells (r = −0.152, p = 2.09e-03) (Fig. 4A). Similarly, in READ, MMP11 expression positively correlated with CD4 + T cells (r = 0.382, p = 3.50e-06), macrophages (r = 0.425, p = 1.88e-07) and dendritic cells (r = 0.209, p = 1.34e-02), but negatively correlated with tumor purity (r = − 0.21, p = 1.29e-02) and CD8 + T cells (r = −0.188, p = 2.68e-02) in READ tissues (Fig. 4A).

Immunotherapy targeting immune checkpoints represents a paradigm shift in cancer treatment, achieving remarkable clinical outcomes through immune system activation [41]. Currently approved therapeutics for solid tumors include PD-1/PD-L1, CTLA-4, CD47, CD73, and LAG-3, with emerging targets such as TIM-3, TIGIT, VISTA, CD99, Nectin-4, NKG2A and B7-H3 [41–47]. We therefore utilized the TIMER database to investigate the relationship between MMP11 expression and the aforementioned immune checkpoint targets. The results demonstrated that MMP11 expression exhibited significant correlations with B7-H3 (CD276) in COAD (B-left, Cor = 0.362, p = 1.27e-15; B-right, Cor = 0.339, p = 2.17e-12) and READ (C-left, Cor = 0.407, p = 7.06e-08; C-right, Cor = 0.371, p = 6.91e-06), and TIM3 (HAVCR2) in COAD (D-left, Cor = 0.274, p = 2.58e-09; D-right, Cor = 0.261, p = 9.21e-08) and READ (E-left, Cor = 0.423, p = 1.95e-08; E-right, Cor = 0.377, p = 4.80e-06) (Fig. 4B-E). However, no significant associations were observed between MMP11 and other CRC immunotherapy targets, including PD-1/PD-L1, CTLA4, LAG3, or TIGIT (Supplement 1). To further elucidate the immunological role of MMP11 in CRC, we employed TIMER to analyze its correlation with diverse immune cell populations based on immune marker gene expression profiles. Our comprehensive immune infiltration analysis in CRC tissues encompassed B cells, T cells and macrophages subsets, monocytes, neutrophils, natural killer cells, dendritic cells, and mast cells. Strikingly, MMP11 expression demonstrated particularly strong associations with monocyte infiltration, tumor-associated macrophages, M2-polarized macrophages, dendritic cells, and mast cells in both COAD and READ. No significant correlations were observed with B cells, most T cell subsets, neutrophils, or natural killer cells (Supplementary 1).

Inhibition of CRC cells proliferation and invasion by RXP03

RXP03 was selected as the specific MMP11 inhibitor for antitumor efficacy evaluation through MMP11 blockade [48]. The viability of HCT116 and SW480 cells was assessed via CCK8 assay following treatment with varying concentrations of RXP03 (0 to 50 µM). The respective IC50 values of RXP03 against HCT116 and SW480 were determined to be 6.48 µM and 9.17 µM. Based on these results, a concentration of 10 µM was selected for subsequent experiments. The plaque colony formation assay was employed thoroughly evaluate the proliferative capability of HCT116 and SW480 following RXP03 treatment (Fig. 5A). Notably, the cellular abundance of CRC cells demonstrated a significant reduction after treatment with 10 µM RXP03 compared to the DMSO control group (Fig. 5B). Furthermore, cell invasive capacity was assessed using Transwell assays, which revealed that RXP03 significantly inhibited the aggressive potential of both HCT116 and SW480 compared to untreated controls (Fig. 5C and D). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that RXP03 treatment effectively suppresses both proliferation and invasion capacities in HCT116 and SW480 cells.

RXP03 inducing apoptosis in CRC

TUNEL staining was performed to detect apoptosis in HCT116 and SW480 cells following 24-hour treatment with DMSO (vehicle control), DNase I (positive control), or 10 µM RXP03. The results demonstrated a significant increase in CRC cell apoptosis following RXP03 treatment, with apoptosis-positive cells increasing by 4.4-fold in HCT116 (Fig. 6A and D) and 5.8-fold in SW480 (Fig. 6B and D). These findings revealed a notable elevation in the percentage of apoptotic cells upon RXP03 administration when compared to untreated cells. Apoptosis was further evaluated by Annexin V-FITC/PI assay in cells treated with DMSO (vehicle control), 0.1 µM paclitaxel (positive control), and 10 µM RXP03 (treatment group). Specifically, the apoptosis rate escalated from 6.08% to 19.60% in HCT116 and from 3.75% to 22.24% in SW480 (Fig. 6C and E). These data collectively demonstrate the pro-apoptotic effect of RXP03 on both HCT116 and SW480 cell lines.

To evaluate the antitumor effect of MMP11 inhibition by RXP03, we conducted a xenografts experiments in nude mice. After 30 days of treatment, tumor status assessment revealed visible shrinkage in the RXP03-treated group, with a significant difference compared to controls (Fig. 7A). Tumor volume was measured every 5 days in both groups. Results showed RXP03 administration significantly slowed tumor growth and reduced tumor size compared to the control group (Fig. 7B). Following euthanasia and tumor excision, the RXP03 group exhibited markedly lower tumor weight versus controls (Fig. 7C). Ki67 immunohistochemical analysis revealed that RXP03 was associated with reduced Ki67 expression. In CRC group, the Ki67-positive cell rate was 47.50%, which decreased to 23.42% after RXP03 treatment, indicating a significant suppression of tumor proliferation by RXP03 (Fig. 7D and E). These findings support the hypothesis that RXP03 could exert anti-tumor effects by promoting apoptosis and inhibiting the growth of CRC tumors in vivo, warranting further investigation into its therapeutic potential. Discussion

Potential role of MMP11 as an independent prognostic marker

Among all malignancies, CRC ranks third in terms of incidence (9.6%) and second in terms of mortality (9.3%), posing a substantial burden on global healthcare systems and socioeconomic structures [1]. While early detection could prevent over 90% of CRC-related deaths, current screening programs face significant underutilization despite the availability of multiple screening modalities [49]. The identification of novel diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers offers considerable potential to improve screening compliance, facilitate earlier cancer detection, and ultimately reduce CRC mortality. The fundamental mechanism enabling malignant cell invasion involves proteolytic degradation of ECM structural components, particularly basement membranes and interstitial collagen networks. MMPs have been recognized as critical mediators of tumor metastasis through their enzymatically remodeling of the ECM to promote invasive progression [6, 8]. This pivotal role in tumor dissemination has established MMPs as attractive molecular targets for anti-metastatic therapies.

MMP11 demonstrates significant overexpression in malignant tissues compared to their normal counterparts, with its elevated expression consistently validated as an independent prognostic biomarker across multiple cancer types that correlates with advanced disease stage and poorer clinical outcomes [22–24, 50, 51]. Our comprehensive bioinformatics analyses of COAD and READ expression datasets confirmed MMP11`s significant elevation in CRC tissues versus normal controls (Fig. 1), aligning with previous studies that established MMP11’s oncogenic role in promoting CRC cell migration and invasion [25, 26, 32]. Our investigation revealed MMP11’s clinical and prognostic significance in CRC, showing high expression levels significantly associated with age, clinical stage, histological grade, and metastasis status in both COAD and READ patients (Fig. 2). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis demonstrated significantly worse OS for patients with high MMP11 expression in both cancer types (Fig. 3). While KRAS mutation status showed no association with MMP11 expression, TP53 wild-type and BRAF-mutated cases exhibited significantly elevated MMP11 levels and corresponding survival disadvantages (Fig. 3).

Immunoinfiltration analysis showed MMP11 expression positively correlated with CD4 + T cells, macrophages, and dendritic cells, while negatively correlating with tumor purity and CD8 + T cells, without significant associations with B cells or neutrophils in both COAD and READ (Fig. 4A). Among immune checkpoint markers in solid tumors, MMP11 showed specific associations only with B7-H3 (CD276) and TIM3 (HAVCR2), but not with well-known targets such as PD-1/PD-L1 (Fig. 4B-E). These findings position MMP11 as a potential independent prognostic biomarker in CRC, with distinct associations to tumor progression and immunoinfiltration patterns. While biologically plausible, these findings have limitations including biomarker generalizability constraints from ethnic variability and tumor heterogeneity, plus the need to better characterize temporal dynamics between MMP11 expression and disease progression [52, 53]. Further validation through multi-omics sequencing, immunohistochemistry, and larger independent cohorts studies remains essential [54, 55].

Potential therapeutic role of MMP11 inhibitors in CRC

Over the past three decades, synthetic MMP inhibitors have been extensively investigated for various cancer types, including hydroxamate-based inhibitors, non-hydroxamate-based inhibitors, antibodies based MMP inhibitors, natural inhibitors, small interfering RNA and micro-RNA based inhibitors [56]. Although promising preclinical results of broad-spectrum MMPs inhibitors were obtained, most failed to advance beyond clinical trials, largely due to the functional diversity of MMPs and suboptimal experimental design, underscoring the complexity of MMP functions in tumor biology [57]. Accumulating evidence demonstrates that therapeutic suppression of MMP11 expression, achieved either through specific neutralizing antibodies or microRNA mediated gene silencing, significantly inhibits tumor progression. MMP11-blocking antibody treatment substantially attenuated epithelial cell migration in inverted papilloma-derived primary cultures, as demonstrated by reduced transmigration through collagen-coated transwell membranes [58]. Zhang et al. further showed that targeted MMP11 suppression via exosomal miRNA-139 delivery markedly inhibited gastric cancer progression [59].

RXP03 is a synthetic phosphinic peptide MMP inhibitor that functions as a transition-state analogue that mimics peptidic substrates [60]. It exhibits potent antitumor activity through selective inhibition of MMP11, MMP8, and MMP13, with particularly high potency against MMP11 (Ki = 5 nM) [18, 48]. These mechanistic properties provide a structural basis for rational drug design of novel phosphinic-based MMP inhibitors [61]. To examine MMP11 inhibition effects in CRC, we used RXP03 to specifically target MMP11 activity. Both in vitro and in vivo experiments showed that RXP03 significantly suppressed proliferation and invasion of HCT116 and SW480 cell lines while reducing tumor growth (Figs. 5 and 6). TUNEL staining and flow cytometry analyses confirmed that RXP03 treatment markedly increased apoptosis in both cell lines (Figs. 6). Collectively, these preclinical findings suggest that MMP11 inhibition may induce programmed cell death in CRC and could represent a promising therapeutic strategy worthy of further investigation.

Given the established role of MMPs in mediating therapy resistance through tumor microenvironment modulation and crosstalk with cellular signaling pathways, targeted MMPs inhibition may represent a viable strategy to overcome resistance to conventional therapies, including chemotherapy and radiation [62, 63]. Notably, ECM homeostasis disturbances can trigger compensatory mechanisms, as demonstrated by the inverse regulation between MMP9 downregulation concomitant and MMP2 upregulation observed in dentin caries [64]. This observation raises important questions about potential compensatory increases in other MMP family members following RXP03-induced MMP11 suppression, a possibility that merits systematic evaluation in future studies. The ClinicalTrials.gov database lists four clinical trials investigating MMP inhibitors for cancer treatment. Most of these studies were terminated due to insufficient efficacy or safety concerns, and none have publicly disclosed detailed results. These outcomes further highlight the significant challenges remaining in the clinical translation of MMP inhibitors. Notably, the broad-spectrum inhibition of MMP inhibitors lacks selectivity, potentially suppressing protective MMPs and leading to unintended side effects. Furthermore, the spatiotemporal functions of MMPs at different stages of tumor growth must be considered to optimize therapeutic outcomes with MMP inhibitor treatment [48]. Currently, numerous research institutions are developing next-generation synthetic MMP inhibitors, with particular emphasis on optimizing molecular design, enhancing target specificity, improving delivery systems for chemical and small-molecule gelatinase inhibitors, and exploring their combinatorial potential with immunotherapy and targeted therapies. Abdou et al. systematically optimized the MMP11-targeted prodrug RXP03, demonstrating that its glycosyl ester derivative significantly enhanced membrane permeability, thereby offering a promising strategy to improve drug delivery across biological barriers [65]. Akram et al. developed a nanoparticle-based formulation incorporating an MMP-responsive polymer and polyethylene glycol-peptide-phosphatidylethanolamine conjugate, which enables targeted delivery to macrophages for the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis [66]. Additionally, although MMP inhibitors have yet to achieve substantial progress in clinical applications, their long-established clinical significance in cancer treatment and diagnosis has spurred growing interest in novel strategies that leverage endogenous MMPs for drug delivery within the tumor microenvironment [67]. These collective efforts may substantially advance anticancer drug development.

Limitations

Several important limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective and cross-sectional nature of the bioinformatics analysis, while useful for hypothesis generation, precludes causal inference. The integration of multi-source public data introduced heterogeneity in patient cohorts, treatments, and follow-up, potentially confounding the observed associations. These findings require validation in prospective clinical cohorts to confirm the predictive value of MMPs. Second, the molecular mechanisms through which MMP11 promotes tumor progression and metastasis, and how RXP03 counteracts its anti-apoptotic effects, remain incompletely understood. Rescue experiments, spatiotemporal analysis of MMP11 expression, and investigation into the impact of MMP11 on the CRC microenvironment based on cell lines, humanized mice or patient-derived xenografts will contribute to a deeper understanding of MMP11 function. Finally, comprehensive preclinical studies are essential to optimize RXP03 delivery strategies and characterize its pharmacokinetics, bioavailability, and toxicity to facilitate future clinical translation.

Conclusion

In summary, our study demonstrates the association between MMP11 expression and immune infiltration patterns and prognosis in COAD and READ. We further establish that RXP03-mediated MMP11 inhibition induces tumor cell apoptosis while suppressing CRC proliferation and progression. However, it is important to noted that this investigation relied primarily on public database analyses and lacked validation through independent patient cohorts. The precise molecular mechanisms of MMP11 and RXP03 intervention in CRC pathogenesis remain to be fully elucidated. These findings position MMP11 as a potential therapeutic target for CRC and provide a scientific foundation for further exploration of MMP11’s molecular mechanisms in CRC, and the development of MMP11-targeted therapeutic agents.

IFPHOR0035 Health Insurance and Maternal Healthcare Utilisation in Nigeria: A Scoping Review of the National Health Insurance Scheme
Authors: Oluwadamisi Tayo-Ladega
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNigeria continues to experience a high burden of maternal mortality despite ongoing health system reforms aimed at improving access to maternal healthcare. Health insurance is widely recognised as a critical mechanism for reducing financial barriers and advancing progress toward universal health coverage. The National Health Insurance Scheme (NHIS) was introduced to improve access to essential health services and reduce out-of-pocket expenditure; however, evidence on its impact on maternal healthcare remains fragmented and context-specific.MethodsThis study conducted a scoping review following the Arksey and O’Malley framework, enhanced by Levac et al., and guided by the Joanna Briggs Institute methodology. Relevant peer-reviewed and grey literature were systematically identified, screened, and analysed to map existing evidence on the influence of NHIS on maternal healthcare access, utilisation, financial risk protection, and quality of care in Nigeria.ResultsFindings indicate that NHIS has the potential to improve maternal healthcare utilisation by reducing financial barriers and enhancing service uptake. However, its effectiveness remains constrained by low awareness and enrolment among women of reproductive age, particularly in rural and low-income populations. Additional systemic challenges include administrative inefficiencies, delayed reimbursement of healthcare providers, weak health system infrastructure, inequitable coverage, and inconsistent availability of essential medicines and services.ConclusionsStrengthening policy implementation, improving community awareness, expanding equitable coverage, and increasing investment in primary healthcare systems are essential to optimise NHIS performance. Addressing these challenges is critical to enhancing maternal healthcare utilisation and accelerating Nigeria’s progress toward universal health coverage and improved maternal health outcomes.

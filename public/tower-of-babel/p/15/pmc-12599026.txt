Pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy followed by maintenance with or without olaparib as first-line treatment for advanced BRCAwt ovarian cancer with LOH-low: a international cost-effectiveness analysis
Authors: Kun Liu, Xiaofang Zhou, Youwen Zhu, Hong Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatients with advanced ovarian cancer (AOC) who are BRCA wild-type (BRCAwt) or exhibit low loss of heterozygosity (LOH-low) typically face a poor prognosis and have limited therapeutic options. Pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy followed by maintenance with or without olaparib (PCO or PC) brings new hope to these patients. However, the substantial financial burden associated with these regimens necessitates a thorough cost-effectiveness evaluation.MethodsThe 20-year horizon Markov model with three health states was developed using clinical data from the KEYLYNK-001 trial and associated treatment costs to estimate the lifetime costs and health benefits of PCO, PC, and chemotherapy. Primary outcomes included total costs, quality-adjusted life years (QALYs), and incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs). Cost-effectiveness was assessed using willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds of $150,000/QALY for the USA and $39,695/QALY for China.ResultsIn the USA, the total costs and QALYs were $934,191 and 4.74 for PC, and $521,690 and 4.02 for chemotherapy. The corresponding ICERs were $568,586/QALY in the general population and $399,919/QALY among those with programmed cell death ligand 1 combined positive scores (PD-L1 CPS) ≥ 10. In China, PC and chemotherapy incurred costs of $126,542 and $106,247, with QALYs of 4.27 and 3.68, respectively. The ICERs were $34,032/QALY overall and $18,462/QALY in the PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 population. In both countries, PCO yielded higher costs but lower effectiveness compared to PC.ConclusionsIn BRCAwt AOC patients with LOH-low, PC is a cost-effective first-line treatment compared to PCO in both the USA and China, particularly among PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13048-025-01827-8.Keywords: Advanced ovarian cancer, BRCA wild-type, Loss of heterozygosity low, Pembrolizumab, Olaparib, Cost-effectiveness

Introduction

Ovarian cancer ranks as the third most common and second most lethal gynecologic malignancy worldwide, accounting for an estimated 324,603 new cases and 206,956 deaths annually. China and the USA contribute 18.8% and 5.6% of global cases, respectively, ranking highest in Asia and the Americas [1]. Approximately 70% of ovarian cancer cases are diagnosed at an advanced or metastatic stage, with a 5-year survival rate of only 30% [2]. The advent of poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors has significantly altered the therapeutic landscape for advanced ovarian cancer (AOC), ushering in a new era of molecularly guided treatments. Genetic testing is now central to treatment decisions, particularly assessments of BRCA mutations and homologous recombination deficiency (HRD). Loss of heterozygosity (LOH) is a key marker used to assess HRD status. Between 15 and 30% of patients harbor BRCA mutations and are classified as HRD-positive, which is associated with improved clinical outcomes. However, the majority of patients are BRCA wild-type (BRCAwt) or HRD-negative (LOH-low), a group that often exhibits significant resistance to therapy and poorer survival outcomes [3, 4]. Therefore, identifying more effective and individualized treatment approaches for this subgroup remains a critical need.

The emergence of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) has generated considerable interest in their potential role in AOC. Pembrolizumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting programmed death-1 (PD-1), has shown promising results in this setting [5]. In the KEYLYNK-001/ENGOT-OV43/GOG-30 (NCT03740165) clinical trial, pembrolizumab combined with chemotherapy (PC) demonstrated a notable progression-free survival (PFS) benefit compared to chemotherapy alone in patients with BRCAwt AOC and LOH-low status (Overall population: median PFS 11.9 vs. 10.1 months; hazard ratio [HR] 0.69; 95% CI 0.53–0.90. programmed cell death ligand 1 combined positive score [PD-L1 CPS] ≥ 10 population: 15.9 vs. 10.2 months; HR 0.53; 95% CI 0.36–0.79). Similarly, the addition of olaparib (PCO) further improved PFS (Overall: 13.7 vs. 10.1 months; HR 0.66; 95% CI 0.51–0.85. PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 population: 16.4 vs. 10.2 months; HR 0.56; 95% CI 0.38–0.84). Notably, overall survival (OS) was significantly prolonged in the PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 population receiving PC versus chemotherapy (median OS not reached vs. 40.5 months; HR 0.61; 95% CI 0.38–0.98) [6]. These findings underscore the potential of novel immunotherapy-based regimens in improving outcomes for LOH-low BRCAwt AOC, as a highly invasive disease and to offer reference data for the wide application and guidelines in future clinical practice.

This study was therefore developed with the aim of assessing the cost-effectiveness of chemotherapy, PC, and PCO regimens in BRCAwt AOC patient with LOH-low under both the USA and Chinese healthcare systems, offering essential evidence to support policy decisions, price negotiations, and clinical guideline development.

Materials and methods

This economic evaluation synthesized clinical and cost data from the KEYLYNK-001 trial and applied the CHEERS 2022, widely accepted by regulatory bodies for health technology assessments [6, 7] (Supplementary Table S1).

Model overview

The Markov model for decision analysis was established to predict the expected lifetime clinical outcomes and costs of 449 patients with BRCAwt AOC with LOH-low (including 210 populations with PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10) under different treatment regimens. The model featured a 20-year time horizon, during which more than 99% of the patients died, using 6-week cycles to capture disease progression and mortality. The baseline characteristics of the virtual patients mirrored those reported in the KEYLYNK-001 trial. Initially, it started from the classic three-state model (progression-free survival [PFS], progressive disease [PD], and death) (Fig. 1), comparing chemotherapy (N = 143; paclitaxel [175 mg/m2] and carboplatin [area under curve 5 or 6] every 3 weeks up to 6 cycles), PC (N = 157; pembrolizumab, 200 mg every 3 weeks up to 32 cycles), and PCO (N = 149; olaparib, 300 mg twice daily up to 32 cycles) (Supplementary Table S2) [6] After the PD, three groups of patients were allowed to receive second-line treatment (Gemcitabine plus carboplatin with or without bevacizumab) until death [8, 9]. Patients who did not receive subsequent treatment received the best supportive care (BSC), and the terminal care before death needed to be accepted. The hypothetical population was calculated based on the therapeutic drugs as 61-year-old females and the assumed serum creatinine of 1 mg/dL [6, 10, 11]. In addition, the body weights (body surface area) of American and Chinese patients were 70 kg (1.84 m2) and 61 kg (1.64 m2), respectively [10, 11] (Table 1).

Model probabilities

To model PFS and OS in the KEYLYNK-001 trial, we first reconstructed individual patient data using GetData Graph Digitizer (v2.26) and R Studio (v4.2.2). These reconstructed survival curves were then fitted with multiple parametric survival distributions, including the exponential, Gompertz, log-logistic, log-normal, and Weibull models. Model selection was guided by both the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) and the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), complemented by visual inspection to ensure optimal fit for each survival curve (Supplementary Figure S1, Table S3) [11]. Once the best-fitting models were identified, the primary distribution parameters shape (λ) and scale (γ) were computed using MATLAB (vR2020a). These parameters were subsequently incorporated into the calculation of time-dependent transition probabilities using the formulas: exp(λ[t-u]γ-λtγ) and 1-exp(λ[t-u]γ-λtγ), where u denotes the Markov cycle length and t represents the time point at which survival status transitions are evaluated [12]. Key outcomes of interest included total costs, life-years (LYs), quality-adjusted LYs (QALYs), incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs), and incremental net health benefits (INHBs). These outcomes were assessed using two willingness-to-pay (WTP) thresholds: $150,000/QALY for the USA and $39,695/QALY for China. All cost and health outcomes were discounted at annual rates of 3% and 5% in the USA and China, respectively [10, 11] (Table 1).

Utilities and costs estimates

Utilities, representing patient preferences for health states, are essential for cost-effectiveness analysis and are measured on a scale from 0 (death) to 1 (perfect health). We derived utility weights from previously published health-related quality of life (HRQoL) studies in patients with AOC. Baseline utility values were set at 0.779 for PFS and 0.753 for PD [10, 11, 13]. These were further adjusted based on the frequency and severity of adverse events (AEs) reported across treatment groups in the clinical trial [10, 14, 15] (Table 1).

Drug cost estimates reflected payer perspectives, with USA prices sourced from Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services and Drugs.com [16, 17]. For China, prices were derived from the National Reimbursement Drug List and the Pharmaceutical Group Procurement System published by the State Council [18, 19]. Additional direct medical costs including laboratory and imaging services, patient management, genetic testing, best supportive care (BSC), and terminal care for AEs (e.g., anemia, neutropenia, and decreased neutrophil count) were estimated using bundled cost data from prior literature [10, 11] (Table 2).

Sensitivity and scenario analyses

To account for parameter uncertainty in the model, we performed both deterministic (one-way) and probabilistic sensitivity analyses involving 10,000 Monte Carlo simulations using parameter distributions based on either 95% confidence intervals or ± 20% variation from baseline values [10, 11]. Cost variables were modeled with gamma distributions, while probability estimates and utilities were modeled with beta distributions. In addition, we conducted scenario analyses to explore the influence of key external variables. Scenario 1 assessed the effect of price reductions in pembrolizumab and olaparib within the USA market. Scenario 2 evaluated the implications of regional economic disparities within China, which may cause substantial variability in WTP thresholds.

Results

Base-case results

In the USA, chemotherapy emerged as the most cost-effective option. For the overall population, the incremental costs of PC and PCO were $412,501 and $567,997, respectively. These strategies yielded incremental QALYs of 0.72 and 0.50 per patient, translating into ICERs of $568,586/QALY and $1,135,994/QALY, respectively. Among patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 population, the incremental costs of PC and PCO were $650,138 and $651,771, with QALY gains of 1.62 and 0.18, resulting in ICERs of $399,919/QALY and $3,620,950/QALY, respectively. In China, PC was the dominant strategy. In the overall population, the incremental costs for PC and PCO were $20,295 and $26,873, with corresponding QALY gains of 0.59 and 0.41, yielding ICERs of $34,032/QALY and $65,544/QALY. For the PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 population, the incremental costs were $24,220 (PC) and $29,860 (PCO), with QALY gains of 1.32 and 0.15, respectively. These values correspond to ICERs of $18,462/QALY and $199,067/QALY. In all populations, PCO was dominated by PC, offering lower cost-effectiveness in both countries (Table 3).

Sensitivity analyses

Tornado diagrams generated through one-way sensitivity analysis identified survival hazard ratios, utility values, and drug prices as the primary drivers of model outcomes. Despite parameter fluctuations within their defined ranges, all resulting ICERs in the U.S. exceeded the $150,000/QALY threshold, indicating a lack of cost-effectiveness for both PC and PCO in the overall population (Fig. 2, Supplementary Figure S2). Cost-effectiveness acceptability curves and scatterplots further illustrated the likelihood of each strategy being cost-effective across varying WTP thresholds. In the U.S., the probability that PC was cost-effective remained at 0% until WTP exceeded $550,000/QALY (overall population) and $382,000/QALY (PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 population). PCO remained non-cost-effective at all WTP levels tested. In contrast, the Chinese model showed more favorable probabilities. For the overall (and PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10) populations, the likelihoods of cost-effectiveness were: chemotherapy 38.72% (9.07%), PC 55.89% (90.57%), and PCO 5.39% (0.36%). The probability that PCO would be cost-effective in China only rose above 0% when WTP thresholds surpassed $100,000/QALY for the overall population and $200,000/QALY for the PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 population (Fig. 3 and Supplementary Figure S3-S4).

Scenario analyses

Scenario 1

In the USA, when the cost of pembrolizumab was lowered to $5.76 per mg, the ICER of the PC regimen compared to chemotherapy was $144,162 per QALY gained in the overall population, and $138,002/QALY in patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10. When the prices of pembrolizumab and olaparib were reduced to $6.91/mg and $0.12/mg, respectively, the ICER of the PCO regimen versus chemotherapy was $146,836/QALY for the overall population and $235,955/QALY in the PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 population. Further cost reduction to $5.76/mg for pembrolizumab and $0.05/mg for olaparib resulted in a more favorable ICER for the PCO regimen-$67,778/QALY in the overall cohort and $142,581/QALY in the PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 population.

Scenario 2

Given the pronounced regional economic disparities across China, we calculated region-specific WTP thresholds based on the 2024 per capita GDP for each area. The WTP thresholds for Beijing, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Fujian, Guangdong, Hubei, Hunan, Xinjiang, Guizhou, Guangxi, Heilongjiang, and Gansu were determined to be $96,072/QALY, $91,362/QALY, $67,707/QALY, $58,182/QALY, $57,306/QALY, $46,968/QALY, $308,391/QALY, $34,149/QALY, $33,303/QALY, $24,711/QALY, $24,012/QALY, $22,929/QALY, and $22,227/QALY, respectively [20]. Notably, under these localized WTP thresholds, the PC regimen emerged as the most economically favorable strategy in both economically advanced and moderately developed regions of China.

Discussion

The projected global expenditure for ovarian cancer treatment from 2022 to 2025 is estimated at $51.9 billion, with the USA and China accounting for 0.4% and 0.3% of this total, respectively [21]. This substantial financial burden underscores the critical importance of evaluating the cost-effectiveness of emerging therapies for AOC. As clinicians, patients, and policymakers increasingly prioritize treatments offering meaningful survival benefits while managing cost uncertainties, economic evaluation has become an essential component of clinical decision-making. Consequently, economic assessments of novel treatment strategies for AOC continue to be updated to reflect evolving therapeutic landscapes. Clinical trials such as SOLO-1 and PAOLA-1 have provided strong evidence supporting the survival benefit of maintenance therapy with olaparib with or without bevacizumab, particularly when chemotherapy options are inadequate [22, 23]. Based on the SOLO-1 findings, studies conducted from various payer perspectives, including those in the USA, Italy, Spain, and Singapore, have demonstrated that maintenance therapy with olaparib following chemotherapy is a cost-effective first-line strategy for patients with BRCA-mutated AOC [24–27]. Similarly, the PAOLA-1 trial informed evaluations in the USA and Spain, which showed that maintenance olaparib plus bevacizumab is also economically justified for BRCA-mutated and HRD-positive patients [28, 29].

Nonetheless, the cost-effectiveness of olaparib in patients without BRCA mutations and with low levels of loss of heterozygosity (LOH-low/HRD-negative) remains uncertain. Zhu et al. reported that although olaparib-based therapies may offer clinical benefits to these patients, the associated economic burden is substantial [10]. This highlights the urgent need to explore innovative treatment approaches that optimize the balance between efficacy and affordability. The KEYLYNK-001 trial has brought renewed optimism by demonstrating promising outcomes for ICI-based first-line regimens in AOC patients, including those with BRCAwt and LOH-low profiles. To assess the value of these new approaches, we constructed a decision-analytic model evaluating the cost and cost-effectiveness of three treatment strategies for patients with highly aggressive AOC and poor prognosis. Despite the encouraging efficacy of ICI-based regimens, their significantly higher costs present challenges for healthcare systems, raising the pressing question of whether these expenses are justified by commensurate health benefits. Our findings indicate that, in the USA, chemotherapy remains the most cost-effective option, whereas in China, the PC regimen offers the best economic value. Probabilistic sensitivity analyses further support these results, revealing that at traditional WTP thresholds, the probabilities of chemotherapy being cost-effective in the USA and China were 55.89% and 100%, respectively.

The KEYLYNK-001 trial demonstrated that incorporating ICIs into treatment regimens provides survival benefits for patients with AOC. However, our findings indicate that the cost-effectiveness of these treatments varies substantially across countries and regions. Specifically, PC was found to be economically favorable in China but not in the USA. These divergent outcomes should not be interpreted as a basis to abandon innovative ICI-based regimens in AOC due to differences in regional cost-effectiveness. Instead, they highlight the critical importance of conducting pharmacoeconomic evaluations before the approval or broad implementation of novel therapies. In the U.S. context, both PC and PCO were not deemed cost-effective under current pricing structures. Scenario analyses suggested that substantial price reductions of between 80 and 95% would be necessary for these regimens to become economically viable. Achieving such price adjustments in practice is challenging and would require complex, multi-level negotiations involving federal health authorities, regulatory agencies, and pharmaceutical manufacturers. One potential strategy would involve the development of a comprehensive national insurance framework to support access to innovative therapies and reduce patient financial burden. China, as a large and economically diverse developing country, faces unique challenges in applying uniform WTP thresholds. The use of national per capita GDP as a proxy for WTP does not accurately reflect economic realities across all regions. Accordingly, we conducted scenario analyses using region-specific WTP values. Results showed that while PC is not a cost-effective option in less affluent provinces, it is the preferred strategy in more developed areas. This suggests a need for targeted subsidy mechanisms. For example, pharmaceutical companies could introduce "buy-one-get-one" or discount schemes for innovative drugs, and national health insurance programs might increase reimbursement ratios for patients in underdeveloped regions. These findings underscore a broader, shared priority among healthcare systems: the integration of pharmacoeconomic assessments into the policy framework guiding innovative treatment deployment. The variable economic contexts across regions mean that cost-effectiveness data from one country or locality should not be directly extrapolated to others. Instead, tailored pricing and access strategies are essential to ensure the equitable and efficient adoption of new therapies globally.

Ovarian cancer remains a "silent killer" positioned among the most lethal gynecologic malignancies worldwide due to its subtle early symptoms and high mortality rate. Therapeutic options remain limited, in part due to the highly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, which hampers the dendritic cell function and antigen presentation critical for eliciting effective anti-tumor responses [30]. A nuanced understanding of the immune landscape, both at primary and metastatic tumor sites, is essential for designing more potent combination therapies involving ICIs. Inter-patient variability in ICI response is largely attributable to tumor heterogeneity. Therefore, deepening our understanding of ovarian cancer genomics and how these genetic features interface with the immune system could help identify reliable predictors of ICI efficacy. Ideally, immunogenomic scoring models could be developed to facilitate more precise patient selection for immunotherapy. This study further explored the role of PD-L1 CPS as a potential biomarker. The cost-effectiveness of PC in patients with PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10 was found to be comparable to, or better than, that in the overall AOC population. These findings suggest that PD-L1 CPS could serve as a useful indicator for selecting patients more likely to benefit from ICI-based treatment. However, the complexity of ovarian cancer extends beyond PD-L1 expression. Heterogeneity at the genomic, proteomic, glycoproteomic, and immunologic levels necessitates broader biomarker discovery efforts [31]. Integrating these biomarkers with genomic and immunologic profiling will provide a more comprehensive framework to inform treatment decisions and guide the development of optimized therapeutic sequences in future clinical trials.

This study is subject to some considerations that may limit its findings. For one, the utilized model relies on data derived from randomized clinical trials and published literature, which may introduce variability and potential bias. Additionally, survival outcomes were projected based on available data, which may not fully align with real-world results. Nevertheless, this research represents the first comprehensive evaluation of the cost-effectiveness of chemotherapy, PC, and PCO in treating BRCAwt, low LOH AOC, based on the most up-to-date clinical evidence. The findings contribute important supplementary insights to existing randomized trial outcomes, offering practical guidance for clinicians and patients when selecting treatment strategies. Moreover, the results provide valuable reference points for shaping international treatment guidelines. Finally, this study only focused on the economic evaluations of the USA and China, without conducting a broader and more detailed discussion, especially for low-income countries. For instance, in the Philippines, the cost of drugs is quite high, which restricts their use by the general public. The WTP (approximately $2,160/QALY) is also much lower than that of developed and developing countries. Public insurance that cannot be widely covered; The infrastructure is not sound. This also brings huge challenges to the promotion of the research results to low-income countries such as the Philippines. In the future, the economic outcomes of more countries need to be taken into account to achieve the global popularization of innovative solutions. However, the core patients included in the KEYLYNK-001 study were from the United States and Asia. Therefore, choosing the USA and China for cost–benefit analysis is of reference significance for other similar countries in the Americas and Asia.

Conclusion

From the perspective of healthcare payers within China's medical system, pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy is a cost-effective first-line treatment compared to chemotherapy alone for patients with BRCAwt, low LOH AOC, particularly for those with PD-L1 CPS ≥ 10. In contrast, PC does not meet cost-effectiveness thresholds in the USA. Furthermore, the addition of olaparib (PCO) fails to demonstrate economic value relative to either chemotherapy or PC in both countries. To optimize resource allocation and treatment access, policymakers should prioritize proactive pricing negotiations across healthcare systems. Tailored, region-specific strategies will be essential to achieve these goals and to maximize the clinical and economic impact of emerging treatments for AOC.

Correction: Microneedle-mediated delivery of Coptis chinensis-derived nanovesicles orchestrating antibacterial and macrophage reprogramming for comprehensive wound healing
Authors: Ke-Min Xu, Jia-Qi Lin, Xin-Xiang-Yi Zhuo, Ling Xiao, Yu-Jie Zhao, Chi Zhang, Shu-Ying Li, Yu-Lin Zhang, Fang Yang, Qi Zhao, Chao-Qing Li, Guo-Jun Zhang
Published: 2026

Correction: Journal of Nanobiotechnology (2026) 24:306

10.1186/s12951-026-04218-3

After the article was published, the author noticed that Figs. 5 and 6 were identical. Upon further examination, it was discovered that during the proofreading process, Fig. 6 was mistakenly pasted twice in the manuscript, resulting in the absence of Fig. 5. For completeness and transparency, the old incorrect and correct versions are displayed below.

Incorrect Fig. 5.

In vitro anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects of CDV@MN. (A) Flow cytometry analysis of the fluorescence intensity of intracellular CD86 and CD206 in RAW264.7 cells after different treatments; (B) Immunofluorescence analysis of the expression of CD206 and CD86 in RAW264.7 cells after different treatments (scale bar: 20 μm). DAPI (blue), CD206 (green), and CD86 (red) staining, with 3D surface images showing the expression of CD206 and CD86. I: Control, II: LPS, III: MN, IV: 5 µg/mL CDV@MN, V: 10 µg/mL CDV@MN, VI: 25 µg/mL CDV@MN; (C) Real-time qPCR measurement of the expression levels of IL-10, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α in RAW264.7 cells after different treatments, presented in a heatmap where the color intensity represents the relative gene expression levels; (D) Western blot analysis showing the expression of iNOS, CD86, CD206, AMPK, mTOR, and β-Actin proteins in different treatment groups. The relative expression levels of (E) iNOS, (F) CD86, (G) mTOR, (H) CD206, and (I) AMPK proteins in different treatment groups; (J) Representative fluorescent images of ROS in NIH-3T3 cells after 12 h of treatment with different groups (scale bar: 100 μm); (K) 2-NBDG fluorescence intensity in different treatment groups, reflecting intracellular glucose content. Data are reported as mean ± standard deviation (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, n ≥ 3)

Correct Fig. 5.

In vitro anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects of CDV@MN. (A) Flow cytometry analysis of the fluorescence intensity of intracellular CD86 and CD206 in RAW264.7 cells after different treatments; (B) Immunofluorescence analysis of the expression of CD206 and CD86 in RAW264.7 cells after different treatments (scale bar: 20 μm). DAPI (blue), CD206 (green), and CD86 (red) staining, with 3D surface images showing the expression of CD206 and CD86. I: Control, II: LPS, III: MN, IV: 5 µg/mL CDV@MN, V: 10 µg/mL CDV@MN, VI: 25 µg/mL CDV@MN; (C) Real-time qPCR measurement of the expression levels of IL-10, IL-6, IL-1β, and TNF-α in RAW264.7 cells after different treatments, presented in a heatmap where the color intensity represents the relative gene expression levels; (D) Western blot analysis showing the expression of iNOS, CD86, CD206, AMPK, mTOR, and β-Actin proteins in different treatment groups. The relative expression levels of (E) iNOS, (F) CD86, (G) mTOR, (H) CD206, and (I) AMPK proteins in different treatment groups; (J) Representative fluorescent images of ROS in NIH-3T3 cells after 12 h of treatment with different groups (scale bar: 100 μm); (K) 2-NBDG fluorescence intensity in different treatment groups, reflecting intracellular glucose content. The original article has been corrected.

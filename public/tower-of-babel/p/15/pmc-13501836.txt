Integrated physiological responses of Macrobrachium rosenbergii to NaHCO₃ stress: growth inhibition, energy metabolism activation, oxidative injury, and autophagic response
Authors: Heng Yu, Songbao Zou, Mei Liu, Meng Ni, Xiaoshan Jiang, Mingfeng Deng, Julin Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSaline-alkaline stress, particularly sodium bicarbonate-induced alkaline stress, can cause tissue damage and oxidative stress in aquatic animals, thereby inhibiting their growth and even threatening their survival. This has become a key factor limiting the utilization of saline-alkaline water resources. This study for the first time systematically established acute and chronic time-series gradient NaHCO₃ stress treatments, and integrated histological observation, ultrastructural examination, enzyme activity determination as well as transcriptomic and metabolomic profiling to reveal the adaptive response mechanism of M. rosenbergii.ResultsIn this study, M. rosenbergii were selected as the research object to evaluate the physiological responses under acute (96 h) and chronic (56 d) alkaline stress at NaHCO₃ concentrations of control (0.0 mmol/L), low (3.2 mmol/L, 30% LC₅₀), and high (6.4 mmol/L, 60% LC₅₀). The results indicated that during acute NaHCO₃ stress, the oxidative stress and energy metabolism-related enzyme activities fluctuated over time, reflecting adaptive response to the stress-induced damage. Chronic exposure results showed activation of a coordinated energy network—evidenced by upregulated glycolysis, accompanied by increased expression of hk2 and glut2, as well as elevated activities of HK, PFK and LDH, increased TCA intermediates (succinate, fumarate), and elevated oxidative phosphorylation metabolites, enhancing ATP production. Fatty acid metabolism exhibited a dose-dependent response: synthesis was upregulated under low stress (elevated fas, acsbgl2, and palmitic acid) likely for membrane remodeling, but suppressed under high stress. Prolonged exposure induced significant oxidative damage, evidenced by increased MDA, accumulated LysoPCs, and decreased SOD activity. Concurrently, a marked increase in autolysosomes indicated autophagy activation. Notably, these physiological disruptions were accompanied by a significant, dose-dependent decline in growth performance (FAW, WGR, SGR).ConclusionsThese findings indicate that NaHCO₃ stress induces integrated physiological responses in M. rosenbergii, including enhanced energy metabolism, oxidative injury, and autophagic activation, providing a reference for the development and utilization of saline-alkaline water resources.Keywords: Macrobrachium rosenbergii, NaHCO₃ stress, Metabolism, Oxidative stress, Autophagy

Introduction

Since the 1950s, global aquaculture production has continued to grow rapidly, while global capture fisheries production has plateaued since the early 1990s [1]. Data indicate that aquaculture has surpassed capture fisheries as the main source of aquatic animals for human consumption since 2016, and contributed 52% of total global aquatic animal production in 2018 [1, 2], which has become one of the key technological pathways for human beings to obtain high-quality protein [3]. However, water scarcity is increasingly constraining the further development of aquaculture. Globally, the area of saline-alkali land reaches approximately 950 million hectares. Efficient exploitation of saline-alkaline water resources can not only alleviate the shortage pressure of freshwater for aquaculture, but also expand the available aquaculture space. Meanwhile, it can bring comprehensive ecological and economic benefits, such as optimizing regional ecological environment, improving land utilization and promoting the growth of fishery economy [4].

Saline-alkaline water, particularly soda-type saline-alkaline water dominated by sodium bicarbonate, poses significant abiotic stress on aquaculture due to its high pH and alkalinity, which has become a key bottleneck restricting its utilization. This stress primarily originates from the high concentrations of carbonate (CO₃²⁻) and bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) ions in the water [5], which can trigger disorders in acid-base balance [6], induce oxidative stress, interfere with the organism’s antioxidant system [7], and cause localized structural damage in the hepatopancreas [8]. The above damages will inhibit the growth of aquatic animals. In addition, the continuous maintenance of physiological homeostasis under alkaline stress consumes a large amount of extra energy, which inevitably redistributes energy allocation away from growth metabolism. Consequently, suppressed growth performance, declined disease resistance, and even mass mortality frequently occur in aquatic organisms under long-term alkaline stress [9]. Clarifying the underlying physiological and molecular mechanisms of growth retardation caused by long-term alkaline stress is therefore of great theoretical and practical significance.

Shrimps constitute a vital component of the global food supply, holding significant nutritional, economic, and cultural value. Although shrimps only account for 4% of global fishery landings in weight, their economic value accounts for 11% of the total commercial fisheries output [10]. Among them, the giant freshwater prawn (Macrobrachium rosenbergii, M. rosenbergii) is a major economic species in freshwater aquaculture, prizedfor its large size, rapid growth rate, and excellent meat quality. According to the China Fishery Statistics Yearbook [11], its aquaculture production reached 245,700 tonnes in 2024, demonstrating considerable potential for application in saline-alkaline water aquaculture.

Currently, preliminary studies have explored the response characteristics of M. rosenbergii under saline-alkaline stress. Chen et al. [12] analyzed the dynamic gene expression changes of M. rosenbergii larvae under acute saline-alkaline stress based on transcriptomic data. Our previous research also found that acute alkaline stress can activate immune-related enzyme in the gill tissue of M. rosenbergii and induce differentially expressed genes (DEGs) involved in endocytosis, autophagy, and the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway [13]. In terms of gene and protein regulatory mechanisms related to alkaline stress, existing studies on crustaceans have shown that alkaline stress can regulate the expression of genes related to antioxidant defense (such as nrf2, gpx, sod), energy metabolism (such as hk and pk) and lipid metabolism (such as acc and fas), and the differential expression of these genes further affects the synthesis and activity of corresponding functional proteins, thereby participating in the regulation of oxidative stress response and energy homeostasis [14, 15]. Nevertheless, physiological regulatory strategies and molecular response patterns differ greatly between acute and chronic environmental stress [16]. Existing studies mainly focus on short-term acute alkaline stress, while the systematic mechanism underlying the effects of long-term NaHCO₃ exposure on growth, physiological metabolism, tissue homeostasis, as well as the associated transcriptomic and metabolomic regulatory patterns of M. rosenbergii remains poorly understood.

The hepatopancreas is the core metabolic and detoxification organ of crustaceans, which undertakes multiple physiological functions including energy allocation, immune regulation and homeostasis maintenance, and plays a central role in coping with environmental stress [8]. Given the research gap in chronic alkaline stress adaptation of M. rosenbergii, we hypothesized that the growth retardation under long-term NaHCO₃ stress is closely associated with energy metabolism redistribution and cumulative oxidative damage. Therefore, this study systematically investigated the oxidative damage level, energy metabolic characteristics and growth performance of M. rosenbergii under gradient acute and chronic alkaline stress, combined with histological observation, ultrastructural detection, enzyme activity determination and multi-omics analysis. The present study aimed to elucidate the physiological and molecular regulatory mechanism of M. rosenbergii in response to NaHCO₃ stress, so as to provide theoretical support for the rational utilization of saline-alkaline water resources in crustacean aquaculture.

Materials and methods

Experimental design and sample collection

Based on the previously determined 96-h median lethal concentration (LC₅₀) (10.62 mmol/L) of NaHCO₃ for M. rosenbergii [13], 30% and 60% of the LC₅₀ value were selected as the stress concentrations for the alkali stress experiment. Three treatment groups were established: a control group (0.0 mmol/L NaHCO₃), a low-concentration stress group (3.2 mmol/L NaHCO₃), and a high-concentration stress group (6.4 mmol/L NaHCO₃).

A total of 180 healthy M. rosenbergii individuals, uniform in size (average body weight 1.24 ± 0.02 g, average body length 3.51 ± 0.02 cm), were randomly divided into the three treatment groups. Each group consisted of three replicates, with 20 shrimp per replicate. The experiment was conducted in 45 L plastic tanks. Water temperature was maintained constant at 28 °C using heaters, and continuous aeration was provided to ensure sufficient dissolved oxygen. Prior to the formal stress exposure, the shrimp were acclimated in the experimental tanks for 7 d and fed a commercial formulated diet twice daily to adapt to the experimental environment and diet. At the start of the stress period, NaHCO₃ was fully dissolved in the culture water in advance. After the water alkalinity stabilized, the shrimp were transferred to the respective concentration groups to initiate the acute stress experiment, which lasted for 96 h. During the stress period, the shrimp were fed twice daily. Water was replaced every two days with pre-aerated tap water pre-warmed to 28 °C. After each water change, NaHCO₃ was re-added to maintain the target concentration, and the water alkalinity was measured simultaneously.

At 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h after stress initiation, 9 shrimp were sampled from each treatment group (3 shrimp per replicate). Hepatopancreas tissues were placed in cryotubes, flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen, and then uniformly transferred to a -80 °C freezer for subsequent enzyme activity analysis.

The basic design of the chronic stress experiment was consistent with the acute experiment described above, with the main difference being an extended stress duration of 56 d. The same husbandry management practices were maintained during the experiment, including feeding twice daily and replacing water every two days with re-adjustment and measurement of alkalinity to ensure long-term stability of the stress conditions. After the 56-d culture period, the shrimp were fasted for 24 h. The final total weight and survival count of M. rosenbergii in each tank were measured to calculate growth performance and survival rate (SR). Sample collection and processing were as follows: From each group, 3 shrimp (1 per replicate) were sampled for hepatopancreas collection. One portion was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for HE staining, while tissue from another 3 shrimp per group (1 per replicate) was pre-fixed in 2.5% glutaraldehyde for transmission electron microscopy. Furthermore, hepatopancreas tissues from 8 additional shrimp per group were collected for metabolomic and transcriptomic sequencing analysis. Hepatopancreas tissues from all remaining surviving individuals were collected, placed in cryotubes, and stored at -80 °C for subsequent quantitative analysis and enzyme activity assays.

Hepatopancreas Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) staining

Following fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde, the tissue samples underwent standard histological processing for H&E staining. The procedures were carried out as follows: paraffin embedding was performed using an embedding machine (JB-P5, Wuhan Junjie Electronics Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China), and sectioning was conducted with a cryostat (Cryostar NX50, Thermo Fisher Scientific Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). After hematoxylin staining (3–5 min) and a tap water rinse, the sections were dehydrated in 95% ethanol for 1 min and subsequently counterstained with eosin for 15 s. A graded dehydration series was then performed using anhydrous ethanol I, II, and III (2 min each), n-butanol I and II (2 min each), and xylene I and II (2 min each) for transparency, before being mounted with neutral resin. Finally, observation and image acquisition were performed under a bright-field microscope (Nikon Eclipse E100, Nikon Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) using an imaging system (Nikon DS-U3, Nikon, Japan) for analysis.

Hepatopancreas transmission electron microscopy analysis

Following initial fixation in electron microscopy fixative (G1102, Wuhan Saiweier Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China), tissue fragments (approximately 1 mm³) were washed three times (15 min each) with 0.1 M phosphate buffer (PB, pH 7.4). The samples were then dehydrated through a graded ethanol series (30%, 50%, 70%, 80%, 95%, 100%, and 100%; 20 min per step) and two changes of 100% acetone (15 min each) at room temperature. Subsequent infiltration and embedding were performed using pure 812 embedding resin (product no. 90529-77-4, SPI). Resin was poured into embedding molds, and tissue samples were positioned in the molds before overnight incubation at 37 °C. Polymerization was completed by transferring the molds to a 60 °C oven for 48 h. For orientation, resin blocks were sectioned on an ultramicrotome (Leica UC7, Leica Microsystems GmbH, Wetzlar, Germany). Semi-thin sections were stained with toluidine blue and examined under a light microscope to identify regions of interest. Selected blocks were further ultra-thin-sectioned using the same ultramicrotome, and sections were collected on 150-mesh copper grids. Grids were stained with 2% uranyl acetate in ethanol for 8 min (protected from light), rinsed three times with 70% ethanol, followed by three washes with ultrapure water, and counterstained with 2.6% lead citrate for 8 min. After a final rinse with ultrapure water, excess liquid was removed with filter paper, and grids were air-dried overnight at room temperature. Finally, the sections were observed under a transmission electron microscope (HT7800/HT7700, Hitachi High-Technologies Corporation, Tokyo, Japan), and images were acquired for analysis.

Enzyme activity assay

Assays for enzyme activities were performed using commercial kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) in strict accordance with the provided instructions. The analyzed indices included superoxide dismutase (SOD), glutathione (GSH), malondialdehyde (MDA), hexokinase (HK), phosphofructokinase (PFK), lactic dehydrogenase (LDH), Succinate dehydrogenase (SDH), triphosadenine (ATP).

RNA extraction, library preparation, and library quality inspection

Following TRIzol reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) extraction, total RNA was isolated from the tissue samples. For library construction, ribosomal RNA (rRNA) was first removed with a commercial rRNA depletion kit. The enriched mRNA was then reverse-transcribed into double-stranded cDNA, which underwent end repair, adapter ligation, and PCR amplification. The resulting libraries were validated and subsequently subjected to high-throughput sequencing. Prior to these steps, RNA quality was verified: integrity and DNA contamination were assessed by 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis in 1× TAE buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA), and purity was determined using a NanoPhotometer spectrophotometer (Implen GmbH, Munich, Germany).

Quality assessment of transcriptome sequencing data

To ensure high-quality downstream analysis, raw sequencing reads underwent rigorous preprocessing to remove low-quality and non-informative data. Initial quality control and adapter trimming were performed using fastp. This step filtered reads based on established criteria, including the presence of adapter sequences, an overrepresentation of poly-A bases, or an excessive proportion (> 50%) of bases with a Phred quality score (Q) ≤ 20 [17]. The resulting high-quality reads were then aligned against a species-specific ribosomal RNA (rRNA) database to identify and exclude residual rRNA sequences. Reads not matching rRNA were retained for subsequent transcriptomic analysis. Alignment of the processed reads against the reference genome was performed using HISAT2 [18]. Following transcript assembly with StringTie [19], gene expression levels were quantified using RSEM. Differential expression analysis was conducted with the DESeq2 package, identifying genes as significantly differentially expressed when meeting the thresholds of a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 and an absolute log2 fold change greater than 1 [20].

Metabolomics analysis

For metabolomics analysis, five biological replicates were set per group. All samples underwent quality inspection, and only those passing the criteria proceeded to formal experimentation and subsequent bioinformatics analysis. To enhance metabolite coverage and detection performance, both positive and negative ion mode were employed during data acquisition in the quality control phase. Data from the two ionization modes were processed separately in later analyses. Based on high-resolution mass spectrometry, this study adopted an untargeted metabolomics approach to capture as many molecular features as possible from the samples. Metabolite annotation was performed by matching against public databases such as Mass Bank, Metlin, and MoNA, along with a self-built secondary mass spectrometry database. To monitor technical variability during the experiment, quality control (QC) sample were incorporated [21]. The tight clustering of QC samples in principal component analysis (PCA) plots indicated high experimental stability and reliable data quality. Orthogonal partial least squares-discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), an extension of PLS-DA [22], was applied to construct models for group discrimination. Subsequent model validation and differential metabolite screening were based on the OPLS-DA results. Similar to PLS-DA, the OPLS-DA model was evaluated using parameters such as R2X, R2Y, Q2, and score plots. OPLS-DA analysis was performed using the ropls package in R [23]. Differentially expressed metabolites (DEMs) were screened by integrating multivariate and univariate statistical results [24]. The selection criteria were: a variable importance in projection (VIP) value ≥ 1 from the OPLS-DA model and a P-value < 0.05 from an independent two-sample t-test.

Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qPCR)

Total RNA was isolated from liquid nitrogen-ground tissue samples with FreeZol Reagent (Vazyme, Nanjing, China). Subsequent qPCR was performed on a LightCycler® 96 instrument (Indelamigen, Switzerland) using the HiScript® II One Step qRT-PCR SYBR Green Kit (Vazyme). Primers were designed from transcriptome sequences, validated with Primer Premier 6 software, and commercially synthesized (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China; sequences listed in Table 1). Beta-actin (β-actin) served as the internal reference gene for normalization, and relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2−∆∆CT method.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed with SPSS 20.0. After confirming the homogeneity of variances, intergroup differences were evaluated. Multiple group comparisons were carried out using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey’s honest significant difference test, provided that the ANOVA was significant. For comparisons involving only two groups, an independent-sample t-test was employed. Results are expressed as mean ± SEM. In all analyses, differences were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05, which are denoted by differing superscript letters in the presentation of results.

Results

Determination of antioxidant indicators

As shown in Fig. 1, the differences in antioxidant-related indicators in the hepatopancreas of M. rosenbergii among various treatment groups were presented at 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h under acute NaHCO₃ stress. Regarding glutathione (GSH) (Fig. 1B), no significant differences (p > 0.05) were observed between any concentration treatment group and the control group throughout the entire stress period. For superoxide dismutase (SOD) (Fig. 1A), at 48 h and 96 h of stress, the 3.2 mmol/L treatment group showed no significant difference compared to the control group, whereas the activity in the 6.4 mmol/L treatment group was significantly higher than that in the control group (p < 0.05). Notably, starting from 24 h of stress, the malondialdehyde (MDA) (Fig. 1C) content exhibited a significant increasing trend positively correlated with the NaHCO₃ stress concentration (p < 0.05).

Dynamic changes of hepatopancreatic antioxidant indicators in M. rosenbergii under acute NaHCO₃ stress at different concentrations over 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h (A–C). Changes of hepatopancreatic antioxidant indicators under chronic NaHCO₃ stress (56 d) at different concentrations (D–F). (n = 3)

After 56 days of long-term stress, compared with the control group, the SOD (Fig. 1D) content in the 3.2 mmol/L and 6.4 mmol/L treatment groups decreased significantly (p < 0.05), while the MDA (Fig. 1F) content increased significantly (p < 0.05). No significant difference in GSH (Fig. 1E) was observed among the treatment groups (p > 0.05).

Measurement of energy metabolism-related parameters

As shown in Fig. 2, the differences in energy metabolism-related indicators in the hepatopancreas of M. rosenbergii among various stress groups under acute NaHCO₃ stress at 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h were compared. The results showed that the activity of succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) (Fig. 2D) did not change significantly throughout the entire stress period (p > 0.05). After 96 h of stress, compared with the control group, the levels of phosphofructokinase (PFK) (Fig. 2B) and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) (Fig. 2C) in all NaHCO₃ treatment groups increased significantly (p < 0.05). Throughout the stress period, hexokinase (HK) (Fig. 2A) activity was continuously inhibited and remained significantly lower than that in the control group (p < 0.05). Additionally, after 48 h and 96 h of stress, adenosine triphosphate (ATP) (Fig. 2E) content increased significantly (p < 0.05).

Dynamic changes of hepatopancreatic energy metabolism-related parameters in M. rosenbergii under acute NaHCO₃ stress at different concentrations over 24 h, 48 h, and 96 h (A–E). Changes of hepatopancreatic energy metabolism-related indicators under chronic NaHCO₃ stress (56 d) at different concentrations (F–J). (n = 3)

After 56 d of long-term stress treatment, compared with the control group, the HK (Fig. 2F) activity in both the 3.2 mmol/L and 6.4 mmol/L treatment groups increased significantly (p < 0.05). The LDH (Fig. 2H) content showed a significant increasing trend with the rise in stress concentration (p < 0.05), while no significant difference was observed in SDH (Fig. 2I) activity among all groups (p > 0.05). Additionally, both the PFK (Fig. 2G) activity and ATP (Fig. 2J) content in the 6.4 mmol/L treatment group were significantly higher than those in the control group and the 3.2 mmol/L treatment group (p < 0.05).

Histological examination of hepatopancreas

As shown in the Fig. 3, after 56 d stress treatment, a small number of circular vacuoles of varying sizes (blue arrows) (Fig. 3B) were observed in the cytoplasm of the hepatic tubule epithelial cells in the control group of M. rosenbergii. In both the 3.2 mmol/L and 6.4 mmol/L NaHCO₃ stress treatment groups, a large number of circular vacuoles of varying sizes (blue arrows) (Fig. 3D, F) were visible, along with occasional deformation of hepatic tubules and luminal dilation (black arrows) (Fig. 3C, E). Furthermore, in the 6.4 mmol/L NaHCO₃ stress treatment group, mild edema (red arrows) (Fig. 3F) and swelling of a small number of hepatic tubule epithelial cells were observed. The black boxes indicate the areas selected for enlarged views.

Ultrastructure observation of hepatopancreas

As shown in the Fig. 4, after 56 d of long-term stress treatment, compared with the control group, some mitochondria in the 3.2 mmol/L NaHCO₃ stress treatment group exhibited increased volume, elliptical morphology, apparent swelling, and structural disintegration, characterized mainly by cristae dissolution or loss (Fig. 4D). In contrast, in the 6.4 mmol/L NaHCO₃ stress treatment group, the mitochondria in the shrimp hepatopancreatic cells not only showed cristae dissolution or loss but also displayed disrupted bilayer membrane structures (Fig. 4F), which may lead to content leakage. Furthermore, quantitative analysis revealed that the number of autolysosomes (Fig. 4G) of the 6.4 mmol/L NaHCO₃ group increased significantly (p < 0.05), while the number of mitochondria (Fig. 4H) decreased significantly (p < 0.05). No statistically significant differences (p > 0.05) were observed in these indicators between the 3.2 mmol/L NaHCO₃ stress treatment group and the control group.

OPLS-DA analysis

Analysis of sample distribution via OPLS-DA score plots showed clear separation between the CH group and the EH1 group (Fig. 5A), the CH group and the EH2 group (Fig. 5B), as well as between the EH1 group and the EH2 group (Fig. 5C) in both positive and negative ion modes. To verify the reliability of the OPLS-DA model, permutation tests were further conducted, and the goodness-of-fit and predictive ability of the model were evaluated using Q2 (cum) and R2 (cum). The results showed that in the permutation test plots for both positive and negative ion modes (Fig. 5D–F), all original R2 and Q2 values on the right side were higher than the corresponding permutation-distributed values on the left, indicating that the OPLS-DA models constructed under both ion modes were robust and reliable without overfitting, thereby supporting the authenticity of the experimental results.

DEMs screening

Furthermore, comparisons of intra-group and inter-group samples using two-dimensional (Fig. 6A) and three-dimensional (Fig. 6B) principal component analysis revealed good reproducibility within groups and differences between groups, further confirming the validity of the aforementioned group separations. Hierarchical clustering of all samples generated a dendrogram reflecting inter-sample similarities (Fig. 6C), demonstrating good sample reproducibility and the absence of outliers. The correlation heatmap illustrated the correlations among samples, and as shown in Fig. 6D, intra-group samples exhibited high reproducibility. DEMs were selected based on the criteria of VIP ≥ 1 and T-test P < 0.05. The distribution of DEMs between each group was displayed in Fig. 6E.

Discussion

NaHCO₃ stress triggered growth inhibition, oxidative damage, and autophagic response

SOD plays a crucial role in antioxidant defense by scavenging superoxide radicals [25]. In contrast, the content of MDA, a well-established marker of oxidative damage, increased significantly with higher stress levels, indicating aggravated oxidative stress [26]. In the current study, under acute NaHCO₃ stress at different time points, compared with the control group, the 6.4 mmol/L NaHCO₃ treatment significantly increased the MDA content; meanwhile, SOD activity in the hepatopancreas was also significantly elevated after 48 h and 96 h of stress. These results indicated that high-concentration NaHCO₃ stress caused oxidative stress, the shrimp also responded by activating antioxidant enzyme activities. A similar phenomenon was observed in Macrobrachium nipponense, where SOD activity in the hepatopancreas was enhanced under high-concentration NaHCO₃ stress [27]. However, under chronic NaHCO₃ stress, the transient activation of antioxidant enzyme observed during the acute phase may not suffice to counteract persistent oxidative damage, consequently impairing hepatopancreatic function and suppressing growth. Previous studies on Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) [9], Gibel carp (Carassius gibelio) [28], and Chinese mitten crab (Eriocheir sinensis) [29] showed that high-concentration alkaline stress inhibited growth. Consistent with trends observed in previous studies, the WGR and SGR of M. rosenbergii in the present chronic NaHCO₃ stress experiment also significantly decreased with increasing NaHCO₃ concentration, further confirming the inhibitory effect of high-concentration alkaline stress on the growth of shrimp. And compared with the control group, the SR of M. rosenbergii in the 6.4 mmol/L NaHCO₃ group was significantly inhibited, while that in the 3.2 mmol/L NaHCO₃ group was not significantly affected. This indicated that under low-concentration alkaline stress, M. rosenbergii could adapt to the environment through self-regulation, demonstrating its potential for application in saline-alkaline water survival.

Chronic high-alkaline stress inhibits growth and elevates mortality in M. rosenbergii, which is largely caused by excessive oxidative damage. In this study, transcriptomic results showed that the relative expression level of sod1l was markedly downregulated in the EH2 group under sustained NaHCO₃ stress compared with the control group. Consistently, the activity of SOD decreased gradually with the increase of alkaline stress concentration. Metabolomic data further confirmed that multiple lysophosphatidylcholines (LysoPCs) involved in glycerophospholipid metabolism were significantly accumulated in response to NaHCO₃ exposure. Correlation analysis also verified that SOD activity was strongly correlated with LysoPC (16:0/0:0), LysoPC (18:0/0:0) and PC (34:1) in the high-concentration EH2 group. After stress treatment, the correlations between antioxidant indicators such as SOD and MDA and metabolites were markedly enhanced, indicating that stress could activate the oxidative stress response and further facilitate the synergistic regulation between physiological indicators and endogenous metabolism. The Mantel correlation network revealed that the EH2 group exhibited denser connections with the physiological and metabolic indicators, indicating that high-concentration NaHCO₃ stress significantly enhanced the overall correlation between physiological indices and the metabolome, thereby activating a more extensive synergistic regulatory network between physiology and metabolism. It has been documented that the accumulation of LysoPCs is closely linked to oxidative stress and cell membrane injury [30]. Integrating transcriptomic and metabolomic evidence, we confirmed that long-term NaHCO₃ exposure triggered dose-dependent oxidative damage in M. rosenbergii. This conclusion was well supported by hepatopancreatic histological alterations and ultrastructural abnormalities observed via transmission electron microscopy. Collectively, alkaline stress could induce lipid peroxidation, activate oxidative stress responses and further provoke inflammatory reactions in hepatopancreatic cells. Similar oxidative stress responses under high carbonate alkalinity have also been reported in Pacific white shrimp (Litopenaeus vannamei) by Zhang et al. [8]. Combined with the growth retardation phenotype of M. rosenbergii under alkaline stress in our experiment, we inferred that NaHCO₃-induced oxidative injury and disturbed cellular homeostasis are the key factors suppressing normal growth performance of prawns.

Autophagy is an evolutionarily conserved cellular process responsible for degrading misfolded proteins and damaged organelles [31]. An autophagosome forms when an isolation membrane non-selectively sequesters a portion of the cytoplasm. Upon fusing with a lysosome, it delivers its inner membrane and cargo to the resulting autolysosome, where they are degraded by lysosomal hydrolases [32]. An increase in the number of autolysosomes typically reflects enhanced autophagic activity as a cellular mechanism to maintain homeostasis [33]. Under environmental stress, crustaceans can activate autophagy as a response mechanism, with an increase in autolysosomes being a common manifestation [34]. It has been reported that under saline-alkaline stress, Chinese mitten crab activates autophagy in response [35]. In the current study, it was found that under chronic NaHCO₃ stress, the number of autolysosomes increased significantly, while the mitochondrial number decreased markedly in the hepatopancreas of the EH2 group. This suggested that M. rosenbergii may activate autophagy to cope with alkaline stress. Given that autophagy activation can mitigate oxidative stress [36], this process might represent a key cellular mechanism for alleviating stress-induced oxidative damage. Furthermore, autophagy can enhance the immune response of M. rosenbergii by regulating cellular metabolism and immune pathways [37]. These results provided a theoretical basis for future strategies aimed at improving the adaptability of crustaceans to saline-alkaline environments through the modulation of autophagy pathways.

NaHCO₃ stress activated the glycometabolism-oxidative phosphorylation pathway for energy supply

A study indicated that crustaceans adapt to environmental stress via remodeling energy metabolism, a process involving alterations in metabolic pathways such as carbohydrates and lipids [15]. Previous studies have revealed that aquatic organisms can initiate adaptive compensation strategies by regulating core glucose metabolic pathways (glycolysis and gluconeogenesis) under acid-base stress, and enhanced glycolytic flux has been proven to be a vital adaptive mechanism against alkaline stress [38]. HK acts as a core rate-limiting enzyme in glycolysis [39]. In this acute NaHCO₃ stress experiment, HK activity was persistently suppressed during the whole stress period, whereas the activities of PFK and LDH were distinctly increased at 96 h post stress. As a characteristic enzyme of anaerobic metabolism [40], the elevation of LDH activity indicated that shrimps activated anaerobic glycolysis to rapidly supplement energy in the late stress stage. SDH, a pivotal functional enzyme and quantitative indicator of aerobic metabolism in the tricarboxylic acid cycle (TCA) [41, 42], exhibited stable activity without significant fluctuation, implying that the basic aerobic metabolic function was not severely disturbed. ATP content increased significantly after 48 h and 96 h of stress, reflecting the organism’s ability to maintain energy homeostasis through multiple compensatory pathways. Overall, M. rosenbergii ensured energy supply under alkali stress by coordinating the balance between glycolysis and aerobic metabolism, a finding similarly reported in Macrobrachium nipponense and Chinese mitten crab [43, 44]. Under chronic NaHCO₃ stress, we integrated transcriptomic profiling, enzyme activity determination and metabolomic analysis to clarify the intrinsic regulatory mechanism of energy metabolism. Transcriptomic results showed that the glycolysis/gluconeogenesis pathway-related gene hk2 was significantly upregulated in the EH1 group relative to the control group. It is widely accepted that organisms tend to upregulate hk2 transcription to accelerate glycolytic process and guarantee sustained energy synthesis under external stress [45]. In accordance with the transcriptional alteration, in vivo enzyme activity detection also verified the notable increase of HK activity in the EH1 group.

Consistent with the transcriptional and biochemical changes, metabolomic data further confirmed that the content of pyruvate, the terminal product of glycolysis, was significantly decreased in the same pathway. As a crucial metabolic node, pyruvate can be converted into acetyl-CoA and further participate in the TCA cycle. Combined with multi-omics evidence, we speculated that moderate NaHCO₃ stress activated the sequential metabolic cascade from glycolysis to oxidative phosphorylation in M. rosenbergii. The enhanced downstream metabolic flow accelerated pyruvate consumption, which ultimately resulted in the reduction of endogenous pyruvate accumulation. Meanwhile, TCA cycle intermediate succinic acid was significantly accumulated in the EH1 group. The TCA cycle provides abundant reducing equivalents for oxidative phosphorylation, which dominates intracellular ATP synthesis and supports almost all physiological activities of organisms [46]. In this study, the significant accumulation of phosphate and pyrophosphate involved in oxidative phosphorylation further verified the activation of this energy-producing pathway, reflecting the high metabolic activity and strong anabolic demand of prawn hepatopancreas under moderate alkaline stress. For the high-concentration alkaline treatment group (EH2), the activities of HK, PFK and LDH were all significantly higher than those in the control group. Metabolomic identification also detected remarkable increases of TCA cycle intermediates (succinic acid and fumaric acid) as well as oxidative phosphorylation-related metabolites (phosphate and pyrophosphate). Combined with elevated enzyme activity and higher ATP level in the EH2 group, we concluded that severe NaHCO₃ stress greatly raised the overall energy consumption of prawns. Excess energy was mainly used to support osmotic pressure regulation, stress resistance response and other stress-adaptive physiological processes, thereby improving the survival adaptability of prawns under extreme alkaline environments.

Transcriptomic data revealed that the expression of glucose transporter 2 (glut2) was significantly upregulated in the EH2 group relative to the EH1 group. As a vital glucose transport mediator, glut2 is essential for systemic metabolic balance and intracellular energy homeostasis. Its transcriptional upregulation can effectively promote cellular glucose uptake and further accelerate the overall glycolytic process [47–49]. This result was consistent with a previous study on Nile tilapia [9], which reported differential glut2 expression patterns under varying alkalinity conditions, further confirming that elevated glut2 expression facilitates carbohydrate utilization during alkaline stress adaptation. Consistent with the transcriptional variation, physiological detection showed that the activities of PFK and LDH as well as intracellular ATP content were markedly higher in the EH2 group than in the EH1 group. Combined with corresponding metabolomic profiles, we found that the contents of TCA cycle and pyruvate metabolism intermediates including succinic acid and fumaric acid were significantly increased in the EH2 group, while the upstream glycolytic substrates D-glucose and α-D-glucose were notably decreased. Integrating transcriptomic signatures, enzyme activity phenotypes and metabolomic alterations within identical metabolic pathways, we confirmed that high-concentration alkaline stress distinctly boosted glucose transmembrane transport efficiency and accelerated in vivo glycolytic flux. The enhanced upstream glucose catabolism provided sufficient metabolic precursors to fuel downstream TCA cycle and oxidative phosphorylation processes, which constituted a core adaptive strategy for prawns to satisfy increased energy demands under severe alkaline stress. In addition, correlation analysis further verified that energy metabolism-related genes exhibited strong positive correlations with corresponding enzyme activities and pathway-specific metabolites. Taken together, evidence obtained from transcriptional regulation, enzymatic function and endogenous metabolic accumulation consistently illustrated that NaHCO₃ stress activated the synergistic regulatory network consisting of glycolysis, TCA cycle and oxidative phosphorylation in M. rosenbergii. Such systematic metabolic reprogramming effectively elevates energy supply capacity to resist adverse alkaline environments. Nevertheless, the persistent increase in energy consumption inevitably consumes substantial biological resources, which is presumably an important internal factor leading to the growth retardation of prawns under long-term alkaline stress.

Fatty acid synthesis was enhanced under low-concentration NaHCO₃ stress for membrane remodeling but inhibited under high-concentration stress accompanied by metabolic disruption

Fatty acids are essential energy metabolic substrates and basic structural constituents of cell membrane lipids, and maintaining fatty acid homeostasis is indispensable for normal physiological activities of aquatic organisms. Environmental stress usually triggers remarkable alterations in lipid metabolism to satisfy cellular requirements for membrane reconstruction, energy reservation and signal transduction [50]. Transcriptomic analysis showed that the expression levels of key genes involved in fatty acid biosynthesis were significantly upregulated in the low-concentration NaHCO₃ group (EH1) relative to the control group, including fatty acid synthase (fas), long-chain-fatty-acid–CoA ligase ACSBG2-like isoform X1 (acsbgl2), and very long chain fatty acid elongase 6-like isoform X1 (elovl6l). Among them, FAS is the core enzyme catalyzing de novo fatty acid synthesis. ACSBGL2 is an enzyme that activates long-chain fatty acids, converting the fatty acids synthesized by FAS into fatty acyl-CoA, which subsequently participates in β-oxidation for energy supply [7]. In line with the transcriptional upregulation pattern, metabolomic identification also detected a marked increase in palmitic acid (16:0), a pivotal end-product metabolite in the fatty acid metabolic pathway. As a dominant saturated fatty acid, palmitic acid widely exists in membrane phospholipids and triglycerides, and can be synthesized endogenously or absorbed from external diets [51]. The consistent upward trend of fatty acid synthesis-related genes at the transcriptional level and pathway downstream metabolites at the metabolic level strongly confirmed that low-alkaline stress comprehensively activated the fatty acid metabolic cascade in M. rosenbergii. Given that lipid metabolism primarily sustains cell membrane structure and function rather than simple energy storage [52], such metabolic reprogramming was inferred to be an active adaptive strategy. Shrimps actively remodeled membrane lipid composition to adjust membrane fluidity, structural thickness and stability, so as to improve environmental adaptability and alkaline stress tolerance.

By contrast, high-concentration alkaline stress (EH2) induced significant downregulation of core fatty acid metabolism-related genes including fas, acsbg2, elovl6l and sod1l when compared with the EH1 group. Meanwhile, metabolomic data verified that the content of pathway-specific palmitic acid was also significantly decreased correspondingly. These multi-omics results fully illustrated that the regulatory effect of NaHCO₃ stress on fatty acid metabolism exhibited obvious dose-dependent characteristics. Organisms could initiate positive adaptive fatty acid synthesis responses under low-intensity alkaline stress, while excessive stress intensity would inhibit fatty acid metabolic processes once exceeding the physiological regulation threshold. Consistent with the results observed in our high-concentration NaHCO₃ stress group, Zhang et al. [15] also found that alkaline stress could inhibit genes expression related to lipid synthesis in Pacific white shrimp. Although that study differed from our observation of upregulated metabolism under low-alkaline stress, both collectively demonstrated that alkaline stress significantly disrupts lipid metabolic homeostasis in crustaceans. Furthermore, Lei et al. [53] reported that carbonate alkalinity stress could induce lipid metabolic disorder and lipid accumulation of Largemouth bass (Micropterus salmoides), further supporting the notion that alkaline stress may disrupt body lipid homeostasis by interfering with the balance between fatty acid synthesis and breakdown.

Conclusion

The present study revealed that high-concentration alkaline stress induced oxidative stress and tissue damage, thereby inhibiting the growth and survival of M. rosenbergii. However, the shrimp could also self-regulate by initiating autophagic responses, activating the glycometabolism-oxidative phosphorylation pathway for energy supply, and modulating fatty acid synthesis to adapt to the alkaline water environment (Fig. 9). This study revealed the response mechanism of M. rosenbergii under alkaline stress, deepened the understanding of its biological characteristics, and provided an important scientific basis for optimizing the management practices of saline-alkaline water aquaculture.

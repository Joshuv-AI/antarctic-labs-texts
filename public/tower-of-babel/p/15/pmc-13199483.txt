A novel strategy for secretory leukocyte protease inhibitor (SLPI) immobilization on alkaline-etched titanium via a plasma-polymerized interlayer and covalent coupling to enhance osteoblast activity
Authors: Wannapat Chouyratchakarn, Wei-Yu Chen, Nithi Atthi, Chayarop Supanchart, Sarawut Kumphune
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTitanium (Ti) implants are the gold standard for orthopedic and dental applications, but their bioinert surfaces lack the osteoinductive properties. Combining alkaline etching for biomimetic surface topography and biofunctionalization with secretory leukocyte protease inhibitor (SLPI) has potential to promote osteogenic activities. Thus, this study is the aim to develops and characterizes a novel strategy to immobilize recombinant human SLPI (rhSLPI) onto alkaline-etched Ti (TiOH) surfaces via plasma polymerization. The grade 5 Ti and TiOH were polymerized utilizing a plasma-polymerized interlayer, followed by UV-graft polymerization of polyacrylic acid (PAAc) and EDC/NHS-mediated covalent coupling to immobilize 10 µg/mL rhSLPI (TiS and TiOHS, respectively) to immobilize 10 µg/mL rhSLPI (TiS and TiOHS, respectively). The ELISA and XPS confirmed the successful rhSLPI immobilization. Moreover, the SEM revealed the pillar-like morphology on the TIOH and TiOHS groups, whereas the Ti and TiS showed smooth surfaces, which is consistent with the profilometer. Additionally, the TiOH and rhSLPI-immobilized groups showed significantly higher hydrophilicity. Moreover, all sample types showed biocompatible to hFOB 1.19 cell. The TiOHS demonstrated highest cell adhesion, while TiS promoted highest cell proliferation. Alizarin red S (ARS) staining demonstrated TiOHS promoted the highest mineral deposition. In conclusion, the rhSLPI immobilization on alkaline-etched Ti via plasma polymerization, PAAc grafting and EDC/NHS covalent coupling. Introduction

Titanium (Ti) and its alloys are the gold standard for dental and orthopedic implant material due to their outstanding mechanical properties, excellent biocompatibility, and high corrosion resistance1,2. The long-term clinical success depends significantly on osseointegration, which is the formation of a direct, structural, and functional connection between the surrounding bone tissue and the surface of the implant3. The native Ti surfaces are considered bioinert and have excellent biocompatibility; however, the Ti lacks the osteoinductive properties to promote bone healing4. Therefore, the native titanium surface may lead to prolonged healing times, inadequate stability, or even implant failure, particularly in compromised patients, such as those with osteoporosis or metabolic disorders5.

To overcome this limitation, the direction of the research on metal implants has been aimed at modifying implant surfaces from bioinert to bioactive, thereby enhancing their biological effects6. Surface modification strategies are broadly categorized into physical and chemical/biochemical approaches7. Physical modification, mainly through the generating of micro- and nano-topographies, has proven highly effective, with alkaline etching using NaOH being a well-known method that generates a porous, mesh-like sodium titanate hydrogel layer on the Ti surface8,9. This nanostructured topography, which effectively mimics the natural structure of the extracellular matrix (ECM), enhances osteoblast adhesion, spreading, and differentiation9,10.

Nevertheless, physical modifications provide an ECM-like structure; however, in clinical settings, patients remain susceptible to infection or inflammation, which may lead to implant failure11. Thus, the development of biofunctionalization surfaces has used bioactive molecules to immobilize onto the implant surface12. Secretory leukocyte protease inhibitor (SLPI) is a multifunctional protein widely recognized for its anti-inflammatory and anti-microbial functions13,14. While these properties are highly advantageous for mitigating implant-associated infections, previous studies have additionally revealed a potent, direct role for SLPI in osteogenesis, promoting osteoblast adhesion, proliferation, and the expression of key osteogenic differentiation markers via simple coating technique15,16. Therefore, rather than evaluating its immunological properties, the primary focus of this current study is to evaluate the osteogenic potential of SLPI. However, the simple coating technique is non-specific and results in a weakly bound layer that can be rapidly denatured or eluted in vivo17. Hence, plasma polymerization has been recognized as a powerful and versatile platform technology18. The plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) was employed to deposit an ultra-thin, stable, and chemically defined polymer interlayer onto the material surface18. This plasma-polymerized layer provides a high density of functional groups, including carboxyl and amine groups that serve as anchoring sites for the covalent conjugation of biomolecules19,20. Therefore, the present study aims to develop and characterize a novel strategy to improve the biofunctionality of titanium surfaces by combining alkaline etching with plasma polymerization-based immobilization of recombinant human SLPI (rhSLPI), thereby creating a suitable surface for promoting bone cell activities.

Results

The validation of rhSLPI immobilization on the modification surface

The immobilization of recombinant human secretory leukocyte protease inhibitor (rhSLPI) onto the Ti surface was confirmed using ELISA (Fig. 1). The ELISA results indicated the presence of rhSLPI on the surfaces of the rhSLPI-immobilized groups, TiS and TiOHS (3.18 0.23 vs. 3.61 0.17, respectively). However, the Ti and TiOH, which are non-immobilized groups, exhibited no detectable rhSLPI protein on their surfaces. Moreover, the TiOHS exhibited a significantly greater rhSLPI protein compared to the TiS.

The confirmation of rhSLPI on the Ti surface. The ELISA results showed the significant difference between the rhSLPI coating groups and the control (uncoated). * p < 0.05 vs. control (ANOVA) (n = 6 per group).

The surface characteristics of the modified surface

The surface characteristics of the modified titanium surface were determined by the surface topology, surface roughness, and surface hydrophilicity. The SEM revealed a slightly rough surface of Ti and TiS groups (Fig. 2a and b, respectively), whereas the surface of TiOH and TiOHS showed pillar-like texture after alkaline etching (Fig. 2c and d, respectively). Moreover, the surface roughness was measured by a 3D laser scanning microscope (Fig. 2e). The results demonstrated that the TiOH (0.31 0.04 μm) showed significantly higher surface roughness compared to the Ti control groups (0.23 0.02). Additionally, the rhSLPI-immobilized groups (TiS and TiOHS; 0.15 0.02 and 0.24 0.01, respectively) showed significantly lower roughness compared to control groups (Ti and TiOH), while the TiOHS demonstrated significantly higher roughness than TiS. The hydrophilicity of the surface was revealed by using a sessile drop method (Fig. 2f). The results demonstrated that after NaOH etching (TiOH, 15.61˚ 1.61˚), it exhibited a significant reduction compared to the control group (Ti, 64.14˚ 7.41˚). Moreover, the plasma polymerization and rhSLPI immobilization groups, which are TiS (2.15˚ 1.39˚) and TiOHS (5.48˚ 1.74˚) showed a significant decrease of water contact angle compared to the control groups (Ti and TiOH, respectively), while there are no significant differences between TiS and TiOHS.

The XPS reveals elemental composition on the surface

The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was employed to observe the surface chemical composition of the modified surfaces and verify the rhSLPI protein immobilization (Fig. 3). The XPS spectra revealed the elemental peaks of the titanium (Ti2s, Ti2p1, Ti2p3, and Ti3p) and oxygen (O1s, 520–540 eV) on the control Ti and NaOH-etched groups. Moreover, a peak of carbon (C1s, 270–300 eV) was present on all types of the titanium surfaces. The nitrogen peak (N1s) was represented on both the TiS and TiOH groups, whereas that was absent on the Ti and TiOH groups. Notably, in the immobilized groups (TiS and TiOHS), the silicon peaks (Si2s and Si2p, 100–150 eV) were detected on the TiS but not on the TiOHS group. In contrast, the TiOHS was detected the titanium peaks like the TiOH, whereas the silicon peaks had disappeared in the TiOHS spectrum.

The quantification and cellular morphology of osteoblast cell adhesion

Determination of surface modification effects on hFOB 1.19 cell adhesion was conducted using the MTT assay and FE-SEM. The MTT assay quantitatively revealed osteoblast adhesion (Fig. 5a), exhibiting that the percentage of adhered cells on TiOH surfaces (147.20% 25.93%) was significantly higher compared with the control group (Ti, 100.00% 16.76%). Moreover, the amount of cell adhesion on plasma polymerization and rhSLPI immobilization groups, which are TiS (140.20% 11.13%) and TiOH (193.40 14.96), was significantly higher compared to each control group. Additionally, the TiOHS demonstrated significantly increased cell adhesion on the surface compared to the TiS group. The FE-SEM finding revealed the morphology of hFOB 1.19 cells on the various modified titanium surfaces. On the Ti surface, the cells showed round shapes with limited cytoplasm expansion (Fig. 5b). Similarly, the cell on the TiS surface exhibited a spherical morphology with minimal spreading (Fig. 5c). However, the cell morphology on the TiOH demonstrated a flattened morphology with extended and spread cytoplasm (Fig. 5d). Moreover, on the TiOHS surface, the cell showed flattened morphology and clear cytoplasmic extensions (Fig. 5e).

The effect of modified surface on human osteoblast cell proliferation

The proliferation of hFOB 1.19 cells on the titanium surface was determined by using the MTT assay for 7 days (Fig. 6). On day 3, the MTT assay demonstrated that the rhSLPI-immobilized titanium (TiS, 108.10% 8.88%) had significantly higher cell proliferation compared with the TiOHS (73.41% 7.83%). On day 5, the proliferation of osteoblast cells on the Ti surface was significantly higher compared to the TiOH, while the TiS demonstrated a greatly higher proliferation compared to both Ti and TiOHS (142.00% 29.76% and 110.80% 13.05%, respectively). Lastly, on day 7, the TiS (251.60% 22.42%) maintains the highest level of cell proliferation, which was significantly higher than both Ti and TiOHS (201.60% 17.69% and 183.00% 3.33%, respectively). Moreover, Ti showed significantly higher compared to TiOH (165.70% 16.65%). Over time, as the incubation period extended from days 3 to days 5 and 7, all groups exhibited a statistically significant time-dependent increase in cell proliferation.

Discussion and conclusion

The present study aims to develop the biofunctionality of the grade V Ti surface using alkaline etching (NaOH etching) and rhSLPI immobilization via plasma polymerization and evaluate the physicochemical and biofunctional performance. The major finding of the present study demonstrated that this is the first study to develop the surface modification strategy via rhSLPI immobilized plasma polymerization on the NaOH-etched titanium that enhanced the human osteoblast cell activities, including cell adhesion, proliferation, and differentiation. This study is intended as an initial framework for further development of practical orthopaedic and dental applications.

Post rhSLPI immobilized on the Ti surface, the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) has been used to confirm protein immobilization on the surface. The rhSLPI-immobilized group demonstrated that rhSLPI was immobilized on the surface. Furthermore, the TiOHS showed a significantly greater absorbance compared to TiS, indicating more immobilization of rhSLPI on the TiOHS surface group. The increase of rhSLPI immobilization on the TiOHS surface could be ascribed to surface hydroxylation through NaOH treatment, which induced the hydroxyl (-OH) group to the surface that is the precursor for the cross-linker and protein immobilization21. Moreover, previous studies indicate that surface modifications, including hydroxylation or activation of the titanium surface, enhance biomolecule immobilization22. From the finding, it was indicated that the plasma-treated alkaline-etched Ti could improve the immobilization of the rhSLPI protein on the Ti surface. The previous study revealed that the surface modification of titanium plays a crucial role in enhancing its physicochemical properties, which directly affect protein adsorption and subsequent cellular responses23. Furthermore, the SEM revealed that the Ti and TiS exhibited relatively smooth and slightly roughened surfaces, while the NaOH-etched groups (TiOH and TiOHS) demonstrated a highly textured, pillar-like morphology. The nano-/micro-scale structures generated by alkaline etching are known to significantly increase the surface area and introduce negatively charged hydroxyl groups, both of which provide the surface roughness and surface hydrophilicity24. Moreover, the quantitative roughness results supported the qualitative SEM, confirming that alkaline etching (TiOH) significantly increased the surface roughness compared to untreated Ti. However, after the rhSLPI immobilization (TiS and TiOHS), the surface roughness was significantly lower than that of the untreated group (Ti and TiOH). This finding suggests that the rhSLPI layers infiltrated and filled the nanoscale pores and valleys of the surface, resulting in a smoother overall surface. The hydrophilicity determination by sessile water contact angle demonstrated that alkaline etching (TiOH) significantly decreased the contact angle compared to the untreated Ti (Ti), indicating that alkaline etching improved the wettability of the Ti surface due to providing the polar functional group on the surface25. Interestingly, plasma polymerization and rhSLPI immobilization (TiS and TiOH) further improved surface hydrophilicity, which was attributed to the introduction of the polar functional groups during the plasma polymerization and the polarity of the rhSLPI13,21. From the findings, coordination of NaOH etching, plasma polymerization, and rhSLPI immobilization resulted in increased surface roughness and hydrophilicity, which are crucial factors for further observation on enhancing osteoblast adhesion, proliferation, and differentiation on titanium surfaces21,26.

Then the XPS revealed the elemental composition on the surface. The titanium (Ti) and oxygen (O) peaks on the surface are the major compositions of the titanium and titanium oxides27. However, the TiS demonstrated the absence of a Ti peak due to the Ti2p peaks in the TiS, which can be attributed to the coverage of the titanium surface by the rhSLPI protein layer, which effectively attenuated the Ti signals detected by XPS. This finding is consistent with previous studies reporting that protein or polymer overlayers reduce the intensity of substrate-related peaks due to limited photoelectron escape depth (5–10 nm)28,29. Whereas, the Ti peaks were still found in the TiOHS group, though, which could be caused by grooves on the surface and greater surface roughness. These topographical features probably allow the rhSLPI molecules to fill in the recessed spaces while remaining on a certain Ti surface to be detected by XPS. In addition, the detection of the C1s peak in non-immobilized groups (Ti and TiOH) indicated the contamination of hydrocarbon compounds on the surface30. Furthermore, the increase of the C1s peak and O1s peak and the exhibition of N1s, which are the protein-related chemical compositions, including the carboxyl and amide functional groups of the protein molecule after rhSLPI immobilization, suggested that the rhSLPI was able to attach to the surface, which is consistent with the ELISA29. However, Si2s and Si2p peaks were detected in the TiS group, whereas they were absent in the TiOHS group. From the remarkable finding, that suggests the signal on the TiOHS surface was covered by the rhSLPI protein layer, resulting in the absence of the Si2s and Si2p peaks. Hence, the XPS analysis and the ELISA confirmed the successful immobilization of rhSLPI on both TiS and TiOHS. Although the functional group density and percentage of grafting efficiency were not quantified via specific titrations, the combined XPS elemental profiling and ELISA quantification demonstrated the efficacy of the functionalization process.

The biocompatibility of the modified Ti was determined by using the MTT assay. The MTT demonstrated that alkaline etching showed no toxicity to the human osteoblast cell, which is consistent with the previous study10. Moreover, both groups of plasma polymerization and rhSLPI immobilization (TiS and TiOH) showed biocompatibility with the human osteoblast. This current finding is consistent with previous reports showing that plasma polymerization exhibited no cytotoxic effects on osteosarcoma cells21. Furthermore, the rhSLPI immobilization exhibited the absence of cytotoxicity to osteoblasts. The evidence from previous studies has demonstrated that rhSLPI-loaded liposomes enhance osteoblast cell activities, and rhSLPI-coated titanium with a simple coat technique on titanium surfaces exhibited no toxic effects on human osteoblast cells14,15. Thus, the plasma polymerization and rhSLPI immobilization is suitable for the furthermore study to reveal the potential of the samples on the human osteoblast cell activities.

The potential of an implant to facilitate rapid and stable cell adhesion is a crucial factor that impacts successful osseointegration31. The finding revealed that both quantitative (MTT) and qualitative (SEM) determinations demonstrated that the alkaline etching, plasma polymerization, and rhSLPI immobilization of the titanium (TiOHS) provided the most effective surface for promoting early osteoblast adhesion and spreading. The effect of alkaline etching, plasma polymerization, and rhSLPI immobilization on the human osteoblast cell adhesion was revealed by the quantitative (MTT assay) and qualitative (FE-SEM) evaluation. The finding revealed the TiOH showed significantly higher induced human osteoblast cell adhesion and cytoplasmic expansion on the surface than the Ti control, indicating that the increasing of the roughness and hydrophilicity promoted cell adhesion behaviour. The enhancing of surface roughness, particularly nanoscale and microscale, that increases contact area for the cell attachment32. In addition, the previous study revealed that the rougher surface promoted higher cell spreading, indicating strong cellular adhesion on the surface through the actin-related mechanism33,34. Moreover, the hydrophilic surface of the implant material facilitated the adhesion-related protein adsorption to the surface and greater interaction between cell and implant surface, leading to improved cell adhesion35. Furthermore, the NaOH etching, plasma polymerization, and rhSLPI immobilization (TiOHS) exhibited the highest human osteoblast cell adhesion and cytoplasmic expansion on the surface due to the enhancing of surface roughness and hydrophilicity. Additionally, the previous studies revealed that titanium coating with SLPI protein demonstrated human osteoblast cell adhesion and cell spreading through the focal adhesion kinase (FAK) pathway and upregulated the adhesion molecule expression, including integrins 1, 2, and 514,36. Moreover, one of the factors that might enhance the human osteoblast cell adhesion was the plasma polymerization since its effect could provide the favourable surface for human osteoblast adhesion, including surface hydrophilicity and hydrocarbon decontamination37. From the finding, the alkaline etching, plasma polymerization, and rhSLPI immobilization revealed the potential for enhancing early human osteoblast cell adhesion and cytoplasmic expansion on the titanium surface. The improvement of cell adhesion behaviour on the titanium surface may translate to enhanced in vivo osseointegration.

The effect of the surface modification, NaOH etching, plasma polymerization, and rhSLPI immobilization on human osteoblast cell proliferation was revealed by MTT assay. The finding showed that the TiS, which involved immobilizing rhSLPI onto the smooth Ti, exhibited the highest cell proliferation at all time points. This finding is consistent with the previous studies that SLPI promoted osteoblast cell proliferation through the FAK-mediated signalling pathway14,36. In contrast, both alkaline-etched groups (TiOH and TiOHS) showed significantly lower cell proliferation compared to the control Ti. This finding indicated that the surface topography created by the NaOH-etching process plays a dominant role in modulating cell behaviour. This low proliferation on rough surfaces is revealed by the previous study, microtopography that mimics the natural extracellular matrix (ECM), resulting in the reduction of osteoblast cell proliferation and beginning to shift toward an osteogenic differentiation pathway38. Although the combined treatment of NaOH-etching and rhSLPI immobilization demonstrated low cell proliferation, this finding instead suggests an induction of cell differentiation.

To evaluate early cell-surface interactions, we employed a complementary approach utilizing both metabolic quantification and morphological observation. While the MTT assay is primarily a measure of metabolic activity, it was utilized here to provide a relative quantitative index of the number of cells successfully attached to the modified surfaces within the initial incubation period. Therefore, MTT assay might not the direct method to measuring cell adhesion. To address the inherent limitations of metabolic-based assays in assessing adhesion, FE-SEM was performed to provide direct evidence of cellular behaviour. The qualitative findings from FE-SEM, showing extensive cytoplasmic expansion and flattened morphology on the TiOHS group, closely corroborate the quantitative MTT results. This dual-modality validation ensures that the observed increase in signal represents not only a higher number of attached cells but also a superior quality of initial cell spreading and attachment.

To evaluate the late stage of bone differentiation, the mineral deposition was quantified by ARS staining after being cultured for 21 days in osteogenic differentiation media. The staining confirmed that combined treatment of alkaline etching, plasma polymerization, and rhSLPI immobilization (TiOHS) was the most effective strategy for promoting human osteoblast differentiation and mineralization. The improved performance of the alkaline-etched surface (TiOH) alone is consistent with previous findings, suggesting its porous, mesh-like structure successfully mimics the natural extracellular matrix, thereby enhancing bone formation10. Moreover, the rhSLPI immobilization (TiS) on the titanium surface demonstrated the enhancing of the differentiation, which is consistent with the previous studies14,15. As our previous studies revealed the biological mechanism of SLPI on osteoblast differentiation, in which is the upregulation of the expression of bone differentiation-related genes, including Alp, Col1, Runx2, Ocn, and Dmp-1, and the subsequent enhancement of ALP activity14,16. This finding suggests an additive effect between the bioactive signalling from the immobilization of the rhSLPI protein and the advantageous microtopography generated via alkaline etching. This combined biochemical and structural biofunctionalization significantly improves the osteogenic environment. Based on these promising results, the TiOHS surface is a suitable candidate for further in vivo development and has potential for application in advanced dental or orthopaedic implants.

The findings revealed the potential of the rhSLPI-immobilized TiOH (TiOHS) for further development in in vivo studies and real clinical dental and orthopaedic applications. Nonetheless, in this present study there are limitations since this study was entirely investigated in an in vitro model, which lacks the physiological environment in a patient’s body, including immune response, vascularization, and the underlying disease. Although rhSLPI was successfully immobilized and demonstrated a clear capability to enhance human osteoblast cell activities, its specific biological functions post-immobilization, such as antiprotease, anti-inflammatory, and anti-microbial activities, were not evaluated in the current scope. These functional assays will be a crucial focus of our future in vivo and advanced in vitro studies to fully confirm the retention of its biological activity and assess the clinical efficacy of the modified Ti. Moreover, the differentiation of human osteoblast cells on modified Ti was alone in evaluating the phenotypic effect that lacks the genotypic and signalling pathways that are involved in osteoblast behaviour. In addition, the long-term stability of the modified titanium surface was not evaluated, encompassing factors such as potential instability after implantation, sample shelf life, and storage conditions. However, the results from this study demonstrated the successful rhSLPI immobilization, which was verified through both ELISA and XPS. The biological efficacy was maintained throughout the 21-day differentiation period, as evidenced by the significantly higher mineral deposition in the TiOHS group. Furthermore, future studies are planned to specifically quantify the shelf-life and stability of these surfaces under physiological flow conditions. Nevertheless, the current study was focused on the physicochemical and biological effect of the modified samples; the mechanical properties, particular in real physiological conditions, were not determined, including the thickness and the bone strength of plasma polymerization and rhSLPI immobilization on the surface.

In conclusion, the immobilization of rhSLPI on alkaline-etched Ti via a multi-step functionalization approach, involving a plasma-polymerized interlayer and subsequent covalent coupling enhanced physicochemical and biofunctional properties, resulting in promoted human osteoblast cell adhesion and differentiation. This research provides a proof of concept for future in vivo studies and actual dental and orthopaedic implant clinical trials.

Materials and methods

The conceptual framework of the present study was separated into 3 parts (Fig. 8); the first part is the sample preparation step, including alkaline etching, plasma polymerization, and rhSLPI protein immobilization onto the titanium surface. Then the sample was characterized to evaluate the physicochemical property of the surface. Lastly, the human osteoblast cells were employed to reveal the biofunctionality of the modified titanium surface.

The experimental diagram. The grade 5 titanium was etched by NaOH solution to create the rough surface, followed by plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) to form an interlayer, UV-graft polymerization of PAAc, and EDC/NHS-mediated rhSLPI immobilization. The modified titanium was observed for physicochemical properties, including surface morphology, roughness, chemical composition, hydrophilicity, and protein coating. The human osteoblast cells were cultured on the titanium to evaluate cell adhesion, proliferation, and differentiation.

Chemicals and materials

Recombinant human secretory leukocyte protease inhibitor (rhSLPI) was purchased from Sino Biology Inc. (Beijing, China), and Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM): Ham’s F-12 media, foetal bovine serum (FBS), and trypsin-EDTA were purchased from Gibco BRL; Life Technologies Inc. (New York, USA).

The grade 5 titanium discs (Ti) were cut into 5.5 mm in diameter and 0.5 mm in thickness and 12 mm in diameter and 0.5 mm in thickness for the 96-well plate and 24-well plate, respectively.

Sample preparation and alkaline etching

The Ti discs were cleaned by ultrasonication in 70% ethanol and deionized (DI) water for 15 min at each step, repeated three times. The Ti surface roughness was performed by using the alkaline etching technique, following the protocol from the previous study39. Briefly, the Ti discs were submerged in a 5 M sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution at 60 °C for 24 h. After treatment, the NaOH-etched Ti (TiOH) discs were thoroughly rinsed using the same above cleaning procedure and sterilized by autoclaving.

Surface functionalization

The immobilization of rhSLPI, a multi-step surface functionalization protocol, was performed on both the Ti and TiOH. The process involved plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD), UV-graft polymerization, and carbodiimide chemistry. The current modification technique was modified from the previous study21. Initially, the titanium samples were placed in a PECVD reaction chamber. The chamber was then evacuated to a pressure below 30 mTorr. Then the functionalization was performed in two stages, which are deposition and activation. In the deposition stage, hexamethyldisilane (HMDS) monomers were fed into the chamber, and oxygen gas was used as a carrier gas, and the pressure was maintained at 150 mTorr. After the pressure stabilized, the gas was ignited by the power supply at 40 W for 10 min to deposit the plasma-polymerized layer on the surface. Following the deposition stage, the surface was activated by re-evacuating and feeding oxygen gas to a pressure of 150 mTorr. Then the plasma activation was activated by a 100 W of power for 5 min.

UV-graft polymerization and rhSLPI immobilization

The plasma-activated titanium (pTi) was polymerized by acrylic acid (AAc) for grafting the polyacrylic acid (PAAc), which is rich in carboxyl groups (-COOH), onto the surface. Firstly, the pTi was immersed in a cold graft polymerization solution containing 10% (v/v) acrylic acid, 1% (w/v) ammonium persulfate, and 1 mM vitamin B. The samples were then exposed to the UV irradiation (365 nm, 1000 W) for 30 min. After 30 min, the samples were rinsed with the DI water for 15 min. The PAAc-grafted Ti, carboxyl-group-rich surface was activated using carbodiimide chemistry. Then the PAAc-grafted Ti was immersed in a cross-linker solution containing 0.125 mg/mL 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl) carbodiimide hydrochloride (EDC) and 0.00315 mg/mL N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS) in 0.1 M MES buffer at 4˚C for 24 h. After incubation, the samples were incubated in 10 µg/mL rhSLPI solution overnight at 37˚C resulting in the amine groups of the protein binding with the surface. At the end of the incubation period, the rhSLPI-immobilized Ti (TiS) and rhSLPI-immobilized TiOH (TiOHS) were continuously determined by the physicochemical properties and the effect on human osteoblast cell activities.

The confirmation of rhSLPI immobilization

The immobilization of rhSLPI on the titanium surface was confirmed through a sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA, Abcam, UK). Following the immobilization of rhSLPI, the 5.5 mm Ti samples were washed three times with PBS and subsequently placed in the 96-well plate. The antibody cocktail was applied to the samples and incubated at room temperature for one hour. Subsequently, the samples underwent three washes with wash buffer, followed by the addition of the TMB development solution. After a ten-minute incubation period, the stop solution had been added into the wells. Absorbance was quantified at 450 nm using a microplate spectrophotometer.

The surface topology

The Ti was observed for the topology, including surface morphology and roughness. The surface morphology was determined by using a field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM, JSM-IT800; JEOL, Tokyo, Japan). Firstly, the 5.5 mm Ti samples were placed on the stub and then coated with gold for 30 s. Then the samples were observed under the FE-SEM at 20,000x magnification for the surface morphology. The surface roughness was determined by using a 3D laser scanning microscope (VK-X3000; Keyence, USA). The surface roughness was represented as average roughness (Pa).

The surface hydrophilicity

The hydrophilicity of the Ti was performed using a sessile drop method. A five-microliter droplet of deionized water was dropped onto the surface of a 12 mm titanium sample. The droplet was subsequently photographed, and the contact angle was analysed using the IC 2.0 Measure program.

The surface chemical property

The X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was performed for surface chemical composition on the Ti surface. The XPS measurements of the scanned areas were at 100 × 100 μm, the pass energy was 224.0 eV, and the binding energy range is 0–600 eV.

Cell type and cell culture

The human foetal osteoblast 1.19 (hFOB 1.19) cell line was used, which was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC CRL-11372TM). Cells were cultured with a 1:1 mixture of Ham’s F12 medium and Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s medium containing 10% (v/v) foetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin without phenol red. The cell was cultured at 37˚C with 5% CO2 and 95% air.

The cytotoxicity on human osteoblast

The Ti samples were observed for the cytotoxic effect on the human osteoblast by using the MTT cell viability assay. The 5.5 mm Ti samples were placed into the 96-well plate, and the hFOB 1.19 was then seeded onto the Ti samples at the concentration of 2.5 × 105 cells/mL. After twenty-four hours of incubation, the culture media was discarded, and 0.5 mg/mL MTT solution was added to the wells and incubated for 2 h at 37˚C. Then the MTT solution was removed, and DMSO was added to dissolve the formazan crystal. The relative cell viability was measured by using an O.D.570 microplate spectrophotometer.

The adhesion effect on human osteoblast

The adhesion assay was performed for the number and the morphology of attached cells. The MTT assay was used to determine the number of attached cells on the Ti surface. Firstly, the 5.5 mm Ti samples were placed into the 96-well plate, and then 2.5 × 105 cells/mL hFOB 1.19 cells were seeded onto the surface. The cells were incubated at 37˚C, with 5% CO2 and 95% air, for 20 min. After incubation, the culture media was discarded and incubated with fresh media for 1 h to stabilize the cell on the surface. Finally, the number of attached cells was measured by MTT assay.

The morphology of attached cells was observed under FE-SEM. The protocol is similar to the above method of quantitative assay. However, after twenty minutes of incubation, the cells were fixed with 2.5% (v/v) glutaraldehyde for 30 min. Finally, the attached cells on the Ti surface were coated with gold, and the morphology was observed under FE-SEM.

The osteoblast proliferation

The cell proliferation assay was performed by using an MTT assay. Firstly, the Ti samples were placed into the 96-well plate, and the hFOB 1.19 cells were then seeded onto the samples at 2.5 × 104 cells/mL. The cells were incubated at 37˚C with 5% CO2 and 95% air for 3, 5, and 7 days. Post-incubation, the culture media was discarded, and the 0.5 mg/mL MTT solution was added. After 2-hour incubation, the MTT solution was removed, and the dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was added to dissolve the formazan. The absorbance was measured at O.D. 570 nm, and the results were represented as relative percent cell proliferation.

The osteoblast differentiation

The differentiation of osteoblasts was induced using osteogenic differentiation media (OM). Firstly, the 12 mm Ti discs were placed into a 24-well plate, and 1 × 105 cells/mL of hFOB 1.19 cells were seeded on Ti discs and cultured overnight at 37 ˚C with 5% CO2 and 95% air. Then the culture media was removed, and the OM (100 nM dexamethasone, 10 mM sodium β-glycerophosphate, and 100 µg/mL ascorbic acid) was added. The cells were cultured for 21 days and renewed with new osteogenic differentiation media every three days. After induction, the induced cells were further observed for mineralization, which is alizarin red S staining.

The mineralization staining

Post twenty-day induction, the Ti samples were moved to a new 24-well plate and fixed with 4% (v/v) paraformaldehyde for 15 min. Then the cells were stained with 2% (w/v) alizarin red S staining (ARS) solution for 20 min. After incubation, the solution was discarded, and the cells were washed with PBS. The Ti samples were air-dried, and a photo was taken. Finally, the strain was solubilized with a 10% (w/v) cetylpyridinium chloride monohydrate solution. The absorbance was measured at O.D. 570 nm by using a microplate spectrophotometer.

The statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using commercially available software (GraphPad Prism version 10, San Diego, CA, USA). To ensure data integrity, statistical outliers were identified and excluded if they fell outside the range of the mean 2SD. Consequently, the final sample size (n) varies slightly across experimental groups, as detailed in the corresponding figure legends. All data are expressed as mean SD. The normality was performed and verified by using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. All comparisons were assessed for significance using an unpaired t-test or ANOVA, followed by, when appropriate, the Tukey-Kramer test. A p-value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

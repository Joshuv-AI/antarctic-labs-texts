Extracellular vesicle-derived lncRNA-NEAT1 serves as potential biomarker for the early diagnosis of hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Chen Li, Qiong Wu, Han Hong, Li Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) ranks as the fourth leading cause of cancer-related mortality globally. Studies have confirmed that long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) derived from extracellular vesicles (EVs) have great potential in the early diagnosis of HCC. LncRNA nuclear enriched abundant transcript 1 (NEAT1) has been shown to be dysregulated in HCC; however, the expression level of EV-derived NEAT1 remains unclear. This study aims to investigate the expression of EV-derived NEAT1 in HCC and evaluate its diagnostic potential.MethodsThe level of EV-derived lncRNA NEAT1 was compared between 57 HCC patients and 32 controls with benign liver disease to investigate its potential as an HCC biomarkers. The diagnostic performance of the combination of NEAT1 and alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. Additionally, the correlation between the expression levels of this biomarker and clinicopathological characteristics was assessed, and its potential value in monitoring disease progression and predicting prognosis in HCC patients was investigated.ResultsThe expression level of EV-derived lncRNA from HCC patients was significantly upregulated compared to that in controls (P<0.0001). EV-derived NEAT1 demonstrated diagnostic significance for HCC, with an area under the curve (AUC) value of 0.718. The AUC increased to 0.814 when combining NEAT1 and AFP (sensitivity: 73.68%; specificity: 78.12%), which outperformed either biomarker alone. No significant correlations were observed between NEAT1 expression levels and either age or gender, but increased NEAT1 expression was associated with tumor diameter and vascular invasion in HCC patients.ConclusionsThere is a significant difference in the level of EV-derived NEAT1 between HCC patients and benign controls. EV-derived NEAT1 shows potential as a biomarker for HCC diagnosis and may serve as a complement to AFP. The combined use of EV-derived NEAT1 and AFP greatly improves the diagnostic efficiency of HCC.

Highlight box

Key findings

• Extracellular vesicle (EV)-derived nuclear enriched abundant transcript 1 (NEAT1) shows significant potential as a biomarker for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) diagnosis and may serve as a supplement to alpha-fetoprotein (AFP). The combined use of EV-derived NEAT1 and AFP greatly improves the diagnostic efficiency of HCC.

What is known and what is new?

• LncRNA NEAT1 has been shown to be significantly dysregulated in HCC tissues and cell lines; however, there are few studies on its detectability in EVs and its significance in the diagnosis of HCC.

• We verified the detectability of NEAT1 derived from EVs and found significant differences in EV-derived NEAT1 levels between HCC patients and benign controls. No significant correlations were found between NEAT1 expression levels and age or gender, but increased NEAT1 expression was associated with tumor diameter and vascular invasion in HCC patients.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• EV-derived NEAT1 can serve as a promising biomarker for the diagnosis of HCC, and the combination of EV-derived NEAT1 and AFP can greatly improve the diagnostic efficiency of HCC.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for 80–90% of all liver tumors and its incidence has been increasing in recent decades (1,2). HCC often lacks obvious symptoms until it reaches the middle or advanced stages, which makes early diagnosis crucial for effective treatment and improved survival rates (3). Histopathological examination is the gold standard for HCC diagnosis, but its invasive nature carries risks of hemorrhage and tissue damage. Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) is the most commonly used serological biomarker in clinical practice, but it has limited sensitivity for early-stage HCC detection, reducing its diagnostic utility (4). Although current imaging techniques like ultrasound and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) have improved HCC diagnostic accuracy but are limited by high cost, invasiveness, and poor sensitivity to small tumors (5). These critical limitations highlight the urgent need for innovative non-invasive biomarkers to enhance diagnostic precision and improve the clinical outcomes of HCC patients.

Liquid biopsy biomarkers are gaining prominence in the diagnosis of HCC owing to their convenience, inexpensiveness, noninvasiveness, and reproducibility (6). Among the various liquid biopsy approaches, analysis of extracellular vesicles (EVs) has emerged as a particularly promising strategy. The non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) contained within EVs have been proven to be stably and characteristically present in the blood, urine, and saliva of cancer patients and can be used as circulating tumor markers for the early diagnosis of cancer (7,8). Although there exist a diverse array of RNA types circulating in the bloodstream, EV-enclosed RNAs offer distinct advantages compared to cell-free RNAs in terms of their biomarker potential. This is mainly because they are protected within the lipid bilayer membrane of EVs, which enhances their stability and integrity (9). Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are a class of ncRNA longer than 200 nucleotides that play roles in tumorigenesis, angiogenesis, and cancer metastasis (10,11). Promisingly, the aberrant dysregulation of EV-enclosed lncRNAs in HCC, coupled with its non-invasive nature, is regarded as having the potential to become a budding star in the prediction and diagnosis of HCC.

During the occurrence and development of HCC, tumor cells undergo metabolic reprogramming to meet their growth requirements (12). Glycolysis and lipid metabolism are two key metabolic pathways in this process. Even under aerobic conditions, liver cancer cells preferentially generate ATP via glycolysis, a phenomenon known as the “Warburg effect” (13,14). Metabolic intermediates produced by glycolysis can be used to synthesize nucleotides and amino acids to meet the needs of rapid tumor cells proliferation (15). In addition, lactate produced by glycolysis can promote the invasion and metastasis of tumor cells and inhibit the function of immune cells by acidifying the tumor microenvironment (16). Lipid metabolism is also involved in the development of early-stage HCC, the tumor cells increase fatty acid synthesis and cholesterol production to provide the necessary lipid components for the growth of membrane structures (17). Meanwhile, the reprogramming of lipid metabolism can also provide signal molecules and energy reserves for tumor cells, supporting cell proliferation and survival (18). The abnormalities in glycolysis and lipid metabolism are not only important supportive pathways for the survival and proliferation of liver cancer cells, but also potential diagnostic and therapeutic targets (19,20). By investigating the key molecules involved in these metabolic pathways, we can potentially identify novel biomarkers for early diagnosis, thereby providing innovative approaches for the early detection and treatment of liver cancer.

Nuclear enriched abundant transcript 1 (NEAT1), a long non-coding RNA, has been demonstrated to interact with glycolysis-related enzymes to promote glycolysis, then facilitating the growth, proliferation, invasion and metastasis of tumor cells (21,22). Meanwhile, NEAT1 can also regulate miR-30a, influencing the expression of COX-2, thus promoting lipid metabolism (23). Studies have confirmed that NEAT1 is abnormally highly expressed in tumor cells and tissues, and its high expression is associated with the invasiveness, metastasis and poor prognosis of the tumors (24). Therefore, in this study, we concentrated on the expression level of EV-derived NEAT1 in patients with HCC, clarified the relationship between its expression and clinical pathological characteristics to evaluate the diagnostic potential of this biomarker in early detection. Additionally, we investigated whether the combined utilization of NEAT1 and AFP is superior to the use of either biomarker alone. We present this article in accordance with the STARD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-aw-2523/rc).

Methods

Patients characteristics

From March 2023 to February 2024, this study recruited 57 patients diagnosed with HCC, as well as 32 individuals with benign liver disease (including 15 with hepatitis B virus infection, 10 with alcoholic liver disease, and 7 with fatty liver disease), who came to the hospital for examinations. Demographic data, including age and gender; clinical manifestations, including the number/diameter of tumors and tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage; and laboratory values, including AFP, alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST), total bilirubin (TB), and direct bilirubin (DB) levels, were obtained and documented. Subjects were excluded if they had diabetes, renal failure, cardiovascular disease, other malignancies (except HCC), or had received any treatment or chemotherapy. The sample size for calculating the area under the ROC curve was determined using the MedCalc 20.0 software (MedCalc Software Ltd., Belgium). Assuming a minimum acceptable area under the ROC curve of 0.7, an area under the ROC curve of 0.5 under the null hypothesis, and a group ratio of 1:2 for negative to positive samples, we calculated a total sample size of 89 at α=0.05 and 90% power. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Review Board of Affiliated Suzhou Hospital of Nanjing Medical University (Suzhou, China) (No. K-2023-028-K01). Oral informed consent for publication was obtained from all participants.

Blood sampling

Five milliliters of whole blood samples were withdrawn from HCC patients and benign individuals using ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) tubes (BD Company, Bergen, USA), and then centrifuged at 3,000 rpm for 10 minutes to obtain plasma samples. Plasma samples were divided into portions and kept at −80 ℃ prior to use.

EVs isolation and characterization

EVs were isolated using the polyethylene glycol (PEG) precipitation method. Firstly, PEG4000 was dissolved in 1 M sodium chloride solution to prepare a 30% PEG4000 solution. Subsequently, the plasma samples were subjected to centrifugation at 2,000 ×g for 10 minutes at 4 ℃, and then further centrifuged at 10,000 ×g for 30 minutes to eliminate cellular debris and apoptotic bodies. The supernatant was gently mixed with the 30% PEG4000 solution at a ratio of 2:1 and incubated at 4 ℃ for 3 hours. Finally, the EV-containing precipitate was collected through centrifugation at 3,000 ×g for 15 minutes. The EVs were characterized using a nanoparticle tracking analyzer (Particle Metrix, USA) and examined via scanning electron microscopy (Hitachi, Japan).

EV-derived NEAT1 expression analysis by reverse transcription‑quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT‑qPCR)

The total RNA was then extracted from the EV samples using TRIzol reagent (Takara, Tokyo, Japan), which helps prevent DNA and protein contamination, and RNA quality was assessed using the NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA). The relative expression levels of NEAT1 were determined using a Custom Gene qRT-PCR Quantitation Kit (Genepharma, Shanghai, China). The primers and probes used for detecting NEAT1 and GAPDH [as an internal reference (25)] were designed and purchased from Genepharma. Quantitative PCR was performed using an AB7500 thermocycler (Applied Biosystems, Foster, CA, USA) with TaqMan Universal PCR Master Mix. The thermal cycling protocol consisted of an initial predenaturation step at 95 ℃ for 3 min, followed by 40 amplification cycles, each including denaturation (95 ℃ for 12 s) and annealing/extension (62 ℃ for 40 s). The relative expression of targets was determined using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Table 1 shows the sequences of the primers and probes used.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve

The ROC curve, which graphs sensitivity on the y-axis and 1-specificity or false positive rate (FPR) on the x-axis, serves as a statistical method for assessing the discriminatory ability of a binary diagnostic test (26). The area under the ROC curve usually falls between 0.5 and 1, and values closer to 1 indicate superior diagnostic accuracy.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using version 27.0 of the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) and GraphPad Prism version 8.0. The data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (mean ± SEM). To assess differences in expression between the HCC and control groups, Student’s t-test was employed. The Whitney U test was used to analyze non-normally distributed data. Pearson correlation analysis was used to evaluate associations between variables, while ROC curve analysis was applied to determine the diagnostic performance of EV-derived NEAT1 and AFP in HCC. Diagnostic performance indices, including the area under the ROC curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, and cut-off values, were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics software. Statistical significance was defined at a P value of below 0.05 and asterisks indicating significant differences (*P<0.05, **P<0.01, ***P<0.001, ****P<0.0001, ns P≥0.05).

Results

Description of subjects

Table 2 shows the demographic and clinical features of the study participants. The cohort included 89 individuals, consisting of 32 benign controls (18 males and 14 females) and 57 HCC patients (46 males and 11 females), with ages ranging from 30 to 79 years. Among the HCC patients, 38 cases were in the early stage (stage I + II), and 19 cases were in stages III and IV. According to the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) staging system, among these 57 patients with HCC, 6 cases were classified as stage BCLC 0 and 23 cases were classified as stage BCLC A. With regard to age and gender, the benign controls and HCC patients were statistically matched (P>0.05). Interpretation of the clinical data revealed that serum levels of ALT and AST, as well as TB, DB, and AFP, were markedly elevated in the HCC group compared to the benign controls, with statistically significant differences (P<0.05).

The expression of EV-derived NEAT1 in multiple cancers

ExoRBase 3.0 is an online database that can serves as a repository of messenger RNA (mRNA), long non-coding RNA (lncRNA), and circular RNA (circRNA). These RNAs were derived from RNA-seq data analysis of extracellular vesicles and particles (EVPs) and cell-free RNAs (cfRNAs) distributed in different human body fluids, such as blood, urine, cerebrospinal fluid, and bile. It facilitates the identification of novel extracellular long RNA (exLR) signatures from human body fluids and aids in the discovery of new circulating biomarkers to improve tumor diagnosis and therapy. By querying the database, we summarized the expression levels of NEAT1 in EVs from benign controls and several cancer patients (Figure 1A). The findings revealed a marked increase in NEAT1 levels within plasma EVs in HCC patients compared to those with benign conditions (P<0.001, Figure 1B). Additionally, NEAT1 expression in plasma EVs from HCC individuals was significantly elevated relative to cfRNAs (P<0.001, Figure 1C).

Characteristics of extracted EVs

After EVs were isolated from plasma, their morphology and structure were observed using transmission electron microscopy (TEM). TEM analysis revealed that the extracted EVs exhibited a typical bilayer membrane structure, consistent with the known characteristics of EVs (Figure 2A). Nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) further confirmed that the majority of EVs ranged in size from 30 to 250 nm, with a main peak and an average particle size of 99.3 nm, which falls within the established size range of EVs (Figure 2B). Collectively, these findings indicate that EVs were successfully isolated.

Upregulation of EV-derived NEAT1 in HCC patients

To explore the potential of EV-derived NEAT1 as a biomarker for HCC diagnosis, we conducted qPCR assays to measure NEAT1expression levels in EVs from HCC patients and benign controls. The results demonstrated a marked increase in NEAT1 expression levels in HCC patients relative to the control group (Figure 3A, P<0.001). In addition, we evaluated the association of NEAT1 levels in EVs with gender and age. The results showed no significant difference in NEAT1 expression between males and females (Figure 3B), and age did not show a linear correlation with relative NEAT1 levels (Figure 3C). We also analyzed the differences in NEAT1 levels among HCC patients with different TNM stages, different tumor diameters, with or without vascular invasion, and with single or multiple tumors. The results showed that the EV-derived NEAT1 level in stage III/IV HCC patients was higher than that in those stage I/II, and the difference was statistically significant (P<0.05, Figure 3D); the EV-derived NEAT1 level in HCC patients whose tumor diameter was >5 cm was higher than that in those with a tumor diameter ≤5 cm, but the difference was not statistically significant (P>0.05, Figure 3E); the EV-derived NEAT1 level in HCC patients with vascular invasion was higher than that in those without vascular invasion, and the difference was statistically significant (P<0.05, Figure 3F).

Diagnostic performance in HCC patients with EV-derived NEAT1 and AFP levels

Analysis of the ROC curve demonstrated that both NEAT1 and AFP levels could effectively distinguish the HCC group from benign controls, yielding an AUC of 0.718 for NEAT1 (sensitivity =64.91%, specificity =68.75%, P<0.001; Figure 4A) and an AUC of 0.699 for AFP (sensitivity =49.12%, specificity =90.62%, P<0.001; Figure 4B). To evaluate whether the combination of EV-derived NEAT1 and AFP outperformed each biomarker individually, a new predicted probability (p) variable for HCC was constructed based on the equation derived from binary logistic regression. The model combining AFP and NEAT1 showed better predictive performance (AUC =0.814, sensitivity 73.68%, specificity 78.12%), outperforming either EV-derived NEAT1 or AFP used individually (Figure 4C).

Discussion

HCC is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Current diagnostic tools for HCC, including medical imaging, serum AFP levels, and tissue biopsies, have notable limitations, such as invasiveness and low sensitivity. The abundance of EV-lncRNA in different body fluids of patients, especially blood, is characterized by differential expression across different disease stages. A large body of research has demonstrated that EV-derived lncRNAs play pivotal roles in various cellular and molecular processes associated with cancer, highlighting their potential as novel diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets (27,28). In our study, the expression levels of EV-derived lncRNA NEAT1 in HCC patients was significantly higher compared with those in benign controls. ROC curve analysis showed that for comparison between HCC and benign controls, the AUC of EV-derived NEAT1 combined with AFP was 0.814, the sensitivity was 73.68% and specificity was 78.12%. EV-derived NEAT1 alone was superior to AFP, and their combined efficacy was better. EV-derived NEAT1 may serve as a potential biomarker for diagnosis of HCC.

The lncRNA NEAT1 is well-recognized to be significantly dysregulated in numerous cancers. In the majority of cases, it is associated with aggressive disease and poor outcomes. Cancer cells exhibit distinct metabolic patterns compared to normal cells. Metabolic reprogramming is an important hallmark of liver carcinogenesis and tumor progression (29,30). Abnormal glycolysis and lipid metabolism are important supporting for HCC cell survival and proliferation, and are also potential diagnostic and therapeutic targets. In the cytoplasm, NEAT1 interacts with the glycolytic enzymes phosphoglycerate kinase, phosphoglycerate mutase, and alpha-enolase, which in turn promotes glycolysis and subsequently facilitates cell growth, proliferation, invasion, and metastasis. This suggests that NEAT1 may serve as a crucial target for metabolic reprogramming and is expected to be a potential biomarker for the prediction of early-stage HCC. NEAT1 can affect the chemotherapy resistance of cancer by promoting aerobic glycolysis (31). Research has confirmed that the NEAT1/hsa-miR-372-3p axis is involved in the regulation of lipid metabolism (32). Therefore, this study aims to focus on the metabolism-related lncRNA NEAT1, which has been found to be abnormally expressed in HCC tissues and cell lines (33). Nevertheless, limited research has explored whether NEAT1 can be detected in EVs or examined its potential as diagnostic biomarker in HCC. It is very important to determine the detectability of EV-derived NEAT1. Following the isolation and purification of EVs, characterization analyses revealed vesicles exhibiting a disc-like shape and ranging in size from about 30 to 250 nm, consistent with the conventional morphological features of EVs (34). Then, the RT-qPCR results demonstrated that, compared to controls, the expression of EV-derived NEAT1 was significantly upregulated in HCC samples (P<0.0001), which was consistent with those obtained from the ExoRBase 3.0 database query (35). We found no correlation between EV-derived NEAT1 levels and age or gender. Analysis across clinical parameters showed that EV-derived NEAT1 levels were higher in patients with TNM stage III/IV, tumor diameter >5 cm, or vascular invasion. Consistent with Wang et al.’s findings, upregulation of NEAT1 was associated with larger tumor diameter and vascular invasion in HCC patients (36). However, no significant difference was observed in EV-derived NEAT1 levels between patients with tumor diameter >5 cm and those with tumor diameter ≤5 cm (P>0.05), which we speculated may be attributable to the insufficient sample size included in this study. A meta-analysis indicates that the elevated expression of NEAT1 can serve as a biomarker for poor prognosis in patients with digestive system tumors (37). Patients exhibiting higher levels of NEAT1 expression tend to have a shorter survival time.

The intercellular communication through exosomes to transfer biological information to neighboring cells not only maintains physiological functions but also participates in the occurrence and development of liver diseases (38). Non-coding RNAs associated with exosomes have the potential to serve as novel diagnostic biomarkers and new therapeutic targets for liver diseases. This is because the alterations in non-coding RNAs associated with exosomes can reflect the underlying status of liver diseases (39). Multiple studies have shown that lncRNA NEAT1 can promote the malignant behavior of HCC cells, while silencing NEAT1 can inhibit this behavior, suggesting that NEAT1 may function as an oncogene (40). Pan et al.’s findings confirmed that lncRNA NEAT1 can enhance the proliferation and metastasis of HCC through modulation of the FOXP3/PKM2 signaling pathway (41). Another study indicated that NEAT1 suppressed HCC progression by targeting miR-320a/LAGE3 axis (42). NEAT1 mainly functions as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) to sponge various miRNAs to regulate different functions.

Regarding liver cancers, increased NEAT1 expression has been associated with cancer cell proliferation, migration, angiogenesis, apoptosis, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) (43). Moreover, increased expression of NEAT1 has been implicated in the development of various liver disorders, including hepatic injury and fibrosis (44). Our findings demonstrated the expression of NEAT1 was positively correlated with the liver function tests, suggesting that it might play a role in maintaining normal liver function. In the HCC group, a positive correlation was observed between the expression of NEAT1 and the serum level of ALT and AST. This is consistent with other studies that concluded using siRNA to target lncRNA NEAT1 expression markedly decreased while enhancing liver function, decreasing lipid levels, successfully regulating inflammatory responses, preventing hepatocyte apoptosis, and slowing the development of liver fibrosis (24,45). Furthermore, in this study, the level of NEAT1 exhibited a positively correlation with AFP, which further supports the tumor-promoting potential of lncRNA NEAT1.

ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate the diagnostic value of EV-associated NEAT1. The results showed that NEAT1 exhibited a certain discriminatory ability between HCC patients and benign controls, with an AUC of 0.718. It achieved a sensitivity of 64.91% and a specificity of 68.75%, indicating its potential as a valuable biomarker for HCC detection. The AUC for AFP was somewhat lower (0.699), with a specificity of approximately 90.62% and a sensitivity below 50% at the clinically conventional cutoff value of 28 ng/mL (46). Our data align with other studies, showing that as many as half of HCC cases have normal AFP levels. AFP is frequently employed as a biomarker for HCC diagnosis; nonetheless, it has a high false-negative rate when identifying small and early-stage tumors (47,48). Moreover, elevated AFP levels can also occur in some benign liver diseases, including chronic hepatitis and cirrhosis without HCC (49). Our data also show that about 13% of benign control patients had AFP levels exceeding 28 ng/mL. As a result, the application of AFP in the early screening of HCC has remained a subject of debate. Using logistic regression, we found that combining EV-associated NEAT1 with AFP effectively distinguished HCC patients from benign controls, achieving an AUC of 0.814, which was substantially higher than that of AFP alone. This might suggest that EV-derived NEAT1 could serve as a supplement to AFP in the clinical diagnosis of HCC.

However, there are some limitations in this study. Firstly, the sample size was relatively small, and there was a notable lack of healthy control samples. Since the normal control group provides important baseline data, the absence of a normal control group in the detection of diagnostic markers may have a negative impact on the accuracy of research results and clinical decision-making. Thus, large-scale studies incorporating healthy controls are essential to validate the findings. Secondly, the underlying biological mechanisms of the tumor demand further in-depth investigation. Thirdly, long-term follow-up was not carried out to dynamically monitor the correlation between EV-derived NEAT1 changes post-HCC treatment and prognosis.

Conclusions

This study confirmed that the expression level of lncRNA-NEAT1 in EVs from HCC patients were markedly elevated compared to those in the benign control group. Increased expression of EV-derived NEAT1 was associated with tumor diameter and vascular invasion in HCC patients. ROC curve analysis revealed that EV-derived NEAT1 holds promise as a biomarker for distinguishing HCC cases from benign liver conditions. Further logistic regression analysis indicates that combining EV-derived NEAT1 with AFP enhances diagnostic accuracy and sensitivity more effectively than either marker alone. These findings suggest EV-derived NEAT1 as a novel, non-invasive, economical and complementary biomarker, potentially improving the current diagnostic performance of AFP in HCC detection.

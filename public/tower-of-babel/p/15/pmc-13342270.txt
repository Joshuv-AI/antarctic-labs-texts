OLGA and OLGIM Stages for Predicting Metachronous Gastric Neoplasm After Endoscopic Submucosal Dissection in Patients With Early Gastric Cancer Without a Current Helicobacter pylori Infection Status
Authors: Young-Il Kim, Myeong-Cherl Kook, Jong Yeul Lee, Chan Gyoo Kim, Il Ju Choi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeThe operative link on gastritis assessment (OLGA) and operative link on gastritis assessment based on intestinal metaplasia (OLGIM) are established tools for stratifying the risk of gastric neoplasm development. This study evaluated whether OLGA and OLGIM staging can predict metachronous gastric neoplasms (adenoma or cancer) after endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) in patients with early gastric cancer (EGC) without current Helicobacter pylori infection.Materials and MethodsThis retrospective cohort study enrolled 494 patients with EGC without current H. pylori infection who underwent ESD between 2004 and 2015. The primary endpoint was the incidence of metachronous gastric neoplasms. High risk was defined as OLGA or OLGIM stage III–IV.ResultsOf the included patients, 55.4% (250/451) and 55.9% (276/494) were classified as high risk according to OLGA and OLGIM staging, respectively. During a median follow-up of 6.8 years, the incidence of metachronous gastric neoplasm was 26.6 cases per 1,000 person-years. According to OLGA staging, the high-risk group had a significantly higher incidence of metachronous neoplasms than the low-risk group (33.3 vs. 17.8 cases per 1,000 person-years, log-rank test P=0.012). Similarly, according to OLGIM staging, the high-risk group demonstrated a significantly higher incidence than the low-risk group (35.2 vs. 16.6 cases per 1,000 person-years, P=0.002).ConclusionsIn patients with EGC without current H. pylori infection, OLGA and OLGIM staging are useful for assessing the risk of metachronous gastric. The current 5-year endoscopic surveillance interval after ESD may be further optimized according to patient risk stratified by OLGA or OLGIM stages.

INTRODUCTION

Endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) is a minimally invasive treatment for early gastric cancer (EGC) in patients with a markedly low risk of lymph node metastasis [12]. Although ESD preserves the stomach, the risk of metachronous gastric cancer remains substantial, with an annual incidence of approximately 3% [34]. Our previous trial demonstrated that successful Helicobacter pylori eradication reduced the risk of metachronous gastric cancer after ESD by 68% in patients with EGC [5]. Gastric premalignant lesions, particularly gastric atrophy and intestinal metaplasia, are important risk factors for metachronous gastric cancer after ESD [6], and moderate to severe gastric corpus atrophy is observed in approximately 70%–80% of patients with EGC [57]. Moreover, even after successful H. pylori eradication, metachronous gastric cancer continues to occur at a relatively high incidence of 9.1–29.9 cases per 1,000 person-years (PYS) [458]. These findings suggest that further risk stratification is needed to guide appropriate post-ESD surveillance in patients with prior H. pylori eradication or without current H. pylori infection.

The operative link on gastritis assessment (OLGA) and operative link on gastritis assessment based on intestinal metaplasia (OLGIM) are staging systems that evaluate the severity and extent of gastric atrophy and intestinal metaplasia and have been proposed as tools for stratifying gastric cancer risk [910]. A meta-analysis of 6 case-control studies and 2 cohort studies reported that OLGA or OLGIM stage III/IV was significantly associated with an increased risk of gastric cancer [11]. In addition, 2 prospective cohort studies demonstrated a significant association between OLGA stages III/IV and subsequent gastric neoplasia in patients with dyspepsia [1213]. A recent study of patients with EGC who underwent ESD similarly suggested that the risk of metachronous gastric cancer increased with higher OLGA or OLGIM stages [14]. However, those studies included patients with positive H. pylori infection status (25.9%–58.5%) [121314], and the predictive value of OLGA or OLGIM staging may therefore have been influenced by the presence of H. pylori infection.

In this study, we investigated the incidence of metachronous gastric neoplasm after ESD according to OLGA and OLGIM stages in patients with EGC who had undergone H. pylori eradication or had no current H. pylori infection.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Patients

This retrospective cohort study included patients who underwent ESD for EGC between 2004 and 2015 at the National Cancer Center of Korea. Eligible patients were those who had previously undergone successful H. pylori eradication or those without current infection, as confirmed by H. pylori evaluation using the rapid urease test (RUT) and histological evaluations of endoscopic biopsy specimens obtained from three predetermined sites: the antrum lesser curvature (LC), corpus LC, and corpus greater curvature (GC). Additional inclusion criteria were as follows: 1) age ≥19 years and 2) EGC meeting the criteria for curative resection by ESD on final pathological evaluation. Patients were excluded if they had persistent positive H. pylori infection during follow-up, underwent additional surgery because of non-curative resection after ESD, participated in a randomized controlled trial investigating the effect of H. pylori treatment on metachronous gastric cancer, lacked complete histological data required for OLGA or OLGIM staging, or had a follow-up period of <1 year after confirmation of the absence of current H. pylori infection.

Baseline demographic and clinical data, including age, sex, alcohol consumption, smoking status, comorbidities, and tumor characteristics (size, location, histology, and presence of multiple initial tumors), were collected. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the National Cancer Center (NCCNHS03024). The requirement for informed consent was waived because the study posed minimal risk to the patients.

ESD procedures

All patients with gastric cancer underwent esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) and abdominal computed tomography (CT). In patients with EGC, ESD was performed for tumors meeting the following indications: 1) mucosal tumor ≤2 cm in size, differentiated histologic type, without ulcer findings; 2) mucosal tumor >2 cm in size, differentiated histologic type, without ulcer findings; 3) mucosal tumor ≤3 cm in size, differentiated histologic type, with ulcer findings; and 4) mucosal tumor ≤2 cm in size, undifferentiated histologic type, without ulcer findings [1]. All ESD procedures were performed by experienced endoscopists, and the detailed procedure has been described previously [15].

On final pathological evaluation of the resected ESD specimen, curative resection was defined as resection of tumors with negative resection margins and no lymphovascular invasion. An additional category of curative resection included tumors with submucosal invasion of <500 μm, differentiated histologic type, and size ≤3 cm [1].

H. pylori evaluation and assessment of OLGA and OLGIM stages

Biopsy specimens from the antrum LC, corpus LC, and corpus GC were obtained during the initial diagnostic EGD to evaluate H. pylori status, gastric atrophy, and intestinal metaplasia. An additional GC biopsy specimen was used for RUT. Histological assessment with hematoxylin and eosin staining and Wright–Giemsa staining was performed for all specimens. The absence of H. pylori infection was defined by negative results on all tests.

The grades of H. pylori density, atrophy, and intestinal metaplasia were evaluated according to the Updated Sydney system (0 = absent, 1 = mild, 2 = moderate, and 3 = marked) [16]. OLGA and OLGIM stages were determined by combining the grades of gastric atrophy and intestinal metaplasia from the antrum LC and corpus LC [910]. Patients with OLGA or OLGIM stage III–IV were categorized as high risk.

Post-ESD follow-up

After confirmation of curative resection on final pathological evaluation, surveillance EGD was performed every 6 months for 24 months and annually thereafter. Abdominal CT was performed annually after ESD. Biopsy specimens were obtained from the ESD site and from suspected recurrent lesions in the remnant stomach during surveillance EGD. Follow-up biopsy specimens from the three predetermined sites were obtained for reassessment of OLGA and OLGIM stages and were performed at least 6 months after H. pylori treatment in patients with initially positive H. pylori infection.

Study outcomes

The primary outcome was the incidence of metachronous gastric neoplasm, including adenoma (dysplasia) and gastric cancer, occurring at least 1 year after ESD. In patients with initially positive H. pylori infection, follow-up began on the date when the absence of current H. pylori infection was confirmed. The secondary outcome was identification of risk factors associated with metachronous gastric neoplasms. Gastric neoplasms included non-invasive low-grade neoplasm (category 3), non-invasive high-grade neoplasm (category 4), and invasive neoplasm (category 5) according to the Vienna classification of gastrointestinal epithelial neoplasia [17]. In this study, gastric cancer included category 4.3 (suspicion of invasive carcinoma) or higher, including category 5.1 (intramucosal carcinoma) and category 5.2 (submucosal carcinoma or beyond), whereas adenomas included categories 3–4.2, including category 4.1 (high-grade adenoma/dysplasia) and category 4.2 (non-invasive carcinoma/carcinoma in situ).

Statistical analyses

Patient characteristics were expressed as percentages (%), medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), or means with standard deviations, as appropriate. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate incidence curves for metachronous gastric neoplasms according to OLGA and OLGIM stages. The Cox proportional hazards model was used to evaluate risk factors associated with metachronous gastric neoplasms. Variables with a P-value <0.05 in univariate analyses were included as covariates in the multivariable analysis. All statistical analyses were performed using STATA software (version 17.0; StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA). A P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Patient and baseline characteristics

A total of 1,498 patients with EGC who underwent ESD between 2004 and 2015 were included in this study (Fig. 1). Among them, 511 had negative H. pylori test results confirmed by both RUT and histological evaluation of biopsy specimens obtained from the three predetermined sites. After excluding 17 patients with follow-up periods of less than 1 year, 494 patients were included in the final analyses. The median follow-up period was 6.8 years (IQR, 4.7–10.9 years).

OLGA staging was available for 451 patients (Table 2), of whom 250 (55.4%) were classified as high risk (stages III or IV). OLGIM staging was assessable in all 494 patients, and 276 (55.9%) were categorized as high risk.

Incidence of metachronous gastric neoplasm

During the median follow-up period of 6.8 years, 53 patients developed metachronous gastric cancer and 49 developed metachronous adenoma. The overall incidence of metachronous gastric neoplasms, including cancer and adenoma, was 26.6 cases per 1,000 PYS.

The incidence of metachronous gastric neoplasm increased significantly with advancing OLGA stage (P for trend=0.001; Fig. 2A). Patients in the high-risk OLGA group (stages III/IV) had a significantly higher incidence than those in the low-risk group (stage 0–II) (33.3 vs. 17.8 cases per 1,000 PYS, log-rank test P=0.012; Fig. 2B).

Risk factors for metachronous gastric neoplasm

Significant factors associated with metachronous gastric neoplasm included age ≥65 years, multiple initial tumors, OLGA stage IV, and OLGIM stages II–IV. Both the high-risk OLGA and OLGIM groups were also significantly associated with metachronous gastric neoplasms (Supplementary Table 2).

In a multivariate analysis according to OLGA stage, age ≥65 years (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR], 1.84; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.16–2.91) and OLGA stage IV (aHR, 3.66; 95% CI, 1.11–12.08) remained significant risk factors for metachronous gastric neoplasms (Table 3). Additionally, OLGA high-risk group was a significant factor for the metachronous gastric neoplasm (aHR, 1.69; 95% CI, 1.05–2.73).

DISCUSSION

This retrospective cohort study evaluated the clinical utility of the OLGA and OLGIM staging systems for assessing the risk of metachronous gastric neoplasm after ESD in patients with EGC without current H. pylori infection. During a median follow-up of 6.8 years after confirmation of the absence of current H. pylori infection, the incidence of metachronous gastric neoplasms increased with advancing OLGA and OLGIM stages. In addition, classification in the high-risk group according to either OLGA or OLGIM stage was independently associated with metachronous gastric neoplasm.

In patients with EGC who undergo ESD, H. pylori eradication reduces the risk of metachronous gastric cancer and is strongly recommended for its prevention [181920]. In prospective studies involving patients with premalignant lesions or dyspepsia, OLGA or OLGIM stages were downstaged in 37%–42% of H. pylori-negative or eradicated patients, and 46%–65% of patients in the high-risk group were reclassified to the low-risk group at the last surveillance endoscopy [132122]. In addition, H. pylori eradication improved the grades of gastric atrophy and intestinal metaplasia after ESD for EGC [5]. To minimize the influence of H. pylori infection on gastric atrophy and intestinal metaplasia, assessment of OLGA or OLGIM stage as a marker of gastric cancer risk after curative ESD for EGC should be performed after the absence of current H. pylori infection has been confirmed. In a previous retrospective study, patients with EGC and OLGA or OLGIM stages II–IV had a significantly higher risk of metachronous gastric cancer after ESD than those with stage 0–I [14]. However, 58.5% of patients in that study had positive H. pylori infection [14], and the potential influence of H. pylori infection on metachronous gastric cancer risk and gastric atrophy was not excluded. In addition, the assessment of gastric atrophy may have been understaged, as the proportion of patients classified as high risk by OLGA (19%) was only approximately half that classified as high risk by OLGIM (37%).14 Unlike the previous study, our findings showed that high-risk OLGA and OLGIM stages (III/IV) were the main predictors of metachronous gastric neoplasm after ESD in patients with EGC without current H. pylori infection.

Surveillance endoscopy at 6- to 12-month intervals for up to 5 years after curative ESD is recommended for patients with EGC [22324]. Beyond 5 years, continued endoscopic follow-up is recommended because the risk of metachronous gastric cancer persists; however, standard strategies, including the optimal surveillance interval and duration, remain undefined [22324]. In our study, the incidence of metachronous gastric neoplasm in the high-risk group was 33.3 cases per 1,000 PYS for OLGA stage III/IV and 35.2 cases per 1,000 PYS for OLGIM stage III/IV. These rates were significantly higher than those observed in the corresponding low-risk groups (17.8 cases per 1,000 PYS by OLGA stage and 16.6 cases per 1,000 PYS by OLGIM stage). These findings suggest that different endoscopic surveillance intervals after 5 years may be considered according to OLGA- or OLGIM-based risk stratification. However, whether the surveillance endoscopic interval can be extended beyond 1 year in the low-risk group remains uncertain. Although the incidence of metachronous gastric neoplasm was lower in the low-risk group than in the high-risk group, the incidence remained substantial in patients with low-risk OLGA and OLGIM stages I and II. In a previous meta-analysis, these stages were categorized as an intermediate gastric cancer incidence group, with rates ranging from 3.14 to 29.4 cases per 1,000 PYS [25]. In contrast, patients with OLGIM stage 0 had the lowest incidence of metachronous gastric neoplasm (2.5 cases per 1,000 PYS). This low incidence raises the possibility that surveillance endoscopy intervals may be extended in patients with OLGIM stage 0. Nevertheless, further studies are warranted to determine appropriate endoscopic surveillance intervals beyond 5 years after ESD, particularly for patients in the low-risk group with stage I or II disease.

Better inter-observer agreement among pathologists has been reported for grading intestinal metaplasia than for grading gastric atrophy [102627]. In contrast, one study found lower discrepancy between general and expert pathologists in the assessment of gastric atrophy than in the assessment of intestinal metaplasia [27]. Thus, the OLGIM stage has generally been regarded as the preferred system for risk stratification of gastric neoplasms over the OLGA stage [102122]. Our findings also suggested that OLGIM performed better than OLGA in assessing the risk of metachronous gastric neoplasm and gastric cancer.

The strengths of this study include the evaluation of both OLGA and OLGIM stages in relation to metachronous gastric neoplasm risk after ESD in patients with EGC without current H. pylori infection, together with a long-term follow-up (median 6.8 years). However, some limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center study conducted at a specialized gastric cancer center. In addition, all participants had EGC and were therefore already at high risk for gastric cancer. Accordingly, the generalizability of these findings to other populations may be limited. Second, the retrospective design made selection bias unavoidable. Third, tests other than histological examination, such as the stool antigen test, were not performed, although serum H. pylori IgG testing was available in a subset of patients. Despite the use of both RUT and histological evaluation with hematoxylin and eosin and Wright–Giemsa staining from 3 predetermined biopsy sites, false-negative H. pylori results cannot be completely ruled out. Fourth, potential heterogeneity within the cohort is another limitation. The study population likely included both truly H. pylori-negative patients and patients with prior infections. Although differences in baseline characteristics and clinical outcomes between these groups may exist, such comparisons were limited by the small number of patients suspected to be truly H. pylori-negative (2.8%).

In conclusion, OLGA and OLGIM stages are useful for assessing the risk of metachronous gastric neoplasms after confirming the absence of current H. pylori infection in patients with EGC who undergo ESD. In patients classified as high risk (stage III/IV), long-term annual endoscopic surveillance beyond 5 years should be considered. In contrast, for those in the low-risk group (stage 0–II), further studies are needed to determine whether surveillance intervals can be extended beyond 1 year after 5 years of follow-up following curative ESD.

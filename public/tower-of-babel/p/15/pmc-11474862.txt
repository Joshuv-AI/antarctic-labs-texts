Comparing qualitative and quantitative echocardiographic markers to assess cardiac contractility in newborn infants: protocol for a prospective cohort study

Introduction

Neonatologist-performed echocardiography (NPE) is gaining momentum among clinicians in the neonatal intensive care unit for targeting treatment and monitoring haemodynamic responses to therapeutic interventions.1 2 The utilisation of NPE not only improves diagnostic precision but can alter the course of treatment, ultimately contributing to enhanced neonatal outcomes.3,8 NPE has varied applications including but not limited to the diagnosis of pulmonary hypertension, patent ductus arteriosus, haemodynamic evaluation, assessment of cardiac function in sepsis and recognition of pericardial effusion and cardiac tamponade in the neonatal intensive care setting.2 9 NPE can provide the intensivist with in-depth information on the haemodynamics which is not available by clinical biomarkers10 or parameters such as blood pressure, pulse oximetry, capillary refill time and various blood tests.11

It is worth noting that low-flow states, resulting from compromised cardiac contractility, can coexist with optimal blood pressure in as many as 25% of newborn infants.12 13 Therefore, reliance on the presence of optimal blood pressure, one of the most commonly measured cardiovascular parameters, would not be sufficient to ascertain the underlying physiology. In conditions like sepsis, hypoxia and cardiomyopathies, assessing cardiac contractility is vital.14,17 Therefore, it is imperative that low-flow states due to poor cardiac contractility are promptly diagnosed so that appropriate interventions can be swiftly administered.

Clinical examination, routine intensive care monitoring and biochemical markers have limitations as they lack sensitivity and specificity to assess cardiac function.18,20 Consequently, the early diagnosis of poor cardiac contractility is best achieved through echocardiography, a readily available tool in most neonatal units.18 21 Well-established guidelines and recommendations exist for clinicians using NPE.22,24 However, it is important to acknowledge that skills among clinicians performing functional echocardiography may vary within and between neonatal units thus making an accurate diagnosis challenging.22 25 26

As part of functional echocardiography, quantitative echocardiographic markers, such as ejection fraction (EF) and shortening fraction (SF), are commonly measured but require a certain level of expertise for accurate acquisition, and the process is time consuming. On the other hand, qualitative assessment of cardiac contractility can be easily performed visually using the apical four-chamber or parasternal views, even by clinicians with limited echocardiography experience. It’s worth noting that, unlike in adults,27 there is a lack of data comparing qualitative and quantitative markers for cardiac contractility in newborn infants using echocardiography.

We hypothesise that qualitative echocardiographic markers (visualising left and right ventricular contractility) are likely to correlate with quantitative echocardiographic markers (SF/EF and tricuspid plane systolic excursion) when assessed by NPE experienced and non-experienced operators.

Objectives

Our primary objective is to compare qualitative and quantitative echocardiographic markers to assess cardiac contractility in newborn infants by NPE and non-NPE operators.

The secondary objectives are as follows: (1) to evaluate the reliability of echocardiographic measurements between and within observers. (2) Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive and negative predictive values of how well qualitative echocardiographic markers compare with quantitative echocardiographic markers (criterion standard), as well as reliability studies on quantitative echocardiographic markers will be assessed.

Outcomes

The primary outcome is the agreement between qualitative and quantitative echocardiographic markers among NPE and non-NPE operators.

The secondary outcome is obtaining sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive and negative predictive values, as well as positive and negative likelihood ratios.

Methods and analysis

Study design and setting

This is a prospective, observational multicentre cohort study. Newborn infants who undergo NPE by the authors at Homerton University Hospital and Aberdeen Maternity Hospital over a 6-month period will be studied. Homerton is a large tertiary medical neonatal unit in the North Central and North East London Neonatal Network.28 Aberdeen Maternity Hospital is a large tertiary neonatal unit serving North East Scotland and is one of the regional perinatal centres.

NPE operators: the authors (SS and SP) are both experienced NPE operators with more than 10 years of experience in performing NPE in day-to-day clinical practice. The authors are actively involved in teaching NPE locally/nationally and actively participate and contribute to national haemodynamics meetings.

Non-NPE operators: operators who have had minimal or no echocardiographic experience. For the purposes of this study, non-NPE operators will not be performing any echocardiograms but will only visually assess echocardiograms obtained by NPE operators.

Study population

Inclusion criteria: newborn infants, both inborn and outborn, who undergo NPE by the authors (NPE operators) as part of routine care at the Homerton University Hospital and Aberdeen Maternity Hospital. NPE may be performed for evaluation of murmur, infants receiving intensive care and evaluation of underlying cardiovascular physiology.

Exclusion criteria: infants with congenital heart disease (except patent ductus arteriosus), missing data or major congenital abnormality will be excluded.

Data collection

Clinical data will be collected from electronic patient records (EPR) and national neonatal database BadgerNet (prospectively entered neonatal clinical database). Clinical diagnosis will be gathered from EPR and BadgerNet at the respective sites. In addition, gestational age, birth weight, gender, inotrope use, type of respiratory support and mean airway pressure used will be collected.

Qualitative echocardiographic markers

Left ventricular contractility

Left ventricular (LV) contractility will be visually estimated using a combination of apical four-chamber and parasternal views. The observer will assess the approximation of the LV free wall and the septum, as well as the reduction in the LV cavity during systole. The observer will then classify LV contractility as either normal or reduced based on these visual observations.

Right ventricular contractility

Right ventricular (RV) contractility will be visually assessed for vertical movement of the RV free wall, approximation of the RV free wall and the septum and reduction in the RV cavity during systole. The observer will then classify RV contractility as either normal or reduced based on these visual observations.

The authors (NPE operators) will conduct a qualitative assessment of cardiac contractility for infants who are not cared for in their site. This approach ensures blinding to quantitative echocardiographic markers. Clinicians without NPE experience (non-NPE operators) will be presented annonymised echocardiographic scans at their respective sites.

Quantitative echocardiographic markers

The collection of echocardiographic markers from infants undergoing routine care aligns with established recommendations.24 RV contractility will be assessed using the apical four-chamber and parasternal views, while LV contractility will be assessed through the apical four-chamber and parasternal views. The parasternal short-axis view at the level of the mitral valve leaflet will facilitate the acquisition of the M-mode image for SF interrogation. Additionally, the apical two-chamber view will be obtained to calculate the EF using Simpson’s biplane method.24

The mentioned markers will be measured using both M-mode and 2-D images. These echocardiographic measurements, previously validated in newborn infants, are routinely collected as part of standard care, using live images during scans and offline analysis following the scans:

Sample size

Infants who undergo NPE by the authors as part of routine neonatal care at the Homerton University Hospital and Aberdeen Maternity Hospital over a 6-month period (1 April 2024 to 1 October 2024) will be included. As such, the authors aim to examine a minimum of 60 infants during the study period.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables, including gestation, birth weight and age at scan, will be presented as mean (SD, range), while categorical variables, such as the proportion of infants with normal and reduced cardiac contractility, will be expressed as frequency and percentage. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive and negative predictive values, as well as positive and negative likelihood ratios, will be calculated. Non-NPE operator visual estimation of cardiac contractility will be compared with NPE operator-derived quantitative echocardiographic markers, considered the criterion standard. The results will include 95% CIs.

To assess reliability between observers, intraclass correlation will be determined using two-way mixed analysis of variance, where observers are fixed factors and measurements being random factors.29 30 Additionally, the coefficient of variation ((mean SD/Mean)*100) will be calculated as an additional measure of agreement, with results expressed as a percentage. The strength of correlation will be evaluated using established methods.31

All statistical analyses will be conducted using IBM SPSS V.29, GraphPad Prism V.10.2.0 and Microsoft excel.

This study has been reviewed and approved by the local Research and Innovation (R&I) department of Homerton Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust and Aberdeen Maternity Hospital, NHS Grampian. The results of the study will be disseminated via multiple methods including but not limited to oral presentation at regional meetings, oral/poster presentations at national and international conferences and via social media. The chief investigator will ensure that the results are also published in peer-reviewed scientific journals, ideally open access for wider impact, in both online and print form which will be valuable to parents, clinical, policy-makers and the wider research community.

Discussion

NPE has gained popularity among intensivists for interrogating underlying pathophysiology, improving diagnostic precision, guiding appropriate treatment and monitoring response to interventions. Its superiority over routine intensive care monitoring and biochemical markers positions it as the preferred diagnostic tool for cardiovascular management.

While the main impediment to widespread use is training and education, there’s a steady increase in adopting this investigation. The learning curve for NPE is steep, necessitating exposure to a wide array of both normal and abnormal cases. However, the availability of simulators, courses and recommendations24 serves to guide and support learners along their journey. Visual estimation remains crucial for assessing cardiac contractility, especially for NPE-experienced clinicians. This study aims to assess the proficiency of clinicians, both experienced and inexperienced in echocardiography, in identifying infants with normal or reduced cardiac contractility, specifically in newborn infants with structurally normal hearts.

The research will not only provide insights into the accuracy of clinicians, with and without NPE experience, in identifying cardiac contractility using qualitative data but also offer reliability data for commonly measured quantitative echocardiographic markers among NPE operators.

The study’s results will interest various stakeholders, including neonatologists, paediatricians with expertise in cardiology, cardiologists, cardiac physiologists, researchers, policy-makers and the broader healthcare team managing infants prone to disturbances in cardiac contractility. Furthermore, this information will contribute to counselling parents and offering guidance on management to support the circulation.

Conclusion

NPE is a vital tool gaining prominence in neonatal units, serving as an adjunct to clinical examination for enhanced diagnostic precision, particularly in promptly identifying poor cardiac contractility. The visual estimation of cardiac contractility remains the prevailing method for qualitative assessment of cardiac function. This study aims to assess the performance of qualitative markers compared with commonly measured quantitative echocardiographic markers, considering clinicians with diverse echocardiographic experience.

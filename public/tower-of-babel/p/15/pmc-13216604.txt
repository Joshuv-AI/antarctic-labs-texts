Multi-resolution field-based algorithm for autonomous robot exploration
Authors: Zhipeng Zhai, Limin Xu, Yuanbin Zhang, Guodong Zhang, Yu Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper proposes a robot autonomous exploration algorithm based on multi-resolution fields to solve the problems of low efficiency, high path repetition, and limited environmental adaptability in complex environments. First, OctoMap’s layered architecture is used to create a multi-resolution voxel map. A coarse-fine dual stage is then used to detect frontiers and balance point numbers. Second, polar coordinate sampling is utilized to avoid the sampling points being too concentrated in the center of the circle, and the scoring function is constructed by combining the distance decay function to prevent the robot from repeating the search. Finally, Monte Carlo integration’s gain calculation method is introduced to eliminate step errors caused by discretization and improve gain calculation accuracy. Simulation experiments are carried out in different environments, and the data prove that the method in this paper is better than the comparison algorithm in terms of moving distance, running time and exploration efficiency. The results show that the algorithm proposed in this paper effectively improves the robot’s autonomous exploration performance in unknown complex environments and provides a new solution for practical applications.

Introduction

As science and technology advance rapidly, robots are being used more and more in exploration missions. From space exploration to deep-ocean mapping to complex and variable rescue missions, robots are ubiquitous1. In these unknown and challenging scenarios, it is crucial for robots to autonomously explore the environment in advance and create preliminary models of it for subsequent rescue and reconnaissance operations2. Robot-constructed maps are significantly more efficient and applicable than traditional manually constructed maps. They provide more accurate, timely, and practical information support for various tasks, greatly improving the efficiency and success rate of task execution.

However, existing algorithms for autonomous robot exploration reveal many pressing problems when encountering complex environments3. Inefficient exploration is a problem common to many algorithms, and robots often find it difficult to quickly find effective exploration paths in complex environments4, resulting in long task completion times and the inability to provide the necessary support for follow-up work in a timely manner. Meanwhile, the instability of path planning also brings great challenges to autonomous exploration5. In complex and changing environments, robots need to adjust their path planning in real time to cope with various obstacles and unexpected situations, but the existing algorithms are still deficient in this aspect, and are prone to frequent changes in paths and problems such as falling into local optimization, which affects the smooth progress of exploration tasks6. In addition, insufficient adaptation to the environment is also a key issue. Different complex environments have their own unique characteristics and requirements, and existing algorithms are often difficult to maintain good performance in many different environments and fail to fully exploit the robot’s autonomous exploration capability7.

To address the above problems, this study proposes a multi-resolution field-based autonomous exploration algorithm for robots, aiming to enhance the autonomous exploration performance of robots in unknown complex environments through a series of innovative improvement measures. First of all, in the process of frontiers extraction, the system tends to detect a large number of boundary points8, which, if not handled in a special way, will degrade the overall performance of the algorithm. To alleviate this problem, this paper effectively filters boundary points by changing the resolution level of points in OctoMap.Secondly, traditional sampling methods may have problems such as low sampling efficiency and uneven distribution of sampling points in complex environments, resulting in the robot’s inability to achieve complete coverage of the environment9. In this paper, we introduce an advanced sampling strategy while using boundary points to guide the sampling, so that the system can improve the exploration efficiency while ensuring the exploration completeness. In real-world autonomous exploration tasks, robots often need to consider multiple goals at the same time, such as rapidly exploring unknown areas, avoiding dangerous areas, and acquiring high-value information. Existing algorithms are usually difficult to simultaneously take into account these multi-objective requirements, while this study organically combines multiple objectives through an ingenious fusion strategy so that the robot can balance the weights of each objective during the exploration process, dynamically adjust the exploration strategy according to the actual situation, and achieve the synergistic optimization of multi-objectives, thus enhancing the comprehensive exploration capability of the robot in complex environments.

Compared to Dual-Stage Viewpoint Planner (DSVP)10 and Hierarchical Framework for Efficiently Exploring(TARE)11, the contributions of this paper are not merely reflected in the common description of “using multi-resolution”. First, TARE and DSVP only use multi-resolution to reduce computational complexity: fine at close range and coarse at long range, but frontier detection is always performed at the same resolution, resulting in a large number of redundant frontiers entering subsequent planning; In contrast, this paper couples frontier quality with resolution–coarse layers only generate candidate regions, while fine layers undergo a secondary filtering via frustum constraints, enabling a reduction in frontier count without sacrificing accuracy. Second, DSVP’s unidirectional angle sampling tends to overlook lateral unknown regions. The polar coordinate uniform sampling proposed in this paper, combined with multi-objective weighting (direction + distance decay), forms an isotropic and decaying sampling density field, reducing the probability of repeated sampling in the same region. Finally, DSVP and TARE still use discrete voxel counting. This paper introduces Monte Carlo integration to estimate information entropy in continuous space, thereby reducing errors and lowering the path backtracking rate.

The main innovations as well as contributions of this paper are as follows: Map construction and updating: Drawing on the map construction method of common autonomous exploration systems, the robot utilizes SLAM technology to sense the environment and construct an initial map point cloud, which is then converted into OctoMap form. On this basis, the resolution is dynamically adjusted according to the distance between the robot and the environment area to realize efficient representation of complex scenes.Boundary point detection method: first in the coarse detection layer to initially determine the candidate region of the boundary voxel, and then in the fine detection layer to carry out strict boundary detection. Compared with the traditional single-resolution boundary point detection, the method in this paper can balance the number of boundary points and detection accuracy more efficiently.Target Point Selection: A high-precision gain calculation method based on Monte Carlo integration is used to evaluate the information gain of candidate points. Different from the previous simple target point selection method of information gain, the calculation of information gain in this paper is more accurate and the overall error of the system is smaller. Thus, it can reduce the reciprocal search of the robot and make the exploration path of the robot more efficient and reasonable.

Related work

Currently, the more widely used exploration algorithms are mainly categorized into boundary-based algorithms and random sampling-based algorithms. Boundary points were first proposed by Yamauchi12 in 1997 and are defined as the intersection of a known obstacle-free region in space with an unknown region. During the exploration process, the robot first needs to extract the boundary points in the environment, and then continuously reaches the already discovered boundary points for observation until the full environment is completed. The primitive way of choosing boundary points considers only transient gains and not global optimality. As a result, there are some limitations in efficiency improvement. In order to eliminate this drawback, some researchers have introduced the traveler’s problem into boundary point-based exploration algorithms. Faigl and Kulich et al.13 proposed an optimal exploration strategy using Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP)14, where they use a similar approach to solving the Art Gallery Problem (AGP)15 to obtain a number of candidate observations that are minimal in number and capable of observing all the boundary points, and then use the solution of the TSP problem to compute a shortest path that can visit all the observation points. The disadvantage of the above approach is that the time cost of solving the TSP problem rises significantly as the number of observation points increases after the environment becomes larger.To address this root problem, Cao et al.11 proposed TARE, a boundary-point based exploration framework. In TARE, the area around the robot will be represented using fine maps, while the area farther away from the robot will be represented using coarse maps. The global optimal path computed in this way will contain very detailed boundary points around the robot while maintaining sparse boundary points in the more distant regions. TARE is not limited by the scale of the environment and maintains smooth computational efficiency even in very open environments.

The exploration method based on random sampling was first proposed by Bircher et al.16 in 2016 at ETH Zurich as Next-Best-View Planner (NBVP). NBVP generates Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT)17 online within the planning horizon, calculates the information gain of each branch on the RRT, and the branch with the most gain will be selected as the target branch. NBVP is the most classical of such algorithms, but it has many drawbacks. The setting of the evaluation function is too homogeneous, which leads to a high chance for the robot to explore the same area repeatedly. After NBVP, most of the exploration algorithms based on random sampling are improvements or continuations of that algorithm. A more classical improved algorithm is the graph-based exploratory planning (GBP) algorithm proposed by Dang et al.18 at the University of Nevada in 2020. Instead of using RRT, this algorithm employs a fast search for random graphs to generate observations.GBP considers the exploration direction at the same time when selecting the target point to avoid the robot exploring the same domain reciprocally.In another paper in 2019, Selin et al.19 merged the boundary point and random sampling approaches. During local exploration, they use NBVP to get the best observations, while during global exploration, they rely exclusively on boundary points to get the still unexplored regions. In doing so, it effectively compensates for the drawback that NBVP is only applicable to small environments and can guarantee complete coverage of the environment while locally obtaining efficiency superior to the boundary point exploration strategy. For exploration algorithms based on random sampling, it is theoretically difficult to achieve complete coverage of the environment because random sampling cannot cover all regions in a finite time. Moreover, most of the algorithms in this category are a continuation on top of the NBVP algorithm, and the previously obtained sampling points are not considered in the new planning cycle, leading to a lot of computational duplication and a long single planning time.

Methodology

This study focuses on the autonomous exploration algorithm design problem, i.e., how to determine the optimal waypoints for the robot at the right time, so that the robot can traverse the unknown environment with less time cost and less energy consumption. In this paper, we use Lidar Odometry and Mapping (LOAM)20 into a map point cloud. Then the map point cloud is converted into OctoMap21, OctoMap is a three-dimensional map representation based on octree. Each cube of OctoMap is called voxel. Initially the whole environment is modeled as a uniform voxel grid, and all voxels are initially labeled as “Unknown”. This indicates that the robot has not yet observed any information about the area. As the robot moves and acquires sensor data, the voxel map is updated according to certain rules.From the sensor origin, rays are emitted to each observation. Frontier detection

Boundary point is the point at the junction of known free area and unknown area. OctoMap has a unique advantage in dealing with 3D data, which recursively divides the space into eight sub-areas until it reaches the set resolution, which can realize multi-resolution representation of complex scenes. In the 3D boundary detection task, OctoMap’s layered architecture can flexibly adjust the detection accuracy according to the actual needs, while effectively reducing the amount of computation and memory occupation.

When performing path planning and navigation in 3D environments, it is often necessary to maintain a high map resolution. If the initial boundary detection is performed at a higher resolution, a large number of boundary points are generated, which may lead to unnecessary computational resource consumption later in the exploration planning process.Based on the characteristics of OctoMap, this paper uses multi-resolution for boundary point detection, and balances the number of boundary points by coarse-fine dual-stage detection and view cone constraints.In OctoMap, the frontier region consists of free voxels with at least one unknown neighbor. The 8-neighborhood indicates a candidate region for initially identifying a boundary voxel by detecting the presence of an unknown voxel within a neighborhood consisting of 8 surrounding voxels centered on that voxel. Boundary points selection

In order to prevent the robot from the phenomenon of searching back and forth in two directions, this paper draws on the treatment of the paper10 and uses boundary points to guide the extension of the RRT. The exact way to bootstrap the RRT extension is not complicated. First a random number is obtained by sampling uniformly between 0 and 1. If the random number is greater than a set value of 0, then it is sampled within the field of view of the chosen boundary point, otherwise, it is sampled in the rest of the area. In this way, the probability of a sample point falling around the boundary point is greater than the probability of it falling in the rest of the region, and the RRT will tend to expand in the direction of the boundary point, producing more observations closer to the current search direction. In terms of exploration efficiency, Fig. 10 illustrates the curve of the different algorithms searching for volume growth over time in Environment 1. In this paper, the total exploration time of the algorithm is 882s and the path length is 1468m. The total exploration time of DSVP is 1103s and the path length is 1670m. Neither NBVP nor MBP completed the exploration task. NBVP (Next Best View Planner) is a random sampling-based exploration method that generates RRTs online within the planning horizon, calculates the information gain of each branch on the RRT, and the branch with the highest gain will be selected as the target branch. From the results of the experiment, it is clear that there are more unexplored regions in the environment. This is because NBVP only considers the selection of localized target points, and when there are multiple passages in the environment, the robot can easily fall into a certain passage without covering other unexplored regions. MBP adds the robot’s kinematic constraints to the graph-based exploratory planning approach, using spline curves instead of straight trajectories, making it more suitable for operation on UAVs. However, in complex environments, the limitations of the model begin to show. Since MBP relies heavily on randomly sampled observation points, areas not covered by observation points are likely to be missed. In some narrow passages or multi-obstacle regions, MBP does not reach the effectiveness of boundary point exploration algorithms in terms of exploration completeness. Both this paper’s method and DSVP use boundary points rather than information gain to determine whether the search is complete locally, so both achieve complete coverage of the environment. Conclusion

In this paper, a multi-resolution based algorithm for autonomous robot exploration is proposed, aiming to solve the problems of low efficiency, unstable path planning and insufficient environmental adaptability faced by autonomous robot exploration in complex environments. By incorporating coarse-fine two-stage detection and view-cone constraints, the robot is able to accurately recognize boundary points while at the same time effectively reducing the computational burden in the far-field region and improving the overall exploration efficiency. Meanwhile, the multi-objective weighted scoring function constructed in this paper integrally considers the direction similarity and distance attenuation factors, which effectively prevents the reciprocal search phenomenon of the robot in the exploration process. The experimental results show that compared with the classical exploration algorithms such as NBVP, MBP and DSVP, the algorithm in this paper performs well in terms of exploration efficiency and coverage completeness. It provides a more effective method and strategy for autonomous exploration of robots in unknown environments.

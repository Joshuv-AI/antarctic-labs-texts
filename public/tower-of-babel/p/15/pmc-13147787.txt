A landscape of epidemiology and strain distribution of Nocardia in China
Authors: Ziyu Song, Bingqian Du, Deming Tang, Jiang Yao, Jirao Shen, Zhiguo Liu, Min Yuan, Wanchun Guan, Zhenjun Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Scientific questions: Nocardiosis is a clinically diverse opportunistic infection caused by bacteria of the genus Nocardia. In China, clinical awareness and diagnostic capabilities for nocardiosis have improved markedly over the past decade, leading to an increase in reported cases. However, significant gaps remain in the overall understanding of the disease, and systematic epidemiological data are severely lacking. This study aims to comprehensively elucidate the epidemiological characteristics of nocardiosis in China and map the distribution of its pathogenic strains through a systematic nationwide investigation.•Evidence before this study: Our previous work systematically investigated the global epidemiology and species distribution of cerebral nocardiosis. This work not only elucidated geographic variations in causative species but also established a correlation between immunosuppression and more severe infections. Furthermore, it underscored the critical importance of personalized, multidrug regimens in managing antibiotic-resistant strains. These findings collectively provide a crucial foundation for optimizing clinical management strategies.•New findings: The distribution of nocardiosis cases in China exhibits distinct geographical patterns. Geographically, more cases are reported in coastal regions than in inland areas, with the southwest showing a notably higher prevalence of Nocardia brasiliensis infections. Temporally, surveillance data from recent years indicate a rising trend in the total number of cases, concurrent with a decline in the case-fatality rate. Furthermore, an immunosuppressed state constitutes a significant risk factor for this disease, accounting for a substantial proportion of patients and markedly increasing the probability of disseminated infection.•Significance of the study: Evidence from this study indicates that approximately 36 clinically relevant species of Nocardia are recognized in China. Early identification of nocardiosis is crucial to prevent adverse outcomes from disseminated infection. Furthermore, vigilance is required regarding the potential risks of resistant Nocardia species. Early initiation of drug susceptibility testing and selection of appropriate antimicrobial treatment strategies are essential. This often-overlooked disease demands heightened global attention to improve diagnostic capabilities and mitigate its impact.

Introduction

Nocardia is a Gram-positive actinomycete ubiquitously distributed in the environment, found in soil, decaying vegetation, and water [1], capable of causing infections in both humans and animals [2]. To date, 132 species of Nocardia have been officially named (https://www.bacterio.net/genus/nocardia) [3], with more than 50 pathogenic species identified [4]. Nocardia infections are clinically prevalent among patients with compromised or deficient immune systems, as well as those who have been using immunosuppressants for a long time, and have a high mortality rate in clinical practice [5].

Nocardia primarily colonizes the host's lungs through the respiratory tract, causing focal infections, and can also invade the brain and/or central nervous system (CNS), skin, liver, spleen, kidneys, eyes, joints, and nearly all organs through skin lesions or blood circulation pathways, resulting in disseminated diseases with significant harm [6], [7], [8]. Additionally, the early diagnosis of Nocardia infection is challenging due to its slow growth [9] and its tendency to be mistaken for Mycobacteria [10]. The treatment regimen for nocardiosis is typically prolonged, often ranging from six months to a year [11], [12].

With the rapid aging of the Chinese population and the growing prevalence of diabetes and acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS), and the increasing number of organ transplant recipients, coupled with the widespread clinical use of immunosuppressive agents, there has been a noted increase in the incidence of Nocardia infections [13], [14], [15]. Nocardiosis is increasingly deserving of our attention. C. Wang et al. [3] conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis on the distribution and drug resistance profile of 791 Nocardia species in China. However, there remains a lack of epidemiological studies on Nocardia in China, which urgently necessitates a comprehensive analysis of its clinical characteristics, geographical distribution, and associated risk factors.

Comprehending the epidemiological trends of Nocardia infection in China is paramount. Consequently, our study aims to clarify the epidemiology, clinical characteristics, diagnostic methodologies, clinical examination features, risk factors, and treatment outcomes, and the distribution of species and antibiotic susceptibility of isolated Nocardia strains. This comprehensive study is based on a systematic research of all articles published from 1990 up to the completion of the literature collection. The goal is to address the existing knowledge gap regarding the epidemiology of Nocardia infection in China and to assist in formulating enhanced surveillance and control strategies of Nocardia infection.

Materials and methods

Search strategies and study design

Our literature search focused on studies published between April 1, 1990, and September 13, 2024. We identified relevant literature by conducting searches in PubMed, Web of Science (WOS), and the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI) to capture all reported cases. The search terms were “[(Nocardia) OR (Nocardiosis)] AND [(China) OR (Chinese)]” in PubMed and WOS, while the search terms were “(Nuokajun) OR (Nukajun)” in CNKI. All references used are available in the Supplementary. To maintain objectivity, two researchers independently collected data from all the full-text articles and organized the numerical data. Any discrepancies in the findings were addressed through discussions with fellow authors.

Case definition and inclusion criteria

This study meticulously followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Guidelines for article selection [16]. A total of 592 eligible articles were retrieved from three databases, comprising 1088 cases with detailed case reports. The following criteria included cases in the final analysis: (1) Cases involving the isolation of Nocardia from clinical samples, such as bronchoalveolar lavage fluid, sputum, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), pleural effusion, serum, abscess, and tissue samples. (2) Cases where culture results were inconclusive, but Nocardia species were identified through genome sequencing or molecular methods. (3) Cases of nocardiosis published between 1990 and 2024. Articles were excluded based on the following characteristics: (1) Duplicate and irrelevant articles. (2) Reviews without detailed clinical information, editorial, conference articles, literature comments, and non-full-text articles. (3) Articles that did not provide data on the nation, gender, age, and infection site of cases.

We extracted the following data from eligible literature: country, city, gender, age, occupation, smoking status, time of infection, type of Nocardia infection, infection type, drug susceptibility, underlying predisposing factors, comorbidities, past medical history, medication history, clinical characteristics, vital signs, sample source, treatment regimen, treatment duration, treatment outcome, and blood parameters. Additionally, climate type and annual average precipitation data were matched to city-level climate data from WorldClim (https://www.worldclim.org/data/index.html).

Group standard

The nocardiosis cases examined in this study were classified into two groups according to a unified standard for disseminated Nocardia infections: the single-site infection group, which encompassed cases with infection confined to a single site, and the disseminated infection group, which included cases with infections affecting two or more sites. Additionally, the cases were segregated into two groups based on a unified standard of death: the survival group and the death group.

Statistical analysis

All descriptive data were calculated and presented as either number (percent) or number/total (percent) for categorical variables, and as mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables. The chi-square test was used for categorical variables, and the t-test was used for continuous variables. Logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify risk factors associated with all-cause mortality among patients with nocardiosis. A 95% confidence interval (CI) was computed by the Wald method. The covariates of the model, including age, gender, and geographical distribution, were determined through a comprehensive literature review. A multivariable logistic regression model was used to examine associations among vital signs, blood parameters, and various nocardiosis case groups, with results expressed as odds ratios (ORs) and their corresponding 95% CIs. All statistical analyses were performed with SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc, Cary, North Carolina, USA). The distribution frequency map of nocardiosis in China and the corresponding Nocardia species were illustrated using the R package pheatmap. Furthermore, we selected patients between 2014 and 2024 as subgroups for the above statistical analysis.

The epidemiological overview of Nocardia infection in China

The prevalence and geographical distribution of nocardiosis in China are shown in Fig. 2. The disease has been reported nationwide, with notable case concentrations exceeding 50 in the following provinces/municipalities: Beijing, Zhejiang, Sichuan, Henan, Jiangsu, Shandong, Guangdong, and Fujian. Eastern China has been identified as the region with the highest disease burden, followed by Northern and Southwestern China. A notably higher number of Nocardia infections was reported in coastal regions compared to inland areas. The distribution of clinical Nocardia species across China shows no significant geographic variation. The predominant species observed nationwide were Nocardia farcinica (N. farcinica) (14.2%), Nocardia asteroides (N. asteroides) (13.1%), Nocardia brasiliensis (N. brasiliensis) (11.6%), and Nocardia cyriacigeorgica (N. cyriacigeorgica) (9.3%). A notable exception was the proportion of N. brasiliensis, which was markedly higher in Southwest China compared to other regions. Among the isolates, 141 and 197 were characterized by matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS) and next-generation sequencing (NGS), respectively (Fig. S1). The profile of major species was similar to the aforementioned results, albeit with a reduced proportion of N. asteroides. Over time, the number of Nocardia infections has shown a marked increasing trend. Prior to 2010, the case fatality rate fluctuated significantly. Between 2010 and 2017, the case fatality rate fluctuated, reaching its lowest point in 2016 (4.44%) and peaking in 2017 (17.02%). Since 2017, however, the mortality rate has gradually stabilized and begun to decline (Fig. 3). Patient characteristics

The clinical and demographic features of the 1,088 nocardiosis cases are presented in Table 1. The cohort had a mean age of 52.6 ± 17.4 years with a male predominance (61.0%, 664/1,088). Disseminated infection occurred in 316 cases (29.0%, 316/1,088), and the overall case-fatality rate was 9.0%. Among all cases, workers and farmers constituted a prominent subgroup (14.4%, 157/1,088). Geographically, the majority of cases were from East China (35.4%, 385/1,088) and North China (20.9%, 227/1,088). Autoimmune disease was a notable comorbidity (24.4%, 266/1,088) and was associated with an increased risk of dissemination (P < 0.001) and higher mortality (P < 0.01), as evidenced by its overrepresentation in the fatal group (36.7%, 36/98). Management of disseminated infection more frequently involved combination antibiotic therapy (79.1%, 250/316) and, in survivors, required a significantly longer treatment course (241.44 ± 151.85 days) than localized disease (147.66 ± 148.07 days). Clinical features

The most frequent clinical manifestations were cough with sputum (52.9%, 576/1,088) and fever (48.3%, 525/1,088), followed by skin symptoms (25.3%, 275/1,088), dyspnea (18.8%, 205/1,088), chest pain (11.9%, 129/1,088), and headache (9.1%, 99/1,088) (Table 2). Disseminated infections were characterized by a significantly higher prevalence of systemic and neurological symptoms, including fever, altered mental status, brain abscess, and seizures, compared to localized disease. Conversely, the mortality group was distinguished by a greater burden of respiratory, neurological, and constitutional symptoms. Comorbidities were common (76.9%, 837/1,088), with renal disease (15.2%, 165/1,088) and diabetes (11.8%, 128/1,088) being most prevalent. Renal disease and systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) conferred a higher risk for dissemination, while chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and bronchiectasis were typically associated with localized lung infections. Pulmonary involvement (69.9%, 760/1,088) was the most common infection site, followed by the skin infection (30.3%, 330/1,088) and brain/CNS (19.3%, 210/1,088). A comparative analysis of infection sites between survivors and non-survivors revealed that fatal outcomes were significantly associated with infections of the lungs, brain/CNS, and bloodstream, but Risk factors

Multivariable analysis identified several factors independently associated with increased mortality from nocardiosis after adjustment for potential confounders (Fig. 5). Methods of diagnosis and laboratory characteristics

Diagnostically, most cases were confirmed by culture or molecular detection. Specifically, 848 cases had bacterial cultures, 406 had advanced testing (mass spectrometry, 16S ribosomal ribonucleic acid (rRNA) sequencing, polymerase chain reaction, or NGS), and 608 underwent imaging. Analysis revealed species-specific susceptibility patterns. While most species were highly susceptible to linezolid, amikacin, and minocycline, notable exceptions were observed. Furthermore, N. farcinica demonstrated reduced susceptibility to ceftriaxone (36.4%, 4/11).

Subgroup analysis

The results demonstrate that the selection of specific studies (cases reported between 2014 and 2024) did not significantly affect the overall findings, indicating a high degree of robustness in outcome assessments (Table S1–S2, Figs. S2–S5). However, it is noteworthy that the isolation and identification of N. asteroides declined markedly over time.

Discussion

Nocardia is an uncommon clinical pathogen that is often overlooked [17]. It can cause abscesses at a single site or multiple organs, potentially leading to irreversible damage or even death [18]. With the growing number of immunocompromised individuals and the widespread use of immunosuppressive agents, the incidence of Nocardia infection has been steadily increasing [13]. Despite this, systematic epidemiological analysis of nocardiosis in China remains scarce [3]. This systematic review presents data on 1,088 cases of Nocardia infection across China, excluding Ningxia Hui Autonomous Region, and involves 36 identified Nocardia species. These findings establish a nationwide overview of epidemiology and species distribution of Nocardia infection in China.

Nocardiosis shows a heterogeneous distribution across China, with more cases reported in the eastern and northern regions than in the northeastern and northwestern areas. However, this disparity could be confounded by factors including population size differences, potentially introducing bias so that reported cases might not represent the actual incidence. Another prominent feature is the concentration of cases in coastal provinces, which may be influenced by local climatic factors such as temperature and humidity [19], [20]. This observation contrasts with reports suggesting that dry and windy conditions favor Nocardia transmission [21], indicating that the environmental determinants of its epidemiology require further clarification.

In China, the predominant Nocardia species are N. farcinica, N. cyriacigeorgica, Nocardia abscessus (N. abscessus), and Nocardia otitidiscaviarum [3], [22]. This distribution differs from those reported in other regions, for instance, large-scale studies from New Zealand [23] and the United States [24] identified Nocardia nova, N. cyriacigeorgica, and N. farcinica as the predominant species. In this study, N. farcinica, N. cyriacigeorgica, N. asteroides, and N. brasiliensis were the most frequently detected species, consistent with earlier findings by J. Shen et al. [15]. However, subgroup analysis (Table S1) revealed that the proportion of early N. asteroides was overestimated. This significant discrepancy primarily stemmed from taxonomic reclassification. In a 2003 study [25], strains previously typed as N. asteroides were divided into three distinct species: N. asteroides, N. cyriacigeorgica, and N. abscessus. Recent studies [3], [22] indicate that, apart from N. farcinica, N. cyriacigeorgica is the most prevalent species. Consequently, earlier reports may have underestimated the true prevalence of N. cyriacigeorgica. While no significant regional differences in species distribution were observed, N. brasiliensis showed a significantly higher prevalence in Southwest China, consistent with previous research [3]. Additionally, the number and diversity of reported Nocardia cases and species have increased markedly since the 2000s, likely due to advances in molecular identification techniques [26], [27]. Early studies relying on phenotypic characteristics or drug patterns for identification were particularly affected by this reclassification. Recent reports employing molecular methods offer superior species-level resolution, our laboratory's research indicates [22].

In this study, the overall mortality rate was 9.0%, lower than those reported elsewhere (14.0%–21.3%) [28], [29]. This discrepancy may stem from other studies focusing on transplant patients or single-center data from high-risk populations. Hence, the true nationwide mortality rate for Nocardia patients may be lower than previously suggested. Male patients accounted for 61% of cases, consistent with the general trend of male predominance in nocardiosis [30], [31]. The age distribution peaked between 50 and 69 years, aligning with prior reports [32], [33], [34], and suggesting that Nocardia infections primarily affect middle-aged and elderly adults. Among cases with available occupational records, farmers and workers are the most common. Given that Nocardia is widely distributed in soil, decaying vegetation, and water [1], these groups are at increased risk of exposure. Workers exposed to soil or dust particles may also be at risk of respiratory infections [35]. Studies have shown that Nocardia infections among patients with AIDS are reported more frequently in rural areas than in urban areas [10]. Additionally, in China, areas with a subtropical monsoon climate exhibit an extremely high incidence of cases, which aligns with the higher infection rates in tropical and subtropical climates compared to temperate climates [27].

Autoimmune diseases and the use of corticosteroids or other immunosuppressants were all associated with increased rates of disseminated infection and mortality, suggesting the central role of cellular immune suppression in severe nocardiosis [36]. Treatment duration in our dataset averaged 147.66 ± 148.07 days for single-site infections and 241.44 ± 151.85 days for disseminated infections, consistent with existing guidelines recommending 6–12 months for pulmonary or non-CNS nocardiosis and at least 12 months for immunocompromised or CNS-involved cases [12], which suggests the importance of sufficient treatment duration to prevent relapse or recurrence.

In our study, the most common site of infection was the lungs (69.9%), followed by the skin (30.3%) and brain/CNS (19.3%). Symptoms of lung infection are typically nonspecific, including cough, dyspnea, and fever [13], while symptoms of brain/CNS infection often include fever, headache, seizures [37], and neurological deficits [38]. It is these nonspecific symptoms that complicate the detection and diagnosis of nocardiosis, often leading to delayed treatment and increased damage. In our study, the most common underlying diseases were diabetes, hypertension, bronchiectasis, and kidney disease, while the most common autoimmune disease was SLE. In contrast, studies from other countries commonly report malignant tumors, solid organ and hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, liver disease, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease [39], [40], [41], which also suggests that relevant departments should strengthen the management of chronic diseases, such as hypertension and diabetes.

The data indicate that disseminated Nocardia infection is independently associated with the risk of death, accounting for approximately half of all fatal cases. These disseminated infections usually result from the spread of the infection from the lungs or skin to other organs [26]. Therefore, early diagnosis and treatment are essential to prevent disseminated infections. Additionally, brain/CNS infections and bacteremia also represent significant risk factors for mortality, as they typically result in severe systemic symptoms, prolonged treatment, and greater tissue damage [27], [42]. While the mortality rate for single-site skin and eye infections is lower, they should still be treated promptly to prevent spread and the formation of severe infections. In patients with severe or immunocompromised conditions, combination antibiotic therapy generally yields better outcomes compared to monotherapy [26]. The combination of imipenem and amikacin, for instance, is increasingly accepted as the initial treatment for patients with pulmonary, brain, and severe Nocardia infections [43], [44]. Therefore, we advocate for the use of initial combination therapy for severe infections to minimize damage and mortality.

In our study, most clinical isolates demonstrated susceptibility to linezolid, amikacin, minocycline, and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, whereas susceptibility to ciprofloxacin and clarithromycin was notably lower. Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole remains the cornerstone of clinical management for nocardiosis, and linezolid has been established to exhibit potent in vitro activity against a broad spectrum of Nocardia species [45]. However, both agents can cause adverse drug reactions, particularly at high doses or in susceptible patients [46], [47]. Given that antibiotic susceptibility in Nocardia is highly species-dependent and has traditionally been used for taxonomic classification [27], clinicians must obtain susceptibility profiles as early as possible to guide appropriate antibiotic selection and avoid delays in effective therapy. The susceptibility rate to trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole in this study was 84.9%, which is lower than the 94.6% reported in a domestic study [22] and the >90.0 % rates documented in Japan [48] and the United States [24]. This observed reduction in susceptibility may be attributable to the specific breakpoint criteria used for Nocardia, which categorize isolates as resistant only when growth exceeds 80% on susceptibility testing. This high threshold potentially leads to the misclassification of some susceptible isolates as resistant, resulting in an underestimation of the true susceptibility rate. Furthermore, the antimicrobial susceptibility data in this study were compiled from reported case reports that employed varying testing methodologies, including broth microdilution, the disk diffusion method, and E-test. Broth microdilution is the standardized reference method recommended by the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI)-M24-S, while the E-test is a reliable alternative or supplementary technique. Although the disk diffusion method is commonly used in clinical settings for routine susceptibility testing, its application to Nocardia species may introduce interpretive discrepancies. For instance, a comparative study between broth microdilution and the disk diffusion method for Nocardia [49] reported high essential agreement (98.5%, 98.5%, and 98.6% for tobramycin, amikacin, and imipenem, respectively). However, for other antibiotics tested (ciprofloxacin, moxifloxacin, amoxicillin-clavulanate, ceftriaxone, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, and linezolid), the results did not meet the Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-recommended criteria for essential agreement. Consequently, the susceptibility profiles aggregated in this study may be subject to some methodological bias.

This study represents the first meta-analysis to delineate the epidemiological characteristics and species distribution of clinically isolated Nocardia in China. However, several limitations warrant consideration. First, the long timespan of the included studies coincides with major diagnostics advances, from conventional phenotypic identification to molecular-based methods, which may have led to an overestimation of N. asteroides and underreporting of novel or rare species. Second, as the analysis relies exclusively on published case reports and series, underrepresentation from certain regions may limit the generalizability of findings. Future studies are warranted to identify novel at-risk populations and deepen the elucidation of Nocardia epidemiology for improved preventive measures. The implementation of ongoing surveillance for long-term immune reconstitution is essential to minimize opportunistic infection risks.

Conclusion

Nocardiosis is associated with a substantial mortality rate, especially among patients with disseminated infections or compromised immune systems. In China, Nocardia infections are more prevalent in the coastal areas of eastern provinces. N. farcinica, N. cyriacigeorgica and N. brasiliensis are the most frequently isolated species. Empirical combination therapy with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, amikacin, linezolid and if necessary, other active drugs may be initiated before susceptibility results are available. Early diagnosis and prompt initiation of therapy are strongly recommended, with surgical intervention considered when appropriate.

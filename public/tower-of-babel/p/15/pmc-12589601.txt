Development of the scientific and innovative potential of future teachers
Authors: Karlygash Polatbekova, Zhanat Yergubekova, Ardak Bostanova, Nurdaulet Shektibayev, Ulzharkyn Abdigapbarova
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The aim of this research was to develop an educational program for enhancing the scientific and innovative potential of both future and current educators. To achieve this, survey methods using the DLOQ, UWES-9, and IWB scales were employed. The study involved 80 active teachers and 200 students from the International Kazakh-Turkish University, named after Khoja Ahmed Yasawi, and the Kazakh National Pedagogical University, named after Abai. In total, 280 participants took part in the study, including 195 women and 85 men. The results of the preliminary testing revealed no statistically significant differences between future and active teachers based on the DLOQ, UWES-9, and IWB scales (p > 0.05). After completing the educational program, significant improvements were observed across all measures among all participants. The results confirm the high effectiveness of the program. Analysis of variance showed that age and work experience had a statistically significant impact on changes in knowledge and engagement levels (p < 0.05), while gender did not show a significant effect (p > 0.05). Age demonstrated significant changes (p < 0.05), while the impact of work experience was significant only for DLOQ and IWB. The study’s results highlight the educational program’s substantial positive impact and emphasize the importance of considering factors such as age and work experience in optimizing the teaching process and professional development of educators.

Introduction

The process of digitalization, which increasingly permeates all aspects of human life (personal, academic, professional, and social), demands that education undergo a profound transformation and that communication and learning processes be redefined1. Digital learning is the organization of the educational process using digital technologies and online tools, which provides personalized and effective learning through innovative means, including smart devices and online platforms2. In this context, it is important to develop the digital competencies of teachers—the knowledge, skills, and attitudes necessary for the critical and effective use of digital technologies in teaching3.

Thanks to networks and constant access to information, any space can become a learning environment1. This innovative shift requires educators to implement flexible educational practices using technology to help students develop competencies that are important for their personal and professional development4. However, international studies indicate a common problem of insufficient development of digital competencies among teachers. Spanish authors emphasize the low level of digital skills, particularly in the areas of creativity, information literacy, and problem solving, despite relatively high levels of communication and collaboration with digital content3,5. At the same time, in the US, it has been established that proper teacher training is closely linked to the adoption of innovative teaching methods6, while in Finland, the need to create systemic educational solutions for the development of competencies needed in the future is emphasized7. Thus, although the situation varies depending on the national context, in all cases the importance of improving digital literacy and adapting educational programs to the challenges of digitalization is emphasized8.

Kazakhstan demonstrates both common and unique features in this regard. Similar to Spain, there is also a lack of digital literacy among teachers: Kazakh studies show that while teachers have high pedagogical competence, their technological training remains at an average level9,10. At the same time, unlike the US and Finland, the key barriers are not only skills but also systemic factors: insufficient funding for educational programs, outdated curricula, and a mismatch between teacher training and labor market needs11. This complicates the implementation of digital innovations, slows down the modernisation of the education system and requires teacher education to seek effective models for developing scientific and innovative potential.

Thus, in the context of rapid technological advancement and the digitalization of education, the development of the scientific and innovative potential of future teachers becomes one of the priority tasks of educational policy. Analysis of international experience shows that countries that actively implement innovative methods of teacher training demonstrate higher quality education outcomes12,13. In this context, studying current trends and developing new approaches to shaping future teachers’ scientific and innovative potential becomes particularly significant and relevant. The contribution of this research to global scholarship lies in the synthesis and systematization of advanced teacher preparation practices, which will allow the creation of a universal model of teacher education that meets contemporary challenges. This may contribute to improving the quality of teacher preparation not only in Kazakhstan but also in other countries striving to modernize their educational systems.

Literature review

Digital competence has gained significant importance in the educational context, being one of the key competencies that teachers must possess in modern society3. Innovative information and communication technologies have transformed higher education from traditional methods to intellectual learning2. Teacher training programs focused on active learning are becoming increasingly popular in higher education institutions; however, there is still little quantitative data on their effectiveness6. Existing international initiatives, such as the American Grand Challenges program, demonstrate that integrating social science issues into educational courses effectively engages students, preparing them for local and global challenges in science education14. Thus, contemporary approaches emphasize the importance of combining digital technologies and active learning to enhance the competence and motivation of future educators.

The development of modern learning environments demonstrates a common trend toward combining technology, active learning, and assessment tools, which is transforming the educational process. The use of mobile devices and computer technologies not only supports various learning strategies but also expands the educational space beyond the classroom, integrating elements of formative assessment and promoting the development of new competencies15,16. Research on the effectiveness of Moodle confirms this trend, as active student interaction with the platform is reflected in higher academic performance and various engagement indicators17. At the same time, the use of online platforms for collaboration, feedback systems, and modeling indicates that technologies not only influence individual results but also create conditions for collective decision-making and the development of innovative pedagogical practices, thereby increasing the overall professional competence of educators18.

Contemporary research demonstrates that the engagement of educators and students is a key factor in the effectiveness of innovative practices, and this idea is supported by both theoretical and empirical approaches. Social exchange theory explains that feeling respected and valued in the workplace motivates teachers to go beyond their basic duties, which promotes creativity and innovation5,19. In a pedagogical context, this logic manifests itself in the use of new educational models: for example, flipped learning has been shown to be effective in increasing student engagement and learning outcomes12. Similar conclusions are confirmed in studies on the integration of digital technologies: teachers’ acceptance of artificial intelligence, its ease of use, and accumulated experience correlate positively with the quality of teaching, although excessive student interest can alter the strength of this relationship13. Thus, regardless of the specific technology or model, the engagement of teachers and students is a critical condition for the effective digital transformation of education. The integration of artificial intelligence is a prime example of this process: it opens up opportunities for personalized learning while requiring teachers to develop new skills, such as designing prompts that enhance critical thinking and the quality of the educational experience20. To realize this potential, educators need not only basic digital skills but also a willingness to apply innovative approaches, which is directly related to the objectives of our study—assessing the initial level of competencies, developing special teaching methods, and analyzing the dynamics of change. Thus, preliminary results confirm that it is the combination of digital literacy, innovative behavior, and engagement that creates the basis for the formation of high scientific and innovative potential in teachers, which justifies the relevance of developing a comprehensive program to improve these competencies.

Contemporary pedagogy and digital learning research underscore the importance of developing teachers’ digital competencies, but certain gaps and limitations remain. First, quantitative evidence of the effectiveness of professional development programs for teachers focused on active learning remains limited6. Second, while the implementation of innovative educational technologies, such as flipped learning, is actively discussed, questions persist regarding the practical aspects of their application in various educational contexts12. Additionally, modern research focuses on developing digital competencies but leaves unresolved the issue of creating conditions for their sustainable development and integration into everyday pedagogical practice. It is essential to correctly understand and address the issues surrounding the creation of conditions for the development and formation of digital competencies in modern educators21. Despite the recognition of the importance of digital literacy, further research is needed to identify the most effective strategies for integrating digital technologies into the educational environment.

Problem statement

Contemporary educational systems must prepare educators with a high level of scientific and innovative potential. This study aims to develop a methodology for enhancing these competencies among future teachers and practicing educators.

Research hypothesis: The development and implementation of a specialized training methodology aimed at strengthening the scientific and innovative capacity of teachers will significantly improve their knowledge and competencies. It is expected that, after completing the training program, participants will demonstrate statistically significant improvements in engagement, innovative behavior, and digital literacy indicators.

Methods and materials

Study design

The study was conducted during the 2024–2025 academic year, I-II semesters. The research design is experimental. It includes two test measurements: pre-and post-training assessments. The experiment utilized the survey method.

Participants

In the course of this study, 200 graduates of pedagogical universities in the Republic of Kazakhstan and 80 active teachers were surveyed, allowing for a comprehensive assessment of the process of developing the scientific and innovative potential of future teachers. The participants included fourth-year students from the Kazakh National Pedagogical University, named after Abai, and the International Kazakh-Turkish University, named after Khoja Ahmed Yasawi, as well as teachers with experience working in educational institutions in the city of Almaty: schools, lyceums, and gymnasiums. The educational experience took place in general education schools, where the participants underwent their practicum, and the teachers performed their professional duties.

The total number of respondents was 280 individuals, consisting of both men and women: 195 women and 85 men. The average age of the respondents ranged from 22 to 35 years. The age of the respondents varied as both students and practicing educators participated in the study. In this study, all graduates of pedagogical universities and active teachers participated in all stages of testing, including pre-test and post-test assessments. There were no cases of refusal to participate or missing measurements, ensuring completeness of data for analysis. Table 1 presents the characteristics of the study participants.

The selection of participants was carried out through purposive sampling of students from pedagogical universities with experience in educational institutions in the form of practicum hours, as well as teachers from schools, lyceums, and gymnasiums. Invitations to participate in the study were sent by the departments of pedagogical universities where the students were enrolled, as well as by the administrations of educational institutions. Informational sessions were organized to explain the purpose of the study, the conditions of participation, and the expected outcomes. The main inclusion criteria for the sample were enrollment in the 4th year of a pedagogical university or prior teaching experience in an educational institution, willingness to participate in the study for two semesters, consent to participate in the knowledge assessment, and an interest in innovative teaching methods. The exclusion criteria included lack of experience in educational institutions, enrollment in lower university years, and participation in administrative or managerial activities without direct pedagogical practice.

The selection of this group of participants was driven by the need to study the development of scientific and innovative potential at the stage of completing pedagogical education and the early years of professional activity. Fourth-year students already possess pedagogical experience, making their experience relevant for analyzing the implementation of innovative technologies. Current teachers, in turn, can assess the effectiveness of such approaches from the perspective of their professional practice.

Procedure

The program for developing future teachers’ scientific and innovative potential is aimed at preparing educators who are capable of adapting to changes in the educational environment and actively utilizing innovative methods and technologies. The program is presented in Appendix 1. The primary goal of the program is to prepare teachers for innovative activities in the educational sphere through the implementation of interactive teaching methods, professional development, and the enhancement of digital skills.

The program is based on the concept of continuous professional development for teachers and integrates the SECI model (Socialization, Externalization, Combination, Internalization) to support the creation and exchange of knowledge among teachers and to foster the development of more adaptive and innovative educational environments22. The program also adapts the Teacher Training and Support for Innovative Pedagogical and Creative Development (TTS-IPCD) approach, which emphasizes the cyclical process and aligns vision, policy, and organizational structures with practical teaching initiatives23.

Stages of applying the methodology:

Stage 1 - Active participation and learning through practical sessions. At this stage, the participants of the experiment engage in intensive educational activities that familiarize them with innovative digital methods and tools. The courses are structured in such a way that participants not only learn theoretical concepts but also actively engage in practical activities. These events made use of various applications and platforms, each serving a specific function in the teacher preparation process (Table 2).

Stage 2 — Application of Skills in the Classroom: Participants have the opportunity to test and apply the knowledge and skills they have acquired in their classrooms. This stage focuses on experimentation and adaptation, allowing participants to analyze the effectiveness and suitability of the established methods within specific educational contexts. This is a crucial step for testing theory in practice and identifying potential issues and their solutions.

Stage 3 — Reflection and Self-Assessment: In this stage, participants reflect on and evaluate the success of implementing new approaches. They use reflective practice to identify strengths, challenges, and opportunities for further improvement. This stage helps participants organize and make sense of their experiences, clarifying areas that require enhancement to increase their effectiveness.

Stage 4 — Evaluation of Professional Growth and Development: This stage involves testing the methodology developed to assess the effectiveness of the learning process.

Research tools

The evaluation of the program’s effectiveness includes measurement using the Dimensions of the Learning Organization Questionnaire - DLOQ24, the Utrecht Work Engagement Scale - UWES-925, and the Innovative Work Behavior - IWB26. These scales allow for the analysis of the level of engagement, innovative thinking, and readiness to learn among the respondents. All questionnaires and scales were adapted for a Russian-speaking audience.

In this study, the shortened 21-item version of the DLOQ was used to measure the cultural dimensions of the learning organization. The questionnaire assessed seven dimensions of the learning organization in the workplace, including continuous learning (items 1, 7, 14), dialogue and inquiry (items 2, 8, 15), team learning (items 3, 9, 17), integrated systems (items 4, 10, 18), empowerment (items 5, 6, 19), communication systems (item 20), and strategic management (items 13, 16, 21). Each dimension was measured by three items on a six-point Likert scale (1 = almost never to 6 = almost always). The reliability of the DLOQ was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha. The coefficients obtained for continuous learning, team learning, inquiry and dialogue, integrated systems, empowerment, communication systems, and strategic leadership ranged from 0.63 to 0.81. The overall reliability of the questionnaire was 0.95.

The IWB questionnaire consisted of 9 items. Three items related to idea generation (items 1, 4, 7), three to idea promotion (items 2, 5, 8), and three to idea implementation (items 3, 6, 9). A five-point Likert scale ranged from 1, “very little,” to 5, “very much.” The reliability score for the scale, calculated using Cronbach’s alpha, was 0.85. Additionally, the reliability scores for idea generation, idea promotion, and idea implementation were 0.82, 0.78, and 0.81, respectively.

The UWES-9 scale also consisted of 9 items: three items measuring vigor (items 1, 4, 5), three items measuring dedication (items 2, 3, 7), and three items measuring absorption (items 6, 8, 9). The questionnaire used a 6-point Likert scale (from 0 = never to 6 = always). Participants selected the statements that best fit their experience. The reliability of the questionnaire was also assessed using Cronbach’s alpha, which was 0.91. The alpha coefficients for the dimensions of vigor, engagement, absorption, and the overall scale were 0.88, 0.87, 0.76, and 0.93, respectively.

The developed program represents a comprehensive approach to developing the scientific and innovative potential of future teachers, combining advanced pedagogical practices, digital technologies, and mentoring elements. Thus, the program for developing the scientific and innovative potential of future teachers ensures the integration of scientific approaches and practical tools, thereby helping prepare teachers for contemporary pedagogical challenges.

Data collection

The research procedure consisted of two main stages of assessment: a pre-test and a final test. The goal of the preliminary assessment was to determine the baseline level of knowledge and skills of the respondents in the areas of scientific and innovative potential and digital educational technologies, as well as to confirm the equality of groups (potential and current educators) based on the key questionnaires of the DLOQ, UWES-9, and IWB scales. The pre-test process was conducted online through the Google Forms platform. The testing was supervised by faculty members from the departments of pedagogy and teaching methods of universities and independent assessors. The test duration was 60 min.

All participants completed the test individually, without the use of additional materials. The preliminary assessment utilized the following resources: classrooms with computers connected to the Internet, testing platforms, and paper versions of the tests for respondents who could not complete the tests online. Methodological guidelines for the questionnaire were also provided on each desk.

After the completion of the training program, a final test was administered. The structure was identical to the pre-test, allowing for an objective comparison of results and the evaluation of the program’s effectiveness. The final audit was conducted under the same conditions as the initial test, with the same supervising specialists. The duration of the testing was also 60 min. For result analysis, a comparative method was used: the scores of the pre-test and final test were compared using statistical data processing methods, including the calculation of the mean score, analysis of variance, and determination of the statistical significance of differences. This enabled the identification of the most effective aspects of the training and the evaluation of the development of future teachers’ scientific and innovative potential as a result of the program’s implementation.

Data analysis

Data analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics software (version 28.0) and Microsoft Excel for preliminary data processing and visualization. The choice of SPSS was based on its extensive capabilities in statistical analysis, including the testing of differences between groups, correlation analysis, and the evaluation of data reliability. For statistical processing of the pre-assessment and post-assessment data, the Student’s t-test for independent samples was used. This test allowed for the comparison of mean values between the two groups (future teachers and current teachers) based on their initial level of knowledge. The t-test also helped determine whether there were statistically significant differences between the groups at the beginning of the study, which was necessary to confirm the comparability of the samples. 95% confidence intervals indicate the range within which the true difference between the means lies with 95% probability. If the interval contains 0, the difference is insignificant; if it does not contain 0, the changes are statistically and practically significant.

Additionally, analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to assess the influence of various factors (age, professional experience, gender) on the development of knowledge levels. This helped identify which respondent characteristics might have affected their success in implementing innovative teaching methods. Cohen’s effect size d was used to assess the practical significance of the changes. A value of d = 0.2 corresponds to a small effect, d = 0.5 to a medium effect, and d = 0.8 to a large effect. In the study, the effect sizes allowed us to assess the intensity of changes in the level of knowledge, engagement, and innovative activity, confirming the practical significance of the results. The results of the data analysis allowed for conclusions about the significance of the proposed program for the development of the scientific and innovative potential of future teachers and highlighted the most effective aspects of the implemented teaching methods.

Ethical considerations

The study was based on the principles of voluntary participation, and all respondents gave their consent for the processing of their data. Participants were informed in advance about the goals of the study, the methods of experimenting, the expected outcomes, and the potential consequences of their participation. They were allowed to withdraw from the study at any time without any repercussions.

Confidentiality of the participants’ data was ensured. All collected information was stored in an anonymized form, and personal data (such as name, surname, and place of work or study) were not recorded in the research reports or publications. Access to the raw data was restricted to the researchers directly involved in data analysis.

Research limitations

The limitations of the study included the potential subjective influence of individual participant characteristics on the learning outcomes. For example, differences in motivation, previous professional experience, or the level of knowledge regarding digital tools could have affected the dynamics of implementing innovative methods. Additionally, due to the limited duration of the study, it was not possible to assess the long-term impact of the implemented program.

It should also be noted that the study sample was limited to students from two pedagogical universities in Kazakhstan. This could have affected the generalizability of the results to a broader context of teacher education. However, the use of both quantitative and qualitative data analysis methods helped minimize these limitations and provided objective research findings.

Age had a statistically significant impact on all three dependent variables—older participants demonstrated more pronounced changes in their results. For DLOQ, the ANOVA result showed a statistically significant effect with F = 4.82 and p = 0.03, d = 0.45. In the case of UWES-9, the effect of age was also significant (F = 6.25, p = 0.01, d = 0.51), and for IWB, the result was also significant (F = 5.76, p = 0.02, d = 0.48). All these p-values were less than 0.05, and the effect size was moderate, confirming the statistical and practical significance of the influence of age.

Work experience showed a statistically significant impact on changes in DLOQ (F = 3.95, p = 0.05, d = 0.42) and IWB (F = 4.32, p = 0.04, d = 0.44), with p-values less than 0.05. However, for UWES-9, the effect of work experience was not statistically significant (F = 2.88, p = 0.09, d = 0.31), with the p-value exceeding 0.05. The effect sizes for all three variables are within the average range, indicating a noticeable practical impact of age on the results. Therefore, both age and work experience have a significant impact: professional experience influences the perception of organizational learning and innovative behavior more than the level of work engagement.

The results of the study show that training using modern approaches and technologies contributed to a significant improvement in several indicators, such as work engagement (UWES-9), innovative activity (IWB), and personal success in skill development (DLOQ). These indicators improved significantly after the training program, as evidenced by the statistically significant results obtained through t-tests. This indicates the positive impact of educational technologies on the preparation of future teachers. To sustain this development, the continued integration of new digital and innovative technologies into the educational process is necessary. The use of modern teaching methods, such as interactive platforms and educational mobile applications, can contribute to the development of professional and research skills in student teachers and current educators. It is also important to emphasize the significance of using new technologies in real educational situations, as this will help future and current educators more effectively adapt to the changing educational environment.

Discussion

After completing the training program aimed at developing future teachers’ scientific and innovative potential, significant changes were observed in the test results, as evidenced by the comparison of mean scores before and after the training. Specifically, on the DLOQ scale, the mean score increased from 3.48 (SD = 0.69) to 4.32 (SD = 0.67). Similarly, scores on the UWES-9 scale increased from 4.10 (SD = 0.77) to 4.85 (SD = 0.73) and on the IWB scale from 3.96 (SD = 0.62) to 4.65 (SD = 0.59). All changes were statistically significant, indicating a positive impact of the program on the participants’ knowledge and professional activity levels.

Previous studies, such as the CIMIT (Combined Individual Microteaching, Modeled Teaching, and Inquiry-based Team-Teaching) model27 and the SIMBL (Socioscientific Issue and Model-Based Learning) concept28, have confirmed the effectiveness of structured professional development and reflective practices in improving teacher competencies. In CIMIT, training consisted of three stages—individual microteaching, demonstration research training, and team research training—and the experimental group showed significantly greater improvements in scientific skills and knowledge compared to the control group. SIMBL emphasized the crucial role of sustained professional development, reflective practices, and alignment with teachers’ beliefs in implementing innovative educational frameworks. The results of our study are consistent with previous work, showing significant improvements on all key scales: DLOQ (from 3.48 to 4.32, p < 0.001), UWES-9 (from 4.10 to 4.85, p < 0.001), and IWB (from 3.96 to 4.65, p < 0.001). Unlike CIMIT and SIMBL, our program combines digital and interactive teaching methods and integrates the SECI model to support knowledge creation and sharing among teachers. This allowed us not only to assess but also to directly stimulate the development of competencies and innovative behavior, creating a more adaptive and innovative educational environment and highlighting the unique practical contribution of the program.

Previous studies confirm the effectiveness of structured professional development and innovative approaches in improving teacher competencies. The results of Slovenian authors showed that the TTS-IPCD model increased teachers’ creative potential by promoting innovative behavior, including acceptance of diversity, solving problems with new approaches, and applying a holistic perspective23. The results of the Iranian study indicated that the organization of learning not only positively predicted work engagement but also served as a significant positive predictor of innovative behavior at work19. The results of the methodology development in this article, based on the UWES-9 scale (from 4.10 to 4.85, p < 0.001), further confirm the importance of motivation and teachers’ engagement in the learning process.

In a Dutch study, management support and the influence of innovation also contributed to an increase in IWB scores29. It was found that a critical factor for the development of innovative behavior is a work environment where employees encounter innovation and receive support, while characteristics such as gender had little influence. Similar results were obtained in our study: on the IWB scale, the average score increased from 3.96 to 4.65 (p < 0.001), indicating the development of innovative behavior and positive changes in pedagogical practice through reflection and professional development. According to our study, the critical factors influencing the development of innovative behavior are the age and professional experience of teachers. ANOVA showed that age had a moderate statistically significant effect on all scales, while professional experience affected DLOQ (F = 3.95, p = 0.05, d = 0.42) and IWB (F = 4.32, p = 0.04, d = 0.44) with a medium effect. Gender had no statistically significant effect on any of the scales, corresponding to a small effect.

Another Chinese study developed an AI-based chatbot in the context of guided discovery learning (GDL) and integrated a motivational model to create an innovative learning strategy30. The results showed a significant improvement in the knowledge of future teachers and positive motivation across all categories of the motivational model. The digitalization-based approach enhanced student attention, perceived relevance, self-confidence, and learning satisfaction. This work also emphasizes the use of digital tools and innovative teaching approaches, which significantly improved knowledge and motivation levels (mean scores on the UWES-9 scale increased from 4.10 to 4.85, p < 0.001).

A study by Kazakhstani scholars revealed that students from the Kazakh National University, named after Al-Farabi, demonstrated high competence in using digital technologies for communication and sharing educational content. However, they indicated a lower level of competence in other key areas of digital literacy necessary for academic activities31. Statistical analysis [t(381) = 4.562, p < 0.00001] identified a significant difference in the degree and purposes of digital technology usage among students. These results partially align with ours, as this study also observed significant improvements in participants’ knowledge and motivation, particularly regarding the use of digital tools. Specifically, the UWES-9 scale showed a significant increase in the mean score from 4.10 to 4.85 (p < 0.001), confirming the positive impact of digital technologies on motivation and engagement. New empirical data were obtained regarding personality predictors and the activation of the existing scientific-innovative potential of future teachers in Kazakhstani universities32.

Conclusions

The study demonstrated a significant improvement in results after completing the training program aimed at developing the scientific and innovative potential of future teachers, as evidenced by statistically significant changes across all three scales (DLOQ, UWES-9, and IWB). All participants, regardless of their initial level, showed improvement in professional self-efficacy, engagement, and the ability to adopt innovative behavior. These changes are highly statistically significant, based on 95% CI, p-values, and effect size, indicating the success of the program.

The results of the analysis of variance (ANOVA) showed that the factors of age and professional experience significantly influenced the dynamics of the results. Age had a significant impact on all three scales (DLOQ, UWES-9, IWB). The effect of professional experience was statistically significant only for DLOQ and IWB, while gender did not significantly impact the results. This suggests that to achieve positive changes, it is important to consider the age and experience of participants when designing training programs.

The practical value of the study lies in the development of an effective educational program that can substantially improve the professional skills of future and current teachers. The uniqueness of the program lies in the integration of the concept of continuous professional development and the SECI model to support knowledge creation and exchange among teachers, which directly stimulates the development of competencies and innovative behavior: the program combines digital and innovative technologies, professional development, and reflective practices, providing a comprehensive approach. Particularly important is that the program is successful for different participant groups, regardless of their initial knowledge level. The scientific value lies in the fact that the implementation of the developed program will not only enhance teachers’ competency levels but also foster a sustainable culture of innovative learning, contributing to the development of the modern educational system.

The study may be useful for Kazakhstani educational institutions that develop teacher training courses and specialists who evaluate the effectiveness of educational programs. The results obtained allow for the optimization of professional development programs, taking into account the level of knowledge, age, and experience of participants, which increases the effectiveness of training and contributes to better results in the preparation of future teachers.

Prospects for further research include a detailed study of the impact of age and experience on the perception of innovative educational technologies and the ability to adapt to new methods. In addition, expanding research to different educational levels and countries will help identify cultural and institutional differences that influence the implementation of innovative approaches. This will lay the groundwork for the development of international professional development programs based on best practices and promote the exchange of experience between educators from different countries.

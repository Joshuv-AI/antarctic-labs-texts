Hierarchical multi-attention neural networks for sensor fault diagnosis and mitigation in digital twins
Authors: Long Pan, Hong Li, Xifeng Li, Dongjie Bi, Libiao Peng, Yongle Xie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Digital twin technology has emerged as a quintessential facilitator of industrial digitization within the paradigm of Industry 5.0. Nonetheless, its effectiveness is inherently dependent on the establishment of robust sensor fault management within expansive Internet of Things ecosystems. This paper focuses on the sensor fault diagnosis and mitigation in digital twins. To address this challenge, we propose a hierarchical architecture predicated on the parallel fusion of multi-level attention neural networks, comprising five distinct blocks: the input vector block, the virtual sensor block, the residual calculation block, the fault diagnosis block, and the decision block. In particular, the virtual sensor is realized through a neural-network-based estimator, which integrates a multi-head-attention-based Transformer encoder for modeling long-term dependencies and a globally-attention-optimized bidirectional gated recurrent unit network for spatial feature extraction. The fault diagnosis block is distinguished by a dual-branch convolutional neural network architecture that synthesizes squeeze-and-excitation operations with global attention mechanisms, thereby enabling the extraction of fault-sensitive features from the residuals that emerge between the actual sensor readings and those of the virtual sensor. Ultimately, an autonomous multiplexer system is introduced to dynamically substitute faulty sensors with their virtual counterparts, effectively preventing the propagation of faults. Experimental validation conducted using two publicly accessible datasets demonstrates noteworthy improvements in fault detection accuracy and system resilience.

Introduction

Within the cyber-physical ecosystem of Industry 5.0, digital twin (DT) technology has emerged as a pivotal innovation driving industrial digitization1. This advanced paradigm establishes a virtual counterpart that replicates both structural and functional attributes of physical entities or processes (termed physical twins), facilitating real-time data exchange between tangible assets and their digital representations. Through this symbiotic relationship, practitioners gain capabilities to model intricate system dynamics, assess potential operational outcomes under predefined conditions, and execute remote management protocols encompassing monitoring, adjustment, and control functions.

The burgeoning expansion of Internet of Things (IoT) and Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) systems has emerged as a pivotal catalyst for the implementation of digital twins (DT), enabling a seamless quasi-real-time data interchange between digital and physical twins. Advanced sensing architectures, featuring distributed sensor arrays, meticulously monitor a plethora of multidimensional operational parameters of physical assets, incessantly transmitting these multifaceted datasets to their corresponding digital counterparts for predictive modeling, scenario analysis, and the execution of optimization strategies2. This data-driven paradigm, while transformative, introduces significant challenges pertaining to reliability. Malfunctions in sensors or corruption of data can reverberate through interconnected systems, potentially culminating in cascading failures that jeopardize operational integrity3.

The effective implementation of digital twins necessitates persistent quality surveillance and calibration of incoming sensor data streams prior to their integration into the virtual model. Contemporary research emphasizes the critical role of intelligent fault management systems – particularly those addressing the triad of anomaly identification, source localization, and error correction in sensing apparatus – as vital safeguards for data-driven cyber-physical systems. While traditional hardware redundancy solutions employing triplicate sensor arrays with majority voting mechanisms offer a conceptually simple diagnostic approach, their practical application incurs substantial infrastructure costs4,5. This economic constraint has driven accelerated exploration of model-based alternatives that exploit inherent inter-sensor correlations through advanced analytical frameworks, demonstrating comparable diagnostic capability without requiring physical redundancy6–8.

Contemporary data-driven diagnostic methodologies heavily utilize computational intelligence techniques, with machine learning and deep learning algorithms becoming predominant solutions for diverse equipment malfunction identification scenarios9–12. Current research demonstrates varied implementations across different technical domains. Within machine learning frameworks, support-vector classifiers have demonstrated effectiveness in identifying malfunctioning nodes within wireless sensing architectures, though exhibiting scalability constraints when handling intricate data patterns13–15. Dimensionality reduction techniques like principal component decomposition have been successfully applied to IoT sensory ecosystems16. Probabilistic graphical models (specifically dynamic Bayesian architectures) achieved partial success in vehicular network diagnostics but proved inefficient when processing multidimensional information streams17,18.

In recent advancements of deep learning methodologies for IoT sensory system fault diagnosis, researchers have investigated diverse neural network architectures including multilayer perceptrons (MLPs)19–21, recurrent neural networks (RNNs)22–24, convolutional neural networks (CNNs)25–27, long short term memory (LSTM)28, and Transformer29–31. Autoencoders (AEs) represent a sophisticated class of neural networks (NNs) characterized by the presence of both an encoder and a decoder. These networks are adept at generating compressed representations by transforming input data into a latent-space format, thereby facilitating the extraction of meaningful features from complex datasets. Aboelwafa et al. proposed a method for anomaly (i.e., fault) detection and accommodation based on AEs has been developed for IIoT system32. Although data-driven methodologies have gained significant prominence in contemporary research due to their capacity to eliminate the necessity for explicit sensor relationship modeling (in contrast to conventional model-driven frameworks exemplified in these references33–35), these approaches remain constrained by three principal limitations. First, the operational efficacy of elementary machine learning algorithms demonstrates strong dependency on three critical system attributes: dynamic nonlinearities, dimensional complexity, and heterogeneous data characteristics. Second, conventional neural architectures with limited hidden layers frequently exhibit constrained generalization capacity, a phenomenon primarily attributable to their insufficient representational depth for extracting sophisticated data patterns. Third, these methods face substantial scalability challenges where computational resource requirements escalate in nonlinear proportion to operational scale, typically accompanied by correlated diminishing predictive accuracy.

Notably within digital twin implementations, studies have introduced a modular sensor fault detection, isolation and accommodation system employing MLP networks36. The foundational design specifically addressed single-sensor fault scenarios through real-time detection, precise fault identification, and automated data correction mechanisms. Subsequent enhancements to this framework incorporated multi-tiered decision-making protocols and regulatory components, enabling the system to concurrently handle multiple sensor malfunctions37. This extended configuration effectively mitigates fault propagation risks within interconnected IoT architectures while maintaining system integrity during simultaneous fault events.

Digital twins are poised to emerge as high-fidelity digital replicas of complex physical systems in the coming years. To achieve this, the deployment of ubiquitous sensor networks across physical infrastructures has become an essential prerequisite for real-time data acquisition. Nevertheless, the integrity of such large-scale sensor data streams hinges critically on the implementation of a robust sensor fault tolerant framework. Only through continuous monitoring and validation by the framework can the digital representation maintain synchronization with its physical counterpart while ensuring measurement reliability across all operational phases.

The attention mechanism in neural networks is a sophisticated technique that mimics human visual and cognitive processes38,39. It empowers neural networks to focus on critical segments of input data during processing. By incorporating the attention mechanism, these networks can autonomously discern and prioritize essential information within the input, thereby enhancing both the model’s performance and its capacity for generalization. Although neural networks based on attention mechanism have recently been applied to anomaly detection, there has been rare exploration in the aspects of sensor fault identification and fault tolerance in digital twin systems.

Accordingly, in this paper, we propose a hierarchical architecture based on parallel fusion of multi-level attention neural networks for sensor fault identification and fault tolerance in the digital twins of large-scale Internet of Things networks. The entire architecture consists of five blocks, namely the input vector block, the virtual sensor block, the residual calculation block, the fault diagnosis block and the decision block. The virtual sensor block employs neural networks operating in parallel configuration, fulfilling sensor modeling based on data-driven. The residual calculation block dynamically generates the residual vector through real-time comparison between observed sensor measurements and the virtual sensor values. The fault diagnosis block is implemented by a CNNs-based classifier, which executes fault analysis through the residual signals. The decision output block is responsible for substituting faulty sensor by virtual sensor, thereby preventing the spread of faults across the system. The main innovations of this work are summarized as follows.

1) To effectively model sensor behavior from time series samples, we propose a parallel fusion approach utilizing multi-level attention neural networks, comprising two parallel computational branches. Sensor readings processed through a sliding window are fed into a Transformer encoder layer leveraging multi-head attention to capture long-term dependency features, while the same input concurrently traverses a global attention-optimized bidirectional gated recurrent unit (BiGRU) network to capture comprehensive spatial features. Feature fusion is achieved via a cross-attention mechanism that calculates attention weights to emphasize critical features and enhance integration, with final high-precision predictions generated through a fully connected layer. Existing studies have adopted single-branch architectures for sensor time series modeling, such as standalone Transformer29–31, BiGRU40, or LSTM28. Transformer excels at long-term temporal dependency modeling but neglects fine-grained spatial feature extraction29,30, while BiGRU/LSTM capture sequential correlations but struggle with long-range dependencies in large-scale sensor time series28,40. In contrast, our parallel fusion framework synergistically integrates the strengths of both models, and the cross-attention mechanism further enables dynamic information interaction between temporal and spatial features. This addresses the inherent tradeoffs of single-branch designs reported in the aforementioned literatures, and significantly improves representational capacity compared to conventional concatenation or weighted summation fusion methods widely used in existing sensor modeling studies.

2) A lightweight parallel CNN architecture with a multi-level attention mechanism is proposed for sensor fault identification. Residuals between real and virtual sensor readings are input into two parallel CNN branches: one integrated with squeeze-and-excitation (SE) operations and the other embedded with a global attention mechanism. Global spatial features and local discriminative features extracted by the two branches are fused via cross-attention, followed by a fully connected layer for classification. Existing CNN-based fault diagnosis methods for sensor systems25–27 either lack effective channel-wise feature calibration or rely solely on local receptive fields without global context awareness. Although recent works have incorporated SE operations or attention mechanisms into CNNs for fault diagnosis41, they typically adopt sequential rather than parallel architectures, which limits the simultaneous extraction of complementary feature types. Our parallel design not only mitigates the channel redundancy issue of traditional CNNs through SE operations but also enhances the model’s focus on fault-sensitive regions via global attention. Compared with single-branch CNNs with attention or SE-only CNNs41, our method achieves superior performance in capturing multi-scale fault features from residual signals, which is critical for subtle sensor fault identification.

3) Real sensor readings, virtual sensor estimates, and fault diagnosis results are transmitted to a decision block implemented as an autonomous multiplexer, which dynamically replaces faulty sensors with their virtual counterparts in real time based on diagnosis outcomes. Existing sensor fault accommodation methods42 often rely on pre-defined replacement rules or offline calibration, leading to delays in fault mitigation or suboptimal adaptability to dynamic fault scenarios. Although modular data-driven architectures have been proposed for digital twin sensor fault tolerance36, these methods lack real-time adaptive substitution mechanisms and require manual intervention for complex fault combinations. Our multiplexer system achieves end-to-end dynamic replacement without human involvement, which addresses the propagation risk of faults in interconnected IoT ecosystems more effectively than the static replacement strategies in reference36,42. For complex fault scenarios common in industrial practice, the autonomous multiplexer can realize parallel and real-time substitution of multiple faulty sensors, which is a key improvement over the existing fault accommodation methods for digital twin sensor systems.

Finally, Section “Conclusion” offers a comprehensive conclusion, summarizing the key findings of the article.

Block diagram of the proposed architecture.

As depicted in Fig. 1, the architecture of the proposed method consists of 5 blocks: the input vector block, the virtual sensor block, the residual calculation block, the fault diagnosis block and the decision output block. The input for the virtual sensor block is the measurements obtained from physical sensors within a sliding window. The virtual sensor block constructs sensor models for every physical sensor in the system. The fault diagnosis block provides insights into the fault status of each sensor, based on the discrepancies observed between each physical sensor and its corresponding virtual counterpart. The decision-making process is responsible for detecting faulty conditions and pinpointing the faulty sensors. Once the faulty sensor or sensors are detected, the proposed architecture isolates these faulty sensors (i.e., marks them as being in a failure state). During the entire process, it replaces the measurements of the faulty sensors with the relevant virtual readings in the digital twin. The following section presents a high-level specification for each component of the architecture.

Unlike single-branch attention-based models (e.g., Transformer, BiGRU) that prioritize either temporal dependency capture or computational efficiency, the proposed architecture adopts a parallel fusion of Transformer encoder (multi-head attention) and BiGRU (global attention), enabling simultaneous capture of long-term temporal dependencies and bidirectional spatial features while balancing efficiency.

Fault Diagnosis Mechanism: Existing CNN-based methods or Autoencoders lack targeted feature extraction for multi-fault coupling scenarios. The proposed dual-branch CNN integrates squeeze-and-excitation (channel-wise attention) and global attention (spatial-wise attention), enhancing the discriminability of overlapping fault patterns.

The estimator is actualized through the parallel integration of a Transformer encoder and a BiGRU network under multi-level attention. As shown in Fig. 2, the entire network can be divided into two branches, i.e. the multi-head attention Transformer encoder branch and the Global attention BiGRU branch. The first branch is implemented by a Transformer encoder based on the multi-head self-attention mechanism, whose purpose is to capture the long-term features from the sensor time series. The second branch is realized by global-attention-optimized BiGRU networks, aiming to capture bidirectional sequential dependencies.

Multi-head attention transformer encoder branch

The Transformer encoder employs a cascaded configuration of six homogeneous processing layers. Each layer integrates two computational sub-layers: a multi-head self-attention architecture for contextual pattern extraction, followed by a position-wise dense feed-forward neural network that applies nonlinear transformations to spatial feature. The core component is the self-attention mechanism43.

The core function of the self-attention layer is to automatically identify important features in the time series. This is achieved by calculating the weighted associations between each time step and the rest of the time steps in the sequence. The weights are generated by three matrices: the query matrix Q, the key matrix K, and the value matrix V. Cross-attention-based feature fusion

The cross attention mechanism combines the outputs from above two branches, enabling the model to utilize multiple features simultaneously. This enhances the model’s feature representation, allowing it to identify more complex patterns in sensor time series. Furthermore, the cross attention mechanism supports the model in dynamically adjusting the importance of various features, which strengthens its adaptability and robustness across diverse sensor time series. The cross-attention mechanism is the core of our multi-level attention parallel fusion strategy, differing fundamentally from traditional feature fusion methods (e.g., concatenation, weighted summation) in both working principle and performance impact.

Traditional Feature Concatenation: Simply appends feature maps from different branches without considering feature relevance. This approach often introduces redundant information and increases computational complexity, as irrelevant features are treated equally to critical ones. For sensor data with strong temporal-spatial correlations, concatenation fails to highlight complementary patterns between temporal (Transformer-derived) and spatial (BiGRU-derived) features, leading to suboptimal feature representation.

Weighted Summation: Assigns fixed or manually tuned weights to different branches (e.g., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H_f= \alpha H_1+(1-\alpha )H_2$$\end{document}Hf=αH1+(1-α)H2) to balance feature contributions. However, fixed weights cannot adapt to dynamic sensor data characteristics (e.g., varying fault types or noise levels), and manual tuning is labor-intensive and lacks generalizability. In scenarios with concurrent faults or data corruption, weighted summation may overemphasize noisy features or underutilize discriminative ones.

Cross-Attention Fusion: Dynamically calculates attention weights between features from different branches, enabling the model to autonomously identify and prioritize complementary information. As shown in Equation (10), cross-attention computes the relevance between each element in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H_1$$\end{document}H1 and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H_2$$\end{document}H2, then generates weighted feature maps that emphasize meaningful correlations. This adaptive weighting mechanism: 1) suppresses redundant or noisy features by assigning low attention weights; 2) amplifies complementary patterns (e.g., long-term temporal trends and local spatial anomalies) that are critical for sensor modeling; and 3) maintains feature dimensionality efficiency compared to concatenation, reducing computational overhead.

The impact of cross-attention on model performance is twofold: For the virtual sensor block, it fuses temporal and spatial features to improve prediction accuracy of sensor readings; for the fault diagnosis block, it integrates global and local fault-sensitive features from dual CNN branches, enhancing the model’s ability to distinguish subtle faults. Without cross-attention, parallel branches would operate independently, leading to fragmented feature representation and degraded performance in complex fault scenarios.

Numerical results

Datasets description

This study forms hybrid datasets by superimposing simulated faults onto real measurement data. It is worth noting that injecting synthetic faults into datasets containing real measurement values has become a widely recognized method in the performance evaluation of sensor fault-tolerant systems. This strategy not only enables us to conduct comprehensive statistical analysis and evaluate the system’s performance from a panoramic perspective but also creates diverse fault scenarios, providing strong support for more in-depth research.

Measurements

For real-world measurements, we utilized two publicly available datasets.

1) Electricity Transformer Temperature(ETT)45: The ETT is an important index to measure the long-term operation state of power system. This dataset was collected from two independent power transformers and stored in.csv files.

2) Water Tank Pressure (WTP)46: The WTP dataset is collected from a network of 100 interconnected tanks. Pressure sensors are used to measure the pressure within each tank, which reflects the internal water level. If the water level in a tank falls below a specified threshold, the tank initiates a refill process until it reaches full capacity. Additionally, the flow rate between two linked tanks depends nonlinearly on both the pressure difference and the spatial distance between them.

Bias faults are commonly induced by sensor aging, improper installation alignment, electromagnetic interference, or calibration drift in long-term operation. Studying bias faults is critical for avoiding misoperation of protective devices, ensuring accurate condition assessment of industrial equipment, and reducing unnecessary maintenance costs caused by false diagnostics.

This variation stems from the intrinsic characteristics of the faults and the model’s feature extraction mechanisms:

As multiplicative faults (Equation 19), gain faults introduce a consistent scaling of sensor signals, leading to abrupt and large-magnitude residuals between real and virtual sensor readings. These distinct residual patterns are easily captured by the dual-branch CNN’s global attention mechanism, which prioritizes spatial feature regions with significant amplitude changes. Additionally, the squeeze-and-excitation (SE) operation amplifies channel-wise features associated with scaled signals, further enhancing discriminability.

Drift faults are time-varying and progressive (Equation 18), with residuals increasing gradually over m samples before stabilizing. This gently residual evolution results in less distinct spatial-temporal features compared to gain/bias faults. The model’s sliding window may partially miss early-stage drift signals, and the cross-attention fusion of local/global features is less effective for subtle, incremental changes–leading to slightly lower recall rates.

Regarding the applicability to complex fault scenarios (e.g., concurrent multi-sensor faults and fault coupling) commonly encountered in actual industrial settings, the proposed hierarchical architecture inherently possesses structural advantages that support potential extension to these scenarios. For concurrent multi-sensor faults, the framework is designed to model N sensors independently through the virtual sensor block, where each physical sensor corresponds to a dedicated data-driven estimator. The residual calculation block computes discrepancies for each sensor individually, and the fault diagnosis block outputs a fault status vector \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$D[n]\in \mathbb {R}^N$$\end{document}D[n]∈RN that simultaneously indicates the health condition of all N sensors. This per-sensor processing paradigm, combined with the autonomous multiplexer in the decision block, enables the system to detect, localize, and replace multiple faulty sensors in parallel without mutual interference. Notably, the multi-level attention mechanisms in both the virtual sensor and fault diagnosis blocks enhance the model’s ability to capture distinct features of concurrent faults–even when multiple sensors malfunction simultaneously–by dynamically weighting the contributions of temporal and spatial features for each sensor via cross-attention fusion.

For fault coupling scenarios, where fault characteristics of different sensors or fault types overlap and interfere with each other, the proposed model’s feature discrimination and anti-interference capabilities are strengthened by its dual-branch attention-driven design. In the virtual sensor block, the parallel fusion of a Transformer encoder (capturing long-term temporal dependencies) and a globally optimized BiGRU (extracting bidirectional spatial correlations) via cross-attention effectively models complex inter-sensor relationships, reducing ambiguity caused by fault coupling. In the fault diagnosis block, the integration of squeeze-and-excitation (SE) operations (modeling channel-wise interdependencies) and global attention mechanisms enables the network to isolate subtle, coupled fault features from residual signals. The hierarchical feature fusion across branches further suppresses irrelevant noise induced by coupling effects, enhancing the model’s ability to distinguish overlapping fault patterns. These structural merits lay a solid foundation for extending the proposed model to complex fault scenarios.

Conclusion

This paper presents a hierarchical architecture for sensor fault tolerance in digital twins, addressing three critical challenges: real-time anomaly detection, precise fault localization, and dynamic sensor substitution. The proposed framework leverages the parallel fusion of multi-level attention neural networks to enhance the reliability of digital twin systems in industrial IoT environments. The core innovation lies in the integration of two parallel computational branches within the virtual sensor block. The multi-head attention Transformer encoder effectively captures long-term temporal dependencies across sensor data streams, while the global-attention-optimized BiGRU network extracts bidirectional spatial features. This dual-branch architecture enables high-fidelity modeling of sensor behavior through cross-attention-based feature fusion, achieving robust predictions even under complex temporal-spatial correlations. For fault diagnosis, the dual-branch CNN architecture synergizes squeeze-and-excitation operations with global attention mechanisms to amplify fault-sensitive features from residuals. This design significantly improves the discriminative power for identifying subtle anomalies while maintaining computational efficiency. The autonomous multiplexer system further ensures operational continuity by dynamically replacing faulty sensors with virtual counterparts, thereby mitigating fault propagation risks. Experimental validation on two publicly available datasets demonstrates superior performance in fault detection accuracy and system resilience compared to traditional methods.

Future work will focus on advancing the practical applicability of the proposed method in real-time industrial environments, with specific emphasis on dynamic sensor networks and computational resource constraints. Industrial sensor networks are often dynamic–sensors may be added (e.g., new equipment integration), removed (e.g., maintenance), or repositioned (e.g., production line adjustments). To adapt to such changes, we will develop an incremental learning framework that updates the virtual sensor and fault diagnosis models in real time without retraining on the entire historical dataset. This will involve: 1) freezing pre-trained feature extractors (Transformer encoder and BiGRU) to preserve learned patterns; 2) using knowledge distillation to transfer existing model knowledge to the updated network, avoiding catastrophic forgetting; and 3) designing a lightweight adaptation layer that only updates parameters related to new/modified sensors. Additionally, federated learning will be integrated to enable distributed model updates across edge nodes, ensuring data privacy while accommodating network dynamics.

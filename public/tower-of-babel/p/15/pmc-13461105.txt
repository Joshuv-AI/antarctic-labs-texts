Amylin-based obesity therapy: a meta-analysis of Cagrilintide and CagriSema versus placebo
Authors: Mohammad Yaseen, Akmal Ameer, Zulfiqar Ali, Jamil Ahmed Jamali, Aneesh Kumar, Muhammad Basit Ali Siddiqui, Payal Kumari, Muhammad Hassaan, Pooja Nanwani, Muhammad Abdul Basit, Nimerta Lohana, Rehan Qureshi, Mahir Tesfaye
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Obesity remains a major global health challenge, driving demand for effective pharmacotherapies. Cagrilintide, a once-weekly amylin receptor agonist, and its fixed-dose combination with semaglutide (CagriSema) represent novel therapeutic approaches. This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated their efficacy and safety in adults with overweight or obesity.Methods:Four databases were searched from inception to March 2026. Eligible randomized controlled trials (RCTs) assessed Cagrilintide monotherapy or CagriSema versus placebo. Outcomes included percentage and absolute body weight change, waist circumference, blood pressure, HbA1c, and adverse events. Data were pooled using random-effects models in RevMan, with results expressed as mean differences (MD) or risk ratios (RR) with 95% confidence intervals.Results:Four RCTs encompassing 5425 participants were included. Cagrilintide monotherapy produced significant reductions in body weight (MD: −6.08%; MD: −5.89 kg) and blood pressure, without meaningful HbA1c improvement. CagriSema significantly reduced body weight (MD: −5.98%; MD: −4.68 kg), waist circumference (MD: −10.91 cm), systolic blood pressure, and HbA1c. Both treatments showed modest but statistically significant increases in adverse events. Substantial heterogeneity was observed across most outcomes.Conclusion:Cagrilintide-based therapies produce clinically meaningful weight loss and cardiometabolic improvements. CagriSema demonstrates additional glycemic benefits over monotherapy. Larger, longer-duration trials are needed to confirm long-term efficacy and safety.

Introduction

Obesity is a chronic and complex disease that is a global health problem, with more than one billion people affected by it, being a cause of type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, dyslipidemia, hypertension, and premature death[1]. In spite of many years of public health campaigns against obesity, it still shows an increasing prevalence rate among people in both low- and high-income communities, placing further strain on individuals and healthcare systems alike[2]. Although dietary changes are an important part of obesity treatment, pharmacotherapy has become an essential addition in cases where lifestyle changes are not effective[3].

Pharmacologic therapy for obesity has seen a paradigm shift toward incretin-based agents. Semaglutide 2.4 mg is a once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist that showed an average body weight loss of about 15% in the STEP program and quickly emerged as a key player in anti-obesity pharmacotherapy[4]. Tirzepatide, a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist, has also been able to produce body weight losses greater than 20% through the SURMOUNT program, thus raising the bar for pharmacologic obesity management[5]. This has spurred efforts toward combining drug targets aimed at modulating overlapping neurohormonal systems involved in energy homeostasis.

Cagrilintide marks an innovative mechanistic approach in this treatment regimen. This long-acting, acylated form of the pancreatic peptide hormone amylin, meant for once-weekly subcutaneous use, is an advancement from its preexisting version, pramlintide, which necessitates several doses a day. Unlike pramlintide, Cagrilintide works as a nonselective agonist of amylin receptors (AMY1-3) and calcitonin receptors (CTR), causing significant and prolonged inhibition of appetite through homeostatic and hedonic pathways within the brain[6]. The data collected from various preclinical studies and phase trials showed that its dual receptor activation results in a superior effect in terms of weight loss compared to pramlintide, with improved safety[7]. In combination with semaglutide in a fixed-dose therapy called CagriSema, it is thought that the synergistic action of amylin analog-induced satiety and GLP-1-induced appetite suppression and delay in gastric emptying will be more effective in reducing body mass, taking into account the adaptive appetite response that compromises the effectiveness of monotherapies[8]

Cagrilintide and CagriSema’s clinical development has been rather fast-tracked. The Phase 1b and Phase 2 trials provided dose-response relationship data and efficacy indicators over the entire dose range, eventually leading to the completion of the Phase 3 REDEFINE program. However, there has yet to be a systematic review of randomized controlled trial (RCT) data for the full scope of metabolic benefits of the compounds, whether administered alone or in combination. Trials differ in many aspects, such as dosing regimens and duration of treatment, making any reliable estimate of overall efficacy and safety challenging to produce from current literature data alone.

This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Cagrilintide, alone or in combination with semaglutide (CagriSema), compared with placebo or active pharmacologic comparators for weight management in adults with overweight or obesity. Specifically, we sought to compare Cagrilintide-based therapies with placebo or active pharmacologic comparators in RCTs and to quantify their effects on body weight, glycemic outcomes, cardiometabolic risk factors, and treatment-emergent adverse events. By synthesizing the available phase 2 and phase 3 evidence, this study aims to define the clinical benefits and risks of Cagrilintide-based regimens and clarify their potential role in contemporary obesity pharmacotherapy.

Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed the PRISMA 2020 reporting guidelines[9] and was conducted in accordance with the AMSTAR 2 quality standards[10,11]. Any use of AI tools was restricted to language editing, consistent with the TITAN Guidelines 2025[12]; no AI contribution was made to the design, analysis, or interpretation. The review was prospectively registered in PROSPERO (CRD420261392878).

Search strategy and study selection

Four electronic databases were searched from inception to 30 March 2026: PubMed, ScienceDirect, Cochrane Library, and ClinicalTrials.gov. The reference lists of the retrieved articles and relevant trial registries were manually screened to identify additional eligible records. Search terms were developed around the core concepts of “Cagrilintide,” “CagriSema,” “obesity,” and “overweight,” combining both MeSH vocabulary and free-text keywords. The complete search strings for each database are listed in Supplemental Digital Content Table S1, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277.

Records were de-duplicated and screened in two sequential stages: title/abstract and full-text review, by two independent reviewers. Any disagreement at either stage was resolved through discussion; unresolved disagreements were settled by a third reviewer. The exclusion reasons at the full-text stage were recorded for each rejected study.

Eligibility and exclusion criteria

Eligible studies were RCTs that: (1) enrolled adults (≥18 years) with overweight or obesity, with or without type 2 diabetes; (2) tested Cagrilintide as monotherapy or in combination with semaglutide (CagriSema) against a placebo comparator; and (3) reported extractable quantitative data on at least one pre-specified efficacy or safety outcome.

The efficacy outcomes of interest were body weight (percentage and absolute change), waist circumference, blood pressure, and HbA1c levels. Safety outcomes included adverse and serious adverse events. Inclusion was restricted to English-language publications with numerical data.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) observational studies, (2) non-randomized designs, (3) trials lacking a placebo arm, and (4) studies evaluating agents other than CagriSema or Cagrilintide monotherapy.

Data synthesis

Data extraction was performed independently by two reviewers using a pre-piloted, standardized form. The extracted fields covered study design, participant characteristics, intervention dose and duration, follow-up length, and all relevant outcome measures.

The primary outcomes were the percentage change in body weight, absolute weight change, and waist circumference. Secondary outcomes included systolic and diastolic blood pressure, glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), adverse events, and serious adverse events.

Risk of bias assessment

Each included trial was assessed for risk of bias using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 1), which evaluates seven domains: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data, selective outcome reporting, and other potential sources of bias[13]. Each domain was classified as low, unclear, or high risk. Assessments were made independently by two reviewers, and disagreements were resolved through consensus discussion.

Statistical analysis

All meta-analyses were conducted in RevMan using a random-effects model chosen beforehand. This model helps account for differences between studies in terms of populations, doses, and follow-up times. Continuous outcomes were pooled as mean differences (MD) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Dichotomous outcomes were pooled as risk ratios (RR) with 95% CIs.

Cagrilintide monotherapy and CagriSema are pharmacologically unique, with distinct mechanisms and varying effects. They were evaluated in separate pooled comparisons – Cagrilintide against placebo and CagriSema against placebo – to maintain clinical uniformity. Each participant’s data were included in only one of these comparisons. This method resulted in two independent forest plots for each outcome.

Between-study heterogeneity was quantified using the I2 statistic[14]; values above 50% were considered to indicate substantial heterogeneity. The stability of the pooled estimates was examined using a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, performed separately for each intervention comparison. Publication bias assessment and meta-regression were not performed because fewer than 10 studies contributed to any single pooled comparison. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Study selection and characteristics

The study selection process followed PRISMA guidelines, as illustrated in Figure 1. A total of 374 records were initially identified through database and registry searches. After removing 58 duplicates, 316 records were screened, leading to the exclusion of 301 records. Of the 15 reports assessed for eligibility, 11 were excluded for being review articles, conference abstracts, or failing to meet inclusion criteria. Ultimately, four studies were included in the final review and meta-analysis. Figure 1.PRISMA flow chart.

PRISMA flow chart.

Across the four trials, a total of 5425 participants were enrolled, comprising 706 in the phase 2 study, 96 in the phase 1b study, and 4623 across the two phase 3a trials. The study designs were consistently randomized, double-blind, and placebo-controlled, with treatment durations ranging from 20 weeks to 68 weeks. Participants were generally adults with a BMI of 27 kg/m2 or higher, representing both healthy individuals and those with comorbidities such as type 2 diabetes.

The baseline clinical parameters were well-balanced across treatment arms within each study. The mean age of participants ranged from 37.0 to 56.5 years, and the mean body weight at baseline varied from 92.1 kg to 111.0 kg. Mean glycated hemoglobin levels were approximately 5.2%–5.6% for most cohorts, though the Garvey 2025 study included a population with a higher mean baseline of 8.0%. Other baseline metrics, including waist circumference, systolic blood pressure, and diastolic blood pressure, were similarly distributed between the Cagrilintide, CagriSema, and placebo groups. Risk of bias assessment

The methodological quality of the included trials was generally high, as illustrated in Figure 2A and B, with a comprehensive assessment summary detailed in Supplemental Digital Content Table S2, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277. All studies (D.C.W. Lau 2021; L.B. Enebo 2021; M.J. Davies 2025; and W.T. Garvey 2025) exhibited a low risk of selection, detection, attrition, and reporting bias. While performance bias was low in most trials, Lau (2021) was rated as “unclear” due to insufficient reporting on personnel blinding. Notably, the 2025 trials by Davies and Garvey maintained a low-risk profile across all domains, whereas the 2021 studies by Lau and Enebo were flagged for high risk in the “Other Bias” category. Overall, the evidence base reflects a strong methodological foundation, particularly within the most recent literature. Figure 2.(A) Risk of bias graph. (B) Risk of bias summary.

Outcome measures

Cagrilintide monotherapy

Weight outcomes

Cagrilintide reduced body weight to a statistically significant degree relative to placebo. The pooled percent change in body weight was −6.08% (95% CI: −8.02 to −4.14; P < 0.00001; I2 = 89%; Fig. 3A), and the corresponding absolute reduction was −5.89 kg (95% CI: −7.18 to −4.59; P < 0.00001; I2 = 84%; Fig. 4A). Substantial heterogeneity was present in both estimates, indicating variability in effect sizes across trials. The effect on waist circumference did not reach statistical significance (MD = −4.85 cm, 95% CI: −9.81 to 0.12; P = 0.06; I2 = 98%; Fig. 5A), and the wide CI alongside very high heterogeneity reflects considerable uncertainty around the effect on central adiposity. Cardiometabolic outcomes

With respect to cardiometabolic parameters, Cagrilintide was associated with small but statistically significant reductions in systolic blood pressure (MD = −3.30 mm Hg, 95% CI: −5.78 to −0.81; P = 0.009; I2 = 77%) (Supplemental Digital Content Figure S1, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277) and diastolic blood pressure (MD = −1.70 mm Hg, 95% CI: −3.05 to −0.34; P = 0.01; I2 = 70%) (Supplemental Digital Content Figure S2, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277) compared with placebo. No significant effect on glycated hemoglobin was observed (MD = −0.06%, 95% CI: −0.30 to 0.18; P = 0.61; I2 = 0%) (Supplemental Digital Content Figure S3, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277), indicating that Cagrilintide monotherapy did not produce meaningful glycemic improvement beyond its effect on body weight.

Safety outcomes

Regarding safety, Cagrilintide was associated with a statistically significant increase in overall adverse events relative to placebo (RR = 1.16, 95% CI: 1.05–1.29; P = 0.005; I2 = 71%) (Supplemental Digital Content Figure S4, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277). A possible increase in serious adverse events was also observed (RR = 1.46, 95% CI: 1.00–2.13; P = 0.05; I2 = 0%) (Supplemental Digital Content Figure S5, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277), though the lower confidence limit at the null introduces uncertainty regarding a true excess. The overall tolerability profile was considered acceptable within the study durations.

CagriSema combination therapy

Weight outcomes

CagriSema produced a statistically significant reduction in body weight as a percentage of baseline compared with placebo (MD = −5.98%, 95% CI: −10.64 to −1.32; P = 0.01; I2 = 100%; Fig. 3B), along with a significant reduction in absolute body weight (MD = −4.68 kg, 95% CI: −7.08 to −2.28; P = 0.0001; I2 = 99%; Fig. 4B). Maximal heterogeneity in both estimates indicates marked variability in effect sizes across contributing trials, and these pooled figures should be interpreted accordingly. CagriSema also produced a statistically significant reduction in waist circumference (MD = −10.91 cm, 95% CI: −16.00 to −5.81; P < 0.0001; I2 = 98%; Fig. 5B), the magnitude of which was numerically greater than that observed with Cagrilintide monotherapy.

Cardiometabolic outcomes

CagriSema was associated with a significant reduction in systolic blood pressure versus placebo (MD = −5.52 mm Hg, 95% CI: −8.06 to −2.98; P < 0.05; I2 = 81%) (Supplemental Digital Content Figure S6, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277). The reduction in diastolic blood pressure did not reach statistical significance (MD = −1.92 mm Hg, 95% CI: −4.27 to 0.43; P = 0.11; I2 = 93%) (Supplemental Digital Content Figure S7, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277), with very high heterogeneity limiting confidence in this estimate. In contrast to Cagrilintide monotherapy, CagriSema produced a small but statistically significant reduction in HbA1c (MD = −0.25%, 95% CI: −0.45 to −0.06; P = 0.01; I2 = 97%) (Supplemental Digital Content Figure S8, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277), consistent with an additional glucose-lowering effect beyond that attributable to weight loss alone.

Safety outcomes

The increase in overall adverse event risk with CagriSema versus placebo was statistically significant but modest in magnitude (RR = 1.06, 95% CI: 1.01–1.10; P = 0.01; I2 = 31%) (Supplemental Digital Content Figure S9, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277). The pooled analysis demonstrated a statistically non-significant difference in serious adverse events between CagriSema and placebo (RR = 1.22, 95% CI: 0.63–2.3; P = 0.56; I2 = 78%) (Supplemental Digital Content Figure S10, available at: http://links.lww.com/MS9/B277), though high heterogeneity and the wide CI limit the precision of the estimate. Within the durations studied, CagriSema was generally well tolerated, with a safety profile consistent with that expected for incretin-based combination therapies.

Discussion

In this review, we have analyzed data from four RCTs concerning the effects of Cagrilintide monotherapy and CagriSema combined therapy in managing weight among adult patients suffering from overweight or obesity. The treatment approaches under evaluation resulted in clinically and statistically significant weight loss, as well as positive changes in other cardiometabolic markers. This is especially pertinent given the increasing prevalence of obesity around the world, making the need for effective pharmaceutical products that can induce significant weight loss very pressing[1].

The enhanced effectiveness of the combination therapy using CagriSema, when compared to the use of monotherapeutic regimens, stems from its synergism on neurohormonal pathways. The first of the active ingredients in CagriSema is Cagrilintide – an analog of amylin, which is a long-acting peptide. Cagrilintide is a non-selective receptor agonist of AMY1, AMY2, AMY3, and the CTR. Cagrilintide triggers satiation pathways in both homeostatic and hedonic brain regions[6]. On the other hand, semaglutide acts as a GLP-1 agonist and induces suppression of appetite by affecting the hypothalamus region. Moreover, this drug improves insulin secretion and slows down gastric emptying[6]. Thus, the simultaneous effect of these drugs on two different neural pathways may inhibit the body’s defensive response to weight loss caused by the activation of any of these two pathways[15,16]. This mechanistic synergy offers a compelling pharmacological rationale for the additive or supra-additive efficacy observed with CagriSema in the included trials.

The pooled estimate of percent body weight loss for monotherapy Cagrilintide (−6.08%) and dual therapy CagriSema (−5.98%) from our meta-analysis is based on the earlier phase 1b and phase 2 studies that covered a wide dose range (0.16–4.5 mg once weekly) and shorter duration of treatment than the later pivotal phase 3 studies. Although these outcomes are clinically significant, they should be understood in the context of the latest pivotal data from the REDEFINE study program. In the first phase 3 study, involving 3417 non-diabetic subjects and lasting 68 weeks, the CagriSema 2.4 mg/2.4 mg combination resulted in a 20.4% body weight loss compared to a 3.0% body weight loss in the placebo group, with 60% of subjects losing ≥20% of their body weight and 23% losing ≥30% of their body weight, making this combination one of the most effective drugs against obesity[17]. In REDEFINE 2, which included 1206 patients with type 2 diabetes, CagriSema demonstrated a mean percentage body weight loss of 13.7%, versus 3.4% for the placebo group, along with meaningful reductions in glycemia as a secondary objective[18]. The phase 3 outcomes are markedly higher than our pooled effect sizes, owing to dose optimization and extended treatment periods, and serve as a clear indication of the significant dose-response relationship seen in the Cagrilintide and CagriSema programs.

Contextualizing the above results within the broader realm of anti-obesity pharmacotherapeutic agents is valuable. The dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist tirzepatide has already demonstrated weight loss results of 15–22.5% in the SURMOUNT series of studies, which have been found to be superior to semaglutide monotherapy, achieving weight loss results that are, on average, 4.23%[19,20]. While the results of the phase 3 REDEFINE trial indicate that the agent CagriSema could offer equal or even better efficacy than the aforementioned drug for use in non-diabetic patients, there has been no direct comparison between the two[18]. For patients suffering from type 2 diabetes, the use of GLP-1 monotherapy is associated with inferior weight loss effects. However, the results from the REDEFINE 2 study suggest that the addition of Cagrilintide for amylin receptor activation may address this issue.

Apart from weight loss, both regimens showed significant improvement in cardiometabolic risk factors. CagriSema significantly reduced waist circumference (−10.91 cm compared to placebo), but this benefit was not statistically significant with Cagrilintide monotherapy, despite a similar trend, with high heterogeneity observed[21]. Waist circumference is a well-established biomarker for visceral adipose tissue and is a predictor of cardiometabolic risk independent of body mass index. Thus, the combination regimen may have distinct advantages in reducing central adiposity[8]. In the REDEFINE 1 trial, CagriSema showed significant improvement in lipid profile and systolic blood pressure, and 88% of patients with prediabetes achieved normoglycemia after 68 weeks of therapy[17].

Blood pressure systolic was found to be decreased with both Cagrilintide (−3.30 mmHg) and CagriSema (−5.52 mmHg), consistent with the cardiovascular effects of weight loss and incretins on metabolism. Changes in blood pressure within this range have been linked to decreased cardiovascular risk in large-scale studies[22], thus providing another level of clinical importance for this research besides weight loss itself. The lack of significance in the blood pressure diastolic change with CagriSema can be explained by the extremely high heterogeneity (I2 = 93%) between the trials involved in the study.

It is evident that there is quite a distinction in the effects on glycosylation seen between Cagrilintide alone and the combination of the two drugs in CagriSema. While the former was unable to result in any substantial change in HbA1c levels (−0.06%), the latter saw a small but still statistically significant improvement in the same parameters (−0.25%) due to the glucose-lowering capabilities of semaglutide[6]. This is a mechanistically understandable conclusion, as amylin has been shown to influence appetite and weight gain only and does not contribute to the secretion of more insulin or the inhibition of glucagon in a glucose-dependent manner, which is characteristic of GLP-1 agonists. The decrease in HbA1c levels in the diabetic population (REDEFINE 2) reached an impressive 2.2 percentage points[18].

Both were generally well tolerated. Treatment with Cagrilintide showed a significant increase in adverse events (relative risk [RR] ≈ 1.16), and treatment with CagriSema had a slightly elevated rate (RR ≈ 1.06); however, absolute rates remained very high in all arms of both trials, which could be explained by the underlying cardiometabolic comorbidities in the study participants[23]. The slight increase in serious adverse events in patients treated with Cagrilintide (RR ≈ 1.46; 95% CI: 1.00–2.13) should be monitored further; however, the lower limit of the CI approaching one may suggest otherwise. Notably, there was no difference in discontinuation due to adverse events between Cagrilintide and placebo treatment, and according to the phase III REDEFINE trial, there were single-digit discontinuations because of adverse events during the CagriSema treatment, primarily gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms, which were mostly mild or moderate in severity[17,18]. This safety profile is broadly consistent with the class effects expected from incretin-based therapies[8].

Statistical heterogeneity was substantial across several pooled outcomes, with I2 values ranging from 70% to 100%, and should be considered an important limitation when interpreting the results. Given the small number of included RCTs, formal meta-regression analyses were not statistically reliable and were therefore not performed. Nevertheless, several clinically plausible sources of heterogeneity likely contributed to the observed between-study variability, including differences in lorundrostat dosing regimens, treatment duration, baseline blood pressure, patient characteristics (particularly renal function and concomitant use of renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system inhibitors), and definitions of uncontrolled versus resistant hypertension.

Importantly, all included studies were judged to have a low risk of bias according to the RoB 2 tool, suggesting that the observed heterogeneity is more likely attributable to genuine clinical and methodological differences among trials rather than systematic bias. Sensitivity analyses demonstrated that the direction of the treatment effect remained generally consistent across studies, although the magnitude of benefit varied.

In addition, the absence of head-to-head randomized trials comparing lorundrostat with currently available mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists limits direct comparative assessments of efficacy and safety. Finally, the relatively short follow-up periods of the included studies preclude conclusions regarding long-term blood pressure control, cardiovascular outcomes, and the durability of both efficacy and safety effects. Ongoing larger and longer-term trials will be essential to better define the clinical role of lorundrostat.

Limitations

There are several weaknesses in this meta-analysis that need to be taken into account when evaluating the results. The first limitation is the insufficient number of RCTs included in the study due to the relatively early phase of the development of Cagrilintide and CagriSema, which can affect the estimation and precision of pooled effect sizes and the possibility of conducting reliable subgroup analyses. Another weakness is the heterogeneity of the clinical characteristics of patients included in different studies, which may have affected the metabolic parameters evaluated in the study, such as FPG and HbA1c.

Thirdly, differences in dosages, duration of treatment, and follow-up periods were among the methodological heterogeneities in the included studies. Fourthly, a lack of information on long-term effects, especially related to cardiovascular outcomes and long-term weight loss, made it difficult to establish long-term effectiveness and safety. Lastly, there were not enough studies to perform a publication bias analysis.

The above limitations clearly illustrate the importance of designing larger, properly conducted randomized studies that extend over longer durations in order to more accurately assess the efficacy and safety of Cagrilintide treatments for obesity.

Conclusion

Analysis of pooled estimates suggests that Cagrilintide monotherapy and the combination therapy, CagriSema, are associated with clinically meaningful reductions in body weight in adults with overweight or obesity. Combination therapy demonstrated greater improvements in glycemic parameters and adiposity measures compared with monotherapy in the included trials. However, given the limited number of studies and the between-trial heterogeneity, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Overall, Cagrilintide-based regimens represent a promising therapeutic approach for obesity management and related cardiometabolic risk factors.

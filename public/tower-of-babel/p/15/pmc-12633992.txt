Prevalence and awareness of median nerve compression (carpal tunnel syndrome) in Hail City residents, Saudi Arabia: A cross-sectional study
Authors: Walid Alesefir, Sumayyah Mohammad Almarshedy, Ali Ahmed Alghassab, Abdulmohsin Homoud Almalaq, Boshra Abdullah Alzahrani, Ali Turki Alodayli, Mazen Ahmed Almansour, Fadi Sulaiman Alqassim, Hisham Ali A Al Yateem, Sami Ibrahim Alzabni, Saeed Abdullah Alzahrani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS) is a common peripheral neuropathy causing pain, tingling, and numbness in the hand and arm due to compression of the median nerve. While the cause is often idiopathic, several wrist-related conditions are associated with its development. Splinting is the first-line treatment in mild to moderate cases. Corticosteroid injections offer symptom relief for up to 10 weeks and may delay surgery for up to a year. In severe cases, surgical intervention is recommended, providing better long-term relief than conservative measures.Aims:Due to the scarcity of research on CTS in Saudi Arabia, particularly in Hail City, and conflicting findings in previous studies, this study aims to assess the prevalence and awareness of CTS among residents of Hail.Materials and Methods:A cross-sectional study was conducted involving 517 residents of Hail City. Participants completed a 14-question survey (closed and open-ended). Data were collected through Google Forms and analyzed using SPSS software.Results:Only 56 participants (10.8%) showed good awareness of CTS, while 461 (89.2%) had poor awareness. A significant association was found between awareness level and demographic factors such as age and gender. The prevalence of CTS in the Hail region was 10.6%, which is considered high given the low awareness level.Conclusion:Adults in Hail City have insufficient awareness of CTS and its symptoms. Public health campaigns should be implemented regularly to raise awareness, promote early detection, and encourage timely treatment to prevent complications.

Introduction

Carpal tunnel syndrome (CTS) is a group of symptoms such as numbness, paresthesia, discomfort, and progressive thenar muscle weakness caused by localized median nerve compression at the wrist.[1] It is the most common peripheral neuropathy, with a prevalence of up to 5.8% of the general population in the Netherlands.[2]

The cause of the majority of CTS is unknown. However, many diseases that affect the local tissue of the wrist are associated with it, including age,[3] sex, diabetes, obesity, hypertension, and hypothyroidism.[4] CTS is diagnosed on clinical grounds through history and clinical examination.[5] Moreover, electrodiagnostic tests such as nerve conduction studies can exclude other diseases like polyneuropathy and radiculopathy, and together with cross-sectional ultrasonography can confirm the diagnosis.[67]

CTS management depends on severity; mild to moderate cases are usually treated conservatively with splinting, which is affordable and tolerable. Corticosteroid injections can relieve symptoms for up to 10 weeks and delay surgery by a year, though most patients opt for surgery after a year of injections. Oral corticosteroids provide relief for up to 8 weeks but are less effective. In severe cases, surgery offers long-term relief and better nerve conduction improvements, making it the preferred treatment for CTS.[8910]

Comorbidities such as diabetes, hypertension, and obesity have a high risk of developing CTS. Diabetes may result in median nerve microvascular abnormalities and chronic peripheral neuropathy, leading to moderate to severe CTS. On the other hand, long-term hypertension can cause microcirculatory sclerosis, which is correlated with an increased risk of CTS. Alternatively, obesity is one of the statistically significant risk factors for CTS.[1112]

In this study, we aim to explore the prevalence and awareness of CTS in Hail City residents in Saudi Arabia.

Materials and Methods

This cross-sectional study was initiated in Hail City, Saudi Arabia, and primarily targeted its residents from September 2022 to January 2023. All participants were Saudi, aged 18 and above, with special attention to those diagnosed with CTS.

For the data collection phase, the Google platform was used to create and share the survey. The questionnaire, designed to match our research objectives, contains 14 questions, including both closed-ended and open-ended types.

Guided by a previous study that identified a 16% prevalence of CTS in western Saudi Arabia, and according to the recent national census, the estimated population of Hail was 713,147. This study initially aimed at 162 individuals. However, to account for nonresponse and other variables, the goal was adjusted to 210 participants. Ultimately, this study enrolled a total of 517 participants, of whom 10.6% were diagnosed with CTS.

Ethical approval

The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles set forth in the Declaration of Helsinki. It was reviewed and approved by the Research Ethics Committee at the University of Hail (Approval Number: H-2023-330) on 28 August 2023.

Data analysis

After the data were extracted, they were revised, coded, and fed to the statistical software IBM SPSS version 22 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, IL). All statistical analysis was done using two-tailed tests. A P value < 0.05 was statistically significant. Concerning awareness and perception of CTS, the overall score was obtained by summing up all discrete item scores. Participants with an overall score less than 60% of the maximum score were considered to need better awareness.

In comparison, others with an overall score of 60% or more were considered to have an overall good awareness level. Descriptive analysis based on frequency and percent distribution was done for all variables, participant bio-demographic data, education, and smoking. Also, participants’ awareness regarding median nerve compression (CTS), the prevalence of CTS, associated health problems, and received management were tabulated, and their overall awareness level was graphed. Cross-tabulation graphs were used to assess factors related to participants’ awareness and perception of CTS using Pearson’s Chi-square test and exact probability test for small frequency distributions.

Results

A total of 517 eligible participants completed the study survey tool. Participants’ ages ranged from 18 to more than 60 years, with a mean age of 28.1 ± 12.7 years. 330 (63.8%) participants were females, and 329 (63.6%) were single. Regarding occupation, 243 (47%) worked in the nonmedical field, 174 (33.7%) worked in the medical field, and 71 (13.7%) were students. Exactly 65 (12.6%) were smokers [Table 1].

Generally, 52.6% of the study participants knew that the clinical features of CTS include pain in the wrist, 38.6% reported tingling and numbness in the thumb, index, and middle finger, 28.7% talked about decreased overall hand grip power, and 23.5% reported weakness affecting the thumb muscle. Discussion

In this study, the prevalence of CTS is 10.6%, less than the prevalence of 14% calculated by Alyousef,[13] and much less than a study done in Kuwait, where they found a prevalence of 18.7%.[1314] On the other hand, a study in the western region of Saudi Arabia has found a prevalence of 2%.[15] These variations are most likely due to the difference in sample size and the methods used. This emphasizes the importance of a national size study to give the most likely prevalence of CTS in Saudi Arabia.

In addition, 38.2% of CTS patients had other comorbidities, including diabetes, hypertension, and obesity. These comorbid conditions are identifiable in most previous studies.[6711121516] Screening patients with chronic diseases, especially diabetic patients, could help identify CTS early. Screening CTS patients for chronic diseases (diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and hypothyroidism) may also be justifiable.

Regarding the relationship between sociodemographic information and awareness level, young participants had an overall good awareness of CTS compared to older participants; this finding contradicts Alyousef’s study.[13] Also, we found a significant correlation between gender and level of awareness, where females had a better awareness level than men; this correlation is also noted in Kuwait[14] and another study in the western region of Saudi Arabia.[15] This suggests that public health efforts should concentrate on the older male population to raise awareness of CTS.

Regarding treatment modalities, the majority of our patients were treated with oral analgesics. This is surprising given that many studies recommend surgery for the treatment of CTS,[101718] but only four patients have undergone surgery. This low number could be due to the fear of surgery, poor knowledge about CTS, and the lack of specialized centers and surgeons in our region.

Our study shows that the vast majority of participants had an overall poor awareness of CTS, 89.2%. Similarly, Alqunai[19] and Tawakul et al.[15] found that 74.8% and 47.5%, respectively, of the participants had a poor level of awareness. On the other hand, our findings are irreconcilable with the results of the research conducted by Alyousef,[13] who concluded that the community’s awareness was sufficient.

The low awareness level is considered more significant regarding the treatment options and the relation of CTS with other diseases. The poor knowledge could be attributed to the limited information available to the general population about CTS in the Arabic language.

It is of substantial importance to empower the general population about the signs and symptoms of CTS and the treatment options available that could be achieved using social media channels and campaigns focused on CTS.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations that must be considered when interpreting the results. Firstly, since this is a cross-sectional study, the results are shown at a single point in time. Second, the study relied on self-reported data, so there is a risk of response bias. In addition, there could be a nonresponse bias, as individuals who chose not to participate might differ systematically from those who participated. Despite these limitations, the research provides valuable insights into the prevalence and awareness of CTS in the Hail region, Saudi Arabia.

Conclusion

There is insufficient knowledge about CTS among the adult population in the Hail region of Saudi Arabia. We found a clear relationship between age, gender, and the level of awareness about CTS. The prevalence of CTS in the Hail region, Saudi Arabia, is 10.6%, which is considered high compared to poor awareness. Campaigns to raise awareness of CTS and its symptoms must be run often in various public and private settings. This will encourage patients to seek medical attention early, with the likelihood of successful surgical or nonsurgical treatment.

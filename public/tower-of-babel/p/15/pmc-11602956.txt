Factors affecting peripheral neuropathy induced by nanoparticle albumin‐bound paclitaxel in patients with pancreatic cancer
Authors: Minseo Kang, Seunghyun Yoo, Yeolmae Jung, Hayun Lim, Myeong Hwan Lee, Ji Kon Ryu, Jangik I Lee
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimChemotherapy‐induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN) is a major toxicity limiting the use of nab‐paclitaxel (Nab‐P) in treating patients with pancreatic cancer. The aim of this study was to identify the factors affecting CIPN using patient‐reported outcome measures and the minimally invasive volumetric absorptive microsampling (VAMS) technique.MethodsThe maximum concentrations of paclitaxel (C max) were measured from 81 VAMS samples collected from 44 participants with pancreatic cancer. The association between CIPN development and demographic, clinical and pharmacokinetic factors was determined using univariable and multivariable logistic regression. The association between CIPN severity and the factors was evaluated using Spearman's rank correlation. The impact of C max and the number of treatment cycles on the severity was assessed using multivariable linear regression.ResultsThe development of CIPN was significantly associated with cumulative dose (odds ratio 1.005, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.003‐1.007), treatment cycles (3.47, 2.25‐5.85), alkaline phosphate (0.992, 0.985‐0.998) and age (1.092, 1.020‐1.179), with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.89 (95% CI 0.83‐0.95). The severity of CIPN significantly worsened with increasing cumulative dose (coefficient 0.58, 95% CI 0.44‐0.69), treatment cycles (0.57, 0.44‐0.68) and age (0.18, 0.00‐0.35). The severity of CIPN was predictable from treatment cycles (P = .0002) and C max (P = .01).ConclusionThe higher the cumulative dose of Nab‐P, treatment cycles and age, the more frequently and severely do the patients experience CIPN. In predicting the severity of CIPN using C max, minimally invasive VAMS is a feasible alternative to venous blood sampling.Keywords: chemotherapy‐induced peripheral neuropathy, nanoparticle albumin‐bound paclitaxel, pancreatic cancer, patient‐reported outcome measures, volumetric absorptive microsampling

INTRODUCTION

Nanoparticle albumin‐bound paclitaxel (Nab‐P), in combination with gemcitabine, serves as a first‐line treatment option for patients with advanced or metastatic pancreatic cancer, demonstrating improved survival compared with the administration of gemcitabine alone. 1 , 2 , 3 Chemotherapy‐induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN), one of the major toxicities that limits the use of taxanes in cancer treatment, is a prevalent adverse drug reaction associated with Nab‐P, occurring even more frequently than just with paclitaxel (73% vs 46%, P = .028). 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 CIPN often leads to dose reduction or discontinuation of Nab‐P, which may potentially impact the survival of patients. 5 , 8 , 9 , 10 Moreover, CIPN may persist for years even after the discontinuation of chemotherapy. 11 Duloxetine, pregabalin and gabapentin are frequently prescribed to alleviate the pain or discomfort associated with CIPN, but a definite prevention or treatment for CIPN has yet to be established. 11 , 12 , 13 The identification of the associated risk factors with CIPN is therefore imperative to assist patients with averting the development or progression of CIPN and retaining their chemotherapy.

In a clinical practice or research, the assessment of CIPN is predominantly carried out by healthcare professionals, using grading systems driven by physicians. 14 , 15 However, discrepancies have been noted in the assessment of CIPN between clinicians and patients, with patients reporting symptoms more frequently and severely (69.4% vs 90.7%, P < .001). 16 Given the subjective nature of CIPN symptoms and their considerable impacts on patients' quality of life, patient‐reported outcome (PRO) measures are increasingly being recognized as important tools for the assessment of CIPN. 14 , 15 , 17 , 18 , 19 The utilization of PRO measures for identifying the risk factors of CIPN is essential not only to sustain the effectiveness of treatment but also to maintain the patients' quality of life.

In patients with solid tumours, mostly consisting of breast and lung cancer, the development of CIPN was associated with the exposure time of total paclitaxel concentrations above 0.05 μmol/L (T >0.05) exceeding 10.6 h, and the area under the concentration‐time curve for total paclitaxel exceeding 4.7 μg·h/mL. 20 In patients with breast cancer, the disruption of chemotherapy owing to peripheral neuropathy has been linked to T >0.05 and the maximum concentrations (C max) of paclitaxel. 21 However, studies that explore the relationship between Nab‐P exposure and CIPN in patients with pancreatic cancer are rarely found.

The levels of drug exposure are typically determined by analysing the blood collected through venous sampling, which is invasive and necessitates a trained phlebotomist. As a practical alternative to traditional venous blood sampling, volumetric absorptive microsampling (VAMS), which is notable for its minimal invasiveness and feasibility without a phlebotomist, has been increasingly adopted in a wide range of clinical studies. 22 , 23 , 24 This study was conducted to evaluate the association between CIPN and demographic, clinical and pharmacokinetic factors in patients with pancreatic cancer, utilizing PRO measures and the minimally invasive VAMS technique.

METHODS

Study participants

This study was conducted on patients who were aged 18 years or older and histologically diagnosed with pancreatic cancer at Seoul National University Hospital, Seoul, Republic of Korea. The study included participants who were scheduled to undergo chemotherapy with Nab‐P/gemcitabine. Patients were excluded if they had a history of prior chemotherapy, severe psychiatric or comorbid conditions (ie, end‐stage renal disease, severe heart failure, advanced chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, poorly controlled blood sugar), coagulation disorders or were pregnant. The participants initially received a dose of Nab‐P 125 mg/m2 body surface area, followed immediately by a dose of gemcitabine 1000 mg/m2, via intermittent intravenous infusions on days 1, 8 and 15 of each 28‐day cycle. This study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the guidelines for Good Clinical Practice from the International Council for Harmonization, following the approval of the protocol by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the hospital (IRB number: H‐2103‐093‐1205). On providing an explanation about the purpose of the study, written informed consent was received from all the participants before the start of the study procedure.

Study design

Blood samples were collected from each participant's fingertip using 20‐μL VAMS tips (Mitra® 20‐μL; Neoteryx, Torrance, CA, USA) immediately before the end of Nab‐P infusion to obtain the C max values of paclitaxel on the first day of each treatment cycle. The samples were stored at −70 °C along with a desiccant in a sealed bag until the measurement of paclitaxel concentrations. The demographic and clinical data of participants were collected from the electronic medical records of the hospital and recorded on a case report form.

Assessment of peripheral neuropathy

The development and severity of CIPN was assessed according to the European Organization of Research and Treatment of Cancer – Quality of Life Questionnaire – CIPN (EORTC‐QLQ‐CIPN) prior to the infusion of Nab‐P on the first day of each cycle. 14 Despite that the EORTC‐QLQ‐CIPN comprises 20 items, eight items from the sensory scale (CIPN8) were employed for CIPN assessment, given Nab‐P's primary impact on sensory nerves. 21 , 25 , 26 The last item of the sensory scale, Item 18 (difficulty in hearing), was excluded from the CIPN assessment according to the earlier research suggesting that the item had distinctive characteristics as compared with other items in the sensory scale. 15 , 18 , 21 All items were assessed on a four‐point Likert scale (1 = not at all, 2 = a little, 3 = quite a bit, 4 = very much), where higher scores indicate participants experiencing worse neuropathic symptoms. The total scores of CIPN8, ranging from 8 to 32, were linearly converted into a 0‐100 scale.

To identify factors that affect the development of CIPN, it was defined as either an increase in the converted CIPN8 score by 9.60 points or greater from baseline 19 or the initiation of pregabalin or gabapentin administration for the treatment of CIPN. In identifying the factors that affect the severity of CIPN, it was graded using the converted CIPN8 score with baseline subtraction.

Sample preparation

A frozen VAMS sample was thawed at room temperature and the tip of the sample was placed in a polypropylene tube for the extraction of analytes. Before extraction, a working solution of 20 μL of internal standard (IS; docetaxel in methanol at a concentration of 10 μg/mL) and 200 μL of ultrapure water was added to each tube. The mixture was vortexed at 1400 rpm for 1 min and sonicated at room temperature for 10 min. For liquid‐liquid extraction, 1000 μL of methyl tert‐butyl ether was added to the tube, which was then centrifuged at a speed of 15 000 x g at 4 °C for 10 min and frozen at −80 °C for 20 min. The organic phase was separated from the frozen aqueous phase, transferred to a new tube, evaporated at 40 °C for 40 min and reconstituted in 1000 μL of 0.1% formic acid in 70% acetonitrile. The paclitaxel concentration in the reconstituted solution was measured using ultra‐high‐performance liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry (UHPLC–MS/MS; EVOQ ELITE, Bruker, Billerica, MA, USA).

Bioanalytical methods

An Aegispak C18 column (100 × 2.0 mm, 3 μm; Young Jin Biochrom, Seongnam‐si, Republic of Korea) was used for chromatographic separation. The temperatures of the column and autosampler were kept at 35 and 10 °C, respectively. The analyte was eluted using isocratic mobile phases (mobile phase A, 0.1% formic acid in ultrapure water; mobile phase B, 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile) for 2.5 min at a flow rate of 0.25 mL/min. The interface of MS detection was an electrospray (positive ion mode) with a voltage of 5000 V. The detected masses of parent and daughter ions for paclitaxel were 854.4 and 285.9 g/mol, respectively. The detected masses of parent and daughter ions for the IS (docetaxel) were 808.1 and 226.0 g/mol, respectively. The temperatures of the cone and probe were 300 and 200 °C, respectively.

The linearity of the analytical method was evaluated by constructing a calibration curve with eight standard concentrations (0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10 and 20 μg/mL). The formula of the calibration curve was computed using a weighted (1/x 2) linear regression of the theoretical values of standard concentrations against the corresponding response. All calibration standards for the measurement of paclitaxel concentrations from VAMS samples were prepared by diluting the working solution of analyte (paclitaxel) into pooled venous blood of six healthy individuals and quantitatively absorbing the analyte into VAMS tips using micropipettes. For the validation of the analytical method, the concentration of lower limit of quantitation (LLOQ) was set to 0.1 μg/mL, whereas those of low (LQC), medium (MQC) and high‐quality controls (HQC) were set to 0.3, 1.6 and 16 μg/mL, respectively. The accuracy and precision of the analytical method were evaluated with relative bias (% bias) and the coefficient of variation (% CV), respectively. All quality controls for the measurement of paclitaxel concentrations from VAMS samples were quantitatively absorbed into VAMS tips using micropipettes and used for the method validation.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics that summarize the demographics and characteristics of participants were presented as a median with a range for continuous variables and as the number of participants with a percentage for categorical variables. Univariable and multivariable logistic regressions were performed to evaluate the association between the development of CIPN and demographic, clinical and pharmacokinetic factors. Univariable and multivariable linear regressions, and Spearman's rank correlation were performed to evaluate the association between the factors and the severity of CIPN. All factors with P < .1 in univariable logistic or linear regression were included in multivariable logistic or linear regression, respectively. In establishing the model for multivariable logistic or linear regression, the factors with P < .05 were included through forward addition, while those with P ≥ .05 were excluded through backward elimination. The predictive performance of the logistic regression model was evaluated using the area under the curve of receiver operating characteristics (ROCs). All associations with two‐sided P value less than 0.05 in the analyses were considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 10.2.1 (GraphPad Software Inc., San Diego, CA, USA).

RESULTS

Participant characteristics

A total of 66 patients newly diagnosed with pancreatic cancer were enrolled in the study (Figure 1). Five participants were excluded because CIPN assessment was not performed at baseline. Seventeen participants were excluded because they were lost to follow‐up and unable to provide blood samples and clinical data owing to the withdrawal of informed consent in the middle of study participation. Hence, the clinical and Nab‐P concentration data from a total of 44 participants were evaluable for the analysis. The data consisting of 81 values of C max, 110 sets of clinical laboratory tests and 121 CIPN8 scores were analysed. The participants, consisting of 25 males (57%) and 19 females (43%), had a median (range) age of 65 (43‐79) years (Table 1). The median (range) dose and C max of paclitaxel were 120 (97‐130) mg/m2 and 5.65 (0.15‐14.70) μg/mL, respectively. The median (range) score of CIPN8 evaluated at baseline was 0 (0‐4.2). In the study period, 30 out of 44 participants developed CIPN with a median (range) severity of 16.7 (0‐87.5).

Bioanalytical performance

The calibration curve was linear at the concentration range of paclitaxel from 0.1 to 20 μg/mL (r 2 ≥ 0.996). The accuracy (% bias) within runs for the samples of LLOQ, LQC, MQC and HQC was in the range of −2.1% to 4.3%, −5.7% to −2.3%, −3.8% to 5.3% and −4.2% to 10.2%, respectively. The accuracy (% bias) between runs was 0.5%, −3.6%, 1.0% and 3.7%, respectively. The precision (% CV) within runs for the samples of LLOQ, LQC, MQC and HQC was in the range of 4.5% to 5.6%, 4.8% to 7.1%, 5.2% to 5.9% and 2.7% to 6.9%, respectively. The precision (% CV) between runs was 3.3%, 1.9%, 4.5% and 7.1%, respectively.

Identification of risk factors for CIPN development

In univariable logistic regression, the development of CIPN was significantly associated with the cumulative dose of Nab‐P (odds ratio [OR] 1.004, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.002‐1.005, P < .0001), the number of treatment cycles (3.474, 2.246‐5.846, P < .0001), the level of alkaline phosphate (ALP; 0.995, 0.989‐0.999, P = .032) or the level of blood urea nitrogen (BUN; 1.084, 1.003‐1.179, P = .049; Table 2). However, the development was not significantly associated with estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR; 0.963, 0.924‐0.998, P = .053), age (1.042, 0.994‐1.095, P = .093), haemoglobin, haematocrit, serum creatinine (SCr), absolute neutrophil counts (ANC), sex, Nab‐P dose, white blood cell counts (WBC), alanine transaminase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST) or paclitaxel C max. When multivariable logistic regression was performed including factors with P < .1, the development of CIPN was significantly associated with the cumulative dose of Nab‐P (OR 1.005, 95% CI 1.003‐1.007), ALP (0.992, 0.985‐0.998) and age (1.092; 1.020‐1.179). The area under the ROC curve of the regression model was 0.89 (95% CI 0.83‐0.95), indicating an excellent predictive performance. 27

Identification of risk factors for CIPN severity

The risk factors for CIPN severity were identified using the data obtained from 43 participants, excluding one who declined to complete the EORTC‐QLQ‐CIPN8 survey after the initiation of Nab‐P administration. In univariable linear regression, the severity of CIPN was significantly associated with the cumulative dose of Nab‐P (estimate 0.02, 95% CI 0.01‐0.03, P < .0001), the number of treatment cycles (7.41, 5.10‐9.72, P < .0001) and age (0.52, 0.04‐1.00, P = .036; Table 3). However, the severity was not significantly associated with C max (1.31, −0.01‐2.63, P = .053), ALP (−0.04, −0.08‐0.00, P = .057), haemoglobin (1.70, −0.23‐3.63, P = .083), haematocrit (0.71, −0.13‐1.56, P = .097), eGFR, Nab‐P dose, ALT, ANC, BUN, SCr, AST and WBC. DISCUSSION

Using PRO measures and the VAMS technique, this study examined the demographic, clinical and pharmacokinetic factors that affect the development or severity of peripheral neuropathy induced by Nab‐P in patients with pancreatic cancer. The development of peripheral neuropathy reported by patients themselves was associated with the age of patients, the cumulative dose of Nab‐P, the number of treatment cycles and the level of ALP (Table 2). The severity of CIPN reported by patients was associated with the age of patients, the C max of paclitaxel, the cumulative dose of Nab‐P and the number of treatment cycles (Table 3). Since VAMS is minimally invasive and does not require a phlebotomist, it appears to be a more desirable alternative to venous blood sampling for the measurement of C max in predicting the severity of CIPN.

CIPN, a remarkable dose‐limiting toxicity of Nab‐P therapy in cancer treatment, has a considerable impact on patients' quality of life. 4 , 5 , 14 , 17 , 28 There are two types of tools for CIPN assessment, clinician‐based and patient‐based, applicable to clinical settings. 14 , 29 The risk factors of CIPN have predominantly been assessed using clinician‐based tools to help optimize the therapeutic outcomes of neurotoxic chemotherapy. 3 , 30 , 31 , 32 However, PRO measures, specifically CIPN8, were utilized as the tool for the assessment of CIPN in this study, since PRO measures provide unique information on the effects of medical conditions, and the benefits and drawbacks of drug therapy from the perspective of patients. 33 , 34 , 35 This information may enhance the understanding of the patient's conditions by complementing the assessments and interpretations by clinicians. In general, PRO measures have the capability to detect symptoms earlier and with greater reliability than other measures, and potentially serve as predictive and prognostic markers for patient survival. 34 , 36 , 37 Considering such capabilities, the importance of incorporating PRO measures in clinical studies and trials is growing, which reflects the recognition that the patient's quality of life is increasingly considered as being equally crucial as survival.

In predicting the severity of CIPN, the C max value of paclitaxel appears to be useful (estimate 1.74, 95% CI 0.54‐2.95; Table 3) when considered alongside age (0.55, 0.05‐1.05) and the number of treatment cycles (7.06, 3.81‐10.31). The formula of linear regression derived in this study (Equation 1) indicates that C max will likely have a greater impact on reaching the threshold of CIPN8 score that significantly affects the patient's quality of life on occurring CIPN at later treatment cycles. Namely, as the number of treatment cycles increases, the threshold value of C max at which CIPN develops or worsens will likely decrease.

Some articles reported a specific threshold value for changes in PRO measures that is deemed clinically significant (ie, development of CIPN) or important (ie, deterioration of CIPN) for assessing the worsening of CIPN symptoms. 19 , 38 For example, the change of 9.60 in CIPN8 scores from baseline was reported as the threshold value for the development of CIPN. 19 Hence, at the median age of 65 years in the regression formula (Equation 1), the values of 8.0 and 3.9 μg/mL are the threshold C max values for the first and second cycles, respectively, that lead to the development of CIPN. For deterioration of CIPN, the change of 29.65 in CIPN8 scores from baseline was reported as the threshold value. 19 Therefore, at the median age in Equation 1, the values of 7.3 and 3.3 μg/mL are the threshold C max values for the fourth and fifth cycles, respectively, that lead to the deterioration of CIPN. This trend may be attributed to increased sensitivity or accumulation of paclitaxel from continued administration. Hence, lowering the C max below the decreased thresholds for the later cycles of Nab‐P therapy may assist in averting the development or deterioration of CIPN.

VAMS is a blood sampling technique that collects a minuscule volume of blood by pricking the fingertip of patients, whereas venous blood sampling requires a large volume from puncturing the veins. 24 , 39 , 40 VAMS enables the measurement of paclitaxel C max without requiring additional venipuncture while administering Nab‐P to patients, making the technique as a less invasive option. Moreover, VAMS samples can be self‐collected by patients or their family members, 23 , 24 , 39 , 40 which eliminates the need for a phlebotomist and thereby reduces the burden of healthcare providers for blood sampling. Given these advantages, VAMS seems to be a more favourable alternative to venous blood sampling in clinical settings for measuring C max to predict the severity of CIPN in patients with pancreatic cancer.

The higher cumulative dose of Nab‐P (OR 1.004, 95% CI 1.002‐1.005) and the later cycles of treatment (3.474, 2.246‐5.846) were significantly associated with a greater risk of CIPN development (Table 2). Similarly, the higher cumulative dose of Nab‐P (estimate 0.02, P < .0001) and the later cycles of treatment (7.41, P < .0001) were significantly associated with greater severity of CIPN (Table 3). Consistent with the results of this study, a higher cumulative dose of paclitaxel has been identified as a factor that increases both the risk (clinician‐assessed, OR 4.842, 95% CI 1.702‐13.77) 30 and severity (patient‐reported, coefficient = 0.42, P < .0001) 21 of CIPN in patients with breast cancer. Furthermore, later cycles of treatment have also been identified as a factor that elevates the odds of CIPN (clinician‐assessed, OR 1.17, 95% CI 1.06‐1.30) in patients with pancreatic cancer. 3 Hence, both the cumulative dose of Nab‐P and the number of treatment cycles appear to be reliable predictors for the development and severity of CIPN, irrespective of the assessment tool employed.

Older age was significantly associated with an increase in CIPN development in multivariable logistic regression (1.092, 1.020‐1.179; Table 2). Older age was also significantly associated with a greater severity of CIPN (estimate 0.52, P = .036; Table 3). Consistently, an increased risk of CIPN development in older patients who were treated with taxane or platinum compounds has been identified across many types of solid tumour cohorts, 3 , 5 , 30 , 41 , 42 with the odds rising by 4% per year. 41 Aging‐associated decline in organ function, which leads to diminished overall body function, including circulation and metabolism, has been proposed as a causative mechanism for the increasing risk of CIPN with age. 30 , 42 Hence, older age appears to be an important predictor for both the development and severity of CIPN, which indicates that elderly patients receiving Nab‐P should be administered the therapy with greater caution and close monitoring.

A lower level of ALP was significantly associated with a greater risk of CIPN development both in univariable (OR 0.995, 95% CI 0.989‐0.999) and multivariable (0.992, 0.985‐0.998) logistic regression (Table 2). Despite this statistical significance, the pathophysiological mechanism behind the findings is unclear given the limited data on the relationship between ALP and CIPN.

There are a few limitations in this study. The sample size was relatively small (n = 44) compared with other prospective studies that have identified the risk factors for CIPN or CIPN‐induced treatment disruption in patients with breast cancer. 21 , 30 Recruiting patients with pancreatic cancer was challenging due to the lower incidence of pancreatic cancer compared with breast cancer. Among the patients enrolled in the study, some received pregabalin or gabapentin, which might have influenced the reported severity of CIPN, 43 , 44 although the effectiveness of these medications has not been definitively established for the treatment of peripheral neuropathy. 12 , 13 Finally, the impact of genetic factors on the development and severity of CIPN should be assessed, which warrants further research.

The application of PRO measures to the assessment of CIPN allowed the identification of age, C max, the cumulative dose of Nab‐P and the number of treatment cycles as the important factors for predicting the development and the severity of peripheral neuropathy induced by Nab‐P. Because of minimal invasiveness, VAMS is a more favourable alternative to venous blood sampling for the measurement of C max. The utilization of the factors identified for the assessment of CIPN risk will likely enable the assortment of patients who are at a high risk for developing or exacerbating CIPN following Nab‐P administration. The dosage adjustment of Nab‐P for patients at a high risk of CIPN may help reduce the incidence and severity of CIPN, and thereby preserve patients' quality of life.

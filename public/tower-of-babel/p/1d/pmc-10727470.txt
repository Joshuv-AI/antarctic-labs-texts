Severe pleural effusion after hepatectomy for hepatocellular carcinoma: multicentre retrospective study
Authors: Zhong-Qi Fan, Yong-Kang Diao, Chao Li, Lan-Qing Yao, Cheng-Wu Zhang, Yong-Yi Zeng, Zi-Xiang Chen, Ya-Hao Zhou, Wei-Min Gu, Hong Wang, Ting-Hao Chen, Yao-Ming Zhang, Xiao Xu, Ying-Jian Liang, Xian-Ming Wang, Wan-Guang Zhang, Jie Li, Ming-Da Wang, Han Wu, Xin-Fei Xu, Timothy M Pawlik, Wan Yee Lau, Feng Shen, Tian Yang, Guo-Yue Lv
Published: 2023

Introduction

Perioperative mortality after hepatectomy for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) has been reduced to less than 3.0 per cent in most centres worldwide, but the incidence of postoperative complications remains high, ranging from 21.4 to 56.4 per cent1,2. Among post-hepatectomy complications, pleural effusion is one of the most commonly observed, occurring in 18.4–54.5 per cent of cases3,4. Severe post-hepatectomy pleural effusion (SPHPE) can have serious consequences, such as respiratory distress with dyspnoea, respiratory failure requiring reintubation and mechanical ventilation5, or chest drainage6. Severe pleural effusions may delay hospital discharge and increase the likelihood of other complications, such as pulmonary infection7–9. A subset of patients with SPHPE may be diagnosed after hospital discharge, which can result in unplanned hospital readmission10–12.

To date, there are little data available regarding clinical features and risk factors of SPHPE, making its prevention and prediction a challenge for clinicians. The aim of the present study was to find the clinical features and risk factors associated with SPHPE among patients with HCC and to create a nomogram-based online calculator for SPHPE.

Methods

The study was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of all the participating hospitals (IRB2022-553).

Patients who underwent hepatectomy for HCC between January 2004 and December 2021 at 15 Chinese hospitals were identified. Patients who were younger than 18 years old, with recurrent HCC or ruptured HCC, or who received preoperative anti-tumour therapy were excluded. Clinical characteristics, including patient-, liver-, and tumour-related variables, were collected.

SPHPE was defined as the accumulation of a moderate to large pleural effusion, with symptoms of respiratory distress, which needed drainage via a chest tube during postoperative hospitalization or within 30 days after hospital discharge (Clavien–Dindo grade greater than or equal to III)13. Variables with a P value of <0.100 on univariable analysis were entered into the multivariable analysis using a logistic regression model by forward stepwise variable selection. The independent risk factors identified were chosen to construct the prediction formula and nomogram model using SPSS® (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) and the rms package in R. Details of methods are given in the Supplementary methods.

Results

Among the 4715 patients who met the study inclusion criteria (Fig. S1), the incidence of SPHPE was 5.3 per cent (250 patients), with a median time to postoperative diagnosis of 7 (interquartile range 5–14) days. A total of 188 patients who had SPHPE (75.2 per cent) developed other postoperative complications compared with 2090 patients who did not have SPHPE (46.8 per cent) (P < 0.001). Of note, 46 instances of SPHPE (18.4 per cent) were detected after hospital discharge, which accounted for 22.5 per cent of all 30-day readmissions among patients in the entire cohort. Mean postoperative hospital stay and postoperative 30- and 90-day mortality were markedly higher among patients with SPHPE than among patients without SPHPE (P < 0.001) (Table 1).

Several preoperative and intraoperative variables associated with SPHPE were identified on univariable and multivariable logistic regression (Table S1), such as ASA grade greater than II, portal hypertension, largest tumour size greater than 5.0 cm, intraoperative blood transfusion, open hepatectomy, hepatectomy involving segment 7/8, and operating time greater than 180 min. As demonstrated in Fig. 1a, a nomogram model to predict SPHPE was then constructed based on the independent risk factors identified on multivariable analysis. A web-based online calculator was developed to make it easy for clinicians to obtain the probability of SPHPE among patients with HCC (http://www.asapcalculate.top/Cal12_en.html; Fig. 1b). Calibration plots for the nomogram model demonstrated favourable agreement between the prediction obtained from the nomogram versus actual observation (Fig. 1c). The resulting model was internally validated using the bootstrap validation method, which demonstrated good accuracy, with a bootstrap-corrected C index of 0.764. The predictive ability of the nomogram model had the highest area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) (0.764, 95 per cent c.i. 0.752 to 0.776) compared with the Child–Pugh grade14 and the albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) score for SPHPE (Fig. 1d). Discussion

In the present study, data from a large-scale multicentre observational cohort of 4715 patients were analysed to examine SPHPE after hepatectomy for HCC. Of note, the overall incidence of SPHPE during the index hospitalization or within 30 days after hospital discharge was 5.3 per cent, with a median time to diagnosis of 7 days, and 75.2 per cent of patients with SPHPE had other postoperative complications. Multivariable analysis identified seven independent risk factors of SPHPE. Based on these results, a prediction model to predict SPHPE among individual patients was created and was made available via an online calculator.

Nearly one in five (18.4 per cent) patients with SPHPE had it diagnosed within 30 days after hospital discharge. The proposed nomogram may be helpful in identifying which patients may be at high risk of SPHPE before hospital discharge, may help reduce the 30-day readmission, and may allow more timely treatment. Another interesting finding of the present study was that the likelihood of other postoperative complications was markedly higher among patients with SPHPE than among patients without SPHPE. These data suggest that the occurrence of SPHPE should be suspected among patients with other complications and perhaps lead to a higher level of clinical suspicion and screening for SPHPE. Considering that the median time to SPHPE diagnosis was 7 days after surgery, screening for pleural effusion may be best performed around the seventh postoperative day.

Intraoperative haemodynamic fluctuations and hepatic ischaemia time have been reported to influence the occurrence of pulmonary complications after hepatectomy15. As such, the occurrence of PHPE may partially result from disrupted hepatic homeostasis. In the present study, patients with SPHPE had higher incidences of baseline cirrhosis and portal hypertension than patients without SPHPE and portal hypertension was independently associated with SPHPE.

The risk factors associated with SPHPE after hepatectomy were consistent with previous reports that identified these variables as being associated with higher postoperative complications4–8. In the present study, the risk factor most strongly associated with SPHPE on multivariable analysis was hepatectomy involving segment 7/8. A previous single-centre study noted that right diaphragmatic incision was also independently associated with a higher occurrence of SPHPE5. The present study strongly suggests that resection of HCC tumours abutting the diaphragm may be a surrogate risk marker for SPHPE.

The lack of external validation is a significant limitation of this study. Further validation in other cohorts will be needed in the future. The online calculator for SPHPE demonstrated good performance and may be useful to enable clinicians to make better and more timely clinical decisions to manage this post-hepatectomy complication.

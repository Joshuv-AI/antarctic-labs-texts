Can case control study design investigate the association of BPG levels, inflammatory cytokines with valvular dysfunction
Authors: Sushma Muraraiah
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

I read with great interest the article titled “Inflammatory cytokine levels in rheumatic heart disease and their association with use of benzathine Penicillin: A case-control pilot study”.

The article1 provides valuable insights into the relationship between BPG use and inflammatory markers. In view of limited literature regarding the same, this study offers significant evidence. However, we would like to present some additional perspectives regarding the article.

1.The study objective quoted was - a case–control pilot study to investigate the levels of inflammatory cytokines and their association with BPG use and mitral stenosis. The design chosen for the present study to investigate the objective was case control study. Case control study is inherently retrospective in nature. It looks back ward in time to investigate the potential risk factors that could be linked with the outcome.2 In the article, the cases and controls were chosen based on exposure i,e with or without BPG administration. It is not in accordance with case control study design. The article quotes that the study is performed to generate hypothesis. Usually cross sectional designs are preferred for hypothesis generation.

2.The article states that the controls were age and sex matched with cases. There is significant difference in the age between both groups as stated in table 1a. The matching in case control study should be done for all confounders. We would like to know apart form age and sex, any other confounders were expected in the study?

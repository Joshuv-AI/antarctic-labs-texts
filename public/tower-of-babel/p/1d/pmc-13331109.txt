Characteristics and clinical outcomes of patients with severe asthma receiving tezepelumab after marketing approval in Spain (T-ROSS II study): an observational study protocol
Authors: Christian Domingo, Gema Monteagudo, Francisco Ramos-Lima, Marta Alegría, Gabriel Niza, Marta Robles, Javier Domínguez-Ortega
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAsthma is a prevalent chronic respiratory disease characterised by airway inflammation, reversible airflow obstruction and airway hyper-responsiveness. Most patients with severe asthma (SA) remain uncontrolled despite the availability of multiple therapies, representing a significant global health burden.Thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP) is an epithelial cell-derived cytokine that plays a central role in asthma initiation via both allergic and non-allergic mechanisms as well as in the persistence of airway inflammation in patients with asthma, acting upstream in the inflammatory cascade.Tezepelumab, a monoclonal antibody that targets TSLP, has shown clinical efficacy and safety in pivotal trials in patients with T2 and non-T2 asthma. Tezepelumab was approved for use in Europe in October 2022, and the marketing authorisation was issued in Spain in October 2023.Methods/designThe T-ROSS II study is a retrospective, observational, single-arm, multicentre study planned to include 400 patients aged ≥12 years treated in SA units. T-ROSS II will represent the largest published series of patients with SA who initiated tezepelumab after its market authorisation.Eligible patients must have at least 12 months of medical history prior to treatment initiation and a minimum of 3 months of follow-up data recorded in the electronic health records.The co-primary outcomes are to describe baseline patient demographics and clinical characteristics and evaluate changes in the annualised asthma exacerbation rate before and after the initiation of tezepelumab. Discussion These findings will complement clinical trial data and inform physicians and healthcare decision-makers about the real-world effectiveness and utilization of tezepelumab in patients with SA. This study is expected to provide valuable insights into the clinical outcomes and treatment patterns of this population.Ethics and disseminationThe final protocol of the study has been approved by the ethics committee/Institutional Review Board (EC/IRB) of the University Hospital 12 de Octubre (Madrid, Spain) (EC/IRB number: 25/226). The results will be disseminated through scientific publications, including manuscripts submitted to peer-reviewed journals and presentations at national and international congresses.Trial registration numberNCT07013760.

Introduction

Asthma is a heterogeneous disease characterised by airway inflammation, reversible airway obstruction and airway hyper-responsiveness. The prevalence of asthma is highly variable across different geographical areas, both in adults (between 0.2% and 21%) and in children (between 2.8% and 37.6%).1 In 2019, it was estimated that it affected 262 million people worldwide and caused 461 000 deaths.2

Patients with severe asthma (SA) often require multiple drug combinations at different dosages, sometimes requiring high doses of inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) for their management.3 4 Despite the availability of numerous therapeutic options, including biologic medication, most patients with SA have uncontrolled disease (64.1% in the BRAVO study), which is associated with a high risk of exacerbation, hospitalisation and increased healthcare costs.57 In Spain, the prevalence of asthma is estimated to be 5.5%, whereas SA represents 7.7% of these patients. The mortality rate of patients with SA in Spain is estimated at 5.5%, and the total annual cost of patients with uncontrolled SA was found to be two times the cost of patients with controlled SA.7

Different phenotypes can be identified in patients with SA, currently named T2 or non-T2. Nevertheless, a biomarker and phenotypic overlap can be observed, which appears to have the greatest disease burden and airflow limitation, highlighting the complexity of the disease.8 9

Thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP) is an epithelial cell-derived cytokine that plays a central role in the initiation and persistence of airway inflammation in asthma, as it participates upstream in the asthma inflammatory cascade. It is triggered by both allergic and non-allergic stimuli, and it mediates the activity of several innate immune cells.10 11

Tezepelumab is a human monoclonal antibody that has a novel mechanism of action. It binds to human TSLP with high affinity and prevents its interaction with the heterodimeric TSLP receptor. The clinical development programmes for tezepelumab include the Phase 2b PATHWAY trial, the Phase 3 NAVIGATOR trial and the long-term extension (104 weeks) DESTINATION trial. These trials demonstrated the clinical efficacy and safety of tezepelumab in patients with SA.5 12 13

Tezepelumab was approved by the European Medicines Agency in September 2022 and was marketed in Spain for hospital use and fully reimbursed by the Spanish National Health Service in October 2023 for patients diagnosed with SA who are inadequately controlled with a combination of high-dose ICSs and a long-acting β2-agonist (LABA), with or without additional medications for asthma control (Summary of Product Characteristisc (SmPC)).14 Given its relatively recent market availability, a paucity of local real-world data exists. The T-ROSS II study follows the T-ROSS study (NCT06487065), a study performed in Spain, which aimed to retrospectively characterise and assess clinical outcomes of patients treated with tezepelumab under the ‘foreign medication supply’ (FMS) programme prior to 1 October 2023.15 The aim of the T-ROSS II study is to generate evidence on the use of tezepelumab in patients with SA in routine clinical practice after its local marketing authorisation.

Materials and methods

Study design

The T-ROSS II study is an observational, retrospective, single-arm, multicentre study in patients with SA who received tezepelumab, according to routine clinical practice, following the criteria of a physician working in SA units of the Spanish National Health Service after its marketing authorisation in Spain. The index date for each patient is defined as the date of administration of the first dose of tezepelumab. The follow-up period will start just after the index date and continue until the earliest of the following: the end of study follow-up (patient’s inclusion in the study), switch to another biologic treatment for asthma, death or loss to follow-up; therefore, the follow-up time will differ among patients. The baseline (BL) period is defined as the 12-month period just before the index date (figure 1).

The first patient was included in June 2025, and the database was closed in April 2026.

There are two co-primary outcomes in the study: the description of the demographics and clinical characteristics of patients with SA who initiated treatment with tezepelumab, and the description of the annualised asthma exacerbation rate in the 12-month period before (BL period) and in the 12-month period (follow-up period) after initiating treatment with tezepelumab.

As a secondary outcome, the study will include the description of oral corticosteroid (OCS) consumption, asthma control and health-related quality of life (HRQoL), lung function, biomarkers, other asthma treatments and asthma-related healthcare resource utilisation in patients with SA during the BL and follow-up periods for the total population as well as predefined subgroups (eg, biomarker levels, allergic status, biologic-naïve vs biologic-experienced, among others). Additionally, adherence and persistence (time on treatment) to tezepelumab, duration of therapy, switching, discontinuation and reasons for discontinuation will be documented for the follow-up period.

As an exploratory outcome, the number and proportion of patients who met clinical remission (both individual and composite components) before and after the initiation of treatment with tezepelumab will be measured. Study participants

The study population will consist of patients with SA, aged ≥12 years, who initiate tezepelumab according to clinical practice based on the local product label (SmPC).14 Patients must have recorded information in their e-folders for at least 12 months prior to tezepelumab initiation (index date) and 3 months after to ensure that BL data and a minimum of 3 months of follow-up data are available in the medical records.

Patients who have received current or previous exposure to tezepelumab as part of a clinical trial or FMS and patients who have initiated three or more non-tezepelumab biologics prior to the index date will be excluded from this study.

Given the retrospective and non-interventional nature of the study, and to ensure its appropriate and non-biased performance, a written informed consent request is not planned for this study.

Data collection

Subject’s medical records will be used to determine the eligibility of patients to be enrolled in the study according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria, and they will be considered the source of data for the study. 25 sites have been invited to participate in the study, mostly from centres with accredited SA units. Data previously collected as part of routine clinical practice and included in medical records (BL characteristics, follow-up information and patient-reported outcomes) will be transferred to an electronic case report form (eCRF) via pseudonymised subject identifiers.

The eCRF will capture detailed information, if available in the patient’s medical records, surrounding the treatment and clinical and demographic information during the BL and follow-up periods.

Sample size estimation

T-ROSS II is a descriptive study planned to include 400 patients who meet the study eligibility criteria. As such, the expected precision of a proportion is calculated using a normal approximation. The exacerbation rate is based on the NAVIGATOR study, which assumes an average exacerbation rate of 0.93 with an SD of 1.52 and is based on a t-distribution. The expected precision for the exacerbation rate is 0.78–1.08.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analyses will be performed to gain an understanding of the qualitative and quantitative nature of the data collected and the characteristics of the sample studied, after BL and follow-up data collection are complete.

Continuous variables will be summarised by providing the mean, SD, median, minimum, maximum and IQR, as appropriate. Categorical variables will be summarised by frequency and percentages (n, %). For both continuous and categorical variables, the number of valid and missing observations will be provided. When appropriate (eg, for exacerbations, hospitalisations, etc), the annualised rate will be calculated as the number of events divided by the corresponding overall sample follow-up time (patient-years).

Missing data will not be imputed; therefore, only observed data will be used in the analysis. The proportion of missing data per analysis will be reported in addition to the output for each analysis.

Discussion

The retrospective T-ROSS II study will provide relevant real-world data on the use of tezepelumab according to routine clinical practice. These data will complement findings from Phase 3 clinical trials as well as those from the earlier T-ROSS observational study conducted in patients enrolled in the Spanish early access programme prior to the commercial availability of tezepelumab.15 Notably, T-ROSS II represents the largest cohort reported to date in Spain, comprising approximately 400 patients, thereby exceeding previously published cohorts from Germany (n=129),16 the UK (n=175),17 Israel (n=103),18 Italy (n=30)19 and smaller Spanish series.2023

A key strength of this study lies in the opportunity to stratify patients according to asthma phenotype while also distinguishing between biologic-naïve patients and those who have switched from prior therapies. This distinction is clinically relevant, as treatment history may substantially influence observed effectiveness outcomes when analysed in aggregate populations. The study will try to collect oscillometry data, an emerging and valuable tool for detecting small airway dysfunction, which is increasingly recognised as a critical component of asthma pathophysiology.24 25 Furthermore, T-ROSS II will explore different definitions of remission, acknowledging the current lack of a universally accepted consensus regarding clinical and biological remission in SA.18 20 26 Another significant focus is the reduction in OCS use, which remains a central therapeutic goal given the well-established burden of long-term OCS exposure. Previous real-world studies have consistently highlighted the importance of achieving OCS-sparing biologic therapies, and this outcome will be comprehensively evaluated in the present study.16 20 27

As with all retrospective collection data, this study has inherent limitations. These include the potential for missing or incomplete data, reflecting the variability of routine clinical documentation, as well as the inability to attribute observed clinical changes exclusively to the therapy due to the absence of a controlled study design. These factors should be carefully considered when interpreting real world evidence (RWE) findings.

These data will help support decision making by providing physicians and payers with important data on the real-life benefits of tezepelumab for both clinical and non-clinical outcomes in patients with SA.

Transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy vestibular approach versus open thyroidectomy in surgical resection of the thyroid: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Wurood Alharbi, Ibrahim Omer, Khalid M. Alkhalifah, Mohanned Alsaeed, Adnan Balamesh, Hani Z. Marzouki, Hoda Alsayid, Nora Almonawar, Razan Mohammed Saleh, Ali Alkhamis, Nouf Yaqoub
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:The increasing incidence of thyroid nodules and thyroid cancer, particularly among women, has driven the evolution of minimally invasive thyroidectomy techniques. The transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy vestibular approach (TOETVA) has emerged as a promising alternative to conventional open thyroidectomy (OT). This study aimed to compare operative time, blood loss, drainage volume, lymph node yield, and postoperative complications between TOETVA and OT.Methods:A comprehensive search was conducted across PubMed, Embase, ClinicalTrials.gov, and Web of Science. Randomized controlled trials, cohort studies, and retrospective studies comparing TOETVA and OT were included. Statistical analysis was performed using RevMan version 5.3. Standardized mean differences and odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals were calculated. A P-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.Results:A total of 22 studies involving 4397 patients were included. TOETVA was associated with a significantly longer operative time and higher drainage volume. However, no significant differences were observed in blood loss, number of retrieved central lymph nodes, metastatic lymph node yield, recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy, seroma formation, or wound infection.Conclusion:TOETVA represents a safe and feasible alternative to OT, offering comparable surgical and oncological outcomes with the added benefit of superior cosmetic results.

Introduction

The escalating incidence of thyroid nodules and cancers, particularly affecting young females[1], has spurred continuous exploration into diverse surgical approaches. One such method that has gained prominence is the transoral endoscopic thyroidectomy vestibular approach (TOETVA), which presents itself as a compelling alternative to conventional open thyroid surgery.

The global prevalence of thyroid nodules, detected through ultrasound, poses a substantial health challenge. Well-differentiated thyroid cancer, including papillary and follicular types, presents a prevalent concern despite a generally favorable prognosis[1]. Several thyroidectomy approaches have been employed, including axillo-breast, transaxillary, or retroauricular methods[2]. TOETVA emerges with attention-worthy advantages, including providing the shortest distance route from the incision to the thyroid gland. This method provides convenient and easy access to both the bilateral thyroid and the central compartment. Notably, TOETVA is recognized for leaving no cutaneous scar, achieved by placing incisions in the oral vestibule beneath the lower lip. This unique feature has contributed to the increasing adoption of TOETVA in thyroid surgery[3].

A systematic review encompassing 15 studies revealed that TOETVA demonstrates a comparable rate of complications to that of conventional open thyroidectomy (OT). However, this innovative approach introduced potential new complications, including mental nerve palsy and cervical and/or mediastinal emphysema[1]. Additionally, because it is a clean-contaminated surgery, there is a likelihood of an increased rate of deep-seated neck infection. While the reported incidence of these complications is rare, they are considered additional complications added to the realm of thyroid surgery[4].

However, significant data comparing the outcomes between TOETVA and conventional OT (COT) approaches are lacking. Consequently, we conducted this study to assess and compare the outcomes of these approaches, aiming to conclude the differences in operative time, blood loss, and various postoperative outcomes between TOETVA and OT[1]. This review follows the Transparency in the reporting of Artificial Intelligence – the TITAN guideline 2025 on the declaration and use of artificial intelligence in research and writing[5].

Methods and materials

This systematic review and meta-analysis adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines[6]. It was conducted in accordance with the AMSTAR (Assessing the Methodological Quality of Systematic Reviews) Guidelines to ensure methodological rigor.

Eligibility criteria

The studies included in this systematic review were randomized controlled trials (RCTs), cohort studies, case reports, and retrospective studies. Only English publications were included. Participants with thyroid masses undergoing TOETVA or OT were considered eligible.

Search strategy

A comprehensive literature search covered four databases – PubMed, Embase, Clinicaltrials.gov, and Web of Science – was conducted from 3 September 2023 to 1 October 2023 to identify studies investigating two distinct surgical approaches for thyroid resection: TOETVA and OT. The search terms employed included “Endoscopic thyroidectomy (OR) ETOVA (OR) transoral (OR) vestibular (OR) minimally invasive (AND) Open thyroidectomy (OR) COT.”

Selection criteria

The initial search through the databases was performed. Duplicates were removed before the process of screening, and then the articles were divided between two reviewers for initial screening based on title and abstract for inclusion manually, followed by full-text screening.

Data collection process and data items

Two reviewers conducted the data extraction process. They divided the articles between them and systematically extracted relevant information. The collected data were then compiled and organized into a structured Excel sheet to facilitate analysis and presentation.

Information such as study characteristics, intervention details, and inclusion criteria was collected. The documented outcomes were operative time, blood loss, transient and permanent recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) palsy, transient hypocalcemia, number of retrieved central lymph nodes, number of metastatic central lymph nodes, amount of drainage, unintended parathyroidectomy, seroma, and wound infection.

Study risk of bias assessment

Two reviewers independently assessed the risk of bias (ROB) using the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme (CASP) checklists[7]; this tool was selected due to the variety of studies included. The CASP checklists specific to cohort studies, case–control studies, and RCTs were used according to the study design. These checklists, consisting of 11 questions across three sections – study validity, study results, and local applicability of results – were utilized. Any disagreements between the researchers were resolved. For publication bias, a funnel plot was used.

Effect measures

Odds ratios (ORs) were applied to analyze outcomes related to wound infection, seroma, transient hypoglycemia, and both transient and permanent (RLN) injuries. Conversely, for continuous outcomes such as blood loss, operative time, amount of drainage, and the counts of retrieved central and metastatic lymph nodes, the standardized mean difference (SMD) was utilized. Both of them have a 95% confidence interval (CI).

Meta-analysis

The meta-analysis was conducted using RevMan (Review Manager) 5.3 version (Cochrane Collaboration) and a random-effect model to pool data. A 95% confidence level was used to ensure the reliability of results and set a significance threshold of P < 0.05 to determine statistical significance. We used the I2 statistic for heterogeneity testing.

Results

Study characteristics

The systematic review initially identified 706 articles, which were narrowed down to 691 unique records after duplicates were removed. Following the screening and full-text assessment phases, 22 studies were found to be eligible and were included in the meta-analysis. The articles included a total of 4397 patients: 1928 patients who underwent TOETVA and 2469 patients who underwent OT. Intraoperative outcomes

Operative time

For instance (Figs 1 and 2), in Ahn and Yi [25], the TOETVA group averaged 110.26 min (SD 35.74) versus 86.12 min (SD 24.76) in controls, while You et al[18] reported a longer operative time, 201.8 min (SD 30.1) for TOETVA. Both studies had sizable sample sizes, with Ahn and Yi[25] including 150 patients in the TOETVA group and 125 in the control group, and You et al[18] having 186 patients in each group. The pooled analysis showed a statistically significant longer operative time in TOETVA (SMD = 1.56; 95% CI: 1.47–1.64; Z = 34.77; P < 0.00001).

However, the meta-analysis identified an extremely high level of heterogeneity among the studies. This indicates that nearly all the variability in operative times across the studies is due to heterogeneity rather than chance, suggesting significant differences in how much longer operative times are for TOETVA across different studies. The chi-squared value, being very high at 682.71, confirms the presence of significant heterogeneity.

However, when all studies were combined to obtain the pooled estimate, the overall effect (pooled SMD) was −0.03, suggesting no significant difference in blood loss between the groups on average. The 95% CI crossed zero, further indicating the lack of statistical significance.

Discussion

Many remote-access thyroidectomy techniques have been developed over the years; one of these is ETOVA, which is done through a natural orifice and is characterized by less tissue dissection and hidden incisions in the vestibular mucosa that end without a skin scar[26]. Previous systematic reviews regarding the TOETVA concluded that it has similar safety compared with the traditional incision[27–29]. However, in our systematic review and meta-analysis, we aim to specifically compare TOETVA and traditional incision in the total thyroidectomy, analyzing more variables that were not examined in previous studies, like seroma, permanent RLN, and amount of drainage. Our comprehensive search identified 22 relevant studies, providing substantial pooled evidence with important implications for both clinical practice and future research.

Although operative time was significantly longer in the TOETVA group, it seems to be expected that the operation time will be longer due to the learning curve because it is a relatively new technique and also a confined space compared to the traditional thyroidectomy[10,30]. Our findings align with a recent meta-analysis based on 12 studies[27], while our analysis of 17 studies further strengthens this evidence. Additionally, Oh et al report a notable reduction in operative time following the adoption of balloon dilators to facilitate flap dissection[31]. Additional time requirements may also reflect the preparation and setup needed for laparoscopic equipment[8].

With the transoral technique, there has been considerable concern about the risk of infection at the surgical site[26] arising from the clean-contaminated nature of the transoral route and the presence of oral bacterial flora[32,33]. However, our pooled analysis showed no statistically significant difference in wound infection rates when compared with OT[8,11,17,18,21,23].

The significantly higher postoperative drainage volume observed in the TOETVA group can be attributed to the wider subcutaneous flap dissection required to access the thyroid gland via the vestibular approach[8].

This review was conducted systematically and appropriately. However, certain limitations need to be acknowledged. None of the included studies employed randomization, which may introduce selection bias. In addition, long-term outcomes were not reported across the included studies. As previously mentioned, the surgical approach has a learning curve, and the experience and skills of the surgeons may impact the results of the studies included. Additionally, a limitation in our meta-analysis is the heterogeneity seen in some outcomes.

Conclusion

TOETVA is a safe and effective alternative to OT, offering comparable complication rates and oncological outcomes with superior cosmetic benefits. Although operative duration is longer, this limitation may diminish with increasing surgical experience. Patient selection and surgeon expertise remain critical for optimal outcomes.

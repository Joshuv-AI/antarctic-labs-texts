Improving oxygen prescription on the acute medical unit: a quality improvement project
Authors: Ciara Martin, Justine Lo, Luke Boyle
Published: 2023

Introduction

In accordance with British Thoracic Society (BTS) guidelines,1 the policy of the trust is that all patients should have a target oxygen saturation established and prescribed at the time of admission so that appropriate oxygen therapy can be started in the event of unexpected clinical deterioration with hypoxaemia, and also to ensure that the oximetry section of the National Early Warning Score (NEWS) can be scored appropriately. This is important as studies have shown adverse effects for patients with a COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) exacerbation who are over-oxygenated;2 others have demonstrated serious adverse outcomes for patients presenting to the emergency department who are under-oxygenated (<88%).3 While regular medicines are reviewed in conjunction with the London Care Record during the medical clerking process, prescription and documentation of target oxygen saturation is often overlooked. We undertook a quality improvement project with the aim of improving the percentage of patients on the acute medical unit (AMU) with target sats prescribed.

Methods

Between September and December 2022, using sequential Plan, Do, Study, Act (PDSA) cycles and improvement methodology, we audited the prescription of target oxygen saturations for all patients admitted to the 36-bed AMU at St Mary's Hospital over several 2-week periods by reviewing both drug charts and clinical notes on our electronic patient record and prescribing system (Cerner EPR). Where target oxygen saturations were prescribed, in secondary analyses we also evaluated whether the saturation target chosen was appropriate for the patient by reviewing several criteria including whether they had a COPD diagnosis, history of hypercapnic respiratory failure or raised bicarbonate on blood gas analysis. Three improvements were implemented: (1) design and display of educational posters, (2) teaching sessions for foundation doctors and (3) engagement with pharmacists and wider multidisciplinary team. Data collection periods followed each improvement introduced.

Results

PDSA 1: 23% (n = 75) of patients on AMU had target saturations established / PRN oxygen prescribed. 68% (n = 51) of these patients had saturations set to 94–98% and 32% (n = 24) to 88–92%. Saturation targets had been selected appropriately in 96% (n = 72) of cases.

PDSA 2: The percentage of patients with target saturations prescribed increased from 23% to 37% (n = 121). As before, 68% of these patients had saturations set to 94–98% (n = 82) and 32% to 88–92% (n = 29). Target saturations had been appropriately prescribed in 88% of cases (n = 106).

PDSA 3: The percentage of patients admitted who had target saturations prescribed decreased from 37% to 25% (n = 100). In total, 79% of patients had saturations set to 94–98% (n = 79) and 21% to 88–92% (n = 21). Target saturations had been appropriately prescribed in 96% of cases (n = 96).

Discussion

Overall, only a quarter of patients on our AMU had target oxygen saturations prescribed on admission to hospital, further improvements are needed. The initial improvement to 37% was not sustained, likely because of several junior doctor changeovers and the arrival of new starters during data collection periods. Interestingly, of the improvements trialled, educational sessions made the greatest impact. Reassuringly, where target sats were prescribed, these were appropriate for the patient in over 90% of cases.

Strict Interactions of Fifth Letters, Hydrophobic Unnatural Bases, in XenoAptamers with Target Proteins
Authors: Michiko Kimoto, Hui Pen Tan, Ken-ichiro Matsunaga, Nur Afiqah Binte Mohd Mislan, Gota Kawai, Ichiro Hirao
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractXenoAptamers are DNA fragments containing additional letters (unnatural bases, UBs) that bind specifically to their target proteins with high affinities (sub-nanomolar KD values). One of the UBs is the highly hydrophobic 7-(2-thienyl)imidazo[4,5-b]pyridine (Ds), which significantly increases XenoAptamers’ affinities to targets. Originally, Ds was developed as a third base pair with a complementary UB, 2-nitro-4-propynylpyrrole (Px), for replication, and thus it can be used for aptamer generation by an evolutional engineering method involving PCR amplification. However, it is unclear whether the Ds base is the best component as the hydrophobic fifth-letter ligand for interactions with target proteins. To optimize the ligand structure of the fifth letter, we prepared 13 Ds variants and examined the affinities of XenoAptamers containing these variants to target proteins. The results obtained using four XenoAptamers prepared by the replacement of Ds bases with variants indicated that subtle changes in the chemical structure of Ds significantly affect the XenoAptamer affinities. Among the variants, placing either 4-(2-thienyl)pyrrolo[2,3-b]pyridine (Ys) or 4-(2-thienyl)benzimidazole (Bs) at specific Ds positions in each original XenoAptamer greatly improved their affinities to targets. The Ys and Bs bases are variants derived by replacing only one nitrogen with a carbon in the Ds base. These results demonstrate the strict intramolecular interactions, which are not simple hydrophobic contacts between UBs and targets, thus providing a method to mature XenoAptamers’ affinities to targets.

Introduction

DNA aptamers are single-stranded DNA fragments that bind specifically to targets such as small molecules, proteins, and cells. DNA aptamers are generated by an evolutional engineering method called SELEX (systematic evolution of ligands by exponential enrichment) with repetitive processes of selection and PCR amplification, using DNA libraries with randomized sequences.1 After the sequence determination of the aptamers generated by SELEX, the production and modifications of aptamers are performed by chemical synthesis, thus providing highly pure DNA aptamers as antibody alternatives with excellent quality control and site-specific modifications. As compared to protein-based antibodies, DNA is relatively hydrophilic and highly water-soluble, which solves the sticky problem of antibodies.2 However, this hydrophilicity weakens the hydrophobic interactions between DNA aptamers and the target proteins. Thus, the affinities of conventional DNA aptamers with the natural four letters of A, G, C, and T are insufficient for practical use,3 and several modification methods have been reported.4 A representative modification method is the conjugation of hydrophobic residues to natural bases in DNA aptamers.5

To improve aptamer affinity by a new approach, we developed a genetic alphabet expansion method by introducing a highly hydrophobic unnatural base (UB), 7-(2-thienyl)imidazo[4,5-b]pyridine (Ds), as a fifth letter into DNA aptamers.6 The Ds base specifically pairs with diol-modified 2-nitro-4-propynylpyrrole (Px), and the Ds–Px pair functions in PCR amplification as a third base pair with high fidelity (Figure 1A).7 Using the Ds–Px pair, we developed an aptamer generation method called ExSELEX (genetic alphabet expansion for SELEX) using DNA libraries containing Ds bases as a fifth letter (Figure 1B) and obtained several Ds-containing DNA aptamers (XenoAptamers) with high affinities to target proteins.6,8 Only one or two hydrophobic Ds bases in DNA aptamers significantly increase their affinities, with sub-nanomolar KD values, and thus the entire structures of XenoAptamers maintain their high hydrophilicity.

XenoAptamer creation and design of the Ds variants. The structure of the unnatural base pair, Ds–Px(diol) (A), and the scheme of ExSELEX for XenoAptamer generation (B). Main modules and side-chain modules (C) were used for the design of Ds variants (D).

However, it is still unknown whether Ds is the best UB as a high-affinity ligand to interact with proteins among all possible hydrophobic UBs. The Ds base was initially developed as a third base pair with the Px base for replication and not as a component of a ligand for protein interactions. Thus, there is a possibility that other UBs could be components that further reinforce hydrophobic interactions by van der Waals forces, base stacking involving π–π, CH−π, and polarity. Besides the hydrophobic interactions, some nitrogen atoms in UBs might act as hydrogen-bond acceptors for interactions with target proteins.

Here, we chemically synthesized 13 Ds variants and examined the binding affinities of four XenoAptamers targeting interferon γ (IFNγ),6,9 von Willebrand factor A1-domain (vWF),8a and dengue serotype 1 and serotype 3 NS1 proteins (DEN1-NS1 and DEN3-NS1),8b in which the original Ds bases were replaced with these variants. Their binding affinity data showed that most of the variants reduced the affinities of the XenoAptamers compared to those of the original Ds-containing XenoAptamers. However, replacing Ds with either one of two variants, 4-(2-thienyl)pyrrolo[2,3-b]pyridine (Ys) or 4-(2-thienyl)benzimidazole (Bs), increased the XenoAptamers’ affinities. These data indicate that the atomic-level changes of the hydrophobic UBs significantly affect their XenoAptamers’ affinities to targets, and the role of the fifth letter is not a simple hydrophobic interaction between a XenoAptamer and its target. The findings reveal a novel aspect of molecular recognition and provide a method for further improvement of XenoAptamer generation.

Results

Design and Chemical Synthesis of Ds Variants

We designed Ds variants by dividing the structure of the Ds base into two modules, the main module of 1-deazapurine (imidazo[4,5-b]pyridine) and the side-chain module of the thienyl group.10 The hydrophobicity, hydrogen-bonding atoms, such as nitrogen, oxygen, and sulfur, and polarity of each module might be involved in the interactions with the local structures of target proteins. Thus, we first considered possible alternatives for each module (Figure 1C). We chose the structures of the modules that are similar to those of Ds, to maintain the shape fitting between Ds variants and the local structures of the target proteins. Thus, the main modules of the imidazo[4,5-b]pyridine (D) body are pyrrolo[2,3-b]pyridine (Y), purine (P), indole (I), benzo[d]imidazole (B), and pyrrolo[2,3-d]pyrimidine (E). The thienyl (s) side-chain modules are furanyl (o), imidazolyl (i), thiazolyl (t), pyridazinyl (p),11 and their modified components, such as methylthienyl (sm) and bithiophenyl (ss) moieties. Each component of both modules was then combined to make 13 Ds variants, Bs, Is, Ys, Yi, Do, Dp, Dss, Dt, Yo, Ps, Es, Ysm, and Yss, as novel UB units (Figure 1D).

The nucleoside and nucleotide derivatives of these Ds variants were prepared by a conventional chemical synthesis. For the synthesis of Bs, Is, Ys, Yo, Es, Ysm, and Yss, the bromo derivatives of the main module compounds were conjugated with the tributylstannyl derivatives of the side-chain module compounds, followed by ribosylation of each UB variant using Hoffer’s chloride (Scheme 1). The nucleoside derivatives were converted to amidite units for DNA chemical synthesis by conventional methods (Supporting Information).

XenoAptamers for the Ds-Variant Experiments

The XenoAptamers containing each Ds variant were chemically synthesized using Ds-variant amidites by replacing Ds in the original XenoAptamer (Ds-XenoAptamer) with each Ds variant. For the experiments, we chose four XenoAptamers (AptIFNγ, AptvWF, AptD1, and AptD3) obtained by ExSELEX targeting IFNγ,6 vWF,8a and dengue serotype 1 and serotype 3 NS1 proteins,8b respectively (Figure 2). These aptamers contain two essential Ds bases and are connected by a biotin-conjugated mini-hairpin sequence, CGCG(biotin-T)AGCG, for stabilization against heat and nucleases.9,13 The original internal stem-loop structures in the anti-IFNγ and vWF aptamers were replaced with mini-hairpin sequences CGCGAAGCG and CCGAAGG, respectively. Each Ds base in these aptamers was replaced with one of the Ds variants, and a series of these XenoAptamers (Supplementary Table S1) were chemically synthesized by a standard phosphoramidite method.

Secondary structures and KD values of the Ds-XenoAptamers targeting IFNγ, vWF, DEN1-NS1, and DEN3-NS1.

First, the two Ds bases in each XenoAptamer were replaced with the same type of Ds variants, such as two Ys (YsYs) and two Yo (YoYo), and their affinities to targets were evaluated by a gel-electrophoresis mobility shift assay (EMSA) at 30–37 °C. For the anti-IFNγ and -vWF aptamers (AptIFNγ and AptvWF), we employed a gel containing 3 M urea for the EMSA, because of the high stability of these aptamers with the internal mini-hairpin structure (Figure 2).8a,9 The EMSA conditions are summarized in Supplementary Table S2. The shifted band densities for each aptamer–protein complex were compared to those for the Ds-XenoAptamers and quantified as the relative binding abilities (%) to those of their original Ds-XenoAptamers.

If more than one type of the main module of Ds variants improved the aptamer affinities, as compared to the original Ds-XenoAptamers, then the binding affinities of a series of XenoAptamers with several combinations of two different Ds variants, such as YsBs and BsYs, were examined to identify the best combinations of Ds variants. Finally, the dissociation constants (KD values) of the best XenoAptamers were determined by surface plasmon resonance (SPR), using a Biacore T200 system (GE Healthcare) at 25 °C, in which the biotinylated XenoAptamers were immobilized on the SPR sensor chip.

Anti-IFNγ XenoAptamers (AptIFNγ) by Replacement with Ds Variants

Eleven anti-IFNγ aptamers containing the same UB variants (AptIFNγ-BsBs, -IsIs, -YsYs, -YiYi, -DoDo, -DpDp, -DssDss, -DtDt, -YoYo, -PsPs, and -EsEs) at positions 29 and 40 in the original Ds-XenoAptamer (AptIFNγ-DsDs) were chemically synthesized. The EMSA experiments were performed with mixtures of 25 nM each aptamer and 50 nM IFNγ, on a polyacrylamide gel containing 3 M urea at 30 °C (Figure 3). Most of the Ds variants reduced the binding abilities of their aptamers. However, AptIFNγ-YsYs and -YoYo resulted in stronger shifted bands corresponding to the complex with IFNγ than those of AptIFNγ-DsDs.

Binding analysis of anti-IFNγ aptamers. EMSA using a gel containing 3 M urea at 30 °C. The relative binding efficiency (%) of each aptamer was determined by normalization to the binding efficiency of AptIFNγ-DsDs.

The higher affinities of AptIFNγ-YsYs and AptIFNγ-YoYo than that of AptIFNγ-DsDs were confirmed by their dissociation constants. The KD values of these two aptamers were determined (19.5 pM for AptIFNγ-YsYs and 24.4 pM for AptIFNγ-YoYo), and they were superior to that of the original AptIFNγ-DsDs aptamer (42.4 pM) (Figure 4).

KD values of AptIFNγ-DsDs, -YsYs, and -YoYo were determined by SPR.

Anti-vWF XenoAptamers (AptvWF) by Replacement with Ds Variants

First, 11 chemically synthesized anti-vWF aptamers containing the same Ds variants (AptvWF-BsBs, -IsIs, -YsYs, -YiYi, -DoDo, -DpDp, -DssDss, -DtDt, -YoYo, -PsPs, and -EsEs) at positions 10 and 31 in AptvWF-DsDs were examined by EMSA on a gel with 3 M urea at 37 °C (Figure 5A). Besides YsYs and YoYo, the anti-vWF aptamer tolerated BsBs. Based on the first screening, we next prepared three anti-vWF aptamers (AptvWF-BsYs, -BsYo, and -YsBs, corresponding to the Ds positions 10 and 31) with the combination of Ys, Yo, and Bs and performed EMSA and KD determinations. All three aptamers, AptvWF-BsYs, -BsYo, and -YsBs, as well as AptvWF-YsYs, -BsBs, and -YoYo, exhibited affinities higher than that of AptvWF-DsDs (Figure 5B).

Binding analysis of anti-vWF aptamers. EMSA was performed by electrophoresis on a gel containing 3 M urea at 37 °C using aptamers with the same UB variants (A) and different combinations (B). The relative binding efficiency (%) was determined by normalization to the binding efficiency of AptvWF-DsDs.

The KD values of AptvWF-BsBs, -YsBs, -BsYs, and -BsYo (24.0, 27.4, 24.2, and 22.3 pM, respectively) were superior to those of AptvWF-DsDs (54.7 pM) (Figure 6). The KD values of these aptamers revealed that the Bs base at position 10 is favorable compared to that at position 31, and the best combination with the highest affinity is AptvWF-BsYo.

KD values of AptvWF-DsDs and their variants, determined by SPR.

Anti-DEN1-NS1 XenoAptamers (AptD1) by Replacement with Ds Variants

Eleven aptamers with the same Ds variants at positions 19 and 25 in AptD1-DsDs were prepared. EMSA on native gels at 30–37 °C using 50 nM of each aptamer and 25 nM DEN1 NS1 protein were performed. The molar concentration of the NS1 protein is indicated as that of its hexamer. The EMSA data showed that the affinities of all anti-DEN1-NS1 DNA aptamers with Ds variants, AptD1-BsBs, -IsIs, -YsYs, -YiYi, -DoDo, -DpDp, -DssDss, -DtDt, -YoYo, -PsPs, and -EsEs, were lower than that of AptD1-DsDs (Figure 7A). However, considering the position dependency of the Bs base, as shown in the anti-vWF aptamer, there may be more and fewer preferred positions for the Ds variants. Since AptD1-BsBs, -YsYs, and -DtDt produced clearly shifted bands, we focused on Bs, Ys, and Dt, as well as Ds, for further experiments.

Binding analysis of anti-DEN1-NS1 aptamers. EMSA on a native gel at 30–37 °C. The relative binding efficiency (%) of aptamers with the same UB variants (A) and different combinations (B and C) was determined by normalization to the binding efficiency of AptD1-DsDs.

We prepared AptD1 aptamers with several combinations of Ds and either Bs, Ys, or Dt (AptD1-DsYs, -YsDs, -DsBs, -BsDs, -DsDt, and -DtDs) and performed EMSA using these aptamers (Figure 7B). Surprisingly, the AptD1-YsDs and -DsBs aptamers exhibited higher affinities as compared to the other combinations, indicating that Ys at position 19 and Bs at position 25 were the best combination. Thus, we prepared AptD1-YsBs, -YoBs, and -YsmBs with different thienyl side-chain modules as well as AptD1-BsYs as a control. As expected, AptD1-YsBs exhibited high affinity to DEN1-NS1 (Figure 7C). In addition, the small modification of the Y side chain in AptD1-YoBs and -YsmBs did not affect their high affinities in the EMSA experiments. In contrast, AptD1-BsYs exhibited a significantly reduced affinity. Among these aptamers, AptD1-YsBs had the lowest KD value (35.9 pM), with 7-fold higher affinity than that of AptD1-DsDs (254 pM) (Figure 8). Interestingly, the affinity of AptD1-BsYs (8.55 nM) was 19- and 193-fold lower than those of AptD1-DsDs and AptD1YsBs, respectively. Thus, subtle modifications of the main module can positively and negatively affect the aptamer affinities for their targets, depending on their positions.

KD values of AptD1-DsDs and their variants, determined by SPR.

Anti-DEN3-NS1 XenoAptamers (AptD3) by Replacement with Ds Variants

Eleven aptamers with the same Ds variants at positions 15 and 23 in AptD3-DsDs were prepared. EMSA on native gels at 30–37 °C (Figure 9A) showed that among these aptamers, AptD3-YsYs, -DssDss, and -YoYo exhibited relatively high affinities to DEN3-NS1. Thus, we screened the aptamers with combinations of Ys and Dss at positions 15 and 23 of AptD3. Position 15 was more acceptable for Dss than position 23, and AptD3-DssYs exhibited a higher shifted-band intensity in comparison to AptD3-DsDs (Figure 9B). Since the affinity of AptD3-YsYs was also high, we chemically synthesized Yss nucleoside derivatives. We then performed EMSA and SPR studies using a further set of AptD3 variants (AptD3-YssYs, -YssYo, and -YssYsm), and we found that Yss is compatible with Dss at position 15 of AptD3 (Figures 9C and 10). The KD values of AptD3-DssYs and -YssYs were 41.9 pM, which are lower than those of the original AptD3-DsDs (71.4 pM) (Figure 10).

Specificities of AptIFNγ, AptD1, and AptD3 with Ds Variants

We confirmed that the improved XenoAptamers with Ds variants maintain high specificity to targets. First, the specificity of AptIFNγ-YsYs, as well as AptIFNγ-DsDs, was tested by EMSA using human, canine, and mouse IFNγ, which were prepared by an Escherichia coli expression system. Both XenoAptamers bound to only human IFNγ (Figure 11A). The amino acid homologies between human and canine IFNγ and between human and mouse IFNγ are 63% and 39%, respectively.

High specificities of AptIFNγ-YsYs (A), AptD1-YsBs (B), and AptD3-YssYs (C) to each target. EMSA on a native gel at 30–37 °C.

Second, the specificities of AptD1-YsBs and AptD3-YssYs were examined by EMSA, using all four serotypes and variants of dengue NS1 and Zika NS1 proteins. AptD1-YsBs and -DsDs only bound to the dengue serotype 1 variant 1 NS1 protein (DEN1(v1)) (Figure 11B). The homology among the four serotypes of dengue NS1 proteins is 69–80%. In Singapore, there are two main variants in serotype 1, in which only 10 amino acids among 352 residues (homology: 97.2%) are mutated.8b AptD1-DsDs was generated by ExSELEX targeting the serotype 1 variant 1 NS1, and AptD1-YsBs and -DsDs distinguished the few amino acid differences of the nontarget proteins.8b

AptD3-YssYs and -DsDs also exhibited high specificities to the dengue serotype 3 NS1 protein (Figure 11C). Slight smearing of the aptamer bands of AptD3-YssYs and -DsDs was observed in the DEN1(v1) lanes, indicating the weak interaction between the aptamers and the serotype 1 variant 1 protein. In the tests using clinical samples of dengue patients’ serum, AptD3-DsDs detected only serotype 3.8b

Application of AptIFNγ-YsYs and AptIFNγ-YoYo to Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA)

To evaluate the improved Ds-variant XenoAptamers for practical use, we tested an ELISA system using a commercially available kit (human IFN gamma uncoated ELISA kit, Invitrogen), which employs a sandwich method with a captured immobilized antibody and a detector biotinylated antibody. For colorimetric detection, the biotinylated antibody is conjugated with streptavidin horseradish peroxidase (HRP).

We replaced the detector biotinylated antibody in the kit (Figure 12A) with AptIFNγ-DsDs, -YsYs, or -YoYo, which contain the biotinylated mini-hairpin sequence at their 3′-termini (Figure 12B), and examined the sensitivity of IFNγ detection. The signal intensities of all AptIFNγ aptamers were higher than that of the antibody, and AptIFNγ-YsYs and -YoYo were much more efficient relative to those of AptIFNγ-DsDs (Figure 12C). Since the site-specific biotinylation of antibodies is difficult, multiple biotinylations of the antibody might occur, thus increasing the signal intensity. In contrast, one biotin was incorporated into the terminal mini-hairpin sequence by chemical synthesis. Nevertheless, the signal intensities of the XenoAptamers were much higher than that of the antibody.

Comparisons of the antibody and XenoAptamers as the detector agents for ELISA. A commercially available kit (human IFN gamma uncoated ELISA kit, Invitrogen) was used for the experiments. The detector antibody (A) was replaced with biotinylated AptIFNγ-DsDs, -YsYs, or -YoYo (B). Signal intensities of ELISAs using the antibody, AptIFNγ-DsDs, -YsYs, or -YoYo, in which the detector concentration is fixed at the final 120 nM concentration (C). The detector concentration dependency, in which the target concentration is fixed at 15 pM (D).

Next, we examined the dependence of the detector-reagent concentration. The ELISA kit protocol recommends the use of a 120 nM detector antibody. By reducing the antibody concentration, the signal intensities were significantly reduced (Figure 12D). In contrast, reducing the concentrations of AptIFNγ-DsDs, -YsYs, and -YoYo did not drastically affect their signal intensities, and 10 nM concentrations of AptIFNγ-YsYs and -YoYo were still sufficient for highly sensitive detection as the detector reagents. The signal intensities using AptIFNγ-YsYs at 120 and 10 nM were higher by 5.3- and 18.8-fold, respectively, as compared to those using the antibody.

Discussion

The improvements in the XenoAptamer affinities by Ds variants are summarized in Table 1. By replacement with specific Ds variants, both kinetic parameters, kon and koff, for the affinities of these aptamers surpassed those of the original Ds-XenoAptamers. The results revealed that the main module is more important for aptamer improvement than the side-chain module. In the main module, either the pyrrolo[2,3-b]pyridine (Y) or benzo[d]imidazole (B) increases the aptamer affinities. For the side-chain module, the original thienyl group is the best and other side chains are less tolerated. Only the furanyl group can be replaced with the thienyl group. In addition, if there is extra space in the Ds binding site of target proteins, then bithienyl groups are acceptable and increase the aptamer affinity, as shown in AptD3. Therefore, the shape fitting between UBs and targets in the binding site might be important, and among the Ds variants that we examined, Ys and Bs are the key compounds to increase the affinities of XenoAptamers.

Kinetic Parameters for Affinities of Ds- and Ds-Variant XenoAptamers to Targets

The higher affinities of the XenoAptamers containing Ys and/or Bs at specific positions, as compared with those of the original Ds-DNA aptamers, suggest the importance of polarity rather than simple hydrophobic interactions (Figure 13). The AptD1 experiments revealed that the subtle structural differences between the Ys and Bs bases greatly affect the aptamer affinities to targets: Only Ys at position 19 and Bs at position 25 are acceptable (KD = 35.9 pM). In contrast, the exchange of the Bs/Ys variants at these positions significantly reduced the affinity (KD = 8.55 nM). The affinity of AptD1-BsYs is 238-fold lower than that of AptD1-YsBs. In most cases, each UB position in the four XenoAptamers prefers Ys and/or Bs. Improvements in the binding affinities of AptIFNγ and AptD3 were observed solely with Ds replacement using main module Y, while both Y and B were favorable for AptvWF. However, the replacement of the other hydrophobic main modules, I, P, and E, reduced the XenoAptamers’ affinities, as compared to those of their original Ds-XenoAptamers. These aptamer affinities are not correlated with the hydrophobicities of these nucleosides, dIs > dYs > dBs > dEs > dDs, as assessed by the retention periods in C18 reverse-phase HPLC (Supplementary Figure S44). These results suggest that the role of the UB is not simply to form hydrophobic or hydrogen-bond interactions involving nitrogens in the main module with targets. One of the differences among Ys, Bs, and Ds might be the dipole moments of their Y, B, and D modules. The dipole moment of nucleobases is an important factor for base pairing and base stacking.14 Accordingly, the different polarities among Ys, Bs, and Ds might drastically affect the affinities of AptD1-YsBs, -BsYs, and -DsDs.

We calculated the dipole moments of Ds, Ys, and Bs, as well as the natural bases (Figure 13). The QM/MM calculations revealed that the effects of the rotational motion of the thiophene moiety on the absolute values of the dipole moments are small and the fluctuations are similar to those of natural bases (Supplementary Figure S1). The dipole moment of Ds is quite small and relatively nonpolar, as compared to those of Ys, Bs, and the natural bases. The direction of the dipole moment of Ys is similar to that of Ds, but 2-fold larger. Bs has a large dipole moment in a different direction from that of Ds. Interestingly, the dipole moments of Ys and Bs are very similar to those of A and G, respectively, and thus Ys and Ds are A- and G-like hydrophobic bases. Ds has moderate and broad specificities as a hydrophobic ligand to interact with hydrophobic site targets. If the site has any polarity, then Ys or Bs reinforces the interaction. However, the unfitted polarity between these UBs and the hydrophobic site causes repulsion, significantly reducing the XenoAptamer’s affinity to its target, as in the case of AptD1-BsYs.

Another factor is the desolvation effect at the nitrogen atoms of the Ds variants. Solvating waters at the nitrogen atoms increase the sizes of the van der Waals surfaces of the variants, which are disfavored in target binding and must be desolvated.15 The desolvation effect might affect kon of the kinetic parameters.16

Besides the direct interactions of the UBs with target proteins, UBs might affect the tertiary structures of XenoAptamers. Most of the natural bases in the loop regions of XenoAptamers are critical for their tight binding. For example, 22 bases in the 25-base single-stranded region of AptIFNγ and 16 bases in the 19-base loop region of AptD1 are essential and involved in their target binding.17 The hydrophobic nonpolar Ds and polar Ys and Bs vary the stacking interactions with their neighboring hydrophilic polar natural bases, thus creating novel XenoAptamer structures for fitting with target proteins.

To scrutinize the role of the UBs, structural analysis of the complex of AptD1 and its target protein is ongoing. The preliminary data by cryoelectron microscopy support the contribution of the dipole moment of the Ds variants to the XenoAptamers’ affinities to targets (unpublished data by Sawada and Nureki).

The replication fidelity and efficiency of Ys–Px and Bs–Px as third pairs are inferior to those of the Ds–Px pair (data not shown). Thus, the initial XenoAptamer generation by ExSELEX is performed using the Ds–Px pair. Then, screening by the replacement of the Ds bases with Ys or Bs could improve the XenoAptamers’ affinities, even if the Ds-XenoAptamers generated by ExSELEX do not achieve sufficient affinities to targets. Thus, the introduction of the Ds variants increases the success rates of the high-affinity XenoAptamer generation.17 In addition to the main module variations, small modifications to the thiophene module might be effective in some cases. The interactions between UBs and targets in XenoAptamers are strictly accurate at atomic levels and involve their hydrophobicity, shape fitting, polarity, desolvation, and partial hydrogen bonds.

Conclusion

XenoAptamer affinities are significantly improved by introducing suitable Ds variants with a unique hydrophobicity and polarity. Since XenoAptamers contain only two hydrophobic Ds variants, they retain higher hydrophilicity overall, which solves the sticky problem of antibodies. Our ELISA experiments demonstrated the superiority of XenoAptamers over antibodies for improved sensitivity of target detection with lower ligand concentrations. XenoAptamers with Ds variants are expected to be useful in diagnostic and therapeutic applications as antibody alternatives. Furthermore, this UB alteration method could be applied to aptamer generation using other UB pair systems.18

Methods

Materials

Unnatural amidites of dDs, dDss, and dPs (ds′)19 for aptamer derivative synthesis were prepared according to the previous reports.10,11,20 Unnatural amidites of dDt, dYi, dDp, and dDo were synthesized by similar methods. Chemical synthesis methods for the other unnatural amidites are described in the Supporting Information, and their NMR spectra are in Supplementary Figures S2–S43. The series of XenoAptamers’ variants in Supplementary Table S1 were chemically synthesized with a DNA/RNA synthesizer using phosphoramidite chemistry with commercially available natural base amidites, Biotin-dT amidite, and the synthesized unnatural base amidites. After deprotection with a concentrated ammonia solution, the full-length DNA fragments were purified by denaturing polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis.

EMSA

The binding (%) of each aptamer variant relative to the target protein was determined by EMSA, based on the shifted band patterns on the gels, which were detected with a bioimager after staining with SYBR Gold. The band densities of free and complexed DNAs were quantified using the software provided with the bioimager, and relative binding (%) was calculated by normalization with the shifted band ratio for the original Ds-XenoAptamer in the same gel. The conditions for the binding and gel electrophoresis are summarized in Supplementary Table S2.

SPR Analysis

The SPR analysis was performed to compare the binding affinity profiles of the novel XenoAptamers with the original Ds-XenoAptamers using a Biacore T200 (GE Healthcare) platform. Each biotinylated aptamer variant was immobilized on the streptavidin-coated sensor chips in the flow cells, by injecting 0.5 nM of aptamer solution in running buffer (1× PBS with 0.05% Nonidet P-40 for vWF, 1× PBS supplemented with 50 mM NaCl and 0.05% Nonidet P-40 for IFNγ, and 20 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 1 mM MgCl2, and 2.7 mM KCl with 0.05% Tween 20 for AptD1 and AptD3), at a flow rate of 5 μL/min for 150 to 960 s at 25 °C. Analyte (target protein) solutions with concentrations ranging from 0.156 to 40 nM (adjusted depending on the target and signal patterns) were prepared in running buffer and injected at a flow rate of 100 μL/min, with an association duration of 150 s and a dissociation duration of 450 s (IFNγ and vWF) or 600 s (DENV1 and DENV3 NS1). The sensor chip was regenerated with a 5 s injection of 50 mM NaOH, followed by a 10 min equilibration with running buffer. Using the BIAevaluation T200 software version 1.0 (GE Healthcare), the KD values were determined by fitting the blank-subtracted kinetics data with a 1:1 binding model.

ELISA

To compare the biotinylated detector capabilities between antibody and aptamer for IFNγ detection, we used a commercially available ELISA kit according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Thermo Fisher Scientific, cat. # 88-7316-88). The antibody detector concentration used in the protocol was estimated to be 120 nM, based on the absorbance of the stock solution at 280 nm. Thus, we also used the biotinylated aptamer at 120 nM as the standard working concentration. As the target, we used two purchased recombinant human IFNγ proteins from Peprotech (catalog no. 300-02, the amino acid residue at position 160 is Q) and Abcam (catalog no. ab51240, the amino acid residue at position 160 is R), which gave similar results.

QM/MM Calculations

The energy-minimized models for Ds, Ys, and Bs were prepared by the modeling function of UCSF Chimera,21 and the structural parameters for molecular dynamics calculations were prepared by the Antechamber module of the AMBER22 suite.22 Atomic charges were calculated by the charge method of AM1-BCC. The base molecules were surrounded by TIP3P water molecules, and 20 ps of QM/MM calculations was performed with the sander module of the AMBER22 suite, in which each base molecule was treated using QM with the PM3 semiempirical Hamiltonian. The numbers of water molecules were 572, 587, and 578 for Ds, Ys, and Bs, respectively. The vectors and absolute values of the dipole moments were obtained every 0.2 ps during the QM/MM simulations. The QM/MM calculations were also performed for the natural bases. The images showing the atomic charges and dipole moments were prepared with UCSF Chimera.

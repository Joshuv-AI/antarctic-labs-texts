Effects of antifibrotic agent Nintedanib on experimentally generated priapism model in rats
Authors: Gökhan Çeker, Reha Girgin, Onder Cinar, Esin Kaymaz, Salih Erdem, Inci Turan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study investigated the effects of Nintedanib, a triple tyrosine kinase inhibitor, on cavernosal tissues in a rat model of ischemic priapism (IP), focusing on fibrosis and erectile function.MethodsSixty male Wistar Albino rats were divided into five groups (n = 12): Group 1 (control), Group 2 (1-hour IP + penectomy), Group 3 (1-hour IP + 6-week follow-up), Group 4 (1-hour IP + Nintedanib treatment for 6 weeks), and Group 5 (Nintedanib only). Erectile function was evaluated via intracavernosal pressure (ICP) during nerve stimulation. Fibrosis was assessed using Masson’s trichrome staining and immunohistochemistry for VEGFR-2, PDGFR-β, and FGF-1. Oxidative stress markers (MDA, GSH, SOD) and growth factors were analyzed via ELISA and spectrophotometry. Statistical analysis used Kruskal-Wallis and Mann-Whitney U tests (p < 0.05).ResultsGroup 4 had significantly lower mean intracavernosal pressure (MeICP) values (median 26.5 mmHg) than Group 3 (49 mmHg) (p < 0.001). Masson’s trichrome showed highest fibrosis in Group 4 (score 3 [2–4]) compared to Group 3 (score 3 [1–4]) (p < 0.001). VEGFR-2 ELISA levels dropped in Group 4 (6.55 ng/mL [5.5–7.1]) vs. Group 3 (7.75 ng/mL [7–7.9]) (p < 0.001). MDA levels peaked in Group 4 (33.66 nmol/g [24.39–45.39]), while GSH values remained comparable between Groups 3 and 4. SOD increased significantly in Groups 2–4 (Group 4: 6.25 ng/mL [5.2–7.0]) compared to Group 1 (4.95 ng/mL [3.9–5.6]) (p < 0.001).ConclusionAlthough Nintedanib is antifibrotic in pulmonary contexts, it appears to promote penile fibrosis post-IP, likely via excessive VEGFR-2 inhibition, impairing vascular recovery and erectile function. Further studies are needed.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00345-025-05993-8.Keywords: Ischemic priapism, Nintedanib, Penile fibrosis, Erectile dysfunction, VEGFR-2, Oxidative stress

Introduction

Priapism is defined as a penile erection lasting more than four hours in the absence of sexual stimulation [1]. Ischemic priapism (IP), the most common subtype (>95%), results from low-flow conditions within the corpora cavernosa, leading to hypoxia, acidosis, and ultimately irreversible smooth muscle damage if untreated [2]. Fibrosis ensues through a cascade involving inflammatory responses and maladaptive tissue remodeling [3]. This sequence can culminate in permanent erectile dysfunction (ED) [4].

Previous studies have shown that key profibrotic mediators, including fibroblast growth factor (FGF), transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β), and platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), are involved in the fibrosis process observed in penile ischemia and Peyronie’s disease [5–7]. These signaling pathways are also central to the pathogenesis of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) [8], suggesting a molecular overlap between the two conditions. This similarity raises the possibility that antifibrotic agents used in IPF could be repurposed for treating penile fibrosis.

Nintedanib (BIBF 1120) is a low molecular weight tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting VEGFR, PDGFR, and FGFR, approved for IPF and certain cancers [9–11]. Its anti-angiogenic and antifibrotic actions are well-documented in pulmonary models [12]. Penile tissue recovery after ischemia is highly dependent on angiogenesis, which restores oxygenation and prevents irreversible smooth muscle damage. By contrast, Nintedanib exerts a potent anti-angiogenic effect through VEGFR-2 inhibition. This creates a clear paradox: while beneficial in halting pathological remodeling in the lungs, suppression of angiogenesis in penile tissue could impair post-ischemic repair, worsen hypoxia-induced injury, and accelerate fibrosis.

This study aimed to explore Nintedanib’s role in cavernosal tissue remodeling and erectile function post-IP. We hypothesized that despite its antifibrotic properties, Nintedanib’s potent inhibition of VEGFR-2 would paradoxically impair erectile recovery and increase cavernosal fibrosis by hindering essential post-ischemic neovascularization.

Methods

Animal Model and Ethics: This study was approved by the Zonguldak Bülent Ecevit University Medical Faculty Animal Experiments Ethics Committee (Protocol No: 2019-02-10/01). The experiments were conducted in the institution’s experimental animals research laboratory. Sixty healthy adult male Wistar Albino rats (370–420 g) were obtained from the Zonguldak Bülent Ecevit University Experimental Animals Research and Application Center (Zonguldak, Turkey). Rats were housed in standard cages with a 12-hour light/dark cycle at 20 ± 2 °C. They were fed a standard diet with free access to food and water, except for a 2-hour fasting period before anesthesia to minimize complications.

Experimental design

The rats were randomly divided into five groups (n = 12 per group).

Group 1 (Control): Intracavernosal pressure (ICP) measurement followed by penectomy. Cavernosal tissues were analyzed histopathologically and biochemically.

Group 1 (Control): Intracavernosal pressure (ICP) measurement followed by penectomy. Cavernosal tissues were analyzed histopathologically and biochemically.

Group 2 (Acute IP): 1-hour ischemic priapism was induced via vacuum erection and constriction band. After band removal, ICP was measured, followed by penectomy and tissue analysis.

Group 2 (Acute IP): 1-hour ischemic priapism was induced via vacuum erection and constriction band. After band removal, ICP was measured, followed by penectomy and tissue analysis.

Group 3 (Untreated IP): Following 1-hour IP induction, rats were monitored for 6 weeks without treatment. ICP measurement and penectomy were performed at the end of the follow-up.

Group 3 (Untreated IP): Following 1-hour IP induction, rats were monitored for 6 weeks without treatment. ICP measurement and penectomy were performed at the end of the follow-up.

Group 4 (IP + Nintedanib): After 1-hour IP induction, rats received Nintedanib (60 mg/kg/day in two divided doses) orally for 6 weeks, followed by ICP measurement and penectomy.

Group 4 (IP + Nintedanib): After 1-hour IP induction, rats received Nintedanib (60 mg/kg/day in two divided doses) orally for 6 weeks, followed by ICP measurement and penectomy.

Group 5 (Nintedanib only): Received oral Nintedanib (60 mg/kg/day, twice daily) for 6 weeks without IP induction. ICP measurement and penectomy followed.

Group 5 (Nintedanib only): Received oral Nintedanib (60 mg/kg/day, twice daily) for 6 weeks without IP induction. ICP measurement and penectomy followed.

In this study, the dosing of Nintedanib was selected based on previously reported effective and safe ranges demonstrated in experimental models of pulmonary fibrosis [13–15]. The chosen dose (60 mg/kg/day) reflects the therapeutic dose shown to exert antifibrotic efficacy in preclinical studies without causing toxicity. Nintedanib was suspended in 0.5% (w/v) sodium carboxymethylcellulose (CMC) and administered orally by gavage in two divided daily doses for 6 weeks. Anesthesia was induced by intraperitoneal injection of ketamine hydrochloride (50 mg/kg) and xylazine (10 mg/kg). After confirming adequate anesthesia, the surgical site was cleaned with the animal in the supine position.

Induction of ischemic priapism

A vacuum erection device was constructed by cutting the tip of a 5-cc syringe to fit the base of the flaccid penis. Before application, 2-mm thick constriction bands were cut from a 16 Fr Foley catheter and loaded onto the tip of the syringe. The device was then placed at the base of the penis and the plunger gently withdrawn to induce erection. Once sufficient erection was achieved, the constriction band was slipped onto the base of the penis (radix penis) to maintain the erection for 1 h (Fig. 1), thereby inducing ischemic priapism. This procedure was adapted from previously published models in the literature [16].

Measurement of erectile function

Following appropriate anesthesia, the neck region was shaved and sterilized. A heparinized catheter was placed in the carotid artery to monitor arterial blood pressure. This allowed stabilization and real-time monitoring of systemic circulation during ICP measurement. After cleaning and sterilizing the penile area, a bipolar stainless-steel stimulating electrode was placed at the posterolateral side of the prostate to stimulate the cavernous nerves bilaterally [17, 18].

A 25-gauge needle connected to a heparinized polyethylene cannula was inserted into the left corpus cavernosum and connected to a data acquisition system (Biopac MP30 System, Santa Barbara, CA, USA). Electrical stimulation was applied to all rats with parameters of 4 volts, 15 Hz frequency, and a 15-millisecond pulse width for 60 s. Simultaneously, intra-aortic blood pressure (IABP) monitoring was performed via carotid artery catheterization. The following erectile parameters were recorded: maximum intracavernosal pressure (ICP max), baseline ICP (BICP), mean ICP (MeICP), mean arterial pressure (MAP), area under the curve (AUC), and the ratio of MeICP/MAP (Fig. 1). To minimize bias, all intracavernosal pressure (ICP) measurements were performed by investigators blinded to the group assignments.

Histological and Immunohistochemical Analysis: After ICP measurement, rats were euthanized, and penectomy specimens were collected. Tissues were fixed in 10% formalin, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 4 μm thickness. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining was used for general histopathology. Masson’s trichrome staining was performed using the Ventana Roche BenchMark Special Stain system to evaluate the ratio of collagen to smooth muscle. Images from four random fields (×40) were analyzed with ImageJ software. Fibrosis severity was graded semi-quantitatively as follows: Grade 1 for < 30% collagen content, Grade 2 for 30–50%, Grade 3 for 50–70%, and Grade 4 for >70% (see Fig. 3, Supplementary material) [19].

Immunohistochemical staining for VEGFR-2, PDGFR-β, and FGF-1 was conducted using BenchMark ULTRA system. The following antibodies were used: VEGFR-2 (D5B1) Rabbit mAb, Cell Signaling Technology, 1:1600 dilution; PDGFR-β (D-6) Mouse mAb, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-374573, 1:500 dilution; and FGF-1 (B:3) Mouse mAb, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, sc-55520, 1:500 dilution. Slides were deparaffinized at 72 °C, and antigen retrieval was performed with CC1 citrate for 64 min. Each slide received 100 µL of antibody. Staining durations: VEGFR-2 (52 min), PDGFR-β (1 h 36 min), FGF-1 (1 h), all at 36 °C. Hematoxylin I/II and Bluing Reagent were used as counterstains.

All three immunohistochemical markers were evaluated under light microscopy according to the scoring method proposed by Aversa et al., based on staining intensity in cavernosal tissues [5]. Staining intensity in five random fields (×40) was graded as follows: Grade 1 for staining in 0–10% of cells, Grade 2 for 10–25%, Grade 3 for 25–50%, and Grade 4 for >50%. Positive staining for VEGFR-2 was defined as brown cytoplasmic endothelial signal; PDGFR-β and FGF-1 were assessed in smooth muscle, endothelium, and fibroblasts (see Figs. 4, 5 and 6, Supplementary material). Histological scoring and immunohistochemical evaluations were conducted by independent observers blinded to group allocation.

Biochemical analysis

Following penectomy, cavernosal tissues were dissected and divided longitudinally into two portions. One half was fixed for histological evaluation, while the other was snap-frozen at − 80 °C for biochemical assays.

Tissue Homogenization: Frozen samples were thawed on ice and homogenized in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) using a mechanical homogenizer. Homogenates were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 20 min at 2–8 °C. Supernatants were collected and used for ELISA and spectrophotometric evaluations.

ELISA Measurements: Quantification of SOD (ng/mL), VEGFR-2 (ng/mL), PDGFR-β (pg/mL), and FGF-2 (pg/mL) was performed using commercial ELISA kits (SunRed Biotechnology and Cloud-Clone Corp.) according to manufacturers’ protocols. Each assay included appropriate standards, and all measurements were performed in duplicate. Reactions were terminated with stop solutions, and absorbance was read at 450 nm.

Spectrophotometric Assays:

MDA: As a marker of lipid peroxidation, MDA was measured using thiobarbituric acid reactive substances (TBARS) assay. Tissue homogenates were mixed with trichloroacetic acid, butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT), and thiobarbituric acid (TBA), then boiled for 15 min. Absorbance was recorded at 535 nm. Results were expressed in nmol/g tissue.

MDA: As a marker of lipid peroxidation, MDA was measured using thiobarbituric acid reactive substances (TBARS) assay. Tissue homogenates were mixed with trichloroacetic acid, butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT), and thiobarbituric acid (TBA), then boiled for 15 min. Absorbance was recorded at 535 nm. Results were expressed in nmol/g tissue.

GSH: Reduced glutathione levels were determined by reacting samples with dithiobisnitrobenzoate and Na₂HPO₄ buffer. Absorbance at 412 nm was used to quantify GSH content, expressed in µmol/g tissue.

GSH: Reduced glutathione levels were determined by reacting samples with dithiobisnitrobenzoate and Na₂HPO₄ buffer. Absorbance at 412 nm was used to quantify GSH content, expressed in µmol/g tissue.

All biochemical analyses were also performed under blinded conditions with respect to treatment groups.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics version 19.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Data are presented as median values with minimum and maximum ranges [median (min–max)]. The normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Since the data were not normally distributed, non-parametric statistical methods were employed. Comparisons among multiple groups were performed using the Kruskal-Wallis H test. In case of statistical significance, pairwise comparisons between groups were carried out using the Mann-Whitney U test. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Hemodynamic and electrophysiological results

Mean arterial pressure (MAP) values did not differ significantly among the groups (Group 1: median 130.5 mmHg, Group 2: 120 mmHg, Group 3: 128 mmHg, Group 4: 130.5 mmHg, Group 5: 127 mmHg; p = 0.110).

Baseline intracavernosal pressure (BICP) was slightly lower in Group 2, with a median value of 15.5 mmHg, compared to Group 1 (19 mmHg), but this difference was not significant (p = 0.147). Group 3 showed a significant increase, with a median BICP of 21 mmHg, compared to Group 2 (p = 0.039), although values did not differ from controls (p = 0.269). In contrast, Group 4 demonstrated markedly reduced BICP, with a median of 11.5 mmHg, compared to both Group 1 (p < 0.001) and Group 3 (p < 0.001). Group 5 (17 mmHg) was comparable to controls (p = 0.116).

Mean ICP (MeICP) was significantly reduced in Group 2, with a median of 34.5 mmHg, compared to Group 1 (61 mmHg, p < 0.001). Group 3 partially recovered, with a median of 49 mmHg, but values remained lower than controls (p < 0.001). Group 4 showed a pronounced decline, with a median MeICP of 26.5 mmHg, compared to Group 3 (p < 0.001). Group 5 also remained impaired, with a median of 46.5 mmHg, compared to Group 1 (p < 0.001).

Maximum ICP (MICP) followed a similar pattern. Group 2 showed a median of 55 mmHg, significantly lower than Group 1 (82 mmHg, p < 0.001). Group 3 recovered partially, with a median of 62.5 mmHg, but values remained significantly below controls (p < 0.001). Group 4 further declined, with a median MICP of 32.5 mmHg compared to Group 3 (p < 0.001). Group 5, with a median of 57.5 mmHg, was also significantly reduced compared to Group 1 (p < 0.001).

Area under the curve (AUC) was significantly reduced in Group 2, with a median of 605, compared to Group 1 (1111.5, p < 0.001). Group 3 (640) showed partial recovery but remained below controls (p = 0.001). Group 4 demonstrated further reductions, with a median AUC of 284, compared to Group 3 (p < 0.001). Group 5, with a median of 671.5, was also significantly reduced compared to Group 1 (p < 0.001).

MeICP/MAP ratios mirrored these findings. Group 2 showed a median ratio of 0.29, significantly lower than Group 1 (0.50, p < 0.001). Group 3 partially recovered, with a ratio of 0.37, but remained lower than controls (p < 0.001). Group 4 declined further, with a median of 0.20, compared to Group 3 (p < 0.001). Group 5 also showed impairment, with a ratio of 0.36 compared to Group 1 (p < 0.001).

Pathology & immunohistochemistry

Fibrosis scores assessed by Masson’s trichrome staining increased after ischemic priapism. Group 2 showed higher fibrosis, with a median score of 2 (1–2) compared to Group 1 (1 [1–2], p = 0.039). Group 3 further increased to a median of 3 (2–4), significantly higher than controls (p < 0.001). Group 4 reached the highest level, also with a median of 3 (3–4), although significantly greater than Group 3 (p = 0.026). Group 5, with a median of 1 (1–2), was comparable to controls (p = 0.356).

VEGFR-2 expression did not differ significantly between Group 2 (1.5) and Group 1 (2, p = 0.162). Group 3 increased to a median of 3 compared to controls (p = 0.007), while Group 4 declined to 2, significantly lower than Group 3 (p = 0.015) (Fig. 2). Group 5 also showed reduced expression, with a median of 1, significantly lower than controls (p = 0.036).

Biochemical analyses

VEGFR-2 levels were lower in Group 2, with a median of 5.3 ng/mL compared to Group 1 (6.25 ng/mL, p = 0.018). Group 3 increased to 7.75 ng/mL, significantly higher than controls (p < 0.001). Group 4 declined to 6.55 ng/mL, significantly lower than Group 3 (p < 0.001). Group 5, with a median of 5.1 ng/mL, was also reduced compared to controls (p = 0.002).

PDGFR-β levels were higher in Group 2, with a median of 366 pg/mL compared to Group 1 (302.5 pg/mL, p = 0.002). Group 3 further increased to 561.5 pg/mL, significantly higher than controls (p < 0.001). Group 4, with a median of 541 pg/mL, did not differ from Group 3 (p = 0.057). Group 5, with a median of 299.5 pg/mL, was comparable to controls (p = 0.862).

FGF-2 levels remained stable in Group 2, with a median of 14 pg/mL compared to Group 1 (13.2 pg/mL, p = 0.773). Group 3 decreased to 11.05 pg/mL, significantly lower than controls (p = 0.005). Group 4, with a median of 10.8 pg/mL, did not differ significantly from Group 3 (p = 0.488). Group 5, with a median of 14.15 pg/mL, was similar to controls (p = 0.931).

MDA levels were elevated in Group 2, with a median of 21.41 nmol/g compared to Group 1 (17.14 nmol/g, p = 0.001). Group 3 increased further to 29.76 nmol/g, significantly higher than controls (p < 0.001). Group 4 reached the highest level, with a median of 33.66 nmol/g, though this did not differ significantly from Group 3 (p = 0.273). Group 5, with a median of 18.46 nmol/g, was comparable to controls (p = 0.419).

GSH levels decreased significantly in Group 2, with a median of 0.51 µmol/g compared to Group 1 (0.96 µmol/g, p < 0.001). Group 3 partially recovered to 0.80 µmol/g, which was significantly higher than Group 2 (p < 0.001) but remained lower than controls (p = 0.002). Group 4, with a median of 0.81 µmol/g, did not differ from Group 3 (p = 0.795). Group 5, with a median of 1.00 µmol/g, was comparable to controls (p = 0.356).

SOD levels increased in Group 2, with a median of 7.25 ng/mL compared to Group 1 (4.95 ng/mL, p < 0.001). Group 3 remained elevated at 6.45 ng/mL, significantly higher than controls (p < 0.001). Group 4, with a median of 6.25 ng/mL, did not differ significantly from Group 3 (p = 0.434). Group 5, with a median of 4.80 ng/mL, was comparable to controls (p = 0.885). (Table 1).

Discussion

In this study, ICP parameters including mean intracavernosal pressure (MeICP), maximal ICP (MICP), and MeICP/MAP ratio were significantly reduced in rats treated with Nintedanib, both in the post-priapism (Group 4) and healthy settings (Group 5), compared to their respective controls. These findings suggest that despite Nintedanib’s documented antifibrotic efficacy in pulmonary [20] and hepatic [21] fibrosis, its antiangiogenic action—particularly through VEGFR-2 inhibition—may impair endothelial recovery in cavernosal tissue. In models of ischemia-reperfusion injury, vascular regeneration is critical for restoring erectile function. The marked decrease in ICP measurements after Nintedanib administration implies that suppression of neovascularization may lead to reduced blood filling capacity and corporal compliance. This contrasts with prior studies where antifibrotic agents such as pirfenidone preserved or improved ICP responses following similar injuries [19]. The VEGF/VEGFR axis exhibits tissue-specific dynamics, which are well-studied in pulmonary fibrosis but less understood in penile physiology. While elevated serum VEGF levels have been associated with disease progression in IPF patients, its local expression in fibrotic lesions is often reduced, suggesting a spatially heterogeneous pattern of action [22–24]. In contrast to this pattern, several studies have confirmed VEGF, VEGFR-1, and VEGFR-2 expression in the corpus cavernosum tissues of both humans and rats, underscoring a critical role in erectile function and vascular integrity [25–27]. In our priapism model, VEGFR-2 levels decreased in the acute ischemic phase (Group 2) and showed compensatory elevation in the chronic phase (Group 3), likely reflecting a hypoxia-induced angiogenic rebound. These findings align with reports demonstrating increased VEGF expression in cavernous tissue under hypoxic stress, contributing to endothelial recovery and antifibrotic remodeling [28, 29]. However, Nintedanib administration significantly suppressed VEGFR-2 expression in both the post-ischemic (Group 4) and non-ischemic (Group 5) settings. While this aligns with Nintedanib’s anti-angiogenic mechanism, it may hinder reparative angiogenesis in vascular-rich tissues like the penis. Notably, Group 5—despite the lowest VEGFR-2 levels—showed less cavernous fibrosis than Group 4, suggesting that VEGFR-2 expression alone does not linearly predict fibrotic burden. Instead, erectile function likely involves a complex interplay between vascular, muscular, and neural factors.

The fibroblast growth factor (FGF) family plays a critical role in cellular proliferation, differentiation, and tissue repair. In our study, FGF-2 levels decreased in the chronic phase following priapism, and this reduction was not significantly altered by Nintedanib administration. In contrast, the literature reports FGF-2 as a potent stimulator of fibroblast and myofibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis, contributing to fibrotic progression in pulmonary conditions such as IPF [30, 31]. Increased FGF-2 expression and FGFR activation have been observed in IPF patients [31, 32]. Conversely, FGF-1 has been shown to exert anti-fibrotic effects by inhibiting fibroblast proliferation, inducing apoptosis, and suppressing differentiation into myofibroblasts [33]. Our findings indicate that Nintedanib significantly decreased FGF-1 levels only in the presence of priapism (Group 4 vs. Group 3), suggesting that it may inadvertently suppress a potentially protective factor. This suppression might contribute to the increased fibrosis and impaired erectile function observed in treated animals. Therefore, the distinct roles of FGF-1 and FGF-2 in penile tissue remodeling post-ischemia, as well as their modulation by Nintedanib, warrant further investigation in future studies.

The platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF) pathway is a well-established mediator of fibrosis through its effects on fibroblast proliferation, migration, and extracellular matrix (ECM) accumulation [34, 35]. Experimental studies have demonstrated that PDGFR-specific tyrosine kinase inhibitors reduce lung fibrosis in various models [36, 37]. In this context, our priapism model showed elevated PDGFR-β expression during the chronic phase (Group 3), suggesting its involvement in penile fibrosis. However, administration of Nintedanib—despite its established efficacy in inhibiting PDGFR signaling in pulmonary fibrosis—did not significantly reduce PDGFR-β levels in either the post-ischemic (Group 4) or non-ischemic (Group 5) penile tissue. This finding aligns with previous studies indicating that although PDGF is a potent mitogen in many tissues, its pharmacologic modulation in corpus cavernosum may be limited [35, 38]. These results reflect the findings of Hung et al. [38], who showed that PDGF had minimal effect on iNOS expression in penile smooth muscle cells, indicating limited regulatory influence on nitric oxide-mediated pathways. Although PDGF has been implicated in the fibrotic progression of penile tissue under hypoxic conditions [5] and in disease states such as Peyronie’s disease and venous occlusive dysfunction [39], our data suggest that PDGFR-β modulation by Nintedanib may be limited or that additional profibrotic pathways dominate in penile tissue. Notably, PDGFR-β levels in Group 5 were comparable to controls, suggesting that this normalization may be time-dependent rather than pharmacologically driven. These findings emphasize the need to consider tissue-specific pharmacodynamics when translating antifibrotic therapies from pulmonary to urogenital contexts.

Mechanistically, the paradoxical increase in fibrosis and the decline in erectile function observed with Nintedanib appear to be primarily driven by suppression of VEGFR-2 signaling and the consequent impairment of post-ischemic revascularization. In the untreated chronic priapism group, VEGFR-2 expression increased, consistent with a compensatory angiogenic response to hypoxia, whereas Nintedanib markedly reduced VEGFR-2 expression, thereby limiting vascular repair and endothelial recovery. This insufficient revascularization is expected to aggravate local hypoxia, creating a microenvironment prone to fibrosis and functional deterioration. Oxidative stress markers, including malondialdehyde (MDA), glutathione (GSH), and superoxide dismutase (SOD), did not differ significantly in the primary comparisons; however, the persistently higher MDA levels in the Nintedanib group may indicate an additional burden of lipid peroxidation. Overall, angiogenesis impairment clearly emerges as the dominant mechanism underlying the exacerbated fibrosis and erectile dysfunction in this model.

Clinically, our findings provide a strong preclinical warning against the empirical or off-label use of Nintedanib in urological fibrotic conditions such as Peyronie’s disease or post-priapism fibrosis. Unlike pulmonary or hepatic tissues, erectile recovery critically depends on robust angiogenesis and vascular integrity, making the suppression of VEGFR-2 signaling particularly detrimental in the penile context. Our results also suggest that Nintedanib may act as a profibrotic agent in penile tissue with a history of ischemia and may impair erectile function even under baseline conditions. This raises the possibility that patients receiving Nintedanib who later experience priapism could be at greater risk of post-priapism erectile dysfunction. Although these observations require validation in clinical studies, they underscore the importance of considering erectile function status when antifibrotic therapies are prescribed and highlight the need for tissue-specific strategies that favor pro-angiogenic or vascular-protective pathways rather than angiogenesis inhibition in the treatment of penile fibrosis.

Limitations and future directions

The study did not include a dose-response arm; whether a lower dose might balance antifibrotic versus antiangiogenic effects remains to be explored. The follow-up duration was limited to six weeks; longer-term effects on erectile recovery and fibrosis regression should be examined. Additional studies investigating molecular markers of fibrosis (e.g. TGF-β, collagen I/III) and angiogenesis (e.g. CD31 immunostaining) would further elucidate underlying mechanisms.

Conclusion

While Nintedanib is effective against pulmonary fibrosis, its administration in penile ischemia may paradoxically promote fibrosis and impair erectile recovery. Therapeutic strategies should be tailored to tissue-specific contexts.

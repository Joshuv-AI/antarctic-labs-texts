Large language model-driven agents in nursing practice: A scoping review
Authors: Xinglin Zheng, Huina Zou, Linjing Wu, Peihuang Dong, Wenhui Yuan, Yuan Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThis review aimed to systematically analyze the technological frameworks, application scenarios, and outcomes of large language model-driven agents (LLMDAs) in nursing practice, and to summarize ethical, technological, and practical challenges, guiding future research and clinical implementation.MethodsThis scoping review was conducted following the JBI guidelines. Five databases (PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, APA PsycNet, Cochrane Library) were systematically searched for peer-reviewed English-language studies from inception until September 9, 2025. Eligible studies were screened by title and abstract, with full-text assessments conducted independently by two reviewers.ResultsTwenty-five studies published between 2023 and 2025 were included, involving nine countries, primarily China (n = 9) and the United States (n = 9). Technological architectures were categorized into three types: collaborative models for solving complex tasks through multi-agent division of labor; augmentative models to enhance the accuracy of information outputs; and interactive models focusing on natural interactions and robotic task execution. Application scenarios included clinical, home-based, and community care. Studies indicated that LLMDAs can enhance diagnostic accuracy, optimize resource allocation, and improve patient experience. Primary ethical challenges identified included data privacy, reliability of generated content, and ambiguous attribution of responsibility.ConclusionsLLMDAs offer a novel paradigm for intelligent transformation in nursing care through integrative technological frameworks. They demonstrate considerable potential in enhancing clinical decision-making accuracy, efficiency of care delivery, and patient satisfaction. Addressing existing ethical, technical, and practical challenges is essential for facilitating broader clinical adoption.

Introduction

With the diversified development of medical needs and the comprehensive implementation of health strategies worldwide, nursing services face dual challenges: a shortage of qualified personnel and the need to enhance service quality [1]. Currently, the growing population of disabled elderly represents a major concern, as the number of nursing professionals is insufficient to meet demands, prompting nursing services to explore precision-based care approaches [2]. A recent report [3] emphasizes promoting high-quality nursing development as an essential measure aligned with national health strategies and integral to broader healthcare advancement. Patients’ demands for nursing care have diversified, highlighting the increasing role of information technology within the nursing field [4]. Moreover, interdisciplinary collaboration has gradually become a trend in nursing practice. Influenced by policy initiatives, artificial intelligence technologies are progressively applied to healthcare, providing new opportunities for nursing development. Large language models (LLMs) are transformer-based models containing tens of thousands to trillions of parameters or more. These models primarily employ deep learning techniques and, after extensive training on massive datasets, are capable of complex tasks such as natural language processing and human-computer interaction [5]. In healthcare, current LLMs primarily excel at intent recognition and text generation but face limitations in fully supporting complex real-world clinical tasks [6]. Therefore, additional capabilities beyond language processing are required to meet clinical needs better. In contrast to LLMs, AI agents overcome certain limitations in healthcare by exhibiting high autonomy, adaptability, and executive capability [7]. In October 2024, Gartner’s “Top 10 Strategic Technology Trends for 2025” report identified AI agents as a major technological trend for 2025 [8]. The concept of agent-based systems originated in the 1990s, evolving from traditional architectures toward autonomous agent frameworks, emphasizing systems capable of independent problem-solving [9,10].

However, research has shown that traditional agents lack the ability to decompose complex tasks as humans do and still require explicit instructions to use tools to achieve their goals [10] sequentially. Consequently, large language model-driven agents (LLMDAs) have emerged as the central focus of future agent development. Essentially, LLMDAs are multi-agent systems driven by a large language model that serves as the core engine for problem-solving [11]. The LLM functions as the agent's “brain,” responsible for semantic understanding, logical reasoning, and task decomposition, thereby compensating for the limitations of traditional agents [12]. Ultimately, LLMDAs can autonomously perceive their environment, conduct decision analysis, and connect to external tools to perform specific operations. Currently, both LLMs and traditional agents are developing rapidly, with an exponential increase in published studies. However, each remains constrained by inherent technical limitations and has yet to evolve from single-function modules into systems capable of autonomously executing diverse, complex tasks. LLMDAs are gradually emerging within the healthcare sector, leveraging their advantages to promote a shift in nursing practice from passive response to proactive intervention. Their future applications in nursing are expected to become increasingly practical and deeply integrated.

However, existing research on LLMDAs in nursing remains fragmented and lacks systematic integration. Therefore, a comprehensive, evidence-based synthesis is needed to guide healthcare professionals in both research and clinical practice [13]. This study aimed to examine the current applications of LLMDAs in nursing practice, organize and analyze the existing literature, summarize key challenges, and explore how LLMDAs can drive technological innovation in nursing while complementing traditional methods to address real-world clinical challenges.

Methods

The scoping synthesis adhered to the JBI methodology for the scoping review [13]. In addition, the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA) statement was used to report on our evaluation [14]. The authors were trained in the methodology for scoping reviews using the Joanna Briggs Institute Reviewer’s Manual 2020.

Identifying the research question

This scoping review follows the PCC framework: LLMDAs (Population); technical approaches, application scenarios, and outcomes (Concept); nursing practice (Context). It aims to synthesize the application of LLMDAs in nursing practice systematically, and to address the following questions: 1) What are the technical architectures and application scenarios of LLMDAs in nursing practice? 2) What are the research contents and effectiveness of LLMDAs in nursing practice? 3) To explore the challenges related to LLMDAs in nursing practice, and future development direction thinking.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Literature Inclusion Criteria: 1) studies that explore the theoretical validation, technological development, and other relevant aspects of LLMDAs in nursing practice; 2) study types include, but are not limited to, descriptive studies, experimental studies, and others; 3) studies focusing on the application of LLMDAs in nursing practice scenarios and the effectiveness of the practice; 4) the studies included in this scoping review are not limited to any specific country or healthcare system. Exclusion criteria included: separate application of the concepts of agentic bodies and large language modeling; and application scenarios not involving nursing practice. However, due to our limited financial resources, restricted access to international databases, and the subpar quality of automated translations. Secondly, LLMDAs are currently an emerging field that is undergoing rapid development, with most published articles appearing in English and Chinese. Therefore, this study only includes papers written in these two languages, which results in minimal bias.

Search methods

Initial searches were conducted in Web of Science and PubMed for LLMDAs’ applications in nursing practice. Retrieved studies were analyzed based on their titles, abstracts, and indexing terms, and a customized search strategy was developed for each database accordingly. At a minimum, the following terms were incorporated: large language modeling/generative artificial intelligence, agents/autonomous agents/multimodal agents, nursing practice/clinical care. A combination of MeSH terms, free words, and Boolean connectives was utilized for searching, with relevant references also tracked. Five databases were searched: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, APA PsycNet, and the Cochrane Library. The search period spanned from the establishment of each database to September 9, 2025. For additional details, please refer to Appendix A.

Study selection

All identified records were organized and uploaded into the NoteExpress software to eliminate duplicate records. This process was conducted by two researchers (X. Zheng and H. Zhou) independently and in a blinded fashion. Literature screening was conducted strictly in accordance with the inclusion and exclusion criteria, and the reasons for exclusion during the full-text screening stage were documented. Given the interdisciplinary nature of this study, researchers standardized the screening criteria. They reached a consensus on interdisciplinary terminology related to LLMs and agents to ensure screening reliability during the title/abstract screening phase. Any disagreements that arose at each stage of the study selection process were resolved via team discussions until a consensus was reached.

Data extraction and data analysis

This study follows the JBI data extraction and analysis methodology. Data extraction tools (i.e., Excel) were utilized to collect information from the included studies, including authors, country of publication, publication year, sample size, technical architecture, nursing stage, application scenario, research findings, ethical challenges, and technical and implementation-related challenges. This process was carried out by two independent researchers and cross-verified by a third researcher to minimize bias. Extracted data are presented in the appendix tables. For data analysis, a descriptive approach was employed in line with the review’s objectives. This approach enabled a comprehensive analysis that integrates logical and descriptive components, aligning with the review’s goals while facilitating the identification of gaps in the literature [15].

Results

Search outcomes

A preliminary search yielded 526 articles, with 430 remaining after removal of duplicates. Subsequently, 341 articles were excluded following title and abstract screening, and 64 additional articles were excluded after full-text review, resulting in 25 articles being included in this review. Quality of included studies and description of study types

The included articles primarily focused on development and validation studies, with a smaller subset addressing performance evaluation or application exploration, and collectively demonstrated high overall quality. Most studies focused on developing AI tools/frameworks to address clinical pain points and validated their effectiveness through real-world clinical data, expert validation, and user testing. Many studies integrated technologies such as LLMs, multi-agent systems, and retrieval-augmented generation. Some studies incorporated reinforcement learning and knowledge distillation to optimize performance, thereby balancing innovation with practicality.

Among the 25 included studies, some did not clearly define their sample sizes. Given that LLMDA has remained in the exploratory phase of nursing practice from 2023 to 2025, these studies predominantly utilized simulation-based or qualitative methodologies. These studies relied primarily on database-based or technical validation, with a focus on algorithmic accuracy rather than human subject or case sample sizes. Despite these characteristics, the studies met the inclusion criteria and aligned with the scoping review objectives. Nevertheless, precisely because this is a rapidly evolving emerging field, long-term validation of its application effects remains inadequate and thus awaits further in-depth investigation by subsequent researchers.

Technical architecture for LLMDAs in nursing practice

A technical architecture is a structured description of solutions to technical problems. This study classifies the key technical architectures identified in the included studies based on two dimensions: problem objectives and technical approaches. Of the 25 articles included in the literature, ten studies [17,18,21,25,[29], [30], [31],35,38,40] adopted a collaborative architecture. This architecture aims to utilize multiple LLMDAs to simulate the collaborative work of professional healthcare teams, addressing problems that a single original model cannot solve. Its core lies in the ability to autonomously reason and invoke multiple tools through role division and dynamic collaboration. Nine studies [16,19,20,24,26,32,33,37,39] were categorized as augmented architectures, which aim to mitigate the “output hallucination risk” of LLMs in medical scenarios through methods such as retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and simulation validation frameworks. These augmented architectures strive to balance patient privacy protection with agent language model performance, with a focus on accuracy and responsiveness. Six studies [22,23,27,28,34,36] summarize the interactive architecture. The main technologies involve determining patient needs through speech recognition, sentiment analysis, and 5G networking to transmit real-time condition changes. Various types of medical robots perform intelligently to address the shortcomings of insufficient service accessibility in traditional agent bodies. For example, the system integrates electronic medical records and Internet of Things (IoT) devices to parse patient data in real time through LLM. Scenarios for LLMDAs in nursing practice

This study classified the application scenarios of LLMDAs in nursing practice (from the included studies) into three categories based on service scenarios and functional objectives: clinical nursing, home care, and community nursing. Of the included studies, 13 [[16], [17], [18],20,21,23,31,33,[35], [36], [37], [38],40] focused on clinical nursing scenarios, which are centered on healthcare organizations and target the diagnostic and treatment phases of diseases. Currently, the primary goal remains improving the accuracy of medical diagnosis and optimizing care processes. Augmented architectures enhance the quality of output information. In contrast, collaborative architectures invoke tools to address problems—collectively laying a foundation for the safe and practical application of LLMDAs in nursing to meet clinical practice needs better. Eight studies focused on home care, which serves homebound patients, elderly individuals with disabilities, and other vulnerable groups. Four of these studies [22,25,28,29] provided daily living care, basic rehabilitation, and psychological support; current research in this area includes, but is not limited to, home care robots for individuals with dementia and intelligent follow-up systems for lung cancer patients, with a focus on using algorithms to conduct home-based prognosis assessments. Meanwhile, four other studies [24,26,27,32] aimed to generate executable care plans or rehabilitation recommendations for patients, validating the feasibility of humanoid care robots in delivering home-based rehabilitation. Four studies [19,30,34,39] focused on community nursing scenarios, which provide services such as preventive healthcare on a community-wide scale to residents of all age groups. Existing research in this domain orients LLMDAs toward screening healthy populations for early disease risks, facilitating disease prevention through causal inference, and supporting the design of public health service infrastructures.

The content and effectiveness of research on LLMDAs in nursing practice

The included literature systematically examines innovative research on LLMDAs in the field of nursing and healthcare, organized around three key directions: Twelve studies [[16], [17], [18],20,21,24,26,29,33,35,38,40] demonstrated the ability to enhance the intelligence of nursing decision-making and improve the accuracy of nursing diagnosis. Specifically, these studies found that an innovative autonomous reasoning framework yielded a lower error rate for LLMs than that of healthcare experts. Additionally, the combined application of RAG and knowledge graphs effectively delineated the knowledge sources for LLMs, thereby enhancing the accuracy of health knowledge assessments and prognostic predictions. Ke et al. [21] further validated the holistic role of agents in reducing cognitive bias. Eight studies [19,22,25,[30], [31], [32],34,37] demonstrated significant impacts in optimizing nursing resource allocation. Xu et al. [19] developed automated tools for Mendelian randomization studies, reducing researchers’ workload and associated costs. Liu et al. [25] developed an intelligent follow-up system, reducing the need for nursing interventions in patients with pulmonary nodules. Braga et al. [30] focused on remote rural areas, proposing the development of a medical autonomous decision-making system to empower township-level healthcare services remotely. Furthermore, Lu et al. [31] enhanced emergency resource allocation efficiency through the development of a multi-agent triage system. Five studies [23,27,28,36,39] focused on human-robot collaboration and patient experience. Zhao et al. [27] investigated the feasibility of integrating a dual-armed nursing robot to reduce nursing staff workload; they also used LLMs to power a humanoid robot for emotionally adaptive care. Song et al. [23] integrated a virtual agent with virtual reality (VR), systematically validating its significant value in enhancing doctor-patient communication and emotional support. Overall, LLMDAs demonstrate strong application effectiveness in nursing practice, with particularly notable impacts in enhancing diagnostic and treatment capabilities, optimizing resource allocation, and improving patient experience.

Discussion

This review analyzed 25 articles summarizing the application of LLMDA in nursing practice. Currently, commonly used LLMs carry risks of bias and hallucination, and the clinical reliability of their generated content requires further enhancement [41]. Several studies have indicated that LLMDAs can effectively overcome clinical limitations of traditional LLMs by modeling role distribution and dynamic synergy among clinical teams [42,43]. This review identifies that LLMDAs operate through collaborative, augmented, and interactive technological architectures, enhancing diagnostic accuracy, optimizing resource allocation, and improving patient experience. Key ethical challenges identified include data privacy, reliability of generated content, and ambiguous responsibility attribution. Nevertheless, LLMDAs hold considerable promise for widespread future application in nursing.

Practical value and scenario adaptation principles of technical architectures

In terms of technological architectures, this study emphasizes target problems and technological methods. We also draw upon the augmented architecture described by Singh et al. [44] and the collaborative and interactive architectures highlighted by Agashe et al. [45], providing a comprehensive representation of the technological frameworks. Zou et al. [46] indicated that within the collaborative LLMDAs system, each agent holds domain-specific functionality and cooperatively achieves complex tasks, thus exemplifying the collaborative architecture. Similarly, Gubanov et al. [47] developed a hybrid cancer diagnosis and care system driven by a knowledge graph integrated with LLM, representing another innovative approach to collaborative systems. Additionally, Sun et al. [48] utilized AI-driven real-time monitoring and large models for personalized interventions, along with modular agent-based chain collaboration targeting elderly populations. It is foreseeable that LLMDAs will facilitate whole-life-cycle patient management, from signal detection to autonomous intervention, through collaborative frameworks. Numerous studies have highlighted concerns regarding the low-quality outputs from original LLMs in healthcare, limiting their direct clinical applicability. Therefore, researchers have explored methods to enhance and validate LLM performance. One prominent technique involves combining information retrieval with generative modeling, dynamically integrating external knowledge bases to improve answer quality [49]. This approach essentially functions as an “open-book” model addressing the knowledge limitations and hallucinations common in traditional models. When combined with agent technology, these techniques significantly improve the clinical adaptability of LLMDAs, referred to here as augmentative architectures. Human-robot interaction aims to optimize user experience and operational efficiency by enabling machines to comprehend human intentions [50] naturally; this constitutes the interactive architecture. The integration of LLMs with agents presents an opportunity for advancement in this area. Future nursing robots could serve as patients’ “best friends.” Maalouf et al. [51], in their scoping review on nursing robots, categorized robots into assistive and socially assistive types and emphasized strengthening human-robot interaction research. Significantly, LLMDAs are not restricted to the three technological frameworks mentioned above, nor do these frameworks exist in isolation. The integration of multiple technological architectures is likely to enhance their practical application in nursing practice further.

We found that the application of LLMDAs in nursing practice primarily involves three scenarios: clinical, home, and community care. The coexistence of enhanced effectiveness and ongoing challenges highlights the complexity arising from the deep integration of technological empowerment with nursing demands. Specifically, clinical scenarios employ augmented architectures of large language models to improve diagnostic accuracy, and multi-agent collaborative mechanisms significantly enhance nursing efficiency. However, technical bottlenecks remain, including delayed updates of knowledge bases and unreliable tool invocation. Home care scenarios reduce nursing workloads through dynamic monitoring and personalized rehabilitation plans, yet emotional interactions often fall short of adequately meeting patients' psychological needs. Community nursing scenarios use causal inference models to promote disease prevention and optimize resource allocation, significantly increasing coverage in remote areas; however, tensions remain between population health management and protecting individual privacy. Collectively, these technological limitations hinder the widespread adoption of LLMDAs in nursing practice.

Ethical and technical challenges

The studies included in this review highlight three core ethical concerns: data privacy, reliability of generated content, and attribution of responsibility. Most studies acknowledge that multi-agent systems and LLMs face potential risks of data breaches and privacy violations in clinical settings. Wu et al. [16] reported that nursing recommendations generated by LLMs often lack proper citations and sufficient supporting evidence, potentially conveying misleading information to patients—a frequent issue in current nursing education applications of LLMs. Furthermore, assigning responsibility for errors remains difficult, and mechanisms for tracing accountability in medical malpractice are not yet established. In addition, technical transparency and humanistic care remain prominent concerns, limiting acceptance by some patients [52]. Together, these technical and ethical challenges pose dual barriers to the broader implementation of LLMDAs.

Ethical issues manifest differently across nursing care contexts. For example, the Positive Dementia Caregiving in 30 Days (PDC30) dementia care chatbot developed by Cheng et al. [22] requires real-time collection of family caregivers’ voice interactions and patients’ daily behavioral data (e.g., eating and medication habits). This information encompasses sensitive patient health data and potentially intrusive details about family caregivers’ emotional states and home environments. In clinical care settings, ethical concerns primarily center around multi-agent data sharing, exemplified by Peng et al.’s [18] medical multi-agent system integrating electronic health records with public health databases. In community care scenarios, challenges lie in balancing population health monitoring with individual privacy. For instance, Braga et al.’s [30] distributed architecture for disease screening in remote communities must avoid unnecessary collection of residents’ personal health information.

Regarding data privacy, the U.S. Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) offers valuable guidelines for patient data protection [53]. By establishing standardized electronic data exchange, data security protocols, and confidentiality standards for medical information, HIPAA ensures the efficient exchange of medical data while safeguarding patients’ personal health information. This directly addresses privacy risks associated with multi-source data integration identified in this study. Research on humanoid care robots by Hernandez et al. [28] demonstrated enhanced operational feasibility in privacy protection by adhering to HIPAA’s hierarchical patient-data access permissions. Furthermore, most current studies fail to clearly define responsibility allocation among algorithm developers, hospital administrators, and caregivers in cases of misdiagnosis by intelligent agents. The WHO’s 2023 publication, Regulatory Considerations for AI in Health [54], provides a layered responsibility framework, with developers accountable for algorithmic flaws and users responsible for adherence to operational standards, that offers a viable solution. Standardization through internationally recognized medical ethics and technical guidelines may accelerate the integration of LLMDAs into nursing practice.

Additionally, this study found that Chinese research primarily emphasizes data localization and national security, adopting collaborative architectures that address healthcare resource allocation through multi-agent division of labor. In contrast, American studies prioritize patient autonomy, employing augmented architectures aimed at reducing the impact of LLM hallucinations on clinical decision-making by leveraging external data enhancements. Research in Africa and South America, limited by available resources, often focuses on adapting low-cost technologies. Given these distinct regional preferences, future researchers should integrate the strengths of each approach according to their specific needs.

Feasible recommendations for the development of LLMDAs in nursing

This study recommends that nurses serve as the central coordinating force in developing adaptive LLMDAs for nursing practice. Nurses should engage in phased training to understand the definitions, characteristics, advantages, and limitations of LLMDAs, enabling rapid proficiency in their operation. Additionally, research-oriented nursing staff within hospitals should actively participate, supported by institutional leadership, in developing domain-specific agents tailored to specialized nursing areas. Such involvement ensures that LLMDA applications closely align with clinical nursing needs. Concurrently, efforts should accelerate the establishment of expert consensus on clinical application guidelines for nursing-specific agents, clarifying frameworks for data governance, ethical review, application workflows, and developing comprehensive evaluation systems covering the entire nursing process [55].

It is noteworthy that current research on LLMDAs primarily focuses on short-term technical validation, lacking longitudinal exploration of their impact on patient outcomes. Jain et al. [56], referencing the review criteria of the Journal of the American College of Cardiology (JACC), emphasized that AI research in cardiovascular nursing that solely prioritizes model performance without demonstrating clinical relevance and clear implementation pathways increasingly fails to meet publication standards in top-tier journals. Future nursing researchers are encouraged to conduct longitudinal studies over 1–2 years, specifically targeting chronic disease populations, to evaluate the actual effects of LLMDAs on prognostic indicators such as readmission rates and quality of life, thereby addressing the temporal limitations present in current research. From an international perspective, cross-cultural adaptation of LLMDAs is essential, particularly for underrepresented regions such as Africa and South America, to align with local linguistic systems and regional nursing needs.

Furthermore, standardized evaluation metrics for assessing LLMDA reliability and safety are crucial. Current AI evaluation metrics in healthcare primarily emphasize accuracy, safety, and clinical applicability [57]. Given the existing fragmentation, establishing standardized evaluation criteria specifically relevant to nursing is imperative. The nine task divisions outlined in the Transparent Reporting of a Multivariable Model for Individual Prognosis or Diagnosis (TRIPOD)-LLM reporting guidelines [58] and the Core Outcome Measures in Effectiveness Trials (COMET) manual [59] can serve as valuable references for developing these standardized assessments.

Limitations

This scoping review has several methodological limitations that should be acknowledged. First, most of the included literature to date is in the technology validation phase. There is a notable lack of included studies featuring implemented applications in real-world clinical settings, which is attributed to the fact that the field is in its early stages. Second, the literature sample exhibits geographical limitations, with included studies primarily originating in China and the United States. Coverage of relevant studies from regions like Africa and South America remains significantly inadequate. Resource-constrained areas such as these commonly face challenges, including limited medical data and underdeveloped hardware infrastructure. The current underdevelopment of regional medical collaboration systems also constrains these regions. Therefore, the conclusions of this study regarding the technical characteristics and application patterns of LLMDAs technology have limitations when applied directly to the resource-constrained areas. Future research should include studies from more geographically diverse regions to improve the generalizability of these findings. In addition, the technical architectures categorized in this study were limited to collaborative, augmented, and interactive types—an approach that may be insufficiently elaborated and did not explore the technical aspects related to problem-solving. Although most studies mentioned ethical challenges, only a small number proposed concrete solutions. These limitations underscore that LLMDAs still offer a broad range of research directions and necessitate further investigation by other researchers.

Conclusion

This paper presents a systematic review of the current status and challenges of LLMDAs’ applications in nursing practice. Through a systematic database search, 25 studies were included, and the results indicate that LLMDAs primarily adopt three types of technical architectures: collaborative, augmentative, and interactive. This technology is applied to clinical diagnosis, home rehabilitation, and community health management scenarios. It significantly enhances nursing efficiency and decision-making accuracy. However, there are core ethical issues such as data privacy breaches, insufficient reliability of generated information, and ambiguous responsibility allocation. Moving forward, it is necessary to integrate technical approaches and establish ethical norms to facilitate its safe implementation in nursing practice.

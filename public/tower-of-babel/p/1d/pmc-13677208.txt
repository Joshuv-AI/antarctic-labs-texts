Four-Year Structural and Functional Outcomes of the First Subretinal Voretigene Neparvovec-rzyl Treatment for RPE65-Associated Inherited Retinal Dystrophy in South Korea
Authors: Sungsoon Hwang, Ki Young Son, Sang Jin Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo describe 4-year functional and structural outcomes of the first bilateral subretinal voretigene neparvovec-rzyl (VN) in a South Korean patient with molecularly confirmed RPE65-associated inherited retinal dystrophy, with a focus on the relationship between chorioretinal atrophy and visual function.MethodsThis was an observational case-based study of a 29-year-old female patient with biallelic RPE65 pathogenic variants. Both eyes underwent 25-gauge pars plana vitrectomy and subretinal VN injection 7 days apart. Standardized assessments were performed at baseline and 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, 3 years, and 4 years. Functional outcomes included best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA; Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study [ETDRS] letters), kinetic visual fields, full-field stimulus threshold (FST) testing with white-light, and multi-luminance mobility test (MLMT). Structural changes were evaluated with spectral-domain optical coherence tomography, ultra-widefield color and autofluorescence imaging, and infrared reflectance.ResultsAt baseline, BCVA was 9 and 8 ETDRS letters (right/left), with widespread ellipsoid zone loss but relatively preserved retinal pigment epithelium. BCVA improved to 34 / 33 letters at 6 months and remained higher than baseline in both eyes at 4 years. Goldmann III4e average visual field radius increased from 2.1° / 0.3° at baseline to 8.8° / 15.0° at 3 months, followed by partial contraction with paracentral scotomas after 1 year, yet fields remained wider than baseline at 4 years. FST improved from 0.10 log10(cd·s/m2) to −1.46 log10(cd·s/m2) at 1 month and remained stable at 3 months. MLMT performance improved from 150 lux at baseline to 10 lux binocularly and was maintained at 4 years. Perifoveal, fovea-involving chorioretinal atrophy developed between 3 and 6 months and enlarged mainly during the first year, then progressed slowly thereafter.ConclusionsIn this South Korean patient, VN provided large and durable functional gains over 4 years despite early, severe chorioretinal atrophy, illustrating structural-functional dissociation and underscoring the need for long-term multimodal monitoring in RPE65-associated disease.Keywords: Genetic therapy, Leber congenital amaurosis, Retinal dystrophies

Inherited retinal dystrophies (IRDs) are a heterogeneous group of progressive retinal degenerations that often lead to severe visual impairment from early life. Among more than 200 disease-causing genes, RPE65 encodes an isomerohydrolase essential for regeneration of 11-cis-retinal in the retinoid cycle [1]. Biallelic pathogenic variants in RPE65 give rise to a spectrum of severe, early-onset rod-predominant dystrophies, including Leber congenital amaurosis (LCA), early-onset severe retinal dystrophy, and, less commonly, retinitis pigmentosa [1,2]. These disorders are typically characterized by nyctalopia from early childhood, nystagmus, markedly reduced or nonrecordable electroretinogram responses, and funduscopic signs of retinal degeneration such as attenuated retinal vessels, bone-spicule–like pigmentation, and localized retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) atrophy, and they often progress to near-complete blindness [1,3].

After proof-of-concept studies demonstrating improved retinal and visual function following subretinal delivery of a recombinant adeno-associated virus vector carrying human RPE65 complementary DNA in patients with RPE65-mediated LCA [4], a randomized, controlled phase 3 trial in 31 patients showed that voretigene neparvovec-rzyl (VN) significantly improved functional vision on the multi-luminance mobility test (MLMT) and increased full-field light sensitivity in this previously untreatable condition [5,6]. Based on these data, VN (Luxturna, Spark Therapeutics Inc.) became the first approved gene augmentation therapy for an inherited retinal disease, receiving US Food and Drug Administration approval in 2017, followed by approval by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety of Korea in 2021.

However, pivotal clinical trials and their extensions have predominantly enrolled White patients from Western countries. Although a previous South Korean case report described favorable short-term outcomes up to 3 months after VN treatment [7], long-term outcomes in Asian populations remain largely unknown. Herein, we report 4-year functional and structural outcomes in the first patient treated with subretinal VN for molecularly confirmed RPE65-mediated IRD, thereby providing long-term data from an East Asian population.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethics statement

This study adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Samsung Medical Center (No. 2025-10-096). Written informed consent was obtained from the patient for treatment and publication of clinical data and images.

Patient and study design

A 29-year-old female patient with biallelic pathogenic variants in RPE65 was included in this observational case-based study. She had a history of early-onset nystagmus, poor visual function from infancy, and progressive night vision deterioration beginning before 6 years of age. She had been clinically diagnosed with LCA, and comprehensive genetic testing identified a homozygous c.1067dup (p.Asn356Lysfs) mutation in the RPE65 gene. Both eyes met the indication criteria for VN treatment, with severely impaired visual function but preserved outer retinal structure. The patient underwent standardized examinations of visual and retinal function at baseline and at 1 month, 3 months, 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, 3 years, and 4 years after gene therapy.

Surgical procedure

The worse-seeing eye (left eye) was treated first, followed by the right eye 7 days later. Systemic corticosteroid prophylaxis was administered according to the product label. The patient received oral prednisone at a dose of 1 mg/ kg/day (up to 40 mg/day) for 3 days before the first injection and continued the same dose for 7 days after the first surgery. After the second-eye injection, prednisone 1 mg/kg/day (up to 40 mg/day) was maintained for 7 days and then tapered over 7 days to 0.5 mg/kg/day (up to 20 mg/day).

All surgeries were performed under general anesthesia using 25-gauge pars plana vitrectomy (CONSTELLATION Vision System, Alcon). After core vitrectomy, posterior vitreous detachment was induced using preservative-free triamcinolone acetonide to visualize the vitreous cortex. Two subretinal injections of VN were then performed along the superior and inferior vascular arcades using a 25/38-gauge subretinal cannula (PolyTip Cannula 25g/38g, MedOne Surgical). A total volume of 0.3 mL of vector solution (1.5 × 1011 vector genomes) was delivered into the subretinal space in each eye. After vector delivery, a fluid- air exchange was performed, and all sclerotomies were sutured with 7-0 vicryl. The intraoperative procedure is presented in Supplementary Video 1.

Functional and structural assessments

Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) was measured using Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) charts and recorded as letter scores. Kinetic visual fields were assessed with dynamic Goldmann perimetry, and the average radius of the III4e isopter for each eye was calculated as the total sum across 24 meridians divided by 24. Subjective visual function and vision-related quality of life were evaluated using the National Eye Institute Visual Function Questionnaire (NEI VFQ-25).

Full-field stimulus threshold (FST) testing with white light was performed to assess global light sensitivity in both eyes. Functional vision in real-world–like conditions was quantified using the MLMT at standardized luminance levels; the lowest light level at which the patient could successfully complete the course was recorded at each visit. While the MLMT was conducted following the published protocol [8], the testing environment was not formally standardized or validated to be identical to that of the pivotal phase 3 trial, and thus MLMT results in this study may not be directly comparable with those reported in clinical trials.

Structural retinal changes were evaluated using spectral-domain optical coherence tomography (OCT) (Spectralis HRA-OCT, Heidelberg Engineering), ultra-widefield scanning laser ophthalmoscopy (Optos, Optos Inc.), and color fundus photography. Infrared reflectance and autofluorescence images were analyzed for the development and progression of chorioretinal atrophy (CRA). All examinations were performed in both eyes at each visit whenever possible.

Results

Baseline findings

At baseline, BCVA was severely reduced, with ETDRS letter scores of nine in the right eye and eight in the left eye. Fundus photographs showed generalized RPE mottling with salt-and-pepper–like changes and diffuse pigmentary abnormalities, but no definite bone-spicule pigmentation. Autofluorescence imaging demonstrated extinguished fundus autofluorescence in both eyes. Full-field electroretinography revealed extinguished rod and cone responses. Spectral-domain OCT showed widespread loss of the ellipsoid zone across the posterior pole, whereas the RPE band appeared relatively preserved and the outer nuclear layer showed only mild to moderate thinning without marked foveal atrophy (Fig. 1A–1E).

Structural changes over 4 years

During the 4-year follow-up, CRA developed despite initially preserved outer retinal structure. According to the Bommakanti classification [9], the CRA in this case was categorized as a mixed pattern with perifoveal and nummular components. At 1 month after surgery, OCT appearances of the photoreceptor and RPE layers were not markedly different from baseline. By 3 months, subtle perifoveal CRA became detectable as small hyporeflective areas on infrared imaging. At 6 months, these atrophic changes became more evident on infrared images and corresponded to focal RPE atrophy and increased choroidal transmission on OCT. Perifoveal CRA continued to enlarge progressively during the first postoperative year, predominantly around but not limited to the subretinal bleb areas. Thereafter, from year 1 to year 4, the rate of atrophic expansion appeared slower. Color fundus photography at 1 year and 4 years showed only modest further enlargement of the atrophic zones, suggesting relative structural stabilization after the initial rapid phase (Fig. 2A, 2B). The measured area of perifoveal atrophy at 6 months, 1 year, 2 years, and 4 years was 5.74, 7.08, 10.58, and 12.74 mm2 in the right eye and 4.67, 6.87, 10.94, and 11.52 mm2 in the left eye, respectively. The mean enlargement rate across both eyes was 6.98 mm2/yr during the first year, 3.79 mm2/yr between 1 and 2 years, and 0.69 mm2/yr between 2 and 4 years (Fig. 3A). Bleb-relative mapping showed that the atrophy involved both areas within and outside the original bleb boundary (Fig. 4A, 4B).

Visual acuity and visual field

In contrast to the early structural deterioration, BCVA improved substantially after gene therapy. At 1 month after treatment in the second eye, BCVA improved to 23 letters in the right eye and 24 letters in the left eye. At 6 months, BCVA further improved to 34 letters in the right eye and 33 letters in the left eye. Over the 4-year follow-up, BCVA in the right eye remained relatively stable, with ETDRS letter scores consistently higher than baseline. The left eye showed some decline after the peak at 6 months, in parallel with more pronounced structural atrophy, but visual acuity remained better than pretreatment throughout the observation period (Fig. 3B).

Goldmann perimetry at baseline showed markedly constricted kinetic visual fields in both eyes. The average visual field radius of the III4e isopter was 2.1° in the right eye and 0.3° in the left eye. After treatment, kinetic visual fields expanded substantially. At 1 month, the III4e average visual field radius increased to 3.8° in the right eye and 4.6° in the left eye. At 3 months, further expansion was observed, reaching 8.8° in the right eye and 15.0° in the left eye. From 12 months onward, the III4e isopter began to contract slightly, and new paracentral scotomas emerged in areas corresponding to developing CRA. Consequently, part of the initial visual field gain was attenuated over time, although visual fields remained wider than baseline in both eyes at the last follow-up (Fig. 3C).

Other outcomes

At baseline, the FST threshold for white light in both eyes was about 0.10 log10 (cd·s/m2), indicating profoundly reduced light sensitivity. At 1 month after treatment, the white-light FST threshold improved dramatically to −1.46 log10 (cd·s/m2) and remained stable at 3 months (−1.43 log10 (cd·s/m2)), with no meaningful loss of sensitivity during early follow-up.

MLMT performance also improved rapidly. At baseline, the patient could pass the MLMT only at 150 lux with both eyes. At 1 month, she was able to successfully complete the MLMT at 10 lux binocularly. At 4 years, the patient continued to pass the MLMT at 10 lux with both eyes, indicating durable improvement in functional navigation under dim lighting conditions (Supplementary Video 2).

The NEI VFQ-25 composite score improved by 24.2% within the first 3 months, increasing from 37.42 at baseline to 46.47 at 3 months, reflecting a subjectively meaningful gain in vision-related quality of life.

Throughout the 4-year follow-up, both eyes remained phakic and developed only mild, symmetric nuclear sclerosis without visually significant cataract progression. No clinically meaningful postoperative intraocular inflammation was observed; from 1 week after surgery onward, the anterior chamber and vitreous remained quiet in both eyes. Discussion

Functional effects of voretigene neparvovec

This observational case-based study demonstrates that bilateral subretinal VN that let to marked improvement in multiple functional domains (BCVA, visual fields, and MLMT performance) in South Korean RPE65-associated retinal dystrophy. In the pivotal phase 3 trial, VN produced substantial gains in functional vision, with significant improvement in MLMT and full-field light sensitivity, accompanied by expansion of kinetic visual fields and only modest average BCVA gains [5,6]. Long-term follow-up of the phase 3 cohort showed that improvements in MLMT and FST were largely maintained at 3 to 4 years, whereas BCVA tended to improve by approximately one line at year 1 and then gradually regress toward baseline in a subset of patients [10,11].

Compared with these aggregate data, our patient exhibited a larger improvement in BCVA and visual fields during the first year, together with a robust and durable gain in MLMT that remained stable over 4 years. The time course of improvement is consistent with clinical trial and guideline data indicating that VN-related benefits emerge rapidly and can be demonstrable up to at least 4 to 5 years in some patients. Thus, from a functional perspective, this case aligns with and, in terms of central acuity, somewhat exceeds the magnitude of benefit reported in larger VN cohorts.

CRA and possible mechanisms

In contrast to the favorable functional trajectory, our patient developed early, rapidly progressive, and ultimately severe fovea-involving CRA between 3 and 6 months after VN. CRA has emerged as an important structural complication in post-approval cohorts [9,12–14]. Gange et al. [13] described 18 eyes with progressive perifoveal CRA after VN, with atrophy first detectable at a mean of 4.7 months postoperatively and enlarging over the first year. Despite this, BCVA remained on average stable, FST improved by about 3 log units, and all eyes showed visual field expansion, although approximately one quarter developed paracentral scotomas related to atrophy [13]. The PERCEIVE registry likewise demonstrated durable functional gains after VN, with CRA reported in 13 of 103 patients (12.6%), representing a common real-world ocular adverse event [12]. Bommakanti et al. [9] further classified VN-associated CRA into touchdown, nummular, and perifoveal patterns and estimated that perifoveal atrophy involving the near fovea occurs in a minority of treated eyes (on the order of 10%–15%), with progressive area enlargement over time.

Proposed mechanisms for CRA include mechanical effects from the cannula or bleb formation, shear stress from subretinal fluid propagation, vector- or promoter-related toxicity, target overexpression in metabolically compromised cells, subclinical inflammation, and possible acceleration of the underlying degenerative process in regions already structurally vulnerable [15–17]. In our case, the extent and density of CRA, with direct foveal involvement and marked choroidal hypertransmission on OCT, appear more severe than the largely fovea-sparing perifoveal patterns described in early series. This places our patient toward the severe end of the reported spectrum. Notably, both Gange et al. [13] and Bommakanti et al. [9] emphasized that functional benefits generally persisted in eyes with CRA, and treatment was continued at participating centers because of the poor natural history of RPE65-associated disease.

Recent studies by Stingl et al. [18,19] suggested that greater improvement in full-field scotopic thresholds after VN may correlate with the presence of CRA, raising the possibility that more pronounced biological responses to gene augmentation, or higher effective vector exposure, could be linked to atrophy in susceptible retina. Our case, characterized by both a strong functional response and severe CRA, is consistent with this emerging observation, although causality cannot be inferred from a single patient.

Structural-functional dissociation

A striking feature of this case is the temporal and quantitative dissociation between structural deterioration and functional performance. Despite the development of extensive fovea-involving CRA by 3 to 6 months, BCVA and visual fields continued to show gradual improvement up to 1 year, and even after subsequent partial decline over 1 to 4 years, both remained better than pretreatment levels. MLMT performance, once improved, remained stable throughout the 4-year follow-up with no detectable worsening.

This pattern mirrors earlier gene therapy studies in RPE65 disease, which showed enduring improvements in visual function despite continued photoreceptor loss at rates similar to untreated eyes [20,21]. In the VN phase 3 extension, MLMT and FST gains were durable over 3 to 4 years, whereas central visual function showed a modest decline from its peak, potentially related to progressive central retinal atrophy [10]. Together with these reports, our findings support a model in which gene augmentation restores phototransduction in surviving photoreceptors and improves global light sensitivity and navigational vision, while the underlying degenerative process continues—occasionally accelerated or topographically altered by factors associated with subretinal delivery or vector expression [22].

The sustained MLMT performance in our patient, despite a foveal scotoma and reduced central acuity, underscores that MLMT primarily reflects mobility under low luminance, which may rely more on extrafoveal rod-mediated function and global visual field sensitivity than on foveal structure alone [8]. This observation is clinically relevant: even when OCT shows severe CRA and BCVA declines, patients may retain substantial functional gains in real-world mobility and orientation compared with their pretreatment status.

Clinical implications

Clinically, this case reinforces several points for patient counseling and long-term management. First, VN can provide meaningful and durable improvements in functional vision even in advanced disease, but these benefits do not preclude progressive structural degeneration, including CRA that may eventually affect central visual acuity. Second, the occurrence of severe CRA in a patient with excellent early functional response highlights the need to counsel that (1) post-VN atrophy is a recognized risk; (2) atrophy may appear within the first year and can progress; and (3) functional gains in mobility and light sensitivity may still persist despite structural loss and subsequent partial decline in BCVA.

Limitations

This report has several limitations. First, it describes a single patient and thus cannot establish incidence, risk factors, or causality. Second, we did not perform standardized FST at all follow-up visits. This limits not only direct comparison with published series in which FST was a major functional endpoint, but also our ability to evaluate longitudinal changes in global retinal sensitivity in relation to progressive CRA. Thus, although the early post-treatment FST improvement supports an initial functional response, the long-term interpretation of structure-function dissociation in this case relies mainly on BCVA, kinetic visual fields, and MLMT. Third, NEI VFQ-25 was available only at baseline and during the early postoperative period, and no systematic long-term patient-reported outcome assessment was performed. Therefore, the durability of subjective quality of life benefit could not be evaluated in parallel with the objective functional measures over 4 years. Fourth, although the MLMT was performed with reference to the published protocol, it was not conducted in a fully standardized, trial-equivalent setting. Therefore, the MLMT findings in this case should be interpreted as supportive real-world functional data rather than as directly comparable to MLMT outcomes reported in prior clinical trials. Finally, detailed microperimetric mapping of the scotoma was not available, which constrains precise structure-function correlation in the macular region. The structural- functional dissociation described in this case is based on global functional measures and should not be assumed to apply uniformly at the local macular level.

Conclusions

In this single-patient observational report, VN was associated with sustained improvement in BCVA, visual fields, and MLMT performance over 4 years, even when early and severe fovea-involving CRA develops after treatment. These findings are descriptive and should be interpreted cautiously, but they suggest that functional benefit may persist despite progressive structural change. Systematic, long-term, multimodal imaging and functional testing in larger cohorts will be essential to define the risk-benefit profile of VN in patients at risk for CRA and to optimize both surgical technique and patient selection.

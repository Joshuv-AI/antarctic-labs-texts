Predicting invasiveness of ground-glass nodules in lung adenocarcinoma: based on preoperative 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose PET/computed tomography and high-resolution computed tomography
Authors: Ximei Wang, Chunyan Yang, Xuewei Wang, Dalong Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study was conducted to explore the differential diagnostic value of PET/computed tomography (PET/CT) combined with high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) in predicting the invasiveness of ground-glass nodules (GGNs).Materials and methodsThis retrospective analysis included 67 patients (mean age 62.5 ± 8.4, including 45 females and 22 males) with GGNs who underwent preoperative 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) PET/CT and HRCT examinations between January 2018 and October 2022. Based on the postoperative pathological results of lung adenocarcinoma, the patients were classified into two groups: invasive adenocarcinoma (IAC) and non-IAC. Besides, the clinical and imaging information of these patients was collected. HRCT signs include the existence of air bronchial signals, vascular convergence, pleural indentation, lobulation, and spiculation. Moreover, the diameter of solid components (DSolid), diameter of ground-glass nodules (DGGN), and computed tomography values of ground-glass nodules (CTGGN) were measured concurrently. Furthermore, the mean standardized uptake value, maximal standardized uptake value (SUVmax), metabolic tumor volume, and total lesion glycolysis were assessed during PET/CT. Associations between invasiveness and these factors were evaluated using univariate and multivariate analyses.ResultsThe results of logistic regression analysis demonstrated that DGGN, DSolid, consolidation tumor ratio (CTR), CTGGN, and SUVmax were independent predictors in the IAC group. The combined diagnosis based on these five predictors revealed that area under the curve was 0.825.ConclusionThe DGGN, DSolid, CTR, CTGGN, and SUVmax in GGNs were independent predictors of IAC, and combining 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameters with HRCT may improve the predictive value of pathological classification in lung adenocarcinoma.

Introduction

In recent years, lung adenocarcinoma has ranked high among nonsmall cell lung cancer subtypes [1]. Lung adenocarcinoma is divided into several subtypes, including preinvasive lesions (PL), minimally invasive adenocarcinoma (MIA), invasive adenocarcinoma (IAC), and invasive adenocarcinoma variant [2], according to the revised classification in 2011. More specifically, PL can be divided into adenocarcinoma in situ (AIS) and atypical adenomatous hyperplasia (AAH), and IAC can be divided into five subtypes, including lepidic predominant adenocarcinoma (LPA), acinar predominant adenocarcinoma (APA), papillary predominant adenocarcinoma (PPA), solid predominant adenocarcinoma (SPA), and micropapillary predominant adenocarcinoma (MPA). A new classification standard was formulated in 2015 [3]. Based on the pathological types of lung adenocarcinoma, it can be classified into high, mediate, and low differentiation degrees. Among them, the high differentiation degree includes AIS, MIA, and LPA; the medium differentiation degree consists of APA and PPA; the low differentiation degree comprises SPA and MPA. The classification standards of lung adenocarcinoma proposed in 2011 and 2015 are also used at present. Lung adenocarcinoma has different prognoses based on different imaging manifestations and variant pathological subtypes. Different imaging manifestations can reflect the variant pathological types of lung adenocarcinoma and relevant heterogeneity. As per the high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) results, lung adenocarcinoma can be classified into solid-density nodules and ground-glass nodules (GGNs), which are divided into pure ground-glass nodules (pGGNs) and mixed ground-glass nodules (mGGNs), depending on the proportion of solid components. GGNs included pGGNs and mGGNs. PGGNs refer to the lesions that appear as ground-glass changes, and the normal alveolar structure, trachea, and blood vessels in the lesions can be observed. Solid nodules are defined as lesions with an increased density that covers structures such as blood vessels and bronchial tubes. mGGNs refer to partially solid-density components in ground-glass changes. Some researchers have found that the pathologic type of GGNs may be attributed to one of AIS, MIA, and IAC, but solid-density nodules are generally attributed to IAC [4]. The extensive application of low-dose computed tomography low-dose computed tomography screening contributes to an increasing incidence of GGNs. These GGNs constitute a major clinical problem for surgeons and oncologists due to their heterogeneity and potential malignancy [5]. Lung adenocarcinoma is usually treated by surgical methods, and other optional therapies include radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and targeted therapy. In GGNs, IAC is usually resected by lobectomy, while noninvasive lung adenocarcinoma (AAH, AIS, and MIA) is usually removed by sublobectomy (including wedge resection and segmentectomy) or regular follow-up. Consequently, the selection of therapies for GGNs is closely related to the early diagnosis of this disease [6]. Moreover, the prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma varies according to different pathological types [7], with the 5-year survival rate of non-IAC being about 100% but that of IAC being less than 90%. Patients diagnosed with poorly differentiated lung adenocarcinoma have the lowest survival rate. Because of the advantages of FDG metabolism, the use of 18F-FDG PET/CT in tumors has proved invaluable in the diagnosis and assessment of lung adenocarcinoma. There are, however, only a few reports on the combined application of 18F-FDG PET/CT and HRCT to differentiate GGNs from non-IAC or IAC, and consistent results have not been obtained [8–10].

In this study, the HRCT signs and 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic characteristics of the GGNs were mainly analyzed to investigate the value of 18F-FDG PET/CT combined with HRCT in the differential diagnosis of GGNs invasiveness between non-IAC and IAC groups.

Materials and methods

Patient characteristics

The retrospective study (no. XXXXXX20160008) has been approved by our Human Ethics Review Committee, and the requirement for informed consent has been waived. This study included patients who were diagnosed with GGNs during a PET/CT examination between January 2018 and October 2022 and underwent surgery 1 month later. The exclusion criteria include (1) patients with other pathological types of lung cancer, including squamous, small-cell, and adenosquamous lung cancer; (2) patients with the variant subtype of invasive adenocarcinoma; (3) patients with multisource lung cancer in the lungs; (4) patients who had a history of other malignancies; (5) patients who had received anticancer therapies before PET/CT or before surgery; (6) patients with a nodule less than 8 mm in diameter; (7) patients with solid-density nodules. The eventual study includes a number of 67 patients in total (Fig. 1).

Flowchart of patient inclusion and exclusion into the study.

18F-fluorodeoxyglucose PET/computed tomography and high-resolution computed tomography

First, to ensure that their fasting blood glucose levels were below 10 mmol/l, all patients were fasted for more than 6 h before the examination. After the weight of patients was measured, 18F-FDG was injected through an intravenous indwelling needle with an injection dose of 3.7 MBq/kg based on the calculation results. Subsequently, the patient was transferred to have a rest in a quiet environment for 60 min. When a chest scan was performed during PET/CT scanning, the scanning of image acquisition was performed from the base of the skull to the upper abdomen; when the whole body was scanned, the scanning of image acquisition was taken from the base of the skull to the upper part of the femur. During CT examination, the scanning parameters include tube current of 35 mA, tube voltage of 120 Kv, rotation time of 1.5–1.8 s, selection range is the same as PET scanning range, scanning thickness of 1.0 mm, slice collimation of 0.6 mm or 1.2 mm, layer spacing of 1.0 mm, and machine bed speed is automatically generated. The patient lay in a supine position during the CT scan, followed by the PET scan, and the images of both examinations were postprocessed and analyzed.

After all patients underwent HRCT before PET/CT, they were instructed to breathe calmly and hold their breath during HRCT. The parameters for HRCT scanning included: reconstructed layer thickness: 1 mm, tube current: 110 mA, tube voltage: 120 Kv, lung window position: −700 HU, and window width: 1200 HU.

Image analysis

After the fusion of CT and PET images, the volume of interest was delineated. The SUVmax was measured by the tools included in the software. The metabolic tumor volume (MTV) was measured according to the coronal, axial, and sagittal observations of the delineated target area, with 40% of the SUVmax as the threshold. The MTV was manually delineated and adjusted when the delineation was not good. Finally, the total lesion glycolysis (TLG) was calculated according to MTV and mean standardized uptake value (SUVmean). The CT value of the nodules and the size of the nodules were directly measured by using a tool on HRCT images. Meanwhile, CT signs such as tumor shape, border, spiculation, pleural indentation, lobulation sign, air bronchial sign, and vascular convergence were observed. All images were measured and diagnosed by three senior nuclear medicine physicians. In case of disagreement, the diagnosis should be made after consultation.

In this study, DGGN (diameter of ground-glass nodules) is the maximum diameter of the nodule in the HRCT image of the pulmonary window; Dsolid (diameter of solid components) is the maximum diameter of the solid portion of the mGGN in the HRCT image; CTR (consolidation tumor ratio) refers to the ratio between the diameter of a specific solid nodule and the overall diameter encompassing mGGN. The computed tomography values of ground-glass nodules (CTGGN) can be employed to directly measure the attenuation of ground-glass components with an elliptical region of interest.

Statistical analysis

The clinical information, HRCT, and PET/CT manifestations of patients were compared between both groups. Mean ± SD was used to express the normally distributed quantitative variables. The independent samples t-test was used to indicate group differences. Numbers or percentages (%) were used to express the qualitative data. The chi-square test was used to compare differences between groups. The univariate analysis was performed to analyze the correlation between the two types of variables; the multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed on statistically significant indicators (P < 0.1). The odds ratio was computed. Pearson’s test was used to analyze correlations between continuous variables. The diagnostic value of significant variables was compared using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and the corresponding area under the curve (AUC) in a multivariate logistic regression analysis. The sensitivity and specificity were computed. The Youden Index was used to establish the optimal cutoff value. SPSS25.0 (IBM Corp; Armonk, New York, USA) was used to perform all statistical analyses, with a statistically significant P < 0.05.

There was no significant difference in age, gender, smoking history, location of the primary lesion, and CEA between the non-IAC and IAC groups (P > 0.05). Differences in PET/computed tomography and high-resolution computed tomography parameters among different pathological subtypes of lung adenocarcinoma

The two subtypes of MPA (two instances) and SPA (one case) were included in this study of ground-glass nodular lung adenocarcinoma, but due to the small number of patients, they were excluded from the analysis of pathological subtypes. The number of patients with CTR (≥0.5) in the IAC group (32 cases) was significantly higher than that in the non-IAC group (one case) in the correlation analysis of PET/CT (SUVmax) and HRCT (DGGN, DSolid, CTR, and CTGGN) parameters. Hence, no additional pathological subtype analysis was performed in the analysis of CTR. When the differences were analyzed between MIA and AIS in the non-IAC group, it was confirmed that CTGGN and SUVmax were not statistically significant in subtypes, while DGGN and DSolid were (P = 0.010 and 0.005, respectively). According to the correlation comparison results, there was no statistically significant correlation between the three groups of pathological subtypes of LPA, APA, and PPA in the IAC group. Discussion

Of all the types of cancer diagnosed in China, lung cancer has the highest incidence and the highest mortality rate [11]. Lung adenocarcinoma is highly heterogeneous. New standards for classifying lung adenocarcinoma have been proposed in 2011 and 2015. Lung adenocarcinoma can be divided into high, mediate, and low differentiation according to the revised classification standards. The different degrees of differentiation are closely associated with the prognosis of patients. Owing to its benefits in metabolic functional imaging over conventional imaging techniques, 18F-FDG PET/CT plays a prominent role in the diagnosis, staging, effectiveness, and prognosis of lung cancer [12–15]. Consequently, this study was conducted to predict the invasiveness of lung cancer based on HRCT indicators and PET/CT metabolic characteristics, thus providing more precise data for clinical decision-making.

As revealed in conventional imaging studies, especially HRCT studies, DGGN, CTR, and CTGGN are of great significance for predicting IAC [4,16–18]. In this study, it was found that pGGNs were more common in the non-IAC group (11/14), while mGGNs were more prevalent in the IAC group (34/53). This was consistent with the findings of Zhou et al. [19]. Cancer cells spread along the alveolar wall of lesions in the non-IAC group, manifested as pGGNs, and pGGNs may progress into mGGNs with an increase in the malignancy degree and the dimension and density of nodules may also increase. Previous studies have shown that the diameter of the nodules in the IAC group was larger than that in the non-IAC group [20,21]. In the study, we showed that DGGN was an independent influencing factor in predicting the IAC group (P = 0.033) and found that the mean diameter of lesions in the non-IAC group (14.9 ± 2.0 mm) was smaller than that in the IAC group (19.9 ± 7.3 mm). When the diameter of the lesion was larger than 11.4 mm, the sensitivity for predicting the lesions in the IAC group was 96.2% and the specificity was 42.9%. In terms of the correlation between the increase in CTR and DSolid and the probability of the tumor becoming invasive, previous studies demonstrated that the diameter of DSolid and CTR can be employed to predict the prognosis and degree of malignancy of lung adenocarcinoma [18,22]. In predicting the IAC group, DSolid and CTR (≥0.5) were validated to be independent influencing factors. When the optimal cutoff value of DSolid was 6.9 mm, the sensitivity and specificity for diagnosing lung adenocarcinoma in the IAC group were 50.9% and 92.9%, respectively. The AUC was 0.747, which was comparable to the finding of Yanagawa et al. [18], namely that nodules with a diameter larger than 8 mm had a higher possibility of developing into IAC. The results of this study also showed that a CTR value ≥0.5 was more likely to appear in the IAC group (32/53). Hu et al. [4] conducted an exploration based on 345 patients with GGNs and demonstrated that a CT value larger than −482.5 HU indicated a higher possibility of developing into IAC. We found that patients with a CTGGN value larger than −470.0 HU were more inclined to suffer from IAC, and this conclusion was nearly consistent with the previous study.

The 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic parameter (SUVmax) is also important in distinguishing benign from malignant tumors and reflects the glucose metabolic activity of tumors. In the previous, some scholars believed that FDG PET had limited value in diagnosing GGNs. In clinical practice, however, because patients with GGNs are willing to receive evaluations on their general condition before surgery, PET/CT performed for GGNs, especially for mGGNs, are common in our department. In addition, due to the improvement of the resolution of PET/CT equipment, we found that there were some variables in FDG uptake in GGNs. Therefore, the correlation between FDG metabolism parameters and pathological classification was analyzed in this study. The logistic regression analysis results based on metabolic parameters of SUVmax, SUVmean, MTV, and TLG revealed that only SUVmax was an independent factor in predicting IAC. When the SUVmax was 1.265, the AUC was 0.777, the Youden Index was 0.439, the sensitivity was 86.8%, and the specificity was 57.1%, respectively, which contributed to the diagnosis of IAC. This result was also in line with the finding of Niu et al. [23], who discovered that GGNs with a diameter less than 3 cm had a higher likelihood of suffering from IAC when the SUVmax was larger than 1.3. This study demonstrated that the SUVmax could be used as a conventional semiquantitative index of FDG metabolism in response to tumors in identifying the invasiveness of GGNs. Therefore, the degree of FDG uptake was a crucial adjunct to the conventional imaging HRCT in assessing the invasiveness of GGNs, particularly in identifying the invasiveness of partially solid GGNs. As suggested in a previous study, TLG and MTV can reflect the tumor burden more effectively than SUVmax [24]. There was, however, no statistical significance in the univariate and multivariate analysis results in this study. This may be explained that the software delineated the MTV of the GGN with the threshold (40% SUVmax). Further, it may be difficult and inaccurate to perform manual MTV delineation when the uptake of GGN is low. Moreover, we analyzed the differences between pathological subtypes of GGN in the non-IAC group. The results demonstrated that DGGN and DSolid were statistically different between the MIA and AIS groups (P < 0.05), which was consistent with the results of previous studies on AIS and invasive adenocarcinoma (including MIA and IAC) [25], namely that DSolid > 0.8 cm was an independent predictor of invasive lung adenocarcinoma. In our study, CTGGN and SUVmax had no statistical significance in the pathological subtype analysis in the non-IAC group; DGGN, DSolid, CTR, CTGGN, and SUVmax were found to have no statistical significance in the pathological subtype (LPA, PPA, and APA, respectively) analysis in the IAC group.

The multivariate analysis results indicated that DGGN, DSolid, CTR, CTGGN, and SUVmax were independent predictors of IAC. The sensitivity of combined factors (DGGN, DSolid, CTR, CTGGN, and SUVmax) for diagnosing IAC was 73.6%, the specificity was 78.6%, and the AUC was 0.825, suggesting that PET/ CT combined with HRCT can effectively improve the efficiency in diagnosing IAC.

In summary, there were some correlations between the characteristics of 18F-FDG PET/CT combined HRCT and the pathological types of lung adenocarcinoma. PET/CT contributed to a higher efficiency in the diagnosis of IAC in combination with HRCT. Compared with conventional HRCT examinations, PET/CT combined with HRCT was more effective in diagnosing and predicting IAC. Moreover, PET/CT metabolic parameters can provide additional valuable diagnostic information.

Nevertheless, there are some shortcomings in this study. First, bias may occur in patient selection due to the property of this retrospective single-center study. Therefore, prospective and multicenter verification may be required. Second, the small sample size and absence of multiple comparison corrections in this study may affect the manner of predicting the pathological types of lung adenocarcinoma. Because of this, a bigger sample size is required to further validate the study’s results.

Conclusion

PET/CT metabolic parameters (SUVmax > 1.265) combined with HRCT parameters (DGGN > 11.4 mm, DSolid > 6.9 mm, CTR ≥ 0.5, and CTGGN > −470.0 HU) can be employed to improve the diagnostic performance in predicting IAC.

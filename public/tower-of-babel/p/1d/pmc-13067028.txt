Co-assembled structures of Fe3O4 nanoparticles for therapy of cervical cancer by inducing apoptosis and autophagy
Authors: Sumiao Dong, Waner Liu, Dou Yu, Xian Zhang, Dailing Chen, Ting Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCervical cancer is one of the most common cancers among women globally, and its treatments remain limited, urgently needing new strategies. In recent years, the use of enzyme-like Fe3O4 nanoparticles (NPs) has exhibited promising antitumor potential due to their unique physicochemical properties and biocompatibility. However, the therapeutic effect of Fe3O4 NPs is often limited by their surface hydrophobicity. Therefore, this study aimed to construct Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies to overcome surface hydrophobicity and enhance antitumor efficacy.MethodsOur study designed Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies through a unique oil-in-water (O/W) emulsion restriction strategy. Using HeLa cells as the experimental model, the effect of Fe3O4-CC3 on cell proliferation was evaluated by Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay. Morphological changes were observed under an optical microscope. The impact of Fe3O4-CC3 on cell migration was assessed using the wound-healing assay, and the apoptosis rate was analyzed by flow cytometry. The protein levels of apoptosis protein (Caspase-3), autophagy protein (Beclin1, p62, and LC3-B), and PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway were determined by Western blot. The expression level of LC3-B in cells was observed by confocal fluorescence microscopy. Anti-tumour activity of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies was accessed by subcutaneous xeno-transplanted tumour model of human cervical cancer in nude mice.ResultsCompared to traditional NPs, these co-assemblies are inherently hydrophobic, but they transition to a hydrophilic characteristic through the incorporation of the cationic surfactant (DTAB) via hydrophobic interactions. Our results demonstrated that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies were superior to Fe3O4 NPs in inhibiting cell proliferation and migration. Flow cytometry and Western blot analyses indicated that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies induced apoptosis by activating apoptosis-related proteins and inhibiting the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway, exhibiting multiple anti-tumor mechanisms.ConclusionsFe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies induced apoptosis and promoted autophagy, accompanied by the inactivation of PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway in cervical cancer and further inhibited tumour growth, which may lead to new therapeutic choices for patients and promote the development of nanomedicine in cancer therapy.Keywords: Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies, self-assembly, cervical cancer, apoptosis, autophagy

Key findings

• Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies inhibit cervical cancer cells by inducing apoptosis and autophagy, accompanied by modulation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway.

What is known and what is new?

• Fe3O4 nanoparticles have anti-tumor potential but limited by hydrophobicity.

• Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies enhance hydrophilicity and activity. They uniquely inhibit cervical cancer cells by inducing apoptosis and autophagy.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies are a promising multi-mechanism nanotherapy for cervical cancer.

• Further studies should focus on pharmacokinetics, long-term safety, and combination therapies to advance clinical translation.

Introduction

Cervical cancer ranks fourth among gynecological malignancies and is a leading cause of female mortality worldwide (1,2). Although widespread cytological screening and human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccination are expected to reduce its incidence and mortality (3,4), cervical cancer remains a severe public health burden. Notably, even in developed countries, approximately 45% of patients still die from the disease, while developing countries account for 90% of global cervical cancer deaths (5). While surgical resection, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy have advanced considerably in treating early-stage cancer, the problems of treatment-related multidrug resistance, recurrence risk, and impact on normal cell function still need to be addressed (6,7). Therefore, the development of novel targeted therapeutic strategies has become an urgent clinical need.

In recent years, nanozymes—nanomaterials with enzyme-like activity—have emerged as a highly promising therapeutic approach in cancer treatment due to their unique catalytic properties, high stability, and cost-effectiveness (8-13). Among various nanoparticles (NPs), Fe3O4 NPs, approved by U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for clinical use, are considered as safe are considered as safe and chemically stable nanomedicine (14,15). In tumor therapy, Fe3O4 nanozymes can catalytically decompose hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) into highly cytotoxic reactive oxygen species (ROS), thereby inducing tumor cell death (16-18). However, its therapeutic efficacy is often constrained by inherent limitations, as pristine Fe3O4 NPs primarily exhibit optimal nanoenzyme activity under hydrophilic conditions (19). More importantly, despite their demonstrated potential in various cancers, the potential applications and mechanisms of Fe3O4 NPs in cervical cancer treatment remain unexplored (20,21).

To address these limitations, we previously designed an oil-in-water (O/W) emulsion-confined co-assembly strategy to integrate oleic-acid-capped Fe3O4 nanocrystals with CC3-type porous organic cages (POCs), yielding Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies in which ferrite nanocrystals are organized on octahedral CC3 colloidal crystals (19). Most importantly, interfacial restructuring during ion-surfactant-mediated co-assembly facilitates the dispersion of these hybrid co-assemblies in water, forming bilayer-like nanobiological interfaces. In related Fe3O4/POC co-assembly systems, studies have demonstrated that surfactant-coated interfaces can activate and regulate enzyme-mimetic behavior in Fe3O4 nanocrystals, despite the presence of a hydrophobic ligand shell on their surfaces (22).

We hypothesize that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies, owing to their enhanced water solubility and engineered nano-bio interface properties, can improve cell binding capacity compared to standalone Fe3O4 nanocrystals, accompanied by alterations in survival/death-related signaling pathways. Therefore, this study evaluated the antitumor activity of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies in a cervical cancer model, concurrently detecting apoptosis and autophagy-related responses, as well as molecular alterations associated with the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway. We present this article in accordance with the MDAR and ARRIVE reporting checklists (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-1-2741/rc).

Methods

Materials

The synthesis and physicochemical characterization of Fe3O4 NPs and CC3-type POCs were reported in our previous work (19).

Preparation of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies

The Fe3O4 nanocrystals were first dried and re-dispersed in chloroform at a concentration of 10 mg/mL. Subsequently, a solution containing 150 µL of Fe3O4 nanocrystals (10 mg/mL), 500 µL of CC3 chloroform solution, and 2 mL of DTAB (20 mg/mL) aqueous solution was prepared in a 20-mL glass vial. The mixture was then emulsified using a vortex. Following this, 2 mL of deionized water was added for dilution, and the mixture was heated to 60 ℃ with stirring to remove the chloroform phase. After 40 minutes, the mixture was allowed to cool to room temperature. The resulting colloidal assemblies were washed with deionized water and dispersed in deionized water for further applications.

Cell culture

The HeLa cell line of human origin was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA). Cell lines were cultured in MEM (including NEAA) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Sigma-Aldrich) in a sterile incubator at 37 ℃ with 5% CO2 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA) and routinely passaged using 0.25% trypsin-EDTA. HeLa cells were used as a cervical cancer model in this study.

Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

Cell viability was assessed using the CCK-8 (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Cells were seeded at a density of 5×103 cells per well in 100 µL of the medium in 96-well plates (Corning, NY, USA). Subsequently, cells were treated with varying concentrations of Fe3O4 NPs or Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies (0, 2.5, 5, 7.5 and 10 µg/mL). The concentration range (0, 2.5, 5, 7.5, 10 µg/mL) was based on our prior study on Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies in ovarian cancer (23), ensuring efficacy and safety. After 24 h of treatment, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well and incubated for 1 hour. All experiments were conducted in triplicate. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) and % cell viability was determined as the following formula.

Wound healing assay

Cells were seeded into six-well plates at a suitable density and grown to >95% confluence. A straight scratch was made through the center of each well using a pipette tip. The plate was then gently rinsed with PBS buffer and cultured in a serum-free medium for 24 h. The width of the scratch was observed and captured at 0 and 24 h using a microscope (Olympus) and analyzed using ImageJ software. Flow cytometry assay

HeLa cells (1×105 cells/mL) were cultured in MEM supplemented with 10% FBS for 24 h in 6-well plates. Subsequently, the cells were treated with different concentrations of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies (0, 2.5, 5 µg/mL). After treatment, the cells were harvested and washed with cold PBS buffer. The cells were then resuspended in a binding buffer and stained with 5 µL Annexin V-FITC (Solarbio, Beijing, China) and 5 µL propidium iodide (PI) for 15 minutes at 4 ℃ in the dark. The percentage of apoptotic cells was assessed using flow cytometry (BD, FACSCAriaTMII Sorter, Franklin Lakes, NJ, USA). The experiment was performed in triplicate.

Western blotting

Total protein was extracted from the treated cells using RIPA buffer supplemented with 1 mmol/L PMSF and quantified using a BCA protein detection kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). The protein samples were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto a polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane. After blocking with 5% skim milk for 2 h at room temperature, the membrane was washed with TBST three times for 10 minutes each. Subsequently, the PVDF membrane was incubated with the primary antibody (Proteintech, Wuhan, China) overnight on a shaker at 4 ℃. After three washes, the membrane was incubated with a horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) for 2 hours at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence reagent (Millipore, Burlington, MA, USA), and band intensity was quantified using Image J software. Data analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism software version 8.0.

Reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted using a VeZol-Pure Total RNA Isolation Kit (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) and reversed to cDNA. The RT-PCR examination was performed using cDNA and ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Vazyme, Nanjing, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The relative expression of the target gene was normalized against GAPDH and analyzed using the 2−△△Ct method. The primers for genes were listed as follows: GAPDH forward 5'-GGA CCT GAC CTG CCG TCT AG-3', and GAPDH reverse 5'-GTA GCC CAG GAT GCC CTT GA-3'; Caspase-3 forward 5'-CAT GGA AGC GAA TCA ATG GAC T-3', and Caspase-3 reverse 5'-CTG TAC CAG ACC GAG ATG TCA-3'; Bax forward 5'-CCC GAG AGG TCT TTT TCC GAG-3'; and Bax reverse 5'-CCA GCC CAT GAT GGT TCT GAT-3'; Bcl-2 forward 5'-GAT TGA TGG GAT CGT TGC CT-3', Bcl-2 reverse 5'-CAA ACT GAG CAG AGT CTT CAG-3'.

Immunofluorescence

Following the intervention, HeLa cells were rinsed with PBS, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 minutes, and permeabilized with 0.4% Triton-X100 solution (400 µL). The cells were then incubated on a shaker at room temperature for 15 minutes, washed three times with PBS, and subsequently blocked with goat serum for 30 minutes at room temperature. After another three washes with PBS, the cells were incubated overnight at 4 ℃ with a primary antibody against LC3-B protein. Immunofluorescence-labeled secondary antibodies (Proteintech, Wuhan, China) along with 4',6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) were applied for 1 hour at room temperature in the dark. Images were captured using a fluorescence microscope system (EL6000, Wetzlar, Germany) and analyzed using ImageJ software.

Ectopic tumorigenesis experiment in nude mice

Female BALB/c nude mice aged five weeks (weighing 18–22 g) were obtained from Beijing Vitong Lever Laboratory Animal Technology Co. Ltd. Mice were housed at room temperature (25 ℃ approximately) with a 12-h light/12-h dark cycle and humidity of 50%. Food and water were provided ad libitum. The mice were injected with 100 µL of HeLa cell suspension (concentration: 2×107 cells/mL) into the right axilla. Tumor growth was monitored daily, and as the tumors developed, the long diameter (a) and short diameter (b) of the tumors were measured using vernier calipers. Tumor volume (V) was calculated using the formula V = (a × b2)/2. When the tumor size reached approximately 20 mm3, the mice were randomly divided into three groups, with six mice in each group, namely: PBS group (subcutaneous injection of 100 µL PBS), Fe3O4 NPs group (subcutaneous injection of 100 µL Fe3O4 NPs), and Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies group (subcutaneous injection of 100 µL Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies). Each group (n=6) received a total of two injections, once per week. Mice were weighed and tumor volume was measured every three days until 2 weeks. After the execution of the mice, the xenografted tumors were photographed and the histomorphological changes of the organs were examined by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. Animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee of The First Affiliated Hospital of Shandong First Medical University (Jinan, China) (No. 2023102001), in compliance with the relevant guidelines for laboratory animal experiments in Shandong First Medical University for the care and use of animals.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were independently repeated in triplicate. Statistical analyses were performed using the Graphpad prism software version 8. Data that followed a normal distribution were expressed as the mean ±standard deviation (SD), and comparisons between two groups were performed using Student’s t-test. For data that violated the normality assumption, the Mann-Whitney U test was employed. For comparisons among three or more groups, homogeneity of variances was tested. If the assumption of homogeneity was met, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied. A P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Characterization of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies

We prepared colloidal hybrid assemblies (Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies) using O/W emulsion technology. Fatty chain surfactants (oleic acid) were obtained via the thermal decomposition of iron oleate and coated on the surface of Fe3O4 NPs. Following co-assemblies, colloidal particles with a diameter of around 200 nm were generated. Importantly, due to the strong hydrophobic interactions between the fatty chains of the surfactant and the CC3 pores, these assemblies demonstrated excellent dispersion in water. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed that CC3 molecules were self-assembled into octahedral crystals (Figure 1).

Characterization of the Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies. (A) Fe3O4-CC3 model. (B) TEM images of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies (scale bar =200 nm). NPs, nanoparticles; TEM, transmission electron microscopy.

Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies are superior to Fe3O4 NPs in suppressing the proliferation and migration of cervical cancer cells

To investigate the effects of Fe3O4 NPs and Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies on the proliferation of cervical cancer cells, we used the CCK-8 assay to assess HeLa cell viability after 24 h treatment with various concentrations. As shown in Figure 2A, both Fe3O4 NPs and Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies inhibited the growth of HeLa cells in a concentration-dependent manner. At a concentration of 10 µg/mL, Fe3O4 NPs and Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies reduced tumor cell activity by 40% and 70%, respectively. As concentration increased, the effect of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies on cell viability decreased significantly, while the decrease for Fe3O4 NPs was more slowly. When treated at the same concentration (5 µg/mL), HeLa cells showed distinct morphological changes. Figure 2B illustrated that cells treated with Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies became round, and their nucleus appeared fragmented, indicating more severe damage. Furthermore, the cell scratch assay in Figure 2C demonstrated that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies inhibited cell migration about 1.5 times more than Fe3O4 NPs (P<0.01). In summary, these findings indicated that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies markedly suppressed the proliferation and migration of HeLa cells compared to Fe3O4 NPs.

Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies induced apoptosis in cervical cancer cells

The impact of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies on cervical cancer cells was evaluated by Annexin V-FITC/PI double staining flow cytometry. As shown in Figure 3A, compared to untreated cells, the apoptosis rates in HeLa cells were 5.98%, 8.22%, and 13% as the concentration of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies increased (0, 2.5, and 5 µg/mL). Caspase-3, a key executioner in the apoptosis pathway, along with Bax (a pro-apoptotic protein) and Bcl-2 (an anti-apoptotic protein), were analyzed to understand the apoptosis mechanism. The results from PCR and Western blotting showed that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies increased Caspase-3 expression in a dose-dependent manner in HeLa cells (P<0.05) (Figure 3B,3C). Furthermore, Bcl-2 expression decreased, while Bax expression increased, also in a dose-dependent manner, compared to control cells (Figure 3B,3C). These findings suggested that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies induced apoptosis by promoting Caspase-3 activation.

Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies activated the autophagy pathway in cervical cancer cell

Autophagy is a well-known tumor-suppressing mechanism (24). Studies have shown that excessive autophagy can lead to cell death and help break down carcinogenic substances, thereby inhibiting tumor growth (25,26). As shown in Figure 4A, the expression of Beclin-1, an autophagy-related protein, increased in a dose-dependent manner in HeLa cells (P<0.05). In addition, Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies promoted the expression of LC3-B and the degradation of P62. Immunofluorescence analysis further revealed that LC3-B levels increased dose-dependently in Fe3O4-CC3-treated HeLa cells (Figure 4B). These results suggested that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies induced autophagy.

Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies were associated with inhibition of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway

The PI3K/AKT pathway is crucial for regulating cellular processes like growth, proliferation, cell cycle progression, apoptosis, and autophagy (27,28). mTOR is a key downstream target of the PI3K/AKT pathway. As shown in Figure 5, treatment with Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies significantly reduced the phosphorylation levels of PI3K, AKT, and mTOR in a dose-dependent manner compared to untreated cells. However, the total levels of PI3K, AKT, and mTOR remained unchanged (P<0.05). These results suggested that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies were associated with suppression of PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway.

Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies inhibited tumor growth in vivo

To evaluate the anti-tumor effects of Fe3O4 NPs and Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies in vivo, a HeLa nude mouse tumor model was established (Figure 6A). As shown in Figure 6B, Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies significantly suppressed tumor growth compared to Fe3O4 NPs, which showed only minor tumor suppression. Figure 6C demonstrated a significant decrease in tumor volume in mice treated with Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies. H&E staining of major organs (heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney) revealed no significant organ damage in mice treated with either Fe3O4 NPs or Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies (Figure 6D). These results indicated that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies exhibited strong anti-tumor activity in vivo.

Discussion

Cervical cancer is one of the malignant tumors that seriously threatens women’s health (29,30). At present, surgery is the primary treatment for early-stage cervical cancer, whereas palliative radiotherapy and chemotherapy are commonly employed for late-stage and recurrent cervical cancer (31). Surgical treatment of cervical cancer can cause invasive damage to patients and increase fertility risks for women of childbearing age, while adjuvant radiotherapy and chemotherapy can cause systemic side effects (32). Therefore, it is imperative to seek a new treatment method that can circumvent the shortcomings of traditional treatment methods. As the first reported nanozyme to be thoroughly studied, Fe3O4 NPs combine the properties of inorganic NPs with enzymatic activity and have been widely used in tumor treatment (33). On this basis, we designed colloidal nanomaterials through a unique O/W emulsion restriction strategy. The ferrite nanocrystals self-assembled on the formed POC octahedral crystals to form the Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies, making it another choice for cervical cancer.

In our study, taking into account the fact that natural enzymes are often separated by biological lipid bilayers, such as those within lysosomes, can only achieve their catalytic function under specific conditions. Based on this, we coated the surface of Fe3O4 NPs with the cationic surfactant (DTAB), akin to the structure of the lipid bilayer (Figure 1). These assemblies potentially can interact strongly with membrane-bound cellular structures, consequently leading to enhanced cytotoxicity with the enhanced localized concentration of Fe3O4 NPS. Our results demonstrated a clear dose-dependent cytotoxic effect of the nanomaterials on HeLa cells. For Fe3O4 NPS, cell viability was effectively reduced at a concentration of 10 µg/mL, whereas for the Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies, this threshold decreased to 5 µg/mL.

Apoptosis is an important process for the stability of the body’s internal environment by eliminating abnormal cells, including cancer cells (34). Numerous cancer therapies aim to suppress tumor cell proliferation by promoting apoptosis. Apoptosis can be mediated by surface death receptors (exogenous apoptosis) or through caspase cascade reactions (endogenous apoptosis) (35). In endogenous apoptosis, Caspase-3 is an important caspase family protease that plays a key role in the execution phase of apoptosis (36). Our study confirmed the results of previous studies that have emphasized the involvement of Caspase-3 in the induction of apoptosis, especially in cancer cells exposed to therapeutic drugs (37). Additionally, Bax is a member of the Bcl-2 family, which promotes apoptosis, while Bcl-2, another member of the family, inhibits apoptosis (38).

The balance between Bax and Bcl-2 dictates cellular fate, a mechanism observed in previous studies where Bax/Bcl-2 ratios determined apoptosis susceptibility (39). Upon apoptotic signaling, Bax alters mitochondrial membrane permeability, allowing for the release of pro-apoptotic molecules from the mitochondria and triggering apoptosis. In contrast, Bcl-2 stabilizes mitochondrial membranes, preventing apoptosis signal transmission (35). Our results show that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies significantly upregulated Bax while downregulating Bcl-2 expression, consistent with prior research indicating that nanomaterials can influence the Bax/Bcl-2 ratio to promote apoptosis (40). The Annexin V assay further confirmed that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies induced apoptosis in HeLa cells. These observations suggested that Fe3O4-CC3-induced cell death occurs through a Caspase-3-dependent apoptosis pathway mediated by the Bax/Bcl-2 axis.

Autophagy, a conserved intracellular catabolic degradation mechanism, plays a complex role in cancer biology by both promoting cell survival and enhancing cell death under various conditions (41). The dual nature of autophagy in malignant cells has been well-documented, with previous studies highlighting how autophagy can support tumor cell survival under stress while also serving as a mechanism of cell death when excessively activated (42). Autophagy-related genes Beclin-1, LC3-II, and P62 play crucial roles in autophagy regulation (42,43). LC3 is an indicator of the autophagy process, and Beclin-1 is an autophagy-binding protein. During autophagy, LC3 can be cleaved into LC3-I. Beclin-1 facilitates the conversion of LC3-I into its lipidated form, LC3-II, which serves as a crucial indicator of autophagic activity. Intracellular P62 (SQSTM 1) protein is a substrate preferentially degraded by autolysosomes. In our study, compared to untreated cells, Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies increased the expression of autophagy-related protein Beclin-1 and LC3-II in HeLa cells and promoted P62 degradation. These findings are consistent with previous research on NP-induced autophagy. In comparison to other autophagy-inducing agents, Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies demonstrate a potent ability to trigger autophagy in HeLa cells. These results contribute to the growing body of literature on the potential of NP-based treatments to modulate autophagy in cancer therapy, suggesting that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies may serve as a promising therapeutic approach by specifically activating autophagic pathways that favor cell death over survival.

Mature autophagy-related regulatory pathways, including the PI3K/AKT/mTOR and MAPK pathways, play essential roles in maintaining cellular homeostasis and modulating autophagy (44). The PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway is pivotal in regulating both physiological and pathological processes, such as cell growth, proliferation, survival, and tumor resistance (45). And previous studies have demonstrated the close association between the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and the onset and progression of cancer (46,47), highlighting its potential as a target for the prevention and treatment of cancer (48,49). Therefore, in this study, we evaluated the effect of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies on the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway in HeLa cells. The results showed that the Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies could effectively reduce the phosphorylation expression levels of PI3K, AKT, and mTOR proteins, suggesting an inhibitory effect on cervical cancer cell growth. These results are consistent with prior studies (49). The PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway is also a known initiator of autophagy, with its inhibition leading to enhanced autophagic activity in tumor cells (50,51). In line with previous research, our study found that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies induced autophagy-mediated cell death in HeLa cells, accompanied by inhibition of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway. Similar studies have shown that suppression of AKT phosphorylation and mTOR inactivation promotes autophagic flux, as observed through elevated levels of autophagy markers such as LC3-II and reduced P62 levels, indicating autophagy progression (52). However, our findings extend this knowledge by demonstrating that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies not only inhibit this pathway but may also act as a dual-function agent (Figure 7).

The anti-tumor mechanism of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies.

In order to better imitate the tumor environment in the human germ line, we applied Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies to xenograft tumor models in nude mice. The H&E staining showed normal tissue structure in other organs such as the heart, liver, spleen, lungs, and kidney (Figure 6). Our results verified that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies could inhibit the growth of cervical cancer tumor cells and be safe in the other organs in vivo.

This study mainly evaluated the antitumor activity and associated biological responses of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies. Although apoptosis, autophagy, and suppression of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway were observed, no pathway inhibition experiments were performed and the causal relationships among these events were not fully established. In addition, the in vitro experiments were conducted at a single 24-hour time point, and the in vivo study focused on tumor volume reduction, without evaluating biodistribution, clearance, potential Fe2+/Fe3+ ion release, long-term outcomes, or comprehensive systemic toxicity. These issues will be addressed in future studies.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we successfully demonstrated that Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies exhibit a significantly stronger anti-tumor effect compared to Fe3O4 NPs alone. Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies effectively inhibited cervical cancer cell proliferation by inducing apoptosis and promoting autophagy, primarily accompanied by the inhibition of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway. This anti-cancer activity was dose-dependent, with higher doses leading to more pronounced effects. Our study provides new evidence for the potential of Fe3O4-CC3 co-assemblies as a promising anticancer candidate for cervical cancer treatment.

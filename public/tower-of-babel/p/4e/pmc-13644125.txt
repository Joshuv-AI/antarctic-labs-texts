Association between ERAS compliance and postoperative outcomes after right colectomy for colon cancer: a single-center cohort study
Authors: Mustafa Anıl Turhan, Suleyman Utku Celik, Mehmet Ali Koc, Kaan Sunter, Suheyla Karadag Erkoc, Menekse Ozcelik, Sevda Unver, Neslihan Alkis, Cihangir Akyol
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEnhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols improve perioperative care in colorectal surgery; however, evidence specifically linking ERAS compliance to outcomes after right colectomy is limited. This study aimed to evaluate the association between overall and phase-specific ERAS compliance and postoperative outcomes after elective right colectomy for colon cancer.MethodsThis retrospective analysis of a prospectively maintained institutional ERAS database included consecutive adults who underwent elective standard or extended right colectomy for colon cancer between January 2021 and December 2025. Compliance was assessed at the patient level using 24 predefined ERAS elements across pre-admission, preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative phases. The primary outcome was length of stay (LOS), and the secondary outcome was major postoperative complications (Clavien–Dindo grade ≥IIIa). Correlation analyses, group comparisons using a prespecified 75% overall compliance threshold, and multivariable logistic and linear regression analyses were performed.ResultsAmong 151 included patients, 76 (50.3%) developed any postoperative complication, 14 (9.3%) had major postoperative complications, and 10 (6.6%) were readmitted within 30 days. Overall ERAS compliance was inversely correlated with LOS (ρ = −0.329, p < 0.001). Preoperative (ρ = −0.248, p = 0.002) and postoperative (ρ = −0.301, p < 0.001) phase-specific compliance were also associated with shorter LOS. Patients with overall compliance ≥ 75% had shorter LOS than those with lower compliance, whereas major complication rates were similar. In exploratory multivariable analysis, higher preoperative compliance was independently associated with lower odds of major postoperative complications (OR 0.936, 95% CI 0.884–0.991; p = 0.024). Older age (B = 0.183, p = 0.022) and lower overall compliance (B = -0.265, p = 0.006) were independently associated with longer LOS.ConclusionsHigher ERAS compliance was associated with shorter length of stay, though the contribution of postoperative compliance may partly reflect recovery status rather than an independent causal effect. Higher preoperative adherence was independently associated with lower odds of major complications in exploratory multivariable analysis. These findings support strengthening preoperative optimization and consistent pathway execution to improve recovery after right colectomy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12893-026-04038-0.Keywords: Colectomy, Colon cancer, Enhanced recovery after surgery, Length of stay, Postoperative complications

Introduction

Colorectal cancer is one of the leading causes of cancer-related morbidity and mortality worldwide, and surgical resection remains the cornerstone of curative treatment [1, 2]. Despite advances in surgical techniques and perioperative management, postoperative complications remain common and continue to impair recovery after colorectal surgery [3, 4]. In recent decades, Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) protocols have been developed as evidence-based, multimodal perioperative care pathways to reduce the surgical stress response and accelerate functional recovery, including early mobilization and early oral intake [5, 6].

ERAS protocols integrate preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative care elements, and higher adherence has been associated with lower morbidity, shorter hospital stays, and faster functional recovery [5, 7]. The 2025 ERAS Society recommendations highlight preoperative risk assessment and individualized optimization, including nutritional screening and optimization, and emphasize multidisciplinary team-based implementation to maximize outcomes in colorectal surgery [5]. Importantly, the effectiveness of ERAS depends on adherence to its individual components. Higher compliance has been consistently associated with fewer postoperative complications, shorter length of stay, and lower readmission rates; however, partial implementation remains common due to institutional variation, resource limitations, and patient-related factors [8–12].

Right colectomy may represent a distinct clinical setting in which the impact of ERAS compliance differs from that observed in other colorectal procedures. Despite comparable levels of protocol adherence, previous studies have reported slower gastrointestinal recovery and higher rates of prolonged postoperative ileus after right-sided colectomy compared with left-sided resections, suggesting that procedure-specific factors may influence both recovery trajectories and postoperative morbidity [13–16]. Although the relationship between ERAS adherence and postoperative outcomes has been widely investigated in colorectal surgery, evidence specifically addressing this association in patients undergoing right colectomy for colon cancer remains limited, particularly with respect to postoperative complications [14, 17]. Therefore, the present study aims to assess the association between ERAS protocol compliance and postoperative outcomes in patients who have undergone elective right colectomy for colon cancer within a standardized oncologic resection strategy.

Methods

Study design and patients

Between January 2021 and December 2025, we conducted a retrospective analysis of a prospectively maintained institutional ERAS database, including consecutive adult patients (≥ 18 years) who underwent elective standard or extended right colectomy for colon cancer at Ankara University School of Medicine. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and is reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement [18]. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ankara University School of Medicine Ethics Committee (approval number: İ02-225-26).

Data collection and definitions

Right colectomy included standard or extended right colectomy with ileocolic anastomosis, performed via an open or minimally invasive approach (laparoscopic/robotic). All procedures were performed by two colorectal surgeons with dedicated expertise in oncologic colorectal surgery. The choice of surgical approach was based on tumor characteristics, previous abdominal surgery, patient factors, and surgeon preference, with minimally invasive surgery being the preferred approach. Conversion from minimally invasive to open surgery occurred in 6 patients (4.9% of minimally invasive attempts), and these patients were analyzed within the open surgery group. In our institution, these resections were carried out according to standardized oncologic principles incorporating complete mesocolic excision (CME) with central vascular ligation when anatomically and oncologically appropriate [19]. All ileocolic anastomoses were constructed using a stapled side-to-side technique; isoperistaltic or antiperistaltic in minimally invasive cases depending on intraoperative conditions, and antiperistaltic in open cases. Exclusion criteria were emergency surgery, benign disease, non-curative/palliative resections, and incomplete data precluding analysis (Fig. 1). Patients with metastatic disease were included only if surgery was performed with curative intent and an R0 resection of the primary tumor was achieved. Cases involving stoma formation were excluded because stoma-related factors may independently affect postoperative recovery and discharge timing. Missing data were handled using a complete-case analysis approach, and no imputation was applied. Among the five excluded cases, two lacked complete documentation for the preoperative phase, two for the intraoperative phase, and one for the postoperative phase, preventing reliable calculation of ERAS compliance. The final study cohort comprised 151 patients, all of whom had complete 30-day follow-up data, including readmission and mortality, verified through outpatient clinic records and hospital readmission databases. The final date of follow-up was 31 January 2026.

Flow diagram illustrating patient selection from the institutional ERAS database and formation of the final analytic cohort

Baseline and perioperative variables included age, sex, body mass index, American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status score, Charlson Comorbidity Index, nutritional risk screening (NRS-2002), neoadjuvant therapy, and operative characteristics. Nutritional risk was defined as NRS-2002 ≥ 3. Postoperative complications were graded according to the Clavien–Dindo classification, and the overall complication rate (any grade) was reported descriptively. The primary outcome was length of hospital stay (LOS). Major postoperative complications, defined as Clavien–Dindo grade ≥IIIa [20], were assessed as a secondary outcome. LOS was defined as the number of days from surgery to discharge; days related to readmission were not included.

ERAS pathway and compliance assessment

ERAS compliance was assessed at the individual patient level using 24 prespecified institutional ERAS elements (Supplementary Table S1) spanning the pre-admission, preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative phases [5]. Each element was coded as fulfilled (1) or not fulfilled (0) based on contemporaneous clinical documentation. Elements deemed non-applicable (NA), if any, were excluded from the denominator according to prespecified pathway rules. Overall ERAS compliance (%) was calculated as (number of fulfilled applicable elements / number of applicable elements) × 100. Compliance was calculated in a similar manner across four perioperative phases: pre-admission (n = 5 elements), preoperative (n = 6), intraoperative (n = 7), and postoperative (n = 6). Overall compliance was evaluated using a prespecified threshold (≥ 75%) [8, 10]. Patients were stratified based on this cut-off to assess its impact on outcomes.

Pre-admission care included structured patient education and counseling delivered collaboratively by the ERAS nurses and the anesthesia and surgical teams. During this process, the ERAS pathway and perioperative expectations were thoroughly explained. When indicated, preoperative optimization and prehabilitation strategies were initiated, with exercise programs implemented when sufficient time was available. Nutritional risk was assessed using the NRS-2002 tool, and patients identified as at risk were referred to a dietitian for individualized nutritional support. Anemia identified at the initial evaluation was managed preoperatively following patient blood management principles.

In the preoperative period, postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV) risk was assessed using the Apfel score, and all patients received dual prophylaxis with ondansetron and dexamethasone, with postoperative ondansetron given only if clinically indicated. Pre-anesthetic medications, antimicrobial prophylaxis, and skin preparation were applied according to institutional protocols. For mechanical bowel preparation, non-application was considered compliant when bowel preparation was not indicated by the institutional protocol. Patients were allowed clear fluids up to 2 h before anesthesia, and maltodextrin-based carbohydrate drinks were administered to reduce insulin resistance and support metabolic homeostasis.

During the intraoperative period, anesthesia was induced following a standardized protocol, beginning with 3 min of preoxygenation, maintaining mask pressures below 20 cmH₂O to minimize gastric insufflation, followed by total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA) guided by bispectral index (BIS) monitoring. Intraoperative fluid and electrolyte therapy was guided by invasive arterial monitoring and blood gas analysis to ensure normovolemia and electrolyte balance. Core temperature was monitored by a nasopharyngeal probe with routine lower-body forced-air warming and additional upper-body warming if core temperature was less than 36 °C. Nasogastric tubes were not routinely placed in accordance with ERAS pathway practices.

Postoperative targets were predefined as initiation of oral or enteral intake on postoperative day 0 (POD0), mobilization on POD0, and urinary catheter removal on POD1. Multimodal analgesia, including spinal analgesia and abdominal wall blocks, was used to optimize pain control and minimize opioid use. Thromboembolism prophylaxis and glycemic control were provided according to standard protocols.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were assessed for normality and summarized as mean (standard deviation) when normally distributed, or as median (25th–75th percentile) if non-normally distributed. Categorical variables were summarized as counts and percentages using valid denominators. Continuous variables were compared between groups using the independent-samples t test or the Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate. Correlations were assessed using Pearson’s correlation for normally distributed variables and Spearman’s rank correlation otherwise. Categorical outcomes were compared using χ² tests or Fisher’s exact tests. Variables with a p value < 0.20 in univariable analyses, together with clinically relevant covariates, were considered for inclusion in the multivariable models; to reduce the risk of overfitting and multicollinearity, only selected variables were retained in the final models. Collinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIF); all values were within acceptable limits (range 1.19–2.24), indicating no multicollinearity concern. Because LOS showed a right-skewed distribution, linear regression was retained for interpretability, and model assumptions were assessed using residual diagnostics. Two-sided p values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. As a sensitivity analysis, Firth’s penalized logistic regression was performed to address potential overfitting concerns arising from the low number of events. For LOS, a sensitivity analysis using log-transformed LOS was also performed to assess robustness of the linear regression findings. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics version 30.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R statistical software version 4.5 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

A total of 151 patients undergoing elective right colectomy for colon cancer, performed according to standardized mesocolic oncologic dissection principles, were included. The cohort consisted of 92 (60.9%) male patients, with a median age of 67 (60–72) years. Baseline and perioperative characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median overall ERAS compliance was 75.0% (69.6–78.3), and compliance rates for each phase are shown in Fig. 2. Element-level review showed near-universal adherence to intraoperative and postoperative components, whereas prehabilitation and carbohydrate loading were the lowest-performing elements within the pre-admission and preoperative phases, respectively.

In a sensitivity analysis using Firth’s penalized logistic regression, results were consistent with the standard model (OR per 1% increase: 0.939, 95% CI 0.889–0.992; p = 0.025; Supplementary Table S4). Similarly, sensitivity analysis using log-transformed LOS confirmed the robustness of the primary linear regression findings, with overall compliance remaining independently associated with LOS (B = − 0.018, p = 0.002; Supplementary Table S5). To further assess the robustness of the continuous compliance–outcome associations, patients were stratified into overall compliance quartiles using equal-frequency binning, such that each quartile contained an approximately equal number of patients (Q1: 54.2–70.2%, Q2: 70.2–75.0%, Q3: 75.0–78.3%, Q4: 78.3–90.9%). Median LOS decreased monotonically from 9 days in Q1 to 7 days in Q4, and major complication rates declined progressively from 15.8% in Q1 to 2.9% in Q4, consistent with a dose-response pattern (Supplementary Figure S1). The majority of patients had Stage II disease (n = 54, 35.8%). Pathological stage was not significantly associated with major postoperative complications (p = 0.546) or LOS (p = 0.358); full details are presented in Supplementary Table S6.

Discussion

In this study, right colectomies were performed in accordance with contemporary oncologic dissection principles related to complete mesocolic excision; within this technically standardized setting, ERAS adherence remained associated with postoperative outcomes. Overall compliance demonstrated a clear inverse relationship with LOS across correlation analyses and threshold-based comparisons. Major postoperative complications were relatively uncommon (9.3%), and crude comparison using ≥ 75% cut-off did not significantly discriminate major morbidity, suggesting that categorical thresholds may be less sensitive than continuous compliance measures, particularly in cohorts with low event counts. In multivariable analysis, preoperative ERAS compliance was independently associated with lower odds of major postoperative complications, though this finding should be considered exploratory given the low number of events and wide confidence intervals, whereas overall compliance and age were independently associated with LOS. These findings are in keeping with the broader colorectal ERAS literature, which suggests that clinical benefit depends on implementation quality and adherence rather than the mere presence of an ERAS pathway [7, 8, 21, 22]. This is also consistent with the broader evidence base; in a 2024 meta-analysis of 74 randomized clinical trials including 9,076 patients, ERAS was associated with a 1.88-day reduction in hospital length of stay and a lower risk of postoperative complications (RR 0.71, 95% CI 0.59–0.87), whereas readmission and mortality were not significantly different [7].

A key strength of the present study is its procedure-specific focus on right colectomy, a setting in which recovery trajectories may differ from mixed colorectal cohorts. Prior studies suggest that right colectomies are associated with slower gastrointestinal recovery and higher rates of prolonged ileus compared with left-sided resections [13–16]. Because discharge after right colectomy is often driven by functional milestones, particularly return of bowel function and tolerance of oral intake, several ERAS components may be especially influential in this subgroup. In this context, by quantifying adherence across ERAS phases and linking these measures to LOS and major morbidity, our findings provide practical signals for pathway refinement in right colectomy. Although advanced pathological stage may contribute to operative complexity and postoperative recovery, pathological TNM stage was not significantly associated with LOS or major postoperative complications in the present cohort. Notably, the pre-admission and preoperative phases showed lower adherence than the intraoperative and postoperative phases, underscoring potentially modifiable upstream targets, including standardized counseling and structured preoperative preparation [5, 23]. These findings suggest that future implementation efforts in right colectomy may benefit more from strengthening upstream pathway components with greater variability, rather than already well-standardized intraoperative practices.

The available literature suggests that higher ERAS compliance is generally associated with improved outcomes [7–11, 22–25]. In our study, this relationship was clear for LOS. Accordingly, for LOS we used overall compliance as a pragmatic summary of pathway execution, whereas for major postoperative complications we prioritized preoperative compliance to reduce overlap with postoperative events. This pattern has also been observed in randomized data; Forsmo et al. reported a shorter hospital stay with ERAS [5 vs. 8 days; P = 0.001], despite similar rates of major and minor morbidity, reoperation, readmission, and 30-day mortality [22]. Many studies report lower morbidity with better adherence, but this effect is not always easy to show with simple cut-offs, especially when the number of major events is small [26–28]. This likely explains why our ≥ 75% comparison did not separate major morbidity well, while the exploratory multivariable model still suggested that the preoperative phase matters for severe complications. Similarly, Gustafsson et al. showed that increasing adherence from 43.3% to 70.6% was associated with a reduction in patients with at least one complication from 43.8% to 33.7% and with lower 30-day postoperative morbidity (OR 0.73, 95% CI 0.55–0.98), supporting a dose–response rather than a purely binary effect [21]. Overall, our findings fit the idea that ERAS works best when upstream optimization and consistent postoperative execution are achieved [5, 23].

Clinically, our findings suggest that improving ERAS delivery in right colectomy should start upstream, before the incision. In our cohort, pre-admission and preoperative compliance were the weakest parts of the pathway, highlighting modifiable targets such as structured patient counseling, standardized preoperative optimization, and early identification of nutritional risk [5, 29]. This aligns with current ERAS guidance emphasizing individualized preparation and multidisciplinary implementation, while perioperative nutrition recommendations support early screening and targeted support in at-risk patients [23].

In routine clinical practice, postoperative elements that directly influence discharge readiness, such as early mobilization, early oral intake, and timely catheter or device removal, should be implemented with clearly defined care targets and regular audit. Studies evaluating ERAS compliance have shown that consistent bedside implementation and feedback mechanisms are associated with improved recovery profiles [8, 27, 30]. Although our cut-off analysis did not clearly distinguish patients with major morbidity, preoperative compliance still warrants attention. Prior elective colon surgery data suggest that suboptimal ERAS implementation is associated with major postoperative complications, while registry evidence indicates that adherence is better understood as a continuum rather than a binary threshold. Accordingly, the threshold-based analysis should be considered pragmatic and secondary, and a sensitivity analysis using an 80% compliance threshold yielded directionally consistent results. Subgroup-focused studies, including those in older adults and procedure-specific cohorts, further support the importance of patient-level factors and structured preoperative preparation in shaping recovery and complication risk [31, 32].

Although most ERAS literature includes heterogeneous colorectal cohorts, procedure-specific evidence focusing exclusively on right colectomy remains limited. In a prospective series of 140 elective right colectomies, postoperative morbidity was 31%, mean postoperative hospital stay was 7 days, and successful completion of the ERAS pathway was achieved in 59% of patients; failure was mainly related to nausea/vomiting, postoperative ileus requiring nasogastric decompression, or prolonged LOS, while preoperative anemia (OR 5.2) and antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy (OR 4.5) independently predicted ERAS pathway failure, highlighting the importance of preoperative optimization in right colectomy [33]. Similarly, a prospective multicenter ERAS cohort of right colectomy showed that laparoscopic surgery within an ERAS environment was associated with faster pain control (2.4 vs. 4.2 days) and shorter LOS (4 vs. 6 days), without a significant increase in complications (18.7% vs. 21.3%) compared with open surgery [34]. More recently, a 2024 multicenter retrospective study demonstrated that right colectomy was associated with significantly higher rates of prolonged postoperative ileus and nasogastric tube requirement compared with left colectomy within an ERAS framework, further supporting the need for procedure-specific pathway refinement [17]. Beyond perioperative care, right colectomy for colon cancer is also shaped by specific technical factors. Standardized mesocolic plane dissection and central vascular ligation, as incorporated into contemporary oncologic right colectomy, may influence operative stress, bowel handling, and recovery trajectories. Our findings suggest that even within such a standardized oncologic surgical setting, ERAS compliance was consistently associated with shorter LOS, while preoperative compliance showed an adjusted association with major morbidity in the exploratory analysis. Readmission occurred in 10 patients (6.6%). Although the study was not powered to formally assess the compliance–readmission relationship, shorter LOS may theoretically trade off with higher readmission risk, and this warrants evaluation in larger future studies.

Pre-admission compliance may also reflect patient-level factors that lie outside the clinical team’s control. Patients with frailty, lower health literacy, or limited access to healthcare may find it harder to engage with preoperative education and optimization programs, regardless of how well these are delivered. We did not have formal frailty data; nutritional risk score served as a proxy, though it does not fully capture patient vulnerability. This represents a source of confounding that our retrospective design cannot eliminate.

When interpreting the postoperative compliance–LOS association, the possibility of reverse causation should be acknowledged. Patients with complications or slower recovery may be less able to meet postoperative ERAS targets such as early mobilization, oral intake, and catheter removal — making low postoperative compliance partly a consequence, rather than a cause, of adverse recovery. This should be kept in mind when interpreting postoperative compliance findings. In contrast, preoperative compliance is not susceptible to this form of bias, which strengthens the interpretation of its independent association with major postoperative complications.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The retrospective design limits causal inference and may allow residual confounding. In addition, the low number of major postoperative complications restricted model complexity and statistical power; accordingly, the multivariable logistic model was intentionally parsimonious and should be interpreted as exploratory, particularly in view of the wide confidence intervals. The LOS model showed only modest explanatory performance, and some conceptual overlap may persist because minimally invasive surgery represents both an operative characteristic and an ERAS-related element. Additionally, although pathological stage, surgical approach, and conversion cases were further characterized in the analysis, residual confounding related to operative complexity and intraoperative decision-making may still persist. The use of linear regression for a right-skewed outcome represents a pragmatic choice; sensitivity analysis using log-transformed LOS confirmed the robustness of the findings. The exclusion of cases involving stoma formation and the absence of anastomotic orientation analysis may limit generalizability to more complex right colectomy settings. All 24 ERAS elements were weighted equally in the compliance calculation, which is consistent with methodology used in prior ERAS compliance studies [8, 10, 21]. However, this represents a methodological simplification, as individual elements may not contribute equally to postoperative recovery. The phase-specific compliance analysis partly mitigates this limitation by allowing differentiation between perioperative phases. Additionally, phase-specific compliance scores are based on a small number of components, which limits the precision of percentage-based comparisons between groups. Despite these limitations, the consistent association between compliance and LOS, together with the exploratory adjusted association between preoperative ERAS adherence and major morbidity, provides support for strengthening upstream ERAS elements in right colectomy pathways.

Conclusion

In patients undergoing elective right colectomy for colon cancer, higher ERAS compliance was associated with shorter length of stay, though the contribution of postoperative compliance may partly reflect recovery status rather than an independent causal effect. Higher preoperative adherence was independently associated with lower odds of major postoperative complications in exploratory multivariable analysis. These findings support preoperative optimization and consistent execution of key ERAS elements to improve recovery after right colectomy.

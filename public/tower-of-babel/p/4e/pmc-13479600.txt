Global phylogenomics of Bordetella pertussis ST2 reveals regional heterogeneity in China and lineage-specific genome evolution
Authors: Meng Qi, Wangxiao Zhou, Huili Ye, Deru Lei, Xu Dong, Xinmei Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBordetella pertussis sequence type 2 (ST2) is widely detected in China and in public genome datasets, yet the evolutionary relationships, dissemination patterns, and genomic features that distinguish its major lineages have not been systematically characterized.ResultsIn this study, we conducted a comprehensive genomic analysis of 580 ST2 genomes from 24 countries between 1942 and 2024, integrating 46 newly sequenced clinical isolates from Wenzhou, China. Phylogenetic reconstruction showed that the Wenzhou isolates were not homogeneous but comprised two distinct clades, including a dominant macrolide-resistant ptxP1-associated lineage and a smaller ptxP3-associated lineage. At the global scale, the ST2 population resolved into two major clades and three subclades; the dominant ptxP1-associated Wenzhou lineage was assigned to subclade I, whereas the smaller ptxP3-associated Wenzhou lineage belonged to global clade II, with one isolate placed in subclade III. Two China-enriched subclades were strongly associated with 23S rRNA A2047G mutations, whereas a geographically widespread subclade lacked this determinant. Chinese isolates were distributed across multiple phylogenetic positions, supporting repeated introductions followed by local expansion. Comparative analyses further revealed lineage-specific differences in detected insertion sequence (IS) elements and pseudogene accumulation. IS481 was the predominant IS family, and intergenic insertions were more common than intragenic events across lineages. Clade-specific SNPs and stable pseudogene patterns together indicated partially distinct adaptive trajectories among ST2 clades.ConclusionsThese findings define the local and global population structure of ST2, highlight marked regional heterogeneity in contemporary Chinese pertussis, and show that ongoing diversification of this lineage is shaped by both geographic spread and lineage-specific genome evolution.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-026-13189-6.Keywords: Bordetella pertussis, Sequence type 2, Phylogenomics, Macrolide resistance, Insertion sequences, Pseudogenes

Introduction

Pertussis, caused primarily by Bordetella pertussis, remains one of the most important vaccine-preventable respiratory infections in infants [1, 2]. Although widespread immunization has substantially reduced morbidity and mortality, pertussis has re-emerged in many highly vaccinated populations and resurged again in the post-COVID period, while young infants continue to bear the greatest risk of hospitalization and death [1–4]. In parallel, reported disease has increasingly involved adolescents and adults, who may serve as an important source of infection for incompletely immunized infants [1, 3]. These patterns indicate that pertussis control is shaped not only by vaccine coverage, waning immunity, and changes in diagnosis and transmission, but also by ongoing bacterial adaptation [2–4]. Because B. pertussis is relatively monomorphic by conventional markers, classical typing methods provide limited resolution for defining recent transmission, lineage turnover, or the epidemiologic success of closely related strains, whereas genome-based approaches, including whole-genome sequencing and expanded allele-based frameworks, offer a clearer way to resolve population structure and dissemination dynamics in contemporary B. pertussis populations [5, 6].

Recent genomic studies have shown that contemporary B. pertussis evolution is characterized by vaccine-associated antigenic divergence, including expansion of ptxP3-linked lineages and divergence between vaccine and circulating antigen alleles [4, 7]. Pertactin-deficient variants have further emerged in acellular-vaccine settings [4]. At the same time, large-scale chromosomal rearrangements, insertion-sequence-mediated remodeling, and progressive gene loss indicate that adaptation in this pathogen is not driven by point mutation alone [7, 8]. China represents a distinct epidemiologic context within this broader global landscape. Macrolide-resistant B. pertussis was first reported in China more than a decade ago, and subsequent work has shown that resistance has become an important and largely China-centered feature of current pertussis epidemiology [9, 10]. Regional studies from Chinese cities have further documented coexistence of historically prevalent resistant ptxP1-related backgrounds and more recently expanding ptxP3-associated lineages, including erythromycin-resistant strains carrying prn150 or additional vaccine-antigen deficiencies [11–13]. These observations suggest that lineage turnover in China is ongoing but geographically heterogeneous rather than synchronized. However, the genomic diversity, evolutionary history, and within-China spread of the ST2 lineage—the most common sequence type among Chinese macrolide-resistant isolates—remain poorly understood. Thus, ST2 was used here as an epidemiologic framework rather than as a high-resolution typing unit. To our knowledge, a lineage-centered analysis linking contemporary Chinese ST2 isolates with the global ST2 population and clade-associated genomic features has not yet been reported.

In this study, we investigated ST2 B. pertussis isolates collected at a large public teaching hospital in eastern China. This hospital has 3,320 beds and receives approximately 10 million patient visits annually, and such a high-volume, high-turnover setting may increase opportunities for repeated pathogen introduction and onward transmission. We analyzed 46 clinical ST2 isolates recovered in Wenzhou in 2024 and integrated them with publicly available global ST2 genomes. Through integrated analyses of clinical epidemiology, antimicrobial susceptibility, phylogenomics, geographic dissemination, mobile genetic elements (MGEs) with detailed insertion-sequence profiling, pseudogenes, and clade-specific SNPs, we aimed to define the local and global population structure of ST2 and clarify the genomic basis of its ongoing diversification. These data provide insight into regional heterogeneity in eastern China while placing the Wenzhou isolates within the broader evolutionary and dissemination landscape of the global ST2 lineage.

Materials and methods

Collection of B. pertussis isolates and clinical data

Between January and December 2024, 46 non-duplicate B. pertussis isolates were collected from hospitalized children receiving routine clinical care at the Second Affiliated Hospital and Yuying Children’s Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University, a large tertiary teaching hospital in Wenzhou, Zhejiang Province, China. Only the first isolate recovered from each patient during the study period was retained. Available demographic, clinical, laboratory, vaccination, and ward information were retrieved from the medical records and linked to the corresponding isolates for downstream analyses.

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was performed by the E-test method for erythromycin, azithromycin, clarithromycin, clindamycin, ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, meropenem, ceftriaxone, and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole. Because no species-specific CLSI breakpoints are currently available for B. pertussis, MIC results were interpreted according to the observed MIC ranges and, when applicable, with reference to the Haemophilus influenzae interpretive criteria in CLSI M100.

Whole-genome sequencing and genomic analysis

Whole-genome sequencing of the 46 Wenzhou isolates was performed on the Illumina HiSeq X Ten platform with 2 × 150-bp paired-end reads. Raw reads were trimmed and quality filtered using fastp v0.23.2 [14], followed by de novo assembly with SPAdes v3.14.1 [15]. A total of 2,051 public ST2 B. pertussis genomes retrieved from the NCBI GenBank database in July 2025 were dereplicated with Assembly Dereplicator v0.3.1 (https://github.com/rrwick/Assembly-Dereplicator) using a Mash distance threshold of 0.00001. Because Mash distance roughly corresponds to one minus average nucleotide identity, this threshold was used to remove nearly identical public assemblies. When multiple assemblies were assigned to the same dereplication cluster, the assembly with the highest N50 was retained by Assembly Dereplicator as the representative genome. After dereplication and metadata curation, 534 public genomes were retained for downstream comparative analyses (1942–2024; Asia, Europe, Oceania, the Americas, and Africa). Public genomes were not restricted to complete circular assemblies; instead, assemblies with available collection year and geographic metadata were included to preserve broad coverage of the global ST2 population.

In silico antigen typing, BPagST assignment, and screening for macrolide resistance-associated 23S rRNA mutations were performed using pertpipe v1.0.0 (https://github.com/cidm-ph/pertpipe). Genome annotation and pseudogene identification were carried out using DFAST v1.2.18 [16]. Transposase- and recombinase-related entries were excluded from the summary of candidate lineage-stable pseudogene events. Functional annotation was further supplemented using eggNOG-mapper for COG assignment [17] and InterProScan for GO annotation [18].

Phylogenetic and evolutionary analyses

Phylogenetic analyses were performed for the local Wenzhou collection, the China-only ST2 dataset, and the global ST2 dataset. Core-genome SNP alignments were generated against the B. pertussis Tohama I reference genome (GenBank accession no. NC_002929.2) using a reference-based SNP workflow implemented in Snippy (https://github.com/tseemann/snippy). Recombinant regions were identified and excluded with Gubbins v2.4.1 [19], and maximum-likelihood phylogenies were inferred with RAxML v8.2.12 [20] under the GTRGAMMA model with 1,000 bootstrap replicates. To independently evaluate the robustness of lineage assignment, a gene-by-gene allelic analysis was performed using chewBBACA v3.5.4 (https://github.com/B-UMMI/chewBBACA). Core loci detected in ≥ 95% of isolates were retained, and a cgMLST-based neighbor-joining tree was reconstructed from pairwise allelic distances using SeqSphere + software (Ridom GmbH, Münster, Germany). Pairwise core-genome SNP distances were calculated from the same SNP datasets and used to identify closely related isolate pairs; isolate pairs differing by ≤ 3 core-genome SNPs were considered compatible with recent clonal transmission, but transmission direction was not inferred from genomic distance alone. MST analysis based on pairwise SNP distances was performed using PHYLOViZ v2.0 [21] as a complementary distance-based visualization, whereas clade assignment was based on the maximum-likelihood core-genome phylogeny.

Temporal evolutionary analysis of the global ST2 population was conducted using BactDating v1.1 [22]. The recombination-filtered global phylogeny and the corresponding isolation year metadata were used as input, and the mixedcarc model was applied for dating analysis. Temporal signal was initially assessed by root-to-tip regression, and convergence of the Markov chain Monte Carlo runs was evaluated from the posterior samples. Ancestral geographic reconstruction was performed using RASP v4.2 [23]. For this analysis, isolates were grouped into seven discrete geographic states: China, other Asian countries, Europe, Oceania, the United States, other countries in the Americas, and Africa.

Clade-specific SNPs were identified by lineage-based association screening using Scoary v1.6.16 [24] on the annotated global ST2 variant dataset. Only SNPs meeting the predefined filtering criteria used in the analysis pipeline, including an odds ratio of > 1, sensitivity of > 50%, specificity of > 50%, and Bonferroni-adjusted P value of < 1e-10, were retained.

Analysis of mobile genetic elements

Putative prophages were predicted using Phigaro v2.4.0 [25]. Genomic islands were identified with IslandViewer 4 [26], and insertion sequences (ISs) were predicted using ISEScan v1.7.3 [27]. For downstream IS analyses, detected insertion events were classified according to their genomic context as intragenic, intergenic upstream of one gene, intergenic upstream of two genes, intergenic with no upstream target, or other. Intragenic events were defined as insertions located within an annotated coding sequence. Intergenic upstream-of-one-gene events were located in an intergenic region upstream of one adjacent gene, whereas upstream-of-two-genes events were located between two divergently oriented adjacent genes and were upstream of both. Intergenic events with no upstream target were located in the intergenic region between two adjacent genes transcribed toward each other, on the downstream (3′) side of both genes and therefore not upstream of either annotated coding sequence under this promoter-focused classification scheme. These classifications were used to summarize lineage-level differences in IS insertion patterns and to compare the functional categories of genes affected by different insertion contexts. Because these analyses were performed on draft assemblies, the resulting values were interpreted as IS elements detected by ISEScan rather than complete genomic IS copy numbers.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as medians with interquartile ranges, and categorical variables were summarized as counts and percentages. Pairwise comparisons of lineage-associated distributions, including prophage counts, prophage lengths, genomic island counts, IS burdens, and pseudogene counts, were performed using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests as appropriate. Fisher’s exact tests were used for enrichment analyses of categorical features, including context-specific and lineage-associated functional categories. Multiple-testing correction was performed with the Benjamini–Hochberg method when applicable. For the exploratory comparison of clinical characteristics between Wenzhou clade I and clade II isolates, disease severity distribution was compared using the Fisher-Freeman-Halton exact test, whereas lymphocyte percentage, hospital stay duration, and age distribution were compared using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests. All statistical analyses and figure generation were performed in R, and a two-sided P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant unless otherwise specified.

Results

Clinical characteristics and local genomic features of B. pertussis isolates in Wenzhou, China

Between January and December 2024, a total of 46 non-duplicate B. pertussis isolates were collected from hospitalized children at a large tertiary teaching hospital in Wenzhou, Zhejiang Province, China. Most patients were young infants, with a median age of 3.2 months (IQR, 2.8–7.8 months), and 69.6% (32/46) were aged ≤ 6 months. A documented contact history was available in 60.9% (28/46) of cases, whereas vaccination history was recorded for only 50.0% (23/46). The isolates were recovered mainly from the pediatric infectious diseases ward and pediatric respiratory medicine ward, accounting for 41.3% (19/46) and 37.0% (17/46), respectively. Clinically, most infections were classified as mild or moderate, including 47.8% (22/46) that were mild and 47.8% (22/46) that were moderate, whereas only 4.3% (2/46) were severe. Fever and paroxysmal cough were uncommon, occurring in 15.2% (7/46) and 19.6% (9/46) of patients, respectively, whereas nocturnal worsening, facial flushing, whooping, and vomiting were observed in 58.7% (27/46), 87.0% (40/46), 34.8% (16/46), and 41.3% (19/46) of patients, respectively. The median hospital stay was 9.0 days (IQR, 7.0–10.8 days), and the median white blood cell count and lymphocyte percentage were 18.05 × 109/L (IQR, 13.52–21.60 × 109/L) and 70.0% (IQR, 60.8–73.4%), respectively. The clinical information of these patients is provided in Table 1 and Table S2.

In silico MLST showed that all 46 isolates belonged to ST2, and core-genome phylogeny further separated them into two major clades (Fig. 1A). Clade I comprised 71.7% (33/46) of isolates and was almost exclusively assigned to BPagST37 (ptxP1/ptxC1/fim3-1/fhaB3), with prn1 as the predominant pertactin allele in 93.9% (31/33) of isolates. The remaining 13 isolates belonged to clade II and mainly carried BPagST4 (ptxP3/ptxC4/fim3-1/fhaB1); prn2, predicted PRN deficiency, and prn150 were identified in 84.6% (11/13), 7.7% (1/13), and 7.7% (1/13) of isolates, respectively. Pairwise SNP distances between the two local clades ranged from 75 to 104 SNPs, with a median of 89 and a mean of 88.9. By contrast, within-clade distances were lower, ranging from 3 to 61 SNPs in clade I (median, 30; mean, 31.3) and from 7 to 36 SNPs in clade II (median, 21.5; mean, 22.8). Isolates from different wards, including pediatric infectious diseases, pediatric respiratory medicine, neonatology, and several other pediatric wards, were intermingled across the phylogeny rather than forming strictly ward-specific clusters, suggesting possible cross-ward transmission within the hospital. Pairwise SNP analysis further identified one closely related pair, qm28 and qm47, both recovered from the pediatric infectious diseases ward and differing by only three core-genome SNPs, consistent with recent clonal transmission (Fig. 1B).

Antimicrobial susceptibility profiles were highly concordant with the phylogenetic structure (Fig. 1A). Overall, 73.9% (34/46) of isolates exhibited high-level resistance to erythromycin, azithromycin, clarithromycin, and clindamycin, and all resistant isolates carried the 23S rRNA A2047G mutation. By contrast, all isolates remained susceptible to ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, meropenem, and ceftriaxone. Only one isolate, qm25, showed an elevated trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole MIC (> 32 mg/L). All 33 clade I isolates were macrolide resistant, whereas 12 of the 13 clade II isolates were macrolide susceptible and retained wild-type 23S rRNA alleles. The remaining clade II isolate, qm54, carried prn150 together with the A2047G mutation and therefore represented the only macrolide-resistant isolate in the BPagST4 lineage recovered in this study.

Exploratory comparison of clinical characteristics between the two local clades did not identify significant differences in disease severity distribution (mild/moderate/severe: 17/15/1 vs 5/7/1, p = 0.641), lymphocyte percentage [clade I vs clade II, median 67.9% (IQR, 60.4–74.3%) vs 70.7% (IQR, 63.2–72.9%), p = 0.981], hospital stay duration [9.0 days (IQR, 7.0–11.0) vs 9.0 days (IQR, 7.0–10.0), p = 0.864], or age distribution [4.0 months (IQR, 2.7–8.0) vs 3.0 months (IQR, 2.9–4.0), p = 0.624]. These analyses should be considered exploratory because of the limited number of local isolates, particularly in clade II and severe cases.

Geographical dissemination and phylogenomic diversity of the global ST2 lineage

To investigate the geographical dissemination of the ST2 lineage, 534 de-duplicated public ST2 genomes were retrieved from NCBI and analyzed together with the 46 isolates from this study, yielding a final dataset of 580 genomes (Table S1). These genomes were sampled between 1942 and 2024 from 24 countries (Fig. 2A and Table S3). China contributed the largest number of isolates, accounting for 40.2% (233/580), followed by the United States at 14.8% (86/580), Australia at 6.4% (37/580), and New Zealand at 5.9% (34/580). Within China, ST2 isolates were identified across nine province-level regions, mainly Shanghai and Zhejiang, which accounted for 43.3% (101/233) and 42.9% (100/233), respectively (Fig. 2B and Table S1). Macrolide resistance-associated 23S rRNA mutations were detected in 70.0% (163/233) of Chinese isolates but in only 0.6% (2/347) of non-Chinese isolates (Fig. 2C), indicating that the macrolide-resistant ST2 population was largely restricted to China.

The most recent common ancestor of the global ST2 lineage was dated to 1910.14 (95% highest posterior density [HPD], 1900.57–1918.66) (Figure S1). Core-genome phylogeny resolved the global ST2 population into two major clades, clade I and clade II, comprising 39.5% (229/580) and 60.5% (351/580) of isolates, respectively (Fig. 3). Clade II was estimated to have diverged from the clade I lineage around 1962.21 (95% HPD, 1957.03–1967.11). At the clade level, the two lineages showed distinct vaccine-antigen genotypes: clade I was almost entirely composed of ptxP1 isolates [98.7% (226/229)] and was enriched for BPagST8, BPagST37, and BPagST3, whereas clade II was almost entirely composed of ptxP3 isolates [98.0% (344/351)] and was dominated by BPagST4 and BPagST9, accounting for 69.2% (243/351) and 21.7% (76/351), respectively. Clade I mainly carried fim3-1 together with prn1 or predicted PRN-deficient alleles, whereas clade II predominantly carried fim3-1 or fim3-2 together with predicted PRN-deficient, prn150, or prn2 alleles.

Three major subclades were identified within the global phylogeny (Fig. 3, Figure S1 and Figure S2). Because no universally accepted SNP cutoff exists for ST2 subclade assignment, subclades were defined using a combined criterion of phylogenetic monophyly, bootstrap support > 90% for key nodes, and higher median inter-subclade than intra-subclade core-SNP divergence. Bootstrap support values for the key clade/subclade-defining nodes in the core-SNP phylogeny ranged from 92 to 100% (Fig. 3). The median within-subclade distances were 28, 46, and 8 SNPs for subclades I, II, and III, respectively, whereas the median between-subclade distances ranged from 54 to 78 SNPs. To further evaluate the robustness of lineage assignment, we additionally reconstructed a cgMLST-based neighbor-joining tree using core loci detected in ≥ 95% of isolates. This independent allelic tree was highly concordant with the core-SNP maximum-likelihood phylogeny and recovered the two-clade and three-subclade structure of the core-SNP tree (Figure S2).

Within this global framework, the 33 Wenzhou clade I isolates were assigned to subclade I, whereas the 13 Wenzhou clade II isolates belonged to global clade II; among the latter, 12 were located outside the three major subclades and one was assigned to subclade III. Subclade I, within clade I, emerged around 1993.93 (95% HPD, 1986.40–2000.15), comprised 10.5% (61/580) of isolates, and consisted exclusively of Chinese strains. It was almost uniformly represented by the BPagST37 (ptxP1/ptxC1/fim3-1/fhaB3) genotype, accounting for 98.4% (60/61) of isolates, with prn1 and 23S rRNA mutations each detected in 96.7% (59/61) of isolates. Subclade II, within clade II, emerged around 1972.95 (95% HPD, 1968.95–1976.54), comprised 15.9% (92/580) of isolates, and showed a geographically widespread distribution across multiple continents: the United States contributed the largest number of isolates in this subclade, accounting for 27.2% (25/92), whereas European isolates collectively accounted for 25.0% (23/92). This lineage was predominantly associated with the BPagST9 (ptxP3/ptxC4/fim3-2/fhaB1), which accounted for 82.6% (76/92) of isolates; prn2 and predicted PRN-deficient variants predominated, and no 23S rRNA mutation was detected. Subclade III, also within clade II, emerged around 2000.92 (95% HPD, 1997.96–2003.75), comprised 24.5% (142/580) of isolates, and was enriched in Chinese isolates. Nearly all isolates in this subclade belonged to the BPagST4 (ptxP3/ptxC4/fim3-1/fhaB1) genotype (99.3%, 141/142), with prn150 present in 77.5% (110/142) of isolates and 23S rRNA mutations in 73.9% (105/142). Subclade III also included one isolate from Australia, indicating that this lineage was not geographically restricted to China. This isolate, CIDM-MRBP01 (BioSample accession no. SAMN44480251), was collected in 2024 and carried the 23S rRNA A2047G mutation and the BPagST4 (ptxP3/ptxC4/fim3-1/fhaB1) genotype; however, it was predicted to be PRN-deficient rather than carrying prn150.

Ancestral state reconstruction supported Europe as the most probable origin for the clade I root node, with Africa as the second most probable region (posterior probabilities [PPs], 0.68 and 0.21, respectively). Clade II and subclade II were inferred to have originated in Europe, with PPs of 0.984 and 0.96, respectively. By contrast, subclade I and subclade III were inferred to have originated in China, with PPs of 0.95 and 0.99, respectively (Figure S3). Given the predominance of Chinese isolates in the global ST2 dataset, we further examined their phylogenetic distribution and inferred geographic dissemination pattern (Figure S4). In total, 86.7% (202/233) of Chinese isolates were located in subclade I or subclade III, whereas the remaining 13.3% (31/233) were scattered across other parts of the phylogeny, consistent with multiple independent introductions of ST2 into China. Within the China-enriched subclades, isolates from different Chinese provinces were intermingled across the phylogeny, consistent with interprovincial dissemination (Fig. 3 and Figure S4).

Clade-specific distribution of MGEs

To compare clade-associated variation in MGEs, we examined putative prophages, total prophage length, genomic islands, and insertion sequences (ISs) across the ST2 lineages (Fig. 4). The number of putative prophages per genome was similar among clade I, clade II, and the three major subclades, with a median of one prophage per genome in each group (Fig. 4A). By contrast, total prophage length differed among the three major subclades: subclade II carried the longest total prophage sequence per genome, with a median of 14.99 kb, which was significantly greater than that in subclade I (10.43 kb; p = 1.30e-5) and subclade III (10.43 kb; p = 4.53e-10), whereas no significant difference was observed between subclade I and subclade III (Fig. 4B). Additionally, among the 708 putative prophages identified in the 580-genome dataset, CD-HIT clustering yielded 35 prophage clusters, including seven singletons (Table S4). Four recurrent clusters predominated: prophage-cluster 13 (190 occurrences), prophage-cluster 5 (158), prophage-cluster 1 (108), and prophage-cluster 8 (86). Prophage-cluster 13 was most prevalent in subclade I and subclade III, being detected in 67.2% (41/61) and 64.1% (91/142) of isolates, respectively, whereas prophage-cluster 5 was most common in subclade II, where it was present in 41.3% (38/92) of isolates (Table S4).

The number of genomic islands also differed among the three major subclades (Fig. 4C). Subclade II carried more genomic islands than subclade I and subclade III, with a median of 15.5 per genome versus 10 in both subclade I and subclade III (p = 4.74e-9 and p = 3.73e-13, respectively). In contrast, IS abundance followed a different pattern. Subclade I carried the highest IS burden, with a median of 63 copies per genome, significantly exceeding that in subclade II (median 8; p = 0.029) and subclade III (median 12; p = 6.45e-5) (Fig. 4D). At the broader clade level, the median IS copy number was higher in clade II than in clade I (12 vs 8; p = 8.14e-7), reflecting the relatively low IS burden among clade I isolates outside subclade I. The elevated number of detected IS elements in subclade I coincided with its earlier estimated emergence and longer inferred circulation in China compared with subclade III, and may also be compatible with repeated population bottlenecks that allowed IS expansion; however, this pattern should be interpreted cautiously because IS detection was based on short-read draft assemblies.

IS composition and insertion-context profiles

Given the recognized importance of IS expansion in B. pertussis genome evolution, IS abundance and insertion context were examined in greater detail (Fig. 4E, Figure S5, and Table S5). IS481 was by far the predominant IS family, accounting for 86.2% of all IS-associated events (Fig. 4E). Across the major IS families, associated genes showed distinct functional biases (Figure S5A). Genes linked to IS21 and ISL3 were assigned predominantly to replication, recombination and repair, whereas IS3-associated genes were mainly involved in carbohydrate transport and metabolism. By contrast, the more abundant IS110 and IS481 families were associated with a broader range of functions, particularly amino acid transport and metabolism, cell wall/membrane biogenesis, and inorganic ion transport and metabolism.

Based on the predefined insertion-context categories (see Figure S5 legend), 35.0% of events were classified as intergenic (upstream of one gene), 27.0% as intergenic (no upstream target), 25.2% as intragenic, 8.8% as intergenic (upstream of two genes), and 4.0% as other. Across all major clades and subclades, intergenic insertions were more frequent than intragenic insertions, accounting for 64.4% and 73.0% of IS-associated events in clade I and clade II, respectively, and 60.2%, 74.4%, and 68.5% in subclades I, II, and III, respectively (Figure S5B). Furthermore, IS-associated events involved 490 genes, including 87 genes affected by both intragenic and upstream insertions. Frequently affected loci included genes involved in replication or repair and protein export, such as dnaA, uvrD, and tatC (Table S5).

Functional annotation by insertion context further showed that genes affected by intragenic insertions were enriched in replication, recombination and repair, intracellular trafficking/secretion, and energy production/conversion categories, whereas genes affected by upstream insertions were more often associated with amino acid transport and metabolism, cell wall/membrane biogenesis, inorganic ion transport and metabolism, and transcription-related functions (Figure S4C).

Pseudogene patterns across global ST2 clades

We next compared pseudogene burden across the global ST2 population, as pseudogene accumulation is another prominent feature of genome reduction in B. pertussis (Figure S6). Genome-wide analysis identified 268 genes showing lineage-stable pseudogenization (≥ 50% of strains in any clade) (Table S6). The overall pseudogene burden was similar between clade I and clade II, with medians of 261 and 264 pseudogenes per genome, respectively. At the subclade level, however, subclade I carried the highest pseudogene burden, with a median of 277 pseudogenes per genome, compared with medians of 260 and 264 in subclade II and subclade III, respectively (both p < 0.001). Subclade I-enriched stable pseudogene events were mainly assigned to transcription, energy production/conversion, and inorganic ion transport and metabolism COG categories, suggesting that the increased pseudogene burden involved regulatory and metabolic functions.

The composition of lineage-stable pseudogene events also differed between the two major clades (Table S6). Several events were enriched in clade I, including the inactivation of group_160, which was present in all clade I isolates but absent from clade II; group_1343 and group_1541, which were present in 83.4% (191/229) and 79.9% (183/229) of clade I isolates, respectively, but absent from clade II. By contrast, clade II showed much higher frequencies of pseudogenization in group_1461, group_446, ureF, and hutG, which were disrupted in 86.3% (303/351), 76.9% (270/351), 75.8% (266/351), and 79.5% (279/351) of clade II isolates, respectively, compared with 2.6% (6/229), 4.4% (10/229), 43.7% (100/229), and 48.9% (112/229) of clade I isolates.

Clade-specific SNPs across major ST2 lineages

To gain insight into the genetic changes differentiating the major ST2 clades, we searched for SNPs specifically associated with each phylogenetic group (Figure S7 and Table S7). A total of 1, 32, 40, 2, and 30 clade-specific SNPs were identified for clade I, clade II, subclade I, subclade II, and subclade III, respectively. The single clade I-specific SNP was a missense mutation in a c-type cytochrome gene, whereas clade II harbored 32 lineage-associated SNPs, including representative mutations in genes related to transport, surface-associated proteins, and redox-associated enzymes. At the subclade level, subclade I contained several lineage-defining nonsynonymous or truncating SNPs in genes involved in transport and envelope biogenesis, including those encoding an amino acid ABC transporter substrate-binding protein, TamB, a DMT family transporter carrying a stop-gain mutation, a GAF domain-containing protein, and BamA. By contrast, only two subclade II-specific SNPs were identified, affecting a fimbrial protein gene and prpB. In subclade III, representative nonsynonymous or truncating SNPs affected a ZIP family metal transporter, SphB1, an NAD(P)H-dependent oxidoreductase, and a flavin-containing monooxygenase carrying a stop-gain mutation.

Discussion

Understanding the evolutionary dynamics and dissemination patterns of contemporary B. pertussis lineages is essential for interpreting the current pertussis resurgence and guiding surveillance [28, 29]. Although all 46 Wenzhou isolates collected in this study belonged to ST2, they did not represent a homogeneous local population. Instead, the Wenzhou isolates comprised two phylogenetically distinct lineages that mapped differently onto the global ST2 framework: a dominant macrolide-resistant ptxP1-associated lineage assigned to subclade I and a smaller ptxP3-associated lineage belonging to global clade II, with one isolate placed in subclade III. This local contribution is important because the Wenzhou collection directly captured a contemporary eastern-China ST2 population in which an older ptxP1/A2047G-resistant background remained dominant while a ptxP3-associated lineage, including one erythromycin-resistant prn150 isolate, was also present. By comparison, the expanded global ST2 collection showed clear lineage stratification, broad geographic dissemination, and marked heterogeneity in IS burden, pseudogene accumulation, and lineage-defining SNPs. Together, these findings suggest that contemporary ST2 evolution is shaped not only by clonal expansion and geographic spread, but also by lineage-specific genome remodeling superimposed on a highly conserved genomic backbone.

Our data further showed that the Wenzhou ST2 population did not fully mirror the genotype shifts described elsewhere in China. Recent genomic studies from several Chinese cities have documented expansion of ptxP3-associated lineages, often accompanied by prn150, prn2, or predicted PRN-deficient variants, together with increasing macrolide resistance driven by the 23S rRNA A2047G mutation [30–34]. In Guangzhou, only three isolates collected between 2020 and 2024 retained ptxP1, whereas ptxP3/prn150 became dominant by 2024 [30]. However, differences in sampling years and patient populations (Guangzhou isolates included older children) may partly explain the discordance. Likewise, in Beijing, ptxP3/prn2 replaced the previously prevalent ptxP1/prn1 background and became the dominant genotype among isolates collected between 2020 and 2023 [31]. Similar shifts were also reported in Tianjin, Shanghai, and Yiwu [32–34]. By contrast, the Wenzhou collection remained dominated by the BPagST37/ptxP1/prn1 lineage, whereas the local BPagST4/ptxP3 component was smaller and largely macrolide susceptible. These observations suggest that lineage replacement of ST2 in China is underway but has not proceeded in a temporally synchronized or geographically uniform manner.

This regional discordance is also consistent with the broader historical context in China. Earlier nationwide studies showed that erythromycin-resistant B. pertussis in mainland China was largely linked to the ptxP1/prn1 background and exhibited limited genomic variation [35–37], in contrast to the ptxP3/prn2 or PRN-deficient lineages that predominated in many acellular-vaccine countries [28, 38, 39]. The predominance of a ptxP1-associated lineage in Wenzhou therefore likely reflects continued local circulation of an older Chinese epidemic background, whereas the co-circulation of a BPagST4/ptxP3 clade suggests that replacement by newer ptxP3-associated lineages is underway but remains incomplete. Notably, the detection of one erythromycin-resistant prn150 isolate in Wenzhou indicates that prn150 should not be interpreted as a marker of a single evolutionary trajectory. Recent studies have likewise shown that prn150 can occur within rapidly expanding macrolide-resistant ptxP3 lineages in China and beyond [40, 41]. Taken together, these observations suggest that antigenic divergence, lineage turnover, and macrolide resistance are interacting processes rather than steps in a simple sequential replacement model.

Our local phylogeny also suggested that the patterns observed among hospitalized cases may reflect broader transmission networks rather than repeated independent introductions confined to individual wards. Isolates from the pediatric infectious diseases, pediatric respiratory medicine, neonatology, and several other pediatric wards were intermingled across the phylogeny, and one closely related pair recovered from the pediatric infectious diseases ward differed by only three core-genome SNPs. Although these observations are insufficient to define a nosocomial outbreak, they are compatible with recent transmission and suggest that ward-level clustering should be interpreted within a broader transmission context spanning the hospital, households, and the community. This interpretation is epidemiologically plausible because most patients in our cohort were very young infants (median age, 3.2 months; 69.6% aged ≤ 6 months), a population that is incompletely vaccinated and therefore particularly dependent on cocooning and community-level protection [42]. In this context, the hospital may function less as the sole site of transmission than as one node within a larger pediatric transmission network.

Our phylogenomic analysis further showed that the global ST2 population has diversified into two major clades and three major subclades, highlighting substantial heterogeneity within a lineage that would appear uniform by classical MLST. A minority of Chinese isolates were scattered outside the two China-enriched subclades, supporting multiple independent introductions of ST2 into China followed by local expansion rather than a single ancestral introduction with subsequent nationwide spread. Within subclades I and III, isolates from different Chinese provinces were intermingled across the phylogeny, consistent with frequent interprovincial dissemination. By contrast, subclade II showed a geographically widespread distribution, and the presence of one Australian isolate within the China-enriched subclade III further indicated that at least some ST2 lineages are not geographically restricted. These patterns are consistent with recent genomic studies showing long-distance dissemination and international mixing of B. pertussis lineages [41, 43]. At the same time, B. pertussis is a low-SNP-density pathogen, and phylogenetic proximity alone cannot define transmission direction or epidemiologic linkage. Previous Chinese genomic work also reported that the Chinese ptxP1-ptxA1-fhaB3 population had an estimated mutation rate approximately fivefold higher than that of the global ptxP1-ptxA1 population, providing additional context for the larger SNP distances observed among Chinese macrolide-resistant backgrounds [36]. In our local dataset, the 3-SNP qm28-qm47 pair was compatible with recent clonal transmission, whereas the broader 75–104 SNP separation between the two Wenzhou clades supported co-circulation of distinct ST2 lineages rather than a single outbreak. These observations emphasize that genomic distance should be interpreted together with sampling density, time, ward information, and epidemiologic metadata.

The differences among the three major subclades also provide a useful framework for interpreting Chinese ST2 evolution in a broader global context. Subclade II, which was dominated by BPagST9 (ptxP3/ptxC4/fim3-2/fhaB1) and enriched for prn2 or predicted PRN-deficient variants, resembled the globally disseminated ptxP3-associated populations that expanded in many acellular-vaccine countries under vaccine-driven selection and were frequently associated with prn2 or PRN deficiency [28, 38, 39]. By comparison, subclades I and III were strongly enriched in Chinese isolates and both showed high frequencies of macrolide resistance, although on distinct antigenic backgrounds, with subclade III additionally characterized by prn150. Recent genomic surveillance studies indicate that ptxP3 macrolide-resistant lineages carrying prn150 are now spreading rapidly in China and have begun to appear outside China [40, 41, 43]. Our ST2 data are consistent with this broader pattern, but also indicate that Chinese ST2 evolution cannot be explained by a single recent sweep, because the older ptxP1-associated resistant background remains prominent alongside the expanding ptxP3-associated resistant lineage. From a disease-control perspective, resolving ST2 at whole-genome scale is useful because it distinguishes persistence of older ptxP1/A2047G-resistant backgrounds from the emergence of ptxP3/prn150-resistant lineages, which has implications for regional surveillance, empirical treatment decisions, and vaccine-antigen monitoring. Accordingly, the current Chinese ST2 population appears to reflect coexistence of multiple successful resistant trajectories superimposed on the broader global trend toward vaccine-antigen divergence. Differences between China and many Western countries may also be shaped, at least in part, by distinct vaccine histories and selective environments; recent surveillance from France, for example, further suggests that antigenic trajectories in acellular-vaccine settings remain dynamic rather than uniform [39, 44, 45].

The IS analysis further clarified the genomic processes underlying lineage diversification within ST2. Expansion of insertion sequences, particularly IS481, is a defining feature of B. pertussis genome evolution and has long been associated with host restriction, gene loss, and chromosomal rearrangement [46–48]. Previous comparative genomic studies have shown that adaptation of B. pertussis involved extensive loss and inactivation of ancestral functions, whereas long-read analyses further demonstrated that repeated IS481 copies can mediate large-scale structural rearrangements [46–48]. In our dataset, IS481 overwhelmingly predominated, and intergenic insertions were consistently more frequent than intragenic events across all major clades and subclades. This pattern suggests that the evolutionary consequences of IS expansion are unlikely to be limited to direct gene disruption alone. Insertions in upstream or intergenic regions may alter local transcriptional contexts and facilitate subsequent structural rearrangements, thereby contributing to lineage differentiation even in the absence of extensive point mutation [48, 49]. Notably, intergenic insertions with no upstream target, although not promoter-upstream events in our classification scheme, may still influence neighboring-gene expression through transcript termination or spacing effects. Quantitative reverse-transcription PCR of genes flanking recurrent intergenic IS481 insertions (e.g., dacB, ptxP) could determine whether these insertions consistently reduce transcript levels, providing a mechanism for lineage-specific gene down-regulation. In this context, the markedly higher IS burden of subclade I may reflect more sustained or more lineage-specific IS-mediated genome remodeling, which in turn may have contributed to its distinct phylogenetic position and long-term association with the Chinese macrolide-resistant ptxP1 background.

Pseudogene accumulation and lineage-specific SNPs provide convergent support for this interpretation. Genome reduction and pseudogenization are well-recognized features of the evolution of B. pertussis from a broader-host-range ancestor, and the classical view that host adaptation in the bordetellae has been driven largely by loss rather than gain of function remains highly relevant [46, 47]. In our study, overall pseudogene burdens were similar between the two major clades, but subclade I showed the highest burden at the subclade level, suggesting that functional streamlining has proceeded unevenly among successful ST2 lineages. The clade-enriched stable pseudogene events and lineage-defining SNPs further suggest that different ST2 clades have followed partially distinct adaptive trajectories. Many subclade-specific nonsynonymous or truncating SNPs affected genes related to transport, envelope biogenesis, secretion, metal homeostasis, or surface-associated functions, all plausible targets of adaptation in a pathogen exposed to vaccine-induced immunity, antimicrobial pressure, and repeated host-to-host bottlenecks. Together, these observations support the view that B. pertussis continues to diversify through cumulative sequence change, selective gene inactivation, and IS-associated remodeling, although the functional consequences of these alterations remain inferential and will require experimental validation.

This study has several limitations. First, the Wenzhou isolates were collected at a single tertiary hospital over a single year and may therefore not fully represent the longer-term population structure of B. pertussis in Wenzhou or Zhejiang Province. Second, the global ST2 dataset was assembled from publicly available genomes and was therefore subject to geographic and temporal sampling bias, which may have influenced phylogeographic inference, ancestral-state reconstruction, divergence-time estimates, and the apparent prevalence of individual lineages. Although Chinese isolates were intentionally retained to preserve the genomic diversity of the China-enriched ST2 population after dereplication, this enrichment may also amplify China-associated phylogeographic signals. Therefore, China-enriched subclades and inferred geographic origins should be interpreted as patterns observed within currently available genomes rather than direct estimates of global lineage prevalence or definitive source attribution. Public genomes were also generated using heterogeneous sequencing and assembly strategies, and we did not restrict inclusion to complete circular assemblies because doing so would have greatly reduced geographic and temporal coverage. Consequently, differences in sequencing platform, assembly quality, and genome completeness may influence detected gene content, MGE profiles, IS detection, and lineage-level comparisons. Third, our analyses of insertion sequences and pseudogenes relied primarily on short-read draft assemblies. Because repetitive elements are intrinsically difficult to resolve in fragmented genomes, identical or near-identical IS copies may be collapsed and missed, whereas contig breaks within IS elements or misassembly may also artificially increase apparent counts or obscure exact insertion contexts. Therefore, estimates of IS abundance, insertion location, and lineage-specific IS patterns should be interpreted as draft-assembly-based estimates rather than complete IS copy-number measurements. For the same reason, we did not further classify pseudogene events according to whether they were directly caused by IS insertion, because undetected or misassembled IS elements could lead to misclassification. Finally, the biological significance of lineage-associated pseudogenes and SNPs was inferred from comparative genomics alone and was not functionally tested.

In conclusion, our data show that the contemporary ST2 population in Wenzhou retains marked local heterogeneity and has not yet fully converged toward the ptxP3-dominated pattern recently reported from several other Chinese cities. The global ST2 phylogeny further indicates long-term lineage diversification, interprovincial and international dissemination, and repeated introductions into China. Together, the lineage-associated differences in IS burden, pseudogene accumulation, and lineage-specific SNPs suggest that ongoing ST2 evolution is driven less by large-scale gene acquisition than by progressive remodeling of an already streamlined genome. These findings underscore the need for sustained genomic surveillance across broader geographic scales, ideally integrated with long-read sequencing and richer epidemiologic metadata, to track the future trajectory of this lineage and to clarify how vaccine pressure and antimicrobial selection jointly shape the evolution of B. pertussis.

Computed ECD spectral data for over 10,000 chiral organic small molecules
Authors: Da Long, Zhihao Li, Xin Xu, Chengchun Liu, Hui Zhang, Xiaoyu Cao, Liulin Yang, Xinchang Wang, Fanyang Mo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractDetermining the absolute configuration of chiral molecules is of fundamental importance in the fields of natural products chemistry, asymmetric catalysis, and pharmaceutical development. A widely adopted approach involves the comparison of experimental and theoretical electronic circular dichroism (ECD) spectra, which has proven to be a reliable method for absolute configuration assignment. However, the generation of theoretical ECD spectra via time-dependent density functional theory (TD-DFT) remains the rate-limiting step in this workflow, making its acceleration both essential and challenging. Although recent advances in deep learning offer promising strategies for establishing structure-spectrum relationships and expediting theoretical spectrum prediction, the lack of standardized and comprehensive ECD spectral datasets continues to hinder progress. This study presents the Chiral Molecular Circular Dichroism Spectral (CMCDS) dataset, a systematically structural benchmark dataset that addresses the fragmentation of existing ECD data. Characterized by high standardization, scalability, and broad molecular diversity, CMCDS facilitates deep learning applications in ECD analysis and fosters data-driven discovery of chiral molecules.Subject terms: Computational chemistry, Cheminformatics

Background & Summary

Chirality, a geometric property describing structures incapable of superimposition onto their mirror images via rotation or translation1, is ubiquitous in chemical and biological molecular systems2,3. Chiral molecular architectures play essential roles in a variety of functional materials, including liquid crystal displays4 and nonlinear optical systems5. Owing to the differences in structure-property relationships between enantiomers, the determination of the absolute configuration has become a crucial part of chiral studies6–9.

The absolute configuration of small chiral molecules can be determined using various experimental and computational techniques, such as X-ray crystallography and chiroptical spectroscopy. X-ray crystallography, while offering unambiguous structural information, requires high-quality single crystals and is reliable only when the molecular structure contains atoms heavier than phosphorus, due to the need for significant anomalous dispersion effects. In cases where such heavy atoms are absent or crystallization is unsuccessful, chiroptical spectroscopies provide a powerful alternative. Techniques such as optical rotation (OR), vibrational circular dichroism (VCD), and electronic circular dichroism (ECD) are widely employed for their sensitivity to stereochemistry in solution. Among these, ECD is the most accepted and reliable method, often coupled with time-dependent density functional theory (TD-DFT) calculations to establish structure-spectrum correlations10,11.

The simulation of ECD spectra is computationally demanding, and their cost increases significantly with increasing complexity of chiral structures12. This has gradually emerged as a non-negligible obstacle to the further development of chiral research, making methodological innovations in simulation techniques increasingly imperative. A possible solution to accelerate the acquisition of ECD spectra is deep-learning method. As a data-driven methodology, deep learning extracts patterns from massive datasets through complex neural networks, achieving a paradigm shift from empirical approaches to computational intelligence in diverse fields including protein folding prediction13, targeted drug design14, and organic synthesis pathway optimization15. Based on the deep neural network framework, with the help of 130,000 organic molecules (QM9S dataset) and dynamic configuration data (QM7-X subset) for model training and testing, efficient and high-precision prediction of multi-type organic molecular spectra such as infrared, Raman, ultraviolet-visible and nuclear magnetic resonance can be achieved16. By establishing molecular structure-spectrum correlations through deep learning approaches that bypass wavefunction analysis, this predictive modelling strategy represents a transformative way to innovate ECD simulations.

Critical elements, including high-quality datasets, algorithmic selection and feature engineering, are essential for machine learning predictions. The development of high quality ECD spectral databases required for training sets of large-scale chiral molecules remains limited by the unstructured nature of existing experimental data and the high computational cost of theoretical simulations, posing significant challenges for machine learning applications. To address the scarcity of high-quality chiral ECD spectral datasets for chiral molecule research, this study leverages the previously established Chiral Molecule Retention Time (CMRT) dataset17. Through implementation of a systematic screening protocol, we have identified 11,095 monochiral molecular entities meeting stringent stereochemical purity criteria. Based on the SMILES structural descriptor, high-throughput calculations were performed: firstly, the molecular conformation was globally optimized by the B3LYP/6-31G(d) method18, and then the CAM-B3LYP/6-31G(d) method19 was used to calculate the first 20 excited state transition parameters, and the theoretical ECD spectrum was generated by Gaussian broadening. After data cleaning, a high-fidelity chiral molecular circular dichroism spectral (CMCDS) database containing 10,887 molecules was successfully constructed20. The CMCDS dataset has the characteristics of standardization, strong scalability, and rich variety of chiral compounds, which can significantly improve ECD spectral predictions, and its systematic nature can effectively solve the problem of fragmentation of existing ECD data and provides new perspectives for advancing chiral research.

To promote transparency and reproducibility, we will publicly release all intermediate data associated with the CMCDS dataset, including the curated set of chiral molecules, geometry optimization files, and supporting code. These resources will facilitate in-depth data mining and further exploration of the structure-property relationships in chiral systems. The release of CMCDS not only supports machine-learning applications in chiral molecular research, but also serves as a foundational resource for large-scale, data-driven studies in other spectroscopy Fig. 1.

Overview of the CMCDS Dataset. Chirality is widely present in various molecular structures, involving fields such as asymmetric catalysis6,7, chiral drug development8, and chiral inducement9. Methods

This section describes the methodological framework for generating ECD spectra through large-scale automated workflows. The workflow consists of three essential stages: (1) Molecular Coordinates Generation, (2) High-Accuracy Molecular Structure Acquisition and Excited State Simulation and (3) ECD Spectra Generation.

Molecular Coordinates Generation

For the samples selection, SMILE descriptors of 11,095 organic molecules with mono-chiral center were extracted from the CMRT database. The 3D Cartesian coordinates of each molecule were generated by topologically dimensional expansion of the ASCII strings by RDKit packages21. The specific dimensional expansion method is Experimental-Torsion Basic Knowledge Distance Geometry (ETKDG), which based on empirical structural rules of organic molecular. The initial structures coordinates were stored in MDL Molfile format.

High-Accuracy Molecular Structure Acquisition and Excited State Simulation

To accurately evaluate the ECD spectrum for each molecule, high-accuracy structure acquisition and excited state calculations were performed on Gaussian 16 A03 packages22 using geometry optimization and excited state calculation. The geometry optimization were carried out on B3LYP/6-31G(d) level. Subsequently, the excited state calculations were performed at CAM-B3LYP/6-31G(d) level using time-dependent density functional theory (TD-DFT) with 20 excited states. In this stages, four essential results were obtained for further research. The high-accuracy structures from geometry optimization provide accurate structural information for subsequent spectral property predictions. The excited states and rotatory strengths from excited state calculations detailed reflect the circular dichroism contributions of different conformations.

Due to limited high-performance computing resources, this study adopted a rigid-conformation approximation strategy. Specifically, the default initial molecular conformations generated from SMILES-to-MOL conversion were directly utilized without systematic conformational searches, and their ECD spectra were computed accordingly. From an initial set of 11,095 calculation tasks, 10,887 ECD spectra that satisfied the established quality criteria were selected. Structures with unconverged geometry optimizations or unsuccessful excited-state calculations were excluded from the final dataset. Since the ECD spectra of mirror-image enantiomers are symmetric about the x-axis (wavelength axis), the enantiomeric spectra of 10,887 molecules were generated by sign inversion of their original spectra. This process yielded a comprehensive dataset containing ECD spectra of 21774 molecules in total.

Data Records

All data files associated with the creation of the CMCDS dataset have been publicly archived on Figshare20. This repository includes SMILES molecular descriptors, MOL-format files containing initial molecular structures, Gaussian input files for geometry optimizations, Gaussian output files resulting from these structural optimizations, Gaussian input files for ECD calculations, and corresponding Gaussian output files from ECD spectral simulations. Collectively, these resources provide a transparent, reproducible basis for computational studies involving conformational analyses, geometry optimization, and predictions of chiroptical properties.

As shown in Table 1, the CMCDS dataset compiles excitation energies and rotatory strengths extracted from Gaussian output files of ECD calculations, and generates continuous theoretical ECD spectra via Gaussian broadening. The dataset encompasses data for 10,887 chiral molecules, including molecular identifiers, SMILES representations, excitation energies, and rotatory strengths for each electronic transition in all conformations, along with the corresponding computed ECD spectral plots.

To enhance clarity in the workflow of deriving averaged ECD spectra from chiral molecular SMILES, we have also provided a code repository. This repository demonstrates the end-to-end pipeline: starting from simplified molecular input structures (SMILES), generating three-dimensional molecular geometries, conducting DFT calculations to obtain key chiroptical parameters, and finally generating ECD spectra via Gaussian broadening functions. This workflow is designed to facilitate rapid application of the CMCDS dataset in analogous deep learning tasks, ensuring reproducibility and scalability in computational chiroptical studies.

Technical Validation

Data distribution

The CMCDS dataset exhibits well-defined distributions across molecular weight, functional groups, excitation energies, and chiroptical properties. As shown in Fig. 3, the CMCDS dataset analysis reveals that 68.2% of compounds fall within the molecular weight range of 200-400 g/mol, with merely 0.2% surpassing 800 g/mol. Statistical analysis of functional groups revealed that aromatic rings are present in 99% of the molecular structures, with ketone and ester groups accounting for 56.7% and 52.7%, respectively, whereas carboxylic acids constitute a mere 1.8%. In terms of electronic excitation properties, statistical evaluation of the average excitation energies based on the 20 lowest excited states indicates that 56.4% of the molecules have excitation energies clustered within the range of 6.0–7.0 eV (corresponding to a wavelength range of 177–207 nm). Furthermore, analysis of calculated parameters concerning chirality demonstrates that 86.4% of the molecules display a mean absolute rotatory strength ⟨∣R∣⟩ below 50 cgs units (1 cgs = 10−40 erg-esu-cm/Gauss), a parameter that effectively quantifies the chiral response strength (i.e., molecular chiroptical activity).

Construction of ECD Spectrum Prediction Model

Our group has constructed the ECDFormer model (based on the QFormer architecture) using the CMCDS dataset, achieving efficient generation of theoretical ECD spectra for chiral molecules through an innovative spectral properties decoupling strategy (joint peak attribute learning-prediction framework)23. The workflow involves: 1) embedding atomic topology, bond angles, and chiral descriptors of molecules into a geometric-enhanced graph neural network (Geo-GNN) to generate spatial-aware molecular representations; 2) extracting key peak attribute features (number, wavelength, Cotton effect symbol) via the QFormer encoder through a parallel prediction module; 3) synthesizing complete theoretical ECD spectra based on the predicted parameters. This deep learning model demonstrates precise and efficient ECD spectra prediction on the CMCDS dataset, providing robust support for chiral spectroscopy research while advancing machine-learning applications in chiral-related fields.

Calculation of ECD Spectra of Natural Products Using Quantum Chemical Methods

As shown in Fig. 4, six representative molecular systems with experimental ECD spectra available from previous literature were selected, and their theoretical ECD spectra were computed using the CMCDS dataset construction workflow based on DFT method. Comparative analysis revealed strong agreement between theoretical and experimental spectra across all tested molecules, with high consistency in peak positions and matching cotton effect signs. These results demonstrated that the CMCDS protocol exhibits excellent applicability and reliability in ECD spectrum prediction for complex molecular systems.

Remote monitoring of cardiac implantable electronic devices with heart failure diagnostics detects signs of heart failure exacerbation related to a decrease in biventricular pacing
Authors: Alanna Miller, Mary Friedman, Kimberly Leotta, Margaret Shanks
Published: 2025

Introduction

Early detection of heart failure (HF) exacerbation is crucial in preventing hospitalizations and improving quality of life for patients with HF.1 Cardiac implantable electronic devices (CIEDs) are an important part of guideline-directed medical therapy for patients with HF. Fortunately, all CIEDs are now equipped with remote monitoring technology. Cardiac resynchronization therapy using biventricular (BIV) pacing has also been shown to decrease hospitalizations and symptomatic HF episodes in patients with left bundle branch block and reduced ejection fraction.2

Many CIEDs are now capable of measuring early warning signs of HF exacerbation. A nurse-driven remote HF monitoring program was developed through a collaboration between the HF and electrophysiology (EP) teams. This program used physiologic data from CIEDs to detect early signs of HF decompensation. The described patient was identified as at high risk for HF exacerbation, and the HF remote monitoring program’s protocol was followed. It was through data review by both the EP and HF teams that the cause of decompensation was identified as an acute decrease in BIV pacing. Prompt identification of the problem allowed for early intervention and device reprogramming before clinical decompensation.

Case report

A 75-year-old man with ischemic cardiomyopathy, status post BIV implantable cardiac defibrillator, was found to have an elevated HF score on remote monitoring. Additionally, an EP alert was triggered the same day for noise detected on the left ventricular (LV) lead. Upon review of the presenting rhythm, he was found to have LV lead noise, resulting in inhibition of LV pacing. Further review of the transmission showed a declining BIV pacing percentage correlating with the increased HF risk score, as shown in Figure 1.

A multidisciplinary team from HF and EP reviewed the remote transmission data. The patient was contacted via phone by the HF nurses to screen for symptoms of HF exacerbation. The nurse-driven diuretic protocol was then initiated, and the patient was scheduled for an EP office visit. The patient presented to the EP office for reprogramming of his implantable cardiac defibrillator. Device reprogramming was performed, and LV lead sensing was disabled to prevent oversensing of LV lead noise.

One week after the diuretic protocol was initiated and the LV lead was reprogrammed, a follow-up transmission was obtained and reviewed. The transmission showed a decreased HF score, appropriate LV and right ventricular electrograms consistent with BIV pacing, and an increased BIV pacing percentage of 91%. One month later, a remote transmission was obtained and showed an HF index score of 0.

Monitoring patients using remote HF monitoring technology allows providers to identify patients who are at high risk of developing decompensated HF and proactively intervene to prevent decompensation, improve patient outcomes, and reduced HF hospitalizations. There are often correlations between HF and EP alerts, as was described in this case. This example highlights the importance of a collaborative approach to remote HF monitoring using a multidisciplinary team, including HF and EP nurses, advanced practice providers, and physicians.

The development of a collaborative HF and EP remote monitoring program at the study institution has led to increased communication and collaboration between the 2 teams and overall improved patient care. HF decompensation is often associated with an increased arrhythmia burden or decreased BIV pacing percentage. It is important for EP and HF teams to have clear communication so that these associated remote monitoring findings can be shared, and patients can be managed accordingly to prevent decompensation. Remote HF monitoring using CIED diagnostic data has the potential to benefit thousands of patients nationwide.

Conclusion

Remote monitoring of patients with CIEDs, which are equipped with HF diagnostics, is an effective way to detect early signs of HF exacerbation. Collaboration between the EP and HF teams allows for prompt diagnosis and intervention in patients at high risk for decompensation and HF hospitalization.

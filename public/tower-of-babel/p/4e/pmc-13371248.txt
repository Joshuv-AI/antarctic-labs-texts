The safety and tolerability of oral TDF/FTC as pre-exposure prophylaxis among men who have sex with men in China: a prospective cohort study
Authors: Jie Liu, Songjie Wu, Quanmin Li, Zhuoheng Yin, Chunyan Li, Chengxin Fan, Yifan Dai, Kun Wang, Ivy Jiang, Haojie Huang, Jonathan Lio, Aniruddha Hazra, Joseph D Tucker, Renslow Sherer, Linghua Li, Weiming Tang, Ke Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) has been demonstrated as an effective strategy for preventing HIV infection. However, multiple barriers contribute to its suboptimal uptake in China, with drug safety being a shared concern for both clinicians and patients. This study aimed to assess the safety and tolerability of oral tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF)/emtricitabine (FTC) for PrEP in a real-world setting in China.MethodsAn open-label, prospective cohort study was conducted between September 2021 and September 2024 in Wuhan and Guangzhou, China. PrEP-eligible men who have sex with men (MSM) were enrolled, participants were prescribed TDF/FTC as PrEP and followed up for 12 months. Adverse events (AEs) were self-reported, and participants underwent regular monitoring for HIV, syphilis, liver and kidney function during follow-up.ResultsA total of 1,138 participants enrolled and initiated PrEP, with 1,045 completing at least one follow-up visit. Seven participants were diagnosed with HIV infection, yielding an HIV incidence rate of 0.8 per 100 person-years (95% CI: 0.2–1.4). A total of 62 participants self-reported 106 AEs, the majority of which were grade 1, with no serious AEs observed. Participants reported a significantly higher AE incidence with daily PrEP than on-demand PrEP (14.7 per 100 person-years vs. 9.3 per 100 person-years, P < 0.05). Additionally, 128 episodes of serum creatinine (SCr) and 701 episodes of liver enzyme abnormalities were recorded, and mild elevations in ALT, AST, and SCr were significantly more frequent among participants taking the daily regimen. Significant ALT elevations were observed from the 1st month to the 9th month follow-up, while SCr elevations occurred predominantly in the 1st month.ConclusionsBoth daily and on-demand PrEP regimens with TDF/FTC are safe and well tolerated for the prevention of HIV among Chinese MSM. On-demand PrEP with fewer AEs and comparable effectiveness provides an alternative HIV prevention strategy for Chinese MSM in real-world settings.Trial registrationThe study is registered in Clinical Trial databases in China (ChiCTR2100048981, July 19, 2021) and the US (NCT04754139, February 11, 2021).Keywords: Pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), Men who have sex with men (MSM), China, Safety, TDF/FTC

Introduction

According to the United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS (UNAIDS), approximately 1.3 million new HIV infections occurred globally in 2024, reflecting an overall declining trend [1]. Despite China’s low HIV prevalence (8.41 per 100,000) and high antiretroviral therapy (ART) coverage and viral suppression among those on ART in 2021 (92.9% and 96.1%, respectively), HIV remains a public health concern, with 110,491 new cases reported in 2023 [2, 3]. Sexual transmission remains the predominant route (98.5%) of HIV infection in China. Notably, male-to-male transmission contributed to 25.7% of newly identified cases, representing the fastest-growing subgroup [4]. These facts highlight the critical need for accessible, safe, and effective HIV prevention interventions to reduce transmission among key populations, especially MSM.

Previous studies have demonstrated that pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) is an effective and safe biomedical strategy that significantly reduces HIV acquisition [4]. Real-world studies in regions with high HIV testing and treatment rates have shown that targeted PrEP promotion among key populations can reduce HIV incidence by over 25% at the population level [5, 6]. In China, the clinical implementation of PrEP began relatively recently. Although the Chinese guidelines for diagnosis and treatment of HIV/AIDS have officially recommended PrEP, fewer than 1% of over 8 million MSM are taking PrEP for HIV prevention [4, 7, 8]. Furthermore, real-world data on the safety of PrEP remain limited. Most existing studies in China have focused on the willingness and acceptability of PrEP among high-risk populations, factors influencing adherence, and its preventive efficacy. In contrast, real-world data on the safety and tolerability of oral PrEP are scarce, while safety is one of the key barriers to PrEP uptake in China, which highlights the need for systematic safety evaluations to inform evidence-based policy and clinical practice [9].

To fill this gap, we conducted an open-label pragmatic study in Wuhan and Guangzhou between September 2021 and September 2024. This study aimed to generate reliable evidence on the safety of PrEP and to develop a clinical management and monitoring strategy tailored to the needs of MSM in China.

Methods

Study design

This open-label prospective cohort study was conducted as part of a multicenter research initiative focusing on PrEP initiation in Hubei and Guangdong provinces, China. The study aimed to assess the safety and tolerability of PrEP among MSM in real-world clinical settings. Eligible MSM were recruited and prescribed TDF/FTC as PrEP. This study was conducted at two study sites in collaboration with local community organizations between September 2021 and September 2024, and participants received free PrEP for 12 months. During the enrollment period, participants attended monthly follow-up visits for the first three months, followed by quarterly clinical assessments thereafter.

Participants

Participants were recruited by local community organizations through social media platforms. After rapid testing, eligible participants were referred to designated study hospitals for comprehensive laboratory evaluations and baseline assessments. The inclusion criteria were as follows: HIV-negative MSM aged ≥ 18 years at screening, residing in Wuhan or Guangzhou, with a history of anal intercourse with men, and at high risk of HIV infection [10]. Engagement in any of the following behaviors within the past six months was considered indicative of increased HIV risk: condomless anal intercourse with male partner(s), multiple male partners (regardless of condom use or HIV serostatus), recent history of sexually transmitted infections (STIs), or prior receipt of postexposure prophylaxis (PEP) [4, 11, 12]. Participants had to be willing to comply with the study procedures. The key exclusion criteria were as follows: confirmed HIV infection; symptoms suggestive of acute HIV infection within the previous 30 days; suspected HIV exposure within the preceding 72 h; contraindications to oral PrEP; hepatitis B surface antigen positivity or chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection; creatinine clearance (CrCl) < 60 mL/min (calculated using the Cockcroft–Gault equation) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT) or aspartate aminotransferase (AST) levels > 2.5 times the upper limit of normal (ULN); current oral PrEP use; or participation in other HIV- or PrEP-related studies prior to enrollment [10]. The protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Boards at all participating sites, and all participants provided written informed consent before enrollment.

Study procedures

Participants underwent initial screening assessments at designated study sites, which included a comprehensive panel of tests, including HIV testing, hepatitis B surface antigen, HCV antibody, syphilis rapid plasma reagin test, complete blood count, urinalysis, and assessments of liver and kidney function. Follow-up visits were conducted at 1, 2, and 3 months after PrEP initiation, and thereafter every 3 months for up to 12 months. During follow-up, participants underwent routine testing for HIV antibodies, syphilis antibodies, and liver and kidney function at each visit for safety monitoring. Following the baseline visit, eligible participants were enrolled and provided with free PrEP (TDF/FTC, Truvada, Gilead, Foster City, CA), distributed either in person or by mail after each scheduled visit to support adherence. All participants initiated PrEP on the daily regimen. Switching between daily and on-demand regimens was permitted throughout follow-up until the final study visit, HIV seroconversion, or PrEP discontinuation/suspension due to AEs. The on-demand regimen followed the IPERGAY “2-1-1” schedule: two tablets of TDF/FTC 2–24 h before planned sex, followed by one tablet 24 h and 48 h after the first dose, with daily dosing continued if sexual activity persisted, and the regimen discontinued 48 h after the last sexual exposure [13]. Regimen status was assessed by questionnaire at months 3, 6, 9, and 12. Laboratory test results were communicated to all participants, accompanied by relevant medical recommendations. The administration of PrEP was temporarily or permanently discontinued in case of biochemical abnormalities as defined by the study protocol or HIV seroconversion. Participants who paused PrEP during the follow-up period could resume treatment. Participants diagnosed with HIV were promptly referred for specialized HIV care and management. AEs were captured via participant self-report using an online questionnaire. Participants could report AEs at any time during follow-up via the online questionnaire, and AEs were additionally assessed and recorded at each scheduled follow-up visit. Participants who missed three or more consecutive visits and could not be located at the end of the study were considered permanently lost to follow-up (Fig. 1).

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the safety profile of PrEP. Safety was assessed based on laboratory and clinical AEs, which were collected via self-reporting and evaluated by investigators, irrespective of their association with PrEP use. AEs were graded based on the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE), Version 5.0, with the following classification: Grade 1: mild symptoms without the need for intervention, with laboratory values elevated up to 3 times the upper limit of normal (ULN). Grade 2: moderate symptoms requiring minimal intervention, with laboratory values elevated between 3 and 5 times the ULN. Grade 3: severe but non-life-threatening symptoms, with laboratory values elevated between 5 and 20 times the ULN. Grade 4: life-threatening consequences necessitating urgent intervention, with laboratory values elevated more than 20 times the ULN [14]. For each laboratory parameter, the highest AE grade observed during follow-up was assigned to each participant. Moreover, the incidence rate of HIV was expressed in terms of cases per 100 person-years of follow-up.

Statistical analysis

We conducted descriptive analyses of sociodemographic parameters of MSM participants who enrolled on PrEP at baseline. Categorical variables are presented as proportions (%), and continuous variables as mean ± standard deviation or median with 25th to 75th interquartile range (IQR). AE incidence rates were calculated and expressed per 100 person-years with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) based on the Poisson distribution. Changes from baseline in ALT, AST, TBiL, and SCr across follow-up visits were analyzed by repeated-measures ANOVA. Poisson regression was used to estimate incidence rate ratios (IRRs) and 95% CIs comparing the AEs between daily and on-demand regimens. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed by IBM SPSS Statistics version 29 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). We used GraphPad Prism version 10.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) to plot figures.

Results

Study participants

A total of 3,649 at-risk individuals interested in PrEP were contacted, and 1,200 were screened in Wuhan and Guangzhou. Among them, 1,138 met the eligibility criteria and were enrolled in the study (Fig. 2). Baseline characteristics of the enrolled participants are summarized in Table 1. The median age was 29 years (IQR, 23–30), and most participants were < 40 years. Overall, 1,076 (94.5%) had completed tertiary education; 1,076 (94.5%) were unmarried. Syphilis was diagnosed at eligibility screening in 115 (10.1%) participants.

HIV acquisition

Participants were monitored for a total of 881.75 person-years, with varying durations of observation. During the observation period, 7 participants experienced HIV seroconversions, resulting in an overall HIV incidence rate of 0.79 per 100 person-years (95% CI: 0.21–1.38).

Self-reported AEs

No serious AEs were reported during the post-baseline visits. A total of 62 participants (5.9%) reported 106 AEs while taking PrEP, with 105 (99.1%) of these episodes considered related to the study drug. Participants reported 65 (14.7 events per 100 person-years) AEs during daily PrEP exposure and 41 (9.3 events per 100 person-years) during on-demand exposure, yielding significantly higher AE incidence with daily dosing (P < 0.05). Among these 62 participants, 46 adhered to PrEP and 16 withdrew from the study in the first 3 months of follow-up. Within the first month, 36 (57.1%) of these participants reported AEs, most commonly mild nausea and dizziness. Subsequent follow-up showed a significant decrease in self-reported AEs (Fig. 3). Gastrointestinal AEs were reported by 33 participants (58 cases), while 21 participants reported 24 cases of nervous system AEs. Additionally, 16 participants self-reported allergic symptoms, mostly manifested as skin rash. Two participants experienced transient paresthesia, manifested as facial numbness after each instance of taking PrEP on demand (Table 2).

Confirmed laboratory AEs

Among the 1,045 participants who completed at least one follow-up visit, the most common laboratory abnormalities were mild elevations in ALT and AST. Based on the highest AE grade observed during follow-up, 23.2% (n = 242) of participants experienced any ALT elevation (grade ≥ 1), including 20.3% grade 1, 2.3% grade 2, and 0.6% grade 3. For AST, the corresponding proportions were 18.3%, 15.3%, 2.3%, and 0.7%, respectively. Abnormalities in SCr were less frequent (8.1% grade ≥ 1). No grade 4 AEs were observed (Table 3).

A total of 1,119 laboratory AEs were documented among participants who completed at least one follow-up visit. Based on cumulative person-years of exposure to each regimen, the overall incidence of laboratory AEs was 150.9 per 100 person-years during periods of daily PrEP and 102.7 per 100 person-years during periods of on-demand PrEP (Table 4). Mild (grade 1–2) elevations in ALT, AST, and SCr occurred more frequently during daily regimen periods than during on-demand regimen periods (all P < 0.05, Table 4). In contrast, the incidence of TBiL abnormalities did not differ significantly between the two regimens (P > 0.05, Table 4). At enrollment, 27 participants had slightly elevated SCr above the ULN, though all maintained a CrCl > 60 mL/min. During follow-up, 12 of the 27 participants with elevated SCr at baseline had stable and normal SCr levels, while 15 showed intermittent mild SCr elevations without progressive increases. Among participants with normal baseline SCr, 58 experienced 99 episodes of SCr elevation; most were transient grade 1 events that resolved within 4 weeks or by the next visit, and none led to PrEP discontinuation. Elevated SCr levels occurred most frequently at month 1, and the median SCr change from baseline at this visit was significantly higher than at other time points (P < 0.05; Fig. 4C).

Discussion

This longitudinal cohort study provides real-world evidence on the safety and tolerability of two dosing regimens of oral PrEP with TDF/FTC among MSM. PrEP was effective and well tolerated, with both self-reported and laboratory AEs being predominantly mild and transient. No SAEs were observed, and only a few participants discontinued PrEP due to laboratory abnormalities. Notably, mild elevations in liver enzymes and SCr occurred more frequently during daily dosing periods, particularly within the first three months of follow-up.

Consistent with previous studies, no SAEs or life-threatening AEs were reported in our study. Of note, participants taking daily PrEP had significantly higher rates of self-reported AEs and mild laboratory abnormalities, including elevated ALT, AST, and SCr levels. These findings suggest that continuous exposure to TDF/FTC may increase mild hepatic and renal function disturbances, consistent with pharmacokinetic evidence showing higher cumulative drug exposure with daily dosing regimens [15, 16]. Interestingly, our results differ from recent findings in the CROPrEP project and a Hong Kong cohort study. In the multicenter open-label study of CROPrEP project, daily PrEP users reported lower overall AE prevalence compared with on-demand users (37.1% vs. 47.9%, P < 0.001), including fewer cases of dizziness, gastrointestinal discomfort, and fatigue. However, regimen switching due to AEs was less frequent in the on-demand group (0.2% vs. 2.1%, P = 0.004), and no significant differences were observed in spinal bone mineral density, proteinuria, serum creatinine, or grade 3–4 laboratory abnormalities [17]. Similarly, a Hong Kong study reported that mild and transient AEs such as diarrhea, headache, nausea, and dizziness occurred in 31% during daily dosing and 8% during on-demand dosing, with no serious AEs and comparable user preference for both regimens after follow-up [18]. The discrepancy between our findings and those from other Asian settings may be attributed to differences in AE reporting sensitivity, adherence patterns, and follow-up duration. Participants with consistent daily dosing may experience mild, transient hepatic and renal changes detectable in laboratory monitoring, while self-reported symptoms may vary by population awareness and health-seeking behaviors. Overall, most AEs observed across studies were mild (grade 1–2) and reversible, reaffirming the overall safety of both PrEP regimens.

Longitudinal monitoring of hepatic and renal biomarkers in this study revealed mild, transient fluctuations primarily within the first several months of PrEP use. The patterns of ALT, AST, TBiL, and SCr indicate that mild fluctuations occurred predominantly in the early phase after PrEP dosing and gradually stabilized with continued exposure. The significant increases in ALT and AST from baseline at multiple follow-up points likely represent transient hepatic adaptation to PrEP exposure rather than progressive hepatotoxicity. Preclinical studies in animal models reported reversible TDF-associated hepatic changes (e.g., collagen deposition, hepatocyte cytomegaly) via oxidative stress and lipid peroxidation pathways, but clinical data remain sparse [19].

For nephrotoxicity, the median change from baseline in SCr observed at month 1 is mostly consistent with early proximal tubular injury associated with TDF. Renal impairment is attributed to the concentration of TDF over time in the proximal tubular cells, interfering with mitochondrial DNA synthesis, leading to metabolic disorder and loss of cell function, which may cause Fanconi syndrome or Type IV renal tubular acidosis [20]. The iPrEx study reported a 2% increase in creatinine levels among participants treated with TDF/FTC, which normalized after discontinuation and showed no statistical difference from the placebo group [21]. In our study, we also observed some participants experiencing renal injury, most of which were self-limiting, and not observed in subsequent follow-ups, possibly related to dehydration, physical exercise, etc. Moreover, for participants with mild creatinine elevation at enrollment (grade 1), kidney function remained stable during follow-up, with no continuous increase in creatinine levels, suggesting that PrEP poses a low risk of nephrotoxicity and does not significantly increase renal injury in those with pre-existing renal impairment. The PrEP Brazil study, which investigated the relationship between daily PrEP and kidney function, involved a 48-week follow-up of 392 participants. It observed a significant decline in eGFR at the 4-week follow-up, which remained stable in subsequent visits [22]. The normalization of SCr thereafter in our study further supports the reversibility and clinical safety of these early changes. Regular laboratory monitoring during the first 3 months after PrEP initiation remains essential to differentiate adaptive fluctuations from clinically significant AEs, thereby ensuring safe and sustained PrEP use.

Our results demonstrated that TDF/FTC used as PrEP was effective, generally safe, and well-tolerated among Chinese MSM. Importantly, an on-demand PrEP regimen was associated with significantly fewer AEs compared with a daily regimen, and this lower AE incidence may enhance user comfort and adherence, as fear of side effects is a common barrier to long-term PrEP use in real-world settings [9]. These findings underscore the need for individualized PrEP strategies tailored to risk behaviors, medical profiles, and personal preferences. For individuals with frequent or unpredictable sexual exposure, daily PrEP remains appropriate to ensure consistent protection [23, 24]. Conversely, on-demand PrEP may be preferable for those with infrequent exposures, concerns about drug tolerance, or baseline hepatic or renal vulnerabilities. Offering both options within clinical practice allows for dynamic adaptation, and individuals may transition between regimens according to changes in sexual activity, adherence patterns, or laboratory tests [23].

There are some limitations of our study. First, the overall retention rate was lower than anticipated, with more participants than expected withdrawing during follow-up. Nevertheless, most participants completed at least six months of observation, and AEs were not a major reason for withdrawal. Secondly, although we compared AEs between the daily and on-demand regimens, these comparisons were not adjusted for all potential confounders; therefore, residual confounding may remain (e.g., age, baseline syphilis status, baseline ALT/AST/SCr, and behavioral risk), which could have influenced the observed differences between regimens. Thirdly, we did not objectively monitor medication adherence or quantify actual drug exposure, resulting in uncertainty regarding actual drug exposure and precluding evaluation of any dose–response relationship between hepatic/renal function changes and the amount of medication taken. Finally, the evaluation of symptom-related AEs was based on self-reporting by the participants, which may lead to an underestimation of these events.

Conclusion

In summary, both daily and on-demand TDF/FTC-based PrEP regimens were generally safe and well tolerated among Chinese MSM in real-world settings. The on-demand regimen was observed with fewer self-reported and laboratory AEs, maintaining comparable protective effectiveness. Providing flexible regimen options, counseling on regimen switching, and optimizing follow-up strategies could increase PrEP uptake among key populations and achieve broader and more sustainable HIV prevention coverage.

Heat-killed Lacticaseibacillus casei OS1423 retains immunoprotective functionality across ingredient and adlay tea formulations
Authors: Hye-Jin Park, You-Tae Kim, Joon-Gi Kwon, Hye-Jin Kim, Joo Hyun Lee, Choon Gil Kang, Ju-Hoon Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The development of functional microbial ingredients that retain probiotic-associated benefits without reliance on cellular viability is of growing interest in functional food design. In this study, heat-killed preparations of Lacticaseibacillus casei OS1423 and L. paracasei OS1213 were evaluated to determine whether they could retain immunoprotective activity after heat inactivation while offering advantages for food application. Using a cyclophosphamide (CP)-induced immunosuppressed mouse model, both live and heat-killed forms were assessed for their ability to modulate selected immune parameters. The heat-killed preparations partially restored selected cytokine responses, splenocyte proliferative activity, and systemic immunoglobulin production, indicating that strain-associated immunomodulatory activity was retained in selected readouts despite the loss of viability. Supplementation was also associated with gut microbial reorganization, particularly through partial recovery of bacterial groups commonly linked to intestinal homeostasis and SCFA-producing microbial guilds, although functional predictions were interpreted as inferred and exploratory. To evaluate food application feasibility, heat-killed L. casei OS1423 was incorporated into an adlay (Coix lacryma-jobi L.) tea matrix. The fortified beverage retained selected immunomodulatory activities of HK_OS1423, particularly mucosal cytokine recovery, splenocyte proliferation, and serum IgA restoration, supporting its applicability in grain-based functional beverage matrices. Collectively, these findings support the potential of heat-killed probiotics as functional microbial ingredients and highlight their practical relevance for grain-based beverages designed to support immune health.

Introduction

Dietary components that support immune homeostasis have attracted increasing attention in the development of functional foods aimed at promoting human health (Ashaolu, 2020). Because the gastrointestinal tract serves as a major interface where host immune cells continuously interact with the intestinal microbiota, modulation of the gut–immune axis through dietary intervention has emerged as an important strategy for maintaining immune competence (Y. Liu et al., 2022). Disruption of this balance is frequently associated with alterations in gut microbial composition and impaired immune regulation, which may increase host susceptibility to infectious and inflammatory disorders (Balakrishnan et al., 2024; Lei et al., 2015). Consequently, considerable research efforts have focused on identifying food-derived ingredients capable of modulating intestinal microbial communities and supporting immune homeostasis (Wu et al., 2019). Among the various candidates investigated, probiotic microorganisms have attracted particular attention because of their potential to beneficially influence both gut microbial ecology and host immune responses (Amin et al., 2025).

Probiotics are defined as live microorganisms that confer health benefits on the host when administered in adequate amounts (Hill et al., 2014). Through metabolic activity and interactions with host immune signaling pathways and the intestinal microbiota, probiotic strains have been widely reported to regulate immune responses and contribute to the maintenance of intestinal homeostasis (Sanders et al., 2019; Yan and Polk, 2020). However, the practical use of live probiotics as functional food ingredients remains limited because maintaining cellular viability during processing, storage, and distribution is technically challenging. In experimental studies, the immunomodulatory potential of probiotic strains has often been evaluated using cyclophosphamide (CP)-induced immunosuppressed animal models, which provide a useful platform for assessing immune-supporting effects of functional microbial ingredients. CP administration induces systemic immunosuppression characterized by reduced immune cell activity, splenic atrophy, and impaired intestinal barrier function (Li et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2025). Previous studies have shown that probiotic supplementation can mitigate these adverse effects by improving immune organ indices, enhancing natural killer (NK) cell activity, and restoring cytokine production associated with immune defense (Hassan et al., 2025; S. Kim et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2023).

However, the practical application of live probiotics in functional food products remains challenging because their viability can be readily compromised during food processing and storage (Tripathi and Giri, 2014). Manufacturing steps such as heat treatment, sterilization, and drying can markedly reduce the number of viable cells, making it difficult to ensure a consistent probiotic dose at the time of consumption (Fredua-Agyeman, 2024; Shu et al., 2025). These limitations are particularly relevant in shelf-stable food matrices, including functional beverages and grain-based teas, where thermal processing and complex physicochemical environments can negatively affect microbial survival (X. Liu et al., 2025). To address these challenges, increasing attention has been directed toward microbial preparations that do not rely on cellular viability. In this context, postbiotics have been defined as preparations of inanimate microorganisms and/or their components that confer health benefits on the host (Salminen et al., 2021). This concept includes not only microbial metabolites but also inactivated cells and structurally preserved cellular components that may interact with host pattern-recognition receptors and modulate immune responses even in the absence of metabolic activity (Aggarwal et al., 2022; Teame et al., 2020). Nevertheless, although such inactivated microbial preparations offer clear advantages in technological stability and formulation flexibility, it cannot be assumed that the immunomodulatory properties of viable probiotic cells are fully retained after heat inactivation. Therefore, direct comparative evaluation is required to determine whether heat-inactivated microbial cells can retain selected immunoprotective activities after the loss of viability.

Another important consideration in the development of microbial functional ingredients is their applicability in multi-strain formulations, which are often intended to combine complementary biological activities. However, when live probiotic strains are used together, their interactions within the same environment can be difficult to predict because of competition for nutrients, differences in growth dynamics, or the production of inhibitory metabolites such as bacteriocins (Fijan et al., 2018). Consequently, the functional properties observed in individual strains may not always be maintained in mixed live-cell systems. In contrast, heat-inactivated microbial cells exert their biological effects primarily through structurally preserved cellular components that interact with host immune receptors rather than through active microbial metabolism (Aggarwal et al., 2022; Teame et al., 2020). Because these inactivated preparations do not involve microbial growth or inter-strain competition, they may provide a more controlled platform for evaluating potential complementary or synergistic immunomodulatory effects. In addition, their improved physicochemical stability facilitates incorporation into complex food matrices, including grain-based beverages such as adlay tea, where maintaining the viability of live probiotics is often difficult (X. Zhang et al., 2025). Collectively, these characteristics support the use of heat-inactivated microbial preparations as formulation-friendly functional ingredients for diverse processed food systems.

In this context, the present study was designed to determine whether heat-inactivated probiotic strains could retain selective immunoprotective activities after heat inactivation while offering practical advantage for food applications. Two probiotic strains, Lacticaseibacillus casei OS1423 and L. paracasei OS1213, which had previously demonstrated immunomodulatory potential in their viable forms (Kang et al., 2026), were selected and prepared as heat-killed bacterial formulations. Using a CP-induced immunosuppressed murine model, the immunoprotective effects of these inactivated preparations were evaluated in comparison with those of the corresponding live cells. In addition, the study examined whether combining these structurally distinct heat-inactivated strains could provide complementary or enhanced immunomodulatory effects (Aggarwal et al., 2022; Teame et al., 2020). Finally, to assess their practical feasibility as stable functional ingredients, the validated bacterial preparations were incorporated into an adlay tea matrix to determine whether their immunoprotective activity could be maintained in a complex food system.

Materials and methods

Materials and microbial strains

To induce transient immunosuppression, CP was obtained from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, USA). The microbial strains investigated in this study, L. casei OS1423 and L. paracasei OS1213, were maintained and cultured according to the methods described in our previous study (Kang et al., 2026). Briefly, the strains were grown in de Man, Rogosa, and Sharpe broth (Difco, USA) with 0.05% (w/w) L-cysteine (Sigma-Aldrich; MRSc) at 37°C for 24 h. All other chemicals and reagents used were of analytical grade.

Preparation and quantification of heat-killed probiotics

Preparation of heat-killed probiotics

To prepare heat-killed (HK) probiotics, the harvested bacterial cultures were centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. The resulting cell pellets were washed twice with sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) and resuspended in PBS to a target concentration. The bacterial suspensions were then subjected to heat inactivation at 90°C for 15 min with gentle inversion every 5 min to ensure the complete loss of viability while preserving the structural integrity of the microbial cells. To confirm successful inactivation, aliquots of the treated suspensions were plated on MRSc agar and incubated under appropriate conditions to verify the absence of colony formation. In addition, the morphological integrity of the bacterial cells after heat treatment was examined using microscopy to ensure that the overall cellular structure was maintained. The finalized HK probiotic preparations were stored at 4°C until administration in the animal models and subsequent integration into food matrices.

Precise quantification of heat-killed probiotics

To enable direct comparison between live probiotics and their HK counterparts, the dosage of HK preparations was normalized to the corresponding live bacterial counts (CFU/mL) using quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) with species-specific genomic markers. This approach was used to administer equivalent bacterial quantities in both experimental states. qPCR was performed using a CFX96 real-time PCR system (Bio-Rad, USA) under the following conditions: 95 °C for 1 min, followed by 39 cycles of 95°C for 10 s and 57°C for 30 s. Each reaction was conducted in a final volume of 25 μL containing 12.5 μL of 2× Premix Ex Taq (Takara, Japan), 1 μL each of forward and reverse primers (15 μM), 1 μL of DNA template, and molecular-grade water. Species-specific primer sets for L. casei and L. paracasei were selected based on a previous study (E. Kim et al., 2020). Quantification cycle (Cq) values were automatically determined when the fluorescence signal exceeded the threshold and were converted to bacterial counts using strain-specific standard curves. Because heat inactivation may alter DNA recovery and amplification behavior, separate standard curves were established for live and HK preparations. For standard curve construction, OS1423 and OS1213 were anaerobically cultured in MRSc broth under laboratory conditions. When the cultures reached an OD600 of 1.0, viable counts were determined by plating on MRSc agar supplemented with 1.8% Bacto agar (Difco). Based on the plate counts, bacterial suspensions were adjusted to 104, 105, 106, 107, and 108 CFU/mL. Live-cell suspensions were used directly for DNA extraction and standard curve construction, whereas HK samples were prepared separately and processed in parallel. Genomic DNA was extracted from both live and HK cells using the DNeasy PowerFood Microbial Kit (Qiagen, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The extracted DNA was dissolved in molecular-grade water and stored at −80 °C until analysis. qPCR was then performed using these DNA samples as templates, and the resulting Cq values were plotted against the corresponding CFU concentrations to generate standard curves. All measurements were performed in triplicate, and the final bacterial counts were expressed as CFU per sample or CFU equivalents for HK probiotic powders.

Cytotoxicity assay on Caco-2 cells

The cytotoxicity of live or heat-killed probiotic powders was evaluated to confirm the absence of adverse effects prior to their use in subsequent assays. Caco-2 cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 104 cells/well and incubated at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 for 24 h. Live or HK probiotics powders were then added to each well to achieve final concentrations of 106, 107, 108, and 109 CFU (or CFU equivalents for HK probiotics) per well. As a positive control for cytotoxicity, the same concentrations of Bacillus cereus ATCC 14579 were added to separate wells. Cells were further incubated for 18 h in DMEM without phenol red (Welgene, South Korea), supplemented with 10% (v/v) FBS.

Following treatment, cell culture supernatants were collected and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release was measured using the LDH cytotoxicity assay kit (DoGenBio, Korea) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Optical density was read at 450 nm (OD450) using a microplate reader (Tecan, Switzerland). Cytotoxicity was calculated according to the following equation:Cytotoxicity(%)=(OD450forexperimentalrelease−OD450forspontaneousreleaseOD450formaximumrelease−OD450forspontaneousrelease)×100

The spontaneous release reflects the LDH level in untreated Caco-2 cells, while the maximum release is defined as the LDH released after complete lysis using a lysis buffer in the kit.

Analysis of cytokine responses and synergistic effects in RAW 264.7 cells

Quantification of cytokines from stimulated cell

RAW 264.7 cells (5 × 105 cells/well) were seeded in 24-well cell culture plates and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for 24 h up to 90% confluence. Before treating bacterial strains, the supernatant of the cultured cells was removed and replaced with the fresh antibiotic-free medium. For evaluating immune-enhancing effects of bacterial strains, RAW 264.7 cells were treated with bacterial cells at 1 × 106 cells/well and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for additional 18 h. Additionally, PBS buffer was used as the negative control, while 1 μg/mL of lipopolysaccharide (LPS) served as the positive control for the experiment.

To measure cytokine levels at the protein level, the supernatant was collected after centrifugation of the culture plate (125 × g, 7 min, 4 °C). The proinflammatory cytokines, tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α, interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-6, were determined using mouse ELISA kits (Koma Biotech, South Korea), according to the manufacturer's protocol. After the removal of the culture supernatant, total RNA was extracted from RAW 264.7 cells attached to each well by treating them with TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) and the PureLink RNA Mini Kit (Invitrogen) following the manufacturer's instructions. RNA was quantified using NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Scientific, USA) and then reverse transcribed to cDNA with PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (TaKaRa, Japan). TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 were confirmed by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) with SYBR Green Supermix (Bio-Rad, USA) under the following conditions: 1 cycle at 95 °C for 3 min, and 40 cycles containing 95 °C for 5 s and 58 °C for 30 s. Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) was used as a reference gene for normalization, and the relative mRNA expressions were calculated using the relative 2−ΔΔCT method (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001). All the primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table S1.

Evaluation of combinatorial effects using the Bliss independence model and Highest Single Agent criterion

The combinatorial effects of OS1423 and OS1213 on cytokine production (TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6) in RAW 264.7 macrophages were evaluated using two complementary frameworks, the Bliss independence model (Amzallag et al., 2019) and the Highest Single Agent (HSA) criterion. For both analyses, fractional effects (E) were calculated using the LPS-induced response as a fixed biological reference, in order to avoid sample-dependent normalization. Specifically, E was defined as (response_mean − PBS_mean)/(LPS_mean − PBS_mean), where PBS corresponded to the lower bound (E = 0) and LPS, included as a positive control in every experiment, defined the upper bound (E = 1). Using this definition, EOS1423 and EOS1213 were calculated from the cytokine responses to OS1423 and OS1213 single-strain treatments, respectively, and Eobserved was calculated from the cytokine response to the OS1423 and OS1213 combined treatment. The Bliss-predicted additive expectation (EBliss) for the combined treatment was then calculated from the two single-strain fractional effects as:EBliss=EOS1423+EOS1213−(EOS1423×EOS1213)

The deviation from additivity was quantified as Δ = Eobserved − EBliss. Statistical inference for Δ was performed by bootstrap resampling: 10,000 resamples were drawn with replacement from the original triplicate measurements (n = 3), and 95% confidence intervals together with two-sided p-values (H0: Δ = 0) were derived from the resulting bootstrap distribution. Interpretation was rule-based: a confidence interval of Δ that excluded zero with Δ > 0 was interpreted as synergy, an interval excluding zero with Δ < 0 as sub-additivity, and an interval containing zero as additivity. As a complementary criterion, the HSA analysis compared the combined response against the more responsive single strain in each replicate. A one-sided paired t-test was used to test whether the combined response exceeded the higher of the two single-strain responses at the per-replicate level. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05 for both analyses.

Animal model and treatments

Mouse preparation and experimental design

Male specific pathogen-free (SPF) BALB/c mice (6 weeks old, 18–20 g) were purchased from RaonBio (South Korea). Upon arrival, all animals were acclimated for one week, according to the guidelines of the Seoul National University Institute of Laboratory Animal Resources. After quarantine, mice were acclimated to standard laboratory conditions (23 ± 2 °C, 50 ± 10% humidity, 12-h light and dark cycle) for one week, with free access to food and water. All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Seoul National University (approval number, SNU-240119-4-1) and conducted in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. To evaluate the immunoprotective potential of the microbial interventions, mice were randomly allocated into eight experimental groups, with five animals assigned to each group, after the one-week acclimation period and before treatment initiation. The group size was selected based on previous CP-induced immunosuppression studies using similar immunological endpoints, the approved housing condition of five mice per cage, and the 3R principle, particularly Reduction, to minimize animal use while maintaining feasibility for exploratory preclinical evaluation. A formal a priori power calculation was not performed; therefore, the sample-size rationale and allocation procedure are explicitly reported in accordance with animal research reporting recommendations (Percie du Sert et al., 2020). The experimental groups consisted of a healthy control (control group), a CP-induced negative control (CP group), and six treatment groups. The overall experimental design is shown in Fig. 1. Experimental days throughout the manuscript were defined according to the study timeline after animal arrival and indicate experimental time points rather than animal age. Oral administration was initiated on Day 7 after acclimation, CP was injected on Days 21 and 23, and samples were collected on Day 28. For a duration of two weeks, the treatment groups received daily oral administrations of either live probiotics or HK probiotics equivalents. The live probiotic groups (LP_OS1423, LP_OS1213, and LP_mixture groups) were supplemented with L. casei OS1423, L. paracasei OS1213, or a combination of the two strains at a dose of 1 × 109 cells/mouse. Correspondingly, the HK probiotics groups (HK_OS1423, HK_OS1213, and HK_mixture groups) received the HK probiotics at a precisely normalized dosage of 1 × 109 cells/mouse. In the combination groups (both live and HK), the two strains were mixed in a 1:1 ratio, with the total concentration meticulously adjusted to maintain a consistent dose of 1 × 109 cells/mouse. The administration was conducted via oral gavage using an oral zonde (3.8 cm, 24 G, SamwooKurex, South Korea) attached to a syringe. After administration, CP was intraperitoneally (i.p.) injected at a dose of 150 mg/kg on Days 21 and 23, to induce transient immunosuppression. The control group received PBS buffer injections instead. The body weight of mice was measured daily from Day 7 to Day 28. To specifically evaluate CP-associated body-weight loss after immunosuppressive challenge, post-CP body-weight change was recalculated using Day 21, immediately before CP administration, as the within-animal baseline. The percentage change in body weight was calculated as [(Day 28 body weight − Day 21 body weight)/Day 21 body weight] × 100. Baseline body weight at Day 21 was compared among groups to assess pre-CP equivalence. Fecal samples were collected on Day 7 and Day 28, respectively. Assessment of immunological indicators

The spleen index was calculated as spleen weight relative to body weight, and splenocyte numbers were determined after isolation from spleen tissues. NK cell activity was measured using a LDH cytotoxicity assay kit (DoGenBio, South Korea) following the manufacturer's instructions, and the results were expressed as relative cytotoxicity compared with the control group. Splenocyte proliferation was assessed after stimulation with Concanavalin A (ConA; 5 μg/mL) or LPS (10 μg/mL) using an MTT assay kit (CytoSelect™ MTT Cell Proliferation Assay, Cell Biolabs, United States), with proliferation expressed relative to the control group. Cytokine concentrations in splenocytes and colonic tissues and serum immunoglobulin (IgA and IgG) levels were determined using ELISA kits (Koma Biotech, South Korea) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Additional procedural details were as described previously (Kang et al., 2026).

Histological analysis of colonic tissue

One-centimeter segments of the colon, cleared of fecal contents, were collected and fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin (KBIO Company, South Korea) for 24 h at room temperature. The fixed tissues were embedded in paraffin and subjected to hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining by KP&T Inc. (South Korea). Stained sections were subsequently examined under a light microscope (Leica, Germany). Histological injury was semi-quantitatively evaluated by an investigator blinded to group allocation using predefined criteria based on epithelial architecture/mucosal integrity, crypt structure/organization, and goblet cell preservation. Each category was scored from 0 to 3, with 0 indicating normal morphology and 3 indicating severe alteration. The total histological injury score was calculated as the sum of the three category scores, yielding a maximum score of 9 per sample, with higher scores indicating more severe mucosal injury.

Gut microbiota and metabolic pathway analysis

Bacterial genomic DNA was extracted from fecal samples using the QIAamp Fast DNA Stool Mini Kit (Qiagen, Germany). The V3–V4 region of the bacterial 16S rRNA gene was amplified (Kameoka et al., 2021) and sequenced on the Illumina MiSeq system (Illumina Inc., USA) using a 2 × 300 bp paired-end platform. Sequence data were processed using DADA2 within QIIME2-2023.2 (Bolyen et al., 2019; Callahan et al., 2016), and taxonomic classification was performed using the SILVA v132 reference database. Differential abundance analysis was conducted using the DESeq2 package in R (Love et al., 2014). Furthermore, functional prediction of the gut microbial community was performed using PICRUSt2 to map 16S rRNA gene sequences to relevant KEGG Orthology (KO) pathways, enabling the analysis of metabolic pathway reorganizations (Douglas et al., 2020). Relative abundance represents the proportion of each predicted pathway relative to the total predicted functional pathways in a given sample.

Food application of heat-killed probiotics to adlay tea

A commercial powdered adlay tea product (Ottogi Co., Ltd., South Korea) was used as the food matrix. The tea was reconstituted in boiling water according to the manufacturer's instructions (18 g/180 mL; 0.1 g/mL). After cooling to room temperature, heat-killed L. casei OS1423 was incorporated into the tea to provide a final dose of 1.0 × 109 CFU equivalents/mouse in a total administration volume of 200 μL.

To evaluate the feasibility of this preparation in a complex food system, mice in the food matrix group received 200 μL of the formulated adlay tea by oral gavage once daily for 14 consecutive days in a CP-induced immunosuppressed BALB/c murine model. This treatment was conducted in parallel with a PBS-based HK_OS1423 group for direct comparison of the same bacterial preparation delivered in a food matrix or a simple saline carrier. Subsequent CP challenge and immunological analyses were performed according to the same schedule and procedures used for the other treatment groups.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 26.0 (SPSS, USA) and GraphPad Prism 8.0.1 (GraphPad Software, USA). All data were presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). For experiments involving multiple groups, group differences were evaluated using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by an appropriate post hoc test. For in vitro assays in which multiple treatments were compared with a single reference group, Dunnett's multiple comparisons test was used. For in vivo experiments, group differences were evaluated using one-way ANOVA followed by Dunnett's multiple comparisons test using the CP group as the reference, because the primary objective was to determine whether each treatment alleviated CP-induced immunosuppression. When direct comparisons among treatment groups were required, such as comparisons between single-strain and mixture formulations or between live and heat-killed preparations, Tukey's honestly significant difference (HSD) test was used as an exploratory post hoc analysis.

Results

Quantitative standardization and safety assessment of live and heat-killed formulations

To enable direct comparison between live and heat-killed forms of L. casei OS1423 and L. paracasei OS1213, bacterial concentrations were standardized using a molecular-based quantification approach. Since conventional culture-dependent methods are inapplicable to non-viable cells, species-specific qPCR was employed to enumerate bacterial cells based on genomic equivalents. The accuracy of this DNA-based quantification was established through standard curves, which exhibited high linearity for both live and heat-killed bacteria (Supplementary Fig. S1). Most importantly, this molecular enumeration was cross-validated against conventional plate counts for live probiotics, revealing no significant differences between the two methods (Fig. 2A). Following the quantitative standardization, the safety of the formulations was evaluated through an LDH cytotoxicity assay using Caco-2 human intestinal epithelial cells. Regardless of the viability state, both OS1423 and OS1213 demonstrated no significant cytotoxic effects on the intestinal cells at all tested concentrations, ranging from 109 to 106 CFU (or CFU equivalents) per well (Fig. 2B). The cytotoxicity levels of both the live and heat-killed probiotic treatments were comparable to the negative control, indicating that the heat-treatment process did not generate any harmful by-products or adversely affect gut epithelial integrity. These results establish that the selected strains are non-toxic and that their functional evaluation can be performed reliably across equivalent dosages, providing a solid foundation for the following immunological studies.

Comparative in vitro immunomodulatory activity of single- and multi-strain live and heat-killed formulations

To compare the in vitro immunomodulatory activities of single- and multi-strain formulations, live and heat-killed preparations of L. casei OS1423 and L. paracasei OS1213 were evaluated in RAW 264.7 macrophages. Both live and heat-killed probiotics were assessed for their capacity to stimulate the production of essential pro-inflammatory cytokines, including TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, which serve as key indicators of macrophage activation (Moon et al., 2019). The protein secretion levels were quantified through ELISA, while the corresponding mRNA expression was evaluated via qRT-PCR (Fig. 3). While individual treatments with either strain significantly elevated cytokine levels compared to treatment with PBS, the combined administration of OS1423 and OS1213 resulted in numerically higher or comparable cytokine levels than the respective single-strain counterparts across both viability states (Fig. 3). To quantitatively evaluate whether these observed increases reflected genuine non-additive interactions, the combinatorial effects of OS1423 and OS1213 were re-assessed using the LPS-anchored Bliss independence model with bootstrap-based confidence intervals, complemented by the HSA analysis (Table 2). Under the rigorous Bliss criterion, none of the three cytokines met the threshold for synergy in either the live or the heat-killed formulation. The deviations Δ from the Bliss-predicted additive expectation were either statistically indistinguishable from zero, indicating additive interactions, or significantly negative, indicating sub-additive trends. These results indicate that the combined responses did not formally exceed independent additive expectations, despite the elevated cytokine levels observed in Fig. 3. Nevertheless, the HSA analysis, which evaluates whether the combined response exceeds that of the more responsive single strain, revealed a distinct pattern. For the heat-killed formulation, the combined treatment produced significantly enhanced cytokine output relative to the better-performing single strain for TNF-α (p = 0.043) and IL-1β (p = 0.048), although no such enhancement was observed for IL-6 (p = 0.522). In contrast, the live formulation did not show statistically significant HSA enhancement for any of the three cytokines, consistent with the higher inter-replicate variability typically associated with live-cell-induced responses. Collectively, these findings indicate that, although strict Bliss-defined synergy was not established, the combined heat-killed formulation provided a complementary enhancement of pro-inflammatory cytokine output beyond the effect of either single strain for TNF-α and IL-1β.

Protective effects of live and heat-killed formulations on CP-induced physiological and tissue damage

Effects of live and heat-killed formulations on body weight and splenic parameters

The immunoprotective effects of the live and heat-killed formulations were next evaluated in a CP-induced immunosuppressed murine model. The experimental groups were designed to compare the live and heat-killed forms (LP_OS1423 and LP_OS1213; HK_OS1423 and HK_OS1213), as well as their respective mixtures (LP_mixture and HK_mixture; Table 1). Daily body weight was monitored throughout the experimental period to evaluate the overall physical status of the mice (Fig. 4A). During the first two weeks of oral supplementation (Days 7–21), all groups showed gradual changes in body weight. Although minor visual differences were observed between the Control and CP groups during Days 13–21, baseline body weight on Day 21, immediately before CP administration, did not differ significantly between the Control and CP groups (p = 0.093) or among all experimental groups (one-way ANOVA, p = 0.080). Following CP administration on Days 21 and 23, body weight decreased in the CP-treated groups. Two-way repeated-measures ANOVA revealed a significant effect of time and a significant group × time interaction (both p < 0.001), whereas the main effect of group was not significant (p = 0.346), indicating that body-weight trajectories differed over time but that overall group-level body weight was not consistently different across the full experimental period. To minimize the potential influence of numerical baseline variability, post-CP body-weight change was recalculated as the percentage change from Day 21 to Day 28 (Fig. 4B). The CP group showed a marked reduction in post-CP body weight compared with the healthy control group, confirming the physiological impact of CP-induced immunosuppression. However, none of the probiotic-treated groups showed a significant improvement compared with the CP group under Dunnett's multiple comparisons test. The impact of the formulations on the primary lymphoid organ was further evaluated by measuring the spleen index (Fig. 4C) and total splenocyte counts (Fig. 4D). The CP control group showed a marked decrease in both parameters compared with the healthy control group (p < 0.001), indicating CP-induced splenic atrophy and cellular depletion. For the spleen index, LP_mixture showed a significant increase compared with the CP group (p = 0.010), whereas the single-strain live and heat-killed formulations and the HK_mixture group did not reach statistical significance. For total splenocyte counts, none of the probiotic-treated groups showed a significant increase compared with the CP group, although several treatment groups showed numerically higher values. Additional direct comparisons among treatment groups using Tukey's HSD did not indicate a consistent significant advantage of the mixture formulations over the corresponding single-strain treatments for either splenic parameter. Therefore, splenic anatomical parameters suggested partial or limited recovery, and subsequent functional immune assays were used to further determine whether heat-killed formulations retained immunoprotective activity after heat inactivation.

Protection of colon length and mucosal histological integrity

Intestinal protection was further evaluated by measuring colon length and examining colonic histology, as CP administration is known to induce mucosal damage and colon shortening (Yang et al., 2013). The CP group showed the lowest mean colon length among the experimental groups, suggesting a tendency toward CP-associated intestinal shortening (Fig. 5A). One-way ANOVA showed a significant overall group effect on colon length (p = 0.009). Direct comparisons among treatment groups using Tukey's HSD did not indicate a significant advantage of the mixture formulations over their corresponding single-strain treatments. Restoration of cytokine profiles in mucosal and systemic compartments

To examine immune signaling changes associated with probiotic treatment, cytokine profiles were analyzed at both the mRNA and protein levels in colonic tissues and splenocytes. Overall, the mRNA and protein datasets showed a broadly consistent pattern, in which CP administration was associated with suppression of several cytokine responses, whereas probiotic supplementation partially restored selected cytokine markers depending on the tissue, stimulation condition, and formulation. Because orally administered probiotics first encounter the intestinal immune environment, colonic cytokine responses were evaluated before assessing systemic cytokine production in splenocytes.

At the mucosal level, CP administration was associated with a general reduction in several colonic cytokines, although the extent of suppression differed by cytokine (Fig. 6A). Dunnett's multiple comparisons test using the CP group as the reference indicated that selected probiotic formulations partially restored mucosal cytokine responses, particularly IL-4 and TNF-α, with significant recovery observed in both live and heat-killed treatment groups. The mRNA data showed a more limited but partially consistent pattern, including significant recovery of IFN-γ expression in the HK_OS1423 group. Systemic cytokine responses were further examined in splenocytes stimulated with ConA or LPS (Fig. 6B and C). Under ConA stimulation, cytokine recovery was selective and formulation-dependent. At the protein level, significant recovery was observed for IFN-γ in the LP_OS1423 and LP_mixture groups, and for IL-1β and IL-6 in the HK_OS1423 group. The corresponding mRNA data also showed selective recovery, most notably for TNF-α expression in the HK_OS1423, LP_OS1213, and LP_mixture groups. These findings indicate that probiotic supplementation partially restored T cell mitogen-associated cytokine responses, with HK_OS1423 showing measurable recovery in selected cytokine markers despite heat inactivation.

In LPS-stimulated splenocytes, cytokine recovery was more pronounced. Several live and heat-killed formulations significantly increased IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α production compared with the CP group, whereas IFN-γ and IL-4 showed more limited recovery. Notably, IL-6 production was significantly increased by all live and heat-killed treatment groups, and HK_OS1213 and HK_mixture also significantly increased IL-1β and TNF-α. The mRNA analysis showed a broadly consistent direction, particularly for IFN-γ and IL-6 expression, with significant recovery observed in selected live and heat-killed groups. Together, these results indicate that probiotic supplementation restored splenocyte cytokine responses in a stimulus-dependent manner, with the most consistent recovery observed under LPS stimulation.

Overall, the cytokine data indicate that CP-induced immunosuppression impaired mucosal and systemic cytokine responses, and that probiotic supplementation partially restored these responses depending on cytokine, stimulation condition, and formulation. Although recovery was not uniform across all cytokines, the significant restoration of selected cytokine markers in heat-killed treatment groups supports the conclusion that immunomodulatory activity was retained after heat inactivation.

The proliferative capacity of splenocytes was then evaluated to assess cellular immune recovery. CP treatment markedly impaired both ConA-induced T cell-associated proliferation (Fig. 7B) and LPS-induced B cell-associated proliferation (Fig. 7C). In the ConA-stimulated assay, Dunnett's comparison against the CP group showed significant recovery in the HK_OS1213, HK_mixture, LP_OS1423, LP_OS1213, and LP_mixture groups, with the strongest responses observed in the mixture and live-strain groups. In the LPS-stimulated assay, HK_mixture, LP_OS1423, and LP_OS1213 showed significant recovery compared with the CP group. Direct treatment-group comparisons using Tukey's HSD further indicated that HK_mixture showed higher LPS-induced proliferation than HK_OS1423 (p = 0.039), suggesting a formulation-dependent enhancement in this specific cellular readout. Humoral immune responses were further assessed by measuring serum IgA and IgG levels. CP challenge markedly reduced systemic immunoglobulin levels, indicating impaired antibody-associated immune status (Fig. 7D and E). For serum IgA, significant recovery compared with the CP group was observed in HK_OS1213, HK_mixture, LP_OS1423, LP_OS1213, and LP_mixture groups, with particularly strong responses in HK_OS1213, HK_mixture, and LP_mixture. Tukey's HSD analysis also showed that HK_mixture induced higher IgA levels than HK_OS1423 (p < 0.001), supporting a stronger humoral response by the heat-killed combination in this endpoint. For serum IgG, HK_mixture showed a significant increase compared with the CP group (p < 0.001), whereas the other single-strain formulations did not reach statistical significance. Overall, the functional immune readouts showed that recovery was not uniform across all immune parameters. NK cell activity showed only numerical recovery trends, whereas splenocyte proliferation and serum immunoglobulin responses provided clearer evidence of immune restoration in selected live and heat-killed treatment groups. These findings support the conclusion that heat-killed probiotic formulations retained immunomodulatory activity after heat inactivation, particularly in cellular proliferation and humoral immune responses.

Gut microbiota reorganization and its functional associations with immune recovery

To assess gut microbial changes associated with the immunoprotective effects of the treatments, 16S rRNA gene sequencing was performed. Beta-diversity analysis at Day 28, visualized via PCoA based on Bray-Curtis distances, revealed a profound shift in the microbial community structure following CP administration (Fig. 8A). CP challenge significantly altered the global microbial landscape, as statistically confirmed by PERMANOVA analysis (Supplementary Table S2). This pattern was also reflected in the global shifts observed in the PCoA plot, where both LP_mixture and HK_mixture groups clustered toward the control, indicating a partial restoration of the disrupted microbial community structure. While both live and heat-killed probiotic supplementations significantly differentiated the microbial clusters from the CP-treated group (p < 0.05 for all treated groups), these formulations also maintained statistically distinct profiles compared to the healthy control group (p < 0.05; Supplementary Table S2). This observation indicates that the administration of live and inactivated strains does not simply facilitate a return to the original baseline state. To provide a comprehensive view of the taxonomic shifts underlying these community-level changes, Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) effect size (LEfSe) analysis was extended across all taxonomic levels from Phylum to Genus (Fig. 8B). The results revealed a systemic ecological imbalance. The CP-induced dysbiosis was characterized by a broad expansion of the Muribaculaceae lineage and the Clostridia vadinBB60 group, while the Firmicutes lineage, particularly the families Oscillospiraceae and Lachnospiraceae, served as dominant biomarkers for the healthy control. These taxa exhibited the highest discriminative LDA scores and represented the most prominent bacterial lineages distinguishing the dysbiotic CP group from the healthy microbiota. Therefore, these key families were selected as representative microbial indicators for subsequent relative abundance analysis to further examine how HK probiotic supplementation influenced dysbiosis-associated and health-associated microbial signatures.

To further validate these microbial signatures, the relative abundance of representative taxa associated with these families, including Muribaculaceae, Clostridia vadinBB60 group, Lachnospiraceae, and members of the Clostridium leptum group, was quantified across all experimental groups (Fig. 8C and D). The CP administration markedly reduced the abundance of beneficial commensal taxa, particularly Lachnospiraceae and members of the C. leptum group belonging to the Oscillospiraceae family (Fig. 8C). Supplementation with heat-killed probiotics, especially in the HK_mixture and HK_OS1423 groups, restored the abundance of these taxa. These bacterial groups include taxa frequently associated with short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) production, particularly butyrate, which contribute to intestinal barrier maintenance and immune regulation (Shin et al., 2025; Vacca et al., 2020). In contrast, CP treatment promoted the expansion of the Clostridia vadinBB60 group, whereas heat-killed probiotic supplementation effectively suppressed this increase (Fig. 8D). Meanwhile, Muribaculaceae exhibited relatively minor variation across experimental groups, suggesting that its abundance largely reflects the baseline composition of the murine gut microbiota rather than a primary target of probiotic-driven modulation.

To explore the potential functional implications of the observed taxonomic shifts, predictive metabolic profiling was performed using PICRUSt2 (Supplementary Fig. S2). Although the predicted abundance of genes involved in SCFA metabolism, specifically butanoate (ko00650) and propanoate (ko00640) metabolism, did not reach statistical significance, a notable numerical recovery trend was observed in the HK probiotic-supplemented groups (p = 0.056 by ANOVA). Consistently, key rate-limiting enzymes within these pathways, including butyryl-CoA:acetate CoA-transferase (K01034) for butanoate and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase (K01848 and K01849) for propanoate, followed a similar marginal trend toward restoration. Spearman correlation analysis was further conducted to investigate the associations between the microbial reorganization and these functional shifts (Supplementary Fig. S2). Taxa that showed recovery patterns, such as the genera Blautia and Dorea in Lachnospiraceae family and members of the C. leptum group, exhibited positive associations with the butanoate metabolism pathway, even if these individual correlations reflected the inherent complexity of the microbial network. Conversely, the dysbiosis-associated Clostridia vadinBB60 group showed nominal negative associations with these beneficial metabolic potentials. While these functional observations are based on predictive metagenomic analysis and should therefore be interpreted cautiously, they were consistent with the taxonomic recovery of bacterial groups commonly associated with SCFA-producing microbial guilds.

Spearman correlation analysis was further conducted to explore potential associations between microbial taxa and immune-related parameters across all experimental groups (Fig. 9). Commensal taxa that were restored following heat-killed probiotic supplementation, including Lachnospiraceae, Oscillospiraceae, and members of the C. leptum group, generally exhibited nominal positive associations with several immune indicators. In contrast, the dysbiosis-associated Clostridia vadinBB60 group showed nominal negative associations with immune parameters, including serum immunoglobulins and intestinal cytokines such as colon IL-1B and IL-4 (p < 0.01). Muribaculaceae also displayed negative associations with splenocyte-derived cytokines. After FDR correction, only the positive association between Dorea and serum IgA remained statistically significant (q = 0.026). Retention of immunoprotective efficacy of HK_OS1423 in an adlay tea matrix

Based on its retained immunoprotective activity across selected immune readouts and the lack of a clear in vivo advantage of the combined formulation, heat-killed L. casei OS1423 was selected for validation in an adlay tea food matrix. Accordingly, heat-killed L. casei OS1423 was incorporated into the adlay tea matrix to determine whether its immunoprotective efficacy could be retained in a grain-based food system using a transiently immunosuppressed mouse model. Following the experimental scheme (Fig. 10A), mice were challenged with CP to induce transient immunosuppression after treatment with either the HK probiotic alone or the HK_OS1423-fortified tea (Food + HK_OS1423). Gross physiological and splenic parameters showed limited recovery in the Food + HK_OS1423 group. Food + HK_OS1423 also showed a numerical increase in spleen index, but this difference did not reach statistical significance compared with the CP group (p = 0.103; Fig. 10C). Total splenocyte counts were likewise not significantly increased by Food + HK_OS1423 compared with the CP group (p = 0.845; Fig. 10D). The intestinal effects of the adlay tea formulation were then evaluated by measuring colon length, colonic histology, and mucosal cytokine responses. Food + HK_OS1423 did not significantly increase colon length compared with the CP group (p = 0.472), whereas the PBS-based HK_OS1423 group showed a significant increase under the primary comparison framework (p = 0.031; Fig. 10F). Histological examination via H&E staining showed that the Food + HK_OS1423 group exhibited partially preserved epithelial architecture and improved crypt organization relative to the CP group, suggesting attenuation of CP-associated mucosal disruption (Fig. 10G). Cytokine analysis further showed that Food + HK_OS1423 significantly increased selected colonic cytokines, including IL-1β (p = 0.049), IL-4 (p = 0.017), and TNF-α (p = 0.020), compared with the CP group (Fig. 10H to L). However, systemic cytokine responses in ConA- or LPS-stimulated splenocytes were not consistently restored by Food + HK_OS1423, whereas the PBS-based HK_OS1423 group showed significant recovery of selected splenocyte-derived cytokines, including ConA-stimulated IFN-γ (p = 0.012), IL-1β (p = 0.038), IL-6 (p = 0.030), and LPS-stimulated IL-6 (p < 0.001) (Supplementary Fig. S3). These findings suggest that incorporation of HK_OS1423 into the adlay tea matrix was associated primarily with recovery of selected mucosal cytokine responses, while systemic cytokine recovery was less consistent.

Functional immune readouts were further evaluated by measuring NK cell activity, splenocyte proliferation, and serum immunoglobulin levels. NK cell activity showed a numerical increase in the Food + HK_OS1423 group but did not reach statistical significance compared with the CP group (p = 0.125; Fig. 10M). In contrast, Food + HK_OS1423 significantly increased splenocyte proliferation in response to both ConA (p < 0.001) and LPS (p = 0.002) compared with the CP group (Fig. 10N and O). Direct comparison using Tukey's HSD further indicated that Food + HK_OS1423 induced higher ConA-stimulated splenocyte proliferation than the PBS-based HK_OS1423 group (p = 0.001), although this difference should be interpreted cautiously because an adlay tea-only control was not included. For humoral immune responses, Food + HK_OS1423 significantly increased serum IgA compared with the CP group (p = 0.002), whereas serum IgG recovery was significant only in the PBS-based HK_OS1423 group (p = 0.009) (Fig. 10P and Q). These results indicate that the adlay tea formulation retained selected immunoprotective activities of HK_OS1423, particularly mucosal cytokine recovery, splenocyte proliferative responses, and serum IgA restoration, but did not uniformly restore all immune parameters.

Finally, the impact of the HK_OS1423-fortified tea on the gut microbial ecosystem was investigated to determine its influence on the intestinal environment. At the family level, the abundance of Lachnospiraceae, which was depleted following CP administration, showed recovery patterns in both the HK_OS1423 and Food + HK_OS1423 groups (Fig. 10R). To further distinguish microbial signatures between the CP and Food + HK_OS1423 groups, LDA analysis was performed, identifying Clostridia_vadinBB60 as a key discriminative taxon for the CP group, whereas Lactobacillus emerged as a prominent indicator for the Food + HK_OS1423 group (Fig. 10S). This microbial reorganization was further supported by relative abundance analysis in Fig. 10T, which showed partial recovery of Lachnospiraceae and a modest increase in the genus Lactobacillus in the HK_OS1423 and Food + HK_OS1423 groups. Taken together, these findings indicate that HK_OS1423 retained selected immunoprotective activity when administered together with the adlay tea matrix, particularly in mucosal cytokine responses, splenocyte proliferation, serum IgA production, and microbiota reorganization. However, because an adlay tea-only control was not included, the individual contributions of HK_OS1423 and adlay-derived components could not be separated in the present study.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that heat-killed preparations of Lacticaseibacillus casei OS1423 and L. paracasei OS1213 retained immunoprotective activity across selected immune readouts in a CP-induced immunosuppressed murine model, although recovery was not uniform across all physiological, anatomical, and immune parameters. These effects were accompanied by reorganization and partial recovery trends in predicted microbial functional profiles. In addition, the incorporation of HK_OS1423 into an adlay tea matrix preserved selected immunomodulatory activities, particularly mucosal cytokine responses, splenocyte proliferation, and serum IgA production, supporting its feasibility in grain-based beverage applications.

The present findings suggest that the immunoprotective effects of OS1423 and OS1213 are not strictly dependent on bacterial viability. Under the same CP-induced immunosuppression model, the live strains reproduced the protective effects observed previously (Kang et al., 2026), confirming the reproducibility of their strain-specific functionality. The corresponding heat-killed preparations also showed immunoprotective activity across selected immune indicators, particularly cytokine recovery, splenocyte proliferation, and systemic immunoglobulin responses, suggesting that strain-associated immunomodulatory activity can be retained after thermal inactivation. A plausible explanation is that heat-stable MAMPs, including bacterial structural components such as peptidoglycan and lipoteichoic acid, may remain sufficiently preserved after heat treatment and contribute to host immune stimulation (Gao et al., 2022; Prajapati et al., 2023; Ren et al., 2019; Rocha-Ramírez et al., 2020; Teame et al., 2020). Previous studies have reported that these bacterial cell wall-associated components can be recognized by host PRRs and may contribute to the immunomodulatory activity of inactivated probiotic preparations (Bae et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2020; L. Zhang et al., 2023). Therefore, the present findings are consistent with the possibility that structurally preserved microbial ligands contributed to the observed viability-independent immunoprotective activity. However, MAMP/PRR-mediated signaling was not directly demonstrated in this study, and receptor-level validation using toll-like receptor 2 (TLR2), TLR4, or nucleotide-binding oligomerization domain 2 (NOD2) inhibition, receptor blocking, or downstream signaling assays will be required to define the molecular pathways involved.

In addition to comparing the live and heat-killed forms of each strain, this study also examined whether combining OS1423 and OS1213 could provide broader or enhanced immunomodulatory effects. The rationale for this approach was that structurally distinct strains may contribute different immunostimulatory signals, and their combination could potentially expand the range of host immune activation. In the in vitro macrophage assay, formal statistical evaluation using the LPS-anchored Bliss independence model with bootstrap confidence intervals did not establish strict synergy for any of the three cytokines in either formulation (Fig. 3 and Table 2). Nevertheless, the HSA analysis revealed that the heat-killed combination produced significantly enhanced TNF-α and IL-1β output beyond the more responsive single strain, whereas the live formulation showed no such enhancement. This pattern suggests that the coexistence of structurally distinct microbial components in the inactivated state may provide complementary, rather than synergistic, stimulation of macrophage cytokine output, particularly when viability-dependent variability is reduced. However, this pattern was not consistently translated into superior in vivo efficacy across all immune indicators. Both single-strain and combined heat-killed formulations showed protective effects against CP-induced immunosuppression, including recovery of splenic cellularity, cytokine production, and systemic antibody responses, but the combined formulation did not uniformly outperform the individual treatments. This difference between in vitro and in vivo responses likely reflects the greater biological complexity of the whole-animal system, in which immune outcomes are shaped not only by direct host–microbe interactions but also by intestinal environmental factors, host physiology, and microbiota-mediated modulation. In this context, it is plausible that the two strains provide complementary rather than universally synergistic signals in vivo. Structurally distinct microbial ligands derived from each strain may stimulate partially overlapping immune pathways, thereby broadening the range of immune stimulation without necessarily generating a stronger effect in every parameter (Teame et al., 2020). Therefore, the present findings suggest that strain combination may increase the diversity of immunomodulatory inputs, whereas the overall magnitude of in vivo immunoprotection remains strongly influenced by the intrinsic functional properties of the individual strains.

The gut microbiota findings provide additional ecological support for the immunoprotective effects observed in this study. CP administration induced a dysbiotic shift characterized by the expansion of taxa associated with disturbed intestinal environments, including the Clostridia vadinBB60 group and members of the Muribaculaceae lineage (Lagkouvardos et al., 2019; Yang et al., 2013). Such alterations are consistent with the broad disruption of intestinal homeostasis caused by CP and reflect an imbalanced microbial ecosystem under immunosuppressive stress. In contrast, probiotic supplementation promoted the recovery of commensal bacterial groups linked to intestinal stability, particularly members of the Lachnospiraceae family and the Clostridium leptum group within the Oscillospiraceae lineage (Louis and Flint, 2009; Vacca et al., 2020). These taxa are of particular importance because they are widely recognized as major contributors to colonic fermentation and are frequently associated with the production of SCFAs, especially butyrate (Louis and Flint, 2017). SCFAs are key microbial metabolites involved in the maintenance of epithelial barrier integrity, regulation of mucosal immunity, and modulation of systemic immune responses (Cho et al., 2022; Kwon et al., 2024). Therefore, the restoration of taxa such as Dorea and Blautia within Lachnospiraceae, together with members of the C. leptum group, may reflect partial recovery of microbial functional guilds that support intestinal metabolic balance and immune homeostasis (Louis and Flint, 2017; Vacca et al., 2020). This interpretation is further supported by the concurrent suppression of dysbiosis-associated taxa, particularly the Clostridia vadinBB60 group, following heat-killed probiotic supplementation, suggesting that the observed microbiota changes were not limited to isolated taxonomic fluctuations but instead represented a broader ecological reorganization toward a more favorable intestinal environment. In addition, the PICRUSt2-based functional prediction, although indirect, showed numerical recovery trends in SCFA-related pathways, which is broadly consistent with previous reports indicating that enrichment of SCFA-associated bacterial groups often accompanies improved microbial fermentation capacity and intestinal metabolic activity (Louis and Flint, 2017; Vacca et al., 2020). At the same time, these functional interpretations should be made cautiously because the present study did not directly quantify microbial metabolites such as butyrate or propionate. Nevertheless, the overall concordance between taxonomic restoration, predictive functional trends, and immune-associated correlations supports the view that heat-killed probiotic supplementation was associated with microbiota reorganization toward bacterial groups linked to intestinal homeostasis under immunosuppressive conditions.

Beyond the mechanistic insights into immune modulation and microbial restructuring, the incorporation of HK_OS1423 into an adlay tea matrix provides an important perspective on the practical application of heat-killed probiotics in functional foods. Adlay (Coix lacryma-jobi L.), or Job's tears, has long been utilized as a grain-based beverage ingredient and has been reported to possess various health-promoting properties, including anti-inflammatory and immune-supportive effects (Chung et al., 2011). However, the production of grain-based teas typically involves thermal processing and storage conditions that can significantly reduce the viability of live probiotic cells (Tripathi and Giri, 2014). Under such conditions, maintaining a consistent dose of viable probiotics in the final product becomes technically challenging. In contrast, heat-killed probiotic preparations, which do not depend on cellular viability, can be added after the thermal brewing step and offer greater formulation flexibility in such ready-to-drink beverage systems. In the present study, the incorporation of HK_OS1423 into the prepared adlay tea matrix was associated with retention of selected immunomodulatory activities, particularly mucosal cytokine recovery, splenocyte proliferation, and serum IgA restoration. This interpretation is consistent with the endpoint-specific results, as NK cell activity did not reach statistical significance in the Food + HK_OS1423 group and serum IgG recovery was significant only in the PBS-based HK_OS1423 group. Because heat-killed cells do not depend on cellular viability, they are conceptually well suited for incorporation into brewed beverages such as adlay tea, although direct stability testing under thermal brewing and during storage was not performed in the present study and is required to support broader claims about thermal-processing compatibility. In addition, considering the reported bioactivity of adlay (Chung et al., 2011), the immunomodulatory effects observed in the Food + HK_OS1423 group may reflect the combined activity of HK_OS1423 and adlay-derived components, and the food matrix may provide a favorable context for the expression of immunomodulatory activity (Pihurov et al., 2026). Consequently, these findings support the feasibility of incorporating heat-killed probiotic preparations as stable functional ingredients in grain-based beverages designed to support immune health.

In summary, the present findings support the potential of heat-killed probiotic preparations as functional microbial ingredients capable of delivering selected probiotic-associated benefits without strict dependence on bacterial viability. In the CP-induced immunosuppressed model, HK_OS1423 and HK_OS1213 retained immunoprotective activity across selected immune readouts, suggesting that heat-killed formulations can preserve strain-associated immunomodulatory functionality after loss of viability. The incorporation of HK_OS1423 into an adlay tea matrix further supports the feasibility of using heat-killed probiotics in grain-based beverage matrices, where maintaining viable cells is technically challenging. In addition, the observed gut microbiota reorganization toward bacterial groups associated with intestinal homeostasis provides supportive ecological context for the immune-related findings.

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these results. First, although MAMP/PRR-related recognition provides a plausible explanation for the retained activity of the heat-killed preparations, receptor-level validation was not performed; therefore, future studies using TLR2, TLR4, or NOD2 inhibition, receptor-blocking assays, and downstream signaling analyses such as NF-κB or MAPK activation will be required to define the molecular pathways involved. Second, microbial metabolites such as SCFAs were not directly quantified, and PICRUSt2-based functional predictions should therefore be interpreted as inferred functional potential rather than measured metabolic activity. Third, the animal study was conducted using a relatively small cohort of male BALB/c mice, without a formal a priori power calculation or fully blinded histological and functional assessments. Although the group size was selected based on previous CP-induced immunosuppression studies, approved housing conditions, and the 3R principle, future studies using larger, statistically powered cohorts including both sexes and blinded outcome assessment will be needed to validate the generalizability and robustness of these findings. Finally, the food application experiment did not include an adlay tea-only control, and direct thermal- or storage-stability testing of HK_OS1423 in the adlay tea matrix was not performed. Therefore, future studies using a fully factorial design with adlay-only and HK_OS1423-only controls, together with direct stability validation, will be necessary to distinguish the individual contributions of HK_OS1423 and adlay-derived components and to support broader claims regarding processed or shelf-stable food applications. Nevertheless, the overall findings provide a scientific basis for expanding the concept of probiotic functionality beyond cellular viability and support the development of functional foods utilizing heat-killed microbial ingredients.

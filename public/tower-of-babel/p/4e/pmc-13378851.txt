Comparing the impact of 360-degree evaluation and conventional methods on the performance evaluation of Iranian nurses
Authors: Shiva Khaleghparast, Mahsa Kamali, Samira Chaibakhsh, Leila Sari, Fahimeh Khosrobeigi Bozchaloei, Saeideh Mazloomzadeh, Samaneh Karimian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Performance evaluation stands as a vital process for health delivery organizations as a lack of regular assessments can be likened to a malady afflicting the entity. The current study compared the use of two methods of evaluation, a 360-degree and a conventional method among Iranian nurses.MATERIAL AND METHODS:Based on the 360-degree performance evaluation approach, the current study involved two distinct participants, the evaluators and the evaluatees, evaluating the effectiveness of nurses using the 360-degree model, which encompasses input from Head Nurses, colleagues, and self-assessment, as well as feedback from patients and their family members. In total, 22 cardiac care units were selected by census sampling method. According to the total number of nurses in each ward, the nurses of each ward who were eligible were randomly selected. Ultimately, 137 nurses were assessed based on the two methods of performance evaluation, 360-degree and conventional methods simultaneously. All the data were analyzed using SPSS version 27.RESULTS:Approximately 61% (n = 84) of participants were under the age of 30. The majority of the participants were female (93.4%). The average score of all dimensions was found to be lower when assessed by the head nurse compared to the evaluations from 360 questionnaires. Notably, the most significant disparities between head nurse and 360-degree ratings were identified in individual characteristics (0.2) and humanity skills (0.1). Despite the adequate level of agreement demonstrated by the Interclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) for total score (0.77), humanity skills (0.84), perceptual skills (0.75), and compliance with hospital rules and regulations (0.8), there was only a moderate level of agreement for total score and perceptual skills.CONCLUSION:Based on the results of the present study, the groups have different viewpoints regarding the evaluation of nurses and this evaluation method may provide useful information about nurses’ performance from different aspects. Hence, healthcare policy makers can benefit this method of performance evaluation to improve the clinical performance.

Introduction

Hospitals play a crucial role within the healthcare delivery system, and neglecting their importance would hinder any attempt to enhance the system’s operations.[1] The utilization of performance appraisal, a quantitative tool, equips managers with the necessary data to assess and oversee the hospital’s current functions and activities.[2] Performance evaluation stands as a vital process for all organizations, particularly in the realm of hospitals, as a lack of regular assessments can be likened to a malady afflicting the entity.[3] Misconduct in the performance evaluation procedure can result in heightened stress levels, job dissatisfaction, diminished employee drive, and reduced overall effectiveness.[45] A qualitative study conducted in Iran revealed that a primary obstacle in the screening program for chronic diseases like hypertension and diabetes was the inadequacies in the staff’s performance evaluation system.[6] Another study conducted in Iran demonstrated that the indicators for staff performance evaluation played a significant role in organizational entrepreneurship.[7] The impact of performance evaluation on organizational outcomes can vary, with both positive and negative effects contingent on the chosen evaluation method. Consequently, assessing the performance of healthcare providers poses a challenge due to the demand for more efficient evaluation strategies.[8910] The results of the review study showed that improving the performance of the nurses’ supervisor is essential to improve the happiness of the staff, the quality of the care of patients, and the delivery of healthcare as a holistic approach.[11] The study in Egypt also showed that there is a strong correlation between the performance review system and the empowerment of nurses. This is why the performance review system is successfully implemented, which has a positive impact on the nurses’ empowerment and satisfaction at work.[12] So, we decided to use alternative performance evaluation for nurses in our setting.

360-degree evaluations have been widely adopted since 1980 to assess professional competencies across different settings. This evaluation method distinguishes itself from traditional approaches by incorporating multiple perspectives and information sources. By integrating these varied viewpoints, a holistic understanding of the process, organization, or individual being evaluated can be achieved.[131415] Typically, the 360-degree evaluation process involves the use of questionnaires that encompass self-assessments from the professionals under review, evaluations from their superiors, peers within the same professional sphere, and sometimes subordinates or patients. The amalgamation of these assessments culminates in an overall performance score for the individual.[16] A study conducted in Turkey highlighted the benefits of employing the 360-degree evaluation method as a comprehensive approach to assessing the clinical performance of nursing students.[17] Similarly, research from Iran emphasized the utility and positivity of the 360-degree evaluation approach in evaluating the performance of head nurses.[15]

Since nurses are the front-line healthcare providers, the quality of care is largely determined by how well they perform. The nursing profession is generally recognized as one of the most stressful professions in the world. It is therefore important to have positive working conditions to ensure the commitment and wellbeing of clinical nurses.[18] As such, it is critical to pay close attention to the system for performance evaluation.[19] Nursing evaluations in Iran have always been carried out as an organizational responsibility. The Rajaei Cardiovascular Medical and Research Institute is a national referral center that is required to evaluate the performance of nursing staff members seeking career advancement (accreditation for supervisory roles, departmental transfers, and material and spiritual rewards). So, the current study compared the use of a 360-degree evaluation to a conventional method among Iranian nurses.

Material and Methods

Study design and setting

The objective of the current study was the implementation of a cross-sectional study design between June and December 2023 to compare the use of a 360-degree evaluation to a conventional method among Iranian nurses. The Rajaei Cardiovascular Medical and Research Institute in Iran served as the study’s setting.

Study participants and sampling

The current study involved two distinct participants: the evaluators and the evaluatees. The head nurses, three clinical nurse peers, patients, and self-assessments made up the evaluators. Among the people evaluated were clinical nurses.

Each hospital ward’s Head Nurse rates nurses in an evaluation conducted by the Head Nurse.

At least three coworkers rank nurses in an assessment completed by their peers.

Nurses evaluate themselves when they self-assess. They assessed themselves according to their opinions. In the course of an evaluation conducted by the patients and their companions, patient and companion interviews were conducted.

The requirements for inclusion were a minimum of 1 year of work experience in a designated ward for clinical nurses, a minimum of 1 year of collaboration experience for peers of clinical nurses, and a minimum of 1 year experience as head nurse of clinical nurses for head nurses. The patients’ and families’ complete consciousness, complete mental and physical capacity for decision-making, and the evaluatee’s employment as the chosen patient or family’s nurse for at least one work shift were additional inclusion criteria. The unwillingness to collaborate, the presence of incomplete questionnaire responses, and the presence of pediatric, neonatal, and outpatient wards were deemed to be exclusion criteria.

Based on the results of Rahati et al. study entitled Assessment of the Performance of Nurses based on the 360-degree model and Fuzzy Multi-Criteria Decision-making Method and selecting qualified nurses,[20] considering the power of 0.80, α = 0.05, and d = 0.1, the sample size of 186 participants was calculated [Figure 1]. In total, 22 cardiac care units were selected by census sampling method. According to the total number of nurses in each ward, the nurses of each ward who were eligible were randomly selected by the table of random numbers selected.

Data collection tool and technique

The current investigation conducted a 360-degree assessment based on five specific criteria as stated.[20]

Technical skill

Technical skill, in the context of this study, refers to the knowledge and capacity to execute particular tasks, such as handling medical equipment, executing treatment procedures correctly, and maintaining patient records.

Humanity skills

Humanity skills, on the other hand, denote the capability to interact with individuals and groups, shape their perceptions, and inspire them. This research evaluated humanity skills by analyzing effective communication with patients, patient education, maintenance of patient privacy, respectful interactions with colleagues, and collaboration with peers.

Perceptual skills

Perceptual skills involve the ability to comprehend the intricacies of an entire organization and grasp all aspects and components of activities as a unified entity. This aspect was assessed based on decision-making in ambiguous scenarios, adaptability to changes, identification of strengths and weaknesses, and creativity and innovation.

Individual characteristics

Individual characteristics are defined as the personal attributes of nurses. The study considered individual characteristics such as honesty, emotional regulation, empathy, patience, and prioritization of organizational interests over personal ones.

Compliance with the rules and regulations of the hospital

Compliance with the hospital’s rules and regulations encompasses the guidelines that employees must adhere to upon entering, working in, and exiting the ward. This study encompassed actions such as introducing the patient to the substitute nurse before leaving, wearing appropriate hospital attire, ensuring patient safety, and conducting regular patient visits.

We used the 360-Degree Model and DEMATEL, ANP, and VIKOR combination approach in the fuzzy environment to assess and select qualified nurses. By utilizing a five-point Likert scale based on five evaluation criteria, ranging from very weak (score 1) to excellent (score 5), a comprehensive performance assessment tool was developed. The scale comprised a total of 21 questions, divided among technical skills (3 questions), humanity skills (5 questions), perceptual skills (4 questions), individual characteristics (5 questions), and compliance with hospital rules and regulations (4 questions). Each criterion and evaluator were assigned specific weights: technical skill (weight: 0.166), humanity skills (weight: 0.273), perceptual skills (weight: 0.241), individual characteristics (0.195), compliance with hospital rules and regulations (weight: 0.125), head nurses (weight: 0.521), clinical nurse peers (weight: 0.198), patients/families (weight: 0.239), and clinical nurses (weight: 0.042). Scores were calculated by multiplying the obtained score by the criterion weight and the evaluator weight. The reliability of this scale was reported as α-Cronbach = 0.78 among nursing students in Iran.[21] In another Iranian study, it was 0.86.[13] In addition, the demographic data that were gathered consisted of age, gender, work experience, and educational level.

The five researcher nurses supervised the correct data collection. They explained the purpose of the research and the evaluation methods to each participant. The performance of clinical nurses was assessed by four groups: head nurses, three clinical nurse peers, self-assessment, and patients/families. For clinical nurse peers, six participants ranked the performance of each nurse.

Ethical consideration

All of the data collection procedures for this study were carried out with the Rajaei Cardiovascular Medical and Research Center’s Ethics Committee’s approval (IR.RHC.REC.1402.024). All methods were performed following the relevant guidelines and regulations. Written informed consent was obtained from the participants before the research. In addition, research objectives were explained to the subjects, and they were ensured of confidentiality terms regarding their personal information.

Statistical analysis method

In this study, all the gathered data were analyzed using SPSS version 27 using descriptive statistics (frequency, frequency percentage, mean, and standard deviation) and Intra Class Correlation Coefficient (ICC) for comparing head nurse and 360 questionnaire scores. The level of significance in this study was considered 0.05.

Comparing head nurse and 360 questionnaire scores

The average score of all dimensions was found to be lower when assessed by the head nurse compared to the evaluations from 360 questionnaires. Notably, the most significant disparities between head nurse and 360-degree ratings were identified in individual characteristics (0.2) and humanity skills (0.1). Despite the adequate level of agreement demonstrated by the Interclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC) for total score (0.77), humanity skills (0.84), perceptual skills (0.75), and compliance with hospital rules and regulations (0.8), there was only a moderate level of agreement for total score and perceptual skills. The ICC values for individual characteristics and technical skills fell below the established acceptable threshold as illustrated in Table 2.

Discussion

The present study aimed to compare two assessment methods, 360-degree assessment and conventional one, among Iranian nurses. The results of the study showed that the greatest agreement was achieved on human skills and compliance with hospital rules and regulations. The agreement index also had a moderate but acceptable value with the perceptual skills and the overall score. However, the lowest agreement was found in terms of technical skills and individual characteristics.

The evaluation of performance was generally difficult. In this respect, the Iranian study has shown that the use of routine indicators alone cannot give a good picture of performance. Policy makers typically look for key decision-making methods in managing key indicators.[22]

Results from a United States study of pediatric residents showed that different healthcare evaluators had different perspectives, such that physicians and nurses rated pediatric residents’ professionalism, interpersonal, and communication skills higher than family ratings of the patients perspectives of the residents.[23] The results of an Iranian study of final semester nursing bachelor’s students showed that there was a significant relationship between the evaluation of students, colleagues, and family members and the evaluation of clinical instructors. They also reported that the 360-degree method evaluates multiple perspectives of care.[13] Similarly, in the present study, the evaluation score of the 360-degree method was higher than that of the conventional method. The agreement index also showed a moderate and acceptable value.

One of the highest compliance index scores was adherence to hospital rules and regulations. Since the hospital’s rules, including wearing a uniform, arriving at the ward on time, and regularly staying at the patient’s bedside, are more specific, the approval coefficient was higher from the perspective of the head nurses and the perspective of the 360-degree approach. However, the lowest agreement index value was reported for technical skills and individual characteristics. As patients have less understanding of nurses’ technical skills and their characteristics, this may have led to a decrease in the agreement coefficient between the head nurse assessment and the 360-degree approach. On the other hand, nursing colleagues are more aware of their responsibility than patients or their families.[24]

In the present study, the greatest difference in agreement on individual characteristics was found among caregivers aged over 30 years compared with those aged under 30. This significant difference may be because the head nurse is older than 30 years and the perception of individual characteristics is more similar to that of the nurses under 30 years old, similar to the generation gap in medical sciences.[25]

The largest differences between the two levels of education were noted in technical skills. A Master's degree in nursing has enhanced their skills and allowed them to provide more skilled and evidence-based care to the benefit of clinical practice.[26] Another study also showed that nurses with higher educational levels perform more complex nursing tasks, both those requiring higher managerial skills and those requiring high-level competences.[27] This may be the reason for the high agreement coefficient between nurses with master’s degrees compared to nurses with bachelor’s degrees.

Limitations and recommendations

Since nurses are the largest service provider group in the hospital, evaluating their performance is also necessary for the quality of hospital services, especially in special departments that require more sensitive care. Therefore, this method can replace traditional methods. It is suggested that in future studies, complementary 360-degree methods be used to develop different dimensions of performance evaluation.

The present study had some limitations. Although the rating scale based on the 360-degree approach demonstrated acceptable validity and reliability, confirmatory factor analysis still needs to be performed because larger sample sizes are required. Despite assurances provided to supervisors and fellow nurses regarding the confidentiality of the questionnaires completed as part of the evaluation process, apprehensions about potential breaches of privacy remain prevalent among the participants. This unease appears to stem from fears that the evaluated individual might discover the results, potentially leading to interpersonal discomfort. Consequently, this apprehension has significantly hindered the willingness of many of Fred’s colleagues to engage in the research and complete the questionnaires. Such concerns highlight the critical importance of establishing robust trust and transparency within the research framework, as well as the need for effective communication strategies to alleviate fears surrounding confidentiality and encourage active participation. Addressing these issues is essential for obtaining a comprehensive and unbiased dataset, which is vital for the validity and reliability of the research findings.

Conclusion

The results showed that the groups had different views on the nurses’ performance and that this evaluation method may provide useful information on the nueses’ performance in various aspects. Therefore, healthcare policy makers can benefit from this performance evaluation method to improve their clinical performance.

According to the findings, this level of agreement is indicative of moderate agreement, which may be a valid reason for using a 360-degree scoring system, where the evaluated individual is evaluated by several parties (head nurse, the patient, the colleagues, and themselves) and weighted according to a variety of criteria, depending on the importance of the various factors. Since the evaluation was based on the opinion of only one evaluator, the final grade seems more equitable.

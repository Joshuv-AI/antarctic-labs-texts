Response To the Letter to the Editor Entitled “First Report of Daratumumab Therapy in Refractory THSD7A-Positive Membranous Nephropathy”
Authors: Fatima Ayub, John Arthur, Md Rajibul Hasan, Luis A Juncos, Joseph Hunter Holthoff
Published: 2025

The Author Replies:

We thank the authors for highlighting the case series by Salhi et al.,1,2 describing 5 additional patients with refractory phospholipase A2 receptor antibody-positive primary membranous nephropathy successfully treated with bortezomib. Taken collectively, the earlier single case report by Salhi et al.,3 the subsequently published series by Salhi et al. and our case report expands the collective experience to 9 reported patients. Most patients showed favorable outcomes following plasma cell-directed therapy. Notably, the Salhi et al. series provides longer follow-up, with sustained immunologic responses in 4 of 5 patients, further strengthening the evidence for use of bortezomib.

Several distinctions emerge between our case4 and previous reports. All cases in the Salhi series were male, consistent with most published experiences; Hartono et al.5 reported a female patient treated with bortezomib, but without biopsy or serologic confirmation of phospholipase A2 receptor–mediated disease and without documented failure of conventional therapies. Our patient demonstrated an unusually rapid and robust response to a short, incomplete course of bortezomib, with anti-phospholipase A2 receptor titers and proteinuria improving within just 2 weeks. Much faster than previously reported.

Whether these findings may reasonably be extrapolated to female patients with similar disease trajectories remains an important question and opens new avenues for clinical research. These cases raise the broader mechanistic question of why B-cell depletion achieves remission in some patients but not in others. Systematic flow cytometric analysis of peripheral blood and bone marrow aspirates in patients who fail anti–B-cell therapy, compared with those who respond, may help define cellular and immunophenotypic differences.

Collectively, these cases strengthen the rationale for bortezomib in high-risk or treatment-refractory primary membranous nephropathy and highlight key future directions: early bone marrow biopsy to identify pathogenic plasma cell populations, consideration of plasma cell–targeted therapy before rituximab in selected settings, and the use of flow cytometry to refine therapeutic strategies. Further studies in patients receiving plasma cell–directed therapy could further elucidate resistant disease pathways and facilitate more personalized therapeutic approaches.

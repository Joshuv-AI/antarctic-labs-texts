Echocardiographic insights of right heart dynamics after transcatheter ASD closure: A prospective study
Authors: Ingy Etman, Tarek ElZawawy, Amr Zaki, Ahmed Meawad ElEmam, Ahmed Mokhtar AbdelAzeem
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTranscatheter atrial septal defect (ASD) closure is a minimally invasive procedure, yet variability in right heart reverse remodeling post-procedure remains understudied.ObjectiveTo evaluate the impact of transcatheter ASD closure on right ventricular (RV) parameters and tricuspid regurgitation (TR) severity over six months, and to identify predictors of RV reverse remodeling and TR improvement.MethodsThis prospective study included 60 adult patients who underwent successful transcatheter ASD closure. Transthoracic echocardiography was performed at baseline, and at 1, 3, and 6 months post-procedure to assess RV dimensions, systolic function, and TR severity.ResultsSignificant reductions were observed in RV dimensions—including tricuspid annular diameter, RV basal diameter, RV mid-cavity diameter, and RV outflow tract diameter—at 6 months (p < 0.001). RV systolic function declined at 1 month and remained stable thereafter. TR severity improved significantly at 1 and 3 months. Predictors of RV reverse remodeling at 6 months included younger age (OR: 0.949; 95 % CI: 0.913–0.986), sinus rhythm (OR: 0.113; 95 % CI: 0.022–0.569), lower shunt ratio (OR: 0.259; 95 % CI: 0.068–0.993). Predictors of TR improvement included the same factors, in addition to smaller tricuspid annular diameter and smaller right atrial end-systolic area.ConclusionTranscatheter ASD closure leads to significant right heart reverse remodeling and TR improvement. Younger age, sinus rhythm, and smaller preprocedural chamber dimensions predict RV reverse remodeling and improvement of TR, supporting their role in patient selection and clinical decision-making.Keywords: Atrial septal defect, Transcatheter closure, Right heart remodeling, Tricuspid regurgitation

Introduction

Atrial septal defect (ASD) represents 25–30 % of congenital heart disease in the adult population [1]. It results from an abnormal opening in the atrial septum commonly at the site of fossa ovalis (secundum ASD), leading to left-to-right shunting of blood. If left untreated, this condition can result in right ventricular (RV) volume overload, pulmonary hypertension, and right heart failure [2]. Transcatheter closure of ASD has emerged as a widely accepted, less invasive alternative to surgical repair, offering significant improvements in patient outcomes. Nevertheless, the effects of ASD closure on right ventricular function and tricuspid regurgitation (TR) remain an area of ongoing research.

Echocardiography plays a pivotal role being the initial imaging modality for assessment of cardiac morphology and function and providing detailed insights into RV reverse remodeling and the progression or resolution of TR following ASD closure [3]. While many studies have demonstrated favorable outcomes after percutaneous ASD closure, variability in RV adaptation and TR improvement necessitate further investigation.

Aim of the study

This study aims to evaluate different right ventricular parameters and TR through echocardiographic assessment before and after transcatheter ASD closure. By analyzing these parameters, we can identify factors that influence RV reverse remodeling and TR improvement, helping to determine which patients may benefit from closer follow-up or additional tricuspid valve intervention. The findings of this study may therefore guide treatment planning by distinguishing patients suitable for isolated ASD closure from those who might require concomitant or subsequent valve procedures, ultimately supporting more individualized and timely patient care.

Methods

Study design

Our study is a prospective multicentre observational study that included 60 patients who underwent successful transcatheter ASD closure at National Heart Institute (Cairo, Egypt) and Alexandria University Hospitals (Alexandria, Egypt) between May 2022 and June 2024.

Study population

The inclusion criteria were patients (≥ 16 years) with ASD secundum who have signs of right ventricular volume overload and underwent successful transcatheter closure. Patients who had residual left to right shunt following transcatheter closure were excluded from our study. We also excluded patients who had other hemodynamically significant valvular lesions, angiographically significant coronary artery disease, right ventricular or left ventricular dysfunction and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).

Ethics approval and consent

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Faculty of Medicine, Alexandria, Egypt (IRB number: 00012098, FWA number: 00018699). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients before enrolment.

Data collection and echocardiography

All patients underwent detailed history taking and physical examination. Resting ECG was done to all patients. Two-dimensional transthoracic and two-dimensional / three-dimensional transoesophageal echocardiography was done to all patients to assess candidacy for transcatheter closure with special emphasis on RV dimensions and systolic function, presence of TR and its severity and ASD morphology. Echocardiographic evaluation was done using S5–1 probe (sector array transthoracic cardiac probe) and X8-2t probe (next-generation xMATRIX transesophageal probe) of Philips EPIQ CVx ultrasound machine (Philips Healthcare, Eindhoven, The Netherlands). Echocardiographic measurements were performed in accordance with the recommendations of the American Society of Echocardiography (ASE) [4]. To reduce respiratory variations, measurements were taken at the end of expiration across three consecutive cardiac cycles, with the average value recorded. Tricuspid annular diameter, RV basal and mid diameters were measured at end-diastole in the RV- focused apical four chamber view. In the same view, the right atrial (RA) end-systolic area was measured at end-systole. Proximal right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT) diameter was measured in the parasternal short-axis view. The presence and severity of TR was assessed semi-quantitatively in several views based on valve morphology, visual assessment of the color-flow TR jet, vena contracta, the shape and intensity of the continuous wave Doppler TR jet signal and the hepatic venous flow pattern. Tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE), fractional area change (FAC) and tricuspid annular systolic motion velocity (S′) were used to assess RV systolic function. Pulmonary artery systolic pressure was estimated using modified Bernoulli equation after measuring the peak systolic velocity of the TR jet. Finally, shunt ratio (Qp/Qs) was calculated by echocardiography using continuity equation. Follow-up transthoracic echocardiographic assessment was conducted, at 1 month, 3 months and 6 months after transcatheter ASD closure, using the same methodology to ensure consistency and comparability of measurements. To reduce measurement variability, all echocardiographic measurements throughout this study were performed by the same experienced echocardiographer, thereby eliminating inter-observer differences throughout the dataset. Right ventricular reverse remodeling was defined as a ≥ 10 % reduction in any RV linear dimension (RV basal diameter, RV mid-cavity diameter, or proximal RVOT diameter) at any follow-up time point relative to baseline. The choice of a 10 % threshold was made to ensure that observed changes exceeded the expected intra- and inter-observer variability of two-dimensional echocardiographic measurements. Furthermore, previous echocardiographic and cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) studies have also used an approximate 10 % cut-off to differentiate true measurement changes from variability attributable to measurement error. [5,6]. Improvement of TR was defined as a decrease of at least one grade in TR severity on follow-up echocardiography, regardless of the time point.

ASD closure

All patients underwent transcatheter ASD closure under general anaesthesia using both transoesophageal and fluoroscopic guidance. Procedural data was collected including the type, number and size of the occluder devices used and presence of intraprocedural complications (e.g.: residual shunt). Post-procedural treatment included a six-month regimen of dual antiplatelet therapy with aspirin (100 mg daily) and clopidogrel (75 mg daily).

Statistical analysis

Data was analyzed using IBM SPSS software package version 20.0. (Armonk, NY: IBM Corp). Qualitative data were described using number and percent. Quantitative data were described using range (minimum and maximum), mean, standard deviation, median and interquartile range (IQR). Statistical analysis was performed using repeated-measures ANOVA for normally distributed quantitative variables to compare multiple time points, with a Post Hoc LSD test for pairwise comparisons. For non-normally distributed quantitative variables, the Friedman test was employed to assess changes across multiple time points. When significant differences were detected, Dunn's post hoc test was used for pairwise comparisons. For binary outcome variables, Cochran's Q test, a non-parametric test for repeated measures of dichotomous data, was applied, followed by Dunn's test for post hoc analysis when appropriate. A P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Univariate logistic regression analysis was performed to identify baseline clinical and echocardiographic variables associated with RV reverse remodeling and TR improvement. Candidate variables for regression were chosen based on prior literature and clinical relevance. Variables with p < 0.1 in univariate analysis were subsequently entered into multivariate logistic regression models to determine independent predictors. Multicollinearity among independent variables was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF), with values >5 considered indicative of significant collinearity. Results were presented as odds ratios (OR) with 95 % confidence intervals (CI).

Results

Table 1 shows the baseline clinical characteristics of the studied population. Forty-one (68.3 %) of the enrolled patients were females while 19 (31.7 %) were male. The age of the patients ranged from 16 to 62 years old with a mean age 35.57 ± 14.94 years. The calculated body surface area (according to Du Bois formula) ranged from 1.51 to 2.13 m2 (mean of 1.79 ± 0.14 m2). The most common presenting symptoms were effort intolerance in 41.7 % (25) and dyspnea in 36.7 % (22) of patients. Most patients (58.3 %) were in New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional class II. Seventeen (28.3 %) out of the sixty patients had permanent atrial fibrillation (AF) (as documented by resting ECG) or paroxysmal AF (as documented by Holter ECG). Other comorbidities included hypertension, diabetes mellitus type II and coronary artery disease (non-obstructive stenosis of the left anterior descending artery).

The estimated systolic pulmonary artery pressure (ESPAP) showed a substantial decline over time. One month after ASD closure, a sharp decline was observed from 32.88 ± 9.48 mmHg before closure to 25.33 ± 7.68 mmHg (p < 0.001). This downward trend continued at three months (21.62 ± 7.46 mmHg) and six months (19.82 ± 7.35 mmHg), with all changes remaining statistically significant.

All patients had TR before ASD closure, with severity ranging from mild to severe. One- and three-months post-closure, there was a statistically significant improvement in TR severity, with p-values of 0.024 and 0.004, respectively. The improvement continued at six months; however, this improvement was not statistically significant (p = 0.317). Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed to identify predictors of RV reverse remodeling and improvement of TR severity as shown in Table 3, Table 4 respectively. Age, presence of AF and shunt ratio were significant predictors of right heart reverse remodeling. Predictors of TR improvement included the same factors, in addition to smaller tricuspid annular diameter and right atrial end-systolic area. Multivariate analysis revealed that age, shunt ratio and presence of atrial fibrillation were independent predictors of right heart reverse remodeling following transcatheter ASD closure. However, none of the variables remained significant in the multivariate analysis for TR, suggesting that no single factor independently predicted improvement of TR after ASD closure.

During the 6-month follow-up period, there were no cases of all-cause mortality or heart failure rehospitalization. However, two patients (2.4 %) developed new-onset AF, which was successfully managed with pharmacological cardioversion. Moreover, the functional status improved following transcatheter ASD closure. At 6-month follow-up, 10 patients (16.7 %) were in NYHA class I, 45 (75 %) in class II, and 5 (8.3 %) in class III, with no patients in class IV.

Discussion

Our study showed that there is significant decline in all measured RV dimensions suggesting a steady reversal of right heart dilation over the six-month follow-up period with complete normalisation of RA and RV dimensions in some patients. This reduction reflects unloading of the right heart after closure of the left to right shunt, leading to decreased volume overload and improved cardiac geometry. At the cellular level, the decrease in mechanical stress on the myocardium is accompanied by a downregulation of neurohormonal factors (e.g., angiotensin II and natriuretic peptides) that drive remodeling. Changes in myocardial fibre alignment and adjustments in the extracellular matrix further support the reverse remodeling process [7]. Our findings are consistent with other several studies confirming that RV reverse remodeling occurs relatively early following percutaneous closure, typically within 3 to 6 months. Akula et al. studied 73 patients post-transcatheter ASD closure, noting significant reductions in RV dimensions and RA end-systolic area over six months [8]. Similarly, Al-Samman et al. found significant declines in indexed RA end-systolic area and RV basal/mid dimensions in 100 patients at 1 and 6 months [9]. Bosshardt et al. studied 454 patients, reporting immediate post-procedure improvement in RV and RA dimensions, with continued reverse remodeling at a median follow-up of 569 days [10].

All patients included in our study had normal baseline RV systolic function as measured by TAPSE, FAC and RV TDI S′ velocity. Nevertheless, there was a statistically significant decline of the RV systolic function after one month of transcatheter ASD closure, but no significant variation of these indices was noted later. Our results are concordant to those reported by Akula et al. and Bosshardt et al. [8,10]. On the contrary, Balcı et al. reported that there was continuous improvement of the TAPSE of the studied cases throughout the 3-month follow-up period [11]. Following transcatheter ASD closure, the RV experiences an acute decrease in preload, leading to transient decline in RV function due to altered loading conditions. However, these parameters remain within the normal range according to the ASE guidelines [4].

Our study showed progressive improvement of the severity of TR over the 6-month follow-up and the most statistically significant improvement was 1 month and 3 months after ASD closure. A previously published cohort study that was conducted on 113 adult patients with moderate or severe TR who underwent transcatheter ASD closure with median follow-up period up to 30 months [12]. Before closure, 36 patients had severe TR while 77 patients had moderate TR. Among the 36 patients with severe TR, 17 patients (47 %) improved to mild TR, while 13 patients (36 %) improved to moderate TR. Similarly, among the 77 patients with moderate TR, 81 % (62 patients) experienced an improvement to mild TR. Similarly, Abohamar et al. studied 60 patients following transcatheter ASD closure and found significant improvement in TR severity at 3 and 6 months. These findings are similar to those reported by our study [13]. Additionally, a retrospective cohort study conducted in 2018 on 172 adults post-ASD closure found that 55 % of those with TR showed at least one-grade improvement within six months [14]. The improvement in TR severity after ASD closure is linked to RV reverse remodeling. A left-to-right shunt causes RV dilation, altering its geometry and displacing the tricuspid valve's papillary muscles, leading to tethering of the leaflets. Chronic pulmonary overflow further elevates pulmonary artery pressure, straining the RV and worsening the TR. Following transcatheter ASD closure, RV unloading and normalized pulmonary flow promote reverse remodeling, reducing RV and tricuspid annular diameters, improving leaflet coaptation, and lessening the TR severity. This also decreases the right atrial end-systolic area, potentially lowering AF risk. Persistent TR may indicate incomplete RV and tricuspid valve reverse remodeling [15,16].

We suggest that age, presence of AF and shunt ratio, preprocedural RA end-systolic area, tricuspid annular diameter and RV basal diameter are significant factors that may affect improvement of right heart dimensions after transcatheter ASD closure. This is consistent with a previous study that found that while ASD closure reduces RV size at any age, the best outcomes occur in patients under five years old [9]. We found that the same factors can predict the improvement of tricuspid regurgitation after ASD closure in addition to the right atrial end-systolic area. We postulate that the older the age, the longer the period of left-to-right shunting which leads to mechanical stretching of the atrial fibers and developing AF. Atrial fibrillation leads to right atrial dilatation which leads to worsening of tricuspid valve leaflets' coaptation leading to worsening of TR severity which again leads to RV dilatation and further worsening of the tricuspid valve morphology and function. Accordingly, all these factors can perpetuate a viscous circle with further worsening of the TR. Nassif et al. identified age ≥ 60 years (odds ratio (OR) 2.57; p = 0.095) and RA end-diastolic area ≥ 10 cm2/m2 (OR 3.36; p = 0.032) are predictors of persistent TR, aligning with our findings [14]. They also reported that RV systolic pressure ≥ 44 mmHg (OR 6.44; p = 0.001) and TAPSE ≤2.3 cm (OR 3.3; p = 0.04) independently predicted persistent TR, which is discordant to our study. Similarly, Takaya et al. [12] found that permanent AF predicts persistent TR after ASD closure.

The improvement in NYHA functional class observed in our cohort underscores the symptomatic benefit of transcatheter ASD closure. By reducing right heart volume overload, the procedure not only facilitated structural reverse remodeling but also translated into improvement in patients' functional capacity.

Taken together, our study expands the current body of evidence by documenting the course of right heart remodeling and tricuspid regurgitation improvement at multiple follow-up time points (1, 3, and 6 months). Beyond demonstrating the favorable reverse remodeling process, we identified baseline clinical and echocardiographic predictors of RV reverse remodeling, while showing that no single factor independently predicted TR regression. This underscores the multifactorial nature of TR improvement, which likely reflects the interplay between age, rhythm status, atrial and ventricular geometry, and loading conditions. These findings have important clinical implications. Specifically, they provide insight into which patients with ASD and significant TR are likely to experience sufficient improvement with device closure alone, and which patients may derive greater benefit from concomitant tricuspid valve intervention at the time of ASD closure. Such classification has the potential to optimize procedural planning and improve long-term outcomes.

Limitations of the study

Despite its valuable findings, this study has several limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small, which may limit the generalizability of the results. Second, the pediatric population was not included in the study. Third, although our study identified baseline predictors of RVreverse remodeling, it lacked the scope to define a precise time threshold beyond which further reverse remodeling or improvement in TR would no longer be expected, and at which tricuspid valve intervention should be considered. Future longitudinal studies with extended follow-up and clinical endpoints are warranted to validate the prognostic significance of our echocardiographic findings. Another limitation of our study is the exclusion of patients with RV dysfunction at baseline. While this may limit the generalizability of our findings, as RV dysfunction is not uncommon among patients with ASD, this criterion was applied intentionally to avoid confounding by multifactorial mechanisms as outcomes may be driven not only by volume overload but also by intrinsic myocardial impairment. Finally, the RV assessment was conducted using echocardiography, whereas CMR is considered the gold standard for RV evaluation.

Conclusion

In conclusion, transcatheter ASD closure leads to right heart reverse remodeling, with reductions in RA and RV dimensions, pulmonary artery pressure, and TR severity. Key predictors of RV reverse remodeling and TR improvement included younger age, sinus rhythm, smaller shunt ratio, tricuspid annular diameter, and RA area. These findings underscore the role of echocardiographic assessment in guiding post-procedural management, though further studies are needed to assess long-term outcomes. In patients exhibiting these favorable predictors, ASD closure alone may suffice, with monitoring for the improvement of TR severity during follow-up. However, in the absence of these predictors, concurrent or subsequent intervention, such as transcatheter tricuspid valve repair (e.g., TriClip), may be warranted.

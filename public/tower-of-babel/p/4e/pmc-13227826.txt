Early and high Influenza A circulation alongside reduced Respiratory Syncytial Virus prevalence during autumn 2025 in paediatric patients attending emergency department
Authors: Velia Chiara Di Maio, Rossana Scutari, Stefania Ranno, Luana Coltella, Venere Cortazzo, Luna Colagrossi, Anna Chiara Vittucci, Livia Antilici, Giulia Linardos, Alessandra Salvatori, Leonarda Gentile, Manuela Onori, Barbara Lucignano, Gianluca Vrenna, Mara Pisani, Sebastian Cristaldi, Massimiliano Raponi, Cristina Russo, Paola Bernaschi, Alberto Villani, Carlo Federico Perno
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The recent implementation of preventive strategies against Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV) in infants has raised questions about potential changes in RSV seasonality and the circulation of other respiratory viruses. This study aimed to compare respiratory virus activity among paediatric patients presenting to the Bambino Gesù Children’s Hospital (Rome, Italy), with respiratory symptoms and available BioFire Respiratory Panel 2.1 plus results during September-December 2024 (N = 670) and 2025 (N = 905). RSV detection decreased significantly in December 2025 compared with the same period in 2024 (6.3% vs 29.4%, risk ratio [RR]: 0.21, 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.16–0.28; p < 0.001). By contrast, influenza A viruses had an earlier onset in 2025 and emerged as the dominant pathogen compared with 2024 (21.0%, in 2025 vs 4.5%, in 2024; RR: 4.69, 95% CI 3.23–6.80; p < 0.001). Unlike September-December 2024, parainfluenza viruses (PIVs) 3 and 4 circulated in 2025 (0.4% vs 3.5% for PIV 3; 0.0% vs 3.6% for PIV-4; p < 0.001 for both), mainly observed in infants (median age 0.7 years; interquartile range: 0.2–1.2; mean±standard deviation: 0.9 ± 0.8). Circulation of adenovirus (11.5%, vs 6.1%; p < 0.001) and human coronavirus HKU1 (3.6%, vs 0.1%; p < 0.001) also increased in 2025 compared with 2024. The observed epidemiological shifts suggest potential effects associated with the introduction of RSV prophylaxis in Italy and highlight the importance of continued paediatric surveillance in monitoring evolving viral patterns and guiding public health decisions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13052-026-02285-8.

Background

Respiratory viruses, particularly Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV) and influenza, remain leading causes of paediatric emergency visits during the autumn-winter months. In Italy, the recent implementation of preventive strategies against RSV in infants has raised questions regarding potential changes in RSV seasonality and the circulation of other respiratory viruses. Although early data support the effectiveness of RSV prophylaxis in reducing hospitalizations in Italy [1–3], real-world data on the potential impact of these interventions on respiratory virus epidemiology remain limited.

To evaluate potential changes in the seasonal circulation of respiratory viruses (somewhat associated with RSV prophylaxis), we compared respiratory virus activity among paediatric patients with respiratory symptoms presenting to the emergency department of the Bambino Gesù Children’s Hospital (Rome, Italy), between September and December in 2024 and 2025.

Methods

A retrospective analysis was conducted on data collected from respiratory samples of paediatric patients (aged 0–18 years) presenting with respiratory symptoms to the emergency department of the Bambino Gesù Children’s Hospital (Rome, Italy).

Demographic variables were limited to age, sex, and sample type. Age was categorized into four predefined groups: 0–6 months, 7–12 months, 1–5 years, and >5 years.

Respiratory samples (nasopharyngeal swabs or aspirates) were tested using the BioFire FilmArray Respiratory Panel 2.1 Plus (BioMérieux, France), following standard institutional practice. Testing was generally performed in children with moderate to severe respiratory symptoms requiring hospitalization, in younger patients, or based on epidemiological considerations, at the discretion of the attending physician. Testing criteria and laboratory procedures were consistent across the two analyzed seasons, ensuring comparability of the results.

Approximately 300 μL of each specimen was analyzed using this multiplex nucleic acid amplification assay, which performs automated nucleic acid extraction, nested multiplex PCR, and automated result interpretation within approximately 45 minutes. The panel simultaneously detects 19 respiratory viruses including RSV; Influenza A (A/H1, A/H1-2009, A/H3) and B; parainfluenza viruses 1–4; adenovirus; rhinovirus/enterovirus; human metapneumovirus; human coronaviruses 229E, HKU1, NL63, OC43; SARS-CoV-2; and MERS-CoV, as well as four atypical bacteria: Bordetella parapertussis, Bordetella pertussis, Chlamydia pneumoniae, and Mycoplasma pneumoniae. The software provides qualitative results for each target, reported as “detected” or “not detected,” which are automatically interpreted according to the manufacturer’s validated criteria.

Descriptive statistics are presented as median (IQR) and mean ± standard deviation (SD) for continuous variables, and as counts (percentages) for categorical variables. Group comparisons were performed using Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables and the Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables. For key comparisons, effect size estimates are reported as risk ratios (RR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI). A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

No formal adjustment for multiple comparisons was performed, given the exploratory nature of the analysis.

Results

In the same period of 2025, 30,214 visits were recorded, with 3,517 (11.6%) related to respiratory conditions. Among these, 670 cases from September to December 2024, and 905 cases from the same period in 2025 were analysed with available BioFire Respiratory Panel 2.1plus (BioMérieux, France). In addition, when patients were stratified by age categories the proportion of patients aged 0–6 months was higher in 2024 group (48.2% [N = 323] vs 37.0% [N = 335], p < 0.001), while no significant differences were observed in the older age groups (Table 1).

Epidemiology of RSV and other viruses during 2024 and 2025 season

Paediatric patients presenting to the emergency department during 2025 showed a substantial decrease in RSV detection compared with 2024 (6.3% [N = 57] vs 29.4% [N = 197], RR: 0.21, 95% CI 0.16–0.28; p < 0.001), (Table 2). Of interest, the median age of RSV-positive patients was somewhat higher in 2025 than in 2024 (1.2 years [IQR: 0.4–1.8] vs 0.3 years [IQR: 0.1–0.8]; mean±SD: 1.4 ± 1.6 vs 0.8 ± 1.6), although the difference was not significant (Mann-Whitney U test, p = 0.33). Differently, Influenza A showed an earlier onset in 2025 (approximately one month earlier than the previous season) and became the predominant pathogen compared to 2024 (21.0% [N = 190] vs 4.5% [N = 30]; RR: 4.69, 95% CI 3.23–6.80; p < 0.001; Table 2, Fig. 1). In 2025, H3N2 predominated over H1N1-pdm2009 (63.7% [N = 121] vs 36.3% [N = 69]), whereas in 2024, H1N1-pdm2009 was more frequent than H3N2 (76.7% [N = 23] vs 16.7% [N = 5]).

The analysis was also extended to other respiratory viruses to examine their circulation patterns. Parainfluenza viruses (PIV-3 and PIV-4) were significantly more prevalent in 2025, at 3.5% (N = 32) and 3.6% (N = 33), respectively, compared with very limited circulation in the same period of 2024 (0.4% [N = 3] for PIV-3 and 0.0% for PIV-4; p < 0.001 for both; Table 2).

These viruses were most common in young children (median age 0.7 years, [IQR 0.2–1.2]; mean±SD: 0.9 ± 0.8). The 2025 season was also characterized by increased circulation of adenovirus and human coronavirus HKU1 compared with 2024, with prevalence of 11.5% (N = 104) and 3.6% (N = 33), respectively, versus 6.1% (N = 41) and 0.1% (N = 1) in 2024 (both p < 0.001; Table 2).

Mono-infections predominated in both 2024 and 2025 seasons (70.6% [N = 374] in 2024 vs 71.4% [N = 518] in 2025). Human rhinovirus/enterovirus was the most common pathogen among mono-infections in both 2024 and 2025 (41.4% [N = 155] in 2024 vs 44.6% [N = 231] in 2025). Differently, RSV mono-infection was more common in 2024 (30.7% [N = 115] vs 2.9% [N = 15]), whereas influenza A mono-infection predominated in 2025 (24.5% [N = 127] vs 4.3% [N = 16]). Co-infections were detected in 29.4% (N = 156) of respiratory samples positive for at least one pathogen during the 2024 season, with the most prevalent combination involving RSV and human rhinovirus/enterovirus (31.4% [N = 49] of co-infections) (Fig. 2, panel A). Discussion

Many respiratory viruses, including RSV and influenza, show seasonal circulation. However, the timing and intensity of these seasonal peaks can be influenced by several factors, including climatic changes, viral evolution, and preventive interventions such as prophylactic strategies [4]. In addition, competition of different viruses for the same cellular and organ respiratory target should be taken into account in this evaluation. Considering these factors, our findings indicate a notable shift in the epidemiology of respiratory pathogens in the paediatric emergency department of Bambino Gesù Children Hospital (Rome, Italy), from September to December 2024 and 2025. This suggests that recent interventions, including RSV prophylaxis, may have contributed to changes in the circulation dynamics of some viruses (e.g., Influenza A, PIV3 and PIV4, adenoviruses), whereas others, such as rhinoviruses, Influenza B, and metapneumoviruses, maintained a prevalence similar to that of previous season.

The selective increase in certain viruses (including PIV3 and PIV4) during the autumn-winter season of 2025 primarily affecting infants, may reflect how changes in RSV circulation create temporary ecological opportunities for viruses with overlapping host susceptibility. These dynamics indicate that both ecological interactions and broader epidemiological factors (such as vaccination coverage and seasonal conditions) likely contribute to the observed patterns of respiratory virus prevalence.

An alternative explanation for the changes observed in 2025 in relation to RSV, Influenza A, and other respiratory viruses could be natural year-to-year seasonal variability. However, the viral circulation pattern observed in 2024 was consistent with that of 2023, a pre-prophylaxis season characterized by RSV predominance (52 cases, 24.6%), followed by SARS-CoV-2 (31 cases, 14.7%), while Influenza A accounted for a substantially smaller proportion (14 cases, 6.6%) (data not shown).

This temporal consistency suggests that 2024 is a representative pre-prophylaxis baseline rather than an anomalous year, and indicates that the significant epidemiological changes observed in 2025 cannot be explained solely by inter-annual variability. Instead, they may reflect, at least in part, the impact of RSV prophylaxis on viral circulation dynamics, potentially through mechanisms of viral interference or ecological niche redistribution.

The significant decrease in RSV detection observed in November-December 2025 could be related to the impact of the RSV immunization program introduced in Lazio region starting from November 2024 in newborns and young infants, which was also associated with a significant reduction in RSV-related hospitalizations [3].

Given that RSV primarily affects infants in the first months of life, the lower proportion of paediatric patients aged 0–6 months observed in the 2025 group compared with 2024 may partially reflect changes in viral circulation, as well as the introduction of RSV immunization, which specifically targeted this age group.

From a virological perspective, viral interference among co-circulating viruses, particularly influenza, may play an additional role in RSV epidemic dynamics, affecting both the timing and intensity of viral circulation [5, 6]. This observation highlights the complex interplay among respiratory pathogens, in which the circulation of one virus may alter the epidemiological behaviour of others [5, 6].

The findings of this study should be interpreted considering certain limitations. The comparison between 2024 and 2025 was based on population-level temporal data; individual-level information, including vaccination status and clinical outcomes, was not available and therefore could not be evaluated.

Consequently, the observed reduction in RSV circulation should be interpreted cautiously, as it represents an ecological association rather than evidence of causality, and the relationship between prophylaxis exposure and patient-level outcomes could not be directly assessed. Furthermore, the interpretation of viral ecological niche dynamics remains speculative, since virus-specific factors (e.g., age susceptibility, tissue tropism, transmission patterns) were not assessed. Seasonal constraints may also have influenced the findings, as Influenza analysis was restricted to the autumn period, providing only a partial view of seasonal trends. In addition, regional influenza vaccination coverage, community RSV activity, and broader epidemiological data were unavailable, so results are based solely on hospital-level data which may limit generalizability. Despite these limitations, the findings provide preliminary insights that warrant further investigation. Future studies should address these gaps through multicentre, longitudinal research spanning multiple RSV seasons, incorporating patient-level data, clinical outcomes, and detailed prophylaxis information to gain a more comprehensive understanding of RSV dynamics.

Conclusions

The introduction of RSV prophylaxis in Italy was followed by a substantial reduction in RSV circulation among infants presenting to the emergency department during the first post-implementation season. This reduction may be temporally associated with respiratory viral dynamics, with a relative increase in the circulation mostly of Influenza A, as well as other selected respiratory pathogens. Taken together, these findings indicate possible epidemiological effects related to the introduction of RSV prophylaxis in Italy and underscore the value of ongoing paediatric surveillance to track evolving viral patterns and take appropriate public health decisions.

A residual-learning MMSE neural detector for 6G MIMO-OTFS systems under diverse channel conditions
Authors: Arun Kumar, Mansor Alohali, Venkatachalam Revathi, Mehedi Masud, Nishant Gaur, Aziz Nanthaamornphong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractOrthogonal time–frequency space modulation combined with multiple-input multiple-output transmission (MIMO-OTFS) has emerged as a strong waveform candidate for sixth-generation (6G) wireless networks because of its robustness against high mobility and doubly selective channels. However, reliable signal detection in large-scale MIMO-OTFS systems remains challenging owing to severe delay–Doppler coupling and channel state information (CSI), particularly under Rayleigh and Rician fading conditions. This paper proposes a residual-learning minimum mean square error neural detector (RL-MMSE-ND) to address these challenges under both perfect and imperfect CSI, including scenarios with up to a 20% channel estimation error. The proposed detector integrates a conventional minimum mean square error (MMSE) front-end with a lightweight residual-learning neural network that learns only the residual interference caused by CSI mismatch and delay–Doppler effects, thereby preserving MMSE stability while introducing minimal learning overhead. Extensive simulations were conducted for representative large-scale MIMO-OTFS configurations to evaluate the bit error rate (BER) versus signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), BER versus delay spread, power spectral density (PSD) characteristics, inference latency, and training convergence. Numerical results demonstrate that the proposed RL-MMSE-ND achieves 10–13 dB SNR gain at a BER of 10−3 compared to zero-forcing equalization and MMSE detectors, while requiring 3–6 dB lower SNR than the maximum likelihood, QR decomposition–based M-algorithm detection, and deep learning–based detectors under both Rayleigh and Rician fading. Moreover, the proposed method reduces inference latency by over 60% compared to long short-term memory and bidirectional long short-term memory detectors and achieves 5–8 dB lower out-of-band emissions while adding only linear computational overhead beyond MMSE detection. These results confirmed the novelty and practical significance of the proposed approach for robust, low-latency, and low-complexity MIMO-OTFS detection in future 6G wireless systems.Keywords: MIMO-OTFS, Residual learning, Minimum mean square error detection, Imperfect channel state information, 6G wireless communicationsSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

The evolution toward sixth-generation (6G) wireless networks is driven by the need to support ultra-reliable low-latency communications, extremely high data rates, and seamless connectivity in highly dynamic propagation environments. Emerging 6G use cases, such as high-speed vehicular communications, unmanned aerial vehicles, extended reality, and integrated sensing and communication, impose stringent requirements on waveform designs and receiver architectures1. In this context, Orthogonal Time–Frequency Space (OTFS) modulation has recently gained significant attention owing to its inherent robustness against high Doppler shifts and severe time-varying multipath channels. By operating in the delay–Doppler domain, OTFS effectively converts time-varying channels into a nearly time-invariant representation, making it particularly attractive for high-mobility 6G scenarios. When combined with multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) technology, OTFS enables substantial gains in spectral efficiency and link reliability; however, it also introduces new challenges in signal detection owing to increased dimensionality, inter-symbol interference, and coupling across delay–Doppler bins2.

Signal detection in MIMO-OTFS systems is fundamentally more complex than in conventional orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) systems3. Linear detectors, such as Zero-Forcing Equalization (ZFE) and Minimum Mean Square Error (MMSE) detection, offer low computational complexity and scalability to large antenna configurations; however, their performance degrades significantly under ill-conditioned channels, high Doppler spreads, and strong inter-user interference. On the other hand, optimal Maximum Likelihood (ML) detection achieves the best bit error rate (BER) performance, but is computationally prohibitive for practical MIMO-OTFS systems because of its exponential complexity. Reduced-complexity near-ML schemes, such as QR-decomposition-based M-algorithm detection (QRM-MLD), alleviate this burden to some extent. However, they still incur substantial computational overhead and latency, which limits their suitability for real-time 6G deployments. Recent advances in machine learning (ML) have opened new avenues for signal detection in complex wireless systems4. Data-driven detectors based on recurrent neural networks, including long short-term memory (LSTM) and bidirectional LSTM (Bi-LSTM) architectures, have demonstrated promising performance in learning channel dynamics and mitigating nonlinear distortions5. Nevertheless, purely ML-based detectors often suffer from several practical limitations including high training complexity, large model sizes, sensitivity to channel mismatch, and limited interpretability. Moreover, when deployed without exploiting the underlying signal models, such approaches may struggle to generalize across diverse channel conditions encountered in realistic 6G environments, such as varying Doppler spreads, delay profiles, antenna correlations, and signal-to-noise ratios6. To address these limitations, model-driven or hybrid detection frameworks that integrate classical signal-processing principles with learning-based refinement have emerged as a compelling research direction. By embedding domain knowledge into the learning architecture, such hybrid methods can significantly reduce the model complexity, improve the convergence behavior, and enhance the robustness to channel variations. Existing model-driven deep learning (DL) approaches are mainly designed for conventional massive MIMO systems, where signal detection is performed in the time–frequency domain and channel effects are predominantly linear and frequency-selective. In contrast, OTFS transforms the channel into the delay–Doppler domain, where symbols experience inherent two-dimensional coupling across both delay and Doppler bins. This creates structured intersymbol interference that is fundamentally different from the interference patterns seen in standard MIMO systems. Most existing model-driven DL methods, such as unfolding-based or AMP-inspired networks, assume separable or weakly correlated interference and do not explicitly exploit the sparse 2D convolutional structure of the OTFS channels. Consequently, they struggle under high Doppler spread, severe mobility, and fractional delay effects. Moreover, the coupling and channel state information (CSI) mismatch in OTFS introduces compounded residual interference across the delay–Doppler grid, in which conventional massive MIMO-oriented networks are not effectively suppressed.

Approximate Message Passing (AMP)-and unitary AMP (UAMP)-based OTFS detectors are powerful iterative inference algorithms that exploit the inherent sparsity of delay–Doppler channels. Nevertheless, their derivation typically assumes accurate CSI and statistical independence conditions that justify Gaussian residual approximations for the state evolution and convergence analysis. When the CSI is imperfect, as is common in high-mobility OTFS systems, these assumptions are violated, leading to biased residual updates, error accumulation across iterations, and possible convergence instability or BER floors. In addition, the AMP/UAMP performance is highly sensitive to channel matrix conditioning, power normalization, and precise noise variance knowledge, all of which are difficult to maintain under fractional Doppler, rapidly time-varying fading, or pilot contamination. The iterative structure further introduces variable latency and increases computational overhead, particularly for large OTFS grids and higher-order modulation schemes. In contrast, the proposed RL-MMSE-ND employs a deterministic MMSE front-end that ensures stable baseline detection, whereas a lightweight residual learning module directly compensates for CSI-induced distortions in a single forward pass. This non-iterative hybrid framework avoids divergence issues, maintains predictable complexity, and achieves improved robustness under realistic imperfect-CSI conditions, making it more practical for scalable 6G MIMO-OTFS deployments.

Motivated by this paradigm, this study proposes a Residual-Learning MMSE Neural Detector (RL-MMSE-ND) for 6G MIMO-OTFS systems operating under diverse channel conditions. The proposed approach leverages a low-complexity MMSE detector as a principled front-end to capture the dominant linear structure of the MIMO-OTFS channel, whereas a lightweight neural network is employed to learn and compensate for the residual interference, noise enhancement, and nonlinear distortions that remain after MMSE processing. By focusing on the learning task of residual errors rather than full symbol estimation, the proposed detector achieves a favorable tradeoff between performance and complexity. Unlike conventional ML detectors that attempt to entirely replace classical detection, RL-MMSE-ND adopts a residual-learning strategy that preserves interpretability and stability while significantly enhancing the BER performance. Furthermore, the proposed framework is inherently adaptable to diverse channel conditions because the MMSE front-end provides robustness against channel uncertainty, and the neural residual module adapts to variations in Doppler spread, delay dispersion, and interference patterns. This design philosophy makes the proposed detector particularly well-suited for practical 6G MIMO-OTFS deployments.

The performance of the proposed RL-MMSE-ND was systematically evaluated and compared with widely used benchmarks, including the ZFE, MMSE, ML, QRM-MLD, LSTM, and Bi-LSTM detectors. The simulation results demonstrate that the proposed method consistently achieves superior BER performance while maintaining improved power spectral density (PSD) characteristics and significantly lower computational complexity than ML and QRM-MLD detectors. Compared with purely learning-based approaches, the proposed detector offers enhanced generalization and reduced inference latency, making it a strong candidate for next-generation 6G receiver architectures.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work to jointly achieve substantial BER, spectral containment, and inference latency improvements for MIMO-OTFS detection under both perfect and imperfect channel state information using a residual-learning-assisted MMSE framework. Literature review

Signal detection in large-scale MIMO systems continues to be a critical challenge for beyond-5G and emerging 6G networks, especially under high-mobility and doubly selective channel conditions. Conventional linear detectors, such as ZFE and MMSE detection, remain attractive owing to their low computational complexity and scalability. However, recent studies have shown that their BER performance degrades significantly in high-Doppler and strongly frequency-selective channels, motivating the need for more advanced detection strategies7. OTFS modulation has gained renewed attention in recent years as a key waveform candidate for 6G owing to its robustness in rapidly time-varying environments. Several recent works have investigated MIMO-OTFS detection and demonstrated that despite the near-time-invariant channel representation in the delay–Doppler domain, symbol coupling across two dimensions leads to severe inter-symbol interference and elevated detection complexity8,9. Linear detectors applied directly in the delay–Doppler domain have been shown to be insufficient for high-order modulation and dense multipath scenarios10. To overcome these limitations, near-optimal detection techniques, such as Maximum Likelihood (ML) and QR decomposition-based M-algorithm detection (QRM-MLD) have been revisited for OTFS systems. Recent studies have reported that QRM-MLD achieves significant BER improvements over linear detectors in MIMO-OTFS systems; however, its computational complexity and detection latency increase rapidly with antenna count and modulation order, limiting its practicality for real-time 6G applications11,12. Machine learning (ML) has emerged as a powerful tool for signal detection in advanced wireless systems. Deep neural network (DNN)-based detectors have been proposed for massive MIMO and OTFS systems, demonstrating improved BER performance by learning nonlinear interference patterns and channel distortions13. Recent studies have further extended ML-based detection to massive MIMO-OTFS scenarios under Rayleigh and Rician fading, showing improved robustness compared to MMSE and ZFE baselines, while reducing reliance on iterative detection14. In our earlier work4, a Modified Deep Neural Network (M-DNN) was proposed for massive MIMO-OTFS detection, where the network performs end-to-end signal recovery by directly learning the mapping from the received vector and the channel matrix to the transmitted symbols. Although that approach demonstrated improved BER performance over conventional linear detectors, it relies on a fully data-driven architecture in which the entire detection burden is placed on the deep network, with no analytical front-end and no explicit modeling of the channel-estimation error. As a consequence, its computational cost grows rapidly with the network depth, its interpretability is limited, and its robustness to imperfect CSI is constrained by the training distribution. The present study addresses these limitations by introducing a fundamentally different model-driven hybrid framework. Specifically, the proposed RL-MMSE-ND combines an analytical MMSE front-end with a lightweight residual-learning module that learns only the post-MMSE residual interference, explicitly incorporates the CSI-error term H = Ĥ + E into the signal model, and adopts a residual-based loss function rather than the end-to-end MSE used in4. The two works therefore differ in (i) detector architecture (model-driven hybrid vs. fully data-driven), (ii) mathematical formulation (two-stage residual correction vs. single end-to-end mapping), (iii) explicit imperfect-CSI modeling, and (iv) learning objective. The proposed framework consequently represents a clear methodological evolution beyond4 rather than an incremental extension. Recurrent neural network architectures, including LSTM and Bi-LSTM, have also been explored to capture the temporal dependencies in time-varying channels. Recent studies have reported that LSTM-based detectors outperform feed-forward DNNs in fast-fading MIMO environments; however, they incur high training complexity and inference latency, which can be prohibitive for large-scale MIMO-OTFS systems15. Moreover, purely data-driven ML detectors often exhibit limited generalization when deployed under channel conditions that are not observed during training. To address these challenges, model-driven and hybrid detection approaches have gained increasing attention in the period 2024–2025. These methods integrate conventional signal-processing techniques with learning-based refinement to achieve a better performance–complexity tradeoff. Recent hybrid detectors combine linear MMSE or AMP front-ends with neural residual learning to achieve near-ML performance with significantly reduced complexity in large MIMO systems16. Despite these advances, the application of residual-learning-assisted MMSE detection specifically tailored for 6G MIMO-OTFS systems under diverse channel conditions remains largely unexplored, which motivates the proposed work.

RL-MMSE-ND is a model-driven hybrid detection framework specifically designed for 6G MIMO-OTFS systems operating under diverse channel conditions, including high mobility, severe multipath, and mixed Rayleigh and Rician fading. The detector integrates a conventional MMSE front-end with a lightweight residual-learning neural network, thereby achieving a superior balance between performance, complexity, and robustness.

Unlike the ZFE, which suffers from severe noise amplification, the MMSE stage of the proposed detector provides stable initial symbol estimates by jointly mitigating interference and noise. Compared to pure MMSE, which cannot fully suppress delay–Doppler coupling and residual interference in OTFS, the neural residual module adaptively learns and compensates for the remaining distortions. In contrast to Maximum Likelihood (ML) and QRM-MLD detectors, which achieve near-optimal BER at the cost of prohibitive computational complexity and high latency, RL-MMSE-ND avoids exhaustive or tree-search operations, making it suitable for real-time 6G applications.

Furthermore, unlike standalone ML detectors, such as DNN, LSTM, and Bi-LSTM, which require large training datasets and may generalize poorly across channel variations, the proposed model-driven residual learning approach exploits domain knowledge embedded in MMSE detection. This significantly reduces the training overhead while improving robustness across diverse channel conditions. Consequently, the RL-MMSE-ND achieves a better BER, improved spectral behavior, and lower complexity than existing detection algorithms, making it a strong candidate for practical 6G MIMO-OTFS receivers.

The SIMAC framework in17 focuses on semantic-driven integrated multimodal sensing and communication (ISAC), emphasizing cross-layer semantic extraction and task-oriented transmission efficiency rather than physical layer signal detection. Similarly, the “Bedrock Models” concept in18 explores large-scale foundation models aimed at improving generalization and transferability across diverse communication and sensing tasks, prioritizing model universality over task-specific computational efficiency. In contrast, the proposed RL-MMSE-ND is a lightweight, model-driven detection architecture specifically designed for MIMO-OTFS systems under imperfect CSI, targeting explicit improvements in the bit error rate (BER), inference latency, spectral containment, and computational complexity. Furthermore, the adaptive beamforming strategy in19 addresses spatial-domain optimization for extended targets within ISAC systems, focusing on transmit-side array processing and predictive error analysis, whereas our work operates at the receiver detection stage in the delay–Doppler domain. Unlike these system-level or beamforming-centric approaches, the proposed method directly mitigates the delay–Doppler coupling and CSI mismatch through residual learning integrated with MMSE detection. Therefore, while17–19 contributed to semantic integration, foundation modeling, and adaptive beamforming in 6G ISAC systems, the present work provides a complementary and implementation-oriented advancement at the physical-layer receiver level, ensuring robust and low-complexity symbol recovery in large-scale MIMO-OTFS deployments.

Background and terminology

Multiple-Input Multiple-Output Orthogonal Time Frequency Space (MIMO-OTFS) is an advanced waveform and transmission framework designed to support reliable high-data-rate communication in highly time-varying and high-mobility environments, which are the key characteristics of emerging 6G systems20. Unlike conventional OFDM, which operates in the time–frequency domain and suffers from severe inter-carrier interference under high-Doppler conditions, OTFS modulates information symbols in the delay–Doppler domain. This representation captures the physical characteristics of wireless channels more naturally as multipath delays and Doppler shifts remain nearly constant over an OTFS frame. Consequently, the rapidly time-varying channel is transformed into a quasi-static two-dimensional convolution in the delay–Doppler domain21. When combined with MIMO technology, OTFS exploits spatial diversity and multiplexing gains alongside delay–Doppler robustness, significantly enhancing spectral efficiency and link reliability. However, MIMO-OTFS systems introduce new signal processing challenges owing to symbol coupling across antennas, as well as across delay and Doppler bins. This coupling increases the detection complexity and limits the effectiveness of the conventional linear detectors. Nevertheless, the structured sparsity of the delay–Doppler channel can be leveraged to design efficient detection and equalization algorithms. Overall, MIMO-OTFS is regarded as a strong waveform candidate for 6G applications, such as high-speed rail, vehicular communications, unmanned aerial vehicles, and integrated sensing and communication, where robustness to mobility and channel dynamics is critical22.

Figure 1 illustrates the end-to-end architecture of an MIMO-OTFS communication system, highlighting the signal-processing stages at the transmitter, wireless channel, and receiver. At the transmitter, the input data symbols are first mapped onto a two-dimensional delay–Doppler grid, where each symbol represents a specific delay and Doppler index. This mapping enables the system to directly exploit the physical characteristics of the time-varying multipath channels. The delay–Doppler symbols are then transformed into the time–frequency domain using a two-dimensional inverse symplectic finite Fourier transform (ISFFT). Subsequently, a MIMO precoder distributes the transformed symbols across multiple transmit antennas, enabling spatial multiplexing and diversity gains. The transmitted signals propagate through a time-varying multipath wireless channel, characterized by delay spreads and Doppler shifts. Unlike conventional OFDM channels, the OTFS framework captures these effects in a structured manner, resulting in a quasistatic representation over the OTFS frame duration. At the receiver, multiple antennas collect superimposed signals that are affected by channel fading, delay, and Doppler distortions. A multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) detection block is employed to separate spatially multiplexed streams and mitigate inter-antenna interference. The detected signals are then transformed back to the delay–Doppler domain using a two-dimensional SFFT, followed by delay–Doppler demapping to recover the original data symbols. Overall, the figure emphasizes how MIMO-OTFS jointly exploits the spatial, delay, and Doppler dimensions to achieve robust performance in high-mobility environments.

Proposed system model

The residual-learning MMSE neural detector (RL-MMSE-ND) is a hybrid model-driven detection framework designed for 6G MIMO-OTFS systems operating under diverse channel conditions27. In this approach, a conventional MMSE detector first estimates the transmitted symbols, thereby providing a low-complexity and stable baseline. A lightweight neural network was then employed to learn the residual error between the MMSE output and the true transmitted symbols, effectively compensating for residual interference, noise enhancement, and nonlinear distortions caused by delay–Doppler coupling and channel variations. By focusing on residual correction rather than full symbol detection, RL-MMSE-ND achieves improved BER and spectral performance while maintaining low computational complexity and strong robustness across Rayleigh and Rician fading environments. Residual learning in the delay–Doppler (DD) domain introduces a distinct advancement because the interference structure in the OTFS is inherently two-dimensional and mobility-dependent. In OTFS systems, each symbol is coupled across both delay and Doppler bins because of the 2D circular convolution of the channel, leading to structured intersymbol interference that differs fundamentally from the primarily linear frequency-selective interference in conventional massive MIMO systems. Unlike traditional residual learning, which typically refines scalar post-equalization errors or noise enhancement effects, DD-domain residual learning targets deterministic interference patterns caused by Doppler shifts, fractional delays, and CSI mismatches across the entire grid. Therefore, the neural module learns to suppress the structured DD-domain residual distortion that remains after MMSE processing rather than replacing the linear detector. This structured correction mechanism enables improved robustness under high mobility and severe channel uncertainty while maintaining interpretability and scalable computational complexity. Figure 2 illustrates a block diagram of the proposed RL-MMSE-ND for MIMO-OTFS systems. The received symbol vector , obtained after OTFS demodulation and MIMO combination, is first processed by a conventional MMSE detector to generate an initial symbol estimate . This linear stage provides low-complexity and stable baseline detection while suppressing a major portion of the multi-antenna interference and noise. The residual error, defined as the difference between the received signal and the MMSE estimate, captures the remaining interference, delay–Doppler coupling, and nonlinear distortions. This residual was fed into a lightweight neural network trained to learn and predict the correction terms. The network output was then combined with the MMSE estimate to produce an enhanced symbol estimate. By restricting learning to residual correction, the proposed architecture achieves an improved detection accuracy with minimal additional complexity, making it suitable for real-time 6G MIMO-OTFS deployments.

Simulation results

In this study, we used MATLAB to analyze the performance of the proposed and conventional signal detection algorithms under Rayleigh and Rician channels with perfect and imperfect CSI. To ensure the statistical reliability of the reported BER results, each SNR point was averaged over at least 105 transmitted symbols corresponding to multiple independent channel realizations and OTFS frames. Specifically, for every SNR value, 1,000 independent channel realizations were generated, and 100 OTFS frames (32 × 32 grid) were transmitted for each realization. This results in a total of 100,000 symbols per SNR point, ensuring a stable BER estimation without variance-induced fluctuations. Across both channel models, the proposed RL-MMSE-ND offers the best performance–complexity trade-off, combining low, near-quadratic complexity with the robustness required for scalable 6G MIMO-OTFS receivers.

The current implementation of the RL-MMSE-ND employs offline training with fixed neural network parameters during inference. Although the proposed model-driven residual learning framework already demonstrates strong robustness across unseen fading types, Doppler spreads, antenna sizes, and CSI error levels, rapidly time-varying 6G environments may benefit from lightweight online adaptation. In the revised manuscript, we discuss a practical extension in which the residual network can be updated using minibatch fine-tuning based on periodically received pilot symbols or high-confidence detected symbols. Because the neural module is lightweight (2–3 fully connected layers with limited parameters) and operates only on residual corrections, online adaptation can be performed with low computational overhead compared with full retraining. Furthermore, complexity-aware inference allows selective activation of adaptation only when the confidence metrics fall below a predefined threshold, thereby preventing unnecessary updates. This enables continual learning while preserving the real-time latency requirements. Future work will focus on implementing adaptive learning-rate scheduling, meta-learning initialization for rapid convergence, and reinforcement-based adaptation strategies to further enhance the robustness under highly dynamic mobility and hardware-impaired 6G scenarios.

Conclusion

This paper presents a residual-learning-assisted signal detection framework for large-scale MIMO-OTFS systems that effectively addresses the challenges arising from severe delay–Doppler coupling and channel uncertainty. By integrating a model-driven minimum mean square error front-end with a lightweight neural residual correction module, the proposed RL-MMSE-ND achieves a robust balance between detection accuracy, spectral containment, and computational efficiency. Comprehensive numerical evaluations were conducted for representative MIMO-OTFS configurations under both Rayleigh and Rician fading with perfect and imperfect channel-state information. At a target bit error rate of , the proposed detector consistently achieved a 10–13 dB signal-to-noise ratio gain over conventional zero-forcing equalization and MMSE detectors, while requiring a 3–6 dB lower signal-to-noise ratio than advanced nonlinear and learning-based detectors, including QR decomposition–based M-algorithm detection and model-driven deep learning approaches. In addition, the proposed method reduces inference latency by more than 60% compared to long short-term memory and bidirectional long short-term memory detectors, and achieves 5–8 dB lower out-of-band emissions, demonstrating superior spectral containment. Despite these advantages, the present study is limited to simulation-based evaluations and assumes ideal hardware components. Moreover, the neural network parameters are trained offline and remain fixed during inference, which may restrict adaptability in rapidly time-varying environments. The worst-case complexity is still influenced by the MMSE front-end for very large antenna dimensions. Future work will focus on online adaptive learning, joint channel estimation and detection, and experimental validation using hardware platforms. Investigating the robustness under practical hardware impairments and extending the framework to ultra-high-order modulation schemes constitute promising directions for advancing the practical deployment of the proposed RL-MMSE-ND in future 6G wireless systems.

Association between adjuvant chemotherapy and outcomes in resected locoregional recurrence of hormone receptor-positive HER2-negative breast cancer: a multi-institutional retrospective study
Authors: Y Ozaki, E Tokuda, Y Sagara, F Hara, S Sasada, M Sawaki, C Kanbayashi, T Yamanaka, T Onishi, Y Fujiki, A Suto, Y Takahashi, E Tokunaga, T Aruga, R Nakamura, T Fujisawa, S Saji, H Iwata, T Shien
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo evaluate the association of adjuvant chemotherapy and prognosis for locoregional recurrence (LRR) in hormone receptor (HR)-positive human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative subtype breast cancer.Patients and methodsWe carried out a multi-institutional retrospective cohort study in patients with breast cancer who developed HR-positive HER2-negative LRR. Patients who underwent curative surgery for LRR between 2014 and 2018 were categorized based on whether they received adjuvant chemotherapy for LRR (CTx versus no-CTx). Invasive disease-free survival (iDFS) was evaluated between the groups by Cox proportional hazards analysis. The primary analysis used a double-robust Cox model incorporating inverse probability of treatment weighting, and a sensitivity analysis using propensity score matching was also carried out.ResultsA total of 958 patients were included. The median time from the primary surgery to LRR diagnosis was 9.5 years (interquartile range 3.1-10.1 years). There were 235 patients (25%) in the CTx group and 722 (75%) in the no-CTx group. Among all patients, the 5-year iDFS rate was 75.4% [95% confidence interval (CI) 72.4% to 78.2%]. Multivariate analysis showed better iDFS in the CTx group (hazard ratio 0.70, 95% CI 0.49-0.99, P = 0.045). Sensitivity analysis supported these findings. Subgroup analyses showed that the CTx group had better iDFS in cases with non-ipsilateral breast tumor recurrence (IBTR), recurrences during adjuvant endocrine therapy for primary breast cancer, and without perioperative chemotherapy for primary breast cancer. Secondary analysis showed no significant difference with a worse trend toward overall survival in the CTx group with multivariate Cox proportional hazards analysis.ConclusionAdjuvant chemotherapy for HR-positive HER2-negative LRR was associated with better iDFS, particularly in cases of non-IBTR, recurrences during adjuvant endocrine therapy, and no prior perioperative chemotherapy for their primary tumor. However, the retrospective design and inability to distinguish true recurrences from new primary tumors in IBTR warrant cautious interpretation.Key words: breast cancer, locoregional recurrence, adjuvant chemotherapy, inverse probability of treatment weighting

Introduction

Breast cancer is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality among women globally.1 Multimodal therapy, including systemic therapy, surgery, and radiotherapy, is used to treat resectable breast cancer, but ∼20%-30% of patients experience recurrence after curative surgery.2 Recurrence is classified as locoregional recurrence (LRR) or distant recurrence, with LRR occurring in ∼3%-5% of patients with hormone receptor (HR)-positive human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative breast cancer in recent clinical trials.3 The prognosis after LRR is poor, with a 5-year distant recurrence-free survival rate of 51.4% for ipsilateral breast tumor recurrence (IBTR) and 18.8% for non-IBTR, including chest wall or skin recurrence and regional lymph node recurrence.4,5 There is therefore a need for new treatments to improve the prognosis of LRR.

Treatment for LRR involves surgery if resection of the recurrent lesion is possible, followed by multimodal therapy including radiation and systemic therapy.6,7 Evidence for systemic treatment for LRR is limited, however. The phase III SAKK 23/82 trial showed the efficacy of adjuvant tamoxifen for LRR.8 Another pivotal study, the CALOR trial (NCT00074152), showed the clinical benefit of adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with HR-negative LRR after curative surgery, but failed to demonstrate any significant benefit of adjuvant chemotherapy in improving outcomes for patients with HR-positive LRR.9,10 There is therefore no clear consensus on the use of adjuvant chemotherapy for patients with HR-positive HER2-negative LRR, and National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines recommend that decisions be made individually, considering clinical and pathological factors.11 Addressing this controversy is essential for developing evidence-based treatment strategies to improve patient outcomes and provide more precise guidance for clinical practice.

The main aim of this study was to evaluate the effectiveness of adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with HR-positive HER2-negative LRR of breast cancer. We conducted a comprehensive retrospective analysis of data from multiple institutions to address the existing uncertainty regarding the clinical relevance of chemotherapy in this specific patient population, and to identify patient subpopulations in whom chemotherapy might be more favorably associated with outcomes.

Methods

Study design

This was a multi-institutional retrospective cohort study conducted by the Japan Clinical Oncology Group Breast Cancer Study Group. The overall objective of the research was to evaluate the prognosis of HR-positive HER2-negative LRR of breast cancer. The primary endpoint of this report was invasive disease-free survival (iDFS) focusing on the association of adjuvant chemotherapy with prognosis in this patient population.

Patient selection

Patients included in this study were diagnosed with HR-positive HER2-negative LRR after primary breast cancer surgery. All consecutive patients who received curative surgery for HR-positive HER2-negative LRR between 1 January 2014 and 31 December 2018 were included in this study. Patients who received surgery for LRR after neoadjuvant chemotherapy were also included. Eligible patients had complete medical records and available follow-up data. The exclusion criteria were the presence of distant metastases at the time of LRR diagnosis, patients diagnosed as LRR of non-invasive breast cancer, and patients with bilateral invasive breast cancer.

Data collection

Data were collected retrospectively from electronic medical records of 41 institutions. The collected data included patient demographics (age, sex, menopausal status, BRCA status), details of the initial breast cancer diagnosis and treatment (surgery type, pathological tumor size, lymph node involvement, perioperative systemic therapies), specifics of LRR (date of diagnosis, location, subtype), treatment details for LRR (surgery, adjuvant chemotherapy, radiotherapy, endocrine therapy), and follow-up data (date of recurrence, progression, survival status). IBTR was defined as recurrence of breast cancer within the ipsilateral breast following initial treatment for primary breast cancer, most commonly after breast-conserving surgery. It includes both true recurrences and new primary tumors. Non-IBTR was defined as recurrences in the ipsilateral chest wall or ipsilateral regional lymph nodes, including the axillary, supraclavicular, infraclavicular, and internal mammary lymph nodes. Recurrences in the contralateral breast or contralateral axillary lymph nodes were excluded from this study.

Treatment

Adjuvant chemotherapy for LRR was administered at the discretion of the treating physician using standard regimens, including anthracycline-based regimens such as doxorubicin/epirubicin and cyclophosphamide and taxane-based regimens such as paclitaxel or docetaxel. The decision to administer adjuvant chemotherapy for LRR was made at the discretion of the treating physicians based on individual clinical judgment and patient preferences, in the context of shared decision-making at each participating institution. The study included patients who received adjuvant chemotherapy as well as those who underwent neoadjuvant chemotherapy before surgical resection of LRR, although the latter group was limited in number. Patients who received adjuvant chemotherapy for LRR were included in the CTx group and patients without adjuvant chemotherapy were included in the no-CTx group. Adjuvant therapy was carried out according to the standard treatment for HR-positive operable breast cancer, including adjuvant endocrine therapy such as tamoxifen, toremifene, aromatase inhibitors (letrozole, anastrozole, exemestane), or luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone agonists (leuprolide, goserelin). For patients who experienced LRR during ongoing adjuvant endocrine therapy for primary breast cancer, treatment was generally switched to an alternative endocrine agent at the discretion of the treating physician (e.g. switching from tamoxifen to an aromatase inhibitor, or from one aromatase inhibitor to another), although detailed treatment patterns were not uniformly recorded due to the retrospective nature of this study.

Statistical analysis

The primary endpoint was iDFS, defined as the time from surgery for LRR to the first occurrence of invasive breast cancer recurrence, distant metastasis, or death from any cause. The secondary endpoint was overall survival (OS), defined as the time from surgery for LRR to death from any cause. A P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Subgroup analyses of iDFS were conducted based on clinicopathological factors. The primary analysis of the association between adjuvant chemotherapy and iDFS was carried out using a double-robust Cox proportional hazards model combined with inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW), allowing for adjustment of both measured confounders and weighting for baseline characteristics.

Propensity scores were estimated using a logistic regression model including variables such as year of LRR diagnosis, age category, recurrence type (IBTR versus non-IBTR), recurrence-free survival from primary surgery, timing of LRR in relation to endocrine therapy, prior chemotherapy for primary breast cancer, Ki-67/grade category (Ki-67 ≥30% or grade 3), use of CDK4/6 inhibitors, and receipt of radiotherapy for LRR. IPTW generated a weighted pseudo-population, and covariate balance was evaluated. As a sensitivity analysis, we also conducted a propensity score-matched analysis using 1 : 1 nearest-neighbor matching without replacement. Both the primary and sensitivity analyses demonstrated consistent trends in the association between chemotherapy and iDFS.

To illustrate the distribution of estimated propensity scores, histograms for each treatment group are provided in Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105889. These figures demonstrate sufficient overlap between the groups and the absence of extreme values, supporting the appropriateness of IPTW without the need for trimming.

Additional stratified Cox proportional hazards models were conducted using menopausal status and type of surgery as stratification variables to account for potential time-varying effects. These models yielded results consistent with the primary analysis for iDFS and are presented in the Supplementary Tables S1 and S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105889.

Ethical considerations

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional review boards of Fukushima Medical University (#2022-055). The need for informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the study, but patient confidentiality was maintained by anonymizing personal information.

Results

Patient characteristics and treatment for primary breast cancer

A total of 958 patients with HR-positive HER2-negative LRR of breast cancer were included in this analysis (Figure 1). The median age at the time of LRR diagnosis was 55 years [interquartile range (IQR) 47-68 years]. Among 78 patients who received germline BRCA1/2 genetic testing, 16 had pathogenic variants. Most patients were female; five patients were male. Information on the subtype of the primary breast tumor was also collected. A summary of subtype concordance and discordance between the primary tumor and the LRR is presented in Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105889.

Regarding treatment for primary breast cancer, 343 patients (36%) underwent mastectomy, while 611 patients (64%) underwent breast-conserving surgery. Sentinel lymph node biopsy and axillary lymph node dissection was carried out in 607 patients (63%) and 340 patients (35%), respectively. Pathological stage of primary breast cancer was stage 0 in 162 patients (17%), stage I in 406 patients (42%), stage II in 322 patients (37%), and stage III in 68 patients (7%), indicating that the majority of patients had early-stage disease (stage 0-II). Information on Ki-67 and histological grade was not available in all cases; Ki-67 data were missing in 26% and histological grade data were missing in 58% of patients, as detailed in Table 1.

A total of 248 patients (26%) received perioperative chemotherapy for primary breast cancer, including anthracyclines, taxanes, and cyclophosphamide, and 687 patients (72%) received adjuvant endocrine therapy for primary breast cancer (Table 1). Radiotherapy following surgery for the primary tumor was administered to 411 patients (43%).

The median time from the initial surgery for primary breast cancer to LRR diagnosis was 9.5 years (IQR 3.1-10.1 years), which was from December 1989 to September 2018. Radiotherapy was carried out in 222 patients (23%) after surgery for LRR.

Treatment for LRR

Among the 958 patients, 509 (53%) had IBTR and 449 (47%) had non-IBTR. At the time of LRR surgery, number of positive lymph nodes was 0 in 514 patients (53%), 1-3 in 214 patients (22%), 4-9 in 59 patients (6%), ≥10 in 25 patients (3%), and 141 patients (15%) did not receive axillary surgery or sentinel lymph node biopsy. After surgery for LRR, 235 patients (25%) received chemotherapy for LRR (CTx group) and 722 (75%) did not (no-CTx group). Among the 235 patients who received chemotherapy for LRR (CTx group), most received adjuvant chemotherapy following surgery. A small subset of patients (n = 11) received neoadjuvant chemotherapy before surgery for LRR. Thirty-one patients (3%) in the CTx group received anthracycline-based regimens, 85 (9%) received anthracycline and taxane-based regimens, 77 (8%) received taxane-based regimens, and 42 (4%) received other regimens. Compared with the no-CTx group, the CTx group included more patients aged 41-60 years, non-IBTR, and recurrences during adjuvant endocrine therapy for primary breast cancer. Mastectomy was carried out in 405 patients (42%) and breast-conserving surgery in 126 patients (13%). Chest wall/skin tumor resection was carried out in 205 patients (21%), sentinel lymph node biopsy was carried out in 214 patients (22%), and axillary dissection was carried out in 290 patients (30%). Most patients received post-operative endocrine therapy, with tamoxifen (30%), aromatase inhibitors (50%), and luteinizing hormone-releasing hormone agonists (11%) being the most commonly used medications. Only 10 patients (1%) received fulvestrant, and only 4 received CDK4/6 inhibitors.

Radiotherapy after LRR was administered in 222 patients (23%). Among those who did not receive radiotherapy at recurrence, 52% had previously undergone radiotherapy for their primary breast cancer. Among the 509 patients with IBTR, 345 (68%) patients underwent mastectomy for LRR and 49 (10%) received radiotherapy after LRR. In contrast, radiotherapy was administered in 79 of 243 patients (33%) with lymph node recurrence and in 93 of 204 patients (46%) with chest wall or skin recurrence. These data indicate a differential application of radiotherapy after LRR depending on the site of recurrence and prior treatment history. The relatively low use of radiotherapy after LRR can be partly explained by clinical considerations such as prior irradiation and surgical approach. In particular, re-irradiation is generally avoided in patients with IBTR who previously received radiation following breast-conserving surgery, or in those who underwent mastectomy for LRR. These patterns are consistent with clinical practice and highlight the variation in radiation use based on recurrence sites and prior treatment history.

iDFS after treatment for LRR

The overall 5-year iDFS rate after treatment for LRR in the entire cohort was 75.4% [95% confidence interval (CI) 72.4% to 78.2%]. Subgroup analysis revealed a 5-year iDFS rate of 83.0% (95% CI 79.2% to 86.2%) in patients with IBTR and 66.8% (95% CI 62.0% to 71.3%) in those with non-IBTR. Among all patients, the 5-year iDFS rate was 75.8% (95% CI 69.4% to 81.2%) in those who received radiotherapy for LRR and 75.3% (95% CI 71.8% to 78.5%) in those who did not. In the non-IBTR subgroup, the 5-year iDFS was 72.4% (95% CI 64.8% to 78.9%) with radiotherapy and 63.2% (95% CI 57.0% to 69.1%) without radiotherapy. In contrast, in the IBTR subgroup, there was no appreciable difference in iDFS between patients with and without radiotherapy.

The 5-year iDFS rate after treatment for LRR was comparable between the CTx and no-CTx groups (76.3% versus 75.0%, respectively) (P = 0.8357), without adjustment for patient characteristics.

The multivariate Cox proportional hazards model was used to adjust for patient characteristics and evaluate the association with adjuvant chemotherapy for LRR. The analysis indicated that chemotherapy was associated with a significantly better iDFS [hazard ratio (HR) 0.70, 95% CI 0.49-0.99, P = 0.045] (Table 2). Given the potential differences in chemotherapy efficacy around the age of 50 years, an analysis using 50 years as the age cut-off was conducted, which showed a consistent result [chemotherapy of hazard ratio 0.66 (95% CI 0.47-0.94) for iDFS].

As a sensitivity analysis, a propensity score-matched Cox regression model was also carried out, yielding a consistent trend with a hazard ratio of 0.66 (95% CI 0.40-1.10), further supporting the robustness of the observed association (Figure 2B).

In addition, we carried out stratified Cox proportional hazards analyses using menopausal status and surgery type (mastectomy versus breast-conserving surgery versus surgery plus reconstruction) as stratification variables. These analyses allow the baseline hazard to vary across strata and account for differences in clinical management strategies. The association between chemotherapy and improved iDFS remained statistically significant in both models, stratified by menopausal status of hazard ratio 0.69 (95% CI 0.48-0.98; P = 0.041) and stratified by surgery type of hazard ratio 0.67 (95% CI 0.47-0.96; P = 0.031), as shown in Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2025.105889.

Subgroup analyses for adjuvant chemotherapy for LRR

Subgroup analyses indicated a significant association with improved iDFS for patients with non-IBTR (hazard ratio 0.43, 95% CI 0.27-0.68), those diagnosed during adjuvant endocrine therapy (hazard ratio 0.41, 95% CI 0.23-0.75), and those who did not receive perioperative chemotherapy for primary breast cancer (hazard ratio 0.47, 95% CI 0.29-0.77) (Figure 3). In the non-IBTR subgroup, 5-year iDFS rate was 62.3% (95% CI 56.2% to 68.0%) in no-CTx, and 76.0% (95% CI 67.9% to 82.5%) in CTx. In contrast, 5-year iDFS rate was 84.3% (95% CI 80.2% to 87.7%) in no-CTx and 77.0% (95% CI 66.9% to 84.7%) in CTx, with hazard ratio 1.19 (95% CI 0.67% to 2.10%) in IBTR subgroup. Other subgroup analyses, including age, recurrence-free interval, Ki-67 labeling/proliferation index, and tumor grade showed no significant association with improved iDFS of CTx in patients with LRR.

OS

The 5-year OS rate for the entire cohort was 93.1% (95% CI 91.1% to 94.6%). A significant difference was observed between the CTx group (88.6%) and no-CTx group (94.6%) before adjustment (P = 0.004).

After adjustment, IPTW analysis also showed a poorer OS in the CTx group compared with the no-CTx group (hazard ratio 1.48, 95% CI 1.07-2.06). In the double-robust Cox proportional hazards analysis, however, there was no significant difference in OS between the two groups (hazard ratio 1.40, 95% CI 0.83-2.35). Similarly, propensity score matching analysis indicated a trend toward poorer OS in the CTx group compared with the no-CTx group (hazard ratio 1.69, 95% CI 0.83-3.46), although this difference was not statistically significant.

Discussion

This study suggested that adjuvant chemotherapy may improve iDFS after resection of LRR in patients with HR-positive HER2-negative breast cancer. The observed association was particularly evident in patients with non-IBTR, those diagnosed during adjuvant endocrine therapy, and those who did not receive perioperative chemotherapy for primary breast cancer. These subgroup findings suggest that adjuvant chemotherapy may be associated with favorable outcomes in selected patients with locoregional recurrence who exhibit higher-risk features. The retrospective design, however, limits any definitive conclusions regarding treatment responsiveness. This multicenter retrospective analysis was conducted after adjusting for background factors using propensity scores based on clinicopathological factors in 958 cases. This finding notably contrasts with the results of the CALOR trial, which found no significant benefit of adjuvant chemotherapy in patients with HR-positive HER2-negative LRR.9,10

The CALOR trial did not fully analyze these factors because of the small number of patients. The observed improvement in iDFS for these subgroups suggests that specific patient characteristics and treatment histories may be associated with differential outcomes following chemotherapy, thus highlighting the importance of individualized treatment strategies to optimize treatment decision-making.

Histological grade 3 or Ki-67 index ≥30% was identified as a poor prognostic factor for iDFS in the multivariate analysis (Table 2). While these factors were anticipated to predict chemotherapy sensitivity, subgroup analyses demonstrated that the association between chemotherapy and iDFS was comparable regardless of tumor grade or Ki-67 status (Figure 3). These findings suggest that although tumor grade and Ki-67 are important prognostic indicators, they did not significantly modify the association between chemotherapy and iDFS in patients with HR-positive HER2-negative LRR.

Despite the significant improvement in iDFS in the CTx group, OS showed an opposite trend. Several factors may underlie this discrepancy. Firstly, although we used IPTW and double-robust methods to minimize baseline imbalances, residual confounding due to unmeasured variables—such as performance status, comorbidities, or tumor biology beyond HR, HER2 status, Ki-67, and grade—may have influenced survival outcomes. Secondly, the follow-up period may have been insufficient to capture differences in OS, especially in HR-positive breast cancer, where events accumulate more slowly over time compared with other subtypes. Thirdly, post-recurrence treatment strategies were not standardized or uniformly captured in this retrospective study, and variability in subsequent therapies—including access to novel agents such as CDK4/6 inhibitors—may have affected OS independently of initial treatment for LRR. Moreover, we were unable to evaluate treatment-related adverse events or toxicity-related mortality. Some patients who received adjuvant chemotherapy after LRR might have experienced serious complications or treatment intolerance, potentially contributing to poorer long-term outcomes despite improved disease control. Such adverse effects could disproportionately impact OS without affecting iDFS, especially in older or comorbid patients. Future prospective studies with more granular data on toxicity profiles and post-recurrence management are needed to clarify the balance of benefit and risk in this population.

In this analysis, we could not evaluate the impact of chemotherapy regimens. When anthracycline-based regimens were used for primary breast cancer, anthracycline-based CTx regimens were avoided for LRR, and the docetaxel and cyclophosphamide TC regimen was administered in such cases. Across institutions, various standard regimens—including anthracycline-based, taxane-based, or TC protocols—were selected based on physician discretion and prior treatments. The study was not designed to compare the efficacy between these regimens, however. Our findings therefore cannot inform the optimal choice of adjuvant chemotherapy regimen for HR-positive HER2-negative LRR. Further studies are needed to determine whether anthracycline plus taxane or TC should be preferred in this setting.

This study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective, multi-institutional cohort study, the decision to administer adjuvant chemotherapy was not standardized and may have been influenced by physician and institutional preferences, patient characteristics, and other unmeasured factors. Although we adjusted for known clinicopathological factors using multivariable and propensity score analyses, residual confounding cannot be ruled out. Secondly, the number of patients who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy for LRR was small, limiting the ability to evaluate treatment sequencing. Thirdly, pathological data such as Ki-67 index and histological grade were missing in a considerable number of cases, which may have limited our ability to fully characterize tumor biology and its influence on prognosis. The proportion of missingness was comparable between treatment groups, however, minimizing the risk of bias from differential missingness.

Another limitation is that we did not distinguish between true recurrences and new primary tumors in cases of IBTR. This distinction is often difficult to make in retrospective, multi-institutional datasets, especially in the absence of detailed pathological or molecular comparison. This heterogeneity may have attenuated the observed association between adjuvant chemotherapy and outcomes in this subgroup. Without genomic distinction, our interpretation of chemotherapy benefit in IBTR cases remains limited. Consequently, our IBTR cohort may include a biologically heterogeneous population, which could contribute to variability in clinical outcomes. Finally, although overall survival was analyzed as a secondary endpoint, the follow-up period may not have been sufficient to capture long-term differences in survival, and the number of events was relatively limited.

Despite these limitations, the present study has several notable strengths. It represents one of the largest multi-institutional cohorts to evaluate treatment patterns and outcomes in patients with HR-positive HER2-negative LRR of breast cancer. The use of multivariable and propensity score-based statistical approaches allowed for more robust adjustment of confounding factors. Furthermore, subgroup analyses provided insight into specific patient populations in whom chemotherapy was associated with improved iDFS.

Further research into biomarkers that can predict the response to chemotherapy in patients with HR-positive HER2-negative LRR could also help to refine treatment strategies and improve outcomes. The CDK4/6 inhibitors abemaciclib and ribociclib, but not palbociclib, have demonstrated effectiveness for HR-positive HER2-negative high-risk primary breast cancer12, 13, 14, 15, 16; however, the current study was unable to evaluate the effects of adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibitors for LRR. The ongoing POLAR trial is evaluating the efficacy of adjuvant palbociclib for LRR (NCT03820830), and we are also conducting the JCOG2313: AURA trial (jRCTs031240666, NCT07190443) to evaluate the efficacy of adjuvant abemaciclib following curative treatment of LRR. Data from these studies may contribute to further improvements in treatment strategies.

Conclusions

This study suggests that adjuvant chemotherapy is associated with better iDFS in patients with HR-positive HER2-negative LRR of breast cancer, particularly in patients with non-IBTR, recurrences during adjuvant endocrine therapy for primary breast cancer, and those who did not receive perioperative chemotherapy for primary breast cancer. However, as this study is a retrospective analysis, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Further studies are needed to determine the optimal treatment for HR-positive HER2-negative LRR.

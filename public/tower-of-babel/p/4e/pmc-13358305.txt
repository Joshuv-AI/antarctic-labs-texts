Patient preferences for treatment attributes in osteoarthritis: protocol for a systematic review of stated preference studies
Authors: Min Liu, Tara Officer, Mickael Hiligsmann, Elaine Dennison
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionOsteoarthritis (OA) is a common chronic condition requiring long-term management, for which shared decision-making is increasingly recognised as an important component of care. Stated preference (SP) methods, including discrete choice experiments (DCE), conjoint analysis (CA) and best–worst scaling (BWS), are increasingly used to quantify patient preferences for treatment attributes and to inform clinical and policy decisions. However, evidence from SP studies in OA has not been comprehensively synthesised. This protocol describes a systematic review aiming to identify, appraise and synthesise evidence on the relative importance of treatment attributes for patients with OA.Methods and analysisA comprehensive search will be conducted in PubMed, Embase, CINAHL, Scopus, Dimensions and OpenAlex from database inception to January 2026. Eligible studies will include quantitative SP studies—encompassing DCE, CA and BWS—evaluating patient preferences for pharmacological, non-pharmacological or surgical treatments in OA. Two reviewers will independently screen studies, extract data using a standardised form and assess methodological quality using the Purpose, Respondents, Explanation, Findings and Significance (PREFS) checklist, the International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research (ISPOR) Good Research Practices for CA and DCE and the DIscrete choice experiment REporting ChecklisT (DIRECT) checklist for reporting quality. A structured narrative synthesis will summarise and categorise key treatment attributes (eg, efficacy, safety, cost and mode of administration). Where studies are sufficiently comparable in attributes and outcome reporting, a random-effects meta-analysis may be conducted to pool preference estimates, such as relative importance or willingness-to-pay values.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required, as this review will use published data only. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publication and conference presentations.PROSPERO registration numberCRD420251181273.

Introduction

Rationale

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a common and disabling condition that poses growing challenges to healthcare systems and broader society, impairing individual quality of life and generating significant socioeconomic costs.1 2 OA management is inherently preference-sensitive, encompassing non-pharmacological, pharmacological and surgical options. Each approach involves trade-offs in terms of efficacy, side effects, costs and patient experience. While the clinical efficacy of these treatments is well established, their real-world effectiveness is often compromised by poor patient adherence, highlighting the need for preference-sensitive approaches to treatment planning.3 4 Developing and implementing treatment strategies that take patient preferences into account is essential to improving adherence and treatment outcomes.

Stated preference (SP) techniques offer a systematic means of eliciting and quantifying what patients value in their care. These methods are widely used in healthcare research to investigate patient preferences and evaluate the relative importance of treatment characteristics and outcomes.5 Within the SP framework, the most commonly applied approaches—including discrete choice experiments (DCE), conjoint analysis (CA) and best–worst scaling (BWS)—are designed to evaluate preferences for multiattribute healthcare interventions and quantify the trade-offs individuals are willing to make between different treatment features. Among these, DCE have become one of the most widely applied methods in health economics, owing to their ability to simultaneously estimate preferences for multiple attributes and the trade-offs between them.6 7

Despite growth in SP studies investigating OA treatment preferences in recent years, evidence remains fragmented and geographically concentrated, with most studies originating from Europe and North America.8 9 Furthermore, existing studies often focus on specific treatment modalities without systematically comparing interventions or synthesising the attributes most valued by patients. To date, no systematic review has comprehensively mapped this body of research.

Accordingly, this systematic review adopts a broad methodological scope—encompassing DCE, CA, and BWS—with the aim of comprehensively synthesising evidence from quantitative SP studies to identify the treatment attributes and preferences most valued by patients with OA.

Our systematic review aims to identify, synthesise and critically appraise quantitative SP studies, including DCE, CA and BWS, examining treatment preferences among patients with OA. Specifically, this review seeks to:

Identify the treatment attributes and levels used in existing SP studies of OA interventions and determine the most important attributes driving patient preference.

Methods

This systematic review protocol is reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) 2015 statement.10 The completed review will be reported following the PRISMA 2020 guidelines.11 The literature search strategy will be reported in line with PRISMA-S12 recommendations and reviewed by an experienced university librarian with reference to the Peer Review of Electronic Search Strategies (PRESS) checklist13 to ensure transparency and reproducibility. The protocol was prospectively registered with the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; registration number: CRD420251181273).

Eligibility criteria (PICOS)

Eligibility criteria are defined using the PICOS framework:

Population (P): studies will be included if they involve people with OA, including knee, hip, hand or generalised OA. Studies primarily focused on other rheumatological or musculoskeletal conditions, such as rheumatoid arthritis, osteoporosis or gout, will be excluded unless quantitative preference data specific to the OA subgroup are reported separately; no minimum proportion of OA participants is required, provided that separate subgroup data are independently extractable. Studies combining patient data with those of other stakeholders (such as clinicians, caregivers or policymakers) will also be excluded unless patient preference data are independently reported and extractable.

Intervention (I): all treatment modalities for OA will be considered, including non-pharmacological interventions (eg, exercise, physiotherapy and lifestyle modification), pharmacological treatments (eg, analgesics, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and biologics) and surgical options (eg, joint replacement). Studies that do not focus on treatment preferences (eg, diagnosis, screening or prevention) will be excluded.

Comparators (C): no explicit comparator is required. SP methods (DCE, CA or BWS) inherently involve comparisons across attributes, attribute levels or intervention alternatives. Therefore, studies that elicit preferences through stated trade-offs will be considered eligible, regardless of whether an external control or reference group is included.

Outcomes (O): eligible studies must report quantitative measures of patient preferences for OA treatments derived from SP methods (DCE, CA or BWS). The primary outcomes will include estimates of preference weights or utilities, relative importance of treatment attributes (eg, efficacy, side effects, cost, convenience) and willingness-to-pay (WTP), where available. Secondary outcomes will include analyses of preference heterogeneity across patient subgroups (eg, age, sex, disease severity or country). Studies reporting only qualitative findings without quantitative modelling will be excluded.

Study design (S): this review will include quantitative SP studies that apply DCE, CA or BWS to examine treatment preferences among individuals with OA.

Information sources

A comprehensive literature search will be conducted across the following electronic sources: PubMed (including MEDLINE) for biomedical and clinical research, Embase for pharmacological and international biomedical studies and CINAHL for nursing and allied health literature. To broaden the scope and incorporate multidisciplinary perspectives, additional databases will be included: Scopus, Dimensions and OpenAlex, which provide access to a wide range of scholarly and open-access research outputs.

The search will cover studies published from the inception of each database up to January 2026. Only studies published in English will be considered for inclusion.

Search strategy

The search strategy will combine controlled vocabulary (eg, MeSH in PubMed, Emtree in Embase) and free-text keywords, structured around two core concepts:

Condition: OA

Condition: OA

Methodology: DCE, CA, BWS and other SP methods.

Methodology: DCE, CA, BWS and other SP methods.

To maintain inclusivity and avoid unnecessary restrictions, population filters (eg, limiting to patients only) will not be applied at the search stage to maximise sensitivity and avoid inadvertently excluding relevant studies. Studies involving non-patient populations will be addressed at the screening stage following the eligibility criteria.

Preference-related terms (eg, willingness to pay and treatment preference) will also be included in the search strategy to improve the identification of studies employing SP methods.

Search strings will be constructed using Boolean logic, combining the core concepts with “AND” and linking synonyms within each concept using “OR”. The search strategy, developed by the authors, will be independently reviewed by an experienced university librarian to ensure comprehensiveness and accuracy. The draft MEDLINE (via PubMed) strategy is provided in online supplemental appendix 1 and will be adapted for use across the other databases listed in the Information Sources section. Of note, a potential limitation of this review is that it excludes qualitative studies, which may identify patient-relevant treatment attributes not yet evaluated in quantitative stated-preference research. Consequently, some emerging or context-specific patient preferences may not be captured.

Study records

Data management

All search results will be imported into Rayyan for collaborative screening and data management. Duplicate records will be automatically removed and manually verified. Screening will be conducted in two stages (title/abstract and full text) based on predefined eligibility criteria. All screening decisions will be managed in Rayyan, and the final extracted data will be securely backed up in encrypted cloud storage.

Selection process

Study selection will follow a structured process. Initially, two reviewers will independently screen titles and abstracts of all retrieved records and classify each study as include, exclude or uncertain/maybe. All records marked for inclusion or as uncertain by either reviewer will advance to the full-text stage. The same reviewers will then independently assess the full texts to determine final eligibility. A calibration exercise will be conducted before title and abstract screening. Any discrepancies, including disagreements or persistent uncertainties, will be resolved through consensus or, if necessary, by a third reviewer. The entire selection process will be documented and presented in a11 flow diagram (see figure 1), including reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage. Reviewers will not be blinded to journal titles, authors or institutions.

Data collection process

Data extraction will be carried out using a structured form specifically designed for this review. For clearly defined, objective study-level details, such as publication year, country of origin and sample size, one reviewer will extract the data, and a second reviewer will check for accuracy. For elements requiring interpretation, including the classification of attributes, levels, and analytical approaches, two reviewers will independently extract the data to ensure consistency and minimise potential bias. Discrepancies during data extraction will be resolved via discussion or arbitration by consulting a third reviewer.

Before starting full data extraction, the form will be tested on several included studies to check its clarity and make necessary adjustments. A separate calibration exercise will be undertaken before formal data extraction. If additional information cannot be obtained, the study will be included using the available data, and any resulting limitations will be documented and considered when interpreting the findings.

Outcomes and prioritisation

This review will focus on quantitative estimates of patient preferences for OA treatment attributes, elicited through SP methods. Key attributes include treatment efficacy, risk of side effects, cost and convenience of administration. These dimensions are prioritised due to their frequent appearance in the literature and their relevance to real-world treatment adherence and effectiveness. Secondary outcomes will include:

Relative importance of treatment attributes, quantified through preference weights or utility values.

Relative importance of treatment attributes, quantified through preference weights or utility values.

WTP estimates, where available, to assess the economic valuation of treatment benefits.

Preference heterogeneity, explored through subgroup analyses (eg, by age, sex, disease severity or country) and model-based approaches (eg, latent class analysis and mixed logit models).

Understanding these secondary outcomes will offer deeper insights into how patient preferences vary across populations and contexts. Such understanding is essential for informing the design of patient-centred interventions and guiding health policy decisions—particularly in under-represented regions, such as parts of Asia and the Pacific, and other under-represented health systems.

Risk of bias in individual studies

The methodological quality of all included studies will be assessed at the study level using the Purpose, Respondents, Explanation, Findings and Significance (PREFS) checklist,17 a tool developed for evaluating preference studies, which assesses quality across five domains: Purpose, Respondents, Explanation, Findings and Significance.

To ensure a comprehensive assessment of methodological rigour, additional criteria adapted from the International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcomes Research (ISPOR) Good Research Practices for CA and DCE will be incorporated.14 15 Furthermore, reporting quality will be evaluated using the DIscrete choice experiment REporting ChecklisT (DIRECT) checklist,16 which provides structured guidance for assessing the transparency and completeness of DCE reporting. Two reviewers will perform the assessments independently, with discrepancies resolved following the same procedure outlined in the Data Collection Process section.

Data synthesis

Given the inherent variability in SP study designs, a structured narrative synthesis will be the primary approach to integrate findings, following the framework by Popay et al.18 This will focus on categorising core treatment attributes (eg, efficacy, safety and cost) and interpreting observed trade-offs across different patient populations.

However, if a subset of studies is found to be sufficiently similar in design, attributes and model specifications, the feasibility of a quantitative synthesis will be explored. Preference estimates, such as relative importance scores or WTP values, will be standardised where possible (eg, using purchasing power parity for WTP). In such cases, a random-effects model may be applied to account for between-study variability, with statistical heterogeneity assessed via the I2 statistic.

Bias(es)

We will consider the following potential sources of bias:

Selective reporting bias: each study will be assessed for completeness and transparency in reporting key methodological elements, including attribute identification and selection, experimental design, model specification and presentation of all estimated parameters. Studies reporting only significant or primary findings will be flagged for potential selective reporting bias. To minimise the risk of multiple reporting, we will cross-check author names, funding sources, sample details, study contexts and data collection timelines to identify any overlapping or duplicate datasets. If several papers stem from the same study, we will extract data only once, giving precedence to the version that offers the most comprehensive and methodologically robust information.

Confidence in cumulative evidence

As this review synthesises evidence from quantitative SP studies rather than interventional trials, the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluations framework will not be applied directly. Instead, overall confidence in the cumulative evidence will be evaluated through a domain-based assessment that combines methodological quality, reporting transparency and the consistency and applicability of findings across studies.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design of this systematic review protocol. However, patient involvement is planned in subsequent stages of the broader research programme, including qualitative interviews and/or focus groups to inform the development of attributes and levels for a future DCE. Findings from the present review will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations. Summaries of findings from this review and subsequent research will be made available to patients where appropriate.

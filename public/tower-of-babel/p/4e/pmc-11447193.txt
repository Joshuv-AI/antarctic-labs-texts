Screening of prognostic factors and survival analysis based on histological type for perimenopausal endometrial carcinoma treated with hysterectomy
Authors: Luyao Kang, Gaili Ji, Duan Liu, Yaping Wang, Jie Meng, Hongyu Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeThis study aimed to explore the prognostic factors and survival patterns based on the histological type for the perimenopausal endometrial carcinoma (PIPEC) patients treated with hysterectomy.MethodsThe PIPEC patients were selected from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database. Methods of random survival forest (RSF) and Cox regression were used to identify the possible prognostic factors of PIPEC patients. Then overall survival (OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS) of PIPEC data were analyzed by histological types with regional lymph nodes status and SEER-stage to investigate the survival patterns of the PIPEC patients.ResultsA total of 14,178 PIPEC patients were included in the study. We found tumor size, grade, histology, SEER-stage, AJCC-stage, AJCC-T stage, metastasis to distant organs and regional lymph nodes status had a significant survival outcome for PIPEC both for OS and CSS (all p < 0.05). Regardless of regional lymph nodes status and SEER-stage for OS and CSS, the low-grade endometrioid carcinoma had the best prognosis outcome, followed by the mix cell adenocarcinoma and high-grade endometrioid carcinoma, while the carcinosarcoma and undifferentiated carcinoma had relatively poor prognosis outcome. And the survival patterns of different histological types of PIPEC were diverse and changed along with the time.ConclusionWe identified the possible prognostic factors of PIPEC patients treated with hysterectomy. And survival analysis based on the regional lymph nodes status and SEER-stage revealed the different histological types of PIPEC had diverse survival patterns, which will be helpful for guiding clinical practice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-024-01403-4.

Introduction

Endometrial cancer is the second most prevalent tumor in the female reproductive system [1]. 67% of patients with endometrial carcinoma (EC) can be diagnosed at an early stage, and the 5-year survival probability can reach 81%; however, the 5-year survival probability of advanced patients is only 15% to 17% [2]. The WHO classifies endometrial carcinoma according to morphology with the aid of immunohistochemistry [3], which divides EC into endometrioid carcinoma, serous carcinoma, clear cell adenocarcinoma, mixed cell adenocarcinoma, undifferentiated carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, mesonephric adenocarcinoma, mesonephric-like adenocarcinoma, mucinous carcinoma, intestinal type and carcinosarcoma. Endometrioid carcinoma is graded according to their architectural complexity, including the low-grade tumors (grade 1 and 2) and the high-grade (grade 3) tumors. Compared to low-grade endometrioid carcinoma, high-grade endometrioid carcinoma and non-endometrioid tumors have been identified as high-risk subtypes with a poorer prognosis [4–6], accounting for more than 50% of uterine cancer deaths [7, 8].

The mainstay of treatment for EC is total hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy [9–11], which enables the elimination of the primary tumor and identifies the patients who may be at risk of experiencing recurrences, thus, tailoring the optimal adjuvant treatment [12]. Recent research indicates that compared to conventional open surgery, minimally invasive surgery (MIS) was not associated with adverse survival outcomes with similar overall and disease free survival [13–15], which was associated with less tissue trauma, lower estimated blood loss, less pain, shorter hospital stay, and higher quality of life [16]. And the application of enhanced recovery after surgery could also produce a significant positive impact on health-related quality of life [17].

EC is commonly observed in postmenopausal women [18], but there is an alarming increase described in the 40–44 years age group experiencing the perimenopausal period [19]. At the same time, perimenopausal women always have irregular menstruation with abnormal uterine bleeding due to changes in hormonal status in the body [20–22] which overlaps with postmenopausal abnormal uterine bleeding which is one of the typical manifestations of EC[10], making it important to take a concern for the perimenopausal endometrial carcinoma (PIPEC) patients.

Due to the prognostic factors and survival patterns of the PIPEC patients treated with hysterectomy being still vague, therefore, in this study, we aim to explore the prognostic factors and survival patterns based on the histological type for the PIPEC patients treated with hysterectomy. The data we used were obtained from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database (https://seer.cancer.gov) which contains a large number of tumor patient data related to demographic characteristics, disease status, treatment methods and prognosis information.

Materials and methods

Study population

We used SEER*Stat software [version 8.4.3; SEER 17 Research Data, November 2022 sub (2000–2020 varying) database] to identify the target PIPEC patients. Women meeting the following criteria were included in the study: I: primary site: ICD-O-3 of C54.1, endometrium; II: unique patient IDs; III: single primary tumor; IV: age 45 to 55 years; V: hysterectomy performed; VI: histological types of 8002/3, 8070/3, 8144/3, 8310/3, 8323/3, 8380/3, 8441/3, 8980/3, 9110/3, 9113/3; VII: known grade; VIII: SEER-stage; IX: AJCC-stage; AJCC-TNM stage; X: known state of bilateral salpingo-oophoron; XI: known regional lymph nodes status; XII: known operation of regional lymph nodes; XIII: complete follow-up with survival months more than 0 month. Study variables

Clinical variables extracted from the SEER database included age at diagnosis (y), race, marital status, location, income, time from diagnosis to treatment, tumor size (cm), grade, histology, SEER-stage, AJCC-stage, AJCC-T stage, AJCC-M stage, metastasis to distant organs, regional lymph nodes status, peritoneal cytology, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, operation of regional lymph nodes, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, neoadjuvant treatment. Derived from the Grade Record (thru 2017) variable, the grade in this study was a four-grade system in which grade III (poorly differentiated) and grade IV (undifferentiated) are equal to G3 (poorly differentiated or undifferentiated) in the three-grade system [23]. SEER-stage was derived by the derived Summary Stage 2000 variable [24]. The SEER staging system corresponds to the commonly used International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) staging system in the following way: localized (FIGO IA, IB, IC, I not otherwise specified [NOS]), regional (FIGO IIA, IIB, IIC, II NOS, IIIA, IIIB, IIIC, III NOS), distant (FIGO IVA, IVB) [24]. Regional lymph nodes status was derived from the CS (Collaborative Stage Data Collection System) lymph nodes variable, which collects not only the pathologic information but also the non-pathologic information, such as imaging examination or other non-invasive clinical evidences. Operation of regional lymph nodes was derived from the RX Summ-Scope Reg LN Sur variable.

Statistical analysis

Patient clinical characteristics were displayed based on the histological type. The continuous variables were represented by mean ± SD, and categorical variables were represented by frequency. Univariate Cox regression was used to analyze the correlation between variables and survival outcomes. The variables with significance (p < 0.05) for OS and CSS were separately brought into the RSF analysis. RSF was generated by creating 500 trees, and variables with permutation importance > 0.01, which were most frequently split the branches near the trunk, were screened for subsequent multivariate Cox regression. We utilized the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and the area under the curve (AUC) to measure the discriminative performance of the RSF model with filtered variables. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was employed to assess the clinical usefulness of the RSF model. Additionally, the calibration curve was utilized to evaluate the calibration of the RSF model. The variables with significance (p < 0.05) in the multivariate Cox regression were identified as the independent prognostic factors for OS and CSS which were verified by the Kaplan–Meier analysis to evaluate the survival outcome in the total PIPEC patients.

The survival time was defined as the time from diagnosis to death or to the last follow-up. The 1-, 3-, 5-, and 10-year OS and CSS estimates were calculated according to the histological types and regional lymph nodes status (pelvic and para-aortic lymph nodes) and SEER-stage (localized, regional/distant). And the 95% confidence interval (CI) was also calculated. The Cox model was divided into four time periods, including ≤ 4 years, 4–8 years, 8–12 years, and > 12 years after diagnosis. The hazard ratio was calculated according to the regional lymph nodes status and SEER-stage with histological types in which a small number of special types of EC were combined and collectively named as special histological types, which included the serous carcinoma, carcinosarcoma, clear cell adenocarcinoma, undifferentiated carcinoma, and squamous cell carcinoma.

All analyses were performed using packages of R 4.3.3 (https://www.r-project.org/), containing “randomForestSRC” [25], “ezcox” [26], “survival” [27], “survminer” [28], “timeROC” [29], “tidyverse” [30], “compareGroups” [31]. A bilateral p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient clinical characteristics

In our study, a total of 14,178 eligible PIPEC patients (Table S1) were included in the study. For the overall eligible PIPEC patients, the most common histological type was low-grade endometrioid (82.1%), followed by high-grade endometrioid (10.1%) and mixed cell (5.32%), and squamous cell was quite rare (0.11%). Compared to other histological types, low-grade endometrioid was diagnosed at younger ages, and serous and clear cell were relatively older. The prevalence of undifferentiated carcinoma was found to be higher among the Asia–Pacific population. Carcinosarcoma was more frequently observed in cases with tumor sizes exceeding four centimeters, advanced AJCC-stage, AJCC-T4, AJCC-M1, and distant organ metastasis. Furthermore, a significant number of patients with low-grade endometrioid carcinoma did not receive chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or neoadjuvant therapy. Additionally, among different histological types, patients with low-grade endometrioid carcinoma had the highest proportion of all negative regional lymph nodes, and the rate of lymphadenectomy was the lowest. Because the variable of the regional lymph nodes collects not only the pathologic information but also non-pathologic information, thus, there were some patients who did not undergo lymphadenectomy while still having information about regional lymph nodes status.

Survival analysis by histological types and regional lymph nodes for OS and CSS

The 1-, 3-, 5-, and 10-year OS estimates by histological types and regional lymph nodes status were provided in Table S3. For the regional lymph nodes all negative group, the 1-, 3-, 5-, and 10-year survival probability of the low-grade endometrioid, high-grade endometrioid, mixed cell, and clear cell carcinoma were all above 80%, while the carcinosarcoma and undifferentiated carcinoma were less than 60%, except for the 1-year survival probability. The survival probability of the serous carcinoma decreased after diagnosis above 5 years, and the 10-year survival probability decreased to less than 70% (68.2, 95% CI = 58.5 to 79.5). Within 4 years after diagnosis, our study found that the hazard probability of the high-grade endometrioid, mixed cell, and special histological types carcinoma was higher than that of the low-grade endometrioid carcinoma. And the high-grade endometrioid carcinoma continuously had a higher hazard probability at 4–8 years after diagnosis (HR = 2.04, 95% CI = 1.50 to 2.77) (Table S4). For the pelvic or para-aortic lymph node positive group, except for the carcinosarcoma and undifferentiated carcinoma, the 1-year survival probability of other histological types carcinomas was estimated to be about 80% or higher. As for the 3- and 5-year survival probability, except for the low-grade endometrioid and mixed cell, other histological types carcinomas were lower than 80%, among which the undifferentiated carcinoma (33.3, 95% CI = 58.5 to 79.5) had the worst prognosis. Carcinosarcoma had the worst survival at 10 years (35.3, 95% CI = 19.9 to 62.6). And the time interval hazard probability showed that the high-grade endometrioid and special histological types carcinoma had a higher risk of mortality within 4 years, and the mixed cell adenocarcinoma had a higher risk of mortality in the 4–8 years survival interval, while there was no statistical significance at other time (Table S4). For the all positive (pelvic and para-aortic lymph node positive) group, the 1- and 3-year survival probability of the low-grade endometrioid carcinoma were still more than 90%, and the 1-year survival probability of the serous carcinoma and carcinosarcoma was more than 80%.

Survival analysis by histological types and SEER-stage for OS and CSS

For the localized stage group, survival probability was more than 80% (Table S5) except for the carcinosarcoma at the 3-, 5-, and 10-year. Compared with the low-grade endometrioid carcinoma, there was no statistical difference with other histological types carcinomas in the survival interval within 4 years. But the hazard probability of the high-grade endometrioid carcinoma increased (Table S6) in the interval of 4–8 years after diagnosis (HR = 1.94, 95% CI = 1.28 to 2.93). For the regional/distant stage group, except for the undifferentiated carcinoma and carcinosarcoma, the survival probability of other histological types carcinomas was more than 80%. Ruling out the low-grade endometrioid carcinoma, the survival probability of other histological types carcinomas decreased significantly among which the hazard probability of the undifferentiated carcinoma was the worst (33.3, 95% CI = 18.2 to 61.0). The changes of the 5- and 10-year risk of mortality were similar to the 3-year. Carcinosarcoma had the worst survival probability in 5-year (36.2, 95% CI = 27.1 to 48.5) and 10-year (34.0, 95% CI = 24.8 to 46.7) (Table S5). In contrast to the low-grade endometrioid carcinoma, other histological types carcinomas had an increased risk of mortality at a survival interval of ≤ 4 years. Among them, special histological types carcinoma had the highest hazard probability (HR = 2.09, 95% CI = 1.65 to 2.63). During the survival interval of 4–8 years, the hazard probability of the high-grade endometrial carcinoma persisted (Table S6).

The estimation results for CSS were similar to the OS, and the data was displayed in Table S7 to Table S10. There was slightly different in the negative regional lymph nodes group and localized stage for CSS. In the negative regional lymph nodes group, compared with the low-grade endometrioid carcinoma, the high-grade endometrioid carcinoma had higher hazard probability in the 4–12 years survival interval and mixed cell adenocarcinoma and special histological types carcinoma had higher hazard probability in the 4–8 years survival interval (Table S8). In localized stage, compared with the low-grade endometrioid carcinoma, there was statistical difference with other histological types carcinomas in the survival interval within 4 years (Table S10).

Regardless of the regional lymph nodes status and SEER-stage, the survival probability of the PIPEC for OS (Fig. 5) and CSS (Fig. S2) from high to low was the low-grade endometrioid, mixed cell and high-grade endometrioid carcinoma, while the survival probability of the carcinosarcoma and carcinoma, undifferentiated were relatively low. At the same time, due to the small number, the other special types of EC could not effectively draw an unsmooth survival curve and calculate the survival probability in some time.

Discussion

EC is the second most common female reproductive system tumor worldwide [1], the first in high-income countries [32]. This disease poses a significant threat to the lives of most women worldwide. However, most of the patients are diagnosed at an early stage with a relatively favorable prognosis [2]. The primary treatment modality for EC is total hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy [9]. EC commonly occurs in postmenopausal women, but between 15 and 25% of cases are found in premenopausal women. It has been estimated that 4% of cases occur in women under the age of 40 years [10, 18]. But there are alarming increases described in the 40–44 years age group [19]. In addition, abnormal uterine bleeding is a predominant symptom in perimenopausal women, overlapping with postmenopausal abnormal uterine bleeding, which is a typical clinical symptom of EC [10]. Therefore, it is vital to take a look at PIPEC. Because the reasons for the rapid growth of these patients and the prognosis of PIPEC are not clear, it is of great importance to try to use new methods to screen more reliable prognostic factors and explore the survival patterns of PIPEC patients treated with hysterectomy so as to achieve better prognostic evaluation.

Machine learning [33] is a branch of artificial intelligence that is utilized for variable screening and predictive model building in the field of oncology. Random survival forest (RSF) [34] is a machine learning model that is capable of processing and assessing high-dimensional data with minimal error and bias, thereby enabling the identification of key factors. It is widely used in genetic research [35, 36], but there are few screening applications in clinical phenotypes. RSF combined with Cox regression were used to screen the prognostic variables in this study. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to evaluate independent prognostic factors for OS and CSS. The independent prognostic factors both for OS and CSS were tumor size, grade, histology, SEER-stage, AJCC-stage, AJCC-T stage, metastasis to distant organs, and regional lymph nodes status. The marital status, location, and time from diagnosis to treatment were statistically significant only for OS. For the possible prognostic factors identified in this study, there are many articles studying them [2, 37–41]. The location is related to the economic situation and the surrounding health configuration, which may affect the prognosis of the disease. Time from diagnosis to treatment has been investigated in some kinds of carcinoma [42–45]. In this study, we found that only when time from diagnosis to treatment was above two months, the risk of mortality increased for OS.

In this study, the initial tumor size was categorized into four groups: ≤ 2 cm, 2–4 cm, 4–6 cm, and > 6 cm. The findings were consistent with those reported in previous studies [46, 47]. We found that, compared with ≤ 2 cm, when the tumor diameter was > 2 cm, the hazard ratio increased in the multivariate Cox regression for CSS, while the threshold value was > 4 cm in OS. The reason for the different threshold value might be the occurrence of competitive events of non-cancer events in the analysis of OS, which might lead to the bias of the multivariate Cox regression. The study also showed that metastasis to distant organs (bone, brain, liver, lung) [48] was more important in predicting the prognosis of PIPEC than the AJCC-M stage. For premenopausal EC patients in the early-stage, preserving ovarian function can be considered in order to prevent the adverse effects of surgical menopause while maintaining favorable survival outcomes [49–51]. For the prognosis of the surgery of removing the genital organs [50] was filtered in the analysis of RSF for OS and CSS, more research is needed to explore the effect of keeping the genital organs for the PIPEC patients. Besides, in multivariate Cox analysis, the similar manifestation was observed in chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and neoadjuvant treatment [52], which all had no statistical significance for the prognosis of PIPEC patients. Further prospective studies evaluating the treatment effect of adjuvant and neoadjuvant treatment for different histological types are necessary.

In this study, we found there were still more than half of low-grade endometrioid carcinoma patients who underwent lymphadenectomy. There is research showing that even in the early stage of EC, because of the possibility of inaccuracies of pre-operative biopsies and the risk of metastatic disease, and the impact of surgical staging on post-operative treatment decisions, lymphadenectomy should be taken into consideration to reduce the risk of recurrence and make appropriate post-operative management [53–56]. In recent years, sentinel lymph node (SLN) biopsy has gained popularity in managing apparent early-stage EC. Giorgio et al. evaluated the long-term survival of three different approaches of nodal assessment [12]. They reported that the execution of systematic lymphadenectomy, SLN biopsy and lymphadenectomy, or SLN biopsy alone is linked with similar disease-free and overall survival in EC patients. At the same time, SLN biopsy is superior to lymphadenectomy in identifying patients with lymphatic dissemination because of the identification of low volume metastases [12, 55] and the isolated tumor cells [57], which is of great importance in guiding adjuvant treatment strategy. Though the variable of operation of lymph nodes was also filtered by RSF due to the low permutation importance in our study, the operation of lymph nodes in clinical practice deserves considerable attention. In the analysis of multivariate Cox regression, the data showed that compared with pelvic lymph nodes metastasis, para-aortic lymph nodes metastasis had a higher hazard ratio, suggesting that nodal skip metastasis of isolated para-aortic metastasis in PIPEC might have a remarkable impact on survival [58, 59].

The WHO classification of EC provides a clear roadmap about its histological types, which have different biological behaviors and prognosis [3, 6]. In this study, we used the latest WHO guideline for female reproductive system tumors to analyze and investigate the prognosis of different histological types of the PIPEC population. We observed that, regardless of regional lymph nodes status and SEER-stage [60], the low-grade endometrioid carcinoma had the best outcome, followed by the mixed cell adenocarcinoma and high-grade endometrioid carcinoma, while the carcinosarcoma and undifferentiated carcinoma had a very poor prognosis [61, 62]. In addition, in any regional lymph node positive group and regional/distant disease stage, except for the low-grade endometrioid carcinoma, the survival probability of other histological types dropped significantly. For the localized stage group, the survival probability of the high-grade endometrioid carcinoma and mixed cell adenocarcinoma was close. When the disease progressed to the regional/distant disease stage, the prognosis of the mixed cell adenocarcinoma had a better survival probability than that of the high-grade endometrioid carcinoma. These were rarely reported and analyzed in recent articles. As for the mixed cell adenocarcinoma, it is a special type of EC [63], which is defined as a combination of two or more different histological subtypes [64], including serous or clear cell carcinoma. And the mixed cell adenocarcinoma can exhibit aggressive characteristics similar to those of the pure serous or clear cell adenocarcinoma [65]. However, for the analysis of PIPEC patients in our study, we found that the prognosis of the mixed cell adenocarcinoma was relatively good, only junior to the low-grade endometrioid carcinoma, and the survival outcome was far superior to the high-grade endometrioid carcinoma, serous, and clear cell adenocarcinoma [66].

There were still some limitations in this study. Firstly, the number of special types of endometrial carcinoma was significantly less than the endometrioid cancer. Some histological types cannot be effectively analyzed for prognostic survival due to the small number, which might produce bias. Secondly, since the incidence of EC gradually increases with age and the population mainly included in this study was American white people with PIPEC, the survival patterns in this study might not be extended to the other age groups and other populations. In addition, the inherent biases of the retrospective study design are the main weaknesses of this study. Retrospective cohort studies are subject to selection bias, recall bias, and unknown confounding variables that can negatively impact the veracity of the results.

In summary, this study was successful in identifying the possible prognostic factors of PIPEC patients treated with hysterectomy and providing survival patterns by histological types. We observed substantial histological type-specific survival differences among low-grade endometrioid carcinoma, high-grade endometrioid carcinoma and non-endometrioid tumors a few years after diagnosis. And we found the survival prognosis of mixed cell carcinoma was better than high-grade endometrioid carcinoma. These results highlight the importance of developing treatments that specifically target the distinct histopathology characteristics of each histological subtype to reduce the mortality of this deadly disease. At the same time, we successfully evaluated the clinical application value of the CS lymph nodes staging system in endometrial carcinoma. Further studies, including prospective and retrospective studies in larger and more diverse patient populations, are needed to validate the findings of this study and to explore the prognostic factors associated with different molecular subtypes. Research is encouraged to integrate the information of the molecular characterization by the Cancer Genome Atlas with histological types to explore personalized treatment regimens for improving prognosis.

Conclusion

We identified the possible prognostic factors of PIPEC patients treated with hysterectomy. And survival analysis based on the regional lymph nodes status and SEER-stage revealed the different histological types of PIPEC had diverse survival pattern, which will be helpful for guiding the clinical practice.

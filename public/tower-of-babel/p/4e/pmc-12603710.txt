Left main percutaneous or surgical revascularisation and subsequent risk of transient and persistent renal dysfunction
Authors: Laura Novelli, Jorge Sanz-Sanchez, Alessandra Iaccarino, Angelo Oliva, Riccardo Terzi, Gabriele Luigi Gasparini, Damiano Regazzoli, Antonio Mangieri, Gaia Cantisani, Alessandro Barbone, Jose Sorolla Romero, Giuseppe Ferrante, Luis Martínez Dolz, Antonio Colombo, Roxana Mehran, Lucia Torracca, Bernhard Reimers, Jose L Luis Diez Gil, Giulio Stefanini, Mauro Chiarito
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAcute kidney injury (AKI) often complicates percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and coronary artery bypass graft (CABG). However, evidence on the incidence and prognostic impact of AKI after revascularisation for left main coronary artery disease (LMCAD) is scant, especially in terms of subsequent risk of persistent renal dysfunction (RD).MethodsAll consecutive patients undergoing PCI or CABG for LMCAD in two European institutions from 2015 to 2022 were enrolled. The coprimary endpoints were AKI, defined as an increase in serum creatinine (sCr) levels ≥0.3 mg/dL or increase by >50% as compared with baseline levels, and persistent RD, defined as a persistent increase of sCr at 1 year. The secondary endpoint was all-cause mortality. The risk of AKI with PCI versus CABG was assessed with multivariable logistic regression and inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW). The prognostic impact of transient and persistent RD at 1 year was evaluated with Cox regression analysis.Results1047 patients were included (PCI: 617, CABG: 430). Patients undergoing PCI were older, more often male and affected by chronic kidney disease. AKI occurred in 17% and 28% of patients after PCI and CABG, respectively (adjusted OR 2.82; 95% CI 1.89 to 4.21). Consistent findings were observed after IPTW. AKI was associated with increased 1-year risk of all-cause death, irrespective of revascularisation strategy, but only persistent RD (HR 9.56; 95% CI 4.06 to 22.53) worsened patients’ prognosis, unlike AKI with only transient RD (HR 0.65; 95% CI 0.08 to 5.04).ConclusionsAKI is common after LMCAD revascularisation and occurred more frequently following CABG than PCI. AKI has a substantial prognostic impact irrespective of revascularisation modality, but only when resulting in persistent RD.

Introduction

Left main coronary artery disease (LMCAD) poses significant challenges in daily clinical practice, given its epidemiologic relevance, being detected in 5–10% of patients undergoing coronary angiography, and the large amount of myocardium at risk.1 Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) represents the gold standard for LM revascularisation, but technological advances and improvements in operator expertise and secondary prevention strategies have made percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) an effective and safe option in selected patient subsets.2 According to current guidelines, PCI should be considered for LMCAD with low-to-intermediate anatomical complexity, defined as a Synergy Between PCI With Taxus and Cardiac Surgery (SYNTAX) score ≤32.3 Besides anatomical complexity, all potential procedural complications and their prognostic implications should be comprehensively evaluated to determine the most appropriate revascularisation strategy to each patient.3 Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a common and serious complication following both CABG and PCI, consistently associated with increased risk of morbidity, mortality, prolonged hospital stay and obvious economic entailments.46 Importantly, renal function often recovers within a few weeks in patients who develop AKI and survive to hospital discharge,79 but data related to the risk of subsequent persistency of renal dysfunction (RD) after LMCAD revascularisation and its prognostic impact are scant.911 Indeed, the only available evidence in this setting comes from a prespecified subanalysis of the Evaluation of XIENCE versus Coronary Artery Bypass Surgery for Effectiveness of LM Revascularisation (EXCEL) trial, showing that PCI was associated with a lower rate of acute renal failure and 30-day major adverse events than CABG. Of note, patients in the EXCEL trial were at low surgical risk and presented only low to intermediate anatomical complexity, limiting the generalisability of these findings to high-risk patients. Moreover, the EXCEL trial did not report any data on persistent RD. The aim of the present study is to determine the incidence and the prognostic impact of AKI and of transient and persistent RD following CABG and PCI in a real-world cohort of patients with LMCAD.

Methods

Study design

All consecutive patients who underwent PCI or CABG for LMCAD from 2015 to 2022 at two European tertiary hospitals (Humanitas Research Hospital, Milan, Italy, and Hospital La Fe, Valencia, Spain) were considered for enrolment in the present observational analysis. Inclusion criteria were broad and reflected current clinical practice, including all patients presenting with LMCAD or LMCAD equivalent, defined respectively as >50% stenosis in LM or >70% stenosis in ostial left anterior descending or left circumflex arteries by contrast angiography or intracoronary imaging,12 irrespective of age, comorbidities, clinical presentation, previous intervention and number of treated lesions or vessels or lesion length. We excluded patients undergoing concomitant valve surgery or structural transcatheter interventions and patients in chronic dialysis at the time of revascularisation. The revascularisation strategy (ie, PCI vs CABG) was chosen based on heart team evaluation and patients’ preference. The study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent for the procedure and subsequent data collection based on local practice or local institutional review board approval.

Data collection and follow-up procedures

The institutional cath-lab databases were used for prospective data collection. Baseline patients’ features were systematically obtained from electronic medical records or using standardised forms at the time of index hospitalisation for revascularisation. Active follow-up was performed by dedicated personnel through telephone interviews or mails and review of electronic medical records. No angiographic follow-up was scheduled, unless clinically indicated. Baseline renal function was assessed by the most recent biochemical determination of serum creatinine (sCr), obtained the day of revascularisation for stable patients and within 6 months before index hospitalisation for patients admitted for acute coronary syndromes. After revascularisation, levels of sCr were collected within 48 hours, up to 7 days after the index procedure, and at 1-year follow-up. During the hospital stay, sCr was collected daily for all patients. Patients with a hospitalisation period shorter than 5 days were requested to undergo sCr analysis at 5– 7 days after the procedure. Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated according to the Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) Epidemiology Collaboration equation, endorsed by consensus guidelines as superior to other equations to discriminate between patients with versus without RD.13 CKD was defined as an eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 according to the National Kidney Foundation Guidelines and corresponded to CKD stage 3A (45–60 mL/min/1.73 m2), 3B (30–45 mL/min/1.73 m2), 4 (15–30 mL/min/1.73 m2) or 5 (<15 mL/min/1.73 m2).14 SYNTAX score was determined and reported in tertiles (1–22, 23–32 and ≥33) according to increasing burden of CAD complexity.15

Endpoint definitions

The coprimary endpoints were AKI and persistent RD at 1 year. AKI was defined according to the Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria as elevation of sCr ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours from revascularisation or increase by >50% from baseline within 7 days after the procedure16 and staged for severity according to KDIGO criteria in stages I, II and III.16 Urinary output contraction was not included in the definition of AKI considering under-reporting, limited availability in medical records and uneven availability in the two groups, due to higher rate of intensive care unit admission in the CABG than in the PCI group. Persistent RD was defined as a persistent increase in sCr levels ≥0.3 mg/dL or increase by ≥50% at 1 year as compared with baseline levels.11 16 The key secondary endpoint was all-cause mortality. Other endpoints were periprocedural major adverse events, defined as the composite of in-hospital death, stroke, myocardial infarction (MI), major bleeding and new need for renal replacement therapy (RRT), its single components, 1-year major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), defined as the composite of all-cause death, MI, stroke or heart failure hospitalisation at 1 year after the index procedure and its single components. Major bleeding events were defined according to Bleeding Academic Research Consortium (type 3–5) definition.17 All the other endpoints were defined according to Academic Research Consortium criteria.18

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were reported as mean±SD or median and IQR and compared using Student’s t test or Mann-Whitney or Wilcoxon tests, based on normality of data assessed with the Shapiro-Wilk test. Categorical data were reported as frequencies (percentages) and compared using the χ2 or Fisher exact test. Clinical follow-up was censored at the date of death or latest available follow-up. ORs with 95% CI were reported as estimates of the risk of AKI and in-hospital adverse events. Adjusted OR (adjOR) was calculated for both groups using multivariable regression analyses, including in the model covariates considered to be relevant (online supplemental Table S1). Sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the risk of AKI according to the definitions most used in literature (online supplemental Table S2). Additionally, inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was employed to evaluate the risk of AKI in patients undergoing PCI vs those undergoing CABG (variables included in the propensity score model are listed in the online supplemental Table S3). Adverse events at follow-up are reported as observed number of events and as Kaplan-Meier estimated rates, and the cumulative incidence of events at follow-up was compared between groups with the log-rank test. Results of the Cox regression analyses, employed to evaluate the prognostic impact of transient and persistent RD on all-cause death, are reported as HR and 95% CI. Cox regression was not performed to evaluate the association of relevant covariates and persistent RD due to the limited number of patients with persistent RD. Two-sided p values<0.05 were considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using Stata (V.16.0, StataCorp, College Station, Texas, USA).

Results

Study population and baseline features

A total of 1047 patients who underwent LM revascularisation at the two institutions between 2015 and 2022 were enrolled: 617 (58.9%) underwent PCI, while 430 (41.1%) underwent CABG. Baseline demographic and clinical features according to revascularisation strategy are summarised in table 1. Patients who underwent PCI were older, more often male, with higher rates of previous myocardial revascularisation (both percutaneous and surgical), CKD, heart failure and presented with a lower median left ventricle ejection fraction compared with those who underwent CABG. The prevalence of DM was similar among the two groups. Stable and silent angina was the most common clinical indication for PCI, while CABG was more frequently performed after admission for acute coronary syndrome. Mechanical circulatory support (MCS) was more frequently employed in the PCI group (14.4 vs 9.7%, p=0.025). Patients undergoing CABG were more likely to have a SYNTAX score ≥33 (43.7 vs 31.7%, p<0.001). Angiographic and procedural features are summarised in table 2.

Risk of AKI

AKI occurred in 222 patients (21.64%), more frequently after CABG (n: 120, 28%) than after PCI (n 102, 17%; OR 1.89; 95% CI 1.40 to 2.57). Consistent findings were observed after multivariable adjustment (adjOR 2.82; 95% CI 1.89 to 4.21) and IPTW (OR 1.14; 95% CI 1.07 to 1.21). Similarly, sensitivity analyses, performed to evaluate the risk of AKI according to different definitions, provided consistent findings (online supplemental table S2). At multivariable analysis, eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 and the use of MCS were associated with an increased risk of AKI following both PCI and CABG. Additionally, STEMI and NSTEMI as clinical presentation were associated with the risk of AKI after PCI but not after CABG, while increasing age was associated with the risk of AKI only in patients who underwent CABG (table 3 and online supplemental table S4). Regarding the severity of AKI, 71 patients (11.5%) in the PCI group developed stage I, 20 (3.2%) stage II and 11 patients (1.8%) stage III AKI. In the CABG group, 83 patients (19.3%) developed stage I AKI, 24 (5.6%) stage II and 13 patients (3%) stage III AKI (figure 1). The need for new RRT was reported in 14 patients (1.3%), eight (1.3 %) in the PCI group and six (1.4%) in the CABG group (p=0.89).

Impact of renal dysfunction on clinical outcomes

Patients who developed AKI had increased rates of periprocedural major adverse events (30.8 vs 12.1%; p<0.001), including more frequent need for blood transfusions, stroke, major bleeding and death (table 4), regardless of the type of revascularisation (AKI vs non-AKI in patients undergoing PCI: 39.6 vs 10.3%; OR 4.85; 95% CI 3.00 to 7.83; AKI vs non-AKI in patients undergoing CABG 23.3 vs 6.8%; OR 3.08; 95% CI 1.81 to 5.24; online supplemental table S5). The detailed distribution of periprocedural major adverse events by AKI stage is presented in table 5 and online supplemental table S6. The median follow-up was 458 days (IQR 324–891 days). Clinical follow-up and 1 year sCr values were available for 906 of the 1000 (91%) patients who survived to hospital discharge. The occurrence of AKI during the index hospitalisation was significantly associated with an increased risk of 1-year all-cause death (7.9 vs 2.8%; unadjusted HR 2.95; 95% CI 1.52 to 5.73) and MACE (12% vs 6.5%; unadjusted HR 1.93; 95% CI 1.17 to 3.16; figure 2). Among patients who experienced AKI, RD persisted in 41.1% of patients (44.2% in the PCI group and 38.8% in the CABG group, p=0.47). The increase in sCr during hospitalisation was found to be significantly associated with the risk of persistent RD (OR for sCr increase of 1% from baseline levels 1.26; 95% CI 1.20 to 1.32). Cox regression analysis showed that (among survivors of the initial 48 hours post PCI) patients with persistent RD after AKI had a significantly higher 1-year risk of all-cause death compared with those who did not have AKI (HR, 9.56; 95% CI, 4.06 to 22.53; p<0.001) and those who had a complete recovery of renal function (transient RD) (HR, 7.72; 95% CI, 1.71 to 34.87; p=0.008). Event curves diverge progressively after the first month of follow-up (figure S1). Conversely, there was no difference in the risk of all-cause death between patients with transient RD and those who did not experience AKI (HR, 0.65; 95% CI, 0.08 to 5.04). The prognostic impact of persistent RD was consistently observed in both revascularisation groups (online supplemental central illustration). Persistent RD after AKI also significantly increased the 1-year risk of MACE compared with patients without AKI (HR 4.31; 95% CI 2.37 to 7.84; p<0.001), while patients with transient RD were not exposed to any additional risk (HR 0.74; 95% CI 0.26 to 2.09; p=0.58, figure 3 and table 6).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study specifically evaluating the risk of AKI following surgical or percutaneous LM revascularisation and evaluating the prognostic impact of transient and persistent RD. Our main findings can be summarised as follows:

Among patients undergoing LMCAD revascularisation, AKI is frequent (21.6% in the overall cohort), occurs more commonly after CABG than PCI and is associated with an increased risk of in-hospital and 1-year adverse events, irrespective of revascularisation strategy.

Risk of AKI after percutaneous or surgical LM revascularisation

Current guidelines recommend considering both anatomical and clinical criteria to tailor the best revascularisation modality in patients with LMCAD.3 These recommendations are largely based on randomised evidence, but the generalisability of these findings cannot be extended to patients at increased surgical risk, as randomised studies often excluded elderly and comorbid patients, who mainly represent the contemporary real-world population referred for LMCAD.1921 Moreover, besides anatomical complexity, the risk of procedural complications and their prognostic implications should be carefully considered. AKI is among the most common and serious adverse events following both PCI and CABG, and its aetiology is complex, multifactorial and differs among percutaneous and surgical revascularisation. In PCI, a central role has been ascribed to contrast-induced acute tubular necrosis, due to medullary hypoxia or direct cytotoxic effects of contrast agents.5 Nonetheless, the causative role of contrast agents in the development of AKI has been repeatedly questioned, especially with the use of low-osmolality or iso-osmolality in place of high-osmolality contrast agents and preventive measures such as intravascular volume expansion, interruption of nephrotoxic medications and judicious use of contrast media volume.22 Indeed, a large body of evidence supports that substantial worsening of kidney function is very infrequent after contrast media administration. For instance, a meta-analysis including 25 950 patients who underwent procedures with and without intravenous administration of iodinated contrast material showed no significant difference in the risk of AKI,23 and similar findings were reported by other studies focused on specific settings, such as patients with acute ischaemic stroke who underwent CT with intravenous administration of contrast material24 or patients with STEMI with and without contrast material exposure.25 In line with these findings, our study reiterates how the risk of AKI in current clinical practice is not strictly related to the use of contrast agents, considering the lack of association between amount of contrast medium and AKI observed after adjustment for other relevant covariates (except for a non-significant trend observed in patients who received 200–299 mL of contrast volume). Moreover, the association with CKD, NSTEMI or STEMI, and the use of MCS with the risk of AKI reiterates how pre-existent RD and acute haemodynamic disturbances are more relevant than contrast media in the development of AKI in current clinical practice among patients undergoing cardiac procedures.26 The incidence of AKI in patients undergoing PCI widely varies (3.3–14.5%), according to the definition used, the timeframe of collection of sCr or other markers of renal function and the risk profile of the cohort considered in each study.6 27 28 The incidence of AKI after CABG has been reported to be slightly higher as compared with PCI cohorts, ranging from 11% to 30%.29 Unfortunately, there is limited evidence from studies directly comparing the risk of AKI after CABG or PCI, but available data in the context of multivessel CAD suggests that the risk of AKI is up to 1.5–3 times increased with CABG.1 30 In a prespecified subanalysis from the EXCEL trial, PCI was associated with significantly lower 30-day rates of acute renal failure as compared with CABG, in both patients with and without CKD, without significant differences in terms of new requirement for dialysis.31 Notably, the rate of acute renal failure was remarkably low (ranging from 0.3% in patients without CKD in the PCI group to 7.6% in those with CKD undergoing CABG), due to the restrictive definition (increase in sCr≥5 mg/dL or new requirement for dialysis, corresponding to stage III AKI in KDIGO classification) and the short timeframe for sCr collection. In line with these findings, the incidence of AKI after LM revascularisation in our cohort was 28% after CABG and 17% after PCI. The higher incidence of AKI in our PCI group compared with previous studies may be attributed to the high-risk population enrolled, with a large prevalence of patients presenting with MI and high coronary anatomical complexity (73% with SYNTAX score >22). Another factor contributing to the high rate of AKI in our registry is the timing of the collection of sCr values, reaching up to 7 days after revascularisation. This extended monitoring likely unveiled occurrences of AKI due to a delayed creatinine peak, as recommended by the KDIGO definition.16 Indeed, most of the current evidence on AKI after PCI relies on studies assessing sCr values within 2 days after revascularisation, which may have resulted in a high rate of undiagnosed cases.

Risk and prognostic impact of transient and persistent RD after LM revascularisation

Numerous studies assessed the prognostic impact of AKI after either CABG or PCI separately, highlighting the association with an increased risk of in-hospital mortality.3 6 32 Our analysis shows that, despite the incidence of AKI being higher after CABG than PCI, the risk of adverse events after AKI was substantial in both groups. Consistent findings were reported in a substudy from the Future Revascularization Evaluation in Patients with Diabetes Mellitus: Optimal Management of Multivessel Disease trial, which investigated the incidence of AKI after revascularisation in patients with diabetes and multivessel disease: AKI was significantly more frequent after CABG than with PCI, but was associated with an increased risk of 5-year MACE irrespective of the revascularisation strategy (pinteraction=0.89).33 Unlike the large number of studies evaluating the risk of AKI after myocardial revascularisation and its association with mortality, only a small number of studies evaluated if RD in patients who developed AKI persisted at follow-up, and even less evidence is available on the different prognostic impact of transient and persistent RD, especially in case of AKI following CABG.9 11 34 Moreover, the lack of a standardised definition of persistent RD makes it difficult to generalise these findings. To the best of our knowledge, this is the study with the highest completeness of data on renal function at 1 year after coronary revascularisation. Kurogi et al reported that following PCI for acute coronary syndromes, 8.8% of patients developed AKI, with 34% of them showing residual renal impairment at 2 weeks. Persistency of RD at 2 weeks was associated with a higher risk of death at 3 years compared with patients who recovered from RD and those who did not experience AKI.9 A recent study evaluating patients undergoing primary PCI after acute MI reported that AKI occurred in 9.4% of the cohort, with 30.5% of these patients presenting with persistent RD at 1 month and that only persistent RD was an independent predictor of MACE.35 The only study evaluating persistency of RD at 1 year to date included 5516 patients undergoing elective PCI from the CREDO-Kyoto PCI/CABG Registry Cohort-2 and confirmed that only persistent RD was associated with increased long-term mortality. However, sCr at 1 year was available only in 72% of patients.36 In our registry, reporting sCr at 1 year in 91% of patients, 41% of patients who had AKI showed persistency of RD at 1-year follow-up, and only patients with persistent RD had a higher risk of adverse events than those who did not have AKI, irrespective of type of revascularisation. Of note, the risk of death gradually accrued after the first month of follow-up. These results indicate that in patients undergoing LM revascularisation, whether percutaneously or surgically, AKI during hospitalisation is not a significant predictor of clinical events at follow-up by itself. Instead, the failure to recover renal function plays a crucial role. Thus, it can be hypothesised that after overcoming the critical phase of AKI, which exposes to an increased risk of events during hospitalisation, for those who subsequently recover from RD, the risk of long-term events is comparable to the risk of those who did not develop AKI. The frequency of persistent RD and the prognostic impact of chronic kidney disease imply the need to tailor the most appropriate revascularisation strategy in patients presenting specific risk features to develop AKI after PCI or CABG, to improve preventive measures and to schedule strict follow-up in patients with AKI after coronary revascularisation, possibly in conjunction with nephrologists, in order to minimise the risk of persistency of RD and its sequelae.

Study limitations

Our results must be interpreted in light of several limitations. First, given the retrospective nature of the analysis, some relevant variables—such as preventive measures, periprocedural hydration and nephrotoxic drugs—were not systematically collected. Second, the revascularisation strategy (PCI vs CABG) was not randomised and might have been influenced by unmeasured confounders, potentially introducing treatment bias. However, the results remained consistent after multivariable adjustment and IPTW. Third, the broad inclusion criteria, reflecting a real-world population, as well as the time frame of SCr assessment, may have introduced variability in outcomes, particularly considering the inclusion of patients with both acute and chronic coronary syndromes. Fourth, there are no validated cut-offs to define persistent and transient RD. The lack of standardised criteria could impact the consistency and comparability of results across studies. Similarly, the lack of albuminuria data collection precluded the assessment of its prognostic value, a limitation commonly shared with previous studies on this topic. Finally, we did not include urinary output in our definition of AKI, despite being included in the KDIGO definition of AKI. However, AKI is rarely independent from sCr changes37 and the assessment of sCr values after hospital discharge up to 7 days after revascularisation remarkably increases the chance to detect AKI as compared with previous studies reporting only values collected during hospitalisation. Moreover, a very small number of patients was lost to follow-up, which minimises potential bias and enhances the accuracy of 1-year outcomes assessment. The completeness of data on sCr values at 1-year follow-up and the limited exclusion criteria enhance the generalisability of the reported data, rendering it representative of real-world clinical practice. However, the relatively small sample size may have limited the statistical power of some analyses, as well as the precision of the risk estimate provided by logistic and Cox regression.

Conclusions

In-hospital AKI is a common complication after LM revascularisation and occurs more frequently after CABG than PCI. The prognostic impact of AKI is substantial irrespective of the revascularisation modality, but only in case of persistent RD. Patients who had a complete recovery of renal function at 1 year had a similar risk of mortality as compared with those who did not experience AKI during the index hospitalisation.

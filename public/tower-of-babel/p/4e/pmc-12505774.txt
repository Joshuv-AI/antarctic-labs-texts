Development and validation of a Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice Questionnaire Related to Prone Positioning among Intensive Care Unit Nurses
Authors: Xueyi Wei, Xiuwen Chen, Huiqiong Chen, Jing Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAs a non-invasive and economically feasible treatment method, prone position ventilation is a significant adjunctive therapy for patients with moderate and severe acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) as well as those with severe pneumonia.AimsThis study aimed to develop a Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice Questionnaire Related to Prone Positioning among Intensive Care Unit Nurses (PPICUNs-KAP) and assess the validation of this questionnaire in ICU nurses.MethodsThe draft questionnaire was formed by literature reviews and 2 rounds of expert consultations. This questionnaire was used to survey 267 ICU nurses from three tertiary hospitals in western Hunan Province, China in August 2024. Content validity and construct validity were evaluated by content validity index (CVI), exploratory factor analysis, respectively. Reliability was tested by internal consistency and test-retest reliability.ResultsAfter the exploratory factor analysis, three items(knowledge, attitude and practice) were excluded. Finally, the newly developed questionnaire included 42 items. It consisted of three dimensions: Knowledge (20 items); Attitude (7 items), and Practice (15 items). The Cronbach’s α and test-retest reliability of the questionnaire were 0.970 and 0.962, respectively.ConclusionsPPICUNs-KAP is valid and reliable, and it is recommended for use in clinical practice and research to assess knowledge, attitude and practice related to prone positioning among ICU nurses.Keywords: Intensive care units, Nurses, Prone position, Reliability, Validity

Introduction

Prone position ventilation (PPV) is defined as the therapeutic maneuver of placing a patient in a prone position, leveraging gravity to promote re-expansion of collapsed alveoli and optimize the ventilation-perfusion ratio in gravity-dependent lung areas [1]. Consequently, PPV can enhance oxygenation, aid in airway clearance, and reduce the incidence of secondary multiple organ dysfunction and mortality caused by oxygenation disorders in patients [1, 2]. Since the 1970s, prone ventilation has been used in patients with acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) with invasive mechanical ventilation [3]. At present, PPV has been strongly recommended as an adjunctive therapy for patients with moderate and severe ARDS as well as patients with severe and critical pneumonia [4, 5]. In addition, several studies have shown that for patients with mild to moderate ARDS and mild pneumonia who do not need ventilator assistance to breathe, awake prone positioning can effectively improve oxygenation, reduce the rate of tracheal intubation. Furthermore, PPV basically does not require additional equipment costs, greatly reduces the input of human and material resources, and can prevent lung damage caused by mechanical ventilation [6, 7].

As a non-invasive, economically feasible and important treatment method, PPV has been widely concerned by ICU medical staff, but it is not ideal in clinical practice. An international prospective epidemiological study analyzed the treatment of 2377 ARDS patients, the results showed that only 16.3% of severe ARDS patients were implemented PPV [8]. The reasons for this phenomenon may be related to the complicated operation process of PPV, the lack of systematic theoretical training of medical staff, and the concern about complications during PPV [9, 10]. As we all know, the implementation of PPV requires the mutual cooperation of a professional team, and ICU nurses play a crucial role in the team, almost determining the success and effect of the implementation of PPV [10].

Prone position ventilation therapy requires high professional knowledge and skills of ICU medical staff. The extent to which medical staff understand the principles of PPV, master its operational procedures, recognize operation-related risks, and implement preventive measures will influence clinical practice [10–12]. Improving the basic knowledge, attitude and practice(KAP) of medical personnel is important to increase the use of PPV and improve the quality and safety of health care [13]. However, at present, there is a lack of assessment tools for the level of KAP of PPV in ICU nurses. It is difficult to obtain the root cause of the obstruction of PPV implementation, so it is impossible to implement targeted measures. In this study, based on the theory of KAP, a questionnaire of prone position ventilation for ICU nurses is developed. Additionally, its reliability and validity are tested to provide a scientific evaluation tool for evaluating the level of KAP of PPV among ICU nurses.

Aims and objectives of study

The aim of this study was to develop and assess the validation of a Knowledge, Attitude, and Practice Questionnaire Related to Prone Positioning among Intensive Care Unit Nurses (PPICUNs-KAP).

Design and methods

Design

This methodological study used a standardized questionnaire development process [14], which was divided into two stages: development and validation (Fig. 1). The first stage included development of item pool, expert consultation and pilot survey. The items were generated by literature review and the questionnaire was developed by Delphi method. The validity of the content was evaluated by revising the preliminary draft according to expert review, and the surface validity was evaluated by pre-testing the revised questionnaire. In the second stage, the results of item analysis, exploratory factor analysis and reliability test were used to further modify the questionnaire and evaluate its validity and reliability.

Questionnaire development and validation flow chart

Phase1: development of the questionnaire

The establishment of the research group

The research team comprised 8 participants, including 2 chief nurses (1 director of nursing department, 1 head nurse of ICU), 2 IUC supervisor nurses, 2 ICU clinicians (1 chief physician, 1 deputy chief physician), and 2 nursing master students. These participants were drawn from Xiangya Hospital of Central South University. Among them, a chief nurse and a chief physician were responsible for the project design and quality control. Another chief nurse and a deputy chief physician led the construction of the initial questionnaire item pool and the selection and engagement of expert participants. Two supervisor nurses, possessing extensive experience in clinical practice and familiarity with hospital protocols, were responsible for the calibration of the initial questionnaire which involved reviewing items for clinical applicability, ensuring consistency in language and adherence to hospital guidelines and managing the distribution and collection of correspondence questionnaires. The nursing master students supported the project by conducting literature analysis, formulating expert correspondence questionnaires, and undertaking data sorting and statistical analysis.

Development of item pool

Theoretical basis

This study is based on the theory of KAP, which proposes that knowledge is the foundation, positive attitude is the inner motivation, the generation and transformation of practice is the goal [15]. According to this theory, the research team sorted out the three elements of KAP within the framework, and preliminarily determined the three dimensions of the questionnaire. 1)Knowledge: including ICU nurses’ understanding of treatment plan, basic principles, indications and contraindications, treatment duration, implementation significance, monitoring points, operational procedures, nursing points, risk plans and shift handover of prone positioning; 2)Attitude: refers to the attitude of ICU nurses to learn relevant theoretical knowledge and the belief in the safe and effective implementation of prone positioning; 3) Practice: under the guidance of the existing knowledge framework and positive attitude, practice refers to ICU nurses’ initiative in learning the relevant theoretical knowledge of prone positioning, coupled with their consistent and skillful application of standardized procedures. This reflects their ability to translate knowledge and attitude into effective bedside practice, thereby ensuring proper patient positioning and management.

Literature review

With “intensive care unit*/ICU/ critically ill " and “prone positioning” as the English search terms, we searched CNKI, Wanfang Database, VIP database, PubMed, Web of Science, Embase and other databases, the search period was from the 1980 to January 2024. After literature collation, theoretical analysis and group discussion, the initial item pool was formed after collation and refining, including knowledge (28 items), attitude (13 items) and practice (18 items), with a total of 59 items.

Delphi expert consultation

Selection of experts

Purpose sampling method was adopted to select 19 experts in related fields from March to April 2024. The inclusion criteria of experts were as follows: (1) having bachelor degree or above; (2) having intermediate and above professional titles; (3) engaged in critical care medicine for 10 years or more; (4) familiar with the questionnaire development process and prone positioning; (5)volunteer to participate in the correspondence process.

Preparation of expert consultation questionnaire

The expert consultation questionnaire consisted of the following three parts. (a)Questionnaire guidance: including research background, research content and purpose, form filling instructions, etc; (b) Consultation Questionnaire on PPICUNs-KAP: Experts were invited to rate the importance and relevance of each item using a 5-point Likert scale. Importance was rated from ‘very unimportant’ (1 point) to ‘very important’ (5 points), and relevance was rated from ‘very irrelevant’ (1 point) to ‘very relevant’ (5 points). After the entry, there were “expert modification column” and “suggestion to add items column” for experts to propose additions or modifications, which can evaluate the comprehensiveness of the entire questionnaire and ensure that it adequately covered all essential aspects of prone positioning knowledge, attitude, and practice. (c) Expert general information: including age, education, working years, the experts ' familiarity with this research field and the basis for judgment.

Implementation of expert consultation

After obtaining the consent of the experts, the research team sent a questionnaire to them by email and asked them to return the questionnaire within 2 weeks. After the first round of consultation, the opinions put forward by the experts were discussed and modified in the research group, then the second round of consultation questionnaire was formed and sent to the experts. After two rounds of consultation, the opinions of experts often reached a consensus, so concluded the consultation. Items with the mean value of expert score assignment ≥ 3.50 points and coefficient of variation < 0.25 were retained [16]. Expert opinions were sorted out and the contents of the questionnaire were revised.

Expert positivity, judgement coefficient, familiarity coefficient, authority coefficient and content validity

The expert enthusiasm coefficient was used to measure the degree of expert participation positivity, reflecting the degree of expert response to the survey. The reliability of expert consultation was judged by the judgement coefficient (Ca), the familiarity coefficient (Cs) and authority coefficient (Cr). Normally, Cr ≥ 0.7 signified higher authority of the questionnaire [16]. The degree of concentration of expert opinion were expressed by coefficient of variation (CV) and Kendall coordination coefficient. P < 0.05 indicated that the difference was statistically significant. The scale level content validity index (S-CVI) and item level content validity index (I-CVI) were calculated according to the scores of each item of the questionnaire by the experts, if both S-CVI and I-CVI were above 0.8, it indicated that the questionnaire has good content validity [17].

Pilot survey

In June 2024, 25 ICU nurses from Xiangya Hospital of Central South University were conveniently selected to carry out a pilot survey to test whether the contents of the items were clear and understandable. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age > 18 years old; (2) having the nurse practicing certificate; (3) worked in the ICU for 1 year or more; (4) having experience in implementing prone positioning; (5) volunteer to participate in the study. Exclusion criteria: (1) nurse in further education and nurse intern; (2) nurses who did not work in ICU due to leave during the study period.

Phase2: evaluation and validition of the questionnaire

Setting and sample

A convenient sampling method was used to select ICU nurses from three tertiary hospitals in western Hunan Province in August 2024. The initial questionnaire was analyzed by item analysis, reliability test and exploratory factor analysis. The inclusion and exclusion criteria were the same as in the pilot survey. The sample size of the survey should be 5 to 10 times the number of items in the questionnaire [18]. The initial questionnaire contained 43 items, considering the sample loss rate of 20%, the sample size was at least 258 cases.

Instruments data collection tools

Demographic information questionnaire

According to the purpose of the research, the general information questionnaire was made, and the participants’ demographic information included age, sex, the highest level of education, professional title, and working years.

Knowledge, attitude, and practice initial questionnaire related to prone positioning among intensive care unit nurses

After literature analysis, two rounds of Delphi expert consultation and pilot survey, the questionnaire was formed. All items were assessed using a 5-level Likert scale. The Knowledge dimension comprised items scored from 1 (“don’t know”) to 5 (“very well know”), with a total score ranging from 20 to 100. Higher scores indicated a better understanding of prone position ventilation. For the Attitude dimension, items ranged from “completely disagree” (1) to “completely agree” (5), yielding a total score of 8 to 40. Higher scores signified a more positive attitude towards learning related knowledge and implementing prone ventilation safely and effectively. The Practice dimension included items scored from 1 (“never”) to 5 (“always”), with total scores ranging from 15 to 75. Higher scores indicated better practice of prone positioning.

Data collection

After explaining the study and obtaining consent from nursing administrators at each hospital, research team members distributed a link to participants via a QR code. The initial page of the questionnaire provided an introduction to its purpose and instructions for completion. Data collection was open for one week, during which survey participants answered the questionnaire once and submitted it upon completion of all items. Following the submission deadline, the questionnaires were exported, and the data were subsequently checked by two researchers. Questionnaires with a response time of less than 1 min or those exhibiting clear logical errors in response choices were excluded.

Data analysis

Excel 2019 and IBM SPSS v25.0 were used for data entry and analysis. Counting data were expressed by frequency and percentage, measurement data conforming to normal distribution were expressed by mean and standard deviation, and measurement data not conforming to normal distribution were expressed by median and quartile. The critical ratio method was used to sort the collected questionnaires from highest to lowest in total score, with the top 27% as high group and the bottom 27% as low group. The independent sample T-test was used to compare the differences between the two groups, and items with P < 0.05 were retained. Pearson correlation analysis was used to calculate the correlation coefficient between each item and the total score. If the correlation coefficient was less than 0.4, it indicated that the correlation was not ideal and was deleted [19]. Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) was conducted using principal component analysis and maximum variance orthogonal rotation. Factors with an eigenvalue greater than 1 and a cumulative variance contribution rate exceeding 50% were retained. Items with factor loadings below 0.4 on their primary factor were deleted [20]. We chose principal component analysis to reduce multiple related indicators into a smaller number of independent principal components, achieve dimensionality reduction, and thereby most truly reflect the information of the original indicators [21]. The internal consistency of the questionnaire was evaluated by Cronbach α coefficient, while the stability was evaluated by retest reliability. The reliability index greater than 0.6 was considered acceptable [22].

Ethical and research approvals with the date

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Xiangya Hospital, Central South University (registry number 2022040863) on April 8, 2022. The study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki, all participants signed informed consent forms and participated anonymously.

Results

Delphi expert consultation results

In expert consultation, a total of 19 experts were finally included, including 4 doctors, 15 nurses. The effective recovery rate of the questionnaire was 100.0%, indicating that the experts had a high degree of enthusiasm and cooperation in this study. There were 2 males and 17 females, ranging in age from 36 to 54 (46.63 ± 4.35) years. The working experience ranged from 11 to 36 (21.79 ± 6.49) years. Among the experts included, 3 had bachelor’s degree, 14 had master’s degree and 2 had doctorals’ degree. There were 9 intermediate titles, 8 associate senior titles and 2 senior titles. For the two rounds of consultation, the Cs value was 0.88 and 0.92, the Ca value was 0.92 and 0.94, the Cr value was 0.90 and 0.93, and the Kendall coefficient was 0.366 and 0.212 (P < 0.001). After two rounds of correspondence, the mean importance scores of each item in the questionnaire ranged from 4.26 to 5.00, which met the selection criteria for items in this study (≥ 3.50). The coefficient of variation was 0–0.324 and 0–0.205. S-CVI values were 0.940 and I-CVI values were 0.930–1.000, indicating that the questionnaire content validity was good. In the first round of expert consultation, a total of 11 experts put forward 36 amendments. After discussion by the research team, a total of 16 items were deleted. For example, we deleted the item ‘How well do you know the infection control protocols for prone positioning?’ and the item ‘Do you ensure the implementation of infection control protocols during the application of prone positioning therapy?’.

We revised 17 items to enhance clarity and rigor. For instance, two items were merged into one: ‘Would you consider applying prone positioning to patients with severe ARDS?’ and ‘Would you consider applying prone positioning to patients with severe pneumonia?’ were combined into ‘Would you consider applying prone positioning to patients with severe ARDS or severe pneumonia?’. Additionally, the item ‘How well do you understand the basic principles of prone positioning therapy?’ was revised to ‘How well do you understand the pathophysiological mechanisms involved in prone positioning therapy?’. The item ‘Do you inquire about the standard prone positioning procedure when implementing prone positioning therapy?’ was replaced with ‘Do you follow the standard prone positioning procedure when implementing prone positioning therapy?’. Similarly, ‘How well do you understand the disease monitoring indicators during prone positioning therapy?’ was refined to ‘How well do you understand the key points for patient monitoring during prone positioning therapy?’.

Based on expert recommendations, we added new items: ‘Do you clear the patient’s oral and nasal cavities, as well as airway secretions, before turning over in the prone ventilation position?’ and ‘Do you continuously monitor the patient’s condition during position changes in prone positioning therapy?’. After the second round of expert consultation, substantial agreement was reached on all items. Following two rounds of expert consultation, the questionnaire, comprising 43 items across three dimensions (Knowledge: 20 items, Attitude: 8 items, Practice: 15 items), was established.

Pilot survey

A total of 25 questionnaires were sent out in the pre-survey, and all of them were recovered. The effective questionnaire recovery rate was 100%. The nurses all said that they could fully understand the contents of the questionnaire, and the completion time was 8 ~ 15 min, so the questionnaire items were not deleted, added or revised.

There were 13 nurses (4.9%), 108 senior nurses (40.4%), 134 supervisor nurses(50.2%), 6 deputy chief nurses (2.2%) and 6 chief nurses (2.2%). Item analysis

The results of this study showed that the difference between high group and low group was statistically significant (all P < 0.05). The attitude item ‘You believe that medical cooperation is one of the important reasons for the compliance of prone positioning therapy’ exhibited a correlation coefficient of 0.273 with the total score. As this value was below the criterion of 0.4, the item was subsequently removed following group discussion. After deletion, the correlation coefficient between each item and the total questionnaire was 0.450–0.791 (P < 0.05), indicating high homogeneity.

Construct validity

The results showed that the KMO value was 0.928 and Bartlett’s sphericity test χ2 was 17216.920 (P < 0.001), which were suitable for factor analysis. In this study, principal component analysis was used to extract common factors, and the factor with feature root greater than 1 was extracted by orthogonal rotation with maximum variance. The cumulative variance contribution rate was 77.641%, the load value of each item on the corresponding factor was greater than 0.4, and there was no double load. After this process, a formal questionnaire with a total of 42 items in 3 dimensions, namely knowledge (20 items), attitude (7 items) and practice (15 items), was finally formed (Table 3).

Reliability

The overall Cronbach’s α coefficient for the questionnaire was 0.970, with values for individual dimensions ranging from 0.960 to 0.981. Furthermore, deleting any single item did not lead to a significant improvement in the Cronbach’s α coefficient. Regarding stability, the test-retest reliability was 0.962 for the total questionnaire and ranged from 0.732 to 0.833 for the dimensions. See Table 4 for details.

Discussion

This study develops a questionnaire based on the theory of KAP, integrating literature reviews and expert consultation. Through iterative expert feedback, the language, semantics, content, and structure of each item are refined, leading to significant improvements in the questionnaire’s clarity, item relevance, and overall expression. The expert panel comprises individuals from multiple medical institutions and diverse fields. Based on their feedback, new items are also added to address identified gaps and enhance the questionnaire’s comprehensiveness. The positive coefficient and authority of the experts are high, which ensure the authority and standardization of the questionnaire [16]. Subsequently, a pilot test is conducted, followed by item analysis to screen and revise the items. This rigorous process results in a clear, easily understandable questionnaire comprising 42 items across three dimensions.

Content validity and structure validity are used to evaluate the validity of the questionnaire. The I-CVI ranges from 0.74 to 1.00 and S-CVI is 0.94, confirming good content validity. EFA is used to assess structural validity. Three factors are extracted, explaining a cumulative variance of 77.641%, which are consistent with the intended three-dimensional structure of the questionnaire. Furthermore, all items demonstrate factor loadings greater than 0.4 on their primary factor, supporting the alignment of item distribution with the theoretical structure. For reliability, the.

Cronbach’s α coefficient and retest coefficient of this questionnaire are 0.970 and 0.962 respectively, indicating excellent internal consistency and good temporal stability.

The level of knowledge, attitude, and practice regarding prone positioning among ICU nurses represents a significant area of concern for clinical management, impacting the effective application of this technology. Studies [23] have shown that only 39% of ICU nurses believed that prone positioning was beneficial for patients with severe ARDS. Another study found that half of 49 surveyed nurses could not articulate the mechanism and advantages of prone positioning for improving oxygenation, nor were they proficient in its operational procedures [24]. These findings suggest that insufficient knowledge and a lack of confidence in performing prone positioning operations hinder its optimal development and implementation in clinical practice. To address this, our questionnaire assesses theoretical knowledge, positive attitudes, and normative behaviors related to prone positioning through the three dimensions of knowledge, attitude and practice. Furthermore, the questionnaire items are clinically relevant and easy for medical staff to understand.

By using this questionnaire, managers can understand the current level of ICU nurses, analyze the factors that affect the KAP of prone positioning, and implement accurate management according to the influencing factors. Our previous investigation showed that [13] the basic knowledge, positive attitude and correct practices of ICU nurses were necessary to increase the use of prone positioning, reduce the incidence of prone positioning related complications, and improve the quality and safety of medical care. Therefore, it is necessary to strengthen the training and learning of knowledge and skills related to prone positioning for ICU nurses. Then they can put their knowledge into practice, and finally promote the safe, effective and standardized implementation of prone positioning.

Limitation

This study only investigates ICU nurses from three tertiary hospitals in western Hunan Province, and the sample representativeness is limited, which may limit the extrapolation of the findings. Future studies need to adopt large sample and multi-center research methods, expand the scope of the survey population, and further test the reliability and validity of the questionnaire to improve the generalization of the questionnaire. Another limitation of this study lies in the expert consistency analysis of the Delphi method. Kendall’s W coefficient indicates that the overall consistency level of experts in the two rounds of assessment is relatively low, and it has declined in the second round of assessment. Due to the significant differences among the included experts in their professional fields, work experience or practical applications, their focuses and understandings of certain concepts may vary. Despite this, this study still confirmed the validity of the content through the CVI indicator, indicating that experts generally recognize the assessment of content quality.

Conclusion

The questionnaire prepared in this study includes 42 items and has good reliability and validity, all of which are in line with the measurement standards. It can be used to evaluate the level of knowledge, attitude and practice of prone positioning among ICU nurses, and provide a scientific basis for promoting the effective implementation of prone positioning.

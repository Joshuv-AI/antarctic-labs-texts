Comparative effectiveness of endoscopic plantar fasciotomy, needle knife therapy, and conventional painkillers in the treatment of plantar fasciitis: a real-world evidence study
Authors: Zhang Yingjie, Md Miftahul Mithu, Md Ariful Haque, Xiao Jiayu, Lu Jipeng, Chen Shuai, Wu Tong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPlantar fascia (PF) is the commonest causes of foot pain in the adult population. Several surgical treatments are available to treat PF. This study was aimed to investigate the clinical efficacy of three different treatments for plantar fasciitis. It was conducted among 60 patients, divided equally into three treatment groups named Needle Knife Therapy, Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy, and Conventional Painkillers. Descriptive and analytical analysis were done by using SPSS 25 software. VAS and AOFAS scores were analyzed. The maximum (n = 31) participants were in 41–60 year age range group with normal BMI. These differences of mean VAS and AOFAS pain scores between different treatment groups were statistically significant (One-way ANOVA, p < 0.01). Both scores found lower in the Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy group. This study opens a new window of knowledge to achieve sustained pain relief and functional improvement. Moreover, the superiority of Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy in treating plantar fasciitis compared to Needle Knife Therapy and Conventional Painkillers was explored.Keywords: Plantar fascia, Foot pain, Needle knife therapy, Endoscopic plantar fasciotomy, Conventional painkillers

Introduction

Plantar fascia is an anatomical structure made of dense connective tissue and acts as a support of the medial longitudinal arch of the foot [1]. Plantar fasciitis (PF), is a clinical condition that occurs at the insertion of plantar fascia. PF is characterized by pain and the pain is intensified as one takes his first steps in the morning. In addition, the pain of PF can also be sharpened after taking rest for a long period. PF can develop in one foot or both feet [2]. It is one of the most common causes of foot pain in the adult population [3]. Both sexes are affected by PF although women are slightly more likely to be affected than men [4, 5]. There is a high incidence of PF in runners and athletes [4]. Although PF was considered to be caused due to chronic inflammation, in recent times, its degenerative pathology has come to light. It has more similarity with tendinopathy and to a disease of chronic nature [3]. Various approaches are available for the treatment of PF. Conservative treatment, surgical; and non-surgical options are available for PF treatment [6, 7]. Non-surgical treatment includes over-the-counter painkillers, corticosteroid injections, instrumental therapy, physical therapy, and so on [7]. Diclofenac patches are used in various injuries as a painkiller [8]. Usually, physicians turn to surgical alternatives when other options fail [6]. Partial or complete fasciotomy is available as a surgical intervention for PF. However, the effectiveness of these treatment approaches bears little evidence [6]. Nowadays, a minimally invasive surgery known as Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy (EPF) is done in PF patients. Sahu has reported that (EPF) has shown improved results for patients with PF [9]. The needle-knife therapy is a newer mode of treatment that combines Western surgical techniques and traditional Chinese acupuncture. This therapy has shown good outcomes in the management of musculoskeletal pain [10].

Very few studies have explored the comparison among different modalities of treatment of PF.

Therefore, in this study, we attempted to look at various treatment approaches of PF and compared them to report which has the best outcome with minimum undesired consequences. The findings of this study would help clinicians make evidence-based decisions while selecting the most appropriate treatment for patients of PF. This might also contribute to assembling a standard guideline for PF management.

Materials and methods

Study design, settings and sample

This study was designed as a real-world evidence (RWE) study to evaluate the clinical efficacy of three different treatments for plantar fasciitis: Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy, Needle Knife Therapy, and Conventional Pain relievers. It was carried out at the Department of Orthopaedics at The Kunming Medical University is affiliated with Yan’ An hospital, China. Following the approval of the study protocol number 2024-007-01, the study took place between 2023 January to 2024 March. The participants comprised of 60 adult patients mean age ± SD was 53.2 ± 13.15 years, diagnosed with plantar fasciitis, with or without a calcaneal spur, for more than six months and failed to respond to initial conservative treatments (e.g., rest, ice, orthotics). Patients with previous history of any kind of intervention for planter fasciitis had been excluded. Furthermore, patients who presented with multiple foot pathologies along with heel pain or presence of any severe comorbidities that could affect outcomes also excluded from this study. Based on the number of patients admitted per month and the time allocated for data collection, we recruited 60 patients for this study.

Diagnosis and definition of patients

The diagnosis of plantar fasciitis is primarily based upon a comprehensive review of the patient’s clinical history and physical examination. The heel pain of these patients started with the inferior middle aspect of the heel and sometimes radiated to arch of the foot. Pain was insidious onset and exacerbated by any type of weight-bearing activities. Patients often described it as throbbing or piercing, particularly unbearable during the initial steps upon awakening or following extended periods of inactivity. Clinical examination revealed tenderness localized to the medial aspect of the calcaneal tuberosity at the site of the plantar aponeurosis in some patients. A number of differential diagnoses were excluded for heel pain by a thorough history-taking and physical examination. Study participants were enrolled in three groups:

Group A: Diagnosed case of planter fasciitis and planned for Endoscopic planter fasciotomy Group B: Diagnosed case of planter fasciitis and planned for Needle-knife procedure.

Group C: Diagnosed case of planter fasciitis and being treated with Pain relief patches (50 mg diclofenac sodium patches). These patches were applied to the area of significant heel pain every other day for a duration of 5 h each time, for a total treatment period of one month.

Treatment procedures

Endoscopic plantar fasciotomy (EPF)

EPF is a minimally invasive surgical procedure utilized in the treatment of plantar fasciitis, a common cause of heel pain. Initially, a local anesthetic was administered via subcutaneous ankle block. The patient was positioned on their back wrapping a pneumatic tourniquet around the area. After standard disinfection and draping procedures, two incisions were made on the medial surface of the calcaneus. Using endoscopic visualization, Normal Saline was injected to create space, allowing access to the plantar fascia and the calcaneal tuberosity. A disposable radio frequency plasma electrode was utilized to remove the synovial tissue around any bone growth, followed by smoothing out any bone protrusions with a grinder. The plantar fascia was then released from its insertion point on the calcaneus. The area was thoroughly irrigated with Normal saline, and the incisions were sutured sequentially. A compression dressing with a cotton pad was applied. The follow-up appointments were scheduled at intervals of 1, 3, and 6 months post-surgery (Fig. 1).

Endoscopic plantar fasciotomy

Needle-knife procedure

The needle-knife treatment was administered once a week over four consecutive weeks. During the procedure, the patient lied in a prone position. The most tender spots on the plantar and dorsal sides of the affected heel, as well as the superficial projection area of the plantar fascia, were identified. Local anesthesia using 1% lidocaine was applied, and the needle-knife was inserted vertically to reach the bone surface at the tender point. Various techniques were utilized, including needling, longitudinal and transverse dredging, horizontal peeling, lifting and cutting, and bone surface scraping, to release and loosen the plantar fascia adhered to the calcaneal spur. After the procedure, pressure is applied for three minutes, and the area was dressed with sterile gauze (Fig. 2)

Needle knife therapy

Conventional painkillers

Pain relief patches containing 50 mg of diclofenac sodium were used in this study. The patches were applied to the area of significant heel pain every other day for a duration of 5 h each time, for a total treatment period of one month. Participants were advised to follow standard pain management practices and avoid activities that could exacerbate their condition.

Data collection process

Data collection was started after obtaining informed written consent from the patients with a clear explanation of study’s purpose. All physicians in the Department of Orthopaedics were briefed about the study to ensure identification and complete diagnostic workup of patients upon admission. Firstly, a questionnaire was structured to gather socio demographic data, procedure data and follow up of the participants. Both the patient-reported scales (VAS score and AOFAS score) were documented by an expert physician in a questionnaire.

Follow up schedule

Patients were on follow-up schedule at 1st, 3rd, and 6th months after treatment.

Outcome measures

Reduction of pain was the primary outcome with functional improvement. Outcomes were assessed by using two validated scales. They are the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) for pain and the American Orthopaedic Foot & Ankle Society (AOFAS) score.

Visual analog scale (VAS) for pain

The VAS is a commonly used as patient reported tool to measure pain intensity. It consists of a 10 cm line: “no pain” is lebeled as 0 and “worst possible pain” as 10. Participants were asked to mark a point on the line that represented their pain level. The distance from the “no pain” end to the marked point was measured in centimeters, providing a continuous variable score from 0 to 10.

American orthopedic foot & ankle society (AOFAS) score

The AOFAS score is a widely used clinical tool to examine the functional status of the foot and ankle. It includes total nine items categorized into three main domains: pain, function, and alignment, with a total score ranging from 0 to 100. Higher scores indicate better function and less pain. The highest scores for the three subscales are 40 points for pain, 50 points for function, and 10 points for alignment, respectively.

Statistical analysis

After data were checked, verified for consistency and tabulated using SPSS version 25 software. Statistical significance was set as a 95% confidence level at a 5% acceptable error level. The sociodemographic data were expressed as the means ± SD for continuous variables and as frequencies (%) for categorical variables. Data were analyzed to compare the mean VAS and AOFAS scores between the three treatment groups at each time point. One-way ANOVA was used to determine the statistical significance of differences between the groups. The p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Additionally, Pearson’s chi-square test was used to assess the association between treatment groups and categorical outcomes such as pain intensity levels (no pain, moderate pain, worst pain) at different time points.

Results

The study included 60 participants, divided equally into three treatment groups: Needle Knife Therapy (20), Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy (20), and Conventional Painkillers (20). The sociodemographic findings revealed that the majority of participants in all three group fell within the 41–60 year age range and had a normal BMI. Regarding BMI, majority that is 65% (n = 13), 75% (n = 15) and 65% (n = 13) of respondents in needle knife, Endoscopic Planter Fasciotomy and conservative treatment group respectively belonged to normal BMI (18.5-<25) (Table 1). By different treatment groups, the mean age of respondents was same in needle knife and conservative groups that is 53.35 (± 13.922) and 53.35 (± 13.922) years. In Endoscopic Planter Fasciotomy group the mean age of respondents was 52.9 (± 12.234) years (Table 2). These differences in mean age were not statistically significant (p = 0.992).

These differences of mean VAS pain score between different treatment groups were statistically significant (p < 0.05) (Table 3).

The American Orthopaedic Foot & Ankle Society (AOFAS) score, which measures functional improvement, also showed significant differences between the therapy groups. Prior to starting therapy, the mean AOFAS scores for each group were similar as well: 51.8 (± 51.80) for Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy, 49.45 (± 49.45) for Needle Knife, and 51.3 (± 51.30) for Conventional Painkillers (p = 0.574). The Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy group had the highest mean AOFAS score of 88.15 six months after therapy (Table 4).

Discussion

This study aimed to evaluate the clinical efficacy of three different treatments for plantar fasciitis: Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy, Needle Knife Therapy, and Conventional Painkillers. The results demonstrated that Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy provided superior pain relief and functional improvement compared to the other treatments, with significant benefits observed at one, three, and six months post-treatment.

The excellent efficacy of Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy can be attributed to its minimally invasive nature, enabling precise release of the medial one third to one half of the plantar fascia while causing minimal injury to the surrounding tissues. The mechanical stress that perpetuates inflammation and discomfort is likely to be reduced by releasing the tension in the plantar fascia. Minimal post operative pain with short recovery time promotes healing and alleviates pain. The substantial and continuous decrease in VAS scores over 1st and 6th month reported in the Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy group supports the idea that surgical surgery offers a more definitive relief. In an earlier systematic review on Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy evaluated its effect on 978 feet over 25 months. 18 papers reported the weighted mean AOFAS score before surgery as 55.66 (± 10.3), and it was 89.6 (± 5.2) after surgery. The systematic review reported it as an effective intervention for refractory planter fasciitis as well [11]. A previous case series on 40 patients who undergone EPF due to planter fasciitis with calcaneal spur also reported a promising outcome. The VAS for pain was significantly improved from an average of 80.7 mm preoperatively to 8.8 mm postoperatively. In addition, the patients were assessed using the Japanese Society for Surgery of the Foot score, which showed an improvement from 44.7 to 95.6 points [12].

In contrast, Needle Knife Therapy, while effective in the short term, showed a gradual decline in efficacy over time. Needle Knife Therapy’s initial effectiveness may result from its ability to disrupt localized adhesions and scar tissue, providing immediate relief. Although this method provides initial relief, it may not be as comprehensive in addressing the entire pathological area as the endoscopic approach, leading to a recurrence of symptoms over time. These days Ultrasonographic guided needle knife procedure is showing some promising result. A prospective study, conducted in Spain on 2016, assessed 16 patients diagnosed with plantar fasciitis who underwent ultrasound-guided partial plantar fasciotomy using multiple needle knife incisions.

They observed an immediate decline in VAS scores from the initial score for every clinic session. Among these individuals. However, the decrease between 3 and 12 months was not as significant.

Factors like foot, age, sex, weight, height, and body mass index did not significantly influence the progress of VAS, FADI, and plantar fascia thickness [13]. Another retrospective study comprised 107 patients who were diagnosed with plantar fasciitis and underwent ultrasound-guided needle release of the plantar fascia. 92.5% of patients experienced improvement in heel pain, and there were no recorded cases of nerve complications, vascular damage, or wound-related issues. At initial follow-up visits, the VAS score decreased substantially in comparison to the baseline, with the exception of the period between 3 and 12 months. Over time, the FADI questionnaire also exhibited remarkable progress [14]. The outcomes of the ultrasound-guided partial plantar fasciotomy with a needle were considered effective for short term pain relief with rapid recovery and low expenses.

The Conventional Painkillers group showed the least improvement, particularly in terms of long-term pain relief and functional gains. Painkillers, such as diclofenac sodium patches, primarily address inflammation and pain but do not resolve the underlying mechanical issues of plantar fasciitis. While they reduce inflammation and pain, they do not correct the biomechanical factors contributing to plantar fasciitis, resulting in less pronounced long-term benefits. This finding underscores the importance of targeted interventions that address the root cause of the condition rather than merely alleviating symptoms. A randomized controlled trial including 140 patients with unilateral plantar fasciitis, showed the efficacy of oral diclofenac sodium with acetaminophen and methylprednisolone local injection and bupivacaine. Both treatment groups revealed a decrease in pain intensity, but the reduction was significant in Group B (Needle Knife group) compared to Group A (Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy).

Limitation

Limitations of this study includes a relatively small sample size which may limit the generalizability of this study. Additionally, being conducted in a single center with specific study setting may limit the applicability of these findings in different tier of hospitals. Furthermore, long term outcomes could not be measured due to the study methods. Implementation research with larger sample sizes and longer follow-up periods may validate these findings and explore additional factors influencing treatment outcomes.

Conclusion

This study provides evidence supporting the superiority of Endoscopic Plantar Fasciotomy in treating plantar fasciitis compared to Needle Knife Therapy and Conventional Painkillers. These findings focused the importance of causal factors of plantar fasciitis to achieve sustained pain relief and functional improvement.

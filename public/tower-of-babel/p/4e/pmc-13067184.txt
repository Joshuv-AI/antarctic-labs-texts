Programmed cell death receptor 1 inhibitors combined with paclitaxel-platinum induction chemotherapy for treating patients with locally advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma: a real-world retrospective study
Authors: Lilan Chen, Xing Song, Junjun Chen, Junchao Huang, Wendong Gu, Wenjie Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundConcurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) combined with induction chemotherapy (IC) is the standard first-line treatment for locally advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma (LA-NPC). However, treatment-related toxicities and the risk of recurrence or metastasis remain clinical challenges, which have prompted the development of strategies that can improve efficacy while minimizing adverse effects. The integration of programmed cell death receptor 1 (PD-1) inhibitors with IC has demonstrated significant survival benefits in pivotal phase III trials. This study aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of PD-1 inhibitors combined with IC consisting of a paclitaxel-platinum (TP) regimen for LA-NPC in a real-world clinical setting.MethodsThis retrospective study enrolled patients diagnosed with stage III or IVA NPC who received treatment between January 2021 and December 2024. Clinical records and radiographic data were systematically reviewed. Based on the administration of PD-1 inhibitor immunotherapy during IC, patients were categorized into a control group and an immunotherapy group. Propensity score matching (PSM) was performed sequentially after IC and again after CCRT to minimize confounding factors. Matching was based on clinically relevant covariates, including age, gender, differentiation, tumor-node-metastasis stage, Epstein-Barr virus DNA level and treatment cycle. Between-group differences in treatment response and adverse events were assessed via Chi-squared tests for categorical variables. The primary endpoint is the disease control rate (DCR) after IC. The secondary endpoints include the objective response rate (ORR) after CCRT, the incidence of adverse events, and their severity.ResultsA total of 150 patients with LA-NPC were included, among whom 76 received standard IC, and 74 received combined immunotherapy. In the PSM analysis, 54 patients were matched to each group, and the immunotherapy group demonstrated significantly higher disease control during the IC phase (P=0.004), with a partial response (PR) rate of 94.4% compared to 70.4% in the control group. Following CCRT, 49 patients per group matched by PSM showed no significant differences in complete response (CR) or PR rates (P=0.25). The CR rates were 10.2% and 18.4% in the control group and the immunotherapy group, respectively, while the PR rates were 89.8% and 81.6%, respectively. Furthermore, the groups showed no statistical differences in terms of the incidence of radiotherapy-related adverse events, including oral mucositis (P=0.26), skin reactions (P=0.20), and myelosuppression (P=0.33). No grade 3 or higher immune-related adverse events (irAEs) were reported in the immunotherapy group.ConclusionsThis real-world study successfully verified that adding PD-1 inhibitors to IC improves early efficacy without increasing toxicity in LA-NPC, providing external validation of key trial results related to routine clinical practice.

Key findings

• This real-world study confirmed that adding programmed cell death receptor 1 (PD-1) inhibitors to induction chemotherapy (IC) improves early tumor response in patients with locally advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma (LA-NPC) without increasing the risk of radiotherapy-related toxicity and immune-related adverse events.

• Landmark phase III trials have established that combining PD-1 inhibitors with IC improves survival outcomes in patients with LA-NPC.

• Adding PD-1 inhibitors to IC showed significantly better treatment response compared to the standard IC without increasing toxicity after radiotherapy. This study provides external validation from routine clinical practice that the early efficacy and favorable safety profile of combining PD-1 inhibitors with IC is translatable to a real-world setting.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The findings support the clinical adoption of PD-1 inhibitors into IC for treating LA-NPC. Future efforts should focus on long-term outcome tracking and optimizing the treatment sequence and duration.

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a common malignancy of the head and neck associated with regional epidemiological patterns, with >70% of new cases being diagnosed at advanced stages (1). As a highly radio- and chemosensitive tumor, chemoradiotherapy is the preferred treatment, providing a 5-year survival as high as 80% (2-4). However, a portion of patients still experience recurrence or distant metastasis after treatment.

Immunotherapy has become a research hotspot in the treatment of NPC. Approximately 89–95% of patients with NPC express programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) on tumor cells and have a tumor microenvironment characterized by abundant immune cell infiltration, which is conducive to immunotherapy achieving substantial efficacy (5-7). Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs), particularly programmed cell death receptor 1 (PD-1) inhibitors, have demonstrated satisfactory antitumor activity in multiple clinical trials. Three multicenter phase III randomized controlled trials—RATIONALE-309, CAPTAIN-1st, and JUPITER-02—on patients with recurrent or metastatic NPC receiving first-line therapy reported that the combination of PD-1 inhibitors with chemotherapy significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) (8-10).

The application of PD-1 inhibitors has been extended to the radical treatment of locally advanced NPC (LA-NPC). Immunotherapy combined with induction chemotherapy (IC) helps optimize target volumes and dosage while reducing toxicity. Additionally, the activation of the tumor immune microenvironment may further potentiate the efficacy of radiotherapy (11). The recent publication of several landmark phase III randomized controlled trials (e.g., CONTINUUM and DIPPER) has established a new standard of care, with the integration of PD-1 inhibitors into IC-based regimens significantly improving survival outcomes in patients with LA-NPC (12,13).

However, the optimal application timing, cycles, and drug combinations for the immunotherapy of LANPC have not yet been clarified (14). In addition, the relevant phase III trials were conducted under strictly controlled conditions with select patient cohorts, and their outcomes have not been verified in real-world contexts in which patient characteristics, treatment adherence, and supportive care may differ (15). Therefore, we conducted a single-center, retrospective study in order to assess the translatability of these major trial findings into routine practice. Specifically, we sought to verify whether the early benefits and safety profile of PD-1 inhibitor combined with IC could be replicated in our institutional cohort, thereby providing external validity and generating real-world evidence supporting this treatment strategy. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-1-0029/rc).

Methods

Patient and clinical data

Patients with LA-NPC (without distant metastasis) treated at The Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University between January 2021 and December 2024 were enrolled in this study. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) age 18–80 years; (II) pathological diagnosis of NPC confirmed by biopsy; (III) clinical stage III or IVA according to the eighth edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system (16); (IV) Karnofsky performance status score ≥70; and (V) expected survival time >3 months. Meanwhile, the exclusion criteria were as follows: (I) history of severe allergy; (II) contraindications to radiotherapy or systemic therapy; (III) previous or concurrent diagnosis of other malignancies; (IV) prior radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or targeted therapy; (V) pregnancy or lactation; and (VI) severe dysfunction of major organs. The following data were collected for all enrolled patients: complete medical history, physical examination, hematological and biochemical profiles, nasopharyngoscopy, histopathological diagnosis, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the nasopharynx and cervical or contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) if MRI was contraindicated for primary tumor staging. Distant metastasis was evaluated via thoracic and abdominal CT. For patients with advanced nodal stage or clinical suspicion of metastasis, 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography-CT was recommended. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Third Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (approval No. 2023-190). Informed consent was obtained from all patients.

Treatment

Patients in the standard treatment group received the following paclitaxel-platinum (TP) regimen as IC: 230 mg/m2 of albumin-bound paclitaxel plus 75 mg/m2 of cisplatin (100 mg/m2 of nedaplatin or carboplatin at an area under the curve of 5 for cisplatin-ineligible patients). Each cycle was administered every 21 days, and the number of induction cycles was adjusted based on tolerance and tumor response. The immunotherapy group received PD-1 inhibitor therapy in addition to the TP regimen. Following IC, both groups proceeded to concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) combined with the targeted agent nimotuzumab (200 mg, once weekly). All patients underwent intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT). Gross tumor volume was delineated based on MRI, and target volumes were contoured on contrast-enhanced CT for treatment planning. Prescription doses were as follows: 69.96–71.94 Gy in 33 fractions to 95% of the planning target volume of the primary nasopharyngeal tumor, 66.00–69.96 Gy/33 fractions to 95% of the nodal planning target volume, and 50.96–60.06 Gy/33 fractions to 95% of the clinical target volume encompassing the primary site and bilateral cervical lymphatic drainage areas, delivered in 5 fractions per week. Plan evaluation and organ-at-risk constraints followed the criteria of the Radiation Therapy Oncology Group (RTOG 0615 and RTOG 0225) protocols (17,18). Treatment planning included 6-MV X-rays with inverse planning IMRT, followed by plan optimization.

Indices and efficacy evaluation criteria

During treatment, laboratory parameters such as complete blood count and hepatic or renal function were regularly monitored. Adverse events, including radiotherapy-related toxicities and immune-related adverse events (irAEs), were recorded and compared between the two groups. The radiotherapy-related toxicities contain leukopenia, decreased hemoglobin, thrombocytopenia, oral mucositis, and dermatological reactions while irAEs contain endocrine dysfunction, immune-related rash, pneumonitis, hepatitis, diarrhea/colitis and nephritis. Toxicity was graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0 (19). Nasopharynx and neck MRI scans were performed at baseline, after induction, and after radiotherapy. Treatment response was evaluated according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 (20), which includes the following definitions: complete response (CR), disappearance of all target lesions; partial response (PR), ≥30% decrease in baseline lesion diameters; stable disease (SD), decrease in the total length of baseline lesions without PR or an increase without progressive disease (PD); and PD, ≥20% increase in lesion diameters or appearance of new lesions. The primary endpoint of this study was the disease control rate (DCR), defined as the proportion of patients achieving a CR, PR, SD or PD after the completion of IC. Key secondary endpoints included the objective response rate (ORR, CR + PR) after CCRT, incidence and severity of treatment-related adverse events (including both conventional chemoradiotherapy toxicities and irAEs), and OS and PFS data which are under ongoing follow-up and analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed with SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Propensity score matching (PSM) was applied at a 1:1 ratio to balance the baseline characteristics between the standard treatment group and the immunotherapy group, thereby minimizing potential confounding effects. The first PSM was conducted to assess the impact of immunotherapy on treatment response after IC, with the matching covariates being gender, age, tumor differentiation, clinical stage, and number of IC cycles. A second PSM analysis was performed to evaluate the effect of immunotherapy on treatment efficacy and adverse events after radiotherapy, with additional covariates including the number of concurrent chemotherapy cycles and the use of targeted therapy. Between-group comparisons of categorical variables in the matched cohorts were performed via the Chi-squared test, with a P value <0.05 being considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 150 patients with stage III or IVA NPC were enrolled, including 76 patients in the standard IC group and 74 patients in the IC combined with immunotherapy group (Figure 1). PSM after IC

To assess the impact of immunotherapy on tumor response after IC, PSM was performed, with gender, age, differentiation type, clinical stage, EBV DNA level and number of IC cycles serving as the covariates. After matching, 54 patients were included in each group.

CR, PR, and SD of the control group occurred in 1 (1.9%), 38 (70.4%), and 15 (27.8%) of the patients, respectively. In the immunotherapy group, CR, PR, and SD occurred in 0 (0%), 51 (94.4%), and 3 (5.6%) of the patients, respectively. The immunotherapy group showed significantly better treatment response compared to the standard group after IC, with a higher proportion of patients achieving PR (P=0.004) (Table 1).

PSM after radiotherapy

To evaluate disease response after radiotherapy, PSM was again conducted with additional covariates, including the number of concurrent chemotherapy cycles and use of targeted therapy, after which 49 patients remained in each group.

There was no significant difference in postradiotherapy efficacy between the immunotherapy group and the standard group (P=0.25), which may be attributed to the high DCR achieved by radiotherapy in the majority of patients. Nevertheless, the immunotherapy group had a numerically higher number of patients who achieved CR, with 5 (10.2%) in the control group and 9 (18.4%) in the immunotherapy group (Table 2).

Discussion

This single-center retrospective study provides real-world validation that integrating PD-1 inhibitors into IC improves early tumor response among patients with LA-NPC, without augmenting radiation-related toxicity and irAEs. The significant improvement in disease control after IC is consistent with the elevated CR rates (CRRs) observed in the BEACON trial, wherein the addition of tislelizumab to gemcitabine and cisplatin IC resulted in a near doubling of CRR to 30.5% in high-risk LA-NPC patients as compared to those who did not receive this regimen (16.7%). However, immunotherapy was not used in the CCRT stage of the BEACON study as prophylactic irradiation of large cervical lymph nodes during radiotherapy may directly eliminate memory lymphocytes and weaken the efficacy of immunotherapy and because radiation may change the tumor microenvironment and weaken the antitumor immune response of PD-1/L1 inhibitors. The lack of a significant difference in response rates after CCRT in our cohort may be attributed to the powerful disease-control effect of radiotherapy itself, which achieved high cure rates in the majority of patients. Nonetheless, the numerical increase in CR observed in our immunotherapy group suggests the potential for the benefit to be durable, echoing the reports of long-term survival advantages achieved in larger prospective studies (21).

CCRT combined with IC and adjuvant chemotherapy has significantly improved the survival outcomes of patients with LA-NPC. The definite synergistic effect and favorable patient tolerance have established this treatment model as the current guideline-recommended first-line approach (22). However, the long-term adverse effects after radiotherapy—including radiation-induced skin reactions, oral mucositis, dysphagia, xerostomia, and hearing loss—compromise the quality of life in long-term survivors. Therefore, there is need to devise strategies capable of enhancing efficacy while reducing toxicity within the current treatment paradigm, such as those excluding chemotherapy, selecting low-toxicity equivalent chemotherapeutic agents, or selectively omitting low-risk lymph node regions during prophylactic radiotherapy (23-25). Immunotherapy has demonstrated its potential for efficacy enhancement, and when combined with IC, decreases the size of the primary lesions, which is conducive to the precise delineation of the target volume, accurate targeting of the primary tumor, and reduction of damage to normal tissues (26). PD-1 inhibitors promote greater T-cell activation and expansion, thereby fully activating the antitumor immune response before radiotherapy (27,28).

Indeed, the survival benefits of combining immunotherapy with standard treatment have been consistently demonstrated in landmark trials. For instance, the CONTINUUM trial, an open-label, multicenter and phase III study, showed that adding sintilimab to the entire treatment course of induction therapy, CCRT, and subsequent consolidation phases significantly improved 3-year event-free survival (EFS) (86% vs. 76%; P=0.02), establishing a new benchmark for survival outcomes (12).

Similarly, the DIPPER study provided the first evidence that camrelizumab as adjuvant immunotherapy for patients with LA-NPC administered for 12 cycles following standard therapy significantly improves 3-year EFS (86.9% vs. 77.3%), distant metastasis-free survival (92.4% vs. 84.5%), and locoregional relapse-free survival (92.8% vs. 87.0%) compared with the control group that did not receive adjuvant monotherapy and entered follow-up observation (13). Administering immunotherapy after standard chemoradiotherapy ensures that patients can fully complete the established and effective standard treatment. The DIPPER study indicates that subsequent immune consolidation therapy further extends survival benefits while maintaining a manageable toxicity profile.

Cisplatin-based CCRT remains the cornerstone of treatment for NPC. However, it is frequently associated with hematological and gastrointestinal toxicities, and when combined with radiotherapy, often leads to severe oral mucositis and weight loss (23,29). These adverse effects prevent many patients from completing the full course of standard treatment, thereby compromising therapeutic continuity and overall efficacy (30). Recently, the DIAMOND trial examined the feasibility of omitting concurrent cisplatin chemotherapy (31). In this trial, both study arms received 17 cycles of toripalimab, consisting of 3 cycles during the induction phase, 3 cycles during radiotherapy, and 11 cycles in the adjuvant phase. The results indicated that the experimental group without concurrent cisplatin chemotherapy had noninferior failure-free survival and a lower incidence of vomiting events of all grades, indicating this regimen may provide a less-toxic alternative for select patients.

The achievements in research described above have provided a variety of options for enhancing efficacy and reducing toxicity in the immunotherapy of patients with NPC, with immunotherapy becoming an indispensable component of systemic treatment for LA-NPC. Nonetheless, further investigation into immunotherapy for patients with NPC is warranted, which could include evaluating combinations with capecitabine in adjuvant chemotherapy, integration with targeted therapies or antibody-drug conjugates, and immunotherapy-based adaptive radiotherapy (11,32,33).

The findings of our study demonstrate that combining immunotherapy with IC leads to a notable improvement in tumor response rate, consistent with results reported in previous studies. However, several limitations should be considered. First, due to the retrospective nature of the design, selection bias might have been present despite PSM being applied to minimize it. Second, the sample size remained relatively small and was derived from a single institution. Finally, whether immunotherapy translates into sustained survival benefit remains to be validated through longer-term follow-up.

Conclusions

This real-world validation study confirms that PD-1 inhibitor immunotherapy combined with IC can effectively improve the disease control of patients with LA-NPC without augmenting toxicity, thereby providing robust external validation for previous large-scale studies. However, the optimal timing and duration of induction therapy, CCRT, and adjuvant therapy combined with immunotherapy remain to be determined in future research.

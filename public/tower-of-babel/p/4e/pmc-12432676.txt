Long-term survival outcomes of lorlatinib in advanced anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK)-fusion positive non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Masahiro Morise, Ichidai Tanaka, Makoto Ishii
Published: 2025

Introduction

In 2007, the anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) rearrangement was discovered in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) as one of the driver oncogenes, which has become the focus of attention as a new therapeutic target (1). Crizotinib, the first-in-class ALK tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), proved the prolongation of progression-free survival (PFS) compared to platinum-doublet chemotherapy in the first-line setting in patients with ALK-fusion positive NSCLC (2). Thereafter, as second-generation ALK-TKIs, alectinib, brigatinib, and ensartinib showed superior clinical efficacy to crizotinib in each phase III trial (3-7). Furthermore, the phase III CROWN study proved that lorlatinib, a third-generation ALK-TKI, also improved the PFS compared to crizotinib (8). Among these trials, PFS was set as the primary endpoint and overall survival (OS) as one of the secondary endpoints.

Recently, Ou et al. reported the final analyses in the pivotal phase 2 trial of lorlatinib (9). This early single-arm pivotal trial was conducted prior to the start of the phase III CROWN study. The trial consisted of expansion cohorts (EXP) 1–5 according to the history of prior therapy. Subsequent to the first report indicating a promising efficacy of first-line lorlatinib for ALK fusion positive NSCLC with a 90% objective response rate (ORR) in EXP 1 (10), the current updated report by Ou et al. (9) showed remarkable long-term OS in patients treated with first-line lorlatinib (median OS: not reached, 5-year survival rate: 78% in EXP 1). Although the OS analysis was exploratory, first-line lorlatinib was associated with long-term OS for ALK fusion-positive NSCLC, as well as the results of the CROWN study. Furthermore, in EXP3B, the cohort of previously received non-crizotinib ALK-TKIs with or without chemotherapy, lorlatinib reached a long-term OS (median OS: 37.4 months, 5-year survival rate: 45%). In terms of safety, a grade 5 treatment-related adverse event (AE) was not observed even in the long-term follow-up analysis. The most common treatment related AEs include hypercholesterolemia (84%), followed by hypertriglyceridemia (68%), and edema (45%). The frequency of several all-cause AEs increased slightly; however, the incidence of AEs was generally similar to previous analyses with longer follow-ups (10). Collectively, the current report by Ou et al. (9) provided updated information on lorlatinib regarding long-term survival outcome in both patients with ALK fusion positive NSCLC with first-line setting and those with prior history of non-crizotinib ALK-TKIs in addition to long-term follow-up safety data.

Current ALK-TKI landscape

With the published phase II and III trial evidence of lorlatinib and the second generation ALK TKIs as mentioned above, which ALK-TKIs is a better option in the first-line setting? The clinical question is crucial because no evidence is available on the head-to-head comparison among second- or third-generation ALK-TKIs. Indeed, alectinib, brigatinib, and lorlatinib are stated as the preferred first-line treatment in the European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO) Clinical Practice Guideline (11). The Japanese Lung Cancer Association’s NSCLC treatment guidelines are updated annually (12), and alectinib and lorlatinib are now listed as strongly recommended for first treatment in patients with ALK fusion based on their balance of efficacy and toxicity. The relevant key factors to select first-line ALK-TKIs in each patient are considered as follows: (I) long-term PFS and OS outcome data; (II) clinical intracranial efficacy for central nervous system (CNS) metastasis; and (III) safety profile. Furthermore, the activity of each ALK-TKI for patients previously treated with the other ALK-TKIs is also one of the key factors in discussing the sequential strategy of ALK-TKIs. These data are reported as updated analysis with long-term follow-up and either preplanned or post hoc analysis of phase III trials (13-19). These data should be interpreted with caution because there are limitations when comparing clinical outcome across several trials. Long-term PFS and OS outcome of ALK-TKIs

Regarding long-term PFS outcome, lorlatinib showed remarkable long-term PFS in the CROWN study (median PFS, not reached; 5-year PFS rate, 60%). Furthermore, long-term OS outcomes are reported. The current updated report by Ou et al. (9) showed that the median OS of patients with the first-line setting (EXP 1) was not reached, and the 5-year OS rate was 78%, which is a promising result compared to the historical data of the 5-year OS rate with first-line crizotinib. Further, in the CROWN study, OS follow-up is ongoing because the required number of OS events for a protocol-specified second interim analysis was not confirmed at this point. OS analysis of lorlatinib in the CROWN study including protocol-specified comparison to crizotinib is warranted, which will be one of the important outcomes for the selection of first-line ALK-TKIs in each patient. In terms of alectinib, the 5-year OS rates were 60.9% in the J-ALEX trial and 62.5% in the ALEX trial. Regarding the evidence for alectinib, the following points should be noted: three phase III studies have reproducibly demonstrated the superiority of alectinib over crizotinib in terms of PFS, whereas there is currently only one confirmatory phase III study for ALK TKIs other than alectinib. In ALTA-1L trial, brigatinib proved the PFS superiority over crizotinib with hazard ratio (HR) of 0.48. Further, 4-year PFS rate of brigatinib was 36%, while that of crizotinib was 18% (19). The eXalt3 study was a phase III trials comparing ensartinib to crizotinib in patients with ALK-positive NSCLC who had not received prior treatment with an ALK inhibitor (7). As in the J-ALEX trial, this clinical trial allowed the enrollment of patients who had received prior chemotherapy. Ensartinib showed the superiority over crizotinib in PFS with HR of 0.51, while the OS for ensartinib and crizotinib were similar. In terms of OS, further follow-up is needed due to the immaturity of the data.

Intracranial activity

Intracranial efficacy is also one of the key factors in selecting first-line ALK-TKIs. In an investigator-initiated phase II study of lorlatinib in patients with brain metastasis, the confirmed objective intracranial response rate at 12 weeks was 59%, and the median intracranial PFS was 24.6 months (20). In the CROWN study, the HR of intracranial PFS of lorlatinib to crizotinib was 0.06 (13). In the ALEX study, the intracranial ORR of alectinib in patients without prior radiotherapy to the brain was 78.6% (4). The intracranial ORR of brigatinib was reported as 67% in patients treated with the approved standard dose of brigatinib (180 mg with 7 days lead-in at 90 mg) in the ALTA trial (21). The HR of the intracranial PFS of brigatinib to crizotinib in the intention-to-treat population was 0.44 in the ALTA-1L trial (19). These data provide useful information for considering first-line ALK-TKI selection in each patient. However, intracranial efficacy data should be interpreted with caution as patient characteristics differed across clinical trials, including whether or not patients had a history of prior radiation therapy for CNS metastasis and whether analyses were restricted to only cases with measurable disease. Clinical data on intracranial ORR and PFS for ALK-TKIs are gradually becoming available; however, further studies focusing on the deterioration of activities of daily living and quality of life due to intracranial progression are warranted to validate the utility of ALK-TKIs, which are designed to reinforce intracranial activity. The progression pattern of brain metastasis is heterogeneous, that is, from symptomatic progression requiring radiation therapy for CNS metastasis to asymptomatic progression with the emergence of a minor new lesion. Therefore, the question remains whether intracranial ORR and intracranial PFS are clinically meaningful surrogate endpoints.

Safety

Another key factor to determine the first-line ALK-TKI is the safety profile of each ALK-TKI. With regard to lorlatinib, changes in cognition (memory impairment, disturbance in attention, and amnesia) and mood (anxiety, depression, and affect lability) in any grade were observed in 21% and 16% of the patients, respectively (8). In addition, hypercholesterolemia and hypertriglyceridemia are the most common AEs of lorlatinib (70% and 64% in any grade, respectively) (8). In terms of safety for alectinib, any grade of interstitial lung disease (ILD) was observed in 8% of patients (grade ≥3 ILD in 5%) in the J-ALEX study (3). Sinus bradycardia in any grade was observed in >20% in the ALESIA study although that occurred only in 1% in the J-ALEX study (5). With regard to brigatinib, hypertension was observed in 31 of 163 patients (2%), and grade ≥3 hypertension occurred in 10% of the patients. ILD or pneumonitis was observed in 5 of 136 (4%) patients (6). The most common AE of ensartinib was rash (67.8% in any grade), and pruritus was observed in 26.6% (7). Luo et al. reported a systematic review and network meta-analysis for the comparative safety of ALK-TKIs and concluded that the safety profiles of ALK-TKIs differed from each other (22). The safety profile can be a basis of selection for specific first-line ALK-TKIs from the perspective of each patient’s comorbidities and preferences.

Discussion on sequential strategy of ALK-TKIs

❖ Mechanisms of resistance: despite the high antitumor activity of first-line ALK-TKIs, the majority of patients with ALK fusion positivity showed disease progression on first-line ALK-TKI. The resistance mechanism of first-line ALK-TKI treatment has been identified by re-biopsy study for tumor tissue or blood sampling with next-generation sequencing. One of the major resistance mechanisms is induced by acquired ALK mutations such as G1202R and F1174 C/L.

Therefore, ALK-TKIs not used in the first-line setting can be the second-line treatment option. In the ESMO Clinical Practice Guideline, lorlatinib is recommended for patients who progressed with first-line ALK-TKIs other than crizotinib if lorlatinib is not used in the first-line setting. The Japanese Lung Cancer Association’s NSCLC treatment guidelines recommend lorlatinib, brigatinib, and ceritinib for patients who progressed on first-line alectinib treatment. Several clinical trials have provided the efficacy data of ALK-TKIs for pretreated patients with second-generation ALK-TKIs. In the current pivotal phase II study analysis, the ORR of lorlatinib for patients treated with alectinib was 37.1% (9). Brigatinib is also active in patients treated with alectinib. In the J-ALTA study, the ORR of brigatinib was 34%, with a median PFS of 7.3 months for patients with a prior history of alectinib treatment (23). Conversely, the activity of alectinib for patients treated with the other second- or third-generation ALK-TKIs remains unknown because alectinib was first approved as second-generation ALK-TKI, and we have few clinical trial data of alectinib for patients pretreated with non-crizotinib ALK-TKIs.

Therefore, ALK-TKIs not used in the first-line setting can be the second-line treatment option. ❖ Challenges in sequencing without head-to-head data: collectively, the ALK-TKI sequence strategy is another clinical issue that should be discussed. For instance, first-line alectinib followed by lorlatinib is superior to first-line lorlatinib in the total PFS of ALK-TKIs or OS? In patients with epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation, the treatment strategy of first- or second-generation EGFR TKIs followed by osimertinib for patients with EGFR-resistant T790M mutation is well investigated. Ito et al. reported that first-line afatinib (followed by osimertinib in the case with the emergence of T790M mutation) showed longer OS than first-line osimertinib in patients with EGFR L858R mutation and no brain metastasis (24). A similar concept is now under investigation in EGFR-mutant NSCLC with a phase III study comparing first-line erlotinib plus ramucirumab to first-line osimertinib (25). This study set the primary endpoint as the time to failure of the strategy, and the secondary treatment of osimertinib for patients with EGFR T790M resistance mutation is specified in the erlotinib plus ramucirumab arm.

Conclusions

In conclusion, as with the other ALK-TKIs, evidence of lorlatinib regarding long-term PFS and OS follow-up data with intracranial efficacy is now uncovered based on the pivotal phase II trial and phase III CROWN study. This evidence would provide useful information for the selection of first-line ALK-TKIs among multiple options in each patient. Further new evidence on long-term PFS and OS follow-up data of ALK-TKIs including lorlatinib is warranted to establish the treatment strategy for ALK-fusion NSCLC.

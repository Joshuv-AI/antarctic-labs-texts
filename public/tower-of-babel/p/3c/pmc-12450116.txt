Response to “Classification of intracranial aneurysm remnants after clipping in the era of three-dimensional digital subtraction angiography”
Authors: Jun Thorsteinsdottir, Christian Schichor
Published: 2025

We sincerely thank the authors for their thoughtful and detailed commentary on our study “Detection of remnants in clipped unruptured intracranial aneurysms by intraoperative CT-angiography and postoperative DSA: clinical relevance and follow-up” [13]. We greatly appreciate their recognition of our work and their comprehensive discussion of the role of advanced imaging modalities in aneurysm surgery.

We fully agree that the differentiation between completely and incompletely occluded aneurysms is of major clinical relevance, particularly in light of the markedly different recurrence rates. As highlighted, 3D-DSA represents a substantial advance over conventional 2D-DSA by enabling a more reliable detection and quantification of remnants [1, 3, 6–8, 11]. We also share the view that the development and validation of a robust 3D-DSA-based classification system will be a crucial step toward standardizing outcome reporting and improving postoperative decision-making.

Indeed, iDSA images provides high quality and allows for the immediate assessment of clipping results – especially when 3D-DSA is available – and may ultimately become the standard of care in aneurysm surgery [9]. However, the routine use of intraoperative angiography remains technically and logistically challenging in many institutions. At the same time, we believe that intraoperative tools such as iCTA – although currently limited in spatial resolution – hold considerable potential as resource-efficient and non-invasive adjuncts to established intraoperative monitoring techniques.

iCT and iCTA are already employed in other neurosurgical procedures, such as stereotactic interventions and spinal fusion surgeries. In aneurysm surgery, intraoperative CT perfusion (iCTP) may further aid in detecting regions at risk of ischemia [12]. Looking ahead, advances such as ultra-high-resolution CT-angiography (UHR-CTA) [5], ultrahigh-resoluation photon-counting detector CTA [4], single-energy metal artifact reduction (SEMAR) algorithms [2, 5, 10] and the ability of 3D reconstruction of the iCTA images could significantly enhance intraoperative visualization of aneurysm remnants.

In this way, the intraoperative detection of relevant remnants can directly influence surgical strategy – for example by enabling clip repositioning before the procedure is completed. Future refinements in iCTA technology may further increase its value as part of a multimodal approach to aneurysm surgery.

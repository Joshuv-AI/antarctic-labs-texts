Practical predefined-time adaptive fuzzy control for quantized nonlinear systems via observer-differentiator scheme
Authors: Yuanqing Wang, Juan Chen, Wenyao Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper addresses the predefined-time adaptive fuzzy control problem for non-strict feedback nonlinear systems (NSFNS) with input quantization. First, a novel inverse hyperbolic sine state observer was designed based on fuzzy logic to estimate unmeasurable states. Second, owing to the non-smooth and non-differentiable nature of the quantized output signal, a predefined-time differentiator method was proposed to concurrently handle these issues and reduce control signal fluctuations. Theoretical analysis demonstrates that the observer-based output feedback closed-loop system achieves practical predefined-time stability (PPTS): all signals are bounded, and the tracking error converges to a neighborhood of zero within the desired settling time \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${T_C}$$\end{document}, with the observation error suppressed to a negligible bounded region simultaneously. Finally, a simulation example verifies the effectiveness of the proposed scheme.

Introduction

In recent years, the control design and analysis of non-strict feedback nonlinear systems (NSFNS) have become a research hotspot in the field of control1–5. To address the design challenges posed by the non-strict feedback structure, methods such as variable separation6–8, function transformation9,10, finite-time11–17, and fixed-time18–22 have been proposed successively. Among these methods, the variable separation and function transformation methods achieve asymptotic stability, implying that the theoretical convergence time tends to infinity. In contrast, finite-time and fixed-time methods exhibit faster convergence speeds and have received widespread attention in recent years. Notably, the settling time bounds of the latter two methods cannot be arbitrarily set. However, many engineering applications, such as spacecraft orbital injection, require the system to reach a desired orbit within a predefined time, which has motivated research on predefined-time control.

To overcome these limitations and achieve controllability of the convergence time, predefined-time control was introduced in23,24; this strategy allows designers to pre-specify the settling time bound. Based on the definitions in23] and [24, predefined time control methods for several classes of nonlinear systems were constructed in25,26. Notably, the control schemes in the aforementioned studies require the establishment of precise mathematical models. However, the actual control systems are often unknown. Therefore, the issue of adaptive intelligent predefined-time control based on FLSs/NNs has drawn significant attention from scholars. Specifically, for unknown strict-feedback nonlinear systems (SFNS), adaptive fuzzy predefined-time control was studied in27,28. Non-strict feedback nonlinear systems (NSFNS) refer to a more general class of nonlinear systems. Furthermore29, investigates an adaptive fuzzy predefined-time tracking control for unknown non-strict-feedback systems to ensure zero tracking error within a predefined time. Notably, the issue of signal quantization was not addressed in the aforementioned predefined-time simulation results23–26.

Owing to the limitations of communication bandwidth and data transmission rates, control signals in the system may not be transmitted in real time. Therefore, many scholars have considered introducing quantizers in practical industrial applications to quantize continuous signals into discrete signals before transmission30. However, the presence of quantization errors makes it difficult for traditional control schemes to ensure optimal system performance. Therefore, adaptive quantization control has attracted widespread attention, as shown in31–33. Notably, the works presented in31–33 addressed only input-channel quantization, which means that the control process still requires continuous states/outputs. For practical remote-control systems, where control input and sensor information are transmitted over a network, the limited bandwidth necessitates that both the control input and state/output signals be quantized prior to transmission. To better satisfy the requirements of engineering practice, adaptive feedback controllers were developed in34] and [35 under different input/output quantization conditions. Notably, the adaptive feedback controllers in34,35 require bounded parameter assumptions, and nonlinear functions must satisfy global Lipschitz continuity. To overcome this limitation36, proposed a novel adaptive fuzzy inverse optimal control method. Furthermore, these works34–36 only guarantee asymptotic stability for SFNSs. However, in practical applications, non-strict-feedback nonlinear systems are more common than SFNSs. Moreover, achieving predefined time stability is desirable.

Although predefined-time control and output quantization for non-strict feedback nonlinear systems(NSFNS)have been investigated individually, designing an adaptive strategy that simultaneously addresses input and output quantization for unknown NSFNS remains a challenging open problem30. The primary obstacles are twofold. First, standard fuzzy observers often depend on partial system knowledge to construct rule bases; if the system dynamics are not fully encapsulated during training, estimation performance deteriorates. Thus, developing an observer completely independent of the controlled plant is essential. Second, regarding quantized outputs, existing command filters only resolve non-differentiability. Addressing the inherent no smoothness typically requires auxiliary smoothing functions, which inadvertently exacerbates algorithmic complexity30. Consequently, a unified framework capable of simultaneously handling both the non-differentiability and non-smoothness of quantized signals is required to optimize control performance without inducing a “complexity explosion”. The main contributions of this study are as follows:

(1) A novel unified differentiator scheme is proposed to concurrently resolve the non-smoothness and nondifferent ability of quantized output signals. Unlike existing methods that rely on separate filters and smoothing functions30,31,37, this innovative design eliminates the need for auxiliary smoothing, thereby significantly reducing computational complexity and simplifying stability analysis.

(2) A model-independent inverse hyperbolic sine state observer is developed to estimate unmeasurable states. This observer structure is completely independent of the precise mathematical model of the controlled object, offering superior adaptability for unknown systems compared to traditional fuzzy observation schemes32,33.

(3) Practical Predefined-Time Stability (PPTS)is rigorously established for a general class of unknown NSFNS subject to input-output quantization. The proposed algorithm guarantees that tracking errors converge to a small neighborhood of the origin within a user-defined time, achieving a simpler structure and faster convergence compared to recent works30,38,39.

Conclusion

This study addresses the adaptive predefined-time control problem for non-strict feedback nonlinear systems with input and output quantization. A new observer that uses an inverse hyperbolic sine function is developed to estimate the unknown states. During the recursive control design, a novel differentiator technique was used to avoid calculating the partial derivatives of the virtual control signals. The proposed control approach ensures that the system achieves practical predefined time stability. The effectiveness of this method was verified through a case study. Future research may explore parameter adaptivity in predefined-time control, control input saturation issues caused by actuator physical limitations, and time delay problems resulting from system inertia, to further enhance the engineering application feasibility of the proposed algorithm.

Clinical study on the safety and efficacy of intense pulsed light in the treatment of dry eyes after full femtosecond small incision lenticule extraction
Authors: Meihua Lai, Junmu Zhong, Zhihuan Lu, Hairong Zhao, Kaiping Zhang, Yilong Lin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Dry eye syndrome is a common postoperative complication following small incision lenticule extraction (SMILE) surgery that significantly impacts patients’ quality of life. Intense pulsed light (IPL) therapy is widely used for managing dry eye syndrome, but its efficacy and safety warrant further evaluation. This study first analyzed baseline characteristics of patients with dry eye syndrome after SMILE surgery, comparing differences between the conventional medication group and the IPL treatment group. Multivariable linear regression was then used to assess the effects of IPL treatment and baseline factors on dry eye indicators 6 months posttreatment. To minimize selection bias, inverse probability of treatment weighting was applied. Furthermore, multivariable logistic regression evaluated the impact of IPL on adverse event occurrence, while generalized estimating equations assessed IPL efficacy at multiple time points. Multivariable linear regression revealed that diabetes and prolonged screen time were associated with increased ocular surface disease index (OSDI) scores, while IPL treatment significantly reduced OSDI scores (P < .05). Diabetes correlated with decreased tear breakup time and Schirmer test results, whereas IPL improved these measures. For eye dryness scores and meibomian gland scores, diabetes showed a positive association, but IPL treatment significantly decreased both. After inverse probability of treatment weighting adjustment, the effects of screen time and diabetes on OSDI and Schirmer test values lost statistical significance. Multivariable logistic regression demonstrated that diabetes increased the risk of adverse events by 14.2%, while IPL treatment reduced this risk to 7%. Generalized estimating equations analysis indicated that IPL efficacy improved significantly over time, with marked long-term benefits. IPL therapy offers superior efficacy and safety compared to conventional medication in treating dry eye syndrome after SMILE surgery, particularly in the long term. Diabetes remains a key factor affecting treatment outcomes and adverse event rates in this population.

1. Introduction

Dry eye disease is a prevalent ocular condition characterized by symptoms including dryness, foreign body sensation, blurred vision, and eye fatigue, all of which can substantially impair patients’ quality of life.[1,2] SMILE (small incision lenticule extraction) is a laser vision correction technique used to treat myopia and astigmatism.[3,4] Unlike traditional laser assisted stromal in situ keratomileusis, SMILE does not require creating a corneal flap; instead, it employs a femtosecond laser to cut a lenticule directly within the cornea through a small incision, which is subsequently removed.[5] This approach offers benefits such as less tissue trauma, faster recovery, and a lower incidence of postoperative dry eye. Patients generally experience rapid visual recovery following SMILE surgery. Nevertheless, postoperative dry eye remains a common and troubling complication.[6,7] Studies have demonstrated that dry eye not only impairs visual function but can also contribute to anxiety, depression, and social dysfunction, thereby adversely affecting patients’ daily lives and mental well-being.[8]

Conventional medication treatment is a widely used approach for managing dry eye. This treatment mainly includes artificial tears, anti-inflammatory drugs (such as steroid eye drops), and other supportive agents like ocular surface protectants and repair compounds.[9] Artificial tears, the most commonly used option, help relieve dry eye symptoms by providing lubrication and moisture to reduce discomfort.[10] However, their effects are often temporary, and some patients may experience irritation due to allergies or overuse. Steroid eye drops are effective in reducing inflammation, but long-term use carries risks such as elevated intraocular pressure and cataract formation. While conventional medications can alleviate dry eye symptoms to some extent, their efficacy and tolerability vary among individuals. Furthermore, these traditional treatments often do not address the underlying causes of dry eye, particularly in postoperative patients. Therefore, the search for more effective and safer treatment options remains a key focus in clinical research.

Intense pulsed light (IPL) therapy has emerged as a new management method for dry eye syndrome, gaining attention in recent years.[11,12] IPL effectively improves the lipid layer of the ocular surface, promotes blood circulation in the eye, and alleviates inflammatory responses by emitting light beams of specific wavelengths. Existing clinical studies have demonstrated good efficacy of IPL in alleviating dry eye symptoms[13]; however, systematic evaluations of its long-term efficacy and safety remain insufficient. Data on its application in postoperative SMILE patients is particularly scarce, warranting further exploration.

The aim of this study is to compare the efficacy of conventional medication treatment with IPL therapy in patients suffering from dry eye syndrome following SMILE surgery, evaluating the differences in treatment outcomes at multiple time points. Additionally, we employ multivariable linear regression and inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) to investigate how treatment methods and baseline factors—such as diabetes and screen time—affect therapeutic results. We also examine the influence of treatment approaches and patient baseline characteristics on the incidence of adverse events. Through this research, we strive to provide more personalized and effective treatment options for patients with postoperative dry eye and to offer robust scientific evidence to guide clinical decision-making.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Patient selection

This study is a retrospective cohort study that collected and analyzed data from patients with dry eye syndrome who underwent SMILE at our hospital from September 2022 to September 2023. Inclusion criteria were: patients who met the indications for SMILE surgery and had undergone the procedure; willingness to participate in the clinical study with good compliance; age between 18 and 40 years; stable refractive power; preoperative spherical refractive power between −3.0 and −10.0 D, with astigmatism of ≤2.0 D; development of dry eye syndrome after surgery in the absence of symptoms preoperatively. Exclusion criteria were: use of any medication that might affect ophthalmic examination within the day prior; undergoing any optical surgery or treatment within 6 months prior to the first ophthalmic examination; receiving temporary or permanent punctal occlusion within 6 months prior to the first ophthalmic examination; presence of other ocular diseases.

2.2. Treatment protocol

In this study, the control group received conventional medication treatment, including the use of 0.1% fluorometholone eye drops and hydroxypropyl methylcellulose eye drops for routine eye care and treatment. Fluorometholone eye drops, a corticosteroid, effectively suppress inflammatory responses in the eye and alleviate symptoms of ocular surface inflammation, while hydroxypropyl methylcellulose eye drops act as artificial tears, providing lubrication and increasing the stability of the tear film to relieve discomfort in dry eye patients. The combined use of these 2 medications aims to alleviate common symptoms of dry eye, including dryness, burning, and foreign body sensation, while improving patients’ quality of life.

Patients in the treatment group received IPL therapy every 2 weeks, focusing on the area around the lower eyelid. IPL therapy utilized standard multiwavelength pulsed light, with a wavelength range of 500 to 1200 nm. Treatment parameters were individualized based on the patient’s skin type and severity of symptoms. Both groups underwent a treatment course of 6 months, during which the study conducted regular follow-up and assessment of treatment outcomes to compare the efficacy of conventional medication treatment alone with that of medication combined with IPL therapy for improving dry eye symptoms. Through this 6-month treatment course, we aimed to evaluate the long-term efficacy of IPL therapy in patients with dry eye syndrome after SMILE surgery and provide scientific evidence for the clinical application of this therapy.

Adverse events were categorized into 8 types: skin burns or pigmentation, ocular discomfort or pain, transient blurred vision, phototoxic reactions, eyelid swelling or erythema, meibomian gland dysfunction, corneal or conjunctival damage, and exacerbation of dry eye symptoms. The occurrence of any one of these 8 conditions was considered an adverse event.

2.3. Statistical analysis

Multivariable linear regression models were used to analyze the effects of IPL therapy and baseline patient information on dry eye indicators at 6 months posttreatment, with primary independent variables including diabetes history, screen time, rheumatoid arthritis history, and others. To reduce selection bias, propensity scores were used to adjust for baseline differences between treatment groups. Propensity scores were calculated using logistic regression models based on variables such as age, sex, and diabetes history, determining each patient’s probability of receiving IPL therapy, which was then adjusted using inverse probability weighting. The analysis assessed the impact of IPL therapy and baseline information on the incidence of adverse events (e.g., ocular infections, pain, and visual abnormalities), with results expressed as odds ratios and their 95% confidence intervals. A generalized estimating equation (GEE) model was used to evaluate the efficacy of IPL therapy at different time points (T1: 1 month, T2: 3 months, and T3: 6 months), specifically analyzing dry eye indicators such as ocular surface disease index (OSDI), tear breakup time (TBUT), Schirmer, eye dryness scores (EDS), and MGS.[14]

3. Results

3.1. Baseline differences between the 2 treatment modalities

We first analyzed the baseline information of patients with dry eye syndrome after SMILE surgery and compared the baseline differences between conventional medication treatment and IPL therapy. We found that the proportion of patients with diabetes (P = .0093) and the proportion of those with high screen time (P = 3.3E‐05) were significantly higher in the IPL therapy group. Dry eye-related indicators, such as OSDI scores (P = .0247), EDS (P = .0342), and MGS (P = .0117), were also higher in the IPL therapy group. In contrast, TBUT (P = .0111), Schirmer test results (P = .0091), corneal sensitivity (P = .0129), and visual acuity (P = .0040) were lower in the IPL therapy group. Additionally, the proportion of evaporative dry eye was higher in the IPL group (P = .0033). Other variables, such as age, sex, and rheumatoid arthritis, did not show statistically significant differences (Table 1).

3.2. Multivariable linear regression analysis of factors affecting dry eye recovery

In the multivariable linear regression analysis of the effects of treatment methods and other baseline information on dry eye indicators at 6 months posttreatment, results indicated that diabetes significantly increased OSDI scores (B = 1.948, P = .018), whereas IPL therapy significantly decreased OSDI scores (B = ‐2.829, P = .000). Screen time was also positively correlated with OSDI scores (B = 2.053, P = .005). Furthermore, diabetes significantly decreased TBUT (B = ‐3.283, P = .001), while IPL therapy significantly increased TBUT (B = 4.099, P = .000). In the Schirmer test, diabetes (B = ‐3.497, P = .001) and rheumatoid arthritis (B = ‐1.031, P = .004) significantly decreased Schirmer values, while IPL therapy significantly increased Schirmer values (B = 4.048, P = .000). For EDS and MGS, diabetes was positively correlated with both indicators (EDS: B = 1.466, P = .004; MGS: B = 2.942, P = .032), whereas IPL therapy significantly decreased EDS (B = -2.725, P = .002) and MGS (B = ‐2.725, P = .019). After applying IPTW, the effect of screen time on OSDI scores was no longer significant (B = 0.677, P = .184), and the effect of diabetes on Schirmer test results was also no longer significant (B = ‐0.432, P = .101). Other results were consistent with the trends observed before weighting (Table 2). These results indicate that baseline differences have a certain confounding effect on some variables, but the effect of IPL therapy on improving various dry eye indicators remained significant and robust before and after weighting, suggesting its efficacy for dry eye patients is fairly definitive. At the same time, the influence of diabetes on some dry eye indicators persisted after adjustment, indicating that diabetes is an important factor affecting the treatment outcomes for dry eye syndrome.

3.4. Efficacy of IPL treatment compared to conventional medication treatment at different time points

In evaluating the efficacy of IPL therapy at different time points using generalized estimating equations, results showed that compared to conventional medication treatment, IPL therapy significantly improved dry eye symptoms across multiple indicators. The OSDI score in the IPL therapy group had a B value of −0.810 and a P value of .032, indicating better efficacy than the control group; the positive correlations in TBUT and Schirmer tests were also statistically significant, with B values of 0.536 (P = .008) and 1.138 (P = .007), respectively. Over time, the efficacy became more pronounced, with the most significant improvement in OSDI observed at T3 (B value of −2.133, P = .007). The improvements in TBUT and Schirmer tests were also most notable at T3, with B values of 2.766 (P = .002) and 3.335 (P = .005), respectively. For EDS, the B value was −2.861 (P = .045) at T2 and −2.771 (P = .034) at T3; the MGS at T3 had a B value of −3.884 (P = .002). These results indicate that compared to conventional medication treatment, IPL therapy shows superior long-term efficacy for dry eye syndrome. The interaction effects of group and time further demonstrated that the efficacy of IPL therapy at T3 was significantly enhanced compared to T1 and T2, with OSDI having a B value of −4.460 (P = .000), TBUT a B value of 5.589 (P = .000), and Schirmer a B value of 6.618 (P = .000). Additionally, the EDS and MGS at T3 had B values of −6.044 (P = .000) and −5.739 (P = .000), respectively, indicating that as time progressed, the improvement in all dry eye indicators with IPL therapy became increasingly significant (Table 4).

4. Discussion

This study systematically evaluated the efficacy and safety of conventional medication treatment versus IPL therapy using multivariable linear regression, GEE, and multivariable logistic regression analyses. The findings demonstrated that IPL therapy significantly improved multiple dry eye-related indicators compared to conventional treatment, with its effectiveness increasing over time. Additionally, diabetes and rheumatoid arthritis were identified as negative factors influencing recovery from dry eye syndrome following SMILE surgery, with diabetes also associated with a higher risk of posttreatment adverse events.

Diabetes was found to significantly increase OSDI scores (B = 1.948, P = .018) and decrease both TBUT (B = ‐3.283, P = .001) and Schirmer test values (B = ‐3.497, P = .001). These results suggest that patients with diabetes experience more severe dry eye symptoms compared to nondiabetic individuals, with greater impairment in tear film stability and tear production.[15] The mechanisms through which diabetes impacts the eyes may be related to neuropathy, impaired lacrimal gland function, and systemic microvascular complications.[16–18] These factors could exacerbate the severity of dry eye syndrome and diminish the treatment response in diabetic patients. Although the impact of diabetes on certain indicators, such as Schirmer test values, became nonsignificant after IPTW adjustment (B = ‐0.432, P = .101), its influence on other key parameters—including OSDI scores, TBUT, EDS, and MGS—remained statistically significant. This suggests that even after controlling for baseline differences, diabetes remains a critical factor influencing the severity of dry eye syndrome and treatment outcomes. These findings underscore the importance of considering diabetes as a key determinant in the management of dry eye syndrome, particularly in postoperative patients.[19]

Rheumatoid arthritis significantly lowered Schirmer test values (B = ‐1.031, P = .004), which aligns with the known association between rheumatoid arthritis and dry eye syndrome.[20] Patients with rheumatoid arthritis often experience ocular surface inflammation and decreased tear secretion, likely related to chronic immune-mediated inflammation.[21] Even after weighting, the effect of rheumatoid arthritis on Schirmer values remained significant (B = ‐1.203, P = .005), indicating its important role in tear production impairment.

Compared to conventional medication treatment, IPL therapy demonstrated significantly greater efficacy across multiple clinical indicators, with its therapeutic effects intensifying over time. This suggests that the cumulative nature of IPL treatment plays a pivotal role in its enhanced effectiveness. With each successive session, IPL gradually improves meibomian gland function, reduces ocular surface inflammation, and promotes tear production and secretion. As treatment duration increases, these benefits become more pronounced, leading to greater relief of dry eye symptoms. IPL utilizes a broad spectrum of light wavelengths that penetrate the skin and target the meibomian glands, thereby enhancing their function and improving tear film stability. Since meibomian gland dysfunction is a major contributor to dry eye syndrome, the continuous use of IPL therapy may help restore or improve glandular function over time. As a result, prolonged treatment leads to more substantial improvement in the underlying pathophysiology of dry eye, translating into more robust clinical outcomes. Furthermore, because dry eye syndrome is often associated with chronic inflammation, IPL not only enhances tear film quality but also exerts notable anti-inflammatory effects, further supporting its long-term therapeutic benefits.[22] Over time, multiple IPL treatments may have a sustained inhibitory effect on ocular surface inflammation, gradually alleviating chronic inflammation and making the relief of dry eye symptoms more apparent.[23] This sustained anti-inflammatory effect may be one of the key reasons behind the gradual enhancement of IPL efficacy over time. Additionally, patients often require an adaptation period to become accustomed to IPL therapy. As treatment progresses, patient compliance and acceptance of the therapy tend to improve, which may increase the eyes’ sensitivity and responsiveness to IPL. This, in turn, contributes to progressively better therapeutic outcomes and further amplifies the overall effectiveness of the treatment.

This study employed multivariable linear regression, GEE, and multivariable logistic regression to comprehensively evaluate the effects of different treatment methods, baseline characteristics, and time on dry eye syndrome following SMILE surgery, thereby providing robust and reliable conclusions. The strengths of this study include its assessment of both short-term and long-term efficacy of IPL therapy—particularly the notable improvement observed over time—as well as valuable safety insights derived from the analysis of adverse events. However, several limitations should be noted. The relatively small sample size and single-center design may limit the generalizability of the findings. Moreover, the 6-month follow-up period may not be sufficient to fully capture the long-term efficacy and safety of the treatments. Future studies should aim to include larger, multicenter cohorts and extend the follow-up duration to further validate and strengthen these conclusions.

5. Conclusion

This study demonstrates that IPL therapy significantly outperforms conventional medication treatment in improving multiple indicators of dry eye syndrome following SMILE surgery, with its efficacy increasing over time. Although diabetic patients are at a higher risk for adverse events and exhibit poorer recovery outcomes, IPL therapy remains a generally safe treatment option, showing a lower incidence of adverse events compared to conventional medication.

Dual-self-learning co-evolutionary algorithm for energy-efficient flexible job shop scheduling problem with processing- transportation composite robots
Authors: Meizhou Zhang, Min Zhou, Liping Zhang, Zikai Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The processing-transportation composite robots, with their dual functions of processing and transportation, as well as comprehensive robot-machine interactions, have been widely and efficiently applied in the manufacturing industry, leading to a continuous increase in energy consumption. Hence, this work focuses on investigating robot-machine integrated energy-efficient scheduling in flexible job shop environments. To address the new problem, an innovative mixed-integer linear programming model and a novel dual-self-learning co-evolutionary algorithm are proposed, aimed at minimizing the total energy consumption and makespan. In the proposed algorithm, a three-dimensional vector is first used to comprehensively express the solution, and then a greedy decoding strategy is designed to reduce the idle time and energy consumption simultaneously. A hybrid initialization method with adaptive random selection and chaos mapping is developed to ensure the diversity and high quality of the initial solutions. A dual-self-learning mechanism, including a self-learning evolutionary mechanism and a self-learning cooperation mechanism, is designed to select suitable evolutionary operators and enhance interactions between populations, respectively. Finally, multiple sets of experiments are conducted to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed mathematical model, improved components and algorithm through numerical, statistical, and differential analyses.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-11890-2.

Introduction

With the onset of an aging population and the gradual decline of the demographic dividend, traditional manufacturing industries that rely on resource and labor advantages are facing increasing pressure1. Therefore, the transformation and upgrading of the manufacturing industry, represented by industrial robots, will inevitably become the trend for the long-term development of manufacturing enterprises2. The existing literature on flexible job shop scheduling problem (FJSP) with robots mostly separates the processing and transportation functions of robots for independent study. They have only considered the processing functions of robots in specific tasks, such as welding, polishing, and deburring, or the transportation functions of robots between equipment, such as material loading, transport, and unloading3–5. However, in the face of rapidly changing market demands and a complex, competitive environment, the enterprises often face problems such as insufficient production flexibility and rigid production line layouts2.

As robotics technology advances rapidly, a new production mode considering processing-transportation composite robots (PTCRs), which integrate processing and transportation functions with flexibility, convenience, and efficiency has emerged, significantly increasing flexibility and reducing layout constraints on production lines. PTCRs known for their flexibility in processing tasks and expansive workspaces, are extensively utilized in discrete manufacturing sectors like electronics and medical devices6. It represents a classic FJSP with PTCRs. Current research primarily concentrates on the transportation role of mobile robots7,8, with limited studies on the mobile processing-transportation functionality.

According to the “World Energy Outlook 2021” from 2020 to 2030, global final energy consumption is expected to grow by an average of 1.7% annually, with over 33% of energy consumption coming from manufacturing industries, and at least 26% of global CO2 emissions originating from manufacturing enterprises9–11. Based on this practical consideration, numerous studies have demonstrated that FJSP is an effective method for reducing energy consumption12. Therefore, in the energy-efficient flexible job shop scheduling problem with PTCRs (EFJSP-PTCRs) addressed in this paper, an innovative mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model is developed to simultaneously minimize both total energy consumption (TEC) and makespan.

Since the EFJSP-PTCRs is an NP-Hard problem involving multiple decisions, constraints, and objectives, finding an optimal solution becomes infeasible within a limited time as the number of jobs increases. The co-evolutionary algorithm (CEA) is proven to be the optimal choice for solving multi-objective problems with conflicting constraints related to scheduling or balancing13,14. The algorithm can achieve simultaneous evolution between the corresponding sub-problems by independently and cooperatively evolving multiple sub-populations, thereby obtaining an approximate optimal solution to the problem. Based on the unique features of the EFJSP-PTCRs, new complexities are added, such as determining transportation routes and selecting processing equipment (machines or PTCRs). Hence, a novel dual-self-learning co-evolutionary algorithm (DSLCEA) is designed to solve the EFJSP-PTCRs. Literature review

The EFJSP-PTCRs have garnered widespread attention from researchers, with studies primarily focusing on three aspects: energy-efficient FJSP (Section “The energy-efficient FJSP”), robot-considered (Section “The FJSP considering robots”), and co-evolutionary algorithm (Section “Co-evolutionary algorithm”).

The energy-efficient FJSP

Several researchers have studied the FJSP with time-related objectives15–17. However, with the continuous expansion of industrialization, nearly one-third of energy consumption and greenhouse gas emissions are attributed to manufacturing enterprises, drawing increasing attention to FJSP with energy-related objectives.

Zhang, et al.18 proposed a heuristic algorithm to solve the FJSP with the aim of minimizing energy consumption cost and weighted tardiness. Sun, et al.19 integrated Pareto dominance with indicator-based thinking and employed three existing crossover operators to propose an improved NSGA-III co-evolutionary algorithm, aiming to simultaneously minimize makespan, total carbon emissions, and machine load in FJSP. Caldeira et al.20 presented an improved backtracking search algorithm that addresses FJSP while considering energy consumption. Li et al.21 considered total energy utilization and makespan, proposing an energy-saving strategy to reduce overall energy consumption. Ebrahimi et al.22 designed a hybrid algorithm combining ant colony optimization and simulated annealing to simultaneously minimize equipment energy cost and job tardiness penalties. Geng et al.23 proposed a reinforcement learning-based hybrid memetic algorithm to address energy consumption in the two-stage FJSP. Luo et al.24 developed a knowledge-driven two-stage memetic algorithm to minimize processing time and energy consumption under machine failure scenarios. Li and Chen25 validated the effectiveness of a multi-objective sparrow search algorithm in minimizing makespan and energy consumption in FJSP through numerical experiments.

The aforementioned studies provide valuable guidance for enterprises in terms of energy conservation and consumption reduction. However, they primarily focus on the scheduling of production operations while neglecting transportation activities between machines, assuming that transportation time is negligible compared to processing time. Nevertheless, in the context of small-batch and multi-variety production modes, transportation operations have become increasingly significant. Moreover, there is a strong interaction between production and transportation tasks, meaning that production operations can influence transportation time, and transportation delays may in turn lead to varying machine waiting times.

The FJSP considering robots

Industrial robots are extensively used in production systems because of their large workspace, high flexibility, multiple degrees of freedom, and high quality-price ratio26,27. Although numerous studies have focused on the application of mobile robots in shop scheduling, most of the existing research has concentrated solely on their transportation function. For instance, Sanogo et al.28 adopted a multi-agent system simulation approach to study the collision avoidance problem between transporters in flexible manufacturing systems and successfully avoided algorithm deadlocks. Ren and Liu29 proposed a dueling double deep Q-network to address the FJSP automated guided vehicles (AGVs) under transportation constraints. Zhang et al.30,31 proposed a multi-agent reinforcement learning-based method to solve the production scheduling problem with flexible processing and transportation constraints. Shakeri et al.32 explored the FJSP of material transportation using robots in workshops, improving the real-time and comprehensive scheduling of industry 4.0 systems. Ham7 developed a distinctive constraint encoding technique to solve the job shop scheduling problem (JSP) for material handling robots. Liu et al.33 proposed an improved genetic algorithm to address the integrated process planning and scheduling problem with AGV transportation, and the effectiveness of the integrated encoding method was demonstrated through numerical experiments with varying AGV quantities. Guo et al.34 developed a multi-factor scheduling system based on digital twin theory, enabling the integrated scheduling of machines and AGVs, while optimizing makespan and energy consumption reduction. Xu et al.35 considered the move distance and critical waiting time of the robot in the FJSP.

However, with the advanced algorithms and sensor technologies progress, PTCRs can autonomously navigate both indoor and outdoor environments, perceiving their surroundings, localizing themselves, and planning paths36. Currently, simultaneous localization and mapping technologies using lidar or visual cameras enable robots to autonomously navigate and achieve centimeter-level positioning accuracy37. Additionally, end-effectors equipped with optical measurement systems can achieve processing accuracy within 63μm, which is fully suitable for surface treatment processes that require relatively low machining precision38,39. At present, there is limited research on the scheduling problem in the FJSP scenario when PTCRs perform both processing and transportation tasks at the same time. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the FJSP in scenarios where PTCRs simultaneously perform both machining and transportation tasks.

Co-evolutionary algorithm

The CEA, as a novel class of evolutionary algorithms, is based on the core idea of decomposing a complex problem into subproblems, each handled by a distinct population. These subpopulations evolve independently while simultaneously sharing high-quality decision information through cooperative mechanisms, thereby enhancing the overall problem-solving efficiency40. In recent years, CEA has demonstrated superior performance in solving various scheduling problems. For example, Pan et al.41 proposed a novel cooperative mechanism and a restart strategy to solve the distributed flowshop scheduling problem. Han et al.42 developed a cooperative iterative greedy algorithm to effectively guide the evolution of two populations in the distributed permutation flowshop scheduling problem. Sun et al.43 designed a hybrid cooperative co-evolutionary algorithm for solving the fuzzy FJSP. Cuiyu et al.44 introduced a multi-population cooperative genetic algorithm based on co-evolution for FJSP. Liu et al.45 presented a multi-population CEA for green integrated process planning and scheduling considering logistics systems.

Although numerous customized CEAs have been proposed in the literature to solve various scheduling problems, the FJSP-PTCRs problem involves a large number of decision variables and strong coupling among them. Under such complexity, a single-population cooperative strategy often suffers from slow convergence and a tendency to fall into local optima. In contrast, self-learning optimization mechanisms have been proven to be an effective solution to these challenges. Tan et al.46 designed a matheuristic-based self-learning evolutionary algorithm to address the lot streaming hybrid flow shop group scheduling with limited auxiliary module constraints. And a double layer self-learning evolution is developed to collaborate operators which include six knowledge-based local search operators and six global crossover operators. Zhao et al.47 adopted a self-learning discrete Jaya algorithm for solving the distributed flowshop scheduling problem, in which the selection of operators was guided by their historical success rates, enabling dynamic adaptation during the evolutionary process. Zhang et al.48 developed a self-learning selection strategy for local search operators and applied the MOEA/D algorithm to solve the distributed heterogeneous assembly flowshop scheduling problem. Hao and Zhu49 proposed a two-level cooperative self-learning particle swarm optimization algorithm to address the distributed assembly reentrant flowshop scheduling problem. The algorithm employs an adaptive learning mechanism to dynamically guide job selection, aiming to maximize the total value.

The above studies indicate that self-learning strategies are effective in managing the interaction among different populations and in selecting appropriate evolutionary operators. However, research on the application of such strategies to the FJSP-PTCRs problem remains limited. Therefore, this paper proposes a CEA with dual self-learning strategies to enhance the efficiency and performance in solving the FJSP-PTCRs problem.

Problem description

The first and last operations cannot be processed on PTCRs, and for operations processed on PTCRs, the adjacent upstream and downstream operations must be processed on machines. If a certain operation is processed on an PTCR, the PTCR stays in place to process the job after picking up the materials. Each job is composed of multiple operations. The PTCRs pick up the jobs from the loading station (LA), and after the jobs complete all processing tasks according to the process route on either machines or PTCRs, the PTCRs deliver them to the unloading station (UA). The processing times of operations on different machines are known and vary, while the transportation times of PTCRs are determined by the distance, loading, and no-loading times. All machines and PTCRs cannot be interrupted once they start, until all assigned tasks are completed. The objective of the EFJSP-PTCRs is to simultaneously minimize the TEC and makespan.

Processing-transportation constraints of PTCRs

The processing-transportation constraints of PTCRs consist of two parts. The first part is the constraint of the PTCR’s no-load processing-transportation between different types of operations, which is represented by Eqs. (10)–(22). The second part is the constraint of the PTCR’s load processing-transportation between adjacent operations of the same job, which is represented by Eqs. (23)–(28).

The proposed DSLCEA

Framework of DSLCEA

Due to the EFJSP-PTCRs is a multi-objective optimization problem involving multiple sub-problems, the multi-objective CEA is a better option to solve this type of problems. The algorithm establishes multiple initial sub-populations with the objective of optimizing the sub-problems, and then stores the non-dominated solutions formed by the sub-populations into an elite archive set (EAS). During evolution, sub-populations are updated with specific evolutionary operators and collaborative strategies. However, computational resources are precious, and an equi-probability population collaboration strategy can cause the algorithm to converge too slowly for the EFJSP-PTCRs problem.

Thus, this paper proposes a novel DSLCEA that incorporates a dual-self-learning mechanism, the framework of DSLCEA is illustrated in Algorithm 1. Firstly, a hybrid initialization method with adaptive random selection and chaos mapping is developed to initialize the populations and EAS. Secondly, an indicator-based self-learning strategy is developed to guide the selection of collaboration methods between different populations. Thirdly, multiple problem-specific sub-population evolutionary operators are developed, and a self-learning approach is employed to guide the selection of these operators. Finally, a population restart mechanism is implemented to avoid the algorithm getting trapped in local optima. It is worth noting that the self-learning strategy is utilized twice in the algorithm, each serving a different purpose. The first usage is to select the collaboration method between different populations in each iteration, while the second is to choose a more effective evolutionary operator for generating new solutions. In the following sections, we will introduce the components of each stage step by step.

Greedy decoding strategy with idle time and energy reduction

To convert the solution representation in 4.2 into a feasible scheduling scheme, this paper designs a greedy decoding strategy with idle time and energy reduction. This decoding method consists of three key stages. First, the relative positional relationship between the allocated PTCR and the selected machine is used to determine the transportation route of the PTCR. Then, based on the transportation path of the PTCR, its transportation completion time is calculated. Third, the greedy principle is used to determine the available processing idle time on the machine, and thus the earliest processing start time of the operation is calculated. The details are described below.

Chaos mapping mechanism for operations

In many algorithms, populations are usually initialized using random methods. However, this strategy often results in uneven distribution of individuals and large differences between them, which hinders global optimization and negatively affects the solution quality due to the lack of prior information50. To obtain better initial solutions, chaos mapping is employed for its inherent randomness and sensitivity, which help ensure population diversity and a more even distribution.

Tent chaos mapping, due to its simple structure, is widely used for its better uniformity in traversal and faster search speeds compared to other chaos mappings. Evolution of CAP

Three operators aim to optimize the solution space by replacing or shifting operations based on the PTCR’s workload and invalid time, thereby reducing the overall TEC and makespan. The procedures are detailed below.

Swap of critical path PTCR (SCPR): The critical path of the PTCR is determined by the latest completion time of its operation. Then, the PTCR with the longest completion time is randomly replaced with another PTCR, as shown in Fig. 6.

Reset the PTCR of the preceding operation (RMPO): When invalid time occurs due to the PTCR not being allocated a task for an extended period, the PTCR assigned to the previous operation will be replaced by the current PTCR, as shown in Fig. 7.

Reset the PTCR of adjacent operations (RMAO): When the PTCR is not positioned at the material pickup location, the current operation is moved to follow its preceding operation, and the PTCR performing the preceding operation is reassigned to carry out the current task, as shown in Fig. 8.

Evolution of CSP

To improve CSP, three operators are developed, focusing on critical path optimization and reducing machine idle time. The specific procedures are introduced below.

Swap of critical path operation (SCPO): The critical path of an operation is determined by the latest completion time of the job’s operations. Swap the operation with the longest completion time of one job with the operation of another job, as shown in Fig. 9.

Move the adjacent operation backward (MAOB): When idle time occurs due to the PTCR arriving before the previous operation is completed, the processing order of the operation is delayed by one step, as shown in Fig. 10.

Move operations to enable continuous process (MOCP): Move two adjacent operations, which are processed on the same machine, to be executed consecutively, as shown in Fig. 11.

Self-learning cooperation mechanism of dual-population

To overcome this limitation, this paper proposes a self-learning cooperation mechanism that enables the two populations and the EAS to share and interact with each other, exchanging superior information. This mechanism consists of three collaboration methods and uses an indicator-based self-learning selection criterion for integrated collaboration. The specific details are provided below.

The self-learning cooperation mechanism of the dual-population can be summarized in three main steps. First, a real number between 0 and 1 is randomly generated, and the collaboration method is chosen using a roulette-based approach. Second, evolution and update operations are performed on the evolutionary and collaborative populations according to the selected collaboration method. Third, the selection probability values of all collaboration methods are updated using Eq. (55). Population restart mechanism

The population restart mechanism is designed to prevent the algorithm from getting stuck in local optima. When the frontier solution is not improved after a specified number of consecutive iterations, the restart mechanism generates a specified number of new individuals using the hybrid initialization method. A new population is then formed by randomly selecting collaborators from the EAS, and some individuals in the original population are randomly replaced with the new individuals. Time complexity of DSLCEA

The time complexity of DSLCEA primarily stems from three key operations: (1) The population cooperation mechanism exhibits a time complexity of O(2 × TN × DT × PS), where TN denotes the total number of algorithm iterations, DT denotes individual decoding time and PS represents population size, with the multiplicative factor 2 accounting for bidirectional information exchange between sub-populations; (2) The evolutionary operator selection process demonstrates a time complexity of O(TN × DT × PS); (3) The restart mechanism contributes a time complexity of O(RN × DT × PS), where RN indicates the number of algorithm restarts. Notably, in practical applications, the individual decoding time emerges as the dominant factor determining overall complexity.

Experimental settings

Since no benchmark data is available for the problem considered in existing literature, we generate a series of specialized instances using widely recognized benchmark datasets from FJSP to assess the performance of DSLCEA. According to the constraint conditions of this paper, we extended the instances from Bilge and Ulusoy53 and Yao et al.54 as small-scale and medium-scale instances, and randomly generated large-scale instances. The three different scales of instances are composed of 4 layouts and 15 job sets. Each job set is denoted as j × n × m × r, representing the number of jobs, operations, machines, and PTCRs, respectively. The job sets corresponding to each scale are numbered as follows: 1–5 for small-scale, 6–10 for medium-scale, and 11–15 for large-scale instances, with corresponding PTCR quantities of 3, 5, and 7. As a result, a total of 60 instances are generated. For example, the medium-scale instance MS2-8 refers to Layout 2 and Job Set 8, which consists of 9 jobs, 74 operations, 13 machines, and 5 PTCRs.

This paper uses two metrics, inverted generational distance (IGD)55 and hypervolume ratio (HVR)52, to assess the effectiveness of the Pareto front from various perspectives. When the IGD metric approaches 0 or the HVR metric nears 1, the frontier solutions are considered better. It should be noted that a pseudo-optimal Pareto set is constructed by merging the non-dominated solutions from all algorithms, as the true Pareto front is not available.

The IBM CPLEX solver is used for the MILP models, and all algorithms are implemented in MATLAB R2022b. To ensure fairness, a computer with an Intel Core i9-12900H processor (2.5 GHz) and 32 GB of memory is used to run the algorithms. Each algorithm is independently executed 10 times, with the termination condition set to n × m seconds.

Discussion

The superior convergence and distribution performance of DSLCEA can be attributed to its algorithmic design. First, the co-evolutionary framework with multiple populations enables different sub-populations to share elite genes, thereby enhancing convergence while maintaining population diversity. Second, the proposed hybrid initialization method incorporating adaptive random selection and chaos mapping generates high-quality initial populations to promote diversity preservation. Third, six problem-specific evolutionary operators are designed to improve convergence performance through comprehensive exploration of the solution space, ultimately obtaining superior Pareto-optimal solutions. Then, the dual self-learning strategy dynamically selects optimal cooperation methods and evolutionary operators during iterations, significantly boosting overall convergence performance. Finally, the population restart mechanism can prevent the algorithm from falling into local optima and improves the overall optimization performance. Therefore, the designs of the above strategies enhance the performance of DSLCEA, resulting in the superiority of DSLCEA.

Conclusions and future work

This paper investigates the dual functions of PTCRs in the scenario of FJSP. To address this complex problem effectively, an innovative MILP model and a novel DSLCEA are developed to minimize the TEC and makespan simultaneously. To improve the performance of the DSLCEA, a hybrid initialization method and a dual-self-learning mechanism, including a self-learning evolutionary mechanism and a self-learning cooperation mechanism, are designed.

Based on extensive numerical, statistical, and differential analysis, four conclusions are drawn. (1) The MILP model can search the true Pareto solutions of the small-scale instances by epsilon-constraint method. (2) The hybrid initialization method with adaptive random selection and chaos mapping can generate high-quality and well-diversity individuals for the sub-problems, ensuring fast convergence of the algorithm. (3) The dual-self-learning mechanism can guide the selection of better evolutionary operators and facilitate the sharing of high-quality information between populations, thereby improving the algorithm’s ability to explore high-quality solutions. (4) The DSLCEA proposed in this paper outperforms several well-known advanced multi-objective evolutionary algorithms for solving the EFJSP-PTCRs problem.

In intelligent manufacturing environments, PTCRs play a crucial role in both job processing and transportation between machines. This study provides decision-making support for the integrated scheduling of machines and PTCRs in manufacturing settings. Looking ahead, as PTCRs become increasingly prevalent in intelligent factories, new job combination modes are likely to emerge, such as unit jobs and the division of PTCR functions. Future research could explore scheduling strategies for these PTCR unit combinations. Additionally, considering that PTCRs represent a significant investment for companies, evaluating the optimal number of PTCRs to deploy based on specific operational conditions will be essential for maximizing both efficiency and cost-effectiveness.

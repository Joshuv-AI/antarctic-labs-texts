High Expression of NRF2 and Low Expression of KEAP1 Predict Worse Survival in Patients With Operable Triple-Negative Breast Cancer
Authors: Young Sub Lee, Jun Kang, Eun Sun Jung, Ahwon Lee
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTriple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is an aggressive type of breast cancer. Currently, no effective treatment options for this condition exist. Nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2), encoded by nuclear factor erythroid-derived 2-like 2 (NFE2L2) gene and its endogenous inhibitor, Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (KEAP1), both participate in cellular defense mechanisms against oxidative stress and contribute to chemoresistance and tumor progression in numerous types of cancers. This study aimed to evaluate the expression patterns of NRF2 and KEAP1 and their prognostic value in operable TNBC.MethodsTissue microarrays were prepared using tumor tissues collected from 203 patients with TNBC who underwent surgery. Immunohistochemical staining analyses of NRF2 and KEAP1 were performed. The expression of each immunomarker was categorized into two groups (low or high) based on the median H-score. We analyzed the association between the expression of each immunomarker and clinicopathological information to predict survival. A total of 225 TNBC samples from the Molecular Taxonomy of Breast Cancer International Consortium (METABRIC) dataset were used to validate our results.ResultsNRF2 immunoreactivity was detected in the nucleus and was associated with histologic grade and Ki-67 index, whereas KEAP1 immunoreactivity was detected in the cytoplasm and was associated with the Ki-67 index. Survival analyses showed that NRF2 and KEAP1 expressions were independent prognostic factors for overall survival (OS) (hazard ratio [HR], 2.45 and 0.30; p = 0.015 and 0.016, respectively) and disease-free survival (HR, 2.27 and 0.42; p = 0.019 and 0.022, respectively). NFE2L2 mRNA expression was an independent prognostic factor for OS (HR, 0.59; p = 0.009) in the METABRIC dataset.ConclusionHigh NRF2 and low KEAP1 expressions independently predicted poor survival in patients with operable TNBC. Further investigations are warranted to examine the possible therapeutic benefits of targeting the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway for TNBC treatment.Keywords: Immunohistochemistry, Kelch-Like ECH-Associated Protein 1, NF-E2-Related Factor 2, Survival Analysis, Triple Negative Breast Neoplasms

INTRODUCTION

Breast cancer is the most common type of malignant tumor in women, particularly in Korean women [1,2]. Breast cancer is characterized by the simultaneous loss of immunohistochemical expression of the estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), and this condition is termed triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). TNBC accounts for approximately 10%–20% of all breast cancer cases and is associated with a worse prognosis than other molecular subtypes of breast cancer. Although patients with TNBC receive numerous treatments, the prognosis remains poor owing to frequent disease recurrence and metastasis [3]. Therefore, the identification of novel markers for the prediction of patient survival and disease progression is important to ensure the proper management and treatment of TNBC.

Nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2), encoded by nuclear factor erythroid-derived 2-like 2 (NFE2L2) gene, is a transcriptional activator participating in the cellular defense mechanisms against electrophilic and oxidative stresses and promotes tumor progression by increasing glycolysis through coactivation of hypoxia-inducible factor 1α [4,5,6,7]. Under normal conditions, NRF2 is constantly degraded by its negative regulator, Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (KEAP1), through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway [8]. However, upon exposure to electrophilic or oxidative stress, the KEAP1-NRF2 complexes dissociate, leading to the accumulation of stabilized NRF2 in the nucleus and induction of expression of various cytoprotective genes [9,10].

Recent studies have suggested that the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway plays a crucial role in the development of chemoresistance and tumor progression in various types of cancers, such as colorectal cancer, non-small cell lung carcinoma, ovarian cancer, and hepatocellular carcinoma [6,11,12,13,14,15]. Several studies have suggested that high levels of NRF2 in breast cancer are associated with tumor progression and poor prognosis [5,16]. Previous studies have revealed that NRF2 activation stimulates cell growth and contributes to radioresistance in TNBC cell lines, suggesting that NRF2 is a potential therapeutic target for TNBC [17,18]. Moreover, KEAP1 suppression is frequently observed in metastatic breast cancer cell lines [19]. However, the prognostic value of KEAP1 expression in breast cancer has not yet been investigated.

In this study, we evaluated the expression of NRF2 and KEAP1 in 203 cases of operable TNBC through immunohistochemistry analysis. We assessed the relationship between NRF2 and KEAP1 expressions, patient survival, and other clinicopathological characteristics in patients with TNBC. We validated our results using publicly available data from the Molecular Taxonomy of Breast Cancer International Consortium (METABRIC) dataset.

METHODS

Patients

Specimens were obtained from 203 patients diagnosed with TNBC at Seoul St. Mary’s Hospital, The Catholic University of Korea who underwent surgical resection between 2002 and 2011. All patients underwent adequate tumor resection and received appropriate adjuvant chemotherapy based on their pathologic stage following the National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines [20]. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy was administered before tumor resection for patients with locally advanced breast cancer at initial presentation.

Clinicopathological information including age at diagnosis, use of neoadjuvant chemotherapy, type of surgery, use of adjuvant chemotherapy, chemotherapy regimen, tumor size, pathologic stage (based on the American Joint Committee on Cancer TNM system, 8th edition [21]), presence or absence of lymph node (LN) metastasis, histologic type, histologic grade (based on the Nottingham histologic score system [22]), Ki-67 index, and P53 immunostaining results were collected from medical records.

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Seoul St. Mary’s Hospital, The Catholic University of Korea (IRB No. KC19SISI0943). The requirement for informed patient consent was waived by the IRB.

Tissue microarray construction

The resected tumor tissues were fixed in 10% buffered formalin and stored in paraffin blocks in the Department of Hospital Pathology, Seoul St. Mary's Hospital, The Catholic University of Korea. Tissue blocks were serially sectioned (thickness, 4 μm), mounted on glass slides, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Subsequently, the slides were reviewed and representative areas of interest were marked. Duplicate cores of invasive carcinoma tissue from each patient (length, 1.0 mm) were constructed into a tissue array. Placental and tonsil tissue cores were included as positive controls, and a core of normal breast tissue was included as a negative control.

Immunohistochemistry and pathologic analysis

Prior to immunohistochemical staining, sections of microarrays were incubated in an oven at 60°C for 1 hour. Subsequently, the sections were deparaffinized with xylene thrice, rehydrated using 100%, 95%, and 70% graded ethanol, and washed under running water. Antigen retrieval was conducted by heating in 10 mM sodium citrate buffer (pH 6.0) for 5 minutes at 95°C using a hot water bath, followed by cooling at room temperature for 20 minutes. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked by the addition of 1% hydrogen peroxide for 7 min. Sections were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against NRF2 (mouse monoclonal antibody, 1:100, sc-365949; Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, USA) or KEAP1 (mouse monoclonal antibody, 1:100, sc-365626; Santa Cruz Biotechnology). Immunoreaction signals were amplified and detected using the ImmunoCruz® ABC Kit (Santa Cruz Biotechnology). Thereafter, the sections were counterstained with hematoxylin and mounted on glass slides.

Two expert pathologists (Y.S.L. and A.L.), blinded to the experiment, independently examined the immunohistochemical staining of the TNBC tissue microarray. Each tumor sample was scored according to the fraction of stained cells at each intensity. The intensity of NRF2 nuclear staining and KEAP1 cytoplasmic staining was scored on a scale ranging from 0 to 3 as follows: no staining, 0; weak staining, 1+; moderate staining, 2+; and strong staining, 3+. The H-score of each immunomarker, a continuous variable expressed on a scale ranging from 0 to 300, was calculated using the following formula: 0 × (% unstained tumor cells, 0) + 1 × (% weakly stained tumor cells, 1+) + 2 × (% moderately stained tumor cells, 2+) + 3 × (% strongly stained tumor cells, 3+). The H-scores determined by the two pathologists were averaged for statistical analysis. High and low protein expression scores were defined using the median H-score of each immunomarker as the threshold.

Among the clinicopathological information collected from the medical records, the Ki-67 index was determined in hotspots using eyeball estimation in routine practice. A cut-off point of 20% was used to differentiate between low (< 20%) and high (≥ 20%) Ki-67 index [23]. Additionally, P53 immunostaining was considered positive when > 10% of tumor cells demonstrated a strong nuclear staining [24].

Selection of TNBC cases from publicly available datasets

The METABRIC breast cancer dataset was downloaded from cBioPortal (https://www.cbioportal.org/study/summary?id=brca_metabric) for validation. Collected clinicopathological information was obtained from the data_clinical_patient.txt and data_clinical_sample.txt files. The mRNA expression levels of NFE2L2 and KEAP1 in each sample were obtained from the data_mrna_illumina_microarray.txt file. Of the 1980 samples with available mRNA expression data, ten samples with a “4” in TUMOR_STAGE were excluded. Cases with either a “Positive” ER_IHC; a “GAIN” in HER2_SNP6; a “HER2,” “LumA,” and “LumB” in CLAUDIN_SUBTYPE; an “ER+/HER2- High Prolif,” “ER+HER2- Low Prolif,” and “HER2+” in THREEGENE; a “Positive” ER_STATUS; a “Positive” PR_STATUS or a “Positive” HER2_STATUS were excluded. Additionally, one case with no available survival data was excluded, resulting in 225 cases.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS software (version 26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, USA). Statistically significant differences were denoted as p-values < 0.05. The correlation between the H-scores and mRNA expression levels was measured using Pearson's correlation coefficient. The relationship between immunomarker expression and clinicopathological data was analyzed using the Pearson χ2 test. Survival analyses were performed using Kaplan-Meier curves with the log-rank test for overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS). The OS was defined as the period from the date of diagnosis to death (or last follow-up). DFS was defined as the period from the date of surgery to the date of any recurrence diagnosed by imaging or pathological confirmation. The effects of multiple covariates on OS and DFS were examined using univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses. Variables with p-values < 0.1 in the univariate analysis were considered marginally significant and included in the multivariate model. The results were reported as hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

RESULTS

Patient characteristics

Table 1 summarizes the clinicopathological characteristics of patients with TNBC included in the present study and the METABRIC dataset. The median age of the patients in the present study was 49 years (range, 23–90 years). Neoadjuvant chemotherapy was administered to 14 (6.9%) patients. Wide excision with postoperative radiotherapy and mastectomy were performed in 102 (50.2%) and 101 (49.8%) patients, respectively. Adjuvant chemotherapy was administered to 173 (85.2%) patients. The chemotherapy regimens included cyclophosphamide, methotrexate, and 5-fluorouracil in 19 (9.4%) patients, and anthracycline- and taxane-based regimens such as AC-T (doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide followed by paclitaxel) and EC-T (epirubicin and cyclophosphamide followed by paclitaxel) for 154 (75.9%) patients. More than half of the patients (n = 134, 66.0%) had a tumor size of > 2 cm. Based on the tumor staging system, most patients were classified as stage II (n = 131, 64.5%) or stage I (n = 54, 26.6%). LN involvement was identified in 64 (31.5%) patients. The main histologic type was invasive carcinoma of no special type (including invasive ductal and medullary carcinomas) in 197 (97.0%) patients and metaplastic carcinoma in six (3.0%) patients. The histologic grade was G3 in 135 (66.5%) patients. More than half of the patients (n = 157, 77.3%) showed a Ki-67 index of ≥ 20%. P53 immunostaining was positive in 90 (44.3%) patients.

Immunohistochemistry

NRF2 immunohistochemical staining reactivity was weak in the nuclei of normal breast ductal epithelial cells. It was variably expressed in the nuclei of breast carcinoma cells with an H-score of 0–300 (median, 200; Figure 1A-C), whereas it was not detected in the cytoplasm. Weak immunohistochemical expression of KEAP1 was detected in the cytoplasm of normal breast tissue. KEAP1 expression was mainly detected in the cytoplasm of breast carcinoma cells, with an H-score of 0–300 (median, 100; Figure 1D-F). A weak but significant positive correlation (r = 0.217, p = 0.002) was observed between the H-scores of NRF2 and KEAP1 (Supplementary Figure 1A). Conversely, no statistically significant correlation (r = 0.038, p = 0.567) was found between the mRNA expression levels of NFE2L2 and KEAP1 in TNBC patients in the METABRIC dataset (Supplementary Figure 1B).

Currently, there is no universally accepted H-score cut-off for NRF2 and KEAP1. Therefore, we set the median H-score for each immunomarker as the threshold value for protein expression. Based on these cut-off values, of the 203 patients, 143 (70.4%) and 60 (29.6%) were classified as NRF2-low and NRF2-high (H-score, ≤ 200 and > 200, respectively; Supplementary Figure 2A), and 108 (53.2%) and 95 patients (46.8%) were classified as KEAP1-low and KEAP1-high (H-score, ≤ 100 and > 100, respectively; Supplementary Figure 2B), respectively. Similarly, the median expression level of each mRNA was used as the cut-off value for mRNA expression in the METABRIC dataset. Of the 225 patients, 113 (50.2%) and 112 (49.8%) were classified as NFE2L2-low and NFE2L2-high (expression level, ≤ 9.186 and > 9.186, respectively; Supplementary Figure 2C), and 113 (50.2%) and 112 (49.8%) were classified as KEAP1-low and KEAP1-high (expression level, ≤ 7.978 and > 7.978, respectively; Supplementary Figure 2D), respectively.

Association between NRF2 and KEAP1 expressions and clinicopathological parameters

The correlations between NRF2 and KEAP1 protein expression levels and various clinicopathological parameters are summarized in Table 2. The expression of NRF2 was significantly associated with the histologic grade and Ki-67 index (p = 0.003 and 0.002, respectively). However, this marker was not associated with the other clinicopathological variables. KEAP1 expression was significantly associated with adjuvant chemotherapy and Ki-67 index (p = 0.046 and 0.028, respectively). In the METABRIC dataset, on the contrary, the mRNA expression of NFE2L2 showed no statistically significant association with any clinicopathological parameters. The mRNA expression level of KEAP1 was significantly associated with LN metastasis (p = 0.035; Supplementary Table 1).

Survival analyses

The follow-up period ended on February 24, 2022. The median OS was 12.0 years (range, 0.17–20.25 years), and the DFS was 12.04 years (range, 0.08–20.25 years). Patients with high NRF2 protein expression had worse survival outcomes than those with low NRF2 expression. Patients with high NRF2 expression exhibited a significantly shorter OS (5-year survival rate [5-YSR], 80.0% vs. 91.6%, respectively; p = 0.009; Figure 2A) and DFS (5-YSR,76.7% vs. 88.9%, respectively; p = 0.015; Figure 2B) than those with low NRF2 expression. Patients with low KEAP1 expression had significantly shorter OS (5-YSR, 83.3% vs. 93.7%, respectively; p = 0.004; Figure 2C) and DFS (5-YSR, 78.7% vs. 92.6%, respectively; p = 0.018; Figure 2D) than those with high KEAP1 expression. The effects of clinicopathological variables on survival are summarized in Table 3. In univariate analysis, the type of surgery, LN metastasis, NRF2 expression, and KEAP1 expression were significantly associated with OS and DFS (all p < 0.05). Multivariate analysis showed that LN metastasis, NRF2 expression, and KEAP1 expression were independent prognostic factors for OS (HR, 2.37, 2.45, and 0.30; p = 0.030, 0.015, and 0.006, respectively). NRF2 and KEAP1 expressions were also identified as independent prognostic factors for DFS (HR, 2.27 and 0.42; p = 0.019 and 0.022, respectively).

Conversely, the median OS was 8.21 years (range, 0.06–25.66 years) and the DFS was 7.11 years (range, 0.06–25.32 years) in TNBC cases from the METABRIC dataset. Patients with low NFE2L2 mRNA expression exhibited significantly shorter OS (5-YSR, 62.8% vs. 70.0%, respectively; p = 0.016; Figure 2E) and marginally significantly shorter DFS (5-YSR, 58.4% vs. 68.8%, respectively; p = 0.070; Figure 2F) than those with high NFE2L2 expression. Combined effect of NRF2 and KEAP1 expressions on survival outcomes

The combined effects of NRF2 and KEAP1 protein expressions on the survival of patients with TNBC were analyzed. The combination of NRF2 and KEAP1 expressions was associated with significant differences in OS (p < 0.001; Figure 3A) and DFS (p = 0.001; Figure 3B). The best outcome was observed in the NRF2-low + KEAP1-high group (group 1; OS rate at 5 years, 96.8%; DFS rate at 5 years, 95.2%), whereas the worst outcome was observed in the NRF2-high + KEAP1-low group (group 4; OS rate at 5 years, 70.4%; DFS rate at 5 years, 63.0%). The other groups (NRF2-high + KEAP1-high [group 2] and NRF2-low + KEAP1-low [group 3]) showed intermediate outcomes. Pairwise survival comparisons revealed that group 4 exhibited significantly shorter OS and DFS than the other groups (all p < 0.05), and group 3 exhibited significantly shorter OS than group 2 (p = 0.038). Multivariate analysis (Table 5) showed that the combination of NRF2 and KEAP1 expressions was an independent prognostic factor for OS and DFS (p = 0.007 and 0.016, respectively).

DISCUSSION

To our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the prognostic value of NRF2 and KEAP1 expressions in patients with TNBC. Mutations in the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway have been identified in numerous types of cancer [12,13,14,15]. These mutations result in constitutive activation of NRF2 and contribute to poor survival by inducing chemoresistance and tumor progression. Existing literature suggests that tumor cells acquire chemoresistance following the constitutive activation of NRF2 because of its dissociation from its negative regulator, KEAP1. Subsequently, NRF2 translocates to the nucleus where it activates the transcription of various cytoprotective genes [25,26,27]. It has been suggested that the cumulative presence of at least one allele mutated in NFE2L2 and/or KEAP1 polymorphisms is related to a worse prognosis in patients with hormone-dependent breast cancer [28]. Moreover, several studies have indicated that NRF2 is a possible therapeutic target in TNBC because its activation promotes cell growth and radioresistance in TNBC cells [17,18]. However, the specific prognostic value of the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway in patients with TNBC remains unclear.

Our results showed that NRF2 was expressed in the nucleus, whereas KEAP1 was expressed in the cytoplasm of TNBC cells. These immunohistochemical staining patterns are consistent with those previously reported in the literature [5]. In our study, NRF2 and KEAP1 expressions showed a weak positive correlation (r = 0.217), weaker than the correlation observed in colon cancer (r = 0.64) and laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (r = 0.4966) [29,30]. The difference in correlations between NRF2 and KEAP1 expressions in various organs may be attributed to the activation of these proteins through pathways other than the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway. Numerous additional pathways regulate NRF2 independent of KEAP1, including beta-transducin repeat-containing protein, protein kinase C, and PRKR-like endoplasmic reticulum kinase [31]. Further investigations are warranted to identify specific pathways other than the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway that are involved in the regulation of NRF2 and KEAP1 in TNBC.

In the present study, the expression levels of NRF2 and KEAP1 were significantly associated with the Ki-67 index. This suggests that the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway facilitates the proliferation of TNBC cells and contributes to tumor progression, which is consistent with previous in vitro studies [7,32]. Furthermore, NRF2 expression was significantly associated with histologic grade. Histologic grade 3 tumors showed higher NRF2 expression than histologic grade 1–2 tumors. This result is consistent with previous data on both ER-positive and ER-negative tumors [33].

Our survival analyses demonstrated that NRF2 and KEAP1 are independent prognostic factors for OS and DFS in patients with operable TNBC. Consistent with previous studies that included hormone-dependent breast cancers [5,16], high NRF2 expression was significantly associated with poor survival outcomes in the present analysis. A study revealed that NRF2 mutation causes constitutive activation of NRF2, and several hotspots (e.g., R34 and D29 positions) have been detected in bladder, lung, and uterine cancers [34]. In our study, low KEAP1 expression was associated with poor survival outcomes. Similar results have been reported for ovarian cancer and renal cell carcinoma [15,35]. Loss of KEAP1 expression by tropomyosin-related kinase B (TrkB)-induced AKT activation contributes to the tumorigenicity and metastasis of breast cancer cells in vitro. Moreover, a significant upregulation of TrkB has been observed in patients with TNBC [19].

Validation of the METABRIC dataset revealed that NFE2L2 mRNA expression was an independent prognostic factor for OS in patients with TNBC and all molecular subtypes of breast cancer. Low NFE2L2 expression was significantly associated with shorter OS, contrary to NRF2 protein expression in our study and consistent with a previous study that included both ER-positive and ER-negative tumors [36]. KEAP1 mRNA expression was not significantly associated with patient survival in TNBC, although low KEAP1 expression was significantly associated with shorter DFS in all molecular subtypes of breast cancer. This inconsistency could be due to the differences in patient characteristics between the two groups. In our study, more patients underwent adjuvant chemotherapy (85.2% vs. 56.9%, respectively) and survival outcomes were better (median OS, 12.0 years vs. 8.21 years, respectively) compared to the METABRIC dataset. Moreover, high mRNA levels do not always correspond to high protein levels owing to epigenetic modifications. A previous study revealed that ER-negative breast tumors with weak NRF2 protein expression showed higher miR-144 expression, although immunostaining was mostly cytoplasmic, and a low NRF2 immunohistochemistry product score was associated with a worse distant relapse-free survival [37]. MicroRNAs, including miR-144, can down-regulate NFE2L2 gene expression post-transcriptionally, resulting in low NRF2 expression. Moreover, one study demonstrated that promoter methylation was the main mechanism regulating KEAP1 expression, and TNBC patients with KEAP1 methylation demonstrated a higher risk of cancer-related deaths than those without TNBC, although there was no comparison between TNBC patients with and without KEAP1 methylation [38]. The molecular mechanisms underlying the regulation of NRF2 and KEAP1 in TNBC require further investigation.

Furthermore, the combination of NRF2 and KEAP1 expression levels was a more powerful predictor of survival outcomes in patients with operable TNBC. The NRF2-low + KEAP1-high group showed the best OS and DFS compared to the other groups. In contrast, the NRF2-high + KEAP1-low group showed the worst OS and DFS among the four patient groups. These results suggest that NRF2, KEAP1, and their combination may serve as prognostic markers and molecular targets for therapies in patients with TNBC. This is the first evidence demonstrating the prognostic value of NRF2, KEAP1, and their combination in patients with TNBC.

Our study has several limitations. First, the retrospective design may have resulted in an unpredictable bias. Second, this was a single-center study with a small sample size. Third, we excluded patients with distant metastasis at initial diagnosis due to the lack of surgical specimens, which may have also introduced selection bias. Finally, we only measured protein expression by immunohistochemistry and did not evaluate the underlying genetic alterations. Despite these limitations, the present study demonstrated the prognostic significance of NRF2 and KEAP1 in TNBC.

In conclusion, we demonstrated that high NRF2 and low KEAP1 expressions are independent prognostic factors for worse survival in patients with operable TNBC. These results suggest that the KEAP1-NRF2 pathway plays an important role in tumor progression and patient survival in this setting. These findings suggest novel prognostic biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets for TNBC treatment.

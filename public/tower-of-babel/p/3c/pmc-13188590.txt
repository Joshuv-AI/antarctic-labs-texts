The positivity rate of opioid drug urine testing in an emergency department between 2016 and 2024, before and after the introduction of fentanyl testing
Authors: Oren Miron, David Zeltser, Shaul Schreiber, Einat Peles
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDespite all Health Maintenance Organizations in Israel having documented an increased use of prescription opioids, a decrease in admissions with positive opioid toxicology was observed in emergency departments (EDs). We hypothesized that following the fentanyl test introduction in 2023 in the largest acute care facility in Israel, opioid detection and naloxone usage would increase, and death rates would decrease.MethodsBetween 2016 and June 2024, 20,337 ED-admitted individuals were screened for drugs via urine toxicology tests. Opioid trends, their characteristics, the administration of naloxone, and death rates were analyzed. The pre- and since fentanyl testing initiation periods were compared.ResultsThe proportion of individuals screened via urine toxicology increased from 0.7% in 2016 to 1.3% in 2024. Individuals screening positive for opioids were predominantly male, older-aged, non-Israeli born, with opioid and benzodiazepine prescriptions, screened positive for other substances, naloxone-treated, hospitalized, and had a higher death rate. Excluding fentanyl results, the proportion of opioid-positive tests decreased from 15.4% in 2016 to 11.6% in 2024, but when including fentanyl (available since 2023), the proportion in 2024 was 14% and did not show a significant trend. A higher opioid proportion was observed at the period with fentanyl tests (2023–2024) (12.7% vs. 10.4%, p < 0.001) with no differences in death within first day rate (0.8% vs. 0.5%, p = 0.1) and naloxone usage (3.4% vs. 3.8%, p = 0.2). During the fentanyl testing period (2023–2024), among those receiving naloxone, a higher opioid proportion observed when fentanyl testing was included (56.7%) than excluded (45%).ConclusionsAs was hypothesized, a gradual decrease in opioid-positive tests was attenuated after the fentanyl test was introduced. Given the absence of evidence for a large-scale illicit fentanyl market in Israel, fentanyl exposure is likely to originate primarily from legally prescribed medications, which may reflect a tendency to prescribe fentanyl more often than less potent opioids (hence not detected before the fentanyl test was added). Additionally, the higher opioid positivity among naloxone-treated patients observed after the fentanyl test was introduced supports the need to add fentanyl to the urine toxicology test battery in all EDs across the country. Still, stable low death and naloxone-use rates suggest no impact on overdose diagnosis and treatment.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: Fentanyl, Opioids, Emergency department, Overdose, Urine drug test, Opioid prescriptions

Introduction

Fentanyl, a highly potent FDA-approved synthetic opioid, has been illegally distributed over the past decade (often termed “illicitly manufactured fentanyl”) and is increasingly present in the street drug supply. [1, 2] During this period, fentanyl has contributed to a dramatic rise in drug overdose deaths in the United States (US). [3, 4], In Israel between 2012 and 2015, opioid consumption increased by 125%, the largest rise among Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) countries [5]. However in contrast to the USA, there is no evidence of a large-scale illicit fentanyl market. Fentanyl is typically prescribed as a potent pain reliever for serious medical conditions, and non-medical use often arises from diversion, misuse of prescriptions, or sharing of legally obtained patches and pills. Data from Israel’s largest health care provider (Clalit Health Services) showed a further increase in outpatient opioid consumption in 2016–2018, largely driven by fentanyl use among non-elderly patients without malignancy [6]. A two-fold increase in opioid dependence and misuse was reported during 2015–2019, with no increase among heroin or opium users but a two-fold rise among individuals endorsing fentanyl use [7], consistent with police reports of increased fentanyl-related misuse and deaths [8]. Despite this, national registry data showed a decline in deaths attributed to heroin or prescription opioids [9], while Clalit data demonstrated a dose-dependent increase in all-cause mortality among individuals prescribed opioids [10].

Fentanyl overdoses are likely underestimated due to limited awareness and the absence of routine fentanyl testing in emergency department (ED) toxicology screens, which may result in overdoses being treated with naloxone but not recorded as opioid-related events. Preliminary unpublished data from Tel Aviv Sourasky Medical Center (TASMC) suggested a decline in opioid-positive screens, raising suspicion of undetected fentanyl exposure. In 2023, fentanyl testing was added to routine ED toxicology screening at TASMC, and we hypothesized an increase in opioid detection following its introduction. Because toxicology screening is not performed universally, naloxone administration serves as a critical indicator of opioid overdose, as demonstrated in US emergency medical services data [11–13].

In this study, conducted at TASMC, Israel’s largest acute care hospital, we aimed to characterize individuals screening positive for opioids, evaluate whether positive opioid screens increased after fentanyl testing was introduced, assess naloxone use trends since 2019, ((when those treated in the ED with naloxone were electronically available) and compare mortality rates between the fentanyl-testing period (2023–2024) and the pre-testing period (2019–2022).

Methods

The hospital’s IRB (‘Helsinki Committee’), in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, approved the study analyses (TASMC-0503-22).

Data source

De-identified admissions data were retrieved from the TASMC ED database using an electronic format. Data analyses included all individuals screened for drug use via urine toxicology at admission to the ED or hospital between January 2016 and June 2024, and individuals who did not screen for drug use but were administered naloxone in the ED between January 2019 and June 2024 (as information for administered naloxone existed in the electronic format since 2019 only).

The decision for drug use via urine toxicology is a clinical one, with no formal method.

Toxicology tests (Toxscreen)

The multi-drug 2–17 Drugs Rapid Test Cassette (Urine) (Acro Biotech, USA) is a lateral flow immunoassay designed for the qualitative detection of multiple drugs and their metabolites in human urine. The test utilizes monoclonal antibodies to selectively detect elevated levels of specific drugs; Methamphetamine- urine, (calibrator d-Methamphetamine, cut-off 1000ng/ml); Cocaine urine (calibrator Benzoylecgonine, cut-off 300ng/ml); Marijuana Cannabinoid urine (calibrator 11 nor THC 9 COOH, cut-off 50 ng/ml); MDMA (Ecstasy) urine (calibrator d,1 MDMA, cut-off 500 ng/ml); Benzodiazepines (calibrator Oxazepam, cut-off 300 ng/ml); Tricyclic antidepressants urine (calibrator nortriptyline, cut-off 1000 ng/ml); Barbiturates urine (calibrator Secobarbital, cut-off 300 ng/ml); PCP (Phencyclidine) urine (calibrator Phencyclidine, cut-off 20 ng/ml); Amphetamines (calibrator d-Amphetamine, cut-off 1000 ng/ml). Opioids included methadone urine (calibrator methadone, cut-off 300 ng/ml); opiates (calibrator morphine, cut-off 300 ng/ml) Oxycodone (calibrator oxycodone, cut-off 100 ng/ml). In 2023, fentanyl was added to the toxicology panel, qualitatively detected in human urine utilizing the ARK™ Fentanyl II Assay (ARK Diagnostics, Inc.), a homogeneous enzyme immunoassay (EIA) with a 1.0 ng/mL cutoff, executed on Siemens ADVIA® 2400 Chemistry Systems.

Sociodemographic variables (sex, age, place of birth), past diagnosis of blood-transmitted disease (HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C) that characterized people who use drugs, chronic medications that can be misused (prescriptions for opioids and benzodiazepines), treatment with the naloxone, and outcomes (hospitalized, death within 1 day from ED admission that may relate to overdose and no other illness).

Statistical analyses

Data analysis was performed using the R statistical software on a Windows computer and SPSS version 29. Proportional differences were analyzed by the χ2 or Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables were analyzed for significant differences with the variance analysis (ANOVA). A comparison between individuals screened positive for opioid vs. screened negative, and a comparison between the two periods tested patients were done. Logistic regression multivariate analyses were used to characterize the opioid screened positive, and to characterize the “fentanyl tested” period, including variables with p < 0.1 in univariate analyses. Significant trends by six-month intervals (as only the 1st half year in 2024 was included) over the years for categorical variables were evaluated using chi-square with Pearson chi-square or linear association, and continuous variables were assessed using the analysis of variance (ANOVA) linearity. Results

Study data characteristics

20,337 individuals met the inclusion criteria. Specifically, 20,032 individuals (98.5%) had a urine toxicology screen, and of those, 229 were administered naloxone. An additional 305 (1.5%) individuals were administered naloxone without a toxicology test.

Trends over 2016–2024

Opioid toxicology trend

During the 2016–2024 period, the overall annual number of ED admissions remained stable, about 100,000 per year. However, the proportion of admissions that underwent urine toxicological screening increased from 0.7% in 2016 to 1.3% in 2024 (p < 0.001). Excluding fentanyl results, the proportion of opioid-positive tests decreased significantly from 15.4% in 2016 to 11.6% in 2024, (chi-square = 21.7, p < 0.001), but when including fentanyl (that was introduced only since 2023), the proportion of patients screened positive for opioids in 2024 was 14% and did not show a significant trend (p = 0.1) (Fig. 1).

Percentage of patients with positive results to specific opioids and any opioids together by years

Prescription opioid trend

From 2016 to 2024, the percentage of any opioid prescription (fentanyl, tramadol, oxycodone, and buprenorphine) increased from 0.5% in 2016 to 3.2% in 2024 (chi-square = 23.6, p < 0.001), with a similar trend observed for fentanyl prescription only (chi-square = 8.6, p = 0.003) (Fig. 2).

Percentage of patients who screened for toxicology, of patients with any opioid prescription, and of patients with a fentanyl prescription, by years

Death rate trend

The total death rate (within one day from admission) in those with toxicology was 0.6% (n = 103), ranging between 0.4% in 2016 to 1.0% in 2024 (linear association, p = 0.2). The death rate within one day was stable (ranged between 0 and 0.7%) among those with negative opioid toxicology (p = 0.6), and in those with any opioids, ranged between 0 and 2.6% with no significant trend (linear association p = 0.08).

Characteristics of the opioid screen-positive

Compared to patients that screened negative for opioids (Table 1), a higher proportion of patients that screened positive were males (64.1% vs. 56.7%, p < 0.001), aged ≥ 40y, an non-Israeli born, were prescribed opioids and benzodiazepine, tested positive for hepatitis B, hepatitis C, HIV, were hospitalized, and died within one date of their ED visit. Additionally, more opioid positive individuals screened positive for all other screened substances, excluding ecstasy, with benzodiazepine, cocaine, and cannabis being the most prevalent.

Naloxone: trends and characteristics − 2019–2024

Overall, between 2019 and 2024, 3.7% (534 of 14,456) patients received naloxone (ranging between 3.6% and 4.2% per year), and 1.6% (229 of 14,151) by those who screened for toxicology and had drug use results (ranging between 1.5% and 2.1%) with no apparent trend (p = 0.6 and p = 0.9, respectively). Overall, the death rate was 0.6% (n = 88) with a nonsignificant increasing trend by years (linear association, chi-square = 3.7, p = 0.055). The death rate was 3.5% in those who received naloxone and 0.9% in those who did not, with no death rate trend change over the years (linear association p = 0.2 and p = 0.1, respectively). Of the 229 patients who received naloxone with drug use results, 48.5% tested positive for any opioid. The proportion of any opioid prescription of the 305 with no toxicology was similar to the 229 with toxicology (22.3% vs. 19.2%, p = 0.5), and the proportion of fentanyl prescription (6.9% vs. 5.2, p = 0.3).

The 2019–2022 and 2023–2024 comparison periods

Comparing the “fentanyl tested” period (2023–2024) with an earlier period that included naloxone (2019–2022), a higher opioid screened positive rate was observed (12.7% vs. 10.4%, p < 0.001), with no significant death rate (0.8% vs. 0.5%, p = 0.1) and naloxone usage (3.4% vs. 3.8%, p = 0.2) differences. Specifically, the death rate by opioid results of the two periods showed no difference between patients’ screen-positive to opioids (1.4% vs. 1.5%, p = 1) and a slightly but not statistically significant higher death rate during the “fentanyl tested” period among patients’ screen-negative to opioids (0.6% vs. 0.3%, p = 0.067). There were no significant differences in treatment and outcome (naloxone used, hospitalized, and died) when comparing individuals with toxicology screened only (Table 2). In patients who received naloxone during 2023 and 2024, when fentanyl was tested, the rate of screening positive for opioids was 56.7% if a fentanyl test was included, and 45% only if a fentanyl test was excluded.

Bullet-point summaries

Opioid prescriptions increased from 2016 to 2024, while opioid-positive screens declined until fentanyl testing was introduced in 2023, after which the decline stopped.

Opioid-positive individuals were predominantly male, aged ≥ 40 years, non–Israeli-born, had higher rates of opioid and benzodiazepine prescriptions, infectious diseases (HBV, HCV, HIV), polysubstance use, hospitalization, and one-day mortality.

Naloxone administration rates remained stable from 2019 to 2024; approximately half of naloxone-treated patients screened opioid-positive (higher when fentanyl test was added), and one-fifth had opioid prescriptions.

Compared with the pre–fentanyl-testing period (2019–2022), individuals in the fentanyl-testing period (2023–2024) were older, had higher rates of hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and HIV, lower rates of benzodiazepine-, tricyclic-, and barbiturate-positive screens, and higher rates of cannabis, methamphetamine, and any opioid-positive screens. Mortality and naloxone administration rates did not differ between periods; however, among those receiving naloxone, opioid-positive screens were more frequent when fentanyl testing was included.

Discussion

In the current analyses, of the individuals who were at risk and were selected for toxicology screening, the opioid screened positive was characterized as the more severe group. Specifically, the opioid screened positive group presented worse outcomes - more of them were hospitalized, and the death rate within the first day was higher among them. They were also screened positive for other substances, mostly benzodiazepine, cannabis, and cocaine, while cocaine was four times more prevalent among the opioid screened positive. Thus, it is important to detect this specific group to improve diagnosis, treatment, and/or patient safety. Adding fentanyl to routine toxicology tests is important for identifying individuals with OUD, yet doing so does not appear to affect clinical outcomes.

Individuals with opioid screened positive were also characterized as male, 40 years old or older, non-Israeli born, with opioid and benzodiazepine prescriptions, and with hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and HIV. Being male, non-Israeli born with hepatitis C, B, and HIV characterizes people who use drugs in Israel [14] and is prevalent with OUD and in methadone maintenance treatment [15].

At the late period 2023–2024 (fentanyl period), based on a multivariate analysis model, individuals were older, with more hepatitis C, B, and HIV, lower benzodiazepine screen positive, but higher cannabis and opioid screen positive results. The usage reflects a trend of these substances over the years, and the characteristics of this population group are slightly different. Due to the reports of an increase in opioid consumption in Israel [6, 7], we expected to find an increased proportion of opioids among individuals admitted to the ED in TASMC who underwent toxicological tests between 2016 and mid-2024. As expected, we found in our study cohort an increase in the rate of prescription of opioids (from less than 1% in 2016 to almost 5% in 2023 and 3.2%, a slight decrease in 2024). However, a decreasing trend of opioids screened positive was observed (from 15.4% in 2016 to 11.6% in 2024). This reflects the undetected positive opioids in urine screened due to the lack of fentanyl tests. Indeed, as we hypothesized, in 2023 and mid-2024, when the fentanyl test was added, the trend reduction was blunted. The opioid-screened positive in 2024, which was 11.6%, turned out to be 14% when fentanyl was included.

While over the years, a stable number of admissions to the ED was observed, an increasing trend in the proportion of those who underwent toxicology screening (our study cohort) was evident. Thus, it must be kept in mind that those individuals who underwent toxicology tests did so following the subjective clinical impression of physicians, not as part of a routine protocol for ED admissions. Therefore, physicians’ personal attitudes and impressions might have determined who should be tested. We cannot exclude the possibility that the reduction in opioid-positive rate over the years may relate to the fact that more were suspected and have been screened, and possibly without a fentanyl test, were undetected. A finding that explained the possibility that some of the opioid users shifted over the years to the much more potent fentanyl, resulting in an apparent decrease in opioid users, while fentanyl has not been tested yet.

In addition to opioid toxicology screening being positive, we can learn about opioid usage according to opioid overdose events that, if suspected or diagnosed, are treated with naloxone, information that, in our data, was available between 2019 and 2024.

However, unlike the USA, which has a high opioid overdose trend, and the naloxone rate is a robust marker [13, 14], we don’t have a high death rate due to opioids in Israel [9, 10]. We did not find a change in the naloxone rate, with or without toxicology results, nor in the death rate over the years. Of the 43% of those receiving naloxone who had toxicology (n = 229), only half (48.5%) screened positive for opioids. Importantly, during the “fentanyl tested” period 2023–2024, of those who received naloxone, the rate of those who screened positive for opioids was 56.7% if fentanyl test results were included and 45% only if fentanyl tests were excluded. In those who received naloxone but had no toxicology screening, the only information regarding opioids we had was opioid prescriptions, which were among one-fifth of them, as in those with toxicology screening. Thus, although the fentanyl test elevated the odds of opioids screening positive, we cannot conclude that persons treated with naloxone and toxicology screened negative for opioids means undetected fentanyl or a mistakenly diagnosed opioid overdose.

A recent Clalit HMO’s analysis from 2015 to 2021 found a correlation between the procurement of opioids per year and opioid-related visits to all emergency departments in the country [16]. Our findings of a gradual decrease in the percentage of positive opioids between 2016 and 2022, before fentanyl tests started, accompanied by a gradual increase in the rate of patients with a chronic fentanyl prescription, and the higher opioid screened positive of naloxone treated individuals, support the need to add fentanyl to the routine toxicological urine test battery in all emergency rooms across the country, and possibly also in other settings (i.e., methadone and buprenorphine maintenance treatment clinics).

It should be mentioned that as soon as fentanyl testing was added to the toxicology screen in the Tel Aviv Medical Center ED, new regulations by the Israeli health care (Clalit and Meuchedet HMOs) increased awareness of physicians to restrict opioid prescriptions, particularly fentanyl [17]. These regulations can be reflected in our cohort by the reduction of opioid prescriptions in 2024. In contrast to the situation in the United States, where the opioid crisis has been driven largely by the widespread availability of illicitly manufactured fentanyl and its analogs on the black market, the main driver of fentanyl use in Israel stems from legally prescribed medications and diversion from the medical system, we believe this may have prevented a greater increase in the rate of fentanyl-positive tests.

Limitations

The subjective group who underwent toxicology tests may also change over the years, which may lead to biased proportions, as not all ED admissions were screened for toxicology. Another limitation is that physician decisions for toxicology screening may include other non-opioid substances as well, influencing results. The trend of other substances is not part of the current manuscript. However, benzodiazepines were reduced, and cannabis increased during the “fentanyl-tested” 2023–2024 period compared with the earlier period 2019–2022. Another limitation is the small duration of the follow-up since the introduction of the fentanyl test in the ED. The regulations published regarding opioid prescriptions, which particularly restrict fentanyl, parallel to the fentanyl screening in ED, may change the opioid increase trend in ED, and it is unknown how it would affect users. Another limitation is that while the studied ED is a large tertiary medical center in the biggest municipal city in Israel, treating diverse populations, this is still only a single hospital study, which may have limited relevance for other EDs throughout the country. Another limitation is the possibility that naloxone was administered empirically in some cases that weren’t opioid-related. Lastly, the analysis is based on de-identified electronic data, with the limitation of no possibility to be sure that all patients tested were included.

Conclusions

A gradual decrease in the percentage of positive opioids that were blunted following adding the fentanyl toxicology test emphasizes the importance of continued fentanyl monitoring and expanding to all EDs in Israel. While identifying individuals with opioid use disorder is important to treating patients, the stable low deaths and naloxone use rates suggest no influence of overdose diagnosis and treatment.

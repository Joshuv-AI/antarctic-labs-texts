BEMM-GEN: A Toolkit for Generating a Biomolecular Environment-Mimicking Model for Molecular Dynamics Simulation
Authors: Takunori Yasuda, Rikuri Morita, Yasuteru Shigeta, Ryuhei Harada
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractUnderstanding the influence of the cellular environment on protein conformations is crucial for elucidating protein functions within living cells. In studies using molecular dynamics (MD) simulation, carbon nanotubes and hydrophobic cages have been widely used to emulate the cellular environment inside specific large biomolecules such as ribosome tunnels and chaperones. However, recent studies suggest that these uniform hydrophobic models may not adequately capture the environmental effects inside each biomolecule. Based on these facts, it is necessary to generate spherical and cylindrical models with varied chemical properties corresponding to the components within target biomolecules. We developed a biomolecular environment-mimicking model generator (BEMM-GEN) that generates spherical and cylindrical models with user-specified chemical properties and allows the integration of arbitrary protein conformations into the generated models. BEMM-GEN provides model and protein complex structures, along with the corresponding parameter files for MD simulation (AMBER and GROMACS), and users immediately run their MD simulation based on the generated input files. BEMM-GEN can be freely downloaded and installed via a Python package manager (pip install BEMM-gen). The source code files and a user manual for operation are provided on GitHub (https://github.com/y4suda/BEMM-GEN).

Introduction

In living cells, protein conformations are influenced not only by their amino acid sequences but also by the surrounding cellular environment. For instance, several studies have reported that proteins adopt specific conformations within organelles1,2 or liquid droplets.3,4 Owing to the critical relationships between environment-specific conformations and protein functions, significant research effort has been dedicated to elucidating the impact of the cellular environment on protein conformations.5−7 However, owing to the challenges of observing protein conformations in specific environments and directly controlling such environments, the effects of cellular environment on protein conformations are still not completely understood.8

With advancements in computer performance, computational methods have been established. Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation, which provides information on protein dynamics at high temporal and spatial resolutions, has become a well-established method for exploring protein conformations in a cellular environment.9,10 Specifically, whole proteins in the bacterial cytoplasm and bacteriophage particles were simulated using all-atom MD simulation.11,12 However, the computational costs of comprehensive all-atom MD simulation remain too high to effectively capture the properties of the target system.13 Consequently, in addition to coarse-grained MD simulation,14,15 several approaches that combine MD simulation with simplified models to emulate cellular environment have been proposed. Typical approaches for mimicking cellular solvents include changing the dielectric constant,16,17 mixing cosolvent small molecules,18,19 and crowding proteins.20−22 Moreover, environments inside biomolecules have been modeled by using various approaches. For example, tunnel-shaped biomolecules, such as channels and ribosome tunnels, have been modeled using carbon nanotube (CNT). According to these studies, the conformational restrictions imposed by tunnel confinement affect the secondary structure of peptides.23,24 Additionally, spherical biomolecules such as protein chaperones have been modeled using hydrophobic cages, which has revealed that the surfaces of these cages influence protein stability.25,26 As mentioned above, these uniform hydrophobic models have been widely utilized and have provided insight into the general effects of the biomolecular environment.

Several experimental studies have indicated that the diverse chemical properties of ribosomes and chaperones, such as electrical charge, hydrophobicity, and direct-binding receptors, also influence the conformation of proteins inside each biomolecule.27,28 Furthermore, we have recently reported that a cylindrical model reflecting the chemical properties of ribosome tunnels more accurately reproduced the experimentally observed secondary structures when compared with a CNT model.29 These studies suggest that the previously established uniform hydrophobic sphere and cylinder models may not adequately represent the effects on the proteins inside each biomolecule. Hence, to more accurately emulate the biomolecular environment, spherical and cylindrical models with varied chemical properties must be generated according to the components of the target biomolecules. Additionally, while several software tools for generating membrane30,31 or mixed solvent32 environments to perform MD simulations have been developed and are widely used, no program has been developed to model special environments inside biomolecules. Therefore, to fulfill these requirements, we developed a new open-source tool called a biomolecular environment-mimicking model generator (BEMM-GEN) for MD simulation.

Functions of BEMM-GEN

BEMM-GEN generates spherical and cylindrical models with user-specified chemical properties, allowing for the integration of arbitrary protein configurations into the generated models. The model and protein complex structures, along with the corresponding parameter files for MD simulation (AMBER and GROMACS), are provided as output files. As input, users specify the shape and properties of the models as well as the details of proteins to be integrated. In addition, parameters for arbitrary compositional (functional) residues can be generated. These functions of BEMM-GEN are illustrated in Figure 1. Specifically, BEMM-GEN includes four functions: (1) generating the proteins inside the model, (2) generating model structures, (3) generating the parameters for the functional residues in addition to default residues, and (4) integrating the protein/peptide and models and generating the input files for MD simulation.

Schematic view of BEMM-GEN.

In the first function, BEMM-GEN accepts PDB-formatted protein structures or amino acid sequences as inputs. In the latter case, BEMM-GEN generates arbitrary peptide structures with specific secondary structures (helices or coils). It is noted that the current version of BEMM-GEN assumes only proteins as inputs and does not support protein–substrate complexes or nucleic acids. In the second function, BEMM-GEN generates model configurations with specific chemical properties. To designate the shape of each model, BEMM-GEN automatically generates spherical and cylindrical structures based on the long and short axes of proteins inside the models. It is also possible to designate the shape based on the distance between the protein, peptide, and model. Each model is constructed by sterically arranging functional residues with specific chemical properties and maintaining a user-specified distance between functional residues. In cylindrical models, functional residues are arranged in a circular pattern in a single layer, and their layers are vertically stacked. Additionally, the order of the functional residues is randomized to maintain model uniformity. Regarding the chemical properties of the model, BEMM-GEN offers 16 types of functional residues, and users designate the compositions and populations of each residue in the model. In the third function, BEMM-GEN optionally generates parameters and structures for the functional residues other than the default 16 types of residues. BEMM-GEN accepts SMILES format information for small molecules to register as new residues. In the fourth section, BEMM-GEN integrates the protein structures generated in Function (1) with the model structures generated in Function (2). Subsequently, based on the integrated complex structures, BEMM-GEN generates the input files for MD simulation in AMBER (.prmtop, .inpcrd) and GROMACS (.top, .gro) format. Because information about the positional restrictions for the models is also included, users may immediately run their MD simulations using these input files.

Application and Prospect of BEMM-GEN

Because BEMM-GEN can easily generate model structures with arbitrary physical parameters (radius and length), chemical properties, and included proteins, it may elucidate the various effects of diverse biomolecular environments on proteins. Herein, we present the potential applications and prospects of BEMM-GEN for modeling spherical and cylindrical biomolecules.

The ribosome tunnel is a well-studied biomolecule for cylindrical modeling. Our previous study demonstrated that cylindrical models with diverse chemical properties reproduced the experimentally observed helix more accurately than CNT.29 However, the impact of each chemical property of the ribosome tunnel on protein conformation has yet to be evaluated. As users can easily generate models with various chemical properties, BEMM-GEN facilitates the evaluation of the individual impacts of each chemical property on the protein inside the ribosome tunnel. In addition to the respective chemical properties, the effect on the internal diameter can be evaluated simultaneously. As mentioned above, BEMM-GEN may provide more insight into the impact of ribosomal tunneling on peptides by arbitrarily setting their chemical and physical properties. Similar to ribosome tunnel, BEMM-GEN may be useful for detecting significant effects inside the channels, nuclear pore complex, and translocon because these environments are known as a tunnel-like biomolecular environment.33−36

Protein chaperones are well-investigated biomolecules for spherical modeling. Inside protein chaperones, in addition to hydrophobic residues, hydrophilic residues and amide and carboxy residues are important for assisting protein folding.37,38 Moreover, chaperones exhibit specificity for the properties of their target proteins.39 However, a comprehensive analysis of the independent effects of each residue on each target protein has yet to be conducted. Thus, BEMM-GEN may offer deeper insight into the detailed mechanisms of protein chaperones by altering the model compositions and proteins inside the model. In summary, BEMM-GEN is versatile as a tool for studying biomolecular environments.

When considering proteins within biomolecules, there is a demand to investigate the properties of protein–ligand complexes within the biomolecular environment. This is because the cellular environment has been reported to affect the binding of proteins and substrates, including drugs.40,41 Furthermore, due to recent progress in RNA-based vaccines, there is also a growing demand to investigate the stability of RNA within specific confined environments.42 Therefore, as a future prospect, BEMM-GEN will also address protein–ligand complexes and nucleic acids, while the current version of BEMM-GEN assumes only proteins as input.

Implementation of BEMM-GEN

BEMM-GEN is an open-source tool designed to generate biomolecular environment-mimicking models for MD simulation that is primarily programmed in Python 3. As a command-line program, it is compatible with Unix- and Linux-based operating systems, including the Windows Subsystem for Linux.

Here, we outline the representative implementation of BEMM-GEN for each function (function details are described in Section 2). For the first function, AmberTools43 was used to generate peptides with specific secondary structures. The second function was implemented by using a general Python library. The third function was employed with Open Babel44 and RDkit45 to generate conformations (.mol2) of user-specified residues in the SMILES format. In addition, the parameters of the functional residues were generated by calculating their restrained electrostatic potential (RESP) charges based on QM calculations after structural optimization. Structural optimization and QM calculations were performed using psi4, which is an open-source ab initio quantum chemistry library.46 A general AMBER force field (GAFF) for organic molecules was adopted to determine the van der Waals and bonded interaction parameters. Finally, the MD parameters for all functional residues based on the RESP charges and GAFF were generated using the RESP package47 and AmberTools. For the fourth function, AmberTools was used to convert the peptide-model complex into Amber input files. Additionally, these input files in the Amber format were converted to GROMACS format using ParmEd.48

Conclusion

Herein, we reported a newly developed a biomolecular environment-mimicking model generator (BEMM-GEN), which is freely available on GitHub (https://github.com/y4suda/BEMM-GEN) and can be installed using Python package manager (pip install bemm-gen). BEMM-GEN generates spherical and cylindrical models with user-specified chemical properties, which allow for the integration of arbitrary protein structures into the generated models. BEMM-GEN provides input files for MD simulation in the AMBER/GROMACS format, and users immediately perform their MD simulations based on these input files. Furthermore, BEMM-GEN optionally generates parameters and structures for user-specified functional residues, in addition to 16 types of default residues, to designate the chemical properties. Technically, BEMM-GEN is primarily implemented in Python with widely established libraries and can be operated via simple command-line instructions. In summary, BEMM-GEN is a versatile tool for studying the biomolecular environment. Moreover, BEMM-GEN is a robust program and is easy to use in performing MD, thereby facilitating the study of protein conformations in diverse biomolecular environments.

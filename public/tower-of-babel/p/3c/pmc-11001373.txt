Clinical risk factors for peritoneal dialysis withdrawal at different dialysis duration
Authors: Weizhen Xie, Li Qin, Jiaying Huang, Aiping Gu, Wei Fang, Na Jiang, Jiangzi Yuan
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe duration of patients maintained on peritoneal dialysis (PD) varied. This study investigated the clinical risk factors for PD withdrawal at different dialysis duration.MethodsPatients who initiated PD from 1994 to 2011 were recruited and followed for at least 10 years until 2021. Patients were grouped into four groups according to dialysis duration or time on treatment (TOT) when withdrew PD.ResultsA cohort of 586 patients were enrolled (mean age of 54.9 years, median dialysis duration or TOT of 47.9 months). Patients who maintained PD for longer than 10 years were younger, with lower prevalence of diabetes, lower serum C-reactive protein (CRP) level and white blood cell (WBC) count, higher serum albumin and pre-albumin level, higher normalized protein catabolic rate (nPCR) and residual kidney function, and more common use of renin–angiotensin system inhibitors (RASi) at baseline (p < 0.05 for all). Peritonitis related death and ultrafiltration failure related HD transferring increased along with time on PD (p < 0.001). Old age, diabetes, low serum albumin, high WBC count, hypertensive nephropathy, and nonuse of RASi were associated with increased risk of non-transplantation related PD withdrawal (p < 0.05 for all). Low baseline CRP and use of RASi were independent predictors for long-term PD maintenance (p < 0.05 for all).ConclusionsLong-term PD patients demonstrated young age, low prevalence of diabetes, better nutrition status, absence of inflammation, better residual kidney function, and higher proportion of RASi usage at baseline. Absence of inflammation and use of RASi were independently associated with long-term PD maintenance.

Introduction

Peritoneal dialysis (PD) is a well-recognized dialysis modality for patients with end-stage kidney disease (ESKD), which utilizes the peritoneal membrane to clear solute wastes and extra fluid from the circulation [1]. PD is considered as a preferred treatment for newly diagnosed ESKD patients, with advantages of simple technique, home-based therapy with better quality of life, better preservation of residual kidney function, and lower costs [1].

PD has been applied in clinic for more than three decades; however, the time of patients maintain on the treatment varies widely [2]. It was reported that 6.9–29% patients withdrew PD in the first year mainly due to catheter-related complications [3,4]. On the other hand, there were 7.8% patients that could maintain PD for over 15 years [5]. The main problem associated with PD withdrawal was the high rate of technique failure, which was due to a variable of patient, center, or treatment related risk factors [4,6]. This may partly explain that PD was utilized in only 9% patients receiving kidney replacement therapy and in 11% patients undergoing dialysis worldwide [7].

How to improve PD technique outcome and consequently lengthen dialysis duration, remain to be challenging for nephrologists. In the current study, we recruited a cohort of PD patients and followed them for at least 10 years. We investigated the risk factors associated with PD discontinuation at different dialysis duration and elucidated clinical strategies to improve long-term PD maintenance.

Methods

Study design and patient recruitment

It was a retrospective study conducted at a single center. Uremic patients who received PD catheter implantation and commenced PD therapy from 1994 to 2011 at the department of Nephrology, Renji Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, Shanghai, China were recruited. We excluded patients with incomplete medical record, PD duration <3 months, whose kidney function recovered, as well as those loss of follow-up. Patients who underwent PD due to technique failure of hemodialysis or allograft failure following kidney transplantation were also excluded. All patients did not change their dialysis modality during the study period. They were followed until end points of death, transferring to HD, kidney transplantation, or 31 December 2021. Patients were grouped into four groups (≤2 years, 2 to ≤5 years, 5 to ≤10 years, or >10 years) based on dialysis vintage in December 2021 or time on treatment (TOT) when withdrew PD. TOT was defined as the duration of PD commencement to termination [8].

The study protocol was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of Renji Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, Shanghai, China (KY2021-150-B). Written informed consent was obtained from patients who were still on PD, and exempted for those who had dropped out.

Clinical data

Baseline demographic and clinical variables including age, gender, height, weight, primary kidney disease, comorbidities, initial PD modality, serological parameters, etc., were obtained. All patients were dialyzed using glucose-based PD solution with glucose concentration of 1.5%, 2.5%, or 4.25%. Dialysis-related parameters were collected at the first peritoneal equilibration test (PET) and dialysis adequacy test, which were performed 1 month after PD initiation.

Patient and PD technique outcome were recorded. Cardiovascular diseases were defined as having heart failure, arrhythmia, coronary heart disease, cerebral infarction, cerebral hemorrhage, or peripheral vascular diseases. PD-related infection included peritonitis, exit-site, and tunnel infection. The diagnosis of PD-associated peritonitis required two of the following features: (1) clinical features of abdominal pain or cloudy dialysis effluent; (2) white cell count >100/µL in dialysis effluent (after a dwell time of at least 2 h), with >50% neutrophils; and (3) positive culture of pathogen in dialysis effluent [9].

Statistical analysis

The data were presented as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed continuous variables, median (25th to 75th percentile) for non-normally distributed variables, and percentages for categorical data. The difference in continuous variables of normally distributed between groups was analyzed by ANOVA, and non-normally distributed variables were analyzed by Kruskal–Wallis H test. Difference of categorical variables between groups was analyzed by using Chi-square test. Cox proportional hazards regression model was used to analyze the risk factors for PD withdrawal and long-term PD maintenance. The following covariates were considered: age, primary kidney diseases, comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular disease), inflammation and infection biomarkers including C-reactive protein (CRP) and white blood cell (WBC), nutritional indicators including albumin, prealbumin, and normalized protein catabolic rate (nPCR), residual kidney function-related indices including urine output and measured glomerulus filtration rate (mGFR), dialysis-related parameters including ultrafiltration volume, Kt/V and Ccr, as well as concomitant medication of renin–angiotensin system inhibitors (RASi) and diuretics. Parameters with p value <0.05 in univariate Cox regression were then involved into a multivariate Cox regression analysis.

p < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant. All analyses were carried out using the SPSS PASW statistics software (version 26.0, IBM, Armonk, NY).

Results

Patient recruitment

There were 1038 uremic patients who received PD catheter implantation from 1994 to 2011 at our center. 452 patients were excluded due to incomplete record (n = 238), PD duration <3 months (n = 112), lost to follow-up (n = 96), or kidney function recovery (n = 6). A cohort of 586 PD patients were included and followed in final analysis, with 172 (29.4%) patients who maintained PD for shorter than 2 years, 161 (27.4%) patients for 2–5 years, 151 (25.8%) patients for 5–10 years, and 102 (17.4%) patients for longer than 10 years. There were 37 (6.3%) patients still kept on PD (Figure 1).

Clinical characteristic of PD patients with different dialysis duration

Patients were divided into four groups based on PD duration or TOT when withdrew from PD. Patients who maintained PD for longer time demonstrated younger age and lower prevalence of diabetes (p < 0.05 for all, Table 1). The medium PD duration or TOT was 64.3 months in young patients (age <55.5 years old (medium value), n = 289) versus 39.2 months in older patients (age ≥55.5 years, n = 297, p < 0.001). The medium PD duration or TOT was 39.2 months in patients with diabetes (n = 463) versus 53.7 months in those without diabetes (n = 123, p = 0.003). A higher percentage of patients who initiated PD after 2006 succeeded to stay on PD for longer than 2 years (49.1% for PD initiation time of 1994–1999, 65.4% for 2000–2005, and 77.7% for 2006–2011, p < 0.01, Table 1). The medium PD duration or TOT was 36.1 months for patients who initiated PD before 2006 (n = 259) versus 55.6 months for those commenced PD after 2006 (n = 327, p = 0.006). 53.6% of the patients used RASi at baseline. RASi usage proportion was higher in patients with longer PD vintage or TOT, and it was highest in patients who maintained PD for over 10 years (p < 0.001, Table 1). Diuretics were applied in 39.9% patients at baseline and the proportion was lowest in patients who discontinued PD therapy within 2 years (p < 0.001, Table 1).

Patients who have maintained PD for longer than 10 years demonstrated higher serum level of albumin, pre-albumin, intact parathyroid hormone, iron, and ferritin at baseline compared to other groups (p < 0.01 for all, Table 2). While patients who withdrew PD early showed higher baseline serum CRP level and WBC count (p < 0.01 for all, Table 2).

Reasons for PD withdrawal at different dialysis duration

There were 549 patients withdrew PD during follow-up and the medium TOT was 43.0 months (Table 4). 313 (57.0%) patients stopped PD because of death, 161 (29.3%) patients transferred to HD, and 75 (13.7%) patients received kidney transplantation. There was a higher percentage of patients with PD duration of shorter than 2 years received kidney transplantation (19.8%) and more patients in the group of TOT >10 years transferred to HD due to technique failure (47.7%, p < 0.001).

Cardiovascular diseases (38.7%) and infection (27.4%) were the leading reasons for patient death. There was a significant increase in infection-related death, mainly due to peritonitis, along with time on PD (p < 0.05). 46.8% of patients with TOT >10 years died of infection and 21.8% of them died of peritonitis.

PD-related infection (65.2%) and ultrafiltration failure (19.9%) were the main causes of PD technique failure and HD transferring. PD-related peritonitis was most common in patients with TOT of 2–5 years (72.5%). There was a remarkable increase of technique failure rate due to ultrafiltration failure accompanied with increasing dialysis duration (7.1% for TOT <2 years group, 5.0% for TOT 2–5 years group, 27.1% for TOT 5–10 years group, and 45.2% for TOT >10 years group, p < 0.001).

Factors associated with PD withdrawal and long-term PD maintenance

Multivariate Cox analysis demonstrated advanced age (HR 1.55, 95% CI 1.38–1.74), prevalence of diabetes (HR 2.20, 95% CI 1.53–3.17), high serum CRP (HR 1.44, 95% CI 1.04–1.99), low serum albumin (HR 1.59, 95% CI 1.14–2.22), high serum WBC count (HR 1.45, 95% CI 1.05–2.00), and hypertensive nephropathy (HR 1.73, 95% CI 1.06–2.83) were independently associated with increased risk of death related PD withdrawal (p < 0.05 for all, Table 5). Low baseline kidney function (measured GFR, HR 1.35, 95% CI 1.02–1.78) was identified as independently predictors of technique failure related PD withdrawal (p < 0.05, Table 5). Old age (HR 1.30, 95% CI 1.21–1.41), diabetes (HR 1.79, 95% CI 1.40–2.29), high serum CRP (HR 1.47, 95% CI 1.12–1.90), low serum albumin (HR 1.37, 95% CI 1.06–1.78), high serum WBC count (HR 1.28, 95% CI 1.04–1.58), and hypertensive nephropathy (HR 1.48, 95% CI 1.06–2.08) were independently associated with increased risk of non-transplantation related PD withdrawal (p < 0.05 for all, Table 5). Furthermore, the use of RASi was associated with reduced risk of death related (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.43–0.80), technique failure related (HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.47–0.88), and non-transplantation related (HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.51–0.77) PD withdrawal (p < 0.05 for all, Table 5).

Discussion

Maintaining PD for long is challenging due to a variable of risk factors affecting patient and technique survival. In this single-center study, we analyzed the data from a cohort of 586 PD patients to investigate patient-related risk factors for PD withdrawal at different dialysis duration. We found long-term PD patients demonstrated young age, low prevalence of diabetes, better nutrition status, absence of inflammation, better residual kidney function, and more common use of RASi at baseline. Peritonitis related death and ultrafiltration failure related HD transferring increased with time of PD. Absence of inflammation and use of RASi were independently associated with long-term PD maintenance.

In the present study, all patients were followed for at least 10 years or until PD discontinuation. We found more than half patients withdrew PD within 5 years and 17.4% patients succeeded to stay on the treatment for longer than 10 years. Median TOT in our study was significantly longer than that in a study from Canada in 2000, which reported that the percentage of patients from six North American centers surviving for more than 10 years on PD varied between 0.8% and 7.3% [10]. Longer TOT in our study may be partly attributed to improvements in PD technology and patient management in the recent years, as we found that patients who initiated PD after 2006 could maintain PD for longer time than those started the treatment before 2006. In our center, we established a serial of standard operation protocols in PD practice based on domestic and international guidelines to make sure all the patients receive professional treatments [11]. A novel PD practice ability assessment form was applied in our center to monitor the feasibility of self-conducted PD over time and decide whether an assistant was needed for the patients [12]. Furthermore, we provided continuous support to our patients throughout their PD journey. Individual adjustments to the treatment plan were made based on person-centered and shared-decision making PD prescription approach. More recently, Kee et al. reported from Korea that 7.8% patients managed to maintain PD for over than 15 years and only 26.3% patients in their center terminated the treatment within 5 years [5]. We noted neutral low GDP solution was applied in approximately half of the patients in their study while all patients in our center were using glucose-based dialysates. It may explain the relative longer TOT as use of neutral low GDP solution was identified as a factor associated with long-term PD maintenance [5]. Another longitudinal observational study from southern China indicated that only 8% of patients who initiated PD from 2006 to 2008 maintained the treatment for longer than 10 years, which may due to a high proportion (25.1%) of patients received kidney transplantation in their study [13].

We found that long-term PD patients (TOT >10 years) demonstrated younger age and lower prevalence of diabetes at baseline. Moreover, advanced age and presence of diabetes were shown to be independent risk factors associated with death related PD withdrawal, but not technique failure censored PD discontinuation. Similarly, two recent studies reported old age was associated with increased risk of composite end points of death or transfer to HD, but lower risk of transfer to HD [8,14]. Generally, young patients showed overall better healthy status than the elderly and they tended to maintain PD for a long time [15]. On the other hand, older patients exhibited a variety of comorbidities and complications, which increased the difficulties of transferring to HD. Prevalence of diabetes has been reported to be an independent predictor for poor patient and technique prognosis in PD patients, because notable comorbidities were presented in these patients [16,17]. Apart from this, fluid ultrafiltration impairment was more common in diabetic PD patients due to lower glucose-based osmotic pressure difference across serum and dialysate, which may further worsen the patient outcome.

Malnutrition was a pronounced risk factor associated with both patient death and technique failure in patients undergoing PD [18]. Moreover, malnutrition usually co-existed with inflammation, which was another nontraditional predictor of poor patient and technique survival [19]. In this study, patients with longer TOT demonstrated better nutrition status (higher serum albumin, globulin, pre-albumin, and nPCR) and lower serum CRP level and WBC count at the initiation of PD, indicating patients without malnutrition, inflammation, and infection were more competent to maintain PD for long time. In addition, low baseline serum CRP was identified as an independent factor associated with long-term maintenance of PD, which highlights the importance of correcting systematic and local inflammation. Furthermore, we found higher baseline residual kidney function was present in patients succeeded to keep long-term PD. The finding was consistent with previous reports, which indicated low estimated GFR was associated with PD discontinuation resulted from both technique failure and patient death [15,20]. It was demonstrated that residual kidney function contributed to uremic toxin clearance, fluid management, and nutrition maintenance, which directly related to better patient outcome [21,22]. In addition, loss of residual kidney function served as a risk factor for the development of PD-related peritonitis [23]. It has been documented that use of RASi was beneficial in slowing down the decline of kidney function [24]. It may explain our findings that patients who maintained PD for over 10 years showed highest proportion of RASi use at baseline and it was an independent beneficial factor associated with long-term PD.

It has been documented that death is the most important reason for PD termination [8,25]. Indeed, 57% patients in the current study discontinued PD during follow-up because of death and there was no significant difference in the proportion of death-censored PD withdrawal in each group with different dialysis duration. The mortality of long-term PD patients in the current study was significantly higher than that in the report from Kee et al. [5], probably due to the relative senior age of our patients. In line with previous study, cardiovascular disease and infection were found to be the common causes for patient death [26]. There was a remarkable increase in peritonitis related mortality along with time on PD, indicating worse outcome of peritonitis in long-term PD patients. The finding was consistent with a recent study form China, which demonstrated that dialysis duration was an independent risk factor for poor prognosis in PD-related peritonitis [27].

We further observed that PD technique failure rate increased accompanied with time on PD and it accounted for 47.7% PD withdrawal in patients with TOT >10 years. It was mainly due to the high prevalence (21.5%) of ultrafiltration failure in long-term PD group, which was comparable to the results of another longitudinal study conducted in China [13]. Kee et al. did not find any difference in the causes of PD discontinuation between short-term and long-term PD patients, and ultrafiltration failure caused technique failure rate in their study was much lower [5]. It may be related to the common use of biocompatible PD solution in their patients, which showed benefits in preservation of the structural and functional integrity of the peritoneum [28]. PD-related peritonitis remained to be the leading cause of PD technique failure in the current study, which was in line with findings in previous reports [25,29]. Peritonitis censored PD withdrawal in our study was most common in patients with TOT of 2–5 years. However, in the study of Chung et al., the association between peritonitis and PD discontinuation was greatest during the first year and persisted significantly after 5 years [30]. It is noted that the follow-up time of patients in Chung et al.’s study was significantly shorter than that in our study and there were 62.1% patients that did not experience peritonitis, which might explain the inconsistent findings of the two studies.

There were several limitations in the current study that needed to be discussed. First of all, it was a retrospective study performed in a single center, and so the findings shall be confirmed by prospective studies. Secondly, we only had baseline characteristics of the studied patients and so were not able to investigate the impact of longitudinal changes of clinical parameters on PD outcome. In addition, all patients were dialyzed with dextrose PD solution and most of them performed PD manually. Thus, the results were not be able to extend to patients using new generations of dialysates including icodextrin-based solution, low GDP solution, etc., as well as those conducted PD using cyclers.

In conclusion, we found young age, low prevalence of diabetes, better nutrition status, absence of inflammation, better residual kidney function, and use of RASi were representative characteristics for long-term PD patients. PD withdrawal due to peritonitis related patient death and ultrafiltration failure related technique failure increased accompanied with time on PD. Use of RASi was associated with reduced risk of PD withdrawal. Absence of inflammation and RASi use were independently associated with long-term PD maintenance.

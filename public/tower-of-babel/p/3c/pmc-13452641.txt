Extensive Axillary Nodal Metastasis Despite a Clinically Negative Axilla in Invasive Lobular Carcinoma: A Case Report
Authors: Rana Alkhelaif, Latifah A Alfaraj, Gheed M Albalali, Saud Alrasheedi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC) is a distinct subtype of breast cancer characterized by an infiltrative growth pattern that frequently limits the accuracy of clinical examination and conventional imaging. Although most ILCs are hormone receptor-positive and are often associated with favorable prognostic expectations, accurate diagnosis and staging require careful integration of imaging findings, histopathologic assessment, and definitive surgical evaluation. We present the case of a 50-year-old premenopausal woman who presented with a progressively enlarging right breast mass of more than two months’ duration. Clinical examination revealed a firm breast lesion with skin tethering and no palpable axillary lymphadenopathy. Imaging demonstrated an irregular non-mass lesion in the right breast, and biopsy confirmed ILC with strong estrogen and progesterone receptor positivity, HER2 negativity, low proliferative index, and loss of E-cadherin expression. Despite favorable tumor biology and a clinically negative axilla, intraoperative sentinel lymph node biopsy using dual tracer mapping with methylene blue dye and technetium-99m radiotracer identified metastatic involvement, prompting completion axillary lymph node dissection. Final pathology revealed eight positive lymph nodes with extranodal extension, corresponding to pT2 pN2a disease. The patient underwent right skin-sparing mastectomy with immediate reconstruction, followed by multidisciplinary planning for adjuvant systemic therapy, radiotherapy, and long-term endocrine therapy. This case highlights that hormone receptor-positive ILC may still harbor significant regional nodal disease, underscoring the importance of comprehensive staging and multidisciplinary management.

Introduction

Invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC) accounts for approximately 10%-15% of invasive breast cancers and represents a distinct histologic and clinical subtype compared with invasive ductal carcinoma [1]. It is characterized by loss of intercellular adhesion, most commonly due to dysfunction of the E-cadherin-catenin complex, resulting in a diffuse, discohesive growth pattern in which tumor cells infiltrate the breast parenchyma in a characteristic single-file (“Indian file”) pattern [1,2].

This infiltrative growth pattern often limits the formation of a discrete mass, leading to delayed detection and underestimation of tumor extent on both clinical examination and conventional imaging modalities [1,3]. Radiologically, ILC may present as subtle architectural distortion, ill-defined hypoechoic regions, or non-mass abnormalities on ultrasound and mammography [3,4]. Breast magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) plays an important adjunctive role, offering superior sensitivity in delineating tumor extent and identifying multifocal or regional non-mass enhancement not readily apparent on conventional imaging [5].

From a biological perspective, most ILCs are hormone receptor-positive and are typically classified within luminal molecular subtypes, which are generally associated with favorable responsiveness to endocrine therapy [2]. However, tumor biology alone does not reliably predict anatomic disease burden. Axillary lymph node involvement remains a major prognostic factor in breast cancer and is critical in guiding adjuvant systemic therapy and radiotherapy decisions [6,7].

Importantly, ILC may harbor occult axillary nodal metastases despite a clinically negative axilla. In a recent study of patients with early-stage, clinically node-negative, ER-positive/HER2-negative ILC, 20.2% were found to have pathologically confirmed axillary nodal metastases, underscoring the limitations of clinical examination and conventional imaging in excluding nodal disease [8,9]. Therefore, surgical staging with sentinel lymph node biopsy remains essential even in patients with apparently favorable tumor biology and no clinical evidence of nodal involvement [10]. The present case highlights these diagnostic and staging challenges by demonstrating extensive axillary nodal metastasis in a patient with hormone receptor-positive ILC and a clinically negative axilla.

Case presentation

A 50-year-old Saudi premenopausal woman was referred to the Breast Surgery Clinic at Security Forces Hospital, Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, in October 2025 for evaluation of a right breast mass that she had noticed gradually enlarging over more than two months. She reported regular menstrual cycles and had four children. There was no personal or family history of breast or ovarian malignancy. She had no history of gynecologic disease. Her past medical history was notable only for well-controlled hypertension and hypothyroidism, both managed with medical therapy.

On clinical breast examination, a firm mass measuring approximately 3 × 1.5 cm was palpated in the upper outer quadrant of the right breast. The lesion was associated with skin tethering, but there was no overlying erythema, peau d’orange, nipple retraction, or nipple discharge. Examination of both axillae did not reveal palpable lymphadenopathy. Diagnostic mammography had been performed at the referring institution before referral and was therefore not repeated at our institution. Breast ultrasonography demonstrated an irregular, heterogeneous, hypervascular non-mass lesion in the upper outer quadrant of the right breast corresponding to the palpable abnormality. The lesion was ill-defined and difficult to measure precisely, consistent with the infiltrative growth pattern of ILC. Additional small, circumscribed lesions with benign imaging features were identified in the right breast and the contralateral breast. The visualized axillary lymph nodes appeared morphologically benign (Figure 1).

Breast ultrasonography demonstrating an irregular, heterogeneous, hypervascular non-mass lesion in the upper outer quadrant of the right breast. The yellow arrows indicate the margins of the ill-defined lesion corresponding to the palpable abnormality, consistent with the infiltrative growth pattern of invasive lobular carcinoma. The visualized axillary lymph nodes were morphologically benign, with a cortical thickness of 0.16 cm and preserved fatty hila.

Given the clinical findings, breast MRI was performed to further delineate the extent of disease and evaluate the axilla (Figure 2).

Breast magnetic resonance imaging demonstrating an irregular, spiculated, heterogeneously enhancing mass in the upper outer quadrant of the right breast measuring approximately 6.1 × 2.6 cm. The yellow arrow indicates the enhancing invasive lobular carcinoma. In addition, regional clumped non-mass enhancement extending beyond the area visualized on ultrasonography was identified, suggesting more extensive disease involvement.

Ultrasound-guided core needle biopsy confirmed ILC, Nottingham grade 1 [6]. Immunohistochemical analysis demonstrated estrogen receptor positivity in 95% of tumor cells (moderate to strong intensity), progesterone receptor positivity in 100% of tumor cells (strong intensity), HER2 negativity (score 0), a Ki-67 proliferative index of 5%, and complete loss of E-cadherin expression. CT imaging and bone scan showed no evidence of distant metastatic disease (Figure 3).

Axial contrast-enhanced CT image of the chest at the level of the mediastinum demonstrating no evidence of distant metastatic disease or suspicious intrathoracic lesions.

The case was discussed at a multidisciplinary tumor board involving breast surgery, radiology, pathology, medical oncology, radiation oncology, and plastic surgery. Given the imaging findings suggestive of extensive disease and the confirmed diagnosis of ILC, consensus was reached to proceed with upfront surgical management.

Intraoperatively, sentinel lymph node mapping was performed using the dual tracer technique with methylene blue dye and technetium-99m radiotracer. A handheld gamma probe was used in conjunction with visual identification of blue-stained lymphatic channels to localize the sentinel lymph nodes. A total of six sentinel lymph nodes were identified and excised for intraoperative assessment.

Frozen section analysis revealed metastatic carcinoma, including micrometastatic deposits. Based on these findings, completion axillary lymph node dissection of levels I and II was performed through the same incision. A right skin-sparing mastectomy was then completed, followed by immediate placement of a tissue expander by the plastic surgery team. The excised breast specimen was oriented and marked prior to submission for definitive histopathologic evaluation (Figure 4).

Intraoperative view of the excised right breast specimen following skin-sparing mastectomy.

Final histopathologic examination of the mastectomy specimen confirmed ILC, Nottingham grade 2 [6]. Two distinct invasive foci measuring 5.0 × 3.0 × 2.5 cm and 0.7 × 0.3 cm were identified, involving the upper and lower outer quadrants of the right breast. Neither ductal carcinoma in situ nor lobular carcinoma in situ was identified. Lymphovascular invasion, perineural invasion, and dermal lymphatic infiltration were not identified. Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) comprised less than 10% of the stromal area. All surgical margins were free of tumor involvement. Representative histopathologic findings of both the breast tumor and axillary lymph node metastasis are shown in Figure 5.

Histopathologic findings. (A) Microscopic view of the breast demonstrating invasive lobular carcinoma characterized by discohesive tumor cells infiltrating the fibrous stroma in a characteristic single-file (“Indian file”) pattern. (B) Histologic section of an axillary lymph node demonstrating metastatic carcinoma with extranodal extension beyond the nodal capsule into the surrounding adipose tissue.

Axillary evaluation demonstrated metastatic carcinoma in five of six sentinel lymph nodes. Completion axillary lymph node dissection yielded 13 additional lymph nodes, of which three were positive for metastatic disease. Extranodal extension was present in the involved nodes. In total, eight of 19 lymph nodes were positive for metastatic carcinoma, corresponding to a final pathologic stage of pT2 pN2a [7]. These findings confirmed a substantial axillary nodal disease burden despite the absence of clinically detectable axillary lymphadenopathy before surgery.

The postoperative course was uneventful. The patient remained hemodynamically stable, achieved satisfactory pain control, and was mobilized early. Surgical wounds demonstrated appropriate healing without evidence of hematoma, seroma, or infection. Drains were maintained under negative pressure with monitored output, and she was discharged home in stable condition with scheduled outpatient follow-up.

The case was subsequently reviewed in a multidisciplinary team meeting. In light of the multiple positive lymph nodes and extranodal extension, adjuvant systemic chemotherapy was recommended, followed by radiotherapy to the chest wall and regional nodal basins. Long-term endocrine therapy was also planned, given the strong hormone receptor positivity. The treatment strategy was determined through integration of tumor biology, final pathologic staging, and multidisciplinary consensus.

Discussion

ILC presents unique diagnostic and staging challenges due to its diffuse infiltrative growth pattern and lack of cohesive tumor architecture [1,2]. Unlike invasive ductal carcinoma, ILC typically spreads in a single-file arrangement without a prominent desmoplastic response, which limits both palpability and radiographic conspicuity [1]. These characteristics frequently result in underestimation of tumor size and extent on physical examination, mammography, and ultrasound [3,4].

Breast MRI has been shown to improve delineation of disease extent in ILC by identifying regional and clumped non-mass enhancement that may represent multifocal or multicentric disease not detected on conventional imaging [5]. In the present case, MRI demonstrated more extensive disease than was appreciated clinically and on ultrasound, consistent with these known limitations.

Although ILC is most commonly hormone receptor-positive and generally associated with favorable biologic behavior, prognosis in breast cancer is determined by both tumor biology and anatomic disease burden [2]. Axillary lymph node involvement remains one of the most important prognostic factors and plays a central role in determining adjuvant systemic therapy and radiotherapy [8,9].

Importantly, ILC may harbor significant axillary nodal metastasis despite a clinically negative axilla, likely due to its diffuse growth pattern and potential for delayed detection [10,11]. In the present case, the absence of palpable lymphadenopathy and benign-appearing axillary nodes on imaging contrasted with the final pathology demonstrating extensive nodal involvement with extranodal extension.

Sentinel lymph node biopsy remains the standard approach for axillary staging in clinically node-negative breast cancer and continues to play a critical role even in patients with favorable tumor biology [8]. In this patient, intraoperative detection of nodal metastasis using dual tracer mapping directly altered surgical management by prompting completion axillary lymph node dissection, ensuring accurate staging and appropriate oncologic treatment.

This case underscores the importance of comprehensive staging and multidisciplinary management in ILC. Integration of clinical assessment, advanced imaging, intraoperative findings, and definitive pathology is essential to guide individualized treatment decisions. Although hormone receptor-positive ILC is generally associated with favorable tumor biology and responsiveness to endocrine therapy, hormone receptor positivity does not reliably predict the absence of regional nodal metastasis. In a recent study, 20.2% of patients with early-stage, clinically node-negative, ER-positive/HER2-negative ILC were found to have pathologically confirmed axillary nodal metastases [9]. In the present case, despite strong estrogen and progesterone receptor positivity, eight of 19 axillary lymph nodes were positive for metastatic disease. Sentinel lymph node biopsy remains the standard approach for axillary staging in clinically node-negative breast cancer regardless of histologic subtype and should not be omitted solely on the basis of favorable tumor biology [10].

Conclusions

ILC may present with significant axillary nodal metastasis despite hormone receptor-positive biomarkers and a clinically negative axilla. This case highlights the importance of comprehensive imaging, careful surgical staging, and multidisciplinary management to ensure accurate assessment and appropriate treatment.

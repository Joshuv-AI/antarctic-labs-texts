VLX-1005, but not argatroban, prevents ITAM-mediated platelet activation and heparin-induced thrombocytopenia
Authors: Adriana Yamaguchi, Victoria Putzbach, Reheman Adili, Sophia Lazar, Livia Stanger, Devin Gilmore, David J Maloney, Steven E McKenzie, James V Michael, Michael Holinstat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Key Points•VLX-1005 demonstrates potential as an effective treatment for HIT by preventing platelet activation without increasing bleeding risk.•The efficacy and safety of VLX-1005 with a direct thrombin inhibitor for patients with HIT have been evaluated in a phase 2 clinical trial.

Introduction

Unfractionated heparin and low-molecular-weight heparin have broad clinical applications as anticoagulants in prophylaxis and in the treatment of thromboembolic diseases and invasive cardiac procedures.1,2 However, heparin can adversely affect a subset of patients by causing heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT).3 In the United States, the incidence of HIT ranges from 0.1% to 5% in heparin-treated patients, with an estimated 30 000 cases of HIT per year. Although HIT was first described in the late 1950s,4 it was not until the 1990s that the target antigen was identified5 and the 2000s when the first therapies were approved by the US Food and Drug Administration.2

HIT is an immune thrombotic thrombocytopenia disorder characterized by platelet-activating immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies that target complexes formed between platelet factor 4 (PF4) and heparin.5 PF4 is an abundant cationic protein stored in the α-granules of platelets that is released upon platelet activation and interacts with heparin through electrostatic interactions, forming PF4/heparin complexes.6,7 These complexes are immunogenic and may trigger the development of specific IgG antibodies targeted against the complex. When IgG antibodies bind to PF4/heparin complexes, it leads to the formation of ultralarge immune complexes.2,8 The ultralarge immune complexes initiate a prothrombotic response, and the risk of thrombosis is sustained by large oligomeric immune complexes on the surface of platelets, endothelial cells, monocytes, and neutrophils, which lead to activation of FcγRIIa receptors.9 More specifically, in platelets, the binding of IgG antibodies to PF4/heparin complexes leads to cross-linking of the immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motif (ITAM)–bearing receptor FcγRIIa, which activates platelets via Syk and the LAT signalosome. Aberrant platelet activation can lead to consumptive thrombocytopenia. The combination of consumptive thrombocytopenia with extensive thrombin generation increase the risk of both venous and arterial thromboembolic complications, which can be limb threatening or result in death.2

In cases involving clinical suspicion of HIT, the current guidelines recommend the discontinuation of heparin therapy.1 However, the cessation of heparin does not eliminate the risk of a thrombotic event. It is important to initiate therapy with nonheparin parenteral anticoagulants to directly or indirectly inhibit thrombin generation.9 Currently, the only therapy approved by the US Food and Drug Administration is the direct thrombin inhibitor argatroban.10 Given the high rates of recurrent thromboembolism and bleeding complications associated with nonheparin anticoagulant therapies,3 there is an unmet need for novel approaches to treat HIT that reduce FcγRIIA-mediated platelet activation in addition to anticoagulation. Our laboratories have previously demonstrated that 12-lipoxygenase (12-LOX), an oxygenase expressed constitutively in platelets, plays a critical role in platelet activation through the immune complex receptor FcγRIIa.11 We have subsequently developed a potent and selective inhibitor of 12-LOX known as VLX-1005, previously known as ML355.11,12 Although VLX-1005 is established to reduce the risk of ITAM-mediated platelet activation, its utility in combination with the direct thrombin inhibitor argatroban to limit platelet activation, thrombin generation, and thrombin effects on platelets and other blood cells has not been assessed. This represents a critical gap in our existing knowledge: whether a dual therapeutic approach with VLX-1005 and anticoagulation for treatment of HIT will be effective in patients after heparin cessation. In this study, we sought to determine the effectiveness of VLX-1005 alone or in combination with argatroban in preventing or treating HIT. This study represents, to our knowledge, the first demonstration that VLX-1005 has the potential to be an effective dual treatment for HIT by preventing ITAM-mediated platelet activation. Importantly, our study suggests VLX-1005 treatment does not carry the bleeding risk frequently associated with direct thrombin inhibitors, such as argatroban.

Materials and methods

Blood collection and platelet isolation

Blood was collected via venipuncture in BD vacutainers containing 3.2% sodium citrate (Greiner Bio-One, Monroe, MC) and centrifuged at 200g for 15 minutes to obtain platelet-rich plasma. Acid citrate dextrose (2.5% sodium citrate tribasic, 1.5% citric acid, and 2% d-glucose) and apyrase (0.02 U/mL) were added to the platelet-rich plasma, which was then centrifuged at 2000g for 10 minutes.13,14 Platelets were resuspended at a physiological concentration of 300 × 109 platelets per L in Tyrode buffer (10mM HEPES [N-2-hydroxyethylpiperazine-N′-2-ethanesulfonic acid], 12mM NaHCO3, 127mM NaCl, 5mM KCl, 0.5mM NaH2PO4, 1mM MgCl2, 5mM d-glucose, pH 7.4), as determined by a complete blood cell counter (Hemavet 950FS; Drew Scientific, Miami Lakes, FL).

Washed human platelet aggregation

Washed human platelets at physiological concentration (3 × 108 platelets per mL) were incubated with VLX-1005 (0.55μM, 1.81μM, 6.02μM, or 20μM) or an equivalent amount of vehicle control (saline) at 37°C for 10 minutes. The concentration range chosen for this study was within the observed achievable range in the phase 1 human multiple ascending-dose study for VLX-1005, in which patients were dosed up to 400 mg per hour, and the phase 2 studies, in which patients are being dosed at 200 mg VLX-1005 twice daily, with a subsequent blood concentration of VLX-1005 >100μM.15 After incubation, a solution of human PF4 (10 μg/mL) and unfractionated heparin (0.1 U/mL) was added to pretreated platelets, and they were incubated at 37°C for 10 minutes. Platelet aggregation in response to PF4- and anti-PF4-H complex antibodies (KKO; 100 μg/mL), a monoclonal antibody directed against human PF4/heparin,16 was measured in a lumi-aggregometer (Model 700D; Chrono-log, Havertown, PA). Light transmission and luminescence were monitored in real time at 37°C for 5 minutes under stirring conditions (1200 revolutions per minute).14

Human whole blood platelet aggregation

Sodium-citrated human whole blood was diluted in saline (1:1 volume-to-volume ratio [v/v]) before treatment with VLX-1005 (20μM, 50μM, or 100μM), argatroban (1μM or 5μM), or an equivalent amount of vehicle control (saline) at 37°C for 10 minutes. Platelet aggregation in response to collagen (5 μg/mL; Chrono-log) was measured in a lumi-aggregometer (Model 700D; Chrono-log). Light transmission and luminescence were monitored in real time at 37°C for 5 minutes under stirring conditions (1200 revolutions per minute).14

Ex vivo microfluidic perfusion flow chamber

Microfluidic perfusion chamber slides (μ-Slide VI 0.1; ibidi, Martinsried, Germany) were coated with 100 μg/mL type I collagen (Chrono-Log) at 4°C overnight. Freshly drawn citrated human whole blood was incubated with VLX-1005 (100μM), argatroban (1μM or 5μM), or an equivalent volume of vehicle control (saline) at 37°C for 10 minutes. Platelets were fluorescently labeled by incubating 2μM DiOC6 (3,3′-dihexyloxacarbocyanine iodide; Invitrogen, Waltham, MA) at 37°C for 5 minutes. Stained whole blood was recalcified with 5mM CaCl2 and immediately perfused at arterial shear (1800 per second) through a coated microfluidic slide heated to 37°C using a syringe pump (Harvard Apparatus, Holliston, MA).13 Platelet adhesion and accumulation were recorded in real time for 4 minutes under an inverted fluorescent microscope (Zeiss Axio Observer Z1 Marianas; 20× objective). Platelet accumulation was quantified by mean fluorescent intensity using Slidebook 6.0 (Intelligent Imaging Innovations).

Mice

All experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee at the University of Michigan (PRO00010098) and Thomas Jefferson University (01203-2). Male and female mice aged 8 to 12 weeks were used for this study. The wild-type (WT) C57BL/6 mice used for this study were purchased from The Jackson Laboratory (Bar Harbor, ME). WT mice do not express FcγRIIa, nor do their platelets express any Fcγ receptors. Therefore, expression of the human FcγRIIa transgene is required when murine models are used to assess immune-mediated thrombotic thrombocytopenia disorders, such as HIT.17 Human FcγRIIa (hFcR) transgenic (tg) mice were used as previously described.18 hFcR mice were bred with platelet 12-LOX knockout mice (12-LOX˗/˗) to generate FcγRIIa tg mice deficient in platelet 12-LOX (hFcR+/+/12-LOX˗/˗) as previously described.11 Blood was drawn from the saphenous vein of the FcγRIIa tg mice and collected in Microvette tubes containing EDTA (Sarstedt). Blood cell count was determined by a complete blood cell counter (Hemavet 950FS; Drew Scientific, Miami Lakes, FL). FcγRIIa tg mice were observed to have body size, platelet, white cell, and red blood cell counts similar to WT mice.

In vivo HIT model

hFcR WT (12-LOX+/+) and hFcR+/+/12-LOX˗/˗ mice were anesthetized and challenged with anti-GPIX (Emfret; M051-0) labeled with Alexa Fluor 750 (Molecular Probes; S30046) at a concentration of 0.1 μg/g bodyweight via a retro-orbital injection. After indicated times, mice were perfused with 1× Dulbecco’s phosphate buffered saline, and the lungs were inflated with 2% agarose before resection and imaging on a LI-COR (Lincoln, NE) Odyssey imager. The Thrombosis score was determined as previously described using Nikon NIS Elements software.19,20

Thromboelastography (TEG) assay

Freshly drawn citrated human whole blood (blood-to-citrate ratio, 1:9 v/v) was incubated with VLX-1005 (20μM or 100μM), argatroban (2 μg/mL), or an equivalent amount of vehicle control (1% d-sorbitol in saline; 1:1 v/v) at 37°C for 10 minutes. Although VLX-1005 was resuspended in saline, argatroban (Viatris Mylan Pharmaceuticals, Canonsburg, PA) was diluted in 1% d-sorbitol, per the manufacturer’s instruction. After treatment, 10mM CaCl2 was added to whole blood, and viscoelastic properties of whole blood clot formation were studied under low shear stress using the Haemoscope TEG 5000 Thromboelastograph hemostasis analyzer (Haemonetics Corp, Braintree, MA), as previously described.21 Major coagulation parameters including R time (the time to formation of the initial fibrin threads), α-angle (the rapidity with which the clot forms), K time (the time until the clot reaches a certain strength), and maximum amplitude (clot’s maximum strength)22 were analyzed and compared.

Tail bleeding assay

Male and female WT mice (aged 8-12 weeks) were anesthetized with ketamine/xylazine and received an IV injection of VLX-1005 (7 mg/kg), argatroban (4 mg/kg), or an equivalent amount of vehicle control (1% d-sorbitol in saline, 1:49 v/v). After 10 minutes, mice were placed on a heating pad in prone position, and the tip of the tail (5 mm) was excised with a sterile scalpel. Tails were immediately immersed into isotonic solution warmed to 37°C. Bleeding was assessed until cessation of blood flow from the tail for 1 minute, as described previously.14

All research involving human participants was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the University of Michigan Institutional Review Board (approval number HUM00188719). Before blood collection, written informed consent was obtained from all male and female participants in this study.

Results

VLX-1005 attenuates KKO-mediated human washed platelet aggregation and prevents ITAM-induced human whole-blood impedance

Our previous study with the 12-LOX inhibitor VLX-1005 (previously known as ML35511,12) has demonstrated attenuation of FcγRIIa-mediated platelet aggregation in response to FcγRIIa antibody cross-linking.11 In this study, VLX-1005 was directly assessed for its ability to regulate human platelet aggregation after challenge with HIT immune complex ex vivo. To mimic the physiopathology of HIT, washed human platelets were treated with vehicle control or VLX-1005 (0.55μM, 1.81μM, 6.02μM, or 20μM) and then exposed to human PF4 and unfractionated heparin (Figure 1A-B). Platelet aggregation was assessed in response to the HIT-like monoclonal antibody KKO, directed against human PF4/heparin.16 Substantial aggregation is observed in platelets stimulated with the HIT immune complex, whereas treatment with 20μM VLX-1005 significantly attenuated platelet aggregation.

Collagen-induced aggregation (5 μg/mL) was assessed in whole blood treated with vehicle control or VLX-1005 (20μM, 50μM, or 100μM; Figure 1C-D). Collagen is the main endogenous agonist of the glycoprotein VI (GPVI) receptor, which, in platelets, signals through an ITAM, such as FcγRIIa.23 Although 20μM VLX-1005 attenuated washed platelet aggregation, in whole blood, treatment with higher concentrations of VLX-1005 (50μM and 100μM) showed significant attenuation of aggregation compared to vehicle control.

Given that argatroban is the only therapy approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for HIT,10 we wanted to determine whether inhibition of aggregation by VLX-1005 persists when it is combined with argatroban in response to ITAM-mediated stimulation. Collagen-induced (5 μg/mL) platelet aggregation was assessed in whole blood treated with vehicle control, VLX-1005 (100μM), and/or argatroban (1μM and 5μM; Figure 1E-F). As expected, no effect on aggregation was observed in whole blood treated with argatroban alone. Notably, VLX-1005 significantly inhibited aggregation in whole blood either treated with VLX-1005 alone or in combination with argatroban.

VLX-1005 attenuates adhesion and accumulation of platelets under shear stress

To assess whether the antiplatelet effects of VLX-1005 would remain under physiological shear conditions, the ability of human platelets to bind to collagen was measured using a collagen-coated microfluidic chamber (Figure 2). Human whole blood was treated with vehicle control, argatroban (1μM or 5μM), or VLX-1005 (100μM). Blood was perfused under arterial flow conditions. Although treatment with argatroban had no effect on platelet adhesion and accumulation compared to vehicle control, treatment with VLX-1005 significantly attenuated platelet adhesion and accumulation under flow. As observed in aggregometry experiments, the effect of VLX-1005 persisted when blood was treated with a combination of VLX-1005 and argatroban.

12-LOX is required for platelet consumption and thrombosis after HIT-like challenge

A HIT-like challenge was induced in mice expressing tg human FcγRIIa that were either WT or knockout for 12-LOX (hFcR+/+/12-LOX+/+ and hFcR+/+/12-LOX˗/˗, respectively) by the administration of Alexa Fluor 750–labeled anti-GPIX. Previous studies by our group have reproducibly demonstrated that anti-mouse GPIX promotes thrombocytopenia and thrombosis in a FcγRIIa-dependent manner.24 A schematic diagram of studies in vivo is shown in Figure 3A. Four hours after retro-orbital administration of anti-GPIX, platelet count was assessed before the resection and inflation of pulmonary tissue to quantify thrombotic burden in the lung. Representative lung images are shown in Figure 3B. In concordance with previous reports, hFcR+/+/12-LOX+/+ mice experienced severe thrombocytopenia and thrombosis.19 Of note, hFcR+/+/12-LOX˗/˗ mice challenged with anti-GPIX were completely refractory to HIT pathology, as assessed by platelet count and thrombosis score (Figure 3C-D). Taken together, these data demonstrate that intact platelet 12-LOX signaling is necessary for severe HIT-mediated thrombocytopenia and thrombosis.

VLX-1005 does not affect coagulation or increase the risk of bleeding

The inhibition of platelet reactivity reduces the risk of thrombosis; however, decreased platelet activation may result in an increased risk of bleeding.25 To examine whether the decreased thrombotic risk resulting from treatment with VLX-1005 alters coagulation or bleeding risk (Figure 4), mice were treated via IV administration of vehicle control, argatroban (4 mg/kg), and/or VLX-1005 (7 mg/kg). After treatment, the distal 5 mm of the tail was resected, and the tail was placed in saline at 37°C. The elapsed time between tail resection and bleeding cessation was recorded as the tail bleeding time. Treatment with VLX-1005 did not alter tail bleeding time in mice compared to the vehicle control. Although increased bleeding time was observed in mice administered argatroban alone or dual treatment with VLX-1005 and argatroban, it did not meet statistical significance (Figure 4A). To further assess the potential of VLX-1005 to alter coagulation in blood, human whole blood was treated with vehicle control, argatroban (2 μg/mL), and/or VLX-1005 (20μM or 100μM), and coagulation was assessed using TEG. TEG activates the contact pathway in blood, and by analyzing the TEG curves, it is possible to assess changes in coagulation activity and clotting time. No TEG parameters were altered in blood treated with VLX-1005 compared to the vehicle control (Figure 4B-F). However, treatment with argatroban alone or argatroban with VLX-1005 showed a significant delay in clot initiation time (R time), time until clot formation (α-angle), and time to reach a defined strength (K time). Taken together, these data suggest that VLX-1005 does not significantly impair hemostatic response or coagulation.

Discussion

In HIT, ultralarge immune complexes formed through the binding of IgG antibodies to PF4/heparin cause thrombocytopenia and thrombosis via activation of platelets through the immune complex receptor FcγRIIa.5 The oxygenase 12-LOX is highly and constitutively expressed in platelets and known to be responsible for the oxidation of arachidonic acid to form the bioactive metabolite 12-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid.26 In addition to its function in fatty acid metabolism in platelets, our group has previously demonstrated that 12-LOX plays a critical role in FcγRIIa-mediated platelet activation.11 Furthermore, we have shown that the 12-LOX inhibitor VLX-1005 inhibited FcγRIIa-mediated platelet aggregation and granule secretion.11 Argatroban is currently the only US Food and Drug Administration–approved drug for the treatment of patients with HIT in the United States. As a direct thrombin inhibitor, argatroban targets thrombin, but it exhibits limited efficacy as an antithrombotic agent and causes major bleeding,27 which demonstrates that alternative approaches to treat HIT are warranted.

Due to the complex nature of HIT, it is important to determine whether dual inhibition targeting both ITAM-mediated platelet activation with VLX-1005 and argatroban is an effective strategy for limiting the life-threatening clots that form in blood during HIT. Therefore, the effects of VLX-1005 were assessed in ex vivo experiments that mimic the physiopathology of HIT. Human washed platelets were treated with VLX-1005 before being exposed to human PF4 and unfractionated heparin. Subsequently, platelets were stimulated with KKO. The concentration range of VLX-1005 chosen for this study fall within the observed concentration of VLX-1005 in the phase 1 and phase 2 studies in which 200-mg VLX-1005 was shown to yield a concentration above the highest dose (100μM) of VLX-1005 used in this study.15 Treatment with VLX-1005 significantly attenuated ITAM-mediated human platelet aggregation (Figure 1A).

Human platelets express several receptors that contain or are associated with ITAM-bearing transmembrane receptors, including FcγRIIa and GPVI.11 Collagen is the main endogenous agonist of the GPVI receptor in platelets.23 Because argatroban directly inhibits thrombin, we wanted to assess whether VLX-1005 is effective in attenuating platelet activation through a non–thrombin-mediated activation pathway. Impedance was assessed as a measurement of blood clotting in human whole blood pretreated with VLX-1005 and/or argatroban after collagen stimulation. VLX-1005 elicited protection against ITAM-induced activation, suggesting that pharmacological inhibition of 12-LOX could attenuate platelet activation via FcγRIIa/ultralarge complex binding (Figure 1C).12 This protection persisted when human whole blood was treated with a dual treatment of VLX-1005 and argatroban but not with argatroban alone (Figure 1E).

Upon initial contact with the injured vessel wall, platelet adhesion is the first step in platelet activation and clot formation.28,29 To assess whether the in vitro inhibitory effects of VLX-1005 remain under physiological shear conditions, platelet adhesion and accumulation were assessed in human whole blood treated with VLX-1005 and/or argatroban as it flowed over collagen at arterial shear rates. In accordance with the observed in vitro inhibition of platelet aggregation, VLX-1005, but not argatroban, was shown to impair platelet-surface and platelet-platelet interactions under arterial flow conditions (Figure 2).

Our findings demonstrate that the administration of VLX-1005 can prevent the drop in platelet count and thrombotic burden in murine models of HIT (Figure 3). This beneficial effect of VLX-1005 might be clinically relevant, although the HIT experiments performed in this investigation in vivo is limited by the administration of anti-GPIX. Our previous reports reproducibly demonstrate that anti-GPIX potently drives cell activation in an FcγRIIA-dependent manner, although the regulatory mechanisms of signaling and kinetics may not completely overlap from FcγRIIA engagement via heparin/PF4/anti-PF4 immune complexes.24,30 Although further studies are needed to confirm this beneficial effect in patients, our data suggest that VLX-1005 might be able to attenuate the drop in platelet count and the development of thrombocytopenia in patients with HIT.

Bleeding is a major concern that has been observed with the use of most antiplatelet31 and anticoagulant drugs.3 To investigate whether VLX-1005 exhibits a bleeding risk, mice treated with VLX-1005 and/or argatroban underwent a tail vein bleeding assay, in which the distal 5 mm of the tail was resected and the time to bleeding cessation measured. In accordance with our previous study,12 no significant increase in tail bleeding time was observed in mice treated with VLX-1005 compared to vehicle control (Figure 4A). These findings suggest that bleeding may not be a risk factor associated with the use of VLX-1005 to treat HIT.

Given the effects of argatroban on coagulation in blood,32 the effects of VLX-1005 on coagulation were assessed by TEG. As expected, argatroban significantly altered overall coagulation; however, our TEG studies support the lack of effect of VLX-1005 on coagulation (Figure 4B-E). Notably, when VLX-1005 was combined with argatroban, coagulation effects or clotting potential were significantly altered compared to argatroban alone, supporting that VLX-1005 may be safe to use in patients with HIT who are already on a factor IIa inhibitor.

The characterization of the safety and tolerability of VLX-1005 alone33 or in combination with argatroban34 were assessed in a phase 1 clinical trial in healthy participants (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05325346). These studies were recently completed with promising findings and a positive safety profile, enabling the US Food and Drug Administration to approve advancement of VLX-1005 to a phase 2 clinical trial. The efficacy and safety of VLX-1005 when added to standard-of-care direct thrombin inhibitor in participants with HIT has been evaluated in a phase 2 clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05785819), and the results are pending.35 In this study, we further demonstrate the rationale that targeting 12-LOX with VLX-1005 might be beneficial for patients with HIT. In addition to the antiplatelet effects in vitro, neither thrombocytopenia nor thrombus formation was observed in hFcR+/+/12-LOX˗/˗ mice after a HIT-like challenge (Figure 3). Indeed, Renna et al20 previously observed that treatment with VLX-1005 prevented thrombocytopenia and thrombus formation in a mouse model of HIT. Altogether, these suggest that platelet 12-LOX signaling is necessary for the development of thrombocytopenia and thrombus formation in HIT.

VLX-1005 elicits antiplatelet effects without an observed bleeding risk in mouse bleeding models and human blood as assessed by TEG. Although more work remains to translate this novel compound to an effective new drug for the treatment of HIT, the work presented here is a major advancement in the field through dual inhibition of platelet activation through VLX-1005 and inhibition of thrombin through direct thrombin inhibitors such as argatroban. Limitations of the study include the use of healthy human blood with KKO as opposed to human HIT anti-PF4/heparin serum. Hence, to our knowledge, we have shown for the first time that inhibition of the oxygenase 12-LOX may represent a novel treatment in combination with direct thrombin inhibitors to improve patient outcomes over current strategies for the treatment of HIT.

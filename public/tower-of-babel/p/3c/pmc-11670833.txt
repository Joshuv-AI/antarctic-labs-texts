The impact of hydroxybutyl chitosan and 5-fluorouracil on the procedure of endoscopic endonasal dacryocystorhinostomy
Authors: Jia Qi Lin, Jin Zhao, Xun Liu, Emmanuel Eric Pazo, Qing Wang, Li Zhu, Ting Lin
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Purpose:To investigate the effects of hydroxybutyl chitosan (HBCS) with and without 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) during endoscopic endonasal dacryocystorhinostomy (Endo-DCR). In addition, the present study observed the impact of HBCS and 5-FU on the functions of the nasal mucosal cell population in vivo.Methods:Patients were randomized into HBCS (group A), HBCS combined with 5-FU (group B), and gelatin sponge control group (group C). 16S rRNA high-throughput sequencing technology examined the conjunctival sac and nasal flora changes. In addition, CCK8, cell scratching, and flow cytometry were used to investigate the effects of HBCS and 5-FU on the nasal mucosal cell populations.Results:Subjects in groups A, B, and C had anastomotic areas of 21.83 ± 12.69 mm2, 21.57 ± 14.53 mm2, and 12.45 ± 8.16 mm2, respectively (P = 0.0359). Group A had less severe epiphora than the other two groups at 1-, 2-, and 12-week postoperative follow-up (P < 0.05). Complications around the anastomosis in group A were the least severe of the three groups (P = 0.0259). After surgery, the proportion of pathogenic bacteria in the conjunctival sac and nasal cavity was higher in groups A and B than in healthy adults. At the 2-week follow-up, the structure of nasal flora in group A was more similar to that of the healthy adults compared to group B.Conclusion:Intraoperative use of HBCS at the anastomose improves the postoperative outcome of En-DCR. 5-FU cannot give better postoperative results in En-DCR and is detrimental to the normalization of the postoperative flora in patients with chronic dacryocystitis. At the cellular level, both HBCS and 5-FU inhibit the migration of nasal mucosal cell populations, and 5-FU inhibits proliferation but does not promote apoptosis.

Endoscopic endonasal dacryocystorhinostomy (Endo-DCR) is a major surgical intervention for patients with chronic dacryocystitis (CD). However, the success rate of Endo-DCR varies widely between 58% and 94%,[1] and surgical failure is primarily due to anastomosis fibrosis, which results in model closure.[2] While several adjuncts are used to improve the success of Endo-DCR, such as mechanical support of lacrimal stents, cortisol for inflammation, antimetabolites for adhesions, and gelatin sponges and other absorbable materials for tamponade, higher surgical success rate requires a combination of hemostasis, antibacterial agents, promotion of mucosal repair, and scar reduction.

Hydroxybutyl chitosan (HBCS) is a Chitosan (CS) ether, and retrospective studies have shown that CS applied at the anastomosis in DCR can improve surgical outcomes.[34] However, CS is not readily soluble in water and cannot be easily positioned; thus, its application is limited. HBCS, like CS, is non-toxic, biocompatible, biodegradable, antibacterial, hemostasis, and hydroxybutylation bolsters CS water solubility and temperature sensitivity.[5] Therefore, it is widely used in postoperative adhesion prevention, wound healing, and drug delivery. However, clinical studies on its use in Endo-DCR are lacking. As the effect of HBCS on different cells may differ, the current study explored the effect of HBCS on mucosal epithelial tissue.

5-Fluorouracil (5-FU) is an antimetabolite, and it is effective in treating keloid scars by inhibiting fibroblasts and thus inhibiting scar formation.[6] Currently, 5-FU is widely used to inhibit fibroblast proliferation and scar formation in follicles following glaucoma filtration surgery, with good safety and efficacy.[7] In a systematic review, Phelps et al.[8] reported antimetabolites as an adjunct to DCR, improving the procedure's success. However, there needs to be more research on 5-FU in DCR. 5-FU can inhibit tumor proliferation while also promoting apoptosis. However, studies were generally limited to tumor cells[9] and epithelial cells derived from the surface, and the concentrations used varied. The effects of 5-FU on cell function were investigated using primary cultured nasal mucosal cell populations in the present study.

HBCS may play a role in granulation growth.[5] Granulation tissue proliferation promotes surgical wound healing, increasing the risk of scar contracture and obscuring anastomosis. 5-FU can also cause a prolonged wound healing time.[6] As a result, we wanted to balance this feature of HBCS by combining it with 5-FU. While various studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of mitomycin C (MMC),[8] the stronger potency of MMC can lead to more side effects.[10] Furthermore, Wu et al.[11] found that 5-FU is more toxic to fibroblasts than MMC.

This prospective study collected patients with CD who underwent Endo-DCR with intraoperative adjuvant therapy with HBCS or HBCS combined with 5-FU. In addition, the effects of both methods were observed at the cellular level. As retained pathogenic bacteria cause an inflammatory response in the mucosa, which can compromise the procedure's success, 16S rRNA high-throughput sequencing technology was used to explore the effect of two adjuncts, HBCS and 5-FU, on local flora.

Methods

Clinical study

This prospective study was approved by the Institution of Human Research Ethics Committee of the Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital, Tianjin, China (Approval No.: 2021KY-27). All adult study participants voluntarily signed an informed consent form and underwent Endo-DCR from December 2021 to June 2023 at the Tianjin Medical University Eye Hospital for CD. All adult patients diagnosed with CD and planning to undergo Endo-DCR surgery were invited to participate in the study. The exclusion criteria were: (i) history of other eye or nose with inflammatory diseases, (ii) ocular or nasal trauma, (iii) ocular or nasal surgery (such as lacrimal duct placement or laser treatment) within 3 months, and (iv) unable or unwilling to provide informed consent. With a random table, the eyes were randomly divided into three groups. A total of 31 cases (37 eyes) were included in group A, 30 cases (32 eyes) in group B, and 29 cases (32 eyes) in group C. In group A, HBCS was applied to the surface of the anastomosis of the nasal mucosal flap and the lacrimal sac mucosal flap. During the aforesaid procedure, 0.3 mL of 5-FU solution (25 mg/mL) was injected under the nasal mucosa around the anastomosis in group B (Sup. 1). Anastomoses in group C patients were covered with gelatin sponges encapsulated with thrombin, tobramycin dexamethasone.

The Endo-DCR was performed mainly as described by Wu et al.[1] (Sup. 2). All surgeries are performed by a single experienced surgeon. An independent clinical staff performed lacrimal drainage system irrigation at 1 week (±1 day), 2 weeks (±1 day), 4 weeks (±3 days), 8 weeks (±3 days), 12 weeks (±7 days), and >24 weeks postoperatively. Lacrimal canal irrigation and postoperative dosing regimens (Sup. 3) were performed in accordance with the methodology previously utilized by my group.[12] Patients were given ephedrine nasal drops and Fluticasone Propionate nasal spray (Glaxo Wellcome, S.A.). We graded patients’ postoperative comfort in conjunction with VAS scores, postoperative bleeding, and epiphora (Sup. 4). An endoscope examined the anastomosis and its surrounding area for complications such as scarring, adhesions, and granulation. The area of anastomosis was calculated by multiplying the lengths of the long and short axes. Because of the small sample size collected at >24 weeks postoperatively, each patient group was scored for complications (Sup. 5).

Patients’ conjunctival sac swabs were obtained preoperatively and at 1, 2, 4, and 8 weeks postoperatively. At each time point, the specimens in group A were recorded separately as A0, A1w, A2w, A4w, and A8w. Patients’ nasal swabs were also taken before surgery and 4 weeks later. At each time point, the specimens in group A were recorded separately as AN0 and AN4w; the naming process for group B was the same as for group A. All patients did not have local antibiotics eye drops before or after the procedure, and all swabs were sampled before lacrimal canal irrigation. Conjunctival sac and nasal swabs from healthy adults were selected as healthy controls, totaling 17 cases and 21 eyes (52.9 ± 9.0 years old), counted as group NC. Analysis of microbial structure changes was performed based on 16S rRNA technology. Swabs were collected and analyzed with the methodology previously utilized by my group.[12]

Vitro experiments

Primary cell line culture

The nasal mucosa tissue from surgery was placed into precooled DMEM/F-12 medium (Gibco, USA) immediately and then transported to the laboratory. Nasal mucosa tissue processing and cell culture were performed in accordance with the methodology previously utilized by my group.[13] The cell populations were obtained from three patients (N1, N2, and N3). The tissues removed from surgery were all identified as nasal mucosa with a normal structure under H and E staining (Sup. 6). Passages 2–4 of the primary cells were used for further experiments.

CCK-8 assay

A CCK-8 assay was applied to assess the effect of HBCS (10, 20, 50, and 100 µg/mL) and 5-FU (10, 20, 50, 100, and 200 µg/mL) on cell proliferation. The optical density (OD) value of each well at 450 nm was measured using a microplate meter (Tecan, Switzerland).

Wound scratch healing assay

Wound scratch healing assay was applied to assess the effect of HBCS (50 µg/mL) and 5-FU (100 µg/mL) on cell migration. ImageJ, Version 1.8.0 was applied to measure the scratch area. The ability of cell migration was expressed by the cell mobility rate as follows: mobility rate (%) = .

Flow cytometry

Flow cytometry was carried out to test the effect of 5-FU (50 and 200 µg/mL) on cell apoptosis. Apoptotic cells were detected by flow cytometry (BD Biosciences, USA). Experimental results were analyzed using FlowJo software, Version 10.8.1 (Tree Star, Inc).

Statistical analyses

For figure modification and statistical analysis, GraphPad Prism 8.0 (La Jolla, CA, USA) was utilized. All vitro tests were conducted separately at least three times. P < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

At 1 week postoperatively, contralateral/ipsilateral regurgitation in situ occurred in 1 eye in group A and 1 eye in group B, respectively. At 8 weeks postoperatively, contralateral regurgitation occurred in the same eye in group A. Scarring and granulation were found at the anastomosis in this patient and were treated promptly. At subsequent follow-up, the tear duct flushing was patent. Irrigation of the lacrimal drainage system was successful in all other patients.

At 8–12 weeks after surgery, endoscopic examination revealed that all patients had no residual HBCS at the anastomosis. Subjects in groups A, B, and C had anastomotic areas of 21.83 ± 12.69 mm2, 21.57 ± 14.53 mm2, and 12.45 ± 8.16 mm2, respectively (P = 0.0359). Two-by-two comparisons revealed no statistically significant differences between the groups [Fig. 1c]. Nasal mucosa was intact in all patients in all three groups, and no bone wall exposure was seen. Complications occurred at a significantly different rate in the three groups (P = 0.0259) [Fig. 1d]. The rate of complications was lowest in group A and highest in group B. Further two-by-two comparisons revealed that the rate of complications was lower in group A than in group B (P = 0.0155) and was lower in group C than in group B (P = 0.0382). Complications included scarring, adhesions, and granulation. Scarring and adhesions occurred at a significantly different rate in the three groups (P = 0.0027) [Fig. 1e]. The rate of scarring and adhesions was lowest in group A and highest in group B. Further two-by-two comparisons revealed that the rate of scarring and adhesions was significantly lower in group A than in group B (P = 0.001). Granulation occurrence did not differ significantly between the three groups (P = 0.9891) [Fig. 1f]. Two-by-two comparisons revealed no statistically significant differences between the groups.

After 24 weeks postoperatively, the anastomotic areas of subjects in groups A, B, and C were 13.33 ± 4.99 mm2, 15.6 ± 16.26 mm2, and 12.5 ± 8.62 mm2, respectively (P = 0.6026). Effect of HBCS and 5-FU on cell function

Establishment of nasal mucosal cell populations N1, N2, and N3

Prior to transmission, the nasal mucosal cell population displayed two distinct morphologies: spindle and oval-shaped cells grew in distinct groups with clear separation. Based on H and E staining results of the nasal mucosa tissue, the two were determined to be fibroblasts and epithelial cells, respectively. Following the first passages, a more uniformly distributed mixture of cells emerged. That has been dubbed the “nasal mucosal cell population,” which can preserve cell-cell interactions and mimic growth patterns in their in vivo state. N1, N2, and N3 nasal mucosal cell populations were stable and uniform (Sup. 5).

Effect of HBCS on cell proliferation and migration

The CCK-8 assay revealed that 100 µg/mL HBCS inhibited the proliferation of N3 (P < 0.0001). All remaining concentrations of HBCS had no significant effect on the proliferation of nasal mucosal cell populations. Furthermore, the solidified HBCS can be seen on the well plate's bottom surface [Fig. 2a].

Bacterial flora analysis

From preoperative to 8 weeks postoperatively, the relative abundance of pathogenic bacteria such as Pseudomonas and Corynebacterium in groups A and B's conjunctival flora increased compared to group NC. The relative abundance of Pseudomonas in the conjunctival sac of patients with CD decreased after 4 weeks and gradually approached levels of group NC. The relative abundance of Streptococcus in the conjunctival sac was lower in group B compared to group A [Fig. 4a].

Preoperatively and at 4 weeks postoperatively, the relative abundance of Pseudomonas in the nasal cavity was higher in groups A and B than in group NC. The species composition of the nasal cavity in group A was closer to that of group NC at 4 weeks after surgery than in group B [Fig. 4b].

Discussion

The results indicate that using HBCS demonstrates both safety and efficacy in Endo-DCR treatments. In vitro experiments revealed that 5-FU has superior efficacy compared to HBCS. However, when administered by submucosal injection in Endo-DCR, 5-FU did not provide beneficial outcomes and instead had a detrimental impact on the procedural result and the structure of the flora. In the general population, CD has a higher prevalence among middle-aged and elderly women.[14] This observation may be attributed to systemic or local estrogen concentration alterations, with the physical characteristic of longer and narrower tear ducts in women.[15] The dimensions of the anastomosis experienced a reduction after the 24-week mark following the surgical procedure, indicating the need for postoperative monitoring beyond 24 weeks after Endo-DCR; this monitoring should include timely interventions to address scarring and adhesions.

Clinical results

CS has been used in various lacrimal procedures, including external dacryocystorhinostomy (Ex-DCR),[3] Endo-DCR,[34] and endonasal laser dacryocystorhinostomy (Endol-DCR).[16] It has been shown that the utilization of CS has improved the success rate of these procedures (Sup. 7). However, CS cannot retain water in the wound and may cause secondary damage when removed.[17] The results of our study indicate that HBCS as an adjunct can better improve the postoperative outcome of Endo-DCR compared to the commonly used gelatin sponge. From 1 day to 12 weeks postoperatively, epiphora was milder in the HBCS group than in the gelatin sponge group. Milder epiphora benefits from less scar proliferation and the larger anastomotic area. From 8 to 12 weeks postoperatively, the anastomotic area was larger in the HBCS group than in the gelatin sponge group, and the rate of peri-anastomotic complications was lower, especially scar formation. HBCS has a unique thermal response that exhibits sol–micelle transition properties at different temperatures.[18] Furthermore, hydrogels provide a moist environment for wound tissue.[19] In minimally invasive procedures, HBCS is easy to inject at room temperature and can form micelle to withstand gravity at body temperature and thus not move out of its desired area of application.[19]

To reduce contracture during the healing process, some scholars have attempted to use 5-FU, a cytostatic drug, as an adjunct to Ex-DCR, Endo-DCR, and Endol-DCR via medical dressing or submucosal injection (Sup. 8). Except for one study that concluded that using 5-FU significantly improved the surgical success rate,[11] the remaining studies[20212223] reported that 5-FU had no significant improvement in surgical success using single or multiple doses of 5-FU. Similarly, in the current study, after 1 day to 24 weeks after Endo-DCR, the use of HBCS in combination with 5-FU as an adjunct did not improve postoperative outcomes in terms of comfort, bleeding, epiphora, anastomotic size, and peripheral complications compared with HBCS alone. The possible explanation is that 5-FU affects only the S-phase of cell proliferation, and cells in other phases can continue to proliferate unaffected. Moreover, the use of 5-FU has the potential to induce an inflammatory reaction inside the mucosal lining, hence resulting in the formation of scar tissue in the mucosa.[24] Several studies have shown that administering 5-FU in systemic chemotherapy may induce mucosa inflammation, leading to lacrimal stenosis, which manifests as lacrimal overflow and epiphora.[24] Furthermore, it was shown that 5-FU exhibited toxicity toward the intestinal mucosa, resulting in the reduction of villi length, rupture of crypts, and an increased presence of apoptotic cells.[25] However, it is different of the effects of 5-FU and its concentration on different cells. In vitro tests were needed to further determine the safe concentration study for use in Endo-DCR surgery.

Effect of HBCS and 5-FU on nasal mucosa

The results of in vivo studies have limitations when applied to in vitro situations, such as adherent cell lines being monolayers with little extracellular matrix in the culture flask. They are surrounded by connective tissue in vivo, which may prevent drug penetration.[26] Furthermore, a study on rabbit corneal epithelial cells supports one of the previous arguments that the drug concentration in vivo is not applicable in in vitro experiments.[9] It was discovered that 5-FU at concentrations ranging from 0.5 to 5 mg/mL promoted apoptosis. However, 10 mg/mL 5-FU had no effect on corneal epithelial healing. There is no clear standard for converting dosing from in vivo to in vitro experiments for topical administration. Therefore, our group calculated the dose per unit volume of HBCS and 5-FU used based on the clinical situation and then converted the concentration used in the in vitro experiments based on the volume of cell culture medium and expanded the range to list the concentration gradient for subsequent in vitro experiments.

In agreement with Cai et al.'s study[27] based on NHDF, HBCS below 50 µg/mL had no significant effect on cell proliferation. Some studies discovered that HBCS promotes the proliferation of lymphocytes in. mice[28] and periodontal ligament cells.[29] These results differed from the present study, probably due to the different effects of HBCS concentration on the proliferation of different cell proliferation. This study demonstrated that HBCS below 50 µg/mL does not promote the proliferation of nasal mucosal cell populations and has a low risk of postoperative adhesions. When the concentration of HBCS is 100 µg/mL, it might have an inhibitory effect on the proliferation of nasal mucosa cell population, which needs to be further investigated and verified by expanding the number of culture cases. Li et al.[28] found that HBCS inhibited the migration of mouse fibroblasts (L929), which is consistent with the effect of HBCS on the migration of nasal mucosal cell populations.

Based on the clinical dosage of 5-FU, we chose several concentrations for in vitro experiments. The current study found that 5-FU at less than 200 µg/mL inhibited the proliferation and migration of nasal mucosal cell populations but did not induce apoptosis. This is beneficial for the postoperative outcome of En-DCR. A review of the literature showed that 5-FU at 10-2000 µg/mL had a promoting effect on skin fibroblast apoptosis, and that this effect increased with increasing 5-FU concentration.[6] Differences in cell types and the low concentration of 5-FU used in this study may account for this difference. It has been found that 5-FU is less toxic to corneal stromal cells than to corneal epithelial cells because of the latter's faster mitotic rate.[30] For scar proliferation, 5-FU can inhibit fibroblasts and inhibit scar formation while being non-toxic to surrounding normal epithelial cells, with few side effects.

Features of bacterial flora in chronic dacryocystitis

Staphylococcus and Streptococcus dominate the conjunctival sac flora of healthy adults,[31] and in the nasal cavity of healthy adults, Propionibacterium, Corynebacterium, Streptococcus, and Staphylococcus are the dominant species.[32] Pseudomonas, Corynebacterium, Staphylococcus, Streptococcus, Hemophilus influenzae, Candida albicans, and Propionibacterium acnes are thought to be common species in the lacrimal sac secretions of CD patients.[31] We found a high proportion of Pseudomonas and Corynebacterium in the conjunctival sac of CD patients, which is consistent with previous studies.[33] This study also discovered that Pseudomonas was most abundant in the nasal cavity of patients with CD. In addition, it was almost non-existent in the nasal cavity of healthy adults. In line with previous research,[34] we discovered consistency in the bacterial flora of the conjunctival sac in patients before and after surgery. Bacterial flora composition tends to normalize, Endo-DCR alters the flora structure of the conjunctival sac, reducing the relative abundance of pathogenic bacteria. In the present study, the relative abundance of Staphylococcus in group A's conjunctival sac decreased after surgery. According to some studies, the antibacterial spectrum of HBCS[29] includes Staphylococcus aureus and Escherichia coli, which are the causative agents of CD. To prevent microbial contamination, HBCS can be used as a medical wound dressing with antibacterial properties. Unlike HBCS alone, the combination of HBSC and 5-FU increased the relative abundance of Staphylococcus in the conjunctival sac of patients with CD. Gut microbiology-based studies have shown that 5-FU increases inflammatory cytokines and interferes with the relationship between the microbiota and the colonic mucosa, which in turn affects the composition of the gut flora[35] The Streptococci were no longer present in the conjunctival sacs of Group B patients 8-week after surgery. A rat study found a reduction in Streptococcus in the intestine after a single intraperitoneal injection of 5-FU.[36] This is consistent with our findings.

The sample collected was small and needs to be increased for further validation. Clinical trials have not established a group using only 5-FU as an adjunct to Endo-DCR, but the available literature has demonstrated that 5-FU alone does not significantly improve postoperative outcomes. The advantages of the HBCS group were no longer significant after 24 weeks and may be related to the insufficient amount of data collected at this time.

In summary, intraoperative use of HBCS at the anastomose improves the postoperative outcome of Endo-DCR. 5-FU cannot give better postoperative results in En-DCR and is detrimental to the normalization of the postoperative flora in patients with CD. At the cellular level, HBCS and 5-FU are safe and effective as intraoperative adjuncts to Endo-DCR.

Effectiveness of Zhibai Dihuang pill (Chinese herbal formula) in combination with western drugs in the treatment of recurrent aphthous stomatitis: A systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Shuang-li Peng, Ya-mei Ge, Xing-yu Yu, Hong-ci Chen, Deng-qin Song, Hui-yun Pu, Ping Yang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:The pain caused by recurrent aphthous stomatitis (RAS) and the recurrent nature of RAS lead to diminished quality of life for RAS patients. An alternative treatment for RAS is the oral administration of the Chinese herbal medicine Zhibai Dihuang pill (ZBDHP). Our study aims to investigate the clinical efficacy of ZBDHP when used in combination with Western medicine (WM) for the treatment of RAS and its effectiveness in preventing the recurrence of RAS.Methods:Following the PRISMA 2020 guidelines, we conducted a literature search on 7 electronic databases according to predefined criteria. The methodological quality of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) was evaluated based on the Cochrane Handbook, and data analysis was performed using RevMan 5.3 software.Results:A meta-analysis which included 7 studies and 669 participants in total was carried out in this study. The quantitative analysis revealed that the combined treatment of ZBDHP and WM has witnessed significantly improved overall clinical efficacy (RR = 1.20, 95% CI [1.12, 1.28], P < .05), reduced recurrence rate (RR = 0.24, 95% CI [0.13, 0.45], P < .05), decreased ulcer area (MD = −0.75, 95% CI [−0.91, −0.59], P < .05), and reduced pain visual simulation score (MD = −0.42, 95% CI [−0.52, −0.33], P < .05). No significant heterogeneity was observed among the studies. Qualitative analysis showed that the combination therapy significantly reduced serum levels of tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-6 and interleukin-10, shortened ulcer healing time and pain disappearance time, with no adverse effects observed.Conclusion:It was found that the combination of ZBDHP and WM is more effective in treating RAS than the use of WM alone, which thus provides clinicians with a more optimal treatment option. However, due to limitations in the methodological quality of the included original studies and the small sample size, we hold the opinion that more rigorous and scientific clinical trials are needed to further evaluate the efficacy of ZBDHP in treating RAS.

1. Introduction

Recurrent aphthous stomatitis (RAS), also known as recurrent oral ulcers, is a chronic inflammatory oral mucosal disease. Patients with RAS develop spherical or oval ulcerated surfaces on the oral mucosa during the course of the disease, which can cause significant pain and affect patients’ daily life. Moreover, the disease tends to recur, and the exact pathogenesis is still unclear. However, current studies suggest that immune dysregulation plays a crucial role in its development. In addition, bacterial or viral-mediated inflammation, local trauma, vitamin deficiency, oxidative stress, and excessive psychosocial stress may also contribute to the etiology of the disease.[1–3] The prevalence of RAS varies depending on the population studied, ranging from 5% to 25%. In general, young people, women and people of higher socioeconomic status are more likely to develop RAS.[4] There is no standardized treatment for RAS, and the possibility of complete cure is low. Therefore, the goal of RAS treatment is to relieve pain, shorten the duration of ulcers, and prevent recurrence.[5] The treatment of this disease is mainly divided into local and systemic therapies. Local treatments include the use of corticosteroids, anti-inflammatory and anti-allergic immunomodulators, nonspecific immunomodulators, and antibiotics. Local treatments can improve the clinical symptoms of patients with RAS. Common systemic treatments include glucocorticoids, immunomodulators, hemorheological agents, biological agents, and vitamin preparations. It should be noted that local and systemic treatments can be used in combination.[6] Recurring episodes of illness can lead to the need for long-term and repeated use of medication, but prolonged use of these drugs can result in certain adverse reactions. For instance, long-term use of glucocorticoids can lead to avascular necrosis of the femoral head,[7] hypertension[8] and central obesity,[9] while long-term use of immunomodulators can cause fetal malformations[10] and an increased risk of developing malignant tumors.[11] Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) emphasizes the use of herbal formulas that combine multiple herbs to achieve better therapeutic outcomes than using individual herbs alone. According to TCM theory, multiple herbs can work together synergistically to enhance their therapeutic effects. As a result, herbs have become a popular complementary and alternative therapy in TCM.[12] Therefore, it would be of great significance if we could find a complementary treatment with superior clinical efficacy and reduced disease recurrence.

Fortunately, we have discovered ZBDHP, a classic prescription composed of 8 herbs, including Anemarrhenae Rhizoma (Zhi Mu in Chinese), Phellodendri Chinrnsis Cortex (Huang Bo in Chinese), Rehmanniae Radix Praeparata (Shu Di Hung in Chinese), Rhizoma Dioscoreae (Shan Yao in Chinese), Cornus Officinalis Sieb. Et Zucc. (Shan Zhu Yu in Chinese), Poria Cocos (Schw.) Wolf. (Fu Ling in Chinese), Alisma Orientale (Sam.) Juz. (Ze Xie in Chinese), and Cortex Moutan (Mu Dan Pi in Chinese). It has good effects of “nourishing yin and clearing heat.”[13] According to TCM theory, the pathology of some RAS patients can be traced to “yin deficiency and heat syndrome,” which is considered as a sub-health state of the body, and ZBDHP can significantly improve this pathological state.[14,15] Basic research has revealed that the primary active components in ZBDHP can enhance the expression of Sirt1 and Foxp3, while inhibiting the expression and acetylation of NF-κB in oral mucosal cells. Therefore, ZBDHP may improve fatty acid metabolism and treat RAS by regulating the Sirt1-NF-κB pathway.[16] A large number of systematic reviews and meta-analyses have been conducted to estimate the efficacy of TCM in the treatment of RAS. For example, Zhou et al[17] systematically reviewed the efficacy and safety of Chinese medicines in treating RAS. Although their study provided some evidence of the effectiveness of TCM in treating RAS, it lacked specificity and did not explore a specific herbal formula. Our study will build on Zhou et al’s research with more precision, aiming to provide clinicians with a clear treatment plan. In addition, as far as we are aware, there is currently no systematic review or meta-analysis specifically investigating the clinical efficacy of ZBDHP as an adjunctive therapy for the treatment of RAS. This paper reports a systematic review and meta-analysis we conducted to evaluate the clinical efficacy of ZBDHP in the treatment of RAS.

2. Methods

The meta-analytical procedures employed in this study were meticulously crafted with close adherence to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) Statement guidelines, and have been duly registered on PROSPERO, the international prospective register of systematic reviews to ensure the methodological rigor and transparency of this study. The registration number assigned to our study is CRD42022372985. This meta-analysis on the use of ZBDHP for treating RAS relied solely on published articles and did not involve any privacy concerns. As a result, the perspectives of the ethics committee and patients were not taken into account.

2.1. Literature search

Two researchers independently searched 7 databases, including PubMed, Cochrane Library, Embase, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), China Biology Medicine (CBM), Wanfang Data Knowledge Service Platform, and Chongqing VIP Database. The search terms included “Zhibai Dihuang,” “ZhibaiDihuang,” “zhibai dihuang,” “zhibaidihuang,” “stomatitis, aphthous,” “aphthous stomatitis,” “recurrent aphthous stomatitis,” and “recurrent stomatitis,” etc, and the Chinese forms of these terms were used for literature search in Chinese databases. In addition, setting the time period from January 2000 to December 2022, the authors also conducted a manual search to identify studies that may have been overlooked in the electronic literature search. 2.2. Study selection

2.2.1. Type of research.

In this study, we included randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating the efficacy of ZBDHP or modified ZBDHP in treating RAS. Article types like basic experiments and personal experience were excluded.

2.2.2. Type of patients.

Patients included must have a clear clinical diagnosis of RAS, while other causes such as Behcet disease, oral lichen planus, radiation therapy-induced ulcers, etc were excluded. In addition, no restrictions were placed on the country, race, gender, or age of the patients studied.

2.2.3. Type of interventions.

The control group received conventional WM treatment, while the treatment group received ZBDHP or modified ZBDHP in combination with the WM the control group received. The dosage and usage of WM remained consistent between the 2 groups, and the treatment period was no less than 1 week. Any RCTs that combined ZBDHP or modified ZBDHP with qigong, Tai Chi, acupuncture, cupping, moxibustion, or massage were excluded from further analysis.

2.2.4. Type of outcome measures.

The primary endpoints were clinical efficiency and recurrence rate, and secondary endpoints included ulcer area, visual analog score,[18] time to pain resolution, ulcer healing time, serological marker levels, and adverse effects.

2.3. Data extraction and processing

Two researchers independently performed literature screening and data extraction. This process involved collecting information from the original literature, such as the first author, publication date, country, sample size, age of patients, treatment duration, intervention measures, and outcome indicators. In cases where disagreements occurred between the 2 researchers, a third researcher was invited to intervene and conduct a review to reach a consensus. Furthermore, if there were errors or missing data in the original data, we contacted the authors of the study to obtain the correct results. Additionally, we used appropriate statistical methods when necessary to transform the data to facilitate subsequent analysis.

2.4. Quality assessment

The quality of the literature was evaluated using the Cochrane risk of bias assessment tool. This tool considered specific assessment items, such as the random and hidden assignment methods, blinding methods for participants and personnel, blinding methods for outcome evaluation, completeness of data, selectivity of outcome reporting, and other biases.

2.5. Data analysis

Data analysis was performed on RevMan 5. 3 software provided by the Cochrane Collaboration Network. For binary variables, the effect size was expressed as the relative risk (RR), while for continuous variables with the same unit of measurement, the effect size was expressed as the mean difference (MD). For continuous variables with different units or measurement scales, the standardized mean difference (SMD) was used. The confidence interval (CI) was set at 95%. In cases with no significant heterogeneity between studies, we adopted a fixed-effects model for analysis, and conversely, a random-effects model was used. The results were presented in a forest plot. For outcome indicators that allowed for data merging, we performed quantitative analysis, while for single outcome indicators, qualitative descriptions were performed.

2.6. Heterogeneity analysis

RevMan 5. 3 software uses the Q-statistic and I2 statistic to assess heterogeneity among studies. Generally, if the P value associated with the Q-statistic is <0.1, it is considered that heterogeneity exists among studies. Additionally, according to the Cochrane Handbook, an I² value >50% also indicates heterogeneity among studies. If there is significant heterogeneity among studies, subgroup analysis will be considered.

2.7. Sensitivity analysis

In order to assess the robustness and reliability of the results of our analysis, we performed a sensitivity analysis using a stepwise elimination of each study.

2.8. Publication bias

To detect publication bias in our meta-analysis, we adopted a 2-step approach. Firstly, we applied inverted funnel plots for qualitative assessment. If substantial publication bias was detected and the number of included studies exceeded 10, we employed funnel plot regression to quantitatively estimate the extent of the bias. This strategy enabled us to provide a comprehensive evaluation of publication bias and enhance the validity of our findings.

3. Results

3.1. Literature searched

As illustrated in Figure 1, we initially identified a total of 258 studies, all of which were authored in Chinese. After removing duplicates, 146 studies remained. We conducted a 2-step screening process involving both the evaluation of titles and abstracts, followed by an in-depth examination of study contents. Consequently, we found that out of the 35 studies under consideration, 22 were non-RCTs, 2 studies lacked well-defined diagnostic and/or efficacy criteria, and an additional 4 studies did not adhere to the specified treatment protocol requirements. At the end, 7 studies[19–25] were remained and included in the analysis. These 7 studies included 669 participants, with a sample size of 336 in the treatment group and 333 in the control group.

3.3. Literature quality evaluation

It can be seen from Figures 2 and 3 that the quality of the included original studies was average. It was claimed in all studies that random assignment was conducted. Five of these studies[19–23] gave specific random assignment methods (random numbers), while the remaining 2 studies[23,25] only mentioned the use of randomization. None of these studies described allocation concealment and blinding. Fortunately, the outcome measures reported by these studies were complete and accurate, and the researchers did not selectively report their study results.

3.4. Quantitative analysis results

3.4.1. Primary endpoints.

3.4.1.1. Clinical efficacy.

As shown in Figure 4, a meta-analysis was conducted on 7 studies[19–25] included in this study to observe the clinical efficacy of the medicine used. There were 336 participants in the test group and 333 participants in the control group, a total of 669 participants. Heterogeneity analysis was performed and the I2 = 0%, P = .53, indicating low heterogeneity after combining the analysis. Therefore, a fixed-effect model was used for analysis. The results showed that RR = 1. 20, with a 95% CI [1. 12, 1. 28] and P < .05.

Forest plot of clinical efficacy.

3.4.1.2. Recurrence rate.

As shown in Figure 5, 4 studies[19–21,24] analyzed the recurrence rate. There were 212 patients in the test group and 210 patients in the control group, a total of 422 patients. Since I2 = 0%, P = .89, a fixed-effect model was used, from which it was obtained that RR = 0.24, with a 95% CI [0.13, 0.45] and P < .05

Forest plot of recurrence rate.

3.4.2. Secondary endpoints.

3.4.2.1. Ulcer area.

As shown in Figure 6, 2 studies[20,21] examined the ulcer area. There were 82 participants in the test group and 82 participants in the control group, a total of 164 participants. Since I2 = 0%, P = .59, a fixed-effect model was employed for analysis, and it was showed that MD = −0.75, with a 95% CI [−0.91, −0.59] and P < .05.

Forest plot of ulcer area.

3.4.2.2. Pain visual simulation score.

As shown in Figure 7, 2 studies[20,21] evaluated the pain visual simulation score. There were 82 patients in the test group and 82 patients in the control group, a total of 164 patients. Since I2 = 0%, P = .77, a fixed-effect model was used, and the analysis showed that MD = −0.42, with a 95% CI [−0.52, −0.33] and P < .05.

Forest plot of pain visual simulation score.

3.5. Qualitative analysis results

Chen et al[19] investigated the levels of interleukin 6, interleukin 10, and tumor necrosis factor (TNF-α) in the treatment and control groups before and after treatment. They found a more significant decrease in these indicators in the treatment group compared to the control group, with pain resolved, ulcer surface healed more quickly in the treatment group compared to the situation in the control group. Some studies reported adverse effects. For example, Chen[22] mentioned that while no adverse reactions were observed in the treatment group during the trial, 2 cases of irregular stools were recorded in the control group which improved after treatment.

3.6. Heterogeneity analysis

As suggested by the results of quantitative analysis, no heterogeneity existed among the included studies for each of the 4 outcome measures (P > .1, I2 = 0%), indicating that there was no need for further subgroup analysis to explore heterogeneity.

3.7. Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis was performed by systematically removing each study, and no significant heterogeneity was found among the studies (P > .1, I2 < 50%). Therefore, we did not proceed with further correlation analyses.

3.8. Publication bias

Publication bias analysis of clinical efficacy was carried out. As shown in Figure 8, the funnel plots are largely symmetrical, which indicates that there is no significant publication bias.

4. Discussion

4.1. Major findings

As a complementary and alternative therapy, the role of traditional Chinese herbal medicine as a complementary treatment to modern medicine is clear. According to the results of the meta-analysis, ZBDWP, as a supplementary therapy, is very useful in combination with other drugs, showing better clinical efficacy (RR = 1. 20, 95% CI [1. 12, 1. 28], P < .05), as well as a lower disease recurrence rate (RR = 0.24, 95% CI [0.13, 0.45], P < .05). At the same time, the analysis of secondary outcome measures showed that the combination of ZBDHP and WM can significantly reduce the size of ulcers during the disease onset period (MD = −0.75, 95% CI [−0.91, −0.59], P < .05) and alleviate patients’ pain levels (MD = −0.42, 95% CI [−0.52, −0.33], P < .05). For outcome measures that cannot be quantitatively synthesized, we have provided qualitative descriptions. Chen et al[19] study suggests that the combination of ZBDHP and Thalidomide is more effective in significantly reducing the levels of TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-10 in the serum of RAS patients. Existing research indicates that TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-10 are highly expressed in RAS patients.[26,27] Meanwhile, Chen et al[19] also found that the combination of ZBDHP and WM helped patients achieve faster pain relief as well as earlier healing of the ulcer surface. Adverse reactions were described by Chen S[22] that there were no adverse effects in the treatment group, 2 cases in the control group had increased and unformed stools which were relieved after treatment.

As mentioned in the introduction, some patients with RAS may have concomitant Yin deficiency and heat syndrome, and immune dysregulation and inflammatory response are the main factors contributing to the disease. Pharmacological studies have shown that ZBDHP can treat Yin deficiency and heat syndrome by regulating immune response and suppressing inflammation.[28,29] Rehmanniae Radix Praeparata (Shu Di Huang in Chinese) can increase IgG and IgM levels, CD4+ levels and CD4+/CD8+ ratio in the serum of animals. It is also effective in reducing CD8+ levels, so that immune organs such as lymph nodes, spleen and thymus that have atrophied can be restored to some extent, thereby regulating the immune function of the body.[30] Yam polysaccharide (RDPS-I) in Rhizoma Dioscoreae (Shan Yao in Chinese) can enhance the phagocytosis of macrophages and the proliferation of T lymphocytes, and increase IgG levels to improve immune function.[31] Poria polysaccharide can adjust T cell subpopulation, promote the synthesis of B cell antibodies and enhance immune function.[32] Cornus Officinalis Sieb. Et Zucc. (Shan Zhu Yu in Chinese) contains polysaccharides, flavonoids, tannins and other active ingredients that work together to regulate immunity, suppress inflammation and relieve pathological pain.[33] The ethanolic extract of Alisma Orientale (Sam.) Juz. (Ze Xie in Chinese) mays can inhibit the production of inflammatory factors such as NO and PGE2 by LPS-activated cells and peritoneal macrophages, thus exerting immunomodulatory effects.[34] Tannin in Cortex Moutan (Mu Dan Pi in Chinese) has anti-inflammatory effects by scavenging DPPH free radicals.[35] Anemarrhenae Rhizoma (Zhi Mu in Chinese) and Phellodendri Chinrnsis Cortex (Huang Bo in Chinese) contain anhydroicaritin and quercetin, Anhydroicaritin can significantly inhibit the production of inflammatory factors such as IL-6, TNF-α and NO, thus exerting anti-inflammatory effects.[36] In Pandya study,[37] 40 RAS patients were divided into groups A and B. Patients in group A used quercetin gel, while patients in group B used benzydamine hydrochloride mouthwash. After treatment, the patients using quercetin gel had a significantly reduced ulcer area compared to those using benzydamine hydrochloride.[37]

Compared to other traditional Chinese herbs that require preparation, ZBDWP as a type of Chinese patent medicine can be easily preserved and taken by patients. Although previous systematic reviews and meta-analyses have confirmed the effectiveness of TCM in treating RAS, the research focus was too broad as various Chinese herbal formulations were studied, which may not be instructive for doctors without a background in Chinese medicine. Based on our research results, it is suggested that in the treatment of RAS patients showing symptoms of yin deficiency and heat syndrome, such as frequent hot palms and soles, night sweats, dry mouth, and lower back pain,[29] a combined therapy of conventional WM with ZBDHP is a promising option.

Based on the results of the meta-analysis, no significant heterogeneity was observed among the included studies. Furthermore, sensitivity analyses were conducted by excluding certain literature sources, and the results remained robust. To assess publication bias, a funnel plot was constructed, showing a symmetrical distribution that suggests the absence of publication bias. As a result, the findings of our research seem robust. However, this could be attributed to the limited number of studies included and the fact that they were all originated from a single country (i.e., China).

4.2. Limitations of this study

However, we must acknowledge that our research still suffers from certain limitations. For instance, in this study, while we adhered rigorously to a scientifically sound literature search strategy, we were only able to identify 7 studies suitable for systematic review and meta-analysis. The quality of these studies was less than satisfactory. Specifically, nearly all of them did not detail allocation concealment and blinding methods, which we consider to be of a high-risk factor. Furthermore, not all studies provided specific details regarding randomization methods, and the pool of results suitable for quantitative analysis was quite limited. Nevertheless, a scientific and objective systematic evaluation method was used to present the results of this study in the hope of providing a direction for subsequent researchers. Additionally, our team will keep track of new research findings to update our research results. We encourage forthcoming researchers to make improvement with a larger sample size, more representative samples, and more robust experimental designs. This course of action will enhance the reliability and applicability of research conclusions.

4.3. Recommendations for reviews based on small sample sizes

While handling a small sample size may present challenges, adhering to a structured approach helps ensure the rigor and effectiveness of a systematic review. First, we need to define specific and focused research questions, followed by devising a comprehensive plan encompassing stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria, a scientifically rigorous search strategy, precise and effective data extraction methods, and unbiased assessment of literature quality. Besides, we must interpret the findings based on existing evidence, highlighting the limitations stemming from the small sample size and its potential impact on results and conclusions. Additionally, when writing the systematic review, it is essential to adhere to established reporting guidelines, such as PRISMA, to enhance transparency and reproducibility.

5. Conclusion

It was found that the combination of ZBDHP and WM is more effective in treating RAS than the use of WM alone. This provides clinicians with a more optimal treatment option. However, due to limitations in the methodological quality of the included original studies and the small sample size, more rigorous and scientific clinical trials are needed to further evaluate the clinical efficacy of ZBDHP in the treatment of RAS.

Functional prognosis of older adults with tetanus in Japan: a single-center retrospective study
Authors: Takeru Yamamoto, Ryuichi Minoda Sada, Shungo Yamamoto, Ryosuke Osawa, Naoto Hosokawa, Satoshi Kutsuna
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGlobally tetanus causes 26,000–48,000 deaths annually, mostly in children in lower and middle-income countries. Background

Globally, tetanus causes 26,000–48,000 deaths annually, 55.8% of which occur among individuals aged 0–14 years, predominantly in countries with limited resources [1]. Although preventable, this disease remains a public health problem in many low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). The pathogenesis of tetanus involves the neurotoxin produced by Clostridium tetani, leading to muscular rigidity and spasms. The most common initial sign is spasm, often of muscles of the jaw, “lockjaw.” Other signs may include painful spasms in other muscle groups in the neck, trunk, and extremities. Most patients with tetanus require admission to the intensive care unit (ICU), and intubation is often necessary. Therefore, the clinical outcomes and prognosis of tetanus remain clinically important.

According to Bennett’s Principles and Practice of Infectious Diseases, “recovery from tetanus takes an additional month and is complete unless complications supervene.” However, data on the functional prognosis of patients with tetanus are limited [2].

The incidence of tetanus is lower in high-income countries than in LMICs, and limited data are available on the prognosis in high-income countries. In Japan, approximately 100 people, mostly older adults, are diagnosed with tetanus each year. A study conducted in Japan described the clinical features and outcomes of tetanus using a national inpatient database. Of 465 tetanus survivors, 290 were discharged home, and the rest were discharged to other facilities [3]. In another retrospective study of 70 patients with tetanus in France, the patients’ general function was assessed [4]. However, to our knowledge, no reports have provided detailed information on the effect of tetanus on activities of daily living (ADL) independence. To address this knowledge gap, this study assessed the functional prognosis of older adults with tetanus using the Barthel Index (BI) [5]. BI is a widely used scale for assessing ADL. We also conducted an exploratory analysis of risk factors for worsening functional prognosis.

Methods

Patients and data collection

The electronic medical records of patients diagnosed with tetanus at the Kameda Medical Center (Chiba, Japan), an 865-bed tertiary-care general medical center, between January 2000 and June 2025 were retrospectively reviewed. During this period, we identified 78 patient records with the diagnosis “tetanus” as the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10) code. Individuals who had been vaccinated only for prevention and those who were not hospitalized for tetanus were excluded.

The records of all included patients were reviewed by the researchers T.Y., R.M.S., and S.Y. (two Japanese board-certified infectious disease physicians and one board-certified member of the Japanese Society of Internal Medicine). Each researcher diagnosed tetanus independently using the discharge summaries. In cases in which the researchers had discordant opinions regarding the diagnosis, a diagnosis of tetanus was established if there were no signs of conditions other than tetanus and if the patient’s clinical condition improved. In patients with tetanus, information was retrospectively collected on vaccination history, trauma history, underlying disease, vital signs on presentation, clinical form of tetanus (generalized, localized, cephalic), ADL (before admission, on admission, and on discharge), length of hospital stay, and outcomes.

Variable measurement and definitions

Adequate vaccination was defined as a three-dose primary vaccination plus a booster vaccination every 10 years [6].

ADL were assessed using BI, which consists of 10 basic aspects of self-care and physical dependency (feeding, moving from wheelchair to bed and return, personal toilet, getting on and off the toilet, bathing, walking on a level surface, ascending and descending stairs, dressing, controlling bowels, and controlling bladder). A normal score is 100, and lower scores indicate increasing disability. In cases in which a BI prior to admission was not recorded in the medical chart, patients whose pre-admission ADL status was documented by a licensed physical therapist as “independent” (full ADL) in rehabilitation records, or if rehabilitation records were unavailable, was documented as “independent” in physician or nursing records, in medical history or life/occupational history, or in the ADL score recorded in the Diagnosis Procedure Combination database, were considered to have a BI of 100 [7].

A worsening functional prognosis was defined as a decrease in the BI. The patients were divided into two groups: those with a decreased BI on discharge compared with that before admission, and those without a decreased BI on discharge.

Comorbidities were assessed using the modified Charlson Comorbidity Index (mCCI). The mCCI score for each patient was assessed as an indicator of the burden of comorbidities [8].

A hospital-acquired infection (HAI) was defined as an infection that was not present on admission, occurring 48 h or more after hospital admission and acquired during the process of receiving health care, with the diagnosis documented by a physician, and requiring antimicrobial therapy.

Rehabilitation during hospitalization was provided by licensed physical therapists. Although the duration and content varied depending on the patient’s condition, rehabilitation sessions generally lasted approximately 20 min and were conducted twice daily.

Data collection and analysis

Based on clinical experience and data collected in a previous study [9], we extracted data on factors of potential prognostic importance—such as age, time from onset to diagnosis, and tetanus severity—that might have influenced the functional outcomes.

The data were analyzed using the R software (Version 4.3.2, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). The change in the Barthel Index (ΔBI = discharge BI – pre-admission BI) was treated as a continuous variable. ΔBI was compared across clinically relevant strata using two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum (Mann–Whitney U) tests: age (≥ 65 years vs. < 65 years), incubation period (≥ 7 days vs. < 7 days), time from onset to diagnosis (≥ 4 days vs. < 4 days), ICU admission, ventilator use, requirement of tracheostomy, presence of an HAI, and Ablett score for defining severity (severe to very severe vs. mild to moderate), tetanus severity score (≥ 8 vs. < 8), Phillips score (≥ 14 vs. < 14), and Dakar scores (≥ 3 vs. < 3) for prognostic stratification [10–12]. p values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the institutional ethical committees at Kameda Medical Center (reference number: 23–085) and The University of Osaka (reference number 231332). Both institutional ethical committees approved that the requirement for informed consent to participate was waived because of the retrospective nature of the study.

The study results were reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines [13].

Results

Patient characteristics

Twenty-four patients were hospitalized with tetanus in our hospital between January 2000 and June 2025 (Supplementary Figure 1, Additional File). A total of 78 patient records with a diagnosis of“tetanus” (ICD-10) were identified. Of these, 53 patients who had received vaccination solely for prophylaxis were excluded. The remaining 25 patients were hospitalized with suspected tetanus. After review by three investigators, one patient was excluded due to a final diagnosis of vasculopathic myositis, leaving 24 patients for inclusion in the study. Their baseline characteristics and clinical presentations are shown in Table 1. The median age was 72 years (range, 40–96 years); with equal numbers of male and female patients (12 each), and all were Japanese. The most common comorbidities were hypertension (33%, 8/24), dyslipidemia (17%, 4/24), and ischemic heart disease (13%, 3/24). Records were not available on whether the patients had received adequate vaccination. Only two patients were born after 1968, when routine tetanus vaccination was introduced. Fifteen patients (63%) had a record of a wound. The median incubation period was 10 days (range, 2–21 days). The most common symptom was trismus, which was present in 21 of the 24 patients (88%).

Treatment, clinical course, and outcomes

All patients were treated with human tetanus immunoglobulin, tetanus toxoid, and antibiotics. (The details of antibiotic treatment are shown in Supplementary Table 1 (Additional File)). The median length of hospital stay was 66 days (range, 6–181 days), and 21 of the 24 patients (88%) required ICU admission. Fourteen patients (58%) developed HAIs, including 10 (42%) with hospital-acquired pneumonia/ventilator-associated pneumonia and 6 (25%) with urinary tract infections.

One of the 24 patients died (case fatality rate, 4%) (Table 2). On discharge, 10 of the 21 surviving patients with BI information available (48%), had a lower ADL level than that before admission (Fig 1).

Barthel Index before admission, on admission, and on discharge

Two patients (8%) had no pre-admission records from which the pre-admission BI could be determined, nor sufficient information available from which to estimate them, and one (4%) had a total BI score recorded on discharge with no record of the breakdown. Of the 20 patients with detailed BI data available on discharge, 9 (45%) experienced a decline in bathing ability; 7 (35%) experienced a decline in stair-climbing ability; 7 (35%) experienced a decline in mobility; and 2 (10%) experienced decreased feeding ability (Fig 2). Factors associated with a worsening functional prognosis

When the Barthel Index was treated as a continuous variable (ΔBI), patients with HAI had a significantly greater decline in BI compared with those without infection (median ΔBI −10 [IQR, −40 to 0] vs. 0 [0 to 0]; p= 0.028). Similarly, patients with a tetanus severity score ≥8 had a greater decline than those with a score <8 (median ΔBI −25 [IQR, −65 to −1] vs. 0 [−5 to 0]; p = 0.027). None of the other factors considered was significantly associated with a worsening of functional prognosis (Table 3). A line-listing of the types of HAI observed, and each patient’s functional outcome is shown in Supplementary Table 3 and a comparison of the factors associated with a severe versus a mild decline in BI is given in Supplementary Table 4 (Additional File).

Discussion

This descriptive epidemiological study was conducted in Japan, a high-income country, and focused on ADL, assessed using the BI, among older adults hospitalized with tetanus.

Almost half of the 23 surviving patients had a BI lower than their pre-admission BI on discharge, and one patient died. Few reports are available on the functional prognosis of tetanus. In a retrospective review of 70 patients with tetanus who survived after ICU admission in France, 39% had an impaired functional prognosis [4]. The patient population and medical resources in the French study did not markedly differ from those in this study; however, functional assessment was performed using Knaus' classification (A = no limitation, B = slight functional limitation, C = severe functional limitation, and D = bedridden), and details of ADL were not reported.

The BI measures 10 basic aspects of self-care and physical dependency. A study conducted in Vietnam evaluated the long-term functional outcomes in adults with tetanus using the BI [9]. However, Vietnam is an LMIC with different medical resources, and the patients were considerably younger than the patients in this study, with a median age of 49 years [9], making comparisons between the two studies difficult. In addition, details of the specific items (of the 10 items in the BI) that declined were not reported.

In this study, treating the BI as a continuous outcome (ΔBI) provided a distribution-sensitive assessment of in-hospital functional change. The data showed statistically significant differences according to HAI status and baseline disease severity (TSS <8 vs. ≥8). These findings align with the clinical trajectory of severe tetanus and the occurrence of in-hospital complications, both of which are plausibly linked to prolonged immobilization, deeper or longer sedation, and delays in rehabilitation. Given the limited sample size, these results should be interpreted with caution. Studies of larger cohorts with multivariable adjustment are required to confirm their generalizability.

In this study, 58% of patients had HAIs, which is higher than the 9.2% average HAI incidence reported among patients admitted to ICUs in Japan [14]. Similarly, in Vietnam, the HAI complication rate among patients admitted to ICUs was 23% overall, and 35% among those admitted with tetanus, suggesting that the HAI complication rate for tetanus is high [9, 15]. This may be attributable to patients with tetanus having longer hospital stays (median 25 days in patients with tetanus compared with a median of 22 days in general patients admitted to ICUs). The higher HAI complication rate in this study than that in the Vietnamese tetanus study may also be owing to the older age (median: 72 vs. 49 years), longer hospital stay (median: 66 vs. 25 days), and higher proportion of patients requiring intubation (75% vs. 40%) in this study. HAI was associated with a worsening functional prognosis. Pulmonary HAIs have been reported to be associated with functional decline [16]. Therefore, preventing HAIs is important.

In this study, only one patient died, a case fatality rate of 4%, which is comparable to the case fatality rate of 7.2% reported in the United States and considerably lower than the 43% case fatality rate reported in some African countries [17, 18]. The higher mortality rates in LMICs can be attributed to the limited availability of ICUs and ventilators. Common causes of death include complications of autonomic dysfunction and respiratory arrest secondary to laryngospasm. In this study, 88% of the patients were managed in the ICU, and 75% required intubation. ICU management is required to achieve good outcomes.

In this study, records were not available on whether patients had received adequate vaccination. In Japan, routine tetanus vaccination started in 1968, and no catch-up vaccination was implemented. A nationwide survey conducted in 2018 showed that the seroprevalence of tetanus toxoid antibody (>0.1 IU/mL) approached 0% in patients aged >70 years. In contrast, it ranged from 60–100% in adults aged <50 years [19]. In this study, only two patients (8%) were aged <50 years. Similarly, a nationwide survey conducted in 2021 in Japan showed that only 13% of patients with tetanus were aged <50 years. Therefore, older adults born before 1968 who are regularly exposed to soil, such as farmers and other high-risk occupational groups, and who have not received adequate vaccination against tetanus, would benefit from tetanus vaccination.

A strength of this study is that we assessed ADL in detail using the BI. Few similar studies have been conducted on tetanus in older adults in high-income countries; therefore, limited information is available on the complications of tetanus and its effects on ADL in older adults. In this study almost 50% of the patients had decreased ADL on discharge, with HAI being a risk factor for decreased ADL; in addition, 58% of the patients developed HAIs, indicating the importance of taking measures to prevent these infections.

This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective, single-center design and the limited sample size may limit the generalizability of the findings. Owing to the limited sample size, detailed analysis to assess the difference in risk of functional decline according to factors such as baseline functional status, comorbidities, ICU management, nutritional status, and medication use, and multivariable analysis to control for potential confounders, was not possible. Furthermore, because this study lacked a non-tetanus comparison group, whether the observed association between HAI and ADL decline is specific to tetanus or whether it applies more broadly to vulnerable older adults requiring ICU admission, is unclear. Information and selection biases may be present. In addition, information was not available on patients’ tetanus vaccination history, so the effect of vaccination on disease severity and functional outcomes could not be assessed. The BI was assessed on discharge, but variation in the length of hospital stay led to variability in the timing of the assessment, which may have affected the ΔBI. No follow-up BI assessments were conducted after discharge; therefore, the BI might have improved after discharge. However, a previous study showed that ADL in older adults with tetanus do not improve markedly after discharge [9]. Changes in treatment protocols and care standards over the study period may also have influenced the results. Missing data were handled by case-wise exclusion, which may have led to bias.

Conclusions

In this study, almost 50% of older adults with tetanus had lower ADL than those prior to the illness, on discharge. Exploratory analyses suggested that HAI might be associated with worsening functional prognosis.

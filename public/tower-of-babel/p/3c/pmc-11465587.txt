Masking ability of CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics with different translucencies and thicknesses combined with four cement shades against varying background colors when facing veneer restorations
Authors: Jiakang Zhu, Yanlin Xia, SiIn Lui, Wenxiang Wang, Shanshan Liang, Cui Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo evaluate the comprehensive effect of translucency, thickness, cement shades, and background color on the masking ability of resin-matrix ceramic veneer restorations.MethodsResin-matrix ceramic specimens with 2 translucencies (LT, HT) and 3 thicknesses (0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 mm) were made of Upcera Hyramic (A2 shade). Cement specimens were made of Variolink N in 4 shades (yellow, white, transparent, and bleach XL). Five background specimens were made of IPS Natural Die Material in 5 shades (ND1, ND2, ND3, ND4, and ND5). Color coordinates of 120 subgroups (n = 5) of combined specimens composed of different ceramic, cement, and background specimens were obtained using a spectroradiometer. Color difference (ΔE00) compared with a 4-mm thick specimen of LT and HT ceramics was calculated and four-way ANOVA was used for statistical analysis (α = 0.05).ResultsTranslucency, thickness, cement shade, background color, and their interaction had significant effects on ΔE00 (p < 0.001). ΔE00 values of HT groups were always higher than that of LT groups and were greater than 1.8 against all background colors. ΔE00 values of LT groups could be achieved to be less than 1.8 with appropriate thickness and cement shade. ΔE00 value decreased with increasing ceramic thickness. The effect of cement shade on ΔE00 had no obvious regularity, but ΔE00 values of bleach XL cement shade group were always lower than other cement shade groups under ND3 and ND5 background color.ConclusionsThe masking ability of CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics can be simultaneously affected by translucency, thickness, cement shade, and background color. Resin-matrix ceramics with low translucency has a better masking ability than that with high translucency. The masking ability of CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics increase with increasing thickness. Cement shade has less impact on the final color of resin-matrix ceramic restorations.Keywords: Resin-matrix ceramic, Masking ability, CAD-CAM, Translucency, Thickness, Cement shade, Background color

Background

Nowadays, the patients presented to the dental clinic tend to pursue biomimetic restoration, especially in the anterior zone with high esthetic requirement [1]. Shape and color are the two important aspects of biomimetic restoration. Currently, computer-aided design and computer-aided manufacturing (CAD-CAM) technology is widely used, which contributes to precisely manufacturing restorations with designed shape [2, 3]. Moreover, CAD-CAM technology realizes chair-side treatment within a short time or even just in a single visit and offers great convenience for both clinicians and patients [4]. Meanwhile, numerous CAD-CAM restorative materials with better quality and reliability are continuously developed for CAD-CAM technology [4, 5].

Resin-matrix ceramics have been introduced to combine the advantages of ceramics such as biomimetic appearance, biocompatibility, color stability, durability, and mechanical properties with the advantages of composite resins such as easy polishing and repairing, low abrasiveness to the opposite enamel [6–8]. CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics are suitable for rapid chair-side treatment because there can be rapidly milled with less wear or heat generation on the milling instruments, and are no need for sintering or crystallization. Currently, resin-matrix ceramics become a popular optional material for veneer restorations [9, 10]. Veneer restorations are a conservative treatment method that requires minimal or even no tooth preparation, generally with a labial thickness of 0.5–1.0 mm and an incisal thickness of 1.0–1.5 mm, or even thinner [11].

Obtaining the final desired color is a major challenge for veneer restorations [12]. It has been reported that the final color of ceramic restorations with a thickness of less than 2 mm could be significantly affected by the color of underlying structure [13, 14]. Therefore, the masking ability of resin-matrix ceramics is an important factor in achieving the desired color, especially for discolored teeth [15]. The masking ability of ceramic restorations mainly depends on their translucency and decreases with the increase of translucency [16]. On the one hand, according to the crystalline content, the translucency of resin-matrix ceramics of the same brand can be divided into low translucency (LT) and high translucency (HT) [16, 17]. On the other hand, the translucency of ceramic restorations will decrease with their increased thickness [18].

In addition, recent studies showed that the final color of veneer restorations were significantly affected by cement shade [9, 19]. Therefore, various resin cement systems with different shades are available in clinic to modify and improve the esthetic results of veneer restorations. When the thickness of a ceramic restoration is less than 1.5 mm, cement shade may play a vital role in masking the color of underlying structure to achieve desirable esthetic results [20, 21].

Although the final color of resin-matrix ceramic restoration is affected by several factors mentioned above including ceramic translucency, restoration thickness, cement shade, and background color, previous studies only conducted a joint analysis of two or three factors [9, 14, 16, 18, 22]. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, no previous study has simultaneously investigated the above four factors as a whole. Moreover, more reliable information is still needed on which ceramic translucency, thickness, and cement shade should be selected when faced with different colors of abutment teeth or other clinical scenarios.

Therefore, aim of this in vitro study is to evaluate the effect of different cement shades on the masking ability of resin-matrix ceramics with different translucencies and thicknesses on multiple background colors. The null hypothesis was that regardless of different cement shades, the final colors of resin-matrix ceramics with different translucencies would be similar at different thickness against varying background colors.

Materials and methods

Two CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics (Hyramic; Upcera, Shenzhen, China) of A2 shade with 2 translucencies (LT and HT), 4 shades (yellow, white, transparent, and bleach XL) of resin cement (Variolink N; Ivoclar Vivadent, Schaan, Liechtenstein), and 5 shades (ND1, ND2, ND3, ND4, and ND5) of laboratory composite die materials (IPS Natural Die; Ivoclar Vivadent, Schaan, Liechtenstein) simulating different background colors were used in the present study. The sample size was estimated using G*Power software (v.3.1.9.6; Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf, Dusseldorf, Germany) showed that the required power (greater than 0.9) could be obtained by using 252 specimens as the total sample size with an effect size = 0.25 and α = 0.05. Therefore, the sample size for each group was 5 (N = 600). The study design was presented in Fig. 1.

Schematic illustration of study design

CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramic blocks were sectioned using a precision saw machine (IsoMet; Buehler, IL, USA) under constant water flow. Fifteen specimens (12 × 14 mm) were fabricated for each translucency and divided into 3 groups based on thickness: 0.5, 1.0, and 1.5 mm (n = 5). A 4-mm thick specimen of each translucency was fabricated as the control group. One side of each specimen was ground with 800, 1200, and 2400 grit silicon carbide papers on a table-top grinder-polisher (EcoMet 250; Buehler, IL, USA) at 150 rpm for 15 s under water cooling and subsequently polished by using a polishing disk (Diacomp Plus Twist; EVE Ernst Vetter, Pforzheim, Germany) with polishing paste using an electric handpiece at 8000 rpm for 15 s [14]. The other surface was only ground with 800 grit wet silicon carbide paper, to simulate the bonding surface of restorations in clinic [9]. The final thickness was confirmed by a digital micrometer and the error was controlled within ± 0.05 mm. All specimens were ultrasonically cleaned in distilled water for 10 min and dried with compressed air.

Twenty cement specimens were fabricated with a thickness of 0.1 ± 0.01 mm and divided into 4 groups based on shades: yellow, white, transparent, and bleach XL (n = 5). The resin cement was syringed into a rectangular cavity (12 × 14 × 0.1 mm) in a hard-plastic plate placed on a glass plate, and another glass plate was placed over the specimens. All cement specimens were light-polymerized for 40 s using a light-polymerization unit (Bluephase II; Ivoclar Vivadent, Schaan, Liechtenstein). Then, the cement specimens were removed from the plastic plate under slight finger pressure. All specimens were immersed in distilled water at 37 ± 1 ˚C for 24 h to ensure complete polymerization.

IPS Natural Die Material with 5 shades (ND1, ND2, ND3, ND4, and ND5) were selected to simulate different colors of abutment teeth in clinic. Each specimen (12 × 14 × 4 mm) was made in a custom-designed, Teflon mold through filling and light-polymerizing for 40 s. Both sides of each specimen were ground and polished with 800, 1200, and 2400 grift wet silicon carbide papers at 150 rpm for 15 s. The final thickness was adjusted to 4 ± 0.05 mm. All specimens were immersed in distilled water at 37 ± 1 ˚C for 24 h to ensure complete polymerization.

CIELAB values (L*: lightness; a*: red-green; b*: yellow-blue) were measured 3 times to calculate the average values using a portable spectroradiometer (Vita Easyshade V; VITA Zahnfabrik, Bad Säckingen, Germany). The spectrophotometer was calibrated in compliance with the manufacturer’s instructions before each measurement. The spectroradiometer tip faced the center of the specimen and contact with the measuring surface during measurement.

For the test groups of combined specimens, the cement specimen with different shades was placed between the ceramic specimen with different translucency and thickness, and the background specimen with different shade as described previously [14]. Therefore, 120 subgroups of combined specimens with different ceramic translucency, ceramic thickness, cement shade, and background color were tested in this study. A drop of the saturated sucrose solution with a refractive index of 1.5 approximately was placed between each layer to provide a good optical connection. Color coordinates were measured and recorded against a black background. The color coordinates of control groups and backgrounds were shown in Table 1.

The color difference between the test and control groups against black background were calculated using the CIEDE2000 color difference formula (ΔE00) as follows:

All parametric factors of the CIEDE2000 color difference formula were set to 1. RT was the rotation factor that considers the interactions between hue and chroma differences in the blue area. ΔE00 values of 1.8 were regarded as acceptability thresholds [23].

Data analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Mac (version 26; IBM, Armonk, NY). Shapiro–Wilk test showed that the data presented normal distribution (p > 0.05). A four-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with the Bonferroni correction was used to assess the effects of translucency, thickness, cement shade, background color, and their interaction color difference. Post-hoc tests using the least significant difference (LSD) test were undertaken when a significant main effect or interaction effect was found in the overall ANOVA. The test standard was bilateral α = 0.05. The significance level was set at p < 0.05.

LT translucency group always had a lower ΔE00 value than HT translucency group when compared under the same background color and ceramic thickness regardless of the cement shade. In addition, ΔE00 value decreased with increasing ceramic thickness when other influencing factors were the same. Regardless of cement shade and ceramic thickness, ΔE00 values of all HT translucency groups were greater than 1.8 against all background colors. However, ΔE00 values of LT translucency groups could be achieved to be less than 1.8 with appropriate thickness and cement shade, regardless of background color. The above results could be intuitively obtained from the Fig. 1, and had been verified by post-hoc tests which showed significant differences (p > 0.05).

Because the effect of the cement shade on ΔE00 value was not intuitive in Fig. 1, pairwise comparison results of ΔE00 values between different cement shade subgroups were presented in Table 3. Although there were statistically significant differences among ΔE00 values of four different cement shade subgroups with the same translucency under the same background color and ceramic thickness, the ΔE00 values were still very close to each other in most cases, especially for HT translucency subgroups. However, it was worth noting that ΔE00 values of the subgroups with bleach XL cement shade were always lower than other cement shade subgroups under ND3 and ND5 background color with large absolute differences while they were always the highest under ND2 background color, for LT translucency ceramic.

Discussion

The null hypothesis was rejected as the results indicated that translucency, ceramic thickness, cement shade, background color, and their interaction had significant effects on the final color of resin-matrix ceramics.

Glass ceramics are the most commonly-used and preferred restorative material for veneer restoration, because of their excellent optical properties and adequate mechanical properties [24, 25]. Numerous studies have demonstrated the relationship between the final color of glass-ceramic restorations and translucency, restoration thickness, background color, and cement shade [15, 19–21]. Besides, some studies have fully analyzed the effect of the four factors on the esthetic results of glass-ceramic restorations as a whole [15, 20], providing complete and reliable evidence support for clinical applications. With the development of CAD-CAM technology and all-ceramic materials, resin-matrix ceramics are introduced which combine the advantages of ceramic and composite resin, while weakening the drawbacks of both materials themselves [18]. Nowadays, CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics become a popular optional material for veneer restorations, especially for chair-side treatment. However, information regarding the effects of translucency, thickness, background color, and cement shade on the color of resin-matrix ceramics is limited. Therefore, this study simultaneously investigated the four factors as a whole to provide references for the clinical application of CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics in veneer restoration.

Translucency is the first factor affecting the masking ability of CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics. If a ΔE00 value lower than 1.8 is considered clinically acceptable for color change, only 18 subgroups of the combined specimens with low translucency could reach below this threshold and most of the restorations had a thickness of 1.5 mm. Besides, none of the subgroups with high translucency were in this range. The results were similar to previous studies on glass ceramics which revealed that the translucency parameter could be used to predict the CAD-CAM ceramic restoration esthetic [20, 26]. Likewise, HT resin-matrix ceramics have less crystalline content than LT resin-matrix ceramics, reducing the internal scattering of light as it passes through the material [16]. Therefore, HT CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics have a poor masking ability, whose application may lead to limited esthetic result when faced with discolored teeth.

However, ceramics with high translucency are frequently used for veneer restorations in clinic, because when facing teeth with normal color, the goal is usually to restore the original color of the teeth rather than masking the original color. In addition to translucency, ceramic shade also plays a vital role in masking ability and final color [20, 27]. For example, ceramic restorations with A2 shade always have a better masking ability with a lower translucency than that with A1 shade [28]. In this study, only A2 shade resin-matrix ceramics were measured because considering that the color of most people’s anterior teeth is A2 shade. However, shade selection for veneer restorations varies among individuals, which mainly depends on the shade of the abutment teeth and the patient’s preference [29]. For example, in order to achieve a more harmonious and natural esthetic result, A1 shade restorations may be more suitable for ND1 shade abutment teeth, while A3 shade restorations may be more suitable for ND4 shade abutment teeth.

ΔE00 value decreased as the ceramic thickness increased when other conditions were consistent. The result was consistent with previous studies and indicated that the masking ability of CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics increased with increasing ceramic thickness [9, 18]. It has been found that the influence of thickness on color is closely related to translucency, that is, an increase in ceramic thickness results in a decrease in translucency [18]. Therefore, when it comes to discolored teeth, adequate tooth preparation is necessary to obtain a sufficient thickness for restorations to get an expected esthetic effect.

Based on the results, it was found that the background color significantly affected the masking ability of CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics. The greater the difference in L*, a*, and b* values between background and resin-matrix ceramic, the greater the color difference between the final color and the target color. Although color difference can be controlled within a clinically acceptable threshold by increasing ceramic thickness when choosing appropriate ceramic translucency and cement shade, an increase in ceramic thickness needs more tooth preparation. The thickness of enamel on the labial surface of anterior teeth is less than 1.5 mm and gradually decreases from the incisal margin to the cervical margin. And the cervical thickness of enamel is only 0.3–0.4 mm on the anterior teeth [11]. Multiple studies have demonstrated that more bonding strength will significantly declines when less than 50% enamel is preserved on the bonding surface, contributing to more debonding of veneer restorations [30, 31]. Therefore, obtaining an expected esthetic result by increasing ceramic thickness is limited for veneer restoration. Dental bleaching should be considered firstly to change the color of the abutment teeth when it comes to veneer restorations for discolored teeth [32].

At present, there is no unified consensus on the effect of cement shade on the masking ability and final color of veneer restorations. Some studies reported that different cement shade could lead to significant color differences in the final color [14, 20]. On the contrary, some studies claimed that the final color of ceramic restorations was not affected by cement shade [33, 34]. The results of this study showed that the cement shade could significantly affect the masking ability of CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics, then affecting the final color. However, ΔE00 values of measured combined specimens with different cement shades were still very close to each other in most cases, which means that cement shade has less of an impact on the final color of restorations. But it was worth noting that the restorations with bleach XL cement shade presented the highest ΔE00 value against background colors of ND1 and ND2 compared with the other cement shades, regardless of ceramic translucency. Nevertheless, the restorations with bleach XL cement shade presented the lowest ΔE00 value against background colors of ND3 and ND5 in LT translucency group. According to the manufacturer’s introduction, bleach XL shade cement has the highest opacity among four cement shades. These results indicated that cement with a higher opacity can help to improve the masking ability of CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics and reduce the color difference between the final color and the target color when facing discolored teeth. However, opaque cement may lead to a great color difference when facing the teeth with a whiter shade [14, 35]. Certainly, it is recommended to use try-in pastes that match the resin cement system to minimize the color difference between the final color and the target color of restorations as much as possible, thereby obtaining an expected esthetic effect [36].

Limitations of the present study include only one brand of CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics was evaluated. The translucency of CAD-CAM resin-matrix ceramics varies among different brands. Composite resins were used to simulate abutment teeth, but the optical properties of natural teeth are different from composite resins. The color performance of different cement brands varies, so studies involved in other cement brands are needed to expand the conclusion on the effect of cement shade. Besides, there are teeth with deeper discoloration in clinic beyond the background color range evaluated in this study. In addition to final color, color stability also should be paid attention to, as it can affect by various factors such as stain shade [37]. Therefore, further studies addressing these limitations are needed and clinical studies are required to further verify the results of this in vitro analysis.

Conclusions

Within the limitations of the current study, it was concluded that:

Cement shade has less of an impact on the final color of restorations. However, Bleach XL cement always presented the highest ΔE00 value against ND1 and ND2 background colors both in LT and HT translucency groups, while presented the lowest ΔE00 value against ND3 and ND5 background colors in LT translucency group.

Cement shade has less of an impact on the final color of restorations. However, Bleach XL cement always presented the highest ΔE00 value against ND1 and ND2 background colors both in LT and HT translucency groups, while presented the lowest ΔE00 value against ND3 and ND5 background colors in LT translucency group.

Regardless of background color, the color difference between the final color and the target color can always be controlled within a clinically acceptable threshold when selecting appropriate ceramic translucency, ceramic thickness, and cement shade. However, dental bleaching should be considered to reduce the required depth of tooth preparation, in order to obtain an expected esthetic effect and reliable bonding strength.

Regardless of background color, the color difference between the final color and the target color can always be controlled within a clinically acceptable threshold when selecting appropriate ceramic translucency, ceramic thickness, and cement shade. However, dental bleaching should be considered to reduce the required depth of tooth preparation, in order to obtain an expected esthetic effect and reliable bonding strength.

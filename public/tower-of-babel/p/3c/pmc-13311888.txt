AQP1/4, CLIC5 dysregulation and lipid metabolism alterations in lung cancer
Authors: Heung-Seok Bae, Je-Yoel Cho
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To advance the development of novel therapies for lung cancer, we investigated tumor-associated molecules implicated in tumorigenesis. RNA-seq data were generated from paired tumors and adjacent normal tissues of four patients with lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) and five patients with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD). Additional analyses utilized RNA-seq data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), including paired tumor and adjacent normal samples (51 LUSC, 57 LUAD) and tumor-only samples (450 LUSC, 461 LUAD). Adjacent normal tissues served as controls. Our RNA-seq results showed strong concordance with TCGA data. Ion channels Aqp1, Aqp4, and Clic5 were significantly downregulated in lung tumors, whereas enzymes involved in membrane lipid metabolism, including phosphatidylcholine (PC), sphingomyelin (SM), and cholesterol (Cho), were upregulated in lung tumors. Cardiolipin (CL), a mitochondrial inner membrane lipid, was downregulated in lung tumors. These changes might have impaired oxygen permeability and mitochondrial function, promoting hypoxia and reactive oxygen species (ROS) production. Hif1a expression was elevated in both LUSC and LUAD, along with a hypoxia-responsive protein kinase Csnk2a1 and its downstream targets Hdac1 and Hdac2. ROS-responsive transcription factors Yy1, Foxm1, E2f1, and E2f8 were also significantly upregulated in both LUSC and LUAD. Notably, the master epigenetic regulator Uhrf1 activated by these transcription factors showed marked overexpression in tumors compared to that in normal tissues. TCGA data corroborated these findings. Our study identified tumor cell membrane-associated molecules, including ion channels (Aqp1, Aqp4, Clic5) and membrane lipid metabolism enzymes (PC, SM, Cho, and CL), as critical contributors to lung tumorigenesis. These molecules represent promising targets for developing innovative anti-cancer therapies.

INTRODUCTION

Lung cancer can be categorized into small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). It remains the leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide (1-3). Early detection and identifying cancer biomarkers and drug targets are crucial for improving diagnostic and therapeutic outcomes. Established lung cancer biomarkers include CYFRA21-1, CEA, SCC, NSE, ProGRP, and EGFR (4). In NSCLC, mutations in proto-oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes such as EGFR (69.5%), TP53 (56.6%), ERBB2 (9.8%), CDKN2A (7.8%), and KRAS (7.0%) have been identified as potential or established drug targets (5).

Going beyond current anticancer drugs that focus on blocking cell division, to understand and access the state of cells, we investigated tumorigenesis-associated molecules in NSCLC. The cell membrane is critical to maintaining cellular homeostasis and mediating responses to external stimuli, with ion channels and lipids being key components (6-9). Ion channels convert external chemical and physical signals into electrical signals, especially in excitable tissues such as nerves and muscles. Dysfunctions in ion channels have been implicated in diseases such as hypertension, insulin secretion defects, cardiac arrhythmias, and neurological disorders (10-13). Notably, several ion channel inhibitors are currently under development as potential therapeutic agents for lung cancer (14-16). Understanding lipid compositions of cell membranes is equally essential for characterizing cancer cells.

Using RNA-seq data, we examined the expression of ion channels and lipid metabolism enzymes to elucidate membrane alterations in NSCLC (17). Our analysis of four patients with LUSC and five patients with LUAD validated by TCGA data revealed a near absence of Clic5 and Aqp1/4 expression in tumors compared to adjacent normal tissues. Concurrently, enzymes involved in the synthesis of PC, SM, and Cho were upregulated, while CL, a critical mitochondrial lipid, was downregulated. These alterations suggest that hypoxia, impaired mitochondrial function, and increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) production are associated with LUSC and LUAD.

Consistent with these findings, hypoxia-related genes (Hif1a, Csnk2a1, and Hdac1/2) and ROS-responsive transcription factors (Yy1, Foxm1, E2f1, and E2f8). were significantly elevated in tumors. The master epigenetic regulator Uhrf1 activated by these transcription factors was also upregulated in tumors.

In conclusion, our findings highlight the importance of ion channels (AQP1, AQP4, and CLIC5) and membrane lipid enzymes (PC, SM, Cho, and CL) in NSCLC tumorigenesis. These molecules represent promising targets for developing innovative lung cancer therapies.

RESULTS

Expression levels of Ion channels Clic5, Aqp1, and Aqp4 are very low in LUSC and LUAD

To investigate differential expression of 215 ion channel genes listed by the HGNC (HUGO Gene Nomenclature Committee), a volcano plot was generated using RNA-seq data from TCGA for 51 patients with LUSC and 57 patients with LUAD (Fig. 1A, B). Among the top 25 genes with the highest −log10 P-values, 18 were commonly expressed in LUSC and LUAD. They were categorized into five ion channel families. Despite significant biological differences between LUSC and LUAD, similar alterations in ion channels suggest the involvement of common factors in tumorigenesis. Notably, several ion channel families, including chloride intracellular channels (CLICs), water channels (AQPs), and those associated with Na＋, K＋, and Ca2＋, showed significant downregulation in LUSC and LUAD (Fig. 1A1, B1).

Acid-sensing ion channels (ASICs) gated by extracellular protons with a high sodium affinity comprise five isoforms (ASIC1-5). Among these, ASIC2a requires a more acidic pH for activation (18). Isoform 2 was upregulated by 4.9-fold in LUSC and by 2.5-fold in LUAD, suggesting increased acid levels in these cancer types. To further investigate expression differences between tumor and normal tissues, a volcano plot analysis based on RSEM expression difference was performed. Consistent with results of the previous log2 fold change volcano plot, Clic5, Aqp1, and Aqp4 exhibited the most significant differences between tumor and normal tissues (Fig. 1C, D).

In our RNA-seq data from four patients with LUSC and five patients with LUAD, expression levels of 215 ion channel genes were analyzed. Among them, Clic5, Aqp1, and Aqp4 were expressed at markedly lower levels in tumor tissues than in adjacent normal tissues (Fig. 1C1, D1). Similar results were obtained from TCGA data analysis of tumor and adjacent normal tissues (51 LUSC, 57 LUAD) as well as tumor-only samples (450 LUSC, 461 LUAD) (Fig. 1C2-C4, 1D2-D4). A detailed workflow for downloading and analyzing TCGA data is provided in Supplementary Fig. 1 and Methods.

Clinical information for the TCGA dataset (504 LUSC and 522 LUAD cases) is summarized in Supplementary Data 2 and Supplementary Fig. 2. In summary, 94.4% of LUSC cases and 82.8% of LUAD cases had a history of smoking. The initial diagnosis of lung cancer was most common in the 60s and 70s. Among patients with LUSC, 74% were males and 26% were females, whereas in LUAD cases, 46% were males and 54% were females.

The chloride intracellular ion channel (CLIC) family comprises six members, CLIC1–6. They exist in soluble and membrane-associated forms (19). CLICs form redox- and pH-sensitive ion channels (20-22). Chloride intracellular ion channel 5 (CLIC5) is localized to the inner mitochondrial membrane. It functions as an ion channel. In its soluble form, CLIC5, participates in actin-dependent trafficking events. Its function is inhibited by F-actin (23). Studies have reported that CLIC5 knockout (KO) mice produce higher levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) than wild-type (WT) mice (24). Beyond its role as an ion channel, CLIC5 also localizes to vesicles, contributing to membrane coupling with the actin cytoskeleton (25, 26). While investigating causal genes for autosomal recessive nonsyndromic hearing impairment (arNSHI), it was observed that CLIC5 was highly expressed in hair cell stereocilia (27). Individuals with CLIC5 mutations exhibit progressive hearing loss, vestibular abnormalities, and kidney dysfunction (27). Based on previous studies, the absence of CLIC5 in lung cancer suggests impaired mitochondrial inner membrane function and increased ROS production.

Aquaporin1 (AQP1) has been identified as a water channel protein involved in oxygen transport (28, 29). High glucose levels can induce mitochondrial ROS production, which can downregulate AQP1 expression, resulting in intracellular hypoxia (30). In lung cancer, a significant reduction in AQP1 may serve as a potential predictor of intracellular hypoxia.

In AQP4 KO mice, mitochondrial genes such as Pgc1a and Cox4 were found to be downregulated (31). Altered expression of other genes, including CytC, Fis1, Mfn1, and Mfn2, further impacted mitochondrial function, ultimately leading to neurodegeneration (31). Additionally, studies using AQP4 KO mice demonstrated that AQP4 could facilitate oxygen diffusion in the brain (32). The significant downregulation of AQP4 in lung cancer suggests that decreased mitochondrial function contributes to ROS generation and intracellular hypoxia. Recent studies employing molecular dynamics simulations, potential of mean force analyses, permeability calculations, and computational electrophysiology have highlighted AQP4’s role in ROS transport (33). In conclusion, downregulation of Clic5, Aqp1, and Aqp4 might lead to cellular hypoxia, mitochondrial dysfunction, and increased ROS production in non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) (Fig. 1E, F, Supplementary Fig. 3).

Increased cholesterol synthesis and decreased cardiolipin synthesis occur in LUSC and LUAD

To investigate changes in cell membrane lipids, we summarized commonly known pathways based on related publications (Supplementary Fig. 4) (34). Fifty genes involved in steroid biosynthesis and metabolism were analyzed using RNA-seq data (Supplementary Data 3). HMG-CoA reductase (Hmgcr), the rate-limiting enzyme gene, showed no significant increase in LUSC or LUAD (Fig. 2A, B). However, secondary metabolites, including geranyl pyrophosphate (GPP), coenzyme Q (ubiquinone), heme A, and dolichol, were produced in the second messenger pathway (Fig. 2E) (35, 36). Enzymes contributing to increased cholesterol levels include Fdft1 and Sqle. Squalene epoxidase (SQLE), the second rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol biosynthesis, has been found to be significantly upregulated in LUSC and moderately but not significantly upregulated in LUAD (Fig. 2A3, B3) (37-43).

Srebp2, a critical transcription factor for cholesterol biosynthesis (44), is slightly increased in LUSC but slightly decreased in LUAD (Fig. 3A, B). Cholesterol is converted to cholesterol esters by Acat1 and bile acids by various enzymes, including Cyp27a1 (45). Expression levels of Acat1 and Cyp27a1 are markedly decreased in both LUSC and LUAD (Fig. 3A, B), suggesting cholesterol accumulation in lung cancer, especially in LUSC (46).

TCGA data reflected findings for LUSC (Fig. 2A1-A6). However, TCGA results for LUAD were different (Fig. 2B1-B6). Despite reduced Srebp2 expression, a decrease in Acat1 and Cyp27a1, enzymes responsible for cholesterol conversion, supports the hypothesis of cholesterol accumulation in LUAD. This aligns with a lipidomics study of tumor tissues from 29 LUAD patients (47). It reported significant increases of cholesterol and cholesterol esters in LUAD compared to adjacent normal tissues (47). Similarly, other studies have documented elevated cholesterol ester levels in LUAD (48). Increased cholesterol in cell membranes can strengthen bonds with PC, creating tight gaps that can inhibit oxygen diffusion and cause hypoxia (Fig. 2F) (49). Cholesterol is normally absent in mitochondrial inner membranes. Its accumulation in mitochondrial inner membranes can disrupt the electron transport chain, increasing ROS generation and decreasing ATP production (Fig. 2F) (50). Thus, ROS production is expected to be increased in LUSC and LUAD.

Cardiolipin (CL), a lipid specific to mitochondrial inner membranes, plays a critical role in mitochondrial function by binding and regulating various proteins and enzymes (51, 52). Six enzymes involved in cardiolipin synthesis were investigated in our RNA-seq data (Supplementary Data 3) (Fig. 2C, D) and TCGA data (Fig. 2C1-C6, 2D1-D6). Important enzymes in cardiolipin synthesis have been reported to be PGS1, PTPMT1, and CRLS1. Since these enzymes are decreased in TCGA, a decrease in CL is expected to occur in LUSC and LUAD (53).

Reduced cardiolipin levels are expected to impair cytochrome C activity, electron transfer, and ATP generation in mitochondria (54, 55). This likely contributes to increased ROS production, as previously demonstrated in studies linking ROS generation to lung cancer and COPD through epigenetic HDAC signaling pathways (56, 57). Excessive ROS can react with double bonds of unsaturated fatty acids, initiating lipid peroxidation (LPO) and producing aldehyde metabolites, which are biomarkers of oxidative stress. Elevated levels of aldehydes have been reported in lung cancer patients, highlighting their potential as diagnostic biomarkers (58, 59). These findings support our hypothesis that hypoxia and increased ROS levels are caused by decreased expression of Clic5 and Aqp1/4, increased Cho, and decreased CL in LUSC and LUAD.

Hif1a and Hypoxia-driven Csnk2a1-Hdac signaling are increased in LUSC and LUAD

The almost complete lack of Aqp1 and Aqp4, which can promote oxygen and water transport, and the increase of Cho suggest intracellular hypoxia. To investigate this, we examined the expression of Hif1a, a master transcriptional regulator of hypoxic adaptation. As expected, Hif1a expression was increased 1.7-fold in LUSC and 1.9-fold in LUAD, consistent with TCGA data (Fig. 3A, B, A1, B1).

Under hypoxic stress, protein kinase CSNK2A1 (CK2) is activated, with its catalytic subunits translocating to the nucleus to phosphorylate and activate HDAC (60). RNA-seq data revealed a 3.5-fold increase in Csnk2a1 expression in LUSC and a 2.2-fold increase in LUAD, corroborated by TCGA data (Fig. 3C, D, C1, D1).

Among the five ASIC isomers, Asic2, which requires a more acidic pH for activation, is upregulated 4.9-fold in LUSC and 2.5-fold in LUAD. This upregulation aligns with a 3.5-fold increase of Csnk2a1 expression in LUSC and a 2.2-fold increase in LUAD, as CSNK2A1 is known to be responsive to hypoxic conditions. Expression levels of Hdac1 and Hdac2, targets of CSNK2A1, were increased in both LUSC and LUAD (Fig. 3E-H), with Hdac1 showing a significant increase in LUAD and Hdac2 in LUSC. TCGA data also confirmed these observations (Fig. 3E1, F1, G1, H1).

Uhrf1 and ROS-responsive transcription factors Yy1, Foxm1, E2f1, and E2f8 are significantly increased in lung cancers

Given that high ROS production and hypoxia were expected due to substantial downregulation of ion channels Clic5, Aqp1, and Aqp4, an increase in cholesterol, and a decrease in cardiolipin, we investigated alterations in associated epigenetic enzymes, including Hdac (Fig. 3) and Uhrf1. UHRF1 is a multidomain protein that serves as a critical epigenetic regulator by linking DNA methylation to chromatin modification (61). Its expression is minimal in normal tissues but markedly elevated, showing a 16-fold increase in LUSC and an 11-fold increase in LUAD (Fig. 4A, B). These findings were corroborated by analyses using TCGA data (Fig. 4A1, B1) and the GEPIA 2 database (Supplementary Fig. 5).

To elucidate the regulatory mechanism underlying the significant upregulation of Uhrf1, we examined transcription factors known to act on UHRF1 (61). Among identified candidates, YY1, FOXM1, E2F1, and E2F8 have been reported to be activated by ROS (62-65). Our RNA-seq data revealed substantially increased expression of Yy1, Foxm1, E2f1, and E2f8 (Fig. 4C-J). This trend was also observed in TCGA data (Fig. 4C1-J1). These findings suggest that elevated expression of Uhrf1 might be attributed to upregulation of ROS-responsive transcription factors.

Epigenetic research focuses on DNA methylation, histone modifications, RNA interference, and histone isoform variations that can modulate cellular phenotypes without altering DNA sequences. These epigenetic traits are faithfully transmitted during cell division. A pivotal protein in this process is UHRF1, which bridges DNA methylation and histone modification through its five domains. UHRF1 collaborates with HDAC for histone deacetylation, with G9a for histone methylation and DNMT1 for DNA methylation (66). Its expression is notably high in tissues with active cell division (such as bone marrow, lymph nodes, appendix, and testis) (Supplementary Fig. 6) (67) and in most cancer types (Supplementary Fig. 5) (68).

UHRF1 interacts with retinoblastoma protein (pRB1), a key regulator of the G1/S transition in the cell cycle. Overexpression of UHRF1 can reduce both pRB1 protein levels and pRB1 mRNA, thereby enabling the perpetual cycling of cancer cells (69). Canonically, UHRF1 facilitates DNA methylation maintenance via DNMT1. Non-canonical functions of UHRF1, which are independent of DNMT1, also contribute to DNA methylation homeostasis (70). In cancer cells, UHRF1 not only propagates the epigenetic state, but also promotes continuous cell division and sustains the unique epigenetic landscape of cancer. Thus, developing therapeutic agents targeting UHRF1 is crucial for cancer treatment.

Cell membrane lipids PC, PE, PS, PI, and SM are elevated in LUSC and LUAD

Analysis of RNA-seq data from 4 LUSC and 5 LUAD samples revealed increases of cell membrane lipids phosphatidylcholine (PC), phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), phosphatidylserine (PS), phosphatidylinositol (PI), and sphingomyelin (SM). Specifically, PE, PS, PI, and SM levels were elevated in both LUSC and LUAD, while PC was only notably increased in LUSC (Supplementary Data 3). Corresponding analyses of TCGA data showed consistent increases of PC, PE, PS, PI, and SM in both cancer types.

For PC, upregulation of Pcyt1a, the rate-limiting enzyme in PC synthesis, was observed in both LUSC and LUAD, accounting for the increase of PC in LUAD as well (Fig. 5A, A1-A5; Fig. 5B, B1-B5). Regarding PE, while Etnk1, which plays a prominent role in breast and pancreatic cancers, was slightly decreased in LUSC, its functional redundancy with Etnk2 and Pisd (which converts PS to PE) might compensate for such decrease, resulting in an overall increase of PE (Fig. 5C, C1-C5; Fig. 5D, D1-D5).

For PS, enzymes Ptdss1 and Ptdss2 responsible for PS synthesis were significantly upregulated in both cancer types (Fig. 5E-H). Similarly, the upregulation of Mboat7, which catalyzes the synthesis of PI, corresponds to the observed increase in PI level (Fig. 5I, J). Finally, the substantial increase in Sms, the enzyme responsible for SM synthesis, accounts for elevated SM levels (Fig. 5K, L).

These findings aligned with a study comparing lipid profiles of 22 LUAD patients to their adjacent normal tissues, which reported increases of PC, PE, PS, PI, and SM (48). The consistency between these independent findings and our study further validated our results.

DISCUSSION

The cell membrane is responsible for maintaining characteristics of the cell from the external environment. Composed primarily of lipids and proteins, the cell membrane serves as the primary gateway to maintaining cellular uniqueness. Consequently, alterations in the cell membrane reflect changes in cellular properties and often precede broader cellular transformations. In this study, we investigated potential causes of lung cancer (LUSC, LUAD) by analyzing changes in ion channels and membrane lipid composition. Although LUSC and LUAD are classified as distinct cancer types, similar alterations in ion channels and membrane lipids were observed.

Both LUSC and LUAD exhibited low levels of Aqp1 and Aqp4 but elevated cholesterol content, leading to reduced oxygen permeability and potential induction of hypoxic conditions. CSNK2A1 has been reported to phosphorylate HDAC under hypoxic conditions (60). Our findings demonstrated activation of Hif1, a hypoxia marker, and the Csnk2a1-Hdac1/2 axis in hypoxia, thereby corroborating previously published studies.

In addition, an increase in cholesterol, a decrease in mitochondrial cardiolipin, and decreases in expression of Clic5 (an inner mitochondrial membrane ion channel) and Aqp4 (involved in mitochondrial function) were observed, suggesting enhanced ROS generation. Furthermore, transcription factors Yy1, Foxm1, E2f1, and E2f8 that can regulate Uhrf1 and become activated by ROS are significantly upregulated in 4 LUSC and 5 LUAD RNA-seq data sets and TCGA data.

In summary, hypoxia, increased ROS levels, and impaired mitochondrial function resulting from downregulation of Clic5 and Aqp1/4, increased cholesterol, reduced cardiolipin, contributed to the upregulation of Hif1a, Hdac1/2, and Uhrf1, thus driving the progression of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) (Fig. 6). We believe this study provides valuable insights to the development of therapeutic strategies targeting NSCLC.

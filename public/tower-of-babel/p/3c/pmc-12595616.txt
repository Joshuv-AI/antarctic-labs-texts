Takotsubo cardiomyopathy with rapid left ventricular recovery unmasking asymptomatic but significant coronary artery disease: a case report
Authors: Hassan Elzain, Mohamed Nadir Mahmoud Karar, Mohamed Adel Mostafa, Jasem H Redha, Anas Bedawi Babiker, Nader Alasousi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTakotsubo cardiomyopathy, also known as stress-induced cardiomyopathy, is a transient cardiac condition characterized by acute but reversible left ventricular dysfunction, typically triggered by emotional or physical stress. Introduction

Takotsubo cardiomyopathy (TC), also known as stress-induced cardiomyopathy or “broken heart syndrome,” is characterized by transient left ventricular dysfunction typically triggered by acute emotional or physical stressors. It predominantly affects postmenopausal women and classically presents with an apical ballooning pattern of the left ventricle, although midventricular and basal variants have also been reported [1, 2]. TC has diverse clinical presentations and outcomes, ranging from mild symptoms to severe complications, highlighting the importance of early recognition and management [3]. Differentiating TC from acute coronary syndrome (ACS) can be challenging, particularly in patients with coexisting significant coronary artery disease (CAD), due to overlapping clinical presentations and cardiac biomarker elevations [4, 5]. Importantly, TC can unmask underlying asymptomatic but significant CAD, potentially necessitating revascularization [6, 7].

Case presentation

The patient was a 56-year-old Asian woman with a history of hypertension and diabetes, managed with metformin. She had been asymptomatic prior to presentation, with no reports of chest pain during her daily activities, no shortness of breath (SOB), and no paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (PND). She works as a customer service representative at a telecommunication company.

The patient experienced chest pain described as a pressure-like sensation. The pain began immediately after an emotional and physical altercation with a customer, who struck her and pushed her to the ground, causing her to hit the back of her head. She did not lose consciousness and arrived at the emergency department shortly afterward.

On examination, the patient was alert, distressed, and hemodynamically stable. Cardiovascular and respiratory exams were unremarkable. A superficial occipital hematoma was noted without neurological deficits.

Initial electrocardiogram (ECG) performed upon admission (Fig. 1) showed small ST-segment elevation in the anterior leads, not fulfilling the criteria for ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI). Serial troponin measurements revealed an initial level of 0.1 ng/mL, rising to 0.6 ng/mL after 2 hours, and peaking at 1.9 ng/mL on the second day. Other investigations, including complete blood count (CBC), renal function tests, lipid profile, and chest X-ray, were unremarkable.

Electrocardiogram at the time of admission showing sinus tachycardia and mild ST-segment elevation in the anterolateral leads

Bedside echocardiogram (limited scan) performed in the emergency department revealed akinesia of the apex. Consequently, the patient was shifted to the catheterization laboratory, where coronary angiography showed significant left main stenosis (~50–70%), diffuse left anterior descending (LAD) disease with critical narrowing in the mid and distal segments, and proximal LCx stenosis (~70–80%) with mild distal disease. The Right Coronary Artery (RCA) showed diffuse atherosclerotic changes but no critical stenosis, maintaining thrombolysis in myocardial infarction (TIMI)-3 flow (Video 1). The findings were consistent with high-risk multivessel coronary artery disease, and the patient was subsequently planned for coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) after heart team discussion.

Detailed echocardiography performed on day 1 (Video 2) confirmed apical ballooning with normally contracting basal segments, and an ejection fraction (EF) of 20%. Speckle-tracking analysis (global longitudinal strain, GLS) further supported the diagnosis by showing the characteristic Takotsubo pattern: severely reduced strain at the apex with preserved basal contraction (Fig. 2). The patient remained asymptomatic while awaiting inpatient CABG. On day 3, follow-up echocardiography (Video 3) demonstrated significant improvement, with seminormal left ventricular function. The ECG evolved to show diffuse T-wave inversions and QT interval prolongation (Fig. 3). By day 5, the patient remained asymptomatic, and echocardiography showed complete normalization of left ventricular function with an EF of 60% (Video 4). GLS had also normalized by that time, confirming full functional recovery (Fig. 4). Cardiac markers, including troponin and B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP), also normalized without any revascularization during this period. These findings indicated that the left ventricular dysfunction was due to Takotsubo cardiomyopathy rather than coronary artery disease (CAD).

Despite initial plans for CABG, the patient strongly refused the surgery. As an alternative, percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) to the left main and LAD was performed with a good final result and Thrombolysis In Myocardial Infarction (TIMI) III flow (Video 5). The patient was subsequently discharged home on full anti-ischemic therapy and scheduled for outpatient follow-up.

At 1- and 6-month follow-up visits, the patient remained asymptomatic, and echocardiography consistently showed normal left ventricular function.

Discussion

Takotsubo cardiomyopathy (TC), often termed stress-induced cardiomyopathy or “broken heart syndrome,” is an acute but generally reversible form of left ventricular (LV) dysfunction. It frequently manifests as apical ballooning, although mid-ventricular and basal variants have also been described [1]. The syndrome is most commonly observed in postmenopausal women, typically following an intense emotional or physical stressor [2]. Although the exact pathophysiological mechanism remains under investigation, an excessive surge in catecholamines is believed to play a central role, leading to transient myocardial stunning rather than permanent damage [2]. This characteristic reversibility differentiates TC from classical ischemic events, where coronary obstruction can result in sustained injury to the myocardium [3].

Distinguishing Takotsubo cardiomyopathy (TC) from acute coronary syndrome (ACS) in the presence of significant coronary artery disease (CAD) poses a diagnostic challenge [4, 5]. In the majority of cases, TC manifests as transient left ventricular dysfunction without obstructive coronary lesions [5]. However, in our patient, the presence of high-risk multivessel CAD made the initial differentiation more complex. The presence of small ST-segment elevation on ECG and elevated troponin levels further complicated the distinction. However, key diagnostic clues favoring TC included the apical ballooning pattern on echocardiography, the normalization of left ventricular function within 5 days, and the resolution of cardiac biomarkers without immediate revascularization. These findings emphasized that TC can coexist with significant CAD and may even serve as an indicator of underlying asymptomatic significant coronary artery disease [6, 7].

This case is particularly notable due to the coexistence of Takotsubo cardiomyopathy (TC) with significant coronary artery disease (CAD), a combination that is not commonly reported [6, 7]. While TC is traditionally associated with patients who have unobstructed coronary arteries, its presence alongside high-risk multivessel CAD introduces a diagnostic dilemma [8]. The transient nature of LV dysfunction, the rapid recovery of ejection fraction to 60% within 5 days, and the normalization of cardiac biomarkers without immediate revascularization strongly support the diagnosis of TC rather than ischemic cardiomyopathy. Additionally, this case underscores the importance of recognizing TC as a potential indicator of underlying asymptomatic CAD, as the patient had no prior history of angina or exertional symptoms. Had the acute stress event not triggered myocardial dysfunction, the significant coronary lesions might have remained undiagnosed, further highlighting the role of TC in unmasking subclinical ischemic disease.

The patient’s initial ECG revealed small ST-segment elevations in the anterior leads (V2–V3) without fulfilling STEMI criteria, a common finding in Takotsubo cardiomyopathy (TC). This was accompanied by sinus rhythm without significant conduction abnormalities. By day 3, the ECG evolved to show diffuse T-wave inversions and QT prolongation, a progression commonly reported in the literature as part of the natural course of TC [9].

Serial troponin measurements showed a modest rise, with an initial level of 0.1 ng/mL, increasing to 0.6 ng/mL after 2 hours, and peaking at 1.9 ng/mL on day 2. While the elevation in cardiac biomarkers indicated myocardial injury, the magnitude of the rise was disproportionate to the degree of left ventricular dysfunction observed on echocardiography [10]. This mismatch between biomarker elevation and clinical findings further supported the diagnosis of TC over acute coronary syndrome (ACS).

The combination of mild ECG changes, modest troponin elevation, and echocardiographic findings of apical ballooning provided strong evidence for TC while highlighting the importance of comprehensive evaluation to rule out coexisting significant coronary artery disease.

The coexistence of Takotsubo cardiomyopathy (TC) and significant coronary artery disease (CAD) requires a nuanced management approach [4, 11]. While TC is typically self-limiting, the presence of high-risk coronary lesions demands careful consideration. In this case, the initial plan was coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) due to the significant left main and multivessel disease. However, despite full recovery of left ventricular function and the patient remaining completely asymptomatic, a heart team discussion determined that leaving such high-risk coronary disease untreated posed an unacceptable long-term risk. Given the patient’s refusal of CABG, the decision was made to proceed with percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) to the left main and LAD as an alternative revascularization strategy.

Rapid recovery of left ventricular function, as observed in our patient, aligns well with the documented clinical course of Takotsubo cardiomyopathy. Although the average duration for complete functional recovery typically ranges between 1 and 3 months, significant improvement within a few days has been reported in multiple studies. This rapid reversibility is most likely related to the absence of irreversible myocardial necrosis and the transient nature of catecholamine-mediated myocardial stunning, which facilitates complete recovery once the acute trigger resolves [3, 12].

In summary, the patient’s acute presentation and symptoms were attributable to Takotsubo cardiomyopathy. This led to coronary angiography, which unexpectedly revealed critical but asymptomatic multivessel CAD. Despite the patient being asymptomatic and achieving full recovery of left ventricular function, inpatient revascularization was considered essential given the prognostic significance of left main and multivessel disease.

This case underscores the importance of individualized decision-making in managing patients with concurrent TC and significant CAD, ensuring that both the transient cardiomyopathy and the long-term cardiovascular risk are appropriately addressed.

Conclusion

Takotsubo cardiomyopathy (TC) and significant coronary artery disease (CAD) are traditionally considered distinct pathophysiological entities; however, they can coexist in the same patient, posing a unique diagnostic and therapeutic challenge. While TC is generally a transient and self-limiting condition, its occurrence in a patient with high-risk CAD underscores the importance of comprehensive cardiovascular assessment rather than assuming a benign course. In this case, stress-induced cardiomyopathy unmasked underlying asymptomatic but significant coronary artery disease. Despite full recovery of left ventricular function, the presence of significant left main and multivessel CAD warranted revascularization to mitigate long-term cardiovascular risk. This case highlights the necessity of an individualized management approach in patients presenting with TC and concurrent CAD, ensuring that both the acute phase and long-term coronary risk are appropriately addressed.

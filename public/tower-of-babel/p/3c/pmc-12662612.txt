Effect of dexmedetomidine combined with ropivacaine in ultrasound-guided paravertebral nerve block for acute herpes zoster neuralgia patients
Authors: Cuicui Liu, Yanting Wang, Pei Wang, Chao Meng, Yanwei Yin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PURPOSE:Early and effective treatment of acute herpes zoster (AHZ) can relief the pain and prevent the occurrence of postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) in patients. The aim of this study is to investigate the efficacy of dexmedetomidine combined with ropivacaine in ultrasound-guided paravertebral nerve block for patients with AHZ neuralgia.MATERIALS AND METHODS:Seventy-four patients suffering from acute thoracic herpes zoster neuralgia were divided into two equal groups in a prospective, randomized, double-blind controlled manner. All patients received paravertebral nerve block under ultrasound guidance with different drugs: Group I: 0.2% ropivacaine with 1 μg/kg dexmedetomidine; Group II: 0.2% ropivacaine, all drugs were diluted with normal saline to a total of 20 ml. The patients’ Visual Analog Scale (VAS) scores were used for assessing pain level while the quality of sleep (QS) scores were used for assessing sleep levels 1, 4, 8, and 12 weeks after the treatment, as well as the pregabalin consumption. Time to pain resolution and skin eruption, total times of block, complications within 24 h after block, and the incidence of persistent PHN at 3 and 6 months were analyzed.RESULTS:There were no significant differences in the VAS scores between the two groups at various time points (P > 0.05). At the time point 1, 4, and 8 weeks after treatment, the QS scores in Group I were significantly lower than that in Group II (P < 0.05). The pregabalin consumption and the incidence of PHN between the two groups were not significantly different (P > 0.05). The total times of block in Group I were significantly less than Group II.CONCLUSION:Both interventions provided similar efficacy in minimizing the pain intensity and reducing the incidence of PHN in AHZ patients. Compared with ropivacaine only, the addition of dexmedetomidine significantly improved patients’ sleep quality and reduced the times of block.

Introduction

Herpes zoster (HZ) is infected by varicella-zoster virus, which can cause chickenpox in childhood and then long latent in the cerebral ganglia and spinal dorsal root ganglia.[1] As the body’s immunity system weakened, the dormant virus is reactivated, replicates, and migrates along the sensory axon to the skin region, then causing the acute HZ (AHZ) in adults and the elderly,[23] which would cause acute pain. Age is illustrated to be the most important factor predicting HZ incidence, and as the world’s population is continuing to age, the HZ rates are continuing to grow, too.[4] The concomitant inflammation of the peripheral nerve and damage to the skin are thought to be responsible for the acute pain in patients with HZ.[5] AHZ neuralgia refers to the course of HZ <1 month. If the early effective treatment is not achieved, some patients may develop into postherpetic neuralgia (PHN).[6] HZ-related pain brings great physical and mental pain to patients, seriously affects their quality of life, and even brings heavy economic burden to patients, their families, and even the society.[7] Therefore, in the acute phase of HZ, effective treatment should be taken as soon as possible to relieve pain and prevent the occurrence of PHN.

The main treatment for AHZ pain includes drug therapy, physical therapy, and nerve block. Paravertebral nerve block is a widely used analgesic method in clinic, and it has similar pain-relieving effects but fewer side effects compared with epidural analgesia.[8] Previous studies have shown that early single paravertebral block (PVB) in the course of acute thoracic HZ is a safe and effective adjuvant treatment modality, and moreover, which has advantages in preventing PHN.[9] PVB is achieved by injecting local anesthetics into the paravertebral space, blocking the paravertebral spinal nerve and its branches as well as the sympathetic trunk.[1011] Depending on the type, dose, and volume of drugs injected into the paravertebral space, the results of PVB often vary.[10] At the present stage, steroids combined with local anesthetic are the main drugs for PVB, but there are some limitations in the use of steroids. A recent systematic review reported that perineural dexmedetomidine is associated with longer durations and faster onset of sensory and motor blockade.[12] Several studies have shown that when added to local anesthetic, dexmedetomidine prolongs the duration of block and postoperative analgesia in various regional blocks.[1314] However, few published studies reported the effects of dexmedetomidine as an adjuvant to local anesthetic in PVB for AHZ patients. We hypothesized that dexmedetomidine combined with ropivacaine in PVB cannot only play an analgesic role but also prolong the block time, and then, the number of blocks required by AHZ patients will be reduced, and considering the sedative effect of dexmedetomidine, the quality of sleep (QS) of patients will be improved. Thus, we designed this randomized, controlled trial to determine the efficacy of dexmedetomidine combined with ropivacaine in ultrasound-guided paravertebral nerve block for AHZ patients.

Materials and Methods

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University and was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki. The informed consent forms were obtained from all patients. From April 1, 2022, to September 30, 2022, 97 adult patients suffering from acute thoracic HZ were sent to the pain management outpatient clinic from dermatology clinic after appropriate antiviral therapy. According to the inclusion criteria, 74 patients were fulfilled (23 patients were excluded: 2 patients had heavy skin infection; 11 patients with diabetes; 3 had hepatic diseases; 2 had renal diseases; 5 refused to participate in the study).

The inclusion criteria were patients aged 50–80 years diagnosed with HZ on the chest wall for the eruption <1 month. Moreover, the patients were treated with antiviral therapy and suffering a pain Visual Analog Scale (VAS) ≥6. The exclusion criteria included patients with mild pain, infection at the puncture sites, or had extensive skin lesions (no clean area for needle entry); patients were reported hepatic, renal, or pulmonary disease, diabetes, and coagulopathy and patients who refused to participate.

Oral drug therapy

All patients received daily 300 mg pregabalin (Pfizer Manufacturing Deutschland GmbH, Betriebsstatte Freiburg, LYRICA, Germany) in divided doses (150 mg/12 h). The pain score was evaluated every day, and once the patients reported mild pain (VAS ≤3), reduced the dose of pregabalin by 75 mg every other day. If the VAS increased to more than 3, then restored to the last pregabalin dose.

Nerve block methods

Seventy-four patients were admitted to the inpatient hospital and then divided into two equal groups using a computer-generated random number table. Group I received paravertebral nerve block under ultrasound guidance with 0.2% ropivacaine (AstraZeneca AB, Naropin, Sweden) with 1 μg/kg dexmedetomidine (Hengrui, Beijing, China) in a total volume of 20 ml; Group II received paravertebral nerve block under ultrasound guidance with 0.2% ropivacaine in a total volume of 20 ml, drugs were diluted with normal saline. All of the procedures in this study were performed by the same blinded pain physician and the drugs were prepared by nurses who were blinded to the trial protocol.

Before the procedure, an intravenous infusion access was established for the infusion of 500 mL normal saline during the procedure. Thoracic PVB (TPVB) was performed under ultrasound with patient in a prone position. The selection of the PVB level was according to the HZ skin segment, the site of pain, and skin lesion. The puncture point was 2–3 cm lateral to the vertebral spine. After the back was sterilized and draped, placed the ultrasound probe along the intercostal space, and got a cross-sectional scanning, then the erector spine, spinous process, transverse process, articular process, thoracic paravertebral space, pleura, and lung tissue could be found. The in-plane puncture technique was adopted to guide the needle puncture from the lateral side of articular process into the thoracic paravertebral space, and 20 ml drugs were injected after confirmed that there was no blood or gas while needle drawing. When the injection is taken, it could be observed that the solution spreads along the paravertebral space and the pleura moving downward toward the abdomen under ultrasound [Figure 1]. After the procedure was finished, the patient was required to lie flat and be monitored in the recovery room for 1 h and then returned to the ward. Patients received treatment once a week up to 4 times, and if the pain relief is more than 1 week, then stop the treatment.

Observation indexes

The patients were taught in detail to use VAS scores and QS scores to assess their pain and sleep quality. All the date collection was achieved by another doctor who was blinded to the assignment groups. The VAS scores and QS scores were recorded before the blockage (baseline) and 1, 4, 8, and 12 weeks after blockage. VAS ranges from 0 (no pain) to 10 (worst pain) while the sleep quality score ranges from 0 (best sleep quality) to 4 (worst sleep quality). The amount of pregabalin was also recorded at different time points. The complications within 24 h after each blockage such as nausea, vomiting, pneumothorax, hypotension (>20% below baseline value), bradycardia (<50 beats/min), and hematoma were recorded. The total times of blockage were recorded, too.

During the following evaluation visits, the time of full resolution of pain (from the first blockage to complete disappearance of herpetic pain) and skin eruption (identified by the last crust drop) were recorded. The PHN was identified as a persistent or constantly pain lasting 3 months or more. The incidence of PHN was statistics after 3 and 6 months. If the patients could not attend the pain clinic, telephone follow-up was allowed for the date collection.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using the SPSS 25.0 software (International Business Machines Corporation, Almonk,New York,USA). Quantitative data were expressed as the means and standard deviation or median (p25–p75) and qualitative data were presented as frequency and proportion. Data obtained at different time points before and after treatment were compared by the one-way repeated measures ANOVA. For quantitative data, comparisons between two groups were made using t-tests or Mann–Whitney U-test. For qualitative data, Chi-square or Fisher’s test was used. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Seventy-four patients fulfilled the inclusion criteria and were randomly assigned into two groups (37 participants in each group). Two patients in Group I and one patient in Group II dropped out during the follow-up period. Finally, 71 participants completed the 6 months follow-up and have been included in the statistical analysis [Figure 2].

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated the efficacy of dexmedetomidine combined with local anesthetic using in ultrasound-guided PVB in AHZ patients. The results showed that both interventions provided similar efficacy in minimizing the pain intensity and reducing the incidence of PHN in AHZ patients. Compared with ropivacaine only, the addition of dexmedetomidine significantly improved patients’ sleep quality and reduced the times of block.

HZ mainly affects the elderly and people with low immunity, leading to a dominant pain and sleep dysfunction. As pain can interfere with sleep, the AHZ patients often report sleep dysfunction, which can contribute to increase pain. Therefore, treatment for AHZ should focus not only on pain relief but also on sleep improvement. The HZ-associated pain is caused by viral infection and increased sensitivity of sensory neurons in affected segments.[15] The reactivated virus destroys the relevant central and peripheral nerves and causes inflammation, immune responses, and varying degrees of neuron loss within the spinal ganglion.[1617] We can observe that although the analgesic effects were comparable in both groups, dexmedetomidine was more effective in improving sleep quality. This may be related to the sedative effect of dexmedetomidine itself and its peripheral analgesic function.

The feasibility of PVB in the treatment of AHZ has been reported by many authors.[1819] PVB is a technique injecting local anesthetics into the paraspinal space containing spinal nerves and sympathetic nerves. Recent years, TPVB has developed as an effective method of providing anesthesia as well as postoperative analgesia in chest surgery and breast surgery.[2021] In the past, PVB was accomplished mainly by a classical blind method through loss of resistance or fluoroscopy guided. In our pain clinics, with the skilled use of ultrasound machines and the easy interpretability of ultrasound images, it makes ultrasound-guided PVB easier to implement. At the same time, the success rate of puncture and patient satisfaction were improved. A complication worth mentioning in PVB is pneumothorax, which may develop into a medical emergency and even endanger the patient’s life. One of our patients arose this condition, but as the pneumothorax was small (<20%) and the patient had only mild symptoms, it was resolved after conservative treatment.

Local anesthetic plus steroids are the mainly drugs using in PVB, which makes it possible to block the persistent pain signals by the direct effects of local anesthetics on spinal nerves. Moreover, the use of steroids may reduce the neuronal inflammation in the dorsal root ganglion and the distal end of the insulted nerve.[22] It is reported that dexamethasone prolonged both sensory and motor blocks in peripheral never block. This may be related to the permissive effect of glucocorticoids, thus increasing the sensitivity of peripheral nerve fibers to local anesthetics.[23] However, in China, some patients may be hesitant to use hormones, and concerning that steroids may lead to an increase in blood pressure and glucose, it brings some limitations in the use of steroids.

Dexmedetomidine, a highly selective α2-adrenorceptor agonist, has been shown to have sedative, analgesic, and sympatholytic effects. In recent years, dexmedetomidine as adjuvant to local anesthetic has been reported to improve the duration and quality of PVB.[2425] This study found that patients in dexmedetomidine group needed fewer times of block to achieve the same analgesic effect, suggesting that dexmedetomidine as an adjuvant to ropivacaine may play a positive role in prolonging the analgesic time than steroids. As we know, bradycardia is the main complication of dexmedetomidine. In our study, bradycardia was only found in patients receiving dexmedetomidine, notwithstanding this difference was not statistically significant, it should be met with caution.

Although it is difficult to demonstrate clinically, preclinical data suggested that peripheral dexmedetomidine may also protect nerves by reducing the inflammatory response to perinerve application of local anesthetics.[26] The mechanism of analgesic actions of dexmedetomidine in peripheral never block is probably multifactorial. The favorable effects of dexmedetomidine may be due to peripheral or central mechanisms of action, as absorption and systemic redistribution occur after dexmedetomidine perineurally administration.[27] However, Fritsch et al. reported that although the peripheral application of dexmedetomidine prolonged nerve block hours, the blood centration of dexmedetomidine was too low to be considered as a systemic mechanism of block prolongation.[28] Moreover, other basic science studies have demonstrated that the effects of dexmedetomidine perineural are not due to systemic analgesia or other α2-adrenergic receptors but to peripheral effects.[29] The peripheral analgesic mechanism of dexmedetomidine may be associated with a reduction in the release of norepinephrine and independent inhibition of nerve fiber action potentials through the α2-receptor.[20] According to Brummett et al., peripheral dexmedetomidine as an adjuvant to local anesthetics prolonged the duration of analgesia by block of hyperpolarization-activated cation current.[30] However, further studies should be taken to investigate the mechanisms of how dexmedetomidine action in the peripheral nerve block.

Limitations

There are some limitations in the study. First, the sample size was small, greater number of patients needs to be included in the future studies. Second, we did not set up a placebo group, so we could not get the data of patients who do not receive PVB. In addition, a Kappa test was lack for inter-relater reliability for the clinical examination and procedure execution. Finally, according to the previous studies,[2125] we chose the dose of dexmedetomidine as 1 μg/kg; however, more dose-response studies should be taken to check the effectiveness of dexmedetomidine in PVB for AHZ patients.

Conclusion

Both interventions provided similar efficacy in minimizing the pain intensity and reducing the incidence of PHN in AHZ patients. Compared with ropivacaine only, the addition of dexmedetomidine significantly improved patients’ sleep quality and reduced the times of block.

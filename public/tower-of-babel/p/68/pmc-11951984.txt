Interstitial brachytherapy for periocular nonmelanoma skin cancers: Impact on organ and function preservation
Authors: Ashwini Budrukkar, Maneesh Singh, Monali Swain, Sarbani Ghosh Laskar, Vedang Murthy, Shrikant Kale, Rituraj Upreti, Shwetabh Sinha, Anuj Kumar, Samarpita Mohanty, Tejpal Gupta, Jai Prakash Agarwal
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Interstitial brachytherapy is safe and effective for periocular skin tumors.•Upfront and adjuvant ISBT yield excellent local control & organ preservation.•ISBT achieved good–excellent cosmesis with preserved function in most patients.

Introduction

Eyelid cancers are a relatively rare entity and account for 3 % of head-neck cancers and 5–10 % of all skin cancers [1], [2]. Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) are the two histological types that occur most frequently [1]. The cosmetic and functional relevance of this anatomical region has led to this subsite being a therapeutic challenge. The objectives of therapy comprise local tumor control as well as good functional and cosmetic outcome. Traditionally, surgery has been the most commonly utilised form of treatment with external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) reserved for medically inoperable patients, those with unresectable disease and as post-operative adjuvant treatment for patients with high-risk features.

Notwithstanding the improvement in radiotherapy (RT) techniques and the increased conformality with contemporary techniques like IMRT, achieving an optimum therapeutic ratio for eyelid tumors still remains a modern-day conundrum owing to the critical anatomic location. With EBRT, delivering high doses to the target volume while sparing nearby normal tissues is fraught with challenges, often leading to unfavourable side effects. Eyelid deformity, dry eye syndrome, discolouration, skin thinning, and telangiectasia are some of the most typical long-term sequelae [2]. Interstititial brachytherapy (ISBT), a form of interventional radiotherapy (IRT), makes possible the placement of tubes inside the target (tumor, or post-operative bed), thus optimizing the dose received by the target whilst mitigating the dose-spill to surrounding critical structures owing to the characteristic sharp dose fall-off.

We present, our series of periocular BCC or SCC patients treated with HDR ISBT. This study, in a tertiary high-volume cancer centre, that aims to further consolidate the growing evidence and role of ISBT in the management of eyelid skin carcinomas.

Methods

Patients with skin cancers of upper and lower eyelid, medial and lateral canthus and the infra-orbital region treated with HDR ISBT between December 2010 and July 2022 were considered suitable for inclusion in the study. Data was collected from the prospectively maintained database of brachytherapy for all head neck subsites and supplemented from the electronic medical records.

Patients were clinically reviewed in the multi-disciplinary tumor board; those with lesions not amenable for surgery or at high risk of a potentially compromising functional and cosmetic outcome were planned for ISBT. Patients who had indications for adjuvant radiation therapy and patients who developed recurrence after surgical excision were also considered suitable. All patients were node-negative and the same was confirmed by clinical examination and ultrasound neck at the time of ISBT. Chest X-ray was used to rule out distant metastasis. The disease was staged by American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 7th and 8th edition of TNM staging.

Brachytherapy

Pre-Application Checklist

As part of the pretreatment evaluation, all patients underwent an ophthalmological assessment, pre-anesthetic fitness for general anesthesia (GA) and baseline clinical photograph (Fig. 1a). Clinical extent of the disease was also documented diagrammatically. For patients who underwent prior surgery, complete wound healing was ensured prior to the baseline photographic assessment. Patients were counselled regarding the procedure and treatment in detail. A customized protective lead shield was made over an eye conformer. Brachytherapy application

The ISBT procedure was done under general anaesthesia. The gross tumor volume (GTV) was marked with ink. GTV with 5–10 mm margin was considered as the Clinical Target Volume (CTV). Entry and exit sites of the catheter were marked taking into account the loading required and need for protecting the normal skin.

A customised protective lead shield was inserted in the eye taking all aseptic precautions. An eighteen-gauge hollow steel needle with a bevelled edge at one end was inserted through the centre of the tumour. Additional needles were passed above and below the first needle at a distance of 8–10 mm with the aim to cover the entire CTV. The needles were then replaced by the plastic tubes with closed end at one side. Plastic beads and buttons were used to secure the tubes in position opposed to the skin (Fig. 1b). Customized eye shield was removed after the insertion of the catheters.

Brachytherapy planning and treatment

Image acquisition for treatment planning was done using CT simulator and 1 mm slice thickness CT images were acquired. CT images were transferred to the Oncentra treatment planning station (TPS) (Elekta Medical Systems, Sweden). Catheters were reconstructed using multiplanar reconstruction module. The source loading was decided by the clinicians based on the CTV. Basal dose points were generated at midpoint of 2 catheters. The dose was normalised on the basal dose points. The prescription isodose was selected based on the target volume coverage. The most common schedule was 49 Gy at 3.5 Gy per fraction twice-daily 6 h apart (Fig. 1c). Treatment was delivered using high dose rate (HDR) using Iridium 192 source. shows the dose distribution of ISBT with brachytherapy application, planning and dosimetry.

The dose-fractionation schedules followed were variable and depended on individual scenarios i.e definitive brachytherapy for de-novo disease, definitive brachytherapy for recurrent disease, adjuvant brachytherapy post-surgery or brachytherapy as adjunct to EBRT. Toxicity evaluation and follow up

Patients were reviewed daily during brachytherapy, weekly for a month for assessment of acute radiation reactions, 3-monthly for 2 years, 6-monthly till 5 years and annually thereafter (Fig. 1d). Follow-up evaluations entailed detailed assessment of any complaints, clinical review pertaining to skin over primary site, ophthalmological evaluation and any other complications (Fig. 2). Clinical examination included but was not limited to ruling out any suspicious disease in the primary site and neck, recording acute- and late-toxicity as well as cosmetic outcome. Acute- and late-toxicity were graded using Radiation Therapy and Oncology group (RTOG). Cosmetic outcomes were assessed and graded using RTOG and European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) late radiation morbidity scoring schema. Evaluation for cosmesis was done at each follow-up visit and has been reported for the most recent follow-up.Fig. 2Pretreatment and post interstitial brachytherapy clinical photographs depicting clinical response and cosmesis.

Statistical analysis

Local recurrence was defined as either presence of residual disease or re-appearance of the tumor at the local site on follow up. Local control calculated from the date of registration until the date of biopsy proven recurrence. Overall survival was defined as the time in months from the date of registration to the date of death due to any cause. Disease free survival was defined as the time in months from the date of registration to the date of any recurrence or death due to any cause. Survival was calculated using Kaplan Meier method. Analysis was done using SPSS version 24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY).

Results

Sixteen patients underwent ISBT for skin tumors around the eye from December 2010 to July 2022. The patient characteristics including clinical and histopathological characteristics are presented in Table 1. Nine patients had lower eyelid tumors, 5 patients had tumors in canthi (3 in lateral canthi, while 2 in medial canthus) and two patients had skin tumor in the infra-orbital region. Upper eyelid involvement was seen in 2 patients who had a right lower eyelid and canthi tumors extending onto the upper eyelid. Basal cell carcinoma (BCC) and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) accounted for 87.5 % (14) and 12.5 % (2) of the patients, respectively. The median dose for ISBT was 49 Gy in 14 fractions in the definitive cohort while it was 35 Gy in 10 fractions in the adjuvant cohort. The median follow-up duration was 73 months (range, 33 – 112). All patients in whom ISBT was done for primary de-novo disease or adjuvant post-excision had a complete response and were locally controlled at the last follow-up. One patient was lost to follow-up and his disease status was not known. Three of the four patients who underwent ISBT for post-surgery recurrent disease recurred locally while 1 patient had a complete response. The local recurrences were subsequently salvaged successfully with surgery alone in two patients and salvage surgery with re-brachytherapy in one patient. There were no regional or distant recurrences. Kaplan Meier Survival for de-novo and recurrent cases showing local control (Fig. 3a), disease-free survival (Fig. 3b) and overall survival (Fig. 3c).

At the time of analysis, 13 patients were alive and disease-free, two patients had died and one patient was lost to follow up. Both the deaths were due to causes unrelated to disease. One patient who had a long-standing ischemic heart disease died due to myocardial ischemia and one succumbed to COVID-19 related acute respiratory illness. The 5-year DFS for patients receiving upfront brachytherapy for de-novo disease was 75 %. The 5-year OS in the patients treated for de-novo disease, recurrent disease and overall was 75 %, 100 % and 82.5 % respectively. Fig. 3b and Fig. 3c illustrate the Kaplan-Meier survival curves for DFS and OS for both de-novo treated patients and patients with recurrent disease.

The skin toxicity noted was Grade I (faint erythema) in 10 patients, Grade II (early desquamation/ pigmentation) in 4 patients. The worst grade acute toxicity of grade IV skin toxicity that led to skin blisters was seen in 1 patient. None of the other patients had grade 3 or more skin toxicity. Mucosal reactions were noted in 7 patients; 5 patients had grade I (mild epiphora) and 2 patients with grade II (moderate fibrinous reaction) and all were treated with conservative medication.

Debilitating and persisting eyelid deformity requiring a surgical lower-lid ectropion release was seen in 1(6.6 %) patient. The most frequently encountered late toxicity noted was persisting grade I hyperpigmentation and thinning of skin over the treated site. No patients had any ulceration, perforation, necrosis of soft tissue, cartilage or bone. The function of eyelid and eye was well preserved in 13 patients (81 %) with preserved organ in all of the patients. None of the patients on follow up had impairment in the quality of vision. Cosmetic outcomes were reported in all but the one patient who was lost to follow-up. At the most recent follow-up cosmesis was scored as excellent in six (40 %) patients, good in six (40 %), fair in two (14 %) and poor in one (6 %) patient. The solitary patient who had a poor cosmetic outcome had persisting late lower eyelid ectropion requiring surgical intervention described previously but had preservation of eye function.

Discussion

This study of 16 patients with skin cancers of the eyelid and infra-orbital region treated with HDR-ISBT showed that HDR- BT in well-selected patients in primary and adjuvant setting was safe and effective with excellent local control in upfront treated lesions. None of the patients who received definitive brachytherapy to de-novo primary, adjuvant brachytherapy after surgery or brachytherapy as adjunct to EBRT had a local disease recurrence. Similar local control rates of 88–96 % have been reported by other studies that have included both brachytherapy or EBRT [2], [3], [4], [5], [6]. In a large single-institution series of 60 patients by Krengli et al in 2014, local control of 96 % was reported with HDR interstitial brachytherapy for eyelid carcinomas [5]. The overall survival in our patient cohort has been found to be a function of advanced age and co-morbidities. Of the two deaths, none were disease related. This is also because of effective salvage treatment options achieving neo-control as in three of our patients.

In our experience, HDR-BT for organ and function preservation was safe and effective. ISBT achieved organ preservation in all patients with fair-excellent cosmesis in over 90 % and functional preservation in over 80 % of the patients [Fig. 2]. This compared favourably to reported literature as summarised in Table 3 [5], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13]. The cosmetic and functional implications associated with managing eyelid cancers contribute to the therapeutic challenge of achieving the dual objectives of tumor control with optimal functional preservation. Notwithstanding the traditional standard of care for eyelid cancers being surgery, tumors of this functionally and cosmetically critical location carry high risk of poor cosmetic outcomes and disfigurement after surgery. Radiotherapy has been used in several ways that include superficial Xrays, orthovoltage Xrays, EBRT megavoltage photons or electrons and brachytherapy, both low dose-rate (LDR) and HDR for eyelid tumors with high local control rates and cosmetic outcomes superior to those of surgery for eyelid tumors [2], [8]. HDR-BT is a relatively newer modality as compared to LDR-BT in treatment of eyelid tumors owing, perhaps, to the larger diameter (1.5–1.8 mm) of the catheters used in the former and anticipation of consequential tissue damage during implantation. Martinez-Monge et al in 2007 published the first account of HDR-BT in eyelid tumor in an individual case and demonstrated the safety of HDR-BT catheters for eyelid implantation [14]. There have been concerns regarding long-term complications due to the high dose per fraction in HDR-BT. However, these stand unsubstantiated by virtue of the fractionated delivery of HDR-BT and the previous studies not showing a correlation between radiation dose-fractionation and late toxicity [2], [15]. Furthermore, HDR-BT offered additional advantages over LDR-BT for radiation protection, ease of optimisation, patient comfort and was increasingly adopted as definitive and adjuvant modality for eyelid tumors.

In the present study, none of the patients had grade 3 or higher acute mucosal reaction. The worst grade of acute adverse effect noted was a solitary grade 4 skin reaction in one patient. A frequently seen late toxicity was grade 1 hyperpigmentation in about three-fourths of the patients. One patient had persisting late eyelid deformity requiring a lower lid ectropion release. Unlike a previous series wherein madarosis was encountered in 65 % of the patients, in our series five (38.4 %) of the patients who were on follow up were noted to have madarosis [8]. Doses over 40–50 Gy have been found to be related to permanent madarosis, fibrosis and ectropion in several reports [16], [17].

Cosmetic outcome in our experience was found to be comparable to the scarce literature available with fair-excellent cosmesis in over 90 % of the patients. Variable and institution-specific cosmetic outcomes have been reported in eyelid ISBT [5], [8], [9], [13]. Good to excellent functional and cosmetic outcome have ranged from 68 % − 100 % in previously published literature as also detailed in Table 3 [5], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11], [13]. Late toxicity of grade 2 or higher was observed in 3 % while optimum cosmetic outcomes in 68 % of the patients in a study by Krengli et al [5]. Our previously published series on adjuvant eyelid ISBT reported 100 % excellent-good cosmetic outcomes based on an indigenous CAIB scale using pertinent late-toxicity metrics [9].

In the present study, the mean biologically effective dose (BED) for alpha/beta of 10 was 61.7 Gy. This is comparable to the doses used in the series by Mareco et al [8]. The consideration for further intensifying treatment by increasing dose per fraction, as discussed by Hennequin et al, does have a sound biological rationale because of a small treatment volume [18]. However, the same needs to be balanced with concerns of toxicity in a disease site with good local control rates. In all the local recurrences seen, brachytherapy was a salvaging measure for post-surgery local failure. All the local recurrences, though, were salvageable; the three patients who failed locally eventually had new local control and were alive without disease at analysis. Based on our results, we believe upfront definitive and adjuvant interstitial brachytherapy has excellent local control and survival outcomes with fair-excellent cosmesis and functional preservation in a majority of patients.

Although the toxicity data has been maintained and updated prospectively, certain limitations inherent to such an analysis are acknowledged. Owing to the rarity of this disease, the cohort size was small. This is re-affirmation of the reason for lack of prospective or randomized evidence in this tumor subsite and consequently there exists a paucity of evidence-based consensus on various aspects of management of eyelid cancers. Patient reported measures could have strengthened the results and are a part of our updated workflow for future studies. This analysis offers a contemporary view of the real-world outcomes of ISBT in eyelid carcinoma in a tertiary cancer referral centre.

Conclusion

Definitive interstitial brachytherapy using Iridium-192 is safe and effective for skin tumors around the eye. Both upfront and adjuvant HDR ISBT resulted in excellent local control, low toxicity and organ preservation outcomes with excellent to good cosmesis in a majority of patients.

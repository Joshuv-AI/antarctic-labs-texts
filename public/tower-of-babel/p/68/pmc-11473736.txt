A new approach to determine occupational accident dynamics by using ordinary differential equations based on SIR model
Authors: Selcan Kaplanvural, Eren Tosyalı, İsmail Ekmekçi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe motivation of this study is to develop and establish an occupational accident dynamical model (OA model) based on Susceptible-Infected-Recovered framework. In order to investigate the dynamics of the OA model, monthly occupational accident data from Turkey between 2013 and 2020 has been selected as dataset. The OA model is defined by a coupled first-order ordinary nonlinear differential equation with four variables. In addition, the relationships between these variables are described with ten parameters. The OA model’s characterization of the equilibrium points is analyzed by investigating the behaviors of these points according to the eigenvalues derived from the Jacobian matrix. Also, the stability of these points is obtained according to the eigenvalues. These results show the behavior of the system near equilibrium points. After that, the reproduction number is computed by using the next-generation matrix method. The calculated reproduction number for given parameters reveals that the OA model is unstable. The OA model is numerically solved in 96 steps, with a time interval of 1 month, using the ODE45 Matlab routine based on the explicit Runge–Kutta algorithm. In addition, a modified OA model is developed by adding the Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) re-training parameter to the OA model to observe a reducing effect on occupational accident numbers. The main results of this study provide a new approach about the future estimation of the number of occupational accidents. Furthermore, through the comparison of numerical results from both models, the study demonstrates that national safety policies, particularly those enhancing the efficacy of OHS training, can effectively mitigate accidents.Keywords: Occupational health and safety, Occupational accident, Nonlinear dynamics, Mathematical modellingSubject terms: Statistical physics, thermodynamics and nonlinear dynamics; Sustainability

Introduction

Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) is aimed at eliminating work-related injuries, diseases, and deaths1. The importance of this field is increased with the industrial developments and the technology in the world2. The matter of OHS benefits from engineering, ergonomics, physics, biology, law, medicine, and social science disciplines. Understanding and prediction of accident causation is the harmonic implication of these disciplines3. Occupational accidents originate in working area safety and require a proactive approach to identify hazards, minimize risks, and improve preventive measures4. Statistical data on occupational accidents provide valuable insights into workplace injuries’ frequency, character, and results. These statistics often include many different kinds of industries, occupations, and regions, helping stakeholders (employees, employers, customers, visitors, and subcontractors, etc.), policymakers, and researchers gain an extensive understanding of the present state of occupational health and safety5,6. Analyzing these statistics enables the identification of hazardous areas and risk factors, facilitating targeted intervention and prevention policies aimed at reducing the occurrence of accidents7. In this context, Carnero and Pedgeral aimed to predict the future of work accidents as a trend forecast in Spain at different levels using multivariate components. Their data consists on occupational accidents that occurred between 1998 and 2009, published by the Spanish state8. In another study, Cheng et. al. investigated the characteristic factors of occupational accident data in small construction enterprises in Taiwan by using descriptive statistical methodologies and correlation coefficient analysis. The data used in their study were collected from the Taiwan Central Government Labor Affairs Council between 2000 and 20079. Shafique and Rafiq presented the current occupational injury trend among Hong Kong construction workers. In their study, a statistical analysis of incidence and mortality rates was performed to evaluate each type of accident by evaluating the annual reports of the Hong Kong labor department between 2008 and 201810. In a different study, Zhu et al. analyzed occupational accident data to evaluate the Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) policies performance within the coal mine industries. The study utilized the coal mine accidents that occurred between 2008 and 2021, as reported by the Ministry of Emergency Management of the People’s Republic of China and the Nation Mine Safety Administration11. Kandemir and ¸Sim¸sek aimed at the costs and psycho-social effects of occupational accidents with a statistical study that relied on the data published by the Social Security Institution of Turkey. Unlike previous studies, the relationship between the gender of the accident victims, the working environment, the duration of incapacity for work, and the type of work accident was evaluated by using the log-linear analysis method and two different models based on their research. The amount of accidental cases is associated with the development level of countries12. Macedo and Silva examined officially recorded accidents and labor-related working conditions in workplaces between 1992 and 2001 based on their research. They stated that occupational accidents have a great impact on human life and also bring high costs for a country’s social security system13. Occupational accidents are a significant problem that may disrupt human body integrity. It may result not only in death but also in serious injury and loss of limb. It is clear that occupational accidents have negatively affected the mental and physical health of society.

Complex systems, spanning disciplines such as physics, biology, ecology, physiology, sociology, and economy, often rely on nonlinear dynamic theory. These fields leverage numerical simulations to unravel intricate patterns, predict emergent phenomena, and understand the underlying mechanisms within these complex systems. Population dynamics is one of the most common areas of study for researchers due to its rich nonlinear interactions14–17. The first study of population dynamics, called the Malthusian growth model, was formulated by Malthus18. Then, the logistic equation model was introduced in 1845 to model the evolution of any population over time19. In this context, Lotka–Voltera equations, which are leaned on the logistic equation, are also used to describe ecological prey and predator models20. It has been shown that these equations are continuous in time evolution and show chaotic behavior when the degrees of freedom are three or more21,22. The spread of infectious diseases, like population dynamics can be described by nonlinear dynamical methods. This process may be carried out mathematically with the SIR, SEIR, SIRD, and SIVR (Susceptible-Exposed-Infected-Recovery-Death-Vaccinated) models depending on the investigated variables23. There are many studies conducted for this purpose. For instance, a SEIR model was developed for tuberculosis transmission (TB) with the time-dependent parameters24.In another study, a predictor-corrector type nonstandard finite difference scheme on the SEIR model was implemented for measles transmission disease25,26. Modeling of COVID-19 infection was applied to different types of models such as SIR, SIRD and SIVR by considering many effects of crowding, transportation, and vaccination27–29. The HIV/AIDS transmission dynamic was explored based on the SIR model by using Runge–Kutta and Galerkin–Petrov time discretization of a high-order scheme30,31. In addition, the cause of ulcer and environment for the spread of Buruli disease was investigated by using a modified SIR model32. Although there are some studies about modeling and prediction of occupational accidents, as mentioned, a study on the non-linear dynamic modeling approach to occupational accident data has not yet been addressed in the literature. In this study, a new model has been proposed to describe the dynamics of occupational accidents. This model relies on population dynamic theory and the SIR model. The model has been solved numerically by the Runge–Kutta algorithm. The parameters’ values have been found by fitting the real data. The error between the real and simulated data has been calculated by using normalized mean square error (NMSE). The stability analysis has been done, and finally, the effect of a new parameter, which refers to OHS re-training, has been investigated. This paper contains three main sections: First, the description of the Occupational Accident model (OA model) is presented under the Methods title. Following that, the stability analysis of the OA model is explained. Subsequently, the numerical results of OA and the modified OA model are examined. Finally, the contribution of the study is mentioned in the Conclusion section.

Methods

Description of OA model

In this section, a mathematical model based on the SIR model is implemented to illustrate occupational accidents. Therefore, the population is categorized into four variables: Susceptible (S), referring to the number of insured persons without accidents; Infected (I), representing the number of insured persons who have had accidents reported within 3 days (Minor accidents) and those with reports exceeding 3 days (Major accidents); and finally, Recovery or Death (R or D), representing the number of insured persons who have had fatal accidents. A diagram is shown in Fig. 1 to visualize the relationship between variables in the time-dependent OA model. In the diagram, diamond, circle, square, and triangle shapes represent a variable which are used in the model. The parameters defining the relations between the variables are illustrated by arrows. λ indicates the recruitment to the population. X population turns into A and B population. A transforms into B. Finally, B transform into Z. The arrowhead toward the variables is meant positive contribution. Similarly, the opposite side of the arrowhead indicates a negative contribution.

In Turkey, the data regarding the number of people having accidents and insured are declared by the Social Security Institution (SSI) every year monthly35. In this study, the announced data of occupational accidents and insured numbers by SSI between 2013 and 2020 are selected. The OHS law in Turkey entered into force in 2013. Since employers started to use online systems to report work accidents,the occupational accident data has been reliable since 2013. Therefore, the year 2013 is chosen as the beginning and the year 2020 is chosen as the ending period of the data (last announced by SSI).

The average number of compulsory insured N is selected as the universe of the system. X data is calculated by subtracting the number of accidents from N cumulatively. The universe of the system is defined N(t)=X(t)+A(t)+B(t)+Z(t). In addition, A, B, and Z data are calculated cumulatively and scaled 1/1000000. According to Table 3, the eigenvalues for the first equilibrium point λ1, λ2, and λ3 are real. Though, λ1, λ2 are positive, λ3 is negative. In this context, the characterization of the first eigenvalue is the saddle point. The eigenvalues for the second equilibrium point λ1, λ2, and λ3 are negative and real. Therefore, the characterization of the second eigenvalue is the node. The eigenvalues for the last equilibrium point, λ1 is real, λ2 and λ3 are imaginary. λ1 is positive, λ2’s real part is negative and the imaginary part is positive, and λ3’s parts are negative. Thus, the last eigenvalue characterization is the spiral saddle point. The stability of three equilibrium points are stable–unstable manifolds for the first one, stable nodes for the second one, and stable spiral saddle and unstable manifold for the third one.

The effectiveness of occupational health and safety training in reducing the number of occupational accidents was taken into consideration during the selection of this parameter. In addition, taking OHS re-training for every person who had an occupational accident is mandatory by regulations in Turkey. Conclusions

The primary objective of this research is to evaluate the number of occupational accidents and the preventive measures’ impact on the dynamics of occupational safety, as viewed through the perspective of the first ordinary differential equation.Therefore, monthly SSI data between 2013 to 2021 are used to analyze the dynamics of the occupational accident model. The OA model’s dynamics have been demonstrated with four variables and each variable’s relations are described with eight parameters. While the parameters β, r1, r2, and ω have been found through fitting to the real SSI data, the others have been calculated by using SSI and announced TUIK data. The OA model has been solved numerically by using the Runge–Kutta method. Numerical results for the OA model shown in Fig. 3 are compatible with real data. The error between the OA model and the real data has been derived by using NMSE. The result for population X, A, B, and Z are 3.9×10-6, 1.7×10-3, 2.8×10-3 and 2.6×10-3, respectively. Also, the OA model has been modified by adding u parameter regarding to OHS re-training. Within the modified OA model, minor, major, and fatal accident rates exhibit a decrease compared to the numerical results of the initial OA model, highlighting the importance of providing OHS re-training to individuals who have experienced occupational accidents. At the end of the 108th month, decrease in population A, B and Z are 24%, 33% and 40%, respectively. Despite requiring all employees who have experienced occupational accidents to undergo OHS re-training before resuming work, the process may be delayed or incomplete due to various challenges, including time constraints, lack of expertise, and financial limitations. In this study, based on the numerical results we have obtained, recommendations are provided to the government to increase the frequency of workplace inspections related to OHS training for employees and to establish regulations concerning penal sanctions. The OA model can also be applied to predict the number of occupational accidents and implement preventive measures using data from other countries. Moreover, it can be adapted for high-risk sectors such as industry, construction, and mining. New prevention parameters could be developed in these areas to enhance safety measures. In addition, our future plans include applying the recommended regression analysis and deep learning methodologies to analyze the dataset.

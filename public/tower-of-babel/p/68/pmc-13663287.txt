Mitochondria-localized protein-encoding genes and programmed cell death-related genes reveal potential molecular perturbations underlying spontaneous preterm birth
Authors: Luguang Zuo, Qingsong Huai, Mingming Zhang, Xin Liu, Xiaoling Shang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMultiple programmed cell death (PCD) modalities, including apoptosis, autophagy, and ferroptosis, are closely implicated in spontaneous preterm birth (SPTB). Mitochondria serve as central regulators of various PCD pathways, playing a critical role in cellular stress responses and homeostasis. However, comprehensive studies integrating mitochondria-localized protein-encoding genes and PCD-related genes to explore the molecular mechanisms underlying SPTB remain limited.MethodsTwo gene expression datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) were analyzed. Mitochondria-localized protein-encoding genes from MitoCarta3.0 and curated PCD-related gene sets were combined to define a mitochondrial PCD-related gene set (mtPCD-RGs). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between SPTB and control samples were identified, and their intersection with mtPCD-RGs was obtained. A protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed, followed by least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression, random forest (RF) modeling to identify hub genes. External validation of gene expression was performed. Subsequent analyses included gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), immune infiltration analysis, and the construction of competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network.ResultsA total of 63 intersecting genes were identified, and 46 genes were retained after PPI network filtering. BST2 and TRIM22 were eventually identified as key hub genes for SPTB, and their incorporation into a nomogram enabled individualized risk prediction for clinical application. Immunological analysis revealed significant differences in naïve CD4 + T cells, resting NK cells, monocytes, and eosinophils in SPTB peripheral blood samples compared to the control group. BST2 and TRIM22 showed positive correlations with monocyte abundance and negative correlations with naïve CD4 + T cells and resting NK cells.ConclusionBST2 and TRIM22 are identified as pivotal genes in SPTB, potentially reflecting different outcomes of pregnancy, respectively. These genes may serve as potential biomarkers and provide mechanistic insights into SPTB, highlighting promising targets for future therapeutic interventions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12884-026-09686-x.Keywords: Spontaneous preterm birth, Mitochondria-localized protein-encoding genes, Programmed cell death, BST2, TRIM22, Machine learning

Introduction

Spontaneous preterm birth (SPTB), defined as delivery before 37 weeks of gestation, remains a major global public health concern and accounts for approximately 60–80% of all preterm births [1–2]. SPTB not only poses a huge economic burden on families and healthcare systems, but also increases the complexity and cost of neonatal care [3–4]. Women with a history of SPTB face a significantly elevated recurrence risk, with a 2.5- to 4-fold increase in subsequent pregnancies [5–7]. Current understanding attributes SPTB to multiple interrelated mechanisms, including intrauterine infection, decidual hemorrhage, disruption of maternal-fetal immune tolerance, excessive uterine distension, maternal-fetal stress, and genetic predisposition.

The identification of reliable biological markers is critical for targeted prevention and early intervention. Emerging evidence highlights the involvement of various programmed cell death (PCD) modalities-such as ferroptosis, apoptosis, and autophagy-in the pathophysiology of SPTB, suggesting that dysregulation of PCD contributes to adverse pregnancy outcomes [8–9]. Mitochondria play a central role in eukaryotic cell-death decisions, and a key function of several PCD modalities is to contribute to mitochondrial quality control by eliminating damaged or dysfunctional mitochondria [10].

However, these pathways do not operate in isolation; rather, they converge at critical cellular nodes. Increasingly, mitochondrial function and PCD homeostasis are recognized as integrative mechanisms in pregnancy maintenance. Mitochondrial dysfunction can exacerbate infection-induced inflammation, compromise maternal-fetal immune tolerance, and impair metabolic adaptations necessary for sustaining pregnancy. Moreover, mitochondrial dysfunction and mitochondria-regulated programmed cell death have been widely implicated in a variety of human pathologies [11–12]. Therefore, regulation of mitochondrial quality through PCD pathways may represent a unifying mechanism linking diverse etiological factors of SPTB.

Despite accumulating evidence, most prior studies have focused on single PCD types, and integrated analyses combining mitochondria-localized genes with comprehensive PCD gene sets are scarce. The molecular link between mitochondrial PCD-related genes (mtPCD-RGs) and SPTB remains poorly characterized. We hypothesize that mtPCD-RGs do not act through isolated linear pathways but engage in bidirectional interactions with infection, inflammation, and energy metabolism, thereby increasing susceptibility to SPTB.

To validate our hypothesis, we conducted an integrative bioinformatics analysis to systematically identify mtPCD-RGs associated with SPTB, aiming to elucidate the molecular landscape of SPTB and support future efforts in early risk stratification and targeted interventions.

Methods

Research workflow

Using the keyword “Preterm Birth,” we queried the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) to retrieve RNA-Seq or microarray datasets that contained whole blood samples obtained from women with SPTB and matched term-delivered controls. We screened genes that were differentially expressed and overlapped with mitochondrial programmed cell death-related genes (mtPCD-RGs), and performed Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway analyses. To reduce high-dimensional noise genes and improve the stability and predictive performance of machine learning models, we retained mRNAs encoding proteins with documented network interactions. This subset was used as feature pool for least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and random forest (RF) modeling. Candidate genes identified by both methods were further validated in an external dataset to determine hub genes. Subsequently, nomograms were constructed to evaluate the capacity of these hub genes to predict spontaneous preterm delivery risk, and conducted immune-infiltration profiling, GSEA, ceRNA network construction (Fig. 1).

Research workflow of this study

Data extraction

The GSE96083 and GSE142974 datasets were retrieved from the GEO. GSE96083 included 38 samples (15 from women with spontaneous preterm birth [SPTB] and 23 from term-delivered controls) based on the GPL16791 platform. The original study reported that controls were selected to match SPTB cases by gestational age at sampling [13]. GSE142974 included 24 samples (8 SPTB and 16 controls) profiled using the GPL6244 platform. Controls were matched to SPTB cases by maternal age (± 5 years), race, and study site, as reported in the original study [14]. For GSE96083 (RNA-seq), raw counts were processed using DESeq2 (v1.42.0). Low-abundance genes were filtered (counts ≥ 10 in ≥ 50% samples), and data were normalized using variance stabilizing transformation (vst). For GSE142974 (microarray), RMA-normalized matrices were used. Probe IDs were mapped to gene symbols, and multiple probes per gene were averaged. For cross-cohort validation, datasets were integrated and batch effects were corrected using ComBat from the sva package. PCA before and after correction (Supplementary Fig. 1) confirmed removal of batch clustering and preservation of biological grouping (SPTB vs. Control).

The mtPCD-RG list was defined as the union (rather than the intersection, to capture functional crosstalk beyond physical co-localization) of 1,136 mitochondria-localized protein-encoding genes from MitoCarta3.0 and 1,548 PCD-related genes curated from literature [15]. After removing duplicates, a total of 2,298 unique genes were included in the mtPCD-RG set.

Identification and functional enrichment analysis of common genes

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in GSE96083 were identified using DESeq2 with thresholds of nominal P < 0.05 and |log₂FoldChange| >1 [16]. Furthermore, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were conducted on the overlapping genes between DEGs and mtPCD-RGs using the “clusterProfiler” package (p.adjust < 0.05, count > 2) [17]. A protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was then constructed using the STRING database (https://string-db.org/) with a minimum interaction confidence score of 0.40. To minimize high-dimensional noise and ensure functional relevance for downstream modeling, only genes with at least one documented interaction (non-isolated nodes) were retained, effectively filtering out “orphan” genes that lacked sufficient biological connectivity. These genes exhibiting verified protein-level interactions were defined as characteristic genes for downstream analysis.

Machine learning-based gene selection

To identify diagnostic markers, LASSO regression and Random Forest (RF) were applied. LASSO was performed using the glmnet package with elastic net penalty (alpha = 0.5) and binomial family, with 10-fold cross-validation (nfolds = 10) to select lambda based on minimum classification error (type.measure = “class”). Variables were standardized, with a convergence threshold of 1 × 10− 7 and a maximum of 100,000 iterations; genes with non-zero coefficients at lambda.min were retained [18]. RF-based recursive feature elimination (RFE) was implemented using the caret package with 5-fold cross-validation to evaluate subsets (size 1–46, step = 3) based on Accuracy. “Highest accuracy” was defined as the maximum mean accuracy across 5-fold cross-validation (CV), while Mean Decrease Gini was used to assess variable importance. The RF model used 500 trees (ntree = 500), mtry = , and a minimum node size of 1, with random seed set to 7 [19]. Final candidate genes were defined as the intersection of both methods.

Identification and validation of the hub genes

To identify robust hub genes, the expression levels of candidate genes were compared between SPTB and control groups in both GSE96083 and GSE142974 datasets using the Mann-Whitney U test (P < 0.05). The genes that showed a significant difference between SPTB and control (P < 0.05), and the expression trend was consistent in the two datasets, were considered as hub genes. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was further conducted to evaluate the diagnostic performance of each hub gene.

Nomogram construction and evaluation

Based on the hub genes, the univariate and multivariate logistic regression models (LRM) were separately built using “rms” [20]. To address potential quasi-separation, Firth penalized logistic regression was implemented via the “logistf” package for robust parameter estimation. A nomogram was developed from the multivariate LRM to predict individualized risk of SPTB. Its predictive accuracy was assessed through calibration curves, ROC analysis, and decision curve analysis (DCA), pand 1000-replicate bootstrapping to calculate the optimism-corrected C-index. Model calibration was assessed via calibration curves, with the intercept and slope calculated using the “val.prob” function in both training and external cohorts to evaluate clinical utility.

Correlation-based gene set enrichment analysis

Using the background gene set from “org.Hs.eg.db” [21], the correlation between hub genes and other genes was separately calculated and ranked. GSEA was subsequently performed using the “clusterProfiler” package (adjusted P < 0.05, q-value < 0.2) to identify biological pathways enriched at the extremes of the ranked list [17].

Immune microenvironment analysis

Immune cell composition in GSE96083 samples was estimated using the CIBERSORT algorithm, which quantifies the relative proportions of 22 distinct immune cell types. To control for the risk of false positives, the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) method was applied to adjust P-values, and cells with adj.P < 0.05 were considered significantly differentially infiltrated. Samples with zero abundance across all cell types were excluded. The differential immune cells between SPTB and control groups were identified by Wilcox rank-sum test (P < 0.05). Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was performed to investigate the correlation between hub genes and differential immune cells (P < 0.05). To test whether hub gene-SPTB associations were independent of immune shifts, multivariable logistic regression included significant immune cell proportions (e.g., monocytes and resting NK cells) as covariates with BST2 and TRIM22.

Methodology for ceRNA network construction

The DE lncRNAs was identified using “DESeq2” (|log2FoldChange|>0.5, P < 0.05). Potential microRNAs (miRNAs) targeting the hub genes, as well as lncRNAs interacting with those miRNAs, were predicted using the starBase database (https://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/). The key lncRNAs were screened by overlapping the predicted lncRNAs and DE lncRNAs. Finally, a ceRNA was constructed based on the de-duplicated miRNAs, key lncRNA, and hub genes.

Validation in clinical specimens

To validate hub gene expression, whole blood samples were collected from women with SPTB (n = 10) and term-delivered controls (n = 10), matched for maternal age and parity. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Hebei North University (k2024019). According to the manufacturer’s instructions, total RNA from tissues was isolated using total RNA extraction kit (RNAsimple Total RNA Kit; Tiangen Biotech; China) and quantified. mRNAs were reverse transcribed by using FastKing RT Kit (with gDNase) (TIANGEN Biotech; China) and SuperReal PreMix Plus (SYBR Green) (Tiangen Biotech; China) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol. GAPDH was used as the internal control. The reactions were performed independently in triplicate. All the primer sequences are listed in Additional file (Table S1). Relative expression was calculated using the method. Differences between groups were assessed using the Mann-Whitney U test, with effect sizes and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) reported to evaluate the magnitude of differential expression.

Results

Forty-six genes were screened by PPI network filtering

A total of 432 DEGs were screened, of which 258 up-regulated and 174 down-regulated (Fig. 2A, B). Overlapping these DEGs with mtPCD-RGs yielded 63 common genes (Fig. 2C). Additionally, GO analysis revealed that common genes were mainly enriched in biological processes such as regulation of apoptotic signaling pathway, intrinsic apoptotic signaling pathway, and chronic inflammatory response (Fig. 2D). These processes have been widely implicated in placental dysfunction and inflammatory activation during pregnancy. KEGG pathway enrichment identified significant associations with the C-type lectin receptor signaling pathway, pertussis, and IL-17 signaling pathway (Fig. 2E). IL-17 signaling is known to play an important role in inflammatory responses associated with infection-induced preterm birth. Notably, the protein-protein interaction (PPI) network of the 46 intersecting genes contained 138 interactions (Fig. 2F), including prominent node pairs such as CXCL8 and IL1B, TNF and JUN, as well as IL1B and JUN, suggesting coordinated regulatory roles in immune and inflammatory responses.

TRIM22 and BST2 were identified as hub genes

From the 46 intersection genes, 18 genes were selected by LASSO analysis (lambda. min = 0.2207) (Fig. 3A, B), and 25 genes that brought the model to the highest accuracy were identified by RF analysis (Fig. 3C, D). 15 candidate genes were screened by overlapping the genes from LASSO analysis and the genes from RF (Fig. 3E). The identified genes span several functional modules: immune/inflammation (BST2/TRIM22, S100A8/A9, IL10, NLRP3), signaling (RELA, STXBP2), remodeling/adhesion (MMP1, CD44/CSPG4, LGALS1), and stress/metabolism (HTRA1, VNN1, SLC25A19). Spearman correlation analysis of these candidates revealed that most gene pairs were positively correlated, with the majority reaching statistical significance (Fig. 3F), indicating co-expression patterns potentially linked to shared regulatory mechanisms in SPTB. Expression validation across both GSE96083 and GSE142974 datasets demonstrated that BST2 and TRIM22 were significantly upregulated in SPTB samples compared to controls (Fig. 4A, B). Therefore, BST2 and TRIM22 were designated as hub genes. Nomogram for Individualized Risk Prediction of SPTB

Univariate and multivariate logistic regression models (LRMs) were constructed using the two hub genes (Table 1). To address the quasi-separation observed for BST2, gene expression values were standardized using Z-score transformation prior to regression analysis. Multivariate logistic regression confirmed that BST2 (OR = 3.5, 95% CI: 1.4–8.9, p = 0.0082) and TRIM22 (OR = 3.0, 95% CI: 1.0–8.6, p = 0.0460) were independent risk factors for SPTB, supporting their inclusion in a predictive nomogram, then a nomogram was used to predict the Odd Ratio (OR) of the SPTB patients (Fig. 5A). In this model, higher total points correspond to increased probability of SPTB. The optimism-corrected C-index via 1000-replicate bootstrapping was 0.82. The calibration curve was optimal in GSE96083 (intercept = 0, slope = 1), likely reflecting cohort consistency and adequate model fit, whereas GSE142974 showed deviation from ideal calibration (intercept = 7.153, slope = 0.419), possibly due to small sample size and cohort heterogeneity. The area under the ROC curve reached 0.841 in GSE96083 and 0.734 in GSE142974-both exceeding the 0.7 threshold for acceptable discrimination (Fig. 5B). Decision curve analysis (DCA) further demonstrated that the nomogram provided greater net clinical benefit than either gene alone or treat-all strategies (Fig. 5C, D), confirming its reliability and potential clinical utility.

Construction of the BST2/TRIM22-centered ceRNA regulatory network

A total of 74 DE lncRNAs (43 up-regulated and 31 down-regulated) were identified (Fig. 8A, B). Then, by starBase database, 16 miRNAs targeted BST2, 105 miRNAs targeted TRIM22, 2620 lncRNAs targeted miRNAs were predicted, respectively. By overlapping the predicted lncRNAs with the differentially expressed ones, 15 key lncRNAs were identified (Fig. 8C). Integration of these elements yielded 59 miRNAs involved in the regulatory network. Ultimately, a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA) network comprising 76 nodes (2 hub genes, 59 miRNAs, and 15 lncRNAs) and 146 edges was constructed (Fig. 8D). Representative regulatory axes include BST2-‘hsa-mir-1251-5p’-SNHG15, TRIM22-‘hsa-mir-520d-5p’-PGR-AS1, highlighting potential post-transcriptional regulatory circuits in SPTB.

Discussion

SPTB remains a leading cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality, yet the molecular mechanisms underlying maternal systemic responses are not fully elucidated. In this study, we constructed a comprehensive machine learning framework that combines mitochondria-localized protein-encoding genes and PCD-related genes to explore the molecular characteristics in whole blood of SPTB. By intersecting this composite gene set with differentially expressed genes and applying machine learning-based feature selection methods, BST2 and TRIM22 were ultimately highlighted as candidate diagnostic genes and therapeutic targets. To mitigate potential false positives from unadjusted P-values, we employed a stringent pipeline intersecting two machine learning models followed by independent cohort validation. The high reproducibility of our key markers confirms the biological robustness of this integrated approach. Similar integrative frameworks combining machine learning and PCD-related gene signatures have been successfully applied in other diseases to identify diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers [22]. To our knowledge, this is the first study to systematically integrate mitochondrial programmed cell death-related genes (mtPCD-RGs) in the context of SPTB, offering a complementary systems-level perspective for elucidating its molecular mechanisms.

It should be noted that the mitochondrial localization protein-encoding genes used in this study includes proteins encoded by both nuclear and mitochondrial genomes [23]. Although only a small fraction of mitochondrial proteins are translated from mitochondrial DNA within the matrix and directly localized to mitochondrial membranes or compartments, the vast majority are encoded by nuclear genes [24–25], synthesized in the cytoplasm, and subsequently imported into mitochondria via specific targeting signals and transport machinery, where they are precisely localized to distinct subcompartments. Therefore, these genes and their protein products likely play roles far beyond classical energy metabolism; changes in their expression may influence cell fate regulation, stress response, and programmed cell death-related signaling pathways at a higher level [26]. Given the well-established interdependence between certain forms of programmed cell death and mitochondrial structural and functional integrity, integrating mitochondrial-localized proteins with programmed cell death-related genes provides a more comprehensive framework for understanding cell fate regulatory networks beyond traditional metabolic paradigms [27].

Over-representation analysis revealed that these overlapping genes are significantly enriched in apoptosis, inflammation, indicating a state of systemic stress characterized by heightened inflammatory signaling at the maternal-placental interface [28]. Further GSEA analysis showed that upregulation of BST2 showed a statistical correlation with pathways associated with mitochondrial stress and PCD-related modules [29]. The functional indications of TRIM22 further suggest that its elevation is positively correlated with autophagy and immune stress-related processes. Autophagy, which is often activated under inflammatory and metabolic stress, plays an important role in maintaining cellular homeostasis by removing dysfunctional mitochondria and damaged proteins [30–31]. However, when stress exceeds the regulatory capacity of the autophagy system, mitochondrial dysfunction and programmed cell death pathways may be triggered. Therefore, BST2 and TRIM22 together reveal not a single event, but two modules of different pregnancy outcomes under a shared upstream pathological context. Our analyses were conducted using whole blood transcriptomic data, prior evidence indicates that the placenta, particularly trophoblast cells, can release exosomes, cell-free RNA (cfRNA), and other bioactive factors into maternal circulation [32–33]. Previous transcriptomic analyses of placental tissue have demonstrated that pregnancy complications are accompanied by distinct alterations in placental gene expression, supporting the idea that placental-derived molecular signals may propagate into maternal circulation and serve as potential biomarkers [34–35].Therefore, this discovery provide a conceptual framework for how potential placental dysfunction propagates might be reflected in the maternal circulation and contributes to preterm birth: when upstream immune and metabolic challenges cannot be effectively counterbalanced by autophagic responses, cell fate shifts toward apoptosis, potentially leading to structural and functional impairment of the placenta and ultimately triggering SPTB [36–37]. Therefore, the observed TRIM22 and BST2 dysregulation may partially mirror underlying placental pathophysiological changes, offering a non-invasive window into placental health. This association supports further validation in placental tissue while highlighting the translational potential of maternal blood transcriptomic signatures as early biomarkers for identifying pregnancies at high risk of SPTB. Nomogram analysis demonstrated that both BST2 and TRIM22 have strong predictive performance and could serve as promising biomarkers for clinical risk assessment of SPTB. Subsequently, the discrepancy in calibration between datasets likely reflects differences in sample size, cohort characteristics, and potential batch effects. The small sample size of GSE142974 may yield unstable estimates, as calibration metrics are sensitive to sample size and outcome prevalence. Therefore, this deviation highlights the need for validation in larger, well-calibrated cohorts. Based on their expression levels in whole blood, clinicians may be able to identify high-risk individuals earlier, providing a reliable foundation for personalized intervention and management strategies. Notably, the wide confidence interval for the BST2 odds ratio (95% CI: 5.2–67,000) in the multivariate analysis likely stems from a limited sample size and high expression variability. This potential instability in the regression estimates necessitates a cautious interpretation and further validation in larger, independent cohorts.

SPTB represents a heterogeneous clinical outcome encompassing subtypes such as spontaneous preterm labor (sPTL) and preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM), which involve distinct biological mechanisms. However, as our analysis was performed at the overall SPTB level, subtype-specific differences could not be resolved. Notably, BST2/TRIM22, as interferon-stimulated genes, may be involved in myometrial activation via monocyte-M1 polarization in sPTL [38], and in fetal membrane weakening through inflammatory and matrix remodeling pathways in PPROM [39]. Future studies in well-phenotyped cohorts are needed to clarify whether they act as subtype-specific markers or universal drivers of inflammation‑mediated preterm birth.

Immune infiltration analysis revealed a positive correlation between BST2/TRIM22 expression and monocyte abundance, suggesting that these genes and monocyte activation may participate in the same inflammatory milieu [40–41]. The positive correlations between hub genes (BST2/TRIM22) and monocytes suggest an M1-type polarization shift. These pro-inflammatory cells secrete labor-inducing cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6) that trigger cervical ripening and contractions [42]. As interferon-stimulated genes, BST2/TRIM22 likely drive this inflammatory activation, linking mitochondrial stress to systemic responses in SPTB [43]. Monocytes, as core mediators of innate immunity, can enhance the NF-κB signaling pathway and promote the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines [44]. Given that both BST2 and TRIM22 are PCD interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs), their elevated expression in SPTB is more likely a consequence of innate immune hyperactivation rather than an initiating factor, serving as a compensatory mechanism to remove damaged or hyperactivated cells and restore tissue homeostasis rather than initiating inflammation. Naïve CD4 + T cells exhibited a negative correlation with BST2 and TRIM22. This pattern suggests that innate immune components are more readily activated in response to inflammatory mediators and Damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), whereas the activation of Naïve CD4 + T cells requires antigen presentation and co-stimulatory signaling, which may not be sufficiently initiated during the early inflammatory state of SPTB. This pattern of innate immunity dominance and relative suppression of adaptive immunity is aligned with known features of immunoregulation during pregnancy, reflecting that SPTB may be mediated by an innate immune-driven inflammatory pathway [45].

Finally, a systematic ceRNA regulatory network was constructed to uncover potential key molecular axes at the post-transcriptional regulatory level, providing new ideas and a theoretical basis for in vitro modeling of SPTB mechanisms [46–47]. Similar integrative bioinformatics strategies have also been used to investigate molecular crosstalk across complex diseases and identify potential diagnostic or therapeutic targets [46]. This network will help guide future functional validation of key lncRNAs and their target genes BST2 and TRIM22, thereby facilitating deeper understanding of the disease pathogenesis [48]. The constructed ceRNA network further elucidated the upstream non-coding RNA regulation of BST2 and TRIM22. Notably, TRIM22 was predicted to be targeted by numerous microRNAs (miRNAs), suggesting its role as a regulatory hub, while 15 lncRNAs-identified by intersecting predicted regulators with differentially expressed lncRNAs-represent the most biologically plausible candidates. These findings imply that dysregulated lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA interactions may lead to abnormal activation or impaired control of BST2/TRIM22, resulting in inefficient removal of proteins or damaged organelles. Such defects in cellular homeostasis may ultimately increase susceptibility to SPTB. Collectively, this work establishes a mechanistic framework and identifies a set of prioritized lncRNA-mediated regulatory axes for future placental experimental investigation.

However, this study has several limitations. First, relying on retrospective GEO datasets introduces potential bias from heterogeneity, while the limited sample size may affect generalizability. Crucially, due to the lack of granular clinical metadata in public repositories, we were unable to adjust for potential confounders such as maternal age, BMI, and infection status. Second, although RT-qPCR validated transcriptomic findings for BST2 and TRIM22, the absence of protein-level and functional experiments necessitates further mechanistic studies. And the small sample size without a priori power design further limits generalizability and may inflate effect estimates. Third, maternal blood transcriptomics may not fully reflect tissue-specific events at the maternal-fetal interface; future research should incorporate placental or single-cell data. Finally, bioinformatic inferences-including CIBERSORT and ceRNA networks-remain predictive and require experimental confirmation. Future studies should prioritize IF co-localization of BST2/TRIM22 with mitochondrial markers in trophoblasts, alongside IHC validation in placental tissues. Furthermore, gain-/loss-of-function experiments in macrophages or trophoblasts and the development of animal models are essential to clarify molecular mechanisms and establish causality in SPTB. Despite these constraints, our findings present an integrated conceptual model connecting PCD with immune/inflammatory dysregulation and energy metabolism disorder in SPTB, delivering both mechanistic insight and potential translational relevance.

Conclusion

This study reveals a potential mechanistic chain underlying SPTB, placental inflammation and immune activation induce metabolic stress, which engages autophagy to maintain cellular homeostasis; when stress exceeds autophagic capacity, cells become vulnerable to apoptosis. BST2 and TRIM22 are identified as hub genes within this chain, representing potential biomarkers associated with different pregnancy outcomes. But, further studies are warranted to elucidate their molecular mechanisms and assess their potential as targets for new SPTB interventions.

The diagnostic efficacy of unenhanced CT for detecting acute central pulmonary embolism
Authors: Ze-Xiang Deng, Heng Wang, Zhen Zhao, Hao-Tian Zhang, Ai-E Liu, Lei Chen, Yun Peng, Liang-Geng Gong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAcute central pulmonary embolism (ACPE) is associated with poor prognosis and higher mortality rates, emphasizing the importance of early detection to improve patient outcomes. The symptoms of ACPE present with non-specificity, and unenhanced chest CT may be employed prior to CTPA for the diagnosis of pulmonary diseases such as pneumonia. In such scenarios, early identification of ACPE is of paramount importance.ObjectivesThis study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic utility of unenhanced CT in ACPE involving thrombosis locted in the main pulmonary artery or the right or left pulmonary artery, as well as to develop a convenient and rapid tool for assessing the risk of ACPE on unenhanced CT.Methods and materialsThis retrospective study developed a diagnostic tool based on variables identified from unenhanced CT images acquired within 12 h prior to computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA) in 122 patients. A stepwise logistic regression approach was employed to construct the diagnostic modeland utilized to designed a nomogram suitable for clinical application. Subsequently, this Nomogram was validated in another cohort of 24 patients.ResultsUnenhanced CT model comprising abnormal density signs, main pulmonary artery and aorta ratio>0.83 and Azygos vein were predictive indicators for ACPE in both the derivation (AUC 0.877; 95% CI 0.805 to 0.930) and validation cohorts (AUC 0.844; 95% CI 0.638 to 0.958) and superior to D-dimer (AUC 0.799; 95% CI 0.717 to 0.866). ConclusionUnenhanced CT is valuable for diagnosing ACPE, especially in patients with non-specificity symptoms.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-025-05207-4.

Introduction

Acute central pulmonary embolism (ACPE) is linked to poor survival outcomes and unfavorable prognoses [1]. Therefore, prompt detection of ACPE and timely implementation of intervention measures are evidently advantageous for improving patient prognosis [1].

Computed tomographic pulmonary angiography (CTPA) is a widely employed diagnostic tool for acute pulmonary embolism (APE) [2–5]. However, a proportion of PE patients do not immediately undergo CTPA. As a consequence, a total of 97% of patients with proximal pulmonary embolism (PE) presented with dyspnea, hemoptysis, or chest pain [6]. These symptoms are nonspecific and may also manifest in patients with pneumonia, pneumothorax, or malignancies. In the primary care setting, general practitioners tend to attribute patients who present with similar symptomsto other cardiopulmonary diseases rather than PE, resulting in an average diagnostic delay of approximately 3.9 days [7]. Unenhanced CT may be used as an initial approach to exclude cardiopulmonary or mediastinal diseases, in contrast to CTPA. In such cases, identifying high-risk PE, such as ACPE, by using unenhanced CT scans is vital.

Previously, numerous studies have explored the diagnostic value of unenhanced CT for the diagnosis of ACPE [8–10], and the results imply that unenhanced CT can serve as a valuable alternative modality for the diagnosis of ACPE. However, identifying ACPE on unenhanced CT images is often incidental. Its manifestations on unenhanced CT images are often overlooked. As a result, the sample size of eligible patients for inclusion in the study was limited. Additionally, because of the short time span of these two studies, the sample size was further reduced. Benefiting from the comprehensive utilization of electronic medical records systems and the Picture Archiving and Communication Systems (PACS) at our institution, the retrospective period of this study was extended to 5.3 years to ensure an adequate sample size.

This study employed CTPA as the gold standard to examine the effectiveness of unenhanced CT for the diagnosis of ACPE and developed a convenient tool for its rapid diagnosis. Compared with previous studies [8–10], the present study also develops a rapid graphical tool—a nomogram—for the prompt diagnosis of ACPE. This tool integrates key findings in unenhanced chetst CT into a user-friendly scoring system, enabling clinicians to estimate the probability of ACPE efficiently at the bedside [11].

Moreover, D-dimer levels assist in excluding PE before CTPA examination to avoid unnecessary examination [2, 12, 13]. D-dimer levels are a routine and classic laboratory test for patients suspected of pulmonary embolism before CTPA. Another question investigated in this study pertained to the diagnostic accuracy of the combination of D-dimer levels and unenhanced CT for identifying ACPE.

Methods and materials

Patients

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) had a confirmed diagnosis of PE in a main pulmonary artery or right or left artery according to CTPA; (2) had undergone unenhanced CT and CTPA examinations within 12 h. For each ACPE patient, we selected one age and sex matched PE-negative control from CTPA examinations during the same period, resulting in a total of 73 matched pairs and an overall study population of 146 participants (Fig. 1).

Patients in both cohorts were included in the flow chart. CTPA: Computed tomographic pulmonary angiography, PE: Pulmonary embolism, ACPE: Acute central PE

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients who had severe pulmonary disease, such as severe pulmonary inflammation, atelectasis, invasive lung cancer involving the pulmonary artery, or primary vascular endothelial disorders. (2) Patients who received anticoagulant or thrombolytic therapy. (3) Patients who did not undergo D-dimer testing within 24 h before or after the CTPA examination. (4) Patients with significant CT image artifacts.

Derivation cohort

From February1, 2018 to March 31, 2023, 61 patients with ACPE were diagnosed by CTPA at a tertiary hospital​ [2].

Validation cohort

From April 1 to June 30, 2023, 12 consecutive hospitalized ACPE patients diagnosed via CTPA at a tertiary hospital​ and 12 patients with CTPA-confirmed PE-negative patients were randomly selected and included in this cohort (Fig. 1). The inclusion and exclusion criteria were consistent with the abovementioned criteria.

Study design

A two-step approach was devised to evaluate the efficacy of unenhanced CT images for the diagnosis of ACPE. Firstly, an unenhanced CT model was constructed by univariate analysis and stepwise multivariate logistic regression. Subsequently, this logistic regression model obtained was applied to the validation cohort to validate its generalizability.

CT scan and images

All patients underwent unenhanced chest CT and CTPA scans using two Philips iCT256 scanners. Continuous scanning was conducted from the thoracic inlet to the apex of the diaphragm after an inspiratory breath-hold in the supine position. The imaging parameters included a tube voltage of 120 kV, a tube current of 168 mA, and the use of automA technology on dual-source CT. The slice thickness and slice distance were set to 5 mm, with thin slice images reconstructed at a slice thickness and slice distance of 1 mm.

Unenhanced CT images and CTPA images were retrospectively retrieved from the PACS. When one of the two observers identified an abnormal density in the pulmonary artery, two regions of interest (ROIs) were positioned in the area of abnormal density and at the origin of the pulmonary artery on the same level. If the difference in the mean CT value between two ROIs was greater than 10 HU, the patient was recorded as positive for either the "hyperdense sign" (Fig. 2-a) or "hypodense sign" (Fig. 2-b). Otherwise, the patient was classified as"normal density" (Fig. 2-c).

In the present study, an image review of unenhanced CT images was performed with 2 different settings: a mediastinal window (width: 350, level: 50) and a narrow window (width: 30-61, level: 110-190). The latter allowed for a better distinction of abnormal densities.

Two additional radiologists with 3-year-experience of chest CT to identify and localize abnormal density signs PE in the bilateral pulmonary arteries on unenhanced CT after a short-term training. The last two observers were blinded to the results of the CTPA examination, as well as the age, gender, hospital, laboratory test, the results of another observer's interpretation, patient cohort, CTPA findings, and patient information during review. In cases of disagreement between the two observers, a final decision was made by a senior radiologist with over 10 years of experience in thoracic imaging.

The initial readings by two junior radiologists were analyzed using the Kappa statistic to evaluate inter-observer consistency. The same radiologist re-evaluated the images one month later to assess intra-observer agreement.The vascular measurements in this study were performed on unenhanced CT transverse scan images using electronic callipers from the PACS. The main trunk of the pulmonary artery (mPA) and the ascending aorta (Ao) were measured at the level of the bifurcation of the main pulmonary artery (approximately at the middle third of the ascending portion) (Fig. 3). The superior vena cava (SVC) and azygos (AzV) arch were measured at the level of the AzV arch emanating from the SVC.

D-dimer

D-dimer levels were measured using a latex immunoturbidimetric assay, and D-dimer test kits were purchased from Sekisui Medical. For patients in the emergency department, the initial D-dimer test results upon admission were documented. For hospitalized patients, D-dimer test results within 24 hours prior to the initial CTPA were recorded.

Statistical analysis

In this study, statistical analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS software, version 25.0, and MedCalc software, version 20.0. The data are presented as frequencies and percentages. For comparisons of continuous variables with uniform variance following a normal distribution, Student's t test was used; otherwise, the Mann‒Whitney U test was used. Enumeration data were analysed using the χ2 test. A two-sided p value of less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

According to Landis Koch [14], the level of agreement was categorized as follows: slight agreement (kappa 0-0.20), fair agreement (kappa 0.21-0.40), moderate agreement (kappa 0.41-0.60), substantial agreement (kappa 0.61-0.80), and almost perfect agreement (kappa 0.81-1).

The minimum required sample size was estimated using the events-per-variable (EPV) criterion [15].\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:N=\frac{10\:\times\:p}{r}\:$$\end{document}

The total minimum sample size (N) was determined using the events-per-variable (EPV) principle, where N is calculated by dividing the product of 10 and the number of predictor parameters (p) by the anticipated event rate (r), ensuring at least 10 events per variable to maintain model stability and minimize overfitting in the regression analysis.

In this study, the logistic regression model incorporated a maximum of 5 predictor variables. According to the minimum requirement of 10 EPV and the formula described in the statistical section, at least 100 cases were required. The sample size in the derivation cohort was sufficient to meet this criterion [15].

A nomogram was constructed according to the logistic regression model and using the rms package in R version 3.4.2 (http://www.r-project.org/).

Ethical approval

This retrospective study was conducted at The Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University and was approved by the Institutional Review Boards (IRBs) of the hospital. In accordance with the IRB's decision, the requirement for informed consent from participants was waived. This authorization from the IRBs added credibility and guaranteed that the study was conducted ethically and in compliance with the necessary regulations and guidelines. Ethical approval for this study was obtained on October 13, 2023.

Interobserver and intra-observer agreement

The intra-observer agreement for abnormal density signs yielded a kappa value of 0.826 (95% CI: 0.745-0.906), while the inter-observer agreement showed a kappa value of 0.796 (95% CI: 0.697-0.895).

Derivation cohort

Interestingly, significant differences were observed in abnormal density signs, mPA, PA/Ao, SVC, and AzV between ACPE patients and non-PE patients in the derivation cohort. Specifically, 51 out of 61 ACPE patients were considered to be positive for abnormal density signs; 39 patients exhibited hyperdense signs (58%), and 12 patients exhibited hypodense signs (25%). However, the images of 14 non-PE patients also exhibited abnormal density signs (Table 1).

Notably, the abnormal density sign had the highest AUC (0.803; 95% CI=0.722 to 0.870). Among the vascular measurements, the AzV had an AUC of 0.665 (95% CI 0.574 to 0.748), which was high but still lower than that of D-dimer, with an AUC of 0.799 (95% CI 0.717 to 0.866) (Table 2).

Validation

The diagnostic capabil of the model was verified using a validation cohort of patients between April and June 2023. 14 patients were diagnosed with ACPE based on CTPA findings. 2 patients were excluded from the study owing to the absence of unenhanced CT images. In the validation cohort, which consisted of 12 patients with ACPE and an additional 12 non-PE patients, the unenhanced CT model demonstrated an AUC of 0.844 (95% CI 0.638 to 0.958) for the diagnosis of ACPE (Table4).

Discussion

The nomogram based on unenhanced CT was composed of abnormal density signs and mPA/Ao>0.83, and AzV achieved favorable diagnostic performance (area under the curve AUC>0.8) in both the derivation and validation cohorts. These findings confirm that the nomogram can serve as an effective and generalizable tool for diagnosing ACPE. This nomogram provides an immediately actionable tool for rapid ACPE triage in emergency settings. Its particular advantage lies in overcoming contrast-related limitations[16], offering critical diagnostic support when contrast use is contraindicated or delayed. By delivering early risk stratification directly at the point of care, this model empowers clinicians to make informed decisions swiftly, bridging a vital gap in acute scenarios.

Integrating this nomogram into clinical practice is straightforward due to its simple graphical format. It can be seamlessly embedded into existing systems such as PACS, enabling ACPE risk calculation during initial image interpretation. Alternatively, it can also be printed as a quick-reference paper tool for use in reading rooms or emergency settings. This flexibility promotes rapid risk assessment, reduces diagnostic delays, and helps accelerate treatment decisions—all while fitting smoothly into existing workflows without requiring significant changes to routine practice.

The robust performance of the nomogram across derivation and validation cohorts underscores its potential for broader adoption. Future steps include external validation across diverse populations and settings, as well as integration into user-friendly clinical decision support systems. Successful implementation could standardize and improve ACPE diagnosis nationally, and eventually contribute to guideline updates.

In addition to the findings previously reported in the literature, this study further evaluated the inter-observer agreement of the abnormal density sings. The results demonstrated that, after a short period of targeted training, radiologists achieved substantial inter-observer agreement (Kappa:0.796) and near-perfect intra-observer consistency (Kappa:0.826). Reports of ACPE with hyperattenuation or hypoattenuation clots in the main or primary pulmonary artery date back two decades[17, 18]. Notably, additional cases of ACPE with hyperdense signs have been documented in recent years[19–22]. In this study, the diagnostic efficacy of abnormal density signs was particularly noteworthy, which is in line with the findings of prior studies[8, 9, 23]. However, abnormal density signs demonstrated a sensitivity of 83.6% and a specificity of 77.1%, in contrast to the lower sensitivity (57.1% to 71.9%) and remarkable specificity (97.6% to 100%) documented in prior studies[8–10, 23]. The potential explanations for this disparity can be delineated as follows: (1) the unenhanced CT images in this study were all 1-1.25 mm thick, whereas Chen et al. and Tatco et al. employed unenhanced CT images with thicknesses ranging from 2-5 mm and 2 mm, respectively[8, 23]; (2) in contrast to the exclusive recording of hyperdense signs in the aforementioned studies[8, 23], in instances where disagreements arose concerning observations of abnormal density signs from two observers, a senior physician was requested to formulate a conclusive decision. Conversely, in Ehsanbakhsh et al.'s study, patients were directly excluded in the event of disagreements between observers[9]. While this approach partly decreases the risk of false positives, this stringent inclusion criterion resulted in a smaller number of ACPE patients in the final queue (28 patients) (3) In the present study, the hypodense sign was also recorded as a positive sign, potentially increasing sensitivity. In contrast, Chen et al. and Ehsanbakhsh et al. observed only the presence of the hyperdense sign [8, 9].

Earlier studies were predominantly retrospective studies with small sample sizes[8, 9]. This study aimed to overcome this limitation by extending the inclusion period to 5.3 years and ultimately included 73 ACPE patients, with 12 patients allocated to the validation cohort, thus enhancing the generalizability of the results. Tatco VR and Piedad HH conducted studies with a comparable number of patients to our study[23]. However, our study included a significantly larger number of ACPE patients (73 vs. 25), and our research specifically focused on abnormal density signs while disregarding the significance of other indirect signs on unenhanced CT scans.

Another novel finding of this study is that mPA/Ao >0.83 can predict the risk of ACPE. mPA/Ao >0.83 was retained in the model after stepwise logistic regression rather than the traditional cut-off value of 1.0 or 0.9 [24]. Patients suffer from APE-increased pulmonary vascular resistance resulting from vascular obstruction, neurohumoral agents, or pulmonary artery baroreceptors, often complicated by acute pulmonary hypertension[25]. Compared to the conventional cut-off values of 1.0 or 0.9, a smaller value of 0.83 was selected because significant dilation of the pulmonary artery typically occurs in chronic pulmonary arterial hypertension, whereas the short-term compensatory capacity of the pulmonary artery is limited. One advantage of using mPA/Ao compared to mPA is that it is less influenced by sex and body weight [26], eliminating the need for sex-specific cut-off values. For the convenience of clinical application,＞0.83 was employed as the cut-off value for mPA/Ao. The subsequent validation step also confirmed the generalizability of the findings. Moreover, based on a retrospective analysis utilizing unenhanced CT cross-sectional scanning, mPA/Ao was demonstrated to be both reproducible and indicative of the presence of pulmonary hypertension [27].

In addition to pulmonary artery dilatation, venous dilatation should not be ignored. Mechanical obstruction, hypoxic vasoconstriction, and vasoconstricting influences of mediators released following APE collectively promote acute afterload challenge, which in turn leads to acute RV dilatation and myocardial injury, eventually causing overt pump failure[5, 24]. Previous studies have established the indicative role of right ventricular insufficiency in pulmonary embolism[5, 28–31]. However, these studies utilized echocardiography or CTPA to observe morphological alterations in the right ventricle. Nevertheless, the ventricular contour is often not visible on unenhanced CT images. The transverse diameter of the AzV has been linked to right heart strain in APE patients, is significantly correlated with poor prognosis [32], and is measurable on unenhanced CT images.

Although D-dimer levels can indicate the presence of blood clots, their diagnostic value in treating ACPE is limited. The combination of unenhanced CT & D-dimer did not improve the AUC in the present study. Notably, the unenhanced CT model also achieved a greater AUC than did the D-dimer model (0.877 vs 0.799), suggesting that the utilization of unenhanced CT alone before performing CTPA for the diagnosis of ACPE is more efficient, as it can save valuable examination time without compromising diagnostic accuracy. However, D-dimer levels still play a decisive role in ruling out ACPE (NPV: 92.9%). This point was also highlighted in the latest guidelines [2].

Limitations

There are still certain limitations inherent in our study that merit acknowledgement. First, our investigation exclusively focused on the mPA or the left and right pulmonary arteries, and additional research is necessary to explore the use of unenhanced CT in patients with peripheral PE. Second, although the unenhanced CT model exhibited satisfactory performance for the diagnosis of ACPE, patients receiving anticoagulant or thrombolytic therapy were deliberately excluded from the study. Consequently, further investigations are warranted to evaluate alterations in unenhanced CT indicators during medical intervention. Moreover, this was a single-center study in which all patients were recruited from a provincial medical center. However, further multicenter studies are needed to validate the accuracy and generalizability of our findings.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrated that the diagnostic performance of unenhanced CT for the diagnosis of ACPE should not be overlooked. The nomogram based on the unenhanced CT model serves as a convenient tool for reducing misdiagnosis in patients with less noticeable symptoms.

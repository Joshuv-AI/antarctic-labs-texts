Development of a diagnostic model of insulin‐related lipohypertrophy for patients with diabetes mellitus
Authors: Xiaohui Li, Yuhua Wei, Dandan Sun, Hongmei Xu, Chun Mu, Di Bao, Yingying Li, Qiuling Xing
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAimsTo develop a diagnostic model of insulin‐related lipohypertrophy for patients with diabetes mellitus.Materials and MethodsA cross‐sectional study was performed. Between December 2021 and November 2022, a total of 395 diabetic patients treated with insulin were enrolled from a tertiary hospital in Tianjin, China. Demographic and clinical factors were collected, and patients were assessed for lipohypertrophy by ultrasound scanning. The rate of lipohypertrophy was investigated, and a nomogram was developed based on a logistic regression model. SPSS 26.0 and R Studio 2022.12.0+353 3.6.3 were used to analyze the data.Results89.6% (354/395) patients were identified with lipohypertrophy. In this study, 693 lipohypertrophy sites in lipohypertrophy patients and 91.5% (634/693) lipohypertrophy were distributed at the abdomen. Self‐injection, duration of insulin injection, and incorrect rotation were selected as predictors to develop a lipohypertrophy diagnosed diagnostic nomogram model of lipohypertrophy in diabetic patients treated with insulin. The accuracy, calibration, and clinical applicability of the model are good; thus, it fits well.ConclusionsA nomogram including three easily available factors (self‐injection, duration of insulin injection, and incorrect rotation) was developed, and it can be used in diabetes management among diabetes patients treated with insulin.Keywords: Lipohypetrophy, Nomogram, Ultrasound scanning

INTRODUCTION

Diabetes mellitus is an increasing public health problem in the world. The global diabetes prevalence in 2021 was estimated to be 10.5% (536.6 million people), and there are about 140.9 million people with diabetes mellitus in China, ranking first in the world 1 . Insulin plays an important role in the treatment of diabetes, and its emergence has effectively improved the quality of life and clinical outcomes of diabetic patients. Almost all T1DM patients and nearly one‐third of T2DM patients are treated with exogenous insulin 2 and the number of patients treated with insulin could be as high as 178.9 million in the world and 47 million in China.

With the improvement of the purity and type of insulin preparations, insulin‐related lipoatrophy has become rare in clinical practice, but subcutaneous lipohypertrophy is still a common complication of insulin injection 3 . Lipohypertrophy manifests as swelling, “rubbery” lesions in the subcutaneous tissue. It has been widely recognized that incorrect insulin injection technique, a long duration of insulin treatment, and higher frequency of insulin needle reuse are risk factors for lipohypertrophy 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 . Lipohypertrophy is associated with blunted and more variable insulin absorption and action, which leads to the deterioration of metabolic control and frequent episodes of hypoglycemia 4 , 12 , 13 . Still, many ignore the social and economic costs of such lesions, including increasing healthcare costs, cosmetic effects, and severe psychological burden 14 .

The methods of lipohypertrophy identification include physical examination (inspection and palpation) and ultrasound scanning. Physical examination is more common in clinical practice and free for patients, and the detection rate is 42.9–69.8% 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 , 12 , 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 . However, this method is highly dependent on the operator's maneuvers and experience, which imposes limitations on the reliability and reproducibility of the results 14 . In contrast, although ultrasound scanning comes at a high cost, but it is more objective, and the results are more convincing. Recent studies have shown that the ultrasound scanning detection rate is higher than that of physical examination, and the detection rate is 69.1–89.7% 16 , 17 , 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 . However, it is obvious that ultrasound scanning requires a high financial cost to the hospital or the patients. In addition, Gentile Sandro's study has shown that the detection rate can be consistent with that of the United States if examiners are trained in a systematic way, but there is no doubt that this will still require the hospital to spend a lot of time, energy, and money. Unfortunately, this method is not widely available. Therefore, in general, ultrasound scanning is more accurate, and its results are more reliable.

Whatever, the hazards and high detection rate of lipohypertrophy have attracted the attention of healthcare providers. Consequently, the expert consensuses on lipohypertrophy were published, recommending that lipohypertrophy screening and insulin injection technique education should be conducted at least once a year, the latter of which should begin when the patient first injects insulin 22 . However, the number of diabetic patients is huge, and in the context of aging, as the increasing duration of the disease in patients with T2DM, when the oral hypoglycemic effect of T2DM patients is not valid or there are contraindications to oral drug use, there will be more and more patients who need insulin therapy. Such screening management workload is an incredible challenge. Considering the time and economic costs, compared to training more lipohypertrophy examiners or squeezing out time in busy clinical work for screening, having a tool for lipohypertrophy diagnosis is a better option for routine primary screening in a large population. Unfortunately, there are currently no relevant lipohypertrophy diagnostic tools at home and abroad.

Because ultrasound examination is expensive, we hope that the model built in this study could be used as an adjunct to physical examination, which is widely used. However, it should not be ignored that the accuracy of ultrasound examination is higher than that of physical examination, and it is reasonable to speculate that the model built with ultrasound results can avoid the missed diagnosis of lipohypertrophy, to ensure that “suspected LH patients” can receive further clinical examination, compared with the model built by physical examination results. So, to assist medical staff healthcare professionals in screening lipohypertrophy more quickly and accurately, the aim of our study was to investigate the rate of lipohypertrophy and develop an inexpensive and convenient screening tool based on the results of ultrasonography.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Subjects

A cross‐sectional study was conducted from December 2021 to November 2022. A convenience sampling method was used to recruit hospitalized patients with diabetes in a tertiary hospital in Tianjin, China.

The sample size was calculated with a formula of cross‐sectional study

P is the estimated incidence rate and ε is the relative error. By consulting the literature, this study predicted that the prevalence of lipohypertrophy identified by ultrasound scanning in patients with diabetes mellitus was about 50%, ε = 10%, confidence level 1 − α = 95%. According to the formula, the sample size of this study is at least 385. Finally, 395 participants were recruited, and the completion rate of the questionnaire is 100%.

Criteria for inclusion were as follows: (1) diagnosed with diabetes (WHO diagnostic criteria for diabetes in 1999); (2) age between 18 and 75 years old; (3) treated with insulin and its analogs for at least 1 year, with the daily injection frequency at least one time/day. The following groups of patients were excluded: (1) Patients with severe complications or comorbidities, such as stage 5 diabetic nephropathy or acute myocardial infarction, who are unable to understand or cooperate with questionnaire filling, visual examination, or ultrasound examination; (2) patients with lipohypertrophy caused by other treatments, such as antiretroviral therapy or long‐term use of corticosteroids; (3) patients with other skin diseases or surgical scars that interfere with the examination and determination of insulin injection sites; (4) patients who have been using other injection drugs (such as heparin) for a long time; (5) patients allergic to ultrasound gel.

General information of participants

Based on a systematic review of domestic and international literature on lipohypertrophy 17 , 22 , 23 , 24 and the practical experience of the research team 25 , 26 , the initial general information questionnaire was self‐designed. Subsequently, the final version was refined through three rounds of small‐scale pilot testing with approximately 20 participants each, followed by multiple rounds of discussion and revision by the research team. This process ensured the instrument was both aligned with the research objectives and easily comprehensible and accessible to participants. Finally, the questionnaire consists of 24 items, including gender, age, height, weight, abdominal circumference, education level, type of diabetes, duration of diabetes, duration of insulin injection, total daily insulin dose, frequency of daily injection, glycated hemoglobin, and hypoglycemia (6 months), education of insulin injection technique, ice‐cold insulin injection, self‐injection, frequency of needle reuse, needle length, injection site, and rotation of injection sites. Among them, there are a few things that need to be pointed out. Glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) values were taken from the most recent biochemical test in the last 3 months from the date of inclusion. BMI was categorized as underweight group (BMI < 18.5 kg/m2), normal group (BMI 18.5–23.9 kg/m2), overweight group (BMI 24.0–27.9 kg/m2), and obese group (BMI ≥28 kg/m2). Normal waist circumference: <85 cm for males, <80 cm for females. The researcher asked each patient who rotated insulin injection in detail, using their professional ability to judge whether the patient's approach was correct. Correct rotation was considered only if the patient performed both inter‐site rotation (injection sites including the abdomen, arms, thighs, and buttocks) and intra‐site rotation (at least 10 mm from previous injection sites); if not, it is recorded as no rotation.

Assessment of lipohypertrophy

Combine our practical experience with relevant literature 12 , 16 , 27 , 28 , the diagnostic criteria for ultrasound are as follows: (1) The boundary between the dermis and the subcutaneous layer is unclear, with hyperechoic lesions (Feature 1); the boundary between the dermis and the subcutaneous layer is clear, and the subcutaneous layer has hypoechoic (Feature 2) or hyperechoic nodular lesions (Feature 3); (2) compared with the surrounding tissues, the echogenicity texture is uneven in the subcutaneous layer; (3) deformation of the surrounding connective tissue in the subcutaneous layer; (4) few or no blood vessels and absence of capsule in the subcutaneous layer.

Study procedure

Researchers used standardized instructions to explain the study's purpose, significance, and procedures to eligible patients who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria. After obtaining informed consent, patient‐related information was collected. Researchers first obtained patient data from the electronic medical system (EMS). Information not documented in the EMS was obtained through face‐to‐face interviews. For patients unable to complete questionnaires independently due to factors such as low literacy or poor vision, assistance was provided by the researchers. A designated researcher reviewed the questionnaires to ensure their quality, accuracy, and completeness. If any data were incomplete or unclear, it was obtained by contacting the patient.

Ultrasound scanning was performed on all insulin injection sites of the patient, applying a handheld high‐frequency linear probe (5–12.5 MHz, Cisco Duo model H31L, China). The steps for ultrasound examination are as follows: (1) Instruct the patient to lie in a supine position with knees bent to relax the abdomen and guide the patient to abduct the upper arms to fully expose the examination area; for the hip examination, instruct the patient to lie on their side with legs bent to relax the hips. (2) Move from the waist toward the midline to comprehensively examine areas the patient may use. Scan the insulin injection sites in parallel, following an outward‐to‐inward and top‐to‐bottom sequence. (3) By carefully comparing the ultrasonic characteristics of the surrounding areas of the lesion and its contralateral midline region, the lipohypertrophy can ultimately be identified. (4) For each participant, the number, specific location, and echo characteristics of LH were recorded. Meanwhile, the research team provided personalized health education to patients with lipohypertrophy.

Statistical analyses

SPSS 26.0 software was used to analyze the data. Patient characteristics were described as the means ± standard deviation (SD) for continuous variables and as the number/percentage for categorical variables. Univariate analysis was performed using the chi‐squared test or Fisher's exact test method, and binary logistic regression analysis was used to establish a mathematical model for the risk of lipohypertrophy, and the model was visualized as a nomogram using RStudio 2022.12.0 + 353 software. The evaluation indicators of the model are as follows: 1,000 bootstrap resamples were used for internal validation of the nomogram; the area under the receiver operating characteristics (ROC) curve (AUC) was used to evaluate discrimination; a calibration plot and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test were used to evaluate calibration; and the threshold of the model was based on the maximum Youden index to calculate the sensitivity and specificity of the model. Values of P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Ethical approval

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chu Hisen‐I Memorial Hospital of Tianjin Medical University, Tianjin, China (DXBYYkMEC2021‐10) and it conforms to the provisions of the Declaration of Helsinki in 1995 (as revised in Fortaleza, Brazil, October 2013). The subject in the protocol gave informed consent and patient anonymity should be preserved. The participants were fully informed and signed the written informed consent before the study.

All patients used insulin pens, with needle lengths distributed as follows: 4 mm (26.8%); 5 mm (40.8%); 6 mm (29.4%); 8 mm (3.0%). 47.8% (189/395) reused injection needles more than five times. Validation and evaluation of diagnostic model for insulin‐related lipohypertrophy

We performed internal validation using 1,000 bootstrap resamples. The C‐index was 0.756 (95% CI: 0.668–0.845), indicating good consistency between our model diagnostics and the actual outcomes.

The AUC of the model is 0.756 (95% CI: 0.668–0.845; Figure 2a). When the maximum value of the Youden index is 0.792, the corresponding predicted probability is 0.904. At this point, the sensitivity is 0.955 and the specificity is 0.463. The model was tested using the Hosmer–Lemeshow test, with a chi‐squared value of 0.553 and P = 0.9998. The calibration plot of the model is shown in Figure 2b. The clinical diagnostic curve indicates that patients can benefit from ultrasound scanning (Figure 2c).

DISCUSSION

Risk factors for insulin‐related lipohypertrophy

Duration of insulin injection

Duration of insulin injection ≥5 years is a risk factor for lipohypertrophy in this study, which is consistent with the results of Li et al. 29 and Vardar et al. 30 Unfortunately, duration is a factor that cannot be intervened or controlled. Studies have demonstrated that lipohypertrophy may be related to the growth‐promoting properties of insulin or mechanical damage to the adipose tissue caused by long‐term injection.

Incorrect rotation

Incorrect rotation is another risk factor for lipohypertrophy, which was consistent with several researches 4 , 9 , 17 , 31 . Incorrect rotation is a controllable risk factor. The intervention was free of charge, but the benefits were substantial. Currently, the main intervention method for incorrect rotation is reinforced insulin injection technique education for patients. Studies have shown that the area of lipohypertrophy, the number of hypoglycemic episodes, and insulin total daily dosage in patients would be decreased after short‐term intensive insulin injection education 32 , 33 , but the long‐term effects of short‐term education in patients are unclear. Currently, Gentile et al. 34 have conducted an 18‐month mid‐term insulin intensive education intervention and reported that a single training course on insulin injection technique is not valuable. Meanwhile, our study found that although 80.3% (317/395) of patients had trained with insulin injection technique education, one patient retrained within the past 6 months. Those show that long‐term education is rare in clinical practice. Therefore, it is necessary to establish a long‐term lipohypertrophy screening and insulin injection education regularly. Health professionals can conduct multiformat, ongoing educational activities online or offline from multiple levels, including hospital wards, outpatient clinics, and the community.

Self‐injection

Self‐injection is a new risk factor for the development of lipohypertrophy. Fewer researchers notice it. We think the possible reasons are as follows: (1) Patients who have insulin injected by others not only have the option of a greater number of injection sites but also an increase in the area injected compared to patients who inject their own insulin. The size of the insulin injection area has been found to be an independent risk factor for lipohypertrophy 35 ; (2) long‐term assistance with insulin injections means that he or she will take a greater family/social support system in the patient's health, and they may take the initiative to learn about insulin injections and perform the correct injections, thus reducing the risk of lipohypertrophy. A study showed that insulin injection behavior was better in diabetic patients living with family members 36 , which shows that having a good external support system is more conducive to the management of diabetes in patients.

Diagnostic model for lipohypertrophy

Currently, there are few clinical diagnostic model studies about lipohypertrophy. We built a clinical diagnostic model based on the patient's three clinical factors. The values of AUC above 0.75 indicate that the model is highly differentiated 37 . Our diagnostic model has an AUC of 0.756 (0.668–0.845), and the model has good discrimination ability. Our calibration plot and Hosmer–Lemeshow test results both showed that the model was well calibrated. Our clinical decision curve shows that patients can benefit from this. Overall, the model fits well.

Limitations

To the best of our knowledge, it is the first risk diagnostic model developed for lipohypertrophy. Subsequent studies attempt to establish various diagnostic models, such as machine learning, and compare the predictive abilities of multiple models to select the optimal model for clinical application. In addition, our model is based on Chinese patients, where insulin injection sites are more likely to be recommended in the abdomen, while patients in certain countries are more likely to be in the arm, such as India and Algeria 38 , 39 . For these patients, our model may not be suitable, and as a result, more samples from patients of other countries are needed. Due to constraints in both time and financial resources, convenience sampling was adopted as the method of data collection, and external validation was not performed. In the future, we will conduct further in‐depth investigations based on this foundation and employ stratified sampling to improve the diagnostic model. It is worth noting that physical examination is more commonly used in clinical practice, so the model built based on ultrasound results is used to assist physical examination. The advantage of the model based on ultrasound examination is that it can provide more sensitive screening of suspicious patients. Based on reducing hospital resources, it is necessary to avoid missing the diagnosis of LH patients.

Finally, the nomogram of insulin‐related lipohypertrophy was developed, incorporating self‐injection, duration of insulin injection, and incorrect rotation. The model demonstrated great accuracy, calibration, and clinical applicability. The establishment of this tool provides a novel possibility and direction for professionals to efficiently identify lipohypertrophy in an economical and easy way, benefiting patients, healthcare providers, and the healthcare system.

Tongue and teeth hyperpigmentation in etoposide, prednisolone, vincristine, and cyclophosphamide regimen in the treatment of cutaneous lymphoma
Authors: Soumya Shivalingappa Morabad, Zahed Ullah Khan, Richa Gad, Nikitha Kallur, Irappa Vithoba Madhabhavi, Mallappa Hanumanthappa Shalavadi, Chandrashekhar Venkaraddi Mangannavar
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Cutaneous extranodal non-Hodgkin lymphoma is a rare cancer and chemotherapeutic agents like etoposide, vincristine and cyclophosphamide are the drug of choice, which rarely cause hyperpigmentation in skin and nails. However, herein we present a case of hyperpigmentation that was seen in tongue and even in teeth. The hyperpigmentation of the tongue and teeth occurred shortly after the initiation of chemotherapy. Hyperpigmentation was self-limiting and rectified in a week without need of any pharmacological, surgical, or lifestyle interventions.

Introduction

Cutaneous T-cell lymphomas are a group of heterogeneous extranodal non-Hodgkin lymphoma characterized by cutaneous infiltration of malignant T cells.[1] Moreover, they are characterized by bumps on the skin, most of which are classified as mycosis fungoid and Sezary syndrome.[2] Early diagnosis of T cell lymphoma might be difficult as it has no specific presentations and early lesions lack diagnostic features as seen in advanced stages.[3] The exact pathophysiology of cutaneous T cell lymphoma is not understood but may involve skin lesions, location, and signaling.[4] Diagnosing cutaneous T cell lymphoma consists of precise histopathological investigations, ancillary tests including CD4 cell identification, aberrant immune phenotypes, T cell receptor rearrangements, skin lesions, and peripheral blood.[5] EPOCH regimen consisting of etoposide, prednisolone, vincristine, cyclophosphamide, and doxorubicin is the most effective regimen in treating cutaneous T cell lymphoma.[6] However, the regimen has side effects and adverse effects, some of which are commonly seen and some of which are very rare. Nausea, vomiting, alopecia, neutropenia, cardiac heart failure, anemia, anorexia, are most commonly seen adverse effect.[7891011] In this case since the patient had a history of cardiomyopathy, doxorubicin is avoided in EPOCH therapy as doxorubicin is known to cause cardiomyopathy. Hence, hyperpigmentation of tongue and teeth was due to cyclophosphamide therapy alone in this patient.

Case Report

A 67-year old male patient who was all right initially then had complaints of rash, eruption and scaly patches over the left arm associated with weight loss and upon investigations and by histopathological report, a malignant neoplasm of the left forearm was confirmed, which was further supported with an immunohistochemistry yielding positive CD30, CD45 and CD4 report, positron emission topography- computed topography scan was done and which confirmed cutaneous T cell lymphoma of the left forearm. Patient was then to be treated with radiation chemotherapy. Accordingly, 2 cycles of 40 Gy radiations were given from February to March at a different hospital and were referred for further chemotherapy with a dose-adjusted EPOCH regimen. Routine blood work was done which revealed decreased protein levels (albumin - 2.1 g/dL, globulin 3.8 g/dL) and elevated lactate dehydrogenase (339 U/L). The patient had a history of cardiomyopathy owing to which doxorubicin had to be avoided as it has a risk of worsening existing cardiomyopathy and contraindicated the use of doxorubicin in such scenarios.[7] Patient received 6 cycles of the regimen with Cyclophosphamide 750mg/m2, Vincristine1.4mg/m2, Etoposide50mg/m2, and Prednisolone 10mg. The patient was taken up for chemotherapy at the day care unit and on day 2 after the first cycle patient noticed discoloration of the tongue and teeth [Figures 1 and 2], which were associated with complaints of taste disturbances, although disturbance in taste is quite common adverse drug reaction and which subsided on their own in a week without any therapeutic interventions. When the patient came for his 2nd cycle, he had the same complaints of discoloration on day 2 and patient has same discoloration throughout 6 cycles with above chemotherapy.

The exact pathophysiology behind discoloration in the above regimen is not understood. However it is hypothesized that the discoloration may be due to interactions with melanocyte stimulating hormone, which may be due to altered gene coding for normal melanin, thereby altering normal melanin production leading to hyperpigmentation and also probably due to deposition of dead papillae of the tongue and which was eventually discolored or hyperpigmented.[12]

Discussion

Hyperpigmentation of skin due to EPOCH therapy has been reported in a few case reports.[89 101113] However, no case reported for the teeth and tongue discoloration due to EPOCH regimen. In present case with proper investigation and evidence confirmed the first case of teeth and tongue discoloration due to cyclophosphamide. Although factors such as improper oral hygiene can be a possible explanation of dental hyperpigmentation. But this kind of pigmentation resolved on its own after a week, this further confirms that observed discoloration was indeed a consequence of adverse drug reaction of cyclophosphamide. Further using the Naranjo probability score clearly indicated the probable association between cyclophosphamide and hyperpigmentation, which are similar to the previous result of Santosh Kumar et al.[14]

Conclusion

Hyperpigmentation of the skin is quite commonly seen in patients receiving cytotoxic drugs, but in present case a rare incidence of hyperpigmentation involving the tongue and teeth was seen, our proper investigation and thorough literature evidence suggested the possible agent to be a cyclophosphamide, additionally bluish discoloration of teeth was also noticed in our patient following reception of regimen. This is the first case of teeth and tongue discoloration due to cyclophosphamide and we recommend physicians, pharmacists, nurses and the therapeutic team to increase vigilance towards the incidence of more of such instances.

When care demands rise: Exploring the impact of hemiparesis dependency and healthcare challenges among caregivers at a tertiary hospital in Bhubaneswar
Authors: Samapika Rout, Sasmita Das, Madhusmita Nayak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The burden of hemiparesis on caregivers is significant, often leading to physical strain, emotional stress, social isolation, depression, and a decline in their own health. The aim is to investigate the degree of dependency of hemiparesis patients and their healthcare challenges among caregivers of stroke survivors. The current study has adopted a correlational research design to assess the degree of dependency of hemiparesis patients and their health care challenges among caregivers of stroke survivors at the neurology ward of IMS and SUM hospital, Bhubaneswar. A total of 92 samples were chosen using the total enumerative sampling technique. The tools used were structured sociodemographic proforma for hemiparesis patients and caregivers, functional independence measure (FIM) tool for measuring degree of dependence, and structured five-point rating scale for measuring caregiver challenges. Collected data were analyzed by using descriptive (frequency and percentage) and inferential statistics (association and correlation analysis). The findings of the study reveal that the maximum hemiparesis patients were aged over 65 years (48%) and were males (68%). Most of the caregivers were spouse (62%). Most of the hemiparesis patients were partially dependent (74%) on caregivers revealed by FIM score, and caregivers moderately face challenges (57%). There is a strong positive correlation found between the degree of dependency and caregiver’s challenge (r = 0.01, P = 0.00). The Chi-square test reveals that there is a no association between the caregiver challenges and sociodemographic variables. Hence, the study concluded that mostly hemiparesis patients were partially dependent on their caregivers and the caregivers face many challenges as they take care of their close ones.

Introduction

Stroke continues to be the significant global health concern including India. The incidence of stroke in India ranges from 119 to 145 per one lakh population over the year, with a higher prevalence observed in urban areas compared to rural regions.[1] Stroke always leaves a person with jeopardy with multiple complications such as paresis of limbs, neurological impairments, various opportunistic, thrombotic disorders due to long immobility, pneumonia, infections, and emotional breakdown. Some authors of Chennai studied 100 stroke patients for the possible complications. They observed 67% patients developed facial palsy, 62% with hemiparesis. The major risk factors were high BP, diabetes, and obesity.[2] Similarly, another study has screened 480 stroke patients for the presence of hemiplegic shoulder pain. The prevalence of hemiplegic shoulder pain was 55.6% among post-stroke patients. This further lowers the score of ability of daily living.[3]

Another review has reported that post-stoke patients often suffer with disability and paresis in developing countries. The reason is not getting in-time treatment. This calls for a more in-depth population study on stroke patients in difficult-to-reach areas to develop strategies for the smooth access to the health facility.[4] The South East Asia Region (SEAR) countries go through the epidemiological transition with high mortality due to stroke and cardiovascular conditions. They bear the burden of more than 40% of mortality due to stroke. In witnessing such a high prevalence of stroke, the adequacy of current data on burden of stroke and hemiparesis with challenges calls for urgent high-quality hospital-based data, which will bring light on the further care plan pathways.[5] Due to post-stroke complications like hemiparesis, the level of dependency increases. Sennfalt et al.[6] have reported with an increased level of post-stroke dependency; the comorbid burden was high, which delays the prognosis.

The caregivers play an important role in the recovery process of stroke survivors. A study of Australia emphasized on the role of community, family members, and spouses in caring for stroke patients. They further stressed on the programs to strengthen the caregivers’ preparedness impacts on care. No or little preparation lowers the quality of life among the care givers.[7] The burden of stroke and its complications like hemiparesis is not limited to the stroke patients; rather, it extends to their caregivers. Caregivers of stroke survivors develop significant physical, emotional, financial, and social challenges, which impacts their own wellbeing and prolongs the recovery process of their loved ones. A study has explored caregiver burden among those caring for older stroke survivors in urban Malaysia. Results showed over half of the caregivers experienced mild to moderate levels of burden. Key predictors of burden included higher caregiver education, caregiver and patient hypertension, patient cognitive decline, incontinence, greater dependency, and longer daily care hours. The findings suggest that cultural values, such as filial piety, may moderate the perceived burden in this population.[8]

Another study has assessed caregivers’ burden along with associated factors among primary caregivers of stroke survivors in Ethiopia. The study revealed 67% of caregivers experienced burden. Key factors linked to higher burden included being female, low household income, longer caregiving duration (over 3 months), and providing care for more than 6 hours per day. The study emphasizes the need for healthcare providers to recognize caregiver strain and offer targeted support.[9] A recent study from Bahir Dar, Ethiopia, found a high prevalence of post-stroke depression (42.9%) among stroke patients, with significant predictors including prior depression, ischemic heart disease, and physical disability, particularly hemiparesis. This further brings emotional burden on caregivers by prolonged care demands, highlighting the need for routine nursing assessments not just for patients but also for caregivers. Actively involving caregivers in the care process is essential to mitigate further psychological distress and improve overall stroke recovery outcomes.[10]

A recent paper of China has reported a positive correlation between the quality of life of caregivers of stroke survivors and the early preparedness for discharge (P <0.01). Further, the multiple linear regression has indicated the factors which influence the burden are the patient’s severity, caregiver’s QOL, the level of readiness of the patient for discharge, and the time spent by the caregiver for care (R² = 0.589, P <0.001).[11] As the role of health professionals was important speeding the recovery of a stroke survivor, so also the role of a well-dedicated caregiver. For this to happen, the caregivers must be in good state of health. According to a qualitative study, exploring the support needs of caregivers, key areas identified were physical empowerment, emotional and social support, coping with stigma, and dynamic educational needs.[12] Going further, a report also suggested the need for community youngsters to have knowledge on primary management of stroke. This may also be helpful with lowering the caregiver’s burden in future. The knowledge on stroke management was provided to villagers through attractive strikers for better understanding and memorization, and the intervention was found effective.[13] Another similar paper also showed effective implementation of home-based health promotion programs for stroke patients.[14]

This study aims to assess the correlation between the degree of dependency among hemiparetic stroke survivors and the healthcare challenges faced by their caregivers, while also exploring the association of these factors with selected sociodemographic variables. Hemiparesis is a common and often disabling consequence of stroke, leading to prolonged immobility and a heightened dependency on caregivers. This dependency places considerable physical, emotional, and psychological strain on caregivers, who are integral to the patient’s rehabilitation journey. In developing countries like India—and particularly in underserved regions such as Odisha—these caregiver challenges are frequently overlooked due to systemic limitations, cultural expectations, and scarce resources. A review of existing literature highlights a significant research gap in understanding caregiver experiences within this context. The findings of this study are expected to inform the development of targeted, multimodal support strategies to enhance the wellbeing of both stroke survivors and their caregivers.

Study Novelty

This study uniquely highlights the often-overlooked burden faced by caregivers of hemiparetic stroke patients in a tertiary care setting in Bhubaneswar, Odisha. By correlating the degree of patient dependency with the multifaceted healthcare challenges experienced by caregivers, it brings fresh regional insight into an under-researched area. The findings aim to inform the development of targeted support systems and policy interventions to enhance caregiver wellbeing and patient care outcomes in similar low-resource settings.

Materials and Methods

Study design and Setting

The present study has adapted a correlational study design to establish the correlation between degree of dependency and caregiver’s challenges among caregivers of stroke survivors. The study was conducted at the Neurology ward of IMS and SUM Hospital, Bhubaneswar, Odisha, India, between 01.07.2020 and 31.08.2021. IMS and SUM Hospital is a 3000-bedded tertiary care center covering the population in the eastern part of Odisha. The bed strength of the neurology ward is 50 and is mostly occupied. The study was approved by the Institutional review board of SUM Nursing College, Bhubaneswar, and institutional permission was taken from the medical superintendent of the hospital.

Study participants and sampling

The study population comprised all the hemiparesis patients admitted to the neurology ward of IMS and SUM Hospital, Bhubaneswar. All the participants were recruited using the total enumerative sampling technique for the present study.

The study has included all the hemiparesis patients admitted to the Neurology ward of IMS and SUM Hospital, Bhubaneswar, aged above 25 years, with the presence of at least one caregiver for minimum 6 months period consistently and with consent to participate in the study. Further, the cases of hemiparesis patients, those who were admitted to ICU, those with Covid-positive status, those on sedation, those who were unconscious, and presence of any disability previously were excluded from the study.

Sample size calculation

A sample of 92 satisfying the inclusion criteria were taken as calculated through by assuming 36% of proportion of patients with hemiparesis according to a similar study by Behera et al.[15] For the calculation, the absolute perception was taken as 10% at 95% level of confidence. The formula is below.

N = Z2 P(1− P)/d2

where n = sample size Z = Z statistic for a level of confidence level = 1.960 P = expected prevalence = 0.36 d = precision = 0.1. The required sample size as per the abovementioned calculation was 80. To account for a nonparticipation rate of about 12%, another 12 subjects added to the sample size. Hence, the final required sample size was 92.

Data collection tool and technique

The study has used total three tools for data collection. First, structured sociodemographic performa for hemiparesis patients (age and gender) and their caregivers (age, gender, marital status, family relationship, whether living in the same house with the client). The second tool is Functional Independence Measure (FIM) for assessing level of dependency.[16] FIM is a seven-point rating scale with 15 items. The scores were: Independent (7 = complete independent, 6 = modified independent), Partial Dependent (5 = Supervision, 4 = minimal assistance, 3 = moderate assistance), and Complete Dependent (2 = maximal assistance, 1 = total assistance or not testable). The reliability of the tool is 0.95. Third, a structured five-point rating scale for assessing health care challenges among caregivers. This has 12 items, and scores are mild (0–12), moderate (13–38), and severe (39–48). The reliability of the tool is 0.77. Data were collected through interview.

Data were collected between 01.07.2020 and 31.08.2021. Permission of study setting was taken from the Medical Superintendent and HOD of Neurology department of IMS and SUM Hospital, Bhubaneswar. Ethical permission was taken from the Institutional Review Board of Sum Nursing College. The written informed consent taken from each caregiver of hemiparesis patients who were selected as the sample after thorough explanation of the study purpose. Participants’ confidentiality was assured. Data were collected through interview schedule. Before data collection, the samples were identified through electronic medical records with permission. All the stroke survivors were examined for the presence of hemiparesis admitted to the neurology ward for >6 months with a constant caregiver. After that, samples were taken from the list in advancing order until a sample of 92 is met.

The study faced several challenges, primarily due to the overlap with the COVID-19 pandemic. Access to participants and caregivers was restricted by hospital protocols and safety concerns, leading to delays in data collection and reduced sample availability. Communication barriers, logistical constraints, and emotional sensitivity of participants further complicated the process. Additionally, institutional delays and the need to follow strict health guidelines extended the survey period to 1 year. Despite these obstacles, the study was successfully completed with the support of hospital staff and patient families.

Statistical analysis method

After the data collection, data entry to the master sheet was done. Then IBM SPSS Statistics 20 Version software used for data cleaning, coding, and data analysis. The data were analyzed using both descriptive and inferential statistics. Frequency and percentage analysis was done for baseline characteristics of hemiparesis patients and caregivers. Karl Pearson’s correlation coefficient was used for establishing relationship between degree of dependency of hemiparesis patients and their caregiver’s challenges. Chi-square test for association between degree of dependency and caregiver’s challenge with selected sociodemographic variables.

Ethical consideration

The research study proposal, protocol, and tool details were submitted, presented, and approved by the Institutional Review Board (letter no.-SOADU/SNC/IRB235/2020), adhering to ethical guidelines and principles. The permission was taken from the medical superintendent of IMS and SUM Hospital, Bhubaneswar. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, outlining the study’s purpose, procedures, potential risks, and benefits. Participants were assured that they were free to withdraw from the study at any time, and it will not affect the course of treatment. The investigator ensured anonymity and confidentiality of samples.

The study was conducted under the institutional project ID SOADU/SNC/IRB235/2020, as approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee.

Results

The current study has recruited a total of 92 hemiparesis patients to investigate the level of dependency on caregivers and their healthcare challenges. Among them, the highest proportion of patients (48%) were aged over 65 years, and the majority were male (68%). The baseline characteristics of caregivers revealed that most were over 50 years old (62%) and female (58%). A majority were married (64%), most lived in the same household with the patient (77%), and most of the caregivers were patients’ spouses (62%). Similarly, the data on challenges faced by caregivers indicate that 48% reported occasional difficulty in communicating with the patient, while 40% found it challenging to manage the patient’s bowel and bladder patterns. A sense of burden was reported by 55% of caregivers at times, with 37% feeling increased worry, 42% experiencing symptoms of depression, and 51% reporting fatigue. Additionally, 50% of caregivers stated that they fairly often had trouble managing their own rest or sleep patterns, and 48% faced financial difficulties. Furthermore, 46% of caregivers occasionally encountered challenges in providing care due to a lack of knowledge, and an equal proportion felt a lack of support from family and friends. Difficulties in assisting patients with exercises were fairly often reported by 45% of caregivers, while 41% indicated that they were unable to concentrate on their personal responsibilities due to the demands of caregiving [Table 1].

Second, the level of dependency among hemiparesis patients was assessed and revealed that the majority (74%) were partially dependent on their caregivers. Additionally, 19% were completely dependent, while only a small proportion (7%) were independent. Similarly, an evaluation of caregiver challenges showed that most caregivers (60%) experienced a moderate level of difficulty, 33% faced severe challenges, and only a few (7%) reported mild challenges in providing care for hemiparesis patients [Figure 2].

Bar graphs showing the percentage of degree of dependency of hemiparesis patients (a) and caregiver’s challenges (b)

Further, to examine the relationship between the level of dependency in hemiparesis patients and the healthcare challenges faced by their caregivers, a correlation analysis was conducted. The results indicated a statistically significant positive correlation (r = 0.017, P < 0.001), suggesting that as the level of patient dependency increases, the burden on caregivers also rises. This finding highlights the critical need for targeted support and interventions for caregivers who provide comprehensive care to fully or partially dependent hemiparesis patients [Table 2]. Last, to determine the association between caregivers’ challenges and selected sociodemographic variables, a Chi-square analysis was performed. The results revealed no statistically significant association between the healthcare challenges faced by caregivers and variables such as age (P = 0.168), gender (P = 0.606), and marital status (P = 0.134). These findings suggest that the difficulties experienced by caregivers appear to be consistent across different demographic groups, indicating that such challenges are not significantly influenced by these particular characteristics [Table 3].

Discussion

The current study has adapted a correlational design to highlight the intense challenges experienced by the caregivers of hemiparesis patients due to their various degrees of dependency on them. The study revealed that the majority of the study participants were elderly (48%) and were male (68%). Our finding is similar to a study by Punna et al.[17] They have studied on clinical profiles of hemiplegic patients. The study showed that the majority of the samples were in the age group of 50–70 years and were male. Our study further reported that most of the patients were partially dependent (74%) on the caregivers impacting their quality of life, which is consistent with a finding by Khan et al.,[18] who reported stroke survivors were dependent on caregivers, P = 0.02, and due to that, caregivers exhibit depression (P = −0.02) and anxiety (P = −0.12), and these factors are responsible for impaired QOL.

Another similar study by Chakrabarty et al.[19] on caregivers’ burden among stroke survivors implied similar findings as of our study. They reported the mean age of the stroke patients as 65.7 ± 14.21 years, and most were male. The mean age of the caregivers was 44.8 (±23.7). The study further found 56.1% of caregivers experience burden while caring for stroke survivors. Hence, the study strongly suggested stroke rehabilitation which will work both ways in recovery of patients as well as alleviating caregivers burden. Further, a retrospective study by Maggio et al.[20] also established a relation between family burden and caregiver role in stroke rehabilitation; the finding is of interest to the current study. Authors highlighted a positive relationship between the patients’ level of dependance and the level of depression and anxiety the caregiver possesses. These data are similar to our study findings. Giving importance to caregivers’ health and their emotional health is the call for the hour. Focusing on that, a study has investigated the need of emotional help, physical support, and healthcare requirements among the caregivers of stroke survivors. A study by Aboobacker et al.[21] revealed low median scores for self-efficacy, functional ability, quality of life, and perceived dependence among stroke survivors. It further showed a negative correlation between the perceived caregivers’ dependence and quality of life of caregivers, which is different from our study finding which showed a positive correlation between the level of dependance of hemiparesis patients and caregivers’ challenges.

Further, our study has highlighted that 48% caregivers faced difficulty to communicate with their patients; sometimes, 40% caregivers reported that it is difficult to manage bowel and bladder pattern, 55% caregivers reported that sometimes they feel burden, and 37% caregivers feel more worried, depressed (42%), and fatigue (51%). 50% caregivers reported that managing their rest or sleeping pattern difficulties is fairly often, and 48% caregivers reported they faced financial difficulties. 46% caregivers sometimes faced problems in providing care due to lack of knowledge, and 46% caregivers sometimes feel lack of support from other family members and friends. 45% caregivers reported that they fairly often face difficulty in assisting patients’ exercise. 41% caregivers felt that they could not concentrate on own personal work due to patient work load, which coincides with a study by Devyani et al.,[22] which reported some of the health challenges stroke survivors face, which increases the burden among caregivers. The reported factors were need for long-term follow-up care, and medication adherence, distance from the health facility, and mode of transportation. Hence, the study advocates for a multidisciplinary, network-based stroke care model to enhance long-term outcomes. These findings show similar interest to our study. Further, a study by Sohkhlet et al.[23] investigated the caregivers’ stress as a challenge while caring for stroke patients. The study showed that the majority of caregivers (51.32%) were female, similar to our study. The average age of the caregiver was 42 years, and the minimum care duration is 6 months. The perceived burden score is low (mean – 19.61), but each burden score is significantly corelated. All these findings were quite similar to the current study.

In continuation to the above finding, a study by Kumar et al.[24] describing on caregivers’ burden of stroke survivors elicited that the major causes of stress among caregivers are long caring hours (P < 0.001), feeling anxious (P < 0.001), insomnia (P < 0.001), financial crisis (P < 0.001), at low age (P = 0.002), and being daughters-in-law (P = 0.039). So, the study concluded the more severe the stroke, the worse is the physical and psychological burden among the caregivers, revealing similarity of our paper. Gaur et al.[25] studied on adverse consequences of caregivers of stroke survivors, as they care for them. The study reported the majority of the stroke patients were male (57.6%), and the majority of caregivers were wives (28.8%). The study further showed a positive relation between the age of the patients and the burden on the caregiver, like our study finding (r = 0.373, P < 0.001). Here is another study by Wu et al.[26] on caring stress of caregivers of disabled hemiparesis patients. The study indicated the primary sources of stress for caregivers are literacy level of caregivers, economic status, level of the dependency of patients, and extent by which a caregiver is caring and decision-making (P < 0.05 or P < 0.01). This seems similar to our study finding.

Strengths, limitations, and recommendations

One of the key strengths of this study lies in its focused exploration of the correlation between the level of dependency among hemiparesis patients and the healthcare challenges faced by their caregivers, which is an area that is often under-researched, particularly in the Indian context. Conducted at a tertiary care hospital in Bhubaneswar, the study offers valuable insights specific to an urban healthcare setting in a developing region. The use of standardized tools for assessing patient dependency and caregiver burden enhances the reliability and validity of the findings. Moreover, the study’s emphasis on both physical and emotional dimensions of caregiving provides a comprehensive understanding of the multifaceted challenges caregivers face. By shedding light on these dynamics, the study contributes significantly to the growing body of literature on stroke recovery and caregiver support in resource-limited settings, offering potential pathways for policy interventions and improved clinical practices.

The presence of outliers such as concurrent treatments or undisclosed patient or caregiver conditions may have influenced the findings. Second, the relatively small sample size and the single-center design limit the generalizability of the results. However, similar studies addressing this issue have been conducted, suggesting that this may not significantly affect the overall relevance of the findings. Additionally, demographic characteristics of hemiparesis patients and caregivers, levels of patient dependency, and caregiver challenges may vary across different regions or healthcare settings, which should be considered when interpreting the results.

Future research should focus on longitudinal studies to track changes in caregiver burden over time and explore the effectiveness of nursing interventions aimed at reducing physical, emotional, and financial strain on both patients and caregivers. The competent researcher can also plan for comparative studies across regions, and investigations into the psychosocial and economic impacts of caregiving are recommended. Additionally, evaluating the role of healthcare systems, exploring technology-based support solutions, and conducting qualitative research can provide deeper insights into caregiver experiences and inform targeted support strategies.

Conclusion

The study concluded that the elderly population in Odisha is predominantly affected by hemiparesis following a stroke, with a higher prevalence among men. This condition often leads to profound immobility, leaving patients largely confined to bed and entirely dependent on caregivers for daily living activities. Such dependency imposes a significant physical, emotional, and financial burden on caregivers. In line with national and state-level health policy benchmarks that emphasize equitable access to healthcare and integrated support systems, this study underscores the urgent need for comprehensive caregiver support mechanisms. These should include structured respite care, targeted mental health services, financial assistance, and community-based rehabilitation programs. By bringing to light the often-overlooked plight of caregivers in Odisha, the findings advocate for their formal inclusion in stroke rehabilitation frameworks, thereby ensuring a more holistic and sustainable approach to long-term stroke care.

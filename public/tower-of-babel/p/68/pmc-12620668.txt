Integrative Machine Learning and Bayesian Analysis Reveals Atrial Fibrillation as a Key Predictor of Severe COVID‐19 Outcomes
Authors: Young Sook Ku, Go Woon Lee, Hyundeok Seo, Jin Yeon Gil, Kyung Hyun Min, Jun Hyeob Kim, Jun Hyuk Park, Woorim Kim, Kyung Eun Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis study aimed to identify predictors of critical outcomes, including mortality, in hospitalized COVID‐19 patients treated with remdesivir, using statistical, machine learning, and Bayesian methods. A retrospective multicenter cohort of 1628 patients hospitalized between January 2021 and August 2022 was analyzed. Clinical data were collected from electronic medical records. Multivariable logistic regression, machine learning models (LightGBM, Elastic Net) with SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP), and Bayesian logistic regression were applied. Among the cohort, 14.5% experienced critical outcomes or death. Advanced age (≥ 65 years; aOR 3.950), atrial fibrillation (aOR 4.087), and kidney disease (aOR 1.939) were identified as significant predictors. Machine learning models achieved moderate predictive performance (AUROC: LightGBM 0.705, Elastic Net 0.698), with SHAP highlighting atrial fibrillation and age as key contributors. Bayesian analysis confirmed a strong association between atrial fibrillation and adverse outcomes (adjusted OR 5.121). Atrial fibrillation emerged as a consistent and strong predictor, underscoring its relevance in clinical risk assessment.Keywords: atrial fibrillation, Bayesian analysis, COVID‐19, machine learning, risk factor

Introduction

Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID‐19), caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS‐CoV‐2), emerged in late 2019 and rapidly escalated into a global health crisis. By early 2025, over 760 million cases and approximately 6.9 million deaths had been reported globally, representing a case fatality rate (CFR) of approximately 0.9% [1]. Additionally, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) recently reported a hospitalization rate of 2.9 per 100,000 population, indicating ongoing healthcare burdens associated with severe COVID‐19 cases [2]. Despite approximately 13 billion vaccine doses having been administered worldwide, emerging SARS‐CoV‐2 variants continue to present significant challenges, including increased transmissibility, altered clinical severity, and reduced vaccine efficacy [3, 4]. Thus, continued research to identify key clinical predictors and risk factors associated with critical COVID‐19 outcomes remains crucial.

Early identification of individuals at high risk for critical COVID‐19, defined as individuals with acute respiratory failure, septic shock, or multiple organ dysfunction, is essential to facilitate timely interventions, optimized resource allocation, and reduced mortality. Prior studies identified age, male sex, chronic conditions (e.g., diabetes and cardiovascular diseases), obesity, elevated inflammatory markers (e.g., C‐reactive protein, D‐dimer), and immune‐related markers such as lymphopenia as predictors of critical outcomes [5, 6, 7]. However, variability across patient populations and SARS‐CoV‐2 variants highlights the ongoing need for continued investigation into population‐specific risk factors.

In South Korea, the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency specified remdesivir administration for COVID‐19 patients in 2021 for hospitalized patients confirmed by PCR testing with severe COVID‐19 characterized by pneumonia requiring oxygen therapy (room air SpO2 ≤ 94%, radiographic pneumonia evidence, and ongoing oxygen supplementation excluding invasive mechanical ventilation or ECMO). The standard regimen involved a 5‐day course (6 vials), starting with 200 mg on day one followed by 100 mg daily. The duration could be extended to a maximum of 10 days (11 vials) based on clinical judgment [8]. Subsequently, in January 2022, guidelines expanded remdesivir use to include mild to moderate COVID‐19 patients at high risk of progressing to severe disease, defined by age (≥ 60 years) or underlying conditions (e.g., chronic respiratory or cardiovascular disease, diabetes, obesity, immunocompromised status). For these patients, remdesivir was recommended as a 3‐day regimen (4 vials), administered within 7 days of symptom onset [9]. These guidelines reflect Korea's evolving strategy to optimize clinical outcomes through targeted therapeutic approaches based on severity and individual patient risk profiles.

Meanwhile, machine learning, an advanced analytical approach utilizing artificial intelligence, has increasingly been adopted in healthcare due to its capacity to handle complex data and provide more precise outcome predictions than traditional statistical methods [10, 11]. Machine learning models have shown significant predictive accuracy in forecasting critical COVID‐19 outcomes such as mortality, intensive care unit admissions, and disease progression [10, 11]. Thus, machine learning‐based predictive models can greatly enhance clinical decision‐making by proactively identifying patients at increased risk for critical disease progression.

Therefore, this study aimed to identify predictors associated with critical COVID‐19 outcomes, including mortality, among hospitalized patients receiving remdesivir. By integrating traditional statistical methods, advanced machine learning algorithms, and Bayesian analyses, this study intended to identify and validate significant predictors to aid clinicians in early risk identification and personalized clinical management. Additionally, the analysis aimed to explain key clinical variables significantly correlated with critical outcomes and explore potential causal relationships underlying severe disease progression.

Methods

Study Patients and Data Collection

This retrospective two‐center follow‐up study included hospitalized COVID‐19 patients who received remdesivir treatment at Bestian Hospital (Cheongju, Republic of Korea) from January 2021 to June 2022 and Chungbuk National University Hospital (Cheongju, Republic of Korea) from July 2020 to August 2022. Identical inclusion criteria were applied at both centers: all hospitalized COVID‐19 patients aged 18 years or older who received remdesivir treatment according to the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency guidelines. Specifically, patients received a minimum of a 3‐day remdesivir course (4 vials), with an initial dose of 200 mg on day one, followed by 100 mg daily. Patients with severe COVID‐19 characterized by pneumonia requiring oxygen therapy (room air SpO2 ≤ 94%, radiographic pneumonia evidence, and ongoing oxygen supplementation excluding invasive mechanical ventilation or ECMO) typically received a 5‐day regimen (6 vials), extendable to a maximum of 10 days (11 vials) based on clinical judgment [8]. From January 2022, the guidelines also included mild to moderate patients at high risk for severe progression, who received a standard 3‐day course initiated within 7 days of symptom onset [9]. Exclusion criteria included patients younger than 18 years, incomplete data, insufficient follow‐up, discontinuation of treatment, or lack of pre‐ and post‐treatment laboratory results such as complete blood count (CBC), liver function tests (LFT), inflammatory markers, and renal function tests.

Data were retrospectively collected by reviewing electronic medical records. Baseline characteristics were defined as demographic information, underlying comorbidities, and any prior or concomitant medications documented immediately before remdesivir initiation. Demographic variables included sex, age, and body mass index (BMI). Comorbidities comprised hypertension, diabetes mellitus, ischemic heart disease, atrial fibrillation, kidney disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), chronic pulmonary disease, cancer, neurological disorders (e.g., dementia, Parkinson's disease), and other relevant chronic illnesses. Medication history included various therapeutic classes such as antihypertensives (e.g., angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors/angiotensin receptor blockers [ACEIs/ARBs], calcium channel blockers [CCBs], beta blockers), antiplatelet agents, anticoagulants (e.g., direct oral anticoagulants [DOACs]), antilipidemic agents, antidiabetic medications, gastrointestinal agents (e.g., proton pump inhibitors/potassium‐competitive acid blockers [PPI/K‐CAB], histamine‐2 receptor antagonists [H2RAs]), psychiatric medications (e.g., benzodiazepine‐class drugs, selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors [SSRIs]), corticosteroids, and immunosuppressants. Vaccination records, prescription records, and co‐medication history were also reviewed. Vital signs and laboratory values, such as CBC, LFT, inflammatory markers, and renal function, were recorded pre‐ and post‐remdesivir treatment to assess patient status. Critical outcome was defined as meeting the critical classification according to the FDA criteria for COVID‐19 severity in adults, defined as individuals with acute respiratory failure, septic shock, or multiple organ dysfunction [12].

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of Bestian Hospital (approval number: 2022‐05‐001‐001) and Chungbuk National University Hospital (IRB File No. 2022‐08‐027). Statistical Analysis

Categorical variables were compared using the Chi‐square test or Fisher's exact test between patients with critical outcomes and those without. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was conducted to identify independent risk factors associated with critical outcomes. Variables demonstrating statistical significance (p < 0.05) in univariate analyses, along with clinically relevant confounding factors, were included in the multivariable logistic regression analysis to identify independent risk factors associated with critical outcomes. To mitigate multicollinearity, variables exhibiting a phi correlation coefficient greater than 0.4—indicative of moderate correlation as defined in previous literature—were carefully reviewed [13]; in cases where a medication and its associated disease were highly correlated, the disease variable was prioritized for inclusion. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test was conducted to evaluate the goodness‐of‐fit of the final model. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and statistical significance was defined as a p‐value < 0.05.

Machine Learning

Feature selection was performed using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) values derived from a LightGBM model [14]. LightGBM is a gradient boosting framework that grows trees leaf‐wise, resulting in flexible and unbalanced structures [15]. SHAP quantifies each feature's contribution using cooperative game theory and is particularly effective for interpreting tree‐based models like LightGBM [16]. To build a parsimonious model, the top 20 variables with the highest predictive importance based on their SHAP value rankings (listed in Table S1) a common practice in similar studies were selected [12, 17, 18]. For validation, SHAP‐selected features were compared with those identified by the Boruta algorithm. Analyses were conducted using the SHAP and BorutaPy libraries in Python.

Ten supervised machine learning algorithms were evaluated to predict binary critical outcomes using features independently selected by SHAP and Boruta: Logistic Regression, Elastic Net, Support Vector Machines (SVMs with linear and RBF kernels), Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, K‐Nearest Neighbors, and Naive Bayes. Prior to modeling, 92 missing values were imputed as 0, indicating the absence of the corresponding factors. Details regarding the count and percentage of missing values for each variable can be found in Table S2. To address class imbalance, internal weighting strategies were applied (e.g., class_weight = ‘balanced’, scale_pos_weight, or is_unbalance = True). All data splits were performed in a stratified manner to maintain the original class distribution. Model‐specific details on imbalance handling are available in the Supplemental File [19].

Models were trained using repeated stratified 5‐fold cross‐validation with 100 repetitions. Performance was evaluated using Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUROC) and Area Under the Precision‐Recall Curve (AUPRC), averaged across repetitions. Threshold‐based metrics were computed using the threshold that maximized the F1 score in each validation fold. Hyperparameters were optimized via grid search for conventional models (Logistic Regression, Elastic Net, Random Forest, SVM) and via Optuna for LightGBM. Details on tuning and the selected hyperparameters are available in Table S3.

To improve predictive performance and robustness, ensemble learning techniques were implemented [20]. A Soft Voting Classifier combined LightGBM, XGBoost, and Elastic Net through probability averaging. A Stacking ensemble was constructed using LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost, and Elastic Net as base models, with a logistic regression meta‐learner trained on out‐of‐fold predictions [21]. Additionally, a Weighted Voting Classifier assigned weights to each model based on individual AUROC scores. A Super Learner ensemble was also implemented, combining multiple optimized base learners through meta‐model stacking. All ensemble models were trained and evaluated using the same repeated cross‐validation scheme, and performance was reported with 95% confidence intervals (CI) [22]. All models were implemented using the scikit‐learn framework in Python.

Bayesian Logistic Regression

Bayesian logistic regression was conducted using the PyMC framework to assess associations between clinical variables and outcomes [23, 24]. Weakly informative Normal (0,10) priors were applied to the intercept and coefficients. Posterior inference was performed using the No‐U‐Turn Sampler, a Hamiltonian Monte Carlo method, with three chains of 3000 post‐warm‐up samples following 1500 tuning steps [25]. Convergence was assessed via Gelman–Rubin statistics (R̂ < 1.01) and adequate effective sample sizes. Posterior medians and 95% credible intervals were reported and exponentiated to yield adjusted odds ratios (ORs) [26].

Results

Among 1862 patients initially enrolled, 234 were excluded due to age under 18 years (n = 51), incomplete data (n = 172), or discontinuation of treatment (n = 11). Data from 1628 patients treated with remdesivir were included in the final analysis. Of these, 235 patients (14.5%) experienced critical outcomes or death (cases = 215), including 46 cases requiring continuous renal replacement therapy and 14 cases requiring extracorporeal membrane oxygenation. The mean duration between the last administration and death was 9.6 days (range: 0–101 days). Demographic and clinical characteristics are summarized in Table 1, presented as counts and column percentages (n, %). Patients aged 65 years or older accounted for 81.7% of critical outcomes or deaths. Body Mass Index (BMI), alcohol history, hypertension, diabetes, ischemic disease, atrial fibrillation, benign prostatic hyperplasia, kidney disease, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, pulmonary disease, benign prostatic hyperplasia treatment drugs and antiplatelet medication use were associated with critical outcomes or death (all p < 0.05). Furthermore, phi correlation coefficients revealed strong associations, notably between diabetes and antidiabetic drugs (φ = 0.734), dementia and dementia medications (φ = 0.555), hypertension and angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers (φ = 0.480), hypertension and calcium channel blockers (φ = 0.466), benign prostatic hyperplasia and benign prostatic hyperplasia treatment drugs (φ = 0.458), antiplatelet agents and antilipidemic agents (φ = 0.449), and angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers and calcium channel blockers (CCB) (φ = 0.441); due to the high correlation between benign prostatic hyperplasia and benign prostatic hyperplasia treatment drugs (φ = 0.458), only benign prostatic hyperplasia was included in the multivariable analysis to avoid multicollinearity.

The results from the univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses are presented in Table 2 as crude and adjusted OR, respectively, with their corresponding 95% CI. In multivariable logistic regression analysis (Table 2), age ≥ 65 years (OR 3.489; 95% CI, 2.385–5.198), atrial fibrillation (OR 3.483; 95% CI, 1.600–7.563), and kidney disease (OR 1.895; 95% CI, 1.072–3.248) were associated with critical outcomes or death. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test yielded χ 2 = 8.389 (df = 8, p = 0.396).

Discussion

In this study of 1628 patients receiving remdesivir, 14.5% experienced critical outcomes or death. Advanced age, atrial fibrillation, and kidney disease were consistently identified as significant risk factors through multivariable logistic regression, machine learning, and Bayesian analyses. Machine learning models trained on features selected by SHAP and Boruta demonstrated moderate predictive performance, with LightGBM and Elastic Net achieving the highest AUROC values. SHAP analysis specifically highlighted age ≥ 65 years and atrial fibrillation as the most influential predictors of critical outcomes or death, while DOAC use exhibited a negative association.

This study highlights an important methodological consideration regarding the incremental value of machine learning approaches beyond traditional multivariable logistic regression analyses. While standard multivariable logistic regression effectively identified clinically meaningful predictors of critical COVID‐19 outcomes, incorporating advanced machine learning techniques, specifically LightGBM combined with SHAP feature importance analysis, enabled the detection of more nuanced, non‐linear relationships and complex variable interactions that might otherwise remain obscured. Furthermore, Bayesian logistic regression provided rigorous estimation of uncertainty around the effects of these predictors, contributing to more reliable interpretations of clinical significance. Thus, although conventional statistical analyses and advanced modeling techniques yielded similar key predictors, the integrative use of machine learning and Bayesian methods in this study allowed for deeper insights, ultimately supporting more precise, personalized clinical decision‐making and risk stratification strategies.

Specifically, SHAP analysis highlighted age ≥ 65 years and atrial fibrillation as the most influential predictors of critical outcomes or death, while DOAC use exhibited a negative association. The integration of SHAP, a game‐theoretic method that quantifies individual feature contributions, provided interpretable insights into tree‐based predictive models [27]. SHAP is increasingly utilized in clinical decision‐support systems due to its capacity for delivering individualized predictions [28]. In this study, the SHAP‐based selection of 20 features was validated using the Boruta algorithm, which independently identified nine variables, all overlapping with SHAP‐selected features. This overlap underscores atrial fibrillation's strong and consistent predictive importance. The SHAP summary plot confirmed atrial fibrillation as having the strongest positive correlation with critical outcomes or death.

Furthermore, to place our findings within the context of previous research, it is important to note that several studies have also reported atrial fibrillation as a predictor of poor COVID‐19 outcomes. However, most of these investigations were performed in Western populations and were based on registry or claims data, which often lacked detailed clinical and temporal information. Many also focused exclusively on critically ill patients treated at single institutions, which limits the generalizability of their results. In contrast, our study used multi‐center electronic medical record (EMR) data from both tertiary and secondary hospitals, covering a wide range of disease severities and healthcare environments. This approach made it possible to include detailed laboratory results, comorbidities, and therapeutic data, allowing a more comprehensive evaluation of the independent prognostic role of atrial fibrillation. By using detailed EMR data collected from diverse clinical settings, this study adds new evidence that supports the reliability and broad applicability of atrial fibrillation as a prognostic factor for COVID‐19.

Additionally, Bayesian logistic regression analysis supported the association between atrial fibrillation and critical outcomes or death. Although associations for DOAC and H2RAs did not reach statistical significance, their posterior distributions suggested trends toward reduced risk. Despite previous studies questioning atrial fibrillation's role as a risk factor among remdesivir‐treated patients [29, 30], our consistent findings across multiple analytical approaches—including traditional regression, machine learning, and Bayesian methods—provide stronger evidence of this association. These advanced analytical techniques offer complementary insights beyond conventional analyses and may help explain potential causal links between atrial fibrillation and severe outcomes. Notably, although the overall predictive performance of machine learning models was moderate, their capacity to confirm and prioritize key predictors, such as atrial fibrillation, validated their clinical utility. The concordance between machine learning‐derived importance rankings and statistical significance in logistic regression reinforces the robustness of these risk factors. Moreover, SHAP analysis enabled intuitive interpretation of individual‐level risk contributions, which may enhance real‐world clinical decision‐making by offering both global and patient‐specific insights. This layered analytic approach demonstrates that even when machine learning does not substantially outperform traditional methods in prediction, it can still meaningfully contribute to feature validation, interpretation, and individualized risk assessment.

Moreover, COVID‐19 itself is known to enhance the thrombo‐inflammatory cascade, intensify coagulation, and potentially increase the risk of cardiac embolism in patients with atrial fibrillation [31]. Mechanistic studies confirm that SARS‐CoV‐2 triggers a markedly prothrombotic state characterized by pronounced endothelial activation and excessive activation of the coagulation cascade [32, 33]. Consistent with these findings, recent Korean COVID‐19 clinical guidelines recommend prophylactic‐dose heparin (unfractionated or low‐molecular‐weight) for critically ill COVID‐19 patients requiring intensive care, and therapeutic‐dose heparin for non‐critically ill patients without contraindications to anticoagulation, reflecting robust clinical evidence supporting an increased risk of thrombosis in COVID‐19 patients [34]. Thus, in patients with atrial fibrillation—already prone to clot formation within the fibrillating atria—the COVID‐19‐induced surge in inflammation and coagulation further elevates the risk of thrombus formation and subsequent cardioembolic events. Further research is needed to clarify the exact underlying mechanisms.

This study also revealed that advanced age is a significant risk factor for critical outcomes or death in COVID‐19 patients. Aging is accompanied by immunosenescence, characterized by a gradual decline in immune system functionality. Specifically, older adults exhibit reductions in naïve T‐cell populations, decreased B‐cell responsiveness, and impaired innate immune responses, collectively weakening their ability to combat novel infections [35]. In the context of COVID‐19, age‐related impairments in interferon signaling and T‐cell responses result in higher initial viral loads and delayed viral clearance, significantly contributing to disease severity among elderly patients [36]. For instance, a large multicenter cohort study of hospitalized COVID‐19 patients reported dysregulated immune responses in older patients, including delayed interferon responses, lower proportions of naïve T cells, and increased immune cell exhaustion, underscoring the critical role of impaired adaptive immunity in older adults [37]. Additionally, observational studies have demonstrated that advanced age independently associates with prolonged viral RNA shedding, likely due to weakened T‐cell responses [38]. Such delays in viral clearance allow prolonged viral replication, directly exacerbating disease severity and increasing mortality risk in elderly patients.

This study has several limitations. Its retrospective design may introduce selection bias and confounding effects. Despite the multicenter approach, variations in clinical practice and patient characteristics across institutions could affect data consistency. Additionally, the absence of detailed genetic, biomarker, and laboratory data limits a comprehensive exploration of underlying biological mechanisms influencing patient responses to remdesivir. Information on concomitant therapies was limited to medications administered during the remdesivir treatment period, which may not fully capture the broader therapeutic context or potential cumulative effects. In addition, despite our systematic approach to multivariable logistic regression involving univariate screening and backward elimination, the lack of formal multiple comparison correction in our multivariable analysis may have increased Type I error risk, necessitating cautious interpretation of our findings. Furthermore, an external validation was not performed in this study. All analyses were conducted using the available dataset with internal validation (cross‐validation) only, without reserving a separate portion of the data or using an independent cohort for validation. This limitation may restrict the generalizability of the predictive models, and future research should incorporate external datasets to confirm model performance across different patient populations and clinical settings.

Nevertheless, the comprehensive analytical approach of this study—integrating traditional statistical methods with advanced machine learning and Bayesian analyses—facilitated robust identification of risk factors. Additionally, the multicenter and EMR‐based design improved generalizability by including patients from both tertiary referral centers and secondary community hospitals, reflecting diverse clinical contexts and a wide range of disease severities. All patients received remdesivir according to standardized national guidelines, ensuring consistent treatment exposure. These strengths support the clinical relevance and applicability of the findings.

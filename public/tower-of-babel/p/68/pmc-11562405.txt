Introduction

Gorham's disease is characterized by excessive proliferation of blood vessels in the bones leading eventually to progressive destruction of the bone itself [2]. The clinical features of Gorham syndrome are varied and many, including pain episodes and pathological fractures, in addition to lymphatic malformations or thoracic involvement [3]. Yet, rare diseases like these can have diverse clinical pictures making it hard for physicians to diagnose and manage them.

The work has been reported in line with the SCARE criteria [4].

Case presentation

An 88-year-old female presented with progressive pain in her left arm, along with stiffness, and decreased ability to do work for sixteen years. Her condition continued to deteriorate despite having received two intra-articular Triamcinolone injections 14 and 13 years ago without any relief of symptoms. Her symptoms worsened progressively as she kept on attending regular physiotherapy sessions.

In addition, she developed an increase in the severity of bone pain and stiffness in bilateral shoulders with the upper left side being worse off compared to the right. As a result, this decline affected her activity of daily living. The physical assessment demonstrated tenderness and swelling around the right shoulder joints, reduced movements, and muscle wasting in the affected limb.

Her sensation was preserved, and her muscle strength around the shoulder joint was 4/5 (by neurological examination). She previously had a history of repetitive episodes of shoulder instability, along with pain, which aggravated her further functional limitations as reported by the patient. Blood parameter evaluation showed a decreased level of Vit D (5 ng/ml), otherwise all other parameters are within the normal range (serum calcium, TSH, RBS, CBC, LDH, RFT).

Imaging studies showed entire resorption and cortical disappearance of the humeral head, which are in line with a diagnosis of Gorham syndrome involving the shoulder girdle (Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4). A follow-up bone scan (Fig. 5) showed correspondingly increased tracer uptake in the involved bone, consistent with ongoing osteolysis.

Clinical course

Based on these typical findings and clinical course, the patient was diagnosed with Gorham syndrome of the proximal humerus, presenting with slow progressive resorption of bone tissues. The treatment plan aimed to control pain and improve her functional status. She was started on analgesics and bisphosphonate (Inj zoledronic acid) for symptom relief and delay of bone loss.

Follow-up and monitoring

The follow-up of the patient has been structured to occur every three months, allowing for a thorough assessment of her progress. During these evaluations, there has been a noticeable improvement in her functional abilities. Specifically, she has experienced a gradual increase in strength and a corresponding decrease in pain, which is encouraging for her overall well-being.

Despite these positive developments in her functional status, radiological assessments have not yet shown significant changes. Currently, only one month has passed since the last follow-up, and the second follow-up is anticipated soon.

Continuous monitoring remains crucial in this case. It will not only help in evaluating the effectiveness of the treatment plan but also allow for timely adjustments as needed.

Discussion

The case of Gorham syndrome presented here exemplifies the complexities and challenges associated with managing rare diseases, particularly in an elderly patient. Gorham syndrome, also known as vanishing bone disease, is characterized by progressive osteolysis due to the proliferation of lymphatic or vascular tissue within the bone, leading to significant bone loss and functional impairment [5,6]. The patient's long-standing symptoms, which were inadequately addressed by multiple intra-articular triamcinolone injections, highlight the limitations of conventional therapies in managing the underlying pathology of Gorham syndrome [7].

As the patient's condition deteriorated, it became increasingly clear that standard treatments were insufficient to halt the progression of the disease. This observation aligns with the literature, which indicates that traditional therapies often fail to address the fundamental defect in Gorham syndrome, necessitating a more tailored approach to management [8,9]. The bilateral shoulder symptoms in this case further illustrate the diverse clinical presentations of Gorham syndrome, underscoring the importance of comprehensive evaluations that consider atypical differential diagnoses [5,6].

The progressive loss of function experienced by the patient, coupled with the dramatic bone destruction evident on radiographic imaging, emphasizes the debilitating nature of Gorham syndrome [10]. The X-rays revealing marked resorption and thinning of the humeral head serve as a stark reminder of the urgency for effective treatment modalities that can provide symptomatic relief and prevent further deterioration [11,12]. In this context, the initiation of bisphosphonate therapy, specifically zoledronic acid, represents a promising strategy for managing Gorham syndrome. Bisphosphonates are known for their anti-osteoclastic effects and have shown efficacy in stabilizing bone loss in various osteolytic conditions [5,12,13].

The individualized management plan developed for this patient aimed not only to alleviate pain but also to restore functional capacity. The successful control of her pain through analgesics and bisphosphonate therapy underscores the necessity for personalized treatment strategies in rare diseases like Gorham syndrome, where standard protocols may not apply [14,15]. This case reinforces the need for a holistic approach that prioritizes symptom management, functional rehabilitation, and ongoing monitoring to optimize patient outcomes and enhance the quality of life [8].

Moreover, the literature supports the notion that early identification and accurate diagnosis of Gorham syndrome are crucial for effective management [16,17]. Despite the challenges posed by the patient's advanced age and the complexity of her condition, her resilience serves as a testament to the human spirit's capacity to endure and adapt in the face of adversity [5,6]. The clinical course of this case highlights the critical need for continued research and development of targeted therapies for Gorham syndrome, as well as the importance of collaborative care models that involve multidisciplinary teams to address the multifaceted needs of patients with rare diseases [12,18].

This case of Gorham syndrome affecting the proximal humerus not only contributes to the existing body of literature but also emphasizes the importance of individualized care and the need for innovative treatment approaches in managing rare and complex conditions. The insights gained from this case may inform future clinical practice and research efforts aimed at improving outcomes for patients suffering from Gorham syndrome and similar osteolytic disorders.

Conclusion

The clinical course of the patient's journey with Gorham syndrome involving the proximal humerus emphasizes the significance of early recognition, accurate diagnosis, and tailored management. Despite the complexities associated with the patient's advanced age and the multifaceted nature of her condition, her resilience serves as a powerful testament to the human spirit's capacity to endure and adapt in the face of adversity. This case illustrates the need for a holistic and patient-centered approach that prioritizes symptom management, functional rehabilitation, and regular monitoring to optimize outcomes and enhance the overall quality of life for individuals affected by this rare disease.

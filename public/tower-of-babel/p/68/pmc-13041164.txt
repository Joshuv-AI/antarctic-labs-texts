Characterization of chloroplast genome and phylogenetic analysis of a geographic provenance of Glyptostrobus pensilis
Authors: Bo Lu, Wenjie Fan, Yanwei Zhou, Xinran Chong, Ting Zhou, Chuanyong Wang, Fan Zhang, Hong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGlyptostrobus pensilis (Staunton ex D. Don) K. Koch, 1873, an endangered plant, belonging to the genus Glyptostrobus of Taxodiaceae family. In this study, we report the complete chloroplast (cp.) genome sequence of a geographic provenance of G. pensilis from the Yiyang area of Jiangxi province (JX, 117°13′E, 28°30′N). Results indicated that the cp. genome of JX provenance was 132,255 bp in length with a GC content of 35.3% and contained 119 genes (including 83 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 32 tRNA genes and four rRNA genes). Among them, 15 genes contained at least one intron, and ten cis-spliced genes and one trans-spliced gene were identified. Phylogenetic analyses based on 77 common PCGs showed that the JX provenance is most closely related to the provenance from Fuzhou area of Fujian province (119°17′E, 26°01′N).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13104-026-07709-6.Keywords: Glyptostrobus pensilis, Geographic provenance, Complete chloroplast genome, Phylogenetic analysis

Introduction

Glyptostrobus pensilis is the only species of the genus Glyptostrobus in the family Taxodiaceae [1]. G. pensilis is generally considered to be a typical tertiary relict species, and due to habitat changes is now mainly distributes in southeastern China, central Viet Nam, and eastern Lao People’s Democratic Republic [2]. This ancient species usually grows in deltas, near or in water, and has important uses in improving wetland ecosystems. Disturbance by human activities (e.g., agriculture) over the years has led to further declines in its natural habitat [3]. For instance, G. pensilis was a common species in the Pearl River Delta until the 1950s, but now there are only 14 individuals remaining in eight populations in Guangzhou [4]. Except for a few localities such as Youxi in Fujian, Shanglou in Pingnan County, and Yiyang in Jiangxi, it is difficult to detect natural forests of G. pensilis, which are mostly sporadically distributed [5]. The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) Red List of Threatened Species classified the penstemon fern as critically endangered under the C-level criteria, i.e., the most threatened category, in 2011 [6], and it is a national class I protected species in China [7].

Due to the low natural turnover of G. pensilis, artificial propagation has become a conservation priority [8]. The local provenance from Yiyang, Jiangxi (JX), is particularly valuable due to its high cutting survival rate. However, its morphological similarity to other provenances [9] hinders reliable identification. While the chloroplast genome is a well-established molecular marker for phylogenetic studies [10], applying it to precise conservation actions requires resolving genetic variation at the finer provenance level. To address this, we sequenced the complete chloroplast genome of the JX provenance. By comparing it with the existing genome from the Fujian (FJ) provenance, we aimed to identify provenance-specific variants and evaluate their potential as molecular markers for germplasm authentication and conservation management.

Materials and methods

The provenance of G. pensilis was collected from the Yiyang area of Jiangxi province (117°13′E, 28°30′N), and planted in Nanjing Botanical Garden, Mem. Sun Yat-sen (118°49′E, 32°3′N), Nanjing, China (Fig. 1). The voucher specimen (NO. NBGJIB-Glyptostrobus-0001) was deposited in the Institute of Botany, Jiangsu Province and Chinese Academy of Science. Fresh leaves were harvested from two-year-old cuttings. A specimen was deposited at the Institute of Botany, Jiangsu Province and Chinese Academy of Science (https://www.cvh.ac.cn, contact person, Hong Chen, chenhong@jib.ac.cn) under the voucher number NBGJIB-Glyptostrobus-0001. The DNA library was constructed using the Illumina TruSeq™ Nano DNA Sample Prep Kit (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA), followed by a 150-bp paired-end sequencing using the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA).

Two-year-old JX provenance seedlings. The leaf morphology of JX provenance is similar to that of Taxodium distichum (L.) Rich, with linear leaves that are flattened, thin, and alternate on annual branchlets. (photographed by Fan Zhang, in Nanjing Botanical Garden, Mem. Sun Yat-sen (118°49′55″E, 32°3′32″N), Nanjing, China)

De novo cp. genome assembly and annotation were performed using NOVOPlasty [8] and GeSeq [9], respectively. The accuracy of the genome annotation was validated, and gene structures were characterized using the Chloroplast Genome Viewer (CPGView) tool [10]. The annotated chloroplast genome of JX provenance was deposited in the GenBank database under the accession number OR264360.

To explore the phylogenomic relationship of the JX provenance, the entire cp. genomes of 11 species including a geographic provenance (GenBank ID: KU302768) from Fuzhou area of Fujian province (FJ, 119°17′E, 26°01′N) were downloaded from the NCBI database. Within the 12 cp. genomes, the common protein-coding genes (PCGs) were selected and used for multiple sequence comparison. MAFFT software was used for sequence alignment [11], and phylogenetic trees based on the maximum likelihood (ML) method were constructed using IQ-TREE2.2.0 [12]. The best-fit substitution model (TIM3 + F+R4) was selected automatically by ModelFinder according to the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC). Branch support was assessed with 1000 ultrafast bootstrap (UFBoot) replicates. Taking the annotation of G. pensilis as the reference, we compared the complete chloroplast genome of two geographic provenances (JX and FJ) using the mVISTA program.

Results

The entire cp. genome of the geographic provenance JX is 132,255 bp in length with a total GC content of 35.3%. A circular cp. genome of JX was assembled (Fig. 2) with an average sequencing depth of 700x (Figure S1). Similar to cp. genomes of other coniferous species which have been sequenced [13], no complete IRs structure was found in the cp. genome, as well as the LSC and SSC regions. Therefore, the typical quadripartite structure was not found in the JX provenance cp. genome, consistent with the results of the previous study [14].

Circle diagram of the JX provenance cp. genome. Genes drawn inside the circle are transcribed clockwise, and genes drawn outside the circle are transcribed counterclockwise (as indicated by grey arrows). Genes with different functions are represented by different colours. the amount of GC and AT are represented by the dark grey and light grey areas in the inner circle, respectively

A total of 119 genes were detected in the cp. genome of the JX provenance, including 83 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 32 tRNA and 4 rRNA (Fig. 2). Protein-coding genes were 74,457 bp in length, accounting for 56.3% of the cp. genome, while rRNA and tRNA genes were 2409 bp and 7592 bp in length, and accounted for 1.82% and 3.47% of the cp. genome, respectively. Fifteen genes contained introns, of which 14 (trnK-UUU, trnG-UCC, rps16, atpF, rpoC1, trnV-UAC, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhB, trnI-GAU, trnA-UGC, and ndhA) contained a single intron, and only one (rps12) contained two introns. Ten cis-spliced genes (petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhB, ndhA, pafI, rpoC1, atpF, and rps16) and one trans-spliced gene (rps12) were identified (Figure S2 and S3).

Since the genus Glyptostrobus contains only one species, G. pensilis as well as 11 conifer species were selected to analyse the phylogenetic position of the JX provenance. A phylogenetic tree constructed on the basis of 75 common protein-coding genes showed that, as expected, the JX provenance is closely related to the FJ provenance, with 100% bootstrap support (Fig. 3).

ML phylogenetic tree of the JX provenance with 10 related species based on 75 PCGs. Numbers on the nodes are bootstrap values from 1000 replicates. Discussion and conclusions

In this study, we analyzed the complete chloroplast genome sequence and phylogenetic position of a G. pensilis provenance from Jiangxi Province. The cp. genome of the JX provenance is 132,255 bp in length, lacks a typical quadripartite structure due to the absence of complete IR regions, and contains 119 genes. Phylogenetic analysis confirmed its close relationship with the published FJ provenance (KU302768). Comparative genomics revealed that the primary differences between the JX and FJ provenances are located in specific intergenic regions (e.g., rps11 - rpl36, trnN-GUU - ndhF). Beyond adding another genome sequence, the key novel finding lies in identifying these provenance-specific variants. They offer new genetic insights and hold direct application potential as molecular markers for germplasm authentication and assessing genetic purity in the conservation of this endangered species. The fixed intergenic differences identified here provide a concrete foundation for future development of diagnostic molecular markers (e.g., based on SNP or indel polymorphisms). A comprehensive catalog of such polymorphisms and the design of specific assays would be a logical next step to translate these genomic findings into practical conservation tools. Furthermore, the expanding database of chloroplast genomes within the Taxodiaceae family presents an opportunity for future broader-scale phylogenetic studies to elucidate relationships at the genus or family level.

In conclusion, our findings provide a valuable genomic resource and candidate markers that can aid in the precise identification of G. pensilis provenances and support its future breeding and conservation strategies.

It should be noted that this study is based on a single individual from the Jiangxi provenance. Future investigations with larger sample sizes are needed to better evaluate the genetic diversity within G. pensilis and to further validate the genomic variations identified between different provenances.

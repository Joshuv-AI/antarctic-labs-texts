Echocardiographic evaluations of right ventricular functions and dimensions in children with bronchial asthma in Ile-Ife, Nigeria
Authors: Hammed Hassan Adetola, Bankole Peter Kuti, Tinuade Adetutu Ogunlesi, John Akintunde Okeniyi, Perpetua Okwuchi Obiajunwa, Abiodun John Kareem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAsthma is a chronic inflammatory airway disorder with cardiovascular effects. This study aimed to determine the relationship between right ventricular function and dimensions, and the severity of bronchial asthma.MethodsChildren aged 6–14 years with asthma were recruited for this comparative cross-sectional study. The severity of asthma and the level of symptom control were documented using GINA criteria. The lung function tests by spirometry, conventional echocardiography, and tissue Doppler echocardiography were performed.ResultsThe majority (71.2%) had mild intermittent asthma. The symptoms of asthma were well-controlled in 75.0% of participants. The mean right ventricle (RV) anterior wall thickness of children with asthma was significantly thicker than non-asthmatics [10.1 (1.3) vs 9.0 (1.3) mm; t = 5.709; p < 0.001]. The difference in the mean tricuspid ratio of the early to late diastolic velocities (tricuspid E’/A’) between the subjects [1.8 (0.4) mm] and control [2.0 (0.4) mm] was statistically significant [t = 1.980; p = 0.047] but the mean trans annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE) in subjects, 23.4 (3.9) and the control, 23.4 (2.8) were similar and not statistically significant (t = 0.009, p = 0.990). The mean RV wall thickness in persistent asthma [10.5 (1.4)] was thicker than in intermittent asthma [9.8 (0.9)] and was statistically significant (t = 2.750; p = 0.006). The TAPSE (F = 4.010; p = 0.022), RV isovolumetric relaxation time (F = 10.380; p = 0.005), and tricuspid E’/A’ (F = 9.500; p = 0.008) significantly increased as the level of asthma symptom control worsened.ConclusionChildren with worsening asthma symptoms present with right ventricular dysfunction, hence a need for echocardiographic monitoring in children with asthma.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-05718-8.Keywords: Asthma, Children, Nigeria, Right ventricular function

Background

Asthma is a chronic airway inflammation characterized by bronchial hyper-responsiveness, variable but reversible airway obstruction, airway inflammation, and eventual remodelling [1]. Asthma affects about 300 million people worldwide, with prevalence ranging from one to 18% [2]. Highest prevalence rates of asthma are found in Australia, the United Kingdom, and New Zealand, while the lowest are found in Eastern Europe, China, and India, with lower rates in rural communities in developing countries [3].

In Nigeria, asthma is second only to pulmonary tuberculosis as the most common cause of chronic respiratory disease in children [1]. Studies across Nigeria suggest a prevalence rate of between 5.1 and 18.6% [4, 5]. The prevalence is widely accepted to be about 5–10%, with a further 3–5% still undiagnosed [1].

Asthma is a primary lung disease; however, it has effects on the growth of children and on other organ of the body, especially the cardiovascular system [6, 7]. Children with asthma may develop pulmonary hypertension leading to right ventricular hypertrophy and enlargement [6, 8]. The right ventricular dysfunction in children is due to the progressive increase in pulmonary resistance which is contrary to what is observed in adults; as a result of high steady-state serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels [9]. In assessing the right ventricular systolic dysfunction, the Trans annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE) has been noted as a validated marker and it is an easily measurable parameter [10, 11]. However, an elevated tricuspid regurgitation velocity (TRV), TAPSE, and Tricuspid inflow (e/a) velocity (TV E/A) are surrogate markers for pulmonary hypertension which are vital indices of the right ventricular function [11, 12].

The pulmonary hypertension in asthma can be due to the recurrent hypoxaemia and hypercapnia and is associated with chronic inflammation with release of mediators and cytokines in asthmatic leading to pulmonary arterial vasoconstriction and hypertension which subsequently leads to hypertrophy and dilation of the right ventricle with changes in structure, function and impaired cardiac function known as cor-pulmonale [13, 14]. The diastolic dysfunction of the right ventricle is the earliest haemodynamic changes in asthmatic due to afterload increase in the ventricle which in turn can affect the left ventricle and the degree of dysfunction is dependent on RV hypertrophy and total pulmonary resistance [15].

The development of right ventricular dysfunction negatively affects cardiac function. They present with clinically apparent cardiac dysfunction, worsening the prognosis in children with asthma [6]. Patients with asthma have a greater risk of cardiovascular pathologies than non-asthmatic patients [16]. The association between asthma and pulmonary arterial wall rigidity, which is an independent predictor of cardiovascular events, is an important prognostic factor that is noted to increase with increasing severity of the asthma symptoms [17]. Many studies on cardiac involvement in childhood asthma have not related the degree of structural and functional ventricular involvement to the level of asthma symptom control. This is an important knowledge gap that this study hopes to fill. Similarly, the use of TAPSE and Tricuspid inflow (e/a) velocity (TVE/A) in assessing right ventricular function is rarely used, which creates a gap in determining the actual ventricular function in children with asthma as the routine use of the size and dimension of the chamber have lots of flaws. The use of TAPSE has better predicting effects on right ventricular systolic and diastolic dysfunction when compared with other methods such as M mode and myocardial contractility [18].

This study hopes to establish the association of asthma symptom control on the heart, particularly the right ventricular functions and dimensions. Therefore, this study aimed to determine the relationship between right ventricular function and dimensions and the severity of bronchial asthma, as well as the level of asthma symptom control. This will help to justify the need for routine cardiac evaluation of children with bronchial asthma to prompt earlier detection of cardiac disease, enhances risk stratification, and allows timely interventions. It will also help optimise the management and reduce the risk of ventricular dysfunction as a consequence of poorly managed bronchial asthma.

Methodology

Study design

This was a hospital-based, comparative, and cross-sectional study that assessed the right ventricular function and dimension among asthmatic children in Osun state, Nigeria. The control were apparently healthy non-asthmatic children.

Study location

The study was conducted at the Paediatric Cardiology Clinic, the Paediatric Pulmonology Clinic, and the Children’s Outpatient Clinic of the Obafemi Awolowo University Teaching Hospitals Complex (OAUTHC), Ile-Ife, Osun State, South-West of Nigeria. The children with bronchial asthma were recruited from the Pulmonology Clinics, while the controls were age and sex-matched non-asthmatic children from the Children’s Outpatient Clinics. Cardiac examinations such as the pulse rate, active precordium, location of the heart, heart sound and rate, and echocardiography was performed using the recommendations of the American Society of Echocardiography and the European Association of Echocardiography [19, 20]. Routine 2D transthoracic echocardiographic examination, Colour Doppler scanning, Conventional echocardiography, and Tissue Doppler echocardiography (TDE) studies were done on all participants in the Paediatric Cardiology Clinic.

Study population

Subjects were children aged six to 14 years with bronchial asthma in a stable state (children with no acute exacerbation of asthma requiring a hospital visit in the last four weeks) seen at the pulmonology clinic of OAUTHC, Ile-Ife, and the controls were sex and age-matched apparently healthy non-asthmatic children. The children with asthma had attended the clinic for a minimum of four weeks to correctly assess the level of symptom control as stipulated by the Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA). Children with congenital or acquired heart diseases (seen on echocardiography), acute exacerbation of asthma within four weeks preceding presentation, or other chronic pulmonary diseases like tuberculosis, lung abscess, and bronchiectasis were excluded.

Sample size determination

The sample size was calculated using the formula.

Using a previous study by Shedeed et al. [21] on right ventricular function in children with bronchial asthma: a tissue doppler echocardiographic study.

The sample size was approximated to 80, giving a minimum of 80 subjects and 80 controls.

Procedure

Subjects were recruited by stratified proportionate sampling (the patients were stratified based on age, and then proportionate sampling was done based on the number in each age, to spread across the study age limits, in an order that would be representative of all the ages) using the clinic’s register. The controls (children without asthma), apparently healthy age and sex-matched children without a diagnosis of asthma, were recruited consecutively from children who came for routine laboratory tests such as pre-school entry medical examination. The study proforma was used to obtain information from the participants. The information includes socio-demographic information (age, sex, parental educational background, and occupation). The control group was screened using the modified International Study of Asthma and Allergies in Childhood (ISAAC) questionnaire [22] which has been validated for use among children five years and above, was used to rule out asthma and other atopic conditions.

For children with asthma, questions were asked on history of asthma, its severity, and degree of control. These include frequency of day and nighttime symptoms, interference of symptoms with daily activities, and frequency of use of asthma reliever medications. Study participants and parents or caregivers were also asked questions regarding other atopic manifestations such as recurrent brownish itchy eyes, recurrent nasal discharges with exposure to common allergens (allergic rhinoconjunctivitis), nose and flexural skin lesions, which may be weepy, dry, scaly, and recurrent (atopic dermatitis). Questions to rule out acquired and structural heart diseases were asked, such as easy fatigability, recurrent blueness of the lips and extremities, palpitation and squatting. All the study participants then had a transthoracic echocardiography.

The subjects were classified using the National Asthma Education and Prevention Program Expert Panel Report-3 [23] as having intermittent, mild persistent, moderate persistent, or severe persistent asthma, which entails day and nighttime symptoms, the effect of the disease on the child’s activity, and the lung function test at the first clinic visit. In this study, the severity of asthma was classified into intermittent and persistent asthma (that is, combining mild and moderate persistent asthma), as none of the participants had severe persistent asthma. Patient’s level of asthma symptom control was classified using the Global Initiative for Asthma 2017 Guidelines into uncontrolled, poorly controlled, or well-controlled [24].

Physical examination, such as examination for cyanosis, finger clubbing, heart rate, and respiratory rate, was done. Blood pressure was measured using an EN-Professional blood pressure digital sphygmomanometer by Omiron Health Care Co., Ltd, United Kingdom (UK), and peripheral oxygen saturation (SPO2) was obtained using a portable pulse oximeter (Model 512™ Digital Oximeter by Novametrix Medical System Inc, UK). Peripheral oxygen saturation (SPO2) values less than 90% were considered to be hypoxaemia [25]. All participants had pulmonary function tests and transthoracic echocardiography.

Pulmonary function test

The Pulmonary Function Tests (PFT) were performed according to the European Respiratory Society and the American Thoracic Society (ERS/ ATS) recommendation using a standard spirometer, SpiroLab III spirometer™ (MIR Medical International Research srl, Italy) [26]. The procedure was well explained and demonstrated to the participants, and each participant was required to blow as fast and as long as possible via a disposable mouthpiece into the spirometer from full inspiration to full expiration. This was done in a sitting position with a nose clip in place. Each participant had a minimum of three and a maximum of eight recordings, recommended by the ERS/ ATS [26]. The best reading was used for the interpretation of the pulmonary function parameters. Forced Expiratory Volume in one second (FEV1), Forced Vital Capacity (FVC), the ratio (FEV1/ FVC), and Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR) were obtained, and these were recorded. The predicted values were based on the data of Knudson [27], which was used to determine bronchial asthma severity in the patient.

Echocardiography

Ventricular functions and dimensions were assessed using transthoracic echocardiography (Esaote™ My Lab30 Gold® cardiovascular echocardiography machine serial number 08538, by Esaote group, made in Italy, and phased array transducer probes with frequencies of 3 Hz and 7 Hz) while the participants were in supine and left lateral position. Echocardiography was performed using the recommendations of the American Society of Echocardiography and the European Association of Echocardiography [19, 20]. Routine 2D transthoracic echocardiographic examination, Colour Doppler scanning, Conventional echocardiography, and Tissue Doppler echocardiography (TDE) studies were done on all participants. To eliminate congenital heart disease, atrioventricular and semilunar valves were scanned with Colour Doppler and any regurgitant jet of more than trace severity was documented and such patients were excluded from the study.

The parameters assessed by the conventional echocardiography were divided into the ventricular geometry (Right ventricle free wall thickness; Interventricular wall thickness in diastole and systole; Right ventricular end-diastolic and end-systolic diameters; Right ventricular end-diastolic and systolic volume) to measure the dimensions of the ventricle; the ventricular functions determined by diastolic function (Early diastolic filling velocities peak (E) of the right ventricle; late diastolic filling velocities (A) of the right ventricle; ratio between the height of early and late diastolic filling velocity (E/A); and Deceleration time (DT) in the right ventricle) and systolic [Tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE); Right ventricle fractional area change (RV-FAC); and Right ventricular Ejection time (ET)] performance.

The ventricular geometry was mainly derived from the apical four-chamber view (AP4CV) and left parasternal long axis (PLAX) view on transthoracic echocardiography. The results were compared with standard values based on body surface area (BSA) and sex of the individual and healthy control. Values within 2 standard deviations of normal for the body surface area were considered normal.

The lateral tricuspid annulus parameters (Tricuspid annular peak velocity during early diastole (E’); Tricuspid annular peak velocity during late diastole (A’); Tricuspid annular peak velocity during systole (S’); The ratio of the early to the late tricuspid annular velocities (E’/ A’); Right Ventricle Isovolumetric Relaxation Time (IVRT); right Ventricle Lateral Isovolumetric Contraction Time (IVCT); Doppler-derived Myocardial performance index (MPI) were assessed using the Tissue Doppler Echocardiography (TDE) by placing the Pulsed Doppler via the Apical 4-chamber echo windows with the pulsed-wave (PW) Doppler at the lateral tricuspid annulus. Normal value of TAPSE was taken as 0.9–30 mm, while normal right ventricular diastolic dysfunction (TV E/A) was taken as 0.8 to less than 2.1 [11].

Data analyses

Data were entered and analysed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 27.0. Categorical variables such as sex, age group, socio-economic class, and ethnicity were presented using proportions and percentages. The differences in the means and standard deviation (SD) of right ventricular (RV) dimensions (RV anterior wall thickness, RV end diastolic diameter, RV end systolic diameter)/ right ventricular functions (TAPSE = Tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion; RVE = Right ventricular early diastolic filling velocities; RVA = Right ventricular late diastolic filling velocities; RV E/A = The ratio between the early and late diastolic velocities in the right ventricle among others) between children with asthma and controls using conventional and tissue doppler transthoracic echocardiography were compared using the Independent Student t-test. Similarly, the association in the mean (SD) of right ventricular dimension/right ventricular functions between intermittent asthma and persistent asthma was determined using the Independent Student t-test. The association in the means (SD) between the right ventricular dimensions/right ventricular functions and the level of asthma symptom control was compared using the Analysis of variance (ANOVA). The difference between proportions of categorical variables such as sex, age group, socio-economic class, and ethnicity between the children with asthma and the controls were compared using Pearson’s chi-squared (ꭓ2) test. Statistical significance was established when the probability value (p) is less than 0.05.

Discussion

This study demonstrated significant differences in right ventricular dimensional and functional values derived by conventional and tissue Doppler echocardiography in children with bronchial asthma compared to age and sex-matched non-asthmatic controls. In our study, the right ventricular wall thickness was within the normal range for the participants, although thicker among children with asthma than the non-asthmatic children. The relatively shorter duration of symptoms combined with the absence of clinical/ echocardiographic evidence for chronic lung disease may explain the reason for normal findings. However, the thicker right ventricular wall thickness among children with asthma might be due to repeated hypoxaemia and hypercapnia with associated inflammation leading to pulmonary arterial vasoconstriction which subsequently leads to hypertrophy and dilation of the right ventricle in them [13, 14].

These echocardiographic findings of the right ventricular dimensions in this study were similar to those reported by Ozkan et al. [7] in Turkey, Shedeed et al. [21] in Egypt, and Ghaderian et al. [28] in Iran. Conversely, a study by Sobhy et al. [29] in Egypt revealed a contrary echocardiographic structural parameter in children with asthma when checked for the calculated body surface area. The likely reason for the deranged structural echocardiographic parameter, as compared to this present study, is that the aforementioned study was done in middle-aged and older adults. The longer duration of symptoms, chronic inflammation, and hypoxia, as seen in older (adults) participants, the more likely the onset of pathological changes and clinical/ echocardiographic evidence of chronic lung disease may manifest on the ventricular dimensions.

Ozkan et al. [7], De-Paula et al. [30], and Abdalla et al. [31] reported no significant difference in the ventricular dimensions between asthmatics and non-asthmatic children. These might be due to the short duration of symptoms in children, combined with the absence of clinical/ echocardiographic evidence of chronic lung disease, which may also account for the lack of significant difference.

In the conventional echocardiography derived functional parameters of the right ventricle, there was significantly higher late diastolic filling velocity in the right ventricle of the asthmatics (RVA) than that of the controls. However, there were no significant differences between the other major parameters to assess diastolic function between the controls and the subjects, that is, right ventricular early diastolic velocity (RVE) and the ratio between the right ventricular early and late diastolic velocities (RV E/ A). The RVAs of both subjects and controls are within normal ranges despite the differences in values, indicating no appreciable right ventricular diastolic dysfunction. The fractional area change (RV FAC) was significantly higher among the controls than the subjects. The higher RV FAC in the control showed that the global right ventricular systolic function was better in the controls than in the subjects; however, both groups had normal values of RV FAC.

This finding of no demonstrable significant difference in the ventricular functions of the subjects and controls using the conventional echocardiography was similar to those reported by Shedeed et al. [21] in Egypt, Ghaderian et al. [28] in Iran, and De-Paula et al. [30] in Brazil. This could be due to reported reduced sensitivity of conventional echocardiography in imaging the heart’s ventricular functions; as a result, sub-clinical cardiac functional pathologies may not be readily detectable.

On the right ventricular function using TDE, findings suggest impaired diastolic function and some degree of systolic dysfunction in subjects compared to the controls in our study. These were demonstrated by significantly higher tricuspid TDE derived ratio of early and late diastolic velocities (E’/ A’) and TDE derived ejection time (ET) in controls than subjects, however, the right ventricle TDE derived isovolumetric contraction time (ICT), isovolumetric relaxation time (IRT), myocardial performance index (MPI) and the ratio of early ventricular filling velocity to TDE derived early ventricular filling velocity (E/ E’) were significantly higher in subjects than the controls. This suggests that the asthmatic children had impaired right ventricular diastolic and systolic functions when compared to the non-asthmatic controls.

The TDE findings demonstrated higher sensitivity of the Tissue Doppler echocardiography compared to the conventional echocardiography, as the TDE detected some sub-clinical differences when most of the conventional echocardiographic findings showed no significant differences. This finding was similar to that of earlier studies showing higher sensitivity of TDE [32].

The reported findings in our study showed some degree of sub-clinical diastolic and systolic dysfunction in the right ventricular functions using TDE despite normal conventional echocardiography parameters, which are similar to those reported by Ozkan et al., Shedeed et al., Sobhy et al., Abdalla et al., and Ozdemir et al. [7, 21, 29, 31, 33]. They all reported higher sensitivity of TDE and some degree of diastolic and /or systolic dysfunction in one or both ventricles. These cardiac changes may be a result of the recurrent hypoxaemia, hypercapnia, and inflammation of the airway and the lungs, leading to sustained pulmonary vasoconstriction and eventually, pulmonary hypertension, which then secondarily affected the right ventricle, causing right ventricular enlargement, hypertrophy, and impaired cardiac function. Various inflammatory mediators and cytokines are produced during early and late phases of asthma, including interleukins like IL-3 and IL-5, as well as tumour necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), which are all potent depressants of cardiac contractility [34]. Similarly, blood viscosity from polycythaemia secondary to chronic hypoxia can lead to increased right ventricular afterload and pulmonary hypertension.

The echocardiographic findings concerning the severity of the asthma symptoms in our study showed some dimensional and functional differences; there was a significant direct relationship between the right ventricular wall thickness, the end-diastolic and the end-systolic dimension of the right ventricle, and the severity of asthma symptoms. This demonstrated the effect of asthma and its severity on the right ventricle, which may be related to the recurrent hypoxia and other pathophysiological mechanisms, eventually leading to pulmonary vasoconstriction and having a direct effect on the right ventricle, leading to significant differences in variations with the severity of the illness [16, 34].

The conventional echocardiographic parameters used to assess the right ventricular functions showed no significant variation with the severity of the asthma symptoms; however, this was different for the TDE parameters. The tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion (TAPSE) of the right ventricle increases significantly with the severity of asthma. This finding demonstrates no significant echocardiographic and clinical diastolic dysfunction in the right ventricle using the conventional echocardiography, although echocardiographic findings were still sub-clinical using TDE. This demonstrated the higher sensitivity of TDE over conventional echocardiography, as it detects sub-clinical changes when compared to the latter.

The findings of no appreciable significant diastolic dysfunction with the severity of asthma symptoms might be because all the asthmatics in this study had either intermittent or mild to moderate persistent asthma, with none having a severe persistent form of asthma. This showed that most of the participants had mild to moderate forms of asthma symptoms, and this may account for the non-significant differences across the severity, although some echocardiographic parameters differ significantly using TDE. This is, however, different from that reported by Shedeed et al. [21] and Sobhy et al. [29] who reported significant diastolic dysfunction with increasing severity of the asthma symptoms, especially in the right ventricle. This is likely because the studies had more asthmatics with the moderate and severe forms of asthma, making the effect of the asthma severity fully manifest on the heart and thus detected by echocardiography as statistically significant differences in E’, A’, E’/ A’, IVRT, and the lateral and septal tricuspid S’. These might be due to the recurrent hypoxaemia and inflammatory mediators produced, which increase as the severity of the asthma leading to more pathological effects on pulmonary blood vessels and even the myocardium.

This present study revealed that three-quarters of the asthmatics had well-controlled asthma symptoms. Significant echocardiographic right ventricular dimensional changes were demonstrated as the level of asthma symptoms worsened. This is demonstrated by significant differences in the right ventricular anterior wall thickness and right ventricular end-systolic volume as the level of asthma symptom control worsens. This demonstrates the effect of poor asthma symptom control on the right ventricular wall, as it may have to do more work to overcome the resistance in the pulmonary vessels, leading to hypertrophy of the right ventricle and its dilatation.

The conventional echocardiographic findings demonstrated a significant degree of diastolic function in the right ventricle across the worsening degree of asthma symptom control, as shown by significantly increasing RV E/ A in this study. These minimal changes were, however, more obvious with the use of TDE. Using the TDE to determine the variation in the right ventricular functions across the level of severity of asthma symptoms control demonstrated diastolic and systolic dysfunction as the level of asthma symptoms control worsens. The diastolic dysfunction of the right ventricle was demonstrated by the significant difference in the TD Tricuspid E’, TD Tricuspid E’/ A’, and TD Tricuspid IRT, while the systolic dysfunction was demonstrated by a significant difference in TAPSE of the right ventricle. All these were pointers towards sub-clinical systolic and diastolic dysfunction across a worsening level of asthma symptoms control, detected mainly by tissue Doppler echocardiography, unlike conventional echocardiography.

The systolic and diastolic dysfunction seen across the worsening degree of asthma symptoms control among the subjects may be a result of the various direct and indirect mechanisms by which asthma affects the heart. These mechanisms include the risk of developing pulmonary hypertension due to the recurrent hypoxaemia and hypercapnia in asthma associated with chronic inflammation with release of mediators and cytokines in asthmatics, leading to pulmonary arterial vasoconstriction and hypertension, and in turn leads to hypertrophy and dilation of the right ventricle, leading to changes in structure and function and impaired cardiac function. The inflammation in asthma and the mediators directly lead to narrowing of the bronchial smooth muscle as well as the pulmonary muscle, especially smaller pulmonary arteries, leading to pulmonary vasoconstriction, muscularization, and remodelling, resulting in distortion of pulmonary vessels by parenchymal changes, reducing their expansibility, thereby increasing pulmonary resistance.

Children with asthma have exaggerated respiratory efforts, which may lead to increased intrathoracic pressure and subsequently increased right-ventricular afterload. This can lead to pulmonary hypertension, right ventricular hypertrophy, and/ or dilation from chronic pressure overload on the right ventricle, eventually causing systemic and diastolic dysfunction.

The limitation of this study is its cross-sectional nature, which limits its ability to detect long-term or evolving cardiac dysfunctions in children with asthma.

In conclusion, there were notably systolic and diastolic ventricular dysfunctions as the level of asthma symptom control worsened. Similarly, children with asthma had sub-clinical diastolic and systolic dysfunction, and the Doppler echocardiography showed higher sensitivity in detecting sub-clinical dysfunction than conventional echocardiography. Interval echocardiography could be incorporated into the management of children with bronchial asthma, especially for those with poor symptom control and persistent asthma.

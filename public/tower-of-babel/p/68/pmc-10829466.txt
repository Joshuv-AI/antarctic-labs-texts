Living legend: Dr. Bijaya Kumar Das
Authors: Jay G. Ray
Published: 2023

Dr. Bijaya Kumar Das was born on 9 March 1954 into a very humble family in Orissa. After his initial schooling, he came to Calcutta to pursue his higher education in the mid-70s. He fought against all odds ever since at that time in West Bengal, the overall socio-political situation was passing through a major turmoil. In spite of the language barrier, he got absolutely adapted with the social and linguistic aspect of Bengal. He pursued Bachelor of Dental Surgery course from Dr. R Ahmed Dental College and Hospital and finally graduated and worked in the same institution for some time, following which he joined the Orissa government job, and thereafter, he went on study leave to pursue his post-graduation in oral pathology at CODS, KMC, Mangalore. In 1991, when I joined the course in the same institution, he was in the exam going batch and I clearly remembered his hard work and perseverance within limited resources. He was a great inspiration for me, and being from the same undergraduate college, we shared some of our very personal and affectionate moments. Many times, Dr. Das used to say why he was so penchant for Oral Pathology. He believed that the “clinical profiles of a disease may be found to be falsifiable and therefore remain unsupported and anonymous with that of its unannounced arcane hideouts befuddling the diagnosis”. I clearly remembered one such incident: When I came across him in his room, he told that he can study for long hours in a day uninterruptedly and preferred having just one meal a day for his sustenance. I saw dip in stacks and piles of literature on many relevant topics in Oral Pathology and Microbiology.

During his post-graduate study, he worked sincerely on DMBA carcinogenesis in animal models and inspired his fellow Post Graduates and juniors to work on pre-malignant and malignant disorders. His perspicaciousness of the subject was intent, which was many times evident in the departmental scientific discussions. Such fundamental clarity laced with logical explanations was refulgent, which was unbiasly liked by our revered teacher Prof Sastry. Dr Das transformed so much by the cognitive wisdom of Dr Sastry and said proudly that he was a student of such an exceptional academician and scholar.

On successful completion of his post-graduate course, he went back to Orissa and joined the Dental wing of SCB Medical College, Cuttack. His passion for the subject facilitated the department to grow by leaps and bounds. In 2010, SCB Dental College started the post-graduate course under the altruistic and visionary leadership of Dr. B. K. Das, shaping the subject of Oral Pathology in the emerging perspective. During his 29 years of teaching experience at SCB Dental College, he exhibited his versatility by holding various posts like the member of the state advisory committee on biomedical waste management, Govt. of Orissa. He has conducted a number of training programs on biomedical waste management among medical graduates, nursing graduates, and veterinary services. He received training from national AIDS control organization and was the Nodal officer of AIDS for sensitization and awareness programs for SCB Medical College and Hospital.

He has contributed a chapter on oral complication of HIV in the text book on clinical management of HIV and AIDS, which is under printing now. He was a convener for nearly 10 years of staff academy society, the highest academic body of the Medical College, and conducted monthly clinical meetings of various departments of SCB Medical College and Hospital, Cuttack.

Dr. Das has conducted under his able and dynamic leadership the 17th IAOMP conference at Bhubaneswar a few years back, which was one of the most well-organized conferences in the recent past. He was also the past president of our association and has always taken active participation in all the conferences.

He has several scientific publications in national journals. From the above narratives, I may reiterate that Dr. Das is an oral pathologist not by chance but by intensive choice and has always tried to correlate clinical dentistry and pathology.

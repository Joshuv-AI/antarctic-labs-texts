Value of intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics based on DCE-MRI in predicting HER2-low and HER2-zero in breast cancer
Authors: Min Sun, Yuwei Wang, Ruoming Li, Weining Zhao, Haiqing Yang, Lixia Zhou, Duo Gao, Shuai Quan, Zuojun Geng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe aim of this study was to develop and validate intratumoral and peritumoral radiomic models based on DCE-MRI to predict the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) low and zero states of breast cancer(BCa).MethodsThe clinical data of 168 patients with BCa were retrospectively analysed and divided into a training set and a validation set. The intratumoral and peritumoral radiomic features were extracted from DCE-MRI. Variance analysis, univariate logistic analysis, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) were used as selection operators for dimension reduction. Feature selection and radiomics model construction were performed using max-relevance and min-redundancy (mRMR) and the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) on the training cohort. Logistic regression (LR) was used as a classifier to construct the intratumoral, peritumoral and intratumoral combined peritumoral radiomic models of DCE-MRI. The optimal model was selected to construct the fusion model combined with the screened clinical independent risk factors, and the model was displayed as a nomogram. The performance of each model was evaluated via the area under the curve (AUC) value of the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve.ResultsIn the end, a total of 12 features were retained, and the AUC values of the DCE-MRI intratumoral + peritumoral model in the training set and validation set were 0.845 and 0.836, respectively. Age was identified as an independent risk factor for predicting HER2 status in BCa through Univariate and multivariate analysis in the training set.The AUC values of the final constructed nomogram were 0.862 and 0.844 in the training set and validation set, respectively.ConclusionIntratumoral and peritumoral radiomic methods based on DCE-MRI have good value in the identification of HER2-low and HER-zero BCa.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-026-02304-4.

Introduction

According to the latest global cancer data report, breast cancer(BCa) was diagnosed in approximately 2.3 million people in 2022, making it the second most common cancer in the world after lung cancer [1]. Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is a biological marker of BCa, and its status plays an important role in the diagnosis and treatment of this disease [2]. In the past, BCa was divided into HER2-positive and HER2-negative BCa according to the different expression states of HER2 [3]. The anti-HER2 antibody trastuzumab and other drugs provide targeted treatment of HER2-positive BCa [4]. In a recently proposed classification system, HER2-negative patients were subdivided into HER2-low and HER2-zero [5]. An increasing number of studies have shown that the biological characteristics and clinical characteristics of patients with HER2-low BCa are different from those of patients with truly HER2-negative BCa [6–8]. A study revealed that the novel antibody‒drug conjugate fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki, which is used for the treatment of advanced BCa patients with HER2-low expression, improved progression-free survival in these patients [9].

At present, the detection of HER2 status is based mainly on puncture biopsy or postoperative immunohistochemical (IHC) detection. However, in addition to its invasive nature, needle biopsy samples sometimes fail to represent the nature of the tumour as a whole [10, 11]. The timely and accurate detection of HER2 status plays an important role in the treatment and prognosis of BCa patients. Radiomics can noninvasively extract quantitative radiomic features that cannot be observed by the human eye and obtain more information [12, 13]. At present, studies on HER2-low BCa patients based on magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) have focused mainly on the intratumour region, whereas relatively few studies have focused on HER2-low BCa patients via peritumour MRI. The purpose of this study was to explore the value of intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics based on DCE-MRI in the identification of HER2-low and HER2-zero BCa patients, with the goal of providing a new, noninvasive method for the clinical differentiation of HER-low and HER-zero BCa patients.

Materials and methods

Study population

Patients with pathologically proven BCa at Cangzhou Central Hospital from January 2019 to October 2023 were retrospectively analysed, and the clinical and imaging data of the patients were collected. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) complete MRI data; (b) IHC and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) results, if necessary; (c) complete clinical and pathological data on confirmed BCa; (d) biopsy and chemoradiotherapy were not performed. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) nonmass lesions; (b) poor image quality and lesion imaging size < 1 cm, making delineation of the tumour margin difficult; (c) previous history of other malignancies (Fig. 1). Finally, 168 patients were enrolled, including 104 patients with the HER2-low type and 64 patients with the HER2-zero type. The cases were randomly divided into a training set (n = 117) and a validation set (n = 51) at a ratio of 7:3. This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Cangzhou Central Hospital, exempting subjects from informed consent (approval number: 2023-279-01).

Instruments and methods

The images of all patients were obtained with a GE Signa Discovery MR750W 3.0T MR scanner (GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, WI, USA) in the prone position with a special breast coil (8 channels). The DCE-MRI sequence scanning parameters were as follows: TR, 7.3 ms; TE, 1.2 ms; layer thickness, 2 mm; no spacing; FOV, 360 mm×360 mm; and matrix, 288 × 288. Before the contrast agent was injected, the mask was scanned, starting from the second phase, and then scanned for 7 consecutive periods, each period of 60 s, for a total time of 480 s. The contrast agent used was gadodiamide (10 mL: 2.87 g General Electric Pharmaceutical (Shanghai) Co., Ltd.), and the contrast agent was injected through an elbow vein and rinsed with 20–30 mL normal saline. The enrolled images were exported from the postprocessing workstation in DICOM format.

Pathological evaluation of HER-2 status

The HER2 status of all BCa patients was detected by IHC or FISH according to the guidelines of the American Society of Clinical Oncology/College of American Pathologists [3]. HER2-zero is defined as IHC 0. HER2-low is defined by the new classification system as either IHC 1 + or IHC 2 + without FISH amplification [5].

Evaluation of clinical and conventional imaging features

Data such as age, menopause status, maximum tumour diameter, axillary lymph node metastasis, and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) were collected. R software (version 4.3.0) was used to perform Univariate and multivariate analysis on these data in the training set to screen out the optimal features, which could be used as independent risk factors for identifying HER2-low and HER2-zero BCa. To avoid confusion with radiomic features, non-imaging MRI features such as tumor maximum diameter, presence or absence of axillary lymph node metastasis, and ADC values were included in the clinical baseline features.

Image segmentation and radiomics feature extraction

DCE-MRI images from 168 patients from the same institution were imported into the open source software ITK-SNAP (version 3.8.0). The region of interest (ROI) was mapped independently by two radiologists. Intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) analysis was used to assess the consistency of the ROI-extracted radiomic features between the two radiologists. An ICC > 0.75 indicates high consistency, and further study is needed. Considering the importance of tumour heterogeneity analysis and the need for automatic peritumour expansion, necrotic and cystic areas were included in the delineation. The volume of interest (VOI) was generated via layer-by-layer sketching, and 4 mm peritumour expansion was performed via PHIgo-AK software (Fig. 2).

Using the pyradiomics package on Python (version 3.7.0), high-throughput radiomics features were extracted from each patient’s VOI. The voxel spacing of all images was resampled to isotropic 1.0 mm × 1.0 mm × 1.0 mm. The gray values were discretized into 32 Gy levels using the Fixed Bin Width method. Before extracting the features, the original images in the training set were normalized using the Z-Score standard method to adjust the gray values of the original images to a normal distribution to improve the comparability between the data. Features with an ICC ＞ 0.75 were selected. 1316 radiomic features were extracted based on each patient’s images, including 42 histogram features, 18 morphological features, 196 texture features, and 1060 high-order features.

Feature screening and model building

In the training set, Pearson correlation coefficient, univariate analysis method, univariate logistic regression method, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression were used to select the optimal number of features for DCE-MRI within the intratumoral, peritumoral, and both intratumoral and peritumoral (Fig. 3 shows the feature screening process for DCE-MRI within the intratumoral and peritumoral). Logistic Regression (LR) was used as the classifier. Based on DCE-MRI, separate models for intratumoral, peritumoral, and both intratumoral and peritumoral (combined) were established. ROC curve analysis was used to calculate AUC, sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy, and the optimal model was selected. This model was combined with the independent risk factors selected in the final training set to construct a fusion model. To ensure the reproducibility of the results, random seeds were set during data partitioning, model training, and evaluation to fix the initial state of random number generation, thereby ensuring the consistency of each experimental result.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed via R software and SPSS 25.0 software, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The data from the training set were used to build the model, and the data from the validation set were used to validate and evaluate the model. For the clinical and imaging data of the patients, normally distributed data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (xˉ±s), and an independent sample T test was used. Counting data are represented by the number of cases (%), and the chi-square test was adopted. The fusion model is presented as a nomogram. Calibration curves were used to evaluate the consistency of the model, and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test was used to evaluate the goodness of fit of the nomogram. The Delong test was used to compare the AUC values of the models.

Results

Baseline characteristics of patients and screening for independent risk factors

A total of 168 patients were included in this study and were randomly grouped at a ratio of 7:3. As a result, 117 patients were included in the training set, including 72 HER2-low patients and 45 HER2-zero patients; 51 patients were included in the validation set, including 32 HER2-low patients and 19 HER2-zero patients. The clinical baseline data of the training set and the validation set were statistically analysed, and the results revealed that there was no statistically significant difference in the clinical data between the training set and the validation set (P > 0.05). There were significant differences in age and ADCmax between the HER2-low group and the HER2-zero group in the training set and validation set (P < 0.05). In addition, the ADCmean in the validation set was significantly different between the HER2-low group and the HER2-zero group (P < 0.05). The other clinical data were not significantly different (P > 0.05), as shown in Table 1.

In addition, we also established a combined model by integrating the maximum diameter of the tumor in the clinical data with the imaging biomarker model. The results showed that the AUC values in the training set and validation set were 0.838 and 0.829 respectively, which were slightly lower than those of the constructed imaging biomarker nomogram (0.862 and 0.844 in the training set and validation set respectively). This further indicates that age has a greater predictive value for the HER2 status of breast cancer than the maximum diameter of the tumor.

Radiomics feature screening and model construction

First, radiomic features extracted from the ROI were screened via the Pearson correlation coefficient, and radiomic features with correlation coefficients lower than 0.9 were selected. Finally, 653 features were retained. Then, univariate analysis was used to screen the features with P<0.05, and 178 features were retained. The univariate logistic regression method was subsequently used to screen the remaining 68 features. Finally, LASSO was used to determine the optimal value of the hyperparameter λ and it finally achieved feature screening, retaining 12 features (Table 3). The Radscore for each patient is calculated via a linear combination of selected features and their regression coefficients (Fig. 4 shows the Radscore distribution of the combined model.)

In this study, a total of three radiomic models were established on the basis of the features screened by DCE-MRI. The results revealed that the AUC values of the intratumour model and the peritumour model were 0.825 and 0.803, respectively, in the training set and 0.821 and 0.799, respectively, in the validation set of the DCE-MRI sequence. However, for both the training set and the validation set, the AUC values of the combined models were the highest, 0.845 and 0.836, respectively. (Table 4; Fig. 5), suggesting that the intratumoural and peritumour areas may contain complementary information. Moreover, according to the calculated AUC value, sensitivity, specificity and accuracy results, the performance of the combined model is the best. The DeLong test revealed that there was a significant difference between the combined model and the DCE-MRI intratumoral model in the training set (Z = 2.997, P = 0.003), and in the DCE-MRI peritumoral model (Z = 5.232, P<0.01). In the later stage of this study, the combined model was selected as the best radiomics model to further establish the fusion model.

The construction of the fusion model

Statistical analysis revealed that age was an independent risk factor for predicting the HER2 status of patients with BCa, which was combined with the Radscore of the combined model to construct a fusion model. The results revealed that the AUC of the training set was 0.862 and that of the validation set was 0.844 (Table 4; Fig. 5) and the combined model was presented in the form of a nomogram (Fig. 6). The calibration curve of this model is close to the ideal curve (Fig. 7). The Hosmer–Lemeshow test results of the fusion model were not significantly different between the training set (P = 0.157) and the validation set (P = 0.338), indicating that the model had good goodness of fit.

Discussion

In this study, on the basis of the radiomics labelling of DCE-MRI data from BCa patients, intratumoral, peritumoral and combined radiomic models were established, and a fusion model was established in combination with an independent risk factor, namely, age, to predict the HER2-low and HER2-zero status of BCa patients. The results revealed that the AUC values of all the models exceeded 0.8, and the AUC values of the fusion model were 0.862 and 0.844 in the training set and validation set, respectively, which were the highest among the constructed models, indicating good performance in predicting the HER2 status of patients with BCa. The construction of a radiomics nomogram provides a visualization tool in clinical practice.

Previously, HER2 status was tested on the basis of IHC and/or FISH tests. HER2 expression status with an IHC score of 3 + or an IHC score of 2 + and amplification of the HER2 gene detected by FISH is defined as HER2-positive. This type of BCa is highly invasive and is usually associated with the high risk of tumour recurrence [14]. Anti-Her2 monoclonal antibody drugs, such as trastuzumab and pertuzumab, and targeted drugs, such as tyrosine kinase inhibitors and Antibody-drug conjugates, are used in the targeted treatment of HER2-positive patients [15–17]. When the IHC score is 0 or 1+, or the IHC score is 2 + and there is no amplification of the HER2 gene by FISH detection, the patient is considered to be HER2-negative, and patients with this type of BCa cannot benefit from traditional targeted therapy [18]. However, an increasing number of studies have shown that tumours with HER2-low expression (IHC score of 1 + or 2+/FISH negative) have their own clinical and pathological characteristics [5]. A cohort study revealed that in hormone receptor-negative BCa, a HER2-low expression status was associated with better survival than a HER2-negative status [19].

Fan et al. introduced the concept of antibody‒drug conjugates for treating BCa from HER2-positive to HER2-low expression [20]. Although the application of traditional targeted drugs for treating patients with HER2-low expression is still controversial, the emergence of new targeted drugs provides a possibility for the treatment of such patients. A phase Ib clinical trial revealed that a new HER2-targeting drug, T-DXd, has promising antitumour activity in patients with HER2-low BCa, with an objective response rate of 37% [21]. In the global phase 3 DESTINY-Breast04 study have shown that anti-HER2 antibody drug conjugates play a certain role in the treatment of patients with HER2-low metastatic BCa who had received 1–2 prior lines of chemotherapy [22]. The expert consensus published by the European Society of Medical Oncology specifically analysed the definition, diagnosis and treatment of BCa patients with HER2-low expression, providing important guidance for the diagnosis and treatment of patients with HER2-low expression in the clinic [23].

Many previous studies have demonstrated the value of MRI-based radiomics in predicting HER2 status in patients with BCa. Initial research focused on distinguishing HER2-positive and HER2-negative BCa [24, 25]. With the recognition of HER2-low patients, research on radiomics for HER2-low patients has gradually developed. Liang et al. distinguished HER2-low and HER2-zero based on the radiomics of DCE-MRI and ultimately retained 16 radiomic features and adopted LR for modelling. In the training set combined with the clinically independent risk factor TIC classification and Radscore, the AUC of the radiomics nomogram was 0.892, and that of the validation set was 0.886 [26]. In addition, some studies used multiparameter MRI radiomics data to distinguish HER2-zero, HER2-low, and HER2-positive patients, all of which had a certain predictive value [27–29]. These studies demonstrate the value of radiomic features in identifying HER2-low patients.

However, these studies did not consider peritumoral conditions. Some studies have shown that some characteristics of the microenvironment around the tumour may be the driving force of cancer progression and metastasis and may also be related to angiogenesis, lymphatic and vascular peritumoral infiltration, and interstitial reactions [30–32]. Chun et al.‘s study revealed that the AUC value of the intratumoral + peritumoral radiomics model based on DCE-MRI in predicting HER2 in BCa patients was 0.808, which was greater than that of the single intratumoral or peritumoral model, reflecting the value of the peritumoral model in predicting the HER2 status of BCa patients, which was consistent with the results of this study. In this study, the peritumoral data were included, and the results revealed that the combined model had the best results, with an AUC of 0.862 for the training set and 0.844 for the validation set [33].

At present, there is no uniform consensus on the peritumoral range. The radiomics model constructed by Chun et al. was based on a 4 mm peritumoral range in and around the tumour and it had a certain value for predicting HER2 status in BCa patients [33]. Ding et al. conducted a study on BCa radiomics on the basis of DCE-MRI intratumour and peritumour data (2, 4, 6, and 8 mm), and the results showed that the combined peritumour model could always yield higher AUC values. Moreover, two different ROIs were selected in this study, and the results showed that 4 mm, 6 mm, and 8 mm produced higher prediction efficiency in the two ROIs [34]. Braman et al. defined peritumoral DCE-MRI sequences of BCa patients as regions ranging from 2.5 to 5 mm, and this scholar also studied five ring regions with a diameter of 3 mm around the tumour to study the intrinsic molecular subtypes of HER2-positive BCa and the effects of targeted therapy. All of these studies have shown that the peritumoral status has certain value [35, 36]. Some scholars have studied the HER2 status of BCa patients on the basis of multiparameter MRI intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics data, and the results have shown that the optimal peritumoral status of a single sequence is 4 mm [25]. In this study, a distance of 4 mm around the tumour was selected as the peritumoral range.

Study limitations

Although this study showed good predictive power in predicting the HER2 status of patients with BCa, many problems remain. First, this was a single-centre retrospective study, and multicentre datasets are needed for validation in the future. Second, this study employed a manual delineation of the intratumoral area, which is time-consuming and laborious and may be subjective to some extent. In recent years, automatic and semiautomatic delineation methods have gradually emerged, and these methods are expected to be included in future research. Third, relatively few clinical and imaging data were included in this study, and only age was statistically significant. More clinical and imaging data should be included in the future. Fourth, in this study, only a 4 mm area around the tumor was selected. In the future, it is necessary to explore the value of different tumor surrounding ranges.At present, precision medicine is widely accepted in people’s hearts, and with the development of artificial intelligence and big data, radiomics is expected to be increasingly applied in practice.

Conclusions

In this study, a fusion model established on the basis of intratumour and peritumour radiomics data from DCE-MRI of BCa patients combined with clinical and imaging characteristics had high accuracy in predicting HER2-low and HER2-zero status of BCa patients, which is helpful for providing guidance for the precision treatment of these patients.

Predicting Temporal Trends and Hospital-Level Variation in Calcium Modification Strategies Adoption in Percutaneous Coronary Intervention
Authors: Omar M. Abdelfattah, Omar Chaabo, Joe Aoun, Umamahesh Rangasetty, Alpesh R. Shah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe adoption patterns of novel cardiovascular interventional technologies significantly impact patient care and health care resource allocation. This study analyzes and projects the adoption trends of intravascular lithotripsy (IVL) compared to coronary atherectomy (CA).ObjectivesThe objective of the study was to investigate the predicted utilization trends of various calcium modification strategies by year 2035.MethodsLeveraging data from multiple U.S.-based registries (2009-2022), we developed Monte Carlo simulations (n = 100,000 simulated iterations) to model adoption trends and hospital-level variations. Logistic growth models were used to project technology adoption rates through 2035.ResultsIVL showed rapid adoption with a projected terminal rate of 32.41% by year 2035 (95% CI: 28.76% to 36.06%) and mean annual growth of 2.5% (95% CI: 2.1% to 2.9%). CA (rotational and orbital) adoption is projected to reach 4.5 to 5.5% by 2035 (10% to 90% CI: 3.8% to 6.2%). Hospital-level analysis revealed increasing variability in adoption patterns, with high-volume centers (>10% CA use) increasing from 8.6% (2020) to 29.6% (2035).ConclusionsThis analysis demonstrates divergent adoption patterns between IVL and CA, with IVL showing more rapid uptake. The findings highlight important implications for clinical practice, resource allocation, and health care policy.

Coronary artery calcification (CAC) has remained of the main challenges for optimizing outcomes in patients undergoing percutaneous coronary interventions (PCIs) with drug-eluting stent implantation. Several factors contribute to the increasing prevalence and severity of CAC, including diabetes mellitus, advanced age, renal insufficiency, and hypertension.1, 2, 3 CAC interacts with device crossing, stent delivery, and optimal expansion, resulting in procedural failure and increased complications.1 Calcium modification strategies enhance procedural success and promote optimal stent expansion; these strategies are recommended by the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association/Society for Cardiovascular Angiography and Interventions 2021 revascularization guidelines in fibrotic or heavily calcified lesions (Class 2 A/2B).1 The landscape of calcium modification strategies has continued to evolve with the introduction of novel technologies for treating calcified coronary lesions. Coronary intravascular lithotripsy (IVL) and mechanical coronary atherectomy (CA) represent 2 distinct approaches to managing heavily calcified lesions, each with unique advantages and limitations. Understanding their adoption patterns is crucial for health care planning and resource allocation. The introduction of IVL has presented a potentially paradigm-shifting approach to treating calcified coronary lesions, whereas traditional atherectomy techniques have maintained an established role in specific clinical scenarios. This study aims to analyze and project the adoption patterns of these technologies, providing insights into their relative uptake and implications in optimizing clinical outcomes for coronary artery disease.

Methods

Data sources and study population

We analyzed data from several U.S.-based cardiovascular registries spanning 2009 to 2022, including the National Cardiovascular Data Registry (NCDR) CathPCI Registry (2,000 centers),4,5 Blue Cross Blue Shield of Michigan Cardiovascular Consortium (BMC2) Registry (48 centers),6,7 and Cardiac Care Outcomes Assessment Program (COAP) Washington Registry8 reporting on utilization trends of mechanical CA and IVL. These registries provided comprehensive information on procedural characteristics and adoption patterns across diverse health care settings. Each of the included registries is weighted based on temporal relevance and data granularity.

Prediction model framework

Monte Carlo-based forecast model was developed, incorporating historical and contemporary data inputs for annual IVL and CA utilization rates. This model aimed to predict the annual utilization rate of IVL or CA in the United States up to 2035. The following variables were included in the forecast model: 1) annual volume of IVL utilization was extracted from the NCDR CathPCI and BMC2 registries and linearly extrapolated to 2035, assuming an unsaturated market; 2) annual CA volume (orbital atherectomy [OA] and rotational atherectomy [RA]) sourced from reports of NCDR CathPCI, BMC2, and COAP registries and prior single and multicenter studies reporting utilization rates; 3) age (age distribution of the patient population across registries spanning 13 years, with projected future procedures performed in age groups ranging from 60 to 85 years); and 4) hospital variation in the use of calcium modification (IVL and CA) for PCI over time.

Statistical analysis

We employed a multistage Monte Carlo simulation framework (100,000 generated iterations) to analyze temporal trends and hospital-level variation in procedural adoption patterns, using bootstrapped samples from registry data to account for sampling uncertainty. Hospital-level variations were modeled using beta distributions with parameters estimated from observed utilization patterns, allowing for asymmetric distributions and bounded ranges appropriate for proportion data. Temporal trends were analyzed using a modified logistic growth model, A(t) = L/(1 + eˆ[-k(t-t0)]), where L represents the asymptotic upper limit, k the growth rate, and t0 the inflection point. For hospital-specific projections, we employed a two-stage approach: first modeling baseline utilization based on published representative data using a beta-distribution (α = 2; β = 5) scaled to observed ranges, then generating terminal rates using a modified beta distribution (α = 3; β = 4) to reflect practice convergence. CIs were computed using the percentile method from the Monte Carlo distributions, with uncertainty ranges reported as IQRs for hospital-level metrics and 90% CIs for aggregate projections. The Gaussian kernel density estimation method was applied to the simulated hospital usage data to produce smooth density curves. The ECLIPSE (Evaluation of Treatment Strategies for Severe CaLcIfic Coronary Arteries: Orbital Atherectomy vs. Conventional Angioplasty Technique Prior to Implantation of Drug-Eluting StEnts: The ECLIPSE Trial) trial impact analysis on atherectomy trends was simulated (n = 100,000 iterations), incorporating trial outcomes through a Bayesian updating framework, with pretrial projections serving as prior distributions. Posterior distributions were estimated using Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods with 100,000 iterations, 3 chains, and a burn-in period of 10,000 iterations. Convergence was assessed using Gelman-Rubin statistics (R̂ < 1.01 for all parameters). All temporal trends were assessed for nonlinearity using restricted cubic splines, with knots placed at the 5th, 35th, 65th, and 95th percentiles (year 2010, 2015, 2018, and 2021, respectively) of the time variable distribution, following Harrell recommended approach to balance model flexibility and overfitting. Model selection between linear and spline models was performed using likelihood ratio tests, with spline models retained when P < 0.05. In addition, hospital-level clustering was accounted for using mixed-effects models with random intercepts. All statistical analyses and figure generation were performed using Python (version 3.11) with the following scientific computing libraries: NumPy (1.24.3) for numerical computations, Pandas (2.0.3) for data manipulation, SciPy (1.11.2) for statistical testing, and Statsmodels (0.14.0) for time series analysis and mixed-effects modeling. Visualizations were created using Matplotlib (3.7.1) and Seaborn (0.12.2) for statistical plotting. Monte Carlo simulations were implemented using NumPy's random number generators with fixed random seeds for reproducibility. The Bayesian analyses for the ECLIPSE trial impact assessment were conducted using PyMC3 (3.11.5). All CIs and uncertainty bands were generated using bootstrapped samples with the scikit-learn (1.3.0) library. Data processing and analysis scripts are available on reasonable request.

Hospital volume variation

To assess institutional heterogeneity in calcium modification usage and its potential effect on overall adoption trends, we performed a Monte Carlo simulation with 10,000 iterations to capture variability. This simulation generated trajectories for 500 simulated hospitals from 2025 to 2035.

Based on historical data from NCDR CathPCI Registry from 2009 to 2016, CA usage was relatively low. Early reports indicated a CA usage rate of approximately 1.7% of all PCI procedures during 2009 to 2010, rising to 2.3% in 2015 to 2016. Discussion

Our study investigated the predicted adoption trends of calcium modification strategies for calcified coronary lesions during the period from 2025 to 2035. Our main findings are as follows: 1) the overall adoption of IVL is likely to accelerate over the next 5 years, with adoption rates approaching parity with CA in high-adoption centers; 2) in contrast, CA adoption is projected to plateau in many institutions; 3) significant heterogeneity was observed at the institutional level where some centers rapidly adopted IVL compared to a lower adoption rate with CA; 4) the predicted interhospital variability in IVL usage is likely to decrease over time; and 5) the predicted impact of the results of ECLIPSE trial on OA utilization in clinical practice indicates a 14% reduction over the upcoming decade.

Over the past decades, calcified coronary lesions have been conventionally treated with high-pressure balloon expansion and mechanical atherectomy. Balloon-based techniques are dependent on high-pressure inflations for plaque modification, associated with a risk of lesion barotrauma and an increased risk of dissection and perforation, particularly in noncompliant calcified lesions. In addition, mechanical atherectomy devices (RA or OA) are debulking strategies often used in lesion preparation of calcified lesions. However, there remains a risk of debris embolization, thermal injury to the vessel treated, and vascular complications. In addition, the wire bias remains a limiting factor for mechanical thrombectomy devices.10

Since the introduction of IVL in 2021, following FDA approval based on the favorable results of the Disrupt CAD III study, which paved the way for U.S. regulatory approval,11 a surge in uptake of IVL as a modification strategy for severely calcified coronary disease has been reported across the United States.4 Several factors may have contributed to the observed increase in IVL utilization, including the safety procedural profile, higher procedural success with considerably lower procedural complications compared to mechanical atherectomy, the relatively straightforward learning curve during early operator experience with atheroablative technologies compared to mechanical atherectomy, and the growing need for modification strategies for lesions with challenging anatomy not suitable for mechanical atherectomy devices.12, 13, 14 Furthermore, the use of IVL has been associated with favorable angiographical and clinical outcomes, including successful stent expansion (defined as residual in-stent stenosis <20%) in up to 70% of cases, along with low rates of short-term major adverse cardiovascular events in real-world registries.12,15 Moreover, there have been increasing reports of expanding use of IVL in calcified lesions beyond those initially included in the study designs, including unprotected left main,16,17 ostial target lesions, tortuous anatomy, bifurcation lesions ranging from 19% to 37% of target lesions, and in stent-restenosis.14, 15, 16,18 In addition, the projected increase in the use of intravascular coronary imaging (intravascular ultrasound/optical coherence tomography) might play a role in the projected increased use of calcium modification strategies.

Our analysis provides valuable insights into the evolving adoption trends of interhospital variation for coronary IVL and mechanical atherectomy across diverse hospital settings in the United States. Our analysis indicates a rapid increase in IVL utilization accompanied by a widening variation among centers—suggesting that although certain institutions advance quickly with this technology, others might lag, potentially reflecting differences in operator training, resource availability, and institutional policies. In contrast, mechanical atherectomy adoption appears to be plateauing, underscoring the potential shift in clinical preference toward IVL, which has been associated with a favorable procedural profile. However, data on the operator learning curve and long-term clinical outcomes for coronary IVL remain limited to date; hence, the projected impact of IVL strategy utilization has yet to be elucidated in long-term reports of ongoing registries.19 Moreover, the projected increase in hospital-level mechanical CA usage, along with the growing proportion of high-volume centers, implies that institutional practice patterns may significantly drive overall trends in atherectomy adoption. In regions or centers where CA is used more frequently, these institutions may influence benchmark practices and clinical decision-making, potentially leading to higher aggregate adoption rates over time. Conversely, the wide interhospital variability may result in regional differences, with some centers adopting CA more aggressively based on local practice norms, operator volume, and favorable clinical outcomes in the hands of experienced operators.20

Overall, the simulation underscores that although the national trend in CA adoption may demonstrate gradual growth, underlying variations at the hospital level could result in heterogeneous implementation across different regions, reinforcing the need for targeted quality improvement and standardization initiatives. Moreover, these projected adoption trends might highlight significant implications for interventional cardiology training and clinical practice, with a greater emphasis on emerging calcium modification technologies. IVL’s anticipated dominance necessitates comprehensive integration into fellowship curricula, leveraging its straightforward learning curve for early trainee competency development. On the other hand, the projected CA plateau presents training challenges requiring centralized high-volume training or simulation-based learning to maintain proficiency despite reduced case volumes comparatively. The 14% projected reduction in OA following ECLIPSE trial results emphasizes the need for adaptive curricula responding to evolving evidence. These projections could help inform strategic decisions for interventional cardiology training program directors regarding curriculum development, hospital administrators for resource allocation, and policymakers for quality improvement initiatives, eventually optimizing care delivery for patients with calcified coronary artery disease through standardized competency requirements and optimized procedural protocols.

Study limitations

Several limitations are worth noting. First, granular data regarding operator-specific learning curves, detailed economic drivers, local regulatory environments, direct cost data, and regional variations for coronary IVL are lacking to date, hindering further analysis of institutional experience and local practice patterns that might fully capture the dynamic nature of real-world adoption patterns. Second, the retrospective nature of data incorporated into the simulation model as opposed to prospectively collected, detailed patient- or operator-level data, makes it prone to selection bias. Third, the evolving nature of potential new calcium modification technologies, combined with the emergence of complementary innovations currently being investigated in the Short-Cut trial (NCT06089135) and FORWARD CAD IDE Study (NCT06662500), may further alter real-world adoption patterns in ways that our current model cannot predict. However, the adoption cycle of new technologies, from initial feasibility studies to commercial use in interventional cardiology practice, typically takes 5 to 7 years. Hence, newer technologies might not drastically impact the projected trends of CA and IVL over the upcoming 10 years. Fourth, although the Monte Carlo simulation approach robustly accommodates uncertainty in variables such as procedural time and complication rates, it remains constrained by the limitations of the underlying data sources. Last, operator experience and career stage may have influenced adoption trends; early to mid-career interventions are often more likely to adopt newer calcium modification technologies compared to more senior interventionalists. However, the magnitude of impact cannot be determined given the limited available data for adoption patterns based on career stage. Hence, these limitations highlight the need for prospective, multicenter studies that capture detailed operator performance metrics, economic variables, and regional policy differences.

The current analysis provides a valuable framework for understanding current adoption trends of calcium modification strategies and can serve as a nidus for future investigations, which are critical as the field continues to evolve.PerspectivesCOMPETENCY IN PATIENT CARE: This study provides critical insight into future trends in calcium modification strategies for calcified coronary artery lesions.

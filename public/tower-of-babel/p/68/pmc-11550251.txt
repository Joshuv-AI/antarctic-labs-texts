A Phase 3, Open-Label, Single-Arm Trial of the Efficacy and Safety of Triptorelin 6-Month Formulation in Chinese Children with Central Precocious Puberty
Authors: Xiao Yu, Xinran Cheng, Haiyan Wei, Xu Xu, Chunxiu Gong, Guimei Li, Hui Yao, Li Zhou, Yan Zhong, Yu Yang, Feihong Luo, Yining Zhang, Frank Huang, Xiaofeng Shi, Patrick Cabri, Xiaoping Luo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThis phase 3 study assessed the efficacy, safety, and pharmacokinetics of the 6-month prolonged release (PR) formulation in Chinese children with central precocious puberty (CPP).MethodsIn this open-label study (NCT05029622), Chinese children (girls < 9 years, boys < 10 years) received two doses of triptorelin pamoate 22.5 mg (day 1 and month 6). Primary endpoint was the proportion at month 6 with luteinizing hormone (LH) suppression (stimulated peak LH ≤ 5 IU/L after gonadotropin-releasing hormone stimulation). Secondary endpoints included safety assessments, hormone level changes, and clinical parameters from baseline.ResultsOverall, 66 children completed the study (93.9% girls; median age 8.0 [range 5–9] years). At month 6, all patients had LH suppression; this was maintained at month 12 in 98.5% of patients. Mean basal and peak LH and follicle-stimulating hormone levels were suppressed throughout follow-up. All patients at months 3 to 12 had sex hormone suppression to prepubertal levels. Stable or reduced breast development was seen for 98.4% and 93.5% of girls at month 6 and 12, respectively; all boys had regression or stable genital development until month 12. Compared with baseline (9.82 cm/year), mean growth velocity was 5.88 cm/year at month 6 and 5.17 cm/year at month 12. Mean bone age/chronological age ratio decreased from 1.27 at baseline to 1.23 and 1.21 at month 6 and 12, respectively. In girls, 64.5% showed decreased uterine length at month 6 and 12 versus baseline, while 75.0% of boys showed stable testicular volume versus baseline. Thirteen patients (19.7%) had 22 drug-related treatment emergent adverse events (TEAEs); no grade ≥ 3 TEAEs were reported.ConclusionThe efficacy and safety profile of triptorelin 6-month PR in Chinese children with CPP was consistent with data previously reported in non-Chinese children with CPP, supporting this as a viable treatment option for Chinese children with CPP.Trial RegistrationTrial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov identifier, NCT05029622.Keywords: Central precocious puberty, Chinese children, Triptorelin

Key Summary Points

Why carry out this study?Central precocious puberty (CPP) is a condition in children in which premature activation of the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis leads to early development of secondary sexual characteristics.A 6-month prolonged release (PR) formulation of two injections/year is available in numerous countries for the treatment of CPP, but at the time of this study it was not licensed for children with CPP in China.This phase 3, prospective, open-label, multicenter, single-arm study assessed the efficacy and safety of triptorelin pamoate 6-month PR formulation in Chinese children with CPP over a period of 12 months.What was learned from the study?Six months after treatment, the triptorelin 6-month PR formulation was observed to suppress luteinizing hormone levels to prepubertal levels (≤ 5 IU/L) following gonadotropin-releasing hormone stimulation in all patients and it had an acceptable safety profile.Findings from this study confirm the efficacy and safety profile of the triptorelin 6-month PR formulation in Chinese children with CPP and support using two injections/year of this treatment as a viable option for Chinese children with CPP.

Introduction

The prevalence of central precocious puberty (CPP) in China has been estimated at approximately 5% of the school-based population [1, 2] and it is more commonly diagnosed in girls than boys [3]. CPP is a premature maturation of the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonad (HPG) axis. This early activation of the HPG axis, which occurs before the age of 8 years in girls or 9 years in boys, leads to rapid development of internal and external reproductive organs and secondary sexual characteristics [4].

The clinical manifestations of CPP include early menarche, caused by early sexual development in girls, an increase in the speed of bone maturation (which can ultimately lead to a reduction in final height), and effects on health in later life (such as a heightened risk of metabolic comorbidities including hypertension, obesity, and type 2 diabetes) [4]. In parallel with premature secondary sexual characteristic development and sexual maturity, accompanying psychological problems or abnormal social behavior may also occur, leading to both patients and caregivers experiencing negative psychological effects [4, 5].

Worldwide, CPP is predominantly treated using a range of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) agonist preparations [6, 7] which downregulate and suppress the HPG axis. In China the monthly formulations of triptorelin or leuprorelin are currently the most widely used for treating CPP [8–11]. Triptorelin was developed as a synthetic GnRH agonist with enhanced pituitary receptor affinity and resistance to enzymatic degradation, which increases both its plasma half-life and potency. Long-acting formulations of triptorelin are achieved by the use of microparticle formulations which include specific copolymers that degrade over different timescales, thereby allowing prolonged release of triptorelin over the required period [12].

In China, Triptorelin is currently marketed in 1-, 3-, and 6-month prolonged release (PR) formulations with the first two approved for the treatment of CPP. However, while the 6-month PR formulation is approved for treatment of locally advanced or metastatic prostate cancer and CPP in Europe, it is not currently approved for CPP in China [12]. Triptorelin 6-month PR formulation has been made available in numerous countries for the treatment of CPP [12, 13]. A previous international phase 3 study reported that the 6-month PR formulation of triptorelin was well tolerated and effective in suppressing the HPG axis in children with CPP [14]. In this multicenter, open-label, noncomparative study of 44 children with CPP (39 girls, 5 boys), patients received two doses of triptorelin 6-month PR formulation over 12 months and 93.2% showed prepubertal luteinizing hormone (LH) levels (stimulated LH ≤ 5 IU/L) in response to the GnRH test at month 6, which was maintained through to month 12. Additionally, no unexpected drug-related adverse events (AEs) were reported. Similar findings were also observed in a second more recent study, where 42 patients (33 girls and nine boys) with CPP were treated with triptorelin 6-month PR formulation for more than 12 months [15]. In this study, the initial treatment administered was the triptorelin 1- or 3-month formulation, but the treatment was changed (at an average of 4 months after treatment initiation) to the 6-month PR formulation to reduce the frequency of pain resulting from the injection, increase convenience, and improve compliance. Throughout treatment, the basal LH level was suppressed to below the cutoff value (< 0.6 IU/L), suggesting that HPG axis suppression was effectively achieved. Furthermore, sexual development did not advance and bone age (BA) progression slowed. Temporary induration and pain at the injection site were reported, but no long-lasting local complications occurred.

One of the main AEs reported with triptorelin treatment of CPP is injection site pain [15–17]. Because the triptorelin 6-month PR formulation has an extended injection interval compared with the 1- and 3-month PR formulation, this may improve compliance and increase comfort in the management of CPP. However, at the time of this study, clinical experience was lacking for use of the triptorelin 6-month PR formulation among Chinese children with CPP. This phase 3 study assessed the efficacy, safety, and pharmacokinetics (PK) of the triptorelin 6-month PR formulation in Chinese children with CPP.

Methods

Study Design

This was an open-label, multicenter, single-arm, interventional study evaluating the efficacy and safety of the triptorelin 6-month PR formulation in Chinese children with CPP over a period of 12 months (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier, NCT05029622). Patients were enrolled from 12 investigational sites in China between August 2021 and February 2022. Patients received two doses of triptorelin pamoate 22.5 mg (Diphereline®, Ipsen), administered on day 1 and month 6 of this 12-month study. Triptorelin 6-month PR formulation was administered by intramuscular injection in the upper outer quadrant of the buttock, thigh, or deltoid. Similar methodologies to those used in this study have been published previously in a study of the 3-month triptorelin formulation in Chinese population with CPP [18].

The study was conducted under the provisions of the Declaration of Helsinki, in accordance with International Conference on Harmonization (ICH), Consolidated Guideline on Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and in compliance with independent ethics committee and informed consent regulations. The study protocol, amendments, and informed consent form and updates were reviewed and approved by an ethics committee prior to commencement of the study, and during the study where applicable. Written informed consent was acquired from one or both of each participant’s parents or guardians, and signed approval was obtained from the child when applicable, prior to study entry.

Procedures and Assessments

The study consisted of a screening visit for eligibility (up to 28 days prior to treatment initiation); a day 1 visit for drug administration; a month 6 visit for efficacy and safety assessments and the second drug administration; and month 12 visit (end of study) for efficacy and safety assessments, or early withdrawal visit for early discontinuation procedures and assessments. Additional efficacy and safety assessment visits were performed at month 3 and 9 (Fig. 1).

Study design

The primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of patients with LH suppression, defined as stimulated peak LH ≤ 5 IU/L after GnRH stimulation at month 6. The GnRH stimulation test was performed using an intravenous injection of gonadorelin to stimulate gonadotrophin release; blood samples were collected prior to and 30, 60, and 90 min after the gonadorelin injection for central assessment of serum LH levels. Safety assessments included incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) throughout the study (including local tolerability at the injection site immediately and 2 h after triptorelin injection), clinical laboratory parameters (blood biochemistry, hematology, and urinalysis), and measurements from physical examination/vital signs. All AEs were recorded and graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0.

For the PK endpoint, sparse plasma triptorelin concentrations at day 1 and months 3, 6, and 12 were analyzed using a validated, specific, and sensitive liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry method. PK samples on day 1 were taken pre-dose and at 4 h post-injection (± 2-h window), at the month 3 visit, at month 6 (pre-dose of second injection, and 4 h post-injection [± 2-h window]), and at month 12 end of study/early withdrawal visits pre-GnRH agonist stimulation. Sparse sampling was used to minimize invasive blood sampling in the children.

Statistics

The following assumptions were made based on the previous global pivotal study [14] for determining the required sample size: the expected outcome for the proportion of children with a suppressed LH response to the GnRH stimulation test (stimulated peak LH ≤ 5 IU/L) at month 6 was 93%; the null proportion was 80%; an exact binomial test of a proportion with a one-sided nominal significance level of 0.025 and power of 85%; and an expected common dropout rate of 5%. Under these assumptions, enrollment of approximately 66 patients was planned.

The analysis sets used included:

Intention-to-treat (ITT) population: All patients who received at least one dose of study medication.

PK set: All patients who received at least one dose of study medication and had at least one valid and quantifiable triptorelin concentration.

For the primary efficacy analysis, the two-sided 95% confidence interval (CI) was computed by SAS® version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC) using exact binomial distributions on the ITT population. Supportive analysis of the primary efficacy endpoint was based on the mITT and PPS populations. The secondary efficacy analyses were based on the ITT population. Two-sided 95% CIs were also constructed for all secondary endpoints expressed as percentages. Where relevant, efficacy endpoints were also analyzed in the mITT and PPS populations.

Results

Patient Characteristics

In total, 88 patients were screened, of whom 22 were not suitable for enrollment (did not meet the entry criteria, n = 15; withdrawn by parent or guardian, n = 6; unable to attend hospital because of COVID-19 restrictions, n = 1) (Fig. 2). Of the 66 eligible patients, all were enrolled and received study treatment. Sixty-two (93.9%) were girls and four (6.1%) were boys; median age 8.0 years (range 5–9 years), mean age (standard deviation [SD]) was 7.5 (0.9) years (Table 2). Mean (SD) body weight was 32.59 (7.35) kg, mean (SD) growth velocity was 9.82 (5.08) cm/year, and mean ratio (SD) of BA to CA was 1.27 (0.10). Six patients had experienced menarche at the time of enrollment. The distribution of patients across the Tanner development scale (combined girls [breast development] and boys [genital development]) was 0% (stage I), 43.9% (stage II), 43.9% (stage III), 10.6% (stage IV), and 1.5% (stages V). Mean baseline GnRH-stimulated peak values for LH and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) (± SD) were 27.82 ± 20.31 IU/L and 13.41 ± 5.20 IU/L, respectively.

Hormonal Suppression

In the analysis of the primary endpoint, all 66 patients in the ITT population (100%; 95% CI 94.6–100.0%) had LH suppression (≤ 5 IU/L) after GnRH stimulation at month 6 (Fig. 3). The lower limit of the 95% CI was higher than the null hypothesis (80%) and similar results were observed in the mITT and PPS populations. At month 3 and 12, 64 (97.0%; 95% CI 89.5–99.6%) and 65 patients (98.5%; 95% CI 91.8–100.0%) had LH suppression after GnRH stimulation, respectively.

Regarding pubertal stage and gonadal development, breast development stage for girls and genital development stage for boys were stable or reduced in 98.5% of participants between baseline and month 6 and in 93.9% of participants between baseline and month 12 (ITT population; Table 4). Relative to baseline, 98.4% at month 6 and 93.5% at month 12 of girls had regression or stable breast development stage. For breast development stage, the proportion of girls at Tanner scale I, II, III, IV, and V at screening was 1.6%, 42%, 44%, 11%, and 1.6%, respectively, and at month 6 and month 12 the values were 11%, 44%, 37%, 6.5%, and 1.6% and 11%, 48%, 27%, 9.7%, and 1.6%, respectively. All boys had regression or stable genital development at month 6 and 12. In boys, the proportion at Tanner scale I, II, III, IV, and V for genital development stage at screening, month 6, and month 12 was unchanged at 0%, 50%, 50%, 0%, and 0%, respectively. Compared with baseline, pubic hair development stage was stable or reduced in 92.4% of participants at month 6 and in 86.4% participants at month 12 (ITT population; Table 4). At month 6 and 12, 91.9% and 87.1% of girls had regression or stable status of the pubic hair development stage, respectively. All (100%) and three (75%) boys had regression or stable status of the pubic hair development stage at month 6 and 12, respectively. At month 6 and 12, absence of progression compared with baseline of uterine length was seen in 79.0% and 71.0% of girls (Table 4), respectively, and regression of uterine length from baseline was observed in 40 (64.5%) girls. Safety

In the safety population, 59 patients (89.4%) reported a total of 173 TEAEs, all of which were non-serious TEAEs (Table 5). The most frequently reported TEAEs (> 10%) were upper respiratory tract infection (47.0%), COVID-19 (15.2%), pyrexia (13.6%), weight increase (13.6%), cough (10.6%), and overweight (10.6%). Of the 173 TEAEs, 22 events in 13 patients (19.7%) were considered to be drug related. The most frequently reported drug-related TEAEs were weight increase (9.1%), obesity (3.0%), vaginal hemorrhage (3.0%), and hepatic steatosis (3.0%). All TEAEs were grade 1 or grade 2, and no participant experienced a TEAE grade ≥ 3. No TEAEs led to treatment discontinuation, and there were no deaths.

Discussion

This open-label, single-arm phase 3 study assessed whether the efficacy and safety of the triptorelin 6-month PR formulation in Chinese children with CPP were consistent with data from previous studies in non-Chinese populations [14, 15]. Our results confirm that the triptorelin 6-month PR formulation adequately achieved and maintained suppression of the pituitary–gonadal axis in Chinese children with CPP. LH suppression to prepubertal levels (≤ 5 IU/L) after GnRH stimulation was observed in all (100%) patients at month 6 and almost all patients at months 3 (97.0%) and 12 (98.5%). Suppression of pituitary release of LH and FSH, and consequently the gonadal secretion to prepubertal levels of estradiol in girls and testosterone in boys, was associated with a reduction in clinical signs of puberty.

The proportion of patients with suppressed hormone levels (confirmed by the effective suppression of pubertal development) observed in the current study align well with those from previous studies of the 1-, 3-, and 6-monthly triptorelin formulations in non-Chinese CPP populations [14–21]. Of particular relevance are prior efficacy assessments of the triptorelin 6-month PR formulation conducted in the Americas (USA, Chile, and Mexico) and in South Korea [14, 15]. In the phase 3 trial of 6-monthly triptorelin conducted in the Americas (n = 41), LH suppression (stimulated LH ≤ 5 IU/L) was observed in 93.2% of children at month 6 and maintained at month 12 [14]. In the South Korean study of patients switching from 1-monthly or 3-monthly triptorelin formulations to the 6-month PR formulation (n = 42), effective LH suppression (to prepubertal levels, defined as basal LH < 1 mIU/mL) was observed throughout the treatment period [15]. Further, the LH suppression rates observed in our trial of 6-monthly triptorelin are consistent with those reported for other triptorelin formulations in non-Chinese CPP populations. A retrospective cohort study involving 106 South Korean girls with CPP, for example, reported similarly high rates of LH suppression (serum LH < 2.5 IU/L) for the long-acting triptorelin 11.25 mg 3-month PR formulation and for the 3.75 mg 1-month preparation (90.0% and 98.2% at month 6 and 93.5% and 100% at month 12, respectively) [20]. Of note, comparable LH suppression rates reported from our study were achieved with a less frequent dosing regimen and, for the first time, in a CPP population from China.

For children affected by CPP, the changes in physical appearance associated with early development of secondary sexual characteristics and sexual maturity can cause significant psychological stress [5]. The present study showed a regression or stabilization of pubertal stage, deceleration of gonadal development, and a decrease in growth velocity in patients treated with 6-monthly triptorelin PR formulation; improvements in physical and visible symptoms of CPP that may have important psychological benefits for children and their caregivers. Indeed, most patients in this study experienced a stabilization or reduction in Tanner stage (versus baseline) at months 6 and 12, demonstrating the sustained effect of 6-monthly triptorelin on arresting (or reversing) the progression of the clinical signs of puberty. Sex steroid levels were also suppressed to within prepubertal ranges (defined as estradiol ≤ 20 pg/mL in girls, and testosterone ≤ 30 ng/dL in boys) at months 3, 6, 9, and 12; reductions positively correlated with the observed regression/stabilization of gonadal development. The reductions in serum FSH and sex steroid concentrations and the slowing of pubertal development observed in the present study were (again) comparable with those previously reported for non-Chinese CPP populations [16, 18–20], including the aforementioned studies involving the 6-monthly triptorelin in the Americas and South Korea [14, 15].

As anticipated, we observed a decrease in growth velocity and in the rate of bone maturation over the course of the study. The decrease in mean growth velocity at months 6 and 12 (versus baseline) indicated a slowing of the accelerated growth velocity associated with CPP and a reversion toward a normal prepubertal growth rate. Notably, for the girls, mean growth velocity values observed at baseline and months 6 and 12 were within the normal range for Chinese children [22]. Mean growth velocity at baseline in the boys was higher than normal ranges previous reported in Chinese children, but it fell to within the normal range at month 6 and 12 [22]. In addition, the majority of patients showed no increase in BA/CA ratio at month 6 (more than 95%) and at month 12 (92%), indicating a normalization of the accelerated bone maturation associated with CPP.

Administration of triptorelin 6-month PR formulation in our study was well tolerated and no patients reported any local tolerability reactions. The most frequently reported drug-related TEAE was abnormal weight gain, which is a documented side effect of triptorelin treatment [12, 18]. The drug-related TEAEs weight increase, overweight, and obesity were reported in six, one, and two patients, respectively. Of note, it has been reported that GnRH agonist treatment can potentially increase BMI in the short term, but likely returns to a normal range once adult height is reached [23, 24]. However, another study found no impact of GnRH agonist treatment on BMI trajectories [25]. Hepatic stenosis was reported in one girl and one boy. In each case, the patients’ body weight and BMI were at the higher end of the range seen in the study population both at baseline and at month 12. No new or unexpected safety findings were reported; no patients experienced a TEAE of grade 3 or higher, and no TEAEs led to treatment discontinuation or death. The safety results of our study are in keeping with the known safety profile for 6-month triptorelin PR documented in non-Chinese CPP populations; commonly reported AEs have included headache, rhinitis, abdominal pain, upper respiratory tract infection, gastroenteritis, rash, hot flush, vomiting, weight gain, and injection site pain [14–18].

With respect to PK parameters, again the results of this study were in line with those seen in other CPP populations. In the present study, triptorelin plasma concentrations levels remained detectable at months 3, 6, and 12, and plasma concentrations at 4 h post-injection [18] and maximum plasma concentrations [26] were similar to those described from studies of the triptorelin pamoate 3-month PR formulation.

Strengths of this phase 3 study include its prospective and multicenter design and its inclusion of a sizable number of Chinese children. Limitations include the open-label and single-arm study design. Although the inclusion of a comparator arm would have strengthened the study, given that long-acting analogues of GnRH agonist have been the gold-standard treatment of CPP worldwide [6, 7], and there is a large body of evidence confirming that triptorelin has a beneficial effect in patients with CPP [14–21], it would be unethical not to provide effective treatment to children with CPP when available. The design used in this study is consistent with that of previous studies [14–19], including a recent study on the use of the triptorelin 3-month PR formulation in Chinese children with CPP [18], which assists when comparing the present findings with those previously published. Reassuringly, the use of objective measures (e.g., hormone levels) means that the open-label design should not have significantly biased the overall findings. In addition, the overall sample size is appropriate for the statistical tests applied. Although the number of boys included in the study was small, the lower number of boys recruited to the study is not unexpected considering the prevalence of precocious puberty is reported to be 10 times higher in girls than boys [27]. Efforts were made to include boys in this study to ensure that the effect of triptorelin treatment was explored in this group.

In China, CPP is commonly managed with the support of monthly hormone injections of triptorelin or leuprorelin [8, 10]. More recently a 3-monthly treatment regimen has become available with the approval of triptorelin 3-month PR formulation [28]. The present study supports triptorelin 6-month PR formulation as an efficacious and tolerable treatment option for children with CPP in China. A CPP treatment option offering sustained efficacy over a 6-month period would bring several potential benefits for the patients and their caregivers. A lower frequency of injections could, for example, reduce the impact of treatment on aspects of daily living, such as reduced travel to/from the hospital and reduced workplace and educational disruptions. Fewer treatment-related disruptions to daily living may also offer psychological benefits by reducing the frequency of treatment-related considerations and the anticipation and potential anxiety associated with pending clinic visits. However, not all patients and caregivers may welcome the opportunity of reduced hospital/injection visits; those who derive reassurance from regular face-to-face interactions with clinicians may prefer more frequent hospital visits. From a healthcare perspective, a less onerous treatment regimen offers the potential to reduce administrative time and costs, and to alleviate capacity pressures by reducing the time associated with coordinating, preparing, and administrating injections. These benefits have previously been modeled for triptorelin use in another setting [29]. The availability of different treatment regimens ultimately affords clinicians greater ability to tailor treatment selection to individual patient/caregiver preferences and to different clinical settings and scenarios.

Conclusions

This study confirms that the treatment efficacy and safety profile of triptorelin 6-month PR formulation in Chinese children with CPP is consistent with data previously reported in non-Chinese CPP populations, supporting it as a viable treatment option for Chinese children with CPP.

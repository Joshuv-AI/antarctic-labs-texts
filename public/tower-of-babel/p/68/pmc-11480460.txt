A deep learning approach for line-level Amharic Braille image recognition
Authors: Nega Agmas Asfaw, Birhanu Hailu Belay, Kassawmar Mandefro Alemu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Braille, the most popular tactile-based writing system, uses patterns of raised dots arranged in cells to inscribe characters for visually impaired persons. Amharic is Ethiopia’s official working language, spoken by more than 100 million people. To bridge the written communication gap between persons with and without eyesight, multiple Optical braille recognition systems for various language scripts have been developed utilizing both statistical and deep learning approaches. However, the need for half-character identification and character segmentation has complicated these systems, particularly in the Amharic script, where each character is represented by two braille cells. To address these challenges, this study proposed deep learning model that combines a CNN and a BiLSTM network with CTC. The model was trained with 1,800 line images with 32 × 256 and 48 × 256 dimensions, and validated with 200 line images and evaluated using Character Error Rate. The best-trained model had a CER of 7.81% on test data with a 48 × 256 image dimension. These findings demonstrate that the proposed sequence-to-sequence learning method is a viable Optical Braille Recognition (OBR) solution that does not necessitate extensive image pre and post processing. Inaddition, we have made the first Amharic braille line-image data set available for free to researchers via the link: https://github.com/Ne-UoG-git/Am-Br-line-image.github.io.

Introduction

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), over 1 billion people worldwide are blind or have vision impairments1. More than 1.6 million Ethiopians are blind, and over 3.7 million are visually impaired, including students, teachers, artists, lawyers, and employees who contribute significantly to society in areas such as politics, religion, economics, and social affairs2. To address the needs of visually impaired individuals, various means of systems have been developed to access printed documents3. Braille is a basic and easily used writing method containing a series of dots that allows vision impaired individuals to read and write through touch, using their fingertips4,5. The Braille writing system, created by Louis Braille in 1829, is a tactile method of written communication that is widely used by low vision and blind people worldwide6. It uses patterns of raised dots that are grouped in a cell to inscribe characters on paper7. In addition, Braille is versatile and compact, able to express the languages and scripts of Europe, Asia, and Africa, and adaptable to various purposes such as mathematics and musical notations6. It is also simple and easily used by the blind for transcribing their ideas8.

The Braille system includes symbols which the blinds are able to review and study the written words. A Braille cell can hold a group of 6 dots, 2 columns and 3 rows, and is considered the basic unit for all Braille symbols. To identify easily, these dots are numbered downward 1, 2, 3 on the left, and 4, 5, and 6 on the right. As a single braille cell contains six dots as prescribed in Fig. 1, it can represent 64 (26) different characters.

Amharic writing system

Amharic language is the second most spoken Semetic language in the world next to Arabic, and the official working language of the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia9. It is also the most spoken language in various regions including the Amhara regional state and the multi-ethnic Southern Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples regional state. In addition, it is used by Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church and is spoken in a number of countries outside Ethiopia, particularly in Eritrea, Egypt, Israel and Sweden10. According to Senior Cultural Heritage Conservator and Researcher Hailemelekot Agizew states at the Authority for Research and Conservation of Cultural Heritage, Amharic is spoken by more than 50 million peoples as mother language and over 100 million as a second language in the country10.

The current writing system of Amharic is taken from Ge’ez which was indigenous and born in Axum9. All the 26 symbols of Ge’ez were adopted in the Amharic writing system with eight additional symbols to represent sounds not found in the Ge’ez script9 Accordingly, since Amharic language has its own scripts, understanding of the characteristics and distinct features of the scripts is important to follow the appropriate approaches of Braille recognition system2. Finally, the current Amharic writing consists of 33 core characters and 1 special character with six derived variants for each. In addition to these 238 characters, Amharic consists of 44 other characters called labialization.

Amharic writing system uses both Ethiopic and Hindu-Arabic numerals. According to Ref.6Amharic writing system has eight punctuation marks, but some other punctuation marks are adapted from Latin based symbols like question mark (?), exclamation mark (!), quotes (“”) and parenthesis. Finally there are 310 unique characters in Amharic language as prescribed in Table 1 below.

Modern touching education for visually impaired people in Ethiopia was started by the end of August 19242. Later, in 1934 Braille in Ethiopia was introduced by missionaries and was designed by taking the 26 English characters pattern and 8 newly added patterns (Nebyeluel (1954), cited by12. In Ethiopia, mainly in Addis Ababa, there are different education centers, such as AAU, Misrach Center, Entoto Blind people school, for visually impaired people at primary, secondary and tertiary levels6. As a result, there have been massive Braille documents produced and used by visually impaired people. As Amharic is an official working language of Ethiopia, is spoken by more than 50 million people as mother language and over 100 million as a second language in the country10, there is a massive Amharic braille document. But there is a communication gap through written documents between vision and visually impaired people.

To provide this solution, many attempts have been made to develop Optical Braille Recognition (OBR) systems for various language scripts, including Amharic, using both statistical and deep learning methods. From these Arabic Braille Recognition and Transcription into text and voice Based on the comparison of braille dot position Extraction in each cell19, Braille Character Recognition Using Find Counter Method20 with different cropping, thresholding, erosion and dilation as image processing, Braille Character Recognition using Associative Memories to English text and voice21, Optical Braille Character Recognition with Support-Vector Machine Classifier22, introduces a specific image processing approach, which is simple, convenient, and easy to operate and also able to extract dots online in real time. Another attempt proposed by David Bridson et al.23 aimed to increase the robustness at each stage of the process, even if with poor quality documents. hey proposed a system to perform contextual post-processing at the word level using knowledge of likely specific Braille production errors. Another attempt, Abdul Malik et al.5, Studied to develop an algorithm to recognize images of Arabic Braille printed characters. They have presented a new thresholding algorithm. After they convert the image into grayscale, they divide it into three parts to remove the highest and lowest values to leave out distinct values and calculate the average gray level for the whole image.

Some other attempts have been done on deep learning24–26 on braille character recognition. A Deep learning algorithm for Braille recognition based on Stacked Denoising AutoEncoder (SDAE) to solve the problems of automatic feature extraction and dimension reduction was studied in24. The researchers25 developed an artificial neural network to identify letter’s images of Cyrillic alphabet in the Braille representation system. They have aimed to develop a system that can adapt to factors like quality of input patterns and differences between them dynamically. A novel approach for automatic Braille characters recognition was presented on26 using CNN deep learning to avoid difficulties to memorize the pattern of dots.

Attempts for Amharic Braille documents were started recently and only a number of studies are conducted. The system proposed by Teshome2 was the first to develop Amharic Braille recognizer on both manual and typewritten single-sided Braille documents. The researcher uses a global thresholding technique for binarization of images scanned with 200 dpi. The segmentation was done in two steps: first gridlines mesh was constructed and then dots were searched following the grids mesh with thresholding. For this purpose both global and adaptive mesh techniques had been tested and a modified region based approach was adopted for feature extraction. Each character is described in terms of dot positions and the six possible positions of the dots are known for each individual Braille character in the image. Finally MATLAB implementation of the feed forward artificial neural network had been trained on the extracted features to create a model which can be used for Braille characters recognition. The attempt Recognition of Ethiopic Braille Characters27 performs preprocessing by using Gaussian filters and derivatives of Gaussians as low-pass and high-pass filters, respectively for noise suppression and to detect and localize edges. The researchers have used direction field tensors for noise removal and isolation of braille dots from their background. They have proposed a new skewness correction technique, that exploits the horizontal direction nature of braille dots and a half character detection method, which differentiates braille dots from noise, was applied. Half characters have been recognized through analysis of their dot positions; the braille cells were formulated by examining horizontal distances between the half characters.

The other effort made on recognition of Amharic braille documents28 applies adaptive histogram equalization for contrast adjustment, Gaussian and adaptive Median filtering, thresholding/binarization, and morphological operation for preprocessing. They use Otsu’s global Binarization technique on the gray scale filtered image by decreasing the threshold value by 0.065 from the Otsu’s global threshold value after much experimentation done with different values. After Binarization processing, to avoid remaining noises they analyze the pixel size of the dot through experiment and they continue to perform morphological operations that process images based on shapes, size, and neighborhood using MATLAB built in function ‘bwmorph’ majority operations. They also used mesh grid segmentation algorithms adopted by Teshome2. Although the variation of the Braille dots greatly affects the construction of mesh grid (which is size dependent)28, they use a modified mesh grid segmentation algorithm such that the mesh grid is constructed by finding the minimum and maximum dot size of the Braille dots. At the end they used a context based feature extraction algorithm adopted by Teshome2 with some modification and artificial neural network for training and recognition.

The recent attempt done by Hassen Seid et al.29, which is the only study for Recognition of Double sided Amharic braille documents, proposed a system for recognition of double sided Amharic Braille documents, which needs identification of recto and verso dots. Hassen Seid et al.29, use direction field tensor for preprocessing and segmentation of dots from the background. They used a gradient field for identifying a dot as recto or verso dots and used Braille dot attributes (centroid and area) to identify Overlapping dots. After dots are identified, they are grouped into recto and verso in pages. They have designed Braille cell encoding to encode dots into a Braille code and Braille code translation algorithms to translate Braille codes into a print text. Moreover, they have used the concept of reflection to reverse wrongly scanned Braille documents automatically.

However, these systems have been hampered by the need for character segmentation and half-character identification, particularly in the Amharic script, which uses two braille cells to represent a some single characters. To address these issues, this study aimed to develop a deep learning model that combines a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and a Bidirectional-LSTM Network, which is suitable for this spatio-temporal problems, with Connectionist Temporal Classification (BiLSTM-CTC) to reduce the detailed processing requirements of Amharic OBR. The first module (CNN), which act as feature extractor module, the middle layer(RNN) acts as sequence learner predicts the sequential output per time-step, and finally transcription to the equivalent characters is done using the CTC layer.

The purpose of this study is to create a dataset to alleviate the lack of resources for an underserved language and to encourage researchers to focus on sequence-to-sequence deep learning techniques, which eliminate the need for thorough pre and post processing in OBR systems. As a result, we published the first publicly available dataset of 2100 Amharic Braille line images with their labels and can be accessed via the link: https://github.com/Ne-UoG-git/Am-Br-line-image.github.io. Furthermore, we contributed by developing an end-to-end trainable CNN-RNN hybrid model for sequence-to-sequence transcription of Amharic braille line images, which eliminates the need for extensive image processing and character segmentation. This is suited for spatiotemporal data issues and eliminates the limitations of statistical feature extraction. We also employed a Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) algorithm to avoid pre and post-processing chores for character encoding and decoding, which was integrated into the RNN module during training. Finally, we tested our proposed approach and received a positive outcome, providing new insights into this problem domain.

Skewness detection and correction of scanned document images is done by calculating the horizontal projection based on horizontal alignment of dots (cells). It is done on sobel filtered grayscale image to extract the bare bones (edges) to emphasize dots in the image by eliminating any noise. Horizontal projection array of the inverted image is implemented on sobel filter image at rotation angles between − 10 and 10. Then we have calculated the median of each projection array in each angle and pick the one which has the highest median, which is the angle of rotation that create bigger horizontal white regions. Finally we have rotated the image by this angle, which has highest median of horizontal projection array.

Proposed model

The classical machine learning techniques ANN and other statistical classifiers such as Support Vector Machines (SVM), K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN), and Random Forests (RN) and so on, have been implemented for various applications. In recent years, deep learning has become the familiar technique, overshadowing the classical machine learning algorithms, due to its superior performance on wide ranges of tasks including speech, natural language, vision, and playing games.

Generally for processing of both spatially and temporally data like image captioning, question answering, and optical character recognition, researchers agree for using CNNs and RNNs in combination30. Accordingly, we have proposed a CNN-RNN hybrid model, which is illustrated in Fig. 5. In this framework, we employed three modules; CNN for feature extractor, RNN for sequence learner, and CTC as transcriber module. All three of these modules are integrated into a single framework and trained in an end-to-end fashion.

Fig. 5The proposed CRNN model.

The proposed CRNN model.

We have proposed a hybrid network model that is well adopted to spatio-temporal data problems. According to31, for image based (spatial structure) problems, CNNs are the best solutions and for sequence-based (sequential structure) data, RNNs are the most suited recognition32. The CNNs and RNNs are trained together in a supervised manner using braille line image and the corresponding ground-truth sequence of train dataset. At the top the Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) network is employed, which is responsible for alignment of output activations with the ground truth, error calculation, and transcription of the softmax prediction into labels. All these three modules are integrated into a single framework and trained in an end-to-end fashion, as illustrated in Fig. 6.

Sequence-to-sequence learning for OBR

Sequence-to-Sequence Learning is a means of training the model that is able to map an input sequence to an output sequence18,33. The use of CRNNs in combination with the CTC approach has gained significant traction for many sequence to sequence problems. This study focuses on employing a CTC-based sequence approach for Braille recognition, specifically avoiding segmentation and character-level alignment.

CTC in sequence learning

The connectionist temporal classification (CTC) is an algorithm introduced by Graves34 used for training RNNs such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, which enables end-to-end sequence learning by maximizing the probability of correctly recognizing sequences during training. This study34 describes a novel method for training RNNs to label unsegmented sequences directly, overcoming RNN’s requirements for pre-segmented training data and post-processing to convert their outputs into label sequences. Researchers conducted experiments on the TIMIT speech corpus, demonstrating its advantages over both a baseline HMM and a hybrid HMM-RNN.

For CTC-based sequence learning, the input is a sequence of observations, and the output is a sequence of labels with blank tokens as an additional output34. However Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are great for processing sequential data, the output of a RNN needs a lot of post-processing to match it with appropriate labels, and that is where the Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) layer is able to help us for these applications. It transforms the outputs of the RNN into a conditional probability distribution over the possible label sequences. So CTC enables the output of the RNN to be directly usable without requiring additional pre or post processing steps. It tries all possible alignments of the Ground Truth text in the input and takes the sum of all scores, thereby making it alignment free.

The Ground Truth text is encoded in a special way to address problems related to duplicate characters and characters spanning across multiple time-steps. To handle the case of blank character at a time-step we use a special character and call it blank (-). When encoding a text, we can insert arbitrary many blanks at any position, which would be removed when decoding it. We can repeat each character as many times as we want. For a given Ground Truth alignment, the Neural Network outputs a matrix containing a score for each character at each time-step. The score for entire text is calculated by multiplying the corresponding character scores together. This gives us the probability of a particular Ground Truth alignment.

Experiments are run following the network architecture and classification settings parameters such as kernel size and stride for each layer and the corresponding feature maps for input image dimension of 48 × 256 as described in Fig. 6.

We applied a reshape operation using a squeeze function on the output sequence from the feature extractor (CNN) layer and obtained the sequence of 63 vectors of 512 elements prepared for input to the LSTM layers. Training and validation

Once we build the model with Keras Application Program Interface (API) on a TensorFlow backend, variable length line-images have been resized to fixed length, since the deep learning framework, Keras implementation, used in this study requires a limited sequence length and to make it easy for batch processing. And also minimizing the line-image lengths help to decrease computational cost for processing. Accordingly, the length of all input text-line images is normalized to a fixed size. As well as, after we read the equivalent text labels, we find the unique number of characters to decide our class size and the labels are encoded to numbers. Then all label lengths are padded with zero until they reach maximum label sequence length, in our database which is 24.

Conclusions

There are several braille documents because braille is the most preferred method for visually impaired persons to formalize their knowledge. As a result, numerous attempts have been made on OBR systems to bridge the textual communication gap between visually impaired persons and visible people. In terms of braille, most studies on OBR systems focus on extensive pre-processing approaches. The majority of attempts are made at character level recognition, with performance dependent on the success of character segmentation algorithms. In this study, we created an enhanced deep learning method suitable for spatiotemporal data that can transcribe line images rather than recognizing each letter image individually. We addressed the problem of Amharic Braille Document Recognition by avoiding the most difficult obstacles in Amharic OBR by utilizing the Sequence-to-Sequence learning approach.

Our main contribution was algorithmic, in which we created an end-to-end trainable CNN-RNN hybrid model for sequence-to-sequence transcription of Amharic braille line images, eliminating the requirement for comprehensive image processing and character segmentation and allowing us to perform better. This is appropriate for spatiotemporal data challenges and overcomes the constraints of statistical feature extraction. In addition, we used a Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) algorithm to avoid pre and post-processing duties for character encoding and decoding, which was integrated into the RNN module during training. We trained the model using our supplied datasets, and we utilized the Adaptive Gaussian Thresholding approach for line image binarization. We trained our suggested model with image dimensions of 32 × 256 and 48 × 256, adjusting the learning rate between 0.001 and 0.0001, and we received encouraging results. Despite the fact that deep learning algorithms require large training datasets, the proposed algorithm outperforms when trained with tiny datasets taken from real-world documents without the use of noise removal techniques.

Furthermore, to address the shortage of datasets in this field, we provided the first publicly available dataset of 2100 Amharic braille line images with their labels. The dataset can be accessed freely via the link: https://github.com/Ne-UoG-git/Am-Br-line-image.github.io. Finally, we believe that this study creates new insights to solve gaps encountered in most attempts done on Amharic braille character recognition and possibilities to extend the current work are presented in the following section.

Recommendation and future work

As we have reviewed, studies on braille document recognition face challenges in preprocessing and post-processing tasks. Based on our investigation, a CNN-RNN hybrid sequence-to-sequence learning approach is the best solution for this problem domain, which deals with spatio-temporal data. We have obtained promising results without the need for any preprocessing or noise removal. Applying appropriate noise removal techniques could further increase the accuracy of the proposed model. However, the employed CTC transcription algorithm performs better in avoiding pre and post-processing tasks and efficient loss calculation. Still, it predicts target characters for every frame without making assumptions about conditional dependencies in target characters. To further improve the results, advanced decoders30 such as beam-search decoding, prefix-search decoding, or token passing can be used, which take into account language structure information.

For future work, it would be beneficial to extend this study to double-sided braille documents by applying more advanced processing and line segmentation techniques to identify the verso and recto dots. As this study focuses on the algorithmic contribution, and the data used for training is real data that contains errors, applying the best error removal techniques could increase the accuracy. However deep learning algorithms need a large number of training dataset, the proposed model was trained in small dataset and can only 238 unique Amharic characters have been included. Training the model with a large dataset that contains all characters in Amharic script will be a future work.

Research on the influence of micro-pile layout of stability of landslide resistance
Authors: Hongru Li, Min Yang, Zhongkai Lan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Although traditional landslide mitigation measures, such as conventional anti-sliding piles, benefit from relatively mature technology and design principles, they still present disadvantages including high cost, limited applicability in certain environments, and slow construction speed. As a new type of retaining structure, micro-piles have gained widespread recognition in engineering practice due to their unique advantages: rapid installation, flexible layout, minimal workspace requirements, and high load-bearing capacity. However, research on the mechanical behavior and support effects corresponding to different micro-pile configurations remains relatively limited in China. This paper takes a landslide of a resettlement project in Haiyuan County, Gansu Province as the research object, analyzing the safety factor, pile bending moment, and seismic acceleration response at monitoring points to comprehensively assess the influence of micro-pile layout on landslide stability. The research results show that: (1) The arrangement of micro-piles is relatively sensitive to the impact of pile bending moments, while its effect on the overall safety factor and acceleration response is comparatively minor. (2) The quincunx layout enhances load-sharing coordination among the piles, allowing the anti-sliding capacity of each row to be fully mobilized. This integrates the pile group better become a whole, playing a crucial role in preventing landslide reactivation. (3) The optimal arrangement scheme for micro-piles is a quincunx layout and each row of piles is arranged unequally, which facilitates the development of a soil arching effect and promotes composite action between the pile group and the surrounding soil mass. The results of this study provide a theoretical basis for informing the design and construction of micro-piles in landslide mitigation projects.

Introduction

China is a country frequently affected by landslides and rockfalls. According to incomplete statistics, economic losses caused by these geological hazards amount to US$3–5 billion annually, underscoring the critical importance of research on landslide prevention and mitigation technologies. As a highly effective measure, retaining structures have seen particularly rapid development and application. Common examples include conventional anti-slide piles, retaining walls, and pre-stressed anchor cable anti-slide piles. Through extensive engineering practice, the associated technologies and design methodologies for these structures have become highly mature. However, these traditional measures also reveal certain limitations, such as slow construction speed, high cost, and restricted applicability in specific environments. In contrast, micro-piles, as a new type of retaining structure, have garnered significant attention within the engineering community due to their unique performance characteristics.

Micro-piles, first proposed by the Italian engineer Fernando Lizzi in the 1950s, are small-diameter bored piles generally not exceeding 30 cm in diameter. The pile structure comprises pressure-grouted cement mortar or fine-aggregate concrete combined with reinforcement materials1,2. Compared to conventional mitigation measures, micro-piles offer advantages such as rapid installation, flexible layout positioning, minimal workspace requirements, high load-bearing capacity, and minimal settlement3, which has been widely used in landslide prevention, deep foundation pit excavation and support, slope seismic reinforcement and other aspects4. Ma et al.5used the scheme of micro-pile combined with retaining wall in one of the excavation sections of Qianjiang-Pengshui section of Yuxiang Expressway, and achieved certain results. In the slippery slope control of K31 mileage in the middle section of Zaba-Hacheng Highway in Qinghai Province, Sun et al.6adopted the implementation of the micro-pile treatment scheme, which not only shortened the construction period, but also reduced the capital investment.

Although micro-piles have a long history of application and are supported by substantial empirical experience, the mechanism of slope reinforcement by micro-piles in slopes has lagged considerably.

Sun et al.7 carried out the model test of the centrifugal vibration platform to analyze and discuss the seismic response characteristics of the micro-pile-cantilever retaining wall supporting slope. Hu et al.8 carried out large-scale model test on the reinforced rock slope of micro-piles, and studied the double row single pile and combination of micro anti-slide piles. During the anti-slide characteristics of piles in the slope reinforcement, they found that the reinforcement of combined pile was better, and there were three damage methods of micro anti-slide piles: Pile bending, pile soil empty, pile fracture. Through the centrifugal model test, Li et al.9 conducted a series of centrifuge model tests to observe and compare the deformation response of shallow foundations and the underlying subsoil under both reinforced (with micro-piles) and unreinforced conditions, thereby analyzing the mechanisms governing the reinforcement of shallow foundations using micro-piles. Huang et al.10 conducted uplift tests on single and group piles of micro-piles in soft soil foundation to study the influence of construction technology on the uplift bearing capacity of micro-piles, as well as the distribution of axial force and lateral friction resistance of single piles. Xin et al.11 conducted three sets of large-scale physical model tests on the reinforcement of cohesive soil slopes with single row and three row micro anti slide piles research, and analyzed the failure mechanism and failure mode of micro anti slide piles in soil slopes. Ma et al.12 carried out large-scale indoor model tests were conducted using grouting conventional piles and grouting flower tube piles for slope protection in the Southwest mountainous region, with a focus on the emergency rescue project of a natural gas pipeline landslide. Sun et al.13 compared and analyzed the characteristics of micro-pile groups and ordinary anti slide piles through indoor experiments, and disscussed the landslide thrust research on the force mechanism of micro-pile groups under force action and the feasibility of replacing ordinary anti slip Piles with micro-pile groups. Wang et al.14 conducted a large-scale physical model test to simulate and analyze the deformation and failure process of a slope reinforced with novel polymer micro-piles under top-loading conditions. This was combined with Digital Image Correlation (DIC) technology to capture the full-field displacements of both the slope and the pile bodies. Yan et al.15 studied the landslide thrust of micro-pile group and the resistance distribution of the soil mass in loess landslide through the large-scale model test. Chen et al.16 analyzed and disscussed the anti-sliding characteristics of the high-pressure grouting steel-tube micro-pile under the real stress state through the full-scale experiment. Through the model test, Liu et al.17 studied the variation law of internal force of micro-pile system and the stress condition of each row of piles caused by landslide thrust. Ren et al.18 compared the filed test results with the theoretical calculation results, discussed the pile group effect and vertical bearing of micro-pile group foundation in silty clay formation. Zhu et al.19 carried out the in-situ test of the mico pile group, and analyzed the reinforcement effect and stress characteristics of the slope. Li et al.20analyzed the acceleration response law of landslides under earthquake action, the distribution characteristics of soil pressure in front and behind the pile body, the stress situation and failure mode of the pile body. Xiao et al.21 conducted a segmented study on the internal forces of the composite anti slide structure of micro-piles. The sliding surface was used as the interface, and after equivalent decomposition, the m method was used for analysis. The lower part of the sliding surface was calculated using the K method, meeting the engineering requirements for calculation results.

Early research on the mechanical behavior of micro-piles primarily relied on model and in-situ tests, focusing on pile forces and soil deformation. However, the influence of pile arrangement on stress and deformation received little attention.

With the rapid development of computer technology, more and more finite element numerical simulation software has been developed, which is a new type of micro-pile. The research on retaining structures plays an important role, such as Feng et al.22 proposing the interaction mode and force calculation analysis model of pile- rock soil mass-pile, which can simulate the transmission mechanism of landslide thrust between each row of piles. The model was applied to the reinforcement calculation of layered rock slopes on the Chongqing Huaihua Railway and achieved good results. Jeng et al.23 analyzed that finite-element analyses were performed using the shear-strength reduction method for limited equilibrium analysis (i.e., factor of safety). Based on a dynamic visco-elastoplastic constitutive model (VEP model), Xie et al.24 investigated the seismic interaction mechanism between micro-piles and landslides, with the aim of determining an optimal pile spacing configuration. Chen et al.25 used finite element software to numerically simulate on-site flexible micro-pile tests, exploring the working performance of micro-piles under horizontal loads, analyzing the influence of various parameters of micro-piles on their horizontal bearing capacity, and the reasonable range of elastic modulus values.

With the advancement of numerical simulation techniques, research on micro-pile reinforced slopes has emerged. However, these numerical studies were often focused on analyzing the behavior of a single pile.

In summary, the research of micro-piles is mainly based on model tests, focusing on design theory, practical application technology of micro-piles, the stress analysis and support reinforcement of piles under various conditions. The investigations specifically addressing the seismic performance and reinforcement efficacy of different arrangements of micro-piles remain relatively scarce. For the above deficiencies, this study takes a landslide case from a resettlement project in Haiyuan County, Gansu Province as the research object. Utilizing the finite element method, this paper conduct static and dynamic analyses by employing the Duncan-Chang model26–28 and an equivalent linear model29, respectively. The investigation is carried out by examining the factor of safety, bending moments in the piles, and seismic acceleration responses at monitoring points. This work provides a comprehensive analysis of how micro-pile configuration influences landslide stabilization, thereby offering a theoretical reference for the design and construction of micro-piles in future landslide prevention engineering projects.

Parameter calculation analysis

This article adopts finite element software modeling and uses the Duncan Zhang nonlinear elastic model to conduct nonlinear static calculation and analysis of landslides; Perform dynamic calculation and analysis of this landslide using an equivalent linear model. Due to the fact that a landslide enters an unstable state in an instant under impact loads, it does not necessarily indicate complete failure of the landslide. Therefore, evaluating the seismic stability of a landslide using the minimum safety factor that occurs throughout the entire earthquake duration is not rigorous30. So it is necessary to comprehensively consider and analyze the minimum and average safety factors.

Parameter calculation of micro-pile

The slope was reinforced with micro-piles having a diameter of 300 mm. The reinforcement cage for each pile consisted of three 32-mm-diameter Grade II thread steel bars, and the piles were cast from C30 concrete. For computational and modeling convenience, the micro-piles were designed as square sections and idealized as a homogeneous material, necessitating an equivalent stiffness conversion33. Furthermore, given that flexural failure is the primary failure mode for micro-piles, a critical bending moment threshold was defined. A micro-pile is considered to have failed if the calculated bending moment exceeds this threshold; otherwise, its structural integrity is maintained.

Determination of position of micro-pile

The location of micro-piles plays a critical role in the stability of reinforced slopes. Usually, the upper section of a landslide, characterized by a steeper slip surface, is unsuitable for pile installation. However, the middle and lower sections generally exhibit gentler slip surface gradients, and the sliding force is relatively small, often providing more favorable conditions for generating stabilizing resistance. Therefore, anti-sliding piles are commonly installed in the mid to lower portions of the slip surface in practical engineering. Accordingly, the micro-piles were positioned within a zone located 20 m from the slope toe and 50 m from the slope crest in this study. Based on a comprehensive analysis of the major principal stress nephograms at the micro-pile group along the slip surface at time 17.3s for the four schemes (with the top of the figures indicating the slope crest direction and the bottom the slope toe direction), the analyses are made regarding the stress distribution. The major principal stress contours for Schemes 1 to 4 are shown in Figs. 11, 12, 13 and 14, respectively. Figures 11, 12, 13 and 14 show that after micro-pile installation, the stress within the soil at the slip zone intersecting the piles is significantly reduced, albeit to varying degrees across the schemes. The main stress reduction in Scheme 2 was the largest, while Scheme 3 shows the least. With the exception of Scheme 2, the other three schemes exhibit stress concentration to varying degrees, most prominently in Scheme 1. Comparison of Scheme 3 and Shceme 4 revealed that the overall stress level in Scheme 3 is higher. The main reason was that the overall spacing between each row of piles in Scheme 4 was 0.2 m smaller than that in Scheme 3, resulting in a denser pile arrangement compared to Scheme 3. Therefore, a greater proportion of stress is transferred from the front to the rear rows in Scheme 3. The quincunx layout demonstrates the most effective reinforcement performance. No significant stress concentration is observed between the piles, and the stress distribution follows a distinct pattern: ahead of the first row, stress is distributed in a layered manner. Starting from the first row, as the inter-pile soil stress shifts towards the rear, it is directly obstructed by the second row of micro-piles. It is then diverted laterally before continuing towards the next row, where the process repeats until reaching the fourth row. This indicates that the quincunx layout does not have a one-time effect on the reduction of landslide stress, but rather a stepwise process achieved through the interaction between successive pile rows. This arrangement facilitates the development of soil arching effects between the rows, which can better make each row of piles a whole. Consequently, the landslide support effect of the quincunx layout is better than parallel arrangement.

Acceleration analysis of seismic response

Acceleration of seimic response is an important criterion when studying landslide stability. Wang et al.34 analyzed acceleration patterns at different locations within a pile group using acceleration sensors and compared them with the acceleration response of a homogeneous slope without weak layers to evaluate the stabilizing effect of micro-piles. Takano et al. found that the seismic intensity on the slope increased by nearly 1 degree compared to the slope toe, with acceleration amplification becoming more pronounced closer to the slope35. Therefore, it is necessary to monitor the acceleration during earthquake. Six monitoring points were selected on the slope: Point 1 at the slope toe; Point 2 at the slip surface exit; Point 3 at the last row of micro-piles; Point 4 at one-third of the distance from the last row of micro-piles to the slope crest; Point 5 at two-thirds of the distance from the last row of micro-piles to the slope crest; and Point 6 at the slope crest. Conclusions

Based on the Duncan-Chang nonlinear model and the equivalent linear model, static and dynamic calculations were performed on the landslide to obtain the static safety factor, minimum safety factor, and average safety factor. By analyzing the layout of micro-piles, the distribution characteristics of the maximum principal stress diagram of the pile bending moment and the seismic response acceleration were compared, leading to the following conclusions:

(1) A comparison of the four schemes reveals that the configuration of micro-piles influences the slope’s safety factor, pile bending moment, and acceleration response. Among them, the influence on the pile bending moment is more sensitive. In contrast, the variations in the safety factor and acceleration response are comparatively minor. This indicates that the pile layout has a less pronounced effect on the overall stability of the reinforced slope. Consequently, for practical engineering purposes, when the total number of piles is fixed, the specific arrangement may not be a primary design concern. quincunx layout.

(2) The layout configuration of micro-piles significantly influences the load distribution among multiple rows. When the quincunx layout was employed, the bending moments measured in the front and rear rows of piles were notably similar. This suggests that the quincunx layout enhances load-sharing coordination between the piles, making the pile group better as a whole, which is crucial for landslide stabilization. This finding indicates that adopting a quincunx layout in slope reinforcement design helps mitigate the uneven distribution of stress and bending moment between front and rear piles. Therefore, it serves as a valuable reference for optimizing engineering design practices.

(3) Observation of the major principal stress nephograms on the slip surface reveals that the quincunx layout facilitates better coordination among the piles, enabling them to mobilize their anti-sliding capacity more effectively. This configuration also improves the overall stress concentration within the pile group. Consequently, the quincunx layout demonstrates superior in landslide stabilization compared to the parallel arrangement.

(4) Based on a comprehensive comparative analysis, the optimal layout scheme of micro-piles is the quincunx layout with each row spacings of 2.0 m, 1.8 m, 1.6 m, and 1.4 m, respectively. This specific layout facilitates the development of a soil arching effect between the piles, thereby integrating the pile group with the surrounding soil mass to function as a whole.

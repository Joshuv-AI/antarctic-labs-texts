Implantation of asymmetrical corneal intrastromal rings: A case series
Authors: Marina S Saito, Ana P C Silva, João M C Ribeiro, Delso Bonfante, Pablo F Rodrigues, Bernardo K Moscovici, Guilherme N C Barboza, Marcello N C Barboza
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This case series reports eight eyes with keratoconus treated with laser implantation of one or two segments of progressive thickness corneal intrastromal ring (PT-ICRS). In this case series, it was evident that the insertion of PT-ICRS induces more pronounced corneal flattening at the thickest point, causing a reduction in distortion (coma) and lower astigmatism, resulting in a remarkable improvement in vision. Compared to the implementation of traditional intrastromal rings, the PT-ICRS variant showed superior results despite the small sample size. However, the same degree of asymmetry enhancement was not observed in cases in which a 330° PT-ICRS was implanted, despite the improvement in visual results when replacing a 320° traditional ring with a 330° PT-ICRS. These conclusions are limited as this is a case series with few cases.

Keratoconus is a progressive, asymmetric, bilateral corneal disease that results in a conical shape, with the development of low visual acuity (LVA) in many cases.[1234]

Keratoconus treatment is divided into strategies to halt the disease progression and to improve visual acuity (VA). To stop the progression, we can use environmental attitudes and medications to avoid rubbing the eyes, and if the progression persists, we can use corneal crosslinking (CXL) as an option. CXL is a surgical procedure that strengthens the cornea by forming collagen covalent bonds after a photochemical reaction between ultraviolet radiation and riboflavin.[2345678]

We have some options to improve visual quality. We can use spectacles and soft contact lenses to correct low-order aberrations. Furthermore, we have rigid contact lenses and surgical options to correct high-order aberrations (HOA) and irregular astigmatism. We can highlight the surgical possibilities of stromal ring implantation (ICRS), topo-guided PRK (t-PRK), and corneal transplantation.[2345678]

LVA occurs due to irregular astigmatism and HOA, usually with corneal asymmetry. In this sense, implanting progressive thickness rings (PT-ICRS), which are thicker in some regions, could present more interesting results.[678]

This case series aims to report eight eyes with keratoconus treated with laser implantation of one or two segments of the PT-ICRS.

Case Series

All patients signed an informed consent form, and this study respects the Declaration of Helsinki and its principles. This research was approved by the institution’s ethics committee (70766523.2.0000.0088).

Patient selection

In all cases, we opted for the implantation of PT-ICRS after the attempt of treatment with a rigid gas-permeable contact lens, which did not present positive results in terms of visual quality or tolerance. As t-PRK is yet to be approved, ICRS or PT-ICRS is our country’s first choice of surgery to improve LVA.

Surgery

We used Keraring (Mediphacos, Belo Horizonte, Brazil) in all cases, and corneal ring parameters were selected as the nomogram depending on the patient’s allocated group.

All surgeries were performed by the same surgeon using the IntraLase IFS femtosecond 150-Hz laser (Johnson&Johnson, New Brunswick, NJ) with a corneal incision depth of 70% of the thinnest point in the tunnel path. Other parameters were optical zone, internal diameter, and external diameter of 5, 4.8, and 5.6 mm, respectively. The energy used for channel creation was 1.4 J, and the entry cut energy was 1.4 J. The procedures were performed using topical anesthetics. All patients received the same postoperative therapy with antibiotic eye drops of moxifloxacin 0.5%, one drop 6/6 h for 07 days, and prednisolone acetate 10 mg/mL 1 drop 6/6 h for 14 days.

Postoperative evaluation: Patients were evaluated on days 1 and 7 with an evaluation of VA and biomicroscopy.

Discussion

As all surgeries were uneventful, PT-ICRS led to favorable visual outcomes. In cases where it was decided to replace it with PT-ICRS, the results increased with this approach.

The enhancement in visual outcomes and quality of life following traditional and long arc rings is well-established. Recent studies indicate that PT-ICRS also demonstrates effectiveness and safety. However, more comprehensive investigations are warranted, particularly those comparing the efficacy of ICRS and PT-ICRS.[12345678910]

The underlying principle guiding the development of PT-ICRS adheres to the Blavastkaya principle: a thicker ring corresponds to greater corneal flattening. Consequently, the implant PT-ICRS should ameliorate corneal asymmetry, thereby reducing comatic aberrations and enhancing visual quality in cases of asymmetric keratoconus.[12345678910]

We observed outcomes consistent with the principles above, revealing flattening disparities ranging from 1.2 D to 3 D between the thicker and thinner regions of the rings in segments measuring equal to or shorter than 180°. However, this principle was not upheld in the 330° segment. However, the two patients exchanging the symmetrical 320° for a PT-ICRS 330° ring improved visual results (gain of line and vision and reduction of 46% in astigmatism).

In this series of cases, it became evident that inserting a PT-ICRS induces more pronounced corneal flattening at the thickest point. This reduction in distortion (coma) and inferior astigmatism culminates in a notable improvement in vision, particularly in cases of keratoconus that resemble pellucid marginal degeneration or exhibit asymmetrical morphologies such as the “croissant,” “duck,” or “snowman.” Arbelaez and Coskunseven and Kayhan studied PT-ICRS 160 arcs in the “duck” phenotype with good results.[1112] Prisant et al.[13] also studied PT-ICRS with good results and improvement in HOA, especially coma.

Our case series has limitations because this was not a randomized controlled trial, and we had a limited sample.

Compared to the implementation of traditional intrastromal rings, the progressive thickness rings variant yields superior results. However, the same degree of enhancement in asymmetry was not witnessed in cases where a 330° PT-ICRS was implanted, despite the improvement in visual results when exchanging an ICRS 320° ring for a PT-ICRS 330°. Consequently, further studies are imperative to corroborate its efficacy.

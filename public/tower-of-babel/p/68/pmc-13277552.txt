Expert Consensus on the Clinical Application of Oral Small-molecule Antiviral Drugs Against COVID-19
Authors: Society of Bacterial Infection and Resistance of Chinese Medical Association, National Clinical Research Center for Respiratory Disease, National Center for Respiratory Medicine, Wei Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough COVID‑19 no longer constitutes a “public health emergency of international concern”, which still has being spreading around the world at a low level. Small molecule drugs are the main antiviral treatment for novel coronavirus recommended in China. Although a variety of small‑molecule antiviral drugs against COVID‑19 have been listed in China, there is no specific drug recommendation for special populations. Society of Bacterial Infection and Resistance of Chinese Medical Association, together with the National Clinical Research Center for Respiratory Disease, and the National Center for Respiratory Medicine, organized domestic experts in various fields such as respiratory, virology, infection, critical care, emergency medicine and pharmacy to release Expert Consensus on the Clinical Application of Oral Small‑Molecule Antiviral Drugs against COVID‑19. The main content of this consensus includes the introduction of seven small‑molecule antiviral drugs against COVID‑19, focusing on the drug recommendations for 14 special groups such as the elderly, patients with complicated chronic diseases, tumor patients, pregnant women, and children, and providing suggestions for clinicians to standardize drug use.Practice guideline registration:International Practice Guideline Registration for Transparency (PREPARE-2023CN617)Keywords: Coronavirus infections, Antiviral therapy, Small molecule drugs, Early stage, Expert consensus

SARS-CoV-2 infection primarily affects the upper respiratory tract and is highly infectious, posing a risk to the whole population. Individuals infected with SARS-CoV-2 are the main source of transmission, with the highest infectiousness occurring within the first 3 days after symptom onset. The main clinical manifestations following infection include dry throat, sore throat, cough, and fever, with most cases presenting moderate- to low-grade fever. In some instances, patients may develop pneumonia-related symptoms as the disease progresses. In severe cases, symptoms such as dyspnea and (or) hypoxemia typically appear 5 to 7 days after onset. These severe cases can rapidly escalate to acute respiratory distress syndrome, septic shock, and multiple organ failure.[1]

For more than three years, SARS-CoV-2 infection has caused serious damage to human health and life around the world. Although the World Health Organization declared on May 5, 2023, that the COVID-19 epidemic no longer constitutes a “public health emergency of international concern,” this does not imply that COVID-19 has ceased to be a major threat to global health. Current epidemiological trends indicate that COVID-19 remains prevalent at a low level globally, with SARS-CoV-2 continuing to mutate and causing a slight increase in incidence in certain areas. In response, the Comprehensive Group of the State Council’s Joint Prevention and Control Mechanism for COVID-19 has issued the “Overall Plan for the Implementation of ‘Class B Disease Class B Management’ for SARS-CoV-2 Infection”. This plan mandates the ongoing implementation of “Class B Disease Class B Management” measures, emphasizing the need for scientifically informed and precise prevention and control strategies. It also calls for continuous improvement in the preparedness of drugs related to the treatment of COVID-19, including anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs, traditional Chinese medicine, symptomatic treatment medications, and other therapeutic preparations, as well as the enhancement of clinical treatment capacities.[2]

At present, seven oral anti-COVID-19 small-molecule drugs have been conditionally approved by the China National Medical Products Administration (NMPA). To promote the rational use of anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs in clinical practice, the Society of Bacterial Infection and Resistance of Chinese Medical Association, in collaboration with the National Center for Respiratory Medicine and the National Clinical Research Center for Respiratory Disease, organized a group of Chinese experts from various fields, including respiratory medicine, virology, infectious diseases, critical care, emergency medicine, and pharmacy. These experts reviewed and analyzed relevant data and discussed issues related to the application of small-molecule drugs against COVID-19. Based on the Diagnosis and Treatment Plan for COVID-19 (Trial Tenth Edition)[1] and combined with clinical experience in China, this expert consensus was compiled. Its aim is to improve clinicians’ understanding of oral small-molecule drugs against COVID-19 and to provide guidance for the standardized use of these medications.

1. Methodology for the development of this consensus

1.1. Literature retrieval

The main keywords searched included: COVID-19, antiviral therapy, small-molecule drugs, early stage, and special population. Literature and other evidence up to February 2024 were obtained through a systematic search, which included (1) the PubMed database; (2) the Wanfang data resource system and CNKI series database; and (3) manually searched evidence related to SARS-CoV-2 infection and antiviral therapy. This comprehensive search incorporated relevant clinical studies, guidelines, consensus statements, reviews, and other literature.

1.2. Methods for forming expert consensus

After thorough discussion and consultation by renowned experts in respiratory medicine, virology, infectious diseases, critical care, emergency medicine, and pharmacy, the recommendations for diagnosis and treatment were summarized.

1.3. Recommendations

The recommendations were determined by the experts through collective discussion and deliberation, and the voting method was adopted when necessary.

1.4. Level of evidence

Based on epidemiological data, evidence-based medical evidence, and clinical research data on COVID-19 from China and internationally, this consensus was formulated using the GRADE (Grading of the Recommendation Assessment, Development, and Evaluation) system. This process involved extensive discussions and consensus-building among experts (Table 1).

2. Overview of different small-molecule drugs against COVID-19

Anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs are used to treat COVID-19 by preventing the replication of SARS-CoV-2. These include inhibitors of 3-chymotrypsin-like protease (also known as master protease, 3CLpro) and inhibitors of RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp) (Figure 1). As of March 4, 2024, NMPA has conditionally approved the marketing of seven oral anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs. Four of these are 3CLpro inhibitors, including nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, simnotrelvir/ritonavir, leritrelvir, and atilotrelvir/ritonavir. The other three are RdRp inhibitors, including molnupiravir, azvudine, and mindeudesivir (VV116).

Mechanism of action of antiviral drugs against COVID-19.[4] ① Shedding of SARS-CoV-2. ② Translation of SARS-CoV-2. ③ Hydrolysis of proteolytic enzymes during the replication of SARS-CoV-2. ④ Replication of SARS-CoV-2 mediated by RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (RdRp). ⑤ Newly synthesized viral nucleic acids and proteins are assembled into new viral particles. 3CLpro: 3-chymotrypsin-like protease.

2.1. Basic information of currently approved anti-novel coronavirus small-molecule drugs

2.1.1. Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir

NMPA conditionally approved for import registration in February 2022, 3CLpro inhibitor. It has similar activity against SARS-CoV-2 variants α, γ, δ, λ, Mu, and Omicron (B.1.1.529), with a median effect concentration (EC50) of 62-186 nmol/L. Phase II/III clinical studies showed that for non-hospitalized patients with severe risk factors, the viral load decreased by about 0.9 lg copy/ml on the 5th day of treatment with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, which reduced the risk of hospitalization or death by 87.8% compared with the placebo group.[5] Most of the adverse events were grade 1 or 2, and safety was good.

2.1.2. Azvudine

On July 25, 2022, the NMPA conditionally approved its application for additional COVID-19 indication registration on an emergency basis, RdRp inhibitors. It showed inhibitory activity against BetaCoV/Wuhan/WIV04/2019 strain with an EC50 of 4.31 μmol/L. Phase III clinical studies showed that within 7 days after the first dose of azvudine, the proportion of patients with clinical improvement was nearly three times higher than that of the placebo group, and the median improvement time of clinical symptoms was shortened by 3 days. Overall safety and tolerability were good.[4]

2.1.3. Molnupiravir

NMPA conditionally approved for import registration on an emergency basis on December 29, 2022, RdRp inhibitor. It has similar inhibitory activity against α, β, γ, δ, λ, Mu, and Omicron (B.1.1.529/BA.1 and BA.1.1) strains with EC50 values of 0.95–2.60 μmol/L. The MOVE-OUT study showed that in mild-to-moderate patients at high risk of severe disease, viral load declined more rapidly in patients treated with molnupiravir compared with placebo, significantly reducing the risk of hospitalization or death by 31%.[6] Most of the adverse events were grade 1 or 2, and safety was good.

2.1.4. Simnotrelvir/ritonavir

Conditional approval by NMPA in January 2023, 3CLpro inhibitor. Its P1, P2, and P4 groups are optimized so that simnotrelvir/ritonavir has stronger 3CLpro inhibitory activity than the original inhibitor. It has inhibitory activity against WIV04, Delta, and Omicron strains (EC50 of 26, 34, and 43 nmol/L, respectively).[7] The Phase II/III clinical trial showed that, compared with the placebo group, the administration of simnotrelvir/ritonavir could shorten the remission time of 11 preset clinical symptoms, reduce the viral load, and shorten the nucleic acid negative conversion time by 2.2 days in patients with severe high-risk subgroups. Most of the adverse events were grade 1 or 2, with good safety.

2.1.5. VV116

Conditionally approved for marketing by NMPA on January 29, 2023, RdRp inhibitor. It is a deuterated triisobutyrate prodrug of the parent nucleoside of remdesivir and is rapidly metabolized to the parent nucleoside (116-N1) in vivo. Intracellular conversion of 116-N1 to a nucleoside triphosphate active form interferes with the function of the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase of SARS-CoV-2, thereby exerting an antiviral effect. The EC50 values of VV116 and 116-N1 were 0.041 μmol/L and 0.186 μmol/L for Omicron variant (B.1.1.529), and 0.189 μmol/L and 0.310 μmol/L for Omicron variant (BA.5.2), respectively. The phase III clinical study showed that, compared with the placebo group, the VV116 group had rapid remission of 11 preset clinical symptoms, shorter median time to first sustained recovery, and better virological indicators in both high and low risk factors for progression to severe disease. Most of the adverse events were grade 1 or 2, and safety was good.

2.1.6. Leritrelvir

Approved for marketing with conditions by NMPA on March 23, 2023, 3CLpro inhibitor. The three groups from P1 to P3 were optimized, which effectively delayed the dissociation rate in the protease inhibitor complex and significantly prolonged the residence time of the drug target.[8] EC50 values for different SARS-CoV-2 variants were 95 nmol/L (WT), 130 nmol/L (α), 277 nmol/L (β), 97 nmol/L (δ), 86 nmol/L (Omicron BA.1), and 158 nmol/L (Omicron BA.5).[8] The phase II study showed that compared with the placebo group, the negative conversion time of viral nucleic acid was shortened by 4.2 days.[9] The phase III clinical study showed that, compared with the placebo group, the 11 preset clinical symptoms of the leritrelvir group were relieved rapidly, and the median time to first sustained recovery was shortened by about 1 day, which was also observed in the high-risk subgroup and the baseline viral load subgroup. Most of the adverse events were grade 1 or 2, and safety was good.

2.1.7. Atilotrelvir/ritonavir

Approved for marketing with conditions by NMPA on November 24, 2023, 3CLpro inhibitor. It has broad-spectrum anti-SARS-CoV-2 activity against different SARS-CoV-2 variants (including WT, β, δ, and Omicron).[10] The phase Ⅲ clinical study showed that, compared with the placebo group, the 11 preset clinical symptoms in the atilotrelvir/ritonavir group were relieved rapidly, and the median time to first sustained recovery was shortened by 2 days; the viral load was also reduced rapidly, and the median time to nucleic acid negative conversion was shortened by 2 days. Most of the adverse events were grade 1 or 2, and safety was good.

3. Recommendations for the use of small-molecule drugs against COVID-19 in special populations

Elderly people, people with chronic diseases, people with cancer, pregnant women, and other special groups may face a higher risk of hospitalization or death than the general population after being infected with SARS-CoV-2 due to their low immune function,[12] so they need active antiviral treatment. Although a variety of anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs have been marketed in China, there is no specific drug recommendation for special populations in China at present, so this consensus will focus on some drug recommendations for special populations for clinical reference.

3.1. Severe/critically ill population

Recommendation 1: For severe patients within 5 days of onset, it is recommended to use anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs. Anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs should be recommended for severe patients with more than 5 days of onset but still a high viral load (Ct value < 30), and the use of anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs should be extended appropriately. (2D)

Explanation of recommendation: Although there is no clear evidence-based medical evidence that existing anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs can treat severe/critically ill patients with COVID-19, some clinical studies also suggest that reducing viral load may improve the prognosis of severe/critically ill patients.

Severe patients with multiple comorbidities or low immune function have a higher viral load on admission than severe patients without comorbidities or normal immune function.[13] The duration of viral shedding is long, which can be as long as 40 days,[14] and the time of viral shedding and viral load are closely related to the prognosis of patients. Studies have found that 42.2% of critically ill patients have persistent viral shedding, which is significantly associated with an increased risk of death at 6 months.[15] High viral load (Ct value <25) is associated with an increased risk of hospitalization within 30 days, intensive care unit admission, and mortality.[16] There is considerable heterogeneity in the duration of SARS-CoV-2 replication in the upper and lower airways, and in most mechanically ventilated patients, persistent viral replication and excessive pro-inflammatory responses occur simultaneously, rather than sequentially, as commonly believed.[17] Therefore, the use of antiviral drugs in critically ill patients is still of great significance, and the use of antiviral drugs within 5 days of onset is more likely to benefit. For severe patients who have been ill for more than 5 days but still have a high viral load (Ct value <30), the use of antiviral drugs may still be beneficial.[18] However, severe patients are often accompanied by a variety of complications, and adverse drug reactions and interactions with other drugs, especially those medicine containing ritonavir, should be closely monitored during medication. Considering the interaction between ritonavir and multiple drugs, which may affect the efficacy or increase the risk of adverse reactions when combined, special attention needs to be paid to drug interaction screening.

3.2. People with cardiovascular diseases

Recommendation 2: For people with cardiovascular disease infected with SARS-CoV-2, it is recommended that they use anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs in the early stages of infection, and pay attention to drug interactions. (1A)

Explanation of Recommendation: Patients with cardiovascular diseases, such as hypertension, have a significantly increased risk of severe illness and death after being infected with SARS-CoV-2.[19] Studies have shown that for patients with cardiovascular disease infected with SARS-CoV-2, the use of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir or molnupiravir in the early stages of infection can shorten the time to reach low viral load (Ct value ≥ 30) by about 2.5 days, reduce the risk of progression of complex disease by about 40%, and reduce the risk of all-cause mortality by more than 50%.[20] However, the combination of some small-molecule drugs with cardiovascular disease drugs increases the risk of drug interactions.[21] Most antihypertensive drugs of calcium channel blocker (CCB) are mainly metabolized by CYP3A4, and their combination with simnotrelvir/ritonavir, nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, or atilotrelvir/ritonavir may increase the blood concentration of the drug and increase the risk of adverse reactions. Some ARB drugs (irbesartan, losartan) and torasemide are mainly metabolized by CYP2C9, which can produce certain interactions when combined, and need to be monitored.[11] Therefore, people with cardiovascular disease need to focus on drug interactions when choosing COVID-19 treatment options.

3.3. People with diabetes

Recommendation 3: For diabetic patients infected with SARS-CoV-2, it is recommended to use anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs in the early stages of infection, and attention should be paid to drug interactions. (1A)

Explanation of Recommendation: Patients with diabetes mellitus infected with SARS-CoV-2 have a significantly increased risk of severe illness and death.[19] Study has shown that oral nirmatrelvir/ritonavir or molnupiravir significantly reduces the risk of all-cause death and hospitalization in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus by 29% compared with placebo.[22] Another study showed a 56% reduction in the risk of severe illness or death in diabetic patients with the use of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir.[23] However, the combination of some anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs with diabetes drugs increases the risk of drug interactions.[21] Therefore, people with diabetes also need to focus on drug interactions when choosing treatment options.

3.4. People with long-term COVID-19

Recommendation 4: For people with long-term COVID-19, anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs can be selected as appropriate after symptomatic treatment and full consideration of the benefit-risk ratio. (1D)

Explanation of Recommendation: Long COVID-19 is a multisystem disease after SARS-CoV-2 infection, with common symptoms including fatigue, shortness of breath, cough, and cognitive dysfunction, which can last for weeks, months, or years.[24] At present, a small number of studies have reported that the use of anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs in the early stages of infection can reduce the risk of long COVID-19. Cohort studies have shown that the use of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir in patients with severe risk factors can reduce the risk of developing long COVID-19 by 26%.[25] Other studies have also demonstrated that patients taking nirmatrelvir/ritonavir or molnupiravir early in infection can reduce the risk of developing long COVID-19.[26] A prospective cohort study showed that administration of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir after SARS-COV-2 infection reduced the risk of developing long COVID-19 6 months after discharge.[27] At present, there is no effective treatment for long COVID-19, and the clinical diagnosis and treatment are mostly empirical symptomatic treatments. A very small number of case reports have shown that anti-SARS-COV-2 small-molecule drugs can improve the symptoms of COVID-19. In another patient with long COVID-19 lasting for 2 years, the symptoms of COVID-19 were relieved by taking nirmatrelvir/ritonavir after re-infection with SARS-COV-2.[28] In the future, large sample clinical studies are still needed to confirm the efficacy and safety of small-molecule drugs against COVID-19 in populations with long COVID-19.

3.5. Population with rebound positivity

Recommendation 5: It takes a long time for patients with rebound positivity to turn negative. It is suggested that patients with rebound positivity should use anti-SARS-COV-2 small-molecule drugs with different mechanisms in combination or alternately to speed up the negative conversion of patients. There is no safety data for combination therapy, and clinical applications need to weigh the advantages and disadvantages and closely monitor possible adverse reactions. (2D)

Explanation of Recommendation: Patients with COVID-19 may have recurrence of viral infection symptoms within 2 to 8 days after initial recovery or become positive again after turning negative.[29] Retrospective studies have shown that it takes a long time for patients with rebound positivity to turn negative, and 95% and 45% of patients need 21 days and 42 days, respectively, to turn negative completely.[30] Long-term positive patients (persistent positive 53 to 112 days after the first symptom) may be associated with decreased gamma interferon produced by natural killer cells and low neutralizing antibodies.[31] It is suggested that anti-SARS-COV-2 small-molecule drugs with different mechanisms should be used in combination or alternately in patients with rebound positivity to reduce viral load and turn nucleic acid negative as soon as possible.[4]

3.6. Immunosuppressed population

Recommendation 6: For immunosuppressed people infected with SARS-CoV-2, the patient’s disease and the use of immunosuppressants should be considered comprehensively. Anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs should be selected reasonably in the early stages of infection, and the course of treatment of anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs should be extended appropriately according to the negative conversion of patients. Prolonged treatment may increase the risk of adverse reactions and requires close monitoring. (1A)

Explanation of Recommendation: Immunosuppressed people refer to people with low immune function caused by congenital immune deficiency or secondary immune suppression, such as solid organ transplant recipients (SOTRs) and patients taking immunosuppressants. Their risk of severe illness and death is significantly increased after SARS-CoV-2 infection. Studies have shown that nirmatrelvir/ritonavir can significantly reduce the risk of severe illness/death in immunosuppressed people by 71%, with good safety.[23] Other studies have also shown that treatment with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir or molnupiravir reduces the risk of death from SOTRs.[32]

Because of the long-term use of immunosuppressants, some SOTRs will continue to be positive for SARS-CoV-2 nucleic acid even after receiving antiviral therapy, but not all patients need to receive antiviral therapy again. When the Ct value of nucleic acid detection for SARS-CoV-2 is more than 30, the possibility of virus replication or transmission is low. Therefore, continuation of antiviral therapy is recommended only when the Ct value is ≤ 30. Combined with case reports and the clinical experience of experts, it is suggested that the original drug treatment time should be extended appropriately. In addition, other antiviral drugs with different mechanisms of action can also be switched or combined.[33]

When using anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs, attention should be paid to the interaction with immunosuppressants such as cyclosporine, tacrolimus, and sirolimus. It has been reported in the literature that serious adverse reactions requiring hospitalization occurred when SOTR continued to take tacrolimus when using nirmatrelvir/ritonavir.[34] Therefore, in the course of clinical treatment, we should focus on the combination of drugs and drug interactions. However, if SOTR receiving immunosuppressant therapy such as cyclosporine, tacrolimus, and sirolimus needs to take Reltevir, it is not recommended to stop immunosuppressant therapy.

3.7. Population with tumors

Recommendation 7: For patients with tumors infected with SARS-CoV-2, the patient’s condition and the use of anti-tumor drugs should be considered comprehensively, and anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs should be selected reasonably in the early stages of infection to reduce the risk of severe illness and death. (1A)

Explanation of Recommendation: Patients with tumors face higher risks of severe illness and death after infection with SARS-CoV-2. Early antiviral treatment can reduce the risk of severe illness and death. Prospective studies have shown that antiviral therapy with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir or molnupiravir is associated with a low risk of severe illness and a good safety profile in patients with hematologic malignancies infected with SARS-CoV-2.[35] The EPICOVIDEHA study showed that treatment with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir reduced the risk of death in patients with hematologic malignancies by 81.8%.[36] However, special attention should be paid to the interaction between chemotherapy, targeted drugs, and antiviral drugs during antitumor therapy for patients with tumors. For example, neratinib, venetoclax, apalutamide, dabrafenib, regorafenib, enzalutamide, lorlatinib, and toremifene are not allowed to be combined with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir.

3.8. Elderly population

Recommendation 8: For elderly people infected with COVID-19, it is recommended that anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs be used in the early stages of infection. Molnupiravir or a single-agent 3CLpro inhibitor (leritrelvir) is recommended in the early stages of infection in elderly patients at risk for drug interactions. (1A)

Explanation of Recommendation: The elderly population is a high-risk group for SARS-CoV-2 infection, and their risk of progression to severe disease after infection is high. Clinical studies have shown that the use of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir[5] or molnupiravir[6] in the early stages of infection in the elderly population can reduce the risk of severe illness and death, with good safety. Real-world studies have shown that taking molnupiravir 5 days before the onset of the disease can reduce the risk of death by 58.5% in patients aged 80 years and over, with good safety[37]; taking Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir within 5 days of the onset of the disease can significantly reduce the risk of hospitalization, with good safety.[38] However, the elderly population is prone to multiple diseases, such as hypertension and diabetes, and multi-drug combinations increase the risk of drug interactions.[21] Therefore, safety is a key concern when selecting treatment options. Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir may interfere with CYP3A enzyme activity, and there are many risks related to drug interactions. Before use, patients should be asked in detail about their diseases and drug use. Leritrelvir is a 3CLpro inhibitor, and its chemical structure formula has been optimized, which effectively delays its dissociation rate in the protease inhibitor complex, effectively avoids the risk of drug interaction caused by the combination of ritonavir, and has higher clinical safety.[8] The phase III clinical study shows that the use of leritrelvir in the elderly population is safe and effective. Based on the limited known data, no clinically significant drug interactions with molnupiravir have been identified. Therefore, it is recommended that elderly patients using multiple drugs choose leritrelvir or molnupiravir in the early stages of COVID-19. Clinical trials of azvudine and VV116 included a limited number of elderly subjects, and there is no data on the use of simnotrelvir/ritonavir and atilotrelvir/ritonavir in the elderly, so the use of these anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs in the elderly population needs to be closely monitored.

3.9. Pregnant women

Recommendation 9: For pregnant women infected with SARS-CoV-2, it is recommended that after fully assessing the risks and benefits, they can choose to take nirmatrelvir/ritonavir for antiviral treatment in the early stages of infection. Leritrelvir should only be used when it is determined that the benefits outweigh the risks, and other anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs are not recommended. (2D)

Explanation of Recommendation: Pregnant women have a higher risk of hospitalization after being infected with SARS-CoV-2.[39] Small-molecule anti-SARS-CoV-2 drugs currently on the market are extremely limited in the observation data obtained by pregnant women. Preclinical studies have shown that nirmatrelvir/ritonavir and leritrelvir have no relevant effects on animal embryonic and fetal development. Observational studies have shown that the average duration of COVID-19-related symptoms was shorter in pregnant women treated with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir than in the controls. No serious adverse events were observed in the study group, with dysgeusia and diarrhea being the most common adverse reactions. A higher rate of cesarean section was observed in the nirmatrelvir/ritonavir group, requiring long-term follow-up of pregnant women and their offspring using nirmatrelvir/ritonavir.[40] Relevant large samples of safety data are still needed in the future, so it is recommended that pregnant women should conduct a risk and benefit assessment before using it. The preclinical reproductive toxicity study of leritrelvir in rats did not show evidence of developmental toxicity and teratogenic toxicity to fetuses. However, there is no safety data related to the use of leritrelvir in clinical trials. Therefore, when it is necessary to use this product, obtaining full informed consent is required, and this product can only be used when the benefits outweigh the risks. Preclinical reproductive toxicity studies of molnupiravir, VV116, azvudine, and atilotrelvir/ritonavir have shown that these drugs can cause damage to rat embryos, and it is recommended that these drugs should not be used during pregnancy.

3.10. Lactating women

Recommendation 10: It is not recommended for lactating women to take any anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs. (1D)

Explanation of Recommendation: At present, there is no relevant data on the use of anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs in lactating women, so it is recommended that women should not use these drugs during lactation. If the condition is severe, the benefits of taking anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs are significantly greater than the risks, and it is prudent to take molnupiravir, VV116, leritrelvir, nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, and simnotrelvir/ritonavir. However, do not breastfeed during treatment and for 4 days after the last dose of molnupiravir, and do not breastfeed during treatment and for 7 days after the last dose of VV116, leritrelvir, nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, simnotrelvir/ritonavir, and atilotrelvir/ritonavir.

3.11. Children

Recommendation 11: For children infected with SARS-CoV-2, it is forbidden to take molnupiravir, and other anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs should be used with caution. (2D)

Explanation of Recommendation: The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has approved the use of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir in pediatric patients over 12 years of age.[41] A retrospective study showed that the treatment of nirmatrelvir/ritonavir was effective and relatively safe in 3 children (aged 12-18 years) with COVID-19.[42] Molnupiravir may affect bone and cartilage growth in children and should be contraindicated in children; the safety and effectiveness of other drugs in children have not been established, and caution is recommended.

3.12. People with hepatic and renal insufficiency

Recommendation 12: For patients with hepatic and renal insufficiency infected with SARS-CoV-2, it is recommended to prioritize the use of molnupiravir and leritrelvir in the early stages of infection. However, patients with hepatic insufficiency should be closely monitored for liver function when using leritrelvir. Nirmatrelvir/Ritonavir: no dose adjustment is needed for patients with mild renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 60 to < 90 ml/min) and mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child Pugh A, Grade B); the dose should be halved for patients with moderate renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 30 to < 60 mL/min); it is not recommended for patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min) and severe hepatic impairment. Atilotrelvir/Ritonavir: no dose adjustment is required for patients with mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child Pugh A, Grade B); it is not recommended in patients with severe hepatic and renal impairment. Other anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs are not recommended. (2D)

Explanation of Recommendation: Patients with severe underlying diseases infected with SARS-CoV-2 have a higher rate of severe illness and mortality than the general population.[1] Patients with hepatic and renal insufficiency need to be extra cautious in the selection and use of anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs. Nirmatrelvir is mainly excreted by the kidney, and ritonavir is mainly cleared by the hepatobiliary system. Clinical trial results demonstrated that exposure to nirmatrelvir increased with the severity of renal impairment. The exposure levels in subjects with mild and moderate hepatic dysfunction were equivalent to those in healthy subjects. There was no data on severe hepatic dysfunction. Therefore, it is suggested that patients with mild renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 60 to < 90 mL/min) and mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child Pugh A, grade B) do not need to adjust the dose, and the dose of nirmatrelvir should be halved in patients with moderate renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 30 to < 60 mL/min). It is not recommended for patients with severe renal impairment (eGFR < 30 mL/min) and severe hepatic impairment. Based on the Phase I clinical results of atilotrelvir/ritonavir in subjects with hepatic impairment and normal hepatic function, no dose adjustment is recommended for patients with mild to moderate hepatic impairment (Child Pugh A, Grade B); there is no study data for patients with severe hepatic impairment and renal impairment, so atilotrelvir/ritonavir should not be used in patients with severe hepatic impairment and renal impairment. Molnupiravir is not metabolized by the liver and kidney, and no dose adjustment is required in patients with hepatic and renal insufficiency. Leritrelvir is mainly excreted in the feces in the form of the original drug, and no dose adjustment is required in patients with renal insufficiency. The incidences of two or more liver function abnormalities (mostly elevated alanine aminotransferase and aspartate aminotransferase) were 1.6% and 1.2%, respectively, and most of them were grade 1. It is recommended that liver function be monitored during medication.[11] Simnotrelvir/ritonavir, azvudine, and VV116 have not been studied clinically in patients with hepatic/renal impairment and should be used with caution in people with hepatic/renal impairment.

3.13. Perioperative population

Recommendation 13: Perioperative SARS-CoV-2 infection can significantly increase perioperative mortality and the incidence of surgical complications. Antiviral treatment should be carried out in the early stages of SARS-CoV-2 infection to accelerate negative conversion, improve symptoms as soon as possible, and reduce the incidence of anesthesia and perioperative complications. (2D)

Explanation of Recommendation: Perioperative SARS-CoV-2 infection significantly increases the incidence of perioperative mortality and surgical complications, and prospective cohort studies have shown that postoperative morbidity and mortality also increase if surgery is performed within 6 weeks of COVID-19.[43] Notably, these increased risks persisted up to 7 weeks after COVID-19. Based on this, the Consensus on Timing and Risk Assessment of Elective Surgery after SARS-CoV-2 Infection recommends that elective surgery should be delayed for 7 weeks after COVID-19.[44] It is important to note that even after 7 weeks, patients with persistent symptoms and patients with moderate to severe COVID-19 may still face a great risk of surgical complications and death. Anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs can shorten the duration of negative conversion and symptoms and reduce the risk of severe illness and death.[4] Perioperative patients often have underlying diseases and need to take other drugs. Adverse drug reactions and drug interactions should be closely monitored during medication, especially those containing ritonavir.

3.14. People in need of tube feeding

Recommendation 14: For people who need tube feeding infected with SARS-CoV-2, they can be treated with nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, simnotrelvir/ritonavir, molnupiravir, VV116, and leritrelvir in the early stages of infection. (2D)

Explanation of Recommendation: Tube feeding is the only route of administration through the gastrointestinal tract in patients with coma, swallowing disorders, or gastrointestinal tract access disorders. Such patients have special medication methods, and attention should be paid to the use of various anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs.

Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir can be taken by breaking or grinding, and patients with dysphagia can choose the appropriate way to take the drug.[45] Molnupiravir can be administered by opening the capsule for nasal feeding. Simnotrelvir/ritonavir, VV116, and leritrelvir can be crushed to prepare the suspension for tube feeding. Azvudine and atilotrelvir/ritonavir cannot be ground up, must be swallowed whole on an empty stomach, and are not recommended for patients with tube feeding.

Drugs that inhibit key steps in the viral life cycle are the most direct and least toxic option against acute viral infections. A large number of studies have confirmed that the use of anti-SARS-CoV-2 drugs in the early stages of COVID-19 can effectively reduce the risk of severe illness and death in high-risk groups.[46,47 and 48] However, there is still a lack of evidence-based medical evidence for the efficacy and safety in the severe/critically ill population, and there is still a lack of unified data on the prevention and treatment of COVID-19. In addition, there is still insufficient evidence on the efficacy and safety of anti-SARS-CoV-2 small-molecule drugs for special groups such as immunocompromised people, elderly people, and pregnant women, and larger-scale clinical studies are needed to verify them.

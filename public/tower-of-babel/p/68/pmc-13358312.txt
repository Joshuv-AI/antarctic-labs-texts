Acute kidney injury-epidemiology in intensive care unit patients 2: study protocol for a prospective international multicentre cohort study
Authors: Antoine G Schneider, Wim Vandenberghe, Rinaldo Bellomo, Lui Forni, Michael Joannidis, Niels Jongs, John A Kellum, Marlies Ostermann, John Prowle, Thomas Rimmelé, Emily See, Alexander Zarbock, Sean M Bagshaw, Eric Hoste, Fabian Peschinka

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionReports on the epidemiology and outcomes of acute kidney injury in the critically ill provide variable estimates based on differences in populations and underlying exposures. Current and generalisable incidence data are needed along with information on long-term patient and kidney outcomes.The acute kidney injury-epidemiology in intensive care unit patients 2 (AKI-EPI 2) study aims to describe the occurrence and associated outcomes of AKI in critically ill patients worldwide. In addition, in selected centres, it aims to further describe the influence of AKI on long-term kidney and patient outcomes.Methods and analysisThis is an international prospective, observational study of patients admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU). Each participating centre will record data from 100 consecutive patients fulfilling the following eligibility criteria: age≥18 years, admission to the ICU for more than 24 hours and informed consent according to the local ethics committee (EC). Exclusion criteria include end-stage kidney disease treated with maintenance renal replacement therapy (RRT), readmission to the ICU or missing AKI-defining data. Particular attention will be paid to recruiting centres from all continents and to ensure geographical diversity and representativeness. The target sample is 10 642 patients. The primary endpoint is the occurrence and maximum severity of AKI within 7 days of ICU admission according to 2012 Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria. Secondary endpoints include AKI severity, AKI duration, RRT use and hospital survival. In selected centres, health-related quality of life, major adverse kidney events and vital status will also be captured at day 90 and 1 year from admission.Ethics and disseminationAKI-EPI 2 has been approved by the EC of the Ghent University and University Hospital. Approval will be sought by corresponding ECs for all participating centres.Results will be presented at conferences, disseminated in peer-reviewed journals and will guide future research in the field of critical care nephrology.Trial registrationNCT07207031.

Introduction

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a frequent complication in patients with critical illness supported in intensive care unit (ICU) settings. In the previous iteration of Acute Kidney Injury Epidemiology in ICU patients (AKI-EPI), an international multicentre study, we found that 57.3% of patients with critical illness experienced an episode of AKI within the first week of ICU admission.1 In addition, we observed that AKI was strongly associated with hospital mortality. These findings were subsequently replicated in other large series.26 In the Epidemiology of Surgery-Associated Acute Kidney Injury (EPIS-AKI), a large international study of patients undergoing major surgery (most of whom were not admitted to the ICU), the incidence of AKI was 20%.7

Since the publication of AKI-EPI in 2015, advances in medical knowledge have influenced ICU practice in many ways that may have greatly impacted the incidence and management of AKI. Indeed, in the past decade, the implementation of nephroprotective care bundles has been shown to reduce the risk of AKI in some populations.810 Similarly, several randomised controlled trials (RCTs) have demonstrated that, in the absence of conventional indications, pre-emptive initiation of renal replacement therapy (RRT) could be associated with harm.1113 Whether these findings have translated into significant changes in clinical practice or altered the incidence and consequences of AKI remains unknown. During the same period, we also witnessed major changes in patient demographics (including ageing and increased burden of comorbidities) and the introduction of new therapeutics and surgical techniques.14 Together, these elements are likely to have modified the incidence and outcomes of patients with critical illness and AKI.15

Previous epidemiological studies of AKI had important limitations that need to be addressed. They only minimally reported on acute kidney disease (AKD), a recently described entity corresponding to kidney impairment persisting beyond 7 days.16 They generally lacked long-term outcomes data and provided little insight into RRT practice. Finally, they had limited representation of low- and middle-income countries.

For all these reasons, the AKI-EPI investigators and the AKI section of the European Society of Intensive Care Medicine (ESICM) concurred that there was a strong rationale for an updated global snapshot of the epidemiology of AKI in critically ill patients.1 Accordingly, we designed the acute kidney injury epidemiology in ICU patients 2 study (AKI-EPI 2).

Objectives and aims

Aim 1

To evaluate the occurrence, severity and short-term outcomes of AKI and AKD in critically ill adult patients admitted to the ICU.

Aim 2

To evaluate the long-term outcomes of critically ill adult patients admitted to the ICU who survived to hospital discharge. More specifically, we will evaluate kidney function, health-related quality of life and the elements of the composite endpoint ‘Major Adverse Kidney Events (MAKE)’, that is, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), use of RRT and mortality at both 90 and 365 days of ICU admission.

Methods and analysis

Design and setting

AKI-EPI 2 is an international, multicentre, observational cohort study to be conducted in more than 100 centres across the world. The list of participating centres will be provided in the study publication. The study was designed by the authors of this manuscript, who formed a steering committee. At this stage, we have identified national coordinators (1–2 per country) among active members of the AKI section of the ESICM, the professional networks of the steering committee members and previous investigators in the original AKI-EPI study (online supplemental file 1). Calls were made during international meetings. Particular attention was paid to recruiting national coordinators from low- to middle-income countries to ensure geographical diversity.

These national coordinators were asked to identify several centres within their country. The number of centres per country will be approximately proportional to the country’s population. National coordinators were responsible for addressing country-specific administrative requirements, including translation or adaptation of the study protocol, obtaining local ethics committee approval, establishing a data transfer agreement and, where applicable, setting up a country-specific mirror Research Electronic Data Capture electronic Case Record Form (REDCap eCRF) (see below).

All participating sites will contribute to the first component of the study (core ICU dataset), while participation in the second component (long-term outcomes) will be optional. Collection of long-term outcome data requires additional resources that may not be available at all centres and substantially changes informed consent requirements. Intervention

AKI-EPI 2 is a purely observational study, and no intervention will be conducted.

Outcomes

Primary outcome

The primary outcome of the study is the occurrence and maximum severity of AKI within the first 7 days of ICU admission. AKI will be defined according to modified KDIGO criteria.17 As shown in table 1, SCr-based criteria will be strictly applied, while a simplified version of the UO criteria will be used. Strict application of KDIGO UO criteria requires collection of hourly UO measurements which may be outside clinical practice in some settings and require unacceptable added workload in the context of this study. AKI staging will be determined by the highest stage based on SCr or UO criteria. Increases in SCr will be documented either within a 48-hour time period or relative to a baseline SCr concentration considered representative of kidney function before admission to the ICU. Data collection

All collected data will be de-identified and contain no identifiers such as name, acronyms, date of birth, exact age (only age categories are collected), date of admission or date of discharge.

Central electronic case record form

Patient data will be collected either manually or electronically from the clinical health record at each participating site and entered in a specific eCRF (REDCap, Research Electronic Data Capture, V.15.0.33, Vanderbilt University). The eCRF includes numerous data validation and consistency rules in order to improve data robustness. The AKI-EPI 2 REDCap eCRF will be hosted, stored and backed up on secure servers at the Ghent University Hospital and the Ghent University and secured by 2-factor authentication. It will be managed and supported by the Health, Innovation and Research Institute Data Management Unit.19 This eCRF is referred to as the ‘central eCRF’.

Mirror eCRF

In some countries, a mirror of this eCRF can be used on investigator’s request. This mirror has the exact same structure of the central eCRF but will be hosted, secured and backed up in the investigator’s institution. Once data collection is complete, data will be securely transferred via REDCap into the central eCRF.

Study conduct and data quality

The investigators will be provided with a bundle that contains all information necessary for correct conduct of the study. This information is also available on a specific AKI-EPI 2 website.20

An operations manual with detailed instructions on correct data recording (online supplemental file 1) as well as instructions related to the eCRF will be made available to the investigators. To further improve compliance, in addition to the English version, a translated version of the eCRF is also available in Chinese and Japanese languages.

The administrative work as well as the support for study sites will be performed by three study coordinators (based in Ghent, Lausanne and Edmonton, see acknowledgements). Data monitoring will be conducted remotely by the Lausanne team. This will predominantly consist of the assessment of data completeness and coherence, as no data source control can be undertaken. The minimal dataset required for data completeness is detailed in the online supplemental material (‘Minimal Dataset Variables’). For any query, investigators will be approached and encouraged to make adequate corrections.

Online start-up meetings will be organised at a country or centre level before official study initiation at local investigators’ request. Live and online investigator meetings will be organised at regular intervals (at least twice per year). During those, an update on the study status will be provided and frequently asked questions addressed.

Data analysis

Power calculation

The primary aim of the study is to estimate the incidence of AKI and to derive the corresponding exact two-sided 95% CI according to Clopper-Pearson exact method. To reliably estimate the number of patients needed to estimate the incidence of AKI with a specific width of the CI, we performed a sample size calculation for a binary outcome.18 Depending on the type of admission, occurrence rates of AKI between 40 and 70% have been reported in the literature. Thus, we assumed a 50% occurrence rate for sample size calculation. Using this assumption, 10 642 patients are needed to limit the width of the 95% CI to 1.9% (figure 2). Thus, with n=10 642 patients, the incidence of AKI can be estimated with at least this precision.

Statistical analyses plan

Prior to completion of recruitment and final database locking, we will develop a detailed statistical analysis plan (SAP). The primary estimand is AKI incidence and is defined as all eligible ICU admissions, the marginal cumulative incidence of any AKI within 7 days of ICU admission, defined by the modified KDIGO SCr and/or urine output criteria. We will estimate the proportion of patients that develop AKI and its 95% CIs using the Clopper-Pearson exact method. For multinomial proportion estimates, for example, KDIGO stages 1, 2 and 3 in AKI patients, a simultaneous 95% CI will be calculated using Goodman’s methods.21

Survival curves will be constructed using Kaplan-Meier estimates. We will compare groups (eg, AKI vs no AKI; RRT vs no RRT) using Fisher’s exact test or Pearson’s χ2 test for categorical variables, and for continuous variables, Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test, depending on data distribution. To identify prognostic factors associated with AKI, multivariable logistic regression analyses will be performed. Variables considered will include demographic, baseline and clinical characteristics and further include those previously described in the literature. To describe the association of AKI (by varying definitions and RRT use) with outcomes, multivariable logistic and linear regression analyses will be performed.

Candidate variables for multivariable models will be selected based on univariate analyses. In addition, we will consider adding pre-specified variables based on the original AKI-EPI study and other recent epidemiological studies.1 2 4 These include age, sex, Charlson comorbidities score, relevant comorbidities (chronic kidney disease (baseline SCr), diabetes mellitus, hypertension), admission type and illness severity (SOFA score). In primary multivariable analyses, all pre-specified covariates will be entered simultaneously. Collinearity will be assessed using variance inflation factors (VIFs), with a threshold of VIF>5 triggering review; where collinearity is identified, covariates will be prioritised based on clinical precedence rather than statistical criteria.

Partial residual plots will be used to assess the functional form of continuous predictors prior to modelling. If non-linearity is identified, skewed continuous predictors will be log-transformed. In addition, we will also use clinically meaningful categorisation of predictors to prevent non-linearity and biased model estimates. Heterogeneity of effect across these categories will be assessed using interaction testing.

With respect to time-dependent exposures: RRT initiation occurs at varying points during the ICU stay and cannot be treated as a fixed baseline characteristic without introducing bias. We have therefore added a prespecified section describing time-varying covariate Cox proportional hazards models for analyses in which RRT initiation is the primary exposure of interest (eg, its association with mortality and AKD). Formal tests for interaction will be conducted for prespecified subgroups (eg, AKI severity, admission type, geographic region) and reported alongside 95% CIs. No internal model validation is planned for the primary analysis, as this is a descriptive epidemiological cohort.

Since the Clopper-Pearson exact method applied to the primary endpoint (AKI incidence as a proportion) does not account for centre-level clustering, we have prespecified a mixed-effects logistic regression with a random intercept per centre, nested within country, as a sensitivity analysis to obtain a cluster-adjusted prevalence estimate. A likelihood ratio test comparing models with and without the random centre effect will formally assess its contribution. For all multivariable regression analyses examining factors associated with AKI and patient outcomes, the centre will be included as a random effect in a two-level hierarchical structure (patients nested within centres).

The risk of type 1 error related to multiple testing is expected to be limited, as the two co-primary endpoints (AKI incidence and maximum AKI severity) constitute the only confirmatory targets. All secondary and exploratory analyses, including subgroup analyses and risk factor identification, will be considered exploratory in nature. For these analyses, adjustment for multiple comparisons using a false discovery rate correction, specifically the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure, will be performed.

Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

The following subgroup analyses are planned: alternative definitions of AKI (based on SCr or UO only), severity of AKI, age, sex, case mix and admission diagnosis, illness severity, country gross domestic product (GDP), hospital type and other potential factors to be defined in the SAP.

As long-term outcomes will not be collected in all centres, this may result in a sub-study population that differs systematically from the overall cohort. This potential bias will be addressed by comparing patients and centres with and without long-term outcome data.

Missing data management

Missing data will be handled according to a prespecified strategy detailed in the SAP. Given the use of REDCap for data collection, the extent of missing data is expected to be low. Primary analyses will be conducted using a complete-case approach. Covariates with a proportion of missing data exceeding a predefined threshold (eg, >5%) will be carefully evaluated and may be excluded from the primary models to avoid instability related to sparse data.

Multiple imputation will not be performed in order to preserve the sustainability and future usability of the dataset beyond the primary publication. A missing-at-random mechanism will be considered plausible, although this assumption will be explored. Sensitivity analyses will be conducted to assess the robustness of the findings, including comparisons across alternative model specifications and, where appropriate, different analytic populations.

Competitive risks management

Outcomes including AKD at ICU and hospital discharge, RRT dependence at day 90 and 1 year, and long-term renal function are all subject to the competing risk of death. For these endpoints, we commit to estimating cumulative incidence functions (CIFs) using the Aalen-Johansen estimator rather than the complement of the Kaplan-Meier estimator, which is known to overestimate the incidence of the event of interest in the presence of competing events. For covariate-adjusted analyses, Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard models will be used as the primary regression approach, as they directly model the effect of covariates on the CIF.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval and consent

The study will be conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki (2024 version).22 The AKI-EPI 2 study has received ethics approval from Ghent University, Ghent University Hospital (15 November 2024, ONZ-2024-0185) and Lausanne (CER-VD, December 2024). Approval from the local research ethics committee will be sought by each participating centre prior to site activation. Written authorisation will be monitored by the study coordinators before activation is granted.

Depending on local jurisdiction, the need for informed consent varies. In some countries, given the observational nature of the study, a waiver of consent will be granted. In others, a generic data reutilisation authorisation will need to have been signed by the patients. Finally, in some countries, specific consent will need to be signed. For the long-term outcomes, at a minimum, verbal consent will be obtained in all cases. In any case, the study coordinators ensured that written authorisation was granted by the corresponding ethics committee before any site was activated.

Dissemination

Reporting will be according to the STROBE guidelines for reporting of observational studies.23 The study results will be submitted for publication in peer-reviewed journals and presented at national and international critical care and nephrology meetings. The study publications will follow the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors criteria and will include investigators who contribute meaningfully to the study design, implementation and analysis.

Access to data

The final anonymised study database and SAP will be made available to investigators and on reasonable written request to the executive committee.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not directly involved in the design of the core study. The design has incorporated the important inferences on endpoints described as relevant for people with lived experience from the 31st Acute Disease Quality Initiative meeting focused on clinical trial design in AKI, in which patient representatives and members of the AKI-EPI 2 executive committee participated.24

A panel of patients and patient representatives has been consulted to prioritise the ‘long-term’ outcomes of the study. Specifically, participants were asked about the relevance of the tested outcomes.

Conclusion

The AKI-EPI 2 study is a large-scale, international, prospective cohort study designed to provide a contemporaneous and comprehensive snapshot of the epidemiology of AKI in critically ill patients admitted to ICUs around the world. By enrolling more than 10 000 patients across over 100 centres worldwide, this study will not only determine the occurrence and severity of AKI according to the KDIGO definition but also offer insights into the use of RRT, AKI duration and short-term outcomes. In addition, for a subset of patients, data on long-term outcomes, including kidney function, mortality and health-related quality of life up to 1 year after ICU admission will be provided. These data are essential to inform current clinical practice, refine prognostic models and guide future interventions aimed at improving both acute and chronic consequences of AKI.

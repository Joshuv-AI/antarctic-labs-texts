Application of nanofluids for performance improvement of plate heat exchangers: A comprehensive review
Authors: Farhan Lafta Rashid, Najah M.L. Al Maimuri, Karrar A. Hammoodi, Muhammad Asmail Eleiwi, Issa Omle, Abdullah Musaab Mousa Alsayyad, Saif Ali Kadhim, Ephraim Bonah Agyekum, Mohamed Bechir Ben Hamida
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe demand for efficient thermal management in industries has driven research into nanofluids (NFs) as potential replacements for conventional heat transfer fluids (HTFs) in plate heat exchangers (PHEs). A key challenge is achieving higher heat transfer rates (HTRs) while minimizing pressure drop, which is crucial for improving energy efficiency in industrial processes. NFs are categorized into two types: simple (mono) NFs, such as single-walled (SWCNTs) and multi-walled carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs) dispersed in base fluids, and hybrid NFs, which combine multiple nanoparticles to enhance performance. Studies show that mono NFs improve thermal conductivity, increasing heat transfer coefficients by 6.18%–16.79%. However, their high viscosity and pressure drop remain challenges. In contrast, hybrid NFs demonstrate superior thermal performance, with heat transfer coefficient improvements of up to 39.16% and only a slight increase in pumping power. This suggests they offer an optimal balance between heat transfer enhancement and operational cost efficiency. Critical factors influencing performance include operating parameters, particle concentration, inlet temperature, and flow rate. Future research should explore nanoparticle shape and size through experimental and numerical studies, along with long-term stability and cost-effectiveness in industrial applications. Addressing these areas could unlock NFs' potential to revolutionize heat exchanger technology, leading to more efficient and sustainable thermal systems.

Introduction

In several engineering applications, efforts have been made for decades to enhance heat transfer process performance to get better efficiency and save money and energy. Because of their larger surface area and smaller hydraulic diameter, plate heat exchangers (PHEs) provide better thermal performance.1 Another benefit of PHEs is the effortless replacement of gaskets.2 PHEs are utilized in numerous industries today, including the processing of food, chemical manufacturing, and other cooling and heating equipment. Unfortunately, because some fluids are highly viscous, this is not a good alternative for around 20% of operations where the PHE needs to be larger to obtain the necessary operating state.3,4

The purpose of designing these exchangers is mainly to enhance the heat exchange between the two fluids inside them. This objective can be achieved by adjusting various parameters that influence the heat transfer, while also taking into account limitations such as the maximum permissible pressure loss. Different techniques can improve heat transmission, including the addition of fins with various geometries to heat transfer surfaces, utilizing nanofluids (NFs), and optimizing fin designs through both computational and experimental approaches to boost their effectiveness. Experimental and computational investigation of flow dynamics into a channel with a side vortex creator employing vanes having a rectangular shape was conducted by Aliabadi et al.5 They found that the substantial temperature gradients in the zones near these rectangular vanes are responsible for the elevated heat transfer coefficients (HTCs) in channels with such vanes. Additionally, the channel with rectangular vanes has a larger area for heat exchange compared to the other two channels. Song et al.6 numerically investigated the heat transfer rate (HTR) and the drop in pressure of staggered fin strips in PHEs. The biggest challenge in improving the design of PHEs is the relationship between the drop in pressure and heat transfer when the fins are in operation. Developing a generic formula is crucial to achieving this goal. They have demonstrated that the model performs well since the results of their simulated model closely match the actual data for the hydraulic parameters and HTCs. Experimental research on heat transmission in finned PHE endplates was conducted by Jin and Hrnjak.7 Their investigation employed a novel method: because of the contact between neighboring wavy plates, the endplates were regarded as finned plates rather than as adiabatic surfaces. Biharaei and Hangi8 conducted an experimental study examining the effectiveness of a magnetic NF as a coolant in a horizontal tube Heat Exchanger (HE) subjected to an exterior magnetic field (MF). This system involved a heated water-based liquid flowing in a countercurrent direction through a circular region. Meanwhile, Mousavi et al.9 explored how a fluctuating MF influenced the heat transfer performance (HTP) into a tube flow HE system that featured a sinusoidal internal tube. In their numerical investigation, Shakiba and Vahedi10 found that employing a heterogeneous transverse MF impacted the ferrofluid flow, leading to enhanced heat transfer. Additionally, Naphon and Wiriyasart11 investigated to assess the overall performance of various heat enhancement methods. Their research examined the effects of two passive techniques alongside two active methods, MF and pulsed flow, on the flow as well as the thermal performance in a helically grooved tube. Furthermore, Mei et al. developed a test section12 to explore the thermo-hydraulic properties of water-Fe3O4 NF in a wavy tube exposed to various MFs. Wang et al.13 examined the friction coefficient and heat exchange rates of corrugated tubes featuring various corrugation pitches. Additionally, experimental investigations were conducted on these corrugated tubes, utilizing water and TiO2-H2O NF as the heat transfer mediums in this research. Teng et al.14 performed an investigation into the performance of carbon-based NFs (CBNFs) at particle concentrations of 0.2% and 0.6% utilizing a welded PHE. Their findings indicated enhancements in system efficiency and maximum heat transfer capacity by 9.19% and 7.98%, respectively. In addition, Huang and colleagues15 also studied the HTP of water/Al2O3 and water/multi-walled carbon nanotube (MWCNT) NFs in a compact V-plate HE. Both types of NFs showed a slight increase in convective HTCs than that for the base fluid (BF) in their study. In a numerical study involving a compact 10-plate PHE, Huang et al.16 reported a modest improvement in HTP employing a hybrid nanofluid (HNF) composed of water/(Al2O3 + MWCNT). In contrast, Batard et al.17 utilized the same hybrid water-Al2O3-MWCNT NF in a compact PHE, demonstrating that this HNF achieved a superior HTC in comparison with the water/Al2O3 NF, attributable to its enhanced thermal conductivity. Additionally, Gupta et al.18 offered a brief study of many advancements in PHEs incorporating NFs, noting that substituting conventional fluids with NFs can lead to improved heat transfer efficiency (HTE) in PHEs. The primary issue associated with this device lies in the drop in pressure occurring within the PHE. However, if the NF is composed of carbon nanotubes (CNTs), the significance of pressure loss diminishes. The effectiveness of utilizing NFs in PHEs featuring various geometrical configurations was examined by Pandya et al.19 The reported investigations may be divided into two primary categories: numerical and experimental. In addition, each section includes a detailed table that contains important data on flow regime, pressure drop, heat transfer enhancement, BFs, analytical techniques, and nanoparticle size. Compared to the BFs, most experiments revealed enhanced thermal performance, improved heat transfer, reduced entropy generation, and minimized energy loss. Increased Reynolds numbers (Re nos.) can lead to more effective heat transmission. The operating temperature of NFs significantly influences the efficiency of heat exchangers, thereby enhancing heat transfer. Finally, Kumar et al.20 provided a comprehensive overview of PHEs as well as notable studies concerning the operation and efficiency of these devices when utilizing NFs. The initial section of the article outlines various authors' viewpoints on experimental and computational findings connected to HTCs—viscosity, thermal conductivity, and specific heat. The later sections focuses on the use of NFs in PHEs. Furthermore, researchers have conducted both experimental and numerical simulations to investigate NFs, particularly within PHEs. A review of previous research, encompassing both computational and experimental methodologies, suggests that NFs present significant potential for enhancing the overall efficiency of PHEs.

Unlike previous reviews that focused solely on a single type of NF, this present review integrates findings from both research categories, providing a comprehensive analysis that aims to optimize NFs for maximum heat transport rates and minimal pressure losses. This review paper stands out for its thorough analysis of the mono nanofluid (MNF) and the HNF, highlighting their benefits and the obstacles they face in PHEs for enhancing the thermal performance. Also, the primary emphasis of the review lies on exploring the existing literature regarding the hydraulic and thermal properties of MNFs and HNFs, identifying the key factors that affect their performance and proposing potential avenues for further investigation to enhance our comprehension of how the NFs can improve the HE systems' performance, while reducing the costs and environmental repercussions.

Enhancing the PHEs’ efficiency with nanofluid

PHEs are extensively used in many industrial processes since they combine low profile and high thermal performance capabilities, coupled with their ability to transfer heat between a broad range of fluids. Nonetheless, some inherent properties are always caused by the generic nature of traditional heat transfer fluids, which cause low efficiency of heat exchange. Some of these limitations have, however, been realized, and researchers have resorted to the utilization of advanced fluids, known as NFs. In comparison to ordinary fluids, NFs exhibit characteristics like elevated HTCs, reduced viscosity, and enhanced thermal conductivity. These modifications enhance heat transmission, resulting in significant energy savings and improved overall system efficiency. The incorporation of NFs into PHEs enhances the HTRs, while also reducing pressure drops, thereby increasing the HE efficiency. This indicates the development potential of more sophisticated and effective heat transfer systems.

Mono nanofluid

MNFs, which contain a single type of nanoparticle in a BF, offer a promising improvement in HTP, particularly in PHEs. They enhance thermal conductivity, reduce thermal resistance, and are easier to formulate than HNFs. Due to their efficiency and simplicity, MNFs are considered key to future thermal management applications.

Pourhoseini et al., 2018,28 developed a basic and inexpensive approach for preparing silver-water NF effectively. The NF is characterized to determine the influence of volume flow rate and concentration of 0 to 10 mg/L on the fluid thermal properties and the overall HTC of a CR14-45 COMER PHE. The outcomes revealed that the overall HTC of PHE rises with the rise of volume flow rate and the concentration of the NF. Also, the outcomes indicated that the volume flow rate may be more significant for the HTC than the NF concentration. Additionally, the maximum thermal conductivity values at the maximum HTR occur at a 2.5 mg/L NF concentration. It is observed that with a rise in the quantity in the solution, the coefficient of thermal conductivity of the silver nanoparticle-water solution increases up to an almost 2.5 mg/L concentration. Furthermore, the thermal conductivity enhancement of the silver-water NF is determined to be 36.6% greater than that of the BF (water).

The heating and cooling processing method produced CBNFs from graphite powder. In a study carried out in 2019, Teng et al.33 utilized CBNFs as the operating fluid in the regime of BPHE. For enhancing CBNFs’ steadiness, the concentration of CBNFs was kept at 0.2 wt % for S1 and 0.6 wt % for S2, with Sodium Dodecyl Benzene Sulfonate (SDBS) utilized as a dispersant. Measurements and analysis of specific heat, density, viscosity, thermal conductivity, and contact angle were conducted on the CBNFs. The CBNFs' performance was evaluated in a BPHE system to assess the System Effectiveness Factor (SEF), Pp, and Qex,avg at various temperatures (35°C, 40°C, and 45°C) under laminar flow conditions. The findings indicated that the S1 and S2 average Qex surpassed that of water across the whole investigational conditions, demonstrating that the CBNFs act as superior media for heat exchange compared to water. S1 exhibited a Pp value that was somewhat lower than that for water.

Bahiraei et al., 2020,34 investigated the thermal and hydraulic performances of a micro-channel PHE using boehmite alumina NF on the hot side and water on the cold side via finite volume simulation. Five nanoparticle shapes (platelet, brick, cylinder, blade, oblate spheroid) were tested at 1% concentration with varying Re nos. (500–1,500) and inlet temperatures (10°C–30°C). The results showed improved efficiency and Number of Transfer Units (NTU) with increasing Re no., with oblate spheroid particles achieving the best performance. However, the performance index (PI) decreased as the Re no. increased across all particle shapes.

Sundar et al., 2021,35 assessed the HTC as well as the thermal entropy creation of PHEs employing NFs of water-based nanodiamond. The tests were carried out within the following ranges: volume concentration (ϕ) = 0%–1.0%, Re no. = 140–610, Peclet no. (Pe) = 895.78–3,882.72, and rate of mass flow (m˙) = 0.05–0.183 kg/s. The study analyzed the effects of Re no., Pe, and the nanodiamond NFs' particle volume loadings upon entropy generation as well as the thermal transport characteristics. The outcomes displayed a straight relation between the NF particle loadings enhancement and the improvement of the Nusselt number and HTC. Also, the study showed that the overall HTC, HTC, Nusselt number, pressure drop, and pumping power were improved by 32.50%, 55.47%, 35.11%, 22.80%, and 18.93% for the BF at Re = 526.37 and ϕ = 1.0%. The present study shows that the exergy efficacy, effectiveness, and transfer units (TUs) were higher than those for the BF data at υ = 1.0% and Re = 526.37, with enhancements of 14.41%, 32.81%, and 19.72%, respectively.

The results indicated that, for a specific Re no., substituting the conventional fluid with an NF enhances energy transfer between the two fluids. Increasing the nanoparticle’s volume fraction improves performance. The investigation concludes that aluminum oxide enhances PHE performance more effectively than titanium oxide at relatively high Re nos. Saleh and Sundar, 2021,37 experimentally studied a nickel-water NF in a corrugated PHE, analyzing friction factor, heat transfer, exergy efficiency, entropy, pumping power, and PI. Nickel nanoparticles (0.1%, 0.3%, and 0.6%) were synthesized via chemical precipitation. The results showed significant enhancements at 0.6% volume and Re = 707: HTC, Nusselt number, and convective heat transfer increased by 38.60%, 57.35%, and 42.68%, respectively. Thermal entropy decreased by 15.70%, while pumping power and frictional entropy rose by 61.77% and 68.29%.

Nessab et al., 2022,38 numerically investigated the features of heat exchange of a PHE with a thin wavy internal wall at a changeable MF created via six sources located on the exchanger’s outer plates. In the upper and lower sections, heated and cold Cu-water NFs moved in parallel by forced convection. The governing equations as well as the corresponding boundary conditions were solved by applying the SIMPLE technique and the finite volume method (FVM). Also, the undulation amplitude effects, intensity ratio, and Hartmann number were the primary subjects of the parametric study. Consequently, the study elevated such factors, revealing enhanced HE efficacy as well as thermal performance coefficients (Ha and RI) in comparison with the scenario without a unit intensity ratio and MF, correspondingly. Enhanced values of ηHa and ηRI were documented at 28% and 17%, respectively. Elevated Hartmann number, undulating amplitude, and intensity ratio accentuate oscillatory flow in two heat exchanger compartments.

Cuhadaroglu and Hacisalihoglu, 2022,39 investigated the impact of thermal and hydrodynamic efficacies of a PHE through a simulation involving a heating regime that utilized CuO NF. The system consists of two main loops: the heating main circuit that circulates CuO NF and the cooling water circuit. In this study, the volume fractions of 1.1%, 0.27%, 0.56%, 0.81%, and 0.11% of NFs were prepared, and three distinct flow rates were employed. The analysis includes two hydrodynamic metrics: flow rate and pressure loss, along with thermal parameters, such as efficiency, Nusselt number, and HTC. The findings indicated that PHE achieves a maximum efficiency of 96% at ϕ = 0.81. Additionally, it was discovered that, in line with other data sources, the coefficient of heat convection was directly proportional to the ratio of nanopowder mixture.

Mariadhas et al., 2022,40 investigated the influence of chemical-based NF, consisting of zinc oxides and carbonated water and zinc, on a regime of heat transfer. The NF production frequently uses chemically synthesized nanoparticles of ZnO having a diameter of 100 nm and a purity of 99%. The present investigation tests the influence of a chemical-based ZnO sparkling/water NF on the drop of pressure and heat transfer in an HE, including corrugated plates inside a domestic hot water system. The influence of changing the Re no. and weight concentration of chemically produced nanoparticles of ZnO on their thermal transmission properties is being analyzed. The highest improvement in the Convective Heat Transfer (CHT) efficiency of an NF for the weight fractions 0.1%, 0.5%, and 1.3% of nanoparticles was 28.9%. A reduced system pressure drop illustrates this advantage.

Following the morphology of fish gills, Goltas et al., 2022,41 constructed fish gill grooves on the PHE plate surface. They substantiate the claim of enhanced performance under the same conditions by comparing it with the current commercial chevron-angled PHE. Furthermore, to align with the gel service PHE’s drop of pressure, concentrations of 0.5 and 1 vol % of Cu and Al2O3 particles in a water solution are employed on the cold and hot flow sides for heat transfer increase. The investigation indicates that the augmentation percentages of the HTR for 0.5 vol % Cu/water and water are 41% and 17.5%, respectively, exceeding those seen in the chevron-angled PHE. The 0.5 vol % Cu/water NF incorporation enhances the rate of heat transfer as well as the efficacy of water utilization into the fish-gill embossed PHE by 19.9% and 24.5%, respectively. The integration of fins with narrow flow channels into the surface of the plate elevates the drop of pressure in the fish-gill embossed PHE.

Singh and Ghosh, 2022,42 experimentally and numerically studied MWCNT-water NF in 30° and 60° chevron PHEs using ANSYS Fluent. The study showed good agreement between models and experiments (1.5% deviation). Heat transfer increased by 9.25% for 60° plates compared to 30° using water, and further improved to 17.27% and 13.64%, respectively, with 1 vol % NF.

Ajeeb and Murshed, 2022,43 conducted an experimental-numerical comparison to elucidate the thermal features of TiO2 and Al2O3 NFs in a CPHE. The NF samples were generated using DW as a BF and five Al2O3 and TiO2 nanoparticle concentrations, respectively. The fluid phase and FVM were employed for the numerical modeling of the study problem. The experimental and theoretical outcomes exhibited that the integration into the BFs improves the thermal CPHE performance. Moreover, at a peak flow rate, the computational results indicate that the CHTs deviate by no more than 12% from the actual values and the values of the coefficient of CHT.

Tian et al., 2023,44 infiltrated the inside of the PPHE with a penetrable material exhibiting a percentage of porosity ranging from 0.9034 to 0.9586 and a pore size thickness between 0.00015 and 0.00065. The material used was the NF of Ag-water (0 ≤ ϕ ≤ 0.06). The HTR of PPHEs was adversely affected, impacting the operational fluid temperature of various coolants. The flow issue addressed in this study utilized the Brinkman-Forchheimer-extended Darcy equation with the FVM to solve the governing equations. Also, the study indicates that heat transfer is enhanced by employing a material characterized by a small pore size and high porosity in the fabrication of PPHE. In summary, the placement of PPHEs in denser porous media enhances heat conduction, hence facilitating more effective heat transmission by PPHEs. As flow resistance diminishes, it was seen that the friction factor and entropy production escalate with an increase in Darcy number, porosity, and size of pore. The context indicates that the entropy generation and f into the examined flow diminish as the Richardson number declines. The velocity has attained its minimal value in the vicinity of the wake zone of welding spots, resulting in a decreased rate of heat transmission. The utilization of a semi-permeable medium infused with NF may significantly improve the thermal and thermodynamic efficiency of the PPHEs.

Cuan and Chen, 2023,45 assessed the fluid dynamics and thermal transfer in organic Rankine cycle inside the power plants of municipal solid waste utilizing NFs in PPHEs. Two-phase numerical modeling was employed to study the PPHE thermal performance and hydraulic properties. Three nanoparticles of Al2O3, Cu, and TiO2 were tested, and the findings underscore the importance of boundary conditions in determining the temperature profile by demonstrating a substantial relationship between temperature and the field of velocity. Furthermore, the two-phase model illustrates the significant dependence of NFs on volume percentage and intake velocity. Also, the volume percentage increasing to 12.8% at a 2 m/s intake velocity enhances the HTCs for water-TiO2, water-Al2O3, and water-Cu by 7.57%, 12.15%, and 2.71%, respectively.

Mehrarad et al., 2023,46 used nanoparticles of MWCNT in a water/EG cooling fluid for investigating the flow and thermal performance characteristics of a BPHE. According to UV spectrum tests, a 0.05 wt % NF exhibits the maximum steadiness. Response surface methodology was used to perform experiments on the two variables: the cold fluid’s volume flow rate and the hot fluid’s temperature, at three distinct levels. The results demonstrate that the HTR and the coefficient of CHT increased by 34.3% and 20.97%, respectively, relative to the BF at 55°C for hot fluid and a 23.4 L/min volume flow rate for cold fluid. Furthermore, enhancing the cold fluid temperature and the hot fluid volumetric flow rate caused improvements in the drop of pressure, friction factor, Nusselt number, and power of pumping. Relative to the BF, these parameters of the NF increased by 94.45%, 11.25%, 12.87%, and 11.36% at a 23.4 L/min rate of flow and a 55°C temperature, respectively.

Montazerifar et al., 2023,47 examined the efficacy of multi-stream plate-fin HEs with an innovative design including fractal-shaped fins with a novel construction for first time. The numerical analysis employs the 3D FVM. Studies were carried out on the influences of utilizing oil/MWCNT NF alongside different fractal fin geometries, with angles of attack spanning from 30° to 100° and Re nos. that are in the range 10,000–40,000. The HE’s temperature distribution stabilizes when the fluid influences the fractal fins at greater attack angles. When Re no. increases from 10,000 to 40,000, the transport of heat may attain an efficiency of 63%. Also, the HTR increased by 48% relative to the BF when the volume fraction of solid nanoparticle in the cooling fluid was elevated to 4% at constant Re nos. The production of longitudinal vortices is enhanced with an increase in fluid velocity.

Tiwari et al., 2023,48 achieved a comprehensive examination of the thermal performance of a commercial corrugated PHE by changing three distinct corrugated angles 30°, 45°, and 60° and modifying the spacing of the plate within the range 5–20 mm. They focused on prospective industrial applications while employing a polar solvent-based innovative nanosuspension of silicon carbide (SiC/DW) as a coolant. Employing the NF of SiC/DW with diverse volume concentrations (0.20%, 0.40%, 0.60%, 0.80%, 1.00%, 1.20%, and 1.40%) at the ambient conditions (T0 = 27°C) as well as a 3 L/min volumetric flow rate for both the milk and NF sides, researchers assessed the improvement in PHE’s thermal performance. The optimal coolant volume concentration, based on the findings, was established at 0.60%. This leads to an important improvement in the overall ratio of HTC of almost 51%, 42%, and 38% for corrugated plate angles of 60°, 45°, and 30°, respectively.

Ajeeb et al., 2023,49 experimentally examined the dense gasketed PHE performance utilizing the NFs of Al2O3 at various low concentrations (0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, and 0.20 vol %) of Al2O3 and BFs comprising DW and its mixtures with EG (15% EG and 30% EG), across a flow rate range (0.03–0.093 L/s). Notable improvements in the thermal conductivity are being recorded; the NFs of Al2O3 at 0.2 vol % exhibited values of 7.3%, 8.4%, and 9.1%, respectively, relative to the BFs of DW, 15% EG, and 30% EG. The results indicated enhanced heat transfer as the nanoparticle concentration reached a 27% peak at 0.2 vol % for the NF of DW-based Al2O3. This was achieved by an 8% rise in pressure decrease. To formulate an NF, Azqandi et al., 2023,50 introduced carbon nanotubes into a PHE and then compared it with the BF. Sodium dodecyl sulfate, a surfactant, stabilized MWCNT in an aqueous solution. A 13-PHE was utilized, employing DW as the warm fluid next to an NF at a mass concentration of 0.01. The outcomes in the system of laminar flow indicated that an increase in the rate of flow of hot fluid enhances the overall HTC by 53.47% as well as reduces the drop in pressure of NF. Furthermore, the results reveal that a 43.4% increase in the NF’s overall HTC is achieved with an elevation rate in flow. Finally, research assessing the influence of NF on heat exchanger efficiency, relative to the absence of NF, demonstrates that augmenting the rates of flow of both cold and hot fluids causes efficiency improvements of 17.3% and 22.9%, respectively, suggesting that CNTs significantly enhance the performance of heat exchanger at the elevated rates of flow.

Marzouk et al., 2023,51 used tungsten carbide (WC) nanoparticles in conjunction with water to conduct a statistical study of a PHE. The influences of mass concentration of the NF on the hot side have been investigated in relation to the Nusselt number, f, velocity, temperature, and the distribution of pressure for Re nos. within the range 3,240–8,840. The findings indicate that, alongside the augmentation in the Re no. and mass concentration of NF, the Nusselt number also rises. The greatest Nusselt number ratio, ranging from 166% to 191%, was attained for WC-water NF with a 0.4 mass percent addition. Also, the f rises with a rise in the NF concentration, thereafter declining to a range of 0.17–0.33 at a concentration of 0.25 g mass%. Also, the f was shown to diminish as the Re no. augmented. The thermophysical characteristics of WC-water NF at 0.4 mass% exhibit the best exergy efficacy, in the range 49.5–64.5.

Avishek and Kumar, 2023,52 experimentally investigated the PHE thermal features employing the alumina/water (Al2O3/H2O) NF in mango juice’s thermal treatment. The structure consists of two loops of flow: one for the cold fluid (mango juice) and another for the hot fluid (NF). The hot fluid input temperature varies between 50°C and 60°C and the rate of mass flow ranges from 4 to 7 L/min, while the cool fluid inlet temperature stays fixed. Also, the investigational outcomes indicate that a rise in particle concentration enhances the thermal performance, namely, the NF heat transfer. Relative to a 0.1% concentration of particles, the HTC enhancement for Re = 350 is doubled for 0.2% and increased by a factor of 2.5 for 0.3%.

Peker et al., 2023,53 conducted a tentative investigation on the counterflow PHE’s thermal performance utilizing the CuO/water and Al2O3/water NFs at various weight ratios. The springs were arranged both parallel and perpendicular to the plate to generate turbulence in the flow. Also, a two-step technique was used for producing the CuO/water and Al2O3/water NFs with three nanoparticle weight fractions: 0.1%, 0.5%, and 1%. Among the ranges that are analyzed, the Al2O3-water NF enhances the HTC of the parallel spring plate HE by around 76.1% compared to the BF. Additionally, the growth rate in the CuO-water NF is 69.9% for a similar spring and mass ratio design. The Al2O3-water NF, with springs aligned parallel to the channel as well as a 5.5 L/min rate of flow, exhibited the highest performance factor (value = 1.51 in this instance).

Calvino et al., 2024,54 investigated the feasibility of utilizing ZrO2 NFs as operating fluids for use of heat transfer springs, specifically concentrating on the renewable energy regimes like low-enthalpy geothermal installations, employing a mix of water and propylene glycol (90:10 vol %). Four unique ZrO2-based NFs are examined and contrasted with the BF with regard to their efficacy in HTE as well as the hydrodynamic features at the mass concentrations 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, and 1.0 wt % of nanoparticles. The coefficients of CHTs and the reductions of pressure were quantified during experiments utilizing a PHE and a double-tube heat exchanger (DTHE) in the regimes of laminar and turbulent flow. Hybrid nanofluid

HNFs, which consist of more than one type of nanoparticle dispersed in a BF, represent a new generation of thermal fluids with enhanced heat transfer characteristics. Compared to MNFs, HNFs offer higher thermal conductivity, improved HTCs, and better viscosity behavior. This synergistic effect allows for tuning thermal properties for specific applications, making HNFs particularly effective in multi-phase systems like PHEs. With continued research, their advantages in efficiency and industrial performance have become increasingly recognized in thermal management systems.

Kumar et al., 2016,55 conducted an experimental study to assess how varying spacings (ΔX = 2.5 mm, 5.0 mm, 7.5 mm, and 10.0 mm) influence the joined energy as well as the PHE exergetic performance. This investigation utilized a range of NFs, such as CeO2, CeO2, TiO2, MWCNT, GnP, Al2O3, and a hybrid of copper and Al2O3. The evaluation of exergetic performance and heat transfer characteristics was specifically performed at a ΔX = 5.0 mm spacing. Results indicated that the NF of MWCNT/water achieved the highest HTC, which is 53% greater than that for water at a 0.75 vol % optimal concentration, as displayed in Figure 16. Bhattad et al., 2018,56 and Kumar & Tiwari, 2018,57 conducted experimental and numerical studies on PHEs using HNFs—Al2O3 + MWCNT/water and TiO2 + MWCNT/water, respectively. Both studies employed discrete phase models using CFD tools to assess the effects of volume concentration, inlet temperature, and flow rate on thermal and hydraulic performance. Bhattad et al. reported a 39.16% increase in the HTC with only a 1.23% rise in pumping power. Kumar & Tiwari confirmed that despite increased viscosity, HNFs significantly improved PHE performance and thermal gradients. In both cases, simulation and experimental results showed strong agreement, highlighting HNFs’ potential for enhancing heat exchanger efficiency.

Bhattad et al., 2019,58 employed Al2O3-MWCNT HNFs as coolant in several experiments to assess the improvement of the exergetic and energetic efficiencies of the counterflow corrugated PHEs. The research by Sokhal et al., 2022,60 sought to evaluate the efficacy of hybrid nanofluids (HNF) utilizing Al₂O₃ and CuO as hybrid nano-additives across various operational circumstances. The performance was quantified through variations in the Reynolds numbers of hot and cold fluids, inlet temperature, and nanoparticle concentrations, with temperature varied from 60°C to 80°C and nanoparticle concentration from 0.1% to 0.5%. Despite the increase in pressure drop associated with nanofluid usage, the improvement in heat transfer exceeded the increase in pressure drop, which we report here as the Performance Gain Evaluation (PGE).

Alklaibi et al., 2022,61 examined the efficacy of the MWCNT + Fe3O4/water HNFs as a coolant in a PHE, utilizing particle volume concentrations in the range 0.05%–0.3% and rates of coolant flow between 3 L/min and 7 L/min. The exergy efficiency of NF is determined to be equivalent to that of deionized water at a 0.3 vol % particle concentration and a 7 L/min flow velocity, exhibiting an increase of 10.5%, as per the data. Bhattad et al., 2023,63 examined the exergetic and energetic performance of a corrugated PHE utilizing an HNF. The brine of propylene glycol-water and the brine of EG-water solutions were amalgamated with the nanoparticles of alumina-silver (Al-Ag) and magnesia-silver (Mg-Ag). Two situations were addressed: one with a defined area and concentration and the other with a determined flow rate. The Fe3O4-SiO2/water HNFs in the PHE maintain laminar flow conditions. The experimental investigations utilized particle loadings between 0.2% and 1.0% and flow speeds from 0.05 to 0.1166 kg/s. The findings indicate that both the quantity and effectiveness of TUs improved with greater Re nos. and volume loadings.

Alklaibi et al., 2024,67 performed an experimental analysis on the f, heat transfer, and thermal performance of PHEs utilizing the HNFs of Fe3O4-SiO2/water as coolant. The nanoparticles of Fe3O4-SiO2 were fabricated by the methods of chemical co-precipitation and in situ growth, with their properties evaluated using FTIR spectroscopy, XRD, and VSM. The rates of mass flow into the f and heat transfer experiments varied from 90.05 (Re = 245) to 0.1166 kg/s (Re = 936), while the range of volume concentrations was 0.2%–1.0%. The HTC, Nusselt number, and f rise by 21.30%, 25.85%, and 37.59%, respectively, when a 1.0 vol % Fe3O4-SiO2/water HNF replaces the BF. Also, this causes an 18.1% improvement in the factor of thermal performance.

Nazari et al., 2024,68 performed a computational scrutiny on a fin-PHE, considering the winglet vortex generators' effects at different angles, HNF, and MWCNT-Fe3O4/water at a concentration of 0.3%. SST turbulence models are employed to implement computational fluid dynamics in this setting. The results of the simulation exhibited that the utilization of Volumetric Generation (VG) and NF enhances HTE. The utilization of VG enhances heat transmission mainly by intensifying turbulent flow and diminishing boundary layers. Furthermore, the thermal performance improvement is ascribed to the NF’s increased thermal conductivity. The maximum enhancement in the rate of heat transfer achievable with an NF without a generator of vortex, with a generator of vortex only, and with both the generator of vortex and the NF is around 5.2%, 69.2%, and 74.6%, respectively.

Bhattad, 2024,69 conducted an energy assessment of a PHE utilizing mixed HNFs of Al2O3 as coolant. Al2O3 (10:0) at a total volume concentration 0.01% and DW act as coolants, while several HNFs (AlN-Al2O3, SiC-Al2O3, and MgO-Al2O3) with similar nanoparticle ratios (5:5) also work as coolants. The study investigates the influence of various nanoparticle amalgamations, rate of flow, and intake temperature on the effectiveness, PI, HTC, HTR, and the work of the pump. Research indicates that the incorporation of nanoparticles and the augmentation of flow rate enhance the HTR, coefficient of CHT, efficacy, and PI. The SiC-Al2O3 HNF exhibits enhancements of 3.8% and 11.7% in HTR and HTC, respectively. Conversely, with Al2O3 (10:0) NF, the pump effort rises by as much as 0.25%. The efficacy and PI of the SiC (5:5) HNF rise by as much as 3.9%. Practical implementation and scale-up considerations

Although numerous laboratory-scale investigations have demonstrated the promising thermal and hydraulic performance of NFs in PHEs, their transition to large-scale or industrial applications remains limited. Practical implementation requires consideration of several critical factors, including long-term stability, compatibility with system materials, maintenance complexity, and economic viability. While some pilot-scale applications have been reported, detailed industrial case studies are still scarce. Moreover, cost analysis comparing NFs with conventional heat transfer fluids—factoring in nanoparticle preparation, dispersion methods, system retrofitting, and potential operational savings—is essential for realistic assessment. Future research should focus on scaling strategies, real-world testing in industrial environments, and life cycle cost-benefit evaluations to bridge the gap between experimental success and commercial adoption.

Environmental and toxicological considerations

Introduction of NFs in industrial heat exchange systems can demonstrate positive aspects in the thermal efficiency of industry, but on the other hand, these new materials present serious questions regarding environmental safety and the field of human health. Lots of NFs employ designed nanoparticles, including aluminum oxide (Al2O3), copper oxide (CuO), and MWCNTs, which, although useful in enhancing heat transfer, are potentially hazardous in that they create a source of toxicity, have a small size being nanoscale, have high surface activity, and are capable of penetrating biological membranes.

Once they are released in an unwanted way due to system leakages, improper operation of the system, or disposal, they can remain in the environment and build up in soil and water reservoirs before entering the food chain. They are bioaccumulative and might interact with the living organisms to cause this to have adverse ecological impact or risk to the health of humans, particularly when they are exposed to the bioaccumulative materials over time. Besides, a lot of nanomaterials have not been clearly understood in their long-term fate in the environment, which further indicates the importance of adopting precautionary measures. The following strategies are proposed to reduce the possible environmental and health consequences and to ensure that NFs will be used successfully in practice and the end use of NFs will be friendly to the environment.(1)Conduct comprehensive life cycle assessments (LCAs): Evaluate the full environmental impact of NFs from raw material extraction through synthesis, usage, and end-of-life disposal. This allows identification of critical stages where sustainability improvements can be implemented.(2)Develop eco-friendly and biodegradable nanoparticles: Prioritize research on the synthesis of green, non-toxic, and biodegradable nanoparticles that retain thermal efficiency, while minimizing ecological and health hazards.(3)Establish standardized safety protocols: Implement clear industrial guidelines for safe handling, storage, transportation, and disposal of NFs. These protocols should include emergency response measures and require appropriate staff training.(4)Perform extensive toxicological research: Conduct both in vitro and in vivo studies to understand the long-term biological and ecological effects of nanoparticle exposure. This will help define safety thresholds and inform regulatory standards.

Stability and corrosion considerations

One of the major challenges associated with NF applications in heat exchanger systems is ensuring long-term stability and material compatibility. Nanoparticles tend to agglomerate or settle over time, especially under fluctuating thermal and flow conditions. This not only reduces the thermal performance of the NF but also may lead to clogging, uneven flow distribution, or damage to heat exchanger surfaces. Furthermore, certain NFs may chemically interact with metallic components in the system, accelerating corrosion or erosion, particularly in stainless steel or copper-based PHEs.

To address these issues, future research must focus on the development and use of effective stabilization techniques such as the addition of surfactants, pH control, ultrasonication, or nanoparticle surface functionalization. In parallel, comprehensive corrosion testing and compatibility studies should be performed to evaluate how different nanoparticle types, concentrations, and BFs interact with commonly used heat exchanger materials over extended periods. These studies are essential for ensuring the safe, efficient, and durable operation of NF-based thermal systems in real-world industrial environments.

Conclusions

This review paper discusses and summarizes the literature information to improve NF use, single NFs, and HNFs in PHEs. The results presented here describe how NFs can significantly improve thermal capacity, while overcoming pressure drop and flow characteristics issues. Also, the important conclusions drawn from the analysis of the present review can be as follows:(1)Both NFs and HNFs have been shown to provide very significant improvements in HTC over the BF. The improvement in the rate of heat transfer for HNFs is particularly significant, with an increase of up to 39.16%.(2)The heat transfer rises in the presence of NFs, but the costs increase with increasing viscosity and pressure drop. This is generally true for HNFs compared to single NFs and shows a better coupling between heat transfer improvements and operating costs.(3)The results obtained for PHEs with NFs depend on certain operating parameters, which include nanoparticle concentration, flow rate, and inlet temperature. Adjusting these parameters is especially important for achieving the potential advantages of NFs.(4)NFs have the potential to be utilized in different productions, like automotive manufacturing and Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning (HVAC), as well as renewable energy systems. To this end, this versatility suggests the ways in which they could participate in enhancing the energy efficiency and the sustainability in thermal administration.(5)It is a necessity to carry out further investigations using experimental and numerical approaches to investigate the long-term behavior, cost efficiency, and environmental consequences of NFs. The studies of this nature would be useful in determining the application of NFs in real-life settings.(6)Hence, the feasibility of implementing NFs in PHEs remains crucial as the usefulness of the enhanced thermal performance must offset the costs of NF preparation and integration.

Critical issues and recommendations for upcoming directions

The present review paper introduces a survey of NFs' future potential in the PHEs; however, many unexplored challenges need to be individually discussed to understand the potential of the NFs in practical scenarios. These include technical factors that may affect the viability of using NFs for thermal management across various industries, cross-cut technical, economic, and environmental factors. The following points outline the key critical issues identified in the review:(1)However, one of the major concerns in these NFs is the issue of stability over long-term application. When dispersed, the nanoparticles are inclined to agglomerate or settle down, which affects the thermal characteristics and efficiency of the material. Stability must also be achieved to ensure that composite dispersions deliver a consistent performance level throughout the application.(2)Despite a higher coefficient of CHT, NFs encounter a greater viscous sensible property in comparison with the regular fluids. This may result in increased pumping power demand, which may break even the advantages achieved by enhancing convection heat transfer. Practical applications require a delicate compromise as to the viscosity as well as the thermal characteristics of the material.(3)The nanoparticles’ suspension nature in BFs and the used construction materials in HEs are the potential causes of corrosion or degradation. It has therefore become important to determine the compatibility of NFs as well as the HE regime for obtaining long-lasting results.(4)One of the main challenges associated with NFs is the cost incurred in the manufacture and application of these materials. Further economic scrutiny is required for comparing the potential increases in performance as well as established reliability gains to the extra costs that are likely to be incurred through the use of NFs, particularly for their production and maintenance.(5)Another issue that remains a concern is that of what happens to the nanoparticles, which, although used in NFs, can be toxic, and how best to dispose of them. More studies should be conducted on the LCAs of NFs to minimize potential hazards that might be posed to humans and the surrounding community.(6)The NF performance assessment in heat exchangers has not had a lack of assessment methods but has had a lack of standard methodologies for measurement tests. Laying down standards for thermal evaluation, stability, and performance will aid in the comparison of the variety of studies and will support the enhancement of the research.(7)Most NF studies are performed at a quenched state and not under service conditions that have been used in the present studies. Additional experimental research must be conducted in the laboratory so as to confirm the field studies' outcomes and to assess the NFs’ effectiveness.

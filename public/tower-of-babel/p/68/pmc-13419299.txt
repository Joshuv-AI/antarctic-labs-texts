Preoperative low-attenuation area on computed tomography is associated with recurrence after curative resection for non-small-cell lung cancer
Authors: Hironobu Samejima, Kensuke Kojima, Shigeki Shimizu, Toshiteru Tokunaga, Kyoichi Okishio, Hyungeun Yoon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRecurrence after curative-intent resection remains a major determinant of long-term outcomes in non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Preoperative computed tomography (CT)–derived low-attenuation area (LAA) is an objective marker of smoking-related parenchymal injury, but its independent association with postoperative recurrence and the shape of its exposure–response relationship remain incompletely defined.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 660 consecutive patients who underwent R0 resection for NSCLC between 2017 and 2021. LAA was quantified on inspiratory CT as the percentage of lung volume with attenuation values < − 950 Hounsfield units. The primary endpoint was recurrence-free survival (RFS). Prespecified multivariable Cox models adjusted for major clinicopathologic and treatment-related factors, with LAA modeled per 1% increase and dichotomized using a Youden index–derived cutoff. Potential nonlinearity was assessed using restricted cubic splines and a one-knot hinge model. Model performance was summarized using time-specific calibration and decision-curve analysis. Machine learning analyses visualized the LAA–recurrence relationship using random forest partial dependence plots and explored predictor importance using permutation-based methods and a random survival forest.ResultsRecurrence occurred in 119 patients (18%), including 32 locoregional-only recurrences and 87 distant recurrences with or without locoregional recurrence. Higher LAA was independently associated with shorter RFS (per 1% increase: adjusted hazard ratio [HR], 1.05; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.01–1.08; P = 0.007). In an exploratory dichotomized analysis using a data-derived 1.3% cutoff, LAA ≥ 1.3% was also associated with shorter RFS (HR, 1.84; 95% CI, 1.20–2.83; P = 0.005). Global spline tests did not show statistically significant nonlinearity across the full LAA distribution; however, prespecified low-range analyses suggested a possible change in the risk gradient around 1%–2% LAA. Machine learning analyses ranked pathologic stage and PD-L1 as the strongest predictors, with LAA contributing additional but smaller prognostic information.ConclusionsPreoperative CT-derived LAA was independently associated with postoperative recurrence after R0 resection for NSCLC. LAA may provide complementary host-lung imaging information alongside established clinicopathologic predictors, but the low-range signal and the data-derived 1.3% cutoff should be regarded as hypothesis-generating pending external validation in multicenter cohorts with standardized CT acquisition and quantification.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40644-026-01054-4.

Background

Recurrence after curative-intent resection remains a major determinant of long-term outcomes in non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [1, 2]. Because recurrence risk informs adjuvant-therapy decisions and the intensity of postoperative surveillance, clinically useful risk stratification beyond pathologic stage is needed [3, 4]. However, recurrence trajectories vary widely within the same stage, underscoring the need for additional, readily available risk markers.

The low-attenuation area (LAA) on preoperative chest computed tomography (CT), commonly defined as the percentage of lung volume with attenuation below − 950 Hounsfield units, provides an objective quantitative surrogate for smoking-related parenchymal injury and correlates with pathologic emphysema [5, 6]. Prior studies have linked CT-quantified emphysema burden to smoking exposure, impaired pulmonary function, perioperative outcomes, and prognosis in NSCLC [7–11], supporting LAA as a candidate host-lung imaging biomarker. In parallel, CT radiomics signatures have been explored for predicting postoperative recurrence risk and estimating the potential benefit of postoperative radiotherapy [12, 13].

However, when recurrence-free survival (RFS) is used as the endpoint, it remains unclear whether LAA independently predicts postoperative recurrence after adjustment for major clinicopathologic and treatment-related factors, including pathologic stage, histology, programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression, and adjuvant chemotherapy. In addition, the exposure–response relationship between LAA and recurrence risk has not been well characterized. Moreover, to our knowledge, few studies have evaluated LAA within a prespecified analytic framework that jointly estimates association, interrogates the shape of the relationship, and formally assesses model reliability within a single cohort.

We therefore investigated the independent prognostic association of preoperative CT-derived LAA with postoperative recurrence after R0 resection for NSCLC and, using a prespecified analytic framework, delineated the exposure–response shape of the LAA–recurrence relationship.

Methods

Study design and patients

We conducted a single-center retrospective cohort study of consecutive patients with NSCLC who underwent curative-intent R0 resection at Kinki Chuo Chest Medical Center between January 2017 and December 2021. Histology and pathologic stage were assigned according to the 2015 World Health Organization classification [14] and the AJCC/IASLC 8th edition TNM staging system [15], respectively. We collected demographic and clinical variables (age, sex, smoking exposure quantified by the Brinkman index, and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio [NLR]), tumor variables (histology, pathologic stage, and programmed death-ligand 1 [PD-L1] tumor proportionscore [TPS] measured with 22C3 pharmDx), and treatment variables (platinum-based adjuvant chemotherapy). Curative-intent resections were performed under institutional standards with routine pathologic confirmation of R0 status and guideline-concordant nodal evaluation. Lymph node dissection was classified according to the Japanese Lung Cancer Society nodal dissection (ND) system (General Rules for the Clinical and Pathological Record of Lung Cancer): ND2a-1 for lobectomy, ND2a-2 for right middle lobectomy and bilobectomy or more extensive resections, ND1b for segmentectomy, and ND0 for wedge resection. Postoperative surveillance followed institutional protocols, and adjuvant chemotherapy was offered, as clinically appropriate, in accordance with contemporary guidelines, and receipt depended on clinical eligibility and patient preference. The institutional review board approved the study (approval no. 2023−102) and waived the requirement for written informed consent through an opt-out process. Eligible patients were consecutive adults who underwent curative-intent, R0 resection for NSCLC during the study period, had preoperative inspiratory CT available for LAA quantification, and had complete data on prespecified covariates. Patients who had received neoadjuvant systemic therapy or thoracic radiotherapy before resection were not included in the analytic cohort. We report this study in accordance with the STROBE statement (Additional file 1), and the study flow is summarized in Fig. 1.

CT quantification of low-attenuation area

Preoperative inspiratory CT images were analyzed using SYNAPSE VINCENT (Fujifilm, Tokyo, Japan). All CT examinations used for LAA quantification were acquired using the same CT scanner model (Aquilion PRIME; Canon Medical Systems, Japan) under the institutional routine inspiratory chest CT protocol, with a tube voltage of 120 kVp and a lung reconstruction kernel of FC51. After automated whole-lung segmentation, the LAA was quantified as the percentage of lung volume with attenuation below − 950 Hounsfield units. The same attenuation threshold, segmentation software, and quantification pipeline were applied to all patients.

Outcomes

For survival analyses, the endpoint was recurrence-free survival (RFS), defined as the time from surgery to the first documented recurrence; patients without recurrence were censored at the last disease assessment. Recurrence pattern was categorized among patients who developed recurrence as locoregional-only recurrence or distant recurrence with or without concomitant locoregional recurrence. Locoregional recurrence included recurrence at the surgical margin, ipsilateral thorax, hilar or mediastinal lymph nodes, and other regional sites. Distant recurrence included contralateral lung involvement and extrathoracic metastasis. For complementary machine learning classification, recurrence was treated as a binary outcome.

Statistical analysis

We evaluated the association between LAA and RFS using multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression. LAA was modeled primarily as a continuous variable per 1% increase. A dichotomized analysis using an ROC-derived Youden index threshold was performed as an exploratory and robustness analysis, not as an attempt to define a clinical decision threshold. Models were adjusted for prespecified covariates: age (≥ 65 vs. < 65 years), sex, Brinkman index, NLR, histology (adenocarcinoma as the reference), pathologic stage (stage I as the reference, with stage II and stage III entered as indicator terms), PD-L1 tumor proportion score, and adjuvant chemotherapy. Estimates were obtained by partial likelihood and are reported with Wald 95% confidence intervals. Because all prespecified covariates were available for included patients, no imputation was required.

Model diagnostics included multicollinearity assessed using the generalized variance inflation factor (GVIF) [16] and proportional hazards assessed using Schoenfeld residual-based tests. When a covariate violated the proportional hazards assumption, we fit stratified Cox models with stratification by that covariate while retaining the remaining covariates.

We assessed the functional form of the LAA–risk association using flexible and piecewise approaches, and summarized performance using time-specific calibration and decision-curve analysis at prespecified time points; the latter used inverse probability of censoring weighting (IPCW). Further details of ROC-derived thresholding, diagnostics, implementation, resampling, calibration, and decision-curve analyses are provided in Additional file 2. Two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Machine learning analyses

Using the same prespecified predictors, we trained recurrence classifiers. Categorical variables were one-hot encoded, and continuous variables were standardized using training-set moments; the same transformation was then applied unchanged to the validation and test sets. Data were split with a fixed seed and outcome stratification into training/validation/test sets (60%/20%/20%). We fit regularized logistic regression, a linear support vector machine, gradient boosting, random forest, and a multilayer perceptron. Hyperparameters were optimized using Bayesian optimization with Optuna to maximize mean ROC-AUC under 10-fold cross-validation in the training set. Final models were refit on the full training set and evaluated on the validation and independent test sets; uncertainty in performance metrics was quantified by nonparametric bootstrap resampling of the relevant evaluation set. Hyperparameter search spaces and tuning specifications are provided in Additional file 2.

As a time-to-event approach, we also fit a random survival forest using time-to-event and event indicator, with Optuna optimizing Harrell’s C-index on the validation set. Random survival forest hyperparameters and tuning specifications are provided in Additional file 2.

Software

All analyses were performed in Python using lifelines, SciPy, scikit-learn, statsmodels, scikit-survival, and Optuna.

Results

Patient characteristics

Of 742 consecutive adults who underwent curative-intent (R0) resection for NSCLC from January 2017 to December 2021, 82 were excluded because pre-specified covariate data were incomplete or preoperative inspiratory CT was not evaluable for LAA quantification, leaving 660 patients for analysis (Fig. 1). There were no missing data for the prespecified covariates used in the multivariable models in the analytic cohort. Using the ROC/Youden-derived cutoff of 1.3% for LAA (Additional file 2: Fig. S1), 349 patients had LAA ≥ 1.3%, and 311 had LAA < 1.3%. The proportion of patients aged ≥ 65 years was similar between groups (78% vs. 74%; P = 0.36). Compared with the LAA < 1.3% group, the LAA ≥ 1.3% group included more men and had greater smoking exposure, higher PD-L1 TPS, and higher NLR (all P < 0.001); histology and pathologic stage also differed between groups. Recurrence occurred in 119 patients (18%) and was more frequent in the LAA ≥ 1.3% group than in the LAA < 1.3% group (24% vs. 11%; P < 0.001). Among patients who developed recurrence, distant recurrence with or without locoregional recurrence was more common than locoregional-only recurrence (87 [73%] vs. 32 [27%]). The distribution of recurrence pattern did not differ significantly between the LAA ≥ 1.3% and LAA < 1.3% groups (P = 0.45). Median RFS and the rate of adjuvant chemotherapy receipt were similar between groups. The median follow-up time, defined as the time to recurrence or last disease assessment, was 855 days (IQR, 386–1318) (Table 1).

Multivariable Cox analyses

In the prespecified multivariable Cox model, with LAA modeled as a continuous variable, greater LAA was associated with shorter RFS (per 1% increase: adjusted HR, 1.05; 95% CI, 1.01–1.08; P = 0.007) (Table 2). Pathologic stage and PD-L1 TPS were also associated with RFS (Table 2), whereas other covariates were not statistically significant.

Model diagnostics and sensitivity analyses

No concerning multicollinearity was detected (Additional file 2: Table S1). The proportional-hazards assumption was not materially violated overall (Additional file 2: Table S2); in stratified analyses addressing covariates with evidence of nonproportional hazards, the association between LAA and RFS was retained (Additional file 2: Table S3). When LAA was dichotomized at 1.3%, LAA ≥ 1.3% remained associated with shorter RFS (HR, 1.84; 95% CI, 1.20–2.83; P = 0.005), with supportive diagnostic findings and stratified sensitivity analyses (Additional file 2: Tables S4–S7).

Functional form analyses

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) analyses showed an increasing adjusted hazard ratio across LAA values (Fig. 2). Likelihood-ratio tests comparing global RCS models with a linear term did not show statistically significant nonlinearity across the full LAA distribution (Additional file 2: Fig. S2a; Table S8A). This global test addresses whether the overall exposure–response curve departs from linearity across all observed LAA values. In contrast, complementary low-range analyses were used to examine whether the risk gradient changed within the lower portion of the LAA distribution. These analyses suggested modest curvature within the 0%–5% LAA range, with candidate change points clustering around 1.3%–1.5%; however, the permutation-adjusted evidence for a single change point was borderline. Accordingly, the low-range pattern was interpreted as exploratory rather than as evidence of a validated threshold.

Calibration and decision-curve analysis

Time-specific calibration at 1 and 3 years showed close agreement between mean predicted risks and Kaplan–Meier–estimated observed risks across most bins (Additional file 2: Fig. S3). In decision-curve analysis at 3 years using IPCW, net benefit exceeded treat-all and treat-none across threshold probabilities of 0.05–0.45 (Additional file 2: Fig. S4).

Machine learning analyses

To complement the survival analyses, we used machine learning models to visualize the exposure–response relationship between LAA and recurrence risk and to assess the relative predictive contribution of prespecified predictors. In the independent test set, the random forest classifier achieved a ROC-AUC of 0.83 (95% CI, 0.74–0.90), a PR-AUC of 0.53 (95% CI, 0.33–0.73), and a Brier score of 0.11 (95% CI, 0.08–0.15) (Table 3).

Random survival forest and feature importance

In the Optuna-optimized random survival forest (Additional file 2: Table S11), pathologic stage and PD-L1 had the highest permutation importance. LAA ranked below these established tumor-related factors but remained among the next most informative predictors (Fig. 4).

Discussion

In this single-center cohort of patients undergoing curative-intent R0 resection for NSCLC, preoperative CT–derived LAA was independently associated with shorter RFS after adjustment for pathologic stage, PD-L1, and other prespecified covariates. The association was consistent across continuous modeling, exploratory dichotomization, and sensitivity analyses addressing potential proportional-hazards violations. Functional-form analyses did not support statistically significant nonlinearity across the full LAA distribution. Rather, complementary analyses restricted to the low-LAA range suggested a possible local change in the risk gradient around 1%–2% LAA. This low-range signal should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating and not as a validated clinical threshold.

Quantitative CT measures of emphysema-related low attenuation have been linked to smoking exposure, perioperative outcomes, and long-term prognosis in NSCLC and related thoracic settings [17–19]. Because LAA is closely linked to smoking-related parenchymal injury, residual confounding cannot be fully excluded despite adjustment for the Brinkman index. The Brinkman index captures cumulative smoking exposure but does not incorporate smoking status at surgery, time since cessation, inhaler use, symptomatic airway disease, radiographic airway remodeling, or longitudinal pulmonary-function trajectories. We therefore interpret LAA primarily as an integrative imaging surrogate of smoking-related lung injury rather than as a marker independent of the smoking-related disease spectrum. Prior studies, however, have often focused on higher emphysema burdens (e.g., thresholds near 5%), emphasized overall survival rather than recurrence-specific endpoints, or provided limited characterization of the exposure–response shape at low LAA values [10, 17, 18]. In addition, few reports have integrated multivariable survival modeling with explicit interrogation of functional form and complementary machine learning evaluation within a single cohort. Our analysis addresses these gaps by evaluating LAA within a prespecified survival-modeling framework and by examining its functional form. The results do not indicate that LAA outperforms established prognostic factors. Pathologic stage and PD-L1 showed greater prognostic importance in the random survival forest, and stage remained the dominant clinical determinant of recurrence risk. The potential value of LAA is therefore best viewed as complementary: it may add host-lung imaging information to conventional tumor- and treatment-related variables rather than replace them. Consistent with this interpretation, the added analysis of recurrence pattern showed that distant recurrence predominated among patients who developed recurrence, whereas the distribution of locoregional-only versus distant recurrence did not differ significantly between the LAA groups. This argues against the association between LAA and recurrence being explained solely by an excess of locoregional events, although the present cohort was not designed or powered to evaluate recurrence pattern as a separate endpoint. If externally validated, LAA could help refine postoperative surveillance discussions among patients with otherwise similar clinicopathologic risk.

The observation that recurrence risk may rise even at very low LAA values is consistent with the possibility that modest parenchymal abnormalities, potentially reflecting early small-airway disease and ventilation heterogeneity, interact with tumor biology. Regional hypoxia and ventilation–perfusion mismatch may promote HIF-1α–related signaling implicated in COPD airways and may influence immune-evasion pathways, including PD-L1 regulation, as well as invasive programs such as epithelial–mesenchymal transition [20–27]. Emphysema-associated microvascular or lymphatic alterations may also impair local immune surveillance or clearance of minimal residual disease, and inflammatory protease activity (e.g., neutrophil elastase and matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs)) may remodel the extracellular matrix toward a permissive microenvironment [28–32]. Although mechanistic inference is limited in an observational study, the broadly consistent low-range pattern across spline-based modeling, complementary change-point analyses, and machine learning partial dependence suggests that the finding was not unique to a single analytic specification.

This study has several limitations. First, as a retrospective analysis from a single institution, it remains susceptible to residual confounding, selection bias, and between-patient variation in surveillance intensity despite prespecified covariates and multivariable adjustment. Although the Brinkman index was included to capture cumulative tobacco exposure, other smoking-related variables, such as smoking status at surgery, time since cessation, and longitudinal pulmonary function, were not uniformly available; residual confounding from smoking-related lung injury therefore cannot be excluded. We chose RFS as the primary endpoint because the study was designed to address postoperative recurrence and because recurrence was ascertained consistently through imaging surveillance and clinical follow-up. By contrast, cause-of-death data were not sufficiently robust to support formal analyses of cancer-specific survival or competing risks in this retrospective cohort. Second, the present findings do not indicate that LAA outweighs established prognostic factors. In the random survival forest, pathologic stage and PD-L1 carried greater prognostic importance, and stage remained the dominant determinant of recurrence risk. LAA is therefore better understood as a complementary marker that may add host-lung information to conventional tumor- and treatment-related variables rather than substitute for them. In keeping with that interpretation, the additional analysis of recurrence pattern showed that distant recurrence was more frequent than locoregional-only recurrence among patients who recurred, whereas the distribution of recurrence pattern did not differ significantly between the LAA groups. This reduces the likelihood that the association between LAA and recurrence was driven solely by an excess of locoregional events. Even so, the study was neither designed nor powered to examine locoregional and distant recurrence as separate endpoints, and recurrence-pattern-specific analyses will require larger cohorts. Third, the number of recurrence events limited precision at the extremes of the LAA distribution; accordingly, the shape of the exposure-response relation and any candidate threshold at very low or very high LAA values, including the data-derived 1.3% cutoff, should be considered exploratory. Finally, quantitative CT measures may vary with scanner model, acquisition parameters, reconstruction kernel, inspiratory effort, and analysis platform. In this study, CT acquisition followed a routine single-center protocol, and LAA was quantified in all patients with the same automated segmentation software and a fixed threshold of − 950 HU, which reduced observer-dependent variability and technical heterogeneity within the cohort. Nevertheless, reproducibility across different scanners and reconstruction settings was not examined directly, and the present model was not externally validated. External validation in multicenter datasets, ideally with harmonized acquisition parameters and appropriate recalibration across platforms, will be necessary before LAA-informed risk stratification can be generalized beyond the current setting. The decision-curve results should therefore be interpreted as supportive rather than definitive evidence of clinical utility.

Conclusion

In this retrospective, single-center cohort of patients undergoing curative-intent R0 resection for NSCLC, greater preoperative CT-derived LAA was independently associated with shorter RFS after adjustment for major clinicopathologic and treatment-related factors. LAA may provide complementary host-lung imaging information alongside established predictors, including pathologic stage and PD-L1. The low-range signal and the data-derived 1.3% cutoff should be regarded as hypothesis-generating. External validation in multicenter cohorts with standardized CT acquisition and quantification will be required before LAA can be incorporated into postoperative risk stratification; if confirmed, LAA may help refine postoperative surveillance strategies among patients with otherwise similar clinicopathologic risk.

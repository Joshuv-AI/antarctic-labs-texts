Decreased concentration of transcription factor 21 in patients with chronic schizophrenia is associated with an increased coronary artery calcium score
Authors: Thung-Lip Lee, Nan-Han Lu, Chin-Feng Hsuan, I-Ting Tsai, Chia-Chang Hsu, Chao-Ping Wang, Wei-Chin Hung, Chuin-Hua Tang, Fu-Mei Chung, Yau-Jiunn Lee, Wei-Hua Tang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTranscription factor 21 (TCF21) encodes a basic-helix-loop-helix transcription factor that is thought to play a vital role in epicardial progenitor cell development, with these cells differentiating into coronary artery smooth muscle cells and cardiac fibroblasts. Reduced TCF21 expression has been linked to coronary artery disease severity; however, its relationship with coronary artery calcium (CAC) and genetic determinants of circulating TCF21 levels has not been examined. This study investigated the associations between plasma TCF21 levels, CAC burden, and TCF21 gene polymorphisms in patients with chronic schizophrenia, a population at increased cardiometabolic risk.MethodsA total of 185 consecutive patients with chronic schizophrenia were enrolled. Plasma TCF21 concentrations were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. CAC was quantified using cardiac multislice computed tomography, with independent assessment by two blinded radiologists. The rs12190287 (G/C) polymorphism of the TCF21 gene was genotyped. Simple linear regression analyses were performed to explore associations between TCF21 levels and clinical variables, followed by multiple linear regression adjusted for age and sex. Multiple testing was addressed using false discovery rate (FDR) correction as a sensitivity analysis. Multivariable logistic regression analyses were conducted to assess the independent association between TCF21 levels and severe CAC after adjustment for established cardiovascular risk factors.ResultsAfter adjustment for age and sex, plasma TCF21 levels were positively associated with triglyceride levels and circulating inflammatory cell counts, including white blood cell, neutrophil, and monocyte counts, and inversely associated with both calcium score-Agatston and calcium score-volume. Following FDR correction, the associations with CAC measures, triglycerides, and inflammatory cell counts remained statistically significant. Lower plasma TCF21 levels were independently associated with severe CAC defined by both Agatston and calcium volume thresholds after adjustment for established cardiovascular risk factors. In genotype-based analyses, patients carrying the CC genotype exhibited significantly lower plasma TCF21 levels and higher CAC burden compared with GG and GC genotypes, and these differences remained significant after FDR correction.ConclusionsLower plasma TCF21 levels are robustly associated with greater CAC burden in patients with chronic schizophrenia, independent of major cardiovascular risk factors. Genetic variation in TCF21 is associated with reduced circulating TCF21 levels and higher coronary calcification. These findings suggest that TCF21 may represent a potential biomarker related to cardiovascular risk in this vulnerable population.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: Chronic schizophrenia, Transcription factor 21, Concentration, Polymorphism, Calcium score-agatston, Calcium score-volume

Introduction

Patients with schizophrenia have substantially higher rates of cardiovascular death and coronary heart disease (CHD) [1–3]. This excess cardiovascular burden is accompanied by a markedly increased prevalence of cardiometabolic risk factors. Epidemiological studies and meta-analyses have shown that diabetes is two to three times more prevalent in individuals with schizophrenia than in the general population [4]. Consistent with these reports, diabetes mellitus was present in 21.1% of patients in our cohort. Hypertension represents a strong independent risk factor for cardiovascular disease, with a prevalence of approximately 15% in the general population of most developed countries [5, 6] and 9–27% among patients with schizophrenia [7–9]. In our study population, hypertension was observed in 49.2% of patients, indicating a substantial cardiometabolic burden in this cohort. In addition, individuals with schizophrenia have a two- to threefold higher prevalence of metabolic syndrome than the general population, affecting approximately 41% of patients depending on diagnostic criteria and medication use [10]. In the present cohort, metabolic syndrome defined by the NCEP-ATP III criteria was identified in 22.2% of patients. Hyperlipidemia was observed in 17.3% of participants, further characterizing the adverse cardiometabolic profile of this schizophrenia population. Moreover, patients with schizophrenia are at markedly increased risk for abdominal obesity, hypertension, low high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), hypertriglyceridemia, and metabolic syndrome, with risk progressively increasing with illness duration and age [11–14]. This risk profile is likely attributable to physical inactivity, higher rates of smoking, an unhealthy lifestyle, adverse effects of antipsychotic medications, and decreased medication adherence [15, 16]. Additionally, biological factors such as chronic inflammation, alongside healthcare system-related factors including stigma and discrimination, have been identified as significant adverse cardiovascular risk factors in patients with severe mental illness [17].

Beyond these established behavioral and healthcare-related contributors, schizophrenia is also characterized by chronic low-grade inflammation and immune dysregulation and is frequently accompanied by antipsychotic-related metabolic abnormalities [2, 17, 18,]. These schizophrenia-related biological and treatment factors may accelerate vascular remodeling and calcification through inflammatory and vascular smooth muscle cell-mediated pathways [18–20]. Given the markedly increased cardiovascular risk and the high prevalence of metabolic and inflammatory abnormalities in schizophrenia, this population provides a clinically relevant model to investigate the association between molecular regulators and coronary artery calcification (CAC). Because TCF21 is involved in CAD susceptibility and vascular smooth muscle cell biology [21, 22], examining TCF21 in patients with chronic schizophrenia may provide clinically relevant insight into CAC burden in this high-risk population.

CAC is consistently observed in patients with CAD. Although CAC was formerly attributed to age-related degenerative processes, the association with CAD has now been recognized. Unstable angina is associated with more discrete smaller calcium deposits in lesions, often described as speckled or spotty. In contrast, stable angina is typically characterized by fewer but larger deposits of calcium [18, 23]. Lesions devoid of calcium are generally non-occlusive, with stenosis less than 25%. Consequently, computed tomography (CT) angiography can serve as a valuable modality for imaging CAC as an indirect indicator of clinically significant atherosclerosis [24]. CAC assessment using CT provides a non-invasive and quantitative measure of coronary atherosclerotic burden and has been widely validated as a surrogate marker of clinically significant coronary artery disease. Compared with invasive modalities such as intravascular ultrasound or conventional coronary angiography, CT-based CAC assessment does not require catheterization or contrast administration, thereby reducing procedural risk and improving patient acceptability. While intravascular ultrasound offers high spatial resolution and detailed plaque characterization, its invasive nature limits its use in large-scale or observational studies [25–27]. Conventional coronary angiography primarily evaluates luminal stenosis and may underestimate total atherosclerotic burden, particularly in early or diffuse disease. Echocardiography, although widely accessible, lacks sufficient sensitivity for direct assessment of coronary calcification. In contrast, CT-based CAC scoring demonstrates high reproducibility, strong prognostic value for cardiovascular events, and broad clinical availability at relatively low cost [28, 29]. These characteristics make it particularly suitable for evaluating subclinical coronary atherosclerosis in populations at elevated cardiometabolic risk, such as patients with chronic schizophrenia. The apoptosis of smooth muscle cells that have migrated from the tunica media to the tunica intima is thought to lead to the development of CAC. Additionally, apoptosis of macrophages contributes to the formation of larger calcific foci [30]. Microcalcifications are observed adjacent to the internal elastic lamina in the lower layers of necrotic cores, and calcium fragments and speckles form after the microcalcifications coalesce. Progression of this process results in the extension of calcification into the matrix, leading to the formation of calcified plates or sheets. These sheets may fracture, resulting in the formation of calcium nodules, which can then distort lining of the endothelium contributing to acute luminal thrombosis. Such nodules are implicated in 2% to 7% of all cases of coronary artery thrombosis [23]. CAC has been associated with migration of vascular smooth muscle cells, induction of bone formation, apoptotic bodies, and calcium-phosphorus imbalance [19, 31–33]. However, the precise mechanisms underlying CAC formation remain poorly understood. Given the significant clinical implications of CAC, elucidating its initiating mechanisms is crucial for developing strategies to prevent, limit, or remove calcium deposition from the arterial wall.

Although the basic-helix-loop-helix transcription factor 21 (TCF21) (also known as epicardin, capsulin, or POD1) has been associated with CAD, it has not been associated with metabolic or environmental risk factors [34–36]. TCF21 plays a vital role in the development of many types of heart, lung, kidney, and spleen cells during embryogenesis [37–40]. TCF21 has also been shown to control key epicardial cellular outcomes, and to serve as a key factor in the differentiation of cardiac fibroblasts from coronary vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) [37, 41]. Under these circumstances, TCF21 is downregulated in cells destined to differentiate into coronary VSMCs, while it is persistently expressed in cells destined to become adventitial and interstitial fibroblasts [41]. Furthermore, Luo et al. described the involvement of TCF21 in regulating the VSMC phenotype, promoting the migration and proliferation of VSMCs, and participating in inflammatory pathway activation [21]. Enhanced VSMC migration and proliferation can result in neointimal hyperplasia following vascular injury [21]. Aberrant inflammation and neointimal hyperplasia are characteristic features in atherosclerosis [42]. Consequently, targeting TCF21 may lead to new therapeutic strategies to attenuate atherosclerosis. Nonetheless, the association between TCF21 and CAC status in patients with chronic schizophrenia remains unclear. In addition, previous research has demonstrated significant associations between an elevation in TCF21 concentration with the GG genotype and G allele of rs12190287 in TCF21, which are linked to the onset and recurrence of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation following ablation [43]. Therefore, we conducted this study to investigate the association between TCF21 with CAC scores assessed by cardiac multislice CT. Additionally, we evaluated whether variants of the TCF21 gene (12190287G/C) could affect TCF21 levels and, consequently, modify CAC scores in patients with chronic schizophrenia.

Methods

Participants

This cross-sectional study enrolled 185 patients diagnosed with chronic schizophrenia. All of the patients were in a stable condition and admitted to the inpatient ward of the Yuli Branch of Taipei Veterans General Hospital between January 1, 2022 and December 31, 2023. The diagnosis of schizophrenia was based on the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fourth Edition (DSM-IV). The use of DSM-IV criteria ensured consistency with long-standing clinical diagnoses and existing medical records in this chronic inpatient population. The exclusion criteria were patients with fever, a major affective disorder (e.g. major depression, bipolar disorder, and mania), an acute physical illness, and those who had undergone treatment with steroids, antipyretics, antibiotics, or anti-inflammatory agents. Patients with a history of CAD, myocardial infarction, or other clinically documented cardiovascular diseases were also excluded. All of the enrolled patients signed informed consent forms. The study was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of Kaohsiung E-Da Hospital and complied with the principles outlined in the Helsinki Declaration.

Data collection

Sociodemographic variables of age, sex, mental status, betel quid, alcohol and tobacco consumption, height and weight were documented. All of the patients received thorough physical and neurological examinations. Blood pressure was measured with a digital device (HEM-907, Omron, Japan) after five minutes of rest. Hypertensive patients were defined as those with a systolic and/or diastolic blood pressure equal to or exceeding 140/90 mmHg, or those undergoing antihypertensive pharmacotherapy. Patients diagnosed with diabetes mellitus were categorized based on the following criteria: hemoglobin A1c concentration 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) or higher, or fasting or 2-hour postprandial blood glucose concentrations of 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) or 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) or above, respectively, following the American Diabetes Association 2016 Guidelines [44]. Additionally, patients undergoing antidiabetic pharmacological therapy were included in this category. Hyperlipidemia was defined by triglyceride levels exceeding 180 mg/dL, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) levels greater than 130 mg/dL, total cholesterol concentrations above 200 mg/dL, HDL-C levels below 40 mg/dL, or patients undergoing treatment for dyslipidemia. A diagnoses of Metabolic syndrome followed the NCEP-ATP III criteria with an adapted criterion for central obesity [45] as the occurrence of ≥3 of the following: (i) elevated blood pressure (≥ 130/85 mmHg), (ii) central obesity, defined as a waist circumference ≥ 90/80 cm in males/females, (iii) serum triglyceride level ≥ 150 mg/dL, (iv) serum HDL-cholesterol levels < 40/50 mg/dL in males/females, and (v) fasting glucose level ≥ 100 mg/dL.

Laboratory data

Fasting blood samples were used to obtain serum triglyceride, glucose, albumin, LDL-C, HDL-C, and total cholesterol levels, and complete blood cell counts [46]. Serum creatinine was quantified using the Jaffe method. Digital analytic systems were used to measure hemoglobin A1c (HLC-723G8, Tosoh Corp., Tokyo, Japan) and total leukocyte count with the proportions of monocytes, lymphocytes and neutrophils (XE-2100 Hematology Alpha Transportation System; Sysmex, Kobe, Japan). Absolute counts were obtained by multiplying the proportion of each subtype by the total leukocyte count. Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was evaluated with the CKD-EPI equation [47]. Quantification of plasma concentrations of TCF21 was performed with an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions (FineTest, Hubei, China), with intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variation (both n = 3) of 3.15% to 6.24% and 5.23% to 6.42%, respectively. An immunochemistry system (Beckman Coulter IMMAGE) with a detection limit of 0.2 mg/L was used to measure plasma concentrations of high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP). All measurements were performed in duplicate in a single experimental run.

128-Slice CT scanning protocol and calcium scoring

Data acquisition was carried out using a 128-slice CT scanner (Optima CT660; GE Healthcare). Two localization scans were performed first with no contrast media to acquire lateral and anteroposterior views of the chest. Before conducting multislice CT angiography, a coronary calcium scan was performed. The single breath-hold technique was used to acquire cardiac images from the tracheal bifurcation level to below the cardiac base, with prospective electrocardiographic triggering set at 70% of the RR interval. The parameters of the scan were: 25 cm field of view, 8 × 2.5 mm collimation, tube voltage/current of 120 kV/400 mA, and gantry rotation time of 0.35 s. Additionally, volume, Agatston and CAC scores were computed with SmartScore (GE Healthcare). Agatston calcium score was calculated as lesion area multiplied by a weighting factor based on peak signal intensity within the lesion. Agatston calcium scores for the whole coronary vasculature were calculated by summing the scores of individual lesions. Total calcium volume was as the section thickness multiplied by area of the calcified lesion. The volumes of all lesions across all vessel sections were summed to obtain the calcium volume for each coronary vessel. The total recorded calcium volume was then derived from the cumulative volume of all vessels.

Quality control and inter-reader reproducibility

All CT images (n = 185) were independently reviewed by two experienced radiologists who were blinded to clinical data. Image quality was evaluated using a standardized 4- point scale (1 = excellent, 2 = good, 3 = acceptable, 4 = non-diagnostic). All scans were considered diagnostic (score ≤ 3), and no examinations were excluded due to inadequate image quality. CAC scoring was independently performed by both readers using SmartScore (GE Healthcare). Inter-observer agreement for categorical Agatston score classification (0, 1–99, 100–399, ≥ 400) was assessed using weighted Cohen’s kappa coefficient. Agreement for continuous variables (Agatston score and total calcium volume) was evaluated using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), calculated using a two-way random-effects model with absolute agreement for single measurements. Discrepancies between readers were resolved by consensus. Inter-observer agreement for Agatston score categories was excellent (weighted κ = 0.91). For continuous measurements, reproducibility was also excellent, with ICC values of 0.96 (95% CI: 0.94–0.97) for Agatston score and 0.95 (95% CI: 0.93–0.97) for total calcium volume.

Genotyping of the rs12190287 variant in the TCF21 gene

After extracting genomic DNA from whole blood samples (QIAamp DNA Blood Mini Kit, Qiagen, Valencia, CA), it was reconstituted in pH 7.8 Tris-EDTA buffer solution [10 mM Tris, 1 mM EDTA]. Quantification was then performed with optical density at 260 nm (OD260); the samples were then frozen at -20 °C before being used as a template for PCR. The rs12190287 variant in TCF21 was genotyped using the TaqMan® SNP Genotyping Assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific) on a PRISM 7900 sequence detection system (Applied Biosystems). Briefly, genomic DNA amplification (5 ng) was performed with a reaction volume (10 µl) with 40× predesigned TaqMan SNP Genotyping Assay (0.25 µl) (comprising VIC® and FAM™ dye-labeled MGB probes, and two unlabeled primers) and 2× TaqMan® GTXpress™ Master Mix (5 µl). Thermal cycling was performed with initial denaturation at 95 °C for 2 min, 40 cycles at 95 °C for 15 s, and 60 °C for 40 s [48].

Statistical analysis

The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was conducted to assess data normality. Continuous data that were normally distributed are given as means ± standard deviations (SDs), while those without normal distribution are presented as medians (interquartile range). Categorical data are shown as numbers (percentages). As the distributions of white blood cell (WBC), neutrophil and monocyte counts, hs-CRP, Agatston calcium scores, volume calcium scores, and TCF21 were skewed, the values were logarithmically transformed before analysis. Differences in baseline data between women and men were examined using the chi-square test or Student’s t test. Simple and multiple linear regression analyses were performed to assess associations between study variables with plasma TCF21. For categorical analyses, severe coronary artery calcification was defined a priori using established thresholds (Agatston score > 400 and calcium volume ≥ 150 mm³), which are recommended by the ACCF/AHA and prior studies as indicative of high to very high cardiovascular risk [49, 50]. Relationships between TCF21 and Agatston score > 400 and calcium volume score ≥ 150 (mm3) status were investigated through multivariate logistic regression analysis, incorporating the following covariates: (1) TCF21, age, and sex; (2) TCF21, age, sex, and body mass index (BMI); (3) TCF21, age, sex, BMI, and triglycerides; (4) TCF21, age, sex, BMI, triglycerides, hypertension, and type 2 DM (T2DM). Median values and interquartile range of CAC scores within the three TCF21 groups were assessed for trends. In addition, comparisons of continuous variables among the three TCF21 genotype groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance. Given the hypothesis-driven nature of the primary analyses, no formal multiple testing correction was applied to define statistical significance. However, to address the potential inflation of Type I error due to multiple comparisons, a false discovery rate (FDR) correction using the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure was additionally performed as a sensitivity analysis for association analyses involving multiple outcomes, and FDR-adjusted q values are reported where appropriate. A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered significant. All statistical analyses were conducted with JMP for Windows (v10.0, SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Characteristics of the patients

The patients had a mean age of 59.5 ± 6.3 years (mean ± standard deviation), with 98 individuals (53.0%) identified as male and 87 individuals (47.0%) as female (Table 1). The prevalence rates for diabetes mellitus, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and metabolic syndrome were 21.1%, 49.2%, 17.3%, and 22.2%, respectively. Furthermore, the mean duration of schizophrenia among all patients was 25.5 ± 11.2 years. The female patients exhibited a higher prevalence of hyperlipidemia and higher platelet count, eGFR, total cholesterol, and HDL-C, along with decreased levels of creatinine, hematocrit, hemoglobin, and WBC count. Additionally, they were older compared to the male patients. However, there was no significant difference in TCF21 levels between the male and female patients (Table 1). Moreover, when stratified by age groups (> 50 years and ≤ 50 years), there was no significant difference in TCF21 level between the two groups (4.9 ng/mL (interquartile range: 3.0-7.5 ng/mL vs. 6.0 ng/mL (interquartile range: 3.3–12.0 ng/mL, p = 0.241). Moreover, there was no significant difference in TCF21 levels between the patients with and without T2DM (5.0 ng/mL (interquartile range: 2.8–6.9 ng/mL) vs. 4.8 ng/mL (interquartile range: 3.1–8.1 ng/mL, p = 0.132). Additionally, 62 patients (33.5%) were treated with first-generation antipsychotics, whereas 123 patients (66.5%) were treated with second-generation antipsychotics. Analysis of TCF21 levels based on antipsychotic treatment (first-generation vs. second-generation) revealed no significant differences between patients receiving first-generation antipsychotics and those receiving second-generation antipsychotics, with levels of 5.8 ng/mL (interquartile range: 2.9–7.9 ng/mL) and 4.8 ng/mL (interquartile range: 3.1–7.2 ng/mL), respectively (p = 0.102). These findings suggest that variables such as age, sex, treatment for T2DM, and antipsychotic treatment did not confound the TCF21 data.

Association of plasma TCF21 with CAC scores

Multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to examine whether plasma TCF21 levels were independently associated with severe CAC. Using predefined thresholds, plasma TCF21 concentrations were significantly associated with severe coronary artery calcification defined by an Agatston score > 400. This association remained significant after adjustment for age, sex, BMI, triglycerides, hypertension, and T2DM, which were selected based on established clinical relevance and prior evidence of strong associations with CAC (Table 4). Similarly, plasma TCF21 concentrations were significantly associated with a calcium volume score ≥ 150 mm³ after adjustment for the same covariates (Table 4). Inflammatory cell counts and hs-CRP were not included as covariates in these models because they may represent downstream inflammatory markers potentially linking TCF21 to atherosclerosis, and adjustment for these variables could result in overadjustment and attenuation of biologically meaningful associations.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that plasma TCF21 levels were associated with multiple metabolic, inflammatory, and hematological parameters, including BMI, triglyceride levels, and circulating leukocyte subsets. After FDR correction, the associations with triglyceride levels, white blood cell, neutrophil, and monocyte counts remained robust, whereas associations with BMI, platelet count, and hs-CRP should be interpreted as exploratory. In contrast, TCF21 levels consistently exhibited a negative association with CAC burden, as reflected by both the Agatston calcium score and calcium score by volume. Multivariate logistic regression analyses further identified reduced TCF21 levels as an independent factor associated with increased CAC burden, remaining statistically significant after adjustment for age, sex, and established cardiovascular risk factors. Moreover, individuals with the CC genotype demonstrated lower TCF21 levels and higher Agatston calcium scores, as well as elevated calcium volume scores, compared to those with the GG and GC genotypes. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate an association between reduced TCF21 levels and increased CAC assessed by CT in patients with chronic schizophrenia. The TCF21 rs12190287 polymorphism was associated with lower circulating TCF21 levels and higher CAC scores in this population. These findings suggest that TCF21 may serve as a potential biomarker of CAC in patients with chronic schizophrenia. However, due to the cross-sectional design, causal relationships cannot be established, and reverse causality cannot be excluded.

CAC, a hallmark of advanced atherosclerosis, represents the ectopic deposition of calcium phosphate salts within vascular tissue. The prevalence of CAC is significantly elevated in individuals with diabetes and chronic kidney disease [51]. Severe atherosclerosis and arterial stiffening, accompanied by extensive vascular calcification, are critical factors contributing to elevated cardiovascular mortality [52]. Vascular calcification is now recognized as an active process, regulated by cellular and molecular mechanisms analogous to those that regulate bone formation. In addition, Cai et al. reported close associations between inflammation and inflammatory cytokines with the development of vascular calcification and atherosclerosis, and suggested that these conditions may be more accurately termed “calcific vasculitis” and “atheroscleritis” [20]. TCF21 is highly expressed in fibromyocytes, a collagen-producing cell type derived from VSMCs, and is also present in atherosclerotic plaques in both humans and mice [22]. In this study, we found a positive association between plasma TCF21 level with inflammatory markers, including WBC, neutrophil and monocyte counts. In addition to inflammatory markers, circulating TCF21 levels were also positively associated with triglyceride levels. These findings suggest that TCF21 is closely linked to the metabolic-inflammatory milieu that characterizes patients with chronic schizophrenia. Metabolic dysregulation and low-grade systemic inflammation are highly prevalent in this population and are recognized contributors to accelerated atherosclerosis and CAC [9, 10, 14]. Therefore, the observed associations between TCF21 and these metabolic and inflammatory variables may reflect shared upstream pathophysiological pathways rather than independent effects. This consideration also supports our decision not to include these interrelated variables simultaneously in multivariable models, in order to avoid potential overadjustment. Similarly, Zhao et al. [53] reported that TCF21 promoted the generation of inflammatory cytokines, interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and IL-6, in endothelial cells, thereby facilitating VSMC osteogenesis. The study further suggests that TCF21 exacerbates vascular calcification through the activation of IL-6/STAT3 signaling and promotion of crosstalk between VSMCs and endothelial cells, providing new insights into the pathogenesis of vascular calcification. Additionally, Kim et al. [54] discovered that the well-known environmental sensor, aryl-hydrocarbon receptor, is regulated by TCF21 and interacts with it to regulate pro-inflammatory gene expressions in coronary artery smooth muscle cells. They further demonstrated that oxidized LDL, which is known to contribute to atherosclerosis, could activate the aryl-hydrocarbon receptor signaling pathway within atherosclerotic plaques [54]. However, the biological mechanisms linking TCF21 to the pathogenesis of CAC remain incompletely understood. Exploring these mechanisms may be particularly relevant in patients with chronic schizophrenia, a population characterized by persistent low-grade inflammation and long-term exposure to antipsychotic medications [2, 17, 55]. Importantly, our findings suggest a close association between reduced TCF21 levels and elevated CAC scores. Consistent with these observations, Liu et al. [56] demonstrated a significant correlation between decreased TCF21 expression and the severity of CAD. Previous studies have identified TCF21 as a particularly compelling candidate among the 13 novel loci discovered in the initial CARDIoGRAM meta-analysis, suggesting it may be a critical link to CHD. TCF21 was originally cloned in their laboratory as well as in two others [38, 57], and it is expressed in epicardial progenitor cells that differentiate into developing coronary artery SMCs [38], highlighting its potential role in vascular remodeling and atherosclerotic progression. Research on the function of TCF21 in adults has been limited by a study in which Tcf21 knockout mice died postnatally due to respiratory failure and pulmonary hypoplasia [39]. TCF21 is a recognized candidate tumor suppressor gene and is often epigenetically silenced in various human cancers [58, 59]. These findings suggest that the loss of TCF21 expression may serve as an early biomarker for an elevated risk of cancer. Furthermore, Ye et al. [60] demonstrated that abnormal TCF21 methylation in patients with clear cell renal cell carcinoma was associated with a poor prognosis. Moreover, Miller et al. [61] suggested that aberrant upregulation of TCF21 in coronary SMCs may elevate the risk of CHD by altering the SMC response to vascular injury. Additionally, Liu et al. [56] reported a reduction in serum TCF21 levels among patients with CAD and suggested that TCF21 may play a protective role in the severity of CAD and the number of vessel lesions. In the current study, lower plasma TCF21 levels were associated with higher CAC scores, supporting the hypothesis that reduced TCF21 expression may be linked to CAC progression. However, causal inferences cannot be drawn due to the cross-sectional design, and the absence of in vivo or in vitro functional assays limits mechanistic interpretation. Further experimental studies are needed to elucidate the molecular pathways through which TCF21 influences vascular calcification.

In our study, individuals with the CC genotype exhibited lower TCF21 levels, higher Agatston calcium scores, and increased calcium volume scores compared to those with the GG and GC genotypes. These findings, together with prior studies, suggest that rs12190287 modulates TCF21 expression and downstream cardiovascular phenotypes in a context-dependent manner. Notably, a previous study found that the G allele and GG genotype of rs12190287 in TCF21, along with increased TCF21 levels, were significantly associated with the occurrence of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (PAF) and its recurrence after ablation. The TCF21 rs12190287 polymorphism regulates the expression of TCF21, and the G allele and GG genotype are linked to the pathogenesis and recurrence of PAF [43]. This suggests that the TCF21 rs12190287 polymorphism may serve as a potential marker of genetic susceptibility to the onset of PAF and its recurrence after catheter ablation in the Chinese population [43]. TCF21 is involved in complex genetic pathways that influence cardiovascular health [48, 54, 62, 63]. Therefore, alterations in TCF21 expression may modify these pathways, potentially leading to increased calcification through various signaling mechanisms. The role of TCF21 in processes such as SMC differentiation and the regulation of genes involved in vascular remodeling supports the hypothesis that changes in its expression could contribute to pathological calcification. Although TCF21 has been implicated in vascular pathology in the general population, its role in patients with schizophrenia has not been previously examined. The present findings therefore extend existing knowledge by providing schizophrenia-relevant evidence, rather than establishing a disease-specific mechanistic pathway.

Limitations and suggestion for further research

This study has several limitations. First, a non-schizophrenia control group was not included; therefore, direct comparisons with the general population could not be performed. As a result, it remains unclear whether the observed association between decreased TCF21 levels and increased CAC scores is specific to chronic schizophrenia or reflects a more general biological phenomenon. Future studies including healthy controls and other disease populations are warranted to clarify the specificity of this association and to further elucidate the potential mediating roles of schizophrenia-related pathophysiological mechanisms, such as chronic inflammation and long-term antipsychotic exposure. Nevertheless, the prevalence of diabetes mellitus, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and metabolic syndrome observed in our cohort reflect the substantial cardiometabolic burden commonly reported in patients with chronic schizophrenia and are consistent with previous epidemiological studies. In addition, although sex was adjusted for in the multivariable analyses, potential sex-specific effects or sex-by-exposure interactions were not formally examined; therefore, the findings may not fully capture sex-dependent associations. Second, the present study was not designed to comprehensively assess lifestyle factors (such as physical activity and smoking status), markers of chronic inflammation, or detailed antipsychotic medication exposure. Consequently, these variables were not included in the statistical models, and their potential modifying effects on the association between plasma TCF21 levels and CAC could not be evaluated. In particular, antipsychotic medications were not analyzed according to drug class (first- versus second-generation), cumulative exposure, or dosage, and were therefore not included as covariates. This limitation may have influenced cardiometabolic outcomes and masked potential differential effects of antipsychotic treatment on TCF21 levels and CAC. Third, in the multiple linear regression analyses, adjustment was limited to age and sex to minimize overadjustment and model instability. In addition, logistic regression analyses were designed to assess the independent association between TCF21 and CAC after adjustment for established cardiovascular risk factors, rather than to construct a comprehensive predictive model. Fourth, the relatively small sample size and the cross-sectional design preclude causal inferences regarding the relationship between TCF21 levels and CAC. The temporal sequence and directionality of this association cannot be determined. It remains possible that CAC progression may influence TCF21 expression, or that both are affected by shared underlying biological mechanisms. Future studies with larger sample sizes, long-term prospective follow-up, and replication in independent cohorts or different ethnic populations are warranted to clarify temporal relationships and confirm our findings.

Implications for practice

The findings of this study suggest that monitoring TCF21 levels in patients with chronic schizophrenia may be a valuable component of cardiovascular risk assessment. Given the observed association between decreased TCF21 levels and elevated CAC scores, healthcare providers should consider incorporating genetic testing for TCF21 polymorphisms as part of a comprehensive evaluation for cardiovascular disease risk in this population. Early identification of individuals at higher risk could lead to more targeted preventive strategies, such as lifestyle interventions and pharmacological treatments aimed at mitigating cardiovascular complications. Additionally, the integration of regular cardiovascular screening into the treatment plans of patients with chronic schizophrenia could improve long-term health outcomes, addressing not only psychiatric needs but also physical health risks.

Conclusions

The findings of this study indicate that reduced TCF21 levels are associated with elevated CAC scores in patients with chronic schizophrenia. The TCF21 rs12190287 polymorphism may be associated with lower TCF21 levels and higher CAC scores. These results suggest that TCF21 may serve as a potential biomarker of CAC in this population. However, due to the cross-sectional design, causal relationships cannot be established.

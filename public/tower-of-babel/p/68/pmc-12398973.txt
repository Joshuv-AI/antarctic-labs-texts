Development and provider assessment of a new oncology network with care pathways for gastrointestinal cancer patients: the collaborative approach

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn an era of increasing demands on healthcare services, the development of regional oncology networks including care pathways may be instrumental to improve the efficiency and quality of care. However, developing such networks involving numerous stakeholders and hospitals is challenging as no templates exist. This study evaluates the development of a large regional network with the objective to provide a practical template.MethodsA multicentre survey was conducted between October 1, 2024 and January 31, 2025. Clinicians from the departments of medical oncology, surgery, gastroenterology, pathology, radiology, nuclear medicine, and radiotherapy at the 13 hospitals in the OncoNoVo+ regional oncology network were invited. The survey included open-ended, multiple-choice, and slider questions (score range 0–100 on a visual analogue scale), focusing on baseline characteristics (n = 7), functioning of the network and care pathways (n = 16), and the development process of the care pathways (n = 6).ResultsOverall, 216 medical specialists, researchers, nurse specialists, and case-managers from 13 hospitals developed six care pathways (esophagogastric, pancreatic, small intestinal, rectal, liver, and biliary tract cancer). In the survey, 135 (63%) clinicians participated. Median scores of 75 or higher were observed in nine out of 11 satisfaction statements. Highest scores were observed for statements about the importance of regional care pathways, the recommendation of pathways outside the region, and the effectiveness of the OncoNoVo+ app (median, 85 [IQR, 75 to 97], 85 [75 to 99], and 84 [75 to 95], respectively). Mentioned ‘essential factors for success’ included coordination, collaboration, technology, and mutual respect. ‘Improvement factors’ included clearer implementation, better communication, regular updates, and equal involvement across hospitals and specialties.ConclusionThe multicenter development and maintenance of regional care pathways for patients with gastrointestinal cancers within a regional oncology network seems feasible and effective with high satisfaction among participating hospitals. Background

Regional collaboration within healthcare systems is increasingly important given the current challenges in healthcare including financial restraints and staff shortage. Centralization with regionalization of care in networks could potentially address these challenges [1]. Particularly in rare malignancies such as hepato-pancreato-biliary and upper gastrointestinal cancers, centralization of care is associated with lower mortality rates and improved long-term survival [2, 3]. Although interhospital unity among clinicians (e.g. gastroenterologists, oncologists, surgeons, pathologists, radiologists, and radiation oncologists) is crucial to optimize the efficiency and quality of care for these patients [4, 5], comprehensive literature on the development of sustainable regional collaboration is limited.

Various challenges arise during the shift toward regionalization of care [6, 7]. First, healthcare providers encounter difficulties in reaching agreements on diagnostic workups and determining what treatments can be administered at which facilities, all while pursuing high-quality care [8]. Second, logistical issues in managing referrals and facilitating patient information exchange are common [9]. Third, patients face increased travel distances between their homes and healthcare facilities, which may lead to delays in presentation or, in some cases, no presentation at all [8, 9]. These challenges are particularly critical for patients with rare malignancies, who require referral to export centers and frequently undergo long-term treatments [9].

To address these challenges, regional oncology networks have been developed worldwide [10]. In the Netherlands, this network was named OncoNoVo+, guided by the motto “nearby if possible, far away only if needed”, aiming to provide treatment close to home whenever feasible, with referrals to specialized and often distant academic hospitals only when necessary [11, 12]. For the 3.5 million inhabitants in the provinces Noord-Holland and Flevoland, the regional network involves approximately 20% of cancer care in the Netherlands [13]. This initiative aims to improve specialized cancer care and enhance regional collaboration among clinicians by establishing care pathways that include regional agreements on referrals and treatment protocols. Since its introduction, several care pathways have been developed in collaboration with the 13 affiliated hospitals [11].

Although regional oncology networks are perceived as highly instrumental in managing the current challenges in healthcare and for ensuring the sustainability and accessibility of care, relevant literature evaluating such pathways and explaining their development is lacking. This survey study aims to describe the development process and to assess the functioning of the Dutch OncoNoVo + oncology network to provide a comprehensive template that could assist others in developing comparable networks.

Methods

Study design, population and data collection

This regional multicenter survey study was conducted between October 1, 2024 and January 31, 2025. Clinicians working in the departments of medical oncology, surgery, gastroenterology, pathology, radiology, nuclear medicine, and radiotherapy at one of the 13 hospitals affiliated with the OncoNoVo + oncology network in the provinces North-Holland and Flevoland in the Netherlands were asked to complete the survey. These hospitals include Amstelland Hospital, Amsterdam UMC, Netherlands Cancer Institute – Antoni van Leeuwenhoek, BovenIJ, Dijklander Hospital, Flevohospital, Noordwest Hospital Group, OLVG, Rode Kruis Hospital, Spaarne Gasthuis, St. Jansdal Hospital, Tergooi Medical Center, and Zaans Medical Center. Of these 13, 12 are regional hospitals (including one comprehensive cancer center), and one is a university medical center. Additional information on these hospitals (including topographic location and hospital size) is shown in Supplementary File 1, p 2.

The online survey was conducted and reported following the Checklist for Reporting Results of Internet E-Surveys (CHERRIES) [14]. Clinicians were informed about the goals of the survey, its duration, and data storage. Given the estimated minimal effect on participants and the absence of patient data collection, official approval by the Institutional Medical Ethics Committee of Amsterdam UMC was deemed unnecessary. Participants were anonymized.

Survey development and procedures

To date, no validated questionnaire exists for evaluating regional care networks and care pathways. However, some indicators are considered important for assessing clinical pathways and integrated care across different domains, following the Leuven Clinical Pathway Compass [15]. This compass, as part of a multicriteria approach, includes domains such as structure, process, and outcome, which served as a framework during the development of our survey [16]. All main authors participated in the development of this survey.

The survey consisted of two parts. Part I aimed to evaluate the general opinion regarding the regional oncology network and care pathways, while Part II focused on assessing the development process of the care pathways. The survey included open-ended, multiple-choice, and slider (score range 0–100 on a visual analogue scale) questions. A score of 0 indicated low satisfaction, while a score of 100 indicated very high satisfaction; scores were displayed in increments of 1 point. The survey addressed baseline characteristics (n = 7), functioning of the network and care pathways (n = 16), and the development of care pathways (n = 6). All participants were asked to complete Part I, whereas Part II was only completed by those who had been involved in the development of one or more of the care pathways. The survey was created and stored in Castor, an online platform compliant with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). An overview of the survey is presented in Supplementary File 2, p 3.

The survey was distributed through two different routes. First, it could be directly completed during an online OncoNoVo + meeting using a QR code. Second, the survey was sent via email to all participants. Contact details were obtained from a pre-existing contact list including 216 clinicians involved within the network and the development of care pathways. Clinicians were invited to complete the survey and to forward it to others who were believed to belong to the target group of the survey (i.e. those who also receive and use the regional cancer care pathways). All clinicians were contacted simultaneously and given four months to complete the questionnaire. Multiple reminders were sent.

Regional oncology network and care pathways

In 2019, the gastrointestinal cancers part of the OncoNoVo + regional oncology network was formed. This consisted of a ‘steering committee’ including three to five representatives for each of the 13 participating hospitals. This steering committee was managed by a chair (MGB), a PhD student coordinator (BTH), and a cancer network staff member (EVK). The representatives per hospital worked at the departments of medical oncology, surgery, gastroenterology, pathology, radiology, nuclear medicine and radiotherapy (Fig. 1). Nearly 50 clinicians participated in this group. During biannual live and online meetings, collaborative decisions are made for instance regarding the next gastrointestinal cancer care pathway to be developed. Each member is responsible for identifying clinicians from the previously mentioned departments within their center who could be interested in contributing to the development of the next care pathway. This process is initiated and overseen by the coordinator who communicates with all members and is available to answer questions.

An organizational chart of OncoNoVo+ (orange) and a cancer care pathway development flowchart (green)

The steering committee consecutively initiated and developed care pathways for esophagogastric, pancreatic, small intestinal, rectal, hepatic, and biliary tract cancer. These six pathways were regionally implemented and published in a mobile app (OncoNoVo+ app). Once the decision for a new care pathway is made, the first step is to appoint a chair and vice-chair. These then establish a care pathway committee, preferably with one delegate from each medical specialty, along with various working groups. Each medical specialty is represented in one of the working groups. The different working groups each collaboratively write a chapter for the care pathway. For example, the gastroenterology working group is responsible for the ‘biliary drainage’ chapter in the ‘biliary tract cancer care pathway’. Once the working groups are established, online meetings are scheduled for each working group. During these meetings, the groups create and discuss their chapters, establishing concrete agreements regarding regional care, diagnostics, treatment, referral, and patient return referral. These meetings are initiated, hosted, and supported by the coordinator.

Once each working group has completed their chapter, a draft version of the full care pathway is reviewed by the steering committee to ensure clarity, conciseness, and consistency in language and agreements. Hereafter, the care pathway is presented during one of the two annual meetings, where it is either approved or minor adjustments are requested. Subsequently, the care pathway is published as a PDF and distributed throughout the region by members of the steering committee or independently. Finally, the care pathway is released on the OncoNoVo+ app to facilitate the accessibility and availability of all care pathways at any time. The care pathway chair and vice-chair, and the steering committee managers (BTH, EVK, MB) together oversee this development process. An organizational chart and a flowchart of the pathway development process is presented in Fig. 1. The OncoNoVo+ app was made freely available yet password secured for privacy reasons as it also included contact details to case managers and medical specialists. As an example, the recently finished biliary tract cancer care pathway is attached in Supplementary File 3, p 5.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS for Windows version 24.0 (IBM SPSS Inc., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the extracted data. Continuous data were presented as median with interquartile range (IQR). A Mann–Whitney U test was used for non-parametric comparisons (academic versus [vs.] regional, with the comprehensive cancer center included as regional hospital). Categorical data were expressed as frequencies and percentages and did not included additional comparisons. Additional sensitivity analyses were conducted to compare academic hospitals, including the comprehensive cancer center classified as an academic hospital due to its expertise in cancer care, with regional hospitals. A satisfaction score of 75 or higher on a visual analogue scale was considered sufficient. P-values were two-sided and a p-value < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant. Figures were created using R, version 4.2.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

Role of funding source

The funder of the study had no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, manuscript development, and decision to publish.

Results

Characteristics of the respondents are shown in Table 1. In total, 135 healthcare providers from all 13 participating hospitals completed the questionnaire. Among the respondents, 36% (48/135) were affiliated with the university medical center, 8% (11/135) were affiliated with the comprehensive cancer center, and 56% (76/135) were affiliated with a regional hospital. Surgery and gastroenterology were the most represented specialties (31% and 25%, respectively). Overall, 88% (113/129) of participants were medical specialists. The pancreatic cancer pathway was most frequently used (67%, 91/135). Colon cancer was proposed as the next care pathway to be developed by 41% (34/83).

Discussion

This study evaluating the development of a regional oncology network (3.5 million inhabitants, 13 hospitals) starting with six care pathways for gastrointestinal cancers, found high satisfaction scores. The highest scores were observed in statements about the importance of regional care pathways, the recommendation of pathways internationally, and the effectiveness of the OncoNoVo+ app.

In general, our findings align with previous studies evaluating care pathways for cancers such as breast, lung, and colon [17–20]. A 2020 Australian literature review examining care pathways for several cancer types in Denmark, Sweden, Norway, the United Kingdom, Japan, and Australia identified key factors for successful implementation of such pathways, including embedding pathways within healthcare systems, assessing consumer awareness, maintaining engagement, and integrating pathways into existing programs. Beyond clinical protocols, these pathways “outline critical elements of care across the entire cancer treatment process”, similar to our approach [18]. Additionally, a survey conducted in Sweden among 298 primary care physicians reported high satisfaction rates with care pathways. The majority of respondents indicated that these pathways improve coordination across healthcare providers and different phases of cancer diagnosis and early treatment [20]. Although some studies have reported outcomes on the evaluation of care pathways, none have specifically included relatively rare gastrointestinal cancers, including several rare hepato-pancreato-biliary and upper gastrointestinal cancers. Such cancers require highly centralized care, which is greatly facilitated by the establishment of networks with care pathways. The implementation of these pathways present additional challenges, including a higher level of regional collaboration and greater mutual trust and cooperation. Furthermore, a comprehensive evaluation of the establishment of a regional oncology network was not included in these studies [17–20].

Some notable outcomes were observed in this survey. Although all median agreement scores exceeded 65, significant differences were identified between the responses of the university medical center and regional hospitals for several statements. For example, regarding statement 5 “Improved patient care after implementation”, the university medical center scored significantly higher compared to regional hospitals. Similarly, respondents from the university medical center reported higher scores for statements 6 “Regional relationships sustainably improved” and 7 “Smoother referrals”. Comparable outcomes were observed in a sensitivity analysis comparing academic and regional hospitals, including the comprehensive cancer center as an academic center, given its high level of expertise in cancer care. These differences suggest that the university medical centers (including the comprehensive cancer center) generally have a more favorable view on care improvements and interhospital collaboration. This also highlights that referral centers (i.e. the university hospital or others) should actively survey their referring centers to early detect issues regarding the referral process or any other issues requiring improvement.

Interestingly, two statements—statement 5 “Improved patient care after implementation” and statement 7 “Smoother referrals”—scored below 75, with lower scores for regional hospitals. These results indicate that smoother referrals, a key benefit of the pathways, were less effectively perceived in the referring hospitals. The overall high satisfaction scores for the network and pathways themselves, contrasted with lower scores regarding their regional implementation, suggest room for improvement. The highest scores were achieved for statement 9 “Regional pathways are essential” and statement 10 “International recommendation of pathways”, with medians of 85, reflecting strong consensus among all respondents. This underscores overall commitment to pathway development. Moving forward, a more inclusive approach involving specialists from both academic and regional backgrounds is crucial. Improving coordination between academic and regional institutions, as well as greater attention to the implementation process by providing appropriate tools and technology [21] is necessary to improve the overall effectiveness of the care pathways. Currently, care pathways are published as PDFs and distributed throughout the region, either via members of the steering committee or independently. The pathways are also accessible through the OncoNoVo+ app, which provides healthcare providers with continuous and easy access to all regional agreements by cancer type. This app is freely available and includes contact details for case managers and medical specialists for consultation when needed. Based on our experience, successful dissemination and adherence depend heavily on enthusiastic and engaged healthcare providers, as well as clear points of contact in the form of coordinators and local stakeholders, who play a crucial role in facilitating communication and supporting implementation efforts.

The present study should be interpreted considering several limitations. First, although the survey was distributed using an open-access link with a request to share it widely, the survey was primarily completed by healthcare providers involved in the establishment of the regional oncology network and care pathways, which could have introduced selection bias. However, given the large scale of the regional network, there are relatively few users who were not already engaged in its development. In this context, successful long-term implementation is most effective when all end-users are also involved in evaluation and feedback. Second, responses were predominantly provided by medical specialists, which might have led to response bias to some extent. Future studies should focus on increased input from nurse specialists, case-managers, and particularly on patients to ensure a more comprehensive perspective. Third, differences in the interpretation of terms such as “effectiveness” or “collaboration” may have influenced internal validity. Future research should incorporate objective outcome measures, such as time to referral, disease-specific outcomes, and overall survival, to strengthen the findings. Fourth, although many items in our study align with the StaRI checklist [22] for process implementation, the focus was on assessing provider satisfaction with the care pathways rather than formally evaluating the implementation process. Therefore, not all StaRI checklist items were addressed. Instead, our study was conducted in accordance with the Checklist for Reporting Results of Internet E-Surveys (CHERRIES) [14].

The main strength of the current study is that, to our knowledge, it is among the first to evaluate the development of a regional oncology network for gastrointestinal cancers and care pathways, including several rare upper gastrointestinal and hepato-pancreato-biliary cancers. These require highly specialized care, such as complex biliary drainage procedures, specific oncological targeted treatments, and complex surgical procedures, demanding the utmost level of regional-academic collaboration. With 135 out of 216 clinicians from 13 different hospitals participating in this multicenter survey, the study achieved both a high response rate and a holistic perspective, attributed to the inclusion of diverse specialties and a well-balanced distribution of hospital sizes and respondents. Since broad involvement across university and regional hospitals was identified as a key condition in the survey itself, it is equally essential in the evaluation process. Given that OncoNoVo+ collectively accounts for almost a quarter of all oncological care in the Netherlands [23] this study provides a strong representation of the Dutch national cancer care. Moreover, better adherence to guidelines through these care pathways may reduce the number of radiological and endoscopic procedures, thereby improving care efficiency.

Future regional oncology networks should focus on ensuring equal involvement of hospitals (both academic and regional), improving implementation, an up-to-date clinical trial overview, and the use of digital tools to enhance utilization. The active involvement of dedicated clinicians, combined with clear coordination by an appointed coordinator, is essential for the success of regional collaboration and must be sustained. Following the criteria for high-quality clinical pathways in oncology described by the American Society of Clinical Oncology, the process evaluation of such networks and pathways should be standardized to further improve our healthcare system [24]. Beyond clinician satisfaction rates, evaluations should include data on patient satisfaction, time to referral, and disease-specific and survival outcomes [17, 25]. Although enhanced regional collaboration does not necessarily entail centralization, the broader impact of centralization on management structures and access to healthcare, particularly in terms of capacity and availability in rural areas, should be investigated, as existing perspectives remain ambiguous [9, 26–29]. Meanwhile, OncoNoVo+ regional oncology network has broadened its scope beyond gastrointestinal cancers and is currently establishing care pathways within the fields of urology, gynecology, and endocrinology. Furthermore, the OncoNoVo+ app has been updated and now includes a clear and up-to-date study overview, managed by designated staff employees employed by the respective hospitals.

Conclusion

Clinicians are highly satisfied with the gastrointestinal cancers part of the OncoNoVo+ regional oncology network with associated care pathways. The described process of network and care pathway development could serve as a template for others who wish to develop a network for intra- and interregional collaboration. Room for improvement lies in ensuring equal engagement, clear implementation, and regular updates of both treatment protocols and the overview of ongoing clinical trials.

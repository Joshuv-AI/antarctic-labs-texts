Prognostic value of combined RDW, NLR, PLR, and mpv detection in patients with acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease: a single-center retrospective cohort study
Authors: Yu-Lan Zhang, Ge Yuan, Yuan-Yuan Xiao, Xing-Jing Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to evaluate the prognostic significance of combined peripheral blood red cell distribution width (RDW), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and mean platelet volume (MPV) in predicting short-term outcomes among patients with acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (AECOPD).MethodsThis retrospective analysis included 165 patients hospitalized for AECOPD at the Departments of General Medicine and Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine of Wusong Central Hospital between December 2023 and November 2024. Patients were divided into a poor prognosis group (64 cases) and a good prognosis group (101 cases) based on re-hospitalization for AECOPD within 3 months post-discharge. Seventy healthy individuals undergoing routine physical examination during the same period served as the control group. RDW, NLR, PLR, and MPV values were compared across the three groups. Multivariate logistic regression was performed to identify independent risk factors associated with short-term prognosis. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted to evaluate the predictive performance of individual and combined hematologic markers.Results(1) patients in the poor prognosis group exhibited significantly elevated RDW, NLR, and PLR levels compared with those in the good prognosis and control groups, with MPV values demonstrating an increasing trend (p < 0.001). (2) Multivariate logistic regression identified absolute neutrophil count and RDW as independent risk factors for poor short-term prognosis in AECOPD. (3) ROC analysis indicated that the area under the curve for combined detection of RDW, NLR, PLR, and MPV was 0.950, surpassing the predictive accuracy of each individual marker.ConclusionThe combined assessment of RDW, NLR, PLR, and MPV offers superior predictive value over individual indicators for short-term prognosis in AECOPD patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12890-026-04192-4.Keywords: Acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, Mean platelet volume, Neutrophil/lymphocyte ratio; platelet/lymphocyte ratio, Prognosis, Red blood cell distribution width

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a prevalent heterogeneous respiratory disorder, primarily characterized by dyspnea, cough, and sputum production. Acute exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD) is defined as a worsening of dyspnea and/or increased cough and sputum production occurring within a 14-day period, with or without alterations in medication. AECOPD is associated with an elevated risk of repeated hospitalization [1]. Although most patients experience symptomatic improvement following treatment, some fail to recover baseline respiratory function after an exacerbation episode [2]. AECOPD represents a significant contributor to COPD-related mortality. Studies have shown that its occurrence and progression are influenced by proinflammatory cytokine activity, systemic inflammatory responses, and environmental factors. Identification of reliable biomarkers that reflect disease severity and short-term exacerbation risk is essential for guiding treatment and reducing the frequency of recurrent episodes [3].

Red blood cell distribution width (RDW) was initially utilized in the diagnostic evaluation of anemia and has subsequently been shown to possess prognostic value in various pathological conditions. In the context of AECOPD, elevated RDW levels have been correlated with increased disease severity, frequency of exacerbations, and greater mortality risk [4, 5]. The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) serves as an indicator of systemic inflammation and infections, while the platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) reflects inflammatory status and has demonstrated prognostic value in cardiovascular disease and malignancy [6, 7]. Mean platelet volume (MPV) reflects platelet size and maturity, with higher MPV suggesting enhanced megakaryocyte activation or the presence of immature platelets. Although the individual prognostic value of RDW, NLR, PLR, and MPV in AECOPD has been investigated, evidence supporting the combined use of these hematologic parameters remains limited [8, 9]. The present study examined the association between combined measurement of RDW, NLR, PLR, and MPV and short-term prognosis, defined as clinical outcomes within three months following an AECOPD-related hospitalization, aiming to provide a reference for optimizing clinical management strategies.

Materials and methods

Study participants

This cohort retrospective study used non-random sampling method. A total of 165 patients hospitalized with AECOPD between December 2023 and November 2024 were included. The cohort consisted of 126 males and 39 females, aged 59 to 92 years, (mean age: 75.12 ± 7.57 years). Healthy controls (n = 70) were selected from the hospital’s routine health examination cohort during the same period as patient enrollment, including 37 males and 33 females, aged 65 to 86 years (mean age: 73.71 ± 5.62 years).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion criteria: (1) Diagnosis of AECOPD based on the Interpretation of Global Strategy for the Diagnosis, Treatment, Management, and Prevention of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease: 2025 Report [1]; (2) Availability of complete follow-up data for three months. Exclusion criteria: (1) Co-existence of chronic respiratory conditions, including primary or secondary pulmonary malignancies, pulmonary bullae, bronchiectasis, bronchial asthma, or pulmonary tuberculosis; (2) History of recent thoracic surgery; (3) Severe cardiac, hepatic, or renal insufficiency; (4) Active malignancies, major trauma, or other life-threatening conditions; (5) Loss to follow-up or death during follow-up. Individuals were excluded as healthy controls if they met any of the following criteria: (1) A current or prior diagnosis of any chronic respiratory disease (e.g., asthma, COPD); (2) Presence of acute infection or any systemic inflammatory condition at the time of blood sampling; (3) A documented history of major cardiopulmonary, hepatic, renal disease, or malignancy.

All patients provided written informed consent for biospecimen and clinical data collection. The study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Wusong Hospital (Approval No. 2024-P-07).

Methods

Detection of peripheral blood RDW, NLR, PLR, and MPV

Venous blood samples (4 mL) were collected from all subjects under fasting conditions in the morning. Critically, the initial blood draw for all AECOPD patients was completed prior to the first administration of any systemic glucocorticoids. Blood samples from the healthy controls were obtained on the day of their physical examination. Laboratory analyses were performed using an XN9000 automated blood analyzer (SYSMEX Corporation). Measurements included hemoglobin (HB), red blood cell count (RBC), white blood cell count (WBC), absolute neutrophil count (NEUT), absolute lymphocyte count (LYM), platelet count (PLT), MPV, and RDW. NLR was calculated as NEUT/LYM, and PLR as PLT/LYM.

Grouping of patients with AECOPD

This observational study did not intervene in clinical decisions. All patients were treated according to individualized regimens based on GOLD guidelines and institutional protocols. Discharge required clinical stability and adequate support. Readmissions within 3 months were primarily tracked via the hospital’s electronic medical record system. An event was confirmed only if the primary diagnosis was AECOPD (ICD-10: J44.1) or explicitly documented as a COPD exacerbation. Two researchers independently verified each case, with a senior researcher adjudicating disagreements.

The primary outcome of this study was rehospitalization due to AECOPD within 3 months after discharge. All patients were required to survive and complete the 3-month follow-up period. Accordingly, patients were divided into two groups: (1) poor prognosis group—patients who required re-hospitalization due to AECOPD within three months post-discharge; (2) good prognosis group—patients who remained clinically stable without re-hospitalization during the follow-up period.

Observational indicators

The following variables were analyzed: (1) Comparison of RDW, NLR, PLR, and MPV levels among poor prognosis, and good prognosis groups. (2) Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis to evaluate the predictive value of RDW, NLR, PLR, and MPV. (3) Multivariate logistic regression analysis to identify independent prognostic factors.

Statistical methods

The statistical analysis comprised two phases. Initially, a three-group comparison (healthy controls, good-prognosis, and poor-prognosis patients) was conducted to characterize the study population and examine trends in markers. Subsequently, Multivariable logistic regression was applied exclusively to the patient cohort (n = 165) with a dichotomous outcome (poor vs. good prognosis) to identify independent predictors of readmission. The healthy control group was excluded from the predictive model as it does not pertain to the prognostic endpoint under investigation.

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software, version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages and analyzed using the chi-squared test. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (X̅ ± S); and analyzed using the independent-samples t-test or analysis of variance (ANOVA), as appropriate. When ANOVA indicated statistical significance, post hoc comparisons were performed using the least significant difference test. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate the association between RDW, NLR, PLR, and MPV and short-term prognosis in AECOPD. The predictive performance of each indicator and their combination was assessed using ROC curve analysis. The combined prediction was calculated as the linear predictor from the final multivariable logistic regression model. the optimal cut-off was selected by maximizing the Youden index. A two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of general characteristics and hematologic parameters among control, poor prognosis, and good prognosis groups

The study demonstrated that 64 patients (38.8%) were classified in the poor prognosis group, while 101 patients (61.2%) were classified in good prognosis group. Anemia (WHO criteria: Hb < 13 g/dL for males and < 12 g/dL for females) was present in 4.3% of the healthy group, 48.5% in the good-prognosis group, and 62.5% in the poor-prognosis group. No statistically significant differences were observed among the three groups in terms of age or comorbidities (p > 0.05); however, a significant difference in sex distribution was identified (p < 0.05). Hematological parameters, including HB, RBC, PLT, WBC, NEUT, and LYM, differed significantly among groups (p < 0.05). The baseline inflammatory markers such as HB, PLT, NEUT, and LYM in the poor-prognosis group were significantly higher than those in the good-prognosis group (p’< 0.05), while there were no significant differences in demographic characteristics such as age, gender and comorbidities between the two groups (p’> 0.05) (Table 1).

Discussion

AECOPD is a critical event in the clinical course of COPD and remains a central focus in its prevention, diagnosis, and management [10]. Studies have shown that individuals with COPD experience an average of approximately 0.5 to 3.5 acute exacerbations annually [11]. The occurrence of a first severe exacerbation increases the likelihood of future episodes, and even a single moderate exacerbation may raise the risk of subsequent events by approximately 70%. AECOPD is the primary contributor to multiple hospitalizations in individuals with COPD [3]. Despite symptomatic improvement following active treatment, about 34% of individuals with AECOPD are readmitted or experience mortality due to another exacerbation within three months after discharge. This suggests a heightened vulnerability to adverse events such as recurrent exacerbations and acute attacks of comorbidities, particularly infections, during this post-discharge period [12]. Therefore, early and accurate identification of individuals at high risk for poor short-term prognosis is crucial for optimizing clinical decision-making, implementing personalized interventions, and improving health outcomes [13].

RDW is influenced various pathophysiological processes commonly observed in COPD, including chronic hypoxemia, systemic inflammation, oxidative stress, dysregulated erythropoietin secretion, and coagulation abnormalities. These factors impair erythropoiesis, leading to increased heterogeneity in red blood cell size and elevated RDW. Elevated RDW may further exacerbate tissue hypoxia and organ dysfunction directly or indirectly through its effects on hemorheology, microcirculatory flow, and inflammatory pathways [14]. The present study demonstrated that the poor prognosis group exhibited not only higher RDW levels but also a significantly greater prevalence of anemia. This suggests that the elevated RDW in this group may be partly attributable to increased heterogeneity in red blood cell size associated with anemia [15], However, multivariable analysis adjusted for hemoglobin confirmed that RDW remained an independent predictor of readmission, and its predictive contribution was significantly superior to that of hemoglobin alone. This indicates that in AECOPD, elevated RDW is a multifactorial, integrative marker: it reflects both an anemic state potentially stemming from chronic disease or nutritional deficiencies, and directly captures the adverse effects of systemic inflammation and oxidative stress on erythropoiesis and red cell survival [14]. Therefore, the value of RDW lies in its ability to extend beyond conventional anemia indicators such as hemoglobin, providing a more comprehensive reflection of the severity of bone marrow stress and systemic dysregulation during COPD exacerbations suggesting that RDW captures pathophysiological risk factors not fully reflected by hemoglobin concentration and may thus serve as a more comprehensive marker of systemic dysregulation than conventional hematological parameters.

NLR reflects the balance between innate immune activation and adaptive immune suppression. An elevated NLR is suggestive of a heightened systemic inflammatory response, frequently associated with bacterial infection or immune dysregulation. Yao et al. found that patients with AECOPD who exhibited elevated NLR levels had a higher 28-day mortality rate, underscoring the prognostic significance of this marker [16]. Moreover, increased NLR has been associated with the severity of pulmonary hypertension (r = 0.355, p < 0.001), suggesting a potential role in prognosis through the inflammation-vascular remodeling axis [17]. In the present study, NLR was significantly elevated in patients with poor outcomes. These observations support NLR as a practical and reliable indicator for early risk stratification in AECOPD, particularly in resource-limited settings where it can serve as an accessible core biomarker.

PLR reflects the degree of platelet activation and systemic inflammation, processes involved in the pathogenesis of AECOPD. Elevated PLR may result from thrombocytosis and/or lymphopenia. Prior studies have demonstrated an association between high PLR and increased mortality within 120 days after discharge among patients with AECOPD (p < 0.05) [18]. This may be attributed to the release of pro-inflammatory mediators, such as CD40 ligand and P-selectin, by activated platelets, which promote monocyte differentiation into macrophages and exacerbate the inflammatory cascade. Additionally, platelet-expressed Fas ligand may induce lymphocyte apoptosis, thereby weakening immune surveillance. In the present study, PLR was significantly elevated in patients with poor short‑term outcomes and was identified as an independent predictor of prognosis. These findings reinforce the potential utility of PLR as an accessible and informative biomarker for risk stratification in AECOPD, especially in clinical settings with limited resources.

MPV reflects platelet size and maturation status, and its fluctuations in AECOPD may follow a bidirectional pattern. During acute inflammatory states, excessive platelet activation may lead to the consumption of large platelets, whereas chronic hypoxia may stimulate increased production of immature, larger platelets via bone marrow compensation. An elevated MPV typically signifies enhanced megakaryocyte activity and increased release of immature platelets into circulation. The current study found that patients in the poor prognosis group demonstrated a trend toward elevated MPV, suggesting a link between MPV and systemic inflammation. These findings are consistent with the findings of Koçak [19], although they differ from the results published by Dai et al. [20]. This discrepancy may be due to differences in study populations or timing of sample collection. These insights reinforce the potential role of MPV as an accessible biomarker reflecting platelet activation and inflammatory status in AECOPD, which may aid in risk stratification during acute exacerbations.

Although each of the aforementioned biomarkers—RDW, NLR, PLR, and MPV—has demonstrated individual prognostic value in AECOPD, their levels may be influenced by various confounding factors. For example, deficiencies in folic acid or vitamin B12 may affect RDW measurements; the use of medications such as indomethacin or underlying conditions like hypersplenism may reduce neutrophil counts, thereby affecting NLR; and excessive alcohol consumption has been associated with alterations in PLR. Given these limitations, a combined multi-marker approach may provide more robust and comprehensive risk stratification by integrating complementary pathophysiological information. For example, a study conducted at Haiyang People’s Hospital reported that a model combining RDW, procalcitonin, C-reactive protein, and the MPV/platelet ratio achieved excellent diagnostic performance, underscoring the potential advantage of integrated biomarker panels in AECOPD prognosis [15].

This study presents a clinically focused innovation by validating a predictive model for rehospitalization within 3 months post-discharge —a key patient-centered outcome—using exclusively complete blood count (CBC)-derived parameters (RDW, NLR, PLR, MPV). This approach prioritizes practical utility, offering superior convenience, cost-effectiveness, and accessibility for primary care settings compared to models reliant on specialized tests. While the model’s AUC (0.950) is inherently different from those predicting in-hospital mortality due to the more complex nature of readmission [15], it demonstrates excellent discriminative power and clear potential for translation into routine risk stratification.

Research limitations

This study has several limitations. Its single-center retrospective design may introduce selection bias and precludes causal inference. Key clinical data were unavailable, including pulmonary function (e.g. FEV1), prior exacerbation history, and detailed systemic corticosteroid use, which may confound the results. Furthermore, excluding patients who died during follow-up without applying a competing risk model could bias rehospitalization risk estimates. Finally, the predictive model requires external validation in prospective, multi-center cohorts to confirm its generalizability.

Conclusion

The combined detection of peripheral blood RDW, NLR, PLR, and MPV provides a more comprehensive reflection of systemic inflammation, immune status, and erythrocyte metabolism in individuals with AECOPD. Integrating these indicators enhances the precision of short-term prognostic assessments and compensates for the limitations of single biomarkers. improving predictive accuracy. It is recommended that these parameters be incorporated into routine clinical monitoring protocols for individuals hospitalized with AECOPD to guide treatment strategies and optimize resource allocation.

Comment on “The development and validation of a risk stratification system for assessing axillary status after neoadjuvant therapy in node-positive breast cancer: a multicenter, prospective, observational study”
Authors: Yiheng Zhang, Ye Liu, Haiyuan Yang, Yating Zhao
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the article by Huang JX, et al titled “The development and validation of a risk stratification system for assessing axillary status after neoadjuvant therapy in node-positive breast cancer: a multicenter, prospective, observational study” published in the International Journal of Surgery (DOI:10.1097/JS9.0000000000002391)[1]. In line with the TITAN 2025 guideline on AI transparency[2], a generative AI–based language model was used solely for language polishing and had no bearing on the scientific content.

This multicenter, prospective study integrates breast shear wave elastography (SWE) with axillary ultrasound lymph node staging to develop a novel, quantifiable five-level risk scoring system. The system is designed to guide decisions on whether axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) can be safely avoided after neoadjuvant therapy in breast cancer patients with positive lymph nodes. By introducing SWE to traditional axillary ultrasound, the model calculates a predicted axillary status, significantly improving diagnostic accuracy for residual axillary lymph node metastasis post-neoadjuvant therapy. This improvement is particularly evident in patients at N0 and N1 stages, where the system reduces both false negatives and false positives. The model showed robust performance in an external cohort, achieving an AUC of 0.93, which notably outperforms single-imaging modalities. Patients with a risk score of 1 can safely avoid ALND, lowering unnecessary surgery rates from 52% to 17%-24%, with a false-negative rate under 2%. This offers a viable tool for “precision axillary surgery.” Additionally, the study revealed that the diagnostic benefit of SWE is primarily seen in US N0–N1 stages, with limited applicability in N2–N3 stages. Molecular subtype analysis further suggested that HR ± HER2 + patients are most likely to benefit from the exemption strategy. However, HR + HER2 − subtype patients, even with low risk scores, remain at a higher risk for residual metastasis, necessitating cautious clinical decision-making[3].

Despite the promising outcomes, there are notable limitations:Variations in imaging depth and differences in the ultrasound probes can reduce the detection rate of deep or high-level lymph nodes.The effectiveness of the technique is dependent on the experience and skill level of the operators[4].The study cohort was restricted to patients who underwent axillary lymph node dissection (ALND), excluding those who had received sentinel lymph node biopsy or targeted axillary dissection. This limitation may result in an overestimation of residual disease[5].Although this study employed a multicenter, prospective design, the sample size was relatively small, and the study was limited to a specific patient group. Therefore, the external applicability of the findings may be limited, particularly in terms of their relevance to different regions and types of medical institutions, which requires further validation. Additionally, the risk stratification model presented in the article did not sufficiently integrate other clinical factors that may affect axillary status, such as tumor molecular subtypes and genetic mutations. These factors could play a significant role in assessing axillary metastasis, thus limiting the statistical power of subtype analysis[6]. The lack of mid-treatment dynamic follow-up results in missed opportunities for early prediction[7]. Using pCR as a surrogate endpoint has not yet been correlated with long-term survival, and the long-term safety of exempting ALND still needs to be validated[8].

To overcome the limitations outlined above, future research should focus on the following areas:

① Technical improvements: Implement deep learning algorithms for three-dimensional reconstruction and automatic segmentation of elastography images, which will enhance the accuracy of detecting deep lesions.

② Population considerations: Prospective inclusion of patients undergoing sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB) or targeted axillary dissection (TAD), with an expanded sample size from triple-negative and HER2-low subtypes. Integrating imaging with biomarkers for multi-omics analysis will help identify the optimal exemption thresholds for different subtypes.

③ Broad validation: Increase the sample size and conduct multicenter, cross-regional studies to improve diversity and representativeness, ensuring the findings have broader applicability. In regions with significant resource disparities, portable elastography devices should be deployed to conduct real-world multicenter studies, recalibrating and sharing risk score thresholds to facilitate consistent implementation in primary healthcare settings.

④ Dynamic evaluation: SWE/US images should be collected at the second and fourth cycles of neoadjuvant therapy and pre-surgery, enabling the development of a longitudinal imaging-omics-pathology-survival model to predict early efficacy and guide adaptive treatment adjustments.

⑤ Endpoint enhancement: A follow-up cohort with ≥5 years of monitoring should be established, using axillary recurrence, disease-free survival, and overall survival as primary endpoints. These should be combined with quality-of-life assessments to evaluate the impact of ALND exemption on both oncological safety and patient-reported outcomes, ultimately providing evidence-based support for inclusion in clinical guidelines.

We appreciate the opportunity to provide feedback on the valuable study by Huang JX et al. We believe addressing the limitations discussed and pursuing the suggested research directions will enhance the model’s applicability and precision. We look forward to the continued progress in this field and hope our suggestions contribute to the further refinement of the approach.

NOTCH2NLA gene amplification is associated with poor prognosis in breast cancer
Authors: Yuchuan Wang, Ye Sang, Qianyi Dou, Mengke Chen, Qin Tang, Bo Lin, Guanghui Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRecently, a high-frequency gain of the human-specific NOTCH2NL gene was found in anaplastic thyroid cancer, yet its amplification status in other cancers is still unknown. In our study, we discovered that NOTCH2NLA, a paralog of the NOTCH2NL gene, exhibited a recurrent genetic alteration (101/1071) in breast cancer (BRCA), with amplification being the primary alteration type (100/1071). Furthermore, BRCA patients with NOTCH2NLA amplification had a significantly worse prognosis in terms of disease-specific survival (DSS), disease-free interval (DFI), and progression-free interval (PFI). Weight correlation network analysis (WGCNA) showed that PRCC and VPS72 genes were the hub genes in co-expression network of NOTCH2NLA amplification in the BRCA cohort. Moreover, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) indicated that NOTCH2NLA amplification was involved in the regulation of multiple signaling pathways such as cell cycle, ribosome biogenesis, and the Notch signaling pathway. Finally, we found that cell cycle inhibitors such as Flavopirdol, AT-7519, and PHA-793,887 were more sensitive for cells with NOTCH2NLA amplification, whereas MEK inhibitors were just on the contrary. In vitro experiments demonstrated that overexpression of NOTCH2NLA in breast cancer cells promoted cell growth and enhanced sensitivity to AT-7519 treatment. Collectively, our findings suggest that NOTCH2NLA amplification may be a new prognostic marker of BRCA and cell cycle inhibitors were more suitable for therapy on BRCA patients with NOTCH2NLA amplification.Keywords: NOTCH2NL, Copy number, Breast cancer, Survival, Analysis

Introduction

Cancer initiation and developments are driven by genetic alterations. Recently, through the whole-genome scale sequencing, driver genetic alterations, including somatic point mutation, copy-number variations and structural variants (SV), have been nearly comprehensively identified in cancers [1, 2]. However, genetic biomarkers that related to diagnosis and prognosis urgently needs to be discovered. Recently, a high-frequency gain of NOTCH2NL (41%) was reported in anaplastic thyroid cancer via WES-based copy number alteration analysis [3]. The variation in the copy number of NOTCH2NL has the potential to serve as a molecular marker for tumors. But the genetic alteration of NOTCH2NL in other cancers is still lacking.

The human-specific NOTCH2NL gene has been implicated in the increase of human brain size, as revealed by evolutionary analyses [4, 5]. As a secreted peptide, NOTCH2NL was initially identified through a yeast two-hybrid system screening for proteins that interact with neutrophil elastase and named by the similarity to the N-terminus of NOTCH2 extracellular domain [6]. Nowadays, four paralogous NOTCH2NL-like genes, including NOTCH2NLA, NOTCH2NLB, NOTCH2NLC and NOTCH2NLR, were depicted in human genome assembly GRCh38. NOTCH2NLA/B/C genes locate 1q21.1 locus and the pseudogene NOTCH2NLR locates near NOTCH2 on the 1p12 locus [4, 7]. NOTCH2NL plays an important role in human brain development, especially in cortical neurogenesis [5–9]. NOTCH2NL promotes the clonal expansion and maintenance of cortical progenitors through increasing self-renewal and/or symmetric proliferative divisions, with its mainly molecular mechanism being that it promotes Notch pathway activity through inhibition of Delta/Notch interaction [7].

At present, a relatively small number of studies have been reported regarding the role of the NOTCH2NL gene in tumorigenesis. NOTCH2NL gene mutation was detected in anaplastic astrocytoma (1/16 cases) and exhibited high-frequency occurrence in brain metastases from non-small cell lung cancer [10, 11]. NOTCH2NLA has been identified as a significant prognostic factor for overall survival (OS) in ovarian carcinoma and NOTCH2NLA silencing reduces cell migration, invasion, and proliferation [12]. The increased amplifications of the NOTCH2NL locus in 44% of H3.3G34R-mutant gliomas were confirmed and enhanced NOTCH2NL expression resulting in drive tumor growth [13]. Somatic copy number gain of NOTCH2NL was observed in 12 patients through evaluation of the whole-exome sequencing data from 29 anaplastic thyroid cancer samples [3]. This somatic copy number gain of NOTCH2NL was considered as a potential oncogenic event in the tumorigenesis of anaplastic thyroid cancer. These findings suggest the significance of NOTCH2NL in tumors.

In this study, we analyzed NOTCH2NLA genetic alterations across pan-cancer. Our findings revealed that NOTCH2NLA amplification is associated with poor prognosis of breast cancer and can serve as an independent risk factor for disease-specific survival (DSS), disease-free interval (DFI), and progression-free interval (PFI), with the exception of OS. Then, Weighted correlation network analysis (WGCNA) was performed to investigate the hub gene of BRCA with NOTCH2NLA amplification. In addition, differential genes expression analysis was performed to compare the difference between NOTCH2NLA amplification and non-amplification groups in BRCA. Finally, we conducted drug sensitivity analysis to screen for drugs suitable for cancers with NOTCH2NLA amplification.

Methods

Data acquisition and processing

The transcriptome data and clinical data of 1084 BRCA cases were downloaded from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. NOTCH2NLA copy number data in BRCA were downloaded from cBioPortal database(https://www.cbioportal.org/). The clinical data were sorted according to NOTCH2NLA copy number. RSEM format data of NOTCH2NLA expression in pan-cancer was obtained from firebrowse database using Firebrowse R package.

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) of NOTCH2NLA amplification and non-amplification in BRCA tumor samples (n = 1066) were analyzed by the “DESeq2” package. |log2Foldchange|≥ 1 and FDR < 0.05 were set as the criterion for screening DEGs.

Enrichment analysis

The Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis and Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) were performed through the clusterProfiler package of R for NOTCH2NLA amplification-related differentially expressed genes, and the screening criterion was p.adjust < 0.05.

Weighted correlation network analysis (WGCNA)

The WGCNA R package (version 1.72) was used to build co-expression networks and identify hub genes associated with NOTCH2NLAamplification in BRCA [14]. The top 10,000 coding genes for network construction were selected basis on the median absolute deviation (MAD). The scale-free topology model fit curve was calculated by various threshold values (from 0 to 30) for choosing the optimal soft threshold(value = 5). The adjacency matrix was clustered using the topological overlap measure (TOM) and dissimilarity (1-TOM) between genes, and a dynamic tree cut algorithm was applied to the dendrogram to identify gene modules with a minimum of 50 genes in each module. Then, dynamic tree cut modules were merged when the correlation between modules was more than 0.7. Pearson’s correlations between modules and NOTCH2NLA amplification status were evaluated to identify the most correlated module for further analysis. Hub genes were then selected by the correlation with the module and the degree with other genes.

Drug sensitivity analysis

The drug sensitivity (IC50) in cancer cell lines, including GDSC1 drug list and GDSC2 drug list, were downloaded from the Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) database (https://www.cancerrxgene.org/) [15, 16]. All cell lines were divided into amplification group and non-amplification group according to the NOTCH2NLA amplification status. Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used to analyze the drug sensitivity (IC50), and p < 0.05 represented statistically significance. The data of gene expression level and NOTCH2NLA copy number in pan-cancer cell lines were downloaded from the Cell Model Passports database. Then, spearman’s-correlation analysis was performed to explore the correlation between drug sensitivity and NOTCH2NLA copy number variation.

Survival analysis

Survival curves for ten years were calculated by Kaplan–Meier. The univariate Cox analyses was used to evaluate the relationship between survival and patient covariates. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models were constructed to evaluate the independent prognostic impact of NOTCH2NLA amplification (amplified vs. non-amplified) on OS, DSS, DFI, and PFI in BRCA patients.

Cell culture

MCF-7, MDA-MB-231, T47D, and HEK293T cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). All cell lines were cultured under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂) using Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM, Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 0.29 mg/mL glutamine, 100 units/mL penicillin, and 0.1 mg/mL streptomycin (Biological Industries). Cells were regularly screened for mycoplasma contamination.

PCR assays for determination of the NOTCH2NLA DNA copy number

Genomic DNA was extracted from breast cancer cells using Omega Bio-tek Tissue DNA Kit (D3396) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. DNA concentration was determined by a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer and adjusted to a concentration of 2.0 ng/mL. Quantitative real time PCR was performed on ABI Quant Studio detector with a SYBR Green kit. NOTCH2NLA primers used for amplification were: 5’-GTTTCTGTTCCAGAAATGCCTC-3’ and 5’-AGGCACAGAGTACTGACAAATGC-3’. Total DNA content was estimated by assaying Line-1 elements using the primers, 5’-AAAGCCGCTCAACTACATGG-3’ and 5’- TGCTTTG AATGCGTCCCAGAG-3’. The relative target copy number level was standardized using normal human genomic DNA as the calibrator [17].

Plasmid cloning and lentiviral production

Human NOTCH2NLA was cloned into the pCDH-CMV-MCS-EF1-coGFP-puro plasmid. HEK 293T cells were seeded at 80% confluence in 10-cm dish and transfected 16 h later with 10 µg of lentiviral vectors expressing NOTCH2NL, 7.5 µg psPAX2, and 2.5 µg pMD2.G using Lipofectamine 2000. The viral supernatant was collected 72 h post-transfection, filtered with 0.45-µm cellulose acetate and used to infect MDA-MB-231 cells.

Wounding heal assay

Cells were seeded into 6-well plates in complete growth medium. A sterile 200-µL pipette tip was used to create a straight, uniform scratch across the cell monolayer by gently scraping in a single motion. The well was subsequently washed twice using PBS and then replaced with DMEM medium containing 0.5% FBS. Photographs were captured at the 0-hour and 24-hour intervals following the creation of the scratch.

Cell proliferation assays

Overexpressed NOTCH2NLA cells and control cells were seeded into 96-well plates with five replicates per group. Subsequently, the plates were incubated with a 10% Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) solution (APExBIO, #K1018) at 37 °C for 2 h. Following incubation, the absorbance of each well was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader. For drug IC50 testing, Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at 1–2 × 10³ cells/well. Drug treatment started 24 h later at 10 µM with two-fold serial dilutions. After 72 h of treatment, cellular proliferation was assessed by CCK8, and IC50s were determined using GraphPad Prism (version 10.5.0).

Cell cycle analysis

For cell cycle analysis, a total of 2 × 105 cells per sample were harvested by trypsinization, washed twice with ice-cold PBS, and fixed in precooled 70% ethanol overnight. Fixed cells were then pelleted by centrifugation (1,500× g, 5 min), washed twice with PBS to remove ethanol, and resuspended in 500 µL staining solution containing propidium iodide (PI, 50 µg/mL) and RNase A (100 µg/mL) from the Cell Cycle and Apoptosis Analysis Kit (Yeasen, #40301ES50). The flow cytometry data were analyzed with FlowJo v10 software to determine the distribution of cells in G1, S, and G2/M phases based on DNA content.

Statistical analysis

Differences in patients carrying NOTCH2NLA amplification status were assessed using χ2 tests for categorical variables. Survival analysis in each group was tested using the Log-Rank test. As to normal distribution data, the Pearson test was used for the correlation analysis. As to unnormal distribution data, spearman test was used for the correlation analysis. Student t-test was used for normal distribution comparing two group data. Nonparametric Mann–Whitney and [3]Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were used for two group comparisons of unnormal distribution data. All P values presented are two sided. P < 0.05 indicated statistical significance. R (4.2.2) software, GraphPad Prism (version 10.5.0) and IBM SPSS (version 26) were used for statistical analysis.

Results

Copy number amplification of NOTCH2NLA may be a prognostic marker of BRCA

Recent research has unveiled a notable high-frequency gain (41%) of the NOTCH2NL gene in anaplastic thyroid cancer [3]. However, it remained unclear whether this genetic alteration also occurred in other types of cancers. To address this question, we conducted an investigation into NOTCH2NL mutations and copy number alterations within the TCGA pan-cancer cohort using the cBioPortal platform. The search results from cBioPortal clearly demonstrated that, among the members of the NOTCH2NL gene family, only NOTCH2NLA exhibited genetic alterations in the TCGA pan-cancer dataset. In contrast, its three paralogs, NOTCH2NLB, NOTCH2NLC, and NOTCH2NLR, showed no detectable genetic changes. Moreover, the genetically altered frequencies of NOTCH2NLA in pan–cancer were shown in Fig. 1A, and copy number variation, which consists of amplification and deletion, is the main variant type, especially in LUAD (53/511), BLCA (37/408), BRCA (101/1071), and LIHC (36/367). Next, we investigated the relationship between the transcriptional level of NOTCH2NLA and its copy number variation. The result showed that NOTCH2NLA copy number variation was significantly positively correlated with its mRNA level in pan-cancer (Fig. 1B). Finally, we evaluated NOTCH2NLA mRNA expression in tumor tissues versus adjacent non-malignant tissues across cancers harboring recurrent NOTCH2NLA alterations. The data revealed a significantly higher mRNA level of NOTCH2NLA in BRCA tumors compared to their corresponding adjacent non-malignant tissues (Fig. 1C). And the above result (Fig. 1A) also showed that NOTCH2NLA was amplified in BRCA, implying that copy number amplification of NOTCH2NLA may be a prognostic marker of BRCA.

NOTCH2NLA amplification significantly shortens DSS, DFI, and PFI of BRCA

Thus, we investigated the relationship between NOTCH2NLA amplification and the prognosis of BRCA. We found that NOTCH2NLA amplification significantly shortened DSS (p = 0.0168, HR = 2.03, 95%CI = 1.12–3.69), DFI (p = 0.0172, HR = 2.07, 95%CI = 1.12–3.82), and PFI (p = 0.0202, HR = 1.74, 95%CI = 1.08–2.80) of BRCA, although it affected little OS (p = 0.0770, HR = 1.54, 95%CI = 0.95–2.50) of BRCA (Fig. 2). These results suggested that NOTCH2NLA amplification may play an important role in BRCA progression. Next, we explored the correlation of NOTCH2NLA amplification with age, gender, distant metastases, tumor size, lymph node and molecular characterization of BRCA. The result showed that NOTCH2NLA amplification was not significantly associated with age, gender, distant metastases, tumor size, lymph node, and HER2 status of BRCA (Table 1). However, NOTCH2NLA amplification was significantly correlated with ER, PR, and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) status of BRCA (Table 1). Compared with non-amplification patients, BRCA patients with NOTCH2NLA amplification showed a significantly higher prevalence of ER receptor-negative, progesterone receptor- negative, or TNBC subtypes (Table 1).

PRCC and VPS72 genes may be hub genes in the co-expression network of NOTCH2NLA amplification in BRCA cohort

To construct a co-expression network of NOTCH2NLA amplification in BRCA, WGCNA analysis was performed by TCGA-BRCA(n = 1066) FPKM datasets. To build a scale-free topology network, we chose the soft threshold β = 5 to construct the co-expression network (scale-free R2 = 0.86) (Fig. 3A). In TCGA-BRCA, the hierarchical clustering tree identified 19 co-expression models (Fig. 3B-D), and the salmon module exhibited the strongest positive correlation with the NOTCH2NLA amplification (Cor = 0.49, P = 4e-66) (Fig. 3D). Therefore, we focused on salmon modules for further investigations. A total of 101 genes were included in the salmon. In the salmon module, scatter plots indicated strong correlations between module membership (MM) and gene significance (GS) for NOTCH2NLA amplification (Cor = 0.82, p = 6.6e-69) (Fig. 3E). We selected genes from the TCGA-BRCA salmon module as highly associated with NOTCH2NLA amplification. Furthermore, PRCC and VPS72 would be the hub gene in the salmon module and had more than 82 degrees interacting with other nodes (Fig. 3F).

NOTCH2NLA amplification plays an important role in cell cycle regulation, ribosome biogenesis, and the Notch signaling pathway of BRCA

Next, we studied the roles of NOTCH2NLA amplification in the development of BRCA. Firstly, we observed that the mRNA expression level of NOTCH2NLA in BRCA tissues with NOTCH2NLA amplification was significantly elevated compared to tissues with other NOTCH2NLA copy number variations, including gain, normal diploid, and shallow deletion. Amplification of NOTCH2NLA obviously increased its mRNA expression in BRCA (Fig. 4A). Then, differentially expressed genes between the NOTCH2NLA amplification group and the non-amplification group in BRCA were analyzed. The result showed that there were 657 differentially expressed genes (only protein coding gene), including 141 upregulated genes and 516 downregulated genes (Fig. 4B). The result of KEGG analysis indicated that neuroactive ligand−receptor interaction ranked the first, implying that NOTCH2NLA amplification play an important role in neuroactive ligand−receptor interaction of BRCA (Fig. 4C). Moreover, the GSEA analysis showed that NOTCH2NLA amplification also significantly affected the cell cycle (NES = 2.11), neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction (NES=-1.69), ribosome biogenesis in eukaryotes (NES = 1.93), and the Notch signaling pathway (NES = 1.72) of BRCA (Fig. 4D-F). Those results suggested that NOTCH2NLA may be involved in BRCA development through cell cycle regulation, ribosome biogenesis, and the Notch signaling pathway.

Tumor cells with NOTCH2NLA amplification are more sensitive to cell cycle inhibitors

To identify drug vulnerabilities associated with NOTCH2NLA amplification, we first analyzed the relationship between NOTCH2NLA copy number variation and mRNA expression across breast cancer cell lines using the Cell Model Passport database. The increased copy number of NOTCH2NLA significantly promoted its expression (Fig. 5A). Cell lines were divided into NOTCH2NLA amplification group and non-amplification group to analyze drug sensitivity from the GDSC database. Then, we found that 7 drugs were resistant in cells with NOTCH2NLA amplification, while 50 drugs were more sensitive in cells with NOTCH2NLA amplification (Fig. 5B). Most drugs resistant in cells containing NOTCH2NLA amplification were MEK inhibitors, such as Refametinib, PD0325901, and Trametinib. Additionally, our findings revealed that cells with NOTCH2NLA amplification exhibited heightened sensitivity primarily to cell cycle inhibitors, such as Flavopirdol, AT-7519, and PHA-793,887 (Fig. 5C-D). Consequently, these cell cycle inhibitors hold promise as effective therapeutic agents for treating breast cancer patients with NOTCH2NLA amplification.

Overexpression of NOTCH2NLA in breast cancer cells promotes cell growth and is more sensitive to AT-7519 treatment

To further confirm NOTCH2NLA function in BRCA, we tested NOTCH2NLA copy number in three breast cancer cell lines. We found that T47D and MCF7 cells exhibited NOTCH2NLA amplification, whereas MDA-MB-231 cells did not (Fig. 6A). NOTCH2NLA overexpression promoted cell proliferation and increased the cell number in S and G2 phases of the cell cycle, as determined by cell cycle analysis, without affecting cell migration (Fig. 6B–F). Besides, we demonstrated that overexpression of NOTCH2NLA in MDA-MB-231 was more sensitive to AT-7519 treatment (Fig. 6G).

Discussion

Copy-number amplification of oncogene is a common activation pattern in cancer [18]. Recently, focal and broad gain of the human-specific NOTCH2NL gene was found in anaplastic thyroid cancer. Whether this copy number variation of NOTCH2NL occurs in other cancers is still unknown. In our study, we identified NOTCH2NLA (a paralog of the NOTCH2NL gene) as a frequently altered gene in BRCA (101/1,071), with gene amplification being the predominant type of alteration (100/1,071). (Fig. 1A). Furthermore, BRCA patients with amplification had a poor prognosis in DSS, DFI and PFI (Fig. 2). Those results showed that NOTCH2NLA may be a prognostic marker of BRCA.

WGCNA analysis results showed that NOTCH2NLA amplification status is correlated with the salmon module gene. In the salmon module gene co-expression network, VPS72 and PRCC were the hub genes with a high degree of interaction with other genes (Fig. 3F). A recent study showed that VPS72 had the highest proportion of amplification in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and overexpression of VPS72 enhanced the initiation and progression of HCC in vitro and in vivo [19]. VPS72 promotes the activity of the MYC transcription factor by interacting with ACTL6A in HCC [19]. ACTL6A activates the Notch signaling pathway through SOX2 to promote HCC epithelial-mesenchymal transition [20]. GSEA analysis revealed that the Notch signaling pathway was activated in the context of NOTCH2NLA amplification. (Fig. 4D). Therefore, VPS72 may be an important regulator in NOTCH2NLA amplification sample for positive feedback Notch signaling pathway. PRCC regulates cell cycle progression through interaction with MAD2L2, consistent with GESA analysis results that the cell cycle pathway was significantly enriched in NOTCH2NLA high amplification changes [21].

Finally, we also screened the drugs that are more sensitive in cells with NOTH2NLA amplification. The results showed that many cell cycle inhibitors were more sensitive in cells with NOTCH2NLA amplification (Fig. 5B), which was consistent with the GSEA result that NOTCH2NLA amplification contributed to the activation of the cell cycle pathway (Fig. 4E). Thus, NOTCH2NLA amplification may be involved in BRCA development through regulating the cell cycle. Otherwise, cells with NOTCH2NLA amplification presented reduced sensitivity to MEK inhibitors, suggesting a potential interaction between NOTCH2NLA amplification and the MEK signaling pathway (Fig. 5B). Consistent with this, the oncogenic role of Notch signaling in breast cancer has been proven through cross-talk with the MEK signaling pathway. This observation aligns with prior studies demonstrating that Notch signaling can modulate MEK/ERK activity in breast cancer. For instance, active Notch1 has been shown to cooperate with MEK signaling to drive transformation of immortalized breast epithelial cells [22]. In trastuzumab-resistant breast cancer cells, Notch-1 correlates with ERK1/2 phosphorylation, promoting proliferation and maintaining the survival of cancer stem-like cells [23]. Combinatorial inhibition of Notch1 and MEK signaling pathways significantly reduces proliferation and survival of breast cancer cells compared to individual inhibition [24].

In GSEA results, we observed significant dysregulation of cell cycle regulators including CCNA and CCNB1. These findings suggest that NOTCH2NLA may promote breast cancer initiation and progression by enhancing cancer stem cell (CSC)-like traits [25]. Studies indicate that NOTCH2NLA primarily interacts with the NOTCH2 receptor to facilitate the activation of the Notch signaling pathway [5]. Protein palmitoylation modulates its subcellular localization, particularly altering its distribution on the cell membrane [26]. Using the CSS Palm software for prediction, we identified a potential palmitoylation motif within the NOTCH2NLA sequence. This modification is hypothesized to increase the membrane localization of NOTCH2NLA, which in turn may activate membrane-associated receptors—particularly those in the canonical NOTCH signaling pathway. Additionally, ZDHHC9, a palmitoyltransferase is significantly upregulated in samples with NOTCH2NLA amplification, and the two exhibit a positive correlation. The regulatory crosstalk between palmitoyltransferases and NOTCH2NLA signaling represents a promising direction for further mechanistic investigation.

In summary, NOTCH2NLA amplification is a recurrent genetic alteration in BRCA and is negatively correlated with DSS, DFI, and PFI of BRCA patients, implying that NOTCH2NLA amplification may be a prognostic marker of BRCA. NOTCH2NLA amplification contributed to cell cycle regulation, and the Notch signaling pathway activation. Based on these observations, we propose that cell cycle inhibitors—including Flavopirdol, AT-7519, and PHA-793,887—may represent targeted therapeutic options for BRCA cases harboring NOTCH2NLA amplification.

Spatiotemporal evolution of online interest in assisted reproductive technology: a two-decade global analysis through google trends
Authors: Ning Zhang, Xingyu Wan, Yuzhou Gao, Yang Yang, Haoyu He, Dongmei Ji
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOnline interest could serve as critical sentinel indicators for monitoring assisted reproductive technology (ART) demands, detecting regions with access inequities, and identifying shortcomings in policy frameworks; however, global online interest of ART has not been assessed. The study aims to investigate the spatiotemporal evolution of online interest in ART globally.MethodsThe study follows retrospective observational design. Data were derived from Google Trends and Relative Search Volume (RSV) data across 230 countries and regions were collected. After quality control, data from 59, 76, 69, and 61 countries and regions were included for different search terms (“Assisted reproductive technology,” “In vitro fertilization,” “Intracytoplasmic sperm injection,” and “Preimplantation genetic diagnosis,” respectively). Weekly percentage changes (WPCs) and annual percentage changes (APCs) were used to quantify the temporal variations in online interest.ResultsGlobally, public online interest in ART demonstrated a distinct seasonal fluctuation, peaking during months 3–5 and 9–11, while declining in months 6–8 and 12 − 2. The global online interest showed a significant downward trend (APC = -11.22%, 95% Confidence Interval [CI]: -18.44 to -8.61) from 2004 to 2011, followed by a gradual increase from 2011 to 2016 (APC = 1.76%, 95% CI: -7.64 to 11.99). Interest then rapidly increased from 2016 to 2019 (APC = 27.24%, 95% CI: 12.48 to 35.20), before continuing to decline after 2020 (APC = -6.54%, 95% CI: -12.52 to -3.05) with predictions indicating further decreases by 2030. Overall, Europe exhibited relatively higher online interest, while Africa and Oceania showed lower levels. In Europe, France (RSV = 76), Italy (RSV = 66), and Spain (RSV = 32) had notable interest in ART. Whereas, in Oceania and Africa, Australia (RSV = 8), New Zealand (RSV = 6), Tunisia (RSV = 16), Algeria (RSV = 16), and Nigeria (RSV = 10) showed lower levels of interest. Israel, Réunion (France), and France ranked as the top three countries or regions with the highest online interest in ART worldwide.ConclusionsGlobal online interest in ART shows significant variation across countries and regions, with a decline after 2020. To address these trends, ART resources and services should be allocated effectively based on seasonal and regional demand. Government action is needed to raise social awareness and improve the accessibility and utilization of ART.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-23990-9.Keywords: Google trends, Assisted reproductive technology, Spatiotemporal analysis, Infodemiology, RSV

Introduction

Infertility now affects an estimated 120 million couples worldwide, establishing assisted reproductive technology (ART) as a critical component of modern reproductive medicine [1]. Recent research has indicated an increase in infertility treatment [2, 3]. For example, French women aged 34 and older experienced a 23.9% increase in the use of infertility treatments [4]. Concurrent demographic shifts, such as delayed childbearing, have further amplified reliance on ART services [5]. As science and technology continue to advance, ART has evolved through successive developmental stages—from first- to fourth-generation techniques—broadening therapeutic options for infertility patients [6]. Despite these technological advances, global access to ART remains unevenly substantiated. A cross-sectional study found that socioeconomically disadvantaged populations are less likely to receive infertility treatment [7], and financial constraints have been responsible for 34.7% of ART treatment discontinuations among respondents from eight nations [8]. In addition, even with free treatment, race/ethnicity and socioeconomic status are still barriers to accessing ART [9, 10]. In fact, strategies to reduce the economic burden of infertility and improve access have emerged as one of the top ten research priorities in the field [11]. Multifaceted barriers, including the uneven distribution of medical resources, educational disparities, social stigma, cost-related misconceptions, and policy restrictions, have driven many individuals to rely on the Internet for ART-related information. Recent study on ART patients’ information-seeking behavior reveals a strong preference for obtaining knowledge through internet searches, social media platforms, and research reports [12]. Consequently, trends in online interest may serve as critical sentinel indicators for monitoring ART demand, detecting regions with access inequities, and identifying deficiencies in policy frameworks.

Digital epidemiology, which leverages online data streams for public health surveillance, offers transformative potential to address these challenges. Online search trend was highly influenced by media [13, 14]. For example, an infodemiology study indicated that online searches related to chilblain-like lesions prevalent in France, Spain, Italy, and the United States, seemingly influenced by press releases regarding outbreaks of acral lesions [14]. Recent years, Google Trends analytics have proven effective in tracking disease outbreaks and research trends [15], as evidenced by applications during the 2014 Ebola crisis [16], in monitoring global COVID-19 research [17], and in assessing post-pandemic public interest in infectious diseases [18]. Moreover, the methodological validity of Google Trends could extend to seasonal pattern analysis. As demonstrated by Deiner et al., search trends exhibited significant seasonal concordance with clinical diagnoses [19]. Despite these advancements, digital epidemiology studies in reproductive health remain fragmented. Although prior research has partially characterized patterns of ART-related information acquisition, a critical knowledge gap persists regarding how online interest in ART evolves across temporal, cyclical, and geographic dimensions globally.

In this study, we analyze global Spatiotemporal trends of online interest in ART, including in vitro fertilization (IVF), intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), and preimplantation genetic diagnosis (PGD), from 2004 to 2024. Our findings aim to enhance understanding of ART accessibility worldwide and to guide governments in formulating policies that facilitate the development and equitable distribution of ART services.

Materials and methods

Data source

Google Trends is a widely used, publicly accessible tool that normalizes and visualizes search query data from Google’s search engine. The data is presented on a scale of 1 to 100, reflecting relative search interest for a term in comparison to its peak popularity [20]. It offers historical search volume data dating back to 2004, enabling researchers to analyze the popularity of specific terms or topics across different time periods and regions [21].

The study follows retrospective observational design. Relative Search Volume (RSV) is a key metric in Google Trends that reflects the relative search interest during a specific time range and region. We retrieved RSV data for 230 countries or regions from January 2004 to December 2024. Searches made by very few people, duplicate searches, and searches with special characters were excluded in the analysis. Countries or regions with low search volume were also excluded (When countries with extreme small values are included, due to the extremely small search base in these countries, the RSV of other countries will be excessively elevated. This distribution feature will mask the real differences in search popularity among different countries, making the data of various countries lose comparability). As a result, the final study included 59, 76, 69, and 61 countries and regions for the search terms “Assisted reproductive technology,” “In vitro fertilization,” “Intracytoplasmic sperm injection,” and “Preimplantation genetic diagnosis,” respectively.

Search input

The search terms chosen for the study were “Assisted reproductive technology”, “In vitro fertilization”, “Intracytoplasmic sperm injection”, and “Preimplantation genetic diagnosis.” These terms are commonly recognized within the field of reproductive medicine. The time range for the study spanned from January 1, 2004, to December 1, 2024.

Search variables

The analysis was performed using the “Topic” filter within the “Search Terms” module of Google Trends. The “Topic” includes terms with the same concept in different languages, which allows us to obtain online search data on ART in any language. The dataset covered January 2004 to September 2024, with weekly RSV values (weeks 1–52) retrieved for each year. This approach allowed for detailed visualization of both weekly and annual RSV trends. To discern overarching trends, annual aggregated RSV data were evaluated over the 21-year period. Additionally, weekly RSV data were gathered for a year-by-year assessment, accounting for all 52 or 53 weeks per year.

Construction of time series models

TimeGPT is the first large-scale pre-trained model designed specifically for time series forecasting [22]. The TimeGPT model was used to analyze Google Trends data spanning from January 2004 to January 2025 and to forecast the trends by 2030. The model was fine-tuned on the training set using its API and subsequently employed to generate predictions on the test set.

Statistical analysis

The joinpoint regression model was employed to examine temporal trend data and to identify statistically significant turning points, known as “joinpoints”, via the Monte Carlo permutation test. Weekly percentage changes (WPCs) and annual percentage changes (APCs) were calculated between the identified joinpoints to quantify the magnitude and direction of these shifts. Specifically, WPCs capture the relative weekly changes in the trend, offering insights into short-term fluctuations, and APCs provide an estimate of the long-term average annual trend changes. Both WPCs and APCs are accompanied by 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs). Because the RSV, a normalized index, represents the search interest in a specific topic within a given region, there is no country-population size bias. P-values below 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Global overall online interest trends in ART from 2004 to 2024

Over the past 21 years, the global overall online interest in ART has exhibited a distinct seasonal trend in most years except for 2004, 2005, 2013, 2016, 2019, 2020, and 2021.

The RSV typically rises during months 1–4, drops to a trough between months 5–7, rises again during months 8–10, and declines after reaching a peak around week 45, presenting an approximate “M” shape characterized by higher values in months 3–5 and 9–11, and lower values in months 6–8 and 12–2 (Fig. 1; Table 1, Figure S1).

Overall WPCs for ART from 2004 to 2024 (%)

In 2004, RSV declined during weeks 1–34 (WPC = −1.28%, 95% CI: −2.32 to −0.69), rapidly increased to a peak between weeks 34 and 37 (WPC = 20.67%, 95% CI: 2.90 to 28.16), and subsequently dropped between weeks 37–52 (WPC = −4.48%, 95% CI: −7.47 to −2.86). In 2005 and 2013, RSV initially exhibited a slight increase (2005: WPC = 0.14%, 95% CI: −9.03 to 3.52) or decrease (2013: WPC = −0.72%, 95% CI: −2.14 to 0.26), then underwent a rapid ascent to the peak at week 22 or week 23 (2005: WPC = 10.47%, 95% CI: 6.80 to 24.99; 2013: WPC = 22.27%, 95% CI: 9.44 to 29.13), before falling during months 5–12 (2005: WPC = −30.17%, 95% CI: −40.73 to −17.49; 2013: WPC = −20.49%, 95% CI: −24.70 to −11.40). In 2016, RSV showed a slight increase during weeks 1–19 (WPC = 0.02%, 95% CI: −0.71 to 1.40) before descending to the nadir at week 20 (WPC = −3.76%, 95% CI: −11.74 to 2.14), and subsequently continued to rise during weeks 20–53 (WPC = 3.03%, 95% CI: 2.46 to 3.72). In 2019, RSV continued rising until it peaked at week 38 (WPC = 22.84%, 95% CI: −12.56 to 33.67) and then began to decline (WPC = −7.17%, 95% CI: −12.28 to 0.51). In 2020, RSV declined continuously (WPC = −4.58%, 95% CI: −7.88 to −2.70) until week 14, when it rose sharply (WPC = 10.82%, 95% CI: 0.35 to 20.00), before continuing to fall after week 18 (WPC = −0.57%, 95% CI: −1.78 to −0.03). In 2021, RSV exhibited a downward trend throughout the entire year (WPC = −0.24%, 95% CI: −0.59 to 0.10) (Fig. 1; Table 1).

Online interest trends in different ART in US, UK, and the world

The global online interest in ART has shown a clear trend of decline, followed by a rise, and then another decline. Specifically, the interest rapidly decreased from 2004 to 2011 (APC = −11.22%, 95% CI: −18.44 to −8.61), increased slowly from 2011 to 2016 (APC = 1.76%, 95% CI: −7.64 to 11.99), grew rapidly from 2016 to 2019 (APC = 27.24%, 95% CI: 12.48 to 35.20), and then started to decline after 2020 (APC = −6.54%, 95% CI: −12.52 to −3.05) (Fig. 2; Table 2). IVF showed a slight decline from 2004 to 2014 (APC = −0.23%, 95% CI: −2.04 to 0.98), followed by a consistent and rapid rise (APC = 5.64%, 95% CI: 4.60 to 7.29). ICSI steadily rose from 2004 to 2018 (APC = 4.92%, 95% CI: 2.88 to 6.27; APC = 19.97%, 95% CI: 11.90 to 24.15) and then gradually decreased (APC = −2.31%, 95% CI: −4.81 to −0.44). PGD declined continuously from 2004 to 2014 (APC = −5.82%, 95% CI: −8.43 to −4.16), rose rapidly until 2017 (APC = 20.46%, 95% CI: 8.03 to 27.02), and then rapidly decreased from 2018 (APC = −7.60%, 95% CI: −11.58 to −5.24).

Both the US and the UK are English-speaking countries, with complete 20-year Google Trends data. Additionally, they boast higher internet penetration rates and greater accessibility to Google, along with more advanced and accessible ART. For these reasons, we selected the US and the UK to conduct a further analysis on the temporal trends of online interest in ART in these two countries. In the US, overall online interest in ART declined from 2004 to 2015 (APC = −6.41%, 95% CI: −11.29 to −3.90), then gradually increased (APC = 16.86%, 95% CI: −4.14 to 34.19) before declining again after reaching a peak in 2019 (APC = −11.54%, 95% CI: −26.61 to −5.43). IVF generally followed an upward trend. ICSI decreased steadily from 2004 to 2014 (APC = −3.87%, 95% CI: −8.29 to −2.44), then rose rapidly (APC = 12.51%, 95% CI: −8.51 to 18.94), fluctuated from 2017 to 2024 (APC = −0.28%, 95% CI: −9.39 to 3.45). PGD declined year after year from 2004 to 2014 (APC = −4.90%, 95% CI: −8.85 to −0.30), rose rapidly (APC = 22.29%, 95% CI: −11.38 to 34.51), and declined rapidly after reaching its peak in 2018 (APC = −19.04%, 95% CI: −24.70 to 25.61) (Fig. 2; Table 2).

In the UK, overall online interest in ART generally showed a downward trend (APC = −5.88%, 95% CI: −9.22 to −4.97) before rising after 2021 (APC = 9.96%, 95% CI: −3.97 to 34.40). IVF exhibited an upward trend after 2008 (APC = 5.00%, 95% CI: 4.08 to 9.73) but declined after reaching its peak around 2018 (APC = −5.91%, 95% CI: −9.30 to 2.90). ICSI generally showed a fluctuating upward trend (APC = 159.44%, 95% CI: 68.73 to 335.37; APC = 4.62%, 95% CI: 2.58 to 8.49) and declined after reaching its peak in 2018 (APC = −4.38%, 95% CI: −19.31 to −0.02). PGD increased rapidly from 2004 to 2006 (APC = 61.51%, 95% CI: 20.59 to 102.94), then gradually declined (APC = −7.96%, 95% CI: −15.69 to −5.49), rose again after hitting the trough in 2014 (APC = 26.88%, 95% CI: 9.19 to 37.92), and continued to decline after reaching its peak in 2017 (APC = −14.19%, 95% CI: −20.05 to −10.64), following a characteristic M-shaped pattern (Fig. 2; Table 2).

Global distribution of online interest of ART

Overall, Europe displayed relatively higher online interest in ART, while Africa and Oceania had lower levels of interest, with many African countries excluded from the analysis due to their low relative search volume (RSV) (Fig. 3; Table 3). Israel stood out as the Asian country with the highest online interest, achieving an RSV of 100, ranking first globally. In Europe, France (RSV = 76), Italy (RSV = 66), and Spain (RSV = 32) had notable interest in ART. In North America, The US had an RSV of 5, reflecting relatively low interest. In South America, Argentina was the only country with a higher interest level, achieving an RSV of 29. In Oceania, Australia (RSV = 8) and New Zealand (RSV = 6) showed lower levels of interest. In Africa, Tunisia (RSV = 16), Algeria (RSV = 16), and Nigeria (RSV = 10) were the most interested African countries (Fig. 3A; Table 3).

Prediction of global online interest trends in ART by 2030

Using monthly RSV data from 2004 to 2024, we applied timeGPT to forecast the global online interest in ART by 2030. This nearly three-decade dataset reflects the evolving public demand and changes in global accessibility to ART, as illustrated in Fig. 4. For Overall ART, the trend showed an initial decline in RSV from 2004 to 2016. After 2016, there was a gradual increase, followed by another decline in 2020. By 2030, the interest is predicted to continue a slow downward trajectory (Fig. 4A). For IVF, RSV experienced a generally fluctuating upward trend over the period. Although there was a decline at the end of 2020, the trend stabilized between 2021 and 2023. Following this period of stability, the interest ascended again, ultimately leveling off at a relatively high plateau by 2030 (Fig. 4B). For ICSI, the trend followed a similar pattern of fluctuation, with an overall upward trend. Notably, there was a sharp increase in interest around 2016, after which the RSV continued to fluctuate but remained generally stable by 2030 (Fig. 4C). For PGD, the trend closely mirrors that of ART. It exhibited a downward trend before 2016, experienced a rapid increase afterward, declined again in 2019, and is predicted to remain stable by 2030 (Fig. 4D).

Discussion

Main findings

The results indicated that the online interest in ART exhibited distinct seasonal patterns in most years, characterized by high levels in months 3–5 and 9–11, and low levels in months 6–8 and 12 − 2. The global online interest significant decreased from 2004 to 2011, followed by a gradual increase from 2011 to 2016, then rapidly increased from 2016 to 2019 prior to a decline after 2020. Our predictions indicated that the decrease would persist by 2030. The online interest significantly varied among different countries and regions. Europe showed a higher level of online interest, whereas Africa and Oceania had lower levels. Israel, Réunion(France), and France were the top three countries or regions with the highest online interest in ART globally.

Interpretation

Temporal trends of global online interest in ART

Global online interest in ART exhibited distinct temporal trends from 2004 to 2024, likely driven by technological advancements, evolving public perceptions, and socioeconomic factors. The sharp decline in public engagement between 2004 and 2011 appears linked to nascent technologies and delayed public awareness campaigns. During the early 2000 s, ART failed to gain widespread adoption due to suboptimal success rates, prohibitive costs, and waning public confidence [23, 24]. Persistent sociocultural stigma surrounding infertility significantly stifled demand for ART services, particularly in culturally conservative regions, which discouraged open discussion and limited online information-seeking [25]. Widespread gaps in internet accessibility and insufficient public education about ART’s clinical potential further curbed online interest [26].

The gradual resurgence in interest from 2011 to 2019 aligned with parallel advancements in technology and global economic recovery. Refinements in ART protocols and increased media coverage of successful outcomes heightened public attention [27]. Concurrently, many countries liberalized ART regulations, while evolving societal attitudes reduced stigma surrounding infertility, enabling families to publicly pursue treatment. Post-2008 financial crisis economic stabilization also bolstered household disposable incomes, broadening access to ART services [28]. The abrupt decline after 2020 correlates strongly with the COVID-19 pandemic and its disruption of healthcare systems and the society, which is further discussed in "The effect of COVID-19" section.

The trend and seasonal patterns of global online interest in ART

The seasonal pattern of online interest in ART reflect the variations in the public demand for and attention to ART, which may be associated with temperature, fertility, and personal choices. The regulation of reproductive function through photoperiod and melatonin corresponds with fertility peaks in the spring and autumn, while summer heatwaves hinder spermatogenesis [29, 30]. Similarly, extreme cold events have been shown to increase the risk of clinical pregnancy failure in women undergoing fresh embryo transfer [31]. In addition, ART procedures involve multiple hospital visits. Considering comfort, individuals often avoid undergoing ART during the summer and winter months. Meanwhile, due to the impact of extreme temperatures on adverse birth outcomes and pregnancy complications, individuals tend to avoid conceiving during the summer and winter months [32–35]. Additionally, the holiday season can affect the availability and accessibility of ART services in certain regions. In US, fertility laboratories are temporarily closed for cleaning and maintenance purposes, coinciding with the holiday-dense winter season [36].

The variations among different countries and regions

The results show significant regional variations in global online interest toward ART, IVF, ICSI, and PGD, which might reflect the complex interplay among ART demand, policies, and healthcare system capacities. The highest incidence of male infertility occurs in Central and Eastern Europe, suggesting that the higher online interest may be attributed to the high fundamental demand for ART in Europe [37]. Furthermore, the European Society of Human Reproduction and Embryology (ESHRE)’s focus on ART has significantly influenced the region’s interest [38]. In Asian country Israel, the highest interest in ART might be related to its policies. Israel has incorporated ART into its national health insurance system, demonstrating how policy frameworks directly amplify public engagement [39, 40]. The high online interest in Russia may be attributed to its aging society. Confronting dual challenges of population aging and declining fertility rates, IVF technology has become a crucial solution for addressing reproductive needs [41]. The geographical differences are also related to socio-economic and cultural factors. In Africa, more than 98% of the population cannot afford ART due to high financial burden. some viewpoints also hold that, against the backdrop of overpopulation in Africa, ART technology should not receive government support. Limited funds should be used to treat infectious diseases such as malaria and HIV, rather than providing ART for infertile couples [42]. The use of ART is also related to racial/ethnic groups. In the US, there are significant racial and socioeconomic differences among patients receiving ART. Black women are more likely to report that their race is an obstacle to accessing ART and non-Hispanic Native American/Alaska Native women have the lowest rates of ART use [10, 43]. The attention to ART is also related to religion. People with a higher level of religious participation and those living in countries with a stronger religious atmosphere are more likely to oppose the use of ART [44]. In Nigeria, 53% of people are more inclined to spiritual exercise (praying and fasting) as an alternative to ART and most people believe that spirituality played a vital role in resolving infertility [45].

ICSI garners significant search interest in Libya and Egypt, linked to the higher prevalence of infertility in North Africa, where 14.2% of women report fertility challenges [37]. Moreover, the high level of interest in PGD is primarily driven by the prevalence of genetic disorders. Nigeria, one of the worst differentials in health indices in the world, exhibits significant online interest of PGD [46]. Studies have reported a high incidence of genetic diseases such as sickle cell disease in Nigeria, and the demand for reducing the incidence has fueled the high need for PGD technology, thereby increasing public attention [47, 48]. The top countries for PGD are North African and Middle Eastern countries, which may result from marriages between relatives. In these countries, consanguineous marriage is a deeply-rooted tradition, leading to an increase in the prevalence of various autosomal recessive genetic diseases [49]. The incidence of inborn errors of metabolism in the Middle Eastern region was reported the highest in the world, which is also related to the higher parental consanguinity [50]. It is noteworthy that, apart from several countries, online interest in ART in most regions of Africa remains extremely low, with insufficient data for inclusion in the study. This is partly due to the low rate of network coverage in Africa, but it also largely reflects the limited availability of ART technology in these regions.

The effect of COVID-19

Our results indicate that after 2020, public online interest in ART shows a downward trend, which contrasts with the previous upward trend, and this change can largely be attributed to the impact of COVID-19 [51]. This shift likely reflects the broader disruptions caused by the pandemic, which not only affects the accessibility of ART services but also reshapes societal attitudes and priorities surrounding reproductive health. Factors such as the temporary closure of fertility clinics, resource diversion in healthcare, and the general health concerns related to the pandemic might have led to a decrease in public interest in ART [52–54]. As noted by Cutting et al., fertility clinics around the world were forced to delay or suspend treatments, with an average delay of 56 days for IVF/ICSI cycles and 57 days for frozen embryo transfers during the shutdown period [55]. During assisted reproduction lockdown for COVID-19 pandemic, infertile couples have tried to spontaneous pregnancy and some of them succeeded. The high pregnancy rate is attributed to the high frequency of sexual intercourse during the lockdown period [56]. The increased stress and economic insecurity due to the pandemic, along with the decreased fertility intention caused by the pandemic, have also reduced the online interest in ART technology [57, 58]. Due to the high price of ART technology, the persistent economic depression caused by the pandemic has also reduced the online interest [59]. Notably, the long-term effects of the pandemic on fertility intentions have further compounded this trend. For instance, a survey in China revealed that metropolitan women with higher education exhibited significantly reduced childbearing desires in the post-pandemic era [60]. Additionally, the overwhelming focus on pandemic-related information (e.g., vaccines, infection rates) may have “crowded out” public attention to non-urgent health topics such as ART, as evidenced by parallel declines in Google Trends in elective surgery [61]. These complex factors have led to a decline in the online interest of ART after the COVID-19 pandemic.

The decline of online interest in ART by 2030

Our prediction results suggest that the decline trend of global online interest in ART will persist by 2030. A survey shows that economic factors and age are important factors influencing the fertility intention [62]. Over the past few decades, with remarkable shifts in the socioeconomic landscape, an increasing number of women may opt to postpone marriage or remain unmarried, substantially influencing the online interest and demand for ART [63]. In addition, financial, demographic, normative cultural, and religious composition of a country also influence the demand of ART [64]. To stop the decline, the government should formulate policy-driven public education programs. It should also engage in more extensive public discourses, more explicitly acknowledge, and strive to understand the crucial role of cultural and normative values in shaping ART policies, accessibility, and usage. Besides, expanding the coverage of medical insurance and reducing the economic costs of ART will also increase the online interest in ART [65, 66].

Strengths and limitations

A strength of this study is that we assess the spatiotemporal trends of online interest in ART across 230 countries and regions globally in the past two decades. There are still several limitations. Firstly, RSV is a relative ratio rather than an absolute value. The reliance on RSV as a metric means that we cannot provide absolute measures of online search interest. For this reason, we can’t compare the trends of online interest among different “Topics”, nor can we comprehensively analyze the trends of all “Topics”. The exclusion of countries/regions with low search volume could also introduce bias. In addition, we are unable to uncover the specific factors leading to increases or decreases in the online interest. Moreover, there is a bias inherent in our study, as the RSV data from Google Trends only represent individuals who have internet access and use Google as their search engine. This does not encompass the entire global population, nor does it include the individuals who prefer other search engines. The study does not account for the digital divide, which may skew the results towards regions with higher internet penetration. Finally, Google Trends lacks full transparency and replicability due to its reliance on unpublished mathematical assumptions for the calculation of RSV, which may not accurately reflect actual trends [21]. The lack of transparency in Google Trends’ algorithms is also a notable limitation. Nevertheless, we believe that our study could satisfactorily clarify the general online interest and reflect public awareness and demand regarding ART.

Conclusions

The global online interest in ART exhibits variability over time and across regions, demonstrating a seasonal trend of fluctuation. The trend shows a decline after 2020 and is expected to persist until 2030. To address this, we recommend: (1) policymakers should launch targeted awareness campaigns during low-interest seasons and integrate digital literacy initiatives into national reproductive health strategies to bridge the information gap; (2) healthcare providers should implement clinic-based “ART information hubs” combining multilingual educational materials and real-time telehealth consultations to address identified knowledge barriers. These findings offer valuable insights for researchers, clinicians, and policymakers to strengthen social awareness and increase the accessibility and utilization of ART.

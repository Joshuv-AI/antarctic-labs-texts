Aquifer Leakage Recharge Controls on CBM Production: A Case Study in the Sanjiao Block, Eastern Ordos Basin, China
Authors: Xinlu Yan, Suoliang Chang, Shuheng Tang, Yanjun Meng, Taotao Yan, Sheng Zhang, Dekang Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Aquifer leakage recharge poses a prevalent challenge in coalbed methane (CBM) development, severely impeding its efficient production. This study focuses on the Sanjiao Block, located on the eastern margin of the Ordos Basin, and provides a comprehensive evaluation of the impact of aquifer leakage recharge on CBM well productivity, employing hydrochemical characteristics and numerical simulation. Fisher’s discriminant analysis reveals a close association between external water sources for CBM wells in the Taiyuan Formation and the hydrodynamic environment: in the western stagnant zone, hydrochemical characteristics resemble those from the Shanxi Formation; in the eastern strong runoff zone, water from the Taiyuan Formation directly contributes to CBM well development; in the central weak runoff zone, hydrochemical characteristics suggest mixed water sources from the Shanxi Formation and Taiyuan Formation. The numerical simulation employs orthogonal experiments to quantify the sensitivity of aquifer geological parameters to CBM production, ranked from strong to weak for aquifer types, leakage channel properties, and aquifer physical properties. The fundamental reason for disparities in the efficacy of leakage recharge lies in categorizing aquifers into finite and infinite recharges based on their water supply capacity. The properties of leakage channels, including the location and scale, manifest as effects on the magnitude and shape of gas production characteristics in infinite and finite recharge aquifers, respectively. Furthermore, a discriminant flowchart of CBM production is presented, delineating the production characteristics of CBM wells under the influence of aquifer leakage recharge into six patterns and illustrating their distribution in the study area. This discriminant process provides scientific guidance for analyzing CBM production characteristics and evaluating potential under the influence of aquifer leakage recharge.

Introduction

Mature commercial development of coalbed methane (CBM) has been established in the Ordos Basin and the Qinshui Basin in China, with CBM resources becoming increasingly essential in the energy sector.1,2 However, several issues, including low operating efficiency, low single-well production, and low recovery efficiency, impede the rapid development of the CBM industry.3 One significant reason is the influx of water from aquifers, which troubles many CBM fields. Its impact on the productivity of CBM wells can be summarized in two main aspects: first, the continuous influx of external water provides sufficient material and energy supplementation for coal reservoirs, making it difficult to depressurize the reservoirs and disrupting the normal production process of CBM.4−6 Second, due to factors such as the wetting properties of coal rock and capillary force, a competitive adsorption phenomenon occurs between water and CBM. The influx of external water not only causes CBM to dissipate but also produces reservoir damage effects such as “water block”, reducing the gas content of coal reservoirs and the permeability of various phase fluids.7−10 Therefore, clarifying the capability of aquifer leakage recharge and determining its impact on the productivity of CBM wells have become current challenging issues.

Previous studies have extensively investigated the hydrogeological conditions of gas-bearing systems and their control mechanisms on CBM development. It is widely recognized that the water content of the aquifer, its spatial relationship with the coal seam, and the coupling of dynamics and resistance are critical parameters for assessing the capacity of aquifer leakage recharge.11,12 Coal seam itself is a weakly water-bearing layer, with the abnormally high water production primarily attributed to recharge from adjacent aquifers. The existence and distribution of water in coal-bearing strata are influenced by the material composition of formation, burial history, hydrocarbon generation, CBM preservation conditions, regional structure, and hydrological conditions.13−16 The hydrochemical composition and hydrodynamic conditions serve as valuable indicators for identifying the characteristics of “recharge-runoff-discharge” processes in groundwater. Geological parameters obtained through drilling and logging are used to construct the spatial distribution framework of the strata. Integrating these factors can provide references for determining the water content of the aquifer and delineating the “sweet spot” for CBM.17−20 Furthermore, natural structures, such as faults and collapse columns, along with reservoir reconstruction effects, such as artificial fractures, provide open channels for material migration and energy transfer between aquifers and coal seams. The degree of channel openness and its location are also direct factors influencing CBM well production.21,22 Thus, aquifer recharge types can be classified according to various criteria. For instance, based on water sources, the recharge water of CBM wells can be divided into three types: surface water, surrounding rock water, and no recharge.23 Additionally, based on channel characteristics, the recharge water of CBM wells can be classified into types such as low-altitude water convergence, natural fracture (faults) connection, hydraulic fracture connection, karst collapse column connection, and formation contact penetration.24 Despite the fruitful achievements and understandings in previous studies, there are still certain limitations: (1) most studies focus on specific geological parameters, lacking comprehensive and holistic evaluation; (2) qualitative analysis prevails, while it is necessary to quantify the production characteristics and potential of CBM wells under the influence of aquifers; (3) unclear identification of the main geological factors affecting aquifer recharge hampers guidance for engineering construction design and development strategies in CBM fields. Therefore, comprehensively evaluating geological parameters related to aquifer recharge and quantifying the impact of aquifers on CBM well productivity may be key to achieving breakthroughs in productivity for CBM wells troubled by exogenous water.

Numerical simulation is favored by researchers for its intuitive geological model and quantitative evaluation results. The modeling process can generally be divided into two methods: the first method, represented by Eclipse and Simdwin numerical simulation software, directly establishes geological models of coal reservoirs, aquifers, and aquifuges. This method involves gridding the geological model and assigning the corresponding geological parameters based on actual geological conditions, approximating the division of lithology and characteristics of rock layers under in situ conditions.25,26 This method produces a clear and intuitive geological model, facilitating the reflection of the dynamic situation of each layer during CBM development. However, the extensive grid division in the geological model increases the memory and calculation time, significantly escalating the workload of numerical simulation and leading to potential issues such as software lagging, crashes, and others (Figure 1a). To address this issue, numerical simulation software, represented by Comet3.0, numerically models the aquifer by incorporating a professional aquifer function to depict the recharge of external water and further simulates the production characteristics of CBM wells under the influence of the aquifer (Figure 1b). This method effectively resolves lag and crashes during software runtime, saving model grids, reducing computation time, and simplifying complexity.

Numerical simulation modeling methods of aquifer leakage recharge.

This study focuses on the Sanjiao Block, situated on the eastern margin of the Ordos Basin, and provides a comprehensive evaluation of the impact of aquifer leakage recharge on the productivity of CBM wells. Initially, the hydrogeological conditions of the study area and the water sources from CBM wells are analyzed based on hydrochemical data. Subsequently, the sensitivity of geological parameters of aquifers to CBM production is evaluated using numerical simulation methods. Finally, the establishment of a discriminant process provides scientific guidance for analyzing the production characteristics and potential of CBM wells under the influence of aquifer recharge, aiming to promote CBM exploration and development in related areas.

Regional Geology

The Sanjiao Block is located on the eastern edge of the Ordos Basin, characterized by a monocline structure with a southeastern high slope and a northwestern low slope (Figure 2a–c). The coal-bearing formations mainly consist of the Carboniferous Taiyuan Formation and the Permian Shanxi Formation, containing a total of 16 coal seams. CBM extraction primarily targets seams 3 + 4 + 5# in the Shanxi Formation and seams 8 + 9# in the Taiyuan Formation, with coal seams exhibiting vitrinite reflectance (Ro) ranging from 0.9% to 1.44%. The coal seams are buried at depths ranging from 300 to 1200 m beneath the surface. Seams 3 + 4 + 5# have thicknesses ranging from 2.11 to 6 m, averaging approximately 4.05 m, with gas contents ranging from 8.2 to 12.3 m3/t, averaging 10.25 m3/t; whereas seams 8 + 9# have thicknesses ranging from 6 to 9.8 m, averaging approximately 7.9 m, with gas contents ranging from 7 to 12.5 m3/t, averaging around 9.75 m3/t.22,27

Location and stratigraphic column of the Sanjiao Block. (a) Location of the Ordos Basin; (b) location of the Sanjiao Block; (c) elevation profile of the 8# coal seam in the Sanjiao Block; (d) stratigraphic column of the Sanjiao Block.

Three sets of aquifers directly relevant to CBM development are found in the area: the Shanxi Formation aquifer, the Taiyuan Formation aquifer, and the Ordovician aquifer (Figure 2d). (1) The aquifers of the Shanxi Formation mainly comprise sandstone, interspersed with mudstone and sandy mudstone aquifuges, exhibiting a stacked arrangement. Thick aquifuges and undeveloped fractures weaken the hydraulic connection with surface water, resulting in a low water content in these aquifers. (2) The aquifers of the Taiyuan Formation consist of bioclastic limestone and sandstone layers. The limestone is extensively developed in the roof of the coal seams, which can be divided into five layers (labeled L1–L5 from bottom to top), with a cumulative thickness of 30 m, averaging 20.1 m. In most areas, the roof of seam 8# directly contacts the L1 limestone, with only thin mudstone interlayers developed in the roof of the coal seams in the northeast region. The limestone development is generally stable, with occasional bifurcation and merging phenomena and thinning occurring in the northern part. In the northernmost and central parts of the area, a small portion develops two to three layers of limestone, while most areas develop four to five relatively continuous layers of limestone. The limestone aquifers generally have developed fractures, and some local areas exhibit strong water content, presenting challenges to CBM production (Figure 3). (3) The aquifers of the Middle Ordovician are highly heterogeneous due to their tectonic activity. However, the distance between the target coal seam and the Ordovician aquifer is approximately 50 m, with mudstone and sandy mudstone aquifers predominantly interbedded between them, exerting a sealing effect on the aquifer. Consequently, without large faults or fractures, establishing a connection to the Ordovician aquifer is generally challenging.

Well profile in the NE-SW direction.

In this block, a total of 44 CBM wells are producing, comprising 24 wells in the Shanxi Formation (3 + 4 + 5#) and 20 wells in the Taiyuan Formation (8 + 9#). These wells have been in production for an extended period, with most having operated for over 2000 days and some even surpassing 4000 days. At present, CBM wells in the Shanxi Formation demonstrate higher gas production, predominantly concentrated in the central region of the block (Figure 4a), whereas those in the Taiyuan Formation have an average daily gas production of less than 1000 m3/d, with lower overall production concentrated in the midnorthern and western regions of the block (Figure 4c). The main reason for the difference in gas production between the two strata lies in the discrepancy in water production. Overall, the water production of the CBM wells in the Shanxi Formation is relatively low, with an average daily water production of less than 20 m3/d (Figure 4b), while those in the Taiyuan Formation generally have higher water production, with most wells producing more than 50 m3/d on average, and some even exceeding 100 m3/d (Figure 4d). Excessive water production and low gas production lead to a low input–output ratio for CBM wells, resulting in economic losses. As a result, they are permanently shut down for treatment after a period of production (Figure 5). Furthermore, abnormal production characteristics contribute to frequent equipment damage, necessitating frequent shutdowns for well repairs, which are not conducive to long-term and stable drainage and depressurization. Hence, it is necessary to further explore the impact of aquifer leakage recharge on CBM production.

Identification of External Water Sources

Hydrochemical Characteristics

In the eastern part of this block, where the burial depth is relatively shallow, the strata are partially exposed, allowing groundwater replenishment through infiltration from atmospheric precipitation, river water, and other surface water sources. Erosion influences the formation, resulting in the development of karst fissures, strong water-bearing and permeable properties, and intense runoff and leakage recharge capabilities. Groundwater flows westward along the structural slope direction, passing through permeable pathways, such as rock fissures, karst channels, faults, mining-induced fractures, and hydraulic fracturing fractures. Eventually, it flows out of the area in the form of seepage, with salinity increasing from the east to the west. In the western part of the block, where the burial depth is deeper, karst fissures are not developed, resulting in mostly stagnant conditions (Figure 6).

(a) TDS distribution map of CBM wells in the Taiyuan Formation; (b) hydrogeological profile and groundwater runoff diagram.

Hydrogeological data from the Shanxi Formation, Taiyuan Formation, and Ordovician system have been obtained from groundwater monitoring wells in this block. Piper and Stiff diagrams depict the distinct hydrochemical characteristics of the three aquifer systems (Figure 7). In the sandstone aquifer of the Shanxi Formation, Na+ and K+ account for more than 95% of the cations, while carbonate (CO32–) and bicarbonate (HCO3–) ions dominate the anions. The total dissolved solid (TDS) concentration is high, reaching a maximum of 4579.09 mg/L. In the limestone aquifer of the Taiyuan Formation, CO32– and HCO3– continue to dominate among the anions, but the concentration of Cl– and SO42– ranges from 20% to 30%. Moreover, the proportion of Ca2+ among the cations increases. The TDS concentration is low, with a maximum of 1276.76 mg/L. The hydrochemical characteristics are similar, indicating a hydraulic connection between these two aquifer systems. The hydrochemical type of the limestone aquifer in the Ordovician system differs significantly from those of the Shanxi Formation and Taiyuan Formation aquifers. The TDS concentration is relatively low, with a maximum of 2512.92 mg/L. Cl– and SO42– predominate among the anions, indicating a weak connection between the limestone aquifer of the Ordovician system and the overlying aquifers.

Identification Results and Discussion

The low water production and high gas production characteristics suggest that CBM wells in the Shanxi Formation are unaffected by the aquifers. Conversely, CBM wells in the Taiyuan Formation clearly experience the intrusion of external water. Consequently, water samples are collected from 13 CBM wells producing the 8 + 9# coal seams of the Taiyuan Formation in the study area at different times, totaling 60 samples, and their chemical composition is analyzed. The results show that the proportions of Na+ and K+ cations are extremely high, exceeding 95%, with Na+ being predominant; whereas the proportions of Ca2+ and Mg2+ are relatively small, totaling about 5%. Among the anions, the proportion of HCO3– + CO32– is large, ranging from about 60% to 90%, with HCO3– being predominant, followed by Cl–, which accounts for about 10% to 40%, while the content of SO42– is the lowest, less than 5%. To explore the source of water production from these CBM wells, this study employs Fisher’s discriminant analysis, a multivariate statistical analysis method. This method reduces the dimensionality of the data based on the categories of samples, seeking an optimal projection vector to maximize between-class scatter and minimize within-class scatter after projection in this space.28−30 The specific implementation steps are as follows:

(1) Hydrochemical data from groundwater monitoring wells are collected, categorizing them into water samples from the Shanxi Formation, Taiyuan Formation, and Ordovician Formation.

(3) Fisher’s discriminant analysis is applied to elucidate the relationship between hydrochemical data from 60 samples across 13 CBM wells and the characteristic indicators from groundwater monitoring wells, based on the principle of shortest distance discrimination. Subsequently, the results of discriminating water sources are obtained (Figure 8).

The results indicate that hydrochemical data from 60 samples identify water from the Shanxi Formation and the Taiyuan Formation (Figure 9a). Some wells, such as SJ9-V1, SJT14-D3, SJT10, and SJT17-2, are identified as water from the Shanxi Formation at different times, labeled as Type A. Others, including SJT3, SJT17-V1, SJT17-V2, SJT17-V3, SJT18, and SJT18-2, are identified as water from the Taiyuan Formation at different times, labeled as Type B. However, wells like SJT5, SJ18-V1, and SJ16-V1 show varying identification results for hydrochemical data over time, being identified sometimes as water from the Shanxi Formation and sometimes as water from the Taiyuan Formation, and are labeled as Type C. Wells categorized as Type A are situated at low elevations within the stagnant zone of the hydrodynamic field in the block. This indicates that the hydrochemical characteristics of water produced from wells in the stagnant zone are similar to those of water from the Shanxi Formation and the water production is relatively low. Consequently, CBM wells in this category are less susceptible to an external water influx. Wells categorized as Type C are situated in the eastern and southern parts of the block within the strong runoff zone of the hydrodynamic field. This indicates the direct involvement of water from the Taiyuan Formation on CBM well production, resulting in higher water production. Wells categorized as Type B are located between Types A and C within the weak runoff zone of the hydrodynamic field. This indicates that when local groundwater supply is abundant, the hydrochemical characteristics of water produced by CBM wells resemble those of water from the Taiyuan Formation. However, when groundwater supply is weak, the hydrochemical characteristics of water produced by CBM wells are similar to those of water from the Shanxi Formation, hence resulting in a mixed water type (Figure 9b). Fortunately, there is no indication of water from the Ordovician Formation, suggesting the absence of significant faults or fractures near these wells.

Fisher’s discriminant analysis results of hydrochemical data. (a) Piper trilinear nomograph; (b) distribution map.

The hydrodynamic and hydrochemical fields in the study area, categorized into the stagnant zone, strong runoff zone, and weak runoff zone, directly indicate the impact of aquifers on the CBM wells. Stagnant zones indicate limited water content, typically found in areas with effective formation sealing and restricted connectivity to other hydrodynamic fields, such as lens-shaped aquifers with limited water exchange with adjacent aquifers. CBM wells located in the stagnant zone experience weak aquifer leakage recharge, resulting in a minimal impact on production. The strong runoff zone indicates an abundant water supply, which typically occurs in areas with extensive outcrop ranges, direct connections to surface water, or strong hydrodynamic conditions, significantly enhancing water inflow into the aquifer. CBM wells situated in the strong runoff zone are directly affected by the aquifer’s leakage recharge, thereby exerting a significant influence on production. For CBM wells in the weak runoff zone, variations in aquifer recharge capacity during the production cycle lead to fluctuations in their impact on well productivity. Therefore, it is crucial to delineate the aquifer’s leakage recharge capacity and its impact on the productivity of CBM wells.

Numerical Simulation Research

Numerical Simulation Principle

In numerical simulations, aquifer types are generally classified as infinite-recharge and finite-recharge based on the aquifer’s inherent water supply capacity. An infinite-recharge aquifer refers to an aquifer with an abundant water supply, where the pressure in the aquifer remains constant even after prolonged drainage by CBM wells. A finite-recharge aquifer has a fixed total water content internally with no other water sources replenishing it. This means that with the drainage of CBM wells, the aquifer pressure decreases, leading to the depletion of formation water.

Orthogonal Experimental Design

A sensitivity analysis of CBM production is conducted using the SJ-EP well, which exploits the 8# coal reservoir, as an example to evaluate the aquifer’s recharge capacity based on three attributes: aquifer types, aquifer physical properties, and leakage channel properties. Aquifer types include infinite- and finite-recharge aquifers. Aquifer physical properties comprise size, thickness, porosity, and permeability, which characterize the volume of water and the permeability of the formation. Leakage channel properties include the range and distance, reflecting the scale of developed water-conducting channels, such as faults and fractures and their spatial relationship with production wells. A 200 × 200 m geological model of the coal reservoir is established in the numerical simulation software Comet3.0 based on the actual well spacing of the well group and geological conditions of the target well (Figure 10 and Table 1).

Accumulated Gas Production Calculated by Orthogonal Experiments

The sensitivity of the infinite-recharge aquifer to cumulative gas production decreases in the following order: leakage channel distance, leakage channel range, aquifer thickness, permeability, porosity, and size (Table 5). This sequence remains consistent throughout the production lifecycle. The sensitivity of the leakage channel parameters surpasses that of other parameters, suggesting that in the connection of a CBM well to an infinite-recharge aquifer, the spatial relationship between the leakage channel and the CBM well serves as the primary controlling factor influencing CBM production. A leakage distance of zero indicates a direct connection between the CBM well and the aquifer, where abundant external water flows directly into the production well, hindering effective drainage and depressurization in coal reservoirs and resulting in the production well primarily yielding water with minimal gas production. With the increasing leakage distance, the influence range of the aquifer decreases, allowing localized reservoirs to achieve certain levels of depressurization, desorption, and permeation, consequently boosting CBM production (Figure 11a). Additionally, a larger scale of the leakage channel signifies a stronger leakage recharge capability from the aquifer to the coal reservoir, resulting in a larger impact range and less favorable drainage and depressurization (Figure 11b). The sensitivity sequence is determined by the inherent characteristics of infinite-recharge aquifers, which include an abundant water supply, constant formation pressure, and the absence of elastic expansion in the pores, fractures, and internal fluids of the aquifer. This enables the exclusion of geological factors such as aquifer physical properties (eqs 3 and 4).

The sensitivity of the finite-recharge aquifer to cumulative gas production varied across different stages. During the early to middle stages of production, the distance and range of leakage channels are the two most sensitive factors. As the CBM well produces, the sensitivity of parameters characterizing water content, such as aquifer size, thickness, and porosity, gradually increases, becoming the main controlling factors (Table 6). Combining single-factor sensitivity curves, it is evident that when the leakage channel is located at the well-controlled edge, daily gas production decreases by 30% to 64%, with the decline increasing gradually in the middle and later stages. As the leakage channel distance decreases, the impact of aquifers on CBM production occurs earlier. When the CBM well is directly connected to the aquifer through fracture systems, there is an initial drainage period of nearly 500 days during early production, with a maximum daily water production of up to 35 m3/d. The CBM well only produces water without gas in this period. As the water content decreases in the aquifer, the impact weakens, and gas production gradually increases, stabilizing after 2,200 days (Figure 12a). In the later stages of production, when parameters such as aquifer size, porosity, and thickness are larger, the water content in the finite-recharge aquifer is high, which is not conducive to effective depressurization during the production lifecycle, resulting in low gas production and high water production (eqs 5–9). Conversely, when the water content in the aquifer is low and groundwater is adequately drained, the impact of the aquifer on the CBM well decreases, allowing for effective depressurization of the coal reservoir in later stages, resulting in a smaller decline in daily gas production (Figure 12b).

Discussion and Application

Sensitivity studies indicate that geological parameters of aquifers show differences and similarities in their impacts on production. The properties of leakage channels exhibit considerable sensitivity to production, varying between infinite-recharge and finite-recharge aquifers, thereby influencing the magnitude and shape of gas production characteristics. Therefore, a detailed description of fracture channel development is crucial for evaluating the aquifer’s impact. Different types of aquifers show significantly varied sensitivities to the external water content. The size, thickness, and porosity of aquifers have minimal to negligible effects on production in infinite-recharged aquifers, whereas they exert a significant impact on production in finite-recharge aquifers. Therefore, restricting external water influx is a key indicator that distinguishes between the two types of aquifers and is the fundamental cause of the differences in the effectiveness of aquifer leakage recharge between them.

Using the sensitivity of geological parameters, a delineation process is established to assess the impact of aquifer leakage recharge on CBM production, sequentially identifying the aquifer type, leakage channel distance, leakage channel range, and aquifer size (Figure 13). In cases where infinite-recharge aquifers directly connect to CBM wells, the leakage recharge capacity of the aquifer is strong. This prevents effective depressurization within the well-controlled range, resulting in stable high water production in CBM wells but limited gas production potential. When the leakage channel distance is far, external water influx prevents partial reservoirs from achieving effective depressurization. Although water production remains relatively stable, severe declines in gas production occur during the middle and later stages, limiting production potential. When finite-recharge aquifers are closely connected to CBM wells or when large-scale leakage recharge channels develop, if the water content in finite-recharge aquifers is low, CBM wells gradually resume normal production after external water drainage, indicating greater production potential. With increasing water content in finite-recharge aquifers, the drainage period lengthens, gradually reducing production potential. When the leakage recharge channel distance is long, localized depressurization of coal reservoirs is restricted, leading to a significant decline in gas production in the middle and later stages, which is unfavorable for maintaining stable production (Figure 14).

Through the production discriminant process, we identify the production characteristics of all 44 CBM wells in the block. The production characteristics of CBM wells in this area include six patterns of aquifer leakage recharge, showing significant differences between the Shanxi and Taiyuan Formations (Figure 15). In most regions of the 3 + 4 + 5# coal seam in the Shanxi Formation, there is no influence from aquifer leakage recharge. However, in the central and southern parts, there is partial evidence of finite-recharge aquifer-lead-age recharge patterns. The 8 + 9# coal seam in the Taiyuan Formation is primarily characterized by infinite-recharge aquifer leakage recharge. However, there are also areas in the west that are unaffected by aquifers (Figure 16). Therefore, despite the absence of developed faults or natural fractures in the Shanxi Formation, comprehensive evaluations of coal seam roof and floor sealing, as well as the scale of artificial fracturing, are necessary to mitigate adverse effects from finite-recharge aquifers on CBM development. Importantly, prior to drilling in the Taiyuan Formation, it is imperative to ascertain the development of faults and natural fractures, avoid leakage channels, and implement precautions to prevent production wells from connecting to infinite-recharge aquifers.

Conclusions

1. Fisher’s discriminant analysis is used to examine the water sources of CBM wells in the Sanjiao Block, revealing that the hydrodynamic and hydrochemical conditions in the Taiyuan Formation directly indicate the impact of aquifers on CBM wells. The hydrochemical characteristics of water extracted from wells in the stagnant zone resemble those from the Shanxi Formation, suggesting that CBM wells are less susceptible to an external water influx. Water from the Taiyuan Formation directly contributes to the development of CBM wells in the strong runoff zone, leading to significant water production. In the weak runoff zone, fluctuations in aquifer leakage recharge capacity cause changes in hydrochemical characteristics during the production cycle, resulting in variations in their influence on well productivity.

2. Numerical simulation employs orthogonal experiments to quantify the sensitivity of aquifer geological parameters to CBM production. These parameters are ranked in the order of sensitivity from strong to weak: aquifer types, leakage channel properties, and aquifer physical properties. The fundamental reason for disparities in the efficacy of leakage recharge lies in categorizing aquifers into finite and infinite recharge based on their water supply capacity. The location and scale of leakage channels exert a significant influence on CBM production, as evidenced by the magnitude and shape of the gas production characteristics in infinite and finite recharge aquifers, respectively.

3. A discriminant flowchart of CBM production is presented to delineate the production characteristics of CBM wells under the influence of aquifer leakage recharge, categorizing them into six patterns. In most regions of the 3 + 4 + 5# coal seam in the Shanxi Formation, aquifer leakage recharge has no discernible effect. However, in the central and southern areas, there is partial evidence of finite-recharge aquifer leakage. The 8 + 9# coal seam in the Taiyuan Formation is predominantly affected by aquifer leakage recharge with infinite recharge characteristics. Nevertheless, there are also regions in the west where aquifers have no influence. This discriminant process provides scientific guidance for analyzing CBM production characteristics and evaluating potential under the influence of aquifer leakage recharge.

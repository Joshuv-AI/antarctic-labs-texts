Combination of minocycline and ciprofloxacin enhances the inhibitory effect of tumor necrosis factor-alpha production in lipopolysaccharide-stimulated THP-1 monocytic cells
Authors: Ippei Sakamaki, Yukie Tanaka, Hiromichi Iwasaki
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•MINO and CPFX together suppressed TNF-α in LPS-stimulated THP-1 cells.•The combination also reduced IL-8, IP-10, MIP-1α, and MIP-1β production.•CPFX inhibited NF-κB and ERK phosphorylation, key cytokine signaling pathways.•MINO + CPFX showed additive anti-inflammatory effects without cytotoxicity.•This combination may help alleviate severe symptoms in Japanese spotted fever.Keywords: Japanese spotted fever, Minocycline, Ciprofloxacin, Tumor necrosis factor-alpha, Cytokine storm

Introduction

Infectious diseases caused by rickettsia are characterized by clinical manifestations including high fever, rash, and eschar formation.1,2 These symptoms are often similar across related infections, such as Japanese spotted fever (JSF) and tsutsugamushi disease (TD), the latter being a mite-borne infection caused by Orientia tsutsugamushi. Tetracycline antibiotics, such as doxycycline or minocycline (MINO), are recommended for treating clinically suspected cases of these infections. However, the clinical course of some infections is more severe than that of others, and tetracycline monotherapy may occasionally be insufficient for patients with severe illness.3,4. Several physicians have reported that a combination of tetracycline (TC) and fluoroquinolone (FQ) is effective in treating severe cases,5,6 with earlier resolution of fever than that with tetracycline monotherapy.7 Combination therapy using tetracycline and corticosteroids has also been administered to patients with severe infections.8,9 Antigen-specific T cells and cytokines, produced by activated T lymphocytes and macrophages, play important roles in host immune responses.10 High levels of various cytokines, including interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), have been observed during the acute phase of infection.11 Suppression of hypercytokinemia may therefore explain the rationale for adjunctive steroid use in severe cases.

The levels of cytokines, including tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and IFN-γ, as well as chemokines such as interleukin-8 (IL-8), interferon gamma-induced protein 10 (IP-10), monocyte chemoattractant protein 1 (MCP-1), macrophage inflammatory protein (MIP)-1α, and MIP-1β, have been reported to be significantly elevated in the acute phase of infection.12 MINO, a tetracycline antimicrobial agent, inhibits the production of inflammatory cytokines in lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated THP-1 cells and suppresses the nuclear factor of kappa B (NF-κB) signaling pathway.13 Additionally, MINO has been shown to modulate inflammatory cytokines in the treatment of patients with rheumatoid arthritis or Japanese encephalitis.14,15 In contrast, ciprofloxacin (CPFX) exhibits antimicrobial activity against Rickettsia japonica, with a minimum inhibitory concentration ranging from 0.5 to 1 mg/L.16 CPFX reduces the levels of lung TNF-α in mice.17 However, the precise mechanisms remain unclear.

In the present study, we used a basic research model with cultured cells to examine whether a combination of MINO and CPFX inhibits TNF-α production in LPS-stimulated monocytic THP-1 cells. Although this study did not directly involve specific pathogens, the in vitro approach provides insights into the potential anti-inflammatory synergy of MINO and CPFX, which may help explain their clinical benefits in the management of hypercytokinemia.

Materials and methods

Reagents

Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 medium, fetal bovine serum (FBS), MINO, and CPFX were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). MINO and CPFX were diluted in culture medium at a concentration of 1.0 mg/mL to create a stock solution. LPS from Pseudomonas aeruginosa 10 (Merck Ltd./Sigma-Aldrich Japan G.K., Tokyo, Japan) was used to induce an inflammatory response. LPS was dissolved in water at a concentration of 1.0 mg/mL and stored at −80°C until use.

Cell viability assay

THP-1 cells were cultured with 0.1 µg/mL of LPS alone or with 10, 30, or 50 µg/mL of either CPFX or MINO for 4 h and then analyzed using CellTiter 96® AQueous One Solution Cell Proliferation Assay (MTS) (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). Samples were read using an automated plate reader (Multiskan Spectrum; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA).

LPS and antibiotics treatment

THP-1 cells were purchased from the ATCC (VA, USA). Cells were cultured in 10% FBS-containing RPMI 1640 medium at 37°C in humidified air with 5% CO2, and cells in the exponential growth phase were used for all experiments. THP-1 cells (2 × 105 cells/mL) were cultured with 0.1 µg/mL of LPS from Pseudomonas aeruginosa 10 in the presence or absence of antibiotics (MINO or CPFX) for 4 h. Following incubation, the supernatants were collected via centrifugation at 1500 r/min for 5 min at room temperature and stored at −80°C until further analysis.

TNF-α ELISA assay

TNF-α concentrations were determined using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), according to the manufacturer's instructions. An ELISA kit (Cytoscreen) for TNF-α was purchased from Invitrogen (Camarillo, CA, USA). Samples were run in triplicates and read using an automated ELISA reader (SPECTRA max 250; Molecular Devices, Tokyo, Japan).

Multiplex bead immunoassays

Multiplex bead immunoassays (Bio-Plex Suspension Array System; Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA) were used for the simultaneous quantification of the following three chemokines: MIP-1α, MIP-1β, and IL-8.

Western blot analysis

Total protein was extracted from LPS-stimulated cells treated with antibiotics using 200 µL of radioimmunoprecipitation assay buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Rockford, IL, USA) containing a protease inhibitor cocktail (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) and phosphatase inhibitor. The lysates were clarified by centrifugation (15,000 r/min for 10 min at 4°C). The protein concentration was determined using a Protein Assay BCA Kit (Nacalai Tesque, Kyoto, Japan). Samples containing 10 µg of protein were run on 10% polyacrylamide gels and electrotransferred onto polyvinylidene fluoride filter membranes. The membranes were blocked for 60 min at room temperature in 1% bovine serum albumin and Tris-buffered saline containing Tween 20, followed by overnight incubation with a primary antibody, anti phospho-NF-κB p65 rabbit monoclonal antibody (1∶1,000; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA), anti-phospho extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) rabbit monoclonal antibody (1∶1,000; Cell Signaling Technology), anti-phospho-p38 alpha mouse monoclonal antibody (1∶1,000; Cell Signaling Technology) at 4°C. The primary antibodies were detected by incubation with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies and visualized using ImmunoStar Zeta (FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Corporation, Osaka, Japan). Expression of each protein was analyzed using a Multi-Imager II Chemi BOX (BioTools, Gunma, Japan).

Statistical analysis

Each experiment was performed in triplicate, and all data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Differences in data were determined using Student's t-test and statistical software (Microsoft Excel 2021; Microsoft Corporation, Redmond, WA, USA), and p < 0.05 was considered significant.

Results

Both CPFX and MINO did not show cytotoxicity in THP-1 cells

THP-1 cells (2 × 105 cells/mL) were incubated in RPMI1640 for 4 h and number of cells was evaluated. When the number of THP-1 cells cultured in RPMI1640 after 4 h incubation was 1, the number of cells treated with CPFX for 4 h was 1.14 ± 0.093 (10 µg/mL), 1.00 ± 0.23 (30 µg/mL), and 1.19 ± 0.50 (50 µg/mL) cells, respectively. No significant differences were observed between the cells alone and those treated with CPFX (Fig. 1).

The number of cells treated with MINO was 1.06 ± 0.07 (10 µg/mL), 1.14 ± 0.00 (30 µg/mL), and 1.11 ± 0.10 (50 µg/mL), respectively. No significant differences were observed between cells alone and those treated with MINO (Fig. 1). Neither CPFX nor MINO treatment induced cytotoxicity in THP-1 cells.

Effects of antibiotics on TNF-α production in LPS-stimulated THP-1 cells

TNF-α concentrations were evaluated in the supernatants of LPS-stimulated THP-1 cells cultured with antibiotics (CPFX alone, MINO alone or a combination of CPFX and MINO) for 4 h. CPFX alone (10, 30 or 50 µg/mL) reduced TNF-α concentration to 2011.28 ± 26.64 (pg/mL) (p < 0.05), 1322.53 ± 98.93 (pg/mL) (p < 0.05) or 1050.44 ± 179.63 (pg/mL) (p < 0.01) compared to that obtained using LPS alone (2158.00 ± 19.80 pg/mL). MINO alone (10, 30 or 50 µg/mL) also reduced TNF-α concentration to 1716.97 ± 159.36 (pg/mL) (p < 0.05), 1002.06 ± 395.67 (pg/mL) (p < 0.05) or 739.48 ± 261.89 (pg/mL) (p < 0.01) compared to LPS alone (2158.00 ± 19.80 pg/mL) (Fig. 2, Table 1). TNF-α production was significantly inhibited by combination of 10 µg/mL MINO and 10 µg/mL CPFX (1217.30 ± 101.30 pg/mL) compared to that obtained using LPS alone (2158.00 ± 19.80 pg/mL) (p < 0.01) (Fig. 2, Table 2). TNF-α production was also significantly inhibited by the combination of 30 µg/mL MINO and 30 µg/mL CPFX (602.30 ± 144.50 pg/mL) (p < 0.01) and the combination of 50 µg/mL MINO and 50 µg/mL CPFX (206.30 ± 7.10 pg/mL) (p < 0.01) compared to that obtained using LPS alone (2158.00 ± 19.80 pg/mL) (Fig. 2, Table 1). The combination of CPFX and MINO showed additive effects on the inhibition of TNF-α production compared with CPFX or MINO alone.

Effect of the low-dose antibiotics on TNF-α production in LPS-stimulated THP-1 cells

TNF-α concentrations were evaluated in the supernatants of LPS-stimulated THP-1 cells cultured with low-dose antibiotics (CPFX alone, MINO alone, or a combination of CPFX and MINO) for 4 h. CPFX alone (1 or 5 µg/mL) reduced TNF-α concentration to 912.50 ± 53.86 (pg/mL) (p < 0.01) or 905.80 ± 20.45 (pg/mL) (p < 0.01) compared to that obtained using LPS alone (1036.90 ± 24.82 pg/mL). MINO alone (1 or 5 µg/mL) also reduced TNF-α concentration to 876.55 ± 21.40 (pg/mL) (p < 0.01) or 831.55 ± 45.70 (pg/mL) (p < 0.01) compared to LPS alone (1036.90 ± 24.82 pg/mL) (Fig. 3). TNF-α production was significantly inhibited by combination of 1 µg/mL MINO and 1 µg/mL CPFX (810.00 ± 31.77 pg/mL) compared to that obtained using LPS alone (1036.90 ± 4.82 pg/mL) (p < 0.01) (Fig. 2). TNF-α production was also significantly inhibited by the combination of 5 µg/mL CPFX and 5 µg/mL MINO (733.30 ± 41.90 pg/mL) (p < 0.01) compared to that obtained using LPS alone (1036.90 ± 24.82 pg/mL) (Fig. 3). The combination of low-dose CPFX and MINO showed additive effects on the inhibition of TNF-α production compared to CPFX or MINO alone.

Effects of the combination of MINO and CPFX on chemokines production in LPS-stimulated THP-1 cells

Based on the finding that combination of MINO and CPFX inhibited TNF-α production, we examined the effects on chemokine production. The production of several chemokines was significantly affected by the combination of 30 µg/mL MINO and 30 µg/mL CPFX. The production of chemokines (IL-8, IP-10, MIP-1α, and MIP-1β) in LPS-stimulated THP-1 cells was suppressed by the combination of MINO and CPFX compared with the LPS control, MINO alone, or CPFX alone (Fig. 4).

THP-1 cells (5 × 105/mL) were stimulated by LPS (0.1 µg/mL) with or without CPFX (30 µg/mL) for 1 h. Levels of the phosphorylated form of p65 (NF-κB) and ERK decreased in the cells treated with CPFX and LPS compared to those in cells treated with LPS alone (Fig. 5A–B). Discussion

In the present study, treatment with MINO and CPFX suppressed TNF-α levels. Suppression of TNF-α was observed not only at high concentrations of antibiotics but also at a low dose comparable to the plasma concentration achieved in humans. It also suppressed the production of inflammatory chemokines such as IL-8, IP-10, MIP-1α, and MIP-1β. The combination of MINO and CPFX had an additive effect on TNF-α and chemokine production. Neither MINO nor CPFX exhibited cytotoxicity in THP-1 cells. TNF-α and inflammatory chemokine production were not suppressed by cytotoxicity. MINO significantly improves asthma symptoms by preventing IgE production in patients with asthma.18 MINO also has modest efficacy in treating rheumatoid arthritis (RA), with an effect similar to that of disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs,14 and reduces IL-6 production in patients with RA.19 Thus, MINO is considered to have an inhibitory effect on the production of inflammatory cytokines.

Quinolones have been reported to exhibit immunomodulatory effects. Garenoxacin (GRNX) exhibits anti-inflammatory activity by inhibiting IL-8 production and ERK1/2 activation.20 Moxifloxacin (MFLX) suppresses the secretion of proinflammatory cytokines by inhibiting NF-κB, ERK, and c-Jun N-terminal kinase (JNK).21 Sitafloxacin (STFX) blocks the release of TNF-α into the extracellular space by inhibiting the phosphorylation and activation of TNF-α converting enzyme.22 We evaluated whether CPFX inhibited the signaling pathways that produce TNF-α in THP-1 cells. We confirmed that CPFX inhibited the phosphorylation of two different signaling pathways for TNF-α production.

MINO inhibits the NF-κB signaling pathway by inhibiting phosphorylation of p65 to reduce TNF-α production in THP-1 cells. CPFX alone or MINO alone inhibited p65 phosphorylation, and the combination of MINO and CPFX enhanced the inhibition of p65 phosphorylation. In addition, CPFX inhibits not only NF-κB pathway but also the ERK pathway; therefore, we observed an additive effect of the combination of MINO and CPFX.

Recent clinical studies have reported the utility of TC monotherapy and TC + FQ combination therapy.23,24 One of these studies used diagnosis procedure combination, a case-mixed patient classification system in Japan, to retrospectively analyze hospitalized patients diagnosed with JSF from July 2010 to March 2021. Patients who received TC + FQ were compared with those who received TC alone. The primary outcome was in-hospital mortality rate. Secondary outcomes included in-hospital complications, total hospitalization costs, and length of stay. There were no significant differences between in-hospital mortality, in-hospital complications, total hospitalization costs, or length of stay for the two groups.23 Another retrospective study revealed that combination therapy did not improve mortality outcomes and instead revealed its deleterious effects.24

In contrast, the data from 102 published cases with JSF (84 in the TC group and 18 in the TC + FQ group), including temperature data, were from another report and analyzed; the TC + FQ group subjects had significantly lower temperatures on days 3 and 4 than those in the TC group.7 TC monotherapy for JSF eventually leads to fever resolution; however, the duration of fever is longer than that of other rickettsial infections, such as TD. Therefore, TC + FQ may exert a relatively greater antipyretic effect and shorten the duration of febrile symptoms. Combination therapy may not improve the mortality rates. However, it may strongly suppress the production of inflammatory cytokines and rapidly relieve the fever.

This study has several limitations. The combination of CPFX and MINO enhanced the suppression of TNF-α production in LPS-stimulated THP-1 cells. Stimulation with Rickettsia japonica was not performed. We did not determine whether the combination of CPFX and MINO suppresses cytokines in vivo. Therefore, the findings of this study cannot be directly applied to the treatment of patients with severe infectious diseases such as JSF.

Conclusions

From a clinical perspective, this combination therapy with CPFX and MINO is expected to provide both antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory effects. Enhancement of cytokine modulation by antibiotics may be one of the key mechanisms for suppressing hypercytokinemia in severe infectious diseases such as JSF.

Optimizing first-line therapy for double-expressor lymphoma: a systematic review and meta-analysis of novel agent-augmented R-CHOP regimens
Authors: Min-Yue Zhang, Fei Xiao, Zi-Hua Li, Hong-Hui Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDouble-expressor lymphoma (DEL) shows an inferior survival with R-CHOP therapy. While R-CHOP combined with novel agents (R-CHOP + X) demonstrates promising benefits in DEL, the optimal front-line regimen remains controversial. Therefore, we performed a systematic review and meta-analysis to compare the efficacy and safety of R-CHOP + X regimens and identify optimal first-line strategies for DEL.MethodsPubMed, OVID, Cochrane Library, and clinical trial registries were systematically searched (up to May 31st, 2025) for studies evaluating first-line treatment with R-CHOP, R-DA-EPOCH, or R-CHOP + X [including BTK inhibitors (BTKi), polatuzumab vedotin, lenalidomide, venetoclax, tucidinostat, and bortezomib] in DEL. Outcomes included 2-year progression-free survival (PFS), 3-year overall survival (OS), complete remission (CR) rates, and adverse events (AEs) incidence. Single-arm, pairwise, and Bayesian network meta-analyses were performed using R software (version 4.3.2) and RevMan 5.4. Efficacy rankings were assessed via Surface Under the Cumulative Ranking Curve (SUCRA).ResultsTwenty-three studies (2,735 patients) evaluating eight regimens were included. Single-arm meta-analysis showed that R-CHOP + X achieved pooled 2-year PFS of 72%, 3-year OS of 85%, and CR rate of 75%, without increasing the incidence of AEs compared with R-CHOP. Pairwise meta-analysis indicated that R-CHOP + X was significantly superior to R-CHOP in 2-year PFS [odds ratio (OR) 1.69, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.39–2.05], 3-year OS (OR 1.51, 95% CI 1.08–2.11), and CR rate (OR 1.56, 95% CI 1.24–1.95). Based on SUCRA-based ranking probabilities from the network meta-analysis, R-CHOP+BTKi tended to rank highest for 2-year PFS (SUCRA 0.79) and ranked second for 3-year OS (SUCRA 0.76), although these rankings should be interpreted cautiously given the predominance of indirect comparisons. Crucially, among R-CHOP + X regimens, the BTKi-containing combination emerged as the sole intervention demonstrating concurrent significant outperformance over R-CHOP in both 2-year PFS [OR 2.07, 95% credible interval (CrI) 1.24–3.70] and 3-year OS (OR 1.76, 95% CrI 1.07–2.93). Notably, R-CHOP + X did not increase the incidence of severe hematological toxicities or infections in comparison to R-CHOP alone.ConclusionR-CHOP + X regimens, particularly BTKi-based combinations, may improve PFS and OS versus standard R-CHOP in newly diagnosed DEL, without a clear signal of excess hematologic toxicity or infections in the available evidence, pending confirmation in prospective comparative trials. These results suggest that incorporating targeted agents into frontline R-CHOP backbones could be a promising strategy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12935-026-04293-4.Keywords: Double-expressor lymphoma, Meta-analysis, Novel agents, BTK inhibitor

Introduction

Double Expressor Lymphoma (DEL), a high-risk subtype of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), is defined by the co-expression of the MYC and BCL2 proteins (without underlying genomic rearrangements) detected by immunohistochemistry (IHC) [1]. It accounts for 20–30% of DLBCL cases [2] and is characterized by aggressive clinical behavior, driven by MYC-mediated proliferation and BCL-2-induced apoptosis resistance, resulting in a poorer prognosis compared to non-DEL DLBCL [3–5]. Historically, the standard first-line therapy for DLBCL, R-CHOP regimen (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone), was used for DEL patients, achieving 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) rates of 39% and 42%, respectively [5]. Owing to the limited efficacy of the R-CHOP regimen in this subtype, intensified chemotherapy regimens like R-DA-EPOCH, have been explored to improve survival outcomes. Results from these efforts were inconsistent [6–8], with a recent meta-analysis indicating that R-DA-EPOCH does not significantly outperform R-CHOP in terms of PFS or OS for DEL patients [8].

Recent strategies have focused on integrating novel targeted agents into frontline R-CHOP-based regimens (R-CHOP + X), yielding promising results [4, 9–11]. For instance, BTK inhibitors (BTKi) such as ibrutinib and zanubrutinib, combined with R-CHOP, have shown encouraging outcomes [4, 10]. The phase III PHOENIX trial demonstrated improved event-free survival (EFS) in DEL patients treated with R-CHOP plus ibrutinib [4]. Similarly, the POLARIX trial evaluated polatuzumab vedotin (Pola)-based R-CHP+Pola, reporting an approximate 2-year PFS of 75% in DEL subgroups [9]. However, most studies to date have relied on pairwise comparisons, assessing R-CHOP + X regimens solely against R-CHOP, without direct comparisons among the various targeted therapies. As a result, the optimal treatment for DEL remains undefined.

Given that prior meta-analyses in DEL primarily evaluated chemotherapy intensification (e.g., R-DA-EPOCH vs. R-CHOP) [8], we performed a comprehensive single-arm, pairwise, and network meta-analysis to assess the efficacy and safety of multiple frontline novel agent–augmented R-CHOP regimens (R-CHOP + X) in newly diagnosed DEL patients, and to compare their outcomes within an anchored evidence framework.

Methods

Search strategy

This systematic review was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA statement for reporting systematic reviews and network meta-analyses in healthcare interventions [12]. The study protocol was prospectively registered in the International Prospective Register for Systematic Reviews (registration ID: CRD420251000659). We performed the literature search in the PubMed, OVID, Cochrane Library, and ClinicalTrials.gov databases for clinical trials on the treatment of DEL up to May 31st 2025 with the following search terms: “double-expressor” OR “diffuse large B-cell lymphoma”, AND “polatuzumab vedotin” OR “ibrutinib” OR “zanubrutinib” OR “orelabrutinib” OR “lenalidomide” OR “venetoclax” OR “chidamide/tucidinostat” OR “bortezomib” OR “EPOCH” (Supplemental Table 1). We also reviewed abstracts from major international conferences, relevant meta-analyses and systematic reviews. The language is limited to English. In case where a publication overlapped with other publication of the same trial, only the article with more details or the most recent articles were included.

Selection criteria

Studies included in our meta-analysis were required to meet the following criteria: (1) published and unpublished phase 1, 2, or 3 clinical trials; (2) studies enrolling participants newly diagnosed as DEL histologically and excluded double-hit lymphoma by fluorescence in situ hybridization or cytogenetics; (3) receiving one of the following treatment regimens: R-CHOP, R-CHOP like + X or R-DA-EPOCH; (4) trials evaluating at least one of the clinical outcomes of interest, including PFS, OS, and complete remission rate. (5) number of included participates in each treatment regimen more than 10. Studies not meeting the inclusion criteria were excluded. When the relevant data was not reported in the publication, we contacted the corresponding author to obtain the relevant information via e-mail.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Two reviewers (Zhang MY and Li ZH) independently extracted data and outcomes using an electronic standard form. The following details from each study was summarized in Table 1 and Supplemental Table 2: (1) first author, (2) publication year, (3) country, (4) treatment, (5) sample size, (6) demographic information (sex, age, Ann Arbor stage, IPI score, and DLBCL subtype per Hans algorithm), and (7) outcomes (2-year PFS rate, 3-year OS rate, and the number of participants with complete remission), and adverse events (AEs), including grade 3–4 hematologic AEs (neutropenia, thrombocytopenia), any grades of infection, and other non-hematologic AEs where available (e.g., cardiovascular events, diarrhea, elevated alanine aminotransferase or aspartate aminotransferase, peripheral neuropathy). Discrepancies between the two reviewers were resolved by a third investigator, Huang HH.

Data analysis

Data analysis was performed using R software (version 4.3.2) and RevMan 5.4. The meta package of R software was used for single-arm, RevMan 5.4 for pairwise meta-analyses, and the gemtc package of R software for the network meta-analysis [15]. The primary outcome was 2-year PFS, with secondary outcomes including 3-year OS, complete remission (CR) rate, and incidence of adverse events (grade III-IV neutropenia/thrombocytopenia and any grades of infections).

The single-arm meta-analysis was performed to calculate the pooled 2-year PFS, 3-year OS, CR rate, and the incidence of adverse events for each treatment strategy from all including studies reporting these outcomes. The pairwise meta-analysis was conducted for head-to-head comparisons between R-CHOP and R-CHOP + X treatment strategies. Odds ratios (ORs) were determined for binary outcomes (2-year PFS, 3-year OS, and CR rate), with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). The heterogeneity across studies was assessed using the Chi-square based Q-statistic test. When statistical heterogeneity was substantial (I² >50% or P Q−test < 0.05), the random-effects model was used; otherwise, the fixed-effect model was used [16]. Additionally, meta-regression analyses were performed to identify and adjust for potential confounding variables influencing the outcomes of 2-year PFS, 3-year OS, CR rate. Variables with a p-value < 0.1 in univariate meta-regression were included in the multivariate model. Meta-regression was performed using the metareg function under a mixed-effects framework with default settings.

The network meta-analysis was conducted to compare the difference of 2-year PFS, 3-year OS, and CR rate across all interventions. This analysis incorporated direct and indirect comparisons of relevant therapeutic effects by using Bayesian hierarchical model to provide an estimate of maximum efficacy. The results of network meta-analysis were demonstrated as network geometry diagrams, league tables, Surface under the Cumulative Ranking Curve (SUCRA), and forest plots. In the network geometry diagram, each node represents an intervention with the number of participants in brackets, and connecting lines indicate direct comparisons with study counts above; absence of lines denotes indirect evidence only. League tables present matrix of odds ratio between all treatment pairs. Additionally, for efficacy rankings, we have elaborated on SUCRA alongside PbBT (probability of being best treatment) and PoRa (probability of being ranked above average). SUCRA, a Bayesian metric derived from rank probabilities in the network meta-analysis, quantifies the relative position of each treatment by computing the surface under the cumulative probability curve from rankograms, yielding values from 0 (worst) to 1 (best), where higher scores indicate a greater likelihood of outperforming competitors, accounting for uncertainty in posterior distributions [17, 18]. SUCRA rankings are presented as supportive summaries of rank probabilities and should not be overinterpreted as definitive clinical superiority, especially when credible intervals overlap, or evidence is indirect. Therefore, we prioritize statistically significant contrasts versus the R-CHOP anchor [effect estimates and credible intervals (CrIs)] for inference and interpret SUCRA primarily as hypothesis-generating for trial prioritization. Forest plots display ORs and 95% CrIs relative to R-CHOP. Even when differences in effect sizes among treatments were small, clinical decisions about the priority treatment selection may be guided based on the SUCRA values of treatment ranking. Global inconsistency was evaluated by comparing the fit of a consistency model to an inconsistency model. Model fit was assessed using the Deviance Information Criterion (DIC). Global inconsistency was inferred if a DIC difference (ΔDIC) between the two model is greater than 5 [19].

The publication bias was evaluated using Funnel plots and Begg’s tests. All p-values were 2-sided, with P < 0.05 considered indicative of a statistically significant difference.

Results

Study characteristics

Through the literature search and selection based on the criteria above, 35 articles were identified by reviewing 5,489 potentially relevant publications. Then 12 publications were excluded after further assessment of the full text or abstracts. Finally, 23 articles [4, 6–11, 20–35] with 2735 enrolled participants met the inclusion criteria for the meta-analysis (Supplemental Figure.1). The characteristics of the included studies were summarized in Table 1 and Supplemental Table 2. Of these, 8 studies were single-arm trials, 14 were two arm trials, and 1 trial had three comparator arms. Sample size ranged from 12 to 223. Eight treatment strategies were assessed: R-CHOP, R-DA-EPOCH, R-CHOP+BTKi (including ibrutinib, zanubrutinib, and orelabrutinib), R-CHOP+lenalidomide (Len), R-CHOP+venetoclax (Ven), R-CHOP+tucidinostat (Tuc), R-CHOP+bortezomib (Bor), and R-CHP+Pola. The results of bias assessment were summarized in Supplemental Fig. 2, which indicated reliable quality and low risk of bias.

Single-arm meta-analysis

For R-CHOP + X regimens, 12 studies (968 participants) [4, 9–11, 21, 23, 25, 26, 29–32] reporting 2-year PFS rate, 8 studies (494 participants) [4, 10, 21, 23, 25, 29, 31, 32] reporting 3-year OS rate, and 15 studies (909 participants) [4, 10, 11, 21, 23, 25, 27–35] reporting CR rate were included in the single-arm meta-analysis. The pooled estimates were as follows: 2-year PFS rate at 72% (95% CI: 68%-76%, I² = 53.9%, Fig. 1A), 3-year OS rate at 85% (95% CI: 79%-90%, I² = 50%, Fig. 1B), and CR rate at 75% (95% CI: 72%-78%, I² = 36.9%, Fig. 1C).

Pooled efficacy estimates for R-CHOP plus each novel agent in the single-arm meta-analysis. (A) 2-year progression-free survival rate; (B) 3-year overall survival rate; (C) Complete remission rate. (R-CHOP: Rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone; Ven: venetoclax; Tuc: tucidinostat; Pola: polatuzumab vedotin; Len: lenalidomide; Bor: bortezomib; BTKi: BTK inhibitor)

Compared to patients receiving R-CHOP, those treated with R-CHOP + X showed significantly higher pooled 2-year PFS [72% vs. 57%, P < 0.0001, Supplemental Fig. 3A], 3-year OS [85% vs.71%, P = 0.0005, Supplemental Fig. 3B] and CR rate [75% vs. 65%, P < 0.0001, Supplemental Fig. 3C]. Similarly, patients treated with R-CHOP + X also demonstrated significantly superior pooled 2-year PFS [72% vs. 60%, P = 0.0011, Supplemental Fig. 3D], 3-year OS [85% vs.70%, P = 0.0003, Supplemental Fig. 3E], but similar CR rate [75% vs. 72%, P = 0.3769, Supplemental Fig. 3F] in comparison to those receiving R-DA-EPOCH. There was no difference of pooled 2-year PFS, 3-year OS, and CR rate between patients treated with R-CHOP and R-DA-EPOCH (data not shown).

Regarding the pooled incidence of adverse events of R-CHOP + X regimens, single-arm meta-analyses included 11 studies (698 participants) [10, 11, 25, 27–29, 31–35] for grade III-IV neutropenia, 9 studies (655 participants) [10, 25, 27, 29, 31–35] for grade III-IV thrombocytopenia, and 7 studies (514 participants) [10, 11, 25, 28, 29, 31, 35] for any grades of infections. The results indicated that the pooled incidences of grade III-IV neutropenia, grade III-IV thrombocytopenia, and any grades of infections were 40% (95% CI: 29%-51%, I² = 77.9%, Supplemental Fig. 4A), 15% (95% CI: 10%-22%, I² = 61.8%, Supplemental Fig. 4B), and 19% (95% CI: 12%-28%, I² = 71.8%, Supplemental Fig. 4C), respectively. No significant differences were observed in pooled incidence of these adverse events between patients receiving R-CHOP and R-CHOP + X.

The funnel plot and Begg’s publication bias test and are shown in Supplemental Fig. 5A-F and Supplemental Table 3. There was publication bias for the outcomes of complete remission rate, grade III-IV neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia.

Pairwise meta-analysis

The pairwise meta-analysis was conducted to compare 2-year PFS, 3-year OS and CR rate between R-CHOP + X and R-CHOP. The heterogeneity test demonstrated that no statistically significant heterogeneity among the selected literature was found (I2 = 0%), and fixed effects model was selected for meta-analysis. The results showed that R-CHOP + X was significantly associated with prolonged 2-year PFS (OR 1.69, 95% CI: 1.39–2.05, P < 0.0001, Fig. 2A), 3-year OS (OR 1.51, 95% CI: 1.08–2.11, P = 0.02, Fig. 2B) and improved CR rate (OR 1.56, 95% CI: 1.24–1.95, P = 0.0001, Fig. 2C) compared to R-CHOP. There was no statistically significant level of publication bias, as evidenced by the funnel plot and Begg’s test (Supplemental Fig. 6A-C and Supplemental Table 3).

Efficacy comparisons between R-CHOP and R-CHOP plus novel agents (R-CHOP + X) regimens in pairwise meta-analysis. (A) Progression-free survival; (B) Overall survival; (C) Complete remission rate. (R-CHOP: Rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone)

Furthermore, subgroup analyses were performed when restricted in RCT studies. The results also demonstrated that R-CHOP + X significantly outperformed R-CHOP in terms of 2-year PFS (OR 1.61, 95% CI: 1.26–2.05, P = 0.0001) and CR (OR 1.46, 95% CI: 1.11–1.91, P = 0.007). Regarding 3-year OS, only one RCT reported this endpoint, thereby precluding a pairwise meta-analysis limited to RCTs.

Network meta-analysis

Single-arm and pairwise meta-analysis indicated that incorporating novel agents into the R-CHOP regimen could enhance efficacy and survival outcomes in patients with DEL. To determine the optimal treatment strategy for DEL, a network meta-analysis was conducted to assess 2-year PFS, 3-year OS and CR rate among different interventions. We grouped BTK inhibitors (Ibrutinib/Zanubrutinib) as a single node to evaluate a BTKi-class effect, given sparse DEL-specific data per individual agent. Across active regimens, direct evidence was almost exclusively R-CHOP + X/R-DA-EPOCH versus R-CHOP. Only one study provided a direct comparison between two active regimens (R-CHOP + Len vs. R-CHOP+BTKi); all other between-active contrasts were indirect via the R-CHOP anchor.

Firstly, a total of 14 studies [4, 6–9, 11, 20, 22–26, 29, 31] involving 2323 patients were included to assess for 2-year PFS among eight treatment regimens. Figure 3A depicted the evidence network diagram of these regimens, which comprised R-CHOP, R-DA-EPOCH, R-CHOP+BTKi (ibrutinib and zanubrutinib), R-CHOP + Len, R-CHOP + Ven, R-CHOP + Tuc, R-CHOP + Bor, and R-CHP+Pola. Pairwise comparisons of 2-year PFS across all treatment strategies were presented in a league table (Fig. 3B). SUCRA, PbBT, and PoRa for each regimen were detailed in Fig. 3C, suggested that R-CHOP+BTKi had the highest likelihood of being among the better-performing regimens (SUCRA 0.79) for improving 2-year PFS, followed by R-CHP+Pola (SUCRA 0.68) and R-CHOP + Tuc (SUCRA 0.66). Compared to R-CHOP, R-CHOP+BTKi (OR 2.07, 95% CrI 1.24–3.70) and R-CHOP + Tuc (OR 1.75, 95% CrI 1.06–3.02) showed significant superiority in 2-year PFS (Fig. 3D). However, no substantial differences in 2-year PFS were observed among various R-CHOP + X regimens.

Next, 11 studies [4, 6–8, 22–25, 29, 31, 32] involving 1403 patients were analyzed to evaluate 3-year OS across six treatment regimens: R-CHOP, R-DA-EPOCH, R-CHOP+BTKi (ibrutinib and zanubrutinib), R-CHOP + Len, R-CHOP + Ven, and R-CHOP + Tuc (Fig. 4A). However, among R-CHOP + X regimens, only R-CHOP+ BTKi significantly outperformed R-CHOP for 3-year OS (OR 1.76, 95% CrI 1.07–2.93) (Fig. 4D).

Regarding the CR rate, 10 trials [4, 7, 8, 11, 23, 24, 27, 29, 31, 32] involving 1782 participants assessed six interventions: R-CHOP, R-DA-EPOCH, R-CHOP+BTKi (ibrutinib and zanubrutinib), R-CHOP + Len, R-CHOP + Ven, and R-CHOP + Tuc. Overall, global consistency was confirmed for these three outcomes, as the consistency and inconsistency models exhibited a comparable fit (ΔDIC < 5). Low heterogeneity was found among those pairwise comparisons of different regimens (Supplemental Fig. 7A-C). The funnel plots and the results of Begg’s publication bias test for these outcomes, presenting in Supplemental Fig. 7D-F and Supplemental Table 3, suggested no significant publication bias.

Meta-regression analysis

Although no significant publication bias and global consistency were found in pairwise and network meta-analysis, studies differ substantially in patient demographics including median age, gender, IPI score, Ann Arbor stages, cell-of-origin subtype, and treatment modalities. To examine the potential impact of heterogeneity across studies on the outcomes, we conducted meta-regression analyses using a mixed-effects model. Univariate meta-regression identified R-CHOP + X as the sole significant moderators for 2-year PFS and CR rate. For 3-year OS, univariate and multivariate analysis revealed that Ann Arbor stages III/IV and R-CHOP + X regime were both significantly correlated with OS outcomes (Table 2). These results indicated the impact of treatment regimen (R-CHOP + X) on CR, PFS, and OS remains robust despite baseline imbalances that could potentially confound comparison.

Discussion

Over the past decade, numerous studies have investigated the impact of various treatment strategies on the outcomes of DEL, yet results remain inconclusive, and the optimal approach is still debated [4, 10, 11, 26, 27]. In this context, we systematically evaluated the efficacy and safety of incorporating novel targeted agents into the R-CHOP regimen as a frontline therapy for DEL patients, yielding several noteworthy observations.

In contrast to prior meta-analyses that mainly assessed conventional chemotherapy (e.g., R-DA-EPOCH vs. R-CHOP) in DEL [8], this was the first study that specifically evaluated frontline R-CHOP augmented with novel agents and compares multiple targeted strategies using single-arm, pairwise, and Bayesian network meta-analyses. By updating the search through May 31, 2025, we incorporate recently published trials and provide an updated efficacy–safety synthesis for DEL. Importantly, the baseline clinical features of the studies included in our updated synthesis were broadly comparable to those reported in earlier meta-analysis about DEL [8], supporting the interpretability and clinical relevance of our updated estimates. To reduce design-related bias, we analyzed single-arm estimates separately from comparative syntheses. Comparative conclusions were derived primarily from pairwise and anchored network analyses versus R-CHOP. We assessed the risk bias of included studies with Cochrane ROB (RCTs) and MINORS (non-RCTs), as well as explored key baseline effect modifiers via meta-regression. Given evidence supporting PFS at 24 months (PFS24) as a surrogate for subsequent survival in first-line DLBCL [36–38], 2-year PFS was used as the primary endpoint for meta-analysis.

Historically, standard regimens such as R-CHOP and intensified chemotherapy approaches like R-DA-EPOCH have yielded limited benefits for DEL patients [6, 8, 20, 24]. Our findings from single-arm and pairwise meta-analyses highlighted incorporating novel agents into the R-CHOP backbone was associated with improved outcomes compared with R-CHOP. Network meta-analysis revealed that R-CHOP+BTKi tended to rank highest for 2-year PFS and a relatively high rank probability for 3-year OS. Notably, among R-CHOP + X combinations, only the BTKi-containing regimen was significantly superior to R-CHOP in both 2-year PFS and 3-year OS. Mechanistically, DEL was predominantly observed in non-germinal center B-cell (non-GCB) or activated B-cell (ABC) subtype of DLBCL [3, 4, 32], and MCD-like genetic features associated with MYD88L265P and CD79B mutations have been reported to be linked to BCL2/MYC double expression [39]. As co-expression of MYC/BCL2 in ABC DLBCL as well as MCD-like subtype of DLBCL is frequently associated with aberrant activation of B-cell receptor (BCR) [40], the BCR signaling pathway may be more active in DEL [41]. Therefore, targeting BCR pathway with BTK inhibitors may plausibly enhance disease control in DEL patients.

We also observed that the divergent rankings between CR and PFS among different regimens: BTKi-based regimens show superior PFS but “modest CR rates,” while HDAC inhibitors show high CR but moderate PFS. Statistically speaking, CR reflects depth of treatment response but is an imperfect surrogate for durability, while PFS is sensitive to sustained disease control and is more closely associated with long-term outcomes in DLBCL [36–38]. One possible explanation is that continuous BCR pathway suppression with BTKi may maintain durable disease control and inhibit secondary resistance in DEL patients even without deep early responses. Conversely, HDAC inhibitors yielded higher initial response rates via epigenetic chemosensitization, but subsequent relapse may occur through alternative escape mechanisms, such as compensatory tumor microenvironment adaptations [42]. In addition, trial-level factors (response assessment timing/criteria, baseline risk, follow-up duration) may also contribute this pattern. Importantly, the results of network meta-analysis for BTKi should be interpreted cautiously at class-level; drug-specific differences in selectivity and toxicity between first and second class of BTKi may contribute to heterogeneity and cannot be excluded. We believe these observation and hypotheses warrant further investigation in future research, ideally with regimen-specific and drug-specific reporting.

Another relevant observation relates to safety. In the single-arm meta-analysis, we did not observe a clear signal of increased grade 3–4 hematologic toxicities or infections with the addition of novel agents to R-CHOP compared with R-CHOP alone. However, the toxicity estimates were largely derived from indirect comparisons with substantial heterogeneity, and the reporting of non-hematologic adverse events (e.g., cardiovascular issues, hepatotoxicity, neuropathy) was unavailable across studies. Therefore, definitive conclusions on tolerability must await randomized trials with standardized safety reporting.

Translation of our findings into clinical practice may be influenced by regional disparities in regulatory approval, availability, and affordability of novel agents. Although BTKi as well as other agents such as Pola, lenalidomide, venetoclax, and tucidinostat have been approved for B-cell malignancies, their use as first-line treatment for DEL remains off-label in many countries, which may limit access to novel R-CHOP-based combination regimens, particularly in resource-limited settings. Conducting cost-effectiveness analyses to compare the economic value of R-CHOP + X vs. R-CHOP will serve as a critical basis for evaluating their real-world application. Furthermore, ongoing RCTs will provide essential evidence for regulatory approvals and reimbursement decisions.

Despite these strengths, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the overall sample size was modest, and some regimens were supported by only a single trial, warranting cautious interpretation until larger confirmatory studies become available. Second, the single-arm meta-analysis showed considerable heterogeneity, likely driven by inclusion of small retrospective cohorts, baseline imbalances (e.g., IPI score and Ann Arbor stage), variations in treatment delivery (including dosing schedules and different BTKi molecules), and difference in follow-up. Accordingly, random-effects models and 95% prediction intervals were used to reflect between-study dispersion; such heterogeneity may widen the expected range of real-world outcomes and supports prioritizing comparative evidence when available. Third, publication bias was detected for pooled CR rate, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia in the single-arm meta-analysis, which may have overestimated response and underestimated toxicity. Fourthly, although our comparative conclusions relied primarily on pairwise and network analyses and consistently favored R-CHOP + X versus R-CHOP, most active-to-active contrasts in the network were indirect via the R-CHOP anchor, therefore, transitivity cannot be fully verified, and uncertainty (wider credible intervals) may persist; thus, SUCRA-based rankings should be regarded as supportive rather than definitive, with emphasis placed on statistically significant contrasts versus R-CHOP. Lastly, limited data precluded evaluation of the efficacy of R-CHOP + X in specific DLBCL genetic subgroups (e.g., MCD) as well as investigation of the impact of other key clinicopathologic features (geographic region, different BTKi agents, and IHC cutoffs for MYC/BCL2, etc.) on the outcomes. Accordingly, head-to-head RCTs are needed, prioritizing R-CHOP+BTKi versus R-CHP+Pola, versus R-CHOP + Len, and versus R-CHOP + Tuc, ideally in multi-arm designs with standardized DEL definitions, response criteria, and molecular stratification to refine efficacy and safety estimates across DEL populations.

Conclusion

Our meta-analysis suggests that R-CHOP + X regimens, particularly BTKi-based combinations, may be associated with improved PFS and OS compared with R-CHOP alone. No consistent signal of increased hematologic toxicity or infections was observed, but safety comparisons remain limited by cross-trial heterogeneity and incomplete reporting. In the absence of multi-arm RCTs encompassing all common regimens, these findings may help inform frontline treatment selection for DEL, while underscoring the need for prospective head-to-head RCTs with standardized safety assessment.

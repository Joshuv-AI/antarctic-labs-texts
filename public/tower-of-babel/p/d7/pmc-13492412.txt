A Clinically Applicable Conversion Formula Between NT-proBNP and BNP in Light-Chain Amyloidosis
Authors: Eli Muchtar, Allan S Jaffe, Andrew Staron, Vaishali Sanchorawala, Heather Landau, Oren Pasvolsky, Rajshekhar Chakraborty, Suzanne Lentzsch, Morie A Gertz, Angela Dispenzieri
Published: 2026

Cardiac involvement is the principal determinant of prognosis in light-chain (AL) amyloidosis. B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) and N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) are central for diagnosis, risk stratification, and response assessment.1,2 However, the use of BNP or NT-proBNP varies across institutions and clinical trials, complicating application and comparison of staging systems. Prior studies in the general heart failure populations have proposed conversion formulas between NT-proBNP and BNP,3,4 demonstrating variable performance, especially at the higher-value ranges and in the presence of renal dysfunction. The unique pathophysiology and frequent renal dysfunction in AL amyloidosis underscore the need for a disease-specific conversion tool.

We conducted a retrospective, multicenter study including AL patients who underwent autologous stem cell transplantation at five tertiary centers between 2003 and 2020. The study was approved by the institutional review boards of all participating institutions and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Patients were included if paired BNP and NT-proBNP measurements were obtained from the same blood draw. A total of 606 paired measurements from 353 patients were analyzed.

We applied logarithmic transformation to develop a conversion formula between BNP and NT-proBNP. A linear model was applied to the natural logarithms of BNP and NT-proBNP to derive the conversion formula. To evaluate whether renal function modifies the relationship between biomarkers, interaction analyses between log(NT-proBNP) and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) were performed. Additional covariates (age, sex) were similarly assessed. Regression-derived estimates were back-transformed to the original units by exponentiating the resulting value to facilitate clinical interpretation. Clinical applicability was evaluated by examining concordance of cardiac staging assignments using established NT-proBNP-BNP thresholds and corresponding BNP values derived from the model. Statistical analyses were performed using JMP statistical software (Version 19.0.0, JMP Statistical Discovery LLC).

The median age was 59 years (Q1-Q3: 52-65), and 37.6% were female. Cardiac and renal involvement were documented in 51.8% and 72.0% of patients, respectively. Across 606 paired BNP and NT-proBNP measurements, the median NT-proBNP concentration was 605 pg/mL (Q1-Q3, 173-1876), while the median BNP concentration was 130 pg/mL (Q1-Q3, 50-283). Twenty paired measurements (3.3%) had an NT-proBNP value exceeding 8500 pg/mL. The median eGFR was 70 mL/min/1.73 m2 (Q1-Q3, 55-88).

A strong linear association was observed between log-transformed BNP and NT-proBNP values (R2 = 0.815). The derived conversion formula was: [Log BNP = 0.3142036 + 0.7014077∗Log NT-proBNP]. Calculated BNP values were subsequently back-transformed from logarithmic to absolute concentrations (Table 1). Established NT-proBNP cutoffs of 332, 1800, and 8500 pg/mL corresponded to BNP values of 80, 263, and 781 pg/mL, respectively.

Subgroup analyses demonstrated consistent performance across sex, age, and body mass index (R2 ∼0.81-0.83). The interaction between NT-proBNP and eGFR was not statistically significant (P = 0.14), indicating insufficient statistical evidence to claim that renal function modifies the relationship between biomarkers. Adding age, sex, and their interactions to the multivariable model did not improve performance (P = 0.80 for age interaction; P = 0.49 for sex interaction). Sensitivity analysis restricted to patients with eGFR ≥50 mL/min/1.73 m2 (n = 480 measurements) yielded a similar log-linear relationship (Log BNP = 0.3303262 + 0.706791∗Log NT-proBNP; R2 = 0.823) and comparable threshold estimates.

Cardiac staging based on converted values demonstrated low discordance compared with NT-proBNP-based staging, ranging from 9% to 16% depending on the staging system. These findings were comparable to the discordance observed when applying established BNP-based thresholds directly.

We developed a disease-specific log-linear conversion formula between BNP and NT-proBNP that preserves concordance across clinically relevant staging thresholds. This approach facilitates harmonization of natriuretic peptide interpretation across institutions and clinical settings, where inconsistent assay use complicates longitudinal assessment and comparison of staging systems. Notably, the BNP value of 80 pg/mL corresponding to NT-proBNP 332 pg/mL closely approximated the established BNP threshold of 81 pg/mL4, supporting external validity of the model at lower biomarker concentrations. At higher values, divergence becomes more apparent, likely reflecting biological variability and limited representation of extreme values.

Renal function is a recognized modifier of natriuretic peptide concentrations. Although both peptides are strongly associated with eGFR, the underlying mechanistic basis of this relationship remains incompletely understood. To address this, we evaluated for a potential interaction between NT-proBNP and eGFR across the full cohort and found no significant modifying effect, supporting stability of the relationship across renal function ranges.

Clinically, this conversion supports harmonizing biomarker interpretation across studies and longitudinal tracking when different assays are used. To facilitate clinical use, the conversion formula derived in this study is available as an online calculator.5 It should be applied cautiously at extreme biomarker values, above NT-proBNP 5000 to 8500 pg/mL, because relatively few paired measurements were available in this range. Study limitations include its retrospective design, inclusion of a fitter AL population with less-advanced cardiac disease, and multiple assays used for BNP measurements (Biosite, Abbott ARCHITECT, Siemens, Beckman Coulter). The NT-proBNP assay used in this study was the Roche platform, and derived conversion may not be directly applicable to newer NT-proBNP assays. The absence of an external validation cohort warrants future study, particularly in nonautologous stem cell transplantation populations that encompasses a wider spectrum of disease severity and renal function.

We present a clinically-applicable, disease-specific conversion model between BNP and NT-proBNP in AL amyloidosis that maintains cardiac staging concordance across commonly used thresholds. This approach offers a practical tool for harmonizing natriuretic peptide interpretation across clinical care and research contexts, while acknowledging reduced precision at extreme biomarker values and the need for external validation.

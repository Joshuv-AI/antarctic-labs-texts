RART-08 TITLE: BILATERAL THALAMIC GLIOMA IN A PAEDIATRIC PATIENT: A RARE CASE HIGHLIGHTING DIAGNOSTIC AND THERAPEUTIC CHALLENGES
Authors: Lilian Garapo, Shungu Ushewokunze, Simbarashe Sanyamandwe, Killon Madondoro, Kazadi Kalangu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionBilateral thalamic gliomas (BTGs) represent an extremely rare subset of central nervous system tumors. Primary thalamic gliomas account for only 1–1.5% of all intracranial neoplasms, with bilateral involvement being exceptionally uncommon. By 2010, fewer than 50 cases had been reported in the literature, only 15 of which occurred in pediatric patients. BTGs are associated with poor prognosis regardless of histological grade, and their deep-seated, bilateral nature significantly limits surgical options.Case PresentationWe present the case of a 9-year-old female referred with a two-week history of severe headaches, vomiting, and generalized body weakness on a background history of chronic headaches for 8 months. Patient had MRI imaging done at the referring hospital which showed deep seated bilateral thalamic masses associated with active hydrocephalus. On examination, the patient appeared ill, was apyrexial, and had a Glasgow Coma Score of 11/15 (E4, M5, V2). Neurological assessment revealed right hemiplegia with hypertonia and hyperreflexia, though cranial nerve examination was unremarkable. MRI imaging identified bilateral thalamic masses. MR spectroscopy (MRS) done at our centre and demonstrated elevated choline peaks and reduced N-acetylaspartate (NAA) and creatine (Cr) levels — findings characteristic of high-grade glioma. Given the radiological features, clinical course, and financial constraints limiting tissue diagnosis, a diagnosis of bilateral thalamic glioma was made. Neurosurgical intervention focused on CSF diversion. Bilateral VP shunts were successfully placed via the Kocher’s point.ConclusionThis case highlights the rarity, diagnostic and therapeutic challenges posed by bilateral thalamic gliomas, especially in resource-limited settings. Surgical resection remains unfeasible due to diffuse thalamic involvement, and treatment options are largely palliative. Reporting such rare cases adds valuable data to the limited literature and underscores the need for collaborative efforts to develop evidence-based management guidelines.

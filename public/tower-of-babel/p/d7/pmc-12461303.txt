A Multisource Transformer-Guided Graph Representation Learning Framework for circRNA-Disease Association Prediction
Authors: Si-Zhe Liang, Lei Wang, Zhu-Hong You, Chang-Qing Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Circular RNAs (circRNAs) possess structural stability and tissue-specific expression patterns, making them potential disease biomarkers. Exploring the associations between circRNAs and diseases is crucial for early diagnosis and targeted treatment. However, due to the complexity of biological relationships and the presence of multisource heterogeneous data, traditional prediction methods often face challenges such as insufficient information integration, limited semantic expression, and information loss. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a computational model, MTGCDA, based on a multisource heterogeneous graph transformer for high-accuracy prediction of circRNA-disease associations. Specifically, MTGCDA first integrates multisource biological information about circRNAs and diseases, constructing a heterogeneous graph containing multiple node types and multiple edge relationships. Representation learning of the graph structure is performed using a heterogeneous graph neural network, fully exploiting the latent semantic features of different node types. The model then fuses the embeddings of circRNA and disease nodes in a multilayer heterogeneous graph convolutional network to construct a joint feature representation, which is then fed into a CatBoost classifier to accurately score circRNA-disease associations. Experimental results on the CircR2Disease data set demonstrate that the MTGCDA model achieves an AUC of 0.9756, significantly outperforming several existing methods. At the same time, among the 20 selected circRNA-disease association pairs, 17 pairs were verified by literature reports, further demonstrating the high accuracy of the model in predicting potential associations and its biological practicality.

Introduction

Circular RNA (circRNA) is a covalently closed RNA molecule formed by reverse splicing and is abundant in eukaryotes. ,, In multicellular organisms, circRNA expression is highly tissue-specific, and its expression level is to some extent not regulated by linear host genes, and accumulates in neural tissues with age. ,, Exon-derived circRNAs are produced by reverse splicing, in which the 5′ splice donor binds to the upstream 3′ splice site to form a 3′-5′ phosphodiester bond, thereby generating a circular RNA molecule. ,,, Due to its unique closed-loop structure, circRNA can resist degradation by RNA exonucleases and exhibit a long half-life in cells, which makes it potentially important in regulating cellular homeostasis. As a class of functionally diverse noncoding RNAs, circRNAs not only participate in gene expression regulation, transcriptional regulation, protein translation regulation, and miRNA function regulation, , but may also affect cell fate decisions by regulating key signaling pathways. For example, circRNAs can act as miRNA sponges to regulate target gene expression, thereby indirectly affecting cell proliferation, apoptosis, differentiation, and metabolic processes, suggesting that they have important regulatory functions under physiological and pathological conditions. , The tissue-specific and developmental stage-specific expression of circRNAs further suggests that they may play a key role in cell type-specific biological processes. In addition, the dynamic expression patterns of circRNAs vary significantly under different physiological and pathological conditions, suggesting that they may be involved in the occurrence and development of diseases. , In disease research, the functional role of circRNAs is particularly prominent. A large number of studies have shown that circRNAs play an important role in the occurrence, development, metastasis, and drug resistance of cancer. ,, For example, ciRS-7 regulates the expression of multiple cancer-related genes by binding to miR-7, promoting the proliferation and invasion of tumor cells. , Some circRNAs, such as circPVT1, are highly expressed in multiple cancers and are closely related to patient prognosis, ,, showing their application value as potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers. The role of circRNA in neurodegenerative diseases has also been gradually revealed. Studies have shown that circRNA is involved in the pathological process of neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer’s disease, , Parkinson’s disease, and Huntington’s disease. In summary, circRNA is not only an important biological regulatory molecule, but its stability, tissue specificity, and regulatory function in disease make it an important target for studying disease pathogenesis, finding potential biomarkers, and developing precision treatment strategies. As research deepens, understanding the biological functions of circRNA and its role in disease will further reveal its regulatory network at the molecular level and its potential clinical application value.

With the continuous deepening of circRNA research, a large amount of circRNA-related disease data has been continuously accumulated. ,,, These data not only provide valuable resources for exploring the mechanism of action of circRNA in the occurrence and development of diseases, but also lay a solid foundation for the prediction of circRNA-disease association (CDA) based on computational methods. ,,, With the help of these rich data, researchers can build more accurate and efficient computational models to explore potential circRNA-disease relationships, thereby promoting the application of circRNA in disease diagnosis, prognosis assessment and targeted therapy. , At present, the methods for exploring circRNA-disease associations mainly include methods based on network analysis, matrix decomposition, and a combination of machine learning and deep learning.

These methods integrate a variety of biological information to accurately predict potential circRNA-disease associations, thereby promoting in-depth research and application of circRNA in the biomedical field. For example, in the network analysis-based method, Lei et al. proposed a new path weighting calculation strategy. This method first calculates the similarity scores between disease-related features and circRNA-related features, then constructs multiple heterogeneous networks based on these similarities and connects the paths with corresponding relationships in the network. Finally, by calculating the path weighting score between circRNA and disease, the potential association between them is evaluated. Sun et al. proposed an innovative computational framework MVGNCDA, which combines multiview, graph convolutional network (GCN) and biased random walk-based network embedding to determine whether there is an association between the corresponding circRNA and the disease. In terms of matrix decomposition, Lu and Li introduced a framework based on non-negative matrix decomposition to calculate the similarity scores between circRNA and disease, reconstruct the network by filling in missing links based on neighboring circular RNA and disease interactions, and finally identify potential associations. Wei and Liu proposed the iCircDA-MF calculation method, which uses the calculated similarity between disease and circRNA to obtain potential associations and corrects false negative associations through neighborhood interaction profiles. Finally, matrix decomposition is performed on the updated circRNA-disease interaction profile to predict circRNA-disease associations. In terms of machine learning and deep learning, Wang et al. proposed a machine learning framework called MLCDA for circRNA-disease association prediction. This framework effectively integrates circRNA sequences and disease-related information as well as its inherent heterogeneous information from multiple sources, successfully capturing the potential association between circRNA and disease. Wang et al. proposed a multisource similarity information-based prediction circRNA-disease association integrated model (LMGATCDA), which first extracts the display features and graph structure features of diseases and circRNAs, fuses them, and further extracts implicit features through graph sampling aggregation (GraphSAGE) and multihop attention graph neural network (MAGNA), and finally obtains the prediction score of circRNA and disease relationship pairs through a fully connected layer. Although the above methods perform well, they fail to fully explore the potential feature relationships between multiple views and ignore the different weights of each view in association prediction.

In order to address the challenges of circRNA-disease association prediction and improve the prediction performance, we proposed a new computational method, MTGCDA. This method is based on a multilayer heterogeneous graph encoder with a Transformer structure, which aims to capture the potential association between circRNA and disease. In the model design, we first aggregate multisource information into circRNA and disease nodes to construct an initial feature representation, and further aggregate and update the features through a multilayer heterogeneous graph encoder. Subsequently, the circRNA and disease embedding features extracted from each layer are spliced to maximize information retention. In the decoding stage, we use the spliced features as the input of the CatBoost classifier for the association prediction task. To verify the effectiveness of the model, we conducted 5-fold cross-validation (5-CV) on the benchmark data set and conducted a comprehensive comparison with 8 advanced methods. The experimental results show that the MTGCDA model shows significant advantages in accuracy and robustness, proving its effectiveness in the circRNA-disease association prediction task. Methods

Data Set

As people’s research on the association between circRNA and diseases deepens, many public databases have been constructed. We constructed four circRNA-disease association data sets from many databases, namely CircR2Disease, , CircAtlas v2.0, Circ2Disease , and CircRNADisease. , The details of the four data sets after we extracted and processed are shown in Table . These data sets cover rich circRNA and disease information and have been strictly screened to ensure the reliability of the data. We selected the CDA data provided by the gold standard data set CircR2Disease for model evaluation. This data set contains circRNA-disease associations verified by biological experiments. After deredundancy and preprocessing, 607 groups of circRNA-disease associations were finally screened as positive samples, involving 100 diseases and 561 circRNAs.

Similarity Feature Extraction Based on circRNA and Disease

In our experiments, the similarities constructed using circRNA data include circRNA functional similarity, circRNA sequence similarity, and Gaussian kernel similarity. The similarities constructed using disease data include Gaussian kernel similarity and disease semantic similarity.

In order to measure the semantic similarity between diseases, we obtained the MeSH vocabulary of disease ontology markers from the MeSH disease database and constructed a directed acyclic graph (DAG) by obtaining disease semantic information to reveal the association between different diseases. When constructing a DAG for disease d, the set of diseases related to it is represented by N d , and the semantic relationship between different diseases is represented by edge E d . Then disease d can be represented as DAG d = (d,N d ,E d ) in DAG. GIP Nuclear Similarity Feature Extraction of Diseases and circRNAs

Gaussian integral projection (GIP) kernel similarity is a similarity calculation method based on known interaction information. Based on existing research, in this study, we use GIP kernel similarity to measure the similarity between circRNA and disease. We construct disease similarity models based on adjacency matrices and circRNA models based on adjacency matrices, respectively. The adjacency matrices are obtained from known association relationships. When constructing the interaction representation between diseases, an V(d(i)) is used to represent it. For the selected circRNA, if it has a known association with a certain disease d(i), the corresponding binary vector position is set to 1, otherwise the corresponding position is set to 0. Therefore, according to the above settings, we can construct the disease similarity based on the GIP core and the circRNA similarity based on the GIP core, respectively. In the above formula, θ c and θd are the influence range parameters of the related GIP core, m represents the number of diseases, and n represents the number of circRNAs. Therefore, we can construct the GIP core similarity matrix based on circRNA and disease, respectively.

Constructing circRNA Sequence Similarity Based on PCC

In order to quantify the similarity between different circRNAs, the PCC method was used to measure the sequence similarity between circRNAs. First, we collected and sorted circRNA sequence data from relevant databases and used the k-mers method for numerical processing. In the experiment, according to the best practices of existing studies, k was set to 3. Since the circRNA sequence consists of four bases, we converted the circRNA sequence into a 64-dimensional numerical matrix for subsequent calculations. Subsequently, the Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) algorithm was used to calculate the similarity between circRNA sequences. Among them, the PCC similarity (CPS) of circRNA is a statistical indicator used to measure the linear correlation between two circRNA sequences u and v, including direction and strength, and its value range is [−1,1]. The similarity calculation formula is as follows: 10 CPS(u,v)=∑i=1nui−u̅∑i=1nvi−v̅∑i=1n(ui−u̅)2∑i=1n(vi−v̅)2

Where n is the number of circRNAs, and the two means u̅=1n∑i=1nui and v̅=1n∑i=1nvi are used to measure the central tendency of circRNA sequence characteristics.

Multisource Heterogeneous Similarity Information Fusion

In order to enhance the information richness of similarity, we fuse multiple similarity information. Specifically, after constructing different similarity matrices, we use a multisimilarity fusion strategy to integrate the similarity information on circRNA and diseases. This method can effectively reduce the limitations of a single feature, make full use of the features of different data sources, and improve the comprehensiveness of similarity measurement.

In terms of circRNAs, their similarity is represented by C FUS . Assuming that there is functional similarity between circRNA c(i) and c(j), this information is integrated into the circRNA similarity matrix. If no functional similarity is detected between the two, the GIP nuclear similarity (GIPK) of the circRNA is used to supplement and incorporate it into the CFUS calculation, as follows: 11 CFUS(c(i),c(j))={CFS(c(i),c(j))ifc(i)andc(j)existCGIP(c(i),c(j))otherwise

Similarly, we adopt the same fusion strategy when constructing the disease similarity information representation. Let the disease similarity be D FUS . If there is semantic similarity between diseases d(i) and d(j), their semantic similarity information is fused first; if there is no semantic similarity between the two, the GIPK similarity of the disease is used to supplement it. The calculation method is as follows: 12 DFUS(d(i),d(j))={D(d(i),d(j))ifc(i)andc(j)existDGIP(d(i),d(j))otherwise

Finally, we used the multisimilarity information fusion method to construct the circRNA fusion similarity network (C FUS ) and disease fusion similarity network (D FUS ).

Construction of Multilayer Heterogeneous Graph Transformer

Traditional heterogeneous graph models are difficult to characterize complex dynamic relationships, while the Transformer-based multilayer heterogeneous graph can achieve adaptive modeling and accurately capture the deep associations of heterogeneous data through relationship-aware self-attention and relationship-specific parametrization, thereby improving expression and generalization capabilities. Therefore, this multilayer heterogeneous graph is used for node representation learning to accurately capture the complex association characteristics and deep interaction patterns between circRNA and diseases. Here, we first need to calculate the heterogeneous mutual attention. Since the target node circRNA and the source node disease are connected through a heterogeneous graph, in order to effectively transfer information, it is necessary to calculate the attention weight between the target node circRNA and its adjacent disease node. This weight reflects the role of the disease node in updating the circRNA node representation during the information aggregation process. Association Prediction

The circRNAs extracted from the multilayer heterogeneous encoders were spliced with disease signatures to construct a comprehensive representation of circRNA-disease associations as shown below: 24 Zij=[Fc(i),Fd(j)]

Among them,Fc (i)­represents the vector representation of the i–th circRNA in feature F c , and Fd (j) represents the vector representation of the j–th disease in feature F d . CatBoost is an efficient classification method based on gradient boosting. By constructing a symmetric decision tree and adopting an ordered Boosting strategy, it effectively reduces the gradient calculation bias and improves the stability of the model. In this study, we used CatBoost as a classifier for circRNA and disease embedded feature fusion descriptors. It can not only retain as much encoding information as possible to improve the accuracy and robustness of prediction, but also efficiently process large-scale circRNA and disease data, optimize computing performance, and combine gradient boosting with regularization strategies to effectively reduce the risk of overfitting. Experimental results show that this method shows good generalization ability on new data, further improving the robustness and reliability of the model.

Results and Discussion

Evaluation Indicators

In order to measure the performance of the proposed method MTGCDA in prediction tasks to a certain extent, we introduced multiple evaluation indicators to cover the responsiveness of the model at different decision-making levels. Specifically, they include: Accuracy (ACC), Precision, Recall, F1-score, and Specificity. The specific calculation is as follows: Accuracy=TP+TNTP+TN+FP+FN Precision=TPTP+FP Recall=TPTP+FN F1=2·Precision·RecallPrecision+Recall Specificity=TNTN+FP

Among them, TP means the predicted relationship is positive and its true label is also positive, TN means the predicted relationship is negative and its true label is also negative, FP means the number of negative samples incorrectly predicted as positive by the model, and TN means the number of positive samples incorrectly predicted as negative by the model.

Empirical Analysis of MTGCDA through 5-CV

In order to systematically evaluate the stability and generalization performance of the proposed method, we used 5-CV for training and testing in all experiments. In the experiment, we evenly divided the complete sample set into five subsets, selected one of the subsets as the test set in each iteration, and the remaining four groups were used for model training. This cycle was repeated five times to ensure that each subset could participate in one evaluation as test data. The final experimental results were measured by the average of the five evaluation results to minimize the interference of chance on model performance. In the sample construction process, considering that the number of verified associations between circRNA and disease is far less than that of unknown association pairs, if all unobserved associations are directly equated as negative samples, a large number of potential true positive samples may be introduced, resulting in label errors and interference with model training. Therefore, in each cross-validation partition, we regarded the known circRNA-disease pairs in the database as positive samples, and randomly selected an equal number of pairs from unknown associations as negative samples. This strategy not only balances the ratio of positive and negative samples and avoids the bias of the model due to class imbalance, but also effectively reduces noise interference and improves the quality and effectiveness of training data. Through such a sample construction and evaluation mechanism, not only the robustness of the model in the training stage is enhanced, but also its generalization ability on unknown data is improved, laying a solid foundation for subsequent association prediction tasks.

During the model training process, we reasonably configured various key parameters to ensure the stability of the model performance and the efficiency of the training process. All experiments were completed under the same hardware environment. We set the initial learning rate to 0.001 and the weight decay coefficient to 1 × 10–5 to suppress the occurrence of overfitting. The training round epoch = 600, the hidden layer dimension of the model was set to 128, and the activation function used ReLU to enhance the nonlinear expression ability of the model. During model construction, we explicitly set key CatBoost hyperparameters, including learning_rate to 0.01, depth to 6, and iterations to 100. The above parameters were obtained through multiple groups of pre-experimental adjustments to balance accuracy and training efficiency. Evaluation of the Impact of Multiview Integration on Model Performance

In order to further verify the effectiveness of various views in this model and explore the impact of different view combinations on prediction performance, we designed a series of combination experiments. The main features involved in the experiment include circRNA functional similarity (C_fun), sequence similarity (C_Se), GIP nuclear similarity (C_GIP and D_GIP) and semantic similarity of diseases (D_SE). During the experiment, different features were combined in multiple ways, and the prediction ability of the combined model of different features was evaluated by inputting different combinations of these views into the model. The experimental results are shown in Figure .

Performance comparison of multiview information fusion strategies in MTGCDA model.

From the experimental results, we can see that the combination C_fun + C_GIP + C_Seq + D_GIP + D_SE achieved the best results, with AUC of 0.9756, AUPR of 0.9599, F1 score of 95.13%, ACC of 94.98%, Recall of 98.03%, Specificity of 91.94%, and Precision of 92.96%. This combination performed best in indicators such as AUC, AUPR, F1, and ACC, indicating that the various features in the combination have a synergistic effect in improving the predictive performance of the model and can effectively enhance the model’s ability to predict the association between circRNA and disease.

In contrast, although the combinations C_fun + C_GIP + D_GIP + D_SE and C_fun + C_Seq + D_GIP + D_SE also performed well in some indicators, they failed to surpass the optimal combination overall. Specifically, although the AUC and AUPR of the combination of C_fun + C_GIP + D_GIP + D_SE also reached a high level (0.9619 and 0.9478 respectively), the F1 score and ACC dropped slightly, indicating that the combination has certain deficiencies in some evaluation dimensions. This shows that the advantages of a single feature may be difficult to fully exert in all dimensions, and the complementarity of multiple feature combinations is crucial to improving performance. We further explored the complementarity of features and their impact on enhancing the generalization ability of the model.

The Impact of Different Feature Dimensions on Model Performance

In order to further evaluate the impact of feature dimensions on model prediction performance, we designed experiments under different dimension settings to compare and analyze the performance of the model on multiple indicators. In this experiment, while keeping other parameters unchanged, the feature dimensions were set to 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, and 512 respectively, and 5-fold cross validation was used to obtain the average performance index. The experimental results are shown in Figure .

Impact of feature dimension on the prediction performance of MTGCDA model.

Experimental results show that model performance steadily improves with increasing feature dimensionality at low dimensionality. When the feature dimension increases from 16 to 128, the model shows significant improvements across all metrics, including AUC (from 0.9446 to 0.9756), AUPR (from 0.9290 to 0.9599), and F1-score (F1-value) and ACC (accuracy) reaching 95.13% and 94.98%, respectively. This suggests that at low dimensionality, the model’s ability to capture the characteristic information associated with circRNAs and diseases is limited, and a moderate increase in feature dimensionality can help more comprehensively characterize potential associations. However, when the dimensionality is further increased to 256 and 512, model performance decreases. At 256 dimensionality, the AUC drops to 0.9364 and the AUPR to 0.9036, both lower than the results with 128 dimensionality. This suggests that excessively high feature dimensionality may introduce redundant information or noise, disrupting the model during training and affecting prediction accuracy and generalization. Overall, the experimental results suggest that a trade-off should be made between information representation ability and redundant noise when selecting feature dimensions to obtain the best prediction performance.

In order to further explore the impact of model structural hyperparameters on performance, this paper designs an ablation experiment on the number of attention heads and network layers in the multihead attention mechanism. Through the control variable method, under the premise of fixing other parameter configurations, the number of attention heads and network layers are systematically adjusted to evaluate their specific impact on model performance, so as to optimize the model structure design and improve its generalization and expression capabilities.

In the experiment of the number of attention heads, we set 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, and 64 heads respectively, and evaluated their performance in multiple indicators such as AUC, AUPR, F1, ACC, Recall, Specificity and Precision. The experimental results are shown in Figure .

The experimental results show that the model performance is optimal when the number of attention heads is 4, at which time AUC and AUPR are 0.9756 and 0.9599 respectively, and the F1 value also reaches 0.9513, showing good stability and accuracy. As the number of attention heads increases, the overall model performance shows a downward trend. The reason is that the multihead mechanism introduces too much redundant attention distribution, which dilutes the effective representation and makes it difficult for the model to focus on key semantic dependency features, which in turn weakens the discrimination ability.

However, after more than 5 layers, the model performance fluctuates and falls back, especially in indicators such as F1 and Precision. This phenomenon may be caused by problems such as gradient disappearance, redundant feature stacking, and overfitting brought by deep networks, which affects the generalization performance of the model on the test set. Conclusions

In recent years, a large number of studies have shown that circRNA plays a key regulatory role in the occurrence and development of diseases. Therefore, building an efficient and accurate prediction model to identify potential circRNA-disease associations is of great significance for disease mechanism research and target discovery. This paper proposes a novel multisource heterogeneous graph joint attention mechanism model MTGCDA to systematically mine potential circRNA-disease associations. MTGCDA integrates a variety of biological feature information, including sequence similarity, functional annotations, semantic hierarchical relationships, and GIP kernel similarity, constructs a multisource heterogeneous graph, and introduces an attention mechanism to dynamically model the importance of feature channels, thereby accurately capturing complex biological relationships.

Experimental results show that MTGCDA performs significantly better than existing mainstream methods on multiple real data sets, and achieves leading results in indicators such as AUC, AUPR, and F1, verifying its effectiveness in feature fusion and representation learning. Parameter sensitivity analysis and ablation experiments further confirm the core value of the multisource heterogeneous structure and attention mechanism in the model. At the same time, the case study section demonstrates the application potential of MTGCDA in actual circRNA-disease association mining, in which several high-scoring prediction pairs have been confirmed in the literature, indicating that this method not only has good generalization ability, but also has strong biological explanatory power.

Although MTGCDA has achieved satisfactory results, the heterogeneous graph constructed by the current model has not yet covered more potential types of biological information, and there is still room for further expansion. Although MTGCDA performs well in integrating multisource features and predicting circRNA-disease associations, the model still has several limitations: First, it relies heavily on the integrity and quality of the input data, and prediction accuracy may be affected when data is missing or noisy. Second, the model has high computational cost and memory consumption when processing large-scale data or high-dimensional features, and it does not fully model the complex nonlinear relationships between features. Future research can further explore meta-pathway design, graph structure expansion, and cross-modal information fusion to enhance the model’s ability to express and explain complex disease mechanisms, providing stronger theoretical support and practical tools for circRNA-related research.

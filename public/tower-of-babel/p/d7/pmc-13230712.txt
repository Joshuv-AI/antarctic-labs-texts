A hybrid decision-support framework for selecting sustainable domestic heating systems in cold climates
Authors: Krishnan Suvitha, Veeramuthu Murugesan, Ramasamy Jaisankar, Samayan Narayanamoorthy, Naif Almakayeel, Hasan Dincer, Serhat Yuksel, Jeonghwan Jeon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study proposes a probabilistic hesitant fuzzy multi-criteria decision-making approach for assessing sustainable heating system alternatives under uncertain situations. The selection of heating systems is a major issue in sustainability in cold climate regions due to its high energy requirements. The proposed approach integrates the Probabilistic Hesitant Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (PHF-AHP) for determining the weights of decision criteria and the Evaluation Based on Distance from Average Solution (EDAS) for ranking alternatives. The probabilistic hesitant fuzzy approach allows decision-makers to reflect uncertainty and hesitation in decision-making under expert judgments by considering various membership values with their respective probabilities. To validate the proposed methodological framework, a case study of five heating system alternatives is presented. The results reveal that the proposed PHF-AHP-EDAS approach is reliable in ranking alternatives under ambiguity and uncertainty in sustainability decision problems.Keywords: Sustainable heating systems, Domestic heating selection, Energy efficiency, Probabilistic hesitant fuzzy sets, Hybrid MCDM, AHP–EDASSubject terms: Climate sciences, Energy science and technology, Engineering, Environmental sciences, Environmental social sciences, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Heating is necessary in the winter to provide a comfortable and safe living environment. Effective heating systems are essential in nations like Canada, Russia, Turkey, and others where temperatures can fall below 0 degrees Celsius. From antiquated fireplaces to contemporary technology, heating systems have undergone substantial change. Different heating systems may be classified according to the energy source, heating unit location, heat transport method, and technique of heat transmission. Systems are categorised as local, central, or district heating systems based on where the heat-generating equipment is located. The majority of heating systems use solid fuels, gaseous fuels, diesel, or electricity. As a result, solar heating systems depend mostly on solar radiation and, occasionally, waste energy. Ranjan and Kanitkar1 assessed the energy thresholds necessary to promote sustainable human growth, providing information on fair access to energy.

Convection, radiation, or the movement of heated air across the space are the three ways that heat is transported. Electric current, water, and air are common heat transporters. Buildings use about 40% of the world’s total power output and 32% of its ultimate energy consumption, according to global energy statistics2. The overwhelming majority of heating systems continue to depend on fossil fuels, which greatly contribute to pollution and emissions that worsen the environment. By presenting energy as a “community currency,” Anto et al.3 offered an innovative perspective and demonstrated its revolutionary influence on the socioeconomic development of recently electrified communities. China, the US, the EU27, India, Russia, and Japan were among the largest emitters of carbon dioxide in 2021. Collectively, it accounted for 62.4% of the world’s GDP, 66.4% of fossil fuel consumption, and 67% of CO emissions from fossil fuels4. In comparison to 2020, the CO emissions of all six nations grew in 2021, with Russia and India exhibiting the largest percentage increases. The importance of renewable energy in accomplishing sustainable development goals has been highlighted by recent research. The intricate relationships between the use of renewable energy and other aspects of sustainable development were studied by Boe et al.5, emphasising the necessity of coordinated policy approaches.

Literature review

Multi-Criteria Decision-Making (MCDM) is a branch of operations research that focusses on getting optimal results in problems with many indicators, competing objectives, and different criteria. In order to prepare for uncertainty and imprecision in the decision-making process, MCDM techniques are frequently combined with fuzzy set theory. P-HFEs, or probabilistic hesitant fuzzy elements, were initially presented in continuous form by6. subsequently, new fundamental operations for P-HFEs were defined using the Frank t-norm and t-conorm operators7. For MCDM issues including probabilistic hesitant fuzzy information, a dominance measure and Best-Worst Method (BWM) within a P-HFE framework were suggested8. A unique MCDM method utilising sophisticated aggregation operations in a q-rung dual hesitant fuzzy environment9. Additionally10, proposed a method for making decisions that uses probabilistic hesitant fuzzy preference relations to rank options more reliably. To ascertain the probability distribution of components in a P-HFE and the likelihood of risk situation, two nonlinear programming models were also included11. Recent advances in multi-criteria decision-making research have increasingly focused on hybrid fuzzy frameworks that can handle uncertainty, ambiguity, and complicated interrelationships among assessment criteria. Specifically, spherical fuzzy and probabilistic fuzzy environments have been extensively used to improve the modelling performance of traditional MCDM techniques. For example, for the purpose to prioritise low-carbon policy options in climate change mitigation planning, recent research has used combined SWARA–WASPAS techniques under spherical fuzzy environment. These models show determining if robust ranking strategies may be used with subjective weighting techniques to enable sustained policy evaluation in unpredictability12. For the assessment of environmental, social, and governance (ESG) performance in high-tech industrial parks, hybrid decision frameworks that combine fuzzy Delphi methodologies with statistical clustering approaches have been created13. These methods enable the discovery of crucial sustainability indicators across intricate corporate structures and offer organised methods for the aggregation of expert information. Sustainability-focused decision frameworks have been explored in the agriculture sector in addition to industrial and policy applications. The significance of rigorous assessment frameworks for ecosystem restoration, sustainable land management, and long-term environmental resilience is highlighted by recent research on regenerative organic agriculture14. These studies demonstrate how multi-criteria decision-support models are becoming increasingly important for assessing sustainability plans in a variety of industries.

The Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) is a structured technique for organizing and analyzing complex decisions. It is a well-established MCDM method that ranks alternatives based on a hierarchical framework of criteria and sub-criteria. AHP enables decision-makers to identify the most important factors influencing a decision and evaluate alternatives based on those factors. Due to its simplicity and practical applicability, AHP has been widely adopted in various research domains15. For instance, Santos et al.16 identified AHP as the most suitable method in their study. AHP is increasingly used in construction management to tackle complex problems and guide strategic decisions15. The fuzzy AHP was employed to identify relevant sustainability issues17, while another utilized it to select the optimal vendor for supplying materials for pre-insulated pipe manufacturing18.

Wolnowska et al. applied AHP to evaluate potential transit routes for oversized freight through the city of Szczecin19. The VIKOR method, developed by Serafim Opricovic20, has also been employed extensively. Ali Ebadi Torkayesh et al.21 evaluated waste-to-energy technologies using the VIKOR approach. In China, distributed renewable energy systems for restaurants were ranked using interval VIKOR, which accommodates data ambiguity through interval values22. VIKOR is particularly beneficial for addressing MCDM problems with incommensurable and conflicting criteria and is known for generating compromise-based optimal solutions23. Ming Chang Ong et al. applied the VIKOR technique to rank alternatives in a study on reusing cleaning agents in rubber glove manufacturing24. Its utility has been widely documented in numerous studies25,26.

The integration of AHP and VIKOR is a popular approach in contemporary research27–29, another study combined AHP and VIKOR in an MCDM framework to elucidate the mathematical underpinnings of selection comparisons30. In recent years, various hybrid MCDM models have been developed by incorporating fuzzy theories to enhance decision accuracy as shown in Table 1. Among these, the Evaluation Based on Distance from Average Solution (EDAS) method, introduced by Keshavarz Ghorabaee et al. in 20158, has gained prominence. Alongside TOPSIS and VIKOR, EDAS is now recognized as one of the most widely used MCDM methods31.

Expert panel and data collection procedure

To mitigate the issue of uncertainty and hesitation while conducting evaluation processes, a probabilistic hesitant fuzzy approach was adopted to collect expert opinions. A group of experts was consulted to perform the evaluation processes. The experts were chosen based on their qualifications, experience, and expertise in the domain of energy systems, renewable technologies, and sustainable assessment. During the data collection process, the experts were asked to evaluate the importance of evaluation criteria and the performance of different alternatives for heating system technologies. The experts were asked to express their opinions using multiple values to represent their uncertainty while conducting the evaluation processes. The experts also assigned probabilities to each value to represent their confidence levels for the evaluation processes. The evaluation processes were represented using probabilistic hesitant fuzzy numbers to develop a rigorous mathematical framework for dealing with uncertain expert opinions.

Aim and motivation of the research

In many practical scenarios of decision-making problems, experts often face challenges in providing an accurate assessment when dealing with multiple criteria evaluation problems. The challenges may be due to a lack of sufficient information, vagueness, or even a lack of experience with specific technologies. Hence, experts may be reluctant to provide precise numerical values for their assessment. In the context of this study, reluctant information is defined as hesitant evaluation results where experts are reluctant to provide a single deterministic value but rather multiple possible evaluation values. Generally, most of the conventional MCDM models assume that experts can provide precise evaluation results for the decision-making problem. However, in many practical scenarios of decision-making problems, vagueness, ambiguity, or even a lack of sufficient knowledge can affect the applicability of conventional deterministic MCDM models. To solve the challenges associated with conventional deterministic MCDM models, fuzzy set theory, hesitant fuzzy sets, or probabilistic hesitant fuzzy sets have been introduced to handle vagueness or even hesitation while making decisions.

Contribution of the research

Recently, developments in fuzzy set theory have led to the concept of PHFSs, which improves the handling of uncertainties in multi-criteria decision problems. Unlike other fuzzy approaches that rely on triangular fuzzy numbers, PHFSs allow decision-makers to present various membership levels with their respective probability measures, which is closer to reality in terms of decision-makers’ hesitation. This paper utilizes the PHF-AHP method in determining the relative weights of decision criteria and the EDAS method in ranking heating system alternatives. The proposed hybrid approach is applicable in decision situations where uncertainty and ambiguity exist, offering a better solution in sustainability assessment compared to other conventional approaches.

Proposed methodology

In the current research, the applicability of the PHF-AHP method and the EDAS approach is evaluated for decision-making under conditions of uncertainty and expert hesitations. The PHF-AHP method offers the opportunity to consolidate various membership values as well as probabilities. This increases the reliability of the weight structures in the evaluation of the criteria. The EDAS method allows for the evaluation of alternatives based on the calculation of the deviations from the average performance. This approach helps to increase the stability of the ranking results without the use of ideal points. The above approaches deal with the ambiguity as well as the incompatibility of the criteria, which makes the evaluation of sustainable heating systems suitable. Algorithm of PHF-based AHP method

The suggested framework, PHFEs are used to represent the judgments of the experts during the pairwise comparison step of the AHP. Case study: identification and evaluation of household heating system alternatives

The study focuses on identifying appropriate heating system alternatives and evaluating them using a multi-criteria decision-making framework. A detailed assessment of household heating technologies and sustainable energy systems resulted in the selection of five heating system solutions. The review examined prior assessments of heating system performance, energy efficiency, and renewable heating technologies in an effort to identify feasible alternatives. To confirm that these strategies are feasible, discussions with specialists in energy engineering and sustainable energy systems were conducted. These experts validated the significance of the selected alternatives, which are regularly utilised heating methods in modern residential and commercial systems. The study constructed evaluation criteria using a systematic procedure that included both literature analysis and expert assistance. Relevant criteria reflecting financial success, sustainability, and technical proficiency were selected and refined with advice from experts, ensuring that the final criteria set precisely reflect the critical factors influencing heating system selection while retaining the evaluation process’ quality. Results and discussion

Five heating systems were evaluated using the suggested PHF-AHP–EDAS methodology: , , , , and . The efficiency () criterion ported the greatest priority (eigenvalue: 0.4617), demonstrating its dominating role in decision-making, when the PHF-AHP technique was initially employed to generate the criteria weights based on expert judgement through pairwise comparison.

PHFEs were then used to calculate the performance values of the alternatives under each criterion. The PHF-based decision matrix was produced by defuzzifying them using predicted score functions. Positive Distance from Average (PDA) and Negative Distance from Average (NDA) scores were calculated using the EDAS approach on this matrix. These were used to calculate weighted scores ( and ), normalised values ( and ), and final appraisal scores ().

The alternatives are ranked as follows based on the final assessment scores: System of HVAC () > electric heating system > pumps for heat () > heating of biomass () > central heating system. In comparison to other systems, the HVAC system () had the highest appraisal score (), which was explained by its balance between high efficiency, moderate cost, and comparatively reduced environmental effect. Its supremacy was helped by its success on operating efficiency and energy source criteria, although with greater beginning costs.

The electric heating system () ranked second, with high rankings for running time cost and installation simplicity. Third place was to the heat pump system (), which performed effectively in terms of the environment however terribly in terms of cost. Central heating systems () and biomass () were placed lower because of their high installation complexity and emissions, respectively. These results show that under fuzzy MCDM, contemporary, integrated systems that are compatible with renewable energy (such as HVAC) are more profitable.

The results of the current research confirm the significance of climate awareness and environmental literacy for the implementation of sustainable technologies. Indeed, the efficiency of the alternatives to the heating system is a significant factor. However, it is also important that the stakeholders involved in the implementation of the sustainable technologies should have a strong level of awareness regarding climate change. There is strong evidence that the level of climate literacy is a significant factor for the interpretation of the climate-related information45,46. Additionally, the evaluation of the systematic policy frameworks is a significant factor for sustainable decision-making. Indeed, the recent research highlights the significance of the multi-criteria decision-making models for the selection of the sustainable policies under conditions of uncertainty. The proposed evaluation framework for the selection of the sustainable alternatives to the heating system is based on probabilistic hesitant fuzzy models. The results of the current research can help the policymakers, engineers, and planners to make decisions regarding the selection of the energy-efficient solutions that are consistent with the climate mitigation strategies.

Practical implications

The proposed framework acts as a practical guideline for all the stakeholders involved in sustainable energy planning as well as the selection of the heating system. The framework is particularly important to the policymakers as it acts as a transparent decision support tool that helps in the selection of the heating system based on economic, environmental, as well as technical factors. This is important in the development of energy efficiency policies, the promotion of clean energy, as well as the provision of incentives for sustainable energy solutions. The framework is also important to the engineers as it helps in the evaluation of the heating system based on factors such as energy efficiency, cost, as well as environmental impact. The framework is important under conditions of uncertainty. The framework is important to the manufacturers as it helps in the identification of the strengths as well as weaknesses of the various technologies. This helps in improving the efficiency of the energy system as well as the environment. Therefore, the framework is important as it helps in the provision of a transparent decision support tool for the selection of the heating system for various applications.

Conclusion

This study proposes a MCDM approach, which integrates the Evaluation Based on Distance from Average Solution (EDAS) method with the Probabilistic Hesitant Fuzzy Sets (PHFS) concept, while the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) is used to determine the relative importance of the evaluation criteria. The practical application of the proposed approach is demonstrated through the selection of the most suitable residential heating system, according to a variety of technical, financial, and environmental criteria. The results of the evaluation indicated that the heat pumps were the most appropriate option for the specific problem, which can be mainly justified by the high efficiency of the energy transfer, the low environmental effect, and the compatibility with the use of renewable energy sources, such as solar power. Although the use of heat pumps involves a high cost for the installation, the long lifespan of the system, as well as the low maintenance requirements, result in a significantly low cost of ownership.

The advantages of the heat pumps, which are justified for the specific problem, demonstrate the affordability, sustainability, and maturity of the technology, justifying the selection of the heat pumps as the most appropriate option from the available alternatives. The PHFS framework was shown to have a strong capacity for handling the reluctance and ambiguity of the expert evaluation, which results in a more credible and precise decision-making approach. The integration of the AHP with the PHFS framework, which involves a systematic and organized approach for computing the weights of the criteria according to the expert evaluation, enhances the credibility of the decision-making approach.

Future research directions

The proposed framework is an appropriate approach for making comparisons regarding heating system options when the conditions are uncertain. However, it is possible to take this approach to the next level by incorporating better computational tools. For example, machine learning and deep neural networks have been found to be promising tools for dealing with complex nonlinear relationships and dealing with complex data sets47. Deep learning has been found to be particularly promising for dealing with temporal patterns and complex interactions between features of data sets. For example, deep learning has been found to be particularly promising for dealing with complex data sets in fields like smart healthcare and speech recognition48. The future direction of research could be to develop intelligent decision frameworks based on tools like fuzzy multi-criteria decision making coupled with tools like deep learning, convolutional learning, or recurrent learning.

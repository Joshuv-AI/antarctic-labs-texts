Epidemiological and microbiological profiles of microbial keratitis in a tertiary eye center in Eastern India (Bihar)
Authors: Rakhi Kusumesh, Anita Ambastha, Lalan K Arya, Anita Kumari, Neha Kumari, Bibhuti P Sinha, Nilesh Mohan, Namrata Kumari
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Purpose:To determine the demography, risk factors, and causative organisms of microbial keratitis (MK) in Bihar, an eastern state of India.Design:Retrospective study.Methods:We reviewed the demographic, clinical, and microbiological data of 2303 patients with MK (non-viral) presenting between January 2019 and December 2022.Results:This study revealed a predominance of males (65.0%) compared to females (34.9%), with a mean age of 48.4 ± 16.5 years. The majority of patients (63.1%) presented after 2 weeks from the onset of symptoms. The most common risk factor observed was corneal injury (58.1%), followed by ocular surface diseases (13.6%) and diabetes mellitus (13.3%). The majority of patients (73.16%) were involved in agriculture. Prior to presentation, almost all patients (92%) had received topical antibiotics. Unsupervised use of topical corticosteroids was observed in 29.2% of the patients for the median duration of 3 days (odds ratio, 0.17). At presentation, the median size of corneal ulcers was 5 mm, the best-corrected visual acuity was less than 20/400 in 51.4% of patients, and corneal perforation was in 14% of patients. The smear and culture positivity rate were 75.4% and 47.9%, respectively. The common causative organism was fungus (48.8%), followed by bacteria (17.4%). Aspergillus spp. and Staphylococcus spp. were the most commonly identified organisms; a quarter of the patients (24.5%) remained unidentified. All bacteria showed good sensitivity to vancomycin.Conclusion:MK is a significant cause of ocular morbidity in Bihar. The knowledge of epidemiology, risk factors, and microbiological profiles of MK can provide a valuable approach to disease prevention, diagnosis, and management.

Microbial keratitis (MK) is the most common non-surgical ophthalmic emergency. It is the fourth leading cause of blindness worldwide, and 1.5 to 2.0 million new cases occur yearly in developing countries.[1,2,3,4] Considering the significant ocular morbidity resulting from its complications in a low-income setting, the global consortium has recently proposed to include this entity as a “neglected tropical disease”.[5] Its purpose is to draw attention to countries such as India and help ameliorate the social and economic burden of MK.

The epidemiology of this disease varies considerably in different parts of the world depending on the socio-economic condition and health care facility. Knowledge of the specific regional epidemiological factors underlying the disease is therefore important in diagnosis and treatment of this disease. In India, most of the epidemiological studies are from the northern and southern parts.[3,6,7,8,9,10,11] Only a few studies are available in the literature from eastern India, resulting in under-reporting of the disease.[12,13,14] This study aimed to determine the demography, clinical presentation, risk factors, and predominant causative organisms of MK in Bihar, an eastern state of India.

Methods

A retrospective review of medical records was conducted for all patients clinically diagnosed with MK at Indira Gandhi Institute of Medical Sciences (IGIMS), Patna, Bihar, India, between January 2019 and December 2022. IGIMS serves as the largest referral eye hospital in the state of Bihar. MK was defined as the presence of suppurative corneal infiltration characterized by an intact epithelium or an epithelial defect accompanied by signs of inflammation, with or without hypopyon.[11] Polymicrobial keratitis was defined as the cases in which corneal samples revealed the presence of two or more distinct pathogens. Our analysis excluded cases suspected of herpetic infection, neurotrophic ulcers, keratitis linked to auto-immune conditions, those lacking corneal scraping, presentations during the healing stage, and concurrent endophthalmitis. The study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional ethics committee (676/IEC/IGIMS/2018). Informed consent was obtained from adult patients. For patients below 18 years of age, parents or legal guardians provided informed consent.

Following a standardized protocol, comprehensive clinical and microbiological examinations were carried out for all patients. Important parameters, including symptom duration, predisposing factors, prior treatments (including the use of topical corticosteroids), access to the nearest health center, contact lens usage, and history of previous surgeries, were meticulously documented. Ocular status prior to the development of the ulcer and the patient's systemic condition, with particular attention to diabetes mellitus, were also recorded for analysis.

Clinical Examination: An analysis of the slit-lamp examination findings was conducted for both the affected eye and fellow eyes for the following parameters: ulcer size, location, depth, presence of perforation, height of hypopyon, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), and intra-ocular pressure. This study included patients who had undergone a follow-up period of at least 3 months.

Laboratory Procedures: For each patient, all laboratory procedures were performed under standard protocols. Multiple corneal scrapes were collected for direct microscopy and culture analysis. The collected corneal scrapings were utilized to prepare smears on sterile glass slides for direct microscopy using Gram staining and 10% potassium hydroxide. The material obtained was then directly inoculated onto solid media (10% sheep blood agar, Sabouraud's dextrose agar, non-nutrient agar, and chocolate agar) in a row of C-shaped streaks. In addition, liquid media such as brain heart infusion broth and thioglycollate medium were used. Conventional and modified Ziehl–Neelsen (ZN) stains using 1% H2SO4 when necessary were performed. A positive culture was defined as the growth of the same organism on two or more media, confluent growth at the site of inoculation on one solid medium, growth in one medium consistent with direct microscopy findings, or growth of the same organism on repeated corneal scrapings.

Antibiotic susceptibility for bacterial isolates was performed using the Kirby–Bauer disc diffusion method in accordance with the guidelines outlined by the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI), which classify organisms as susceptible, resistant, or intermediate to antibiotics. In this study, an antibiotic was labeled resistant if the zone of inhibition was categorized as intermediate or resistant. Susceptibility for fungal isolates could not be performed due to the unavailability of the necessary facility.

Statistical analysis: To analyze both continuous and categorical variables, descriptive statistics were used. Pearson's Chi-square test was used for the analysis. The odds ratio (OR) with a 95% confidence interval (CI) was computed when appropriate. The IBM SPSS statistical software program (version 26) was utilized for all analyses. P < 0.01 was considered to be statistically significant.

Results

Demography and presenting features: The demographic and clinical features of the 2303 patients are summarized in Table 1. Unilateral involvement was observed in all cases. Of these, 65% of patients were male, and 34.9% were female, with a male-to-female ratio of 1.86 to 1. The mean age was 48.4 ± 16.5 years (range, 2–92 years). The majority of patients (61.1%) were in middle ages of 21 and 50 years, and most of them (34.6%) had fungal keratitis (P < 0.01). In contrast, patients above the age of 50 years (10.7%) had a significantly higher proportion of bacterial keratitis compared to those below 50 years (7.3%) (P < 0.01). A considerable proportion of the patients (78.6%) belonged to rural areas, with the majority (73.1%) living at a considerable distance from the tertiary care center. The majority of patients (73.16%) were from rural backgrounds involved in agriculture and related occupations. More than half (57%) of patients belong to a lower-middle socio-economic status.

Before the presentation to our institute, most patients (94%) had sought medical treatment from various sources including chemists at pharmacies (41%), non-ophthalmologists (25%), homeopathy practitioners (2%), and ophthalmologists (26%). Only 6% of the patients reported to us without any prior therapy. At the time of presentation, nearly all patients (94%) were using topical medications (92% topical antibiotic, 30.6% antibiotic with antifungal, and 13.9% antiviral with antibiotic and antifungal). Surprisingly, 29.2% of the patients used topical steroids along with topical antibiotics for a median duration of 3 days (range, 1 to 9 days). Commonly used drugs included Pyremone, Ciplox-D, Festive-D, or Betnesol eye drops, mainly dispensed by local chemists. The majority of patients who used topical steroids prior to presentation had perforation requiring immediate surgical intervention. Only 31 patients (1.3%) of the total study population had undergone microbiological examination prior to presenting our institute.

In this study, only a small proportion of the patients (12.6%) presented early within the first week of symptom onset; the majority presented after 2 weeks and 20.2% after 4 weeks. Nearly half of the patients (51.4%) presented with a BCVA of less than 20/400. The median size of corneal ulcers was 5 mm (IQR 4–7, range 1–11 mm), often involving the visual axis. Hypopyon was present in 32.8%, with a mean height of 2.2 ± 1.6 mm. At the time of presentation, 14% of patients had corneal perforation, and 5% of patients were found to have impending corneal perforation, requiring immediate surgical intervention.

Predisposing factors: Table 2 summarizes the risk factors associated with microbial etiology, which was identified in 85.2% of the patients. No apparent risk factor was identified in 14.7% of the patients. Among the various risk factors assessed, recent corneal injury was the most common risk factor observed in 58.1% of the patients compared to other risk factors observed in 27.2% of the patients (OR, 3.6; 95% CI, 3.3 to 4.2; P < 0.001). Among all agents of trauma, corneal injury by vegetative matter and dust/sand was most frequent (75.3%, 1008/1337), particularly in the cases of fungal keratitis (72.9%, 735/1008) (OR, 25.7; 95% CI, 19.9 to 33.0; P < 0.001). A significant proportion of the patients with polymicrobial keratitis (73.8%), Acantheomba keratitis (33.3%), and unidentified microbial keratitis (48.8%) also had a history of corneal injury. On the other hand, in cases of bacterial keratitis, the prevalence of coexisting ocular diseases predisposing to keratitis was higher compared to other risk factors (OR, 5.1; 95% CI, 3.7 to 6.9; P < 0.001). Furthermore, known diabetes or diabetes diagnosed at presentation was a significant systemic disease predisposing to all forms of keratitis, irrespective of the causative organisms. In the pediatric age group (<20 years), besides injuries with the vegetative matter, trauma with a fingernail (18.7%) and a pencil tip (26.2%) were also frequently identified as predisposing factors. Among previous surgeries, keratoplasty was the most common risk factor accounting for 1.4% of the cases of keratitis.

Microbiology and sensitivity profile: Microbiological examination of corneal scrapings from all patients diagnosed with MK was performed. In the examined scraping, 75.4% tested positive on smear examination, whereas culture-proven etiological organisms were identified in 47.9% of cases. Nearly a quarter (24.5%) of cases tested negative on smear or culture examination. Table 3 shows the distribution of microbial etiology. Fungal pathogens (48.8%) were the most frequent causative organisms. Among the isolated fungi, Aspergillus spp. (41.6%) was the most common, followed by Fusarium spp. (19.7%) and Dematiaceous fungi (8.5%). Bacterial keratitis accounted for 17.4% of cases, with Gram-positive constituting 70.8% of the isolates and Gram-negative organisms 29.1%. The predominant bacterial organisms identified were Staphylococcus aureus (22.9%), followed by Staphylococcus epidermidis (12.7%) and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (7.7%). Polymicrobial infection was found in 7.8% of cases, with both fungal and bacterial organisms being identified in 5.2%. Acanthamoeba was detected in only 1.4% of smear-positive scrapings, and of them, 0.34% were confirmed by culture examination. The detailed distribution of isolated organisms is shown in Table 4.

The antimicrobial sensitivity test was performed for 353 bacterial isolates. Gram-positive organisms showed maximum sensitivity to ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, vancomycin, cotrimoxazole, tobramycin, gentamycin, and cefoxitin. Nearly all methicillin-resistant staphylococcus spp. were found to be sensitive to linezolid and gentamycin. Gram-negative organisms showed good susceptibility to fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, and colistin. Nocardia showed good sensitivity to amikacin and other aminoglycosides but demonstrated an intermediate response to fluoroquinolones.

Discussion

Despite the introduction of numerous diagnostic and therapeutic advancements in the field of ocular infections, MK and its associated complications continue to contribute significantly to ocular morbidity and blindness, particularly in developing nations. To address this issue effectively, a comprehensive understanding of its epidemiology, risk factors, and causative agents is imperative. Therefore, this retrospective study aimed to investigate the demographic characteristics, risk factors, and etiological agents associated with MK in the state of Bihar, located in eastern India.

This study found male preponderance in MK (65%), similar to the previous published reports.[15,16] Higher incidence in men could be due to more outdoor activities and more frequent seeking of medical attention by men in India. Similar incidence of MK in male and female patients has been reported in Western countries.[16,17] The majority of patients (61.1%) developed corneal ulcers in the mid-decades of life, 21–50 years.

Previous studies have reported a seasonal variation in MK, with a peak incidence during winter, which coincides with the harvesting season from October to January.[18,19] However, in our study, no significant seasonal variation was observed. This discrepancy may be attributed to the inclusion of a higher proportion of referral patients in our study.

Concurrent with the prior studies from developing countries, ocular trauma was found as the most common predisposing factor for MK constituting 68% of cases in this study.[12,16] In low-income countries, predisposing factors differ from high-income countries, with a greater proportion of trauma-related diseases; this may account for higher incidences of fungal keratitis in India.[4,5] In this study, vegetative matter, dust, and sand were the commonest traumatic agents for the development of fungal keratitis, whereas bacterial keratitis developed in the setting of bullous keratopathy or compromised ocular surface. Unlike other studies, we observed a very low incidence (0.3%) of contact lens use as a risk factor for MK; this may be explained by the rural background and the low socio-economic level of patients in this state.[16,20]

Most patients (63%) in this study had delayed presentation (after 2 weeks). The delayed presentation was observed in the patients because 94% of them had already been undergoing therapy, particularly with topical antibiotics, antifungals, and even topical corticosteroids, either obtained from local pharmacies without prescription or administered by unqualified healthcare providers. The lack of eye care facilities in their area may be another factor for late presentation. Given that this study was conducted between 2019 and 2022, apart from the aforementioned reasons contributing to the delayed presentation, the imposition of movement restrictions during the COVID-19 pandemic also exerted a detrimental impact on the situation. The detrimental effects of delayed presentation were evidenced by the presence of large-sized ulcers (5 mm, IQR 4-7), including cases of perforation (14%), as well as poor vision at the time of presentation (51.4%), which ultimately contributed to a worsened prognosis. In contrast, a study conducted in South India reported that 60% of patients sought medical attention within the first week, and 65.4% received evaluation and care from ophthalmologists, indicating a more favorable scenario in that region.[11] It is therefore important that local healthcare workers receive comprehensive training, enabling them to promptly identify ocular emergencies and refer patients to adequately equipped eye centers.

A serious concern highlighted in this study relates to the unregulated use of topical corticosteroids, which was observed in 29.2% of patients. This prevalence was surprisingly higher compared to findings from other studies, in which the reported rates ranged from 7% to 24%.[11,12,21] In Bihar, the sale of topical corticosteroids without a prescription is widespread. This unrestricted availability poses challenges in clinical diagnosis and identification of causative organisms in cases of suspected keratitis following their use. This study evaluated the clinical characteristics of keratitis treated with steroids and compared them with cases where steroids were not administered. Those cases with prior steroid use had a more severe and rapidly progressive course, necessitating immediate surgical intervention to manage perforation in 30.5% of cases. Histological examination showed that in steroid-associated fungal keratitis cases, hyphae were located more vertically causing deeper penetration and increased virulence, resulting in early perforation.[22] These findings are consistent with the results of other studies that have suggested a correlation between prior steroids in fungal keratitis and poor outcomes.[22,23,24] Moreover, it is well known that the use of topical corticosteroids carries potential risks such as increased microbial replication, corneal melting, and steroid-induced glaucoma.[25] The Steroids for Corneal Ulcers Trial (SCUT) and other randomized control studies strongly advise against the use of topical corticosteroids for suspected microbial keratitis, unlike the empirical use of topical antibiotics.[26,27,28] Therefore, raising awareness among the at-risk population about the detrimental effects of the unsupervised use of corticosteroids is important.

The microbial profile of infectious keratitis demonstrates regional, climatic, and predisposing factor variations. In this study, fungal keratitis accounted for 48.8% of cases, while bacterial keratitis accounted for 17.4%. The preponderance of fungal keratitis in our state can be attributed to the predominantly agricultural and rural background of our study population. Additionally, the sensitivity of the 10% KOH test in detecting fungal filaments ranges from 90% to 99%, whereas the sensitivity of Gram stain in detecting bacteria ranges from 36.0% to 40.9% in cases of early to advanced keratitis.[29,30] This discrepancy in sensitivity may contribute to the lower detection of bacterial keratitis in our series. Overall, this study demonstrates a higher microorganism detection rate of 75.4% by smear examination compared to the detection rate of 47.9% by culture examination. A recent publication also observed poor culture growth in referred patients, possibly due to the bacteriostatic effects of topical antibiotics received prior to referral.[15]

Fungal keratitis predominantly prevails in most parts of India, accounting for reported proportions ranging from 38.7% to 70%.[12,29,31,32,33] Although initially, both fungal and bacterial keratitis were comparable in the southern regions, recent studies have indicated an increase in positive fungal organism cultures over the past 2 decades.[33] In the current study, filamentous fungi were identified as the dominant fungal pathogen, with Aspergillus spp. being the most commonly isolated fungal organism. This pattern aligns with findings reported in other regions of the country as well as in neighboring countries.[34,35,36] Among culture-positive bacterial keratitis cases, 70.8% were Gram-positive, with Staphylococcus spp. being the most frequently encountered organism. Similar results have been consistently reported across various studies conducted throughout India, with Staphylococcus spp. and Streptococcus spp. being the most commonly isolated organisms. Recently, Pseudomonas has also been documented a common causative organism compared to Gram-positive cocci from central India.[31] In our study, both showed good sensitivity to vancomycin. A recent update from South India also demonstrated similar sensitivity profiles for vancomycin and fluoroquinolones.[15] Notably, a few reports have indicated decreased antibiotic susceptibility in keratitis cases, potentially linked to the widespread availability of topical fluoroquinolones.[37]

Although this study involved a substantial sample size, it was conducted at a single tertiary care center; therefore, its results may not be generalized to other states or countries with large urban populations and improved healthcare systems. Another limitation of this study was lack of advanced microbiological examination techniques for the detection of organisms that are challenging to culture using conventional methods, such as in vivo confocal microscopy, polymerase chain reaction, and next-generation sequencing. No microorganism could be detected in a quarter of cases in this study using smear and culture examination. Furthermore, the study lacked a comprehensive assessment of susceptibility and resistance profiles, which is particularly relevant given the variation in commonly used topical antibiotics in ophthalmology compared to other medical specialties. To address this issue, future studies should collaborate with the microbiology department to standardize in vitro antimicrobial susceptibility testing. These measures would not only contribute to more accurate diagnoses but also reduce the need for more costly interventions such as keratoplasty.

Conclusion

Our study emphasizes the substantial burden posed by MK in Bihar, India. The majority of the affected patients were from rural areas with agricultural backgrounds, making them more susceptible to corneal injuries. Delayed presentation is often attributed to a lack of awareness and tertiary health care facilities in Bihar. Fungal keratitis remains the most prevalent form of keratitis. The unsupervised use of topical corticosteroids, commonly obtained from local pharmacies, is a serious concern in this state. We hope that the findings of this study will provide valuable insights to guide the implementation of a simple and effective community-based approach, aiming to address these challenges and improve patient outcomes.

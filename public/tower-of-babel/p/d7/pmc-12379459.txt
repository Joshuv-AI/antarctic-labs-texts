Experiences, challenges, opportunities of nursing interns in Saudi Arabia: a systematic review and synthesis of qualitative studies
Authors: Emad Althobaiti, Aryam Almutairi, Renad Muawwadh, Atheer Aljehani, Fai Alqarni, Bayan Alilyyani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundInternship is considered as a main requirement for nursing students in Saudi Arabia in order to acquire the degree in bachelor of nursing. Also, it is mandatory for them to be registered and qualified nurses in healthcare settings in Saudi Arabia. Thus, there is a need to understand their experiences during the internship to enhance their experiences and maximize the benefits of internship.ObjectiveThe aim of this review was to understand and synthesize qualitative research systematically on the experiences of nursing interns during their internship in Saudi Arabia.MethodsThis review used a qualitative systematic review methodology with thematic synthesis. Nine databases (ProQuest Dissertations & Theses, ERIC, Scopus, PsycINFO, PubMed, Web of Science, ABI/INFORM, CINAHIL, Saudi Digital Library (SDL)) were searched from 1 January 1967 to 1 December 2023, and 56 abstracts were screened. Two screening stages were applied to review studies. Only qualitative studies, conducted in Saudi Arabia, explained the experiences of nursing students during their internship, published in English were included.ResultsThirteen studies were included in this review. Four themes were identified based on the nursing interns’ experience during their internship in Saudi Arabia which are challenges and growth opportunities in a nursing internship, the critical role of preceptorship in nursing internship, challenges and barriers faced by male nursing interns, and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on nursing interns.ConclusionsThis review identifies the importance of understanding of nursing interns’ experiences and highlights the keys elements that are necessary to improve the internship experience. The usefulness of internships in the nursing profession can be greatly increased by addressing the interns’ concerns which in turns could enhance the outcomes related to interns, patients, and healthcare settings. This study recommends that preceptorship programs should be enhanced by including formal training for preceptors and structured support for interns. However, some limitations were found for this review, including geographical scope, heterogeneity of studies, potential publication bias.

Background

The establishment of the first nursing baccalaureate program was in 1976, a Bachelor of Science in Nursing (BScN), took place in Riyadh, King Saud University, under the Saudi Ministry of Higher Education [1]. A bachelor’s degree in nursing science is offered by twenty-five public and twenty-two private nursing colleges in Saudi Arabia, the program includes a mandatory internship time [1]. Upon successful completion of the initial four years, which involve academic and clinical learning, including theoretical foundations in nursing, nursing care management, and nursing professional development, the student is recognized as a graduate [2].

The internship year is a mandatory and intensive training period for nursing students [3]. The internship is organized and monitored by the university, while internship sites provide the internship training [4]. Nursing interns undertake their rotations in several nursing departments, including emergency, intensive care, medical-surgical, pediatric, maternity (for female students only), and psychiatric, as well as primary health care clinics [4]. According to Althaqafi et al., the internship is mandated by Saudi Commission for Health Specialties (SCFHS) and demonstrated its effectiveness in raising nursing students’ preparation for the board test, boosting their clinical competency, and improving the delivery of healthcare services [5]. In addition, the internship program helps nursing students prioritize their tasks, develop their clinical skills, manage the burden of caring for several patients who require care at all times, and obtain experience putting nursing procedures into practice. Additionally, an experienced nurse has the chance to actively participate in teaching, developing critical thinking, and mentoring and supporting the students under their supervision through the internship program [6].

Their leadership, coping, and communication skills were enhanced by clinical experiences, and their internship program gave them a broader understanding of the nursing field, which improved their ability to make wise career selections [7]. Through internships, students can bridge the gap between theory and practice, gaining the confidence and competence needed to excel in their future roles as healthcare professionals [8]. As a result, offering a well-designed internship program enables nursing students to efficiently acquire various clinical skills, combine theory and practice, and enhance their interpersonal and problem-solving abilities [9]. The nursing interns described unpleasant clinical experiences that negatively affected their attitudes and confidence, which ultimately hampered their ability to learn [10]. Moreover, nursing interns show different factors including negative factors that might affect their clinical practices as mentioned by [11]. Also, the interns felt that the clinical experience was not a good place to learn [7]. Studies related to the experience of nursing interns have been published [12–15].

Although there are studies on the experiences of nursing interns in Saudi Arabia, no qualitative systematic review has synthesized these findings to identify key themes and inform practice and policy. The knowledge gap that exists is in synthesizing these findings and allowing for patterns and themes to be recognized and used in improving the experience of nursing interns. This qualitative systematic review synthesizes the findings of qualitative studies on the experiences of nursing interns in Saudi Arabia, involving stakeholders in the review process to ensure the relevance and applicability of the results. A qualitative systematic review – a relatively new methodology in systematic reviews of evidence-based qualitative studies – is capable of shedding greater light on interns’ experiences by synthesizing them systematically. Thus, this review aims was understand and synthesize qualitative research systematically the experience of nursing interns during their internship in Saudi Arabia.

This qualitative systematic review aims to address the following question: ‘What are the experiences and challenges faced by nursing interns during their clinical internships in Saudi Arabia?’ By synthesizing available qualitative evidence, we aim to provide an in-depth understanding of these experiences, thereby informing educators, administrators, and policymakers on how to improve nursing internship programs in the Saudi context.

Methods

This study was conducted using a qualitative systematic review design, aiming to comprehensively gather qualitative research studies to investigate the experiences of nursing students during their internships in Saudi Arabia. This review was guided by PRISMA guideline for systematic reviews [16]. The current study employs Thomas and Harden’s [17] thematic analysis approach as a method for identifying, assessing, and synthesizing qualitative research. This facilitates a thorough integration of available evidence, leading to a more nuanced comprehension of the experiences and enabling the identification of shared themes and patterns across various studies.

Search strategy

Upon completion working with the following nine electronic databases including, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, PsycINFO, Education Resources Information Center (ERIC), ProQuest Dissertations & Theses (PQDT), Cumulative Index to Nursing and Allied Health Literature (CINAHL), Saudi Digital Library (SDL), and ABI/INFORM. The search strategy (Table 1) for this work was completed in December 2023. Different search terms was applied to search in databases. MeSH terms was also applied in some databased such as PubMed and Scopus. The terms that were used in search were related to the topic and goal of this review. Every database was searched using a mix of related search terms. Experience, nursing students, internship, Saudi Arabia, qualitative, and mixed methods were the search terms that were chosen. Alternative strategies were used, such as going through the reference lists of every study included by hand.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were carefully developed in adherence to the PICOS framework, detailed in Table 2, to guide the development of criteria for inclusion and exclusion. These criteria encompassed considerations of populations, interventions or comparators, outcomes, and study designs. Specifically, peer-reviewed research articles written in English and available as full-text publications between 1976 and 2023 were included. The population of interest comprises nursing students, including nursing interns, investigating their experiences during internships, particularly within Saudi Arabia. Exclusions were applied to studies focusing on non-nursing interns and experiences outside Saudi Arabia, as well as those lacking full-text availability or not published in English, and those employing a quantitative research design.

Screening and study selection

Two screenings were used by the reviewers (EA, AM, RM, AA, and FA) to determine whether the included studies were eligible. Once duplicates were removed, all titles and abstracts were screened using inclusion criteria in the first screening, and then all titles and abstracts were screened. Following the first phase of screening, all manuscripts that were obtained through manual searches and database retrieval and met the inclusion criteria were then subjected to full-text screening. When there were disagreements amongst reviewers, a sixth author (BA) was consulted and consensus was always obtained. EndNote was used as a reference management.

Reflexivity

As researchers conducting this review, we acknowledge that our backgrounds and experiences may influence our interpretation of the data. While this insider perspective provides valuable insights, it may also lead to preconceptions about intern experiences.

To mitigate potential bias, we:

Engaged in regular team discussions to challenge each other’s interpretations.Maintained reflexive journals throughout the review process.Sought input from an external advisor not familiar with the Saudi context to provide an outsider perspective.Actively searched for and presented disconfirming evidence in our findings.

Quality appraisal

The thirteen eligible papers were evaluated for quality using the Critical Appraisal Skills Program (CASP) Checklist for Qualitative Research [19]. A prominent critical evaluation instrument for evaluating the overall quality of research results reports, particularly qualitative literature reviews, is CASP. Two reviewers independently assessed each study, with disagreements resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer. The CASP scores ranged from 7 to 10 out of a possible 10 points, indicating generally good methodological quality across the included studies. A conventional content analysis approach

In order to discover reoccurring themes in the chosen studies, a thematic analysis and synthesis developed by Thomas & Harden [17] was employed for data synthesis. To extract meaningful qualitative data, the authors carried out three stages of thematic synthesis: First, creating initial codes from the chosen studies, Second, using similarity to look for descriptive themes within the codes, and Third, abstracting and reviewing themes. To guarantee methodological rigor, each author independently created codes and aggregated analytical themes twice for each investigation. The writers subsequently conducted code reviews and theme discussions in order to get to an agreement.

Thematic synthesis process

The thematic synthesis followed the steps proposed by Thomas and Harden [17], including line-by-line coding of the results of included studies, the identification of descriptive themes, and the generation of analytical themes. The themes were refined and validated through team discussions and consultation with stakeholders.

Iterative approach

This review adopted an iterative approach, allowing for refinements in the research question, inclusion criteria, and analysis strategy as needed. Initially, our research question was broad, focusing on ‘the experiences of nursing interns in Saudi Arabia’. After an initial search and review of titles and abstracts, we refined the question to specifically include the challenges faced, as this emerged as a prominent theme in the initially identified studies. Similarly, our inclusion criteria were adjusted to encompass a longer time period than initially planned, ensuring the capture of older yet still relevant studies. The theme development process was iterative, with initial coding of a sample of studies, followed by discussion and refinement before proceeding with full analysis.

Quality appraisal

The summary results of the quality appraisals are reported in Table 3. All studies assessed met the inclusion criteria, none of them were excluded. As mentioned in Table 3, all the included studies (n = 13) had clear research aims, appropriate recruitment strategy and data collection; 12 studies had appropriate methodology and enough data analysis; 11 studies had appropriate research designs, mentioned the connection between the researcher and participants described, and talked about ethical issues considered; and 10 of the included studies had clear findings statements.

Descriptive synthesis: characteristics of included studies

Details about each study included in this systematic review of qualitative studies are comprehensively outlined in Table 4. The selected studies period the publication years from 2017 to 2023, a total of 13 studies. The primary geographic focus of these studies is centered around Riyadh, with three conducted in that area. Similarly, three studies were carried out in Jeddah, and one study took place in Saudi Arabia through virtual means. Additionally, one study was carried out in various regions, including Makkah, Jeddah, and Madina collectively in the western region. Individual studies were also conducted in Taif, Qaissm, Madina, Shaqra provinces, and northern/middle regions (n = 5).

Participants

All participants in the included studies were nursing interns, aged between 23 and 26 years old. Sixth studies included interns currently undergoing their internship programs. Among them, two studies focused on interns who practiced during the COVID-19 pandemic. Additionally, one study involved students under internship for a minimum of 6 months, while another study included those who completed at least nine months of internship. Three studies encompassed students with a minimum of three months in an internship, while one study focused on students with internship durations exceeding three months and another study focused on interns who worked a 12-hour shift. The sample composition comprised nursing interns in eleven studies, with two studies involving mixed groups. Notably, one study specifically examined nurses and clinical instruction, while another study exclusively featured male interns, and yet another exclusively featured female interns.

Settings

The majority of the studies (n = 7) were conducted across various health institutions and colleges. Additionally, studies were carried out within the same university/college setting, involving participants (n = 5). Notably, one study was conducted virtually, reaching multiple hospitals in Saudi Arabia.

Study designs

The research predominantly employed qualitative descriptive, exploratory, phenomenological, and naturalistic inquiry (n = 11), while two studies utilized a mixed-methods approach.

Data collection

The primary studies (n = 11) involved data collection through interviews, encompassing semi-structured, and focus group interviews. In contrast, two studies utilized a questionnaire with open-ended questions for data collection.

Narrative synthesis of the included studies

Upon reviewing the findings, it was observed that nearly all of the topics mentioned could be categorized into four broad themes: Challenges and Growth Opportunities in Nursing Internship, The Critical Role of Preceptorship in Nursing Internship, Challenges and Barriers Faced by Male Nursing Interns, and The Impact of the COVID-19 Pandemic on Nursing Interns. These themes offer a thorough framework for analysis to obtain an extensive understanding of what interns experience during their nursing internship. There is some overlap between themes such as “Challenges and Growth Opportunities in Nursing Internship” and “Challenges and Barriers Faced by Male Nursing Interns” because of the obstacles faced by interns.

Theme 1: challenges and growth opportunities in nursing internship

The theme ‘challenges’ was mentioned in some of the included studies [5, 7, 15, 20]. For interns, the nursing internship is a crucial stage in their development as they learn how to take on total accountability while understanding real-world situations, which will improve their knowledge and abilities. Their ability to engage with a varied patient base enhances their clinical skills, communication, and cultural awareness [7, 15]. Nevertheless, problems still exist, especially when it comes to patient cultural differences, as many patients are unaware of the presence of Saudi nursing interns in the hospital, which creates a barrier to effective communication and the provision of safe nursing care [5].

Clear goals for every rotation are crucial for ensuring a successful clinical experience, as are monthly educational seminars for reviewing information [20]. According to Althagafi et al. [5], hospitals have a major role in offering educational activities that support interns’ learning. The hospital internship program’s structure and curriculum are essential components of success and was considered as a strength in internship program [21]. Although certain details may differ, interns usually go through a general orientation at the institutions where they work. They become familiar with hospital policies and procedures during this orientation, which enhances their educational experience [22]. Interns’ professional identities are positively shaped by their rotations in specialized units and participation in nursing activities [20]. Diversity in training departments improves learning chances, yet different hospitals assign interns different levels of responsibility and tasks rather than nursing jobs [5, 7]. Interns’ communication with healthcare professionals is hindered by issues including language and cultural difficulties [15, 23].

Furthermore, working 12-hour shifts remained a significant challenge, causing worries about future family plans, social life, and loneliness., and because of their long hours and lack of rewards, interns could feel underappreciated [15, 21]. Interns’ experiences are further impacted by shift scheduling and restricted access to hospital facilities [23]. Interns sometimes experience emotions of inadequacy and lack of support due to differences between the theoretical information they learned in college and its practical implementation in actual healthcare settings [22]. Despite these obstacles, interns study on their own throughout the internship, which advances their general education [22].

Once everything is considered, the internship program enhances interns’ leadership and communication abilities [7]. Students become more adaptive in working environments when hospitals and colleges work together [21].

Theme 2: the critical role of preceptorship in nursing internship

Several studies underscore the pivotal role of preceptorship and supervision during internships [2, 13, 22]. Aboshaiqah & Qasim highlights how interns gain confidence in clinical procedures due to excellent preceptorship, which eventually leads them to transition into staff roles [2]. Unlike Alanazi et al. [22], the interns received assistance and support from certain nurses, who were eager to work with them and help them develop. Alamri and Alhamidi [13] suggests assigning one intern to one expert nurse for comprehensive learning.

Preceptors must prioritize hands-on training, delegate assignments, and provide interns with the freedom and confidence to apply their knowledge, as advocated by Aboshaiqah and Qasim [2]. This approach fosters integration between theory and practice, addressing interns’ need for guidance and autonomy in patient care. Interns express frustration at the absence of guidance or orientation, as noted by Balay-odao et al. [12] and Alharbi & Alhosis [14], emphasizing the crucial role of clinical instructors and preceptors in providing support and direction, as emphasized by AlThigaa et al. [20].

Conversely, Alamri and Alhamidi [13] notes the confusion and lack of skill development when interns aren’t assigned a dedicated preceptor. This sentiment is echoed by Alharbi & Alhosis [14], Bahari et al. [21], and Althagafi et al. [5], who observe a lack of clarity in the roles of preceptors and nursing staff, resulting in interns feeling exploited and performing tasks beyond their scope.

Nursing interns report feeling neglected by nursing staff, with queries left unanswered and menial tasks dominating their responsibilities, according to Alnajjar & Rawas [7] and Alamri & Alhamidi [13]. Additionally, communication barriers with non-Saudi nurses, highlighted by Alharbi & Alhosis [14] and Banakhar et al. [15], contribute to misunderstandings and hinder cooperation.

Theme 3: challenges and barriers faced by male nursing interns

Challenges faced by male inserts were mentioned in some of the included studies [15, 22–24]. During their internship, interns experienced a profound sense of pride, particularly amidst the pandemic, which elevated the nursing profession’s importance in society [24]. However, challenges persist, notably among male interns who often lack awareness about nursing before enrollment [15]. These interns may face unsupportive families and societal stigma, affecting their confidence and interest in nursing [23].

Some male interns enter nursing based on external advice or the attraction of rapid employment opportunities [15]. However, cultural norms in Saudi Arabia often isolate male nurses, restricting their work to male-only departments [23]. Additionally, certain patients and families prefer female nurses due to cultural beliefs and the commitment of female nurses, exacerbating male interns’ feelings of exclusion [15].

Male nursing interns face extra obstacles, such as having nursing courses taught by faculty members with non-nursing specialties and having trouble obtaining hospital placements [15]. Furthermore, male interns often feel sidelined in leadership opportunities, such as attending meetings with head nurses, which impacts their professional development [23]. This lack of inclusion contributes to patient distrust in male interns’ capabilities, particularly in fundamental nursing principles like empathy and compassion. Biased policies further compound the issue, as male interns are evaluated using tools designed for female staff [23]. Despite the critical need for male nursing interns due to shortages [15], many feel inadequate in providing care due to patient mistrust and institutional barriers [22].

Theme 4: the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on nursing interns

The theme ‘impact of COVID-19’ was mentioned in some of the included studies [12, 24, 25]. Interns faced heightened stress during the COVID-19 pandemic, feeling unequipped to handle cases due to limited knowledge and experience [12, 25]. Their knowledge and experience were mainly derived from online classes conducted during this period [12]. Some hospitals, along with the Ministry of Health, implemented new roles for handling COVID-19 cases, often resulting in interns being put on standby, which significantly affected their training [24].

Despite their willingness to complete the internship during this critical period, they feared it would impact their future careers [25]. Due to lacking an infection control certificate, interns were not allowed to treat COVID-19 patients, leading to limited rotations in certain departments, further impacting their training [24].

Families of interns expressed fears of COVID-19 infection, often leading to interns being prevented from completing their internship. Educating families about the safety measures in place for dealing with COVID-19 patients is crucial [12]. The lack of experience and the vulnerability of interns and their families to infection led to significant mental distress among interns [24, 25]. The deaths of nurses during the pandemic also heightened interns’ fears [24]. Furthermore, interns often lacked orientation regarding their responsibilities and duties, and due to understaffing, they were not assigned to staff, exacerbating their challenges [12].

Discussion

This review is a systematic analysis of qualitative studies that includes thirteen studies to understand the experience of nursing interns during their internship in Saudi Arabia. The findings of this study are supposed to provide insights and focus into some areas that should be improved during the internship of nursing interns. According to the nursing interns in all studies addressed in this review, their experience during the internship was crucial to their future in different ways. However, this study synthesized and categorized four main and broad themes: challenges and growth opportunities in nursing internships, the critical role of preceptorship in nursing internships, challenges and barriers faced by male nursing interns, and the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on nursing interns.

Firstly, the nursing internship allowed the interns to be involved in the journey of healthcare, especially dealing with different patients, enhancing their clinical skills, communication, and being aware of various cultures, which allowed them to discover the real application of nursing practice [26]. The internship should be well-structured by hospitals as an intensive program offering general orientation, diversity of departments with clear goals for each rotation, and scheduled education like workshops or seminars to review their information. This structure might ensure the success of the nursing internship. This aligns with Roush et al. [27], who emphasize the importance of educational seminars and preceptorship opportunities to become more involved in the healthcare system. Nursing interns face challenges and obstacles, the most significant being the 12-hour work shifts which might affect their future career and lifestyle. Interns also can’t use all the facilities of their hospitals due to restrictions. Moreover, interns feel unsupported and experience emotions of inadequacy due to discrepancies between what they learned in college and what they practice in hospitals during the internship. This finding is consistent with previous research highlighting the importance of aligning theory and practice in nursing education. For example, a study by Ahmadi et al. found that during pre-internship, nursing students were not taught in a practical way, with a primary focus on theoretical content [8]. This gap between theory and practice can lead to delayed learning and feelings of inadequacy among interns.

Interns will transition to the bedside with full responsibility for the patient at the end of the internship, with excellent preceptorship being crucial to developing high confidence. This is consistent with Vihos et al. [28], who highlight that preceptors play a crucial role in establishing secure environments where nursing students can engage with patients, discuss experiences and ethical dilemmas, and gain confidence in providing patient care. Excellent preceptorship involves assigning the intern to an expert nurse who offers appropriate assistance, support, guidance, and orientation. Clinical instructors should be involved in this matter. This aligns with Guerrero et al. [29] who advocate for providing students with greater opportunities for interprofessional collaboration and hands-on learning to enhance their skills in diversified case management and quick decision-making. Preceptors and clinical instructors should provide solid feedback to interns, enabling them to enhance their professional growth and transition smoothly into practice. As emphasized by Mamaghani et al. [30], it is crucial to ensure preceptors receive training related to the growth of clinical reasoning to effectively support newly graduated nurses in their transition to practice. Meanwhile, preceptors should be able to train the interns with hands-on opportunities to combine the theoretical part with the reality of practice, as reported by Alaamri et al. [3], where students reported having multiple opportunities to practice skills during their education program as opposed to dealing with real patients in a clinical setting. Interns and their preceptors should have a clear understanding of their own roles and responsibilities to ensure they positively impact each other and avoid neglect and exploitation by nurses. Due to the diversity of nationalities in Saudi Arabia, interns still struggle with language barriers that might result in miscommunication, leading to neglect and other issues. In contrast to Alaamri et al. [3], students reported feeling comfortable communicating and coordinating care with interdisciplinary team members and delegating tasks to nursing assistants.

In addition, male nursing interns still face persistent challenges in the nursing profession, which they are not aware of before enrollment, resulting in a lack of confidence and interest in nursing due to societal stigma and unsupportive feelings. Preceptors should act as professional role models and display a positive attitude toward the nursing profession and patients while maintaining acceptable levels of conduct [31]. A variety of patients prefer to be served by female nurses who are more committed than male nurses, also due to the cultural norms in Saudi Arabia. Male interns need to be included in leadership roles to develop their professional careers, as emphasized by Miller-Rosser et al. [32]. During their internship, nursing students may encounter unsuitable learning opportunities, such as inadequate guidance and negative feedback from their supervisors or preceptors in clinical settings. These experiences may impact their ability to develop leadership abilities.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, interns expressed emotional stress, as emphasized by Abolfotouh et al. [33]. Several factors contributed to this stress, including a lack of experience and knowledge, which was learned through online classes. Despite these challenges, interns were unhappy when their internships were paused, as shown by Bugis [34]. With no orientation on COVID-19 precautions and how to deal with those patients, safety became a significant concern, as reported by Eweida et al. [35]. During their clinical rotation, nursing interns had to decide whether to prioritize their safety or continue providing care despite the threats. The pandemic imposed new roles for interns, requiring them to obtain an infection control certification to care for COVID-19 patients and complete their internship in certain departments. As revealed by Alshutwi [36], around 50% of nursing interns had adequate knowledge about COVID-19, and about 46% were willing to care for and attend to areas with suspected COVID-19 cases. The impact of COVID-19 did not only affect interns but also their families, as mentioned by Arnetz et al. [37], which prevented some interns from completing their internships and resulted in mental distress for both parties. Due to understaffing, interns were not oriented well regarding their responsibilities and were not assigned to nurses, which affected their internship experience. This was reported by Ulenaers et al. [38], where students expressed regret at the lack of learning opportunities and inadequate supervision provided by their preceptors, making certain competencies impossible to achieve due to a lack of time and attention.

Strengths of review

Numerous strengths of this qualitative systematic review improve its validity and reliability. The extensive search approach used made sure that a variety of research was found and included, offering a detailed summary of the body of knowledge already available on the topic at hand. This systematic approach reduces the chance of missing relevant research and increases the robustness of the analysis, making it the first systematic qualitative evaluation to understand the experiences of nursing interns throughout their internship in Saudi Arabia. The review’s emphasis on qualitative studies offered an in-depth understanding of participant experiences, viewpoints, and settings—aspects of research that are frequently disregarded in quantitative analysis. This breadth of knowledge provides specific insights into intricate topics that are difficult to communicate through numbers alone, which is useful for guiding practice, policy, and future study.

Conclusion

This qualitative systematic review sought to understand the experiences of nursing interns during their internships in Saudi Arabia. Our analysis of 13 qualitative studies revealed four main themes characterizing these experiences: (1) challenges and growth opportunities (2), the critical role of preceptorship (3), specific barriers faced by male interns, and (4) the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on internships. In response to our review question, we found that nursing interns in Saudi Arabia face a complex range of experiences. This review highlights the importance of addressing the experiences of nursing interns holistically, considering the challenges, opportunities, the role of preceptorship, and the impact of external factors such as the COVID-19 pandemic. The findings of this review also express the need for well-structured internship programs, effective preceptorship, and specific support for male interns. Furthermore, the COVID-19 pandemic revealed the need for greater preparedness and flexibility in internship programs to deal with public health crises. Future research could benefit from longitudinal studies to understand how interns’ experiences evolve over time, cross-cultural comparisons to identify universal versus context-specific challenges, and a deeper exploration of effective strategies to support nursing interns in their transition to professional practice.

In conclusion, this review highlights the complexity and importance of internship experiences in shaping nurses in Saudi Arabia. It provides a foundation for developing more effective educational policies and practices, aimed at improving the quality of nursing education and, by extension, the quality of healthcare in the country.

Dose-averaged LET escalation with multi-ion therapy for head and neck cancers: a phase I study protocol for a prospective, open-label, single-arm, single-centre trial (MULTI-ION-HN-I)
Authors: Hiroaki Ikawa, Makoto Shinoto, Masashi Koto, Takamitsu Masuda, Taku Inaniwa, Hirotoshi Takiyama, Tetsuro Isozaki, Shigeru Yamada, Hitoshi Ishikawa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThe standard treatment for unresectable head and neck cancer typically involves radiotherapy (RT) alone or chemoradiotherapy (chemo-RT). Non-squamous cell carcinomas exhibit relatively low radiosensitivity, limiting the efficacy of conventional photon RT. Carbon-ion (C-ion) RT, characterised by high linear energy transfer (LET) and high relative biological effectiveness (RBE), has shown promising outcomes in treating radioresistant head and neck cancers. However, local recurrences still occur, and further improvements in treatment outcomes are needed. To enhance the local control rate, an increase in dose-averaged LET (LETd) to the tumour was considered.Following a simulation study, a clinical trial was conducted to optimise LETd using only C-ion therapy, and its safety was confirmed. However, in this clinical trial, LETd could only be increased to approximately 70 keV/μm. To further escalate LETd, multi-ion therapy using ions heavier than carbon was developed. Simulation studies demonstrated that multi-ion therapy incorporating carbon, oxygen and neon ions could increase LETd up to 90 keV/μm, regardless of tumour size, while maintaining high-dose uniformity within the tumour. Based on these results, a clinical study was planned to evaluate the safety of escalating LETd from 70 keV/μm to 90 keV/μm using multi-ion therapy. The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the safety of escalating LETd to the tumour using multi-ion therapy for head and neck cancer, with the secondary goal of identifying the maximum tolerated LETd.Methods and analysisThis is a non-randomised, open-label, phase 1 study focused on LETd escalation. A maximum of 18 patients with histologically confirmed inoperable head and neck malignancies will be enrolled. All patients will receive multi-ion therapy using helium, carbon, oxygen or neon ions, either alone or in combination, at an RBE-weighted dose ranging from 57.6 to 70.4 Gy, delivered in 16 fractions (4 fractions per week) over 4 weeks. The specific dose will be determined according to histology. LETd escalation will begin at 70 keV/μm and will increase by 10 keV/μm increments, reaching a maximum of 90 keV/μm. The safety of multi-ion therapy will be assessed based on the frequency and severity of dose-limiting toxicities, monitored up to 90 days after the initial irradiation. Patients will be followed up according to the protocol for 180 days after the initial multi-ion therapy irradiation.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol has been approved by the National Institutes for Quantum Science and Technology Certified Review Board (#L24-002). The results will be published in a peer-reviewed journal and presented at a scientific conference.Trial registration numberjRCTs032240451.

Introduction

Standard treatment for head and neck cancers

The standard treatment for head and neck cancers is surgery.13 For unresectable cases, radiotherapy (RT) alone or chemo-RT has been considered.4 5 Unresectable squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) is usually treated with photon RT due to its general radiosensitivity.4 However, non-SCC is challenging to treat effectively because it is relatively radioresistant and there is currently no effective drug therapy available.6 7

Carbon-ion RT for head and neck cancers

Carbon-ion (C-ion) RT is characterised by high linear energy transfer (LET), high relative biological effectiveness (RBE) and superior dose distribution compared with conventional photon RT.8 These properties make C-ion RT a promising treatment for radioresistant tumours, such as non-SCC and hypoxic tumours. To date, C-ion RT has been used for non-SCC, and several outcomes have been reported.915 Previous multicentre studies have shown that the 5-year local control rate of patients receiving C-ion RT for mucosal melanoma and adenoid cystic carcinoma was 72% and 68%, respectively.11 14 While a local control rate of approximately 70% is acceptable, it remains insufficient, necessitating further improvements. Increasing the prescribed dose may improve the local control rate. However, escalating the dose in the head and neck region is challenging due to the close proximity of tumours to critical organs at risk. Therefore, new therapeutic strategies are needed, especially for non-SCC head and neck cancers.

Dose-averaged LET

In C-ion RT, multiple C-ion beams of varying energies are combined to create a dose distribution. The LET of the C-ion beams increases with depth, reaching a peak at the Bragg peak. As a result, the dose-averaged LET (LETd) in C-ion RT is not uniform across the target volume. Low LETd regions within the target are at a higher risk of recurrence compared with high LETd regions, particularly in hypoxic tumours.16 Furthermore, the LETd value may serve as a predictor of local control.1719 Conversely, clinical dose, rather than LETd, has been reported as a predictor of adverse events (AEs).20 21 It is hypothesised that optimising and increasing the LETd within the tumour —without raising the clinical dose to normal tissues—could enhance local tumour control while minimising side effects.

Dose-averaged LET optimisation

Using a treatment planning algorithm for LET optimisation,22 an in silico study using C-ion beam alone was conducted for head and neck cancer patients to maximise the minimum LETd (LETdmin) within the gross tumour volume (GTV) while maintaining the clinical dose within the planning target volume (PTV).23 The results indicated that LET optimisation could increase LETdmin within the GTV by 8–24 keV/μm. Subsequently, a feasibility clinical trial was carried out to evaluate LETd optimisation using intensity-modulated carbon-ion therapy (IMIT) with C-ion beams alone for head and neck cancer.24 In this trial, 12 patients were enrolled. The median LETdmin and median mean LETd (LETdmean) in the GTV increased from 52 keV/μm to 63 keV/μm and from 64 keV/μm to 73 keV/μm, respectively. Although two cases of grade 2 dermatitis and three cases of grade 2 mucositis were observed as acute AEs, no serious AEs (grade 3 or higher) occurred. Additionally, the response rate (complete response +partial response) was 67%. These results demonstrate the safety of increasing LETdmean to 73 keV/μm. However, even in individual cases, the maximum LETdmean achieved with C-ion beams alone was 75 keV/μm, and the LET could not be increased beyond this threshold. Therefore, while investigating the optimal LETd to maximise therapeutic effect, it was challenging to clinically assess the safety and efficacy of administering LET values higher than 75 keV/μm using C-ion beams alone.

Multi-ion therapy for head and neck cancers

Multi-ion therapy was conceptualised to control LETd using not only C ions but also various ion species. In our facility, we have additionally prepared helium (He)-ion, oxygen (O)-ion and neon (Ne)-ion beams in addition to C ions.22 O-ion and Ne-ion beams seem to be particularly effective for hypoxic tumours because of their higher LET compared with C-ion beams.25 26 We performed a simulation study of multi-ion therapy to achieve higher LETd for head and neck cancers.27 In the simulation study, we evaluated 16 head and neck cases with C-ion, O-ion and Ne-ion beams, either alone or in combinations of two ion species (C+C, O+O, Ne+Ne, C+O, C+Ne and O+Ne) to increase the LETd in the GTV while maintaining uniformity of the dose distribution within the PTV at an acceptable level. As a result, a median LETd (LETdmedian) around 90 keV/μm could be achieved in all cases of head and neck cancer with a tumour volume of 150 cc or less. However, when the target LETdmedian was set near 100 keV/μm for tumours of medium or larger size, we found that the uniformity of the dose distribution could not be maintained at an acceptable level in some cases. Although the LET of 90 keV/μm is lower than the LET value that provides a theoretical maximum antitumour effect, the results of calculations of the radiation response of anoxic cancer cells in tumours, which are expected to be hypoxic environments, showed that the LETd of 90 keV/μm in multi-ion therapy improved the effective dose covering 98% (D98%) of the GTV by approximately 30% compared with conventional IMIT.27 Therefore, a clinically optimal target LETdmedian at present was determined to be 90 keV/μm. It was shown that a high LETd distribution with increased uniformity can be obtained by the multi-ion therapy with one or two ion species according to tumour size.

Objectives of this study

The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the safety of each level of LETdmedian escalation for the GTV using multi-ion therapy for head and neck cancer and to identify the maximum tolerated LETd. Safety will be evaluated for acute AEs at each LETd level. The secondary objective is to assess the tumour response and late AEs.

Methods and analysis

Overview of study design

This clinical study (Japan Registry of Clinical Trials: jRCTs032240451) is a phase 1, interventional, open-label, single-arm, LETd escalation study. In this clinical trial, the LETdmedian within the GTV will be escalated from 70 keV/μm to 90 keV/μm by 10 keV/μm steps without changing the clinical dose as in conventional C-ion RT.28 In ICRU83, the median absorbed dose, D50% (ie, Dmedian), is considered to be a suitable indicator of the typical absorbed dose in relatively homogeneously irradiated tumours in photon RT.29 Therefore, we decided to use the LETdmedian as the indicator for the target LETd measure to GTV. We then investigated the optimal LETd with safe and maximal efficacy for head and neck cancer. Our study design is a modified 3+3 dose escalation approach, as shown in figure 1. Patients will be recruited at QST Hospital (Chiba, Japan), and Japanese patients will be enrolled in this study. The dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) is grade 4 mucositis and dermatitis of acute AEs, based on the National Cancer Institute’s Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.30 If no DLT occurs in the first three patients, the LETd will be escalated to the next level. If one of the three patients develops DLT, three additional patients are enrolled. If the first three patients and the additional three patients develop DLT in three of them, the study will be terminated (figure 2). However, if two patients develop DLT, LETd is escalated to the next level. Patient enrolment began in November 2024. The last patient enrolment is expected to be completed by March 2027. A 3-month follow-up is planned for all patients. The final study report will be completed within 6 months. Therefore, the study will be completed in December 2027.

Sample size

The planned levels for increasing the dose-weighted LET are levels 1–3. The number of cases to be enrolled at each level is between 3 and 6. Therefore, the total number of patients ranges from a minimum of 9 to a maximum of 18.

Tumour staging will be assessed according to the Unio Internationalis Contra Cancrum diagnostic criteria, version 8.31 Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrolment. Protocol treatment

Treatment will follow the protocols established for C-ion RT, as approved by the responsible ministries and the Japanese Society of Radiation Oncology. Dose fractions, determined by pathological type and tumour volume, will be as follows: 57.6–64 Gy in 16 fractions (RBE) for carcinomas and melanomas,32 70.4 Gy in 16 fractions (RBE) for sarcomas,33 and 60.8–64 Gy in 16 fractions (RBE) for skull base chordomas.34 No concurrent chemotherapy will be administered for any of the pathologies included in this trial. Patients will be followed strictly according to the scheduled protocol for 180 days after the initial irradiation of multi-ion therapy (figure 1).

Multi-ion therapy in this trial

We previously reported on fixed devices, CT scans for treatment planning and target contouring,35 and the same techniques are used for multi-ion therapy. Multi-ion therapy is administered using a rotating gantry and spot scanning methods in all cases. First, the clinical dose distribution is calculated using intensity-modulated ion therapy with C-ion beams only. Next, one or more ion species—He, C, O and Ne—are selected to optimise the LETdmidian for the GTV while maintaining the clinical dose distribution, previously planned using C-ion beams, without changing the number or direction of the beams. The ions used in multi-ion therapy are selected based on the target LETd value and the size of the GTV, with the goal of increasing LETd while maintaining the uniformity of the LETd distribution. The LETd prescription for the GTV in this multi-ion therapy is optimised as follows: the minimum LETd is set as the target LETd, and the maximum LETd is set as the target LETd plus 5 keV/μm, respectively. This constraint strategy was established to further improve the uniformity of the LETd distribution within the GTV compared with previous studies on LETd-optimised C-ion RT.23 24 The actual LETdmedian for the GTV in multi-ion therapy is considered acceptable within ±5 keV/μm of the target LETdmedian.

To maintain the clinical dose distribution in multi-ion therapy, PTV coverage of 5% (D5%) and 95% (D95%) should be kept within ±1.5% (±2% is acceptable) of the carbon-ion beam distribution. Furthermore, it was determined that the maximum dose to the PTV should not exceed 110% of the prescribed dose or the maximum dose before LET optimisation with C-ion beams alone, as much as possible. Dose constraints for the risk organs have also been confirmed.

The multi-ion doses are expressed as RBE-weighted doses based on the radioresponse of human salivary gland tumour cells under an oxygen partial pressure of 10 mm Hg, calculated using the oxygen-effect-incorporated stochastic microdosimetric kinetic model.253638

Endpoints

Currently, the safety of multi-ion therapy is unknown. Therefore, this phase I trial is being conducted to determine the safety of this approach. The primary endpoint is the severity and frequency of early AEs, including DLT, within 90 days after the initiation of multi-ion therapy. All AEs will be assessed using the CTCAE version 5.0.30 Secondary endpoints will evaluate late AEs from 91 to 180 days after treatment. In addition, secondary endpoints will include a response rate based on Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (RECIST) V.1.1.39

Statistical methods

All data analyses will be conducted according to a predefined statistical analysis plan. For the evaluation of safety, all patients who received at least one irradiation of multi-ion therapy will be included in the analysis to calculate the severity and frequency of AEs. The response rate will be calculated as the percentage of patients who have achieved a complete or partial response up to 180 days after the initiation of multi-ion therapy, as assessed by the investigator according to RECIST V.1.1.

Ethics and dissemination

This study protocol was approved by the National Institutes for Quantum Science and Technology Certified Review Board (#L24-002). This study will be conducted in compliance with the Declaration of Helsinki. This trial is to be regularly monitored by an independent data monitoring committee to ensure compliance with its protocols. The results of the study will be presented at a scientific conference and published in a peer-reviewed journal. This clinical trial will report interim analyses at each level.

Patient and public involvement

No patients are involved at this stage.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the world’s first clinical trial investigating multi-ion therapy that combines multiple ion species for head and neck malignancies. For radioresistant tumours, such as non-SCC, and tumours with severe hypoxia, increasing the LETd in the GTV is expected to result in improved local control. Clinically, tumours or nodal disease that exhibit central necrosis are generally regarded as markers of radioresistance when treated with conventional photon RT. Therefore, patients presenting with central necrosis will also be considered for enrolment under this protocol. We previously conducted LETd optimisation using only C ions and were able to increase the median LETdmean in the tumour to 73 keV/μm, confirming the safety of this approach.24 However, it is difficult to achieve a mean greater than 74 keV/μm using C ions alone. Therefore, it is necessary to incorporate heavier ions to increase the LETd. As per a physics simulation study, multi-ion therapy using C, O and Ne ions can increase the LETd to 90 keV/μm.27

The efficacy and safety of particle therapy using protons and C ions have been well documented,40 but the safety of O-ion and Ne-ion beams remains unknown. Additionally, the safety of combined irradiation with multiple ion species is yet to be established. Therefore, while response rates are important, AEs need to be monitored closely throughout this study.

This study has some limitations. First, the small number of patients enrolled in this trial is a limitation, although this sample size is acceptable for a phase I trial. Once the safety of multi-ion therapy is confirmed in this phase I trial, a phase II trial will be conducted with a larger patient population. In the phase II trial, both the safety and efficacy of multi-ion therapy will be assessed over a longer follow-up period. Other limitations include potential selection bias due to the inclusion of patients with good performance status, which could affect the generalisability of the findings. Additionally, this study will be conducted in a single country and at a single institution. Although the number of facilities capable of providing multi-ion therapy is currently limited, it is expected to increase, allowing broader access to multi-ion therapy for patients in the future.

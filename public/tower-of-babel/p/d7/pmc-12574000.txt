Evaluating the implementation and effectiveness of dispatching EMS volunteers via “interconnected first-aid app” on the outcome of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest patients: protocol for a type II hybrid implementation-effectiveness study
Authors: Huanlin Dong, Linxuan Jiang, Qiling Zhang, Siwei Xie, Zongbing Wang, Suhang Song, Zhijie Zheng, Shuduo Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionOut-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) poses a critical public health challenge globally, with survival rates in China being significantly lower than that in high-income countries. Delayed emergency response, low bystander Cardio Pulmonary Resuscitation (CPR) rates, and limited AED accessibility contribute to poor outcomes. Leveraging smart technology to mobilize trained volunteers via mobile applications has emerged as a promising strategy to bridge these gaps. However, in urban contexts within China, there remains a scarcity of implementation evidence encompassing aspects such as adoption, contextual barriers, and sustainability. This shortage of evidence hinders the integration of policies and systems at the policy and system levels. This study aims to improve the survival rates of OHCA patients in China, by designing, implementing and evaluating the effectiveness of an optimized “Interconnected First-Aid App” dispatching EMS volunteers through a stepped-wedge cluster randomized trial.Methods and analysisA stepped-wedge cluster randomized controlled trial will be conducted in 24 streets/townships across three districts in Shaoguan, China. The 24 streets/townships will be randomly assigned to four clusters, and each cluster will receive staggered interventions over 18 months, including volunteer recruitment/training, app-AED-EMS integration, and community education step by step. The trial adopts the PEDALS framework (Problem, Evidence-based Practice, Determinants, Action, Long-term, Scale) to assess both implementation processes and clinical outcomes. Primary outcomes include survival rates, bystander CPR/AED utilization, and response times. Implementation outcomes will evaluate adoption, feasibility, and sustainability through surveys, interviews, and health system data analysis. Findings will inform scalable, context-adapted implementation strategies for community-based OHCA response, particularly leveraging digital health innovations to address inequities in emergency care access in low- and middle-income settings.Ethics and disseminationThis protocol was approved by the Biomedical Research Ethics Committee of Peking University First Hospital (2025R0017-0001). Findings will be disseminated through traditional academic pathways, including peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations, with policy briefings prepared for stakeholders and leaders at the local, provincial and national levels.Trial registrationThe study is registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry under the ID ChiCTR2500101187, registered on April 21, 2025.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s43058-025-00802-0.

Introduction

Out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) is a significant global public health issue characterized by a high incidence rate and low survival rate, with notable differences in survival rates across different regions and countries [1–3]. As reported by the International Liaison Committee on Resuscitation (ILCOR), the global incidence of OHCA treated by the Emergency Medical System (EMS) ranges from 30.0 to 97.1 cases per 100,000 person-years, with post-treatment survival rates ranging from 3.1% to 20.4%, averaging less than 10%, and survival rate differences between countries/regions can be as much as sevenfold [2, 4]. Without timely and effective intervention, the survival probability of OHCA patients decreases rapidly by 7–10% per minute [5]. Effective rescue of patients relies on urgent and coordinated rescue actions known as the “chain of survival” for OHCA, which contains the activation of the emergency response system, high-quality cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), rapid defibrillation, effective advanced life support, and comprehensive in-hospital post-cardiac arrest care and rehabilitation [6–8].

Survival rates following out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) decrease by approximately 10% for every minute without intervention. Despite the well-established benefits of timely bystander cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and automated external defibrillator (AED) use—both of which can significantly increase survival, with AEDs alone tripling survival chances when used promptly—bystander CPR rates remain as low as 32% globally, and AED utilization in residential settings is below 2% in the United States [9, 10]. Using smart technology and mobile applications to expand CPR and public defibrillation programs by notifying volunteers near OHCA incidents to provide early CPR and defibrillation is receiving increasing recognition [4, 11]. Since 2007, several high-income countries have integrated smartphone-based emergency volunteer dispatch systems into their EMS frameworks to facilitate early CPR and defibrillation for OHCA patients. Countries such as the Netherlands, Sweden, Switzerland, and Denmark have deployed mobile applications that alert registered volunteers within a 0.5–1.0 km radius, resulting in significant improvements in bystander response times and OHCA survival rates. [9, 10, 12, 13]. Research shows that the survival rate of OHCA patients is 2.8 times higher when volunteers are dispatched via an app compared to when no volunteers are involved [9].

In the context of China, the prevalence of cardiovascular disease affects over 230 million individuals, and approximately 550,000 cases of cardiac arrest occur annually [14]. However, the survival rate of OHCA is less than 1% in China compared to 12% in the USA, with an EMS-treated incidence rate of 95.7 per 100,000 person-years [15, 16]. Currently, the overall CPR training rate among the public in China is still less than 1%, with both the bystander CPR implementation rate and OHCA patient survival rate being less than 1% [17], significantly lower than the 10% to 12% rates in European and American countries [18]. The fundamental reasons for this discrepancy in China are the underdeveloped emergency rescue system, low public emergency response awareness, and the general lack of AEDs in public places [19, 20].

App-based volunteer dispatch systems remain nascent in China’s EMS landscape. Some cities, such as Qingdao and Hangzhou, have reported using the Anke system app on the 120 command dispatch platform to dispatch volunteers to participate in on-site emergency rescue [21]. Due to being in the initial stage, there has not yet been a systematic analysis and evaluation of the implementation process and effectiveness of internet-based emergency medical services apps, making it difficult to promote them nationwide. In addition, few studies, to our knowledge, have focused on implementation strategies to provide generalizable data that could inform replication and scale-up activities.

Accordingly, in collaboration with the Shaoguan Emergency Medical Center, this study aims to design an optimized implementation strategy to enhance the adoption, execution, and sustainability of volunteer-based EMS dispatch via interconnected first-aid apps. Building on prior development of internet-based EMS platforms, the project will integrate evidence-based interventions into existing prehospital care models for out-of-hospital cardiac arrest patients, while systematically evaluating both intervention effectiveness and implementation processes.

Study design

To systematically address the implementation of volunteer-driven emergency response systems for OHCA, we adopt the PEDALS framework [22] (Problem, Evidence-based Practice, Determinants, Action, Long-term, Scale) as the overarching theoretical model (Fig. 1).

Theoretical model for this study

The P stands for “Problem”, underlining that implementation studies in Shaoguan will begin by identifying the issue of low survival rates of OHCA through the literature review, case study and qualitative interviews.

The E signifies a dual perspective, combining the reflection on existing EBPs (Evidence-based Practices) with the introduction of innovations. So to address those problems, the study will examine the existing first-aid procedures and supporting systems, and explore an “Interconnected First-Aid APP”-driven dispatch model, which aims to integrate real-time volunteer alerts, standardized training, cooperation of the emergency center, and community education.

The D in PEDALS represents “Determinants”, the barriers and facilitators affecting the implementation of EBPs. A Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR) framework will be used to identify multilevel factors, which involve innovation, inner settings, outer settings, intervention design and stakeholder dynamics, that influence implementation of the internet-based EMS platform. This approach aims to identify areas where adjustments and refinements to the intervention could be made in response to OHCA at this stage [23].

The A is for “Action” to evaluate the implementation strategy of the optimized initiatives. This study will utilize a modified RE-AIM (Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, Maintenance) framework [24, 25] which serves as a pragmatic tool for assessing complex interventions within real-world health care systems, encompassing both clinical effectiveness and implementation strategies.

The L in PEDALS implies “Long-term”, focusing includes embedding the intervention via “train-the-trainer” programs, aligning with provincial EMS standards, and securing sustainable funding, which is critical for overcoming China’s fragmented emergency networks. In the process, we will measure the implementation and intervention outcomes and build a sustainability plan for further interventions and promotions.

Finally, the S (Scale) is achieved through an 18-month stepped-wedge trial across Shaoguan’s districts, randomizing 24 clusters to evaluate generalizability. The stepped-wedge design is particularly advantageous as it enables more generalizable estimates compared to traditional study designs, which is achieved through regular data analysis and iterative dissemination of results, both internally to support programmatic enhancements and externally with partners to facilitate embedded research practices [26].

By systematically addressing each PEDALS component from problem identification to scalable interventions, the study provides a replicable framework, through which we will employ a type 2 hybrid implementation-effectiveness study design to evaluate the implementation and effectiveness of the optimized quality improvement initiatives (Fig. 2).

Setting and facility selection

The study will be conducted in Shaoguan City of China, which comprehensively considered the feasibility of carrying out the intervention, local emergency resources, and the prior cooperation foundation of the project team. Shaoguan City includes three districts: Wujiang District, Zhenjiang District, and Qujiang District (Table 1). To ensure full coverage of beneficial interventions while taking into account the intervention cost and evaluation design, this study will use streets/townships as intervention units. The aforementioned three districts cumulatively cover 24 streets/townships. Based on a stepped-wedge randomized controlled design, the comprehensive intervention measures will be gradually implemented across the 24 streets/townships until full coverage is achieved.

Intervention

This study will implement a phased rollout strategy across 24 streets/townships in three districts every three months, adjusted for local context, budget, and feasibility. As outlined in Table 1 and Fig. 2, the process unfolds in five steps: a baseline period with no intervention, followed by random allocation of areas into four clusters of six. Each cluster will initiate the intervention in one of the remaining four steps, ensuring all areas are covered by the final step.

The intervention centers on a user-friendly “Interconnected First-Aid APP,” which recruits, trains, and dispatches volunteers while coordinating closely with the emergency center dispatch system. This comprehensive approach aims to increase the number of trained volunteers, streamline emergency response, and improve pre-hospital care for OHCA patients (Table 2).

The intervention’s effectiveness in improving OHCA patient outcomes can be attributed to multiple factors. It enhances volunteers’ positive attitudes and perceived control over CPR, strengthening their intention to act. Systematic training and support via the APP boost volunteers’ self-efficacy, increasing the likelihood of prompt and effective CPR. Involving local residents fosters community ownership and readiness. Finally, the integration of the APP with the emergency dispatch system ensures timely response and minimizes resuscitation delays, collectively optimizing the chances of successful resuscitation and improved patient prognosis.

Randomization

Centralized randomization of all 24 streets/townships will be conducted prior to initiating the intervention in the first cluster. The statistician will conceal the allocation codes and distribute them separately to the study personnel tasked with commencing the intervention. An external technical advisor will utilize a random number generator to determine the sequential implementation order of each cluster. Randomization for each subsequent cluster will take place six months before the intervention roll—out, providing an adequate planning period. This protocol ensures that the implementation team remains blinded to the cluster sequence, thereby maintaining the integrity of the study design and allowing sufficient time for logistical and operational preparations.

Sample size

We will include all suspected OHCAs in which EMS responders are activated during the study period. According to the historical data, the number of EMS confirmed OHCAs in the three districts were more than 400 every year. During the two-year intervention, there will be 800 OHCA cases in our study, which will provide 80% power to detect an estimated 3.5% or greater of survival rate from the estimated baseline of 1 per 100 OHCA cases, with an alpha of 0.05, intra-cluster correlation of 0.005, and 10% non-response rate.

Data collection will be conducted through the Shaoguan Emergency Medical Center's data reporting platform. Data variables will include the patient's individual characteristics, the patient's condition at the time of onset, time indicators related to the emergency call and volunteer response, AED usage, and the patient's treatment outcomes. We will exclude OHCAs with obvious signs of death; trauma, drowning, or suicide; EMS-witnessed arrests; and OHCAs with a do-not-resuscitate order or without indication for continuing resuscitation by EMS.

Case study

Case study

This study will carry out case studies on Shaoguan, and will summarize the practices in the implementation of the, analyze the development of healthcare systems, and assess the demographic profile of the population and utilization of emergency and medical services.

2)Qualitative Interviews and formative research

Qualitative Interviews and formative research

The study will utilize a series of interviews (conducted face-to-face, by telephone, and via email) and focus group discussions with key informants from governmental and nongovernmental agencies, organizations, and institutions. These interviews and consultations will be based on the initial outputs of the case study. Subsequently, a list of questions will be developed and submitted to the study organization for consultation, review, comments, and further clarifications.

Guided by CFIR framework, discussions will specifically examine: 1) Innovation Attributes, focusing on stakeholders’ perceptions of the “Interconnected First-Aid APP,” adaptability to local workflows, and perceived complexity compared to existing emergency response systems; 2) Inner Settings, assessing organizational capacity including EMS technological infrastructure, volunteer management frameworks, and institutional readiness; 3) Outer Settings, analyzing policy environments, community demographic profiles, and regional resource availability that may facilitate or impede implementation scalability; 4) Stakeholder Characteristics, addressing role-specific motivations, barriers, and concerns among volunteers, healthcare providers, and policymakers—such as liability risks during emergency response, perceived adequacy of training programs, and effectiveness of incentive structures; and 5) Implementation Processes, investigating historical experiences with technology-integrated health interventions in Shaoguan, existing partnership dynamics, and communication pathways essential for seamless app-EMS integration and workflow optimization.

3)Volunteers’ recruitment, training and incentives

Volunteers’ recruitment, training and incentives

Volunteer recruitment will be conducted through the APP. Then they will be classified and trained as either professionals (medical personnel) or non-professionals (community emergency volunteers). The training will cover essential skills such as CPR, AED usage, and the identification and management of acute myocardial infarction and other critical conditions, provided both offline and through the APP for self-directed learning. To further motivate volunteers to participate in emergency tasks, we will offer economic incentives (50 yuan per participation provided by the project team) and honorary incentives (emergency service excellence medals awarded by the Shaoguan Emergency Medical Center) to volunteers who make significant contributions.

Implementation strategy

While the “Project Activities” section describes the detailed tasks undertaken to develop and deliver the OHCA intervention, this section synthesizes those activities into a clearly defined, theory-informed implementation strategy. This strategy is guided by the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR) and the RE-AIM (Reach, Effectiveness, Adoption, Implementation, Maintenance) framework, aiming to support adoption, fidelity, and sustainability of the Interconnected First-Aid APP model across EMS systems in Shaoguan. The multi-component implementation strategy consists of the following key elements:

Stakeholder Engagement and Co-DesignStakeholders—including EMS authorities, healthcare providers, policymakers, and community leaders—were actively involved from the outset through interviews, workshops, and planning sessions. This participatory approach ensured local relevance, enhanced system alignment, and fostered shared ownership of the intervention.

Statistics methods

Task 1. Develop the comprehensive interventions based on “Interconnected First-Aid APP”

We will use literature analysis and expert discussions to identify the priority focus areas of comprehensive interventions. For the literature review, a data extraction form will be developed based on the specific research questions. Data will be extracted independently by two researchers for each review, with any disagreements resolved through discussion. The facilitated and structured interview matrix technique will be used to analyze interview results for organizational analysis and strategic planning for priority setting. The structured interview matrix will be employed in the following steps: (1) interviews conducted by participants within the group; (2) small group deliberations; and (3) a facilitated plenary discussion with the full group.

Task 2. Implementation and evaluation of the comprehensive interventions

To analyze intervention effects, generalized estimating equation models will be used to account for the clustering within streets/townships. The primary model will include a fixed effect for time and a binary variable for the effect of the intervention. The intervention effects will be summarized as the resulting odds ratios and difference of proportions for binary outcomes or mean differences for continuous outcomes.

To examine the implementation outcomes of the comprehensive interventions through measures of reach, adoption, implementation and maintenance. We will use a mixed-effects generalized linear model to compare pre-intervention to post-intervention proportions for each metric of reach and adoption, while adjusting for clustering at the district level and time and allowing for district-level estimates to be random effects. The indicators of the implementation outcomes are shown in Table 3. Intervention outcomes will focus on survival to hospital discharge, and neurological outcomes at discharge with expected changes of bystander CPR initiation rate, AED utilization within 10 min of arrest, and response time metrics. These factors directly reflect the intervention’s impact on the chain of survival for OHCA patients, aligning with the study’s core goal to improve prehospital care and survival rates in urban China.

Discussion

Reducing emergency response and improve CPR rates in terms of OHCA has been an imminent agenda for policymakers as well as researchers. This project is designed to enhance the outcomes and survival rates of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA) patients by bridging the existing gaps in the current emergency response system and increasing public awareness of emergency response measures through “Interconnected First-Aid App”. Additionally, it aims to generate policy recommendations that can guide the improvement of OHCA treatment in other regions of China. We have described our rationale, study design, and implementation strategy details regarding this stepped-wedge cluster randomized control design.

Feasibility and implementation readiness

This study has received strong support from the Shaoguan Emergency Center. The city of Shaoguan places great importance on the treatment of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest. As early as 2018, it began implementing the MPDS system citywide, and in 2022, public CPR training was included in the “Ten Livelihood Projects of the Government.” Currently, there are over 200 AEDs (including those equipped by organizations) in public places in Shaoguan City, with a per capita density of 15 AEDs per 100,000 people. The 120 Command Center is responsible for the recruitment, registration, and training management of emergency volunteers. There are currently 234 registered emergency volunteers, with 138 of them certified after training and assessment conducted by the Emergency Center. The volunteer dispatch app required for this study was developed by Guangdong Tianying Rescue Technology Service Co., Ltd. It features real-time location tracking, precise dispatching, route navigation, and timely responses. Therefore, the implementation of this study is feasible regarding both the intervention context and the intervention technology. Additionally, the research team is composed of experts in global health, health economics, and emergency care, providing a strong multidisciplinary foundation that is likely to enhance the project's design, execution, and broader impact.

Monitoring of the research process will be conducted according to the tasks and approaches. We will conduct regular meetings with project stakeholders to review progress, address challenges, and make necessary adjustments to our plans. These meetings will promote open communication and facilitate the timely identification of issues. In addition, monthly progress reports will be compiled and shared with all stakeholders, summarizing completed activities, highlighting upcoming tasks, and outlining any deviations from the project plan.

Strengths and limitations

This study will provide contributions to the literature as follows. First, using the CFIR, this study takes lead to systematically identify multilevel factors influencing adoption of volunteer-based EMS dispatch apps in China’s prehospital system. Analysis spans innovation characteristics, inner setting dynamics, outer setting influences, and individual-level determinants. Second, the hybrid effectiveness-implementation design integrates the RE-AIM framework with a stepped-wedge cluster randomized trial to evaluate both clinical outcomes (e.g., survival rates, AED usage) and implementation metrics (e.g., volunteer reach, training adoption). By embedding process evaluations within the trial, this novel approach generates evidence on intervention scalability while addressing critical gaps in China’s OHCA response system. Third, through CFIR-guided stakeholder engagement, the study synthesizes transferable components of the “Interconnected First-Aid APP” model—including volunteer training protocols, incentive mechanisms, and app-EMS integration strategies. Findings identify context-specific adaptation needs for replication where local adaptation is needed for scale-up activities in low- and middle-income settings.

However, this project has at least two limitations. First, in the real-world pragmatic stepped-wedge trial, there will be concerns related to confounding, bias, and temporal trends that may limit the validity of our findings. We will use a modest effect size, a cluster randomization, and an analysis plan to mitigate these limitations.

Secondly, due to factors such as budget and feasibility, we have chosen Shaoguan as the intervention city. Located in the eastern region of China, Shaoguan’s healthcare resources are at a national average level. Therefore, our research may face challenges in being further generalized to other remote areas of China, particularly in the western regions where emergency resources are scarce, medical accessibility is poor, and there is a lack of emergency volunteers.

Expected outcomes and sustainability

From a health economic perspective, while the intervention incurs substantial costs—including promotion, personnel time, APP development, volunteer training, and incentive awards—these investments are positioned to yield critical returns: improved OHCA outcomes marked by higher bystander CPR rates for witnessed arrests, shorter intervals between OHCA onset and CPR initiation, enhanced survival rates, and better neurological outcomes. By establishing a functional volunteer dispatch system supported by a trained, certified responder network, the project aims to reduce the long-term healthcare burden associated with OHCA, including intensive care unit admissions, prolonged hospital stays, and downstream rehabilitation costs. So we believe this approach ensures that resource allocation aligns with both clinical effectiveness and financial prudence, and we will develop an implementation strategy for scale-up activities of the optimized interventions for OHCA patients for other areas in China.

To ensure the sustainability of the outcomes and activities carried out during this project, we will conduct training sessions for local stakeholders and community members to enhance their skills and knowledge related to the intervention. In addition, by fostering strong partnerships with local health institutions, government agencies, and community organizations, we will create a support network that will help maintain the initiatives post-project.

Computational heat transfer analysis of solar based energy systems via employment of numerical calculations and finite volume scheme
Authors: Ammar A Melaibari, Nidal H Abu-Hamdeh, Khalid H Almitani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAdvanced nanoparticles-based systems can improve the heat transfer of the base fluids. Here, a Computational Fluid Dynamics approach is implemented to study the impact of hybrid nanofluid utilisation on the thermal performance of a parabolic trough solar collector (PTSC) with an internal axial helical fin. Our numerical results revealed that as hybrid nanoparticles of SWCNT and Cu were added into Therminol®VP-1 as the base fluid, the thermal performance of the PTSC was improved. Indeed, the combination of axial twisted fin with hybrid nanoparticles with enhanced heat transfer properties increased the thermal mixing of the fluid. As an example, at Re = 4000, increasing the volume fractions of Cu and SWCNT nanoparticles from 0.01 to 0.02 resulted in a 0.74% enhancement in the thermal efficiency of the PTSC.Keywords: Solar energy, Modelling, Computational fluid dynamics, NanofluidSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Nanoscience and technology

Introduction

The rapid growth of global industrialization has resulted in a substantial increase in energy demand, exceeding the availability of fossil fuel resources1. Moreover, the environmental impacts associated with fossil fuel consumption have reached alarming levels2. In response, increasing attention has been directed toward clean and renewable energy sources, with governments actively promoting their adoption. Among these sources, solar energy stands out as one of the most efficient and promising renewable energy resources3. It is utilized in a wide range of applications, including building climate control, water treatment, residential hot water production, and electricity generation4. A critical component in these applications is the solar collector5. The use of concentrators to convert solar energy can significantly enhance energy efficiency. These technologies, known as concentrated solar power (CSP) systems, enable high thermal efficiency even under low solar radiation conditions.

Many studies have shown that the use of hybrid nanofluids as heat transfer fluids in PTSCs can significantly improve the thermal efficiency of the system6. Alqarni et al.7 used an Al2O3–CuO/water hybrid nanofluid in a PTSC under various Reynolds numbers and nanoparticle volume fractions. This approach significantly improved the system efficiency. In addition, they implemented a turbulator featuring holes and ribs within the PTSC. Their study showed that the highest improvement in thermal efficiency, reaching 51%, was achieved with the turbulator at a twist ratio of 0.3. Joseph et al.8 examined the use of a CuO–SiO2–Ag hybrid nanofluid in a PTSC, investigating different volume fractions and flow rates. They found that higher nanoparticle volume fractions led to improved thermal performance in the PTSC.

Mahian9 They investigated the use of an Al₂O₃/MWCNT hybrid nanofluid with three volume fractions of 0.01, 0.02, and 0.04 in a PTSC, resulting in efficiency improvements of 197%, 69%, and 6% compared to water, alumina/water, and MWCNT/water, respectively. The literature indicates a scarcity of studies evaluating PTSC performance using hybrid nanofluids. Given their superior thermophysical properties compared to mono-particle nanofluids, further research on improving PTSC efficiency using hybrid nanofluids is warranted. In particular, comparative studies involving ternary nanofluid systems, alongside mono-particle and hybrid nanofluids, could provide valuable insights into efficiency enhancement. Azizi and Tabatabaeekoloor10 conducted an analysis on the performance of PTSC for different nanoparticles of mono Al2O3, mono ZnO, and hybrid Al2O3-ZnO) at varying volume flow rates. They found that the highest thermal efficiency of the system reached 85.34% with the Al2O3-ZnO hybrid nanofluid operating at a set flow rate of 6000 mL/min.

The introduction of twisted turbulators into the receiver of a PTSC can enhance its thermal performance. Esmaeili et al.11 examined the performance of a PTSC by incorporating conical helical gear rings within the collector tube. Their study demonstrated a significant 36% improvement in system performance compared to PTSC configurations without these gear rings. In their work, the working fluid was an Al2O3–Cu/water hybrid nanofluid. Isravel et al.12 They conducted an experimental study on the effect of using hollow twisted turbulators in PTSCs. The results indicated that incorporating twisted inserts enhanced the thermal efficiency of the absorber tube by 24% compared to conventional twisted fins. Ferdoushi et al.13 presented a CFD-based analysis of a PTSC using a hybrid nanofluid composed of boron nitride and nanodiamond particles suspended in an ionic liquid, together with a helically finned absorber tube. The results showed that both the advanced working fluid and the finned geometry significantly enhanced heat transfer and thermal performance compared to conventional configurations. Overall, the system achieved improved thermal efficiency, with up to about 12% enhancement under optimized operating conditions.

The integration of hybrid nanofluids with internal twisted turbulators has been scarcely addressed in the literature. In particular, the simultaneous application of SWCNT–Cu hybrid nanoparticles and helical fins have not been reported. Nevertheless, previous studies have examined PTSC performance using alternative hybrid nanofluids and helical or twisted inserts independently. Suliman et al.14 examined a (CH₂OH)₂/Cu–SWCNT hybrid nanofluid at different Reynolds numbers and volume fractions, considering two fin configurations with different numbers of turns. The results showed that increasing the number of turns improved thermal efficiency, reaching up to 78.2% at Re = 12,000 and φ = 1%, while the maximum performance evaluation criterion of 4.7 was obtained for the lower-turn configuration. Balaga et al.15 experimentally investigated the usage of hybrid nanofluids containing f-MWCNTs and Fe₂O₃ nanoparticles in tubes equipped with wire coil, twisted tape, and perforated twisted tape inserts. The results showed that increasing nanoparticle concentration enhanced heat transfer by 21.7–31.7% compared to the base fluid, while the combined inserts increased heat transfer by up to 141% relative to a plain tube, albeit with a moderate increase in friction factor. However, their work did not consider the application in a parabolic trough solar collector. Alawi et al.16 numerically investigated Fe₃O₄–TiO₂/H₂O hybrid nanofluids in plain and twisted tubes using the k–ω SST turbulence model. Their results showed heat transfer enhancements of up to 25.7%, while pressure drop increased by more than 320% at higher nanoparticle concentrations. The maximum thermal-hydraulic efficiency reached 1.281 at Re = 18,000, although pumping power requirements increased significantly.

This research aimed to enhance the performance of parabolic trough solar collectors (PTSCs) by simultaneously introducing an internal helical fin and a hybrid nanofluid into the receiver tube. The main focus of the work was a parametric investigation of the effect of hybrid nanoparticle addition on the thermal behaviour of PTSCs equipped with an internal helical fin. While previous studies have extensively examined helical fin design parameters, such as fin pitch and height, as reported by Hou et al. through comprehensive CFD analyses, this study specifically concentrated on the influence of the hybrid nanofluid on system performance17. The working fluid employed is a hybrid nanofluid consisting of SWCNT-Cu dispersed in Therminol®VP-1, with equal volume fractions of Cu and SWCNT nanoparticles. The analysis is conducted using a conjugate heat transfer CFD model. The thermal behaviour of the collector under different operational settings is obtained. In addition, the pressure loss through the receiver tube is calculated to assess the influence of axial twisted turbulator and hybrid nanoparticles on the hydraulic performance of the system.

Collector configuration

Here, the configuration of the parabolic trough solar collector (PTSC) with an internal twisted turbulator was described. The receiver tube had a length (L) of 500 cm and was enclosed by a glass envelope to minimize convective heat losses to the surrounding air while facilitating efficient radiative heat transfer from the parabolic reflector to the absorber surface. The internal helical insert was mounted on the inner surface of the collector tube and extended along the entire axial length of the receiver. The helical fin had a pitch (p) of 37.71 cm and a height (H) of 6 mm, as shown in Fig. 1b, which illustrates the geometric design parameters of the twisted turbulator17. Numerical approach

Here, the finite volume scheme (FVS) was implemented to numerically solve the differential equations of fluid flow23, and the discretised equations were implemented using ANSYS Fluent® software. The solution is adopted from Hou et al.17. Radiative heat transfer within the evacuated space between the collector tube and the glass envelope was modelled using the discrete ordinates (DO) radiation model26. For the energy equation, the residual for a converged solution was established at 10− 7.

Results and discussions

In the present study, the thermal performance and hydraulic behaviour of a parabolic trough solar collector (PTSC) equipped with an internal twisted insert along the axis of the receiver tube were investigated. The helical fin had a pitch (p) of 37.71 cm and a height (H) of 6 mm. The working fluid was a SWCNT–Cu–Therminol® VP-1 hybrid nanofluid, which was introduced at the inlet of the collector tube at a temperature of 300 K. The effects of Cu and SWCNT nanoparticle volume fractions, as well as the mass flow rate of the hybrid nanofluid, on the overall efficiency of the PTSC were systematically examined.

Figure 4 showed the Nusselt number in the collector tube for a range of Reynolds numbers and volume fractions of Cu and SWCNT nanoparticles. As observed, the Nusselt number increased with increasing nanoparticle volume fractions due to enhanced mixed convection heat transfer. The presence of nanoparticles improved thermal mixing within the fluid bulk by increasing the effective thermal conductivity of the hybrid nanofluid. For example, at Re = 4000, when the volume fractions of both Cu and SWCNT increased from 0.01 to 0.02, the Nusselt number increased by approximately 2.7%. Moreover, at Re = 10,000, the increment in the Nusselt number was more pronounced. This was attributed to the compatibility between the axial flow velocity and the strength of vortices and eddies generated by the interaction between the hybrid nanofluid and the twisted fin, particularly in the radial direction. Under these conditions, heat transfer from the lower part of the receiver tube, directly exposed to concentrated solar radiation, to the upper and core regions of the nanofluid was significantly enhanced. As a result, radial mixed convection became comparable in magnitude to axial convection, leading to improved overall heat transfer performance.

As shown, increasing the Reynolds number leads to higher thermal efficiency due to the corresponding increase in mass flow rate of the working fluid. As indicated in Fig. 6, at Re = 10,000, the thermal efficiency of the PTSC shows a significant improvement due to the strong interaction between radial and axial forced convective flows within the fluid domain. In addition, the addition of nanoparticles further enhances the thermal performance of the collector. For instance, at Re = 4000, when the volume fractions of both Cu and SWCNT increase from 0.01 to 0.02, the thermal efficiency increases by approximately 0.74%.

Overall, for the PTSC with an axial twisted tape and SWCNT–Cu–Therminol® VP-1 hybrid nanofluid, the optimal operating condition is achieved at Re = 10,000 with φCu = 0.02 and φSWCNT = 0.02, where the highest thermal efficiency increment is obtained. Under this condition, a large portion of the concentrated solar energy is effectively transferred to the heat transfer fluid through strong radial convective motions induced by turbulent eddies. The results showed that although the thermal efficiency increased gradually with Reynolds number, the rate of increase exhibited a pronounced peak at Re = 10,000. In particular, the sudden change (increment rate) in thermal efficiency was highest at this Reynolds number, indicating a strong enhancement in heat transfer due to intensified mixed convection and vortex formation. To wrap it up, Re = 10,000 resulted in the maximum increment in the thermal efficiency, however, the maximum thermal efficiency was obtained at Re = 100,000.

As observed, increasing the volume fractions of Cu and SWCNT slightly enhances heat transfer from the twisted fin and the collector wall to the surrounding fluid. This improvement is attributed to the combined enhancement of conductive and convective heat transfer mechanisms resulting from the higher nanoparticle concentration.

The turbulent properties within the collector tube are shown in Fig. 11. As illustrated, when the Reynolds number increased from 4000 to 10,000 and 100,000, the turbulent kinetic energy increased by approximately 5.3 and 377.8 times, respectively. Similarly, the turbulent intensity increased by factors of 2.4 and 19.4, respectively. The turbulent intensity was significantly elevated in the vicinity of the fin, where strong fluid–structure interaction enhanced local turbulence generation. This led to intensified mixing of the working fluid near the fin surfaces. Consequently, convective heat transfer from the collector wall to the fluid was improved. Therefore, compared to a smooth receiver tube or configurations with discrete internal fins, the continuous helical fin extending along the entire tube length substantially enhances convective heat transfer within the fluid domain by promoting stronger secondary flow and mixing. In addition, the inclusion of high-thermal-conductivity nanoparticles slightly improves heat transfer through enhanced effective thermal conductivity of the base fluid.

Conclusions

In this study, the operation of a parabolic trough solar collector equipped with an internal twisted turbulator is discussed. The working fluid used is a SWCNT-Cu-Therminol®VP-1 hybrid nanofluid, with equal volume fractions of Cu and SWCNT. The following conclusions are highlighted:

As the volume fractions of Cu and SWCNT nanoparticles increase, the Nusselt number rises, indicating an improvement in mixed convection heat transfer. For example, at Re = 4000, as volume fractions of Cu and SWCNT increase from 0.01 to 0.02 (for each of them), Nu increases by 2.7%.

As the volume fractions of Cu and SWCNT nanoparticles increase, the Nusselt number rises, indicating an improvement in mixed convection heat transfer. For example, at Re = 4000, as volume fractions of Cu and SWCNT increase from 0.01 to 0.02 (for each of them), Nu increases by 2.7%.

Adding nanoparticles results in a slight increased pressure drop within the collector tube. For instance, at Re = 4000, when the volume fractions of Cu and SWCNT increase from 0.01 to 0.02, the friction factor rises by up to 0.81%. Similarly, at Re = 100,000, increasing the volume fractions of Cu and SWCNT from 0.01 to 0.02 leads to a 0.28% increase in the friction factor.

At Re = 10,000, the thermal efficiency of the PTSC significantly improves due to the complementary radial and axial forced convective flows within the fluid bulk. Therefore, for a PTSC with an internal axial helical turbulator and SWCNT-Cu-Therminol®VP-1 hybrid nanofluid as the HTF, Re = 10,000 is the optimal operation condition.

Finally, the present study is limited to thermo-hydraulic performance analysis without second-law analyses including exergy and entropy generation. Future work will address these aspects to provide a more comprehensive evaluation of PTSC performance, along with sensitivity analysis of key geometric and operating parameters.

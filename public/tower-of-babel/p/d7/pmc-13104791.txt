Safety and Efficacy of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (F-CRG-B191): A Gene Therapy for Neovascular Age-Related Macular Degeneration
Authors: Jiemei Shi, Huan Xu, Hongmei Zhao, Jiajie Zhao, Lihui Chen, Ping Xu, Dan Jia, Chunhui Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate the safety and efficacy of a novel gene therapy vector, covalently closed-end double-stranded adeno-associated virus-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE; clinical trial name F-CRG-B191), designed for sustained anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) expression in the treatment of neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD).MethodsWe developed cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE. The therapeutic efficacy of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE was evaluated in both mice and non-human primate (NHP) models with laser-induced choroidal neovascularization (CNV). The incidence of grade IV CNV lesions was quantified. A first-in-human administration of F-CRG-B191 was conducted to assess anatomical and functional outcomes, as well as safety. Immune responses and retinal toxicity were monitored in NHPs and the patient post-injection.ResultsCompared to conventional AAV vectors expressing aflibercept, cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE significantly reduced the incidence of grade IV CNV lesions in both mice and NHP models. In the human subject, F-CRG-B191 administration led to a sustained reduction in CNV area and improvement in visual acuity. No significant immune response or retinal toxicity was observed in either preclinical models or the treated patient.ConclusionscceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (F-CRG-B191) is a promising gene therapy candidate for nAMD, demonstrating potent anti-VEGF efficacy, durable therapeutic effects, and favorable safety profiles in both preclinical and early clinical settings.

Age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is a leading cause of severe visual impairment in the elderly, affecting approximately 196 million people worldwide.1 Late-stage AMD manifests in two main forms: dry (atrophic) and wet (exudative or neovascular). Neovascular AMD (nAMD) is responsible for nearly 90% of AMD-related blindness.2 Choroidal neovascularization (CNV), a key factor in AMD pathogenesis, is primarily driven by overexpression of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF).3 Currently, anti-VEGF therapies are the standard treatment for nAMD,4 effectively maintaining and improving vision in patients. However, these therapies rely on protein-based drugs prone to degradation5 and require frequent intravitreal injections. This repeated invasive procedure imposes a significant burden on patients and the healthcare system, highlighting the urgent need for long-term therapeutic solutions.

Gene therapy has emerged as a promising alternative. Adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors are non-integrating gene delivery systems known for their high efficiency, long-term stability, and low toxicity.6 Current clinical trials are exploring recombinant AAV (rAAV)-mediated anti-VEGF therapies for nAMD, including ADVM-022 (rAAV2.7m8-aflibercept; NCT03748784), RGX-314 (rAAV8-antiVEGF Fab; NCT03066258 and NCT05210803), and 4D-150 (rAAV-R100-aflibercept-VEGF-C RNAi; NCT05197270) has shown varying results in reducing injection frequency and has demonstrated dose-dependent effects. However, high doses of AAV are associated with toxic reactions,7 emphasizing the need to balance therapeutic efficacy with lower vector doses.

Covalently closed-end double-stranded AAV (cceAAV) is a new form of self-complementary AAV (scAAV).8 A key feature of the cceAAV vector is that it entirely bypasses the need for mutant inverted terminal repeat (mITR) resolution during AAV vector production, which is common for conventional scAAV vectors. This unique feature results in substantially improved vector quality and leads to enhanced expression efficiency in both in vitro and in vivo studies. In this study, we developed cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (clinical trial name F-CRG-B191), a cceAAV-based gene therapy drug designed to treat nAMD by expressing aflibercept to inhibit VEGF. The cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE was evaluated preclinically for safety and efficacy. In the clinical study, F-CRG-B191 achieved anticipated therapeutic effects without severe adverse effects.

Materials and Methods

Plasmid and cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE Construction

Aflibercept-Fc-YTE was generated by introducing M252Y/S254T/T256E mutations into the Fc region. Codon-optimized aflibercept-Fc-YTE coding sequence were driven by a cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter, SV40 intron, Kozak sequence, and SV40 polyA, and then inserted into single-stranded AAV (ssAAV), scAAV, or cceAAV genome. The cceAAV system used in this study was designed and packaged following the strategy described by Zhang et al.,8 who demonstrated that cceAAV vectors achieved improved genome integrity, reduced truncated genome formation, and enhanced transgene expression compared with conventional AAV constructs. AAV8 vectors were produced via triple-plasmid transfection in HEK293 cells, and genome titers (GC/mL) were determined by droplet digital polymerase chain reaction (ddPCR).

Preclinical Studies on Mice

Male C57BL/6J mice (6–8 weeks old, 19–22g; Shanghai Jihui, Shanghai, China) were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions (humidity, 40%–70%; temperature, 20°C–26°C; 12-hour light/dark cycle) with ≥3 days acclimation. Anesthesia was induced using tiletamine–zolazepam (25–50 mg/kg intraperitoneally [IP]) and xylazine (5 mg/kg IP). All protocols were approved by the Pengli Biological Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC No.: PL23-0437-1 and PL24-0278-1). Subretinal (1 µL/eye, vehicle or AAV8) and intravitreal (0.5 µL/eye, 20 µg aflibercept) injections were performed bilaterally under stereomicroscopy via 30-gauge puncture and 35-gauge microinjector. The intravitreal aflibercept dose (20 µg/eye) was selected based on prior studies9–11 and our preliminary optimization experiments, representing a moderate, commonly used dose that achieved therapeutic effects without apparent toxicity. Optical coherence tomography (OCT, SPECTRALIS HRA+OCT; Heidelberg Engineering, Heidelberg, Germany) confirmed subretinal delivery. The experimental design is summarized in Supplementary Table S1.

CNV was induced by laser burns (532-nm laser, 100 ms, 250 mW, 50 µm, three spots per eye) as previously described.12 Three laser spots were applied evenly around the optic disc, 1–1.5 papillary diameters (PD) from the disc margin. However, the exact overlap between the injection bleb and the laser lesions could not be unequivocally determined. Fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA) was performed on day 7 using intraperitoneal 10% fluorescein sodium (20 µL), with images captured at 5 and 10 minutes to score leakage. CNV lesion grading was used as a semi-quantitative assessment of lesion severity based on FFA to evaluate the extent of laser-induced CNV and treatment efficacy. Lesions were graded using a standardized leakage grading system as previously described13,14: grade I, no hyperfluorescence; grade II, hyperfluorescence without leakage; grade III, early or mid-transit hyperfluorescence and late leakage; and grade IV, bright early or mid-transit hyperfluorescence and late leakage outside the borders of the treated area. The number of grade IV lesions (the gold standard for evaluating the efficacy of treatment for nAMD15) was quantified. For ELISA, total protein was extracted from the whole mouse eyes and diluted 1:50, and aflibercept was quantified using a human-specific ELISA kit (IG-AA115; ImmunoGuide, Ankara, Turkey).

Preclinical Studies on Non-Human Primates

Male cynomolgus monkeys (3–7 years; InnoStar Biotech, Shanghai, China) were housed at 18 to 26°C and 40% to 70% humidity with a 7-day acclimation. Anesthesia was administered via Zoletil 50 (3–6 mg/kg) and xylazine (0.5–1 mg/kg). All procedures were approved by the IACUC (IACUC-2023-M-611) of InnoStar Biotech. All animals showed no abnormal clinical signs or behavioral changes during the research period. Subretinal injections (100 µL/eye) of cceAAV-aflibercept-YTE (1 × 1010 or 5 × 1010 GC) or vehicle were performed via 38-gauge sclerotomy using a Hamilton syringe. No vitrectomy or additional ocular/periocular injections were conducted. The injection site was located inferotemporal to the macula area, outside the foveal avascular zone, where a single subretinal bleb was created. A 100-µL injection typically produced a localized retinal detachment of approximately 5 to 6 PD. In addition, in the aflibercept group, intravitreal injections of aflibercept (50 µL, 2 mg/eye) were administered. Animals were monitored for 7 days. The experimental design is summarized in Supplementary Table S2.

Four weeks after injection, CNV was induced using a 532-nm laser (0.1 second, 550 mW, 50 µm, eight spots/eye). Bruch's membrane (BM) rupture was confirmed by OCT. The CNV lesions were distributed around the macular region, while avoiding the fovea center, with eight burns applied per eye. In our study, 53% of the lesions were located within the detached area, and 47% were positioned at the periphery or outside the bleb region. At day 7 post-induction, laser-induced CNV lesions were assessed by FFA using a confocal fundus camera; 10% fluorescein (0.1 mL/kg) was injected IP, and red-free images and FFA of the posterior pole were obtained within 1 minute.

OCT (SPECTRALIS and HEYEX 1.6.1) was conducted at baseline and at weeks 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, and 24 post-injection, covering 30° with 31 B-scans spaced 240 µm. Inner and outer retinal thicknesses (internal limiting membrane [ILM]–external limiting membrane (ELM) and ELM–retinal pigment epithelium [RPE]/BM) were measured at injection bleb regions. Sublayer of outer retina thickness analysis was performed following Zhou et al.16 Mean values from three points were analyzed by paired t-test. Color fundus images (53° field of view [FOV]; APS-BER; Goddon, Zhejiang, China) were captured at baseline and at weeks 2, 4, 6, 12, and 24. Scotopic electroretinograms (ERGs) (RETI-port/scan 21; Roland Consult, Brandenburg an der Havel, Germany) were recorded at week 12 after overnight dark adaptation. Rod responses were measured at 0.01, 3.0, and 10.0 cd·s/m2.

For biodistribution, 100 µL of blood was processed using AgMAX buffer, proteinase K, and magnetic bead extraction (KingFisher Flex; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) was conducted with 40 cycles, with a detection limit of 5 copies/µL. To evaluate immune responses against AAV8, neutralizing antibodies (NAbs) were assessed. Serums were collected pre-dose, at day 1, and at weeks 2, 4, 8, 16, and 26. Samples (1:50 dilution) were tested using Meso Scale Diagnostics plates (Rockville, MD, USA) coated with Bio-AAV8 and detected with Ru-AAV8. The signal was measured using MSD instrumentation. At month 9, animals were euthanized. Their eyes and optic nerves were fixed in Davidson and 10% formalin, paraffin-embedded, sectioned, and hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stained.

Clinical Studies

The study was conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board of Eye and ENT Hospital of Fudan University (2023137). The trial was registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (PID:236142). This study enrolled pseudophakic patients 50 to 80 years of age with symptomatic nAMD, an active CNV lesion, and best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) of 19 to 73 Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) letters who had previously demonstrated a treatment response to anti-VEGF therapy. The total lesion area was ≤12 disc areas. Key exclusions included active/recent infections, hemorrhage, uncontrolled glaucoma, axial length > 26 mm, other retinal diseases, prior gene therapy, or contralateral worse vision. Eligible subjects received 100 µL subretinal F-CRG-B191 (1.5 × 1010 GC/eye) following core vitrectomy.17 The injection was performed in the inferotemporal retina, and the bleb did not involve the macular CNV lesion. The detachment covered approximately 5 to 6 PD of the peripheral retina.

For the patient, serum samples were collected at baseline and post-injection to assess anti-AAV8 NAbs and anti-drug antibody (ADA). For NAbs, samples were analyzed using an in vitro transduction inhibition assay. The titer was reported as the reciprocal of the highest serum dilution that inhibited AAV transduction by ≥50% compared with the control without serum (100% transduction). ADA was detected using a validated bridging ELISA. Samples with optical density (OD) values greater than or equal to the screening cut point (SCP) were considered potential positives. These were confirmed using a competitive inhibition step, where samples showing greater than or equal to confirmation cut point (CCP) inhibition were classified as positive. Ophthalmic evaluations included slit-lamp biomicroscopy, BCVA assessment, non-contact intraocular pressure (IOP) measurement, and endothelial cell density assessment using a non-contact specular microscope (Topcon, Tokyo, Japan). Additional evaluations included axial length measurement with the IOLMaster 3.01 (Carl Zeiss Meditec, Jena, Germany), spectral-domain OCT (SD-OCT; SPECTRALIS HRA+OCT) and ultra-widefield photography (UWFP) and ultra-widefield fundus autofluorescence (FAF; Daytona Optomap scanning laser ophthalmoscope; Optos, Dumfermline, UK). Central retinal thickness (CRT) was automatically calculated using the Thickness Map module of the system, as previously described and manually verified by trained personnel.18,19 CNV lesion areas were determined by measuring the cross-sectional maximum area in 1:1 pixel mode using distance measurement tools provided by the SPECTRALIS HRA+OCT system, based on previously described methods.20

Statistical Analysis

FFA leakage scores and lesion areas were analyzed via Kruskal–Wallis or Mann–Whitney U tests; other data were analyzed by ANOVA or t-tests. Data are presented as mean ± SD. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Prism 9 (GraphPad, Boston, MA, USA) was used.

Results

Rational Engineering and Construction of cceAAV-Aflibercept-Fc-YTE

cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE was constructed using cceAAV carrying an improved gene encoding aflibercept-Fc-YTE, a modified format of the VEGF antibody (Fig. 1A). Aflibercept functions as a VEGF trap, incorporating the second immunoglobulin (Ig) domain of human VEGFR1 and the third Ig domain of human VEGFR2, linked as an inline fusion to the Fc region of IgG. The aflibercept-encoding gene we used was engineered to induce specific substitutions (M252Y/S254T/T256E, YTE) in the Fc fragment. The key elements of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE are illustrated in Figure 1, and the schematic structures of three different AAVs including cceAAV, scAAV, and ssAAV are highlighted in Figure 1B.

Expression of cceAAV-Aflibercept-Fc-YTE in Mice

The cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE was delivered into the eyes of the C57BL/6J mice via subretinal injection. The expression of aflibercept-Fc-YTE protein showed a dose-dependent increase, with 69.45 ± 17.19 ng/eye in the 3 × 107 GC dosing group (n = 4), 326.75 ± 59.96 ng/eye in the 1 × 108 GC dosing group (n = 4), and1644.10 ± 288.43 ng/eye in the 6 × 108 GC dosing group (n = 4). To assess the transgene efficiency, aflibercept-Fc-YTE expression was compared among cceAAV, scAAV, and ssAAV vectors. At a dose of 3 × 107 GC/eye, aflibercept-Fc-YTE expression in the cceAAV group was 1.9 times higher than in the scAAV group (P = 0.0493). As the dose increased, the advantage of cceAAV also increased. At a dose of 1 × 108 GC/eye, protein expression in the cceAAV group was 2.5 and 3.2 times higher than in the ssAAV and scAAV groups, respectively (all P < 0.001). At a dose of 6 × 108 GC/eye, protein expression in the cceAAV group was 3.5 and 4.9 times higher than in the ssAAV and scAAV groups, respectively (all P < 0.001) (Fig. 1C). These results indicate that this novel cceAAV vector enhanced aflibercept expression in vivo. The expression of aflibercept-Fc-YTE in the eyes of mice (6.7 × 108 GC/eye) was 906.05 ± 572.38 ng/eye at week 2 and reached a peak of 2332.81 ±333.39 ng/eye at week 4. Expression gradually decreased and stabilized at approximately 1186.25 ± 140.63 ng/eye after week 8, persisting until the final observation at week 24 (Fig. 1D).

Efficacy of Aflibercept-Fc-YTE in Mice CNV Model

The evaluation timeline of cceAAV treatments is illustrated in Figure 2A. FFA was performed at week 5. Compared to the vehicle group, the CNV area was significantly reduced in all cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE groups (all P < 0.001). The highest dose yielded the best results (3 × 107 GC/eye vs. 1 × 109 GC/eye, P = 0.004; 1 × 108 GC/eye vs. 1 × 109 GC/eye, P = 0.011). Compared to the vehicle group, the CNV area was also significantly reduced in all cceAAV-aflibercept groups (all P < 0.001). At the same dose of 1 × 109 GC/eye, the CNV area in the cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE group was significantly smaller than in the cceAAV-aflibercept group (P = 0.0015) (Fig. 2B).

Compared to the vehicle group, the CNV lesion grading was significantly reduced in the cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE, cceAAV-aflibercept, and positive control groups (all P < 0.001). There were no differences in the CNV lesion grading among the three different doses of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE or cceAAV-aflibercept. The CNV lesion grading of the cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE group (1 × 109 GC/eye) was significantly lower than that of the cceAAV-aflibercept group (1 × 109 GC/eye; P = 0.0029) (Fig. 2C). These results indicate that the modified aflibercept-Fc-YTE shows comparable efficacy to conventional aflibercept and potentially better efficacy than cceAAV-aflibercept at the same dose (1 × 109 GC/eye).

AAV Gene Levels and Immune Response of cceAAV-Aflibercept-Fc-YTE in NHP

During the evaluation of the safety profile of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE, serum samples from cynomolgus monkeys were analyzed to monitor the presence of AAV vector DNA and anti-AAV8 NAbs. Following the administration of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE at a low dose (1 × 1010 GC/eye), AAV vector DNA levels were below the lower limit of quantification (BLQ) in two animals at all time points. In the third animal, AAV vector DNA was detected on day 1; it decreased gradually and became undetectable by week 4. In the high-dose group (5 × 1010 GC/eye), AAV vector DNA could be detected in two of three animals on day 1 and in one animal from week 1 to week 6; the other detection values were BLQ (5 copies/µL) (Fig. 3A; Supplementary Table S3). Similar to the AAV vector DNA, NAb levels were higher in the high-dose group (5 × 1010 GC/eye). In both dose groups, the levels of NAbs increased by week 2, decreased by week 4, and returned to near pre-injection levels by week 26 (Fig. 3B; Supplementary Table S4). These findings suggest that cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE administration did not elicit an intense immunogenic response in NHPs.

Retinal Structure and ERG Outcomes After Injection of cceAAV-Aflibercept-Fc-YTE in NHPs

To evaluate the safety profile of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE, either vehicle or cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (1 × 1010 GC/eye or 5 × 1010 GC/eye), 100 µL, was administered into the subretinal space of the cynomolgus monkeys. The injection-induced bleb subsided on the second day. During slit-lamp examination, no significant findings were observed in the cornea, iris, lens, retina (including retinal vessels, macula), or optic disc in the eyes treated with cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE, except for a transient, mild anterior chamber inflammatory response (mild aqueous cells) observed in a few monkeys during the first few days after injection, which resolved spontaneously.

In all groups (vehicle, cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE 1 × 1010 GC/eye, and cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE 5 × 1010 GC/eye), fundus images showed pigmentary changes at the boundary of the injected blebs, particularly prominent in the lower region, 1 week after injection; this change was observed in all eyes and persisted until the end of our observation. OCT revealed granular hyper-reflective deposits above the RPE layer in the same region immediately after injection (Fig. 3C). Follow-up OCT showed progressive resolution of these hyperreflective deposits, and by the end of week 4, these deposits were nearly resolved. Additionally, OCT revealed prominent hyperreflective deposits in the foveal center when the injected fluid reached the foveal region, with greater intensity than in the peripheral retina. These deposits were resolved by week 8.

Quantitative OCT parameters were used to assess the changes in the thickness of the inner and outer retinas of the injected bleb region pre-injection and at 12 weeks post-injection. The inner retinal thickness remained unchanged pre-injection and at 12 weeks post-injection in all three groups (vehicle group, P = 0.304; cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE 1 × 1010 GC/eye group, P = 0.683; cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE 5 × 1010 GC/eye group, P = 0.297). The outer retinal thickness in the cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE 5 × 1010 GC/eye group significantly decreased (pre-injection: 101 ± 0.72 µm; post-injection: 87.11 ± 2.01 µm; P = 0.008), mainly due to RPE layer thinning (pre-injection: 54.11 ± 3.97 µm; post-injection: 43.56 ± 2.94 µm; P = 0.0163) (Supplementary Fig. S2). Meanwhile, no significant change was observed in the vehicle group (pre-injection: 92 ± 5.62 µm; post-injection: 92.33 ± 9.94 µm; P = 0.937) or the cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE 1 × 1010 GC/eye group (pre-injection: 89.33 ± 1.19 µm; post-injection: 79.56 ± 6.68 µm; P = 0.204) (Fig. 3D).

Twelve weeks after the injection, scotopic ERG was performed. The amplitudes of the A-waves and B-waves (measured with dark-adapted light intensity of 0.01, 3.0, or 10.0 cd s/m2) showed no deterioration in either the cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE 1 × 1010 GC/eye group or the 5 × 1010 GC/eye group compared to the vehicle group (all P < 0.05) (Figs. 3E, 3F).

Efficacy Assessment of cceAAV-Aflibercept-Fc-YTE in Laser-Induced CNV Model of cceAAV-Aflibercept-Fc-YTE in NHPs

The efficacy of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE was evaluated using a laser-induced CNV model. Cynomolgus monkeys received subretinal injections of vehicle or cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (1 × 1010 GC/eye and 5 × 1010 GC/eye). Four weeks later, the perimacular area was treated with laser photocoagulation to induce CNV. In the aflibercept group, aflibercept was injected intravitreally on the same day after laser treatment. The number of grade IV CNV lesions (the gold standard for evaluating the efficacy of treatment for nAMD,15 reflecting the severity of neovascular dysfunction) was determined 4 weeks after laser treatment using FFA (Fig. 4A). In the vehicle group, 62.5% of lesions were grade IV. The percentage of grade IV lesions was significantly reduced in both the cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE 1 × 1010 GC/eye group (2.1%, P = 0.0057) and the 5 × 1010 GC/eye group (4.2%, P = 0.0233). In the aflibercept group (2 mg/eye), 3.1% of lesions were grade IV. There was no significant difference among the two cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE groups and the aflibercept group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 4B).

In preclinical trials, we validated the safety and efficacy of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE. This strain was named F-CRG-B191 in our clinical trial. In the clinical case, the enrolled patient had received a total of 13 intravitreal anti-VEGF injections with a favorable response. The most recent injection prior to study enrollment was administered on January 17, 2024. After enrollment, a single intravitreal aflibercept injection was given on February 26, 2024, as a pretreatment to confirm responsiveness, followed by subretinal administration of F-CRG-B191 (1.5 × 1010 GC/100 µL/eye) on March 20, 2024. A single bleb was created in the inferotemporal retina, outside the macular region. The retina in the treated area was reattached by the second day. The mild transient anterior segment inflammatory response resolved within approximately 1 week. No persistent inflammatory responses were observed. In the patient, both NAbs and ADA were measured (Supplementary Table S6). The NAb titer remained consistent with no apparent induction post-treatment. ADA results were negative at all tested time points (day −28, day −7, week 4, week 12). Throughout the 24-week follow-up period, the patient exhibited stable IOP (Fig. 5A), and both clinical imaging and UWFP showed no significant adverse events, such as retinal hemorrhage, detachment, or abnormal pigmentation in the injection area or other areas of the fundus. However, a small area of RPE change was observed in the UWFP at the injection site 1 week after injection (Fig. 5B), with an abnormal punctate hypoautofluorescence signal on FAF. In the injected bleb region, a mottled, heterogeneous autofluorescence signal persisted throughout the follow-up period (Fig. 5C). OCT revealed no significant structural abnormalities (Fig. 5D). The patient had a baseline BCVA of 23 ETDRS letters. After injection, BCVA improved to 31 letters by 12 weeks and 44 letters by 24 weeks, representing a remarkable gain of 21 letters compared to baseline (Fig. 6A).

Discussion

Using an AAV8 serotype capsid combined with a covalently closed-end DNA genome encoding a modified aflibercept fusion protein, cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (clinical trial name: F-CRG-B191) was developed to generate aflibercept-Fc-YTE for VEGF inhibition. This study presents the development and evaluation of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE and its primary clinical results. Compared to other vectors and the original aflibercept, cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE demonstrated high efficiency in reducing CNV in both mice and the NHP model, along with a strong safety profile in NHPs, as shown by the absence of significant immune responses or retinal toxicity. In one patient with nAMD, continuous reductions in CNV and improvements in visual acuity were observed after injection, with no adverse events reported. These results indicate that this is a promising alternative treatment for nAMD.

Anti-VEGF drugs are currently the first line of treatment for nAMD.22 However, the necessity for frequent invasive intravitreal injections and follow-up imposes a severe burden on patients, their families, and the healthcare system. Therefore, long-term therapies are urgently needed. Gene therapy has gained attention as a “biofactory” for the continuous production of anti-VEGF drugs.23–25 Following cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE injection, aflibercept-Fc-YTE expression peaked at week 4 and persisted up to week 24 (our last observation).

The cceAAV vectors used in this study are a novel type of scAAV designed to avoid issues with traditional scAAV vectors. cceAAV vectors significantly enhance transgene expression both in vitro and in vivo, as demonstrated by the increased efficiency of gene delivery and expression stability in animal models.8 To maximize expression, we used a CMV promoter with AAV8, which ensures strong and widespread expression—an advantageous feature for secreted proteins such as aflibercept. Consistently, our study found that aflibercept-Fc-YTE expression was significantly higher in the cceAAV group than in the ssAAV and scAAV groups. Also, cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE demonstrated therapeutic effects similar to aflibercept at doses as low as 3 × 107 GC/eye. This minimum effective dose (MED) was much lower than that reported in earlier studies (4 × 108 to 1 × 1010 GC/eye).8,26–28 This again supports the high efficiency of our cceAAV and aflibercept-Fc-YTE.

Aflibercept, known as a VEGF trap, offers several advantages, including broad binding, high affinity, and long duration. Except for binding to both VEGF-A and VEGF-B, aflibercept was found to be the only drug that could bind to placental growth factor (PlGF), enabling broader angiogenesis inhibition.29 In addition, the long half-life of aflibercept (11 days)30 exceeds the half-lives of ranibizumab (7 days) and bevacizumab (9 days),31 allowing for longer treatment intervals. The triple mutation M252Y/S254T/T256E, termed YTE, has been reported to increase the serum half-life of YTE-containing human IgG in cynomolgus monkeys.32 To further improve its half-life and properties, we genetically modified the aflibercept fusion protein. The Fc segment contains three amino acid mutations, the M252Y/S254T/T256E (YTE) Fc variant. In our experiment, however, the level of VEGF trap in the cceAAV-aflibercept-YTE group was similar to that in the cceAAV-aflibercept group in mice (Supplementary Fig. S1). The reason for this is not fully understood. A previous study noted that the effect of prolonged drug half-life caused by YTE mutations in the Fc fragment differs across species.33 Therefore, future studies using NHPs or clinical studies may provide further insights in this field. The modified group still showed better efficacy in treating CNV than the original cceAAV-aflibercept group.

This study used a subretinal injection (SRI) to deliver cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE, a technique used in most clinical trials for its ability to direct place therapy in situ.25 SRI offers several advantages due to its proximity to target cells such as RPE and photoreceptor cells, as well as its immune-privileged environment.34 In a separate biodistribution study, aflibercept-Fc-YTE levels were quantified in ocular tissues following subretinal administration of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (5 × 1010 GC/eye). Aflibercept was predominantly detected in the retina and choroid, consistent with its intended site of action (Supplementary Table S5). Compared to systemic AAV delivery, this method results in a weaker immune response, including anterior chamber-associated immune deviation (ACAID).35 In our study, no complications (retinal tears, retinal detachment, or retinal/choroid hemorrhage) were observed in either primates or humans following SRI therapy, confirming its safety.

In our study, both NHPs and the single patient with nAMD showed rapid resolution of injection-induced retinal detachment on the second day, accompanied by mild, transient anterior segment inflammation. Instances of intraocular inflammation, such as the presence of vitreous cells, have been documented in other preclinical studies of retinal gene therapy36 associated with high doses and AAV2 capsids; however, no such response was observed in our study. Additionally, mild retinal pigmentary changes have been reported as an adverse reaction in several clinical trials.37,38 In NHPs, we observed hyperpigmentation at the boundary of the injected blebs on fundus images, but not in our patient. Notably, Weed et al.39 observed outer retinal and RPE changes following subretinal injection of AAV2-hRPE65v2 in NHPs but not in humans, a pattern consistent with our findings. This discrepancy may be due to the greater hydraulic force required to overcome the adhesion between the retina and RPE in NHPs compared to humans.40,41 Our patient (78 years old) was much older than the monkeys in our study (3 to 7 years, adolescence42), and inflammatory reactions and cell proliferation are more prominent in younger individuals.43,44

In NHP, retinal thickness measurement by OCT at the injected bleb region revealed that the outer retinal thickness decreased in the high-dose group but not in the low-dose group. The inner retinal thickness remained unchanged. Also in NHPs, histological evaluation at 9 months post-injection showed no discernible structural alterations in injected bleb regions in eyes treated with vehicle or cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (1 × 1010 or 5 × 1010 GC/eye) (Supplementary Fig. S3). However, due to sample limitations, a quantitative analysis was not performed. On the other hand, several studies have reported locally extensive ONL thinning in high-dose AAV-mediated retinal gene therapy, which generally coincided with but was not always limited to the region of the subretinal injection.45–47 However, in our study, the change was limited to the injected bleb area.

The functional integrity of the retina in NHPs was assessed via ERG, following the International Society for Clinical Electrophysiology of Vision (ISCEV) standards. Scotopic responses were recorded at flash strengths of 0.01, 3.0, and 10.0 photopic cd·s/m–2 (dark-adapted light intensity of 0.01, 3.0, and 10.0), evaluating rod and mixed rod–cone responses. In the cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE group, A-wave and B-wave amplitudes remained stable across all light intensities, similar to those of the vehicle group and within the range of normal cynomolgus monkey responses reported in previous studies.48 These findings, together with the absence of pigment change at the injected bleb region in the single patient, indicate the safety of cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE.

In terms of efficacy, cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE showed significant benefits. In mice, the CNV area and CNV lesion grading were greatly reduced in all cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE groups. In NHPs, the grade IV lesion rates were 2.1% in the 1 × 1010 GC/eye group and 4.2% in 5 × 1010 GC/eye group, significantly lower than the 62.5% in the vehicle group. Somehow, the cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE dose did not correlate linearly with CNV reduction; the reason was not fully understood but was likely due to inter-individual differences, viral transduction efficiency, and ceiling treatment effects in the laser-induced CNV model.49 Prior studies have reported that the efficacy of anti-VEGF therapy reaches a plateau beyond certain dosages.21,50 Our results indicate that gene therapy can achieve therapeutic efficacy comparable to that of intravitreal aflibercept. The principal clinical advantage of gene therapy lies in its ability to sustain intraocular aflibercept expression after a single administration, thereby addressing the unmet need for durable treatment and reducing the burden of repeated injections in patients with nAMD. In the clinical component of this study, a single patient treated with cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (F-CRG-B191) achieved an anti-angiogenic effect similar to that of a patient as a previous report,51 but at a lower dose. Previously, 6 × 1010 GC/eye or higher was required to maintain or improve BCVA and central retinal thickness in most patients.25 In contrast, our study showed that the dose of 1.5 × 1010 GC/eye reduced central retinal thickness and CNV area during the entire follow-up period. In addition, compared to baseline, the F-CRG-B191 achieved an eight-letter improvement in BCVA at week 12 and a 21-letter improvement at week 24. These observations suggest a favorable safety profile and potential therapeutic efficacy, which is consistent with our preclinical results on mice and NHPs. This safety and efficacy profile might be related to cceAAV and the modification of the original aflibercept protein.

Although in our patient retinal pigmentary changes were not found in fundus observation or UWFP, FAF imaging revealed abnormal signals at the injected site with hypo-AF signals, and mottled heterogeneous fluorescent signals were observed in the injected bleb region. To the best of our knowledge, FAF has not been specifically used to monitor subretinal bleb regions in previous gene therapy studies. Our results suggest the ability of FAF to detect subtle RPE changes following subretinal injection, making it suitable for monitoring RPE changes after subretinal injection in the clinic. Also, in NHPs, OCT found that, if the fovea was covered by the bleb injected, then the subretinal deposits took a longer time to resolve than those at other fundus areas. This finding reminds us that the injected bleb should not involve the foveal area.

There are several limitations in our study. First, because our efficacy assessment was primarily based on the laser-induced CNV model, future studies incorporating other models of ocular neovascularization, such as oxygen-induced retinopathy, would further strengthen the translational relevance of our findings. Second, CNV induction was performed at approximately 4 weeks after vector administration, corresponding to peak transgene expression. This timing may lead to an overestimation of long-term efficacy, and future studies assessing CNV outcomes during the plateau phase of expression are warranted. Third, it should be noted that the NHP sample size was small, and the observed inter-individual variability in vector DNA levels highlights the need for larger-scale studies to evaluate pharmacokinetics more comprehensively. Although the overall retinal structure remained preserved, mild RPE thinning was observed at the injected bleb region in NHPs receiving the highest dose, suggesting a potential dose-related retinal response to gene therapy. However, we acknowledge that a systematic quantitative analysis of non-injected retinal regions was not performed. Fourth, the clinical component of this study was limited to a single patient with a relatively short follow-up duration. And, as dose–response effects can differ across species, additional dose-ranging studies in patients are required to optimize therapeutic efficacy and establish a rational treatment strategy.

Conclusions

We designed and developed cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (F-CRG-B191) as a gene therapy candidate for nAMD, and other anti-VEGF purpose. In a series of experiments, cceAAV-aflibercept-Fc-YTE (F-CRG-B191) exhibited good safety and strong anti-angiogenic properties. These findings support its use as an alternative therapy for nAMD. Future studies with larger numbers of cases, longer follow-up periods, and refined dosing protocols are necessary. Also, long-term effects on RPE and photoreceptors warrant cautious dose optimization and extended observation.

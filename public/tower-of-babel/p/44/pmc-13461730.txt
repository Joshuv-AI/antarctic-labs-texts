To Evaluate Effect of Mechanical Power Directed Lung Protective Ventilation Strategy on Treatment Outcome in Patients with Acute Respiratory Failure – A Prospective Study
Authors: Sriparna Mandal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionMechanical ventilation is a life-saving therapy for patients with acute lung injury, but unfortunately this invasive procedure has induced further lung injury, ventilator-induced lung injury (VILI). The concept of lung injury with positive pressure ventilation was first described by Fothergill,1 but it was not until 1973,the term barotrauma was associated with ventilation,2 and VILI was introduced in 1993.3 Thus, ventilator parameters should be adjusted to optimize gas exchange, with attempt to minimize injury. It has been hypothesized, that the extent of VILI, depends on amount of energy transferred, and tidal volume(VT), plateau pressure(P plat), respiratory rate(RR), and air flow all relate to the amount of energy generated by the ventilator.4 This amount of energy per unit of time, is calculated in Joules per minute (J/min) as “mechanical power” (MP).5–6 MP can be calculated accurately by “power equation”, increasing its applicability in clinical practice. Studies indicated that MP is an independent risk factor, but limited evidence exists regarding MP adjusted to dynamic lung compliance(Cdyn) as mortality indicator.Aims and objectivesTo find correlation of mechanical power (MP) normalised to lung compliance, with ICU mortality.Materials and methodsIt is a prospective observational study, started on May 2024,being conducted in medical ICU of FEHI, Delhi, including patients with Acute respiratory failure (ARF), defined, as ratio of partial pressure of oxygen (PaO2) to fraction of inspired oxygen (FiO2) less than 300 mmHg on admission in ICU.1 Exclusion criteria, those who received treatment with extracorporeal life support; age < 18 yrs; MP ≤ 10 J/min on admission. After data collection, we are using, the simplified MP equation proposed by Gattinoni.7 MP (J/min) is calculated- 0.098 × VT × RR × (P peak—0.5 × ΔP),P peak-peak inspiratory pressureDriving pressure ΔP (cmH2O) = P plat—PEEP, where P plat is plateau pressure and PEEP is positive end-expiratory pressure.Then, MP is adjusted to compliance, as per Ghiani, A. et al8Dynamic lung compliance(Cdyn),defined as the change in volume divided by the change in pressure, MP is normalized by Cdyn - Mechanical Power/Dynamic lung Compliance (MP/Cdyn) (J/min per ml/cm H2O).It is an emerging stress-intensity marker, safe values < 0.3-0.4 J/min×cmH2O/ml, shown to predict weaning outcomes.9ResultsSince the study is ongoing, we are presenting interim results. A total of 32 mechanically ventilated ICU patients have been included. The mean age is 56.7 years, (range 31-79 years). The mean APACHE II score at admission is 26.4,moderate to severe disease burden(range 18-39). Mechanical Power stratification showed marked mortality difference. Patients with MP, 11-20 J/min have mortality rate of 23%, while MP 20-40 J/min group have a significant higher mortality of 92% .Similarly, Patients with lower MP/Cdyn (<0.5) have mortality of 27 %, compared to 92% mortality in high MP/Cdyn(>0.5) category, indicating a potentially stronger predictor of mortality than absolute MP alone.ConclusionThe interim result analysis reveals that higher MP/Cdyn value, correlate strongly with ICU mortality and may serve as prognostic indicator. However we need to enroll more patients for the outcome.Keywords: Mechanical Power, Dynamic Lung Compliance, Ventilator Induced Lung Injury, ICU Mortality, Normalised Mechanical Power

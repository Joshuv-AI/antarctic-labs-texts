Enhanced forecasting of friction and cohesion of augmented unsaturated soil with nanostructured quarry fines (NQF) addition
Authors: Viroon Kamchoom, Duc Bui Van, Shahab Hosseini, Mohammadreza Alimoradijazi, Hasel Amini-Khoshalan, Ali Mirzaghorbanali, Behshad Jodeiri Shokri, Krishna Prakash Arunachalam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Shear strength parameters such as friction angle and cohesion are fundamental to solving geotechnical engineering problems related to slope stability, foundation design, and earthwork construction. This study presents the prediction of friction angle (Fi) and cohesion (Nc) of an unsaturated lateritic soil using three intelligent learning techniques: Support Vector Machine (SVM), Radial Basis Function (RBF), and Multilayer Perceptron (MLP), with Linear Multivariate Regression (LMR) adopted as a baseline model to evaluate agreement between input and output variables. The motivation for employing machine learning approaches stems from the limitations of complex laboratory testing and the need for reliable predictive tools that can support design and field applications. The investigated soil, classified as A-7-6 and poorly graded, exhibited coefficients of uniformity and curvature of 2.05 and 0.84, respectively. It was characterized by high plasticity and significant clay content, with a clay fraction of 23.02%, clay activity of 2, friction angle of 15°, maximum dry density of 1.84 g/cm3 at an optimum moisture content of 16.2%, and was tested under cyclic direct shear conditions. Multiple datasets were generated from varying treatment conditions and soil descriptors, forming the basis for model development. Eleven input parameters were used to predict Fi and Nc, and model performance was evaluated using Variance Accounted For (VAF), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and coefficient of determination (R2). The results indicate that RBF and MLP outperformed SVM and LMR in both training and testing phases for predicting cohesion and friction angle, demonstrating superior generalization capability. Sensitivity analysis using the Cosine Domain Method revealed that unsaturated unit weight had the greatest influence on friction angle prediction, while clay content was the most influential parameter for cohesion. Among all models, MLP achieved the highest accuracy and overall predictive performance. Based on this optimal model, a Graphical User Interface was developed to enable users to input soil parameters and obtain rapid predictions, providing a practical tool for researchers and practitioners in geotechnical engineering.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-43458-z.

Introduction

An accurate and trustworthy prediction of structural response and displacement properties is required for the safest and a programming (GP) was used by Shen et al. to anticipate the Nc and Fi of sandstone rocks. In the lack of triaxial observations, the developed approach offered sufficient predictivion accuracy and capability1. Utilizing three different variables, namely UCS, UTS, and confining stress (σ3), Mahmoodzadeh et al.2 used Gaussian process regression (GPR), support vector regression (SVR), decision trees (DT), and long short-term memory (LSTM) to forecast both Nc and Fi of intact rocks. To anticipate the Nc of limestone, Khandelwal et al.3 used a variety of techniques, including multivariate regression analysis, multi-layer perceptron neural networks, and optimized artificial neural network by genetic algorithm. In constructing a predictor model, Hiba et al. used real well-logging dataset. In this regard, the ML techniques such as decision tree and random forest, were used to conduct the investigation. The input parameters for the prediction of Nc and Fi were bulk density, neutron porosity, and compression time4. Kainthola et al.5 developed relationships among physico-mechanical parameters using an adaptive neuro-fuzzy inference system (ANFIS) and multivariate regression. In oanother study, Sonmez et al.6 used 529 data points and three parameters involving uniaxial compressive strength, unit weight and elastic modulus of intact rock (Ei). They trained an ANN for predicting rock modulus. The accuracy of their results was acceptable based on R2 of 0.922. Zorlu et al.7 presented the both ANN and LMR models to model UCS of sandstones. They used 138 cases including petrography-based models for predicting UCS. They obtained a correlation of 0.62 and 0.53 between measured and predicted values for testing and training parts of the LMR model, respectively. Notably, the correlation between measured and predicted UCS in the ANN model was 0.87 and 0.82 for testing and training sets, respectively. The UCS prediction of some clay-bearing rocks has been conducted by Cevik et al.8. Cabalar et al.9 reviewed applications of ANFIS model in geotechnical engineering. The researches mentioned above proposes that numerous benefits, although insufficient, insights have been offered in terms of anticipating Nc and Fi. In some situations, the employment of a specific process may be suitable, whereas it may not be. Further specifically, it has been highlighted in the literature that few researches have been conducted to determine Nc and Fi, particularly when employing different types of rock. For p properly installing underground engineering works, innovative methods are required to develop an efficient prediction approach for forecasting Nc and Fi of rocks.

In this work and in order to overcome the challenges of the state-of-the-art experimentation for the determination of shear properties, soil properties with slightly less difficulty in their determination have been utilized to predict the Fi and Nc of unsaturated lateritic soil augmented with hybrid composite binder and treated with nanostructured quarry fines in order to achieve a more sustainable earthwork design, construction and performance evaluation. Meanwhile, the main focus of this prediction exercise is to employ the intelligent procedures to forecast the behavior of Nc and Fi of a treated unsaturated soil in order to designing, constructing and field performance monitoring. The emphasis of the exercise has been; (a) conducting multiple experiments to generate data points for the inputs parameters, (b) conduct linear multivariate regression model as a baseline regression to determine the degree of agreement between the input and output parameters, (c) conduct support vector machine prediction of the outputs, (d) apply radial basis function network to predict the output parameters (e) employ multilayer perceptron technique to also predict the output parameters, and (f) validate and conduct sensitivity analysis of the model. Various other learning techniques have been commonly used in various prediction efforts in the areas of geotechnical engineering but SVM, RBF and MLP have scarcely been used. Hence this work presents an innovative approach to predicting shear parameters of an unsaturated soil utilized in earthwork projects. The main motivation of these study were simultansioulypredition of Fi and Nc. To do this, we have designed an GUI framwork for intelligence predicting friction and cohesion of augmented unsaturated soil with nanostructured quarry fines addition. This environment provides a shematic architecture to enter input values and adjust model setting and then the output values are predcied based on considered input parameters. These GUI environment can be used for all cases that measured input parametes of this study as well as can be updated for another geotechnical fiels.

The strength of untreated or treated compacted lateritic soils especially the problematic ones depend on the ability of the soil to resist shearing forces10,11. As pavement structures are exposed to dynamic axial loading, the subgrade bears the load and tends to fail when the design limits are exceeded. In certain other cases, factors like moisture ingress through the rise and fall of water tables and may be through moisture migration due to run off over cracked pavements may affect the performance or even the durability of the underlying structure12. So, it’s important that the shear parameters like Nc and friction are put into design consideration. In problematic soils also like black cotton soils, unsaturated lateritic soils, etc. that fail due to swell and shrink cycles as they are exposed to continuous moisture effect10,13, shear strength is considered a very important factor, which binds the particles together in order to respond substantially to modification or stabilization protocols aimed at improving their quality11. However, the laboratory determination of Nc and friction requires sophisticated triaxial in order to get reliable values for design purposes11.

LMR is a common technique in multivariate analysis. In general, there is an establishment of a linear connection between the response variable and one or more explanatory variables, the concept of regression analysis14. The fundamental aim of LMR is to explore the relations between several independent or predictor variables and a dependent or response variable16–20. Like many machine learning methods, in this method, the model making process includes two stages of training and testing. At the end of the training phase, the generalizability of the trained model is evaluated using experimental data. Here, the stimulus function in the middle layer is described as the Gaussian function, while the output layer is characterized by a linear function. The training of most RBF networks, is divided into 2 parts, the first part is often unguided learning which is determined by clustering methods in which parameters of basic functions (centers and widths) are achieved using input information and the second part is a guided learning. Linear regression and slope reduction methods are used to obtain the weights between the middle layer and the output layer36,37. Multi-layer perceptron (MLP)

Since I980s, MLP networks are among the most applicable types of ANN models. These networks are structured in such a way that involve of three main layer such as input layer, hidden layer(s) and output layer(s)38,39. Each of the layers of these networks consists of interconnected processing nodes called neurons in which signals are transmitted between these neurons through connecting weighted links40. The number of inputs and outputs in dataset is the same as the number of nodes in the input and output layers. Moreover, number of hidden layers and their related neurons as computational nodes are estimated according to the complicity of the problem and trial and error method is usually applied for assessing the optimum neurons’ number in hidden layer/s despite of some suggested heuristics and formulations for this aim41.

The tansig, logsig and purelin are among the several forms of activation functions predominantly used in MLP networks. Moreover, the Levenberg – Marquardt algorithm is more applied for network training in a feed forward-back propagation process. Simpson gave a detailed documentation of these procedures42.

Research gap and statement of novelty

Although previous studies have successfully applied machine learning techniques to predict cohesion and friction angle, the majority of these works focus predominantly on intact rocks such as sandstone and limestone, or on unconfined compressive strength and modulus prediction rather than direct shear parameters of soils. Moreover, many of the reported models rely on limited input variables or single-algorithm approaches without comprehensive comparative evaluation. There is a clear scarcity of studies addressing the simultaneous prediction of friction angle and cohesion of unsaturated lateritic soils, particularly when such soils are chemically augmented with hybrid cementitious binders and nanostructured additives. In addition, limited attention has been given to integrating extensive experimental datasets with multiple soil index and compaction-related parameters in a unified intelligent modeling framework. The application of SVM, RBF, and MLP collectively for stabilized unsaturated soil systems remains relatively underexplored, especially with sensitivity analysis and practical implementation tools. Therefore, a gap exists in developing a robust, comparative, and practically deployable predictive system for shear strength parameters of treated unsaturated lateritic soils used in earthwork engineering. The novelty of this study lies in the simultaneous prediction of friction angle and cohesion of nanostructured quarry fines–augmented unsaturated lateritic soil using a comparative intelligent learning framework integrating SVM, RBF, MLP, and LMR as a baseline model. Unlike previous studies centered on rock mechanics or single-parameter prediction, this research focuses on a problematic unsaturated soil system enhanced with hybrid composite binders, generating multiple experimental datasets to capture its complex physicochemical and mechanical behavior. The study not only evaluates and compares the predictive capability of different machine learning techniques but also performs sensitivity analysis to identify dominant influencing parameters. Furthermore, the development of a dedicated graphical user interface based on the best-performing model provides a practical and transferable tool for rapid prediction and design support in geotechnical applications. This integrated experimental–computational–implementation approach distinguishes the present work from existing literature and contributes a novel framework for sustainable earthwork design and performance assessment.

Methodology

Materials

The soil sample was gathered at a profundity of 1 m from a borrow pit located in Umuahia South of Abia State, Nigeria, which has been mapped and presented in Fig. 2 and this has been used as an origin of lateritic soil for a long time. The lumps were removed and the soil was prepared and placed in the open air for three days to dry.

Fig. 2A view of the location of the studied areas (a) geographical mapping and (b) google locator43(the figure is based on Google earth).

A view of the location of the studied areas (a) geographical mapping and (b) google locator43(the figure is based on Google earth).

The rice husk was taken from domestic rice mills across Abakaliki, Nigeria as waste of no financial value where rice farming and processing is carried out and the indiscriminate husk disposal managing plan is inadequate. The husk was openly combusted and through this process, the rice husk ash (RHA) was produced. In addition, the RHA was mixed with 5% dosage of hydrated lime by weight of the ash and allowed for 48 h for producing the activated ash called the hybrid cement (HC) composite. The HC is one of the main locally generated green supplementary cementitious materials (GSCMs) in this experiment with developed cementation attributes. Furthermore, quarry dust (QD) was also gathered as waste dust from mine blasting/aggregate processing sites at Amasiri, Ebonyi State, Nigeria. The QD was further pulverized to fineness and sifted using a 200 nm sifter to produce the nanostructured quarry particles applied as a second connection additive in the stabilization protocol. These connection materials; HC and NQF, for possessing the right proportions of Al2O3, SiO2 and Fe2O3 by weight were seen to satisfy the demands for contents to be typed as pozzolans44. Lastly, the soil, HC and NQF were stashed for use in the fixation exercise.

Methods

In order to determine the soil characterization and classification, the specifications of the British Standard International (BSI) were employed to conduct preliminary tests on the materials45. The particle size analysis, Atterberg limits, compaction, specific gravity, Nc and the Fi were performed based on the above design consideration. Furthermore, the x-ray fluorescence (XRF) exposure and the scanning electron microscopy (SEM) tests were conducted following the specifications of the ASTM E1621-13, 2013, in order to find out the morphology and oxides composition of the composite binder materials (RHA, HC and NQF)46. The models were constructed and blended to high homogeneity and the results were seen and saved in order to accomplish reliable and accurate results. In addition, to find out the absorbance of the nanoparticle exposure of the material, the UV-Vis spectrophotometer test was carried out on the NQF. Additionally, the treatment exercise on the soil was carried out according to the demands of the BS1924, 1990, and multiple data entries (mixes) were produced for different dosages of HC and NQF between 0% and 12% by weight of dry soil47. In addition, the manufactured datasets (see appendix) were advanced in the intelligent anticipation exercise utilizing LMR, SVM, RBF and MLP approaches. The total database was partitioned into 70% and 30% for training and validation, respectively.

Predicting with LMR

The relationship between input and output parameters (IAP) was examined by multivariate regression before the prediction of the Nc and Fi, using the SVM, MLP and RBF methods were executed. A database consisting of 121 data sets was prepared with 11 efficient input parameters for forecasting Nc and Fi. Table 2 illustrates the range of the IAP employed in this article. Moreover, the histogram plot for the inputs are demonstrated in Fig. 10 and the relationship between IAP as Pearson’s correlation matrix is provided in Fig. 11. Pearson’s correlation coefficient shows the relationship between two variable and used for detecting multicollinearity problem in the training models. The higher value of this coefficient, the stronger relationship between two parameters and vice versa. Also, this coefficient determines the direction of correlation between two parameters well, whether it is negative or positive.

Further, Table 2 provides important insight into how nanostructured quarry fines (NQF) influenced the behavior of the multiple linear regression (MLR) model through its statistical characteristics relative to other input parameters. Although NQF was introduced in a relatively small dosage range, varying from 0 to 1.2% with a mean value of 0.6%, its role in the regression framework is better understood by examining its dispersion and interaction with other soil descriptors rather than its absolute magnitude alone. The low standard deviation (0.03) and variance (0.12) associated with NQF indicate that the dataset for this variable is tightly clustered and well controlled experimentally. This limited scatter is particularly important for an MLR model, which is sensitive to noise and multicollinearity among predictors. The controlled variation of NQF implies that its contribution to the regression coefficients is stable and not distorted by excessive randomness. Consequently, even though the numerical range of NQF is smaller compared to parameters such as plasticity index or unsaturated unit weight, its effect on the predicted outputs is captured consistently by the linear formulation. From a mechanistic perspective, the introduction of NQF modifies the soil fabric by filling micro-voids and improving particle packing, which indirectly affects parameters such as maximum dry density, partially compacted dry density, and plasticity-related indices. These parameters show comparatively higher variances and standard deviations, suggesting that the influence of NQF propagates through them rather than acting in isolation. In the MLR model, this manifests as NQF contributing to friction angle and cohesion prediction through its linear association with densification- and plasticity-related variables. The relatively narrow spread of NQF ensures that its regression coefficient reflects a genuine trend rather than compensating for experimental uncertainty. In relation to the output variables, friction angle and cohesion exhibit moderate variances of 4.36 and 0.40, respectively, indicating measurable but controlled changes in shear behavior across the dataset. The presence of NQF, even within a small dosage window, contributes to these variations by enhancing interparticle bonding and frictional resistance. In an MLR context, this translates to NQF acting as a stabilizing predictor that improves model fit by explaining part of the incremental increase in Fi and Nc associated with microstructural refinement of the unsaturated lateritic soil. Overall, the statistical indicators demonstrate that NQF positively influenced the MLR model by providing a low-noise, well-distributed input variable whose effects are linearly transferable to key shear strength parameters. Its restrained variability enhances regression robustness, reduces the risk of overfitting, and supports reliable interpretation of regression coefficients, thereby reinforcing the suitability of MLR as a baseline model in assessing the contribution of nanostructured quarry fines to unsaturated soil behavior.

Predicting with SVM method

As introduced by John52, Sequential Minimal Optimization (SMO) is a novel technique for SVM training among many used algorithms. The SMO is a straightforward method that is capable of speedily solving the quadratic SVM programming issue without any added matrix storage and is free from any use of quadratic programming optimization steps. Using the Osuna theorem to ensure convergence, the SMO breaks down the general quadratic programming problem into quadratic programming subsets. SMO is comprised of two components. One is an analytical method of solving for the two Lagrange multipliers, while the other is an innovative technique for selecting multipliers in optimization25,30,33. The fact that the solution of Lagrange multiplication can be analytically obtained makes the SMO advantageous. Therefore, the optimization of numerical quadratic programming is totally shunned. Instead of calling the entire quota library routine, the internal loop of the algorithm can be described in a small code. Even if the problem-solving algorithm requires more optimization components, the overall cost of time is greatly abridged due to the rapid resolution of each subproblem. Furthermore, extra matrix storage is not needed in SMO. Consequently, extensive SVM training issues can be saved in the memory of a usual PC or workstation. SMO does not involve matrix algorithm, this therefore facilitates its numerical accuracy30,52,53. According to the linear regression method, the ratio of the testing to training data is 70% (85 data) to 30% (36 data). It is notable to mention that these training and testing data are randomly selected and used in all applied methods in this study. The incorporation of nanostructured quarry fines (NQF) into the dataset contributed positively to the predictive capability of the SVM model, as reflected in the high performance indicators reported in Table 4. Using the SMO algorithm for efficient training, the SVM model achieved very strong agreement between measured and predicted values for friction angle (Fi), with training and testing R² values of 0.9952 and 0.9960, respectively. The corresponding RMSE values of 0.1490 for training and 0.1309 for testing indicate low prediction error and strong generalization. These results suggest that the inclusion of NQF as one of the input variables enhanced the separability and structure of the dataset in feature space, allowing the SVM to effectively construct an optimal hyperplane that captures the influence of stabilization on frictional behavior. The consistency between training and testing metrics further indicates that the effect of NQF was learned in a stable and non-overfitted manner. For cohesion (Nc), the SVM model also demonstrated strong performance during training, with a VAF of 99.0736% and R² of 0.9908, indicating that NQF contributed meaningfully to explaining bonding improvements within the stabilized soil matrix. Although the testing performance for Nc showed a slight reduction (R² = 0.9577), the predictive accuracy remained high, confirming that the structural changes induced by NQF were sufficiently captured by the nonlinear mapping capability of SVM. The improvement in cohesion behavior can be attributed to the micro-filling and cementitious interaction effects of NQF, which enhance interparticle bonding and are effectively modeled through the kernel-based learning mechanism. Overall, NQF strengthened the input–output relationship within the SVM framework, enabling accurate prediction of both friction angle and cohesion under unsaturated stabilized conditions.

This shows that clay content has a more direct relationship with unsaturated soil cohesion than other selected parameters considered in this prediction exercise. Also, a considerable influence can be observed with the addition of the composite supplementary cementing materials on the Nc but not as felt in frictional angle response.

Comparatively, unlike most previous studies, which primarily focused on intact rocks or sandstones and employed machine learning techniques such as GP, GPR, SVR, DT, LSTM, ANN, and ANFIS to predict shear-related parameters, the present research specifically addresses unsaturated lateritic soil treated with nanostructured quarry fines and hybrid composite binders. Earlier works largely concentrated on rock mechanics problems using limited physico-mechanical indices such as UCS, UTS, elastic modulus, bulk density, or petrographic characteristics as predictors. In contrast, this study targets a problematic A-7-6 lateritic soil under unsaturated conditions, where moisture sensitivity, clay activity, and stabilization additives significantly influence shear behavior. The incorporation of nanostructured quarry fines as a stabilizing agent introduces a material system that has not been sufficiently explored in previous machine learning-based prediction frameworks for shear strength parameters. Another key advancement lies in the simultaneous prediction of both friction angle and cohesion for an augmented unsaturated soil system. Many previous investigations addressed either a single strength parameter or focused on rock materials rather than compacted, treated soils subjected to cyclic loading conditions. The present study integrates extensive experimental data generated from cyclic direct shear tests and uses multiple independent variables representing physical and compaction-related properties that are easier to determine than advanced triaxial parameters. This reduces reliance on sophisticated and costly laboratory testing while maintaining high prediction accuracy. Methodologically, this work introduces a structured comparative framework involving SVM, RBF, and MLP models with linear multivariate regression as a baseline for benchmarking agreement between inputs and outputs. While ANN-based models have been previously used, the combined and systematic comparison of SVM, RBF, and MLP for predicting shear parameters of nanomodified unsaturated lateritic soil is scarce in the literature. The superior performance of RBF and particularly MLP, validated through VAF, RMSE, MAE, and R2 across both training and testing datasets, demonstrates enhanced generalization capability relative to several earlier models reported in rock mechanics studies. Furthermore, the application of the cosine domain method for sensitivity analysis provides deeper insight into the governing influence of unsaturated unit weight on friction angle and clay content on cohesion, offering a quantitative interpretation of parameter importance that is often absent in prior studies. An additional advancement is the development of a Graphical User Interface based on the best-performing MLP model. Unlike many previous predictive studies that remain theoretical or limited to algorithmic reporting, this research translates the trained intelligence model into a practical, user-oriented tool. The GUI framework allows engineers and researchers to input measurable soil parameters and instantly obtain predicted friction angle and cohesion values, thereby bridging the gap between academic modeling and field application. The framework is also adaptable and can be updated for other soil types or geotechnical datasets, enhancing its scalability.

Practical application

Practical applications of this study are significant for earthwork design, pavement subgrade evaluation, embankment construction, and field performance monitoring in regions dominated by lateritic and moisture-sensitive soils. By providing a reliable alternative to repeated triaxial or advanced shear testing, the proposed models can reduce laboratory time, testing costs, and experimental complexity. The predictive system supports rapid decision-making during stabilization design with nanostructured quarry fines, enabling optimization of binder content and compaction parameters. It is particularly useful in resource-limited environments where advanced testing facilities may not be readily available. Ultimately, the integration of intelligent modeling, sensitivity analysis, and a deployable GUI platform represents a comprehensive advancement in the prediction of shear strength parameters of treated unsaturated soils, extending current knowledge beyond existing machine learning applications in rock mechanics and conventional soil stabilization studies.

Does adjunctive endoscopic biliary drainage improve outcomes beyond glucocorticoids alone in IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease?
Authors: Zinan Zhang, Yanbin Wei, Jiafei Peng, Yan Zhao, Xin Yao, Ye Zong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundImmunoglobulin G4 (IgG4)-related pancreatobiliary disease typically manifests as obstructive jaundice, complicating therapeutic choices. Glucocorticoids (GCs) are the primary treatment with many challenges, including high recurrence rates and patient tolerance variability. Endoscopic biliary drainage (EBD) is used to alleviate obstruction, but its role during treatment in IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease is still contested. There remains a paucity of research comparing the potential benefits of combination therapy with GCs and EBD versus GCs monotherapy.MethodsThis retrospective study divided 55 IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease patients into two groups: GCs and GCs + EBD groups. We collected clinical data at the 1st, 3rd, 6th, and 12th months post-treatment to compare treatment efficacy, complication rates, and recurrence. Furthermore, logistic regression was used to analyze independent risk factors for recurrence.ResultsThe results demonstrated that both treatment regimens significantly reduced total bilirubin (TBIL) and IgG4 levels within one year. The common bile duct diameter significantly decreased at the 6th and 12th months post-treatment compared to baseline. Except for significantly greater TBIL improvement in the GCs+EBD group at the 12th month, no other statistically significant differences were observed between groups. No statistically significant difference was observed in the cumulative recurrence rate within one year after treatment between the GCs and GCs+EBD groups. Finally, we identified elevated baseline IgG4 levels as an independent risk factor for post-treatment recurrence.ConclusionsThe combination therapy of GCs and EBD did not exhibit significant differences in immunologic remission, complication rates, or recurrence compared with GCs monotherapy for IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease. Nonetheless, EBD showed certain benefits in alleviating biliary obstruction.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-025-04356-2.

Introduction

Immunoglobulin G4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a chronic systemic inflammatory disorder mediated by immune mechanisms. Its characteristic pathological features include dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltration, storiform fibrosis, and obliterative phlebitis, usually accompanied by elevated serum IgG4 levels [1]. Although global epidemiological data remain limited, a population-based study in the United States has estimated the incidence of IgG4-RD to be approximately 0.78 to 1.39 per 100,000 people [2]. In China, IgG4-related lesions account for up to 95.3% of immune-related pancreatic diseases [3]. IgG4-RD has a marked propensity for multi-organ involvement, among which the pancreatobiliary system is one of the most commonly affected sites. This subset is collectively referred to as IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease, which primarily includes type 1 autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP) and IgG4-related sclerosing cholangitis (IgG4-SC). These two conditions often coexist, with clinical studies reporting a comorbidity rate of up to 70% [4]. The clinical manifestations of IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease resemble those of pancreatobiliary malignancies, frequently presenting as obstructive jaundice, elevated cholestatic enzymes, and strictures of the pancreatic or bile ducts, thereby increasing the complexity of differential diagnosis and therapeutic decision-making [5, 6].

Glucocorticoids (GCs) remain the first-line treatment for IgG4-RD, and international consensus guidelines recommend their use as the standard induction therapy [7]. However, previous studies have demonstrated that GCs monotherapy is associated with a high recurrence rate, ranging from 20% to 40%, either during induction, tapering, or after discontinuation [8–10]. In addition, a subset of patients exhibits suboptimal response or poor tolerance to GCs. These have become a significant challenge in clinical practice and has prompted the exploration of adjunctive treatment strategies.

In recent years, endoscopic biliary drainage (EBD) has gradually emerged as an important intervention for relieving biliary obstruction and accelerating the resolution of jaundice [11, 12]. Currently, emerging studies have EBD to manage biliary obstruction in IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease [13]. However, the necessity of employing EBD for biliary drainage in IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease remains controversial, primarily because EBD typically requires concurrent endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP), which associated with common complications such as cholangitis and pancreatitis [14, 15]. Current study have explored the efficacy of combining EBD with GCs for IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease, as EBD alone has limited therapeutic effects [16]. Nevertheless, there is still a substantial lack of high-quality studies directly comparing the therapeutic efficacy and clinical benefits of combination therapy with GCs and EBD versus GCs monotherapy.

In this study, we conducted a single-center retrospective analysis to evaluate whether the combination therapy of GCs and EBD demonstrates superior efficacy compared to GCs monotherapy, while simultaneously assessing the safety profile of the combined therapy.

Methods

Patients

This retrospective study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Friendship Hospital, Capital Medical University (BFHHZS20250014). As the study retrospectively collected existing medical records from our center, the Ethics Committee of Beijing Friendship Hospital, Capital Medical University waived the requirement for informed consent. All patient treatments were conducted in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations.

Study design

This study retrospectively collected data from patients who were initially diagnosed with IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease at our center from January 2010 to January 2024. Patients with a history of autoimmune pancreatitis were not included in this study. The diagnose of IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease was made in accordance with the Japanese clinical diagnostic criteria [17, 18], integrating clinical manifestations, imaging examinations, laboratory tests, histopathological examinations, and follow-up to establish a definitive diagnosis of IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease. According to the study protocol, patients with initially confirmed IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease who had hyperbilirubinemia [serum total bilirubin (TBIL) levels >17.1 µmol/L] and received either combined GCs therapy with EBD or GCs monotherapy were enrolled in this study. Patients who did not receive any of the aforementioned treatments were excluded. Those who failed to complete the required follow-up visits, fewer than two out of the four scheduled follow-ups at the 1 st, 3rd, 6th, and 12th months post-treatment, were also excluded. The enrollment process of this study, including inclusion and exclusion criteria, is shown in Fig. 1.

All enrolled patients were divided into two groups based on their primary intervention: the GCs monotherapy group (GCs group) and the GCs plus EBD combination therapy group (GCs + EBD group). Both groups received GCs at a dose of 0.6 mg/kg/day (maximum 40 mg/day), followed by a standardized tapering regimen after 4 weeks of induction therapy [7]: (1) the glucocorticoid dose was reduced by 10 mg every two weeks until a daily dose of 20 mg was reached; (2) the reduction protocol was adjusted to 5 mg decrements every two weeks; (3) long-term maintenance was then continued at a daily dose of 5 mg. All treatment protocols were established according to the international consensus guidance statement on the management and treatment of IgG4-RD. When the patient’s aspartate transaminase or alanine transaminase levels exceed twice the normal range during treatment, therapeutic intervention is administered using medications such as polyene phosphatidylcholine and reduced glutathione. And almost all enrolled patients received oral ursodeoxycholic acid (10–15 mg/kg/d) as adjunctive therapy.

ERCP-related procedures

All enrolled patients in the GCs + EBD group received ERCP for the placement of different sizes of plastic biliary stents to achieve EBD. The specific procedure is depicted in Fig. 2. In the present study, plastic stents of various specifications were selected based on individual patient anatomy and clinical requirements, such as 7Fr. The specific model was determined based on parameters acquired from the fluro images. All patients were subjected to a minimum 24-hour postoperative fasting period. Following confirmation of normal amylase levels 24 h after treatment, patients were advised to start with water intake and gradually advance their diet. The prophylactic antibiotic with cefuroxime (3.0 g bid) was administered for three days to prevent post-ERCP complications [19]. Biliary stents placed intraoperatively were removed within three months post-ERCP.

Follow-up

We collected follow-up data at the 1 st, 3rd, 6th, and 12th months after treatment, including laboratory and imaging data. Imaging parameters were primarily observed by either abdominal non-enhanced or enhanced computed tomography (CT) with consistent methodology applied across both initial and follow-up assessments, including the common bile duct (CBD) and pancreatic duct inner diameters, as well as the presence of intrahepatic or hilar bile duct strictures. The CBD diameter measured in this study refers to the dilated segment of the duct above the site of stenosis, rather than the stenotic area itself.

Study outcome

The primary outcome we focused on was the recurrence of patients within one year after treatment with GCs and GCs + EBD. The following three situations are considered as recurrences of IgG4-RD [20]: (1) clinical recurrence: defined as the emergence or worsening of disease-related clinical symptoms and/or new or progressive imaging abnormalities, irrespective of changes in serum IgG4 levels [21, 22]; (2) serological recurrence: characterized by a rise in serum IgG4 levels accompanied by an increase of ≥ 1 point in the IgG4-RD Responder Index (RI) score following treatment, in the absence of any new clinical symptoms or radiological progression [23–25]; (3) mixed recurrence: refers to the concurrent presence of both clinical and serological recurrence. If any one of the three aforementioned criteria is met, there is sufficient reason to consider an IgG4-RD patient as being in a state of recurrence.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was conducted by IBM SPSS version 23.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were assessed using the chi-squared test, while continuous variables were evaluated with two independent samples t-test for normally distributed data (presented as mean ± standard deviation) and Mann-Whitney U test/Wilcoxon signed-rank test for non-normally distributed data [presented as median (P25, P75)]. Binary logistic regression was employed to explore the risk/protective factors related to recurrence. Statistical significance was considered when P-value was less than 0.05.

Results

A total of 55 patients with IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease were enrolled in this study, comprising 32 patients (58.18%) in the GCs group and 23 patients (41.82%) in the GCs + EBD group. No statistically significant differences were observed in demographic and disease characteristics between the groups (Table 1). According to the classification criteria for IgG4-SC proposed by Nakazawa et al. [26], the majority of patients in this study belonged to Type 1 (42/55). Only a small number were categorized as Type 2 (4/55), Type 3 (2/55), or Type 4 (7/55).

Changes in TBIL level, IgG4 level, and CBD diameter before and after treatment

Compared to baseline, GCs and GCs + EBD groups demonstrated significant reductions in both TBIL and IgG4 levels within one year of treatment (Fig. 3A, B). At the 12th month follow-up, the GCs + EBD group exhibited significantly greater reduction TBIL level from baseline compared to the GCs group (152.38 µmol/L vs. 38.18 µmol/L, P ≤ 0.01). However, there was no statistical difference in reduction TBIL level from baseline between GCs and GCs + EBD groups at the 1 st, 3rd, and 6th months post-treatment (P = 0.07, 0.06, and 0.31, respectively). Regarding IgG4 levels, no significant intergroup differences were observed in the magnitude of reduction from baseline at any time point (P = 0.14, 0.13, 0.51, and 0.78, respectively). Due to substantial missing data on CBD diameter at the 1 st and 3rd month post-treatment intervals, the analytical significance of these time points appears limited (Fig. 3C). Compared with baseline, both GCs and GCs + EBD groups showed significant reductions in CBD diameter at the 6th and 12th months post-treatment, with more pronounced improvement at the 12th month (GCs: −0.40 cm, P < 0.001; GCs + EBD: −0.49 cm, P < 0.01). However, no significant difference was observed between the two groups (all intergroup P > 0.05).

Adverse events incidence

In this study, we investigated complications associated with EBD, which primarily included infections, bleeding, perforation, hyperamylasemia, stent migration, and stent dislodgement. We observed that the post-EBD complication rate was 17.39% (4/23), comprising fever in three cases (13.04%) and hyperamylasemia in one case (4.35%) in the GCs + EBD group. No other complications were observed. No GCs-related severe adverse effects were observed in two groups.

Recurrence outcomes at the 1 st, 3rd, 6th, and 12th months after treatment

We analyzed the recurrence outcomes within one year post-treatment in the GCs and GCs + EBD groups. Of these, 34 patients (61.82%) experienced disease recurrence within one year, whereas 21 patients (38.18%) were recurrence-free. The distributions of clinical recurrence, serological recurrence, and mixed recurrence are presented in Table 2. While no statistically significant difference was observed in the cumulative recurrence rates between the GCs group and the GCs + EBD group at the 1 st, 3rd, 6th, and 12th months after treatment (P = 0.36, 0.96, 0.78, and 0.28, respectively), the GCs + EBD group showed a trend toward lower recurrence rates at all these time points.

Independent risk factors for recurrence of IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease

We first performed univariate screening for all patients, and selected variables with P < 0.10 for subsequent multivariate logistic regression analysis (Table 3). Only aspartate aminotransferase (P = 0.07) and IgG4 (P < 0.01) were included in the logistic regression analysis, as all other variables showed P > 0.10 for their association with IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease recurrence. Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that higher baseline IgG4 levels were significantly associated with increased risk of disease recurrence in IgG4-RD patients (OR = 1.141, 95% CI: 1.025–1.270; P = 0.02) (Table 4). Furthermore, we repeated the aforementioned statistical analyses separately for the predefined GCs and GCs + EBD groups, but no significant differences were observed (Table S1-S4).

Discussion

This study presented the first retrospective comparative analysis of treatment efficacy, complication rates, and recurrence between GCs and GCs + EBD groups for IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease. The results demonstrated that both groups significantly reduced TBIL and IgG4 levels within one year. The CBD diameter significantly decreased at the 6th and 12th months post-treatment compared to baseline. Except for significantly greater TBIL improvement in the GCs + EBD group at the 12th month, no other statistically significant differences were observed between groups. Regarding disease recurrence, no statistically significant difference was observed in the cumulative recurrence rate at all time points between GCs and GCs + EBD groups. Finally, we identified elevated baseline IgG4 levels as an independent risk factor for post-treatment recurrence in IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease.

In this study, most of the enrolled patients underwent endoscopic ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration and all patients who underwent ERCP also received endoscopic biliary biopsy to obtained cytological and histological specimens, including biliary mucosa, pancreatic tissue, and duodenal papilla mucosa. No malignant cells were observed. Subsequently, immunohistochemistry was performed. The number of patients with IgG4-positivity was 7/16 (43.75%), which is similar to the findings in the study by Fischer et al. [27]. Furthermore, all patients underwent imaging evaluations by two radiology specialists. No signs of malignant tumors including enhanced wall thickening (≥ 5 mm), lymph node enlargement (≥ 1 cm) and portal vein obliteration were identified in any patients, and no malignant signs were observed during the follow-up period. However, the differential diagnosis between IgG4-RD and malignant tumors remains a challenging issue in current clinical practice. In recent years, some scholars have proposed that CT-based radiomic models and serum exosomal hsa-let-7f-5p can serve as novel technical tools and research directions [28, 29]. These not only provide support for the differentiation of the two aforementioned diseases but also facilitate the conduct of our subsequent prospective studies.

Accurate and timely diagnosis of IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease is critical for determining subsequent treatment strategies. In patients with obstructive jaundice secondary to biliary strictures, EBD represents an important therapeutic option. As an interventional procedure, EBD restores biliary drainage function by placing plastic or metal stents to dilate strictured bile ducts [30]. Numerous studies have demonstrated the significant therapeutic efficacy of EBD in various benign and malignant biliary stricture diseases, including biliary obstruction caused by IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease [15, 31–34]. The typical clinical manifestation of IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease is obstructive jaundice, with pathogenesis primarily involving two mechanisms: (1) diffuse pancreatic parenchymal enlargement mechanically compresses the CBD, leading to biliary strictures; (2) the bile duct wall is infiltrated by IgG4-positive plasma cells and subsequent fibrotic processes, resulting in wall thickening and luminal narrowing [35]. EBD effectively alleviates biliary strictures and improves cholestasis, thereby significantly reducing jaundice symptoms. Postoperative TBIL reduction is commonly used to evaluate the clinical efficacy of EBD. Although no significant intergroup differences were detected at the 1 st, 3rd, and 6th months post-treatment, we noted that the GCs + EBD group exhibited a greater trend of TBIL reduction. Interestingly, according to the magnitude of TBIL reduction from baseline at the 12th month post-treatment between two groups, the GCs + EBD combination therapy may demonstrate a potential trend toward shortening the time to jaundice resolution after the induction therapy phase. However, considering this is a retrospective study, the effect of GCs + EBD combination therapy needs to be confirmed by further prospective studies.

In contrast to the “physical” mechanism of EBD, GCs exert their effects through immunomodulatory pathways. GCs bind to glucocorticoid receptors in the cytoplasm of target cells to form complexes, which then translocate into the nucleus to regulate the transcription of various target genes, thereby achieving anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects [36–40]. Previous studies have shown that the decrease in IgG4 levels is closely related to the suppression of immune activation and the reduction in the number of IgG4-positive plasma cells, with GCs playing a key role in this process [41]. This finding is consistent with our observations. The reduction in IgG4 levels mainly depends on the immunosuppressive effect of GCs. Ultimately, GCs attenuated inflammatory responses and edema in the pancreatic head and bile duct wall, alleviating biliary strictures and cholestasis.

It is noteworthy that EBD is associated with certain postoperative complications, such as infections [42, 43]. In a study by Sugiyama et al., 36.1% of patients experienced late complications, including stent occlusion, infection, and distal migration [44]. Nevertheless, the procedure itself is generally considered safe, which was also confirmed in our study. Only one patient developed post-procedural hyperamylasemia, and three experienced fever, with no cases of bleeding or perforation.

Recurrence after disease remission was an important outcome of interest in this study. In our analysis, although no statistically significant difference was observed, the GCs + EBD group exhibited a trend toward lower recurrence rates at all time points compared with the GCs group. Based on this trend, we speculate that while EBD does not directly regulate the immune response, it may indirectly reduce the risk of recurrence by rapidly relieving biliary obstruction, improving bile drainage, and subsequently optimizing systemic inflammatory status and liver function. Additionally, improved biliary drainage may also enhance the oral bioavailability of GCs, thereby improving the long-term therapeutic efficacy. Tsang et al. reported that serum IgG4 levels exceeding twice the upper limit of normal were significantly associated with recurrence in patients with IgG4-RD [45]. Another study from China further clarified that IgG4 ≥ 6.5 g/L, IgG ≥ 20.8 g/L, an IgG4-RD RI ≥ 9 points, and severe IgG4 + plasma cell infiltration were predictive factors for disease recurrence [46]. Similar to these studies, our research also confirmed the crucial role of baseline IgG4 levels in predicting IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease recurrence. Moreover, a prolonged interval between diagnosis and treatment initiation, as well as a history of allergy, have also been identified as risk factors for recurrence [47]. Therefore, in clinical practice, in addition to regular monitoring of serum IgG4 levels, comprehensive assessments incorporating the IgG4-RD RI score, histopathological features, and disease course characteristics should be performed to more accurately predict recurrence risk and guide individualized treatment strategies.

However, this study has certain limitations. First of all, this is a retrospective study, and some of the conclusions need to be further confirmed by multicenter prospective studies. However, our findings provide a foundation for conducting these studies. Additionally, it is also a single-center study with a relatively small sample size, which may lead to potential bias in the results, although the overall trend remains consistent. Therefore, whether EBD can truly delay or prevent the recurrence of IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease is still lacking support from high-quality prospective studies. In the future, multicenter, large-sample randomized controlled trials are urgently needed to validate the real value of EBD combined with GCs therapy in the long-term management of IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease and to further clarify its role in individualized treatment strategies.

Conclusions

In summary, the combination therapy of GCs and EBD did not exhibit significant differences in immunologic remission, complication rates, or recurrence compared with GCs monotherapy for IgG4-related pancreatobiliary disease. Nonetheless, EBD showed certain benefits in alleviating biliary obstruction. Baseline IgG4 levels played a pivotal role in predicting disease recurrence. Further research is warranted to ascertain the value of EBD in personalized treatment strategies.

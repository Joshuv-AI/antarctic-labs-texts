Peripheral blood CD8 + T-cell count at diagnosis predicts prognosis in R-CHOP-treated diffuse large B-cell lymphoma patients
Authors: Fang Chen, Ping Liu, Jiajia Zhao, Demin Wang, Haifeng Zhen, Juan Jin, Zhi Chao, Dan Zhou, Lu Zhang, Wei Chen, Zimian Luo, Zhongjun Huo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
While the prognostic significance of peripheral blood (PB) lymphocyte counts in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) patients treated with Rituximab, Cyclophosphamide, Doxorubicin Hydrochloride, Vincristine Sulfate, and Prednisone (R-CHOP) is established, the specific role of CD8+ T-cell counts (CD8c) remains unclear. Methods: This retrospective study analyzed 315 newly diagnosed DLBCL patients treated at Central Hospital of Xiangtan from January 2018 to September 2023. We evaluated the prognostic significance of initial CD8c and other clinical parameters using univariate and multivariate survival analyses to identify independent predictors of outcome. An optimal CD8c cut-off value of 264.5×106/L was identified. Low CD8c correlated with significantly improved 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) (88.78% vs 49.18%, p<0.001) and overall survival (OS) (84.02% vs 31.25%, p<0.001). Multivariate analysis confirmed CD8c as an independent prognostic factor for both PFS and OS. CD8c demonstrated significant interactions with lactate dehydrogenase levels. Notably, low CD8c groups consistently exhibited superior survival outcomes, even in the presence of other poor prognostic factors. Initial PB CD8c serves as an independent predictor for R-CHOP-treated DLBCL patients. These findings suggest CD8c may be a candidate for enhancing existing prognostic models, particularly when combined with established risk factors, underscoring the potential importance of host immune status in DLBCL prognosis and treatment outcomes. Independent validation in external cohorts is needed.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s00277-026-07135-5.

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most common and aggressive subtype of non-Hodgkin lymphoma, accounting for approximately 30–40% of all cases worldwide [1]. Despite advances in treatment, particularly the introduction of Rituximab, Cyclophosphamide, Doxorubicin Hydrochloride, Vincristine Sulfate, and Prednisone (R-CHOP), DLBCL remains a significant clinical challenge due to its heterogeneous nature and variable outcomes [2, 3]. Current prognostic models for DLBCL, such as the international prognostic index (IPI) and revised IPI (R-IPI), have been widely used to stratify patients and guide treatment decisions [4, 5]. However, these models primarily rely on clinical and basic laboratory parameters, which may not fully capture the biological heterogeneity of DLBCL or the intricate interplay among tumor cells and the body’s defense mechanisms. This limitation underscores the need for more refined prognostic markers that can better predict treatment outcomes and guide personalized therapeutic strategies [6]. In recent years, there has been growing recognition of the critical role that the immune system plays in cancer progression and treatment response [7]. T lymphocytes, particularly CD8 + T-cell counts (CD8c), are key components of the adaptive immune response against tumors [8]. These cells are capable of recognizing and eliminating malignant cells, and their presence in the tumor microenvironment has been associated with improved prognosis in various cancer types [9, 10]. However, the prognostic significance of peripheral blood (PB) CD8c in individuals receiving R-CHOP for DLBCL remains insufficiently explored.

CD8c, also known as cytotoxic T lymphocytes, play a crucial role in cell-mediated immune defense [11]. They are capable of directly killing virus-infected cells and cancer cells through the release of cytotoxic granules containing perforin and granzymes [12]. Additionally, CD8c produce cytokines such as interferon-γ and tumor necrosis factor-α, which can enhance anti-tumor immune responses and inhibit tumor growth [13]. Given their central role in anti-tumor immunity, the PB CD8c may serve as a readily accessible biomarker reflecting the overall state of the host’s immune response against DLBCL [14]. Despite the potential importance of CD8c in DLBCL prognosis, there is a notable gap in our understanding of how PB CD8c at initial diagnosis relates to treatment responses and survival rates in individuals with DLBCL receiving R-CHOP therapy. Moreover, the interaction between CD8c and other established prognostic factors, such as the IPI score, bone marrow infiltration, and serum lactate dehydrogenase levels, remains largely unexplored [15, 16].

The present research seeks to fill this knowledge void through examining the prognostic significance of initial diagnosis PB CD8c among 315 newly identified DLBCL cases undergoing R-CHOP therapy. Given the exploratory nature of this single-center study and lack of an external validation cohort, this analysis is intended to be hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. Specifically, we aim to: Explore a potential threshold for CD8c and evaluate its association with progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). Investigate the correlation between CD8c and other clinical and pathological characteristics of DLBCL. Evaluate the independent prognostic value of CD8c through univariate and multivariate analyses. Investigate potential interactions between CD8c and other known prognostic factors, including bone marrow infiltration, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels, and beta-2 microglobulin (β2m) levels. By elucidating the prognostic significance of CD8c in DLBCL, this study aims to contribute to the development of more accurate risk stratification models and potentially inform future immunotherapy strategies for DLBCL patients [17]. The findings may provide valuable insights into the intricate relationship linking the host defense mechanisms and DLBCL, ultimately leading to improved patient care and outcomes. However, we emphasize that the proposed threshold requires independent validation in larger, external cohorts before any clinical application.

Materials and methods

Patients

This study, conducted at Xiangtan Central Hospital from January 2018 to September 2023, included 315 patients with DLBCL who had received their initial diagnosis and met the diagnostic criteria established by the global health authority for disease classification. Exclusion criteria encompassed DLBCL arising from other lymphoid malignancies, significant cardiovascular conditions, human immunodeficiency virus infection positivity, or systemic corticosteroid use within 14 days prior to diagnostic blood collection. Every participant gave written authorization for chemotherapy administration and access to their clinical data. The institutional review board sanctioned the historical analysis of patient files, adhering to the principles outlined in the Helsinki Declaration. Baseline demographic, clinical, and laboratory characteristics collected included age, sex, Ann Arbor stage, eastern cooperative oncology group (ECOG) performance status, IPI score, DLBCL subtype (classified as germinal center B-cell-like (GCB) or non-GCB), extranodal involvement, bulky disease, bone marrow (BM) infiltration, Epstein Barr virus (EBV) status, Ki-67 index, LDH level, and β2m level. Laboratory data were collected prior to the initiation of any treatment. No participants discontinued their involvement in the study. Molecular pathological investigations

Double expressor status was determined by assessing MYC and BCL2 protein expression using immunohistochemistry. MYC protein positivity was defined as nuclear staining in ≥ 40% of tumor cells, and BCL2 protein positivity as cytoplasmic staining in ≥ 50% of tumor cells. In the study of DLBCL, MYC protein positivity is usually defined as nuclear staining of ≥ 40% of tumor cells, and BCL2 protein positivity is defined as cytoplasmic staining of tumor cells, with thresholds varying between studies and commonly ranging from > 50% to ≥ 70% [1, 18]. Cases simultaneously positive for MYC and BCL2 proteins were classified as double expressor lymphoma. To determine double/triple-hit (DTH) status, MYC, BCL2, and/or BCL6 gene rearrangements were assessed via fluorescence in situ hybridization [1]. EBV presence was identified by measuring peripheral blood EBV-DNA viral load through real-time quantitative PCR, with a reference threshold of > 1,000 copies/mL [19].

CD8-positive T cells analysis

CD8c assessment was performed using flow cytometry. Whole blood samples were collected, anticoagulated with EDTA-K2, and stored at room temperature, with CD8c detection completed within 24 h. For sample preparation, 20 µL of CD3/CD16 + 56/CD45/CD4/CD19/CD8 reagent (Z6410010-100T, Beijing Zhongshi Biotech Co., Ltd.) was added to the bottom of each flow tube. After thorough mixing, the tubes were incubated for 15–25 min in a dark, room-temperature environment. Subsequently, 450 µL of 1× hemolysin for blood cell analysis was added, mixed for approximately 10 s, and further incubated for 15 min under these conditions. Samples were then analyzed using a BD FACSCanto flow cytometer (4/2 system).

Treatment

Patients received the standard R-CHOP regimen as first-line therapy, planned for 6 to 8 cycles. The total number of treatment cycles administered was individualized based on patient response, tolerance, and multidisciplinary team (MDT) assessment, ranging from 3 to 12 cycles. The distribution of cycles was as follows: 82 (26.03%) patients received 3–4 cycles, 143 (45.40%) received 5–6 cycles, and 85 (26.98%) received 7–8 cycles. Crucially, to adhere to established guidelines and prevent cumulative cardiotoxicity, doxorubicin was administered for a maximum of 8 cycles. For the five patients (1.59%) who received more than 8 cycles of therapy-a decision made by the MDT due to a continued slow but positive response after standard therapy-the regimen was modified to R-CVP (omitting doxorubicin) for consolidation. These five patients underwent regular cardiac function monitoring via echocardiography, with no serious drug-related cardiotoxic events observed. For the 85 patients (26.98%) who received 7–8 cycles of R-CHOP (cumulative doxorubicin 350–400 mg/m²), they are at increased risk for anthracycline-induced cardiotoxicity. Therefore, we emphasize the importance of cardio-oncology unit involvement in this high-risk subgroup, including strict echocardiographic monitoring with left ventricular ejection fraction and global longitudinal strain measurement [20]. In cases where subclinical left ventricular dysfunction is detected, early initiation of cardioprotective drugs such as angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, and/or beta-blockers should be considered [21]. The specific treatment intervention for each patient was determined following discussions in multidisciplinary team meetings. These interventions were thoroughly explained to patients and their families before implementation.

Statistical analysis

For comparing differences between groups, we employed the chi-square method to analyze categorical data and utilized the Mann-Whitney approach for evaluating continuous variables. OS and PFS were evaluated using survival analysis techniques. Statistical comparisons of survival curves were conducted through nonparametric methods. Single-variable proportional hazards modeling was performed for evaluating the predictive significance regarding PFS and OS outcomes. Variables demonstrating statistical relevance in the univariate analysis were subsequently incorporated into a multivariable proportional hazards model to determine independent prognostic indicators. Findings are presented as risk estimates with associated 95% uncertainty ranges. Data analysis and visual representation were conducted using GraphPad Prism 8.0 and R statistics 4.4.0. A p-value < 0.05 indicated meaningful statistical differences.

Results

Patients’ characteristics

This study included 315 patients with DLBCL from a single center. The median age was 64 years (interquartile range [IQR]: 57–72 years), with 174 (55.24%) male and 141 (44.76%) female patients. Regarding disease characteristics, 221 (70.16%) patients were classified as non-GCB subtype, while 94 (29.84%) were of the GCB subtype. According to the Ann Arbor staging system, 187 (59.37%) patients were at stage III-IV, and 128 (40.63%) at stage I-II. The IPI scores indicated that 241 (76.51%) patients had an IPI score ≥ 2, suggesting that the majority belonged to the intermediate-high risk group. Functional status assessment revealed that 144 (45.71%) individuals exhibited an ECOG score ≥ 2, indicating significantly impaired daily activity in nearly half of the cohort. Notably, 144 (45.71%) patients were identified as double expressors. Regarding disease burden, 145 (46.03%) patients presented with extranodal involvement, 27 (8.57%) had bulky disease, and 37 (11.75%) showed bone marrow infiltration. Additionally, 36 (11.43%) patients tested positive for EBV. Laboratory findings revealed a median Ki-67 of 80% (IQR: 70%−90%), indicating high tumor proliferation. The median LDH level was 246 IU/L (IQR: 185.5–438.5 IU/L), and the median β2m level was 2.66 (IQR: 2.07–3.84). Of particular interest was the CD8c, with a median of 264 × 106/L (IQR: 194–378 × 106/L). A statistically notable contrast (p < 0.001) emerged when comparing the deceased (307 × 106/L) and surviving (228.5 × 106/L) groups. According to the ultimate progression results, notable distinctions emerged between the cohorts regarding Ann Arbor stage, ECOG performance status, IPI score, double expressor status, extranodal involvement, BM infiltration, LDH, and β2m levels. In summary, this DLBCL patient cohort predominantly comprised middle-aged and elderly individuals, with most patients presenting at advanced disease stages and exhibiting high-risk features. Given the single-center, retrospective design, these baseline characteristics reflect a selected population and may not be directly generalizable to broader DLBCL cohorts.

Relationship between CD8c and other prognostic factors

To further explore the clinical significance of CD8c, we utilized receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve-based statistical evaluation to identify the most appropriate threshold of 264.5 × 106/L (Fig. 1A), with an area under the curve of 0.656. Notably, this derived optimal threshold was numerically very close to the overall cohort’s median CD8c value (264 × 106/L, as presented in Table 1). Based on this statistically derived threshold of ROC curve, patients were stratified into high and low CD8c expression groups, which were then used for subsequent survival and multivariate analyses. The low CD8c expression group comprised approximately 50% of the patients, providing a balanced stratification for analysis. Pearson correlation analysis (Fig. 1B) demonstrated a strong correlation between T cell counts and CD8c levels (Pearson’s R-value = 0.847). Analysis of CD8c levels against clinicopathological features (Figure S1, Table 2) showed no significant correlations with age (p = 0.151, Figure S1A), DLBCL subtype (p = 0.205), Ann Arbor stage (p = 0.855), ECOG status (p = 0.781), or IPI score (p = 0.067). A significant gender difference was observed (p = 0.011), with males more frequent in the high CD8c group (62.42% vs. 48.10%) and females in the low CD8c group (51.90% vs. 37.58%).

These findings were unexpected, given that these factors are typically considered important prognostic indicators. However, these null associations should be interpreted with caution due to the potential for selection bias inherent to the study’s retrospective design. In addition, CD8c levels were significantly associated with DTH status (p = 0.002) and negatively correlated with bulky disease (p = 0.009): patients with lower CD8c had more bulky tumors (12.66% vs. 4.46%). A significant difference in positron emission tomography-computed tomography scan frequency was also observed between groups (p = 0.032). Among biomarkers, the Ki67 index showed a weak difference between groups (p = 0.042) but no correlation with CD8c (p = 0.381, Figure S1B). LDH showed no significant difference or correlation with CD8c (Figure S1C, Table 1). β2m showed no group difference (Table 2) but a weak correlation with CD8c (Pearson’s R-value = 0.117, p = 0.038, Figure S1D). Therapeutic outcomes according to the CD8c

Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed significant differences in both PFS (Figs. 2A) and OS (Fig. 2B) between patients with high and low CD8c levels. The 5-year PFS rates for low and high CD8c expression groups were 88.78% and 49.18%, respectively (log-rank p < 0.001). Similarly, the 5-year OS rates were 84.02% and 31.25% for low and high CD8c expression groups, respectively (log-rank p < 0.001). The duration of survival was calculated as follows: PFS (all patients): 20 months (interquartile range [IQR]: 7–35 months), high CD8c group: 15 months (IQR: 5–33 months), low CD8c group: 23 months (IQR: 13–37 months), OS (all patients): 28 months (IQR: 15.5–39.5 months), high CD8c group: 23 months (IQR: 12–36 months), low CD8c group: 32 months (IQR: 18.25–45 months). While these differences are striking, they should be interpreted with awareness of the potential confounding introduced by the retrospective, monocentric design.

Univariate and multivariate analyses

Tables 3 and 4 present the prognostic factors showing notable correlation with mortality outcomes according to univariate and comprehensive statistical modeling using proportional hazards regression. Key variables associated with OS and PFS detected during single-factor evaluation were subsequently incorporated into the multivariable proportional hazards model analysis. In the univariate analysis, statistically significant factors associated with PFS were ECOG performance status, Ann Arbor stage, IPI, double expressor status, extranodal involvement, BM infiltration, LDH levels, β2m levels, and CD8c expression. ECOG performance status, IPI, double expressor status, BM infiltration, LDH levels, β2m levels, and CD8c expression remained significantly associated with prognosis in the multivariate analysis. The consistency of CD8c as an independent factor in this single-center analysis supports its potential relevance, but independent validation in external cohorts is necessary to address inherent selection bias.

Discussion

This study comprehensively evaluated the prognostic value of PB CD8c at initial diagnosis in patients with DLBCL, focusing on a population predominantly comprising advanced-stage, high-risk, and aggressive cases. Key findings include: (1) CD8c is an independent prognostic factor in DLBCL patients; (2) Low CD8c is associated with significantly improved PFS and OS; (3) CD8c exhibits complex interactions with multiple clinicopathological features, particularly showing strong associations with DTH status and BM infiltration.

First, we observed a median CD8c of 264 × 106/L (IQR: 194–378 × 106/L) in our study cohort (n = 315). This observation presents an interesting contrast to findings in several other tumor types, where a higher CD8c density is typically associated with enhanced immune surveillance and a better prognosis. For instance, Nakano et al. [22] reported that elevated proliferation rates of intratumoral CD8c correlated with improved survival in renal cell carcinoma. Similarly, Su et al. [23] demonstrated that increased spatial interaction between CD8c and antigen-presenting cells was linked to prolonged patient survival in soft tissue sarcoma. Furthermore, Xi et al. [24] observed that a more robust functional profile of CD8c was associated with superior anti-tumor effects and longer patient survival in gastric cancer. In immune surveillance, Fuse et al. [25] highlighted the critical role of CD8c as key effector cells in viral clearance, noting that high levels of functional CD8c are indicative of stronger immune surveillance. High counts of CD8 + T cells do not invariably correlate with robust function, particularly in contexts such as tumor immune evasion and chronic viral infections. In these scenarios, an elevated CD8 + T cell population may actually signify a state of functional exhaustion. Such cells often exhibit high expression of immune checkpoint receptors, indicating functional suppression, and in some cases, may even secrete factors that promote tumor growth [26, 27]. However, our data suggest that in the context of DLBCL, the interpretation of CD8c levels may require a more complex framework. This apparent paradox might reflect the unique immune microenvironment dynamics in DLBCL, hinting at the presence of tumor immune evasion mechanisms [28, 29]. Because this study detected peripheral blood, which reflects the systemic immune status and is closely related to the tumor microenvironment, it may exist findings similar to recent studies in DLBCL [30, 31]. High levels of CD8c are actually exploited by the tumor, leading to a stronger immunosuppressive microenvironment.

Our analysis revealed complex relationships between CD8c levels and multiple clinicopathological features. A particularly noteworthy finding is the association between CD8c levels and DTH status (p = 0.002). This finding is particularly noteworthy given the well-established poor prognosis of DTH lymphomas, which harbor rearrangements involving MYC and BCL2 and/or BCL6 [32]. This discovery represents, for the first time in DLBCL, a demonstrable connection between an immune marker and specific genomic characteristics, thereby opening new avenues for understanding the intricate interplay between tumor genomics and host immune responses [33, 34]. This association may reflect the impact of double-hit–related genetic alterations on the tumor immune microenvironment, integrative analyses have shown that specific DLBCL subtypes exhibit immune-cold programs with diminished cytotoxic T-cell signatures [35]. Importantly, recent evidence indicates that the tumor microenvironment of DTH lymphomas is characterized by decreased infiltration of dendritic cells and increased infiltration of T cells [36, 37], supporting our observation that circulating CD8 + T cells in DTH patients may represent a functionally exhausted population rather than an effective antitumor response. This interpretation is further supported by recent studies demonstrating that elevated expression of immune checkpoint molecules, such as LAG-3 and CXCR5, on peripheral blood CD8 + T cells is associated with inferior outcomes in DLBCL patients [38]. While Upregulated LAG-3 suppresses T-cell activation and promotes immune exhaustion [39]. Although DTH status was not identified as a significant prognostic factor for PFS in our univariate analysis, this may reflect the high baseline risk of our cohort and does not diminish the biological significance of the CD8c-DTH association.

Furthermore, CD8c levels were significantly associated with BM infiltration (p = 0.027) in our cohort. BM involvement is a well-established adverse prognostic factor in DLBCL, associated with inferior outcomes and increased risk of disease progression [40, 41]. The adverse prognostic impact of BM invasion has been further validated in specific DLBCL subtypes. In Epstein-Barr virus-positive DLBCL, a retrospective study of 57 patients identified BM invasion as an independent risk factor for worse PFS in multivariate analysis [42]. Similarly, in primary bone marrow DLBCL, a rare but aggressive entity with lymphoma confined to the BM, the prognosis remains poor despite immunochemotherapy, with rapid relapse and disease-related mortality frequently observed [40]. The association between CD8c and BM involvement in our study suggests that elevated CD8c may reflect a systemic immune response to disseminated disease. However, rather than mounting an effective anti-tumor response, this elevated CD8c paradoxically signals a dysfunctional immune state, contributing to the poor prognosis associated with both high CD8c and BM involvement. Consequently, high CD8c may simply serve as a marker of widespread disease and immune failure, rather than a indicator of robust anti-tumor immunity. Future studies should validate this hypothesis by directly assessing the functional status of CD8 + T cells in patients with both high CD8c and BM involvement.

The negative correlation between CD8c levels and the presence of bulky disease (p = 0.009) provides an important insight into the intricate interplay between tumor burden and the host’s immunological response. This correlation may indicate a suppressive effect of high tumor burden on systemic immunity or suggest a role for CD8c in controlling tumor growth [43]. This observation emphasizes the need to consider both tumor characteristics and host immune status when assessing prognosis in DLBCL patients.

In addition, PB CD8c at diagnosis was identified as an independent prognostic factor for DLBCL patients treated with R-CHOP, with low CD8c significantly associated with improved PFS and OS. Specifically, 5-year PFS was 88.78% in the low-CD8c group versus 49.18% in the high-CD8c group (log-rank p < 0.001), and 5-year OS was 84.02% versus 31.25% (log-rank p < 0.001). However, interpreting these findings requires considering certain significant biological characteristics of this study cohort. For instance, our cohort had a higher proportion of double-expressor status (45.71%) compared to general populations [44, 45]. Given these unique characteristics of our study cohort, our findings on the prognostic value of CD8c may be more applicable to high-risk DLBCL patient populations with similar clinicopathological features. Consequently, the broad applicability of these conclusions warrants further validation through large-scale prospective studies in more diverse and representative DLBCL patient cohorts. In addition, given that approximately 70% of our patients were of the non-GCB subtype, which has been shown to derive particular benefit from the Pola-R-CHP regimen in the POLARIX trial [46, 47], future studies should validate the prognostic value of baseline peripheral blood CD8c in DLBCL patients receiving firstline Pola-R-CHP therapy.

From a methodological perspective, our study employed flow cytometry to quantify peripheral blood CD8c, underscoring the value of this technique for rapid and reproducible immune monitoring in DLBCL. Moreover, flow cytometry can also be applied to lymph node tissue samples for the rapid detection of neoplastic cell phenotypes, such as CD20 expression on malignant B-cells, the therapeutic target of rituximab in R-CHOP regimens [48, 49]. Integrating peripheral blood CD8c analysis with nodal immunophenotyping may provide a more complete picture of the tumor immune microenvironment and refine prognostic predictions.

While our research offers significant contributions, we must recognize its constraints. Firstly, as a single-center, retrospective study, our results require validation in multi-center prospective studies [50]. Secondly, this study only focused on PB CD8c. We did not evaluate tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes that could provide supplementary information, nor did we analyse other important peripheral T cell subsets such as CD4 + helper T cells or regulatory T cells (Tregs). More comprehensive immunoassays in future studies could provide a deeper understanding of the systemic immune landscape and its prognostic implications. Additionally, our study did not evaluate the functional characteristics of CD8c, such as cytotoxic activity and cytokine secretion capacity [51]. Based on our findings, we propose the following directions for future research: Validate the prognostic value of CD8c in large prospective cohorts and explore its integration with existing prognostic models [52]. Investigate the functional characteristics of CD8c in DLBCL patients, including their antigen specificity, cytotoxic activity, and cytokine secretion profiles [53]. Explore the potential role of CD8c in predicting responses to immunotherapy, particularly checkpoint inhibitor treatments [54, 55]. Study the combined value of CD8c and other immune cell subsets (such as CD4 + T cells, regulatory T cells, and NK cells) in DLBCL prognostic assessment [56]. Elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the association between CD8c levels and specific genomic features. Investigate the impact of dynamic changes in CD8c levels on long-term prognosis in DLBCL patients [57].

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study suggests that PB CD8c at diagnosis may serve as an independent prognostic factor for DLBCL patients, and reveals potential complex relationships between CD8c and multiple clinicopathological features. These findings provide new perspectives on the immunobiology of DLBCL while opening novel avenues for improving risk stratification and personalized treatment strategies. As an easily accessible and cost-effective biomarker, CD8c shows promise for potentially enhancing the clinical management of DLBCL, though this requires confirmation. Future research should focus on independently validating these findings in larger, external, multicenter cohorts, with the ultimate goal of optimizing treatment strategies and prognostic assessment for DLBCL patients through the integration of immunological insights.

Impaired cardiorespiratory fitness in psoriatic arthritis: insights from cardiopulmonary exercise testing
Authors: Marlies Kaerts, Kurt de Vlam, Rik Lories, Barbara Neerinckx, Thijs Willem Swinnen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundData on cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF) in psoriatic arthritis (PsA) are scarce. This study aimed to determine the CRF level in patients with PsA and to examine the relation between CRF and disease parameters, cardiometabolic risk profile as well as patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs).MethodsIn this cross-sectional study, CRF was measured as peak oxygen uptake (VO2peak) during an incremental maximal cardiopulmonary exercise test and compared with reference charts of the general population using the one-sided t-test. Multivariable linear regression models were built to evaluate the associations between VO2peak (mL/min/kg, log-transformed) and disease parameters, cardiometabolic risk parameters and PROMs. Statistical significance was defined as p<0.05 with application of Holm-Bonferroni correction in regression analysis (expressed as p*).ResultsIn 80 patients with PsA (41% females, mean age 51 years (SD=11)), mean VO2peak was 26.03 mL/min/kg (SD=7.56) and significantly decreased compared with the physically active reference population (mean 74.01% (SD=19.19), p<0.001) with 41% having an impaired CRF. In the final multivariable linear regression model, adjusted for age and sex, disease activity (Psoriatic Arthritis Disease Activity Score: β=−0.2757, p*=0.009), waist-hip ratio (β=−0.4193, p*<0.001), patient-reported disease impact (Psoriatic Arthritis Impact of Disease 12-item questionnaire: β=−0.2385, p*=0.015), and moderate-to-vigorous physical activity during commuting and leisure time (minutes/week: β=0.1702, p*=0.015) were significantly associated with VO2peak (adjusted R²=0.71).ConclusionsThe CRF level of patients with PsA was significantly decreased compared to a physically active population with 41% having an impaired CRF. A lower CRF level was substantially associated with impaired disease control, unfavourable body composition, lower self-reported physical activity as well as with higher patient-reported disease impact.Keywords: Arthritis, Psoriatic; Physical Therapy Modalities; Cardiovascular Diseases

Introduction

Psoriatic arthritis (PsA) is a chronic inflammatory joint disease which is characterised by inflammation in the synovium and enthesis as well as by skeletal structural damage.1,3 Most prevalent symptoms are pain, stiffness, functionality loss and fatigue.1 2 The comorbidity burden in PsA is high, including cardiometabolic as well as psychological comorbidities.4 5 The presence of cardiometabolic risk factors influences disease management, worsens patients’ clinical status and might be associated with the onset of PsA disease.6,14 Together, PsA and its associated conditions are negatively impacting quality of life.13 15 16

The current recommendations for cardiometabolic risk management in PsA highlight the importance of controlling disease activity and systemic inflammation, as well as estimating the cardiovascular disease risk by prediction models, for example, Systematic COronary Risk Estimation 2 (SCORE2).17 18 The SCORE2 algorithm estimates a 10-year cardiovascular disease risk using traditional risk factors: smoking status, systolic blood pressure, non-high density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol, age and sex.18 The modifiable risk factors need to be addressed by pharmacological treatment protocols according to national and international guidelines as well as by non-pharmacological interventions, such as a healthy diet, regular exercise and smoking cessation.1719,21 Therefore, exercise and physical activity (PA) are recommended as an essential part of the non-pharmacological management of PsA by leading professional societies in rheumatology.8 9 22

The beneficial effect of cardiorespiratory fitness (CRF) on cardiometabolic and general health in the general population is largely known.23,27 However, CRF is not yet included in structured cardiovascular disease risk assessment of the general population.18 Peak oxygen uptake (VO2peak), the gold standard measure of CRF, is assessed during a maximal cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET).23 28 It is likely that higher levels of CRF in patients with PsA could contribute significantly to PsA disease control by decreasing inflammation as well as reducing cardiometabolic risk factors and comorbidities in general.26 29 Nevertheless, a recent systematic review conducted by our team revealed that data on CRF in patients with PsA are currently scarce.30 Moreover, the associations of disease parameters and cardiometabolic risk profile with CRF in patients with PsA have not been studied before.

We hypothesise that patients with PsA have a decreased CRF level compared with the general population and that CRF is associated with disease parameters, cardiometabolic risk factors and patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs). Therefore, the first objective of this study was to determine the level of CRF in patients with PsA compared with the general population. The second objective was to study the relation between the CRF level of patients with PsA and disease parameters, cardiometabolic risk profile as well as PROMs.

Methods

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional study was conducted in an outpatient rheumatology clinic of an academic hospital in Belgium between October 2022 and April 2024. Patients were consecutively recruited during routine medical care and fulfilled the following inclusion criteria: clinical diagnosis of PsA, minimum age of 18 years, able to understand and answer clinical questionnaires and able to cycle on a stationary bike. Patients were excluded if they had current signs and symptoms of active infection, if maximal exercise testing was considered unsafe according to American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) guidelines, or if they experienced current signs and symptoms of severe, progressive or uncontrolled conditions other than PsA, interfering with a normal physical testing approach.23 All study data, including patient-reported questionnaires, were collected and managed using Research Electronic Data Capture tools (REDCap, Vanderbilt University, USA) hosted at KU Leuven.31 32 This report followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology statement.33

Assessment and outcomes

Cardiorespiratory fitness

Cardiorespiratory fitness was measured as VO2peak during an incremental maximal CPET on a cycle ergometer (Ergometrics 900, Ergoline, Germany) following a standard procedure in which resistance was increased stepwise every minute.34 The CPET was supervised by the Pneumology Department of University Hospitals Leuven trained in standardised CPET protocols, addressing potential confounding factors. Breath-by-breath gas analysis, heart rate, oxygen saturation, blood pressure and ECG were continuously measured throughout the test. Capillary blood lactate and Borg scores for dyspnoea and fatigue in the legs (Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) 0–10) were assessed at the end of the test. VO2peak was recorded as the highest value averaged over 30 s. Oxygen uptake at anaerobic threshold was identified using the V-slope method.35 The ability to reach criteria of maximal CPET, and patient-reported and criterion-based CPET limitation was evaluated.34 A questionnaire monitoring symptoms (NRS 0–10; fatigue, pain, morning stiffness and disease activity) was completed by the patients before and 3 days after CPET. Patient-reported usability (NRS 0–10) was assessed by an internally developed questionnaire, based on feasibility measures advised by OMERACT, 3 days after CPET.36

Demographic and disease characteristics

Demographic patient characteristics were collected by a questionnaire: age, sex, disease duration, smoking, alcohol consumption, and working status . Musculoskeletal disease activity and the presence of psoriasis were examined by the treating rheumatologist. Musculoskeletal disease activity was calculated using the composite score Psoriatic Arthritis Disease Activity Score (PASDAS), consisting of swollen joint count, tender joint count, Leeds Enthesitis Index, tender dactylitis, physician global Visual Analogue Scale, patient-reported disease activity, Short Form Health Survey physical component summary and C-reactive protein (CRP).37 Pharmacological treatment for PsA disease was evaluated by an internally developed questionnaire and checked with medical reports.

Cardiometabolic risk parameters

The following appropriate outcome measures according to European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology and European Society of Cardiology guidelines were selected to evaluate the cardiometabolic risk profile.17 19 Blood pressure, body mass index (BMI) and waist-hip ratio were measured by the physical therapist-researcher. Non-fasting blood samples were analysed for CRP, glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) and cholesterol levels (total cholesterol, low density cholesterol and high density cholesterol). Self-reported PA was measured by the validated questionnaire Short Questionnaire to Assess Health-enhancing physical activity (SQUASH).38 Moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) minutes in 1 week (total MVPA minutes/week) and MVPA minutes per week during commuting and leisure time (MVPA during commuting and leisure time (COM+LT) minutes/week) were calculated. The proportion of patients fulfilling WHO guidelines for aerobic PA, that is, at least 30 min of moderate PA per day on at least 5 days per week, was defined.39 Comorbidities and their pharmacological treatment were evaluated by the Self-Administered Comorbidity Questionnaire and internally developed questionnaire, respectively, and checked with medical reports.40 41 Cardiometabolic risk factors or comorbidities were assigned if patients had an established diagnosis or were using medication, but not if isolated abnormal values were observed during the study visit. 10 years’ cardiovascular disease risk was calculated using the SCORE2 without inclusion of a multiplication factor for rheumatic and musculoskeletal diseases (RMDs).17 18

Patient-reported outcome measures

Patient-reported disease activity (NRS 0–100), patient-reported joint pain (NRS 0–100) and current physical therapy use (yes/no) were evaluated. As recommended by OMERACT, quality of life, physical function, PsA disease impact and fatigue were assessed by 36-Item Short Form Health Survey (SF-36), Health Assessment Questionnaire Disability Index, Psoriatic Arthritis Impact of Disease 12-item questionnaire (PsAID12) and Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy-Fatigue Scale, respectively.42,46

Statistical analysis

Continuous data are presented as mean (SD) and categorical data as percentages (counts). The applied cut-off values for categorising continuous data are specified at the relevant parameter or in (online supplemental table S2 available in Supplemental Material). Normal distribution of data was evaluated by frequency histograms. VO2peak was compared with two different reference charts of the general population: a sedentary general population of the USA (Wasserman) and a Dutch-Flemish physically active population (LowLands Fitness registry 2021).47 48 The relative value of VO2peak was expressed as a percentage of the predicted value of the reference population, in which ‘100% predicted’ represented the median value of the reference population. VO2peak below 80% predicted following the Wasserman 2005 equation (sedentary general population) and/or below the lower limit of normal following the LowLands Fitness registry 2021 (physically active population) was considered as impaired CRF. The one-sided independent t-test was used to examine if CRF of patients was significantly decreased compared with the reference population. Multivariable linear regression analysis, adjusted for age and sex, was performed to evaluate the associations of disease parameters, cardiometabolic risk parameters and PROMs with VO2peak in mL/min/kg. Thereafter, a full multivariable linear regression model with VO2peak in mL/min/kg as dependent variable was built. Using forward stepwise regression, significant disease and cardiometabolic risk parameters as well as PROMs were added to the basic model consisting of independent variables age and sex. Regression coefficients for continuous variables were standardised. Model fit and assumptions were criticised by residual plots, variance inflation factors and Breusch-Pagan test for heteroscedasticity. Due to heteroscedasticity, a log-transformation of VO2peak (mL/min/kg) was applied.

Precision sample size calculation was performed to estimate VO2peak, resulting in a sample size of a minimum 30 patients (SD: 7.00 mL/min/kg; margin error: 2.47 mL/min/kg; 95% CI, mean VO2peak: 30.00 mL/min/kg; PASS software, NCSS, USA).49 50 To allow multivariable regression analysis with inclusion of eight possible predictors of VO2peak, minimally 80 patients with PsA needed to be included according to the rule of 10 events per variable.51 Since VO2peak is dependent on sex and awareness of potential sex-related differences in patients with PsA is growing, we presented results for the total group as well as male and female patients separately.52 53 There was no missing data in CPET outcomes and in less than 5% of the other outcomes, hence no imputation techniques were used. Statistical significance was defined as p value<0.05. The Holm-Bonferroni method was applied in the full multivariable linear regression model to adjust for multiple testing. Statistical analysis was carried out in RStudio V.2024.12.1+563.

Results

Patients characteristics

Of the 161 patients invited to participate, 83 patients with PsA were included in the study, of which 80 completed the CPET and were analysed (figure 1). No significant differences in age and sex were observed between participating and non-participating patients (online supplemental table S1 available in Supplemental Material). All patient’ characteristics of the included patients are presented in table 1. Mean age was 50.99 years (SD=11.01), mean disease duration 11.12 years (SD=7.80) and 41% (N=33) were female patients. 94% (N=75) were treated with at least one disease modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD), 68% (N=54) of the patients had low PsA disease activity and 15% (N=12) had peripheral and axial PsA. 13% (N=10) of the patients were currently smoking and 55% (N=43) reported that they fulfilled the WHO guidelines for aerobic PA. 74% (N=35) of male and 52% (N=17) of female patients, respectively, were being overweight according to the waist-hip ratio criteria. 40% (N=19) of male and 85% (N=28) of female patients, respectively, had low cardiovascular risk according to the SCORE2 calculation. Hypertension, hyperlipidaemia, diabetes mellitus type II and depression or anxiety were present in 45% (N=21), 49% (N=23), 19% (N=9) and 9% (N=4) of the male patients and in 18% (N=6), 30% (N=10), 6% (N=2) and 9% (N=3) of the female patients, respectively. 46% (N=37), 66% (N=52), and 35% (N=28) of the patients reported an impaired physical function level, a moderate to high disease impact, and a moderate to severe fatigue level, respectively.

Cardiorespiratory fitness level

CPET data are presented in table 2 and figure 2 and spirometry results in (online supplemental table S3 available in Supplemental Material). The mean VO2peak was 2533 mL/min (SD=621.12) and 28.05 mL/min/kg (SD=8.23) for men, and 1648 mL/min (SD=373.34) and 23.15 mL/min/kg (SD=5.41) for women. VO2peak of the patients with PsA was comparable to a sedentary reference population (mean 101.50% (SD=22.26), p>0.05), but decreased compared with a physically active reference population (mean 74.01% (SD=19.19), p<0.01). An impaired CRF level was observed in 16% of the patients according to a sedentary reference population, but in 41% according to a physically active reference population (patients’ characteristics in online supplemental table S11 available in Supplemental Material). The mean anaerobic threshold was situated at 65.95% (SD=12.65) of VO2peak in mL/min/kg. An abnormal cardiovascular or ventilatory response during CPET was observed in 13 (16%) patients, for which referral to specialised care was provided when necessary (online supplemental table S8 available in Supplemental Material).

Criteria of maximal CPET were achieved in 94% (N=75) of the patients, of which the majority reached their cardiocirculatory limits at the end of CPET (online supplemental table S4 available in Supplemental Material). Patient-reported reasons to stop CPET were mainly dyspnoea and fatigue in the legs (78%, N=62). 11 patients (15%) stopped CPET due to articular problems in the lower limbs (online supplemental table S4 available in Supplemental Material). 14 patients (18%) reported an increase in at least two disease-related symptoms at 3 days after the CPET (online supplemental table S6 available in Supplemental Material). Relation between cardiorespiratory fitness level and disease parameters, cardiometabolic risk and PROMs.

Linear regression analysis adjusted for age and sex is presented in (online supplemental table S10 available in Supplemental Material). Musculoskeletal PsA disease activity (PASDAS) was inversely related to VO2peak. Disease duration, treatment with biological or targeted synthetic Disease Modifying Antirheumatic Drugs, PsA type and presence of psoriasis were not associated. A suboptimal cardiometabolic risk profile with unfavourable body composition, very high SCORE2 risk, lower HDL-cholesterol, impaired HbA1c level, lower self-reported PA or a diagnosis of hypertension, hyperlipidaemia or diabetes mellitus type II was significantly associated with lower VO2peak. In contrast, no associations were noted for smoking, low density lipoprotein and non-HDL cholesterol levels, treatment with lipid lowering drugs and diagnosis of depression or anxiety. PROMs evaluating disease impact, fatigue, physical function and pain were significantly associated with VO2peak. However, no significant associations for physical therapy use and mental health scores were noted. Ultimately, significant and relevant parameters of the above linear regression analysis were selected to build the full multivariable linear regression model. This final multivariable linear regression model (table 3) revealed that, after adjustment for age and sex, higher musculoskeletal disease activity (PASDAS: β=−0.2757, 95% CI −0.4488 to −0.1025, p*=0.009), unfavourable body composition (waist-hip ratio: β=−0.4193, 95% CI −0.5987 to −0.2399, p*<0.001), worse patient-reported disease impact (PsAID12: β=−0.2385, 95% CI −0.4107 to −0.0663, p*=0.015) and lower self-reported PA (MVPA COM+LT: β=0.1702, 95% CI 0.0358 to 0.3046, p*=0.015) remained all significantly associated with lower VO2peak in mL/min/kg. Moreover, this final model explained 71% of the variance in VO2peak.

Discussion

This cross-sectional study was the first to examine CRF in patients with PsA and its relationship with disease parameters, cardiometabolic risk profile and PROMs. The CRF level of included patients with PsA was similar to a sedentary general population, but significantly decreased compared with a physically active population. 41% of patients with PsA had an impaired CRF according to the reference values of a physically active population. The included patients had low to moderate PsA disease activity and were more overweighted, less likely to meet the WHO guidelines for aerobic PA and more frequently exhibiting cardiometabolic risk factors compared with the general population.54 55 Two previous studies, examining CRF using a cycle ergometer in patients with low-moderate PsA disease activity, reported similar findings when compared with the sedentary and physically active general population.47,4956 We observed lower absolute VO2peak levels (mean 26.03 mL/min/kg, SD=7.56) compared with the Norwegian study (mean 29.51 mL/min/kg 95% CI 27.97 to 31.05).49 Patients did not differ in terms of disease activity and BMI. However, the Norwegian study participants were younger, appeared to have fewer cardiovascular risk factors, and were recruited for an exercise intervention trial, which might have favoured the inclusion of more physically active individuals. Similarly, a decreased CRF level was observed in patients with rheumatoid arthritis (RA) and axial spondylarthritis (axSpA) by several previous studies using a maximal exercise capacity test on a treadmill or bicycle.57,63

A lower CRF level was substantially associated with impaired disease control, an unfavourable body composition, less MVPA in commuting and leisure time as well as with higher patient-reported disease impact. 71% of the variance in VO2peak was explained by these clinical parameters, while the remaining 29% might be accounted for by other established determinants of CRF such as genetic factors and familial environment.64 First, a more favourable body composition is an important determinant of higher CRF levels in the general population, which was confirmed in our patients with PsA and in previous work in RMDs.53 59 60 Second, the beneficial effect of PA on CRF in the general population is largely known.53 Accordingly, we observed a significant association of self-reported MVPA minutes/week during commuting and leisure time, but not total MVPA minutes/week, with CRF in the final multivariable regression model. This finding is supported by the PA paradox indicating that PA during leisure time rather than during occupational time is related to health benefits.65,67 Several studies in patients with RMDs have previously confirmed the relationship between PA and CRF, though they did not differentiate between PA during occupational and leisure time.5760,62

Third, higher disease activity according to PASDAS was significantly associated with lower CRF level. This significant inverse correlation between CRF and inflammation was already noted in the general population.29 53 However, in previous studies in patients with RA and axSpA no significant relation between CRF and disease activity was observed, with the exception of one study reporting a significant association with patient global assessment, another study with enthesitis and a third study with CRP.57,6063 Fourth, the association between self-reported disease impact and CRF remained significant after adjustment for disease activity as well as age, sex, body composition and PA level. The PASDAS composite score for PsA disease activity reflects disease activity and inflammation at a single time point. However, symptoms and disease impact may not resolve immediately after controlling disease activity. The PsAID12 questionnaire assessing patient-reported disease impact might additionally capture the impact of residual symptoms during post-inflammatory state, potential structural damage and the influence of PsA disease on each individual’s life.68 This questionnaire contains items related to fatigue and physical functioning. Other outcome measures targeting these domains were also significantly associated with VO2peak in the preparatory regression analysis. The relation between fatigue and CRF assessed by a maximal exercise capacity test was not demonstrated in previous work in RMDs, though fatigue was identified as an important barrier to PA by patients with RMDs.60 69 70 The association with physical function and quality of life was confirmed in patients with axSpA.58 63 To illustrate the relevance of the measured CRF levels in the context of daily physical functioning, one in four of the included patients with PsA would need approximately their maximum exercise capacity (20.09 mL/min/kg) to perform leisure non-electrical bicycling (20.30 mL/min/kg) or carrying groceries upstairs (18.6 mL/min/kg) according to the Ainsworth compendium of physical activities.71

Some of the expected associations with CRF were not identified in our study results. Smoking is a known cardiovascular risk factor and correlated with CRF in the general population, though it was not associated with CRF in our study.19 53 Previously, conflicting results regarding smoking and CRF in patients with RMDs were reported.57 60 Although literature describes a higher prevalence of psychological comorbidities in PsA and highlights the relation between mental health and CRF in the general population, we did not observe reduced mental health according to SF-36 mental component summary in our cohort, nor have we found an association between mental health and CRF.5 72 73 Lastly, no significant association with disease duration was observed, which is affirmed by previous work in RMDs.57 63 With the current treatment options aiming for remission or low disease activity, flares of disease activity rather than disease duration determine the impact on the individual patient.7,9

The decreased CRF level in patients with PsA and its significant association with disease activity, cardiometabolic risk profile and PROMs, suggest that improving CRF might become a crucial target to reduce PsA disease impact. Previously, high intensity interval training during 3 months resulted in a significant and clinically relevant long-term increase in CRF of patients with PsA.4974,76 This training programme was safe and well tolerated, as there was no deleterious effects on peripheral and axial PsA disease activity.4974,76 A more consistent assessment of CRF in clinical practice may help identify patients with reduced CRF levels and support referral to appropriate exercise interventions.4974,78

Although the study was not designed to examine sex-related differences, distinctions in cardiometabolic risk profile and prevalence of decreased CRF between male and female patients were observed, with males being more affected. A recent systematic review confirms the tendency of higher prevalence of cardiovascular risk factors in male patients with PsA, while mixed data was found for obesity and metabolic syndrome.52 It would be valuable for future research to comprehensively investigate sex-related differences in CRF and cardiometabolic risk profile among patients with PsA.

Some strengths and limitations of this study should be considered. This was the first study examining CRF and its associates with clinical outcomes in a large group of patients with PsA. Broad inclusion criteria were used and an adequate inclusion rate was noted. This monocentric study was conducted in tertiary care in Belgium, but in the Belgium BEPAS (Belgian Epidemiological Psoriatic Arthritis Study) cohort minor differences in patient characteristics were observed between secondary and tertiary rheumatology care.79 Therefore, the findings can be considered representative for patients with PsA treated in Belgium. However, additional research across diverse patient populations is necessary to determine whether similar outcomes can be observed. We measured CRF as VO2peak using breath-by-breath gas analysis during maximal CPET, which is the gold standard measurement. Previously, CRF of patients with RMDs was frequently estimated based on submaximal exercise tests or even non-exercise models, partly due to concerns about the ability of patients to perform maximal exercise.80,84 Nonetheless, criteria of maximal CPET were achieved in nearly all patients and the physiological response was similar to the general population.34 The majority of patients reported no increase in disease symptoms at 3 days after the physical tests. Hence, it is feasible and safe to perform maximal exercise capacity testing on a cycle ergometer and validly measure CRF in patients with PsA.

Unfortunately, due to the cross-sectional study design, the causality and direction of the observed relations between CRF and disease parameters, cardiometabolic risk profile as well as PROMs could not be determined and require further study. As CRF is dependent on age and sex, comparison with age-sex matched controls is needed.53 In our study protocol, it was not feasible to include a large and suitable control group. Therefore, reference charts derived from larger cohorts of the general population were used for comparison, but identifying the most appropriate reference values proved challenging. The Wasserman equations are widely used as reference values for the sedentary general population, but they originated more than 30 years ago in the USA and may not fully reflect current Belgium population characteristics.47 With the current therapeutic options, the goal is for patients with PsA to integrate into the physically active general population.7,9 Accordingly, reference charts of the local Dutch-Flemish physically active general population (LowLands Fitness registry 2021) were included in the analysis.48 However, the higher BMI in the patients with PsA may have contributed to lower relative VO2peak levels expressed in mL/min/kg, thereby accentuating the difference with the reference group. Another potential study limitation is that patients not meeting the maximal CPET criteria were not excluded from the analysis. However, this applied to only 5 out of 80 patients, reasons for not reaching the maximal CPET criteria were mainly disease-related, and criteria of maximal CPET show some variability. Hence, inclusion of the few patients with submaximal CPET in the analysis likely supports the representativeness of the PsA population under study. Lastly, body composition and cardiovascular risk assessments were limited to conventional measures, for example, BMI, waist-hip ratio, blood pressure, smoking status and analysis of blood markers. Cardiovascular risk was calculated using the SCORE2 risk algorithm as recommended in the EULAR guidelines.17 18 However, recent studies have investigated the added value of alternative cardiovascular risk scoring algorithms in patients with RMDs, such as QRISK3, PREVENT-CVD, PREVENT-ASCVD and METS-IR.85,87 Inclusion of alternative cardiovascular risk scoring algorithms as well as advanced measures of body composition and cardiovascular risk, for example, fat mass and fat-free mass by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry or arterial stiffness by aortic pulse wave velocity, might further improve cardiometabolic risk classification in future studies.85,89

To conclude, while the CRF level of patients with PsA was comparable to that of a sedentary general population, it was significantly lower than that of a physically active population, with 41% of patients showing impaired CRF based on reference values of a physically active general population. A lower CRF level was substantially associated with impaired disease control, an unfavourable body composition, less MVPA in commuting and leisure time, as well as with higher patient-reported disease impact. Although further research is needed to establish the causality and direction of these relations, improving CRF in patients with PsA might become a crucial target to reduce disease impact in PsA.

Online Menopause Information–Seeking Search Patterns and Commercial Content Over 2 Decades
Authors: Francesca R. Farina, James W. Griffith, Stephanie S. Faubion, Martha Hickey, Sarah Lensen, Monica Christmas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This cohort study evaluates 20-year trends to quantify changes in menopause-related online searches compared with other health domains.

Introduction

Public attention to menopause has increased in recent years,1 accompanied by growing commercial involvement, raising concerns about the promotion of menopause-related products and services with limited evidence of efficacy.2,3,4 Little empirical evidence has examined how this menopause gold rush4,5 has been reflected in population-level patterns of public interest. This study examined 20-year trends to quantify changes in menopause-related online searches compared with other health domains.

Methods

We used publicly available Google Trends6 data to examine menopause-related online search interest from January 2005 through October 2025. Monthly relative search volume (RSV) data were obtained for the US, United Kingdom, and Australia, selected a priori to represent large, English-speaking countries with distinct health care and media environments. Because this study did not involve human participants or identifiable information, the protocol was deemed exempt from review and the need for informed consent by the University of Chicago institutional review board. The study followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guidelines for cohort studies.

Search queries were prespecified and grouped into 4 domains (eTable in Supplement 1). The primary comparison contrasted menopause-related information-seeking searches (eg, diagnosis, symptoms, and treatment) with commercially marketed products and services (eg, supplements, apps, and natural remedies). Two additional domains were included as to provide context for broader trends in health-related search behavior: a general women’s health domain and a male hormone domain representing a commercially active analogue. Related queries were screened and classified using a preregistered protocol.7 Domain-specific monthly search volume estimates were constructed by aggregating RSVs across all retained queries. Full details are provided in the eMethods in Supplement 1.

The primary outcome was a commercialization index, defined monthly for each country as the proportion of menopause-related search activity attributable to commercial content, calculated as commercial menopause RSV divided by the sum of commercial and informational menopause RSV, capturing changes in relative composition rather than absolute search volume.

Temporal trends were evaluated using linear regression models with time modeled as a continuous variable. Differences across countries and domains were assessed using interaction terms. Inference was based on regression coefficients, Newey-West standard errors (12-month lag to account for serial correlation in monthly RSV data) and statistical significance values at the level of P < .05. Additional details are provided in the eMethods in Supplement 1. Analyses were performed using R, version 4.3.3 (R Project for Statistical Computing).

Results

Over the 20-year study period, the commercialization index increased by approximately 15 to 20 percentage points in all 3 countries (Figure), reflecting a shift in the relative composition of menopause-related searches toward commercial content. The index increased from 0.22 to 0.37 in the US, from 0.16 to 0.36 in the UK, and from 0.14 to 0.33 in Australia (2010 vs 2025). The monthly increase in the commercialization index was 0.0008 (95% CI, 0.0007-0.0009) in the US, 0.0006 (95% CI, 0.0003-0.0010) in the UK, and 0.0006 (95% CI, 0.0003-0.0008) in Australia, consistent with the observed increases over time. Early years (before 2010) were characterized by lower search volumes and greater variability.

Visual inspection suggested earlier increases in the US compared with the UK and Australia. Exploratory analyses found no evidence of plateauing in the US; instead, increases accelerated in the UK and Australia after 2018, leading to convergence across countries, consistent across alternative breakpoint specifications (2017-2019).

Discussion

From 2005 to 2025, the proportion of menopause-related searches that were related to commercial products and services increased by 15 to 20 percentage points in the United Kingdom, Australia, and the United States with no difference in the rate of increase by country. Increases appeared earlier in the US, with subsequent convergence across countries over time. Early variability in search patterns may, in part, reflect changes in menopause-related treatment and information seeking following the 2002 Women’s Health Initiative findings on menopausal hormone therapy.8 By 2025, approximately 1 in 3 menopause-related searches were commercial, compared with roughly 1 in 5 to 7 in 2010. The highest volume commercial menopause searches were related to supplements (black cohosh) and digital services (apps). These results suggest that individuals may be increasingly seeking nonclinical approaches to managing menopause, not only for symptom relief but also for ongoing guidance, tracking, or support outside traditional clinical encounters.

This study has limitations. Google Trends reflects relative rather than absolute search interest, and search behavior does not directly capture clinical behavior or information quality. Despite these limitations, our study provides empirical quantification of increasing commercialization in menopause-related search behavior using population-level search data. As commercial menopause content continues to expand, clinical guidance will increasingly need to compete with product-led messaging in shaping menopause management.

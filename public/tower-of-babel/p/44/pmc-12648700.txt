Unraveling complexity: A rare case of pulmonary sarcoidosis coinciding with systemic scleroderma
Authors: So Yeon Hwang, David Ho, Abhinav Mittal, Favia Dubyk, Felix Reyes
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe present a rare case of a 66-year-old female with systemic sclerosis and Raynaud's phenomenon, diagnosed at age 25, who was found to have multinodular pulmonary sarcoidosis. The coexistence of sarcoidosis and scleroderma, are relatively common, well-documented in medical literatures however, the relationship between the two is complex and multifactorial. She was referred to pulmonology for evaluation of a lung mass and nodule noted on chest x-ray. Chest CT revealed widespread bilateral pulmonary nodularity, more prominent on the right, with multifocal mass-like consolidation. Differential diagnoses included malignancy, infection, or inflammatory disease. A transbronchial lung biopsy of the right upper and middle lobes showed focal granulomatous inflammation with negative AFB staining, and lymph node biopsies were negative for malignancy. These findings favored a diagnosis of sarcoidosis over systemic sclerosis, which typically does not present with granulomatous inflammation. A PET CT was performed to further differentiate between inflammation and malignancy, revealing extensive perilymphatic nodularity and FDG-avid mass-like opacities, especially in the right upper and middle lobes, consistent with the sarcoid Galaxy sign. She was initiated on prednisone 5 mg and methotrexate 25 mg daily. Given the rarity of this presentation, recognizing the co-occurrence of these two autoimmune conditions was critical in reaching the correct diagnosis and initiating appropriate therapy.

Introduction

Sarcoidosis, a rare inflammatory lung disease, can rarely present with a multinodular form [1]. Coexistence between sarcoidosis and connective tissue diseases (CTDs) are relatively common, well-documented in medical literatures [2] however, the relationship between the two is complex and multifactorial which a clinician is faced with challenges in diagnosis and management. Both diseases can exhibit overlapping characteristics, including the presence of autoantibodies and immune mediated tissue injury. Overall, sarcoidosis is linked to a mildly increased risk of certain cancers (notably lymphoma) [3] and a higher rate of coexisting CTDs, likely due to shared inflammatory and immunologic mechanisms [4].New sentence for revision The Prevalence of systemic scleroderma and sarcoidosis overlap syndrome was found to be 0.30 % among patients with systemic scleroderma [5]. Clinical significance of the two diseases overlapping comes with diagnostic complexity, atypical organ manifestation, severe organ involvement, and critical timing in management when the two diseases overlap. Sarcoidosis often precedes scleroderma and pulmonary manifestation, including pulmonary hypertension and interstitial lung disease, tend to be overlapping with scleroderma which can complicate clinical manifestation and require histopathologic confirmation to differentiate between the two diseases [6]. Identifying sarcoidosis is important in patients who have the clinical features of the two diseases overlapping because it may respond to corticosteroid therapy which is not a standard therapy for scleroderma alone [7]. This case discusses a 66-year-old female with both conditions, highlighting diagnostic challenges (see Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3).

Case presention

The 66-year-old female, who had been diagnosed with systemic sclerosis at age 25 with manifestations of calcinosis, GERD, Raynaud's, telangiectasia, sclerodactyly, dysphagia with serology of ANA >1:1280, centromere positive, Scl 70 negative, had been referred to the pulmonology clinic for a pulmonary nodule and lung mass initially seen on chest x-ray from a visit by her primary care on November 2023. The CT chest showed extensive widespread bilateral pulmonary nodularity, more severe on the right, with multifocal areas of mass-like consolidation. These findings raised concerns for malignant, infectious, or inflammatory etiologies.

Interventional pulmonology was consulted for tissue sampling and a lung biopsy had been performed. right upper and right middle lobe transbronchial biopsy showed focal granulomatous inflammation with negative AFB staining, and all four lymph node biopsies had been negative for malignancy.

Her biopsy results raised a higher concern for sarcoidosis rather than lung involvement from systemic sclerosis, given the focal granulomatous inflammation and lack of fibrosis, vasculopathy, and mononuclear cell infiltrates. A PET CT scan was then ordered to differentiate inflammation from malignancy in the setting of autoimmune disease. The PET CT scan resulted in extensive perilymphatic nodularity involving both lungs with FDG-avid mass-like opacities, most pronounced in the central right upper and right middle lobes, compatible with the sarcoid Galaxy sign. The patient was then started on prednisone 5mg and methotrexate 25 mg daily.

Discussion

The patient, diagnosed with systemic sclerosis at age 25, stable without treatment until now, was referred for a pulmonary nodule and mass seen on chest x-ray. CT imaging revealed extensive bilateral pulmonary nodularity with areas of mass-like consolidation, raising concerns for malignancy, infection, or inflammation. Tissue biopsies showed granulomatous inflammation without malignancy, which led to the suspicion of sarcoidosis. A PET CT scan revealed characteristic sarcoid Galaxy sign, confirming the diagnosis. Treatment with prednisone and methotrexate was initiated, emphasizing the importance of recognizing sarcoidosis in patients with autoimmune diseases like scleroderma. This rare presentation required careful differentiation from other potential causes of lung lesions. For this particular patient, the patient was not on any anti-inflammation therapy for her scleroderma. She was initially on methotrexate when she was 25 but was discontinued due to having side effects from the patient report. With respect to time to diagnosis of stage II pulmonary sarcoidosis she was started on oral prednisone 5mg and methotrexate 25mg a month after the PET-CT chest was done. The patient did not want to start on any anti-inflammatory therapy until she saw her rheumatologist. In patients with overlapping autoimmune and sarcoidosis, a more aggressive regimen may be indicated in order to avoid the sequelae of untreated or poorly treated sarcoidosis; especially pulmonary hypertension and fibrosis in scleroderma. In consideration of patients with pre-existing autoimmune conditions, it is especially paramount to prevent progression towards pulmonary fibrosis, heart failure, renal failure etc. With consideration from an infectious disease standpoint, once immunocompromised patients with evidence of granulomas emerge, it becomes of utmost importance to rule out infection as patients have the propensity to quickly decompensate.

Conclusion

The coexistence of sarcoidosis with cancer or CTDs complicates diagnosis and management. It is crucial to thoroughly rule out other potential causes of granulomatous inflammation before confirming a diagnosis of sarcoidosis, particularly in individuals with a history of malignancy or connective tissue disease [2]. New sentence for revision This case underscores the diagnostic challenge of identifying pulmonary sarcoidosis in patients with autoimmune diseases. Clinicians should remain alert to the potential coexistence of sarcoidosis in patients with CTDs who develop new, unexplained, or unusual symptoms, especially persistent lymph node enlargement, lung infiltrates, or granulomatous skin lesions that are atypical for their underlying CTD [8,9]. New sentence for revision Early recognition and appropriate testing, including PET CT, histopathological confirmation was crucial in confirming the diagnosis and guiding treatment. The coexistence of these diseases necessitates a multidisciplinary approach and may alter both prognosis and therapeutic strategies. Survival does not change in coexistence with both diseases compared to scleroderma alone but organ specific complications should be observed with vigilance [1].

A multi-center clinical evaluation on first-in-class ROP-based IGRA for tuberculosis diagnosis
Authors: Qiang Li, Yulin Huang, Lihui Nie, Mengqiu Gao, Ruimei Zhang, Jiawei Yan, Hongyan Jia, Maoying Fu, Aolin He, Qiping Ge, Hua Li, Yanhua Song, Yu Pang, Liping Pan, Shisong Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryTuberculosis (TB) remains a leading global cause of death from infectious disease. Current enzyme-linked immunospot assay (ELISPOT)-based interferon-gamma release assays (IGRAs) rely on pooled synthetic peptides, which pose challenges for large-scale good manufacturing practice (GMP) production and quality control. To address these limitations, we developed recombinant overlapping peptide (ROP) technology and evaluated its diagnostic performance in a multi-center clinical trial in China (September 2021–August 2022). Two reagents, representing distinct antigen presentation pathways, demonstrated high concordance. ROP-TB showed excellent agreement with T-SPOT.TB, yielding positive, negative, and overall agreement rates of 95.29%, 93.51%, and 94.49%, respectively (κ = 0.889, p < 0.001). The sensitivity and specificity of ROP-TB were 88.03% and 76.79%. Notably, ROP-TB elicited a significantly stronger immune response amplitude in individuals with presumed latent and drug-resistant TB. Our findings suggest that ROP-TB is a viable auxiliary diagnostic and screening tool, offering performance comparable to T-SPOT.TB with enhanced detection of latent and resistant strains.Subject areas: Health sciences, Medicine, Respiratory medicine

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) is a chronic infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (Mtb). There were estimated 10.7 million new TB cases and 1.23 million deaths from TB globally in 2024, and TB remains the highest number of deaths of infectious diseases in the world.1 The burden of TB falls disproportionately on low- and middle-income countries, particularly India, Indonesia, the Philippines, China, and many countries in South-East Asia, the Western Pacific, and Africa.1 Although, sputum smear microscopy and bacterial culture are traditional methods for diagnosing TB, but the sensitivities of them are low, and the culture is time-consuming. It was recommended to combine traditional methods with molecular detection technologies, especially in patients with negative sputum smear or culture results.2 The World Health Organization (WHO) recommended the use of nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) for the detection of Mtb.3,4 Although NAATs have high sensitivity and specificity, the detection relies on specimens with bacteria, which requires high laboratory technology, and the cost is expensive, limiting its application in underdeveloped areas.2 In addition, some extra-pulmonary TB (EPTB) specimens are difficult to obtain, such as liver TB, spleen TB, endometrial TB, etc. In 2024, only 54% of the 8.3 million newly diagnosed TB patients worldwide received rapid molecular testing recommended by the WHO.1 Chest radiography or lung ultrasound can reflect structural changes in the lungs over time.5,6 However, these imaging methods cannot accurately distinguish TB from lesions caused by other lung diseases, such as pneumonia and lung cancer,7 so the qualitative diagnosis relies on pathology. Obtaining tissue specimens for pathological examination is an invasive procedure and is not easily accepted by patients. Moreover, the various methods are difficult to make accurate judgment in the early stages of TB infection (TBI). Therefore, the development of rapid, highly sensitive, and specific diagnostic technologies is of great significance for improving the efficiency of TB prevention and control, reducing the risk of TB transmission, and lowering the mortality.

Currently, the immunological methods recommended by WHO for diagnosing TBI mainly include tuberculin skin test (TST), Mtb antigen-based skin tests (TBSTs), and interferon-gamma release assays (IGRAs).4 IGRAs are also recommended as supplementary diagnostic tools for active TB.8 TST is easily affected by the comorbidities and immune status of the patients and has cross-reactions with Bacille Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccination and non-tuberculous mycobacteria (NTM).9 TBSTs rely on the local immune response induced by the injection of Mtb-specific antigens, and it needs to be evaluated 48–72 h after injection.4 The principle of IGRAs is that, following infection with Mtb, the host generates specific memory T lymphocytes. When these cells are re-exposed to Mtb-specific antigens such as early secretory antigenic target 6 kDa (ESAT-6) and culture filtrate protein-10 (CFP-10), they mount a memory response and release interferon-gamma (IFN-γ).10 Since ESAT-6 and CFP-10 are expressed in the Mtb complex but are absent from BCG strains and most NTM, IGRAs provide superior specificity compared with TST.10 IGRAs are primarily performed in two formats: enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) and enzyme-linked immunospot assay (ELISPOT).9 The ELISPOT-based assays are considered the most accurate, detecting Mtb-specific T cells that release IFN-γ in response to ESAT-6 and CFP-10.11 However, current ELISPOT-based IGRAs rely on multiple synthetic overlapping peptides, each requiring separate good manufacturing practices (GMPs) and quality control. This increases complexity and cost, making such assays expensive and less suitable for large-scale use in high TB burden countries. These limitations pose significant barriers to large-scale manufacturing, regulatory approval, and cost-effectiveness. There is therefore a clear unmet need for an economical assay that retains the high diagnostic accuracy of current ELISPOT-based IGRAs.

Recombinant overlapping peptide (ROP) technology overcomes these limitations. ROP-TB derived from ESAT-6 and CFP-10 is artificially synthesized by E. coli.12 In ROP constructs, overlapping peptides are linked by an enzymatic linker, enabling a single GMP process and one quality control step.12 Once processed within antigen-presenting cells (APCs), endogenous enzymes cleave the linker to generate overlapping peptides that comprehensively cover antigenic epitopes, which simultaneously activate helper T cells and cytotoxic T cells, inducing a strong cellular immune response.12,13 This approach preserves the high accuracy of ELISPOT-based IGRAs while simplifying production and reducing cost, making it well suited for large-scale manufacture and deployment in high-burden settings.

We conducted a large, multi-center clinical trial to directly compare ROP-TB with the reference T-SPOT.TB assay, assess the concordance of their results, and verify the clinical utility of ROP-TB for the diagnosis of active TB.

Results

Basic information of the participants

A total of 1,193 suspected TB patients were recruited in four clinical trial centers. 366 patients were excluded for various causes, and there were 827 suspected TB patients in the final analysis (Figure 1). The cohort comprised 478 males (57.80%) and 349 females (42.20%), with 13 pediatric cases (1.57%) under 18 years old and 814 adult cases (98.43%). 405 patients (48.97%) were diagnosed with TB, comprising 237 (58.52%) definite TB cases and 168 (41.48%) probable TB cases. Disease distribution consisted of 373 (92.10%) pulmonary TB cases and 32 (7.90%) EPTB cases. The pulmonary TB included 115 smear positive patients, 211 smear negative patients, and 47 cases without smear results. A total of 422 patients (51.03%) were diagnosed as non-active TB, mainly including pneumonia, lung cancer, bronchitis, bronchiectasis, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, etc.

Flow chart of the enrolled participants

1,193 suspected TB patients were recruited; 366 patients were excluded due to coexisting HIV infection, autoimmune diseases, a diagnosis of TB or receiving anti-TB treatment before enrollment, and so on. 405 active TB patients (including 237 definite TB and 168 probable TB patients) and 422 non-active TB patients were analyzed finally.

Indeterminate results of ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays

Among the 827 analyzed cases in this study, the indeterminate results were observed in 8 cases (0.97%) for the ROP-TB assay including 4 TB cases and 4 non-active TB cases. For the T-SPOT.TB assay, 5 cases (0.60%) were reported with the indeterminate results, comprising 2 TB cases and 3 non-active TB cases. Among the 13 cases, 2 non-active TB cases were reported with indeterminate results for both assays.

Concordance analysis between ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays

54 cases (6.53%) had discrepant results between ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays among 827 patients. Excluding 11 cases with indeterminate results, concordance analysis was conducted in 816 participants who had valid results of ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays (Table 1). Among them, both assays were positive in 425 cases (52.08%) and negative in 346 cases (42.40%), and the overall agreement was 94.49% (95% confidence interval [CI] = 92.69%–95.95%). Statistical analysis revealed excellent concordance between ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays (kappa = 0.889, p < 0.001).

Diagnostic performance of ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays in active TB and presumed latent TBI

The clinical diagnostic performance of ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays in active TB was assessed (Table 2). For the ROP-TB assay, excluding 8 cases with indeterminate results, 353 cases tested positive and 48 cases tested negative in the TB patients, and 97 cases were positive and 321 cases were negative in the non-active TB patients. In comparison, excluding 5 cases with indeterminate results, the T-SPOT.TB assay identified 353 cases as positive and 50 cases as negative in the TB patients, and identified 99 cases as positive and 320 cases as negative in the non-active TB patients. There was no statistically significant difference in sensitivity (p = 0.358), specificity (p = 0.392), and overall concordance (p = 0.123) between ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays.

Given the strong concordance between the two antigens, we performed further analysis of T cell sensitivity to ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB antigens by plotting ELISPOT spot-forming unit (SFU) values for different TB patient groups (Figure 2). After excluding indeterminate results (n = 11), there were 399 active TB patients tested with ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays with valid results; the number of SFUs [M (P25, P75)] were 54 (20, 134) per 2.5∗105 peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and 51 (15, 144) per 2.5∗105 PBMCs, respectively. The difference between the two assays was not statistically significant (p = 0.432). A total of 417 non-active TB individuals were tested using both ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays with valid results. They were further stratified into three groups based on their ELISPOT responses: (1) dual positive, defined as the presumed latent TBI group (n = 85); (2) dual negative, representing no Mtb infection (n = 308); and (3) discordant, including ROP-TB positive but T-SPOT.TB negative (n = 12) and ROP-TB negative but T-SPOT.TB positive (n = 12). Notably, in group 1 (presumed latent TBI), ROP-TB stimulation induced significantly higher SFU counts than T-SPOT.TB stimulation [29 (17, 57)] per 2.5∗105 PBMCs vs. 19 (9, 52) per 2.5∗105 PBMCs, p < 0.001). In groups 2–3, SFU values were either below the diagnostic threshold (group 2) or showed no statistically significant difference between ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays (group 3), with SFUs of 7 (2, 15) per 2.5∗105 PBMCs and 6 (3, 23) per 2.5∗105 PBMCs, respectively (p = 0.679).

Discussion

In this work, we present the clinical evaluation of a novel, first-in-class antigen design for TB diagnosis. Our results show that ROP-TB is highly concordant with the T-SPOT.TB assay and demonstrates increased immune response amplitude in presumed latent and drug-resistant TB. TB is an intracellular pathogen that elicits strong CD4+ and CD8+ T cell immune responses, which subsequently differentiate into long-lived memory T cells and provide sustained protection forming the basis for IGRAs.14 By contrast, antibody-based tests are unreliable due to false-positive or false-negative results and are therefore not recommended by WHO.15 Cellular immunity plays the central role in defense against Mtb, and the release of IFN-γ provides a measurable IGRA readout of this response. Among different IGRA assays, ELISPOT-based IGRAs offer higher sensitivity than ELISA-based formats, as they quantify IFN-γ secretion at the single-cell level, enabling more precise detection of low-frequency Mtb-specific T cell responses.11,16 Therefore, we used the T-SPOT.TB as the reference kit. Zhang et al.12 reported that compared to the Mtb synthetic peptides ESAT-6 and CFP-10, ROP-TB elicited a strong immune response and effectively stimulated memory T cells responses in TB patients with diverse human leukocyte antigen (HLA) types, indicating that ROP-TB can be used as an auxiliary tool for the diagnosis of TB. Our study showed that ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays had excellent concordance (kappa = 0.889) (Table 1) and comparable sensitivity and specificity (Table 2). China was one of the 30 countries with a high TB burden in the world, and the latent infection rate of Mtb was 20.3% in people aged 15 and above.17 The specificity of both assays align with the clinically acceptable range (triage test >70%). In presumed latent and drug-resistant TB patients, the number of SFUs by ROP-TB assay was significantly higher than that of T-SPOT.TB. These findings indicate that ROP-TB may exhibit stronger immune response amplitude than T-SPOT.TB in the subgroups of TB patients, even though the two assays show strong concordance in qualitative diagnostic outcomes.

Subgroup analyses showed higher positivity rates for ROP-TB compared with T-SPOT.TB among patients with drug-resistant TB and EPTB (Tables 4 and 5). Although qualitative analysis showed no statistically significant differences, quantitative ELISPOT analysis revealed significantly higher SFU counts following ROP-TB stimulation than T-SPOT.TB in the drug-resistant subgroup, indicating stronger T cell responses and suggesting greater assay sensitivity in this setting. These findings support the potential utility of ROP-TB in challenging clinical contexts, particularly in drug-resistant TB.

ROP-TB assay has certain advantages: ROP-TB is composed of overlapping peptides linked by the cathepsin S substrate LRMK, covering the full sequences of ESAT-6 and CFP-10. This structure provides a comprehensive repertoire of T cell epitopes, while the LRMK linker has been shown to facilitate peptide loading onto MHC molecules.18 Consequently, this design enhances diagnostic sensitivity and ensures the assay remains robust across diverse HLA phenotypes and within immunocompromised populations.12,13 In APCs, ROP-TB antigen is degraded by cathepsin S, and the peptides can be presented by the MHC II pathway to activate CD4+ T cells and also by the cross-presenting pathway to activate CD8+ T cells.12,13 Following uptake by APCs, the ROP antigen construct is processed via a cross antigen-processing pathway, generating a series of overlapping peptide fragments for MHC molecule loading.12 Once the peptide-MHC complex is formed, its stability on the surface of APCs supports effective T cell activation.19 We hypothesize that ROP proteins engage antigen presentation through a mechanism distinct from that of synthetic peptide pools. Some of the synthetic peptide mixtures might remain extracellular and only partially access APCs, as electrostatic repulsion between similarly charged peptides and the surface of the APC membrane can limit efficient cellular uptake. In contrast, the ROP fusion protein is internalized efficiently as an intact macromolecule via endocytosis or macropinocytosis, enabling coordinated intracellular release and processing of the complete peptide repertoire. This mechanism ensures comprehensive antigen processing within APCs and may enhance both MHC class I and class II presentation pathways. Our results (Figures 2 and 4) show that ROP-TB elicits stronger cellular immune responses, indicating consistent greater potential of ROP-TB for latent and drug-resistant TB detection and future TB vaccine development.12,13

From a manufacturing perspective, the recombinant ROP antigen offers significant industrial advantages over peptide-based assays. As a single, standardized protein entity, it reduces the variability inherent in peptide synthesis, pooling, and degradation, thereby enhancing batch-to-batch consistency and storage stability. Furthermore, while synthetic peptide pools incur high costs due to the requirement for multiple GMP manufacturing runs and complex quality control protocols, ROP-TB is produced via a streamlined E. coli expression system. This allows for a single-stream GMP process, significantly reducing manufacturing costs and facilitating large-scale production.9,12 Consequently, ROP-TB is highly suitable for implementation in low- and middle-income countries.

Limitations of the study

To our knowledge, this study was the largest multi-center clinical trial globally that systematically evaluated the diagnostic performance of ROP-TB assay for the active TB by using the same participants. We acknowledged this study has some limitations. The numbers of drug-resistant TB and EPTB cases were limited, reducing the statistical power of subgroup analysis. This study did not specifically evaluate certain populations such as individuals with HIV co-infection or those receiving immunosuppressive therapy, in whom IGRA testing often yields false negatives.20 However, these groups represent important targets for future research to define the clinical applicability and broader potential of ROP-TB. This study included both male and female subjects. However, since the study design was not intended for analyzing sex-based differences, no sex-based subgroup analysis was conducted. Like IGRAs, ROP cannot distinguish between active TB and latent TBI. Simultaneously, because there is no universally accepted definition of latent TBI, this study only evaluated the performance of ROP as an adjunct diagnostic tool for active TB and did not assess its value as a diagnostic tool for latent TBI. In addition, the prevalence of latent TBI in China may have contributed to the moderate specificity for both assays.

In summary, ROP-TB demonstrated diagnostic performance comparable to T-SPOT.TB in TB patients. Given that ROP-TB assay simplifies the production process and reduces manufacturing cost, its clinical applicability is economical, especially in low- and middle-income countries with high TB burden.

Experimental model and study participant details

Ethics

This study protocol followed the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. And it was approved by the Ethics Committees of Beijing Chest Hospital, Capital Medical University (BJXK-2020-36, 16 December 2020; BJXK-2020-36-01, 20 October 2021), the Fifth People’s Hospital of Wuxi (2021-001-01, 7 February 2021; 2021-001-02, 23 November 2021), Xuzhou Infectious Disease Hospital (XCYPJ-2021012202, 22 January 2021; XCYPJ-20220113, 13 January 2022), the First People’s Hospital of Kunshan (2022-02-003-H01, 4 March 2022). All participants signed written informed consent before their inclusion in the study.

Study design

This was a prospective diagnostic accuracy study conducted in patients with suspected TB, evaluating the performance of the ROP-TB assay, using the T-SPOT.TB assay as the reference standard, with both assays performed in parallel. This study was conducted with blinding at multiple levels to prevent bias: laboratory personnel were blinded to clinical diagnostic information, the clinicians were blinded to the results of both the ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays, and personnel performing the two assays were blinded to each other’s results.

Participants

The multi-center trial was conducted between September 2021 and August 2022. The participants were suspected TB patients with TB-related symptoms (e.g., cough>2 weeks, fever, weigh loss) and signs and/or abnormal radiography. All participants had complete clinical data, including demographics, underlying diseases, medication history, imaging, histopathology and laboratory results. Exclusion criteria included patients who were diagnosed with TB or had received anti-TB treatment before enrollment, human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) positive, terminal cancer with a life expectancy of less than 3 months, autoimmune diseases, the usage of immunosuppressive drugs, severe liver and kidney damage or hematologic disorders, and NTM infection. All enrolled participants underwent ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays simultaneously.

Case definition

After unblinding of the clinical trial, the patient’s medical records were reviewed, and based on the participants' clinical manifestations, etiology, pathology, imaging and laboratory results, combined with their response to anti-TB treatment, the participants were classified as follows: (1) Definite TB: patients with positive smear or culture and the bacteria was identified as MTB complex; or positive MTB deoxyribonucleic acid by real-time fluorescent quantitative polymerase chain reaction or high-throughput sequencing technology; or TB was confirmed by the tissue pathology. Microscopic examination of the tissue reveals exudative, proliferative, and necrotic changes, and the molecular detection was positive, the pathological diagnosis of definite TB was made. (2) Probable TB: patients with good response to anti-TB treatment, but etiology and pathology were negative. (3) Non-active TB: patients with other lung diseases by etiology or pathology, and without any evidence of organ TB, the lung lesions have been absorbed without anti-TB treatment.

Sample size

According to clinical needs, the positive and negative agreement rates between ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays should both reach 85%. Based on the preliminary test results, the positive and negative agreement rates were both expected to reach 90%. Method details

Sample collection

All patients were enrolled and blood samples for ROP-TB assay and T-SPOT.TB assay were collected before the initiation of anti-TB treatment. Six to ten millilitres of fasting venous blood samples were collected in the morning using heparin sodium vacuum tube under aseptic condition. The peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were separated from the samples as soon as possible.

GMP manufacture, design, immune evaluation and stability of ROP-TB

All production activities, including raw material procurement, reagent preparation, filling, assembly, labeling, and packaging, were conducted in a standard GMP conditions. Critical raw materials and essential reagents undergo quality control and testing before use. The production process follows validated standard operating procedures and implements process control to ensure product consistency, traceability, and quality. Each batch of product undergo quality inspection according to specifications before release. Furthermore, the manufacturer should establish a comprehensive quality management system covering personnel training, equipment calibration and maintenance, document control, deviation management, and product traceability to ensure that the product complies with applicable regulations and quality standards throughout its entire process.

The design, synthesis, and preparation of ROP-TB, targeted antigens, and antigen-specific immune responses have been reported previously.12 Briefly, ROP-TB was designed to cover the complete amino acid sequences of MTB RD1 antigens ESAT-6 and CFP-10 via eight 35-peptide segments, with 10 amino acid overlaps between segments. Each peptide was linked by a cathepsin S-cleavable linker LRMK. The synthetic ROP-TB gene was cloned and expressed in E. coli BL21 (Solarbio, China), purified by Ni-NTA (GE, USA) affinity chromatography, followed by dialysis refolding and endotoxin removal chromatography (BIOENDO, China). The final purity was >95%, and the endotoxin was <1 EU/mL, as verified by HPLC and limulus amebocyte lysate assays (BIOENDO, China). The product has undergone repeated rigorous testing to assess its stability and in vitro efficacy. The generation of effective epitopes was verified by in vitro digestion of ROP-TB with cathepsin S (SinoBiological, China) followed by SDS-PAGE, as well as by confocal microscopy (ZEISS, LSM800) to observe the lysosomal processing of ROP-TB in DC2.4 cells (a gift from K Rock of Massachusetts University, United States). Cellular immune responses were assessed by ELISPOT assay, spleen cells from BALB/c mice (age 6–8 weeks, SPF) infected with Mtb (H37R1) and PBMCs from diagnosed TB patients and healthy controls were stimulated with ROP-TB, mixed peptides, or single peptides. In vivo, T cell immune response was evaluated via the delayed-type hypersensitivity skin test in guinea pigs infected with Mtb and cattle infected with M.bovis, with responses detected 24–72 h in guinea pigs and 48–72 h in cattle after intradermal injection.

Three independent production batches (TES210601, TES210602, and TES210603), with seven kits per batch, were sealed, protected from light, and stored at 2°C–8°C. Stability testing was conducted at 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, and 15 months after production. At 0 and 12 months, comprehensive quality evaluations were performed according to predefined release specifications, including assessment of appearance, acceptance rates of negative and positive reference controls, and assay precision (repeatability and inter-batch variability). At 3, 6, 9, and 15 months, appearance and reference control performance were evaluated. All tested batches met the predefined quality criteria at each time point. Based on these results, the reagent demonstrates stable performance under recommended storage conditions, supporting a proposed shelf life of 12 months when stored sealed and protected from light at 2°C–8°C.

ROP-TB assay and T-SPOT.TB assay

ROP-TB assay (Changzhou Wensong Biotechnology company Ltd., China) and T-SPOT.TB assay (Oxford Immunotec Ltd, UK) both used the ELISPOT-based method and were performed according to the manufacturers' instructions. Briefly, PBMCs were isolated and 2.5 × 105 PBMCs per well were incubated in the plates pre-coated with anti-IFN-γ antibody with PHA antigen for positive control, AIM-V medium for Nil control, and ROP-TB or ESAT-6/CFP-10 for TB-specific antigens for ROP-TB assay and T-SPOTTB, respectively, at 37°C and 5% CO2 for 16 to 20 h. After incubation, the plates were washed, and the appropriate detection antibodies and enzyme-labeled solutions were added. Visible spots formed after color development. And the SFUs were read by CTL ELISPOT counter (CTL-ImmunoSpotⓇS5 Versa Analyzer, United States) in Beijing Chest Hospital, and CTL IMMUN. SPOT reader (CTL S6 Entry, United States) in three other hospitals. For ROP-TB assay, the negative control ≤10 SFUs and the positive control ≥20 SFUs were required, otherwise, the result was considered as indeterminate. When the number of the SFUs of the detection well minus the negative control was ≥7 SFUs, the result was judged as positive; on the contrary, the result was judged as negative. For T-SPOT.TB assay, the positive result should meet any of the following conditions: the Nil control was 0–5 SFUs, and the TB-specific antigens (panel A or panel B) minus the Nil control was ≥6 SFUs; or the Nil control was 6–10 SFUs, and the panel A or panel B was twice of Nil control. When the positive control was <20 SFUs, the result was considered as indeterminate, unless the panel A or panel B was confirmed to be positive according to the above two conditions. If the above criteria were not met and the positive control was ≥20 SFUs, the result was considered negative. And the Nil control was ≥10 SFUs was also considered as indeterminate.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as n (%). Continuous variables with nonnormal distribution were presented as median and interquartile range. The diagnostic performance of ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays was evaluated in terms of sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), positive likelihood ratio (PLR), and negative likelihood ratio (NLR). Agreement between the two assays was assessed using concordance rates (positive, negative, and overall agreement) along with Cohen’s kappa statistic, where kappa values were interpreted as follows: <0.20, poor; 0.21–0.40, fair; 0.41–0.60, moderate; 0.61–0.80, good; and 0.81–1.00, excellent. Sensitivity, specificity, and overall concordance were compared between the two assays using McNemar’s test for paired proportions. In addition, the positivity rates of ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB among TB patients were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Comparisons between the ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays within each patient group were visualized using boxplots (showing median, IQR, and Tukey-style whiskers at 1.5 × IQR) with overlaid individual data points. The Wilcoxon signed-rank test was used to assess statistical significance. Two-tailed p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (r) was used to assess the correlation between plotting ELISPOT SFUs values by ROP-TB and T-SPOT.TB assays. The overall correlation analysis was performed in total suspected TB patients (n = 827), with R value and p value calculated, and a scatterplot was generated. Subsequently, correlation analyses were conducted in different TB patients. Correlation strength was interpreted as follows: |r| ≥ 0.7 indicated strong correlation, 0.4 ≤ |r| < 0.7 indicated moderate correlation, and |r| < 0.4 indicated weak correlation. All analyses were performed using SPSS Software (version 23.0).

Special issue on “Advances of Ultrasound Technology in Biomaterial Assembly and Theranostics”☆
Authors: Jiwei Cui, Yunlu Dai, Liang Cheng
Published: 2026

The special issue of Ultrasonics Sonochemistry, titled “Advances of Ultrasound Technology in Biomaterial Assembly and Theranostics” represents an innovative intersection among ultrasound technology, material engineering and biomedical applications. This convergence is explored across a wide range of scopes, including fundamentals of ultrasound-mediated assembly, sonochemistry-assisted materials synthesis, and novel strategies in ultrasonic diagnostics and therapeutics. This issue highlights the role of ultrasound in facilitating the assembly and structuring of biomaterials, with a focus on how ultrasonic techniques can be leveraged for the design of biocompatible materials with functional properties tailored for biomedical applications. The issue encompasses 18 original contributions and 9 reviews demonstrating these cutting-edge applications of ultrasound in biomaterial science and medical innovation.

The smart material integration, multimodal imaging, and precision delivery strategies, examined in several articles, reveals the potential of ultrasonic techniques in developing efficient and innovative biomedical application strategies. Furthermore, studies also demonstrate the application of ultrasound in the precise control over material properties and the formation of complex biological assemblies. These studies illustrate the exploration of ultrasound-assisted technology as a cornerstone in the development of advanced theranostic platforms, which integrate ultrasound-assisted diagnostic and therapeutic functionalities for personalized medicine and open avenues for future clinical biomedical applications. Overall, this special issue provides a comprehensive overview of current trends and future directions in the application of ultrasound technology in biomaterial assembly and theranostics field. It serves as valuable resource for researchers, medical staff, and practitioners seeking to explore transformative potential of ultrasound technology for the future of biomaterial science and medical innovation.

Central to this issue is the exploration of ultrasound-assisted synergistic theranostic applications. Contributions from researchers like Jing, Xie, and colleagues [1] prove that the combination of sonodynamic therapy (SDT), synergistic apoptosis/ferroptosis induction and dual-modal imaging can achieve synergistic tumor inhibition and complete tumor regression. Investigating the SDT-mediated synergistic immunotherapy, studies by Zhang et al. [2], Li et al. [3] and Cheng et al. [4] demonstrate that the amplification of oxidative stress induced by SDT, in conjunction with tumor metabolism regulation, leads to robust immunogenic tumor cell death (ICD) and reverses the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, thereby achieving a potent tumor-inhibitory effect. Song et al. [5] and Zhang et al. [6] further propose the use of ultrasound-driven nanomaterials as non-antibiotic, non-invasive antimicrobial strategies for treating drug-resistant bacterial infections by amplifying oxidative stress on bacteria. In addition, Li et al. [7] illustrate the development of the multifunctional nanoparticles of HA/AIPH@ZIF-8 (HAZ), which amplify sono-oxidative stress and trigger metal ion interference to induce efficient anticancer sono-metalloimmunotherapy. Contributions by Zhu and colleagues [8] review and emphasize the emerging regulatory strategies aimed at enhancing SDT efficacy through the induction or inhibition of autophagy.

The issue also highlights the effectiveness of sonication in achieving controlled drug delivery. For instance, Zhang, et al. [9] and Wang et al. [10] summarize the specific biomedical applications of theranostic nanomaterials combined with ultrasound technology, particularly focusing on ultrasound-responsive micro-/nanoplatforms. They discuss the advantages of these approaches and identify critical barriers to clinical translation. Additionally, Zhang et al. [11] specifically review the therapeutic applications of ultrasound-triggered nanomaterials in inflammatory diseases. A review by Cui, Zhou and their teams [12] elucidates the core physicochemical mechanisms underlying ultrasound-controlled drug release and the associated biological effects, along with outlining the design strategies to optimize responsiveness and therapeutic efficiency. Furthermore, the review by Qiu et al. [13] places special emphasis on the developments in ultrasound-enhanced gene transfection.

Another focus of this issue is the utilization of liquid–gas phase-change nanoplatforms to enhance multimodal ultrasound imaging performance and enable spatiotemporally controlled therapy. For instance, Huang et al. [14] review the fundamental mechanisms and applications of phase-change contrast agents (PCCAs) in improving ultrasound-guided diagnostic imaging, precision delivery, and the enhancement of ultrasound-mediated mono- and combination therapies. The research conducted by Guo and colleagues [15] on the development of an ultrasound-driven, oxidative stress-scavenging, liver-targeted nanobubble system exemplifies this area. The PCCAs system employs a lipid shell and perfluorocarbon (PFC) cores, demonstrating efficient controlled release of therapeutic agents upon ultrasonic activation and synergistic therapeutic effects.

The emerging field of ultrasound-activated piezoelectricity represents a significant area of focus. Contributions from Chen, Tang, and coauthors [16] review the development of ultrasound-triggered piezoelectric biomaterials and discuss advancements in cancer sonodynamic immunotherapy strategies. Furthermore, Wen, Liao, Li et al. [17] elaborate on the application of ultrasound-activated piezoelectric biomaterials in regenerative medicine, specifically emphasizing cartilage regeneration engineering. A study by Gou, Liu and their teams [18] further illustrate the benefits of ultrasound-driven piezoelectric potential derived from β-phase polyvinylidene fluoride (β-PVDF) film material for the programmed regulation of macrophage M1 polarization, thereby effectively promoting bone repair.

The Special Issue also delves into the realm of hybrid processes, where multimodal dynamic therapies are combined to enhance therapeutic performance. For example, the work of Teng, Wang, and their team [19] on biohybrid nano-platform exemplifies the integration of photodynamic and sonodynamic modalities, demonstrating their effectiveness as immunomodulators in precision oncology. Further, the study by Yan et al. [20] illustrates how acoustic cavitation effects generated by nanobubbles during ultrasound exposure can facilitate the delivery of IR808 into tumors, enabling ultrasound/fluorescence dual-modal imaging-guided photothermal treatment of breast tumors. Moreover, Józefczak et al. [21] advances localized ultrasound hyperthermia by incorporating iron oxide nanoparticles (IONPs) and Pickering emulsions into theranostic systems. This multimodal strategy combines ultrasound-induced and magnetic hyperthermia to achieve controlled heat localization and improved ultrasonic focusing.

Exploring the essential clinical efficacy of ultrasound technology, the Liu and Wang teams [22] highlight the benefits of 3D ultrasound as a non-ionizing diagnostic modality for accurate, radiation-free assessment of spinal curvature. Compared to conventional radiography, 3D ultrasound exhibits operational efficiency, diagnostic precision, and an improved safety profile in patients with mild-to-moderate adolescent idiopathic scoliosis.

In the field of synthesis and structural optimization of sonochemistry-assisted materials, Liu et al. [23] present a distinctive Ru(II) sonosensitizer featuring near-infrared absorption, enhanced stability and high 1O2 quantum yields. The complex synergistically integrates π-expansive ligands (IR780) with tetraphenylethylene (TPE), leveraging its exceptional electronic properties to enhance imaging-guided SDT for deep-seated tumors. Moreover, in another aspect of ultrasound-mediated material preparation, research conducted by Gao, Yu, and colleagues [24] investigates the use of high-frequency ultrasound (400 kHz) as an alternative method for the preparation of oxidized low-density lipoprotein.

Another systematic exploration of ultrasound in enhancing cellular interactions presented by Huo, Zhang, and colleagues [25] elucidates the critical role of nanoparticle size in modulating ultrasound-enhanced endocytosis of specific silica nanoparticles (SiO2 NPs), providing specific fundamental guidance for the rational design of ultrasound-responsive nanocarriers optimized for drug delivery. A study by Kolios and their team [26] further utilize the thermal and mechanical mechanisms induced by low-intensity pulsed ultrasound, to promote the endocytosis and sonoporation, thereby improving the cellular uptake of gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) and loaded drugs. Zhang and coauthors [27] demonstrate ultrasound-responsive “cluster bomb” nanodroplets, which integrate PEGylated PAMAM dendrimers with fluorinated surfactants, enable transient blood–brain barrier (BBB) opening and enhance intracerebral siRNA delivery.

Biographies

Jiwei Cui is a professor in the School of Chemistry and Chemical Engineering at Shandong University. His research interests include soft biomaterials, tissue engineering, and therapeutic delivery.

Yunlu Dai is an associate professor in the Faculty of Health Sciences, University of Macau. His research focuses on multifunctional hybrid biomaterials for biomedical applications.

Liang Cheng is a professor in the Functional Nano & Soft Materials Laboratory (FUNSOM) at Soochow University. His research interest focuses on bioactive inorganic nanomaterials for bioapplication.

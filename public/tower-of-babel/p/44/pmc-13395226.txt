LGBTQIA+ suicide in India: Data scarcity, accuracy, and other challenges
Authors: Sujita K. Kar, Ramdas Ransing
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

The National Crime Records Bureau (NCRB) that offers thorough data broken down by gender, occupation, and geographic area, is the main source of official suicide statistics in India.[12] But when it comes to the LGBTQ+ population, the NCRB only records a small amount of information about transgender people; information about other sexual minorities, like lesbian, gay, queer, and bisexual people is completely absent.

The way information is gathered during police investigations is the main cause of this data gap. Police rely primarily on reports from neighbors and family members when a suicide occurs. Informants usually state the deceased’s identity within the conventional gender binary (male or female), leaving out their sexual orientation or gender identity due to social shame. Consequently, this binary prejudice is reflected in police records. Furthermore, even though the “third gender” was formally acknowledged by the Indian government after 2011, this category obscures specific information about different sexual minority groups by generically combining different identities.[3]

This prejudice is further exacerbated by postmortem assessments. Sex is determined solely by anatomical and morphological genitalia in autopsies. Medical investigations are unable to ascertain a person’s self-identified gender identity or sexual orientation, even in rare instances of ambiguous genitalia. Therefore, it is impossible to estimate the suicide prevalence within the LGBTQ+ group using official state information (police and medical).[45]

Researchers need to seek beyond state databases to alternative approaches to gather accurate data:

Respondent-Driven Sampling (RDS): RDS uses the internal networks of the LGBTQ+ community to securely and precisely assess mental health morbidities and suicide prevalence, as home surveys frequently fail owing to family stigma and underreporting.

In India, the Mental Healthcare Act 2017 (MHCA 2017) provides an important legal framework relevant to LGBTQ+ suicide prevention and research.[6] Therefore, NCRB data are helpful for understanding broad suicide trends in India, but they are insufficient for estimating suicide rates among LGBTQ+ communities. More sensitive, confidential, and community-engaged research methods are necessary to collect accurate data for this group. Further, the MHCA 2017 may help to address current gaps in LGBTQ+ suicide data in India through its rights-based legal framework.

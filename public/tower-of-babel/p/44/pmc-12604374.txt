A novel approach for benign parotid tumors: a dual-focused strategy on functional preservation and aesthetics
Authors: Rui Shi, Wen-hao Ren, Shao-ming Li, Ling Gao, Ke-qian Zhi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveBenign tumors of the parotid gland are the most common salivary gland diseases, Conventional parotid surgery often leads to various complications that severely affect the postoperative quality of life of patients.MethodsThis study included 400 patients with clinically benign tumors in the tail of the parotid gland, divided into two groups: one group received the classical surgical method, and the other group received the new surgical method.Through postoperative follow-up, the differences in postoperative quality of life between the two groups were compared by recording the incidence of temporary facial paralysis, facial depression deformity, scar satisfaction, and Frey’s syndrome.ResultsThe new surgical group demonstrated a marked reduction in subjective Frey’s syndrome incidence (12 cases [6%] vs. 24 cases [12%] in 200 patients). The new surgical group also exhibited significantly reduced postoperative drainage volume, intraoperative blood loss hospital stay duration, and extubation time, collectively indicating its less invasive nature.Notably, the new surgical group achieved superior facial nerve function outcomes, with significantly lower postoperative facial nerve function scores (1.29 ± 0.16 vs. 2.15 ± 0.28, P < 0.05),. Additionally, the new technique group showed a marked reduction in facial depression deformity scores (0.59 ± 0.16 vs. 1.15 ± 0.48, P < 0.05). Regarding aesthetic outcomes, the new approach achieved higher patient satisfaction (90% vs. 70%, P < 0.05), further underscoring its clinical advantages.ConclusionThe new surgical method proposed in this article for benign tumors of the parotid gland can effectively reduce complications and improve the quality of life of patients.

Introduction

Tumor is one of the most common diseases of the salivary gland and parotid gland tumors are the most common salivary gland tumors, accounting for about 80% of salivary gland tumors [1]. Among parotid gland tumors, benign tumors account for about 75–80% [2]. Benign tumors of the parotid gland have no obvious symptoms in the early stage, and are usually treated for obvious facial deformities.The most common benign parotid gland tumors are Warthin’s tumor and pleomorphic adenoma, among which Warthin’s tumor most frequently occurs in the tail of the parotid gland. It predominantly affects middle-aged and elderly men aged 40–70 years, with a male-to-female incidence ratio of approximately 6:12. Additionally, patients often have a history of smoking. This tumor accounts for about 5%-15% of all salivary gland tumors, and its incidence has shown a gradual upward trend in recent years.

In recent years, there has been some progress in both clinical and basic aspects regarding benign tumors of the parotid gland [3]. At present, surgical treatment is still the preferred treatment for benign tumors of the parotid gland. The classic surgical method for parotid gland involves complete resection of the superficial lobe of the parotid gland, and an S-shaped incision. This surgical method exposes the field of vision clearly during the operation, with clear anatomical levels, and can also ensure the radical treatment of the tumor. However, there are many postoperative complications, such as facial depression, salivary leakage, temporary facial paralysis. To some extent, it has delayed the time for patients to return to normal life. Since the 21st century, with the development of functional surgery [4], the goal of surgery has been not only to eradicate tumors, but also to ensure the postoperative quality of life of patients, and to preserve the shape and function of organs. The improvement of surgery mainly lies in two aspects: (1) Maximizing the preservation of parotid gland function; (2) Reducing the occurrence of complications. At present, parotid gland surgery considers these factors, and the mainstream surgical method is extracapsular dissection (ECD).

The surgery of ECD is divided into two processes: (1) separating and dissecting important anatomical structures, and stripping the tumor; (2) completely removing the tumor within a certain safe range outside the tumor [5]. It is obvious that how to quickly complete the first step is a problem that needs to be solved in clinical practice. The facial nerve exits the skull through the stylomastoid foramen, and in clinical practice, the position of the stylomastoid foramen is often used to locate the main trunk of the facial nerve during parotid gland surgery. At the same time, the facial nerve divides the parotid gland into two parts: shallow and deep [6]. The new surgical method for parotid gland proposed in this article refers to designing a cosmetic incision during the surgical process, protecting the greater auricular nerve, and then cutting through the superficial lobe of the parotid gland to search for the common trunk of the facial nerve through the stylomastoid foramen [7]. The transverse nerve is dissected in an anterograde manner, and the tumor is completely removed.

This article provides new guidance for the future surgery of benign parotid tumors by comparing the postoperative complications and differences in parotid gland function between two different surgical methods.

Materials and methods

Patients

Select 400 patients(male and female), who underwent surgical treatment for benign parotid tumors at the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University from 2017 to 2022. All patients not underwent preoperative radiotherapy and chemotherapy, and the pathological diagnosis was benign. All patients were divided into two groups, one group using the classical surgical methods and the other group using the new surgical methods, there are 200 patients in each group.All surgeries were completed by two experienced surgeons. The postoperative follow-up period of the patient was 1 year, with monthly follow-up. The follow-up content included questionnaire survey and micro amylase experiment. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao university, and informed consent was obtained from each patient.The manuscript was checked against the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) checklist (Supplemental Appendix).

Surgical methods

The traditional group incision is an S-shaped incision that runs straight down from the front of the earlobe to the tip of the mastoid process, and then turns to the lower jaw at a distance of 1.5–2 cm below the mandibular angle. Cut open the skin, subcutaneous tissue, and muscle, flap along the deep surface of the parotid masseter muscle fascia and the superficial surface of the parotid gland, preserve the parotid masseter muscle fascia, expose and dissect the facial nerve, generally branching to the main trunk. At the same time as dissecting the facial nerve, preserved the great auricular nerve, removed the superficial lobe of the parotid gland. If the tumor does not invade the parotid masseter fascia during the surgery, the retained parotid masseter fascia is sutured to the lower pole of the parotid gland or the surface of the sternocleidomastoid muscle, and a repair film is placed in the surgical area to avoid Frey’s syndrome. The modified group includes the base of the auricular crus and extends 4–6 cm posteriorly (Fig. 1), tightly fitting and bypassing the earlobe to the posterior sulcus of the earlobe, and extending upwards from the posterior sulcus of the earlobe to the posterior sulcus of the auricle. At the corner near the hairline of the ear wheel foot plane, an arc-shaped incision is formed and extends backwards and downwards along the hairline of the ear. The degree of extension is determined by the exposed area during surgery, and the incision at the eardrum can extend upwards to the temporal region. Cut open the skin, subcutaneous tissue, and platysma cervicalis muscle, free and protect the greater auricular nerve, expose the anterior edge of the sternocleidomastoid muscle at the posterior edge of the free parotid gland, passively separate the glandular tissue forward between the mastoid and styloid processes, locate the main trunk of the facial nerve, and then separate its branches. After the tumor and surrounding parotid tissue are completely removed, free the remaining parotid tissue, and reposition and fill the sunken area with parotid tissue (Fig. 2). If the tumor is too large, Preparation of sternocleidomastoid muscle flap for repairing depressed areas. Observation indicators

Record the differences in perioperative indicators of patients: Starting from surgical time, surgical incisions, intraoperative bleeding, postoperative drainage volume, and hospitalization time, compare the perioperative indicators of patients.

All patients will undergo monthly follow-up examinations after surgery, and saliva amylase tests will be conducted during the follow-up. The healthy side serves as a control. Instruct patients to chew vitamin C tablets, after 3 min, observe whether there is flushing and sweating in the affected temporal area. Sweating can cause a chemical reaction between starch and iodine, resulting in a dark blue color. The range of discoloration zone is directly proportional to the amount of sweat produced. Therefore, the presence or absence of gustatory sweating syndrome can be determined based on a trace starch iodine test. Positive individuals are classified into mild, moderate, and severe based on color changes. Mild refers to slight changes in the skin color of the surgical area compared to the control side. Moderate change in skin color in the surgical area. But the change area does not exceed 2 cm, and the severe case is a significant change in skin color in the surgical area with the affected area exceeding 2 cm.

Facial nerve function examination: Facial paralysis is divided into three degrees: Degree I refers to the ability to close the eyes, slight shallowness of the nasolabial sulcus, and no deviation of the mouth angle when quiet. Degree II is marked by a shallower nasolabial sulcus, incomplete eye closure, reduced forehead lines, and slight deviation of the mouth angle when quiet. Degree III refers to the disappearance of the nasolabial sulcus, severe deviation of the mouth angle, disappearance of forehead lines, and inability to close the eyes.

Facial sunken deformity can be divided into three degrees: Degree I refers to facial symmetry when viewed from the front, with mild sunken deformity on the affected side compared to the healthy side. Degree II is basically symmetrical when viewed from the front, with obvious concave deformities on the affected side compared to the left and right sides. Degree III refers to significant facial asymmetry and severe concave deformity on the affected side.

Survey patients’ satisfaction with facial scars in the surgical area, which is divided into: obvious dissatisfaction with scars, basic satisfaction with relatively concealed scars, and satisfaction with concealed scars.

Postoperative analysis

All statistical analyses were made using SPSS (version 13.0, SPSS Inc). Variables are presented as mean (SD). Student’s t test and the chi squared test were used for the comparisons between the two groups, and probabilities of less than 0.05 accepted as significant.

Discussion

The development of surgery for benign parotid tumors has gone from enucleation to standardized parotid lobectomy, and then to partial parotidectomy [8, 9]. With the continuous improvement of parotid gland surgery, there has been a relatively standard surgical procedure for benign parotid gland tumors, which can effectively ensure the radical resection of parotid gland tumors and prevent tumor recurrence. In recent years, with the development of functional surgery, how to improve the quality of life of postoperative patients with benign tumors has become an urgent problem to be solved. This study compared the application of two different surgical methods in benign tumors of the parotid gland and found that both methods were effective in preventing tumor recurrence. However, the improved surgical method can effectively reduce postoperative complications such as facial scars, earlobe numbness, temporary facial paralysis, concave deformity in the surgical area, and taste sweating syndrome.

The main problem with traditional parotidectomy incisions is the formation of scars that affect aesthetics, which is often difficult for many patients with high aesthetic requirements to accept [10]. Scholars have been searching for various methods to improve, but their applications are relatively limited. We have made improvements to this incision. If the tumor is located in the lower part of the parotid gland, we design an incision along the earlobe back to the hairline, without attaching an eardrum incision, and hide the incision behind the ear; If the tumor is located higher, we will add an anterior eardrum incision to the posterior ear incision. After research, we found that this modified incision can meet the surgical requirements for most benign tumors in the superficial lobe of the parotid gland. Through postoperative follow-up, we found that the satisfaction level of patients with scars was significantly higher than that of the traditional group, indicating that the improved cosmetic incision can effectively conceal scars in the surgical area and meet the aesthetic needs of patients.

Facial nerve anatomy is the core of parotid gland surgery [11, 12]. Currently, there are two main methods for dissecting facial nerves: the retrograde method from the main trunk to the branch and the anterograde method from the branch to the main trunk. The drawbacks of using the retrograde method to dissect the sectional nerve are very obvious. The main reason is that if the separation depth cannot be grasped during the separation process, it is easy to cut the sectional nerve, affecting the patient’s facial nerve function [13]. Under the aesthetic incision behind the ear, the visual field is not clearly exposed, and the anatomical position variation will increase the difficulty of the surgery, affecting the anatomy of the facial nerve. The method of anterograde dissection of the sectional nerve involves first separating the upper edge of the posterior margin of the parotid gland, exposing the sternocleidomastoid muscle and the posterior abdomen of the digastric muscle, and then searching for the main trunk of the facial nerve between the posterior abdomen of the digastric muscle and the bottom wall of the external auditory meatus and the tip of the mastoid process. The branches are dissected directly from the main trunk to completely remove the tumor and parotid gland tissue, achieving the goal of radical treatment of the tumor. Due to the large trunk [14], intraoperative traction and displacement of the facial nerve can be minimized, which can effectively protect the nerve and reduce the occurrence of facial paralysis. After our clinical research, we have found that there are three reasons for facial nerve injury: firstly, damage to the nerve sheath and nerve fibers caused by pulling during surgery. The second reason is that the nerve exposure time during the surgery is too long. The third is postoperative tissue swelling. During the functional resection of benign tumors in the superficial lobe of the parotid gland in different regions, there is no need to expose nerves unrelated to the surgical area. During the surgery, attention should be paid to the principle of minimally invasive surgery, and the surgical time and nerve exposure time should be shortened. Therefore, selecting an appropriate facial nerve dissection method is crucial for improving surgical efficiency and reducing surgical trauma. We found that under the aesthetic incision behind the ear, compared with conventional retrograde dissection of the facial nerve, patients who dissect the facial nerve along the main trunk of the facial nerve anterograde have a lower incidence of postoperative complications, which is worthy of clinical promotion and application.The surgical approach combining a postauricular cosmetic incision with antegrade dissection along the facial nerve trunk can significantly reduce the incidence of postoperative complications in patients.Studies have shown that the antegrade dissection method starts with the facial nerve trunk—characterized by a constant anatomical location and a relatively large diameter—resulting in a clearer surgical field. This approach minimizes traction and displacement of the facial nerve, thereby effectively preserving nerve function. Compared with the retrograde dissection method, this technique not only markedly lowers the incidence of temporary postoperative facial paralysis and shortens the recovery time of facial nerve function but also achieves better control over the risk of permanent facial paralysis.

Taste sweating syndrome is a common complication of parotid gland surgery, often manifested as chewing and taste stimulation, causing abnormal sweating, redness [15, 16], and burning sensation on the skin in the parotid gland area. Every part of the parotid gland. The occurrence of gustatory sweating syndrome is believed to be caused by the dislocation and regeneration of sympathetic and parasympathetic nerves distributed in the parotid gland region, and isolation methods are commonly used in clinical practice to prevent gustatory sweating syndrome. The parotid masseter fascia is a natural barrier to prevent taste sweating syndrome. If the tumor does not invade the parotid masseter fascia during surgery, we should preserve the parotid masseter fascia. Repair membranes are artificially synthesized and absorbable natural materials that have a certain barrier effect. After surgery in both the improved and traditional groups, we placed a repair membrane in the surgical area. Through postoperative experiments, we found that the incidence of taste sweating syndrome is extremely low, and the improved group has better results.The advantages of the modified surgery may stem from its more refined techniques.The modified surgical approach typically places greater emphasis on preserving the integrity of the parotideomasseteric fascia. By elevating the flap deep to this fascia, the chance of misplaced regeneration between the glandular nerve fibers and the cutaneous sweat glands after surgery is reduced. Meanwhile, the modified surgery mostly adopts functional partial parotidectomy—with a more precise resection range and a relatively smaller surgical wound. This allows the barrier material used to more completely cover the remaining glandular tissue and form an effective mechanical barrier, thereby blocking the vagal regenerated nerve pathways more efficiently.In addition, the modified surgery’s characteristics of less trauma and faster postoperative recovery may also indirectly promote the integration of the repair membrane and the exertion of its barrier function. Owing to these combined factors, in the long-term follow-up of the modified surgery group, patients not only had an extremely low incidence of Frey’s syndrome.

Many times during parotid gland surgery, the tumor is too large and the remaining depression after resection is too large, which can have a certain impact on the patient’s quality of life [17, 18]. We have made improvements to this. When the tumor is within a certain range, we partially remove the parotid gland and free the remaining parotid tissue to fill the defect area, achieving the effect of repairing facial sunken deformities. However, sometimes the tumor is too large and the remaining parotid tissue cannot fill the defect area. Therefore, we need to combine the sternocleidomastoid muscle flap for repair. It should be emphasized that the broken end of the sternocleidomastoid muscle flap must be tightly sutured with the SMAS fascia in an umbrella shape. During the preparation of the sternocleidomastoid muscle flap, it is advisable to take 1/2 of the muscle thickness, as excessive depth may damage the deep accessory nerve. In summary, the combination of parotid gland flap or sternocleidomastoid muscle flap repair membrane has a better effect in preventing taste sweating syndrome, and can also prevent facial depression and deformity.

Conclusion

While this is a clinical trial, additional extensive data from future studies will still be required to support findings.The improved surgical approach for the parotid gland has hidden scars and fewer postoperative complications, which improves the patient’s quality of social life and meets their aesthetic requirements. With the development of new medical equipment and technology, the modified parotid gland surgery will undoubtedly be widely used in parotid gland surgery.

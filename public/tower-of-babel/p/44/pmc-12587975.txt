Exploring the evidence for the use of protein biomarkers of muscular damage and disease progression in Duchenne and Becker muscular dystrophy: a systematic review and meta-analysis protocol
Authors: Stefania Mondello, Amelia McGill, Ange Lu, Pietro Spitali, Alan J Russell, Ramona Belfiore-Oshan, Paige B Martin, on behalf of the Duchenne Regulatory Science Consortium
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionDuchenne and Becker muscular dystrophies (DMD and BMD) are devastating conditions characterised by progressive muscle degeneration and weakness. Despite advances in understanding their pathogenetic processes, there is a critical need for reliable biomarkers to aid in patient stratification and inform clinical decision-making, predict disease progression and evaluate therapeutic responses. Several promising protein biomarkers have been investigated as potential diagnostic/prognostic tools, but, to date, this evidence has not been systematically synthesised. We aim to comprehensively and critically review and summarise published studies reporting the use of protein signatures of muscular damage in DMD and BMD.Methods and analysisWe will systematically search Ovid MEDLINE (PubMed), OVID Embase, OVID Evidence-Based Medicine Reviews and Cochrane Library to retrieve all relevant articles. For ongoing trials, we will search WHO International clinical trials registry and ClinicalTrials.gov registry. We will include studies that measure circulating and urine levels of established and/or promising protein biomarkers associated with skeletal muscular damage and disease progression, such as creatine kinase, myoglobin, skeletal troponin I fast-twitch (type II), myostatin, creatine/creatinine ratio, creatinine and titin. We will consider randomised controlled trials, observational studies and longitudinal cohort studies with serial sampling, without restrictions on sample size, geographic location or language, while excluding animal and in vitro studies. Two independent reviewers will screen articles for inclusion using predefined eligibility criteria and extract data of retained articles. A third author will be consulted in case of disagreement. The approach recommended by the Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality’s Methods Guide for Effectiveness and Comparative Effectiveness Reviews will be used. The risk of bias and reporting quality will be assessed with standardised scales. The analysis will involve a structured narrative synthesis and evidentiary tables. If a meta-analysis is possible, biomarker data for each outcome will be pooled using random effects models. Subgroup analyses have been planned as a function of age, genetic mutation, disease severity, imaging and clinical assessment, length of the observation and risk of bias.Ethics and disseminationEthics approval is not required for this study as no original data will be collected. The findings will be shared through peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations. Additionally, this systematic review will guide the recommendations of the Duchenne Regulatory Science Consortium. This work will provide a rigorous, exhaustive and accessible evidence synthesis to identify candidate biomarkers of potential clinical value. Furthermore, it is expected that these results could be used to facilitate the development of future research strategies and guidelines, inform resource allocation decisions and accelerate the route towards clinical implementation of biomarkers for DMD and BMD.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42024549471. Introduction

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a lethal X-linked neuromuscular disorder caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene (DMD).1 Since dystrophin plays a fundamental role in maintaining the integrity, permeability and functionality of myofibers, mutations in DMD lead to progressive muscle degeneration and weakness.2 The absence of dystrophin in DMD results in a severe phenotype, with a median life expectancy of 22 years for patients.3 4 This X-linked genetic condition predominantly affects males with a global prevalence of approximately 7 per 100 000 individuals.5 Similarly, Becker muscular dystrophy (BMD) is an X-linked neuromuscular disorder, occurring in a lower frequency of 1 in 30 000 male live births and displaying as a milder phenotype due to low dystrophin levels still present.6 7 Despite advances in supportive care and expanding treatment options, DMD remains a life-limiting disease. BMD, on the other hand, still relies on standards of care and lacks treatments approved specifically for this indication.6

The identification and validation of objective, sensitive and reliable biofluid biomarkers are crucial for advancing the field of DMD and BMD.8 Biomarkers can play a pivotal role in biologically staging and enriching the patient population,9 as well as predicting and monitoring disease progression.10 Moreover, biomarkers can be instrumental in assessing the efficacy of therapeutic interventions,11 facilitating the timely initiation of tailored treatments and the development of new therapies, while accelerating the approval process.12 Evolving therapies are increasingly targeting both skeletal muscle and cardiac complications, which are critical aspects of DMD and BMD (NCT06224660, NCT06224660, NCT03340675, NCT04371666). However, the scope of this systematic review is limited to protein biomarkers of skeletal muscle damage and disease progression. Over the last decades, numerous studies have investigated the value of several promising biomarker candidates to inform clinical management and interventions in both clinical and research settings.8 However, the diversity of biomarkers and analytical platforms, the absence of a defined gold standard and varying study designs and interpretation methods lead to inconsistent, sometimes contradictory, and variable quality results in such studies. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses are therefore urgently needed to summarise the evidence for biomarkers in DMD and BMD.

We will systematically review existing literature, identify all studies exploring protein biomarkers of skeletal muscle injury with potential clinical value in DMD and BMD, and critically assess and synthesise their results. We will collate and appraise evidence across multiple cohorts, different biomarkers and multiple clinical outcomes, thereby enabling comparisons and contrasts, and we will also explore sources of heterogeneity, with a particular focus on study design, and quality of methodology and reporting. Furthermore, this work will provide a clear foundation on which to base further primary research and improve the design and success of future clinical trials in DMD and BMD.

Methods and analysis

Protocol and registration

This protocol has been designed by a team of experts including researchers, clinicians, methodologists and biostatisticians in accordance with the methodological guidelines for overviews provided by the Cochrane Collaboration and the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic review and Meta-Analysis Protocols.13 It is registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews database (registration number: CRD42024549471, https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/prospero/display_record.php?RecordID=549471).

Eligibility criteria

Type of study

This review will include randomised controlled trials (RCTs) and observational studies that investigate the value of protein biomarkers associated with skeletal muscular damage and disease progression in DMD and BMD. Longitudinal cohort studies with serial sampling will also be eligible for inclusion. We will not place any restrictions based on sample size, geographic location or language. Animal studies and in vitro studies will be excluded.

Type of participants

The population of interest includes males with confirmed DMD or BMD, genetically defined by mutations or alterations in the DMD gene that decrease the amount of dystrophin protein and/or cause dysfunction of the protein.14,18

Control populations will be considered as age-matched healthy male individuals.

Type of interventions/exposure

This review will consider studies that evaluate measurements of circulating and urine levels of established and/or potential protein biomarkers associated with skeletal muscular damage and disease progression, such as, but not limited to, creatine kinase, creatinine, myoglobin, skeletal troponin I fast-twitch type II, myostatin (GDF8), creatine/creatinine ratio and titin. To be eligible, studies are required to report sufficient detailed methodology about the sample preparation and analytical approach used to permit the assessment of the reliability and validity of biomarker results.

Outcomes

The studies must determine the association between protein biomarker and disease characteristics defined as (a) functional endpoints, (b) structural indices of disease severity obtained at baseline and/or (c) the transitioning to a more severe disease stage.

Studies that used the following gold standard clinical scales will be included:

Disease severity assessed at baseline using four-stair climb, NSAA total score, NSAA cumulative loss-of-function, time-to-rise, 6 min walk test, 10 meter walk test, performance of upper limbs, elbow flexion, reachable space, SV95C and vastus lateralis fat fraction (VLFF).

Disease severity assessed at baseline using four-stair climb, NSAA total score, NSAA cumulative loss-of-function, time-to-rise, 6 min walk test, 10 meter walk test, performance of upper limbs, elbow flexion, reachable space, SV95C and vastus lateralis fat fraction (VLFF).

Disease progression and therapy response assessed by changes in four-stair climb, NSAA total score, time-to-rise, 6 min walk test, 10 meter walk test, the performance of upper limbs, elbow flexion, reachable space, SV95C and VLFF. Changes are defined as differences in clinical score between baseline and following assessments (eg, 24, 48, 52, 72 weeks). Additional outcomes may include muscle biopsy.

Search strategy

We will systematically search the following databases: OVID MEDLINE (via PubMed), OVID Embase, OVID Evidence-Based Medicine Reviews and Cochrane Library from their inception dates. For possible ongoing trials, we will search the WHO International clinical trials registry and ClinicalTrials.gov registry. Additional studies will be identified by reviewing the reference lists of published clinical trials, relevant review articles and systematic reviews that our expert panel will suggest. An example of our search strategy is provided as online supplemental material. The literature search will be completed by April 2026.

Selection process

The study selection process aims to reflect the best practice guidelines outlined in the Cochrane handbook.19 After removal of duplicates, two reviewers using a web-based systematic review programme (Covidence, Alfred Health Melbourne, Australia, http://www.covidence.org/) will independently undertake the screening of titles and abstracts, followed by a full-text review of retained articles that meet the eligibility criteria. A third author will be consulted for arbitration in case of disagreement. To avoid duplication, if the same study is published more than once, the most complete article will be retained. Studies with overlapping cohorts but assessing different contexts of use will be noted in the inclusion/exclusion criteria. A table of excluded studies and reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage will be presented for transparency.

Data extraction

Data abstraction forms will be created to collect the relevant data from the studies included. Two reviewers will independently extract all data. Any discrepancy in data extraction will be resolved by discussion and consensus with a third author.

The following data will be collected: (1) study description/design such as year, setting, study type, sample size, duration of follow-up, inclusion and exclusion criteria, sources of funding and conflicts of interest; (2) patient characteristics such as age, race, country, time since diagnosis and dystrophin mutation; (3) treatment details; (4) pre-analytical and analytical aspects related to biomarker testing, such as type of assay used including technological platform, type of measurement (absolute or relative), time of sampling and sampling type; and (5) measures of clinical outcomes, including outcome type and timing of assessment.

The study investigators will verify all study data for accuracy and completeness. Authors will be contacted for clarification on the study sample or for missing data.

Risk of bias (quality) assessment

The studies will be assessed for methodological quality by two independent reviewers and any disagreements will be resolved by discussion and consensus between the authors and, if necessary, arbitration by a third author.

The methodological quality of the RCTs retrieved through our search, and included in the systematic reviews, will be evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool.19

On the other hand, observational studies included for evaluation of prognostic accuracy

will be assessed using a modified version of the Quality Assessment of Prognostic Accuracy Studies (QUAPAS)20 supplemented with additional items from the Quality in Prognostic Studies21 and REMARK tools.22 The QUAPAS tool evaluates the risk of bias (RoB) in five domains: participants, type of protein biomarker, outcome, flow/timing and analysis. Each domain will then be assessed in terms of the RoB (high, low or unclear), and the first four domains will also be considered in terms of applicability. Studies with observational cohort designs included for evaluation of diagnostic accuracy will be assessed using the Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies tool,23 which evaluates RoB in four domains: patient selection, type of protein biomarker, reference standard and flow/timing. Each domain will then be assessed in terms of the RoB (high, low or unclear), and the first three domains will also be considered in terms of concerns about the study finding applicability.

Reviews will not be excluded on the basis of the quality assessment; however, if applicable, we will use the quality assessment to inform sensitivity analyses and explore the consequences of synthesising studies of differing quality. We will present the results of the risk of bias assessment in ‘risk of bias’ graph figures and ‘risk of bias’ summary figures. In addition, we will analyse and discuss various aspects of the limitations of the evidence, taking account of the quality of the individual studies. Furthermore, we will identify methodological problems and gaps to inform and make recommendations for future research and assessments.

Assessment of publication bias

We will assess publication bias using visual, statistical and correction methods. Where appropriate, meta-analyses will include studies with sufficient quantitative data; funnel plots will be generated to inspect asymmetry in effect sizes relative to study precision. Egger’s regression test24 and Kendall’s rank correlation25 will be used to statistically evaluate funnel plot asymmetry. If bias is detected, we will apply the trim-and-fill method to estimate and adjust for potentially missing studies, recalculating the pooled effect size accordingly. Sensitivity analyses will be conducted to compare original and adjusted estimates and assess robustness. Findings will be interpreted with caution, acknowledging the potential impact of selective reporting and unpublished data. Evidence of publication bias will inform the GRADE assessment and may lead to downgrading the certainty of evidence. Limitations of each method will be considered, particularly the influence of heterogeneity on bias detection.26

Assessment of the certainty of the evidence

We will also use an adaptation of the Grading Recommendation Assessment, Development and Evaluation (GRADE)27 framework to assess the certainty of the evidence for each outcome reported in this systematic review.

We will use GRADEPro software (GRADEpro GDT: GRADEpro Guideline Development Tool (Software). McMaster University and Evidence Prime, 2025. Available from gradepro.org) to create ‘Summary of findings’ tables. The certainty of the evidence assessments will be integrated into the results and conclusions of the overview.

Data synthesis

The analysis will include a structured narrative synthesis. We will construct evidentiary tables identifying the study characteristics, and we will structure systematic review results by biomarkers and outcomes and then by quality (ie, low to high RoB).

If a meta-analysis is possible, random effects models will be employed as heterogeneity between the results of biomarkers study is expected. We will extract the sensitivity and specificity and their respective 95% CIs for each of our biomarkers and outcomes. This data will be used to create forest plots of the pooled sensitivity and specificity. In addition, to summarise test accuracy data across studies and estimate summary ROC curves, we will fit hierarchical summary receiver operating characteristic (HSROC) models that allow for heterogeneity in threshold and accuracy.

Heterogeneity will be investigated through visual examination of forest plots of sensitivities and specificities and HSROC curves. Analytical methods used to measure biomarkers, duration of the follow-up, participant and treatments characteristics and study design and quality are expected to be the main sources of heterogeneity. If sufficient studies are available, we will perform meta-regression (by including each potential source of heterogeneity as a covariate in the HSROC model) and sensitivity analysis to determine the influence of RoB on the biomarker and outcomes being investigated. If possible, to investigate additional sources of heterogeneity, we plan to evaluate the following subgroups: age, genetic mutation, disease severity, imaging and clinical assessment, length of observation, technical aspects and sampling type.

Ethics and dissemination

The approval of an ethical committee is not required to conduct this study. All trials included will be in accordance with the current ethical standards and the Declaration of Helsinki. The results of this systematic review and meta-analysis will provide a synthesis of evidence concerning circulating biomarkers in urine or blood associated with skeletal muscle damage in DMD and BMD. On the completion of this systematic review, a dissemination plan will be conducted, which includes (1) presenting the findings of this study to the members and advisors of the Duchenne Regulatory Science Consortium; (2) presenting findings of this study at relevant international conferences; (3) publishing our results in peer-reviewed high-impact academic journals; and (4) disseminating the results through specific forums, discussion boards, X.com, LinkedIn and other social media to inform clinicians and researchers as well as decision-makers and guideline developers.

Data statement

The DMD Fluid Biomarkers Systematic Review Meta-analysis dataset generated and/or analysed during the study will be available from the corresponding author on reasonable request. All data supporting the findings of this study will be anonymised and are stored in C-Path, a secure repository, RDCA-DAP (RDCA-DAP Portal).

Patient and public involvement

This study does not involve patients or the public in the design of the research. However, patients with DMD or BMD and/or caregivers have been involved in reviewing the criteria and protocol as part of the member and advisor review period.

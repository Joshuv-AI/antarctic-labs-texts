RNA adenosine modification writers define characteristics of immunity and prognosis in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Rong Wang, Yingpeng Peng, Ruotong Chen, Rui Liu, Yijing Ye, Qijiu Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo identify potential roles of RNA modification patterns in immunotherapy and prognosis in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC).MethodsWe performed a multiomics analysis of 26 “writers” from four major adenine RNA modifications in 866 HNSCC samples from 3 datasets. We identified two distinct RNA modification patterns using unsupervised clustering of 13 RNA modification writers related to prognosis, and compared biological characteristics like immune cell infiltration, stemness, TME and patient prognosis between patterns. The RNA modification score (RMscore) model was constructed to quantify the RNA modification-related subtypes of individual tumors.ResultsThe RMcluster A had a longer median overall survival (OS) than RMcluster B (5.40 vs. 3.82 years, p = 0.017). RMcluster A had higher levels of immune cells infiltration. RMscore was an independent prognostic factor for HNSCC patients. The median OS was 5.04 years (low RMscore) vs. 2.99 years (high RMscore) (p = 0.012). The expression of immune cell infiltrated was significantly enhanced in patients with low RMscore. Besides, we found that RMscore was positively correlated to IC50 of many chemo or targeted therapy drugs. RMscore was validated to be correlated with the efficacy of immune checkpoints. The objective response rate (CR + PR) of immunotherapy in the low RMscore group was obviously higher than the high RMscore group (26% vs. 5%).ConclusionsRMScore can classify RNA modification patterns and independently predict the prognosis of HNSCC patients. It might contribute to the selection of chemo or targeted drugs in HNSCC and predict the efficacy of immune checkpoints.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03733-3.

Introduction

The 5-year survival rate of Recently, more and more studies have been made on epigenetic regulation of RNA modification, and it is no exception in the field of cancer [1]. A new term “epitranscriptomics” is given to stress the potential of RNA modifications to study the post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression mediated by RNA binding proteins (RBPs) [2]. Among the four nucleotides of RNA, adenine is the one modified the most. m6A methylation is the most common RNA modification pattern in eukaryotes and also the main pattern of adenine RNA modification [3]. It affects the function of RNAs by modulating RNA synthesis, RNA splicing, transport, translation, degradation, and miRNA maturation etc [4]. In addition to m6A methylation, adenine RNA modification includes m1A methylation, alternative polyadenylation (APA), and conversion of adenosine to inosine (A-to-I) RNA editing etc [5–7]. The “Writer”, “Reader” and “Eraser” enzymes are responsible for installation, recognization, and removal of the methylation, respectively [8]. “Writers” are the main regulatory proteins for adenine RNA modification [9].

Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) is one of the most common malignant tumors in the world, ranking 8th in incidence [10]. Approximately 60%~70% HNSCC are locally advanced or with distant metastasis at initial diagnosis [11]. HNSCC patients with locally advanced stage is about 60%, despite comprehensive treatment [12]. For HNSCC, the most common mutated genes are TP53, CDKN2A, PTEN, PIK3CA, HRAS, NOTCH1, IRF6, and TP63 etc [13]. , which might be related to the tumorigenesis and development of cancer. And genes like TP53 and PIK3CA are found to be correlated to HNSCC patient prognosis [14]. But, epigenetic regulation such as RNA modification is still unclear.

RNA Modifications, particularly m6A modification, had been reported to play an important role in HNSCC by indirectly regulating the function of mRNA and ncRNA [15]. Although there was no significant difference in the total amount of m6A modification between carcinoma and adjacent tissues of HNSCC [16], several m6A genes, especially METTL14, METTL3, HNRNPC, YTHDC2, IGF2BPs had been implicated in tumorigenesis and related to patient overall survival (OS) in HNSCC [17–22]. It was also found that the writer METTL3 and the reader IGF2BP1 cooperated to promote BMI1 translation in OSCC. Finally, METTL3 promoted the proliferation and metastasis of OSCC through m6A methylation of BMI1 [23]. m1A modification was also correlated with tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) and survival outcomes of OSCC [24].

Apart from tumorigenesis and development, RNA modification could also affect tumor immune infiltration and TIME [18]. The expression of YTHDC2 was significantly correlated with multiple immune cells, such as CD4 + T cells, B cells, dendritic cells and neutrophils [25]. In other cancers, suppressor of cytokine signaling (SOCS) family had been reported to be mRNAs targets of METTL3 modification in T cells, and METTL3 downregulation increased the expression of SOCS [26, 27]. Inhibition of METTL14 or METTL3 could enhance the immune response of melanoma. Suppressive functions could not be performed after removing METTL3 in tumor-infiltrating Tregs [28]. 补充RNA修饰因子如何与免疫浸润相互影响.

There were interactions between RNA modification enzymes, even between writers [29]. Although multiple writers had been reported to be associated with HNSCC prognosis, their potential interactions might affect the evaluation of their predictive value. Therefore, a comprehensive model is needed to stratify patients with HNSCC. In this study, we analyzed the changes in expression of 26 RNA adenosine modification writers in HNSCC, and identified two different patterns of RNA modification, then defined two HNSCC clusters related to RNA modification, further compared and analyzed the characteristics of immune cell infiltration, TIME, stemness, drug sensitivity and prognosis among clusters, finally constructed and verified a scoring model. We hope that our scoring model could predict the prognosis of patients and evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of immunotherapy, so as to improve the treatment strategy for HNSCC patients in the future.

Materials and methods

Dataset source

The work flow chart of this research was shown in Figure S1. Gene expression data and clinical annotation information were collected from TCGA and GEO public databases. After excluding patients with missing survival information or missing expression data, there were a total of 3 eligible cohorts (TCGA, GSE41613, and GSE65858).

The methylation 450 data were from DiseaseMeth version 2.0 (http://bio-bigdata.hrbmu.edu.cn/diseasemeth) [30]. Patients with missing survival data were removed. The CNV data of all RNA methylation regulators were from UCSC Xena browser (https://xenabrowser.net) [31]. Chi-square test was used for the difference of CNV between normal tissue and tumor tissue (P < 0.05). The chromosomal location of the regulatory factors is shown by the Rcircos R package. The red dot in the outer ring indicates amplification, while the blue dot in the inner ring indicates deletion.

Unsupervised clustering

According to literature reports, 26 RNA modification writers were sorted, including 8 m6A writers (METTL3, METTL14, WTAP, RBM15, RBM15B, ZC3H13 and KIAA1429), 4 m1A writers (TRMT61A, TRMT61B, TRMT10C, and TRMT6), 12 APA writers (CPSF1/2/3/4, CSTF1/2/3, CFI, PCF11, CLP1, NUDT21, and PABPN1) and 3 A-to-I writers (ADAR, ADARB1, and ADARB2). Based on the expression levels of those 26 regulators, unsupervised cluster analysis was applied to identify different RNA modification patterns, and patients were classified for further analysis. The ConsensusClusterPlus package in R was employed to investigate the detailed information in unsupervised subclasses discovery and to divide samples into appropriate parts for maximum stability [32].

Genome compilation analysis (GSVA) and functional annotation

In order to study the biological process differences between the RNA modification modes, we used the GSVA R package to perform GSVA enrichment analysis. GSVA is a non-parametric and unsupervised method, usually used to estimate changes in pathways and biological process activities in expression data set samples [33]. The “c2.cp.kegg.v7.4.symbols” gene set was downloaded from the MSigDB database to run GSVA analysis. An adjusted p value less than 0.05 is considered statistically significant. The clusterProfiler R package was used to annotate the RNA modification-related genes with the cutoff value FDR < 0.05.

Single-sample gene-set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA)

The ssGSEA algorithm was used to quantify the relative abundance of each immune component infiltration in the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME). The gene sets for marking immune cell types were obtained from Charoentong’s research [34]. The enrichment score value was standardized to a percentage distribution between 0 and 1. The immune cells (ActCD4, ActCD8, TcmCD4, TcmCD8, TemCD4, TemCD8, Th1, Th17, ActDC, CD56briNK, NK, NKT) were defined as anti-tumor immune cells. The immune cells (Treg, Th2, CD56dimNK, imDC, TAM, MDSC, Neutrophil, and pDC) were defined as tumor-promoting immune cells and immunosuppression. 29 immune gene sets were used to detect immune phenotype, including cell types, functions and pathways [35]. In order to discover the internal mechanisms of different subgroups, typical biological processes including (1) CD8 T-effector signature; (2) Immune checkpoint; (2) antigen processing and presentation were quantified by ssGSEA [36]. The relative proportions of 22 immune cells were quantified by CIBERSORT algorithm [37].

Identification of the differentially expressed genes between different RNA modification phenotypes

In order to identify RNA modification related genes, the limma R package was used to determine the differentially expressed genes between the two RNA modification modes, and the significance standard for determining the differentially expressed genes was set to adjust the p value < 0.001.

Establishment of a scoring system to quantify RNA modification

In order to quantify the RNA modification pattern of a single patient, a scoring system was constructed to evaluate the RNA modification pattern of a single patient, called RNA modification score (RMscore). The process of establishing RMscore was as following:

We used a univariate Cox regression model to analyze the prognosis of 26 RNA modification writers in the feature. Genes with significant prognosis were extracted for further analysis. Then we performed principal component analysis (PCA) to construct RNA modification related gene signatures. Both principal components 1 and 2 were selected as feature scores. The advantage of this method is to focus the score on the set with the largest related (or anti-correlated) gene block in the set, while reducing the contribution of genes that are not tracked with other set members.

Then we define RMscore using a method similar to gene expression grade index (GGI) [38]:

Where i is the expression of RNA modification related genes. PC1 and PC2 are the scores of the two principal components respectively.

Analysis of the association between RNA modification gene characteristics and related biological pathways

A set of genes to store genes related to certain biological processes, including (1) immune checkpoints; (2) antigen processing and presentation; (3) CD8 T-effector signatures; [36, 39]. We conducted correlation analysis on them to further reveal the association between RNA modification gene characteristics and some related biological pathways.

Association analysis of scoring system and drug sensitivity

The transcription profiles and drug response measurements as IC50 for head and neck squamous cancer cell lines are downloaded from Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC) [40]. We performed Spearman correlation analysis to calculate the correlation between IC50 and RMscore, and considered |Rs| >0.3 and FDR < 0.05 as significant correlation.

Verification of the scoring system

We performed a systematical search for the immune checkpoint blockade gene expression profiles, which could be publicly obtained and reported with complete clinical information. An immunotherapeutic cohort was finally included in our study: advanced urothelial cancer with intervention of atezolizumab, an anti-PD-L1 antibody (IMvigor210 cohort) [36]. Based on the Creative Commons 3.0 License, the complete expression data and detailed clinical annotations could be obtained from http://research-pub.Gene.com/imvigor210corebiologies. The raw count data were normalized by the DEseq2 R package and then the count value was transformed into the TPM value.

Statistical analysis

The correlation coefficient between TME infiltrating immune cells and RNA modulator expression was calculated by Spearman and distance correlation analysis. One-way analysis of variance and Kruskal-Wallis test were used to compare differences in three or more groups [41]. According to the correlation between RM score and patient survival rate, the survminer R package was used to determine the cutoff point for each data set subgroup. The “surv-cutpoint” function repeatedly tested all potential cutpoints to find the maximum rank statistic for the dichotomous RM score, and then divided the patients into the high RM score group and the low RM score group according to the largest selected log-rank statistic to reduce the calculated Batch processing effect. The survival curve for prognostic analysis was generated by the Kaplan-Meier method, and the log-rank test was used to determine the significance of the difference. We used a univariate Cox regression model to calculate the hazard ratio (HR). Independent prognostic factors were determined by a multivariate Cox regression model. Patients with detailed clinical data were eligible for the final multivariate prognostic analysis. The Forestplot R package was used to visualize the results of multivariate prognostic analysis of RM score in the cohort. The specificity and sensitivity of RM score were assessed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and the area under the curve (AUC) was quantified using the pROC R package. All statistical p values are on both sides, and p < 0.05 is considered statistically significant. All data processing is done in R 4.1.0 software.

Results

Genetic and expression alterations of RNA modification writers in HNSCC

We analyzed 26 RNA adenosine modification writers including m6A methylation, m1A methylation, APA, and A-to-I RNA editing, based on findings from previous study [9]. Genetic information was obtained from 515 cases in the TCGA-HNSCC cohort. The work flow chart was shown in Supplemental Fig. 1A. Mutational landscape of 26 RNA modification writers was performed in the cohort (Fig. 1A). In total, related genes were altered in 85 (16.50%) of 515 samples. KIAA1429 (or VIRMA) had the highest mutation rate (2.5%), followed by ADARB2 (1.7%) and CPSF1 (1.4%). We also compared all 26 RNA modification writers for differences in copy number variations (CNVs) between HNSCC and normal tissues and also presented the location of CNV on chromosomes (Fig. 1B). Significant differences were found among 25 writers in HNSCC and normal tissues, except for TRMT61A. The CNVs of 13 RNA modification writers such as ADAR, TRMT61B, CFI, METTL14 etc. were amplified in the tumor tissues (red blocks), while that of 12 writers such as RBM15, CPSF3, RBM15B, TRMT10C etc. were deleted (blue blocks). The CNV frequency of all writers were shown in Fig. 1C. The results showed that CNV was prevalent in 26 RNA modification writers and were expressed as either gain or loss in HNSCC tissues.

DNA methylation level analysis showed that there were significant differences between HNSCC and normal tissues in some writers (Fig. 1D). The DNA methylation levels of TRMT61A, TRMT10C, RBM15B and ADARB2 were higher in HNSCC tissues, while that of writers like METTL14, METTL3, RBM15, ADARB1, KIAA1429 and CPSF3 were lower in HNSCC tissues. We further analyzed the gene expression level of 26 writers between normal and HNSCC samples and found that the vast majority of writers were expressed significantly higher in HNSCC tissues than in normal tissues, especially ADAR, TRMT61A and CPSF1 (Fig. 1E). Only CFI, PCF11 and ADARB2 were expressed lower in HNSCC tissues. It suggested that the dysregulated expression of RNA modification writers plays a crucial role in the development and progression of HNSCC.

The network of 26 RNA modification writers was shown in Fig. 1F. It revealed that interactions were existed between all the RNA modification writers. TRMT61A and TRMT10C were negatively correlated to ADARB2 and ADARB1 respectively, while other interactions were positively correlated. According to the results of univariate K-M analysis, 4 writers (RBM15, METTL3, ADARB2 and PCF11, p < 0.05) were favorable factors (green semicircle) of overall survival, while the other 9 writers (ZC3H13, NUDT21, CFI, CSTF2, CPSF3, CPSF2, TRMT10C, TRMT61B and TRMT61A) were risk factors (blue semicircle, p < 0.05). We also analyzed the co-occurrence or mutually exclusivity of gene mutations. We found that CPSF1 and WTAP mutation, CPSF3 and TRMT6 mutation showed significant co-occurrence in HNSCC (Supplemental Fig. 1B, p < 0.01). The interactions of RNA modification writers were confirmed by gene correlation analysis (Supplemental Fig. 1C).

Classification and biological characteristics of two RNA modification patterns

Patients with different RNA modification patterns were then classified based on the expression of 13 RNA modification writer-genes related to prognosis. The unsupervised clustering process of RNA modification writers were shown in Supplemental Fig. 3A-F. k = 2 was the most appropriate choice for consensus clustering matrix. Two different modification patterns were identified using unsupervised clustering, and the prognosis of patients with the two patterns was analyzed, as shown in Fig. 2A. The RMcluster A had a longer median overall survival (OS) than RMcluster B (5.40 vs. 3.82 years, p = 0.017). Gene expression analysis showed that the expression levels of RNA modification writers were higher in the RMcluster B, except for CFI (Fig. 2B). To explore the biological processes between different RNA modification patterns, GSVA pathway enrichment analysis was further performed. Pathways related to metabolism, cell cycle, DNA/RNA repair, RNA polymerase and degradation etc. were all enriched in RMcluster B than RMcluster A (Fig. 2C). We also confirmed the expression levels of 26 RNA modification writers in two clusters and found that they were almost all higher in the RMcluster B (Fig. 2D).

Subsequent analysis of immune cell infiltration showed that RMcluster A had higher levels of nearly all related immune cells infiltration, including activated CD8 T cell, activated dendritic cell, immature B cell, monocytes and macrophages, neutrophil, regulatory T cell and most T helper cells etc. (Fig. 2E). The result indicated more immune cells infiltrated in the tumor microenvironment in patients with RMcluster A. Specific correlation analysis between immune cell infiltration and 26 RNA modification writers was shown in Supplemental Fig. 2. MHC-related gene expressions like HLA-DQB2/DQA2, HLA-B/C, HLA-DRB and HLA-DPB1 were significantly increased in the RMcluster A (Fig. 2F). However, the changes of the expression of checkpoint stimulator-related genes were not very consistent, some stimulator genes such as TNFRSF4, CCL5 and CXCL10 were enhanced in RMcluster A, while some like CX3CL1, HMGB1 and IL12A were significantly decreased in RMcluster A (Fig. 2G). The same situation was seen for expression of checkpoint inhibitor-related genes (Fig. 2H). In general, RMcluster A had higher levels of nearly all components of the tumor immune microenvironment (Fig. 2I).

More importantly, we evaluated the expression differences of stemness indices [42] in RMcluster A and B, in order to study the characteristics of stemness under different patterns. MRNAsi, mDNAsi, EREG-mRNAsi, EREG-mDNAsi were obtained by one-class logistic regression (OCLR) machine learning algorithm [42]. Those stemness indices were all significantly decreased in cluster A (Fig. 2J), which were consistent with the poor prognostic effect of high stemness.

Classification and biological characteristics of four gene clusters

To investigate the underlying biological processes of RNA modification patterns, GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes in two clusters were performed. Results showed that the differentially expressed genes were mainly enriched in RNA modification and RNA modification-related biological processes and carcinogenic activation pathways (Fig. 3A-B). That indirectly proved the relevance of the clustering to transcription. Those results confirmed that RNA modification played an important role in carcinogenic regulation in tumor microenvironment.

To further study the mechanism of differential RNA modification patterns, we performed unsupervised clustering analysis based on differentially expressed genes and divided the patients into four gene clusters: geneCluster A, geneCluster B, geneCluster C and geneCluster D. The unsupervised clustering process of RNA modification writers were shown in Supplemental Fig. 3G-K. k = 4 was the most appropriate choice for consensus clustering matrix. Then we analyzed the prognostic significance of different geneClusters for HNSCC patients. It revealed that patients in geneCluster A had the best median overall survival, followed by geneCluster B and geneCluster C, while patients in geneCluster D had the worst prognosis (Fig. 3C, p = 0.013). We observed that patients in RMcluster A were mainly clustered in geneCluster A (Fig. 3D). That was consistent with the better prognosis of RMcluster A. Most of the 26 RNA modification writers had the lowest expression levels in the geneCluster A (Fig. 3E). Immune cell infiltration analysis showed that geneCluster A had the highest levels of immune cells infiltrated, including immature B cell, monocytes and macrophages, neutrophil, most T helper cells etc. (Fig. 3F), which was similar to the results of RMcluster A. Activated CD8 T cell infiltration was also higher in geneCluster A than that in geneCluster B or C, but was lower than that in geneCluster D. Most tumor immune microenvironment components were significantly enriched in the geneCluster A (Fig. 3G). What’s more, among the stemness indices, mRNAsi was obviously decreased in the geneCluster A (Fig. 3H).

Construction and characteristics of RNA modification signatures

Considering the individual heterogeneity and complexity of RNA modification, we constructed a scoring system to quantify the RNA modification pattern in individual HNSCC patients based on the 13 RNA modification writer-genes related to prognosis (Supplemental Fig. 1D), which we termed RMscore. To further determine the value of RMscore in predicting patient prognosis. Patients were divided into low and high RMscore groups based on the threshold of 1.53. Patients with low RMscore showed a significant survival benefit. The median overall survival of HNSCC patients was 5.04 years (low RMscore) vs. 2.99 years (high RMscore) (Fig. 4A, p = 0.012). There was significant difference in RMscore between RMclusters (Fig. 4B). RMcluster A had lower median RMscore than RMcluster B. The RMscore difference were also statistically significant in geneCluster A vs. B and geneCluster A vs. C (Fig. 4C). The geneCluster A had the lowest median RMscore. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was constructed to evaluate the prediction effect of the scoring system on survival time. The AUC values of 1-year, 3-year and 5-year were 0.5, 0.58 and 0.54, separately (Fig. 4D). Multivariate regression analysis showed that RMscore was an independent prognostic factor (Fig. 4E).

We then confirmed other features like immune infiltration and stemness with RMscore classification. The expression of immune cell infiltrated was significantly enhanced in patients with low RMscore (Fig. 4F), while nearly all tumor immune microenvironment components were significantly enriched in low RMscore patients (Fig. 4G). Stemness indices like mRNAsi, mDNAsi, EREG-mRNAsi, EREG-mDNAsi were all decreased in low RMscore patients (Fig. 4H). Besides, we performed correlation analysis of 50% inhibitive concentration (IC50) of drugs and RMscore (Fig. 4I). It revealed that RMscore was positively correlated with IC50 of most chemo or targeted therapy drugs, such as AZ960, AZD4547, JAK_8517 and Vinblastine etc. It indicated that RMscore might be related to chemo or targeted drug resistance.

Verification of the prognostic significance of RMscore for HNSCC patients

To further verify the stability of the RMscore model to assess the prognosis of HNSCC patients, we collected two HNSCC cohorts from the GEO database. Survival analysis of Cohort GSE65858 showed the median OS in patients with low and high RMscores were 5.13 and 3.01 years respectively (Fig. 5A). The AUC values of 1-year, 3-year and 5-year were 0.62, 0.54 and 0.58, separately (Fig. 5B). Multivariate regression analysis showed that RMscore was an independent prognostic factor (Supplemental Fig. 4A). Cohort GSE41613 also showed significantly longer OS in patients with lower RMscore (6.31 vs. 1.54 years, p = 0.043), with AUC values of 1-year, 3-year and 5-year were 0.64, 0.6 and 0.57, separately (Fig. 5C-D). Multivariate regression analysis also confirmed the independent prognostic value of RMscore (Supplemental Fig. 4B).

Verification of the predictive effect of RMscore on responses to immune checkpoints

In addition, to verify the predictive effect of RMscore model on responses to immune checkpoints, we collected an ICI treated locally advanced and metastatic urothelial carcinoma cohort for survival and immunotherapy efficacy analysis. In cohort IMvigor210, the median OS in ICI treated patients with low RMscore was 9.89 months, while that of patients with high RMscore was 5.13 months (Fig. 6A, p < 0.001). RMscore was an independent poor prognostic factor according to multivariate regression analysis (Fig. 6B). The objective response rate (CR + PR) of immunotherapy in the low RMscore group was obviously higher than the high RMscore group (26% vs. 5%) (Fig. 6C). The RMscore of CR/PR group was lower than that of SD/PD group (Fig. 6D, p = 0.049). The AUC values of 12-month and 36-month were 0.57 and 0.61, separately (Fig. 6E). Thus, we believed the RMscore was significantly correlated with the outcome and efficacy of immunotherapy, and the utility of our scoring system might help predict the response of patients to immunotherapy.

Discussion

HNSCC is a highly malignant tumor with poor prognosis and usually has advanced stage at initial diagnosis. Meanwhile, the survival outcomes of HNSCC, especially advanced HNSCC, are difficult to achieve a satisfied result because of its high rate of recurrence and metastasis. Accumulating evidences showed that HNSCC had been validated to benefit from immunotherapies, with a long survival time in some special patients [43, 44]. However, distinguishing the potential patients with good prognosis and the precise response for immunotherapies is still a puzzle. RNA modifications had been confirmed to play an essential role in immunity and cancer, of which “writer” was the fundamental building block. But how “writer” interacts and affects the prognosis of HNSCC is obscure. We found the expression difference of “writers” between HNSCC and normal tissues, and identified two different clusters with different prognosis in HNSCC. We also analyzed the immune cell infiltration and stemness indices of the two RMclusters. More importantly, the RNA-modification scoring system differentiated two groups based on the optimal value, including the high RMscore group with poor survival outcomes, and the low RMscore group with good survival outcomes. And its discrimination for survival was verified by two validation cohorts. Also, we evaluated the predictive capability of RMscore for the response to immune checkpoints inhibitors.

Previous studies suggest that there was interaction among writers [9, 29]. However, whether the interaction of writers existed in HNSCC remained unclear. Our results found that writers were interacted and associated with clinical outcomes, which indicated that writers should be analyzed comprehensively than separately. Though another study had reported the influence of m6A modification regulators on HNSCC prognosis, validation of ICI-treated cohorts was lacking [45]. In our study, we constructed a comprehensive scoring system and confirmed that RM score was correlated with overall survival and immune cell infiltration for patients with HNSCC, and the significance of RMscore to predict response to ICIs was validated by an ICI treated locally advanced and metastatic urothelial carcinoma cohort. Due to lack of relevant data, we were not able to validate our findings in the ICI-treated HNSCC cohort. That is also a limitation of our study.

Some RNA adenosine modification writers have been confirmed to have crucial functions in proliferation, invasion, migration, and apoptosis of HNSCC, and associated with prognosis [29, 46]. Meanwhile, writers can modulate immune infiltration to influence the anti-tumor effect [47]. Stemness indices, used to quantify stemness of cancer stem cells in human cancers, has been verified to be predictive of prognosis in cancer patients [48–50]. According to previous studies, we put forward a hypothesis that RNA adenosine modification writers probably play an essential role in the development of HNSCC by regulating immune infiltration and stemness. We found that the RMcluster A suffered a better prognosis, more immune cell infiltration and lower expression of stemness indices. But the specific mechanism of RNA adenosine modification writers in HNSCC immune infiltration and stemness remains deeper researches. To some extent, our findings of RMscore potentially provided clinician a criterion to determine the benefit group in HNSCC.

Besides, the RMscore were positively associated with IC50 of most chemo or targeted therapy drugs. Patients with high RMscore might be more resistant to AZ960, AZD4547, JAK_8517 and Vinblastine etc., who had poor prognosis. That means our scoring system might help the clinician make an individualized treatment strategy for HNSCC. At recent studies, PD-1 and TMB have proven to be the mainly promising biomarkers of immunotherapy [51, 52]. However, those two biomarkers are facing the same dilemma, that is the lack of a standard evaluation to accurately and completely discriminate patients benefited from immunotherapy. Our results revealed that the RMscore could serve as a predictor that the low RMscore group had a better prognosis with a median survival time of 5.04 years. In addition, patients with low RMscore took precedence over the response of immunotherapy. The finding of our RNA-modification scoring system might help solve the crux of predicting the curative effect of immunotherapy in HNSCC.

Inevitably, other limitations are in the present study. Firstly, our results were obtained based on data from datasets in TGCA and GEO by bioinformatic analysis including mutations, DNA methylation, mRNA expression, posttranslational modifications and clinical characteristics. More clinical studies are needed to validate our findings though our results of the RMscore system were extrapolated in other cohorts. Secondly, because all sequencing data were from different datasets, intratumor heterogeneity was obliged to be faced, which could influence the tumor immune surveillance and then affect immune infiltration [53]. Moreover, the results of the RMscore system to predict the response of immunotherapy needed further validation for HNSCC patients. Again, in view of the essence of bioinformatic analysis in our study, the underlying mechanism between RNA modification writers and benefits of survival and immunotherapy are still inexplicable. Other limitations also exist, such as inter-patient heterogeneity and technical variability in RNA modification profiling.

In conclusion, our study systematically analyzed four main types of RNA modification “writers”, and their relationships to immune cell infiltration, stemness indices, prognosis and the possible immunotherapy response in HNSCC. Patients with low RMscore were correlated with longer survival and possible better immunotherapy response, which might guide clinicians in clinical determination.

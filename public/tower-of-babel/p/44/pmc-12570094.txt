Limb volumetry using an iPad-Based three-dimensional scanner for the assessment of edema
Authors: Caiyu Gao, Furong Liu
Published: 2025

We read with great interest the recent article by Raju et al1 entitled “Limb Volumetry Using iPad Based 3D Scanner for Assessment of Edema.” This study proposes a novel, convenient, rapid, and reliable method for measuring limb volume, which holds significant promise for the assessment of edema. Lower limb lymphedema is a common complication after treatment for gynecological malignancies. Its diagnosis and management lack definitive guidelines, with current common practice relying on limb circumference measurements, which inadequately reflect the true degree of swelling. Measuring limb volume, as described in this study, offers a more accurate assessment.

However, we note that the standardized protocol developed in the study, which recommends performing volumetry between 3:00 pm and 4:00 pm to minimize diurnal variation, was validated exclusively in healthy volunteers without a history of limb swelling. Pathological lower limb edema results from excessive fluid accumulation. Measuring at 3:00 or 4:00 pm may be influenced by gravity and daytime activity. Crucially, patients with severe edema often experience limited mobility, potentially leading to prolonged bed rest. For these individuals, the influence of gravity on fluid distribution is likely less, compared with ambulatory patients. Measuring during the afternoon might not accurately reflect their baseline edema status and could introduce variability when comparing measurements between patients with different activity levels.

Therefore, we suggest that, for patients with established lower limb edema, volumetry measurements are ideally performed earlier in the day, specifically between 8:00 am and 9:00 am. At this time, after a night of rest (often involving limb elevation), the degree of edema tends to be more stable, minimizing the confounding effects of gravity and activity. This timing would provide a more consistent baseline volume for comparing changes before and after treatment and for evaluating the efficacy of interventions across different stages of edema severity.

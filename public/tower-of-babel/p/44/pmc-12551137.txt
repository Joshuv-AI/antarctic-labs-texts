The effect of servant leadership on medical staff unethical pro-organizational behavior: the role of work meaning and power distance
Authors: Jianji Zeng, Jing Cai, Liuxiang Li, Qing Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimExplore the mechanisms by which servant leadership influences unethical pro-organizational behavior among medical staff, particularly the mediating role of work meaning and the moderating role of power distance.MethodsA questionnaire survey was conducted among medical staff in more than ten public hospitals in Guangdong Province and Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, China. The data were analyzed using SPSS 23.0 and LISREL8.71 statistical software for reliability and validity tests, descriptive statistical analysis, and the hypothesis was tested.ResultsServant leadership had a significant positive effect on medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior, and work meaning significance mediated the relationship between servant leadership and medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior. In addition, power distance moderated the psychological processes through which servant leadership influences the medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior.ConclusionServant leadership and work meaning are predictive of unethical pro-organizational behavior among medical staff. Servant leadership not only has a direct effect on medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior, but also has an indirect effect on medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior through the work meaning. Power distance moderated the psychological processes through which servant leadership influenced the medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior. Specifically, high power distance enhanced the influence of servant leadership on the medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior relative to low power distance. The results of this study reveal the formation mechanism of medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior, and governmental authorities and hospital management should be alert to the negative effects of servant leadership, and develop correct ethical values and work behavioral norms, While considering the positive role of servant leadership, it is also important to foster proper ethical values and workplace behavioral guidelines for medical staff. In addition, it is important to promote communication and interaction between leaders and subordinates, build high-quality superior-subordinate relationships, and reduce the power distance to prevent the induction of unethical behavior.Keywords: Servant leadership, Work meaning, Unethical pro-organizational behavior, Power distance

Introduction

Public attention has always focused on health and wellness organizations with hospitals at their core, the exposure of scandals in Chinese hospitals, such as that of Dr Liu Xiangfeng at the Second Xiangya Hospital of Central South University, has brought unethical behavior in hospitals to the attention of a wide range of public opinion. A survey of medical staff in China showed that 30% admitted that there was a problem with large prescriptions in the medical industry, 32.5% believed that there is a situation in the medical sector where patients were induced to undergo unnecessary tests, and even more than one-third admitted that there was a situation of drug abuse and so on [1]. It is generally believed that unethical behaviors are mainly actions committed by individuals to seek private gain or revenge against organizations that are against society’s moral code [2]. However, some studies have also shown that individuals commit unethical behaviors with complex motives, not necessarily for personal self-interest but possibly for organizational benefit [3]. The China National Healthcare Security Administration investigated 111 typical health insurance fraud cases between September 2018 and March 2022 [4], with medical staff orchestrated some instances to obtain health insurance funds and boost hospital revenues fraudulently. There are serious unethical behaviors in certain hospital organizations.

In recent years, unethical behaviors within organizations have garnered heightened academic interest and emerged as a prominent subject of inquiry in Management Psychology. Scholars refer to persons who participate in actions that benefit the organization or other members (e.g., leaders), yet violate portions of essential core socialist values, morality, and the law as unethical pro-organizational behavior [5]. In essence, unethical pro-organizational behavior, as a specific type of unethical behavior, combines the conflicting characteristics of both ‘pro-organizational’ and ‘unethical’ practices. Organizations frequently overlook, comply with, or even promote it under the pretense of being ‘pro-organizational’ despite its ethical dilemmas and adverse impacts on the interests of customers and other external stakeholders [6]. Ultimately, this activity may seriously influence the reputation and growth of the organization [7]. In China, traditional Confucian culture promotes the values of individual subordination to the collective, subordinates following the leader and valuing collective interests, and these may lead to unethical pro-organizational behavior, which is not uncommon in Chinese social organizations [8], especially in public hospitals where the distance of power is high, as seems to be corroborated by the frequent cases of fraudulent insurance policies that have occurred in Chinese hospitals in recent years. Public hospitals are the primary component of China’s healthcare system, serving as protectors of public health and a crucial assurance for advancing high-quality health services. Consequently, the growth of public hospital healthcare workforce’s is paramount. Therefore, examining unethical pro-organizational behavior among medical staff in public hospitals carries major theoretical and practical significance.

Current literature indicates that leadership style is crucial to an individual’s unethical pro-organizational behavior, including paternalistic leadership [9], transformational leadership [10], empowering leadership [11], and self-sacrificial leadership [12]. Surprisingly, there has rarely been an explicit examination of the impact of servant leadership on subordinates’ unethical pro-organizational behavior, particularly within medical institutions. This leadership style, characterized by its supportive nature, prioritizes the interests of subordinates and emphasizes their growth and career development, making it highly esteemed by social organizations, especially hospitals. Current research on servant leadership effectiveness primarily emphasizes its beneficial influence on employee behavior, including innovative behavior [13] and work prosperity [14]; however, this ostensibly ideal leadership style may also induce negative consequences in specific contexts, such as promoting unethical pro-organizational behavior among subordinates [15]. Examining the adverse repercussions of servant leadership is crucial as it can yield essential insights for interventions aimed at mitigating or eradicating these negative consequences while enhancing the understanding of the essence of servant leadership. Consequently, investigating the mechanisms that influence unethical pro-organizational behavior among medical staff from a servant leadership perspective holds considerable academic significance.

Social information processing theory offers a theoretical framework for comprehending the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff. Social information processing theory posits that the social information available to an individual offers cues that assist in interpreting the organizational environment, and varying interpretations of this environment by an individual affect their attitudes and behaviors [16]. As a result, the medical staff’s work meaning is enhanced through a deep processing process of learning, attributing, coding, and evaluating the words and actions of servant leadership. Research indicates that an individual’s work meaning can profoundly affect their organizational identity [17], which is a crucial predictor of unethical pro-organizational behavior [18, 19]. Therefore, by synthesizing social information processing theory and the literature on unethical pro-organizational behavior, we propose that work meaning mediates the relationship between servant leadership and influencing medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior.

Furthermore, situational factors and personal characteristics impact individuals when processing social information, as postulated by the social information processing theory [16]. Research has demonstrated that individual characteristics serve as crucial boundary conditions in the shaping of unethical pro-organizational behavior [20]. Power distance is both an individual characteristic and an important cultural value that reflects the individual’s acceptance of job hierarchies and power differentials. It significantly influences the process and outcome of interactions between leaders and subordinates [21]. Consequently, the present study sought to ascertain the role of power distance as a moderator, thereby delineating the boundaries of the relationship between servant leadership and unethical pro-organizational behavior that benefits the organization among medical staff. In particular, the indirect effect of servant leadership on the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff was found to be most pronounced at high power distance relative to low power distance. Consequently, by identifying the boundary conditions under which servant leadership influences the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff, we can further comprehend the formation mechanism of unethical pro-organizational behavior based on the social information processing theory.

The contributions of this study mainly include the following three aspects: First, we reveal the relationship between servant leadership and unethical pro-organizational behavior by examining the work meaning as a potential mediating mechanism, and this exploration provides more empirical results for the theory of unethical pro-organizational behavior. Second, this study incorporates power distance, a cultural value, as an important boundary condition into the relationship between servant leadership and unethical pro-organizational behavior, which enriches the discussion on situational variables in the research on unethical pro-organizational behavior and, to a certain extent, makes up for the insufficient attention paid to cultural values in existing literature. Third, the vast majority of empirical studies on unethical pro-organizational behavior have been conducted on enterprise employees, which undoubtedly limits the scope of research on unethical pro-organizational behavior. This study, with medical staff as the sample, expands the research on unethical pro-organizational behavior among non-profit organizations and enriches the understanding of unethical pro-organizational behavior in different organizational contexts.

Research hypotheses

In order to gain a deeper understanding of how servant leadership impacts the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff, we introduce the social information processing theory. This theory serves as a crucial framework for comprehending the formation of individual attitudes and behaviors.The central tenet of the social information processing theory posits that an individual’s attitudes and actions are not solely dictated by their inner psychological processes. Instead, they are shaped by the information gleaned from the social environment. The theory encompasses two fundamental perspectives: (1) An individual’s attitudes and behaviors are significantly influenced by the social context in which they are situated, and through the interpretation of specific social cues and information, individuals formulate their attitudes and determine their behavioral responses. (2) When confronted with a complex social environment, individuals place greater emphasis on the information provided by the social environment, which serves as a pivotal factor in influencing attitude and behavior modifications [22]. Consequently, the social information processing theory demystifies the “black box” of how external social information exerts its influence on individual attitudes and behaviors.

The unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff is, without doubt, an individual-level phenomenon. It is inherently susceptible to the influence of the social environment. Notably, servant leadership, as a key source of information within the workplace, has the potential to shape the motivations and actions of employees. In light of the social information processing theory, servant leadership may inadvertently trigger the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff by enhancing their perception of the work meaning. Simultaneously, it is acknowledged that there exist variations in the power distance among medical staff. During the process by which servant leadership influences the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff, the impact is amplified by a high power distance among them. As a cultural value, power distance serves as a critical moderating variable that influences the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff.

Servant leadership and unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff

Servant leadership advocates that leaders act as servants and put the interests of their subordinates first [23]. Servant leadership assists organizations in fostering a laid-back work atmosphere that fosters a sense of dedication to the organization, where leaders assign authority to their subordinates and foster their growth and success by offering them valuable resources, guidance, and opportunities [24]. Servant leadership is thus characterized by altruism, empowerment, and care for subordinates [25]. Existing studies have shown that servant leadership, as a positive leadership style, can bring a range of positive outcomes to hospitals, such as improving nursing team performance [26], enhancing clinical nurses’ thriving at work [27], and reducing nurse burnout [28]. Although numerous studies have revealed the positive effects of servant leadership on hospitals, its possible negative effects have received little attention from scholars, and it has been shown that servant leadership, under certain conditions, induces employees to engage in unethical pro-organizational behavior, which brings harm to the organization in the long run [15].

Social information processing theory emphasizes that the internal and external environments of organizations are filled with a variety of information and that individuals’ perceptions and interpretations of this received information directly affect their attitudes and behaviors [16]. Leaders, as agents of the organization, are the main source of information for individuals in the organization, especially in organizations with high power distance, such as hospitals [29], where every move of the leaders is the focus of subordinates’ attention. People often view servant leadership as a leadership style that prioritizes personal integrity and prioritizes the interests of others. Furthermore, Servant leadership empowers subordinates by encouraging and facilitating their ability to take responsibility, overcome obstacles, and decide when and how to complete their work tasks [30, 31], these positive messages allow medical staff to perceive that their leaders trust, recognize, and value them, at this point, this people-centered attitude helps to build a strong relationship between leaders and their subordinates [32], and promotes the identification of medical staff with the organization’s identity [33]. High organizational identification situations can induce unethical pro-organizational behavior in individuals [19, 34]. The analysis above suggests the following hypothesis:

Hypothesis 1

Servant leadership significantly and positively influences the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff.

The mediating role of work meaning

Work meaning implies that an individual has a relationship of commitment, loyalty, and dedication to an organization or workplace [35]. Since work plays a crucial and fundamental role in people’s lives, scholars from various disciplines have focused their attention on work meaning [36], but work meaning is a complex social and psychological phenomenon, making it challenging for scholars to reach a consensus on its definition. However, scholars generally believe that the work meaning encompasses both subjective elements from the psychological perspective and socially oriented elements from the sociological perspective, which include the three dimensions of importance, beyond the self-interested purposes of work, and self-fulfillment, the concept of importance refers to an individual’s belief in the value of their work [37], the concept beyond the self-interested purposes of work signifies that the purposes of work is not solely self-directed but also has a positive impact on others and even the world [38], and self-fulfillment signifies the ability to work and realize one’s potential and purpose [39]. Importance is an overall judgment of the value of the work, while self-fulfillment and the self-interested purposes of work are two key underpinnings when making an overall judgment [40]. Thus, work meaning is an individual’s subjective feeling of the value, purpose, and importance assigned to his or her occupational activities [41].

In an organizational context, leaders are important influencers of subordinates’ attitudes and behaviors. Research demonstrates that leadership behavior shapes the work meaning of subordinates [42, 43]. Servant leadership focuses on employees’ needs and growth and demonstrates a willingness to delegate, exemplifying an altruistic leadership style grounded in moral principles [23]. According to the social information processing theory, servant leadership is an important cue and source of information for subordinates to understand the organizational environment and can significantly influence their evaluations and self-perceptions of the organization. Servant leaders are honest, upright, fair, and trustworthy in their dealings [44], and can create an ethical climate in the organization. In a highly ethical environment, employees will take pride in pursuing their goals in the right way, which will contribute to their work meaning [43]. Additionally, servant leadership prioritizes serving subordinates and assisting them in their personal growth and development, as this is one of the primary sources of work meaning [45].

Work meaning is important because it is a basic human need. It serves as a key psychological factor for individuals to increase job satisfaction, high-quality job performance, and lower turnover. Additionally, work meaning may impact more work-related outcomes through job engagement, commitment, and job satisfaction [46]. Research has demonstrated that work meaning can significantly increase an individual’s organizational commitment [47, 48], patient care satisfaction [49], etc. Individuals with high levels of commitment, tend to behave in a way that is more aligned with the expectations, needs, and interests of the organization, and they are usually strongly motivated to engage in pro-organizational behavior to benefit the organization, even if they know that such behavior is unethical for those outside the organization [50, 51]. Therefore, individuals with a strong work meaning are motivated to engage in unethical pro-organizational behavior. The analysis above suggests the following hypotheses:

Hypothesis 2

Work meaning mediates the relationship between servant leadership and unethical pro-organizational behavior among medical staff.

The moderating role of power distance

As an important cultural value, individual power distance represents the extent to which individuals accept the uneven distribution of power in organizations [52], reflecting a set of beliefs about their role in organizations and society [53]. Individuals with high power distance tend to acknowledge the existence of distinct hierarchical and status differences between superiors and subordinates, adhering to their role norms and demonstrating a greater willingness to accept managerial demands and obey leaders’ orders [54]. However, individuals with low power distance hold the belief that leaders and subordinates possess equal rights, regardless of the existence of distinct work roles. They rarely experience constraints from hierarchical relationships, and they often choose to share their ideas and discuss their work with their supervisors [55, 56]. Thus, an individual’s power distance may shape the nature of the subordinate-leader relationship [57, 58], thereby influencing subordinates’ perceptions of and reactions to the leader.

In summary, power distance is a cultural value that is particularly relevant to leadership [59]. It can influence the fit between leaders and subordinates, predict the effectiveness of leadership, and reflect the extent to which subordinates expect and accept that “leaders lead in a top-down direction” [60]. According to the social information processing theory, the organizational environment provides not only cues that individuals use to construct and interpret events but also information that influences their attitudes and perceptions. Medical staff interpret information from servant leadership, and the cultural values they hold, that is, power distance, influence this interpretation, thus affecting the strength of the influence of servant leadership on the medical staff’s work meaning. Specifically, medical staff with high power distance unconditionally respect and obey their leaders [54]. This relationship intensifies the impact of servant leadership on the work meaning among medical staff. On the contrary, medical staff with low power distance do not pay much attention to differences in power level and status; they dare to challenge authority, pursue equal relationships, and do not easily follow the leader’s ideas. Therefore, power distance does not impact the relationship between servant leadership and the medical staff’s work meaning. We derive the following hypotheses from the above analysis:

Hypothesis 3

Power distance plays a moderating role in the relationship between servant leadership and work meaning: the higher the power distance, the stronger the role of servant leadership in positively influencing the work meaning.

Based on the above hypotheses, we further propose a moderated mediated effects model (see Fig. 1); that is, varying degrees of power distance moderated the indirect effect of servant leadership influencing unethical pro-organizational behavior through the work meaning. In other words, servant leadership has a greater role in influencing unethical pro-organizational behavior through work meaning in high power distance contexts than in low power distance contexts.

Theoretical model of this study

Hypothesis 4

Power distance moderates the indirect effect of servant leadership on the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff through work meaning. The higher the power distance, the stronger this indirect effect.

Methods

Research sample

The sample of this study was drawn from the medical staff of more than ten public hospitals in Guangdong Province and Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, China. The questionnaire was distributed using the Questionnaire Star online platform and offline simultaneously, and the screening criteria for the study subjects were the medical staff of public hospitals, including doctors, nurses, medical technicians, and so on. To ensure the quality of questionnaire recovery, in addition to emphasizing the anonymity of the subjects in the questionnaire and the academic research purpose of the questionnaire survey, the multi-stage sampling design took into account the degree of economic development of the region where the public hospitals are located, the level of public hospitals, and so on, and the survey of representative hospitals, including the Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital, Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Traditional Chinese Medicine, Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region Hospital, and other tertiary-level A hospitals. The survey questionnaire started online platform to collect 300 questionnaires, offline collection of 180, a total of 480 questionnaires, excluding incomplete answers, fill-in time less than 2 min, and other non-compliant questionnaires. After screening out the invalid questionnaires, 420 questionnaires were obtained, questionnaire validity rate of 87.5%. There were 240 males, accounting for 57.1%, and 180 females, accounting for 42.9%. In terms of the age of the sample, there are 27 people aged 25 and below, accounting for 6.4%; 93 people aged 26–30, accounting for 22.1%; 49people aged 31–35, accounting for 11.7%; 86 people aged 36–40, accounting for 20.5%; 78 people aged 41–45, accounting for 18.6%; and 87 people aged 46 and above, accounting for 20.7%. About the educational level of the sample, there were 7 persons with high school education or below, accounting for 1.7%;41 persons with specialized education, accounting for 9.8%; 221 persons with bachelor’s degree, accounting for 52.6%; and 151 persons with master’s degree or above, accounting for 36%. Regarding the professional titles, there were 31 people, or 5.8%, who were undecided; 247 people, or 58.8%, who were junior;99 people, or 23.6%, who were intermediate; and 43 people, or 10.24%, who were senior. In terms of the sample’s position, there were 195 physicians (46.4%), 67 nurses (16%), 121 medical technicians (28.8%), and 37 others (8.8%).

Variable measurement

The scales used in this study are all mature scales that have been used many times in authoritative literature at home and abroad, or scales adapted from authoritative scales in Chinese and undergo strict translation-back-translation procedures. Meanwhile, in this study, 88 subjects were recruited through the questionnaire star online platform to pre-test a small sample of the prepared questionnaire, and after removing 16 unqualified questionnaires, a total of 72 valid questionnaires were obtained, and the effective recovery rate of the questionnaire was predicted to be 81.82%. The questionnaire was revised according to the pre-test situation to ensure the validity of the questionnaire in the Chinese context.

Servant leadership

This variable was measured using the Servant Leadership Scale developed by Liden et al. [61] which consists of 7 items and is scored on a 5-point Likert scale. A representative item is “My manager can tell if something work-related is going wrong.” The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for this scale in this study was 0.91.

Unethical pro-organizational behavior

This variable was measured using a questionnaire developed by Umphress et al. [62] with 6 items scored on a 5-point Likert scale. Representative items include “If it would help my organization, I would misrepresent the truth to make my organization look good.” The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for this scale in this study was 0.90.

Work meaning

This variable was measured using the Work Meaning Scale developed by Steger et al. [63] which consists of 10 items and is scored on a 5-point Likert scale. Representative items include “My work helps me better understand myself.” The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for this scale in this study was 0.95.

Power distance

This variable was measured using a questionnaire developed by Dorfman and Howell [64]. on a 5-point Likert scale. Representative items include “I don’t think supervisors need to consult employees when making most decisions” and “I don’t think subordinates should object to supervisors’ decisions”. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for this scale was 0.90 in this study.

Control variables

According to existing studies, individual characteristic variables such as gender, age, education, title, and position will have an impact on the attitudes and behaviors of medical staff. Therefore, to safeguard the scientific validity of the conclusions of this study, the above individual characteristic variables are treated as control variables.

Discussion and conclusion

Discussion

First, servant leadership has a significant positive predictive effect on the medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior. The findings indicate that in hospitals, the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff is positively influenced by servant leadership, which suggests that positive leadership, under certain conditions, can also harm the organization, which is consistent with the findings of similar studies that have been conducted [66]. Compared to the existing literature focusing on unethical pro-organizational behavior in for-profit organizations, this study, based on the study of unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff, expands the study of unethical pro-organizational behavior in nonprofit organizations to a certain extent, and deepens the literature’s understanding of the factors that induce unethical pro-organizational behavior in Chinese healthcare organizations.

Second, work meaning mediates the relationship between servant leadership and the medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior. In recent years, the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff has attracted extensive academic attention, but few empirical studies have been conducted [67]. This study explored the formation mechanisms of medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior from the perspective of positive leadership style based on cultural values. It was found that servant leadership increased the medical staff’s work meaning, which in turn promoted their unethical pro-organizational behavior. This finding reveals the “black box” of servant leadership’s influence on medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior, which can help people to understand the formation mechanism of unethical pro-organizational behavior, and enrich the research literature on the work meaning in managerial psychology.

Finally, power distance moderates the relationship between servant leadership and the medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior. The study showed that the strength of the indirect effect of servant leadership on inducing unethical pro-organizational behavior among medical staff through work meaning was moderated by individual power distance, and that high power distance enhanced the positive effect of servant leadership on medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior relative to low power distance. This finding not only enriches the research on the moderating variables of unethical pro-organizational behavior but also provides avenues of intervention for preventing and controlling the occurrence of unethical pro-organizational behavior among medical staff.

Theoretical contributions

The theoretical significance of this study is mainly twofold: first, based on the social information processing theory perspective, this study reveals the inherent process of servant leadership inducing unethical pro-organizational behavior among medical staff. Most of the existing studies have used social exchange theory to explain the relationship between positive leadership styles and subordinates’ unethical pro-organizational behavior [26, 68], and the present study, based on the social information processing theory, revealed the mediating mechanism of work meaning in the way servant leadership influences the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff. In other words, medical staff were informed of important cues and information about the organizational environment and interpreted them through servant leadership, which influenced their work meaning and ultimately played a role in their engagement in unethical pro-organizational behavior. This suggests that the words and actions of servant leadership, as agents of the organization, profoundly affect medical staff’s work meaning and their commitment to safeguarding the interests of the organization, which may ultimately induce their unethical pro-organizational behavior.

Second, this study further reveals the moderating role of power distance in the relationship between servant leadership and medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior, thus enriching the understanding of the boundaries that shape unethical pro-organizational behavior. Although the existing literature has paid much attention to the boundary conditions in which unethical pro-organizational behavior occurs when exploring them, especially the role of individual morality [69, 70], not enough attention has been paid to the variables of the nature of cultural values, therefore, this study introduces power distance into the model of unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff, which overcomes the problem of insufficient attention to aspects such as cultural values in previous studies of unethical pro-organizational behavior. This study compensates to some extent for the insufficient attention paid to contextual factors such as cultural values in the study of unethical pro-organizational behavior.

Management implications

Based on empirical findings and the fact that unethical pro-organizational behavior is potentially risky for hospitals, the implications of this study for hospital management are as follows: (1)Be alert to the negative impacts of servant leadership. Existing literature focuses on the positive impacts of servant leadership, and many organizations select and develop servant leadership; however, they may have overlooked the dark side of servant leadership. Therefore, hospitals should consider the positive effects of servant leadership, and it is also important to curb the profit-driven behavior of managers at the institutional level and to strengthen the training of medical staff in ethical values and work codes of conduct in annual training programs. (2)Strengthening the servant leadership style. Hospitals should carry out style construction among their cadres, strengthen moral cultivation, establish a sense of rules and regulations, promote the Core Values of Chinese Socialism, resolutely oppose all unethical behaviors in hospitals, and prevent unethical behaviors from occurring. (3)Building high-quality superior-subordinate relationships. There are differences in the perceptions and reactions of medical staff to different power distances from their leaders. For medical staff with high power distance, leaders need to be properly guided, strengthening professional ethics education and guarding against the occurrence of unethical behavior because of their loyalty to the hospital. Therefore, the hospital level to build a regular communication mechanism between managers and subordinates, strengthen the communication and interaction between leaders and subordinates, build high-quality superior-subordinate relationships, and reduce power distance to prevent unethical behavior. In addition, due to the significant differences in cultural values in countries around the world, it is necessary to consider the cultural context of the host country when introducing the results of this study into management practice, focusing on countries with high power distance.

Limitations and future research

First, this study is based on the social information processing theory to investigate how servant leadership influences the unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff, without taking into account other theoretical perspectives. Future research should use social identity theory to explore the relationship between servant leadership and medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior. Second, the survey data were concentrated in public hospitals in Guangdong Province and Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region in China, which may have reduced the external validity of the data. Future research can further improve the sampling region by sampling medical staff in public hospitals from the eastern, central, and western regions according to the economic development of China to improve the external effect of the study. Finally, in conducting the research design of this study, only the cultural value of power distance was considered as a boundary condition for the effect of servant leadership on medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior, while organizational-level moderating variables such as organizational ethical climate were not considered; future research could consider introducing organizational-level moderating variables into the research model to more fully understand the formation process of medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior.

Conclusion

This study was conducted with medical staff from more than ten public hospitals in Guangdong Province and Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region of China. From the perspective of social information processing theory, hypotheses such as the effect of servant leadership on medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior were verified based on the context of Chinese medical organizations. Descriptive statistics show that the mean values of servant leadership, work meaning, unethical pro-organizational behavior, and power distance are 3.294, 3.286, 3.226, and 3.255, respectively, and there is a significant correlation between the four variables. The hypothesis test results showed that servant leadership had a significant positive effect on medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior; work meaning significance partially mediated the relationship between servant leadership and medical staff’s unethical pro-organizational behavior; power distance moderated the indirect effect of servant leadership’s influence on unethical pro-organizational behavior through work meaning, and, relative to a low power distance, in a high power distance context, servant leadership influence unethical pro-organizational behavior of medical staff through work meaning plays a greater role.

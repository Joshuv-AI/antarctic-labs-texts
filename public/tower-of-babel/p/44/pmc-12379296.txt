Decoherence-assisted quantum key distribution
Authors: Daniel R. Sabogal, Daniel F. Urrego, Juan R. Álvarez, Andrés F. Herrera, Juan P. Torres, Alejandra Valencia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
We put forward and demonstrate a controllable decoherence-assisted quantum key distribution scheme. The protocol is based on the possibility of introducing controllable decoherence to polarization qubits using the spatial degree of freedom of light. We show that our method reduces the amount of information that an eavesdropper can obtain in the BB84 protocol under the entangling probe attack. We demonstrate experimentally that Alice and Bob can agree on a scheme that gives low values of the quantum bit error rate, despite the presence of a large amount of decoherence in the transmission channel of the BB84 protocol.

Introduction

Quantum key distribution (QKD) enables the secure communication between two parties using the principles of quantum mechanics. QKD has been implemented using different degrees of freedom of light: polarization1,2, frequency3, continuous variables4, and orbital angular momentum5. The security of some QKD protocols can be proven theoretically. An example of this is the BB84 protocol6, for which it has been demonstrated that an unconditionally secure secret key can be distilled if the quantum bit error rate (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textrm{QBER}$$\end{document}) is below \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$11\%$$\end{document}7,8.

Specific eavesdropper attacks have been considered and analyzed for the BB84 protocol9. For example, the eavesdropper can attack one photon at a time10,11 by ensuring that the photon Alice distributes to Bob interacts, through a unitary transformation, with a probe photon belonging to Eve12. This method, referred to as the entangling probe attack, has been studied in detail13–15. In fact, while the ideal BB84 protocol without any eavesdropper has a \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textrm{QBER}$$\end{document}=0, under the entangling probe attack, the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textrm{QBER}$$\end{document} increases when the eavesdropper obtains information about the key. While more sophisticated attacks operating collectively on multiple photons sent from Alice to Bob are possible, the entangling probe attack serves as a minimally viable, yet quantifiable example of the errors that Eve introduces when Alice distributes a key to Bob.

In the last decade, there have also been significant developments aiming at the real world implementation of QKD protocols, modifying the protocols to improve detection bandwidth16, include decoy states17, enhance the security of real world implementations18, and even implement other techniques of QKD such as measurement device independent QKD19 and twin-field QKD20,21.

Here we introduce a controllable decoherence-assisted scheme for the BB84 protocol. With this method, it is possible to use the ostensibly detrimental effects of decoherence to reduce the amount of information that an eavesdropper can obtain when attacking the standard BB84 protocol with an entangling probe. In particular, our method allows to reduce the amount of information that an eavesdropper can obtain from attacking the channel set between Alice and Bob, while facilitating the detection of the presence of an eavesdropper. Our method takes advantage of the possibility of introducing decoherence in a controllable way that indeed can be canceled when it is induced appropriately on Alice’s and Bob’s sides. The decoherence is induced with a dephasing channel implemented using spatial and polarization photonic degrees of freedom22. Specifically, the transverse momentum of light acts as an environment that induces a tunable dephasing on a quantum system represented by the polarization of light. The coupling between environment and system is controlled by a parameter that can be adjusted at will. While prior approaches in QKD have made use of multiple degrees of freedom of light to enhance the robustness of the protocols to external noise23, this paper adds to the already existing literature that aims at taking advantage of noise for improving quantum communications, either by adding white noise24, by using depolarizing channels25, or by theoretically describing the use of non-Markovian channels26. We experimentally implement a concatenation of two controllable dephasing channels constituting a non-Markovian channel27 to harness decoherence to improve the security of the BB84 protocol.

This paper is organized as follows: We start by introducing a theoretical model that describes the effect of the controllable decoherence-assisted scheme in the BB84 protocol. We then present a brief overview of the entangling probe attack to mathematically demonstrate that, in this case, the amount of information available to an eavesdropper is smaller when the controllable decoherence-assisted scheme is used. We then present experimental results to demonstrate that the specific type of decoherence introduced by a controllable dephasing channel at Bob’s side can cancel dephasing decoherence effects introduced by Alice. We show this effect by introducing the appropriate decoherence in Alice’s and Bob’s sides and recovering the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\textrm{QBER}$$\end{document} of the BB84 protocol in the absence of an eavesdropper. Theoretical background

In this section, we present the theoretical model behind the controllable decoherence-assisted scheme. We start by considering the ideal situation in which there is no eavesdropper. Then, we present a brief overview of the entangling probe attack and move to consider the effect of our method when the BB84 protocol is under such attack.

Entangling probe attack

In the entangling probe attack13, Eve intercepts the state that Alice sends to Bob and entangles it with her probe qubit using a C-NOT gate. The intercepted qubit is sent to Bob, and Eve keeps the probe qubit. The entanglement that the C-NOT gate generates between the two qubits allows Eve to obtain information about the polarization state of the intercepted qubit, and is the tool used by Eve to guess the key that Alice and Bob share.

Experiment 1: BB84 protocol

The photons sent by Alice are produced by a heralded single-photon (HSP) source based on spontaneous parametric down conversion (SPDC). The SPDC photon pairs traverse a beam splitter (BS) and the reflected photon is used as the heralded photon to be sent to Bob. This heralded photon passes through a single mode fiber with a polarization controller (PC) to define the photon polarization state and to obtain a Gaussian spatial distribution.

The recognition of the HSP is done by means of the temporal second order correlation function, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G^{2}(\tau )$$\end{document}. Specifically, recognizing pairs of photons that are within a window of width \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2 \sigma$$\end{document}, centered at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\tau _{0}$$\end{document}, the maximum of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G^{2}(\tau )$$\end{document}. The value of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sigma$$\end{document} is chosen as the standard deviation of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G^{2}(\tau )$$\end{document}, which resembles a Gaussian distribution. Such Gaussian distribution is assumed since the response time of the detectors dominates the shape of the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G^{2}(\tau )$$\end{document} for SPDC.

In order to make our measurements more efficient, the HSP source was based on Type II SPDC followed by a BS. In this way, the heralding photon is either horizontally or vertically polarized. Experiment 2: BB84 protocol under the controllable decoherence-assisted scheme

The second proof-of-principle experiment we present consists of introducing the controllable decoherence-assisted scheme into the BB84 protocol. In order to do so, two dephasing channels are introduced to the setup by placing PBS2 and PBS3 as shown in the yellow box of Fig. 4.

Each dephasing channel is implemented using a polarizing tunable beam displacer (P-TBD)31. This device takes an input polarized beam and divides it into two parallel beams with orthogonal polarizations. The P-TBD consists of two mirrors and a PBS mounted on a rotating platform. The angle that the platform is rotated determines the distance, d, that the two parallel beams are separated. The platform can rotate clockwise or counter-clockwise and this results in the two parallel beams being separated or getting closer, respectively. The P-TBD has been demonstrated to work as a controllable dephasing channel by coupling polarization and transverse momentum variables of light. The controllable feature comes from the fact that the P-TBD is a tunable device in which the distance d acts as the control parameter.

On Alice’s side, the P-TBD-A introduces controlled decoherence, governed by the parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d_a$$\end{document}, after the polarization state is prepared by \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\text {HWP}_A$$\end{document}. Conclusions

We have presented a method to reduce the amount of information that an eavesdropper can obtain in the BB84 quantum key distribution protocol. This approach is based on the deliberate and controlled introduction of decoherence via two tunable dephasing channels applied to the polarization and spatial degrees of freedom of light. We theoretically analyzed this decoherence-assisted scheme under the entangling probe attack and demonstrated that the Rényi information accessible to an eavesdropper is significantly reduced for values of the quantum bit error rate (QBER) below the critical security threshold of 11%.

To illustrate the working principle of the controllable decoherence-assisted scheme, we performed two proof-of-principle experiments using heralded single photons. In the standard BB84 protocol, we achieved an average QBER of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$3.9 \pm 0.3\%$$\end{document} across five keys of 1000 bits each. In the second experiment, we introduced controllable decoherence on both Alice’s and Bob’s sides. Despite this added noise, we experimentally demonstrated that the QBER can be restored to secure levels (consistently below 11%) as long as the decoherence parameters are matched (i.e., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$d_a=d_b$$\end{document}). This was confirmed across seven independent configurations, each showing a minimum QBER well within the secure regime.

These results validate the central idea of the scheme: controlled decoherence, when properly coordinated, can serve as a practical mechanism to degrade eavesdropping effectiveness while preserving key integrity for legitimate users. Beyond the immediate application to BB84, this work contributes to a growing line of research that explores how noise (traditionally considered detrimental) can be repurposed to enhance quantum communication security. Furthermore, although our protocol is a proof-of-principle demonstration, one might envision the emerging techniques of spatial division multiplexing32 for the real-world implementation of quantum networks, requiring the development of fiber-based controllable dephasing channels.

Efficacy of Biologics on Nasal Symptoms in Severe Asthmatics: We Need Additional Biomarkers or Strategies to Assess Phenotypes or Endotypes
Authors: Ho Jin Yong, Young-Koo Jee
Published: 2025

Interleukin 4 receptor a (IL-4Ra), IL-5, and immunoglobulin E (IgE) play central roles in T2 inflammation.1 A recent meta-analysis summarized that anti-IL-4Ra monoclonal antibody (antil-IL-4Ra mAb; dupilumab), anti-IL-5 mAb (mepolizumab and reslizumab), anti-IL5R mAb (benralizumab), and anti-IgE mAb (omalizumab) significantly reduced exacerbations and improved lung function/patient reported outcomes vs. placebo, and that T2–high biomarkers (e.g., blood eosinophils, fractional exhaled nitric oxide [FeNO], IgE) enrich for greater benefit.2

Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRSwNP), characterized by a T2 immune signature, is more frequently observed in patients with severe asthma (57%–90%) than in those within less severe cases (10%–42%) with an overall prevalence of up to 65%.3,4 In contrast, the prevalence of asthma among patients with CRSwNP ranges from 30% to 70%.5 Thus, CRSwNP and asthma are closely linked to each other in pathogenesis and prevalence. Among biologic therapies for CRSwNP, dupilumab showed the highest therapeutic efficacy in terms of polyp size, symptoms, quality-of-life (QoL), and smell; mepolizumab reduced polyp burden with generally smaller effects; reslizumab lacked supportive evidence from RCT in CRSwNP meta-analyses.6 Pairwise meta-analyses demonstrated similar effects in patients with and without asthma, as well as in those with aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease (AERD) vs. non-AERD. Regarding QoL outcomes measured by Sino-Nasal Outcome Test 22 (SNOT-22), dupilumab reduced most effectively (mean difference [MD], −19.91; 95% confidence internal [CI], −22.50 to −17.32), followed by omalizumab (MD, −16.09; 95% CI, −19.88 to −12.30), mepolizumab (MD, −12.89; 95% CI, −16.58 to −9.19), and benralizumab (MD, −7.68; 95% CI, −12.09 to −3.27).6

In the current issue, Shim et al.7 also reported that dupilumab significantly reduced total SNOT-22 in patients with nasal symptoms (odds ratio [OR] for achieving minimal clinically important difference, 2.37; 95% CI, 1.14 to 4.90). The therapeutic effect remained significant after adjustment for age, sex, forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1), and blood eosinophil count (OR, 2.29; 95% CI, 1.08 to 4.87). However, dupilumab did not significantly reduce rhinologic domain (OR, 1.87; 95% CI, 0.87 to 4.03) or ear/facial domain (OR, 2.07; 95% CI, 0.86 to 5.0). In contrast significant improvements were observed in the extranasal rhinologic (OR, 2.80; 95% CI, 1.20 to 6.56), psychological dysfunction (OR, 2.23; 95% CI, 1.03 to 4.83), and sleep dysfunction (OR, 2.50; 95% CI, 1.14 to 5.47) domains. Such inconsistent findings may be attributed to the study’s limitation of relying solely on patients’ self-reported history of physician-diagnosed CRS. Objective diagnostic tools, such as rhinoscopy, paranasal X-ray, or computed tomography, were not employed to confirm CRS or other types of rhinosinusoidal disorders. The heterogeneity of comorbidities—allergic rhinitis (AR, 71.6% of the study subjects), CRS (47.6%), and nasal polyps (22.7%)—complicated the results and their interpretation. In AR, omalizumab, which was not used in this study,7 has been shown to be effective in reducing nasal symptoms and improving overall QoL. Dupilumab also demonstrated positive outcomes, particularly in patients with comorbid asthma and perennial AR. However, mepolizumab lacks sufficient AR-specific data for meta-analysis.8,9,10,11,12 Given that dupilumab was likely administered in this study to patients with severe asthma who met the approval criteria of the Korean Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (KMFDS) (FeNO ≥ 25 ppb and peripheral blood eosinophils ≥ 150/µL), it is highly probable that their nasal symptoms—whether originating from AR, CRSwNP, or CRS without NP—were driven by T2 inflammation. Therefore, dupilumab may have contributed to improved QoL by alleviating nasal symptoms.

Another plausible reason for the selective improvement in only 3 domains (extra-nasal rhinologic, psychological dysfunction, and sleep dysfunction) may be related to the significant influence of asthma disease activity on these domains. Firstly, cough, which is included in extranasal rhinologic symptom domain of SNOT-22, is closely associated with asthma.13,14,15 Psychological and sleep dysfunctions, including sleep disturbance, emotional stress, frustration, and limitation in dailylife activity can also be affected by asthma. Accordingly, asthma QoL also includes items related to these domains.16 Notably, improvement in the extra-nasal rhinologic and sleep dysfunction domains were significantly associated with asthma control and lung function parameters, including pre- and post-FEV1 (%predict) and FEV1/FVC. Taken together, dupilumab may reduce scores in the extranasal rhinologic, psychological dysfunction, and sleep dysfunction domains in SNOT-22 by attenuating type 2 asthma disease activity.

Anti-IL-5/IL-5R mAb (mepolizumab, reslizumab, benralizumab) significantly improved nasal-polyp score and symptom outcomes, especially in eosinophilic/T2-high subgroups of CRS; however, therapeutic effects were generally less pronounced than those observed with dupilumab.6 Yamani et al.8 also reported that anti-IL-5 agents improved nasal symptoms in patients with coexisting AR and eosinophilic asthma or CRSwNP, reflecting the overlap in T2-driven inflammation. Shim and colleagues7 reported that mepolizumab and reslizumab did not significantly reduce domain scores, including total, rhinologic, extra-nasal rhinologic, ear/facial, psychological and sleep dysfunction, in SNOT-22 in patients with severe asthma and nasal symptoms. According to the KMFDS approval criteria for severe asthma, mepolizumab is indicated for patients with peripheral eosinophil counts of ≥ 400/µL, and reslizumab for those with counts of ≥ 300/µL. Therefore, in this study,7 both biologics were administered to patients with severe asthma whose peripheral eosinophil counts were at least 300/µL. To maximize the therapeutic effects of the 2 biologics, more precise patient selection may be required by incorporating additional T2 biomarkers beyond peripheral blood eosinophil counts.

Despite those limitations in the study of Shim and colleagues,7 the study offers several strengths. It is the first large-scale, multicenter (38 centers), real-world analysis conducted in Korea to assess the impact of biologics on nasal symptoms in patients with severe asthma. Especially, the study conducted comprehensive symptom evaluation by analyzing 5 symptom domains (rhinologic, extranasal rhinologic, ear/facial, psychological, and sleep dysfunction) of SNOT-22. Furthermore, the study compared anti–IL-5 (mepolizumab, reslizumab) and anti–IL-4Rα (dupilumab) agents, providing mechanistic insights into how different biologic pathways translate to distinct symptom improvements.

Collectively, large-scale, well-designed prospective studies incorporating a variety of biomarkers are required to determine which biologic agent can maximize therapeutic efficacy in patients with both asthma and chronic rhinosinusitis. In the near future, the use of more accurate biomarkers and refined stratification strategies may enhance the management of these patients.

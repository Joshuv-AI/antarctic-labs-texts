How painful is Implanon NXT® insertion?: A multicenter randomized trial on the use of Acetaminophen, Mefenamic Acid and Diclofenac Sodium for pain management post Implanon NXT® insertion
Authors: Nik Asyraf Nik Azis, Mohamed S Zulfarina, Nurul ‘Izzah Ibrahim, Alina Ali, Alia Syamim ZaharI, Norhalili Mohamad Tamiri, Aisyah Sulaiman, Ng Xue Min, Azura Abdullah, Isa Naina-Mohamed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo determine and compare the pain relief between Acetaminophen, Mefenamic Acid and Diclofenac Sodium in participants who had just had Implanon NXT® inserted.Methods129 healthy female participants were recruited from four clinics prior to Implanon NXT® insertion. They were randomized to receive either tablet Acetaminophen; tablet Mefenamic Acid; tablet Diclofenac Sodium; or no drugs. Post insertion, participants marked their pain score on a 100 mm Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) line during Lignocaine injection, Implanon NXT® insertion, 2 hours post insertion, 4 hours post insertion, and every day thereafter for 7 days; once in the morning before taking any analgesic, and once again after taking the prescribed analgesic. The Pain Intensity Difference (PID) and Sum of Pain Intensity Differences (SPID) were calculated for each drug.ResultsThe Sum of Pain Intensity Differences (SPID) for Diclofenac Sodium group is 1009 mm VAS, for Mefenamic Acid group is 446 mm VAS, for Acetaminophen group is 296 mm VAS, and for Control group is 58 mm VAS. Kruskal Wallis ANOVA indicated that there were statistically significant differences between the Pain Intensity Differences on Day 2 (PID2) assigned to the “Mefenamic Acid” group and “Control” group (p = 0.021, adjusted significance (Bonferroni’s) p = 0.013). The ANCOVA indicated that, after accounting for the effects of different baseline pain intensities at the beginning of the study, there was a statistically significant effect of drugs used on Pain Intensity Difference Day 2 (PID2), p = 0.024, partial ƞ2 = 0.07. Post-hoc testing revealed that the participants in the Mefenamic Acid group reported higher Pain Intensity Difference on Day 2 (PID2) than the Control group.ConclusionAll oral analgesics demonstrated a reduction in pain score. Based on this limited study, the most suitable analgesic for pain management post Implanon NXT® insertion is tablet Mefenamic Acid 250 mg twice daily for up to 2 days. More robust double-blinded studies using placebos as a control are needed to confirm this finding. Background

Implanon NXT®, marketed as Nexplanon® in the U.S.A [1]. is a radio-opaque contraceptive rod measuring 4 cm in length and 2 mm in diameter which releases Etonorgestrel (Etonorgestrel implant) [2, 3] with c3-keto-desogestrel (3-KDSG), a high potency progesterone compound [3]. It is inserted sub-dermally in the inner non-dominant upper arm [2, 4, 5] and provides effective contraception for up to 3 years [1–5]. Its mechanisms of action are: anovulation, thickening of the cervical mucous and endometrial suppression [3]. It has high acceptability [6, 7] and efficacy with a Pearl Index of 0.38 [8]. Implanon NXT® differs from the originally marketed Implanon® etonogestrel implant by the addition of barium sulphate- making it radio-opaque, thus visible via x-ray and CT (computed tomography), and a new applicator design which facilitates single-handed insertion [1, 2].

Known complications of Implanon NXT® insertion and removal include pain at the site, physiological responses to a minor operation, and bruising [5]. An integrated analysis of 11 international clinical trials [8] noted that pain was the most frequently occurring implant site condition, occurring in 27 out of 942 subjects (2.9%) at any given time, and that injection site pain occurred in 43 (4.6%) of subjects [9]. Recently, the Nexplanon Observational Risk Assessment (NORA) study, which involved 428 healthcare professionals and 7364 insertion procedures, noted that the incidence of severe pain after insertion was 1.4 per 1000 insertions. The incidence of severe pain at any time during the follow up phase was 23.8 per 1000 insertions [1].

Pain management is an essential part of post-operative care. It makes patients less anxious about a surgery or procedure, increases patient satisfaction, and leads to better healing [10]. Implanon NXT® insertion is a minor surgical procedure commonly done under local anaesthesia, achieved using 2 ml of 1% Lignocaine [11]. Implanon NXT® insertion has been shown to cause mild pain [12]. Under current guidelines [5, 11], there is no specific recommendation for oral analgesic use for pain management post Implanon NXT® insertion.

According to the WHO, mild pain can be managed using weak analgesics such as Acetaminophen or NSAIDS [13]. The most commonly prescribed analgesics in Malaysia are Acetaminophen, Mefenamic Acid and Diclofenac Sodium [14]. The purpose of this study was to determine and compare the pain relief between Acetaminophen, Mefenamic Acid and Diclofenac Sodium in participants who had just had Implanon NXT® inserted.

Methods

Patient population

Participants were selected from patients attending National Population and Family Development Board (LPPKN) clinics in the states of Melaka, Selangor, Sabah, and Sarawak across Malaysia. Healthy Malaysian ladies of reproductive age who came for only Implanon NXT® insertion, used it only for contraception and were not on any other analgesics during the study period were eligible for inclusion. Excluded from the study were foreigners, ladies who came for Implanon NXT® removal and insertion during the same session, ladies who intended to use it for reasons other than contraception, and ladies who were allergic to any of the study drugs, had chronic kidney disease or ischemic heart disease.

Study design

This randomized, non-blinded, exploratory clinical trial with four parallel groups was conducted from 28th November 2024 to 24th June 2025. This study adhered to CONSORT guidelines [supplementary materials] and was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from the Universiti Kebangsaan Malaysia (UKM) ethics committee (JEP-2024-319) on 23rd August 2024.

All eligible patients in the four study sites were recruited. Each study site had a medical officer in charge of participant allocation, drug administration, and questionnaire collection. All participants also provided written informed consent prior to enrolment. This study was retrospectively registered with the Thai Clinical Trial Registry: TCTR20260331002 (https://www.thaiclinicaltrials.org/show/TCTR20260331002) on 31st March 2026. All drugs used in the study were drugs with proven safety profiles, not experimental drugs. Thus, the authors were not aware that the study design fell into the category of a controlled trial and required registration with a trial registry. The primary outcome and analysis plan had been specified and presented to the Ethics committee prior to study commencement.

Commonly prescribed drugs were chosen for use in this exploratory study [14] and all drugs were administered orally. Participants were randomly allocated by sequential numbering to one of the following groups: Tablet Acetaminophen 1000 mg four times daily (Paracil, SM Pharmaceuticals Sdn. Bhd., Malaysia), Tablet Mefenamic Acid 250 mg twice daily (Dyfenamic Tablet, Dynapharm (M) Sdn. Bhd., Malaysia), Tablet Diclofenac Sodium 50 mg three times daily (Dicloran ®, Unique Pharmaceuticals, India) and Control group that did not receive any medication. Sequential numbering was generated by allocating participant number 1 to the Acetaminophen group, number 2 to the Mefenamic Acid group, number 3 to the Diclofenac Sodium group and number 4 to the Control group. The subsequent participant allocation followed this sequence. Assignment of the participant to the drug group was performed by the site investigator as per sequence. Sequential numbering was chosen due to its ease of application. Allocation concealment was ensured by the site investigator who did not divulge this sequence to participants.

The 100 mm Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) line was used to measure pain score as the primary outcome in this study due to its reliability [15], high sensitivity, and specificity [16]. Participants were asked to mark their anxiety score on a 100 mm Visual Analogue Scale-Anxiety (VAS-A) before the procedure. Implanon NXT® was inserted for all participants following standard guidelines [5, 11]. Participants were instructed to mark their pain score on a 100 mm Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) line during Lignocaine injection, Implanon NXT® insertion, 2 hours post procedure and 4 hours post procedure. They were also asked to mark their post-procedure pain every day for a period of 7 days; once in the morning upon waking up before taking any analgesic, and once again after taking the prescribed analgesic and achieving maximum serum concentration (30 minutes for Acetaminophen [17], 2 hours for NSAIDS [18, 19] and Control group). The 2-hour waiting time duration was chosen for the control group in order to account for the natural decrease in acute procedural pain over time. Compliance to the maximum dose of the drug was advised if the participant felt pain. Participants were allowed to discontinue the drug once they were pain-free at the start of the day.

Standard measures of analgesia were derived from the data collected [20]. Pain Intensity Difference (PID) scores were derived by subtracting each observed pain intensity score from the baseline pain intensity score. The Sum of Pain Intensity Difference (SPID) was calculated for each drug [20].

Statistical analysis

The sample size was calculated based on the smallest pain intensity difference (PID) for the weakest analgesic [21]. Using a formula for comparing means between groups [22], with 95% power and 5% significance level, 27 participants per treatment group were required. Additionally, we included a 10% dropout rate in our calculation. It was decided to include 30 participants per group with a total sample size of 120.

For the first part of the study, the mean and standard deviation for pain scores during Lignocaine injection, Implanon NXT® insertion, 2 hours post procedure and 4 hours post procedure were calculated using data from all participants.

For the second part of the study, Pain Intensity Difference (PID) was calculated by subtracting the post-drug administration pain score from the pre-drug administration pain score [20]. The sum of the PID by drug group for each day was calculated and compared to the control group on a day-to-day basis. Due to non-normality of the data, it was analysed using the one-way analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) and Kruskal-Wallis One Way ANOVA test. Statistical analysis was done using IBM Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) Version 25 (Chicago, Illinois, United States of America).

Results

There were 148 participants who enrolled in the study with 19 participants lost to follow-up, leaving 129 participants included in the final analysis. The highest number of participants came from the state of Melaka (n = 58), followed by Sarawak (n = 32), Selangor (n = 26) and Sabah (n = 13). The participants were randomized to receive tablet Acetaminophen 1000 mg four times daily (n = 36), tablet Mefenamic Acid 250 mg twice daily (n = 30), tablet Diclofenac Sodium 50 mg three times daily (n = 32) and Control group which did not receive any drugs (n = 31). Discussion

From the first part of the study, we can compare the anxiety pre-procedure, pain during Lignocaine injection and pain during Implanon NXT® insertion to a similar study done in the U.S.A [12]. Compared to the findings of Bentsianov et al. where Visual Analogue Scale-Anxiety was 40.23 ± 28.8 mm VAS-A, our anxiety score was lower at 29.9 ± 24.5 mm VAS-A. However, the pain felt during Lignocaine injection and Implanon NXT® insertion was higher in our study at 24.5 ± 20.5 mm VAS and 11.5 ± 14.9 mm VAS respectively, compared to Bentsianov et al. at 19 ± 21 mm VAS and 6 ± 11 mm VAS. The differences in pain score may be due to factors such as needle gauge used, injection speed, operator experience and subcutaneous tissue thickness.

The effect of Lignocaine local anaesthesia begins to wear off at 2 hours and is completely metabolized after 4 hours [24]. The mean and standard deviation of pain felt after 2 hours is 13.5 ± 16.6 mm VAS, while at 4 hours it is 12.3 ± 15.3 mm VAS.

When we observe the baseline pain intensity before taking any drugs on Day 1, it is noted that not all pain scores are the same, even though all participants have been exposed to the same amount of pain i.e. insertion of Implanon NXT®. For Acetaminophen, it is 7.4 ± 11.9 mm VAS, Mefenamic Acid 9.3 ± 11.1 mm VAS, Diclofenac Sodium 14.8 ± 23.6 mm VAS and Control group 7.6 ± 10.3 mm VAS. Diclofenac’s higher score can be explained by the participants’ beliefs, attitudes, and expectations regarding pain relief [25]. Normally, we have ideas about the cause of the pain, its management, and how long it should take for recovery. These expectations appear to drive coping behaviour, even in the seeming absence of actual feedback [25]. The participant upon noticing that she has been given a ‘strong’ analgesic, believes that the pain score must also be higher to justify its use, thus is predisposed to giving the initial pain a higher score. Attention and vigilance to pain is also a specific psychological factor in pain experience [26].

The Sum of Pain Intensity Differences (SPID) for each drug also correlates with its supposed position on the WHO analgesic ladder [13]. Diclofenac Sodium has the highest SPID at 1009 mm VAS, followed by Mefenamic Acid at 446 mm VAS, Acetaminophen at 296 mm VAS, and Control Group at 58 mm VAS. It was observed that baseline pain before treatment was higher in the Diclofenac Sodium group compared to the others. The high baseline may have played a part in the high SPID of Diclofenac Sodium due to the increased potential for improvement. Acute pain naturally decreases over time. This can also explain the discrepancy between the PID of Acetaminophen and the other groups since post medication pain measurement was measured earlier at 30 minutes in the Acetaminophen group compared to 2 hours for the other groups.

Statistical analysis indicated that there were statistically significant differences between the Pain Intensity Differences on Day 2 (PID2) assigned to the Mefenamic Acid group and Control group. Even though Mefenamic Acid has a lower SPID compared to Diclofenac Sodium, the statistically significant tests show that it is more suitable for managing pain post Implanon NXT® insertion.

In conclusion, all oral analgesics used in this study demonstrated a reduction in pain score with Diclofenac Sodium having the highest reduction, followed by Mefenamic Acid and Acetaminophen. This finding is consistent with existing literature regarding the efficacy of analgesics and their position on the WHO step-ladder of pain management. The only analgesic with statistical significance against Control group was Mefenamic Acid at Day 2 post insertion (ANCOVA p = 0.024, partial ƞ2= 0.07, Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.021, adjusted significance (Bonferroni’s) p = 0.013). Based on this limited study, the most suitable analgesic for pain management post Implanon NXT® insertion is tablet Mefenamic Acid 250 mg twice daily for up to 2 days. More robust double-blinded studies using placebos as a control are needed to confirm this finding.

Limitation

This study is limited by non-blinding of both the participants and the study coordinators to the drugs assigned. It can be improved by making it a double-blinded study which will eliminate any biases. The lack of blinding may have also contributed to the higher baseline pain experienced in the Diclofenac Sodium group. The larger observed reduction in pain intensity of the Diclofenac Sodium group may have been, at least partly, due to higher baseline pain rather than pure treatment effect.

A major limitation for this study was the randomization of participants using sequential numbering. Sequential numbering was chosen due to its ease of implementation. Although participant allocation was concealed, the predictability of the allocation sequence leads to a substantial risk of selection bias. This bias could have been reduced by using an unpredictable sequence such as computer-generated randomization.

A very important limitation of this study was the different timings of post-medication pain assessment. Every drug has a different duration of drug action which can be influenced by factors such as metabolic rates, blood flow to tissues and individual patient factors like age, genetics and disease states [27]. Therefore, the authors agreed to use the time taken for the drug to achieve maximum serum concentration, thereby exerting maximum analgesic effect, as the duration of time to measure post medication dose pain. However, we overlooked the fact that time itself reduces pain. The difference in timing may have influenced the observed between-group differences and, therefore, limits the comparability of treatment effects. The longer waiting time in the NSAID group may have led to a larger apparent pain reduction without stronger pharmacological effect since acute procedural pain naturally decreases with time. However, to account for this natural decrease in pain over time, we used the same waiting time (2 hours) in the Control group for comparison.

This study could have been strengthened by formulating all the drugs as similar capsules and using a placebo for the control group. The drugs should also be administered on the same schedule to each participant, with placebo taking the place of any active drug that was not yet scheduled. This would have reduced any potential selection and performance bias.

The study is also limited by the small sample size. A larger sample size would increase the power of the study.

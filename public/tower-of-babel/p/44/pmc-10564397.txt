Beyond the transanal total mesorectal excision moratorium: local and distant recurrence among patients operated for low rectal tumours—5-year follow-up from a Norwegian University Hospital
Authors: Rolf N Riis, Margit H Riis, Jūratė Šaltytė Benth, Knut Magne Augestad
Published: 2023

Dear Editor

The transanal approach combined with the laparoscopic approach (TaTME) was introduced to decrease the risk of an incomplete total mesorectal excision (TME) and positive resection margins in patients with low rectal cancer, a high BMI, a narrow pelvis, and a bulky tumour1. In 2018, after more than 150 procedures, the Norwegian healthcare authorities imposed a moratorium after a high rate of patients with multifocal local recurrence in the pelvis2,3. However, there has not been any long-term follow-up of local recurrence (LR) or metastatic disease after the Norwegian 2018 moratorium.

This is an analysis of a prospectively collected database on patients who underwent TME for adenocarcinoma located less than 10 cm from the anal verge over an interval of 7.5 years (January 2014 to June 2021). Patients were stratified according to surgical approach—laparoscopic TME (LapTME, n = 159), open TME (OpTME, n = 89), or TaTME (n = 55)—and the cohorts compared with respect to histopathology, anastomotic leak, mortality, local pelvic recurrence, and disease-free survival4,5. The full methodology is available in the supplementary material.

Of 303 patients included, 185 (61.1 per cent) were men, with a mean(s.d.) age of 66.8(11.5) years and a mean BMI of 26.3(4.4) kg/m2. There was no significant difference between groups in sex, age, BMI, tumour size, tumour distance from anal verge, or number of T3–4 tumours estimated on MRI before treatment. Around 30 per cent of all patients underwent neoadjuvant radiotherapy (Tables S1).

The hybrid operation TaTME low anterior resection was performed in all patients in the TaTME group, and three-quarters received an anastomosis. In the two other cohorts, about one-third underwent abdominoperineal resection. Only 49 per cent of patients in the OpTME cohort and 43.4 per cent in the LapTME cohort received an anastomosis; 94.2 per cent of the patients with an anastomosis had a divierting stoma, compared with 100 per cent in the TaTME cohort. The median time to stoma reversal was 6 (range 1–31) months.

Sixteen patients (10.3 per cent) across the total cohort developed an early anastomotic leak; there were six leaks (14.3 per cent) in the TaTME cohort, eight (11.6 per cent) in the LapTME cohort, and only two (4.5 per cent) in the OpTME cohort (Table S2).

The quality of the specimen, measured according to the method of Quirke, was significantly poorer in the TaTME cohort, with a complete or near-complete mesorectum in only 67.3 per cent of patients. The distance from tumour cells to the circumferential resection margin was 1 mm or less in 13.5 per cent of patients; the rate was highest in the TaTME cohort (18.2 per cent). The distance from the tumour to the distal resection margin (DRM) was less than 5 mm in seven patients. The mean(s.d.) distance to the DRM was 29.2(16.7) mm (Table S3).

LR was diagnosed in 10 patients (3.3 per cent), with no significant difference between the 3 cohorts (P = 0.571). Three patients had LR in the TaTME cohort (5.5 per cent), two (2.2 per cent) in the OpTME cohort, and five (3.1 per cent) in the LapTME cohort. All LRs in the TaTME cohort were multifocal, whereas none were in the other groups. Thirty-eight patients (12.5 per cent) developed distant metastasis; the rate was lowest in the TaTME group (3 patients, 5.5 per cent) (Table S4).

Mean overall survival was 91.9 months (95 per cent c.i. 87.4 to 96.4) in the LapTME cohort, 88.2 months (82.5 to 93.9) in the OpTME cohort, and 73.5 months (67.3 to 79.7) in the TaTME cohort (P = 0.174) (Table S5 and Fig. 1).

Compared with OpTME and LapTME, TaTME is a safe procedure with acceptable long-term local and distant recurrence and complication rates. Owing to the difficulty of the purse-string suture and the risk of multifocal recurrence during initiation of the procedure (Table S5), TaTME should be performed only in high-volume centres and in selected patients.

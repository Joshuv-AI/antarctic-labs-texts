A multivariable prediction model for left atrial appendage spontaneous echo contrast in patients with atrial fibrillation: development and external validation
Authors: En Zhou, Jing Zhou, Yinghua Zou, Fengdan Wang, Qing He, Junfeng Zhang, Zongqi Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThis study aimed to evaluate the association between LAA metabolic parameters—particularly lactic acid, glucose, and calcium—and spontaneous echo contrast, and to develop and externally validate a multivariable prediction model incorporating these indicators.MethodsConsecutive patients with AF undergoing radiofrequency catheter ablation and/or left atrial appendage occlusion were retrospectively enrolled. All patients underwent preprocedural transesophageal echocardiography with direct LAA blood sampling for metabolic analysis. An internal cohort was used for feature selection by LASSO regression and multivariable logistic regression. Model performance was assessed using ROC analysis, calibration, and decision curve analysis, with external validation in an independent cohort.ResultsA total of 272 patients were included in the internal cohort, among whom 96 (35.3%) had SEC. Patients with SEC showed higher LAA lactic acid levels and lower LAA glucose and calcium levels. Age, persistent AF, LAA blood flow velocity, LAA lactic acid, LAA glucose, and LAA calcium were independently associated with SEC. The resulting nomogram demonstrated excellent discrimination in the internal cohort (AUC 0.895) and maintained robust performance in the external cohort (AUC 0.947). Decision curve analysis indicated a positive net clinical benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities.ConclusionsLAA metabolic characteristics, particularly elevated lactic acid levels, are independently associated with SEC in AF. A prediction model integrating metabolic, clinical, and echocardiographic parameters provides robust and externally validated risk stratification for SEC.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12872-026-06281-y.

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia and remains a major contributor to thromboembolic events, particularly ischemic stroke [1, 2]. Spontaneous echo contrast (SEC), characterized by dynamic, smoke-like echoes within the left atrium or left atrial appendage (LAA) on transesophageal echocardiography, is widely regarded as a marker of blood stasis and a precursor to thrombus formation [3–6]. The presence of LAA SEC has been consistently associated with an increased risk of left atrial thrombus and subsequent embolic events [7, 8].

Current risk stratification strategies in patients with AF mainly rely on clinical scores such as CHA₂DS₂-VASc [9]. However, these scores were developed to predict long-term thromboembolic risk rather than the local hemodynamic and microenvironmental conditions within the LAA. As a result, their ability to identify patients at high risk for SEC is limited [10, 11]. Echocardiographic parameters, particularly LAA blood flow velocity, have been shown to be strongly associated with SEC, underscoring the importance of local flow dynamics [12]. Nevertheless, blood stasis alone may not fully explain the heterogeneity of SEC formation observed in clinical practice.

Increasing evidence suggests that the LAA represents a unique anatomical and functional microenvironment characterized by complex interactions between blood flow, endothelial function, and local metabolic conditions [13, 14]. Beyond hemodynamic impairment, the left atrial appendage represents a unique anatomical and functional microenvironment in which local metabolic alterations may occur in the setting of atrial fibrillation [15]. These metabolic changes may reflect underlying disturbances in blood flow, oxygen delivery, and endothelial function within the LAA. However, direct clinical evidence characterizing the association between LAA metabolic profiles and the presence of spontaneous echo contrast remains limited.

Lactic acid is a key indicator of anaerobic metabolism and local hypoxia. Elevated lactate levels reflect impaired oxygen delivery and altered energy metabolism [16], conditions that may arise in regions of reduced blood flow such as the LAA in atrial fibrillation. Local lactate accumulation has been shown to influence endothelial function, platelet activation, and blood rheology [17], all of which are closely related to thrombus formation. Despite these mechanistic links, the clinical relevance of LAA lactate levels in relation to SEC has not been systematically investigated. In addition to lactate, glucose and calcium play essential roles in cellular metabolism, coagulation, and myocardial and endothelial function. Reduced glucose availability may indicate impaired substrate delivery or altered metabolic demand within the LAA, while calcium is a critical regulator of coagulation cascades, platelet activation, and erythrocyte deformability [18, 19]. Subtle disturbances in local glucose and calcium homeostasis may therefore contribute to a prothrombotic microenvironment, yet their association with SEC has received limited attention in clinical studies.

Taken together, these observations suggest that SEC formation may reflect not only hemodynamic stasis but also a complex metabolic imbalance within the LAA. A comprehensive assessment integrating clinical characteristics, echocardiographic parameters, and LAA metabolic indicators may provide deeper insights into the pathophysiology of SEC and improve risk stratification.

Accordingly, the present study aimed to investigate the association between LAA metabolic parameters—particularly lactic acid, glucose, and calcium levels—and the presence of SEC in patients with atrial fibrillation. Furthermore, we sought to develop and externally validate a multivariable prediction model incorporating these metabolic markers alongside conventional clinical and echocardiographic factors, with the goal of improving individualized risk assessment for LAA SEC.

Methods

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. The requirement for informed consent was waived owing to the retrospective nature of the study. All procedures were performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments.

Patient data

This retrospective observational study was conducted at Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine from March 2023 to May 2025. This study included consecutive adult patients (≥ 18 years) with documented atrial fibrillation who were admitted for catheter-based radiofrequency ablation and/or left atrial appendage occlusion and underwent transesophageal echocardiography as part of routine preprocedural assessment. Patients were required to have complete clinical data, echocardiographic measurements, and left atrial appendage blood gas and metabolic parameters available for analysis. Patients were excluded if they had a prior history of left atrial appendage occlusion or surgical exclusion, evidence of left atrial or left atrial appendage thrombus on transesophageal echocardiography, significant valvular heart disease requiring surgical intervention, severe hepatic or renal dysfunction, active infection, malignancy, or other systemic conditions that could substantially affect metabolic or coagulation status, or if key data were missing.

External validation was conducted using an independent cohort of patients with atrial fibrillation treated at Shanghai Ninth People’s Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine from March 2023 to February 2025. Patients in the external cohort underwent radiofrequency catheter ablation and/or left atrial appendage occlusion and were enrolled according to the same inclusion and exclusion criteria applied to the internal cohort. All patients underwent transesophageal echocardiography, and spontaneous echo contrast was assessed by the same experienced echocardiographers using the identical grading system and classification criteria as those used in the internal cohort. Left atrial appendage blood samples were collected following the same procedural protocol, and all blood gas and metabolic measurements were performed using the same laboratory platform and analytical methods. The external validation cohort consisted of 77 patients, including 29 patients with SEC and 48 patients without SEC. This cohort was used exclusively for independent validation of the prediction model and was not involved in model development, feature selection, or model training.

Definition of spontaneous echo contrast

SEC was assessed during TEE and defined as dynamic, smoke-like echoes with characteristic swirling motion within the left atrium or LAA, distinct from artifact or background noise [20]. SEC severity was graded from 0 to 4 according to the classification proposed by Fatkin et al., in which grade 0 indicates no SEC, grades 1–2 represent mild to mild-to-moderate SEC, and grades 3–4 represent moderate to severe Sect [21]. (Supplement Fig. 1). SEC assessment and grading were performed independently by experienced echocardiographers who were blinded to laboratory and metabolic data.

For the purposes of prediction model development, patients were subsequently classified into a binary outcome according to previous studies: grades 0–2 were defined as SEC-negative, whereas grades 3–4 were defined as SEC-positive, reflecting the established association of moderate-to-severe SEC with increased thromboembolic risk [7]. This dichotomization was performed to distinguish clinically significant SEC from absent or minimal echogenic changes and to facilitate multivariable logistic regression modeling [22].

Echocardiographic assessment

All patients underwent comprehensive transthoracic echocardiography followed by TEE using standardized protocols. Left atrial appendage blood flow velocity was measured by pulsed-wave Doppler at the LAA orifice, and the average of multiple cardiac cycles was recorded. Left atrial diameter, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), and the severity of mitral and tricuspid regurgitation were assessed according to current echocardiographic guidelines [23].

Blood sample collection

Left atrial appendage (LAA) blood samples were obtained immediately after successful transseptal puncture and before the administration of systemic heparin during the procedure. Blood samples were collected anaerobically using blood gas syringes and analyzed immediately after collection using the same blood gas analyzer in both the internal and external cohorts. Lactate, glucose, and ionized calcium concentrations were measured directly by the blood gas analyzer according to the manufacturer’s standard protocol, thereby minimizing pre-analytical variation related to sample handling or processing. Patients received routine oral anticoagulation before the procedure according to contemporary atrial fibrillation management guidelines. The peri-procedural anticoagulation strategy was identical for both cohorts.

LAA blood gas and metabolic measurements

During TEE, blood samples were obtained directly from the left atrial appendage under sterile conditions prior to any contrast or intervention. LAA blood gas parameters, including pH, partial pressure of oxygen (PO₂), partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PCO₂), bicarbonate, total carbon dioxide, and oxygen saturation, were measured immediately using a blood gas analyzer. Metabolic indicators, including lactic acid, glucose, calcium, sodium, and potassium concentrations, were analyzed using standard laboratory methods.

Clinical variables and risk scores

Baseline demographic characteristics, comorbidities, laboratory parameters, and medication use were extracted from electronic medical records. Atrial fibrillation type was classified as paroxysmal or persistent according to established definitions. Thromboembolic and bleeding risk were assessed using the CHA₂DS₂-VASc and HAS-BLED scores, respectively.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables with a normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation, while non-normal data were presented as median (interquartile range). The chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used to analyze categorical variables, while Welch’s two-sample t-test or the rank-sum test was used to examine continuous variables. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) logistic regression analysis was performed using the glmnet package in R for variable selection. Continuous variables were automatically standardized using the default settings of the glmnet package prior to model fitting to account for differences in measurement scales. The optimal penalty parameter (λ) was determined using 10-fold cross-validation. Variables retained by the LASSO procedure were subsequently entered into a multivariable logistic regression model for prediction model development and nomogram construction. Variable retention was based on predictive contribution identified by LASSO rather than statistical significance alone. The performance of the model was assessed using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and calibration curve, with the area under the ROC curve (AUC) ranging from 0.5 (no discrimination) to 1 (complete discrimination). Internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling to calculate a corrected concordance index (c-index). A calibration curve was plotted to assess the agreement between predicted probabilities and observed outcomes. A decision curve analysis (DCA) was also performed to determine the net benefit across prediction thresholds. Results with a p-value < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.2).

Results

Study population and baseline characteristics

A total of 272 patients with atrial fibrillation constituted the internal development cohort, among whom 96 patients (35.3%) were diagnosed with spontaneous echo contrast (SEC). Among patients classified as having SEC, 87 (90.6%) had grade 3 s and 9 (9.4%) had grade 4 s. Baseline characteristics stratified by SEC status are summarized in Table 1. Patients with SEC were significantly older than those without Sect. (73 ± 8 vs. 69 ± 10 years, p < 0.001). Marked differences were observed in echocardiographic parameters: the SEC group exhibited substantially lower LAA blood flow velocity (25 ± 8 vs. 39 ± 16 cm/s, p < 0.001) and a larger left atrial diameter (43 ± 11 vs. 39 ± 9 mm, p = 0.002). Persistent atrial fibrillation was significantly more prevalent among patients with Sect. (72.9% vs. 33.5%, p < 0.001). Regarding hematological and metabolic parameters, patients with SEC had significantly lower platelet counts (180 ± 61 vs. 197 ± 57 × 10⁹/L, p = 0.030). Pronounced differences were also observed in LAA metabolic indicators: LAA lactic acid levels were significantly higher in the SEC group (1.80 ± 0.75 vs. 1.35 ± 0.52 mmol/L, p < 0.001), whereas LAA glucose (6.13 ± 1.17 vs. 6.58 ± 1.65 mmol/L, p = 0.015) and LAA calcium levels (1.12 ± 0.06 vs. 1.15 ± 0.07 mmol/L, p < 0.001) were significantly lower. In addition, patients with SEC had higher CHA₂DS₂-VASc (3.78 ± 1.70 vs. 3.33 ± 1.60, p = 0.034) and HAS-BLED scores (2.17 ± 0.96 vs. 1.85 ± 0.93, p = 0.008), as well as a higher prevalence of moderate or severe mitral and tricuspid regurgitation (both p < 0.001). No significant differences were observed in sex, body mass index, most laboratory parameters, comorbidities, or medication use.

Comparison between internal and external cohorts

The external validation cohort consisted of 77 patients, including 29 patients (37.7%) classified as SEC-positive and 48 patients (62.3%) classified as SEC-negative according to the same TEE-based grading criteria used in the internal cohort. Baseline characteristics of the internal (n = 272) and external validation cohorts (n = 77) are presented in Supplement Table 1. Compared with the internal cohort, patients in the external cohort were younger and exhibited significantly higher LAA blood flow velocity. Differences were also observed in several LAA blood gas and metabolic parameters, reflecting inter-cohort heterogeneity. The distribution of atrial fibrillation type and key clinical characteristics was otherwise comparable, supporting the use of the external cohort for independent validation.

Feature selection using LASSO regression

LASSO regression with 10-fold cross-validation was applied in the internal cohort to identify the most informative predictors. At the optimal penalization parameter (λ = 0.0595), eight variables with non-zero coefficients were retained: age, LAA blood flow velocity, platelet count, LAA lactic acid, LAA glucose, LAA calcium, atrial fibrillation type, and tricuspid regurgitation. (Fig. 1)

Predictive value of individual variables

ROC curve analysis was performed to evaluate the discriminative ability of individual predictors (Fig. 2). LAA blood flow velocity demonstrated the highest discriminative performance (AUC 0.817, 95% CI 0.762–0.872), followed by atrial fibrillation type (AUC 0.707, 95% CI 0.648–0.767) and LAA lactic acid (AUC 0.693, 95% CI 0.619–0.768). Tricuspid regurgitation also showed moderate discriminative ability (AUC 0.671, 95% CI 0.603–0.739). Age (AUC 0.606, 95% CI 0.532–0.681), platelet count (AUC 0.603, 95% CI 0.528–0.677), LAA calcium (AUC 0.625, 95% CI 0.553–0.697), and LAA glucose (AUC 0.556, 95% CI 0.482–0.630) demonstrated modest but statistically significant discriminative ability.

Univariate logistic regression analysis

Univariate logistic regression analyses were conducted to assess the association between each candidate variable and SEC (Table 2). Increasing age was associated with a higher likelihood of SEC (OR 1.05 per year, 95% CI 1.01–1.08, p = 0.005), while LAA blood flow velocity was inversely associated with SEC risk (OR 0.88 per cm/s, 95% CI 0.84–0.91, p < 0.001). Lower platelet count was statistically associated with an increased risk of SEC (OR 0.99 per 10⁹/L, 95% CI 0.99–1.00, p = 0.004), although the effect size per unit increase was small and should be interpreted cautiously. Among LAA metabolic parameters, lower calcium levels (OR 0.00, 95% CI 0.00–0.03, p < 0.001) and glucose levels (OR 0.81, 95% CI 0.67–0.98, p = 0.033), as well as higher lactic acid levels (OR 3.30, 95% CI 2.04–5.33, p < 0.001), were significantly associated with SEC. Persistent atrial fibrillation was associated with a nearly six-fold increased risk of SEC compared with paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (OR 5.90, 95% CI 3.28–10.61, p < 0.001). In addition, moderate and severe tricuspid regurgitation were significantly associated with increased SEC risk.

Multivariable logistic regression and nomogram construction

Variables selected by LASSO regression were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model (Table 3). After adjustment, age, LAA blood flow velocity, LAA calcium, LAA glucose, LAA lactic acid, and persistent atrial fibrillation remained independently associated with SEC, whereas platelet count and tricuspid regurgitation did not retain independent significance. Specifically, older age (OR 1.05 per year, 95% CI 1.00–1.10, p = 0.030) and persistent atrial fibrillation (OR 3.98, 95% CI 1.81–8.74, p < 0.001) were independently associated with an increased risk of SEC. Higher LAA blood flow velocity (OR 0.91 per cm/s, 95% CI 0.87–0.95, p < 0.001), calcium (OR 0.00, 95% CI 0.00–0.22, p = 0.015), and glucose levels (OR 0.71, 95% CI 0.55–0.92, p = 0.010) were protective, whereas elevated LAA lactic acid levels markedly increased SEC risk (OR 3.09, 95% CI 1.62–5.86, p < 0.001). A nomogram incorporating these independent predictors was constructed to facilitate individualized risk estimation (Fig. 3).

Model discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility

The prediction model demonstrated excellent discrimination in the internal cohort, with an AUC of 0.895 (95% CI 0.852–0.939), and maintained robust performance in the external validation cohort, with an AUC of 0.947 (95% CI 0.893–1.000) (Fig. 4). Decision curve analysis demonstrated a positive net clinical benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities in both the internal and external cohorts, indicating potential clinical usefulness of the model (Fig. 5). Calibration analysis demonstrated excellent agreement between predicted and observed probabilities in the internal cohort. Calibration in the external validation cohort was less satisfactory, particularly in the lower predicted probability range, which may reflect the relatively small sample size of the external cohort (Fig. 6).

Discussion

In this study, we developed and externally validated a multivariable prediction model for SEC in patients with atrial fibrillation by integrating clinical characteristics, echocardiographic parameters, and LAA metabolic indicators. The model demonstrated excellent discriminative performance in both the internal and external cohorts. Beyond predictive performance, our findings reveal a robust association between local LAA metabolic profiles—particularly lactic acid levels—and the presence of SEC, providing novel insights into local blood metabolic characteristics associated with SEC. The binary classification adopted in the present study was based on previously published clinical studies demonstrating that moderate-to-severe SEC (grades 3–4), rather than mild SEC, is associated with substantially higher thromboembolic risk. Therefore, this classification was considered appropriate for prediction model development.

Association between LAA lactate and SEC

Among the metabolic parameters examined, LAA lactic acid emerged as one of the strongest independent correlates of SEC. Lactate is widely used as an indicator of metabolic stress and is often interpreted in the context of anaerobic metabolism and/or impaired tissue perfusion, although its specificity for tissue hypoxia is limited and context-dependent [24–26]. In atrial fibrillation, impaired LAA mechanical function and reduced LAA emptying velocity are well-established features and are strongly associated with blood stasis and SEC formation [21, 27–29]. Elevated lactate levels within the LAA may therefore reflect local blood metabolic alterations associated with diminished washout and altered flow-related conditions rather than serving as a direct pathogenic factor.

Importantly, the observed association between LAA lactate and SEC does not imply a causal role of lactate in SEC formation. This interpretation is supported by the strong inverse relationship between LAA blood flow velocity and SEC observed in the present study and is consistent with prior echocardiographic studies linking low-flow states within the LAA to Sects [21, 27]. Emerging experimental evidence suggests that lactate also functions as a signaling metabolite involved in endothelial biology and thrombo-inflammatory pathways [30]; however, whether lactate directly contributes to SEC pathogenesis in atrial fibrillation remains to be established.

It should be emphasized that the metabolic parameters evaluated in the present study were measured in blood samples obtained from the LAA rather than from atrial tissue specimens. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted as reflecting local blood metabolic characteristics within the LAA rather than direct evidence of tissue-level metabolic alterations. Further mechanistic studies are required to determine whether the observed associations are linked to underlying changes in atrial metabolism, endothelial function, or other biological processes involved in SEC formation.

Complementary roles of LAA glucose and calcium

In addition to lactate, lower LAA glucose and calcium levels were independently associated with SEC. Glucose availability plays a fundamental role in cellular metabolism and vascular homeostasis. While systemic hyperglycemia has been shown to promote endothelial dysfunction and prothrombotic states [31, 32], local glucose concentrations within the LAA may reflect site-specific substrate delivery and metabolic balance under low-flow conditions. The association observed in the present study therefore suggests altered local blood metabolic characteristics rather than a systemic metabolic abnormality.

Calcium is a critical mediator of platelet activation, coagulation cascades, and cellular signaling during hemostasis and thrombosis [19, 33]. Altered local calcium homeostasis may accompany changes in blood composition and platelet–endocardial interactions within regions of blood stasis. Although direct clinical evidence linking LAA calcium levels to SEC is currently lacking, our findings indicate that calcium may contribute to a composite metabolic signature associated with SEC presence. These interpretations should be regarded as hypothesis-generating and require further mechanistic investigation.

Integration of metabolic and hemodynamic information

Our results suggest that LAA metabolic parameters provide complementary information to traditional echocardiographic markers. LAA blood flow velocity remains a cornerstone predictor of SEC; however, the persistence of lactate, glucose, and calcium as independent variables in multivariable analysis indicates that metabolic indicators may capture additional aspects of local blood metabolic status within the LAA that are not fully reflected by hemodynamic measures alone. This integrated perspective aligns with the concept that SEC formation is a multifactorial process arising from the interplay between blood stasis, atrial remodeling, and local blood–endocardial interactions [33].

By incorporating metabolic indicators into a prediction model, our study advances beyond purely flow-based assessment and offers a more comprehensive characterization of SEC-associated conditions. Although routine measurement of LAA metabolic parameters is not currently standard practice, these findings provide a rationale for further investigation into their potential role in refined risk stratification.

Interestingly, the model achieved a slightly higher AUC in the external validation cohort than in the internal development cohort. Although external validation cohorts often demonstrate lower discriminative performance, the confidence intervals of the two AUC estimates overlapped substantially, suggesting that the observed difference may be attributable to sampling variability. Moreover, the external validation cohort was relatively small (n = 77), which may have resulted in less stable estimates of model performance. Therefore, the higher AUC observed in the external cohort should be interpreted cautiously and does not necessarily indicate superior model performance.

Although the model demonstrated excellent discrimination in the external validation cohort, calibration performance was less satisfactory than that observed in the internal cohort. This discrepancy is likely attributable, at least in part, to the relatively small external validation cohort, which may have resulted in unstable estimation of predicted probabilities. Therefore, further validation in larger independent cohorts is warranted.

Clinical and research implications

From a clinical perspective, the present findings support the potential value of incorporating LAA metabolic parameters into SEC risk assessment in selected patients undergoing transesophageal echocardiography and catheter-based procedures. Because LAA blood sampling can be conveniently performed during radiofrequency catheter ablation or left atrial appendage occlusion procedures, the proposed model may provide additional risk stratification information in these specific clinical settings. Rather than serving as a general screening tool for all patients with atrial fibrillation, the model is intended to complement conventional clinical and echocardiographic assessment in patients undergoing invasive evaluation of the LAA. Future prospective studies are warranted to determine whether the incorporation of LAA metabolic parameters can improve clinical decision-making and thromboembolic risk assessment.

Conclusion

In summary, this study demonstrates that local metabolic characteristics of the left atrial appendage, particularly elevated lactic acid levels, are independently associated with the presence of spontaneous echo contrast in patients with atrial fibrillation. Alongside established clinical and echocardiographic predictors, LAA glucose and calcium levels further reflect a distinct metabolic microenvironment linked to SEC. By integrating these metabolic indicators into a multivariable model, we developed and externally validated a prediction tool with excellent discriminative performance. Although causality cannot be inferred, our findings suggest that LAA metabolic profiles may serve as complementary markers to hemodynamic assessment for identifying patients at increased risk of SEC. These findings warrant further prospective validation and mechanistic investigation.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. As a retrospective observational analysis, causal relationships between left atrial appendage metabolic parameters and spontaneous echo contrast cannot be established. Metabolic and blood gas measurements were obtained at a single time point, which may not reflect dynamic changes in the LAA microenvironment over time. Other potentially relevant metabolic or inflammatory markers were not evaluated, and the single-center design may restrict generalizability. In addition, although an independent external validation cohort was included, the sample size of the validation cohort was relatively modest, which may have resulted in less stable estimates of model performance and wider confidence intervals. Prospective, multicenter studies with serial metabolic assessments are warranted to further validate these findings and clarify their clinical implications. Therefore, larger multicenter external validation studies are warranted before widespread clinical application.

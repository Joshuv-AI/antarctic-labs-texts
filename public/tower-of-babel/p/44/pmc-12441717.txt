Identification of novel umami peptides from truffle and their effects on umami perception when used in combination with MSG or IMP
Authors: Min Fan, Minbo Li, Yongshuai Ma, Tao Wang, Shengbao Cai, Ming Du, Dafeng Sun, Sakamon Devahastin, Junjie Yi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWhile much research has focused on volatile compounds in truffle, little attention has been given to its umami, an important flavor component. Here, novel umami peptides derived from truffle were screened, and their taste mechanisms were investigated using virtual screening, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics simulations. Sensory evaluation and electronic tongue experiments were performed to assess their taste characteristics. Additionally, molecular docking simulations were carried out to examine umami perception when selected umami peptides were combined with monosodium glutamate (MSG) or inosine monophosphate (IMP). Eleven peptides were identified as potential umami enhancers in truffles. Among these peptides, DPTEGLRSP exhibited the lowest binding energy (−9.9 kcal/mol) toward the T1R1/T1R3 umami receptor, whereas VDAY showed the highest (−6.7 kcal/mol). Both peptides were found to stably bind to the receptor during molecular dynamics simulations. The umami threshold of DPTEGLRSP was assessed to be lower (0.230 mmol/L). Furthermore, stronger umami perception was observed when IMP was combined with DPTEGLRSP and when MSG was paired with VDAY, indicating distinct synergistic patterns for different peptide–enhancer combinations.Keywords: Electronic tongue, Enzymatic hydrolysis, Molecular docking simulation, Sensory evaluation, Synthetic peptides, UPLC-ESI-HRMS/MS

Introduction

Truffle (Tuber melanosporum), together with caviar and foie gras, are known as the three food delicacy kings (Lee et al., 2020). The unique and complex flavor of truffle is its most sought-after characteristic, which is influenced by various factors, including soil composition, climate, and growing conditions+ (Patel et al., 2017). While extensive research has been dedicated to the study on compositions of truffle flavor compounds, an important role played by umami substances on flavor characteristics of truffle is lacking (Piatti et al., 2024). Note that umami substances are derived from amino acids, nucleotides, polysaccharides and umami peptides. The latter not only serves as natural umami substances, but also exhibits such functional activities as antioxidant activity (Zhang et al., 2024), Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) inhibitory activity (Liang et al., 2024) and hypotensive activity (Zhang et al., 2023). With the growing demand for healthier diets, there is a growing interest in umami peptides that come directly from food (Gao et al., 2021; Gu et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024).

Conventional identification of umami-active peptides has long relied on intensive analytical workflows encompassing multi-step physicochemical separations such as membrane ultrafiltration, size-exclusion chromatography, and reversed-phase liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) (Kong et al., 2019). The paradigm is shifting with computational breakthroughs, as evidenced by novel bioinformatics platforms like BIOPEP-UWM for peptide activity prediction (Iwaniak et al., 2024), iUmami-SCM employing scoring matrix algorithms (Charoenkwan et al., 2020), deep learning-based Umami-MRNN (Qi et al., 2023), and feature-optimized UMPred-FRL (Charoenkwan et al., 2021). These in silico methods enable high-throughput characterization of umami peptides with enhanced efficiency. Complementarily, structure-function analyses benefit from computational biochemistry tools: molecular docking elucidates binding geometries at taste receptor interfaces, which has indeed played a significant role in the research on food umami perception (Chang et al., 2023). Molecular dynamics simulation is yet another tool for analyzing and verifying the binding stability between a ligand and a receptor; this simulation technique can significantly reduce false-positive results that can result from molecular docking simulation (Zhao et al., 2024). Integration of these computational strategies establishes a robust framework for umami peptide discovery, overcoming the limitations of conventional biochemical approaches.

Current scientific consensus holds that umami perception originates from ligand-receptor interactions between taste-active compounds and specialized chemosensory proteins located on gustatory epithelial cells, triggering neural transduction cascades to the central nervous system (Wu et al., 2022; Meng et al., 2024). The gustatory perception of umami is mediated by class C G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs), which comprise several types: (1) the T1R1/T1R3 heterodimer, (2) metabotropic glutamate receptor isoforms (mGluR1 and mGluR4) expressed in both neural and gustatory tissues, and (3) putative umami-sensitive receptors such as calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR), GPRC6A, and LPAR5 (formerly termed GPR92) (Zhang et al., 2019). Notably, the T1R1/T1R3 heteromer demonstrates unique dual-affinity for both glutamate and peptide ligands (Dang et al., 2019), making it the primary target for computational screening approaches (Song et al., 2023; Jia et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2024). Current investigations employing organoleptic assessment have demonstrated synergistic umami potentiation through combinatorial applications of umami peptides with classical flavor enhancers such as monosodium glutamate (MSG) and 5′-inosinate (IMP) (Zhang et al., 2019; Song et al., 2023). However, the structural basis underlying these cooperative interactions at receptor level remains largely uncharacterized. This knowledge gap motivates our proposed computational framework to investigate ternary complex formation (peptide-MSG/IMP-receptor) through molecular docking.

This study established an integrated workflow for characterizing umami peptides in truffle through: (1) combinatorial bio-enzymatic release coupled with UPLC-ESI-HRMS/MS for peptide profiling, (2) computational structural biology approaches (docking simulations and MD trajectories) to decipher receptor activation mechanisms, and (3) multimodal sensory validation employing both human panel assessments and biomimetic taste sensor systems. Furthermore, the study elucidated umami effects between truffle-derived peptides and classical umami enhancers (MSG/IMP) through molecular docking.

Materials and methods

Materials and chemicals

Fresh truffle (Tuber melanosporum) (3–5 cm diameter) were sourced from a Kunming local market (Yunnan, China) and immediately transferred to the laboratory under controlled refrigeration (4 °C) with aluminum foil packaging. Enzymatic reagents including cellulase (Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Co., Ltd., China), along with neutral protease and flavor protease (Wuxi Yizhu Biotech, China), were acquired through certified suppliers. Synthetic peptides DPTEGLRSP and VDAY (>95 % purity, Nanjing Peptide Valley Biotech, China) were custom-synthesized using solid-phase methodology. LC-MS grade solvents (acetonitrile/formic acid) originated from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany), with all auxiliary chemicals meeting analytical reagent specifications.

Enzymatic hydrolysis

In order to maximize the extraction of umami peptides, a slightly modified method was used to first hydrolyze the truffle (Gao et al., 2021). The processing procedure is as follows: (1) Clean the dirt on the surface of the sample with ultrapure water. (2) The sample was then sliced, and water was added at a ratio of four times the mass of the truffle. (3) Stir the mixture for 10 min to break the cell walls and obtain the black truffle solution. (4) This solution underwent sequential enzymatic treatments to produce truffle enzymatic hydrolysates: (i) cellulase (2.35 %, 60 °C, pH5, 2 h), (ii) neutral protease (0.75 %, 55 °C, pH7, 4 h), and (iii) flavor protease (4.60 %, 55 °C, pH7, 1 h). (5) Enzymatic activities were irreversibly denatured through thermal treatment (95 °C, 15 min), with subsequent temperature equilibration to 25 ± 1 °C prior to cryogenic storage at −40 °C for downstream analytical procedures.

Identification of peptides by UPLC-ESI-HRMS/MS

The peptide profiles of truffle enzymatic hydrolysates were determined using a high-resolution Q-Exactive Orbitrap mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) interfaced with an Ultimate 3000 UPLC system (Thermo Fisher Scientific), following optimized parameters (Yu et al., 2023). C18 column (2.1 × 100 mm, 2.7 μm; InfinityLab Poroshell 120, Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA) at 30 °C was used. Chromatography was performed using 0.1 % formic acid (aqueous) and acetonitrile (organic) mobile phases at 0.2 mL/min flow rate with 3 μL injections. The gradient profile progressed from 5 % organic (0–3 min) through sequential increases to 10 % (3–6 min), 20 % (6–15 min, maintained until 17 min), then to 95 % (17–30 min) before resetting to starting conditions (30–45 min). Mass spectrometric analysis was conducted in positive electrospray ionization mode with operational parameters set to 3.2 kV spray voltage and 350 °C for both capillary and drying temperatures. Data acquisition employed a data-dependent tandem MS strategy with full scan resolution at 70,000 and product ion scans at 35,000 across a mass range of 100-1000 Da. Subsequent data processing and peptide identification were performed using Peaks Studio 8.0 (Bioinformatics Solutions Inc., Waterloo, ON, Canada).

Screening and prediction of potential umami peptides

In order to screen umami peptide fragments, four umami activity prediction methods, i.e., BIOPEP-UWM (https://biochemia.uwm.edu.pl/biopep-uwm; accessed May 24, 2024), iUmami-SCM (https://camt.pythonanywhere.com/iUmami-SCM; accessed June 10, 2024), Umami-MRNN (https://umami-mrnn.herokuapp.com; accessed June 12, 2024) and UMPred-FRL (http://pmlabstack.pythonanywhere.com/UMPred-FRL; accessed June 15, 2024) (Gu et al., 2024) were used. Then, considering the availability of subsequent peptides, the screened peptides were subject to toxicity prediction using ADMETlab 2.0 (https://admetmesh.scbdd.com; accessed June 18, 2024) and water solubility prediction using Innovagen (https://www.innovagen.com/proteomics-tools; accessed June 20, 2024) (Zhao et al., 2024).

Modelling of the umami receptor T1R1/T1R3 and potential umami peptides

The T1R1/T1R3 heterodimer, a class C G protein-coupled receptor complex, served as the primary recognition receptor for umami peptide interactions. The umami receptor model was derived from previous laboratory studies (Yu et al., 2023). The sequence alignment via the NCBI BLAST tool revealed that the human T1R1/T1R3 receptor shares approximately 37 % sequence identity with the corresponding subunits of the fish receptor, supporting the suitability of T1R2a-T1R3 as a reasonable homology template (Song et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2022a; Dong et al., 2023). In our previous work, the T1R1/T1R3 model was constructed by generating 100 candidate models, selecting the one with the lowest DOPE score, and refining it using a 10-ns molecular dynamics simulation. The Ramachandran plot, generated via PROCHECK, showed that 77.8 % of residues were located in the most favored regions, 20.2 % in additionally allowed regions, 1.2 % in generously allowed regions, and only 0.8 % (12 residues) were in disallowed regions. This distribution indicates good stereochemical quality of the model. In addition, the resulting structure was further validated through docking with classical umami peptide (KGDEESLA) to ensure functional plausibility. Peptide modellings were performed using the YINFO TECHNOLOGY platform (https://cloud.yinfotek.com/console/tools; accessed May 8, 2024).

Molecular docking simulation

Umami peptides and T1R1/T1R3

Initial ligand optimization was conducted with Avogadro software (version 1.2.0) employing the GAFF force field parameters (Song et al., 2023). Subsequent structural refinements included incorporation of non-polar hydrogen atoms and Gaussian charge distributions through Open Babel (version 2.4.1), with final outputs converted to PDBQT format. Receptor preparation involved similar charge and hydrogen adjustments using AutoDockTools (ADT, version 1.5.6), followed by format conversion. The docking pocket was defined to encompass the entire receptor structure, centered at (43.5, 58.8, 42.4) with dimensions of 33.3 × 35.9 × 48.0 Å, ensuring full coverage of potential ligand-binding regions. The molecular systems were then subjected to automated docking simulations with AutoDock Vina (Li et al., 2024a) (version 1.2.0), with resultant complexes visualized and analyzed through PyMol (version 2.4.0) and BIOVIA Discovery Studio 2016 for detailed interaction between the umami ligands and T1R1/T1R3 binding domains.

Umami peptides and T1R1/T1R3-MSG/T1R1/T1R3-IMP

Molecular structures of monosodium glutamate (MSG) and inosine monophosphate (IMP) were retrieved from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (https://www.rcsb.org/stats; accessed October 28, 2024) and subsequently complexed with the T1R1/T1R3 heterodimer through molecular docking. These complexes viz. T1R1/T1R3-MSG and T1R1/T1R3-IMP were then docked with the validated umami peptides (Xu et al., 2024; Dang et al., 2019; Huang et al., 2025). The docking results between ternary complexes were analyzed using the BIOVIA Discovery Studio 2016 Client.

Molecular dynamics simulation

The ligand-receptor complexes comprising umami peptides bound to T1R1/T1R3 underwent 100-ns all-atom molecular dynamics simulations implemented in GROMACS 19.5 with the amber FF99SB-ILDN force field (Zhao et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024b). After transferable intermolecular potential 3-point (TIP3P) water solvation and ionic neutralization, the systems were energy-minimized (steepest descent, Fmax<1000 kJ/(mol·nm)) and equilibrated through NVT (2 ns, V-rescale) and NPT (1 ns, Parrinello-Rahman) phases before production runs at 310.15 K/1 bar. Conformational metrics (RMSD, Rg, H-bond dynamics, SASA) were extracted from trajectories for stability assessment.

Synthetic peptides

The candidate peptides were custom-synthesized via solid-phase peptide synthesis by Nanjing Peptide Valley Biotechnology Co., Ltd., achieving >95 % purity as verified by HPLC, with subsequent cryopreservation at −80 °C prior to analysis. Organoleptic properties were systematically evaluated through human sensory panels complemented by biomimetic taste sensor (electronic tongue) assessments.

Sensory evaluation

The sensory assessment protocol adhered to ISO 8586:2023 guidelines for panelist selection and training. Sixteen trained evaluators (21–29 years, gender-balanced) from Kunming University of Science and Technology, all with demonstrated sensory testing competency and fundamental taste discrimination skills, participated after providing written informed consent.

Taste description

Reference solutions were prepared at specified concentrations: 0.35 % MSG (umami), 1 % sucrose (sweet), 0.25 % caffeine (bitter), 0.08 % citric acid (sour), and 0.35 % NaCl (salty). Panelists quantitatively assessed peptide solutions using a 10-point intensity scale (0 = undetectable, 10 = extremely strong) following established protocols (Zhang et al., 2022).

Umami threshold determination

The synthetic peptide solutions (initial 2 mmol/L in ultrapure water) underwent serial 1:1 dilutions for three-alternative forced (3-AFC) choice testing. Each trial presented two water controls and one peptide sample (randomly coded). The recognition threshold was defined as the geometric mean concentration between the last detectable dilution and first undetectable dilution, following standard psychophysical methods (Chen et al., 2023).

Electronic tongue

To systematically evaluate the influence of umami peptides and different umami substances in umami perception, aqueous solutions containing 1 mmol/L synthetic peptides were prepared either individually or in combination with equimolar concentrations of MSG or IMP (Dang et al., 2019). Comparative taste profiling was performed using an Insent TS-5000Z electronic tongue system (Intelligent Sensor Technology, Japan), with sensor responses quantified for eight gustatory attributes (umami, sourness, saltiness, bitterness, astringency, aftertaste intensity (A/B), and richness). All measurements were conducted against a reference solution (0.3 mmol/L tartaric acid + 30 mmol/L KCl) with triplicate replicates per sample.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted with Origin software (Origin, 2021; Northampton, MA, USA) for data processing. Experimental results were reported as arithmetic mean ± SD derived from triplicate measurements. To determine intergroup significance, Duncan's multiple comparison test was implemented through the SPSS platform (version 26.0, IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) with α-level set at 0.05 for hypothesis testing.

Results and discussion

Extraction, identification and screening of umami peptides

Common techniques for extracting umami peptides encompass solvent extraction, hydrolysis, microbial fermentation, solid-phase synthesis and enzymatic hydrolysis (Gao et al., 2024). Given the ease of control and mild reaction conditions associated with enzymatic hydrolysis, this method was chosen in the present study. Prior research has indicated that use of multiple enzymes could yield more pronounced umami effects than using a single enzyme (Gao et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2023). The present study therefore employed three enzymes for stepwise enzymatic hydrolysis to produce black truffle enzymatic hydrolysates.

Chromatographic-mass spectrometric characterization (UPLC-ESI-HRMS/MS) coupled with Peaks Studio 8.0 bioinformatics analysis (Yu et al., 2023) identified 85 putative peptides (Table S1) exhibiting >80 % average local confidence (ALC) scores, with molecular masses distributed between 400 and 2100 Da and chain lengths spanning 4–19 residues. Short-chain peptides, due to their smaller molecular size and greater accessibility to umami receptor, are generally considered to possess higher umami potential (Fu et al., 2025). Consequently, the present investigation specifically targeted oligopeptides comprising 4–9 amino acid residues for detailed analysis.

In addition, it is generally believed that umami peptides usually contain umami amino acids residues, including glutamic acid (E), glutamine (Q), aspartic acid (D) or asparagine (N) (Zhang et al., 2017; An et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2024). Other amino acids, such as proline (P), valine (V), histidine (H), isoleucine (I), and leucine (L), may also play a positive role in the production of umami (Kong et al., 2019; Fischer et al., 2021). Therefore, peptides containing these amino acids were further screened. In order to screen umami peptides more effectively, four umami prediction models, including BIOPEP-UWM, iUmami-SCM, Umami-MRNN and UMPred-FRL were selected for comprehensive prediction analysis. A peptide would be included in subsequent screening only when such a peptide was determined to have umami properties as per all these four prediction models (Gu et al., 2024).

In view of the potential commercialization requirements, the screened umami peptides were also evaluated for water solubility and toxicity. As shown in Table 1, after rigorous screening and evaluation, 11 peptides that exhibited both umami properties and good water solubility and without toxicity were eventually identified. These peptides not only meet the fundamental criteria for umami activity but also demonstrate favorable physicochemical and safety profiles, making them suitable for application in food systems. Derived from food protein hydrolysates, these representative peptides hold strong potential as natural flavor enhancers in sodium-reduced products, plant-based meat analogs, and clean-label formulations by enhancing umami perception, masking undesirable off-flavors, and aligning with modern consumer and regulatory demands.

Docking sites and interactions between umami peptides and T1R1/T1R3

Umami perception occurs when umami compounds bind to umami receptors located on the tongue's taste bud cells, sending signals to the brain (Zhang et al., 2017, 2019; Diepeveen et al., 2022). Extensive research indicates that human umami perception involves three receptor types: the heterodimeric T1R1/T1R3, mGluR1, and mGluR4, along with their variants (Zhang et al., 2019). Among these, the T1R1/T1R3 heterodimer is the most recognized receptor for umami peptides. However, due to the unresolved crystal structure of T1R1/T1R3, prior studies have used the fish sweet taste receptor (T1R2a-T1R3, PDB ID: 5X2M) as a template for homology modeling (Yu et al., 2023).

The binding affinity between a receptor and its ligand is commonly determined by the magnitude of the affinity energy value. It is generally agreed that when the affinity energy is below −6, receptor-ligand binding is feasible. A lower affinity energy value indicates a more stable complex (Gu et al., 2024). The results on the affinity energy values are given in Table 2: the affinity values of the 11 peptides screened for docking with T1R1/T1R3 ranged from −6.7 to −9.9, implying that all of the umami peptides were able to bind to T1R1/T1R3. Among them, DPTEGLRSP exhibited the lowest affinity value of −9.9 and was considered the most stable peptide for binding.

The Venus flytrap domain (VFTD) of T1R1/T1R3 has been identified as the primary active region for umami binding and is crucial for umami perception (Chang et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2019; Dang et al., 2019; Tang et al., 2024). As shown in Fig. 1, a 3D map of molecular docking of umami peptides and umami receptor shows that all 11 peptides indeed bind to T1R1/T1R3 in the VFTD region; this observation is consistent with those of previous studies (Chen et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2024). The types and quantities of interactions between the 11 peptides and T1R1/T1R3 were statistically analyzed, and the results are shown in Fig. 2(A). Hydrogen bonding, electrostatic and hydrophobic interactions were the main interactions between the umami peptides and T1R1/T1R3. Quantitatively, SPKERLKTL exhibited the strongest interactions with T1R1/T1R3, forming 9 hydrogen bonds, 8 electrostatic interactions and 9 hydrophobic interactions. According to the molecular docking results, these 11 peptides might be umami peptides. However, DPTEGLRSP was identified as the most stable binding peptide for T1R1/T1R3, whereas VDAY demonstrated the weakest binding stability.

Stability analysis of umami peptides-T1R1/T1R3 complexes

Molecular docking aims to assess the potential and likelihood of ligand-receptor binding, whereas molecular dynamics simulation goes further by simulating the ligand-receptor binding process and calculating the parameters and thus assessing its stability (Jia et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024a, 2024b; Qi et al., 2022). Given the significant computational resources required for extended MD simulations, this strategic selection of representative peptides from opposite ends of the affinity spectrum enabled us to conduct a comprehensive comparative analysis of binding stability while maintaining research efficiency. By examining these two extreme cases, we were able to gain valuable insights into the range of peptide-receptor interaction behaviors without compromising the study's scientific objectives. RMSD, SASA, Rg and the number of hydrogen bonding of the complex systems were calculated using GROMACS 19.5.

RMSD serves as a key indicator for assessing the equilibrium state of molecular dynamics systems (Huang et al., 2025). As depicted in Fig. 3(A), both DPTEGLRSP and VDAY showed considerable fluctuations during the first 20 ns, but after this period, their values stabilized, suggesting that the receptor-peptide complexes achieved equilibrium during the 100-ns simulation.

Molecular dynamics simulation results of DPTEGLRSP and VDAY with T1R1/T1R3.

Regarding SASA, which is an important structural parameter indicating the extent of conformational change during peptide binding (Zhao et al., 2024), Fig. 3(B) shows that the SASA values of both peptides decreased over time, with DPTEGLRSP exhibiting a more significant reduction than VDAY. A decrease in SASA implies an increase in hydrophobic interactions between non-polar residues and the development of the protein structure towards a more stable direction, suggesting that DPTEGLRSP might be more stable than VDAY.

Rg, which is the mean square radius of gyration, is used to describe the average radius of rotation of a molecule in space; this can in turn reflect the conformational stability of the molecule under different conditions (Jia et al., 2024). Generally, the smaller the fluctuation of Rg, the more stable the molecular structure is. Fig. 3(C) presents the Rg values of the receptor complexes with both peptides during the 100-ns simulation. The overall fluctuation range of the complexes of DPTEGLRSP with T1R1/T1R3 is within 0.2 nm, which is a smaller fluctuation range. In contrast, the complexes of VDAY with T1R1/T1R3 experienced a fluctuation range of 0.25 nm, which is slightly larger than that of DPTEGLRSP. After 60 ns, the basic fluctuation stability decreased, indicating that the structures of the complexes formed by DPTEGLRSP and VDAY with T1R1/T1R3 were gradually stabilizing.

Hydrogen bonding is the primary interaction between umami peptides and the T1R1/T1R3 receptor, playing a significant role in the overall stability of the complex (Yu et al., 2023). As shown in Fig. 3(D), the DPTEGLRSP-receptor complex exhibited 0–14 hydrogen bonds during the simulation, compared to 0–15 in the VDAY-receptor system. This indicates that the number of hydrogen bonds formed between VDAY and the receptor fluctuated slightly more than those formed by DPTEGLRSP. VDAY, as a shorter peptide, possesses fewer interaction sites and a more flexible backbone. Its compact size facilitates rapid conformational adjustments, particularly at terminal residues, resulting in transient variations in hydrogen bonding. These fluctuations likely represent dynamic shifts between alternative binding modes rather than instability.

In contrast, DPTEGLRSP, with its extended chain and multiple interaction residues, forms a more extensive and stable binding interface with the receptor. This leads to more consistent hydrogen bonding and reduced fluctuations throughout the simulation.

Although a 100-ns molecular dynamics simulation may be insufficient to fully capture long-term conformational dynamics, our results indicate that the peptide–receptor complexes reached equilibrium within this timeframe, supporting its adequacy for assessing initial structural stability and comparative binding behavior. To further enhance the robustness of these findings, replicate simulations were conducted, and the results—presented in the supplementary materials (Fig. S2)—demonstrate consistency with the primary simulation trajectories (Fig. 3). Based on the molecular dynamics simulation results of DPTEGLRSP (the most stable) and VDAY (the least stable), it can be assumed that all 11 peptides screened had the potential to form stable complexes with T1R1/T1R3.

Taste characteristics of synthetic peptides

To better reflect the taste characteristics of DPTEGLRSP and VDAY based on the results from computer simulations, the peptide sequences were sent to Nanjing Peptide Valley Biotechnology Co., Ltd., where they were synthesized using solid-phase synthesis. The synthesized peptides were subject to both sensory evaluation and electronic tongue; the results are shown in Fig. 4. As can be seen from the sensory evaluation results in Fig. 4(A), the umami intensity of DPTEGLRSP is greater than that of VDAY, which is consistent with our previous molecular docking results. It can also be observed that DPTEGLRSP has the taste characteristics of sourness > umami > saltiness > sweetness > bitterness. VDAY, on the other hand, has the taste attributes of umami > sourness > sweetness > saltiness > bitterness. The taste description revealed that both peptides exhibited a variety of taste characteristics, which may be related to the complexity of their molecular structure and chemical properties (Gu et al., 2024; Karl et al., 2020). As the pH values of both peptide solutions were approximately 5, no further pH adjustment was performed. The results show that both DPTEGLRSP and VDAY exhibit distinct umami and sourness characteristics. The sourness may have partially masked the umami perception and is likely attributable to the presence of acidic amino acid residues as well as residual acetic acid from the peptide synthesis process (Zhang et al., 2024).

Fig. 4(B) presents the electronic tongue results of the two peptides. VDAY stood out more in terms of bitterness and sourness, while DPTEGLRSP had a slight edge in terms of saltiness, aftertaste-A and astringency. Both peptides exhibited similar performance in terms of richness, aftertaste-B and umami. Consistent with the results of the sensory evaluation, DPTEGLRSP exhibited more umami than VDAY. Umami peptides have indeed often been noted to exhibit a variety of taste profiles that go beyond a single umami; such an observation was confirmed in the present study (Chang et al., 2023; Song et al., 2023).

The umami threshold results are shown in Table S2. The umami threshold of DPTEGLRSP was 0.230 mmol/L and that of VDAY was 1.125 mmol/L. Therefore, it is possible to assume that DPTEGLRSP exhibited higher umami potential than VDAY.

For comparative analysis of umami perception mechanisms, peptide solutions were prepared using three different solvents: ultrapure water, MSG solution, and IMP solution. Radar charts (Fig. 4(C)) and bar charts (Fig. 4(D) and (E)) are used to show the electronic tongue results. Given that IMP exhibits a lower umami threshold compared to MSG, it is logical to infer that IMP possesses a higher potential to enhance umami flavor when compared to MSG. However, this is not entirely true when MSG or IMP was used in combination with umami peptides. It is interesting to note that the umami tastes presented by DPTEGLRSP dissolved in three different solutions (ultrapure water, MSG, IMP) were significantly different; the umami taste presented by DPTEGLRSP dissolved in IMP was significantly stronger than those in the other two solutions. The umami tastes of VDAY dissolved in MSG and IMP exhibited no significant different, but was significantly stronger than that of VDAY dissolved in ultrapure water. These findings may result from differential interactions between umami peptides and MSG or IMP, indicating the intricate mechanisms through which such compounds influence taste perception (Zhang et al., 2019). In summary, compared with MSG, DPTEGLRSP exhibited a stronger umami taste when used in combination with IMP. VDAY exhibited similar level of umami taste, whether when paired with MSG or IMP.

Interactions between umami peptides and MSG or IMP

Some studies have revealed that umami peptides can interact with umami substances such as MSG and IMP to promote umami perception (Zhang et al., 2019; Song et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023). The sequential docking approach was employed as an initial strategy to explore potential ligand-ligand-receptor interaction mechanisms. Although this method cannot fully capture the complexity of allosteric or cooperative binding effects, it can provide useful preliminary insights for our study. Molecular docking simulation was used to investigate the changes of umami peptides and the umami receptor T1R1/T1R3 after the addition of MSG or IMP, as well as the effect on umami perception (Dang et al., 2019). As is seen in Table S3, the docking affinity energy of T1R1/T1R3-IMP with VDAY was lower than that of T1R1/T1R3 with VDAY. This indicates that the addition of IMP may led to increased affinity between VDAY and T1R1/T1R3 (Gu et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024). Although the other docking groups did not present higher affinity energies, the affinity energies of all groups were lower than −6, indicating that the umami peptides maintained relatively stable affinities with the umami receptor, irrespective of the addition of MSG or IMP (Zhang et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2023).

To further support the enhanced affinity observed in docking simulations, the number and type of molecular interactions were analyzed in detail. A 2D map of two umami peptides binding to different receptor combinations (T1R1/T1R3, T1R1/T1R3-MSG, T1R1/T1R3-IMP) are plotted. Analysis of the interactions depicted in Fig. 5 indicates that the umami peptides, when supplemented with MSG or IMP, exhibited the larger number of interactions with T1R1/T1R3 when compared with that without the addition of MSG and IMP.

Comparative analysis of interactions between T1R1/T1R3 and umami peptides with and without MSG or IMP. A1-A4 are based on results and statistical diagrams of docking with different receptors of DPTEGLRSP, while B1-B4 are based on results and statistical diagrams of docking with different receptors of VDAY.

According to Fig. 5(A4), the total number of interactions between T1R1/T1R3-IMP and DPTEGLRSP was the highest. This may imply that the presence of IMP significantly enhanced the binding ability of that umami peptide to the receptor. The interactions between T1R1/T1R3-MSG and DPTEGLRSP was slightly lower than those of T1R1/T1R3-IMP, but higher than those of T1R1/T1R3, suggesting that MSG also helped enhance the umami peptide-receptor interaction, but the effect was not as significant as that of IMP. The results are consistent with the conclusion of the electronic tongue experiment. Specifically, hydrogen bonds are very likely to dominate in all three receptors, especially in T1R1/T1R3-IMP, where the number of hydrogen bonds increases significantly. This suggests that hydrogen bonding played a key role in the interactions of DPTEGLRSP with the receptor, especially in the presence of IMP (Yao et al., 2024). The number of electrostatic interactions was similar in the cases of T1R1/T1R3 and T1R1/T1R3-MSG, but slightly increased in the case of T1R1/T1R3-IMP. This may indicate that the presence of IMP enhanced the electrostatic interactions and may thus enhance the umami peptide DPTEGLRSP binding to the receptor. The relatively low number of hydrophobic interactions in all three receptors suggests that hydrophobic interactions may not be the primary driver of DPTEGLRSP-receptor interactions. The analysis of umami peptide VDAY, as illustrated in Fig. 5(B4), reveals distinct interactions with T1R1/T1R3 when compared that of DPTEGLRSP. Unlike DPTEGLRSP, which exhibited significant increase in the interactions with T1R1/T1R3-IMP, VDAY did not exhibit marked enhancement in the binding affinity with IMP. Instead, the presence of MSG appeared to be more conducive to increasing the interactions between VDAY and T1R1/T1R3. Consistent with the findings for the peptide DPTEGLRSP, hydrogen bonding may play a key role in the interaction between VDAY and the receptor, whereas hydrophobic interactions are unlikely to be the primary driving force.

According to the molecular docking simulation results, it is evident that the interactions between DPTEGLRSP and T1R1/T1R3 can be enhanced by the addition of either MSG or IMP, with the effect of IMP being more pronounced. Such enhancement may be due to an increase in the hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions. In contrast, the interactions between VDAY and T1R1/T1R3 could be enhanced more markedly in the presence of MSG; the enhancement is mainly attributed to the increase in the hydrogen bonding. These results suggest that the interactions between umami peptides and receptors can be modulated by the addition of other umami substances to enhance the umami perception. In future work, ternary docking or advanced collaborative modeling is still needed to study the interactions among multi-component complexes more comprehensively.

Conclusion

This study characterized 85 peptides derived from truffle enzymatic hydrolysates, with 11 candidates demonstrating optimal umami characteristics, water solubility, and non-toxicity. Computational analysis suggests that these peptides may form stable interactions with the receptor through hydrogen bonding, electrostatic, and hydrophobic forces, potentially involving residues such as ASP147, ARG277, and GLN316. Comparative molecular dynamics simulations of high-affinity DPTEGLRSP (−9.9 kcal/mol) and low-affinity VDAY (−6.7 kcal/mol) complexes revealed stable interactions, correlating with sensory data showing pronounced umami intensity (thresholds: 0.230 vs 1.125 mmol/L, respectively). Importantly, IMP may enhance the interaction between DPTEGLRSP and the receptor by promoting a greater number of hydrogen bonds and electrostatic interactions. Similarly, MSG appears to increase the binding affinity of VDAY through the formation of supplementary hydrogen bonds. Future studies should explore the functional validation of umami peptide–receptor interactions using receptor activation assays or calcium imaging in heterologous cell systems expressing human T1R1/T1R3. Particular attention should be given to the main amino acid residues identified through molecular docking, prioritizing their roles in mediating receptor activation. In addition, optimization of peptide-enhancer combinations under various concentration ratios should be examined to understand their dose-dependent synergistic effects. Application of the identified peptides in real food matrices and assessment of their stability and sensory impact under processing conditions would also be valuable for potential industrial applications. This study provides valuable insights into the industrial application potential of truffle umami peptides as natural flavor enhancers.

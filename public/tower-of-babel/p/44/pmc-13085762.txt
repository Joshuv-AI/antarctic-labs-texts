Clinical efficacy of posterior cervical c3 laminectomy combined with c7 underlying decompression and laminoplasty with support titanium plate fixation for cervical spondylotic myelopathy
Authors: Yang Gao, Liang Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo evaluate the clinical outcomes of a modified technique combining C3 total laminectomy, C4–C6 open-door laminoplasty, and C7 partial laminectomy (undercutting) decompression with extensive open-door laminoplasty and titanium plate fixation in the treatment of cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM).Methods:A retrospective analysis was conducted on 101 CSM patients scheduled to undergo posterior cervical open-door laminoplasty. They were divided into a conventional laminoplasty (C-LAMP) group (n = 50), which received conventional C3-7 open-door laminoplasty with titanium plate fixation, and a modified laminoplasty (M-LAMP ) group (n = 51), which underwent C3 laminectomy, C4–C6 open-door laminoplasty,C7 undercutting decompression, and extensive laminoplasty with titanium plate fixation. The following parameters were compared: operation time, intraoperative blood loss, postoperative drainage volume, Japanese Orthopaedic Association (JOA) scores before and after surgery, JOA improvement rate, cervical curvature and range of motion (ROM) before and after surgery, loss of cervical curvature and ROM postoperatively, visual analog scale (VAS) scores for neck pain, incidence of axial symptoms, and other postoperative complications.ResultsAll surgeries were completed successfully. No significant differences were observed between the two groups in operation time, blood loss, postoperative drainage, length of hospital stay, JOA scores before and after surgery, JOA improvement rate, or overall complication rates (P > 0.05). At the 3-month follow-up, the incidence of axial symptoms was significantly lower in the M-LAMP than in the C-LAMP (5.88% [3/51] vs. 24.00% [12/50], P = 0.010). However, this difference was no longer statistically significant at 1 year (1.9% [1/51] vs. 8.0% [4/50], P = 0.884). Both groups showed some loss of cervical curvature and ROM after surgery. At 1 year, the loss of cervical curvature did not differ significantly between groups (study: -4.1±4.9 vs. control: -3.0±5.9; P = 0.329; 95% CI: [-3.2,1.1]). Although there was no significant difference in ROM at 1 year (34.6±10.1° vs. 32.9±9.0°; P = 0.396), the M-LAMP had significantly less ROM loss compared to the C-LAMP (-2.0±7.2° vs. 2.3±8.8°; P = 0.008; 95% CI:[-7.5,-1.1]).ConclusionThe M-LAMP—C3 total laminectomy, C4–C6 open-door laminoplasty, C7 partial laminectomy (undercutting) and extensive laminoplasty combined with titanium plate fixation—achieved similar improvements in neurological function compared to conventional open-door laminoplasty. However, by preserving the attachments of the semispinalis cervicis at C2, the cervical extensor muscles at C7, and the insertion of the nuchal ligament, this approach significantly reduced the incidence of early postoperative axial symptoms and better maintained postoperative cervical range of motion.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-026-09689-x.

Cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM) is a clinically prevalent and serious condition characterized by a prolonged and insidiously progressive course. It typically arises from cervical disc herniation or facet joint degeneration, leading to spinal cord compression and ischemia. Surgical intervention is recommended upon diagnosis to prevent neurological deterioration [1].extensive open-door laminoplasty (EOL) is a widely utilized posterior surgical approach for CSM. Compared to anterior procedures, EOL offers advantages including an extensive surgical field, relative technical simplicity, better preservation of postoperative cervical mobility, and the ability to decompress multiple spinal levels simultaneously [2, 3].However, the EOL technique necessitates extensive dissection of posterior cervical musculature, resection of spinous processes and associated ligaments, and alteration of native anatomy. These manipulations may lead to complications such as C5 nerve root palsy, biomechanical imbalance of posterior cervical muscles, axial symptoms (neck/shoulder pain), cervical stiffness, loss of cervical lordosis, and restricted flexion-extension range of motion [4].Recent studies emphasize the critical importance of preserving muscular insertions at C2 (specifically the semispinalis cervicis) and the extensor musculature attachments to the spinous processes for preventing postoperative loss of cervical lordosis and axial symptoms [5, 6]. Some researchers propose that performing a C3 laminectomy instead of C3 laminoplasty avoids disruption of the semispinalis cervicis insertion on the C2 spinous process during laminoplasty maneuvers [7].Building upon this rationale, this study enrolled CSM patients scheduled for posterior cervical EOL at our institution. The M-LAMP was applied by C3 laminectomy combined with C7 undercutting decompression (also termed “dome decompression”) and posterior extensive laminoplasty with supportive titanium plate fixation. This approach specifically aims to preserve the muscular insertions (semispinalis cervicis, cervical extensors) and ligamentous attachments (ligamentum nuchae) at the C2 and C7 levels. By comparing outcomes with patients undergoing conventional EOL (C-LAMP), this study evaluates the clinical efficacy of the M-LAMP in reducing postoperative axial symptoms and improving preservation of postoperative cervical mobility.

Materials and methods

Patient selection and characteristics

A retrospective analysis was conducted on 101 patients with CSM who were admitted to the Department of Orthopedics of our hospital between 2023 and 2024. According to the surgical technique used, the patients were divided into an M-LAMP (n = 51) and a C-LAMP (n = 50). M-LAMP (n = 51): 41 males, 10 females; mean age 58.2 ± 10.4 years; C-LAMP (n = 50): 33 males, 17 females; mean age 56.6 ± 9.7 years.All patients had a confirmed diagnosis of CSM.

Surgical procedures

M-LAMP: C3 total laminectomy, C4–C6 open-door laminoplasty, and C7 partial laminectomy (undercutting) decompression with extensive open-door laminoplasty and titanium plate fixation.C-LAMP: Underwent standard posterior extensive C3-7 open-door laminoplasty with unilateral muscle-ligament complex preservation and supportive titanium plate fixation.

Preoperative symptoms and signs

M-LAMP:Symptoms: Limb numbness/aching/weakness (n=37); gait instability with "cotton-like" sensation (n=17); impaired fine motor skills in upper limbs (n=3).Signs: Limb muscle weakness (n=31); sensory abnormalities (n=37); positive pathological reflexes (n=51).C-LAMP:Symptoms: Limb weakness (n=21); limb numbness (n=41); gait instability (n=15); impaired fine motor skills in upper limbs (n=2); involuntary lower limb tremors (n=1).Signs: Limb muscle weakness (n=20); sensory impairment (n=42); positive pathological reflexes (n=50); hyperreflexia (n=5).

Radiographic findings / comorbidities

M-LAMP: Cervical spinal stenosis (n=51); cervical disc herniation (n=48);ossification of the ligamentum flavum (OLF) (n=1); rheumatoid arthritis (n=2).C-LAMP: Cervical spinal stenosis (n=50); cervical disc herniation (n=47); diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis (DISH) (n=1); C2/C3 vertebral segmentation failure (n=2).

All surgical procedures were performed exclusively by senior spinal surgeons proficient in the techniques, utilizing the standardized extensive open-door laminoplasty approach.

Patient selection criteria

Inclusion criteria

Clinically confirmed diagnosis of CSM with ≥ 3 levels of spinal cord compression; Complete postoperative follow-up ≥ 1 year with comprehensive imaging data; Good general health status, deemed a suitable candidate for cervical surgery under general anesthesia; Planned surgical decompression via laminoplasty spanning levels C3 to C7.

Exclusion criteria

Primary diagnosis of cervical spondylotic radiculopathy; Cervical segmental instability or spondylolisthesis; Concurrent cervical trauma, fracture, infection, tumor, or kyphotic deformity; Coagulation disorders; Predominant anterior spinal cord compression requiring anterior approach; Previous cervical spine surgery; Severe preoperative neck pain as the dominant symptom.

Imaging assessment

All patients underwent preoperative and postoperative cervical spine imaging, including: Standard anteroposterior (AP) and lateral radiographs; Dynamic (flexion-extension) radiographs; Non-contrast computed tomography (CT) scans with 3D reconstructions; Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).Imaging served two primary purposes: Preoperative Planning: Identifying the levels and extent of spinal cord compression.Determining the required decompression range for laminoplasty.Measuring the thickness of the open-side lamina to prevent hinge fracture during trough creation (by avoiding excessively deep troughs).Outcome Assessment: Measuring cervical lordosis angle (pre- and postoperatively).Quantifying cervical range of motion (ROM) in flexion-extension (pre- and postoperatively).Calculating the loss of cervical lordosis and loss of ROM postoperatively.

Surgical technique

C-LAMP

Positioning & Preparation: Patients were placed prone with the neck flexed. Skull traction was applied using a Mayfield head holder. The torso was elevated approximately 30°. The surgical field was sterilized with iodine tincture and alcohol, followed by sterile draping.Exposure: A midline posterior cervical incision (~ 12 cm) was made. Skin, subcutaneous tissue, and the ligamentum nuchae were incised. The left paraspinal muscles were bluntly dissected and elevated, with hemostasis achieved via electrocautery.Spinous Process Management: The spinous processes of C3-C7 were transected at their base using an oscillating saw. The spinous processes, along with their attached musculature, were reflected en bloc to the right, exposing the C3-C7 laminae.Detachment of the C2 semispinalis cervicis muscle was performed to facilitate exposure of the C3 lamina.Hinge Creation (Right Side): At the junction of the lateral border of the lamina and the medial border of the facet joint on the right (hinge side), a trough was created using rongeurs or a high-speed burr to remove the outer cortical layer of the lamina, forming the hinge.Open-Door Creation (Left Side): At the junction of the lateral border of the lamina and the facet joint on the left (open-door side), the full thickness of the lamina was completely transected using rongeurs or a high-speed burr.Ligamentum Flavum Release: The ligamentum flavum was incised at the C2-C3 and C7-T1 levels.Decompression: The C3-C7 lamina complex was elevated and reflected en bloc to the right.Fixation: Supportive titanium plates were positioned at the open-door sites and secured with screws.Closure: Meticulous hemostasis was achieved using electrocautery. The surgical field was irrigated copiously with saline. A single closed-suction drain was placed.

M-LAMP

Exposure with Preservation: The interspinalis muscles were identified and protected. The spinous processes of C3-C6 were split sagittally using an ultrasonic bone scalpel. The paraspinal muscles were bluntly dissected bilaterally to expose the C3-C7 laminae, with meticulous care taken to preserve the bilateral muscle-ligament complexes at C2 and C7.Hinge Creation (Right Side): On the right side (hinge side), at the junction of the lamina and the medial facet joint from C4-C6, the outer cortical layer of the lamina was removed using a burr to create a V-shaped trough, forming the hinge.C3 Laminectomy (Preserving C2): Protecting the muscular insertions at C2, the C3 lamina was carefully resected in a piecemeal fashion.Partial C7 Laminectomy: The cephalad half of the C7 lamina was resected using the ultrasonic bone scalpel.Ligamentum Flavum Release: The ligamentum flavum between the lamina and the facet joints was carefully dissected and incised. Particular care was taken to incise the ligamentum flavum between C6-C7.Open-door creation (Left Side): On the left side, at the junction of the lamina and the medial facet joint from C4-C6, the full thickness of the lamina was transected.Decompression: The C4-C6 lamina complex was elevated and reflected towards the hinge side.Fixation & Closure: Remaining steps (plate fixation, hemostasis, irrigation, drain placement) were identical to the C-LAMP.

Postoperative management

Prophylaxis & Support: Intravenous antibiotics were administered for 48 h for infection prophylaxis. Supportive care included intravenous fluid management and analgesia.Mobilization: Patients were encouraged to ambulate 24 h postoperatively, contingent upon their recovery status and drain output.Neurological Monitoring: In cases of worsening neurological symptoms, corticosteroids and dehydrating agents (e.g., mannitol) were administered as clinically indicated.Drain Removal: The surgical drain was removed when output was < 50 ml per 24 h.Discharge & Immobilization: Patients were discharged after confirmation of satisfactory implant position and laminoplasty door status on postoperative cervical radiographs. A cervical collar was worn for 2 weeks postoperatively for support and protection.Rehabilitation: Gentle active cervical flexion-extension exercises were initiated after collar removal to prevent posterior extensor muscle atrophy and cervical stiffness.Follow-up: Scheduled clinical and radiographic follow-up assessments were conducted at 3, 6, and 1 year postoperatively.

Surgical Parameters: Operative time, Intraoperative blood loss, Postoperative drainage volume, Length of hospital stay, Postoperative complications.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS software (version 26.0).Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD).Within-group comparisons (e.g., pre- vs. postoperative JOA scores, VAS scores) were analyzed using the paired t-test or repeated-measures analysis of variance (ANOVA), as appropriate.Categorical variables were compared using the Chi-square test (χ² test) or Fisher’s exact test.A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

All surgeries were successfully completed without intraoperative complications

Postoperative Complications: One case of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leak occurred in each group. Both resolved successfully with conservative management: prolonged bed rest, extended duration of drain placement, increased fluid intake, and extended antibiotic coverage.No other major complications occurred during hospitalization or follow-up in either group, including: C5 nerve root palsy, Surgical site infection, Significant spinal cord injury, Hinge fracture, Screw loosening, breakage, or displacement.

Statistical Comparison: There were no statistically significant differences (P > 0.05) between the study and C-LAMPs regarding gender distribution, age, operative time, intraoperative blood loss, total postoperative drainage volume, length of hospital stay, overall postoperative complication rate, preoperative JOA score, preoperative cervical lordosis, or preoperative cervical ROM. (Detailed patient demographic and baseline characteristics are provided in Table 1).MRI evaluation methods: Postoperative MRI demonstrated complete dural sac expansion without evidence of obvious compression, indicating adequate spinal cord decompression. The proportion of patients achieving satisfactory decompression exceeded 97% in both groups, with no statistically significant difference between them.

Incidence of AS

M-LAMP: These three patients experienced unresolved neck pain, characterized by neck and shoulder pain that worsened after getting up, accompanied by stiffness, muscular soreness, and fatigue. Typical case

A 55-year-old male presented to our hospital with a 2-month history of numbness, weakness, and distending pain in all four limbs, accompanied by clumsiness of both hands. The diagnosis was CSM. Preoperative JOA score was 9. Following comprehensive evaluation, the patient underwent posterior cervical surgery under general anesthesia, consisting of C3 laminectomy, C7 undercutting decompression, spinal canal expansion laminoplasty, and titanium plate fixation. Intraoperative blood loss was 100 ml. Postoperatively, the symptoms of numbness and weakness in the limbs have improved. At the 1year follow-up, the JOA score had recovered to 15.

Preoperative cervical lordosis measured 14.8°, with a ROM from 17.5° extension to -8.1° flexion. At the 1 year follow-up, the neck pain VAS score was 2/10, cervical lordosis was 12.5°, and ROM measured from 20.1° extension to -7.0° flexion.

Discussion

EOL is a well-established surgical approach for CSM, offering reliable clinical efficacy while preserving cervical stability and motion [11, 12]. However, postoperative AS, neck stiffness, and restricted cervical ROM are common complications that significantly impair patients’ quality of life [13]. The pathogenesis of AS remains incompletely understood but is widely considered multifactorial [14, 15]. Chen et al. [16] proposed that AS may be associated with injury to the posterior ligamentous complex and facet joint degeneration. Surgery alters the biomechanics of the cervical spine, disrupting normal anatomical balance. This increases tension in the affected muscles, ligaments, and joint capsules, stimulating nociceptors and triggering symptoms such as pain. Liang et al. [17] observed that patients who develop AS often exhibit preoperative activation of inflammatory mediators, suggesting a lower inflammatory threshold. Intraoperative trauma may further activate inflammatory pathways in peripheral nerve fibers, exacerbating inflammation and leading to pain, muscle spasm, and ultimately AS.

Currently, the assessment of axial symptoms lacks standardization. In a review of reports on laminoplasty published between 2003 and 2013, Duetzmann et al. [18] found that only 26% of the studies used the VAS or other quantifiable measures to compare pre- and postoperative pain. With a mean follow-up of two years, the average VAS score was 2.78. Most studies only evaluated pain as an indicator of severity, while overlooking other symptoms such as stiffness, tension, and muscular soreness. In fact, relying solely on the VAS score is insufficient to fully reflect the severity of axial symptoms. Hosono et al. [19] classified axial symptoms into three grades—mild, moderate, and severe—based on their impact on patients’ daily lives, with moderate and severe cases collectively defined as clinically significant axial symptoms. This classification offers a more accurate assessment compared to evaluating pain alone. Several other studies have also adopted similar classification methods [10, 20].

Current advancements in EOL prioritize muscle preservation and minimizing tissue damage while ensuring adequate posterior decompression. Consequently, refined muscle-preserving techniques aim to protect muscular attachments at the C2 and/or C7 spinous processes and reconstruct the posterior muscular complex [17]. As the primary cervical extensor, the semispinalis cervicis muscle originates from the transverse processes of the upper thoracic vertebrae and inserts onto the spinous processes of C2 to C5, with the C2 attachment being particularly crucial. Disruption of this attachment can lead to cervical kyphosis and AS [17]. Multivariate analysis by Kato et al. [21] confirmed that preserving the C2 semispinalis cervicis insertion significantly reduces postoperative AS risk (OR = 0.13, 95% CI: 0.02–0.98). Takeuchi et al. [10] reported a significantly lower rate of aggravated AS in patients with preserved C2 attachments (17.5%) compared to those without (50%, P = 0.02).

Furthermore, the C7 spinous process serves as a critical lever point for the posterior extensor complex—including the semispinalis, rhomboid, and trapezius muscles [22]—and provides a robust attachment site for the nuchal ligament [23]. Loss of C7 height or disruption of its attachments may also contribute to cervical kyphosis and AS [24]. Ito et al. [25] identified the trapezius as relevant to neck pain, generating maximal force at the C7 level. A randomized single-blind controlled trial (n = 96) by Lin et al. [26] demonstrated that preserving the C7 spinous process better maintains sagittal ROM and alignment while significantly reducing neck pain VAS scores (2.5 vs. 3.2, P = 0.0035). Another 24-month follow-up study (n = 31) of patients undergoing C6–C7 decompression [27] revealed a lower rate of aggravated AS in the supraspinous ligament preservation group (13.3% [2/15]) than in the non-preservation group (43.8% [7/16]).

Therefore, minimizing disruption to the posterior extensor muscle attachments at C2 and C7 is theoretically crucial for maintaining cervical lordosis and reducing postoperative neck pain. To mitigate AP incidence, novel techniques such as skip laminectomy and C7 dome laminoplasty have been proposed [28]. Hosono et al. [29] significantly reduced AS rates (5.4% vs. 29%, P = 0.015) by prospectively limiting laminoplasty to C3–C6. However, shortening the surgical levels (omitting C7) risks inadequate C7 decompression, potentially compromising neurological recovery. To address this, our study implemented a modified strategy: performing proximal hemi-dome decompression (undercutting decompression) at C7 instead of traditional open-door laminoplasty. This approach ensures adequate C7 decompression while effectively preserving muscular and nuchal ligament attachments to the C7 spinous process. Postoperative 1 year follow-up JOA recovery rates showed no significant difference between the M-LAMP (76.0 ± 18.2%) and the C-LAMP (71.5 ± 23.3%; t = 1.096, P = 0.276), confirming comparable decompression efficacy.In the present study, the technique involving C3 laminectomy combined with C7 undercutting decompression effectively preserved the C2 semispinalis cervicis muscle and the C7 spinous process musculo-ligamentous complex, along with the insertion point of the nuchal ligament. This approach enhanced cervical stability and significantly alleviated postoperative axial symptoms resulting from extensive musculo-ligamentous destruction. It also improved postoperative pain, preserved ROM, and facilitated early functional rehabilitation.The results showed that at the 1 year postoperative follow-up, the loss of cervical ROM in the M-LAMP (-2.0 ± 7.2) was significantly lower than that in the C-LAMP (2.3 ± 8.8) (P = 0.008, 95% CI: [-7.5, -1.1]). Additionally, the incidence of (AS at 3 months post-surgery was 5.9% (3/51) in the M-LAMP, significantly lower than the 24.0% (12/50) in the C-LAMP (P = 0.01). However, at the 6-month follow-up, the AS incidence decreased to 3.9% (2/51) in the M-LAMP and 16.0% (8/50) in the C-LAMP, with no statistically significant difference between the groups (P = 0.089).These findings further confirm that this modified technique offers significant advantages in reducing early postoperative pain and improving cervical range of motion.

Surgical techniques preserving the muscular attachments at C2 and C7 offer advantages over traditional EOL. By maintaining the stability of these posterior insertions, they preserve the overall biomechanical integrity of the cervical spine, including postoperative ROM and the load-sharing capacity of the functional spinal units. Improved motion preservation enhances stability, potentially accelerating functional recovery and reducing the risk of kyphosis and AS [17]. Notably, a significant reduction in cervical ROM itself is a risk factor for AS development. Previous research suggests that loss of mobility may not be the cause of AS, but rather its consequence—AS restricts cervical motion [30].

This study utilized C3 laminectomy combined with proximal hemi-dome undercutting decompression at C7 in the M-LAMP. This approach specifically aimed to preserve the bilateral C2 semispinalis cervicis insertions, the C7 spinous process with its muscular attachments, and the insertion site of the nuchal ligament. Reducing intraoperative injury to the musculoligamentous complex lowers postoperative neck pain and facilitates early restoration of cervical mobility. At the 1 year postoperative follow-up, no statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups in cervical curvature (M-LAMP: 15.9 ± 8.1° vs. C-LAMP: 17.5 ± 8.5°; P = 0.333) or loss of curvature (M-LAMP: -4.1 ± 4.9° vs. C-LAMP: -3.0 ± 5.9°; P = 0.329; 95% CI: [-3.2, 1.1]). The lack of significance may be attributed to the relatively small sample size, limited follow-up period, and potential measurement bias.

The indications of this study include: Clinically confirmed CSM; Patients in satisfactory general health, deemed suitable for cervical spine surgery under general anesthesia; Spinal cord compression localized predominantly between the C3 and C7 levels, with a planned surgical decompression via laminoplasty spanning from C3 to C7.The contraindications: Cervical radiculopathy; Spinal cord compression localized at the C1-2 or C7-T1 levels; Severe cervical instability or spondylolisthesis; Patients with tumors or severe kyphotic deformity.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations: (1) Its single-center, retrospective design and relatively small sample size may introduce bias, particularly in measurements like flexion-extension ROM; (2) The potential influence of preoperative mild neck/shoulder pain on postoperative AS incidence was not assessed; (3) The follow-up period was short for some patients, longer-term follow-up is necessary to evaluate sustained outcomes. Future research should involve larger, multicenter studies with extended follow-up periods to further validate and expand upon these findings;4)For cervical spondylosis patients with severe preoperative compression at the C7–T1 level, the M-LAMP cannot achieve adequate decompression, and the C-LAMP should be performed instead.In this study, patients presenting with severe preoperative neck pain (VAS ≥ 5) as a primary clinical manifestation were excluded during preoperative screening and enrollment. This approach aimed to minimize the potential influence of preoperative neck pain on postoperative VAS scores related to neck pain in this research. Nevertheless, some bias may still exist. Future studies should involve larger sample sizes, longer follow-up periods, and multi-center designs to more accurately assess preoperative pain levels and further validate and expand upon the findings of this study.

Summary/Conclusion

Ensuring adequate spinal cord decompression remains paramount. Employing C3 laminectomy (instead of laminoplasty) to protect the C2 semispinalis cervicis insertion and performing proximal hemi-dome undercutting laminectomy at C7 to preserve the posterior extensor muscle attachments and nuchal ligament insertion on the C7 spinous process effectively reduces the incidence of early postoperative AS, alleviate early postoperative neck pain symptoms, reduce the loss of cervical range of motion and improve cervical mobility. This approach does not significantly increase operative time, intraoperative blood loss, or postoperative complication rates, and achieves satisfactory neurological recovery. Surgeons are advised to meticulously preserve the semispinalis cervicis insertion at C2 whenever feasible. Furthermore, if adequate decompression can be achieved without including the C7 level in the laminoplasty, preserving the C7 spinous process and its muscular attachments is strongly recommended.However, this is a retrospective, single-center design and short follow-up study, and some of the results are biased. Prospective, multi-center validation is needed before it is widely adopted.

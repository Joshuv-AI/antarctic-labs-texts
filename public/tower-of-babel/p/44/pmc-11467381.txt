Spectral weighted sparse unmixing based on adaptive total variation and low-rank constraints
Authors: Chenguang Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Hyperspectral sparse unmixing, an image processing technique, leverages a spectral library enriched with endmember spectral information as a prerequisite. It decomposes the hyperspectral data to ascertain the abundance corresponding to each endmember in the spectral library. Currently, the majority of sparse unmixing methods are inadequate in high-noise environments due to their failure to comprehensively account for hyperspectral characteristics. Addressing this challenge, this paper introduces an innovative sparse unmixing approach for hyperspectral images named spectral weighted sparse unmixing based on adaptive total variation and low-rank constraints (SWSU-ATVLR). Initially, the sparse unmixing algorithm is introduced in detail. Subsequently, we present the our method. This method seamlessly integrates the low-rank, adaptive TV and spectral weighting characteristics of hyperspectral data. While preserving the low-rank attributes and sparsity of abundance, the adaptively adjusted abundance matrix exhibits a regularized horizontal and vertical difference ratio across various structures and fully utilizes spectral information, enhancing denoising efficiency. Subsequently, the ADMM algorithm is employed to solve the new model. To validate our proposed algorithm, SWSU-ATVLR method is compared and analysed in detailed experiments with several current state-of-the-art methods through simulated and real data experiments. Experimental results prove that our proposed method is superior to these state-of-the-art methods.

Introduction

Hyperspectral remote sensing imaging technology, developed based on multispectral remote sensing imaging techniques beginning in the 1980s1, acquires hyperspectral images of scenes or targets with high spectral resolution2 and has been widely applied in aviation and spacecraft observations of land3, atmosphere4, and ocean5. Hyperspectral imaging are used for accurate classification of terrain features6, terrain recognition7, extraction of terrain feature information8, and many other applications9–11,46,47. Due to the huge advantages of hyperspectral imaging technology, it has received widespread attention in the field of remote sensing12, and therefore it has become the main technical means of contemporary space earth observation13.

However, due to limitations in sensor resolution14 and the complex distribution of terrain features in imaged areas15, a large number of mixed pixels exist in hyperspectral images14. The existence of mixed pixels causes difficulties in analysing and interpreting ground information16. Therefore, it is necessary to unmix the obtained hyperspectral images, to obtain the pure substance and their corresponding proportion in the mixed pixel17. These pure substance are referred to end members, and their corresponding proportions are known as abundances18.

The hyperspectral mixing models mainly contain linear spectral mixing models and nonlinear spectral mixing models19. The method proposed in this paper focuses on linear spectral mixing models. In the linear unmixing, the sparse regression algorithm is currently more commonly used20. It utilizes a large spectral library as prior knowledge21, combined with some regularization conditions22, to calculate the corresponding abundance coefficients for unmixing of hyperspectral mixing images. The current classic and widely used sparse unmixing algorithm is SUnSAL23, which opens up new avenues for sparse unmixing and brings new insights. However, the SUnSAL algorithm did not consider the spatial factors of the image24, which makes the unmixing robustness bad. To address this problem, SUnSAL-TV25 combining the SUnSAL model with the total variation (TV) term, utilizes the similarity of region adjacency matrix to make the unmixing effect better. Base on the SUnSAL model, Zhang et al.26 imposed spectral and spatial cnstraints on the abundance matrix and proposed a S2WSU method to further improved the unmixing accuracy. However, the unmixing effect of these algorithms is not good when the noise is high. Therefore, Ma et al.27 improved the TV item, and impose ATV regularized for sparse unmixing (SU-ATV). This method adaptively adjusts the ratio of horizontal and vertical difference operator, and further improve the performance of denoising and the precision of unmixing. On the other hand, considered that the abundance matrices containing highly correlated vectors have low rank characteristics28, Some unmixing methods with low rank constraints have been proposed, such as ADSpLRU29 algorithm. Shen et al.44 proposed a local global-based sparse regression unmixing method (LGSU), by introducing a local sparse regularisation based on hyper pixels to help improve the unmixing performance considering only global sparsity. Shen et al.45 proposed a layered sparse regression unmixing (LSU). The method decomposes the sparse unmixing process into multiple layers, each of which interactively learns an abundance matrix that promotes row sparsity and fine-tunes the active library atoms based on the measured activity.

The above algorithms only partially utilise some of the hyperspectral properties for unmixing, such as sparsity, spatial, spectral or low-rank properties, resulting in some of the properties being lost during the unmixing process and thus poor unmixing results in noisy environments. This paper take these factors into consideration, and propose a spectral weighted sparse unmixing base on adaptive total variation and low-rank constraints method, called SWSU-ATVLR. Experiment and discussion

In this paper, we compare the experimental results of the proposed algorithm with other related algorithms (SUnSAL-TV25, ADSpLRU29, SU-ATV27, S2WSU26, LGSU44 and LSU45) to verify the performance advantages of our algorithm. In this experiment, we will use AVIRIS42, which is widely used in hyperspectral remote sensing image analysis, to collect data from Cuprite mining area in Nevada in 1995 as a real hyperspectral image comparison and analysis. Figure 10 indicates the mineral classification map manufactured by the United States Geological Survey (USGS) in 1995 using Tricorder 3.3 software43, which reflects the distribution of each mineral in the Cuprite mine data.Fig. 10Distribution of minerals in nevada cuprite data.

Distribution of minerals in nevada cuprite data.

The original image has 224 bands, ranging in wavelength from 370 to 2480 nm, with a spatial resolution of 20 m and a spectral resolution of 10 nm. The experimental image uses part of the scene of the original image, which has a size of 250 × 191 pixels, and removes the bands affected by high noise and water absorption (1–2, 105–114, 152–172 and 222–224), remaining 188 bands. We compare the abundance estimation graphs of several different sparse unmixing methods to assay their unmixing effects.

However, SUnSAL-TV algorithm has the phenomenon of over-smoothing, resulting in blurred images and low accuracy. The unmixing effect of SU-ATV is better than that of SUnSAL-TV, but there are still some such problems (e.g. Chalcedony). The estimated images of ADSpLRU have a lot of noise (e.g. Alunite). Conclusion

In this paper, a novel approach of sparse unmixing algorithm is proposed, simultaneously using adaptive TV, low rank and spectral weighting, to effectively solve the unmixing problem of hyperspectral images under high noise conditions. The method simultaneously takes into account the spatial information of adaptive TV and low-rank constraints, and the spectral information of spectral weighting, which enables the unmixing process to make full use of the spatial and spectral information. And utilize the current state-of-the-art ADMM to solve this method. In both simulation and real data experiments, detailed analysis and comparison are conducted between the SUnSAL-TV, SU-ATV, ADSpLRU, S2WSU, LGSU, LSU methods and the proposed SWSU-ATVLR method. The proposed SWSU-ATVLR method is experimentally confirmed to be superior to other unmixing methods, showing better unmixing effect. In the future, we intend to build on this to improve the efficiency of the algorithm.

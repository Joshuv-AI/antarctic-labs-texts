Prognostic nutritional index improved the survival prediction of the revised Tokuhashi and Tomita scores for patients with lung cancer spinal metastases
Authors: Tiantian Wei, Guoqing Zhong, Jinfeng Wang, Chongquan Huang, Jin Qian, Yijie He, Yonghan Chen, Jingzhe He, Maolin Zhou, Shi Cheng, Chengzhi Zhou, Yu Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesLung cancer is currently the leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide, with 30%-36% of patients developing spinal metastases. However, the predictive accuracy of commonly used spinal metastasis scoring systems, such as the revised Tokuhashi score and Tomita score, has decreased. This study aims to optimize the revised Tokuhashi and Tomita scores by incorporating new indicators to assist clinicians in making informed treatment decisions.MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 147 patients with lung cancer spinal metastases. Prognostic factors were identified using Cox regression analysis. The predictive performance of different models at the 3-month, 6-month, and 1-year time points was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the DeLong test.ResultsBy incorporating the Prognostic Nutritional Index (PNI) and the presence of non-small cell lung cancer, the optimized P-Tokuhashi and P-Tomita scores were developed. The predictive abilities of P-Tokuhashi at 3 months (AUC = 0.77), 6 months (AUC = 0.71), and 1 year (AUC = 0.74), as well as P-Tomita at 3 months (AUC = 0.74), 6 months (AUC = 0.62), and 1 year (AUC = 0.70), were all superior to the pre-optimization scores. The DeLong test confirmed that the predictive accuracy of the modified scores at 6 months and 1 year was significantly better than that of the original scores (p < 0.05).ConclusionThe new prognostic scoring system for spinal metastasis, which incorporates the PNI and the status of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), provides better prediction of survival in patients with lung cancer spinal metastases, enabling clinicians to select more personalized treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-026-15952-0.Keywords: Spine metastasis, Prognostic, Lung cancer, AUC

Introduction

As of 2020, lung cancer continues to be a major global health concern, with an incidence rate of 32 per 100,000 individuals and a mortality rate of 42.4 per 100,000. This places lung cancer as the leading cause of cancer-related deaths globally, surpassing all other malignancies [1]. The high mortality rate is largely due to its aggressive behavior and its propensity to metastasize to other organs, particularly the bones. Lung cancer is the most common solid tumor to metastasize to bone, with metastasis typically occurring within an average of nine months following the initial diagnosis [2]. In about 70% of cases, lung cancer bone metastases exhibit osteolytic destruction, weakening the bones and leading to significant complications. These complications include bone pain, pathological fractures, spinal cord or nerve compression, and metabolic disturbances such as calcium and phosphate imbalances, collectively referred to as skeletal-related events (SREs) [3]. Patients with lung cancer who develop bone metastases face a poor prognosis, with a median overall survival (mOS) of 6 to 10 months. Once SREs occur, survival declines further, with a median overall survival of just 5 months [4]. Thus, accurately predicting the survival of patients with lung cancer bone metastases is crucial in guiding clinicians to select the most appropriate treatment strategies. Given the high morbidity and mortality risks associated with bone metastases, accurate survival predictions are crucial in guiding treatment strategies. Clinicians rely on survival predictions to make informed decisions about surgical interventions and treatment options. Over the last two decades, the revised Tokuhashi and Tomita scoring systems have been widely employed to predict survival in patients with spinal metastases [5, 6].

Despite their widespread use, these scoring systems have shown limited accuracy, especially for lung cancer patients with spinal metastases. Studies indicate that these scores frequently underestimate patient survival, potentially leading to suboptimal treatment decisions [7–9]. This underscores the need for a more precise scoring system that incorporates additional predictive factors beyond those currently used. Emerging research has identified preoperative blood markers, such as the platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), as valuable predictors of survival in lung cancer patients [10]. The prognostic nutritional index (PNI), calculated using serum albumin and peripheral blood lymphocyte counts, is also employed to evaluate nutritional status and surgical risk [11]. Several studies have established that PNI is strongly correlated with the prognosis of multiple malignancies, including colorectal, esophageal, and breast cancers, positioning it as a promising prognostic marker [12–14]. Therefore, this study aimed to incorporate PNI into the revised Tokuhashi and Tomita scoring systems to develop P-Tokuhashi and P-Tomita scores, and to evaluate whether these PNI-augmented scores provide improved survival prediction and clinically interpretable prognostic stratification for overall survival in surgically treated patients with lung cancer spinal metastases.

Methods

Design and data sources

This study is a dual-center retrospective analysis conducted in China, involving Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital (GDPH) and The First Affiliated Hospital of Guangzhou Medical College (GYFY). The study adhered to the Transparent Reporting of a Multivariable Prediction Model for Individual Prognosis or Diagnosis (TRIPOD) guidelines and received approval from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Guangdong Provincial People’s Hospital (Approval No.: 2019–560 H).

Participants

Inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: (1) age ≥ 18 years at the time of initial diagnosis; (2) pathologically confirmed diagnosis of lung cancer-derived spinal metastases; (3) underwent open or minimally invasive spinal surgery; (4) availability of the date of death or most recent follow-up. Surgery was performed mainly with palliative intent, including decompression and/or stabilization, to relieve neurological deficits, intractable pain, and/or mechanical instability. Extraspinal bone metastases and visceral metastases were recorded to characterize disease extent and were reflected in the Tokuhashi/Tomita scoring systems. Survival time was defined as the period from spinal surgery (open or minimally invasive) to death; patients who were alive at the last follow-up were censored on the date of last contact.

Data and collection

Data extraction was independently performed by two investigators who were blinded to patients’ outcomes; discrepancies were resolved by consensus, and a third author consolidated the final dataset. Demographic characteristics such as age, gender, height, and weight were recorded to contextualize clinical outcomes against the diverse patient population. Biochemical markers were selected for their relevance to cancer prognosis and included PNI, lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and red blood cell count. PNI, calculated as serum albumin (g/L) plus five times the total peripheral blood lymphocyte count (×10^9/L), is a critical indicator of nutritional and immunological status, influencing the patient’s ability to endure surgery and recover postoperatively. These markers offer insights into the body’s inflammatory state and serve as potential predictors of survival and disease progression.

Clinical indicators were focused on factors directly impacting the management and prognosis of spinal metastases. These included the number of spinal bone metastases, which correlates with tumor burden; the number of extraspinal bone metastases, reflecting the extent of disease spread; and visceral metastases, often indicating advanced disease. The Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status and neurological function (Frankel grade A–E) were recorded, with grade A indicating complete motor and sensory loss and grade E indicating normal neurological function (Table S1). Metastatic status was assessed using magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), nuclear bone scans, and positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT). All data were collected within two weeks before surgery to ensure consistency and reliability in subsequent analyses.

Score derivation

Based on the predefined PNI categories and lung cancer histology, integer weights were determined using an enumeration approach under predefined constraints (simplicity, monotonicity, and a maximum of 4 points per item to maintain comparability with the original systems). Candidate integer schemes were evaluated and the final weights (PNI: 1/2/4; histology: 0/4) were selected as the best-performing feasible scheme. Weight directionality was aligned with each original system (Tokuhashi-type: higher scores indicate better prognosis; Tomita-type: higher scores indicate worse prognosis).

Statistical analysis

Data are expressed as means and standard deviations. X-tile software was employed to determine the PNI cutoffs and the optimal cut-points for risk stratification of the revised scores [15]. Cox proportional hazards regression models were utilized to estimate hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) for overall survival (OS). Seven preoperative biochemical indices (PNI, LMR, PLR, WBC, RBC, PLT, and Hb) were assessed by univariate Cox regression. PNI was analyzed as a continuous variable in Cox regression to preserve information and statistical power, whereas the three-category PNI strata were used for descriptive presentation and for point assignment in the revised scoring systems (a categorical-PNI sensitivity analysis is provided in Table S2). To reduce multicollinearity among correlated hematologic indices, only PNI (selected based on univariate significance and predictive performance) and cancer type were included in the multivariable Cox model. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the area under the curve (AUC) were used to evaluate the predictive performance of the scoring systems for 3, 6, and 12 months survival, and AUCs were compared using DeLong’s test. For clinical stratification, X-tile was used to define prognostic strata for the revised scores, and overall survival was compared using Kaplan–Meier curves with log-rank tests. Discrimination for time-to-event outcomes was additionally assessed using Harrell’s C-index with 1,000 bootstrap resamples; horizon-specific C-indices were calculated with administrative censoring at 3, 6, and 12 months. Data analysis and model validation were performed using SPSS version 22.0, GraphPad Prism 9.0 and R (version 4.3.1). A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Preoperative data

A total of 175 patients with pathologically confirmed lung cancer spinal metastases were screened between July 2017 and December 2022. Twenty-eight patients were excluded due to loss to follow-up after surgery or incomplete preoperative data, leaving 147 patients for the final analysis. Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of the included patients are summarized in Table 1. The cohort comprised 91 males (61.9%) and 56 females (38.1%). The median age at the time of surgery was 60.1 years, with a range from 34 to 84 years. The mean postoperative survival time was 15.1 ± 11.2 months. Of these patients, 13 (8.1%) were classified as having poor Karnofsky performance status, 44 (29.9%) with moderate status, and 90 (61.2%) with good status. The Frankel grades of the patients were distributed as follows: A (3 patients, 2%), B (8 patients, 5.4%), C (12 patients, 8.2%), D (55 patients, 37.4%), and E (69 patients, 46.9%) (Table 1).

Factors influencing survival time

We calculated the revised Tokuhashi and Tomita scores for 147 patients and performed a univariate Cox regression analysis, revealing significant associations between the Tokuhashi score (p < 0.001) and the Tomita score (p = 0.015) with OS. Among the assessed biochemical indices, RBC (p = 0.003), PLR (p < 0.001), and PNI (p < 0.001) were significantly associated with OS (Table 2). ROC curves were plotted for these three indicators at 3, 6, and 12 months, and the AUC was calculated. Overall, PNI demonstrated the highest AUC, indicating the strongest predictive ability among the indicators (Fig. 1). Therefore, PNI was selected as the representative biomarker and entered together with cancer type into the multivariable Cox regression model (Table 2).

P-Tokuhashi and P-Tomita

PNI was selected as the indicator for the revised Tokuhashi and Tomita scores due to its accuracy and strong predictive performance. Among the 147 patients, PNI was categorized into three levels: low (PNI ≤ 39.2), intermediate (39.2 < PNI ≤ 43.5), and high (PNI > 43.5). Scores of 1, 2, and 4 were assigned to the low, intermediate, and high PNI levels, respectively, based on weight estimation and score enumeration. Since targeted therapies for non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) significantly improve patient survival [16–19], and the original spinal metastasis scores did not distinguish between small cell lung cancer and NSCLC, we conducted a univariate Cox analysis for NSCLC status, yielding p = 0.016. Multivariate Cox regression analysis was subsequently performed on PNI and NSCLC status, with both showing p-values of less than 0.05. As with PNI, scores of 0 or 4 were assigned based on NSCLC status.

Table 3 displays the revised Tokuhashi and Tomita scores, which incorporate PNI and NSCLC status, referred to as P-Tokuhashi and P-Tomita, respectively (Table 3). Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses for P-Tokuhashi and P-Tomita demonstrated P < 0.001. ROC curves and AUCs were used to evaluate the predictive accuracy of P-Tokuhashi and the revised Tokuhashi scores at 3, 6, and 12 months of follow-up. The results indicated that the AUCs for both P-Tokuhashi and P-Tomita exceeded those of the original scores at all time points. DeLong test results confirmed that at 6 and 12 months, P-Tokuhashi and P-Tomita had significantly better predictive performance than the original scores (p < 0.05) (Table 4). Although P-Tokuhashi’s AUCs consistently surpassed those of P-Tomita at 3, 6, and 12 months, a statistically significant difference was observed only at the 6-month follow-up (Fig. 2). To address potential bias introduced by the small SCLC subgroup, we performed a sensitivity analysis restricted to NSCLC patients, and the overall discrimination patterns remained consistent with the primary analysis (Supplementary Table S3).

Using maximally selected log-rank statistics, P-Tokuhashi stratified patients into three prognostic groups (≤ 7, 8, and ≥ 9) with clear OS separation (log-rank P < 0.001; Fig. 4a). Likewise, P-Tomita stratified patients into ≤ 7, 8–12, and ≥ 13 points, showing significant OS separation (log-rank P < 0.001; Fig. 4b), with corresponding median OS values of 30, 12, and 7 months. Discussion

Patients with lung cancer spinal metastases comprise a substantial proportion of those with spinal metastatic disease, and advancements in lung cancer treatment have correspondingly extended survival times. Accurately predicting survival times for these patients frequently guides treatment decisions [20]. However, current studies indicate that the widely used revised Tokuhashi and Tomita scores inadequately predict patient survival. Furthermore, the revised Tokuhashi score primarily considers a patient’s overall functional ability and the location of primary and metastatic tumors, without incorporating basic biochemical indicators. This limitation hinders its ability to differentiate prognostic outcomes in spinal metastases originating from a single tumor type [21, 22]. By optimizing the scoring system, our goal was to enhance the accuracy of survival predictions, thereby enabling clinicians to make more targeted surgical decisions and ultimately improving both patient survival rates and quality of life.

Given this context, we focused on survival predictions at 3, 6, and 12 months as key reference points for determining surgical suitability and approach. The 3-month survival prediction helps determine the appropriateness of surgical intervention, while the 1-year survival prediction is vital for selecting the specific surgical approach [20]. Among lung cancer patients with spinal metastases, the AUC for the revised Tokuhashi score was 0.72 at 3 months, 0.66 at 6 months, and 0.64 at 1 year, whereas the AUC for the Tomita score was 0.63, 0.55, and 0.59, respectively. These scores are notably insufficient in predicting patient survival, particularly for the 1-year survival period. To enhance survival predictions, we incorporated six routine biochemical indicators, all readily accessible in most hospital patient information systems, ensuring clinical applicability. Among these indicators, PNI has the strongest association with survival in lung cancer patients with bone metastases. Previous studies have demonstrated that PNI has significant predictive value in the prognosis of cancers such as colorectal and esophageal cancer [23, 24]. However, some literature suggests that the predictive capacity of PNI may be affected by the patient’s baseline nutritional status and immune function, potentially leading to inaccuracies in certain cases [25]. Additionally, given the significant differences in chemoradiotherapy sensitivity and the feasibility of targeted therapies between non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small cell lung cancer, we incorporated NSCLC status into the scoring system. Recent studies indicate that targeted therapies for NSCLC significantly extend patient survival; however, they also highlight that individual variability may influence treatment outcomes [26–29]. By incorporating these two new indicators into the existing scoring system, we developed a more predictive and clinically relevant scoring model to offer more precise treatment guidance for patients with spinal metastatic cancer.

In this study, the AUCs of the P-Tokuhashi and P-Tomita scores at 3, 6, and 12 months were all higher than those for the revised Tokuhashi and Tomita scores, with significant improvements at 6 and 12 months by DeLong testing. Furthermore, although the P-Tokuhashi score only showed a statistically significant difference in survival prediction compared to the P-Tomita score at the 6-month follow-up, its AUC remained consistently higher at the 3, 6, and 12-month follow-ups, suggesting that the P-Tokuhashi score may have better predictive ability, in line with previous studies [30, 31]. Importantly, beyond AUC improvement, both revised scores also achieved clear prognostic stratification. Using maximally selected log-rank cut-offs, P-Tokuhashi categorized patients into ≤ 7, 8, and ≥ 9 points, and P-Tomita into ≤ 7, 8–12, and ≥ 13 points, each demonstrating significant OS separation. Harrell’s C statistic further supported the superior prognostic performance of the revised scores.

From a clinical perspective, improved risk stratification may facilitate multidisciplinary decision-making by providing more individualized survival estimates for patients with lung cancer spinal metastases. These thresholds can be practically used to guide the extent of surgical intervention: patients in favorable-risk groups may be considered for more durable decompression, stabilization, or reconstruction when clinically indicated, whereas those in poor-risk groups may benefit from less invasive or palliative strategies to minimize perioperative burden and enable earlier recovery. In addition, risk grouping may assist in coordinating postoperative radiotherapy and systemic therapy, supporting a more coherent treatment plan. Notably, these revised scores are intended to complement rather than replace clinical judgment, and should be interpreted alongside neurological function, spinal stability, systemic disease control, and patient preferences [32].

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design may introduce selection bias and unmeasured confounding. Second, although discrimination improved, performance remained moderate and time-horizon dependent. Third, because the revised scores were developed specifically for lung cancer spinal metastases, generalizability to other primary tumors requires external validation in independent cohorts.

Conclusion

We enhanced two key scoring systems for spinal metastasis by integrating PNI and NSCLC status. The enhanced P-Tokuhashi and P-Tomita scores exhibited higher AUC values at the 3 month, 6 month, and 1 year follow-ups compared to the original scores. Notably, the P-Tokuhashi score consistently outperformed the P-Tomita score in AUCs across all follow-up periods. This improved scoring system for spinal metastasis, which incorporates nutritional indicators and cancer type, offers clinicians a more reliable tool for survival prediction and treatment planning. By facilitating more targeted decisions, this system holds the potential to improve patient outcomes and optimize treatment strategies. Future research should focus on further validating these findings in diverse clinical settings to strengthen their applicability and effectiveness.

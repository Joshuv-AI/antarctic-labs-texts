Association of elevated Alpha-1-Acid glycoprotein with MAFLD prevalence in young and middle-aged women: a cross-sectional study based on NHANES 2017–2020
Authors: Yi Xiao, Xian Luo, Yinghao Fu, Haijuan Huang, Qiaoping Gao, Jiansong Liu, Daxin Pang, Xiaolu Liang, Huadi Chen, Hao Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMetabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) is a prevalent metabolic condition lacking sensitive and accessible biomarkers for early detection and risk stratification.ObjectiveTo investigate the association between serum alpha-1-acid glycoprotein (AGP) levels and MAFLD prevalence among young and middle-aged women, and to evaluate the dose–response relationship and diagnostic performance of AGP.MethodsData were obtained from 2,003 female participants aged 20–49 years in the NHANES 2017–2020 cycles. Weighted multivariable logistic regression models assessed the association between AGP and MAFLD, with adjustments for demographic, metabolic, and inflammatory covariates. Restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression evaluated nonlinear trends, and ROC analysis assessed predictive accuracy.ResultsHigher AGP levels were significantly associated with increased MAFLD risk. In the fully adjusted model, the highest AGP tertile had an odds ratio of 2.63 (95% CI: 1.65–4.19) compared to the lowest. RCS analysis showed a nonlinear, biphasic relationship with an inflection point around 0.9 g/L. ROC analysis indicated moderate discriminatory ability (AUC = 0.734), with AGP outperforming traditional liver enzymes. Subgroup analyses confirmed consistency across metabolic risk strata.ConclusionAGP is independently and nonlinearly associated with MAFLD and may serve as a non-invasive biomarker for early identification of at-risk individuals. These findings support the potential clinical utility of AGP in MAFLD screening and risk assessment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13098-025-01931-1.Keywords: Metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease, Alpha-1-acid glycoprotein, National health and nutrition examination survey, Inflammation, Cross-sectional study

Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD) has recently been proposed as a more inclusive and clinically meaningful term to replace non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) [1, 2]. Unlike NAFLD, which requires the exclusion of alcohol and other liver diseases, MAFLD is diagnosed based on the presence of hepatic steatosis in conjunction with overweight or obesity, type 2 diabetes, or evidence of metabolic dysregulation [3, 4]. This shift emphasizes the metabolic underpinnings of the disease and better captures its heterogeneous clinical spectrum. Epidemiological studies suggest that the global prevalence of MAFLD ranges from 25% to over 50% [5–7], reflecting the rising burden of obesity and sedentary behavior across populations. Beyond liver-related complications, MAFLD has been increasingly recognized as a multisystem disorder, conferring heightened risks of cardiovascular disease, advanced fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma [8]. Given its asymptomatic nature in early stages and complex metabolic background, there is an urgent need for accessible biomarkers that can aid in the identification and risk stratification of affected individuals.

Alpha-1-acid glycoprotein (AGP), or orosomucoid, is an acute-phase reactant predominantly produced in the liver [9]. It is upregulated in response to systemic inflammation [10, 11] and has been linked to various metabolic and cardiovascular conditions [12, 13]. While AGP is unlikely to play a causal role in fatty liver disease, its elevation may reflect the inflammatory milieu that accompanies metabolic dysregulation, particularly in the context of hepatic steatosis. Despite this plausible link, few studies have examined AGP in relation to MAFLD at the population level. Moreover, data from large cohorts exploring whether AGP concentrations correlate with the severity or likelihood of MAFLD are limited.

In this study, we used data from the 2017–2020 cycles of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) to examine the association between serum AGP levels and the prevalence of MAFLD among young and middle-aged women in the United States. Due to the availability of AGP measurements only in females aged 12–49 years during this survey cycle, this subgroup defined our study population. We also assessed whether a dose-response pattern exists between AGP levels and MAFLD prevalence, which could suggest its potential utility as a non-invasive marker of disease burden in a metabolically and hormonally dynamic population.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

This study was based on publicly available data from the NHANES 2017–2020, with a focus on the association between MAFLD and serum AGP levels among young and middle-aged women in the United States. NHANES is a nationally representative, cross-sectional survey conducted by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention using a multistage, stratified probability sampling design to assess the health and nutritional status of the non-institutionalized U.S. population [14]. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. Data were collected through household interviews, physical examinations, and laboratory testing.

The study population was limited to female participants aged 20 to 49 years because serum AGP concentrations were measured only in this subgroup during the 2017–2020 NHANES cycle. Extracted variables included serum AGP concentrations from laboratory data, MAFLD status, and demographic characteristics such as race/ethnicity, education level, and income, as well as metabolic-related covariates including body mass index (BMI), diabetes, and hypertension. Due to the complex sampling design of NHANES, all analyses accounted for sample weights to ensure nationally representative estimates.

The dataset was obtained from the official NHANES website, and the analysis was deemed exempt from institutional review board approval, as it involved only de-identified public-use data. In the data filtration process (Fig. 1), we initially identified 15,646 participants from the NHANES 2017–2020 cycles with clearly defined MAFLD information. We first excluded 12,193 participants due to AGP data. Among the remaining 3,453 participants, we further excluded 1,450 individuals with missing covariate data. Consequently, a total of 2,003 participants were included in the final analysis. These participants were subsequently categorized into the MAFLD group (n = 892) and the non-MAFLD group (n = 1,111).

Flow chart of participants selection from the NHANES 2017–2020. MAFLD, metabolic associated fatty liver disease; NHANES, National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey

Measurement of AGP

Serum AGP concentrations were measured in the NHANES 2017–2020 cycles using a high-sensitivity turbidimetric immunoassay. This technique detects AGP by quantifying the turbidity resulting from the formation of antigen–antibody complexes.

Definition of MAFLD

The diagnosis of MAFLD was based on the 2020 international expert consensus [4]. Briefly, individuals were classified as having MAFLD if hepatic steatosis was present in conjunction with either overweight or obesity, type 2 diabetes, or evidence of metabolic dysregulation.

Covariates

In addition to serum AGP levels, the regression models were adjusted for a comprehensive set of covariates to account for potential confounding. These included demographic variables (age, race/ethnicity, marital status, household income, and educational attainment), metabolic and lifestyle-related factors (BMI, diabetes, hypertension, cardiovascular disease history, smoking status, alcohol consumption frequency, and physical activity), as well as inflammatory and liver enzymes (high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and alanine aminotransferase (ALT)) to control for systemic inflammation and other potential influences on liver function.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as means with standard deviations, while categorical variables were expressed as proportions. Weighted t-tests were used to compare continuous data, and chi-square tests were conducted for categorical variables. To assess the association between AGP and MAFLD, weighted multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed with stepwise adjustment for covariates. Model 1 was unadjusted; Model 2 adjusted for age, race/ethnicity, education, and marital status; Model 3 further included BMI, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, smoking status, diabetes mellitus, cardiovascular disease, and alcohol consumption; and Model 4 additionally accounted for AST, ALT, and hs-CRP to control for liver enzymes and systemic inflammation. AGP levels were categorized into tertiles based on their distribution (0.27–0.69, 0.69–0.89, and 0.89–1.76 g/L), with the lowest tertile serving as the reference group in all regression models. Additionally, restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression was used to explore potential nonlinear associations between serum AGP levels and the risk of MAFLD. The predictive performance of AGP for MAFLD was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The optimal threshold was selected based on the Youden Index (J = sensitivity + specificity – 1), which identifies the point on the ROC curve that maximizes the sum of sensitivity and specificity, providing the best trade-off between true positive and false positive rates. Subgroup analyses were conducted to evaluate whether the association varied across different strata defined by race, hyperlipidemia, hypertension, BMI, diabetes mellitus, smoking status, and alcohol consumption. All statistical analyses were conducted in R software (version 4.2.2), with a two-sided P-value < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Characteristics of participants

A total of 2003 participants were included in the final analysis, stratified into MAFLD (n = 892) and non-MAFLD (n = 1111) groups according to the diagnostic criteria. As summarized in Table 1, individuals with MAFLD had significantly higher serum levels of AGP compared to those without MAFLD (0.91 ± 0.01 g/L vs. 0.72 ± 0.01 g/L, P < 0.0001). Participants in the MAFLD group were generally older and exhibited significantly elevated BMI, ALT, AST, hs-CRP, and other metabolic risk markers (all P < 0.01).

MAFLD prevalence was also associated with several demographic and lifestyle factors. Compared to the non-MAFLD group, a higher proportion of MAFLD participants had a lower education level, were currently married or cohabiting, and reported a history of smoking, heavy alcohol consumption, and comorbid conditions including hyperlipidemia, hypertension, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease (all P < 0.01). No significant differences were observed in physical activity levels or income between the two groups (P > 0.05).

Additionally, significant racial disparities were noted, with the highest MAFLD prevalence among individuals categorized as “Other” race, followed by Black and White participants (P < 0.001). These findings indicate that MAFLD is closely linked with both metabolic dysfunction and sociodemographic disparities, and underscore the importance of comprehensive risk profiling in population-based studies.

Association between AGP and the presence of MAFLD

The association between serum AGP levels and MAFLD prevalence was assessed using multivariable weighted logistic regression analysis. As shown in Table 2, in the unadjusted model (Model 1), higher AGP levels were significantly associated with increased odds of MAFLD. Compared with individuals in the lowest tertile of AGP (Tertile 1: 0.27–0.69 g/L), those in Tertile 2 (0.69–0.89 g/L) and Tertile 3 (0.89–1.76 g/L) had odds ratios (ORs) of 3.86 (95% CI: 2.82–5.28) and 9.38 (95% CI: 6.91–12.73), respectively (P for trend < 0.0001).

In Model 2, which included age, race, education, and marital status, the ORs were 3.80 (95% CI: 2.84–5.09) for Tertile 2 and 10.33 (95% CI: 7.63–13.97) for Tertile 3 (P for trend < 0.0001). Dose–response relationship between AGP and MAFLD

Restricted cubic spline (RCS) regression models were employed to assess the nonlinear association between serum AGP levels and the prevalence of MAFLD, as illustrated in Fig. 2A–D. In the unadjusted model (Model 1, Fig. 2A), the risk of MAFLD increased sharply with rising AGP levels, demonstrating a significant nonlinear trend (P for non-linearity < 0.001). This association remained robust in Model 2 (Fig. 2B), which accounted for age, race, education, and marital status.

Notably, in the more comprehensively adjusted models—Model 3 (Fig. 2C), which included additional adjustments for BMI, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, smoking, alcohol use, and cardiovascular disease, and Model 4 (Fig. 2D), which further adjusted for liver enzymes (ALT, AST) and systemic inflammation (hs-CRP)—a distinct nonlinear and biphasic pattern emerged. Specifically, the risk of MAFLD increased with AGP levels below approximately 0.9 g/L, but began to plateau or slightly decline at AGP concentrations above this threshold, suggesting a potential inflection point around 0.9 g/L. This turning point may reflect a biological threshold beyond which AGP no longer proportionally captures the inflammatory burden associated with MAFLD, or may indicate compensatory regulatory mechanisms.

Comparison of AGP and liver enzymes in predicting MAFLD

To assess the diagnostic performance of serum AGP in identifying MAFLD, ROC curve analysis was conducted (Fig. 3). The area under the curve for AGP was 0.734 (95% CI: 0.7124–0.7559), indicating a moderate-to-good discriminatory ability. The optimal cutoff value for AGP was 0.751, corresponding to a sensitivity of 77.47% and a specificity of 60.85%.

Compared with conventional liver enzymes such as ALT and AST, AGP demonstrated superior performance in predicting the presence of MAFLD, particularly in reflecting hepatic metabolic inflammation and subclinical liver injury. While transaminases are often used in clinical practice, their levels may remain within normal ranges during the early stages of MAFLD, potentially delaying diagnosis and intervention. In contrast, AGP, as an acute-phase reactant, may better capture low-grade systemic and hepatic inflammation characteristic of MAFLD.

The results consistently demonstrated that higher AGP tertiles were significantly associated with an increased risk of MAFLD in all subgroups (P for trend < 0.001). Notably, the strength of association was more pronounced among individuals with hypertension, diabetes, or a history of current smoking. For instance, among current smokers, participants in the highest AGP tertile had an odds ratio (OR) of 23.41 (95% CI: 10.53–52.06) compared to those in the lowest tertile. Similarly elevated risks were observed among those with hypertension (OR = 9.57, 95% CI: 3.12–29.34) and diabetes mellitus (OR = 6.39, 95% CI: 1.30–31.47).

Despite variations in effect size, interaction tests across all subgroup categories revealed no statistically significant interactions (all P for interaction > 0.05), suggesting that the positive association between AGP and MAFLD was consistent and not significantly modified by these covariates.

Discussion

In this cross-sectional study based on NHANES 2017–2020 data, we found a robust and independent association between elevated serum AGP levels and the prevalence of MAFLD in young and middle-aged women. To our knowledge, this is the first study to demonstrate a significant nonlinear dose–response relationship between AGP and MAFLD in a large, nationally representative U.S. female population, highlighting the potential of AGP as a non-invasive biomarker for metabolic liver disease.

AGP, a major acute-phase protein synthesized in the liver, is strongly induced by pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha [15]. Its elevation reflects systemic inflammation [16]—a core mechanism in the pathogenesis of MAFLD. This inflammation contributes to hepatic insulin resistance, promotes de novo lipogenesis, and impairs β-oxidation, all of which facilitate hepatic steatosis and progression to steatohepatitis and fibrosis [17–19]. The strong dose–response pattern observed in our study supports the hypothesis that AGP is not merely a marker of inflammation, but may also be involved in the metabolic and immunologic perturbations driving MAFLD development. Beyond this overall association, our spline analyses further revealed a distinct nonlinear, biphasic pattern in Models 3 and 4. Specifically, the prevalence of MAFLD increased with AGP concentrations below approximately 0.9 g/L, but plateaued or even slightly declined at higher levels. This inflection point indicates that AGP may be most informative as a risk indicator within a specific physiological range. One potential explanation is that moderate AGP elevations reflect early-stage inflammatory activity closely linked to subclinical hepatic steatosis, whereas higher concentrations may reflect a shift toward more complex immune regulation or hepatocellular adaptation, in which further increases in AGP no longer correspond to greater disease risk. Similar saturation effects have been observed with other inflammatory biomarkers in metabolic conditions, reinforcing the biological plausibility of this threshold behavior [20, 21]. Recognizing this inflection point adds interpretive nuance to the clinical use of AGP and may enhance its value in stratifying MAFLD risk across diverse patient populations.

Furthermore, ROC analysis revealed that AGP outperformed conventional liver enzymes such as ALT and AST in predicting the presence of MAFLD. This finding is clinically important because transaminases may remain within the normal range in early or metabolically active MAFLD, leading to underdiagnosis [22]. In contrast, AGP may capture earlier inflammatory or immunometabolic disturbances before overt hepatocellular injury becomes apparent. Subgroup analyses further supported the consistency of the AGP–MAFLD association across metabolic subpopulations. Individuals with diabetes, hypertension, or current smoking status exhibited even stronger associations, suggesting that AGP may be especially useful for identifying high-risk individuals within metabolically compromised groups.

Our findings are aligned with the broader concept that MAFLD is not merely a liver-centered disease, but a systemic condition characterized by chronic inflammation, immune activation, and metabolic dysfunction [23, 24]. As such, AGP may serve as a surrogate marker of these interrelated processes and offer clinicians an accessible tool for early risk assessment. Emerging evidence further emphasizes the critical role of the spleen-liver axis in orchestrating obesity-induced immune dysregulation and chronic low-grade inflammation in MAFLD. Recent studies have demonstrated that fatty liver inflammation is associated with splenic enrichment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells and natural killer T cells, while the depletion of hepatic T and B lymphocytes is not mirrored by changes in splenic lymphocyte populations. This selective enrichment indicates a cell-specific communication between the spleen and liver, modulating immune responses during metabolic dysfunction [25]. Additionally, leukotriene B4, a lipid mediator secreted by the spleen, has been shown to enhance hepatic tumor necrosis factor-alpha production, acting as an endocrine signal that amplifies systemic inflammation [26]. These findings underscore the spleen’s role as a pivotal immune organ that sustains and propagates the inflammatory milieu characteristic of MAFLD. The liver-spleen axis thus represents a key pathway through which systemic immune activation exacerbates hepatic metabolic disturbances, aligning with the broader understanding of MAFLD as a multisystemic disorder [27]. Incorporating this perspective enriches our interpretation of AGP as a biomarker, not only reflecting hepatic inflammation but also capturing systemic immunometabolic dysregulation driven by spleen-liver interactions.

This study has several limitations. First, the cross-sectional nature of NHANES prevents us from inferring causality. Second, MAFLD diagnosis was based on surrogate indices rather than imaging or liver biopsy. Third, due to data limitations, our analysis was restricted to women aged 12–49 years, which may limit generalizability to older populations or males.

In conclusion, elevated AGP levels were independently and nonlinearly associated with increased MAFLD prevalence in young and middle-aged women. As a liver-derived inflammatory marker, AGP may aid in the early detection and risk stratification of MAFLD, particularly in individuals with subtle metabolic dysfunction. Future prospective and mechanistic studies are warranted to confirm these findings and explore AGP’s role in MAFLD progression and clinical management.

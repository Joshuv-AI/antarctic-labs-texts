Efficacy and safety of Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis in patients with physiological vitreous floaters
Authors: Junjie Liu, Huilin Liu, Ying Zhang, Yichen Zheng, Jing Li, Chao Pan, Ke Jiang, Jianhong Cheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo observe the clinical effects of Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis in patients with physiological vitreous floaters by A/B ultrasound and infrared fundus photography., and to evaluate the safety of the treatment by ffERG.MethodsThirty eyes diagnosed with physiological vitreous floaters were enrolled. Examination of BCVA, IOP, SLO, A/B ultrasound, infrared fundus photography and ffERG were performed. The vitreolysis was performed for all patients by the same skilled doctor using an ELLEX YAG laser vitreolysis machine. The IOP was measured half an hour after the operation. After treatment, the patients were followed up at 1 day, 1 week, and 1 month. At follow-up, the same thorough examination as before the operation was performed. ImageJ software was used to measure the size of the vitreous floaters in the infrared fundus photography pictures before and after treatment.ResultsThere was no significant difference in BCVA and IOP before and after treatment (p > 0.05). Infrared fundus photography showed that the vitreous floaters were obviously reduced or disappeared after treatment, and the difference was statistically significant (p < 0.01). The latent period and amplitude of the five response waves of ffERG at each timepoint were compared before and after treatment, and there was no significant difference (P > 0.05). Ultra-wide angle SLO showed no severe complications in the vitreous or retina after treatment.ConclusionsIn short-term observations, Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis is safe and effective for the treatment of vitreous floaters and can be promoted clinically.Keywords: Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis, Physiological vitreous floaters, A/B ultrasound, Infrared fundus photography, Full-field electroretinogram

Introduction

Physiological vitreous floaters is a common ophthalmic condition characterized primarily by visual disturbances, with floaters moving as the eyes or head move, becoming more noticeable in bright environments. In recent years, the prevalence of myopia has increased, and the phenomenon of floaters has become more common and younger, significantly impacting visual quality and life quality [1]. Current treatment methods include medication, vitrectomy, and Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis. The effectiveness of medication is uncertain, and while vitrectomy is effective, it carries high risks [2–4]. Nd: YAG laser treatment has shown clear efficacy for severe visual disturbances caused by Weiss rings [5], but its safety and effectiveness assessments largely rely on subjective experiences, lacking objective quantitative evidence. This study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of Nd: YAG laser treatment through infrared fundus photography and full-field electroretinography (ff-ERG), exploring its impact on retinal function to guide the clinical treatment of patients with physiological vitreous floaters.

Materials and methods

Thirty eyes of 30 patients diagnosed with physiological vitreous floaters in the outpatient clinic of Hankou Aier Eye Hospital from June to December 2018 were enrolled in the treatment group, including 26 women and 4 men, all over 40 years old. The inclusion criteria were as follows: symptoms lasting for at least 2 months without flashes. Patients with vitreous floaters caused by other eye diseases, serious retinopathy (such as peripheral retinal degeneration, retinal hole, retinal detachment, retinal hemorrhage, and other retinal lesions that may cause pathological floaters), serious refractive interstitial opacity, active inflammation, glaucoma, a history of a high IOP, and psychological diseases were excluded. This study was reviewed and approved by the ethics committee of Hankou Aier Eye Hospital for relevant research work, and informed consent was obtained from all patients.

The best corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and noncontact intraocular pressure (IOP) before Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis were examined. The fundus was examined by slit lamp, and the distance between the vitreous floaters and retina was determined by A/B ultrasound (Fig. 1). Ultrawide-angle scanning laser ophthalmoscopy (SLO) and infrared fundus photography on Heidelberg angiography were performed to show the size of the vitreous floaters, and an ffERG examination was also performed.

A/B ultrasound showing the distance between the vitreous floater and the retina before vitreolysis, 5 mm in graph A and 3.5 mm in graph B

Before the operation, the pupil was fully dilated. The patients were anesthetized with obucaine hydrochloride eye drops. A special contact lens was placed on the eye surface with carbomer eye drops as the medium. Laser vitreolysis was performed for all patients by the same experienced doctor. The initial laser energy was 3 mJ, and the maximum energy was not more than 7 mJ. According to the vaporization reaction, the energy parameters were gradually adjusted to vaporize the floaters. The number of pulses emitted per treatment was no more than 500 times, and the average treatment time was approximately 20 min. If there were any floaters remaining, the laser treatment was performed again after 1 week.

The IOP was measured 30 min after the operation. Diclofenac sodium eye drops were applied to the treated eyes four times a day for 1 week. The patients were followed up for 1 day, 1 week and 1 month after the operation. BCVA, IOP, fundus examination with slit lamp, SLO, infrared fundus photography, and ffERG examination were performed. Five standard responses of ffERG were examined: the rod cell response (Rod.R), the maximum response (Max.R), oscillatory potentials (Ops), the cone cell response (Cone. R) and the 30 Hz flicker response (Flicker-R30 Hz). Infrared fundus photography was performed to observe the size changes of vitreous floaters and analyzed by “ImageJ” software. We took the average data from three measurements.

Ethics approval was obtained by the Medical ethic committe of Hankou Aier Eye Hospital in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Statistical methods

Data analysis was conducted using the SPSS 21.0 statistical software. The projected shadows data from infrared fundus photography did not conform to a normal distribution and were subjected to the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Visual acuity (logMAR) also deviated from a normal distribution and was analyzed using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Both IOP and the five response waves of the ffERG adhered to a normal distribution and were analyzed with one-way repeated measures ANOVA. A P-value of less than 0.05 was deemed to indicate a statistically significant difference for all data sets.

Results

Basic characteristics

Thirty patients (30 eyes) were treated with Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis, including 26 women and 4 men, with an average age of 60.34 ± 9 years. The severity of their symptoms was assessed by a questionnaire survey before treatment [6]. Two cases (6.67%) were considered severe, 22 cases (73.33%) were moderate, and 6 cases (2%) were mild. The mean IOP was 13.78 ± 2.42 mmHg, and the mean best corrected visual acuity was 0.083 ± 0.110.

BCVA and IOP

There was no significant difference in the BCVA and IOP before and 1 day, 1 week, and 1 month after vitreolysis (P > 0.05) (Fig. 2), indicating that the treatment had little effect on visual acuity and IOP.

Infrared fundus photography

Thirty eyes were treated with laser vitreolysis. On the first day after the treatment, the floaters were significantly reduced or had completely disappeared (Fig. 3). The Wilcoxon signed rank test showed that z = −4.872, P < 0.001 (Fig. 4). The difference was statistically significant, indicating that Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis is effective in the treatment of physiological vitreous floaters.

Results of ffERG

The amplitude (uV) and latent period (ms) of the ERG response waves at each time point before and after treatment were compared. The P value of Cone. R b-wave amplitude value was 0.004, and the difference was statistically significant. Multiple comparisons were made, and the difference was statistically significant (P = 0.002) between the 1-month and 1-day follow-up. The differences between other amplitudes or latent periods were not statistically significant. Figure 5 shows the amplitude of the cone.R b-wave decreased on the first day after the operation compared with before the operation, but the difference was not statistically significant. The amplitude value increased gradually at 1 week and 1 month of follow-up, which indicated that Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis did not damage retinal function over a short period (Figs. 5 and 6).

Complications

All of the treatments went well, and the patients did not have any discomfort. A small number of patients reported that they observed small black spots floating after vitreolysis, which were confirmed as bubbles generated during the treatment through slit-lamp microscopy examination. We told the patients the bubbles would be absorbed slowly. IOP was measured again half an hour after the operation, and no elevated IOP was found in all 30 eyes. Follow-up SLO examinations showed no vitreous or retinal complications, such as vitreous hemorrhage, vitreous degeneration or proliferation, retinal holes, retinal detachment or other serious complications.

Discussion

Physiological vitreous floaters are a common ophthalmic disease. Although it is generally believed that the symptoms caused by vitreous floaters are not particularly troubling, some studies have challenged this view. They measured the degree of depression, perceived stress, anxiety and other discomfort related to vitreous floaters with a self-administered questionnaire and found that patients with vitreous floaters have a greater degree of psychological distress, and their floater severity was closely related to their degree of distress [7, 8]. For some patients, symptoms of vitreous floaters may persist and be considered acceptable. However, some patients have severe symptoms that seriously affect their visual and life quality. They urgently need safe and noninvasive treatment [9, 10].

In recent years, Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis has gained great popularity and it provides a fast, relatively cheap, noninvasive method for the treatment of vitreous floaters [11]. Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis can reduce or even eliminate the floating substances in the vitreous body by using a 1064 nm laser to cut the vitreous cord and vaporize the collagen and hyaluronic acid molecules within the floating substance [12, 13]. During the treatment process, bubbles will be generated. Because of the rapid cooling of the bubbles, the energy transfer is mainly mechanical, and there is almost no detectable thermal damage outside the lesion [14]. Because of its fast and noninvasive operation, Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis has been favored by an increasing number of patients with physiological vitreous floaters, and its curative effect has been confirmed by some clinical studies [12, 15]. However, during the process of laser treatment, the application of excessive laser energy or an incorrect treatment position may lead to complications affecting visual quality, such as glaucoma, hypertensive eyes, cataracts, posterior capsule injury, retinal tears, retinal detachment, retinal hemorrhage and the creation of new floating objects [16–19]. People’s doubts about the effectiveness of Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis and worries about these potential visual complications prevent the wide application of the technique to a certain extent.

Effectiveness of Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis

In previous studies of the effectiveness of Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis, most evaluated its success by subjective patient questionnaires [5, 20]. A previous study showed that among 52 patients (36 cases in the experimental group and 16 cases in the control group) with clinical manifestations of Weiss ring and symptoms for over 6 months, 19 patients (53%) who received Nd: YAG laser treatment had significant or complete elimination of symptoms, while the control group who did not receive treatment did not [5]. Participants were examined 30 min after surgery and followed up 1 week, 1 month, 3 months and 6 months later. The National Academy of Ophthalmology visual function questionnaire 25 (Nei VFQ-25) score of patients receiving laser treatment was better for general and peripheral vision, the symptom remission was better, and the visual impairment score (0-100% scale) improved more.

In addition to subjective evaluation of the effect of Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis in the treatment of vitreous floaters, there are many studies seeking objective evaluation indexes to quantify and evaluate the degree of visual impairment experienced by patients [11, 21]. It has been reported that with the help of a new type of optical coherence tomography (OCT), vitreous floating objects can form shadows on retinal OCT images. ImageJ software can be used to quantify the shadow size on the retinal OCT images, and the effectiveness can be evaluated by the shadow size changes before and after vitreolysis [22]. In addition, in patients with vitreous floaters, especially in the presence of PVD, the contrast sensitivity will decrease [11]. In the above studies, there were some floating objects that were sometimes irregular in appearance and scattered in three dimensions, but casting shadows could not fully reflect the size and shape of the actual floating objects.

In this study, infrared fundus photography was used to objectively evaluate the changes in floater size before and after laser treatment. Infrared fundus photography is a new method of using confocal laser scanning ophthalmoscopy to obtain fundus images with an 820 nm laser. There is no need for contrast agent imaging, and patients do not have any risk of fluorescence contrast agent allergy. Because of its wavelength characteristics and strong penetration, infrared fundus photography can clearly delineate the boundary of the lesion, which is better than ordinary fundus photography. It needs low illumination intensity but the patients cannot recognize infrared light, there is no stimulation when taking the photos, and they feel comfortable. In addition, the refractive adjustment function of the Heidelberg synchronous confocal laser fundus fluorescein contrast instrument can focus dynamic imaging directly on the vitreous floating object and clearly observe the size and shape of the vitreous floaters (as shown in Fig. 3). ImageJ software can be used to quantify the size of the projected shadows on the infrared fundus photography image, and the effectiveness of treatment can be evaluated by observing the changes in the size of the vitreous floaters projected shadows before and after treatment. In this study, on the first day after Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis, infrared fundus photography was performed. The vitreous opacity was significantly reduced or had completely disappeared in all of the treated eyes, and the effective rate was 100%. The patient’s subjective symptoms were significantly improved. Twenty-eight eyes in 30 eyes were treated once, and 2 eyes were treated twice (one week later). The mean number of treatments was 1.06 times. The degree of cooperation of the patients is related to the need for a second treatment. The results of this study are consistent with the results of the previous effectiveness study discussed above. Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis is obviously effective in the treatment of physiological vitreous floaters.

Safety of Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis

Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis provides a new treatment option for patients with physiological vitreous floaters. However, in addition to the clinical efficacy, the occurrence of complications after laser treatment also needs attention. There are many reports about the complications of Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis [19]. Reports of these complications have also aroused concern about Nd: YAG laser treatment. Whether Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis will damage the retina and cause serious complications and how to reduce the occurrence of complications needs additional studies. One study showed that there was no significant change in the macular fovea thickness or nerve fiber layer thickness around the optic disc before and after vitreolysis, suggesting that the treatment did not cause significant changes to the retinal structure over a short period [23]. However, nearly normal fundus performance does not mean normal retinal function. Whether Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis affects retinal function still lacks an objective and quantitative basis.

FfERG is an objective and quantitative method used to measure retinal function. It can reflect the electrical activity of retinal cells from photoreceptors to amacrine cells. It can objectively evaluate the changes in retinal function in the entire visual field and provide a more accurate basis for the study of retinal function changes. It is the only currently available noninvasive examination that can objectively evaluate the overall retinal function [24]. ffERG is the electrophysiological response of the retina to light stimulation. Flash stimulation is transmitted to the cerebral cortex through the corneal contact electrode, resulting in a biphasic negative/positive waveform. The ffERG response wave is mainly comprised of the A wave, B wave and OPS oscillatory potential wave [25]. The A wave originates from rod cells. Oxidative stress and apoptosis of photoreceptor cells during OS layer reconstruction can cause rod cell damage. The A wave mainly reflects the function of the outer layer of the retina, that is, the optic cell layer [26, 27]. The B wave originates from the Müller cells and bipolar cells and has a positive potential following the wave. B waves depend on the integrity of signal transmission in the retina. Müller cells run throughout the entire retina and mainly play a role in signal transduction, while bipolar cells are located in the inner layer of the retina. The B wave mainly reflects the health status of the inner layer of the retina.

A group of low amplitude and high frequency oscillatory waves appearing on the rising phase of the B wave is called the oscillatory potential. The oscillatory potentials of OPS come from amacrine cells in the inner layer of the retina. Amacrine cells are mainly located in the inner plexiform layer, and the OPS reflect the activity of inner cells caused by the interaction between bipolar cells, amacrine cells and ganglion cells and the blood circulation state of the inner layer of the retina. Whether OPS is normal depends on whether the circulation of the retina and choroid is normal. It has a certain value in the early diagnosis of fundus blood circulation diseases. OPS oscillatory potentials have been widely used in the study of early changes of retinal function during diabetic retinopathy [28].

Most retinal diseases are detected by the attenuation of the amplitude of the A wave and B wave. In some diseases, the latent periods of the A waves and B waves are also affected. In recent years, the wide application of ffERG has greatly enriched research on diabetic retinopathy (DR) [29], retinitis pigmentosa (RP) [30] and toxic retinopathy (TR). Electroretinograms can also be used to evaluate retinal foreign bodies and trauma and to further evaluate the degree of retinal dysfunction. In this study, there were no significant changes in the A wave, B wave or OPS oscillatory potential in the five reactions of ffERG before and after treatment, which indicated that Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis did not damage retinal function in a short time. Thus, this study confirmed that Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis is safe and effective in the treatment of physiological vitreous floaters in the short term. ffERG may be a powerful tool to evaluate the safety of Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis in the treatment of physiological vitreous floaters.

In this study, A/B ultrasound was used to accurately locate the distance between the vitreous floater and the posterior surface of the lens and retina before the operation. Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis was performed for physiological vitreous floaters 2–3 mm away from the lens and 3–4 mm away from the retina. The energy setting gradually increased from the lowest energy value, the initial laser energy was 3 mJ, and the maximum laser energy was not more than 7 mJ. There were no significant changes in visual acuity or IOP before and after the operation. SLO was performed after the operation, and no vitreous hemorrhage, vitreous degeneration, retinal hemorrhage, retinal detachment or other retinal injuries were found in all 30 eyes. No obvious lens damage was found by slit lamp. Therefore, the combined application of A/B ultrasound to locate vitreous floaters and the selection of appropriate patients for Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis treatment can effectively reduce the incidence of complications. Infrared fundus photography showed that the vitreous floaters decreased significantly after treatment. We strictly grasped the indications and contraindications and treated the patients according to the safe operation guidelines of Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis. After treatment, retinal function was evaluated objectively by ffERG. No significant changes in the vitreous and retinal structure were observed, which preliminarily proved the safety of Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis in the treatment of vitreous floaters in terms of retinal function.

This study has some limitations. The sample size of this study was small, the follow-up time was short, and there was no control group. Our small sample is not sufficient to identify any rare complications. Larger studies and longer follow-up times are necessary to verify the probability of adverse events related to Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis. Future studies need to be large, multicenter and long-term (2 year) randomized controlled trials specifically looking for complications. It is also expected that safer, more effective, durable and simplified drugs or treatment methods will become available in the clinic in the near future to treat floaters.

In conclusion, Nd: YAG laser vitreolysis is safe, simple and convenient for the treatment of physiological vitreous floaters. It can quickly improve the symptoms of floaters and relieve the anxiety of patients. It is worthy of clinical promotion.

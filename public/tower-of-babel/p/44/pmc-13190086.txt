Characteristics of patients qualified for aortic valve replacement combined with coronary artery bypass grafting surgery in the era of transcatheter aortic valve implantation dominance study (CAPTOR study)
Authors: Marek Reszczyński, Radosław Litwinowicz, Maciej Brzeziński, Bartłomiej Perek, Mariusz Kowalewski, Kazimierz Widenka, Krzysztof Bartuś, Marian Burysz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe growing use of transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) has significantly changed treatment strategies for patients with aortic stenosis (AS), especially those with coexisting coronary artery disease (CAD). However, the impact of this shift on surgical aortic valve replacement combined with coronary artery bypass grafting (AVR + CABG) remains unclear. This study aimed to evaluate temporal trends, clinical characteristics, perioperative outcomes, and 5-year survival in patients undergoing AVR + CABG in the context of increasing TAVI adoption in Poland between 2014 and 2023.MethodsThis retrospective, multicenter cohort study used data from the Polish National Cardiac Surgery Database. A total of 65,085 patients qualified for aortic valve intervention between 2014 and 2023 were analyzed. Baseline characteristics, procedural details, complication rates, and 5-year survival (Kaplan-Meier method) were assessed. AVR + CABG trends were compared to TAVI volumes over time.ResultsThe proportion of patients undergoing AVR + CABG decreased by 25% over the study period, while TAVI procedures increased fivefold. AVR + CABG patients became younger and predominantly male, with higher prevalence of hypertension and hyperlipidemia but improved functional status. Despite longer cross-clamp times and changes in prosthesis size, perioperative mortality and complication rates remained stable. Mid-term survival was not significantly affected. The growing use of percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with TAVI likely influenced these trends.ConclusionsDespite declining volumes, AVR + CABG remains an effective and safe option for selected patients. In the evolving landscape of valve disease treatment, multidisciplinary Heart Team decision-making and further comparative studies are essential.Keywords: Aortic stenosis (AS), coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), outcomes, surgery, transcatheter valve implantation

Highlight box

Key findings

• Patients undergoing combined aortic valve replacement combined with coronary artery bypass grafting (AVR + CABG) in Poland represent an increasingly selected, high-risk population in the contemporary transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) era.

• Over time, surgical patients demonstrated a higher burden of comorbidities and more advanced disease at the time of intervention.

• Despite this shift, early and mid-term outcomes remained acceptable and clinically relevant.

What is known and what is new?

• The expansion of TAVI has significantly changed the landscape of aortic valve interventions, with lower-risk patients increasingly treated percutaneously.

• This study provides large-scale, real-world data demonstrating a clear shift in the surgical AVR + CABG population toward higher-risk and more complex cases in the TAVI era.

• It highlights temporal trends in patient characteristics and outcomes in a national cohort.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Surgical AVR + CABG should be considered in the context of careful patient selection, particularly in patients with complex coronary artery disease.

• The findings support the complementary role of surgery and transcatheter approaches rather than direct competition.

• Future strategies should focus on optimizing Heart Team decision-making and integrating surgical and transcatheter pathways in high-risk patients.

Introduction

The advent of transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) represented a paradigm-shifting breakthrough in the management of aortic stenosis (AS), particularly among high-risk patients deemed unsuitable for conventional surgical aortic valve replacement (SAVR) (1). Today, TAVI has emerged as the predominant therapeutic strategy for AS, setting new benchmarks in care owing to its minimally invasive nature, shorter hospital stays, and expedited recovery compared to SAVR (1,2). Continuous technological advancements, favorable short- and long-term outcomes, and the growing proficiency of multidisciplinary heart teams have contributed to the broadening of TAVI indications, now encompassing younger patients and those with fewer comorbidities (1). In highly developed healthcare systems, TAVI has redefined the treatment landscape of AS, rendering the procedure more accessible and safer for a wider patient population.

Despite the groundbreaking impact of TAVI on the treatment of AS, comprehensive studies remain lacking on how the widespread adoption of this technique has influenced the management of comorbid conditions that significantly worsen the prognosis of patients with AS. Among these, the most prevalent is coronary artery disease (CAD) (2). It is estimated that approximately 40–75% of patients with severe AS are concurrently diagnosed with CAD (3,4), and its coexistence may increase long-term mortality risk by up to 30% (5). The frequent overlap of AS and CAD, particularly in elderly populations, necessitates a coordinated and individualized therapeutic strategy aimed at optimizing clinical outcomes.

Objective

To analyze changes in the frequency, clinical profile, perioperative outcomes, and 5-year survival of patients undergoing surgical aortic valve replacement combined with coronary artery bypass grafting (AVR + CABG) in the context of increasing TAVI adoption, using data from a national cardiac surgery registry spanning 2014–2023. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2025-aw-2100/rc).

Methods

The CAPTOR study (Characteristics of patients qualified for aortic valve replacement combined with coronary artery bypass grafting surgery in the era of transcatheter aortic valve implantation dominance study) utilized retrospective data from the Polish National Cardiac Surgery Database (the “KROK” registry; www.krok.csioz.gov.pl). KROK is a nationwide registry that includes all adult cardiac surgery departments in Poland. During the study period, 36 adult cardiac surgery centers were operating nationwide, and reporting to the KROK registry was mandatory for all centers. Data submission is directly linked to national reimbursement mechanisms, and centers are financially accountable for data completeness and accuracy. As a result, the registry captures virtually all adult cardiac surgical procedures performed in Poland, ensuring comprehensive national coverage and high representativeness of the analyzed population. The registry is maintained by the Club of Polish Cardiac Surgeons in collaboration with the Polish Ministry of Health. Participating centers are mandated to report all cardiac surgeries to a centralized database managed by the National Centre for Healthcare Information Systems and are held financially accountable for the completeness and accuracy of their data submissions (4). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Patients and methods

This was a retrospective, observational, multi-center cohort study including 65,085 patients. The inclusion criterion was a diagnosis of AS with qualification for aortic valve intervention between 2014 and 2023.

Database

A structured database was developed based on standardized definitions, including demographic information, medical history, on-admission clinical findings, and outcomes. Data were collected either at patient presentation or through physician review of hospital records and were subsequently submitted to the KROK registry. All submitted forms underwent review to ensure clinical face validity and analytical consistency.

Using the KROK database, we identified patients who underwent any form of aortic valve intervention between January 2014 and December 2023. This included isolated SAVR, TAVI, and complex procedures. Complex procedures were defined as SAVR performed in combination with CABG.

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were collected, including age, sex, type 2 diabetes status, body mass index (BMI), body surface area (BSA), hypertension, hyperlipidemia, CAD, chronic kidney disease, extracardiac arteriopathy, chronic lung disease, smoking history, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), Canadian Cardiovascular Society (CCS) and New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional classes, maximum preoperative aortic gradients, prosthesis size, extracorporeal circulation (ECC) time, aortic cross-clamping (ACC) time, intensive care unit (ICU) stay, total hospital stay, in-hospital mortality, and postoperative complication rates. In the KROK registry, CCS class 0 and NYHA class 0 are used to denote the absence of angina symptoms and the absence of heart failure-related functional limitation, respectively. Acute myocardial infarction (MI) was analyzed as recorded in the registry at presentation; this variable reflects local clinical coding and was not centrally adjudicated, and granular subtyping [e.g., ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) vs. non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI)] or standardized biomarker thresholds were not uniformly available across the study period.

Complications were defined as adverse in-hospital events related to cardiac surgery, including but not limited to: re-exploration for bleeding, stroke, surgical site infection, acute renal failure requiring dialysis, recurrent endocarditis, prolonged mechanical ventilation, thromboembolic events, and in-hospital death. In-hospital mortality was defined as death occurring within 30 days of admission. Follow-up mortality was defined as death from any cause occurring within 5 years. Mortality data were obtained from the National Health Fund, Poland’s mandatory public health insurance institution.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Data are presented as means and were compared using analysis of variance (ANOVA) or the Kruskal-Wallis test, as appropriate. Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages and compared using the chi-square test. Five-year survival was assessed using the Kaplan-Meier method. Analyses were performed using available-case data for each variable; no imputation methods were applied. The extent of missing data reflects routine registry reporting and represents an inherent limitation of registry-based studies. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.0.4; Vienna, Austria, 2021) and IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows (version 26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). A two-sided P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Procedure

From a cohort of 224,358 consecutive adult patients who underwent cardiac surgery between 2014 and 2023, 65,085 were identified as eligible for treatment of AS. Among all patients, TAVI was performed in 6.36%, isolated SAVR in 22.65%, simultaneous SAVR and CABG in 5.67%, and other complex procedures involving AS in 9.41%. Over the 10-year observation period, a statistically significant increase in TAVI procedures and a decrease in AVR + CABG procedures were noted. Detailed data are shown in Figure 1. When analyzed in relation to all SAVR procedures, the proportion of AVR + CABG among SAVR cases demonstrated a progressive decline over the study period, consistent with increasing adoption of alternative revascularization strategies in the era of TAVI. Importantly, this reduction occurred despite relatively stable overall SAVR volumes, indicating a selective decrease in complex combined surgical procedures rather than a general decline in surgical aortic valve activity.

Patient characteristics

The average age of patients undergoing AVR + CABG decreased significantly over the study period, from 69.28 years in 2014 to 68.30 years in 2023 (P=0.001). A notable shift in sex distribution was observed: the proportion of women declined from 30.59% [2014–2015] to 20.84% in 2023, while the percentage of male patients increased to 78.61% (P=0.002).

Functional status improved over time, with the proportion of patients in NYHA class IV decreasing from 4.31% [2015] to 2.23% in 2023 (P<0.05). The prevalence of CCS class IV symptoms remained low but showed a modest increase, ranging from 1.71% [2018] to 2.25% [2023] (P<0.05).

Comorbidities became more prevalent: hypertension increased from 81.03% in 2014 to 92.32% in 2023 (P<0.05), and hyperlipidemia rose from 63.73% to 75.58% over the same period (P<0.05).

Left ventricular function showed improvement, with the mean LVEF increasing significantly from 49.21% in 2014 to 51.34% in 2023 (P<0.05).

Hospital stay, operative parameters, and types of valves

The average length of hospital stay for patients undergoing AVR + CABG remained stable over the study period, ranging from 13.24 days in 2014 to 14.63 days in 2023 (P=0.12). In contrast, the mean duration of ICU stay increased significantly, from 3.83 days in 2014 to 4.10 days in 2023 (P=0.01).

Hemodynamic performance, as reflected by the mean pressure gradient (MPG) across the aortic valve, demonstrated a non-significant downward trend, decreasing from 67.69 mmHg in 2014 to 64.26 mmHg in 2023 (P=0.10). The average prosthesis size increased significantly during the same period, from 22.84 to 23.56 mm (P<0.05), suggesting a trend toward implanting larger valve sizes.

The average ECC time remained relatively stable, ranging between 132.15 minutes [2018] and 139.39 minutes [2023], with no statistically significant variation over time (P=0.12). However, the mean aortic cross-clamp time increased significantly, from 95.84 minutes in 2014 to 105.66 minutes in 2023 (P<0.05), indicating a modest prolongation in the intraoperative period.

The proportion of reoperative procedures increased from 1.09% in 2016 to a peak of 2.42% in 2020. However, this upward trend was not statistically significant (P=0.80).

Complications and mortality

The overall complication rate demonstrated a statistically significant decrease over the study period, falling from 29.86% in 2014 to 27.27% in 2023 (P<0.05). Perioperative mortality, defined as any death occurring within 30 days post-surgery regardless of cause or location, ranged from 5.58% [2023] to a peak of 8.05% [2016]. Despite these fluctuations, no significant temporal trend was observed (P=0.06).

Discussion

AS is the most common primary valvular disease requiring either conventional or transcatheter intervention. Due to the aging population, particularly in developed countries, the number of diagnosed cases continues to rise (6,7). TAVI has enabled treatment even in patients at extremely high surgical risk, thereby expanding access to intervention. The availability of both surgical and transcatheter techniques has contributed to an overall increase in the number of AS patients undergoing procedures (8).

The aging population also contributes to the frequent coexistence of other conditions, particularly CAD. It is estimated that approximately 40–75% of patients with severe AS also have concomitant CAD (3,4). This overlap complicates clinical decision-making. In cases where AS is accompanied by uncomplicated CAD, a combined percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and TAVI strategy may be justified. These procedures can be performed either sequentially or concomitantly, depending on the clinical status, severity of CAD, and anatomical significance of the affected vessel (9).

The increase in TAVI procedures observed in our study is consistent with both international and national trends. According to data from the STS/ACC TVT Registry (Society of Thoracic Surgeons-American College of Cardiology Transcatheter Valve Therapy Registry), the number of TAVI procedures in the United States has more than doubled over the past decade, with TAVI surpassing SAVR as the most commonly performed intervention for AS (7).

In Poland, a similarly dynamic growth has been documented. Based on the latest report from the Polish Cardiac Society and the National Health Fund, the number of TAVI procedures increased from approximately 500 in 2014 to over 3,500 in 2022—reflecting nearly a sevenfold increase in less than a decade (10,11).

This trend is driven by multiple factors, including technological advancements, increasing clinical experience, expanding indications for lower-risk patients, and favorable short- and long-term outcomes.

These data confirm that the rise in TAVI is not a transient phenomenon but a lasting shift in clinical practice patterns. Given current projections, this upward trend is expected to continue in the foreseeable future (8,10,11).

The observed increase in ICU length of stay over time should be interpreted cautiously. Although functional status indicators improved, patients undergoing AVR + CABG increasingly represented a more complex surgical cohort in the era of expanding TAVI adoption. This complexity may not be fully captured by functional measures alone and could reflect a higher burden of comorbidities or more advanced coronary disease. Additionally, organizational and pathway-related factors, including evolving postoperative monitoring strategies, changes in ICU discharge criteria, and greater emphasis on early complication detection, may have contributed to prolonged ICU stays. Importantly, this trend was not accompanied by deterioration in mid-term survival, suggesting preserved overall procedural safety.

The gradual increase in implanted prosthesis size observed in the present analysis should be interpreted cautiously. While this trend may reflect evolving sizing strategies, improved preoperative imaging, or changing surgical preferences, the registry does not capture detailed procedural information such as aortic root or annular enlargement techniques. Therefore, it is not possible to determine whether prosthesis upsizing reflects anatomical modification or other factors related to case mix and surgical decision-making. This finding highlights both the evolution of surgical practice and the inherent limitations of registry-based analyses.

While several studies have attempted to analyze the impact of TAVI advancements on the clinical profiles and outcomes of patients undergoing SAVR, the specific influence of the increasing adoption of the TAVI combined with PCI strategy on the population traditionally treated with AVR + CABG remains insufficiently understood (12,13). It should be also highlighted, that in the era of TAVI dominance, patients referred for AVR + CABG increasingly represent a negatively selected surgical cohort—i.e., individuals who are less suitable for transcatheter therapy due to anatomical or clinical factors and/or the need for complete surgical revascularization.

Therefore, the present study aims to address this gap by analyzing trends and outcomes in this patient population over the past decade based on real-life data. Although several baseline characteristics changed over time, the overall operative risk as assessed by EuroSCORE II remained stable; therefore, these findings are interpreted as a shift in patient profile rather than a global improvement in surgical risk. Despite significant temporal changes in patient characteristics and procedural patterns, mid-term survival after AVR + CABG remained stable throughout the study period. This observation suggests that advances in perioperative care, surgical techniques, and patient selection may have mitigated the potential impact of increasing procedural complexity. Importantly, these findings support the continued role of surgical revascularization combined with valve replacement in appropriately selected patients, even in the era of expanding transcatheter therapies.

The increasingly widespread use of the PCI + TAVI strategy, observed both in the analyzed national data and in the international literature, is also supported by the results of two important clinical trials published in 2024—TCW and NOTION-3 (14,15).

The TCW trial, the first randomized comparison of the TAVI + PCI strategy vs. conventional AVR + CABG, demonstrated not only non-inferiority but even superiority of the transcatheter approach in terms of overall mortality, major bleeding events, and the composite primary endpoint at 12 months (14). Notably, this advantage was particularly evident among older patients classified as high surgical risk.

Meanwhile, NOTION-3, which included patients with significant AS and coexisting CAD, provided evidence of the clinical benefit of performing PCI prior to TAVI, specifically in reducing the incidence of MI and the need for urgent revascularization, without an increase in overall mortality (15). At the same time, a higher incidence of bleeding in the PCI group highlights the need for individualized therapeutic strategies.

Nevertheless, due to the limitations of both studies (including short follow-up duration and relatively small sample size in TCW), further long-term comparative analyses—including randomized trials—are warranted to assess the effectiveness of both strategies in terms of survival and quality of life outcomes (14,15).

The analysis of data from 2014–2023 revealed significant changes in the characteristics of patients qualified for AVR + CABG procedures and the number of performed surgeries. The most noticeable trend is a significant increase in TAVI procedures alongside a decrease in AVR + CABG surgeries.

Over the study period, the number of AVR + CABG procedures decreased by approximately 25%, reflecting a substantial shift in clinical practice. In contrast, the number of TAVI procedures increased more than fivefold. This inverse relationship illustrates the progressive substitution of traditional surgical approaches with transcatheter techniques, particularly in older and more comorbid patients.

Similar proportions have been reported in international studies. In the United States, data from the STS/ACC TVT Registry indicated that TAVI has surpassed SAVR in volume since 2019, with TAVI accounting for over 55% of all aortic valve interventions (8). In Western European countries such as Switzerland and Finland, TAVI now represents more than 60% of all aortic valve replacements (14).

In Poland, the percentage of TAVI procedures among all aortic valve interventions also continues to grow, increasing from less than 10% in 2014 to over 25% by 2022 (10,11). Nevertheless, the number of AVR + CABG procedures still remain clinically relevant, particularly for patients with complex coronary anatomy or anatomical limitations for TAVI.

These data support the hypothesis that the growing preference for TAVI, including in lower-risk populations, directly impacts the volume and demographic profile of patients undergoing AVR + CABG procedures. This is consistent with global observations indicating the growing role of TAVI as the preferred treatment method for AS, particularly in high- and intermediate-risk patients (1,2).

Over the analyzed decade, a decrease in the average age of patients undergoing AVR + CABG and a reduction in the proportion of women in this group were observed. A possible explanation for this trend is the increased referral of older patients and women to TAVI procedures, which are associated with lower operative risk and shorter hospitalization times (8). At the same time, despite a decline in the proportion of patients in the highest NYHA IV and CCS 4 classes, the prevalence of comorbidities such as hypertension and hyperlipidemia increased. This may suggest that the qualification for AVR + CABG has become more selective and that these procedures are increasingly performed in patients with a more stable clinical profile. The stability of surgical outcomes, including the absence of a significant increase in mortality, indicates that despite changes in patient characteristics, the effectiveness of AVR + CABG remains high.

The observed increase in the average size of implanted aortic prostheses and the prolonged ACC time may reflect the adoption of surgical techniques that allow for more tailored prosthesis selection based on patient-specific anatomy. The use of advanced imaging modalities and improved surgical instruments may also have contributed to better procedural outcomes and a reduction in postoperative complications (12). The gradual increase in implanted prosthesis size observed over the study period likely reflects multiple factors related to evolving surgical practice. Potential explanations include improved valve sizing strategies, advances in preoperative imaging and annular assessment, and greater awareness of patient-prosthesis mismatch. It should be noted that the KROK registry does not include a dedicated variable capturing the use of aortic root or annular enlargement techniques (e.g., Nicks, Manouguian, or Konno procedures); therefore, it cannot be determined whether the observed increase in prosthesis size reflects wider adoption of enlargement techniques or other factors such as improved imaging, sizing strategies, or changes in case mix.

Simultaneously, the growing role of the PCI + TAVI strategy as an alternative to AVR + CABG warrants further investigation. The optimal treatment approach for patients with AS and concomitant CAD remains an area of active discussion. Prior studies suggest that in selected patient cohorts, PCI + TAVI may be comparable to AVR + CABG, particularly in terms of reduced hospitalization duration and procedural invasiveness (8). However, long-term comparative analyses are needed to evaluate the relative impact of both strategies on survival and quality of life.

The evolving clinical profile of patients undergoing AVR + CABG underscores the need for continued adaptation of treatment strategies. Establishing a multidisciplinary Heart Team—comprising cardiac surgeons, interventional cardiologists, and anesthesiologists—is essential to guide individualized therapeutic decisions (13). Future research should focus on long-term outcome comparisons between AVR + CABG and PCI + TAVI in patients with AS and coexisting CAD. Additionally, there is an increasing need to assess the influence of emerging surgical innovations—such as minimally invasive approaches, advanced myocardial protection techniques, and improved conduit materials—on the safety and efficacy of AVR + CABG. Finally, refining patient selection through enhanced risk stratification models and personalized treatment algorithms will be crucial to improve outcomes in this heterogeneous patient population.

The observed procedural trends should also be interpreted in the context of the updated 2025 EACTS Guidelines, which further expand indications for TAVI, including patients aged 70 years and older. In this evolving treatment landscape, SAVR—particularly when combined with CABG—continues to play a crucial role in patients with concomitant CAD or anatomical features less suitable for transcatheter therapy. Our findings provide real-world, registry-based insight into how these guideline-driven changes may translate into contemporary surgical practice.

This study has several limitations. First, the results are limited to a population of Polish patients drawn from the Polish National Cardiac Surgery Database, which may reduce the generalizability of the findings to other countries with different healthcare systems, referral pathways, and patient demographics. Second, the retrospective nature of the analysis precludes definitive causal inferences and may introduce unmeasured confounding factors. Third, the registry lacks granular procedural data on aortic root or annular enlargement techniques, which limits the ability to analyze their potential contribution to temporal changes in prosthesis sizing and operative times. Next, long-term outcomes—including survival and quality of life—were not analyzed, limiting the ability to evaluate the durability and effectiveness of AVR + CABG over time. Moreover, the study did not assess the frequency or outcomes of combined TAVI and PCI procedures due to the absence of detailed procedural coding in the national database. This limits the capacity to fully evaluate the impact of the increasing adoption of the TAVI + PCI strategy on the volume and clinical profile of patients undergoing surgical AVR + CABG. The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was presented for descriptive purposes only and was not intended to provide comparative or risk-adjusted survival assessment. Detailed coronary anatomical descriptors, including SYNTAX score, lesion complexity, and left main involvement, were not available in the registry in a standardized manner across the entire study period, precluding analysis of coronary disease severity over time. Reported rates of acute MI should therefore be interpreted in the context of registry-based clinical coding and may not be directly comparable to adjudicated MI rates from other national or international datasets. The absence of standardized data on surgical urgency and detailed graft-related variables represents an additional limitation inherent to the registry-based design of the study.

Future research should focus on incorporating prospective data, extended follow-up, and comparative effectiveness studies—including randomized controlled trials—comparing AVR + CABG with alternative treatment strategies such as TAVI + PCI. These investigations are essential for informing clinical decision-making and for the development of evidence-based guidelines for the optimal management of patients with concomitant AS and CAD.

The upward trajectory of TAVI procedures, both globally and in Poland, supports the hypothesis that this method will continue to gain prevalence. This ongoing shift is likely to further reshape the clinical landscape of surgical treatment for AS and concomitant CAD, reinforcing the need for continuous adaptation of treatment strategies and evidence-based recommendations.

Conclusions

Over the past decade, both the volume and clinical profile of patients undergoing AVR + CABG in Poland have undergone significant changes. Most notably, the number of AVR + CABG procedures declined by approximately 25% between 2014 and 2023, reflecting a profound shift in clinical practice. This reduction has paralleled a dynamic increase in the adoption of TAVI, in line with a broader global trend toward less invasive treatment strategies for AS.

Despite the decreasing procedural volume, AVR + CABG continues to play a pivotal role in the management of patients with severe AS and complex CAD, offering durable outcomes and complete revascularization in appropriately selected cases. Notably, perioperative mortality and complication rates remained stable over the decade, despite evolving patient demographics and increasing comorbidity burden—highlighting consistent procedural safety and effectiveness.

The changing characteristics of patients—marked by a trend toward younger age, lower symptom burden, and greater prevalence of cardiovascular risk factors—suggest a more selective and refined approach to surgical qualification. These trends emphasize the importance of multidisciplinary Heart Team decision-making to ensure optimal treatment allocation.

In the context of expanding indications for TAVI + PCI, these findings underscore the urgent need for prospective, long-term comparative studies—including randomized controlled trials—to identify the most effective and durable treatment strategies for patients with concomitant AS and CAD. Only through robust evidence can clinical guidelines be meaningfully updated to match the evolving realities of modern cardiovascular care.

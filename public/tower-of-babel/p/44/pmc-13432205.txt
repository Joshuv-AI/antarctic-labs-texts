Editorial Commentary
Authors: Mohan S. Kamath
Published: 2026

The editorial team welcomes you to the second issue of the year, the April–June 2026 issue of the Journal of Human Reproductive Sciences. There has been a substantial increase in the submission of original manuscripts from investigators across the globe, which is a healthy sign for the journal’s growth.

The current issue has one review, six original articles and three case reports. The first narrative review, authored by the current editorial team, gives an editorial perspective on manuscript writing and we hope the readers find it useful for future manuscript preparation. A case–control study explores the risk associated with a specific cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen variant and recurrent pregnancy loss. Another cross-sectional study also explored the association between altered pulmonary function and psychological distress among women with polycystic ovarian syndrome.

In a large retrospective cohort study from India, the authors have presented the cumulative live births in women aged 40 years and above, following assisted reproductive technology (ART) treatment. Availability of such data can be helpful during counselling couples who are seeking treatment at an advanced age. Another retrospective cohort study investigated the role of specific acupuncture protocols in improving success rates following frozen embryo transfer.

In a prospective cohort study, the authors investigated the role of blastocyst morphological parameters in predicting ART success. In the field of andrology, the investigators explored the association between semen parameters and DNA fragmentation index as well as protamine deficiency.

Finally, we have three case reports, and the first case report highlights a potential obstetric complication following resection of a deep infiltrating endometriosis nodule. In the second case report, the authors have reported a rare chromosomal abnormality as a possible aetiology in a male with non-obstructive azoospermia. In the final case report, the authors report a rare case of balanced translocation in a case of recurrent pregnancy loss.

As always, we invite experts and experienced authors in the field of reproductive medicine to join as peer reviewers and support the growth of our journal. We are looking forward to receiving our second impact factor in the next month.

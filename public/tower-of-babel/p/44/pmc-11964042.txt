Prevalence and Clinical Characteristics of Heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys Variant Carriers Diagnosed With Chronic Thromboembolic Pulmonary Hypertension
Authors: Takatoyo Kiko, Ryotaro Asano, Tomohiko Ishibashi, Hiroyuki Endo, Naruhiro Nishi, Hiroya Hayashi, Jin Ueda, Tatsuo Aoki, Akihiro Tsuji, Yoshikazu Nakaoka, Takeshi Ogo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRing finger protein 213 (RNF213) p.Arg4810Lys is a susceptibility gene for moyamoya disease, peripheral pulmonary artery stenosis (PPS), and other vascular diseases and thrombosis. We investigated the prevalence and clinical characteristics of RNF213 variant carriers diagnosed with chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH).Methods and ResultsWe retrospectively analyzed the prevalence of the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant in patients diagnosed with CTEPH (n=112) and PPS (n=10). Clinical and angiographic characteristics were evaluated between RNF213 variant carriers diagnosed with CTEPH and noncarriers with CTEPH and homozygous variant carriers with PPS. Eight heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers (7.1%) were identified among patients diagnosed with CTEPH, while 5 patients with PPS (50%) carried the homozygous variant. The clinical characteristics of heterozygous variant carriers with CTEPH were not remarkably different from those of noncarriers with CTEPH. All heterozygous variant carriers with CTEPH showed webs/bands lesions at the segmental/subsegmental level, with 75% showing distal tortuous vessels. None of the heterozygous variant carriers with CTEPH exhibited the string‐of‐beads pattern or elongated stenosis. Homozygous variant carriers with PPS showed the string‐of‐beads pattern, elongated stenosis, and distal tortuous vessels without webs/bands lesions.ConclusionsA subset of patients diagnosed with CTEPH (7.1%) carried the heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant. Clinical and angiographic characteristics of heterozygous variant carriers were not remarkably different from those of noncarriers of CTEPH. However, both heterozygous variant carriers with CTEPH and homozygous variant carriers with PPS showed tortuous vessels on angiography.

The ring finger protein 213 (RNF213) p.Arg4810Lys is a susceptibility gene for moyamoya disease in east Asian population. 1 The RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys plays a critical role in the development and maintenance of blood vessels, and mutations in this gene can disrupt normal vascular structure and function. The RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant may have a broader impact on various vascular diseases 2 and thromboembolism. 3 Additionally, several studies have reported the clinical features of peripheral pulmonary artery stenosis (PPS) with a homozygous variant in RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys. 4 , 5 , 6 The heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant was identified in 7.9% of patients with pulmonary artery hypertension (PAH). 7 However, the impact of the heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant on the pulmonary artery is not yet fully understood.

Chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension (CTEPH) is characterized by pulmonary artery obstruction due to chronic thromboembolism and microvasculopathy. 8 , 9 The diagnosis of CTEPH and PPS can be confusing 10 , 11 , 12 because of similarities in clinical symptoms and imaging findings. 13 PPS is defined by the presence of stenosis and narrowing of the pulmonary arteries, 10 , 14 and it is commonly found in pediatric patients with congenital heart disease and genetic syndromes. 15 PPS in adults may be distinguished because of its strong genetic foundation and systemic involvement. 16 The incidence of distal‐type CTEPH is higher in Japan than in Western countries, 17 making the differential diagnosis between PPS and CTEPH difficult.

We hypothesized that a subset of patients with the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant would be diagnosed with CTEPH. The aim of this study was to investigate the prevalence and clinical features of carriers of the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant diagnosed with CTEPH.

Methods

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Patient Selection and Measurements

We retrospectively analyzed data from 112 consecutive patients referred to our center with an initial diagnosis of CTEPH based on standard criteria 9 and 10 patients with PPS. DNA was collected from peripheral blood at our hospital between January 2001 and May 2021. Whole‐genome sequencing was performed using a NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA) to identify the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant. Clinical assessments of clinical data, brain natriuretic peptide levels, hemodynamics from right heart catheterization, enhanced computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA), and 6‐minute walk distance were performed simultaneously. Right ventricular function was evaluated using magnetic resonance imaging. Right heart catheterization was performed to measure mean pulmonary artery pressure, pulmonary artery wedge pressure, right atrial pressure, and pulmonary vascular resistance. The cardiac index was determined using the indirect Fick method and corrected for body surface area, and the pulmonary vascular resistance was calculated. Pulmonary hypertension was confirmed by right heart catheterization with mean pulmonary artery pressure > 20 mm Hg at rest. Pulmonary angiography was performed and classified into proximal and distal types according to the location of the thromboembolic lesion. Distal tortuous vessels were defined as highly tortuous small vessels distal to the subsegmental arteries according to a report by Kawakami et al. 18 Imaging findings indicative of CTEPH are characterized by the presence of pouch defects, webs and bands, intimal irregularities, abrupt narrowing, and complete obstruction. 19 Additionally, features, such as elongated stenosis and a string‐of‐beads pattern, are identified as PPS. 4 , 20 The findings of pulmonary angiogram and CTPA were visually interpreted by at least 2 experienced cardiologists, as described in the clinical reports. Written informed consent was obtained from all the patients, and the study conformed to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of our hospital (M30‐135).

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± SD or median (interquartile range). Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages and were compared using the Fisher's exact test. One‐way ANOVA was used to compare the continuous variables. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 29 software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Of the 112 patients initially diagnosed with CTEPH, the heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant was identified in 8 (7.1%), with no patients carrying the homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant. Of the 10 patients with PPS, the homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant was present in 5 (50%), with no patients carrying the heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant. According to the Integrative Japanese Genome Variation Database (Genome Cohort Study of the Tohoku Medical Megabank Organization), the allele frequency of the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant is 0.79% in the general Japanese population. This indicates a variant frequency ratio of 4.5 among patients initially diagnosed with CTEPH compared with the general population.

The baseline clinical characteristics of the study population are presented in Table 1. Patients carrying the homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant were younger than noncarriers with CTEPH and heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers. The incidence of cerebrovascular disease in patients with either heterozygous or homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variants was higher than that in noncarriers with CTEPH. Among the 2 heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with cerebrovascular disease, 1 had moyamoya disease and the other had middle cerebral artery stenosis. Of the 3 homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with cerebrovascular disease, 2 had moyamoya disease and 1 had anterior and middle cerebral artery stenosis with hypoplasia.

In the pulmonary angiogram, all patients carrying both heterozygous and homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variants showed mainly segmental or subsegmental pulmonary multiple stenosis, whereas 33.6% of patients with CTEPH without RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant had proximal‐type stenosis. The ratio of distal lesions was higher in carriers of the heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant than in those of noncarriers with CTEPH. Both carriers of the heterozygous variant with CTEPH (75%) and carriers of the homozygous variant with PPS (100%) had distal tortuous vessels, in contrast to noncarriers with CTEPH. Table 2 summarizes the imaging characteristics observed in the pulmonary angiogram and CTPA of heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with CTEPH and homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with PPS. Heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers exhibited webs and bands (100%) and intimal irregularities (50%), and none exhibited the string‐of‐beads pattern or elongated stenosis. In contrast, the angiograms of homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with PPS showed the string‐of‐beads pattern (80%) and elongated stenosis (100%), with no webs/bands or intimal irregularities.

Discussion

The results of our study demonstrate that (1) among patients diagnosed with CTEPH, 7.1% were identified as having a heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant; (2) the clinical and angiographic characteristics in heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with CTEPH were not remarkably different from those of noncarriers; and (3) heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with CTEPH or homozygous variants carriers with PPS exhibited distal tortuous vessels in the angiogram. These observations suggest that the heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant may be a risk factor for CTEPH. Nevertheless, the presence of distal tortuous vessels in heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with CTEPH suggests a shared mechanism underlying vascular abnormalities with homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with PPS.

In our study, 8 patients (7.1%) with heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant were diagnosed with CTEPH. The prevalence of RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant is high in Asia. 1 , 27 In Asian populations, the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant has been reported in the general population with an estimated frequency of 0.9% to 2.7%. 1 , 27 , 28 Patients with the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant have a higher prevalence of moyamoya disease and other cerebrovascular diseases, 29 , 30 which is consistent with the results of our study. Furthermore, carriers with either heterozygous or homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variants exhibited a higher incidence of distal tortuous lesions, while noncarriers with CTEPH did not. The incidence of peripheral lesions in CTEPH is higher in Japan than in Western countries. 31 These findings suggest that the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant influences various clinical phenotypes of several pulmonary vascular diseases in the Asian population. However, the CTEPH genome‐wide association study did not identify any RNF213 variant, 32 potentially due to the low number of Asian participants. We highlight the importance of addressing this gap in future studies by incorporating more ethnically diverse samples, which could provide a more comprehensive understanding of the genetic underpinnings of CTEPH.

The heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant may be a possible risk factor for CTEPH. In the present study, the pulmonary lesions observed in heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers, as evaluated by pulmonary angiography and CTPA, exhibited similarities to those observed in CTEPH, including webs and bands, intimal irregularities, and abrupt narrowing. None of heterozygous variant carriers with CTEPH exhibited the string‐of‐beads pattern or elongated stenosis. Conversely, in homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with PPS, the typical features of webs and bands and intimal irregularities commonly observed in CTEPH were absent. This observation is consistent with previous research demonstrating the absence of web or band formation in patients with PPS. 11 , 20 Additionally, we observed that homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers exhibited the string‐of‐beads pattern or elongated stenosis, indicating negative remodeling in the pulmonary artery. Furthermore, the clinical characteristics of heterozygous variant carriers were not remarkably different from those of noncarriers with CTEPH. These findings indicate that heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers exhibited clinical and angiographic characteristics similar to those observed in CTEPH, rather than PPS. Moreover, in our study, the percentage of heterozygous variant carriers associated with a history of acute pulmonary embolism was higher than that of noncarriers with CTEPH. The RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant may be associated with an elevated risk of thromboembolic events leading to CTEPH. Moyamoya disease with the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant results in pathological changes including luminal thrombosis or thromboembolism in the vessels and smooth‐muscle hyperplasia. 33 , 34 The heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant may simply lead to higher rates of CTEPH through increased thrombosis or increased susceptibility to pulmonary hypertension following thrombosis.

Heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with CTEPH may share the mechanism underlying vascular abnormalities with homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with PPS. Closer angiographic examination revealed that heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers had peculiar tortuous vessels in the distal pulmonary artery, which may have been overlooked in non–pulmonary hypertension–specialized centers. Similar tortuosity was also observed in homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with PPS. RNF213 encodes a large protein, mysterin, containing dual AAA+ ATPase domains and an E3 ligase domain, which plays an important role in regulating vascular remodeling, endothelial barrier functions, and angiogenesis. These characteristics of the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant may have implications in various vascular diseases, leading to the proposal of the concept “RNF213‐associated vascular disease.” 2 Recently, several studies have demonstrated the presence and features of PPS in the homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant. 4 , 13 Therefore, the heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant may represent an unrecognized phenotype of PPS. However, this may be matter of contention because the clinical and angiographic characteristics, except for the presence of distal tortuous vessels, in heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with CTEPH do not align with those in homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with PPS.

The precise mechanisms underlying the development of moyamoya disease and vasculopathy in pulmonary arteries remain unclear. The presence of carriers with the heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant among patients with CTEPH is intriguing, as is the presence of both thrombi and tortuous lesions, reminiscent of the cerebrovascular pathology of moyamoya disease. The prevalence of RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys heterozygosity in the present study, which aligns with the reported prevalence of 7.9% in a Japanese cohort of patients with PAH, 7 suggests a broader effect of this variant on pulmonary vascular diseases at various levels of the pulmonary artery tree. This variant may increase susceptibility to pulmonary hypertension, leading to higher rates of CTEPH following pulmonary embolism. Given the overlapping underlying mechanisms of the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant, the pulmonary arteries in heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers may exhibit various phenotypes of the currently recognized conditions: CTEPH, PPS, and PAH.

The clinical impact of the heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant in patients diagnosed with CTEPH remains unknown. We may be treating patients with distal tortuous lesions of CTEPH without recognizing the influence of the variant in the Asian population due to similar features. Patients with idiopathic PAH who carry the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant respond poorly to combination therapy with PAH‐specific vasodilators. 7 Furthermore, instant and early reocclusions are more frequent after endovascular therapy in patients with ischemic stroke who are carriers of the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant than in noncarriers. 35 Vascular remodeling associated with the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant may be less responsive to medication or endovascular therapy. From a genetic perspective, the presence of the variant in patients diagnosed with CTEPH may also have an important impact on prognosis or endovascular therapy, such as balloon pulmonary angioplasty. Nevertheless, for patients with idiopathic PPS with a higher prevalence of the homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant, 6 transcatheter pulmonary angioplasty and PAH‐targeted drugs may be effective. 13 In these studies, several patients underwent stent placement. Further studies are necessary to characterize the prognosis and response to pulmonary vasodilators and balloon pulmonary angioplasty in heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers diagnosed with the distal type of CTEPH.

The current study had several limitations. First, it was a single‐center study with a relatively small number of patients. The small sample size of our study significantly limited the statistical power to detect differences between groups. This limitation implies that some potentially important associations may not have been accurately detected or estimated. Future prospective studies with larger sample sizes are warranted to validate our findings and provide more robust evidence for the association with RNF213 variants. Second, our study acknowledges the possibility of sampling errors owing to the potentially higher baseline prevalence of RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys heterozygosity in our regional population. Further studies are required to accurately establish the allele frequency of the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant and its specific penetrance. Third, this study did not provide information on the mechanism underlying the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant in CTEPH. Intravascular lesions were not assessed using intravascular ultrasonography or optical coherence tomography. We acknowledge the importance of including a prospective imaging cohort to address the controversy regarding the classification of RNF213‐related pulmonary vascular diseases. A prospective study design would enable us to better understand the natural history, progression, and potential treatment responses in RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers. Moreover, histopathological assessment of the pulmonary artery was not performed. Future studies should aim to elucidate the specific pathways and mechanisms involved, which could pave the way for targeted therapeutic strategies for treating patients with the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant. The factors that may modify the phenotype of heterozygous carriers should be explored. The present study highlights the significance of genetic factors in vascular pathophysiology. It is suggested that exploring the role of the RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant may offer valuable insights.

In conclusion, among the patients diagnosed with CTEPH, 7.1% were identified as having a heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant. The clinical and angiographic characteristics of heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers were not remarkably different from those of noncarriers with CTEPH. In addition, heterozygous variant carriers did not exhibit typical findings of PPS. These observations suggest that the heterozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant may be a risk factor for CTEPH. Nevertheless, the presence of distal tortuous vessels in heterozygous variant carriers with CTEPH suggests a shared mechanism underlying vascular abnormalities with homozygous RNF213 p.Arg4810Lys variant carriers with PPS. Further studies are required to determine the significance of this variant in CTEPH. This should include a more comprehensive approach including histopathological observations and molecular analyses.

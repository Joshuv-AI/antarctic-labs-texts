Discovery of a peripherally restricted α2AAR agonist as a safe and broad-spectrum oral analgesic
Authors: Zhiqiang Cheng, Cedric Moore, Haijiao Li, Yang Xu, Larry Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe development of safe, non-opioid analgesics remains a pressing challenge. The α2A-adrenergic receptor (α2AAR) is a valid analgesic target, yet clinical translation has been hindered by dose-limiting central side effects. We discover CC10137, an orally bioavailable, peripherally restricted α2AAR agonist that produces a robust, dose-dependent anti-allodynic effect across eight murine pain models, without causing sedation, hypotension, or hypothermia. Co-administration of CC10137 (1 mg/kg, p.o.) and morphine (1 mg/kg s.c.) robustly reverses allodynia by 86% in a neuropathic pain model, surpassing either drug alone (∼45%) or a 3-fold higher morphine dose (3 mg/kg, s.c., 65%). This synergy is further replicated in a cancer pain model. Unlike morphine, CC10137 does not impair spinal nociceptive reflex in a tail-flick model, preserving an essential protective mechanism. CC10137 can thus selectively suppress allodynia under a broad spectrum of pathological pain conditions with a favorable safety profile, highlighting its potential as a standalone or combination therapeutic for chronic pain.Keywords: α2A-adrenergic receptor agonist, peripherally restricted, chronic pain, neuropathic pain, cancer pain, non-sedating analgesia, preserved protective nociception, opioid sparing, broad-spectrum

Introduction

The escalating public health crisis caused by opioid overuse highlights the need to develop safer and more effective non-opioid analgesics.1,2,3,4,5,6 While the recent approval of suzetrigine is a mark of significant progress for acute pain treatment, the control of chronic pain remains a formidable challenge.7,8 Among potential therapeutic targets, the α2A adrenergic receptor (α2AAR) stands out as clinically validated yet underexploited for chronic pain treatment, primarily due to persistent safety concerns.9,10,11 Although α2AAR activation produces potent analgesia, its clinical application has been constrained by dose-limiting, on-target side effects mediated by the central nervous system (CNS), such as sedation and hypotension.12 This limitation is well illustrated by clonidine, a systemic α2AAR agonist approved in 1999 for severe cancer pain inadequately controlled by opioids.13 When administered via epidural infusion, clonidine can double the rate of pain control, yet its use requires continuous medical monitoring for CNS-mediated side effects, a manifestation of the narrow therapeutic window imposed by its central activity.13,14 These constraints highlight the necessity of a strategy to unlock the full analgesic potential of α2AAR.

The major hurdle in dissociating α2AAR-mediated analgesia from its CNS-driven side effects lies in neuroanatomy: although α2AARs are expressed in peripheral tissues, their expression level is substantially higher in the CNS where activation leads to sedation, hypotension, and hypothermia.15,16 Recent attempts to discover non-sedative α2AAR activators have focused on systemically active molecules, including biased α2AAR agonists and selective α2BAR antagonists.17,18 Although these approaches can reduce sedation, they still impair spinal reflexes in tail-flick assays, indicating persistent central activity.17,18 Indeed, CNS activation by centrally acting analgesics likely impairs the spinal nociceptive reflex, an essential protective mechanism for higher organisms.19,20 Thus, a critical question remains: can sufficient analgesia be achieved through selective activation of peripheral α2AAR while minimizing CNS-mediated effects? To address this gap, we took an approach to develop a peripherally restricted α2AAR agonist, aiming for a paradigm shift in chronic pain management with a markedly improved safety profile.

We designed and synthesized 59 compounds by integrating P-glycoprotein (P-gp) substrate motifs into known α2AAR agonist scaffolds. Among them, CC10137 emerged as an orally bioavailable lead, demonstrating potent α2AAR agonist activity coupled with a high P-gp efflux potential. Critically, CC10137 did not induce sedation, hypotension, and hypothermia or impair spinal nociceptive reflex, a profile that marks a significant safety improvement over current systemic α2AAR agonists. Importantly, it demonstrated potent, dose-dependent anti-allodynia effects across eight murine pain models with different etiologies, including neuropathic pain induced by spared nerve injury (SNI), chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN), diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN), and postherpetic neuralgia (PHN); bone cancer pain induced by mouse NCTC-2572 cells and rat Walker 256 cells; inflammatory pain induced by complete Freund’s adjuvant (CFA); and postoperative pain (POP). Furthermore, CC10137 not only demonstrated robust standalone anti-allodynic efficacy but also revealed a synergistic anti-allodynic effect when co-administered with morphine or pregabalin. Collectively, this compelling preclinical evidence confirms that peripheral restriction of α2AAR agonist successfully dissociated its potent anti-allodynic effect from CNS side effects, thereby positioning the resulting first-in-class, orally bioavailable, peripherally restricted agonist, CC10137, as a development candidate for safe and effective chronic pain management.

Results

Design and identification of peripherally restricted α2AAR agonists

Through structural analysis of clonidine, dexmedetomidine, tizanidine, lofexidine, and tetrahydrozoline, all Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved systemic α2AAR agonists,17,18,21,22,23 we identified a conserved, highly lipophilic core scaffold comprising a substituted phenyl ring linked to a partially or fully unsaturated imidazole ring, a configuration that can easily cross the blood-brain barrier.9 To minimize CNS exposure while retaining α2AAR activation, we systematically introduced polar moieties designed to bias distribution toward peripheral tissues. These modifications also served as P-gp substrates, further limiting CNS penetration.24

Modification at the substituted phenyl ring (series A) abolished α2AAR agonist activity. In contrast, incorporation of polar groups via rationally designed linkers at the phenyl-imidazole bridge (series B) was well tolerated (Figure 1A). Further optimization revealed that phenyl linkers (series D and E) were able to optimally maintain potent α2AAR agonist activation. Comprehensive synthetic procedures and structural characterization for all 59 compounds and a detailed structure-activity relationship (SAR) analysis are provided in Data S1.

To select a lead candidate, these compounds were screened against three key criteria: (1) potent agonist activity comparable to known α2AAR agonists, (2) high P-gp efflux ratio indicating peripheral restriction, and (3) favorable oral bioavailability (Table S1; agonist activity and P-gp efflux ratios are shown in Figure 1B). The detailed agonist activities, binding affinities, and P-gp efflux ratios for all compounds are provided in Method S1 and Table S1.

Among these compounds, CC10137 (Figure 1C, previously named D12-II) met all three criteria. CC10137 demonstrated a high affinity for α2AAR, Ki = 6.0 nM, in a radioligand binding assay, making CC10137 a stronger binder for α2AAR than clonidine, Ki = 43.5 nM, or tizanidine, Ki = 295.8 nM, and matching the 5.5 nM affinity of dexmedetomidine (Figure 1D). Functionally, CC10137 was found to be a potent α2AAR agonist with a 13.1 nM EC50 in HEK293T cells co-transfected with α2AAR and Gα15 plasmids. This was comparable to the 21.2, 37.4, and 4.3 nM EC50 of clonidine, tizanidine, and dexmedetomidine, respectively (Figure 1E). Meanwhile, it demonstrated significant P-gp-mediated efflux (efflux ratio = 41.0), supporting its peripheral restriction, and displayed favorable oral bioavailability (37.9%; Figure S1).

We next evaluated the in vivo distribution of CC10137 following oral doses of 80 mg/kg and 10 mg/kg. At 80 mg/kg, the unbound brain Cmax was 14 nM, while unbound plasma concentrations remained above 1,050 nM at all tested time points (Tables S2 and S3). At the 10 mg/kg dose, unbound brain concentrations remained consistently below 0.7 nM, the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ), whereas unbound plasma Cmax reached 24.3 nM, approximately twice the EC50 (Figure S2). By comparison, systemic agonists (5 mg/kg dexmedetomidine, 5 mg/kg clonidine, and 10 mg/kg tizanidine, p.o.) showed significantly higher CNS exposure, with unbound brain Cmax of 441, 2,170, and 2,980 nM, respectively. The unbound brain-to-plasma partition coefficient (Kp,uu,brain) of CC10137 was 0.013, 90-fold lower than dexmedetomidine (1.17) and >50-fold lower than clonidine (0.74) and tizanidine (0.87) (Figure 2F). Consistent with these findings, total concentrations of CC10137 in brain, spinal cord, and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) all remained below the LLOQ at the 10 mg/kg dose.

CC10137 does not induce CNS-mediated side effects

Systemic α2AAR agonists such as clonidine are known to cause sedation, hypotension, and hypothermia in both preclinical and clinical studies.9 CC10137, being peripherally restricted, should not cause these side effects in the standard behavioral and physiological assays performed in C57BL/6J mice.

First, sedation was evaluated using rotarod and open-field tests to assess its impact on motor coordination and locomotor activity. The rotarod test was conducted first to rule out primary motor impairment before assessing spontaneous activity in the open-field assay. In the rotarod test, 1 mg/kg clonidine caused a significant 90.5% reduction in fall latency (Figure 2A), whereas 10 mg/kg CC10137 had no effect. Even at a low dose of 0.2 mg/kg, clonidine still markedly reduced motor performance, resulting in a 63.5% decrease in fall latency (Figure S3). During the open-field assay, 1 mg/kg clonidine reduced the total distance traveled relative to vehicle controls by 84.2% and demonstrates its profound sedative effect. Conversely, 10 mg/kg CC10137 had no significant impact (Figure 2B).

Second, the effect of CC10137 on blood pressure compared to clonidine was assessed using tail-cuff plethysmography. A rapid 9.6% decrease in mean arterial pressure (MAP) was observed 0.5 h after administration of 1 mg/kg clonidine, with a maximum reduction of 28.3% observed at 2 h. In contrast, 10 mg/kg CC10137 did not alter MAP during the 2-h observation period (Figure 2C). Systolic and diastolic pressures followed similar patterns (Figure S4).

Third, CC10137, at doses ranging from 1 to 10 mg/kg, did not significantly impact rectal temperature. On the other hand, clonidine significantly reduced rectal temperature by 1.5°C and 2.8°C at 0.1 mg/kg and 1 mg/kg, respectively (Figure 2D).

Together, these findings demonstrate that CC10137 (at oral doses up to 10 mg/kg) avoids the major CNS-mediated side effects typically associated with systemic α2AAR agonists.

CC10137 does not impair the spinal nociceptive reflex

The tail-flick assay provides a well-established measurement of spinal nociceptive reflex, an essential physiological protective mechanism against noxious stimuli.25,26,27 Injection of 3 mg/kg morphine, included as a positive control, significantly prolonged tail-flick latency (TFL) from 4.1 to 7.8 s, representing a 90.2% increase. In contrast, 10 mg/kg CC10137 did not alter TFL in mice (Figure 2E), indicating full preservation of this protective reflex. These results demonstrate that peripheral restriction of α2AAR activation does not impair spinal nociceptive reflex, a key safety advantage for patients with chronic pain, who rely on intact protective reflexes to avoid inadvertent injury.12,28

CC10137 demonstrates α2AR-dependent efficacy and broad synergistic potential in a neuropathic pain model

Tactile allodynia, pain resulting from normally non-painful mechanical stimuli, is a common and therapeutically challenging manifestation of neuropathic pain that is routinely studied using the spared nerve injury (SNI) model in C57BL/6J mice.29,30,31 As illustrated in Figure 3A, oral administration of CC10137 dose-dependently reversed mechanical allodynia developed in the hindpaw, with maximum possible effect (MPE) values of 26.5%, 42.3%, 54.9%, and 62.4% at 0.75, 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg, respectively (Figure 3B). This effect by 1 mg/kg CC10137 was partially reversed by the α2AR antagonist 10 mg/kg atipamezole, confirming CC10137 has an α2AR-dependent mechanism (Figure 3C).

In time-course studies, 1 mg/kg CC10137 exhibited a rapid onset of analgesia at 0.5 h, with peak efficacy at 1 to 2 h, and a sustained 50% maximal activity at the end of the 4-h observation window. Notably, this analgesic temporal profile is consistent with the pharmacokinetic profile of CC10137 (Figure S1). This rapid and sustained efficacy was similar to the time courses for 10 mg/kg pregabalin and 1 mg/kg injected morphine (Figure 3D).

Given the distinct mechanism of α2AAR agonists from morphine or pregabalin, we evaluated potential synergistic interactions.32,33,34 When independently administered, 1 mg/kg CC10137, 1 mg/kg morphine (s.c.), and 3 mg/kg pregabalin conferred 49.9%, 42.7%, and 17.3% relief from allodynia 1 hour after administration, respectively. Co-administration of CC10137 with morphine or pregabalin significantly enhanced efficacy (MPE = 86.1% and 90.3%, respectively); this exceeds not only the same dose monotherapies but also the 3-fold higher doses of morphine (3 mg/kg; MPE = 65.2%) or pregabalin (10 mg/kg; MPE = 52.2%). The combination indexes (CIs), calculated with Bliss independence analysis, confirmed strong synergy of CC10137 with morphine (CI = 0.828) or pregabalin (CI = 0.649)35,36 (Figure 3E). These results demonstrate robust anti-allodynic efficacy of CC10137 in neuropathic pain, alongside potent synergy with standard-of-care analgesics, supporting its potential as an opioid- and pregabalin-sparing agent for difficult-to-treat pain conditions.

CC10137 demonstrates broad synergistic potential and sustained efficacy in bone cancer pain models

Bone cancer pain represents a clinically complex and persistent condition that remains challenging to manage.31,37 Given that epidural clonidine is approved for enhancing analgesia by morphine in severe cancer pain,13 we evaluated whether peripheral activation of α2AAR could achieve similar therapeutic benefits without central mechanisms. Interestingly, the peripherally restricted α2AAR agonist CC10137 demonstrated robust anti-allodynia with synergistic potential similar to clonidine and achieved comparable therapeutic benefits without central penetration.

In the NCTC-2472-induced bone cancer pain model in mice (Figure 4A), a dose-dependent response was observed with a 1 to 10 mg/kg titration of CC10137 (Figure 4B). A single dose of 1 mg/kg CC10137 exhibited similar efficacy as 10 mg/kg pregabalin or 1 mg/kg injected morphine during a 2-h observation period (Figure 4C). Co-administration of 1 mg/kg CC10137 with a 1 mg/kg morphine injection achieved a 71.8% response, which is comparable to the 78.0% observed by 3 mg/kg morphine alone. Significantly, when CC10137 was combined with 3 mg/kg pregabalin, the 69.5% analgesia surpassed the 44.2% observed with the 10 mg/kg pregabalin monotherapy (Figure 4D). The calculated combination index values confirmed synergism with both morphine (CI = 0.926) and pregabalin (CI = 0.886).

Beyond opioids and gabapentanoids, combination synergy was also achieved with other analgesic classes recommended by cancer pain treatment guidelines.38 The serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor duloxetine, the tricyclic antidepressant amitriptyline, and the NSAID ketorolac achieved CI values of 0.986, 0.788, and 1.067, respectively (Figure S5). These findings position CC10137 as a promising candidate for combination therapies with a broad spectrum of approved pain medications.

CC10137 was further evaluated in a Walker-256-induced bone cancer pain model in rats (rBCP) to assess its long-term efficacy, as rats are routinely used for long-term behavioral testing according to WuXi AppTec’s standard operating procedures (Figure 4E). Following once-daily administration of CC10137, mechanical allodynia was evaluated after 1 and 2 weeks of treatment. CC10137 maintained consistent anti-allodynic effects throughout the study period, with the 3 mg/kg and 10 mg/kg groups demonstrating approximately 42% and 55% reversal of allodynia, respectively, on both day 7 and day 15 post-administration (Figure 4F). These results demonstrate that CC10137 produces durable analgesia with repeated administration, supporting its translational potential for long-term chronic cancer pain management.

CC10137 demonstrates broad-spectrum anti-allodynic efficacy across diverse pain models

To assess the therapeutic potential of CC10137 across a broader range of chronic pain conditions, we selected five additional clinically relevant models. In the CIPN model established using cisplatin, CC10137 (3 mg/kg, p.o.) reversed mechanical allodynia by 33.2%, which is comparable to duloxetine (10 mg/kg, p.o.), the only FDA-approved drug for this indication (Figure S6). Similarly, in another neuropathic pain model where postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) is induced by the varicella zoster virus, CC10137 (3 mg/kg) produced 23.3% inhibition of allodynia, achieving a similar efficacy to pregabalin (10 mg/kg, p.o.) and amitriptyline (20 mg/kg, p.o.), with efficacy increasing to 44.2% with CC10137 at 10 mg/kg (Figure S7). In the third neuropathic pain model, CC10137 (1mg/kg, p.o.) induced a 33.0% decrease in allodynia in the streptozotocin-induced diabetic peripheral neuropathy model. This matches the effect observed with 10 mg/kg pregabalin (Figure S8). Synergistic or additive effects when combined with standard analgesics were observed in CIPN and PHN models (Figures S9 and S10).

Beyond neuropathy, we further evaluated CC10137 in inflammatory and postoperative pain models. At 30 mg/kg, naproxen, a standard NSAID for inflammatory pain, reduced mechanical allodynia by 39.5% following injection of complete Freund’s adjuvant (CFA). Notably, CC10137 dose-dependently relieved CFA-induced allodynia, providing 20.5% and 35.0% relief at 1 and 3 mg/kg, respectively (Figure S11). Finally, in the Brennan postoperative pain model, 3 mg/kg CC10137 administered orally inhibited mechanical allodynia by 36.6%, achieving similar efficacy as the direct injection of 1 mg/kg morphine (Figure S12).

With robust efficacy across neuropathic, inflammatory, and postoperative pain, these findings establish CC10137 as a strong candidate for broad-spectrum anti-allodynia and supports its translational potential for the management of various pathological pain conditions.

Discussion

A major challenge in the development of safe and effective pain medicine has been the creation of analgesics without CNS-mediated side effects.19,39,40,41,42,43 Although the strategy of developing peripheral-acting analgesics has been explored across multiple targets, the discovery of a peripherally restricted α2AAR agonist as an effective analgesic has remained elusive.9,44 Our work directly addressed this long-standing knowledge gap. By designing and screening a library of 59 compounds optimized as P-glycoprotein substrates, we identified CC10137, which represents a potent, orally bioavailable, peripherally restrictive α2AAR agonist with a favorable safety profile. CC10137 exerted robust anti-allodynic effect in a broad spectrum of animal pain models, while critically avoiding the CNS-mediated side effects, such as sedation, hypotension, and impairment of the spinal nociceptive reflex, traditionally associated with systemic α2AAR agonist. CC10137 exhibited potent α2AAR agonist activity (EC50 = 13.1 nM) with minimal CNS penetration (Kp,uu,brain = 0.013). This peripheral restriction enabled a clear dissociation of anti-allodynic efficacy from centrally mediated side effects. At therapeutically effective doses of up to 10 mg/kg, CC10137 produced robust anti-allodynia without causing sedation, hypotension, or hypothermia. In contrast, clonidine already exhibited significant CNS-mediated side effects as low as 0.2 mg/kg.

A benchmarking study using the SNI model directly compared CC10137 with tizanidine at doses ranging from 1 to 10 mg/kg. At 1 mg/kg, both compounds showed comparable anti-allodynic efficacy without sedation. At 2 mg/kg, however, tizanidine caused significant motor impairment, whereas CC10137 remained free of sedation at 10 and 50 mg/kg (Figure S13). Together, these findings demonstrate that peripheral restriction of α2AAR agonists provides a substantially wider therapeutic window for pain relief compared to systemic α2AAR agonists.

Moreover, combination studies revealed synergistic interactions when CC10137 was co-administered with several classes of pain medications, including morphine and pregabalin. This suggests CC10137 may serve as an effective opioid- and pregabalin-sparing agent, providing clinically relevant benefits by enabling lower doses of each drug to achieve adequate pain control. Furthermore, this approach may extend therapeutic coverage to pain conditions unresponsive to either treatment alone, paralleling the clinical strategy of using epidural clonidine (Duraclon) to treat the severe cancer pain inadequately controlled by morphine alone.13

Ideal chronic pain management will also require alleviation of pathological allodynia without compromising normal nociceptive (protective) function.12,28 Systemic analgesics, such as morphine, or even systemic biased α2AAR agonists and α2BAR antagonists, tend to impair protective nociceptive reflex.17,18 In sharp contrast, CC10137 did not impact TFL at effective doses observed for pathological pain.25,45,46,47 This preservation of nociceptive reflex, a profile rarely observed among systemically administered analgesic of comparable efficacy, establishes a superior therapeutic profile for CC10137, allowing it to specifically alleviate pathological pain while preserving physiological protective function.

Receptor activation assays revealed that CC10137 does not activate α1AAR or β1AR (Figure S14), two receptors critical for blood pressure regulation.48,49 This finding is consistent with our in vivo observations that CC10137, at effective analgesic doses, did not elevate blood pressure in preclinical models. Regarding α2AR subtype selectivity, CC10137 exhibits comparable agonist activity for α2A, α2B, and α2CAR, just like dexmedetomidine (Figure S14), also known as a non-selective α2AAR agonist.17,23 While α2AAR is traditionally considered the primary target of analgesia,50 the present data do not show whether α2BAR or α2CAR also contribute to the peripheral analgesic effects of CC10137, as all three subtypes are expressed in peripheral tissues.51 Importantly, regardless of the precise contribution of α2BAR or α2CAR, the core therapeutic advance remains: CC10137 retains the broad analgesic potential of clinical approved systemic α2AAR agonists such as clonidine and dexmedetomidine while avoiding their dose-limiting CNS side effects through peripheral restriction.

While peripheral restriction offers clear advantages, it also has some inherent limitations. For example, neuropathic pain can be categorized into central and peripheral forms, depending on whether the lesion or dysfunction occurs within the central or peripheral nervous system.30 Due to its peripheral restriction, CC10137 is not expected to exhibit efficacy in pain etiologies driven solely by central origin. However, this limitation is modest, as peripheral neuropathic pain is far more prevalent than central neuropathic pain.52 Pure central neuropathic pain accounts for only about 2.4% of all patients with neuropathic pain, while peripheral forms representing the majority of clinically encountered neuropathic pain cases.53

Mechanistically, the anti-allodynic action of CC10137 is likely to involve the modulation of sensory neuron excitability at peripheral sites outside the CNS, such as the dorsal root ganglia,50,51 a hypothesis that invites exploration in the future. Another area worthy of further investigation is whether peripheral α2AAR activation can modulate tumor progression, relevant to cancer pain treatment.54,55,56,57,58 Notably, clonidine has demonstrated anti-tumor activity in experimental models but at doses that may cause CNS side effects.59 Peripherally restricted α2AAR agonists such as CC10137 may enable sustained anti-tumor efficacy while alleviating cancer-associated pain without CNS side effects, a dual-benefit potential warranting dedicated future investigation.

In summary, this work demonstrates that peripheral α2AAR activation is a viable strategy to achieve potent, broad-spectrum analgesia without central side effects. CC10137 achieves this profile through potent α2AAR activation, minimal CNS penetration, and absence of α1AAR or β1AR activation activity (consistent with its neutral hemodynamic profile). As a result, CC10137 retains the analgesic efficacy of clinically approved systemic α2AAR agonists while circumventing their dose-limiting side effects. With this combination of robust analgesic efficacy, a favorable safety profile, preservation of nociceptive reflexes, and opioid-sparing potential, CC10137 is positioned as a promising development candidate. More broadly, this approach offers a strategy for developing precisely targeted analgesics that selectively alleviate pathological pain while preserving essential protective mechanisms.

Limitations of the study

Our study has several limitations. First, the in vivo efficacy data were generated exclusively in rodent models (mice and rats). With the exception of the rat bone cancer pain model, which used female animals, all other studies were conducted using male animals following WuXi AppTec’s standardized protocols. Whether the findings extend to females in other pain models remains to be determined. Rodent pain responses do not fully replicate the complexity of human pain. Validation in higher-order species has not been conducted, and the clinical relevance of the observed opioid-sparing synergy remains to be established in human trials.

Second, the precise molecular mechanism underlying the analgesic effect of peripheral α2AR activation remains unclear. The relative contributions of α2A, α2B, and α2C subtypes were not delineated, and the exact cellular sites of action (e.g., dorsal root ganglion neurons) have not been identified. These aspects require further investigation using subtype-selective tools or genetic models.

Third, safety assessments were limited to acute endpoints (sedation, motor coordination, blood pressure, and body temperature) following short-term dosing. Chronic toxicity studies have not been performed. GLP-compliant safety pharmacology, genotoxicity, and repeat-dose toxicology studies in both rodent and non-rodent species are necessary to support clinical development.

Fourth, the therapeutic window observed in rodents (greater than 10-fold separation) may not translate directly to humans due to interspecies differences in drug metabolism, P-gp transporter activity, and receptor pharmacology. Addressing these limitations in future studies will help advance the understanding of peripheral α2AR agonism and its potential for clinical application.

Experimental model and study participant details

Experimental models: Cell lines

NCTC-2472 mouse osteolytic sarcoma cells (ATCC, 59368462) and Walker 256 rat mammary carcinoma cells (ATCC, 203271) were used for the establishment of bone cancer pain models. HEK293 cells engineered to express human α2AAR and related receptor subtypes were used for in vitro pharmacological assays. MDR1-MDCKII cells (obtained from Piet Borst, Netherlands Cancer Institute) were used for bidirectional transport studies.

All cell lines were obtained from authenticated commercial sources or established following standardized protocols at WuXi AppTec and were cultured under recommended conditions. The HEK293, NCTC-2472, and Walker 256 cell lines were authenticated using Short Tandem Repeat (STR) profiling, while the MDR1-MDCKII cells were not authenticated. Mycoplasma contamination was routinely tested using a commercial detection kit (Lonza MycoAlert Mycoplasma Detection Kit, LT07-710), and all cell lines in this study were confirmed to be mycoplasma-free.

Experimental models: Organisms/strains

All in vivo studies were conducted at WuXi AppTec. C57BL/6J mice (male, 6–8 weeks old; Shanghai Lingchang Biotech.) were used for pharmacokinetic, tissue distribution, open-field, rotarod, blood pressure, rectal temperature assessment, and allodynia studies (SNI, CIPN, CFA, POP, and DPN models). ICR mice (male, 6–8 weeks old; Vital River) were used for the tail-flick test. C3H/He mice (male, 6–7 weeks old; Vital River) were used for the mouse bone cancer pain model. Sprague-Dawley rats (male, 7–8 weeks old; Vital River) were used for the postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) model, while Wistar rats (female, 7–8 weeks old; Vital River) were used for the rat bone cancer pain model.

Animals were housed in the animal facilities of WuXi AppTec under specific pathogen-free (SPF) conditions, in groups (rats: 2 animals per cage; mice: 3–4 animals per cage) with a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle (7:00-19:00), controlled temperature (20°C–26°C) and relative humidity (40–70%), and ad libitum access to food and water. Cages were provided with environmental enrichment, including shelters and corn cob bedding.

All experiments were performed in accordance with WuXi AppTec’s international guidelines and were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of WuXi AppTec, an Association for Assessment and Accreditation of Laboratory Animal Care (AAALAC)-accredited facility. Each animal experiment has its own independent protocol number and ethical approval number, as listed individually.

Open field model in mice

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for behavioral assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (GP01-QD129-2022v1.0). Specifically, male C57BL/6J mice were transferred to the test room and habituated for 8 h the day before testing. On the test day, animals were further acclimated for at least 2 h before the experiment. Mice were randomly assigned to groups based on body weight measured the day prior to dosing. Test reagents were freshly prepared and administered according to the experimental design. Locomotor activity was assessed in an open-field chamber (Shanghai Lanyuao Industry and Trade Co., Ltd. with internal dimensions: 27.4 × 27.4 × 35 cm). Each mouse was placed in the center of the arena, and activity was recorded for 30 min using a video tracking system, with distance traveled (m) measured every 5 min.

Rotarod model in mice

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for behavioral assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (GP01-QD129-2022v1.0). Specifically, after 3 days of acclimation, male C57BL/6J mice were trained for two days on a rotarod (6 rpm, 300 s), and those that met the performance criteria were included in the study. On the test day, animals were randomized by body weight and received vehicle or test compounds including clonidine (1 mg/kg, p.o.) and CC10137 (10 mg/kg, p.o.) at 10 mL/kg. In a separate experiment, animals received vehicle or clonidine (0.2 mg/kg, p.o.). In another experiment, animals received vehicle, tizanidine (1 mg/kg or 2 mg/kg, p.o.) or CC10137 (50 mg/kg, p.o.). Rotarod performance was assessed at 30 min post-dose, recording the latency to fall (max 300 s).

Tail-flick nociceptive model in mice

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for tail-flick thermal nociception assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (GP01-QD129-2024v1.7). Specifically, upon arrival at the animal facility, male ICR mice were acclimated for 3 days under standard conditions with free access to food and water. Two days prior to testing, animals underwent daily habituation procedures, consisting of gentle handling and water temperature adaptation (36°C–37°C for 20 s, once daily). Prior to study initiation, animals were randomly assigned to groups based on baseline tail-flick latencies (n = 10 per group). Tail-flick assessments were conducted in 50°C water with a 1–2 cm tail immersion depth and a 12 s cutoff time. Three measurements were taken at 3-min intervals using a TW8 Julabo water bath and averaged to obtain baseline latency. One hour after compound administration, tail-flick latencies were measured again under the same conditions. Three readings were recorded for each mouse and averaged, with the value showing the greatest deviation from the mean excluded.

Spared nerve injury model in mice

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for the spared nerve injury (SNI) model. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (GP01-SH059-2023v1.3). Male C57BL/6J mice were acclimated for 3 days prior to surgery. Animals were anesthetized with Zoletil 50 (50 mg/kg) and xylazine (8 mg/kg, i.p.) and subjected to unilateral SNI surgery, in which the tibial and common peroneal nerves were transected while the sural nerve was left intact. Sham-operated mice underwent identical procedures without nerve transection. On day 13 post-surgery, animals with PWT <0.6 g were randomized into treatment groups (n = 8 per group). Test compounds were administered at a dosing volume of 10 mL/kg. The vehicle consisted of 20% HP-β-cyclodextrin (HP-β-CD), except for morphine, which was dissolved in 0.9% saline. A washout period of at least 2 days was allowed between crossover experiments. CC10137 was administered orally at doses of 0.75, 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg, and PWT was measured at baseline and 1 h after dosing. For antagonist reversal studies, atipamezole (10 mg/kg, i.p.) or vehicle was administered 30 min before and 15 min after oral administration of CC10137 (1 mg/kg). Thus, animals received identical handling and injection procedures, with either the active compound or the corresponding vehicle administered at each time point. For time-course studies, CC10137 (1 mg/kg, p.o.), morphine (1 mg/kg, s.c.), and pregabalin (10 mg/kg, p.o.) were evaluated, and PWT was measured at baseline, 0.5, 1, 2, and 4 h after dosing. For combination studies, CC10137 (1 mg/kg, p.o.), morphine (1 mg/kg, s.c.), or pregabalin (3 mg/kg, p.o.) were administered alone or in combination at the same doses. For comparison, higher doses of morphine (3 mg/kg, s.c.) and pregabalin (10 mg/kg, p.o.) were also evaluated. PWT was measured at baseline and 1 h after dosing. In a separate experiment, animals received vehicle or tizanidine (1, 3, or 10 mg/kg, p.o.), and PWT was measured at baseline and 1 h post-dose. Analgesic efficacy in all studies, including antagonist, time-course, and combination experiments, was expressed as %MPE as described in the mechanical allodynia test.

Bone cancer pain model in mice

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for mice cancer pain model assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (GP01-SH082-2024v1.0). Specifically, for the cell culture, NCTC-2472 mouse osteolytic sarcoma cells (ATCC, 59368462) were cultured according to ATCC recommendations. For implantation, cells were pelleted by centrifugation at 1000 rpm for 4 min, washed twice with PBS (2 mL each), and resuspended at a density of 2 × 106 cells/mL in sterile PBS. A total of 10 μL (2 × 104 cells) of this suspension was used for implantation in the mouse bone cancer pain model. After 3 days of acclimation, male C3H/He mice were anesthetized with Zoletil 50 (50 mg/kg) and xylazine (8 mg/kg, i.p.) and placed in the supine position. The right hindlimb was shaved, disinfected, and a small skin incision was made to expose the distal femur. The medullary cavity was perforated using a 0.3 mL syringe needle, into which 10 μL of NCTC-2472 cell suspension was slowly injected. Sham animals received an equal volume (10 μL) of PBS. The day of inoculation was defined as day 0. On day 14 post-surgery, mice with a mechanical withdrawal threshold (PWT) < 0.6 g were selected and randomized into treatment groups (n = 8 per group) for pharmacological evaluation. A washout period of at least 2 days was allowed between crossover experiments. CC10137 was administered orally at 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg, and PWT was measured at baseline and 1 h post-dose. The time course comparison (0, 0.5, 1 and 2 h) with morphine and pregabalin, as well as combination assay were performed using the same protocol as described for the SNI model.

Bone cancer pain model in rats

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for rats bone cancer pain model assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (ON02-SH005-2024V1.0). Specifically, Walker 256 rat mammary carcinoma cells were cultured under standard conditions. Before implantation, cells were pelleted by centrifugation at 1000 rpm for 4 min, washed twice with PBS (2 mL each), and resuspended at a density of 1 × 108 cells/mL. A total of 4 μL (4 × 105 cells) of this suspension was used for rat bone cancer pain model. Female Wistar rats were anesthetized with isoflurane and placed in the supine position. The right hindlimb was shaved and disinfected, and the proximal tibia was punctured using a syringe needle. A 10 μL microsyringe was then inserted into the bone marrow cavity to slowly inject 4 μL of Walker 256 cell suspension, while sham rats received 4 μL PBS. The day of injection was defined as day 0. From day 1, animals were weighed and administered CC10137 (3 or 10 mg/kg, p.o.) daily at a volume of 5 mL/kg for 15 consecutive days (n = 8 per group). Mechanical allodynia was assessed before dosing and 1 h after administration on days 7 and 15.

Cisplatin-induced neuropathic pain model in mice

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for chemotherapy-induced neuropathic pain assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (GP01-SH059 2023v1.3). Male C57BL/6J mice were acclimated for 3 days prior to study initiation. Control animals received intraperitoneal saline injections once daily on Days 1–5 and 11–15, whereas model animals received cisplatin (2.3 mg/kg, i.p.) on the same schedule (dose volume: 10 mL/kg in saline) (n = 8 per group). From Days 13–15, all animals were habituated to the testing environment for 15 min per day. On Day 16, mechanical withdrawal thresholds (PWT) were measured, and animals with PWT >0.6 g were excluded. For pharmacological evaluation, CC10137 (1, 3, or 10 mg/kg, p.o.), pregabalin (10 mg/kg, p.o.), or duloxetine (10 mg/kg, p.o.) were administered at a dosing volume of 10 mL/kg. PWT was measured at baseline and 1 h after dosing. For combination studies, CC10137 (1 mg/kg, p.o.), pregabalin (10 mg/kg, p.o.), or amitriptyline (10 mg/kg, p.o.) were administered either alone or in combination at the same doses. PWT was measured at baseline and 2 h after dosing.

Varicella zoster virus (VZV)-induced postherpetic neuralgia model in rats

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for the varicella zoster virus (VZV)-induced postherpetic neuralgia model. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (ID02-QD010-2024v1.1). On Day 0, male Sprague–Dawley rats were deeply anesthetized with Zoletil 50 (30 mg/kg) and xylazine hydrochloride (6 mg/kg, i.p., 5 mL/kg) and inoculated subcutaneously with 15,000 TCID50 varicella zoster virus (VZV) in 150 μL into the right plantar pad, while naive controls remained uninfected. On Day 8, animals exhibiting PWT <5 g were considered successfully modeled and randomized into six groups, including a naive group (n = 6) and five treatment groups (n = 8 per group). No significant differences in baseline PWT or body weight were observed across groups prior to treatment. For pharmacological evaluation, CC10137 (1, 3, or 10 mg/kg, p.o.), pregabalin (10 mg/kg, p.o.), or duloxetine (10 mg/kg, p.o.) were administered at a dosing volume of 10 mL/kg. PWT was measured at baseline and 2 h after dosing. For combination studies, CC10137 (1 mg/kg, p.o.), pregabalin (10 mg/kg, p.o.), duloxetine (10 mg/kg, p.o.), or amitriptyline (10 mg/kg, p.o.) were administered either alone or in combination at the same doses. For comparison, higher doses of duloxetine (20 mg/kg, p.o.) and amitriptyline (20 mg/kg, p.o.) were also tested. PWT was measured at baseline and 2 h after dosing.

Diabetic peripheral neuropathy model in mice

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for diabetic neuropathic pain assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (GP01 SH059 2023v1.3). Specifically, after 3 days of acclimation, naive male C57BL/6J mice received Standard diet (Lab Rat and Mouse Maintenance Irradiated Diet) (XTI01WC-002, Jiangsu Xietong), whereas model mice were fed a high-fat diet (HFD, D12492I, Research Diets Inc.) for 4 weeks followed by intraperitoneal streptozotocin (STZ, 100 mg/kg). Mice were fasted for 6 h before and 2 h after STZ injection. One week later, after ≥12 h fasting, blood glucose was measured. The mice with a glucose level below 9 mmol/L received a second STZ injection, and HFD was continued for an additional 4 weeks. On Day 0, mechanical withdrawal threshold (PWT) was measured. The model mice without mechanical allodynia (PWT >0.6 g) were excluded. Model mice with PWT <0.6 g entered pharmacology studies and were randomly grouped (n = 8 per group). 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg CC10137 (p.o.) were administered at a volume of 10 mL/kg, and PWT was measured at baseline and 1 h post-dose. In a separate experiment, 10 mg/kg pregabalin were administered and PWT was measured at same condition.

Post-surgical pain model in mice

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for post-operative pain assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (GP01 SH059-2023v1.3). Specifically, after 3 days of acclimation, male C57BL/6J mice were anesthetized with Zoletil 50 (50 mg/kg) and xylazine (8 mg/kg, i.p.). A toe pinch was used to ensure that the animals were fully anesthetized prior to any incision. Put ophthalmic ointment in the rodent’s eyes to prevent the corneas from drying out. The plantar aspect of the left hind paw was swabbed with 3 rounds of alternating betadine and 70% ethanol. A 0.5mm longitudinal incision was made through the skin and fascia, starting 2mm from the proximal edge of the heel and extending toward the toes, the plantar muscle was elevated and injured longitudinally leaving the muscle origin and insertion points intact. After hemostasis with gentle pressure, the skin was opposed with two mattress sutures. Animals were allowed to recover from anesthesia on a warm recovery pad and were administered 1 mL sterile saline orally to prevent dehydration during recovery. The animals were returned to cage after fully awake. On day 1 post-surgery, mice with mechanical withdrawal threshold (PWT) < 0.6 g were selected and randomized into groups (n = 8 per group) for pharmacological evaluation. 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg CC10137 (p.o.) were administered at a volume of 10 mL/kg, and PWT was measured at baseline and 1 h post-dose. In a separate experiment, 1 mg/kg morphine (s.c.) were administered and PWT was measured at same condition.

Complete Freund’s adjuvant (CFA)-induced inflammatory pain model in mice

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for inflammatory pain assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (GP01-QD051-2023v1.7). Specifically, after 6 days acclimation period, male C57BL/6J mice were further habituated to the testing environment for 15 min per day on three consecutive days before the first behavioral assessment, during which the mid-plantar hind paws were touched with von Frey filaments (1.0 g and 1.4 g) for training. The CFA pain model was established by subcutaneous injection of 10 μL CFA into the plantar center of the left hind paw, while six mice were retained as a naive group without CFA injection. Twenty-four hours after modeling, mechanical withdrawal thresholds (PWT) were measured in all animals. Mice that did not exhibit allodynia (PWT >0.6 g) were excluded, and successfully modeled mice were randomly divided into six groups (vehicle and five treatment groups, n = 8 per group) with no significant difference in baseline PWT. 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg CC10137 (p.o.), 10 mg/kg pregabalin and 10 mg/kg duloxetine were administered at a volume of 10 mL/kg, and PWT was measured at baseline and 1 h post-dose.

Regarding sex as a biological variable: Of the 11 preclinical models evaluated, 10 used male rodents and one (rat bone cancer pain, Walker 256) used female animals, following WuXi AppTec’s standardized protocols. Thus, the influence of sex was not systematically evaluated for most models, and whether the findings in males extend to females remains to be determined.

Method details

Chemical synthesis

A total of 61 compounds, including 59 compounds and 2 reference compounds, were synthesized at Accela ChemBio under validated and standardized procedures, each with a purity above 95%. The lead compound, CC10137, was obtained with a purity greater than 99%. As most compounds contain a chiral center, selected candidates were enantiomerically resolved using supercritical fluid chromatography (SFC) on a chiral stationary phase. Detailed information on reagents, chemical structures, synthetic routes, and experimental procedures for all compounds, together with LC-MS, HRMS, NMR spectra, chiral HPLC chromatograms, and chiral characterization of CC10137, is provided in Document S2.

α2AAR, α2BAR, α2BAR, α1AAR and β1AR agonist activity

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vitro pharmacology protocol for GPCR calcium flux assays. HEK293 cells stably expressing human α2AAR and Gα15 were provided by WuXi AppTec and used to evaluate agonist activity. The culture media is DMEM+10% FBS+1% PS + G418 300 μg/mL + BSA 2 μg/mL. The transfected cells were then selected using geneticin and blasticidin S to obtain a stable single clone expressing the α2AAR receptor, which was subsequently used for calcium flux for the agonist activity and binding assays. Cells were washed once with DPBS (21-0310CVR, Corning) and detached using 0.05% trypsin-EDTA (25300-062, Gibco) at 37°C for 1 min. The reaction was stopped by adding culture medium (DMEM+10% FBS+1% PS + G418 300 μg/mL + BSA 2 μg/mL), and the suspension was centrifuged at 1,000 rpm for 5 min. The supernatant was discarded, and the pellet was resuspended in fresh medium. Cell number and viability were determined using a Vi-CELLTM analyzer (Beckman), and the density was adjusted to 1 × 106 cells/mL. A total of 20 μL of the cell suspension (20,000 cells per well) was seeded into 384-well plates (Greiner) and incubated overnight at 37°C in 5% CO2. On the following day, a 2× Fluo-4 Direct dye solution (8 μM, Invitrogen) was prepared by adding 10 mL of FLIPR buffer (20 mM HEPES (Gibco), 1× HBSS, 0.5% BSA) containing 0.2 mL of 250 mM probenecid and mixing for 5 min. The medium was aspirated, and 20 μL of 2× Fluo-4 reagent was added to each well, followed by incubation at 37°C for 50 min and equilibration at room temperature for 10 min. Test compounds were serially diluted 3-fold in DMSO to generate ten concentrations, and 750 μL of each diluted compound was transferred to the compound plate, with DMSO serving as the low-signal control. 30 μL of assay buffer was added to each well of the cell plate, which was centrifuged at 1,000 rpm, and 10 μL of compound solution was added to each well. Fluorescence signals were recorded in real time using an FLIPR instrument (Tetra+ system, Molecular Devices). Activation (%) was calculated as (RFU_sample - RFU_DMSO)/(RFU_HC - RFU_DMSO) × 100, where RFU represents relative fluorescence units (average of readings 1–90) and HC is the highest concentration of the positive reference compound (brimonidine). EC50 values were determined using the “log(agonist) vs. response - variable slope” model in GraphPad Prism 5.0.

The protocols for assessing agonist activity at α2BAR, α2CAR, α1AAR, and β1AR were identical to that used for α2AAR, except that receptor-specific cell lines provided by WuXi AppTec were used. Dexmedetomidine served as the positive control for α2BAR and α2CAR, while norepinephrine was used as the positive control for α1AAR and β1AR.

α2AAR radioligand binding experiment

The assay was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vitro pharmacology protocol for α2AAR binding assessment. Membranes containing human α2AAR (NM_000681) were extracted from a stable α2AAR -expressing cell line. Specifically, the testing compounds and reference compounds Yohimbine was 4-fold serially diluted for 8 point, with a maximum initial concentration of 10 μM and 1000nM, respectively. 1 μL testing compound, 1 μL of 0.2 mM yohimbine and 1 μL of DMSO were transferred to assay plate and nonspecific binding (low control, LC) and total binding (high control, HC), respectively. Then mix the sample with 100 μL 5μg membrane (3 mg/mL stock) and [3H]RX821002 (0.5 nM). The assay buffer and wash buffer are 50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4. Binding reactions were incubated at room temperature for 1 h. Simultaneously, Unifilter-96 GF/C filter plates (6005174, PerkinElmer) were incubated with 50 μL 0.3% poly ethyleneimine (PEI) for at least 0.5 h at room temperature. The binding mixtures were filtered through the GF-C plates using PerkinElmer Cell Harvester (UNIFILTER-96, Perkin Elemer) and wash 4 times with cold assay buffer. After drying for 1 h at 50°C, seal the bottom of the filter plate wells using tape (PerkinElmer Unifilter-96 backing seal tape), 50 μL of PerkinElmer Microscint-O cocktail (6013611, PerkinElmer) was added. Then the top of filter plates was sealed TopSeal-A sealing film (6050185, PerkinElmer). The 3H count was determined with a PerkinElmer MicroBeta2 Reader (CNLL0153). The inhibition was calculated using the following equation: %inhibition = (1 - (assay well - Average_HC)/(Average_HC - Average_LC) × 100%. Data was analyzed with Prism 5 with log(inhibitor) vs. response - variable slope model to fit the data.

MDR1-MDCKII bidirectional transport assay

The study was performed at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vitro pharmacology protocol for MDR1 (P-gp) bidirectional transport assessment. Specifically, MDR1-MDCKII cells (obtained from Piet Borst at Netherlands Cancer Institute) were seeded at a density of 2.33 × 105 cells/cm2 and grown to confluence on 96-well insert system with polycarbonate or polyethylene membranes to form a tight monolayer. Test compounds were diluted to 2 μM in transport buffer (HBSS containing 10 mM HEPES, pH 7.40 ± 0.05) and applied to the apical (A) or basolateral (B) sides of the membranes at volumes of 75 μL and 250 μL respectively, in duplicate. The plates were incubated at 37°C with 5% CO2 for 2.5 h. Digoxin (a known P-gp substrate) were tested at 10 μM bidirectionally in duplicate as well, and nadolol (a low permeability compound) and metoprolol (a high permeability compound) were tested at 2 μM in A to B direction in duplicate. Following incubation, concentrations of test and reference compounds on both sides were determined by LC-MS/MS, based on the peak area ratio relative to the internal standard. The apparent permeability coefficient Papp(cm/s) = (dCr/dt) × Vr/(A × C0), Where dCr/dt is the cumulative concentration of compound in the receiver chamber as a function of time (μM/s); Vr is the solution volume in the receiver chamber (0.075 mL on the apical side, 0.25 mL on the basolateral side); A is the surface area for the transport, i.e., 0.143 cm2 for the area of the monolayer; C0 is the initial concentration in the donor chamber (μM). Efflux Ratio = Papp (BA)/Papp (AB).

Pharmacokinetics analysis

The study was performed at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacokinetic protocol. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (PK01-SH004-2023v1.2). Specifically, male C57BL/6J mice (n = 3 per group) were administered the test compound either orally (5 mg/kg, p.o.) or intravenously (1 mg/kg, i.v.) using a vehicle of 5% DMSO, 5% Solutol, and 90% water. Specifically, approximately 0.1 mL blood samples were collected at each time point from the saphenous vein and transferred to a prechilled EDTA-K2 tube. Blood samples were centrifuged 3,200 x g for 10 min at 4°C, supernatant was quickly frozen over dry ice and stored at −60°C. LC-MS/MS was used to determine the amount of compound in each sample and time point. Percent oral bioavailability = AUCp.o./AUCi.v. x (Dosei.v./Dosep.o.). Data was analyzed with Phoenix WInNonlin 6.3 software.

Plasma protein and brain tissue binding

The study was performed at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vitro protocol for plasma protein and brain tissue binding assessment. Test compounds and control compounds (warfarin or propranolol) were added to mouse plasma or brain homogenate at a final concentration of 2 μM (0.5% DMSO) in a total volume of 600 μL. Equilibrium dialysis was conducted using an HT-Dialysis plate with a 12–14 kDa molecular weight cutoff membrane (HT Dialysis LLC). Aliquots of 100 μL compound-containing matrix and 100 μL PBS were added to the donor and receiver chambers, respectively. The plate was rotated at 100 rpm for 4 h at 37°C in a 5% CO2 incubator. After dialysis, 50 μL samples were collected from both chambers and mixed with PBS and stop solution (acetonitrile containing 250 nM tolbutamide and 250 nM labetalol). Compound concentrations were quantified by LC-MS/MS. The unbound fractions in brain tissue (Fu,brain) and plasma (Fu,plasma) were calculated as: %Unbound = 100 × (F/T), and %Bound = 100 − %Unbound, where F and T represent the peak area ratios (compound/internal standard) from the receiver and donor chambers, respectively, after 4 h incubation.

Tissue distribution

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for tissue distribution assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (PK01-SH004-2023v1.2). Animals were administered clonidine (5 mg/kg), dexmedetomidine (5 mg/kg), tizanidine (10 mg/kg), or CC10137 (10 or 80 mg/kg) by oral gavage (n = 3 per group). The dosing vehicle consisted of 20% HP-β-CD in water. Samples were collected at 0, 0.5, 1, and 2 h after dosing, with an additional 8 h time point included for the 10 mg/kg CC10137 group. Plasma samples were collected as described in the pharmacokinetic analysis section. Brain and spinal cord tissues were harvested, washed with cold saline, blotted dry, and weighed. Tissues were homogenized in homogenization buffer consisting of 15 mM PBS (pH 7.4) and methanol (2:1, v/v) at a ratio of 9 mL buffer per gram of tissue. In addition, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) samples were collected in the 10 mg/kg CC10137 group. Compound concentrations in plasma, CSF, and tissue homogenates were quantified by LC-MS/MS. The lower limits of quantification (LLOQs) were 1 ng/mL for plasma and CSF and 10 ng/mL for brain and spinal cord samples. Drug exposure was calculated by Kp,uu brain = AUCbrain unbound/AUCplasma unbound = AUCbrain/AUCplasma x (Fu,brain/Fu,plasma).

Blood pressure measurement

The study was conducted at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for blood pressure assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (GP01-QD163-2024v1.2). Specifically, male C57BL/6J mice were acclimated under controlled environmental conditions for 3 days. Mice were not fasted prior to dosing and were randomized into groups based on body weight. Test articles or positive control compounds were administered as a single oral gavage dose at a volume of 10 mL/kg. Blood pressure, including systolic and diastolic blood pressure (SBP and DBP), was measured at 0.5, 1, and 2 h post-dose using the CODA 8 noninvasive blood pressure acquisition system (Kent Scientific, Torrington, CT). Mean arterial pressure (MAP) was calculated as MAP = DBP +1/3 × (SBP − DBP). SBP, DBP, and MAP values were normalized to the vehicle group.

Rectal temperature measurement

The study was performed at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for physiological assessment. All procedures were performed in accordance with relevant international guidelines and were approved by IACUC of WuXi AppTec (GP01-SH059-2023v1.3). Specifically, after 3 days of acclimation at 20–26°C, CC10137 or clonidine was administered orally at 10 mL/kg across a series of concentrations to male C57BL/6J mice. Rectal temperature was measured at 1 h post-dose using a digital thermometer.

Mechanical allodynia testing

All mechanical allodynia testing was performed at WuXi AppTec following their standardized in vivo pharmacology protocol for pain behavioral assessment. Specifically, mechanical allodynia was assessed using the von Frey up-down method by an experimenter blinded to treatment. Mice were acclimated for 15 min on wire mesh before testing. Touch-Test Sensory Evaluators (58011, North Coast) was used in this study. Calibrated filaments (0.02, 0.04, 0.07, 0.16, 0.4, 0.6, 1 and 1.4 g for mice; 0.4, 0.6, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 15 g for rats) were applied perpendicularly to the lateral plantar surface for 6–8 s at 5 s intervals. A positive response was defined as sharp paw withdrawal or immediate flinching; ambulation prompted stimulus repetition. Mechanical allodynia was quantified as the 50% paw withdrawal threshold (PWT), calculated using the formula: 50% response threshold (g) = (10ˆ(Xf + k δ))/10,000, where Xf is the log value of the final von Frey filament used, k is the tabular value corresponding to the pattern of positive and negative responses, and δ is the mean difference (in log units) between consecutive stimuli. Analgesic effect expressed as %MPE = [(Test - Vehicle)/(Sham - Vehicle)] × 100. Data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post hoc test (GraphPad Prism), and Figures were generated using OriginPro. Drug interaction effects were evaluated using the Bliss independence model: CI = (EA + EB-EA×EB)/EAB, Where EA, EB and EAB represent %MPEs of CC10137, another analgesic and the combination of CC10137 and the analgesic. The result indicated synergy, antagonism or additivity when CI is under, above or equal to 1, respectively.36

Quantification and statistical analysis

Data presented in all figures are mean values with error bars corresponding to the standard error of the mean (SEM). Normality was evaluated with the Shapiro-Wilk test and visual inspection of sample histogram. Levene’s test was used to evaluate variance. Statistical comparisons of experimental means were determined by: Two-way repeated measure ANOVA with Dunnett post hoc test (Figures 1F, 2C, and S4A–S4D), Two-way repeated measure ANOVA with Bonferroni post test (Figures 3D and 4C), One-way ANOVA with Tukey post hoc test (Figures 2B and 2E), One-way ANOVA with Dunnett post hoc test (Figure 3C), One-way ANOVA with Bonferroni (Figures 2D, 3E, 4D, S5A–S5C, S9, and S10A–S10C), Paired t test (Figures 3B, 4B, S6, S7, S8B, S8C, S11, S12B, S12C, S13A, and S13B), Unpaired t test with Bonferroni post test (Figure 4F), Kruskal Test with Dunn Bonferroni post test (Figure 2A), Wilxocon’s Test (Figures S3, S13C, and S13D). Detailed description of statistical can be found in Document S3. Significance levels are notated in figures with NA, not significant; ∗p < 0.05; ∗∗p < 0.01; ∗∗∗p < 0.001. All statistical calculations were performed in R with the following packages: Linear mixed-effects models (Ime4), Analysis of factorial experiments (afex), Estimated marginal means (emmeans), and Tools for descriptive statistics (DescTools).

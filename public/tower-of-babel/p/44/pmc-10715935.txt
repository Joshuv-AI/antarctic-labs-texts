Atrial Septum Perforation Via Levoatriocardinal Vein in a Newborn With Hypoplastic Left Heart Syndrome
Authors: Jacek Kuźma, Darren James Grégoire, Krzysztof Godlewski, Cezary Niszczota, Michał Buczyński
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractHypoplastic left heart syndrome with intact atrial septum has high perioperative mortality. An interventional septum perforation from the right atrium may be technically challenging in newborns with high risk of tamponade. However, neonates with a levoatriocardinal vein provide an alternative approach for septostomy from the left atrium side. (Level of Difficulty: Advanced.)Key Words: atrial septum perforation, hypoplastic left heart syndrome, intact atrial septum

A male newborn was delivered at term weighing 3,400 g. The vital signs were as follows: heart rate: 160 beats/min; tachypneic: 70 breaths/min; mean arterial pressure: 35 mm Hg; and Sao2: 75% to 84%. Prenatal ultrasonography revealed hypoplastic left heart syndrome (HLHS), requiring urgent transfer to a cardiac surgery center.

Bedside transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) confirmed HLHS with mitral and aortic atresia, intact atrial septum (IAS), extremely hypoplastic ascending aorta (1.7-2.0 mm), and transverse arch (4 mm) with retrograde flow from the patent arterial duct (PAD) (Figures 1A and 1B, Videos 1 and 2). There was a wide levoatriocardinal vein providing unobstructed pulmonary venous drainage into the innominate vein and superior vena cava (SVC) (Figure 1C, Video 3).

Alprostadil infusion was administered, and after multidisciplinary triage, heart catheterization was performed with a 5-F sheath traversing the right femoral vein. The surgical target was to create an atrial septum defect that would avoid cross-clamp circulation during the hybrid procedure. Norwood operation was an alternative option; however, the extremely hypoplastic ascending aorta carried significant mortality risk.

The attempts of atrial septum perforation from the right atrium were technically challenging and unfeasible. Therefore, a multipurpose catheter was introduced via the SVC and innominate and levoatriocardinal veins into the left atrium (LA) with subsequent angiography, which confirmed IAS (Video 4). Atrial septum perforation was performed using the stiff tip of the 0.018- and 0.035-inch guidewires with the approach from the LA under TTE and fluoroscopy guidance (Figure 1D, Video 5). Then, static septoplasty was performed with 6 × 20 mm and 10 × 20 mm Tyshak balloon catheters (NuMed) introduced over a 0.018-inch guidewire (Figure 1E, Video 6). TTE showed LA decompression, yet atrial septum defect remained restricted with left-to-right shunting and a mean pressure gradient of 4 mm Hg (Figure 1F, Video 7). Within 5 days, the newborn underwent a hybrid procedure with bilateral pulmonary artery branch banding, a 7 × 12 mm Palmaz balloon expandable stent deployment into the PAD, and septectomy under cross-clamp circulation. The stent was unstable and embolized into the descending aorta and required Cordis Powerflex 10-mm balloon dilation, avoiding the celiac trunk obstruction. Finally, a 10 × 19 mm Palmaz Genesis stent was deployed into the PAD, allowing sufficient flow (Video 8). An uneventful postoperative period ensued with SaO2 of 80% to 86%, and the patient was discharged home with mild central cyanosis. At the 5-month follow-up, a second palliative hybrid stage involved aortic arch reconstruction, Glenn procedure, and left pulmonary artery Palmaz Genesis 8 × 18 mm stent deployment. Unfortunately, the patient died in the early postoperative period of severe cardiopulmonary decompensation with right ventricle dysfunction and SVC syndrome.

All current treatment options in single ventricle palliation provide only some success and often fail in the interim period toward Fontan completion.1, 2, 3 Percutaneous interventional atrial septum perforation under fluoroscopy and TTE guidance in the presence of a levoatriocardinal vein in HLHS with IAS (Supplemental Figure 1) provides an alternative, technically feasible procedure of LA decompression toward multistage single-ventricle therapy.

Exploring the therapeutic potential of epsom salt bath on pain and intensity of knee osteoarthritis: Randomized controlled trial
Authors: Vijay Kumar, Rajesh Shyoran, Bharat Pareek, Rimple Pathania, Ria Ranaut, Ramya Kundayi Ravi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Osteoarthritis is significantly linked to aging and heavy physical occupational activity, which is a necessary part of many people’s daily lives, and Epsom salt baths are extremely popular among knee pain sufferers. The study aimed to compare the effectiveness of Epsom salt baths in preventing pain and improving activity of daily living among elderly people with knee osteoarthritis.MATERIAL AND METHODS:The randomized controlled trial design was adopted in this investigation, with a posttest-only control group. 64 adult patients with knee osteoarthritis were included in the trial via full enumeration sampling. Selected subjects were randomly assigned to experimental (n = 32) or control (n = 32) groups via statistics and sample size software. The experimental group received Epsom salt baths for seven days, whereas the control group received standard care. The incidence and severity of pain and improving activity of daily living on the seventh day of the intervention were used to determine the study’s results. The trial was registered with the Clinical Trials Registry-India (CTRI). Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) were followed. Descriptive and inferential statistics were used for data analysis.RESULTS:The findings revealed that the intervention was found to be effective in terms of reducing the incidence of pain and improving activity of daily living among subjects of the experimental group as compared to the control group (P < 0.001*). A significant difference was observed in terms of WOMAC score (P < .001*), KATZ score (P < .001*), and NPRS score (P < .001*).CONCLUSION:Knee osteoarthritis is a common condition faced by the elderly population. Epsom salt reduces pain and improves the activity of daily living. Nurse practitioners must recognize the importance of Epsom salt baths and can use them as a complementary treatment along with the standard treatment to reduce suffering.

Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) is the most common form of arthritis and is one of the leading causes of disability worldwide. This degenerative and progressive joint disease affects around 250 million people worldwide. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), over 343 million people are affected by some form of osteoarthritis, and the incidence is higher in women than in men.[1] Worldwide osteoarthritis affects 9.6% of men and 18% of women at the age of 60 or above, and the condition was the 4th leading cause of disability by 2020.[2]

KOA primarily occurs in ≥50-year-old individuals.[3] In India, among OA patients, nearly 80% of the population had knee joint pain, out of which approximately 20% reported incapabilities in daily activities and around 11% needed peculiar care.[4] Approximately 40% of the population had OA above the age group of 70 years, in which nearly 2% had severe knee pain and disability. With the acceleration of population aging and the increase in the proportion of obese individuals, KOA is the 11th leading cause of disability worldwide and ranks 38th among disability factors that affect life expectancy.[5]

Knee pain is the most common symptom of osteoarthritis in the knee, making it painful for jog, run, climb stairs, or kneel. It can also make knees feel stiff or swollen. Over time, osteoarthritis of the knee can change the shape of knee joint, making joint feel unstable or wobbly. The knee pain that is gradual in onset and worse with activity, pain after prolonged sitting or resting, and pain that worsens over time are the cardinal signs of knee osteoarthritis.[6] As a consequence of reduced movement, muscles often lose strength and people become less able to perform physical activities.[7] Knee pain contributes to inactivity thus ultimately impairing quality of life. It is well established that joint pain, swelling, or stiffness can make it difficult for individuals to perform simple activities of daily living (ADL) such as opening boxes of food, tucking in bedsheets, writing, using a computer mouse, driving a car, walking, climbing stairs, and lifting objects.[8]

Evidence-based approaches to the treatment of knee osteoarthritis (OA) include nonpharmacologic, pharmacologic, and surgical modalities targeted at relieving pain, improving joint function, and modifying risk factors for disease progression.[9] Epsom salt, as a complementary therapy is the magnesium sulfate mineral, has been found to help alleviate pain. Additionally, soaking in an Epsom salt and Epsom salt compress helps to pull toxins from the body, which enhances the healing process.[4]

Material and Methods

Study design and setting

A parallel group randomized control trial with posttest only control group design was adopted to evaluate the effect of Epsom salt bath among the subjects having knee osteoarthritis.

64 patients with primary knee osteoarthritis who were primarily diagnosed, aged from 50 to 65 years, residing in the community area of district Mohali Punjab.

Study participants and sampling

Sixty-four patients with knee joint osteoarthritis were selected. Sampling was based on pre-determined inclusion criteria. Inclusion criteria included male and female, aged 50–65 years, Known case of osteoarthritis of the knee for a minimum of 6 months, and available at the time of data collection. Exclusion criteria included patients who were not accessible at the time of data collection, clients with congenital deformity, accidental injury, and old trauma of knee joint. First, the study enrolled the client based on inclusion criteria through complete enumeration sampling. Afterward, with the help of software, subjects were randomized into experimental and control groups using simple randomization. The sample size was calculated based on precision rate (0.8), α (0.05), confidence level, i.e., at 95% (Z = 1.96), and standard deviation of 5.4 (roughly estimated based on the pilot study). Calculated effect size was 0.4. A minimum sample of 60 (30 in each group) was needed. By considering the attrition and loss of subjects during follow-up, a final sample size was fixed to 64 (32 for each group).

Outcome measurement scales

The sociodemographic profile of the participant and family profile was obtained with 20 questions (e.g., age, gender, educational status, occupation, lifestyle pattern, use of toilet style, BMI of participant, family history, duration, any treatment for disease, and use of complimentary therapy), which was completed by question from participant’s self and family. The outcome of intervention was measured by the following tools-

Western Ontario and McMaster osteoarthritis index WOMAC checklist in the form of a Likert scale (no pain, low pain, moderate pain, high pain, and very high pain). The score of each item varies between 0 and 4, and the evaluation is done in three parts: 1. Examination of knee pain (5 items and a score of 0–20), 2. Joint dryness (2 items and a score of 0–8), and 3. Physical performance in the last 48 hrs (17 items and score 0–68). A higher score indicates more severe symptoms. Tool reliability was found to be 0.89. Katz Index of Independence The Katz Index of Independence was used to assess the client’s ability to perform activities of daily living independently. The index ranks adequacy of performance in the six functions of bathing, dressing, toileting, transferring, continence, and feeding. Clients are scored yes/no for independence in each of the six functions. A score of 6 indicates full function, 4 indicates moderate impairment, and 2 or less indicates severe functional impairment. Numerical pain rating scale The Numeric Pain Rating Scale (NPRS) is a unidimensional measure of pain intensity in adults. Scores range from 0 to 10 points, with higher scores indicating greater pain intensity.

Epsom salt intervention

It includes the 30 grams of commercially available Epsom salt to one liter of water, dip the towel in it, and squeeze it. The towel is applied over the knee for 20 minutes for seven days in the experimental group. The control group received routine care. A posttest was conducted on the 7th day of the intervention among the experimental and control group subjects. The study was conducted using the consolidated standards of reporting trials (CONSORT) statement [Figure 1].

Consort chart

Statistical analysis

Collected data were checked for outliers, wild codes, irregularities, and missing values. The final analysis was performed on 64 responses (32 each in the experimental and control group) The equality of variances was determined using Levene’s test. The Chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test were used to analyze the categorical data, and an independent t-test was used to evaluate the effectiveness of the intervention. In all tests, a 95% confidence interval and a significance level of α = 0.05 were considered. Data was analyzed using SPSS 18 software. A P value of less than 0.05 was taken as an indicator of a difference in statistical significance.

Ethical consideration

The research was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) of the SPHE College of Nursing and dated April 2022 with registration No IEC/SPHE/0011/2022). The clinical Trial Registry–India (CTRI) registered the study protocol of clinical trials with reference number-REF/2023/05/066869. Informed consent was obtained from the subjects. Anonymity and confidentiality of data were ensured.

Discussion

The present study aimed to assess the effectiveness of Epsom salt bath intervention in reducing pain and improving activity of daily living among elderly people with knee osteoarthritis.

The intervention reduced the severity of pain and improved the activity of daily living among known cases of knee osteoarthritis though the time between intervention and posttesting was short.

Post-intervention one posttest was conducted on the 7th day of the study, and the scores were X ± SD, F: (P) (25.62 + 07.05, 1.57:<.001 and 73.21 + 13.65, 1.57:<.001) for WOMAC, (04.46 + 0.621, 2.509: <.001 and 02.43 + 0.913, 2.509: <.001) for KATZ and (02.31 + 0.470, 4.830: <.001 and 03.56 + 0.669, 4.830: <.001) for NPRS, respectively, in the subjects in the experimental and control group that is found to be statistically significant.

Study findings were supported by Damor Dinesh Chandra and Pujari Jitendra (2023) revealed that Epsom salt with hot water effectively reduced pain and improved function. The scores for pre- and posttest were X ± SD, F: (P) (47.47 ± 11.17, 10.794:<.001, and 31.90 ± 11.874, 10.794:<.001) for WOMAC, and (07.53 ± 1.525, 13.030:<.001, and 05.33 ± 1.729, 13.030:<.001) for NPRS score,[10] Lavanya Sankar (2019) result expressed that hot water application with Epsom salt is effective in reducing the intensity of knee joint pain among elderly people with score of WOMAC X ± SD, F: (P) (2.93 ± 0.593, 1.794: <.001, and 2.17 ± 0.384, 1.794: <.001) for pretest and posttest, respectively[11] Dr. Deepika R Kumar, Shalika Sandal (2023) concluded that hot water application with Epsom salt was effective in reducing joint pain, with NPRS score was X ± SD, F: (P) (04.60 ± 1.714, 2.550: >.001, and 05.20 ± 2.483, 0.961: <.001) for control and experimental group respectively.[12] Y. John Sam Arun Prabu (2022) concluded that hot water with Epsom salt was effective to reduce pain of knee joint and improve activity with NPRS score was X ± SD, F: (P) (63.41 ± 6.79, 37.43: <.001, and 19.76 ± 5.76, 37.43: <.001) for pre- and posttest, respectively.[13] Sucheta Roy and Ram Kumar Sharma[14] (2023) concluded that Epsom salt hot water application is effective in reducing knee joint pain, with WOMAC score was X ± SD, F: (P) (34.60 ± 4.518, 30.077: >.001 and 30.60 ± 6.526, 0.00: <.001) for control and experimental group respectively. Epsom salt baths in hot water reduce pain severity and enhance daily living activities. Epsom salt bath was effective in reducing pain and improving activity of daily living among elderly people living with knee osteoarthritis.[15]

Limitations and recommendations

The limitations of this research were that the study was conducted in a limited setting that may restrict generalizability, even though a strong research design was used to ensure high internal and external validity. Reliance on self-reported data from participants can cause memory distortion and other biases that might have affected the quality of data even though data was collected diligently using standard protocol and as per the CONSORT guidelines. The study’s timeframe might be insufficient to capture the long-term effects of Epsom salt baths on knee osteoarthritis, and the intervention’s efficacy may vary over time even though statistical effectiveness was observed after the 7-day intervention. Furthermore, factors beyond the scope of the study, such as participants’ treatments, lifestyle, or other medications, could influence the results. Controlling for these variables may be challenging and could introduce confounding effects even though a control group was also taken to make accurate interpretations and limit extraneous variables. Only a posttest design was used. Hence, the initial homogeneity of the subjects in the experimental and control groups was not determined, which might have affected the results, even though randomization was used to ensure group equivalency.

It is recommended to develop strategies to monitor participants’ compliance with the Epsom salt baths, such as regular check-ins, or objective biomarkers. Non-compliance can affect the intervention’s efficacy and should be considered in the analysis and interpretation of results.

Conclusion

Knee osteoarthritis is a common condition faced by the elderly population. Pain and activity of daily living are altered due to this medical condition. Epsom salt reduces pain and improves the activity of daily living. Nurse practitioners must recognize the importance of Epsom salt baths and can use them as complementary and standard treatments to reduce suffering.

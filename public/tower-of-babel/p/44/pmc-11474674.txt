Influence of temperament on early neurodevelopmental disorders: a systematic review protocol
Authors: Lucia Ponce Rodriguez, Carmen M Fuentes Rueda, Maria Rojo Franconetti, Laura Esteban, Joaquín A Ibáñez-Alfonso
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionSeveral studies have highlighted the role of temperament as a relevant construct to understand the wide variability observed in neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs) such as autism spectrum disorder and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder. Some studies have pointed to temperamental traits such as strained control as possible precursors to the development of these disorders. In addition, how temperament influences high-risk populations, as well as intervention programmes aimed at families, has been investigated.Methods and analysisThis paper presents the protocol that will be followed to carry out a systematic review, the objective of which is to know how child temperament is related to the different domains of development in children with NDD or the risk of suffering from it. The search strategy will be implemented in Web of Science (WoS Core Collection), PubMed, ERIC, PsycINFO and Cochrane databases. The risk of bias will be measured by the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale to carry out the integration of the results obtained to synthesis without meta-analysis will be used. This systematic review aims to improve scientific evidence for institutions and professionals and enhance the effectiveness of early care programmes for children with NDD and their families.Ethics and disseminationNo express approval has been sought from any ethics committee because there is no primary data involved and no access to confidential patient information.PROSPERO registration numberCRD42023445173.Keywords: Systematic Review, Developmental neurology & neurodisability, PAEDIATRICS

Introduction

Neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs) in early childhood are a set of disorders that are characterised by being heterogeneous in their clinical manifestations and with a high degree of overlap between the symptomatology of disorders considered different1 The aetiology is usually multifactorial, and similar behaviours or signs may be due to various causes.1 On the other hand, in these early stages, it is difficult to delimit disorders that share symptoms or subthreshold manifestations, such as the boundary between motor clumsiness and developmental coordination disorder, or social communication disorder and autism spectrum disorder (ASD), among others.2 3 The functional consequences of NDDs are not perceived uniformly by the affected child, their family, the professionals who treat them or the society in which they live.2 It is also important to consider the influence of varying developmental contexts and unique interactions between children. According to Roquette et al,4 parental competencies and the degree of family adjustment will provide experiences of greater or lesser responsiveness to the needs of the child with NDD. In addition, each child’s responses to these situations may be variable and not justified exclusively by the NDD.

Some lines of research, mainly focused on attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and ASD, have proposed temperament as a construct that could explain the wide heterogeneity found in these disorders and the transformations in the expression of signs over time.5,11 Temperament manifests itself early in a child’s life and is relatively stable throughout childhood.12 It can be defined as ‘basic dispositions inherent in the person that underlie and modulate the expression of activity, reactivity, emotionality, and sociability. Major elements of temperament are present early in life, and those elements are likely to be strongly influenced by biological factors. As development proceeds, the expression of temperament increasingly becomes more influenced by experience and context’.10

Different theoretical traditions approach temperament from other points of view, positioning themselves in the heredity-environment perspectives, the weight of biological and/or experiential factors and the description and number of traits that make up temperament or the assessment instruments, among others.10 13 However, they agree that temperament is the tendency of a person to respond to the demands of their environment, as well as the ability to modulate/control this response.7 Although some theories propose more traits than others, there are four key constructs on which there is a minimum of consensus: attention/behavioural regulation, emotionality (significantly negative), activity level and surgency/extraversion. It is assumed that combining different traits can predict a child’s behaviour. For example, the ability to regulate protects against the impact of negative emotionality; behavioural inhibition is related to negative emotionality and shyness, fear or social withdrawal; or activity level is linked to upwelling/extraversion and positive affect, pleasure-seeking and impulsivity.5 7 In this sense, temperament plays a crucial role in developing attachment and social relationships, adapting novel or stressful situations and the child’s attitude toward learning and healthcare, directly or indirectly, through interaction with caregivers and parenting practices.5 8

A large body of research proposes that temperament dimensions can positively and negatively influence social-emotional, behavioural and psychopathological development across the lifespan. Considering psychopathology viewpoints, theoretical positions that relate temperament to mental health range from models that consider them within a continuum, with temperament being a subclinical manifestation of the disorder, to models of vulnerability/resilience14 which understand temperament as a predisposing or protective factor for some diseases.15 There are two approaches to understanding how temperament and disorder relate to one another: some suggest that temperament can exacerbate or complicate the course of the disorder, while others argue that the disorder alters temperament. In any case, there are other factors, such as the mother’s gender or educational level, that can interfere with the whole process.5

Specifically, temperament began to be studied in children at risk or NDD, such as premature, developmentally delayed, neurological deficiency or ASD, finding much variability in the results and an association of more specific temperamental profiles in children with disabilities of genetic origin, such as Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome or Fragile X syndrome.16 Currently, associations between temperament and different NDDs continue to be sought, especially with ASD and ADHD. Evidence of specific temperamental profiles has been found in children with ASD concerning effortful control. Thus, it seems that children who have received the diagnosis have a lower ability to concentrate on relevant stimuli, change the focus of attention, perform problem-solving tasks, adapt to changes and have greater sensitivity to both changes and sensory stimulation.6 8 17 On the other hand, high levels of negative emotionality (including sensory perception distress) and low levels of self-regulation are significantly associated with ASD and ADHD.

It has been observed that high levels of activity are associated with ADHD symptoms, while low levels of upwelling/extraversion are associated with ASD6 7 18 Researchers are currently focusing on identifying early temperamental precursors to predict the course of NDDs and at-risk populations.9 11 19 These temperamental markers are linked to specific areas or domains of development. For example, high activity level scores have been associated with low expressive and comprehensible language development20 a negative relationship between negative affect at 6 months and vocabulary at 30 months,21 under effortful control with lower motor coordination development,22 high levels of upwelling/extraversion and negative affect with abrupt or violent play development,23 high levels of negative emotionality, together with low or high cognitive performance, were associated with subsequent ADHD-related behaviours,24 or high levels of positive emotionality at 12 months with high levels in measures of early learning at 24 months in children with special needs.25

According to studies, intervention programmes considering a child’s temperament have positively impacted their development. For example, programmes that involve families with very premature infants have been proven to change the way parents view their children, especially concerning how they express negative emotions. This has enabled parents to improve their ability to create more effective learning environments and encourage joint attention with their children.26 27

Objectives

This article presents the protocol for a systematic review of publications in scientific journals on how child temperament is related to different developmental domains in children with or at risk for neurodevelopmental disorders. When interactions have positive results, they will be considered markers of protection, when they have negative results, they will be considered markers of risk, and when they show no significant relationship, they will be considered unrelated constructs. The results of the review will provide an analysis of the relationships between temperamental traits and developmental domains (socioemotional, motor, linguistic and/or cognitive areas), taking into account the type of NDD, allowing us to know the assessment tools used, the intervention strategies that, were appropriate, could be used and the changes produced over time.

Method and analysis

This protocol was developed following the guidelines outlined in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions28 and the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses Protocols (PRISMA-P).29 It was registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews database (PROSPERO) on 13 July 2023. The study will be started in September 2024, and the expected completion date is May 2025.

The authors established specific inclusion and exclusion criteria to ensure a precise response to the research question. The PICO protocol was followed28 to include only the most relevant studies that fit the research objectives (see table 1).

PICO protocol and eligibility criteria

Type of participants

Studies referring to children under 6 years of age with NDDs or who are at risk of them will be selected. The NDDs included will be those listed in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders 5,30 such as intellectual developmental disorder, communication disorder, ASD, ADHD, specific learning disorder, motor disorders or other developmental disorders not otherwise specified. Children under 6 years of age who present any type of biopsychosocial risk, understood as the result of any prenatal or postnatal history that may result in transient or permanent cognitive, motor, sensory or behavioural developmental problems, including premature newborns or those with early adverse experiences, will also be considered.31 In longitudinal studies, articles whose population meets all the inclusion criteria and begins to be studied before age 6 will be included if they report results from a cohort of fewer than 6 years, considering only such information.

Type of interventions

All those studies carried out with children who are participating in an early care programme will be considered, understood as those interventions carried out with children under 6 years of age who have a developmental disorder or risk of suffering from it, their family or the environment, to intervene as soon as possible on the needs they may present.31

Type of comparators

Comparator groups will include any control groups, active or passive control groups and standardised measures in normative groups.

Outcome measures

To respond to the objective of this systematic review, the results that will be considered relevant will be all those that address the relationship between some temperament trait and the developmental domains, which may be positive, negative or unrelated. Developmental domains will be considered the competencies children show through standardised instruments in aspects associated with the socio-emotional, motor skills, adaptative behaviour, linguistic and/or cognitive areas.32,34 Temperament traits should also be measured through standardised instruments.

Type of studies

All studies that meet the inclusion and exclusion criteria will be analysed, regardless of the design used, the language or the year of publication, if published in a scientific journal. Sources such as books, doctoral theses, reports or other informative materials will not be analysed.

Search strategy

Electronic searches

The search equation will be initially piloted in the Web of Science Core Collection (WOS) database. To that purpose, high-frequency words extracted from the relevant literature and vocabulary controlled and guided by the Thesaurus of Psychological index terms (American Psychological Association -APA-) will be used. The results obtained in the preliminary searches will be taken to meetings with the review team to adjust, if necessary, those terms considered excessively broad or not well represented in the selected words. Finally, the agreed search strategy will be piloted in the WOS, PubMed, ERIC, PsycINFO and Cochrane databases. Table 2 presents an example of the used search strategy. Before the systematic review publication, we will conduct a final search to ensure that the included studies are updated.

Data collection and analysis

The records obtained from the different databases will be managed through the Rayyan online platform (Data Analytics). It is a website developed by Qatar Computing Research Institute to work in parallel with the three reviewers who will collaborate with the selection phase of articles by title and abstract through a blind review system. Initially, duplicate records will be removed. After this, reviewers should decide which articles are included in the full-text review phase, which are discarded and which are doubtful, considering the inclusion and exclusion criteria. In cases where there is no agreement, a fourth collaborator member of the team will participate in the decision-making. In the reading phase of the full text, the articles that are entirely suitable for this review will be chosen, discarding all those that deviate from the fundamental aspects of the study. The reasons why articles are excluded will be recorded for further reporting. The agreement level of the researchers will be calculated using the agreement index provided by the application. An agreement rate of more than 95% will be considered acceptable.

A detailed analysis of the articles finally selected will be carried out. The results will be presented in a summary table with the most relevant data: author, year of publication, country, age/age range, sex, sample size, type of design, type of risk or neurodevelopmental disorder, type of treatment, temperament assessment tools, developmental domain assessment tools, temperament outcomes and neurodevelopmental domains (socio-emotional, motor, linguistic and cognitive) at each moment of assessment (for longitudinal studies) and type of relation (positive, negative or unrelated). Analysis of treatment programmes will include both pre-intervention and post-intervention data.

Risk of bias assessment

Two reviewers will assess bias, and a third reviewer will resolve disagreements. The Newcastle-Ottawa Scale,35 developed for non-randomised studies such as those of interest in this review proposal, will be used to assess the risk of bias. This tool makes it possible to determine, through a star system, the articles that will be part of this review, considering three fundamental perspectives: the selection of the groups (maximum of 4 stars), the comparison of the groups (maximum of 2 stars) and the results of interest of the cohort and the control group (maximum of 3 stars).

Synthesis of results

Given that it is expected to find a high degree of heterogeneity among the selected articles (eg, significant variability in the conceptualisation of temperament, diversity of neurodevelopmental disorders and domains studied in each one, disparity in the age ranges used, no limitation of studies by year of publication or difference in the measurement instruments), the synthesis of data will be carried out following the indications of Synthesis Without Meta-Analysis (SWiM) in systematic reviews guidelines.36 Considering the study’s objective, the results will be grouped according to different temperament traits (eg, effortful control, extraversion or negative emotionality) using standardised values, means and mean differences. It will analyse how the results offered in each study are grouped and what criteria are used in each case, for example, if they are grouped by types of disorders, developmental domains, sex, age or other population characteristics.

Patient and public involvement

Patients will not be involved in conducting the systematic review.

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval has not been sought due to the documentary nature of the study, so no primary or confidential patient data has been accessed. The systematic review results will be presented to the scientific community through publication in peer-reviewed journals, specialised congresses and information channels of the participating entities, Universidad Loyola Andalucia and Hospital San Juan de Dios from Seville (Spain).

Discussion

Temperament plays a crucial role in child development and appears to influence how neurodevelopmental disorders are expressed, directly or indirectly, through the relationship with the environment. To the best of our knowledge, there has not been a systematic review in which temperament is related to the different domains of child development in children with neurodevelopmental disorders or at risk of suffering from it. Therefore, following the Prisma-P statement29 we designed a protocol that answers this research question.

Based on the findings derived from this review, the authors anticipate gaining insights into temperamental profiles linked to proficiency in various developmental domains. The objective is to determine the type of relationship (positive, negative or none) between these profiles and atypical functioning, irrespective of the diagnosed neurodevelopmental disorder.

Finally, it would also be interesting to know if temperament can be considered a risk marker or protection for the trajectories of NDDs. Additionally, it is crucial to identify which aspects of temperament have been targeted in treatment programmes aimed at enhancing competencies across various developmental domains.

Any modifications that occur concerning the initial approach, as well as the progression of the review, will be recorded in PROSPERO.

The final objective of this research will be to improve intervention programmes, considering temperamental traits as elements that could be incorporated into treatments to improve the effectiveness of interventions and children’s relationships with their families and the environments in which they develop. These results will offer a framework based on scientific evidence that will help the decision-making of institutions that want to promote comprehensive child development and improving interventions in Early Intervention.

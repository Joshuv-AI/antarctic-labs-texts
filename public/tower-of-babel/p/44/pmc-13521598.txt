FUT2 non-secretor status is not associated with disease phenotype or outcomes in patients with Crohn’s disease
Authors: Rachel Lombard, Wenfei Wang, Alexander Chervonsky, Sushila Dalal, Russell D Cohen, Atsushi Sakuraba, David T Rubin, Christopher R Weber, Joel Pekow
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe fucosyltransferase 2 gene (FUT2) is responsible for the regulation of mucosal blood type antigens which can act as receptors for bacterial adhesion and regulate intestinal flora. A homozygous nonsense mutation in this gene leads to the absence of these antigens, termed FUT2 non-secretor status, and is associated with the development of Crohn’s disease (CD). The mechanism of this association is likely via changes in homeostatic symbiosis, thus promoting development of inflammation. However, the impact of FUT2 genotype on CD phenotype and outcomes is not known.MethodsSubjects were prospectively enrolled into a single-center translational IBD registry. All patients with CD who had available DNA extracted from peripheral blood for genotyping were included. Genotyping was performed using the rs601338-AA SNP, which represents the nonsense mutation (428GA) shown to be causing non-secretor status. Baseline demographics, disease phenotype, CD-related surgeries, and hospitalizations were also assessed retrospectively and statistically compared based on FUT2 genotype.Results635 patients with CD were included in the analysis, including 178 FUT2 wild-type (WT), 314 heterozygous (HET) and 143 homozygous (O) patients. The mean duration of follow-up was 8 years and 2 months. There were no statistically significant differences detected between genotypes when comparing sex, race, smoking status, body-mass index, and age of onset. Presence of extra-intestinal manifestations including arthritis, uveitis, erythema nodosum, pyoderma gangrenosum and primary sclerosing cholangitis, as well as disease location and behavior were also found to be similar when compared between O and WT, O and HET, and O and HET plus WT populations. The mean number of biologics used between groups was 1.50 (O), 1.39 (HET)and 1.42 (WT) (P = .55), with no difference in total duration of biologic therapy between groups. 60.8% of O patients used immunomodulators compared to 54.8% of HET and 59.6% of WT patients. In addition, there were no differences between groups in the number of patients who required more than one CD-related hospitalization (O = 30.1%, HET = 34.1%, WT = 30.3%) or surgery (O = 32.2%, HET = 39.5%, WT = 32%).ConclusionIn this single-center analysis of 635 patients with CD, the FUT2 genotype was not associated with disease phenotype, disease severity, or clinical outcomes. These data suggest that while lack of fucosylation is associated with developing CD, it does not play a major role in driving disease progression.

Introduction

Crohn’s disease (CD) is a subtype of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) that is characterized by transmural chronic inflammation of the GI tract. The exact etiology of CD is not fully understood, although a leading hypothesis is that the disease occurs due to an interaction of environmental factors, such as drugs, infections, or intestinal microbes with the immune system in a genetically susceptible host. Supporting this hypothesis, population-based studies have provided compelling evidence that genetic factors contribute to the pathogenesis of Crohn’s disease. To date, over 300 genomic loci that have been discovered by genome-wide association studies (GWAS) to be significantly associated with IBD, with over 200 loci associated with CD.1–3 Genomic loci of particular interest include the loss of function alleles for the fructosyltransferase 2 gene (FUT2), which is located in the q13 region of chromosome 19 and encodes alpha-(1,2)-fucosyltransferase. Alpha-(1,2)-fucosyltransferase regulates the expression of ABH antigens in saliva, mucosal tissue, and secretions.3 These ABH antigens act as receptors for bacterial adhesion in the GI tract and therefore may play a role in regulating intestinal flora.4

A nonsense mutation in FUT2 leads to the absence of these antigens, termed FUT2 non-secretor status. Homozygous individuals (O) with nonfunctional enzymes are termed non-secretors, whereas heterozygous individuals (HET) have secretion similar to the wild-type (WT) and are termed FUT2 secretors.5 Several studies have reported a linkage between FUT2 non-secretor status and CD susceptibility particularly in Caucasian and Chinese patient populations.6 Another study examined intestinal microbiota and found unique aspects of intestinal bacterial diversity with respect to both CD disease status and FUT2 genotype.7 However, no previous analysis has investigated the relationship of FUT2 genotype with disease phenotype or outcomes in CD patients. The aim of this study is to explore if disease phenotype and clinical outcomes in patients with Crohn’s disease differ based on FUT2 genotype.

Methods

Patients at the University of Chicago Medical Center with a diagnosis of CD were prospectively consented to genotyping and collection of clinical data under IRB 15073A. Study specific data points were collected retrospectively under IRB 16-1165 in patients with CD who had DNA previously collected. Real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) using functionally tested TaqMan SNP Genotyping Assays (Applied Biosystems) for the single nucleotide polymorphism, rs601338 (W143x), were used to genotype the primary non-secretor allele in the FUT2 gene on an automated platform. Based on genotyping, subjects were grouped as wild type (WT) if no mutation on either allele, heterozygous (HET) if one allele with a mutation, and homozygous mutation (O) if mutation on both alleles.

Baseline demographics, disease phenotype, medication usage, CD-related surgeries, and CD-related hospitalizations were assessed retrospectively using the electronic medical record from 2012 to 2018. Baseline demographics included sex, race, smoking history, and body-mass index (BMI). Disease characteristics included the age at diagnosis of IBD, location of disease, disease behavior and presence of extra-intestinal manifestations. Medication usage was assessed if a patient had prior exposure to immunomodulators (eg, 6-azathioprine, 6-mercaptopurine, methotrexate) or a prior exposure to a biologic medication (anti-TNF therapies, anti-integrin therapies, anti-IL23 therapies). Pairwise comparisons based on FUT2 genotype were made using the Chi-square test, the two-tailed unpaired Student’s t test or the Mann—Whitney U test, while Kruskal–Wallis was used for multiple group comparisons. A P value of <.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 635 patients with CD were included in the analysis. The study population was comprised of 178 FUT2 wild-type (WT), 314 heterozygous (HET), and 143 homozygous (O) patients. 87.7% of patients identified as white (22.6% O), 8.0% black/African American (25.0% O), 2.2% Asian/Mideast/Indian (14% O), 1.4% more than on race (22.2% O), and 0.6% Native American/Alaskan (0% O). The mean duration of follow up was 8 years and 2 months. There were no statistically significant differences detected between genotypes when comparing sex, race, smoking, or BMI (Table 1). Presence of extra-intestinal manifestations, including arthritis, uveitis, erythema nodosum, pyoderma gangrenosum and primary sclerosing cholangitis, as well as disease location and behavior, were also found to be similar when compared between O and WT, O and HET, and O and HET + WT populations (Table 1).

Discussion

This is the largest study evaluating the impact of FUT2 genotype polymorphisms with clinical phenotype and disease course in patients with CD. We studied a cohort of 635 patients with CD, of whom 22.5% had a genotype associated with FUT2 non-secretor status. In this cohort, there was no association between the FUT2 genotype and disease phenotype or disease severity in patients with CD as assessed by biologic usage, IBD-related surgeries, or IBD-related hospitalizations. One previous analysis of 62 patients with Crohn’s disease demonstrated a protective association of a homozygous FUT2 genotype with clinical outcomes.8 Potentially, differences in the outcomes observed in our analysis are related to a larger patient population, a lower proportion of patients with Crohn’s colitis who were nonsecretors, and a longer duration of follow-up to assess outcomes.

It is estimated that 20% of the population are FUT2 non-secretors who do not express ABO antigens in their GI tract and saliva due to homozygous mutation for non-secretor alleles.5 FUT2’s role in the GI tract and its downstream effects on the microbiome make it an interesting target for investigating its potential role in the pathogenesis of CD. FUT2 is the enzyme primarily responsible for fucosylation in the gut, which is activated by IL-22. Once activated, fucosylated proteins produced by the small intestine are then available for consumption for downstream microbiota.9 Microbial fucosidases can liberate fucose from the fucosylated proteins, which enhance the relationship of symbionts through production of metabolites and improve colonization resistance against pathogens, as well as directly suppressing bacterial virulence. When FUT2 is nonfunctional, this beneficial symbiont relationship weakens and leaves the gut vulnerable to pathobionts.10 The presence of such pathobionts may stimulate host inflammatory responses, which could play a role in promoting intestinal inflammation.11 In our study, we did not find an association of FUT2 non-secretor status with disease phenotype or severity. This may be due to the fact that lack of fucosylation is important in development of CD, but does not contribute to site of involvement, disease related complications, or disease progression in patients with existing CD.

Although we report on a large cohort of patients with CD, there were several limitations in the analysis. Our analysis was limited to the rs601338 polymorphism, which has been shown to be associated with FUT2 secretor status primarily in European populations.12,13 Other polymorphisms of FUT2, including rs602662, rs676388, rs492602, rs504963, and rs475176, have been associated with Crohn’s disease.3 All of these polymorphisms are in linkage disequilibrium with rs601338, and a previous analysis found a strong correlation with rs601338 in a cohort primarily of Caucasians.3 Other variants, including rs1047781, are associated with FUT2 secretor status in Asian populations, yet are virtually absent in Europeans.14 Although 88% of the patients in our cohort were white, with 2.2% who identified as Asian/Mideast/Indian, our analysis may not have captured secretor status in all subjects, and the findings may not be generalizable to other populations. Additionally, we only evaluated FUT2 genotype and outcomes in patients with CD and did not have a non-IBD patient population as a control comparison. Finally, the retrospective analysis, including patients from a single tertiary center in the United States, allows for risk of bias and may not be applicable to other populations.

In conclusion, in this large single-center analysis of 635 patients with CD, FUT2 non-secretor status was not associated with disease phenotype or clinical outcomes. Future studies investigating disease progression and response to individual therapies based on genotype are needed to determine the role of the FUT2 genotype on the long-term clinical course of CD.

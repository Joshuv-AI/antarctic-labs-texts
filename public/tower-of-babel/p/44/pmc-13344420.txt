Transcriptional co-activators YAP/TAZ in cancer-associated fibroblasts are dispensable for Notch-driven intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma
Authors: Tingting Deng, Yuemei Qiao, Xinyu Xie, Yanjun Wu, Gaoxiang Ge, Weiwei Yang
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) is a typical desmoplastic tumor featured with abundant cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) in the tumor microenvironment (Affo et al., 2021). CAFs in ICCs are further categorized into the inflammatory and growth factor-enriched CAF (iCAF) and myofibroblastic CAF (myCAF) subpopulations, which regulate proliferation, survival, and migration of ICC tumor cells, as well as remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) (Affo et al., 2021). It is yet largely unanswered how ICC CAFs are activated and regulate ICC development. YAP/TAZ play key roles in regulating organ size, tissue regeneration, and cancer in cooperation with TEAD family transcription factors (Yu et al., 2015). Mechanical signals are potent YAP/TAZ regulators (Dupont et al., 2011). As activated fibroblasts reside in stiff microenvironment in desmoplastic tumors, YAP/TAZ activation is proposed as an early marker of fibroblast activation and potential therapeutic target for cancer (Calvo et al., 2013). However, the functional roles of YAP/TAZ in fibroblast activation and desmoplastic tumor development are still controversial.

Concomitant expression of activated Akt1 and Notch1 intracellular domain (Akt1/NICD1) induced rapid development of typical desmoplastic ICC (Figure 1A; Fan et al., 2012). Employment of reporters driven by hepatic stellate cell (HSC)-specific Lrat-iCre and CAF-specific Col1a2-CreERT, respectively, revealed that ∼85% CAFs in Akt1/NICD1-induced ICC were derived from HSCs (Supplementary Figure S1A–D). Upon activation, YAP/TAZ accumulate and translocalize to the nucleus (Yu et al., 2015). Abundant nuclear YAP/TAZ in Desmin-positive HSCs were present in the livers from mice subjected to Akt1/NICD1-induced ICC but not in normal livers (Figure 1A and B). Multiplex staining further reveled nuclear YAP/TAZ accumulation in the α-smooth muscle actin (αSMA)-positive HSC-derived CAFs but not in the αSMA-negative quiescent HSCs (Supplementary Figure S1E and F). YAP/TAZ protein accumulation in activated HSCs were further supported by western blot analysis with the isolated HSCs (Supplementary Figure S2A). However, only a subset of canonical YAP/TAZ target genes was upregulated in the HSCs isolated from ICC tumors (Supplementary Figure S2B). To investigate whether YAP/TAZ regulate HSC activation and ICC development, Col1a2-CreERT;Yap1f/f;Wwtr1f/f (DKOactHSC) compound mice were used to simultaneously knock out functionally redundant YAP/TAZ (Yu et al., 2015) in activated HSCs (Figure 1C; Supplementary Figure S2C). YAP/TAZ knockout in activated HSCs did not inhibit Akt1/NICD1-induced tumor development, with unaffected liver-to-body weight ratio, tumor number and size, and tumor cell proliferation (Figure 1D–H). Tumors in DKOactHSC livers exhibited characteristic ICC morphology, which did not express the hepatocyte marker HNF-4α but expressed ICC markers SOX9, EpCAM, and cytokeratin-19, similar to the tumors in the control livers (Figure 1D, G, and H). ICC is characterized by a desmoplastic tumor microenvironment with complex TIME landscape (Lin et al., 2022). Expression of pan-CAF markers αSMA and Col-I was unaffected upon YAP/TAZ deletion (Figure 1I and J; Supplementary Figure S3A). Expression levels of the markers for iCAF and myCAF subpopulations that promote ICC development (Affo et al., 2021) were indistinguishable between DKOactHSC and control livers (Supplementary Figure S3B and C). Furthermore, the deposition of hyaluronan, a myCAF-secreted non-protein effector (Affo et al., 2021), and infiltration of macrophages and T cells in ICC tumors were all unaffected upon YAP/TAZ deletion in HSCs (Figure 1I–L). These collectively suggested that YAP/TAZ are dispensable for HSC activation and function in Akt1/NICD1-driven ICC.

YAP/TAZ primarily bind to TEAD family transcription factors to regulate gene transcription (Yu et al., 2015). The Lrat-iCre;loxP-stop-loxP-FLAG-DN-TEAD2 (DN-TEAD2HSC) compound mice were used to express dominant negative TEAD2 mutant (Liu-Chittenden et al., 2012) and thus abrogate TEAD-dependent YAP/TAZ transcriptional activity in HSCs and HSC-derived CAFs (Supplementary Figure S2A and B). DN-TEAD2 expression in the HSCs did not affect HSC activation, Akt1/NICD1-induced hepatocyte-to-cholangiocarcinoma transdifferentiation, cancer cell proliferation, tumor development, and immune cell infiltration (Supplementary Figures S4 and S5).

Despite nuclear YAP/TAZ accumulation in activated HSCs have been reproducibly reported and YAP knockdown impairs in vitro HSC activation (Mannaerts et al., 2015), YAP/TAZ–TEADs may be largely dispensable for HSC activation and fibrotic liver diseases in vivo. Defective YAP/TAZ expression or TEAD-dependent transcription did not affect HSC activation, Akt1/NICD1-induced ICC, and liver fibrosis (Xu et al., 2021). With nuclear YAP/TAZ accumulation in activated HSCs, only a subset of YAP/TAZ target genes increased the expression. In activated HSCs/CAFs, YAP/TAZ transcriptional activity may be highly selective, dependent on the availability of their partner transcription factors. At the cellular level, CAFs are functionally diverse population. YAP/TAZ may execute different, even opposing, transcription programs and functions in different CAF subpopulations. Therefore, the nuclear localization of YAP/TAZ cannot be simplistically equated with being pro-tumorigenic or anti-tumorigenic. ICCs are highly heterogeneous in genetic alterations. Fibroblast YAP/TAZ in ICCs driven by different oncogenes or chronic inflammation may differ significantly. In line to this notion, deletion of YAP in HSCs moderately reduced HSC activation and tumor development in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), maybe due to spatial discrepancy of intratumoral CAFs in ICCs and peritumoral CAFs in HCCs (Filliol et al., 2022). CAFs are the main regulator of immunosuppressive TIME and contribute to drug resistance. CAFs may undergo further changes in a YAP/TAZ-dependent or independent manner in response to immunotherapy or chemotherapy. Such changes may reshape the tumor microenvironment and the properties of the cancer cells, thereafter modulate the responses of cancer cells to therapeutic interventions. Thus, it warrants further investigation to what extent and how fibroblast YAP/TAZ contribute to liver cancer development in a context-dependent manner.

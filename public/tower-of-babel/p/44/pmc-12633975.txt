To gain insights and compare different quality certification programs for public health facilities in India
Authors: Bankim Pankajkumar Gandhi, Dinesh Bhanderi, Hemshree N. Parmar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe Government Health system has started to implement various quality certification programs (QCPs) like National Quality Assurance Standards (NQAS), Kayakalp, Labour room, and Quality improvement initiatives (LaQshya), etc., on a wider scale. Rural and tribal India poses significant challenges when it comes to addressing healthcare quality. So it’s very crucial to compare the checkpoints, standards, and elements of these programs as some might have overlapping which can sometimes lead to extra efforts in implementation, which should not happen. Thus, this present study was designed to find out various means of verification, common standards, and checkpoints amongst them. Understand quality certification programs such as Kayakalp and NQAS. Identify various verification methods, common standards, measurable elements, and checklists associated with these quality certification programs. This study has done a detailed overview of all available quality improvement programs for Primary Health Centre and Sub Health Centre (Health and Wellness Centre). Also, it included 1-2 Quality certification programs specific to community health centers like MusQan in this study to get an idea of the services expected to be available there as its first referral point from PHC. Comparison of these QCPs included a systematic evaluation with the use of their standardized checklists. The researcher studied all QCPs and calculated every QCP’s standards, criteria, checkpoints, and means of verification with the use of an Excel sheet. Standards in NQAS-related QCP like NQAS for PHC, LaQshya (Labour room and Quality improvement initiatives) in the comparison to NQAS PHC’s labor room criteria, Musquan, and NQAS for HWC were relatively the same. In the same way, Kayakalp for PHC and HWC, Indian Public Health Standards for PHC (2022), Bronze certification under AB-PMJAY (Ayushman Bharat-Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojna), and NABH (National Accreditation Board for hospital and healthcare providers) standards for PHC and CHC are relatively guided by IPHS and other general criteria. A systematic evaluation was conducted to assess each program’s features and outcomes, enabling a detailed comparison. Overall, after considering and looking after all standards for quality improvement initiatives, we can say that they are connected. Almost all QCPs have areas like patient safety, infrastructure availability, infection control practices, manuals and SOPs, training of all staff, satisfaction surveys, data collection and management techniques, cleanliness drives, and other support services. So if any facility wants to achieve higher results and have a system of quality care, they should first prefer QCP, which starts with a smaller number of standards like Kayakalp. Subsequently, they can later on go for SaQushal, Bronze for PMJAY, and finally NQAS. This ensures that quality is continuously upheld, and the staff of health facilities becomes well-versed in overall relevant terminologies and processes. QCPs hold great promise as they enable and empower these facilities to achieve quality and higher standards with the availability of better care. Overall, after considering and looking after all standards for quality improvement initiatives, we can say that they are connected. So if any facility wants to achieve higher results and have a system of quality care, they should first prefer QCP which has lower standards and checkpoints.

Introduction

Quality in healthcare is a comprehensive concept that encompasses several important dimensions. It should not only be safe, effective, patient-centric, affordable, efficient, and timely but also accountable and equipped with the necessary resources to meet patient expectations and deliver high-quality health services. In the absence of these elements, the health system can struggle to provide the desired level of quality care.[1]

As per WHO, “Quality of care is the degree to which health services for individuals and populations increase the likelihood of desired health outcomes. It is based on evidence-based professional knowledge and is critical for achieving universal health coverage.”[2]

Quality in the healthcare system is indeed a crucial and multifaceted concept. It encompasses various aspects of patient care and is significant from the perspective of individuals seeking healthcare services. Managing, improving, and controlling quality in healthcare settings is a complex endeavor that does not have a single, straightforward approach.[3]

The National Health Policy (NHP) of 2017 emphasizes the objective of enhancing health status through concentrated policy actions in all sectors and expanding healthcare services with a primary focus on quality. This underscores the importance of providing high-quality healthcare services across the spectrum, including preventive, promotive, curative, palliative, and rehabilitative care.[4]

The National Rural Health Mission (NRHM), initiated in 2005, was established with the mission goal of improving the availability and access to quality healthcare, especially for people residing in rural areas, the economically disadvantaged, women, and children. This mission aimed to provide effective, efficient, and accountable healthcare to the rural population of India.[5]

Additionally, the establishment of Quality Assurance Committees (QACs) initiatives collectively reflect the ongoing efforts to address healthcare quality comprehensively in India.[6]

Various quality certification programs are being implemented in public health facilities to address these aspects of quality. These programs, including Ayushman Bharat Pradhanmantri Jan Arogya Yojna (AB PMJAY) quality certification, National Quality Assurance Standards (NQAS), Kayakalp, Labour room quality improvement initiative (LaQshya), Swachh Swasth Sarvatra mission, National Accreditation Program for hospital and healthcare providers (NABH), MusQan, SaQushal, SaQsham and others, aim to improve healthcare quality and ensure that it meets the required standards.[789101112131415]

The following are a few programs regarding quality certification in India.

Kayakalp Program

Introduction

One of the quality certification programs is the Kayakalp program. Kayakalp’ is a Sanskrit word that means ‘rejuvenation’. This was started in 2015 with a motive to promote cleanliness and hygiene, to guide health facilities for excellent sanitary-related facilities and preparedness, and later on to fix a standard for a clean drive in all public health facilities. Kayakalp Program has provided opportunities for public health facilities to upgrade as per cleanliness standards and to maintain a hygienic culture.[11]

There are mainly 6 thematic areas under Kayakalp.

NQAS (National Quality Assurance Standards)

Introduction

There were various challenges related to quality improvement standards and the proper framework for them. To guide these points and make a strong system for quality improvement, the Government of India has started NQAS.

The main purpose was to have solutions for problems like not enough health infrastructure or health facilities, a lack of numbers as per beneficiary, being unable to access those centers, and quality that was quite low as per standards. So to overcome all these problems, NQAS has been implemented. NQAS standards were launched as per facilities from time to time; the first for District Hospital was launched in 2013, and the standards for PHC and CHC were in 2014. Standards for health and wellness centers were launched in 2020–21. Over the years in India, the first IPHS was started: The Indian Public Health Standards (IPHS) was launched in 2005 with an updated version of revisions in 2011, which have a minimum required set of standards that require minimum infrastructure availability, availability of services, the required workforce at the health facility level, what will be regulatory requirements for medicines, lab tests, etc.[17]

NQAS made excellent progress in the last 7 years. From 10 certified health facilities in 2016-17 to 829 certified health facilities in year 2022-23.

Process

NQAS will guide quality processes and quality improvement at the health facility level. It generally focuses on the public health level, and it includes major eight areas of concern. Each area has its own specific standards, and each has its own various elements.

Mera-Aspataal

The quality of services in any health facility is reflected in real terms when it shows a patient-centric approach. Looking for this approach, the MoHFW has developed a system for measuring patient satisfaction and launched a patient and relative-centric MyGov platform named the ‘Mera-Aspataal’ (My Hospital) initiative on August 29, 2016.

Swachh Swasth Sarvatra

To achieve the target of ‘Health for all’, the coordination of health care providers and communities is very important. Swachh Swasth Sarvatra (SSS), launched in December 2016, is a joint initiative of the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare and the Ministry of Drinking Water and Sanitation (now known as the Ministry of Jal Shakti), connecting and complementing the achievements of ‘Swachh Bharat Mission’ and ‘Kayakalp’ altogether.

LaQshya

India has made good progress in terms of the number of institutional deliveries and maternal and infant deaths since the initiation of the National Rural Health Mission in 2005. To reduce the preventable maternal and newborn mortality, morbidity, and stillbirths associated with the care during the delivery and immediate postpartum period; the ‘LaQshya’ initiative was launched in November 2017 [Table 4].[23]

AB PMJAY Quality Certification

The AB PMJAY Quality Certification Program is a joint initiative by NHA and QCI. The main focus is to provide quality healthcare services and to impact certification for hospitals at various levels, like Gold, Silver, and Bronze. Bronze certification can also be achieved by hospitals that are not impaneled under AB PMJAY. Both types of hospitals, one empanelled and the other non-empanelled hospitals are eligible to apply.

NABH

The National Accreditation Board for Hospitals and Healthcare Providers (NABH) is a constituent board of the Quality Council of India, set up to establish and operate accreditation programs for healthcare organizations. This board is set to have benchmarks in terms of quality and progress of the health industry [Table 5].

MusQan

MusQan is designed to ensure the provision of quality child-friendly services from birth to children up to 12 years of age. MusQan aims to ensure the provision of quality child-friendly services in public health facilities to reduce preventable newborn and child morbidity and mortality.

SaQushal: Safety and Quality: Self-Assessment Tool for Health Facilities

It is built from a patient safety perspective; it builds the capacity of the staff in patient safety with the requisite knowledge and skills. It helps improve patient safety and the quality of health care.

SaQsham Portal

It is an online portal. All types of health facilities can come up for national-level certification with this portal. It has been developed by the NHSRC under NHM. The main objective behind this is to ensure quality services at public health facilities and to improve them further to improve users’ experiences at the facilities.

This study has tried to compare them as standards-wise and objective elements, or checkpoints-wise.

A study conducted by Jain et al.[24] which was conducted among tertiary care hospitals found that AB-PMJAY (Ayushman Bharat- Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana) covers all the quality standards of NABH. Quality Certification Programs (QCPs) typically cover key areas such as patient safety, infrastructure availability, infection control practices, manuals and SOPs, staff training, satisfaction surveys, data collection and management, cleanliness initiatives, and other support services. Facilities aiming for improved outcomes and a sustainable quality care system should consider starting with QCPs that have a smaller set of standards, like NQAS. The gradual approach ensures consistent quality improvement while enabling staff to become proficient in relevant processes and terminologies.

Discussion

In the comparison of these QCPs

Previous studies on certification programs have generally focused on one or two specific programs, whereas this study offers a comprehensive comparison of all available certification programs for public health facilities.[181921] Overall, after considering and looking after all standards for quality improvement initiatives, we can say that they are connected. Almost all QCPs have areas like patient safety, infrastructure availability, infection control practices, manuals and SOPs, training of all staff, satisfaction surveys, data collection and management techniques, cleanliness drives, and other support services.

So if any facility wants to achieve higher results and have a system of quality care, they should first prefer QCP [Image-1], which starts with a smaller number of standards, like Kayakalp. Subsequently, they can later on go for SaQushal, Bronze for PMJAY, and finally NQAS. This ensures that quality is continuously upheld, and the staff of health facilities becomes well-versed in overall relevant terminologies and processes.

Step by step approach for sustainability

In this way, they can step by step achieve higher standards and also sustain a quality culture.

Conclusion

In conclusion, an analysis of quality improvement standards reveals a strong interconnectedness among them. Most Quality Certification Programs (QCPs) encompass areas such as patient safety, infrastructure availability, infection control practices, manuals and SOPs, staff training, satisfaction surveys, data collection and management, cleanliness initiatives, and other support services. For a facility aiming to achieve higher results and establish a robust quality care system, it is advisable to begin with a QCP that has a smaller set of standards, such as Kayakalp. From there, they can progress to SaQushal, Bronze for PMJAY, and eventually NQAS. This stepwise approach ensures sustained quality improvement while enabling the staff to become proficient in the relevant terminologies and processes.

Limitations of the study: This study includes quality certification programs related to public health facilities. Programs for private health facilities were not included in this study.

Future aspects of the study: More studies comparing certification programs across different states in India could be conducted to gain further insights from a state-level perspective.

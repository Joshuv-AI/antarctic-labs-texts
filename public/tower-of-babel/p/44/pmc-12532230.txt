Author Response: Hidden Constraints: Addressing Unreported Limitations in Plasmapheresis Studies for Hypertriglyceridemia-associated Pancreatitis
Authors: Prashant Nasa, Ravi Jain, Deven Juneja, Omender Singh
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

We would like to thank Mahajan et al., for their careful reading and constructive appraisal of our recently published meta-summary of case reports on the use of plasmapheresis for hypertriglyceridemia-induced acute pancreatitis (HTG-AP).1 We appreciate their recognition of our work. We are grateful for their constructive observations, which add important nuance to the interpretation of our findings and the broader discourse on this complex topic.

We concur with the authors that publication bias is a potential limitation of case reports or case series. These type of publications often tend to highlight successful or novel interventions, possibly overestimating their efficacy, while inadequately reporting adverse outcomes. This inherent challenge was present in our dataset which included all published case reports. We acknowledge the need for balanced reporting in future publications, which should include not only positive outcomes but also negative or neutral findings.

The considerations regarding timing and standardized indications for plasmapheresis are particularly significant. Our meta-summary revealed variability in both initiation timing and patient selection which reflects the heterogeneity present in real-world clinical practice. The American Society for Apheresis (ASFA) 2023 guidelines categorized HTG-AP as a class III indication for plasmapheresis, meaning the optimal role of this treatment option is not fully established, and decision-making should be individualized.2 Additionally, the influence of concomitant pharmacological agents—such as insulin, statins, fibrates, dietary modification, and other organ support—remains unavoidable confounding factor in this meta-summary, as many patients received multimodal management. Disentangling the independent effect of plasmapheresis will require carefully planned, controlled comparative studies. Moreover, establishing clear indications and timing will be crucial for the research question in future studies, and until then, recommendations from consensus guidelines could reduce variability that may confound outcome assessment.

Furthermore, we acknowledge the concerns raised regarding inconsistent reporting of adverse events. While our meta-summary found that major complications were not reported in the case reports, the retrospective and selective nature of case reports may have resulted in underestimation of both minor and delayed events. Additionally, the lack of long-term follow-up data precludes any meaningful conclusions regarding recurrence, sustained organ function improvement, or quality of life—critical parameters for comprehensive assessment of therapeutic value.

We also recognize the considerable heterogeneity present in plasmapheresis techniques employed in these patients–exchange volume, replacement fluid, frequency, and device type, as well as the diversity in patient populations (primary vs secondary HTG, pregnancy, comorbid conditions). Indeed, this limits the generalizability of pooled results. However, this also reflects the current off-label use of plasmapheresis for HTG–AP in a variety of patients, including pregnant individuals. Besides, this meta-summary will inform the future study protocols on the inclusion criteria, standardized reporting of plasmapheresis techniques, and comprehensive subgroup analyses.

Finally, we concur that cost-effectiveness and resource feasibility—especially in resource-limited settings—must be addressed before wider adoption can be recommended. However, such economic analyses were outside the scope of our current work, but remain essential to future guideline development.

In conclusion, we express our gratitude for the insightful correspondence in reference to our meta-summary, which aligns with our conclusion regarding the urgent need for well-designed, multicentric, prospective randomized studies. Addressing these methodological gaps are pivotal in establishing the optimal role, timing, and criteria for patient selection regarding plasmapheresis in HTG-AP, thereby promoting evidence-based practice. We are pleased that our article has fostered this valuable exchange and anticipate continued collaborative efforts in enhancing knowledge in this domain.

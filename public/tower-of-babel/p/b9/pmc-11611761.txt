BBOX1‐AS1 promotes gastric cardia adenocarcinoma progression via interaction with CtBP2 to facilitate the epithelial–mesenchymal transition process
Authors: Wenxu Zou, Qing Yin, Wei Guo, Zhiming Dong, Yanli Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIt is recognized that lncRNA BBOX1‐AS1 exerts a crucial oncogenic property in several cancer types. However, the functions and underlying mechanisms of BBOX1‐AS1 in the epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) process of gastric cardia adenocarcinoma (GCA) have remained unclarified. The findings of this study demonstrated that GCA tissues had elevated BBOX1‐AS1 expression levels, which was associated with a worse prognosis in GCA patients. BBOX1‐AS1 dramatically enhanced cell proliferation, invasion, and TGF‐β1‐induced the EMT process in vitro. Further mechanism analysis revealed that BBOX1‐AS1 could combine with CtBP2 and strengthen the interaction of CtBP2 and ZEB1. BBOX1‐AS1 might regulate the E‐cadherin expression through CtBP2/ZEB1 transcriptional complex‐mediated transcriptional repression, further affecting the activation of the Wnt/β‐catenin pathway and the EMT process. Overall, our findings demonstrate that BBOX1‐AS1 might act as an lncRNA associated with EMT for facilitating GCA advancement via interaction with CtBP2 to facilitate the activation of Wnt/β‐catenin pathway and the EMT process, which indicated that it might function as an exploitable treatment target for GCA patients.

INTRODUCTION

Gastric cardia adenocarcinoma (GCA), the most aggressive subtype of gastric carcinoma (GC), is categorized as a type II gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma with high metastatic potential. 1 , 2 The incidence of GCA is high in China, 2 , 3 , 4 particularly in North China's Taihang mountain, 5 which is a high‐risk area for upper gastrointestinal cancer (UGIC). Epidemiological data persistently indicate a concerning rise in the incidence of GCA, with mortality rates remaining elevated. 5 , 6 , 7 Metastasis, particularly in its early stages, is the principal factor contributing to the poor prognosis observed in patients with GCA. Nonetheless, the definitive metastatic biomarkers for GCA have yet to be identified.

Gastric cardia adenocarcinoma is notable for its distinct geographic distribution and its frequent co‐occurrence with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) in China, as shown by epidemiological studies. A similar pattern is observed in other regions with high incidences of ESCC worldwide. 8 The geographical co‐occurrence of adjacent organs suggests that similar risk factors and mechanisms might be implicated in both ESCC and GCA. 9 BBOX1‐AS1 is a novel long non‐coding RNA (lncRNA) that has been found to exhibit protumorigenic and prometastatic behaviors during tumorigenesis, 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 particularly in ESCC. 14 , 15 , 16 BBOX1‐AS1 might exacerbate the progression of ESCC by modulating the HOXB7/β‐catenin axis 15 or the miR‐506‐5p/EIF5A/PTCH1 axis. 16 Additionally, it might influence ESCC progression through the regulation of ferroptosis. 14 However, whether BBOX1‐AS1 exhibits a similar oncogenic effect in GCA has not been clarified, and the specific roles and mechanisms of BBOX1‐AS1 in GCA warrant further investigation.

Epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) has been recognized as an initiating event in early metastasis, 17 and TGF‐β as a signaling molecule might play a crucial role in initiating and driving the EMT process. 18 , 19 Numerous lncRNAs have been reported to be associated with TGF‐β signaling, functioning as downstream effectors to regulate the EMT process, 18 , 20 , 21 , 22 These lncRNAs, including lncRNA‐ATB, 20 LINC01503, 21 and LINC00239 22 , exhibit roles analogous to that of TGF‐β. Targeting the key downstream effectors of tumor‐promoting TGF‐β signaling represents a potential novel therapeutic approach. To gain a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying metastasis in patients with GCA, we established a TGF‐β‐induced EMT model based on a previous approach. 20 The significant upregulation of BBOX1‐AS1 was demonstrated following a TGF‐β‐induced EMT model, suggesting it might be an EMT‐related lncRNA and be involved in the EMT process as a TGF‐β signal's downstream effector.

In the current study, we analyzed the expression of three transcript variants of BBOX1‐AS1 in 139 GCA patients from high‐risk areas of UGIC in China and identified the most significant transcripts that affect the prognosis of patients. We also verified that BBOX1‐AS1 could combine with C‐terminal binding proteins 2 (CtBP2), a transcriptional corepressor, 23 and strengthen the interaction of CtBP2 and ZEB1. BBOX1‐AS1 might regulate the expression of E‐cadherin through CtBP2/ZEB1 transcriptional complex‐mediated transcriptional repression, further affecting Wnt pathway activation and the EMT process. It might offer new perspectives on prognostic markers and therapeutic targets for GCA.

The Fourth Hospital of Hebei Medical University's Ethics Committee granted its approval for all procedures for the collection and utilization of samples (approval number: 2020086). All patients have been fully informed, and their written informed consent was obtained. A total of 139 matched primary tumor and corresponding normal tissues of GCA patients between the years 2012 and 2016 were obtained from this biobank. Cell transfection

Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA) was used to conduct transfection according to the guidelines provided by the manufacturer. For overexpression of BBOX1‐AS1/CtBP2/ZEB1, the synthesis of the full sequence of BBOX1‐AS1/CtBP2/ZEB1 was carried out by Genscript Company and subcloned into pcDNA3.1. The small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) of BBOX1‐AS1 and CtBP2 were synthesized by GenePharma, and the siRNA‐sequences are shown in Table S1. As a negative control, the disrupted order of siRNA (siRNA‐NC) and the relevant empty vector (pcDNA3.1) were used.

RNA pull‐down assays and mass spectrometry analysis

Using the T7 RiboMAX Express RNA Production System (Promega, USA), BBOX1‐AS1 and antisense‐BBOX1‐AS1 fragments were transcribed in vitro after the plasmid DNA was linearized with restriction enzyme Bam HI. Then, the obtained RNA was biotinylated and subjected to the pull‐down analysis. The Pierce Magnetic RNA‐Protein Pull‐Down Kit (Thermo Scientific) was used for the RNA pull‐down assay as specified by the manufacturer. The protein‐eluted solution was subjected to SDS‐PAGE gel electrophoresis to separate proteins following the elution of lncRNA‐interacting proteins. Subsequently, the gels were analyzed by mass spectrometry with a Q‐Exactive mass spectrometer. The mass spectrometry analysis data are listed in Table S2.

Detailed methods for each assay, bioinformatics analysis, and statistical analysis are available in the Appendix S1.

RESULTS

Elevated expression of BBOX1‐AS1 in gastric cardia adenocarcinoma tissues and linked to poor postoperative prognosis of gastric cardia adenocarcinoma patients

The GEPIA dataset demonstrated a significant upregulated expression level of BBOX1‐AS1 in the vast majority of tumor types (Figure 1A), including stomach adenocarcinoma (STAD) (Figure 1B). BBOX1‐AS1 has three transcript variants (Figure 1C), and we first detected the significant transcript variant of BBOX1‐AS1 in 139 GCA patients. The considerably elevated expression level of transcript 1 and 2 of BBOX1‐AS1 was observed in GCA tissues (p < 0.01, Figure 1D). There is no substantial difference in the expression of BBOX1‐AS1 transcript 3 between GCA and normal tissues (p > 0.05, Figure 1D). According to the KM Plotter website analysis, poorer overall survival (OS) and relapse‐free survival (RFS) rates were also observed in the STAD patients with higher BBOX1‐AS1 expression levels (p < 0.05, Figure S1A,B). Therefore, we further evaluated the correlation between the expression levels of the three BBOX1‐AS1 transcripts and OS in 139 GCA patients. Based on the mean value of each BBOX1‐AS1 transcript expression, the participants were classified into two cohorts with relatively low and high expression levels. Kaplan–Meier analysis in the 139 GCA patients revealed that only a higher expression level of BBOX1‐AS1 transcript 1 in GCA tissues was significantly linked to the reduction in OS (log‐rank test, p < 0.05, Figure 1E). Expression of BBOX1‐AS1 transcript 2 and transcript 3 did not correlate with survival of GCA patients (log‐rank test, p > 0.05, Figure 1F,G). Thus, transcript 1 of BBOX1‐AS1 would be a significant transcript variant for further study.

In 139 paired GCA samples, 110 tumor samples showed upregulated expression of BBOX1‐AS1 transcript 1 compared to normal tissue. Of these, 72 patients (51.8%) showed a more than twofold increase, and 18 patients (12.9%) showed a more than fivefold increase (Figure 1F). Upregulated expression of BBOX1‐AS1 variant 1 in GCA tissues was also verified by FISH assay (p < 0.05; Figure 1I). Clinical explorations suggested that an elevated BBOX1‐AS1 expression level was tightly linked to higher TNM stage, lymph node metastasis, and depth of invasion in GCA patients (p < 0.05, Table 1).

BBOX1‐AS1 is upregulated by TGF‐β1 and contributes to the TGF‐β1‐stimulated EMT process

BBOX1‐AS1 was significantly associated with worse prognosis in GCA patients. The EMT process was a critical factor for metastasis and poor prognosis in patients with tumors. 18 Therefore, we further observed the role of BBOX1‐AS1 in the TGF‐β1‐induced EMT process. The cells were treated with TGF‐β1 and changed from their original cobblestone shape to a spindle‐like shape (Figure 2A). Additionally, they showed reduced expression of the epithelial marker E‐cadherin and increased expression of mesenchymal markers (VIM, N‐cadherin, and SNAI1) (Figure 2B). These changes indicated that the treated cells were undergoing an epithelial‐to‐mesenchymal transition.

The considerably improved expression level of BBOX1‐AS1 was found in HGC‐27, BGC‐803, and SGC‐7901 cells after TGF‐β1 stimulation (Figure 2C), suggesting that it might be involved in TGF‐β1‐induced EMT in these cells. To further clarify this speculation, we explored whether silencing BBOX1‐AS1 could affect the TGF‐β1's induction on EMT. Knockdown of BBOX1‐AS1 elevated epithelial marker E‐cadherin's expression levels and could simultaneously partially eliminate the suppression of E‐cadherin with TGF‐β1 stimulation (Figure 2D,E,I). Similarly, the silence of BBOX1‐AS1 resulted in reduced expression levels of VIM, N‐cadherin, and SNAI1 and could reverse the change in mesenchymal markers induced by TGF‐β1 (Figure 2D,F–I). These findings suggested that BBOX1‐AS1 might be an lncRNA associated with EMT, and it contributed to the EMT process as a TGF‐β signal downstream effector gene.

BBOX1‐AS1 facilitates tumor growth and metastasis both in vitro and vivo

The gain and loss of function was conducted in HGC‐27 and SGC‐7901 cells using siRNA‐mediated silencing and plasmid‐mediated overexpression. The qRT‐PCR findings revealed that BBOX1‐AS1 had successful overexpression or knockdown in HGC‐27 or SGC‐7901 cells (Figure 3A–C, and S2A). MTS assay revealed that ectopic overexpression of BBOX1‐AS1 significantly improved cell proliferation in HGC‐27 and SGC‐7901 cells compared to the vector control (p < 0.05, Figure 3D and S2B). Immunofluorescence for the cell proliferation marker Ki67 and the soft agar colony formation assay also verified the promotion of cell proliferative capacity by BBOX1‐AS1 (p < 0.05, Figure 3E,F and S2C). In contrast, overexpression of BBOX1‐AS1 markedly reduced the adhesion ability of cells (p < 0.05, Figure 3H). Meanwhile, BBOX1‐AS1 overexpression also led to considerably stimulated invasion ability (p < 0.05, Figure 3G and S2D). However, knockdown of BBOX1‐AS1 showed the opposite effect on growth and invasion, and further overexpression could markedly reverse the BBOX1‐AS1 siRNA‐mediated suppression (Figure 3I–K). Xenograft tumor assays also demonstrated that tumor volume and tumor weight were significantly increased in mice injected with BBOX1‐AS1‐overexpressing HGC‐27 cells compared to control mice (Figure 3L–N). The effect of BBOX1‐AS1 on tumor metastasis was evaluated by injecting BBOX1‐AS1‐overexpressing or control cells intravenously into the tail vein of mice. The number of metastatic nodules formed in the lungs was clearly increased by the overexpression of BBOX1‐AS1 (Figure 3O,P). To summarize, BBOX1‐AS1 promotes tumor growth and metastasis both in vitro and vivo.

BBOX1‐AS1 interacts with CtBP2 and strengthens the combination of CtBP2 and ZEB1

For identifying BBOX1‐AS1‐related potential molecular mechanisms, we first assessed its subcellular localization. FISH as well as qRT‐PCR analysis confirmed that BBOX1‐AS1 was localized both in the nucleus and cytoplasm, mainly in the nucleus (Figure 4A,B and S3A,B), which was consistent with the prediction based on the lncLocator online tool (Figure S3C). Given that lncRNAs can directly bind to proteins to exert regulatory functions, RNA pull‐down and mass spectrometry analyses were performed to investigate the proteins that BBOX1‐AS1 might attach with (Figure S3D,E). Eighty‐nine proteins were significantly enriched on BBOX1‐AS1 compared to antisense (listed in Table S2), among which CtBP2 attracted our attention for its effect as an extensive transcriptional co‐repressor in oncogenesis and was tightly related to the EMT process. 23 , 24 The online database RPISeq also showed that the CtBP2 protein and BBOX1‐AS1 had a strong ability to combine (Figure S3F).

Subsequently, RNA pull‐down and RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays further confirmed that BBOX1‐AS1 could specifically bind to CtBP2 (Figure 4C,D). In addition, the qRT‐PCR, western blot, and immunofluorescence demonstrated that knockdown or overexpression of BBOX1‐AS1 does not affect CtBP2 expression (Figure 4E–G). The findings illustrated that BBOX1‐AS1 could directly bind to CtBP2 protein but has no effect on CtBP2 expression. Previous reports showed that the CtBP2 protein is strongly linked to multiple tumor progression. 25 , 26 , 27 In our study, significantly upregulated mRNA and protein expression of CtBP2 was also observed in GCA tissues and GC cell lines (Figure 4G,H and S4A). The higher expression of CtBP2 was associated with lymph node metastasis and TNM stage (Figure 4I,J), as well as the poor prognosis of patients (Figure S4B,C). Meanwhile, the GEPIA dataset also showed that the CtBP2 expression level was considerably elevated in STAD tissues compared to adjacent normal tissues (Figure S4D). The results illustrated that BBOX1‐AS1 might promote GCA progression by interacting with CtBP2.

CtBP2 acts as a transcriptional co‐repressor, forming a transcriptional complex with several DNA‐binding transcription factors, such as ZEB1, and is involved in the transcriptional repression of tumor suppressor genes. 28 Subsequently, Co‐immunoprecipitation (CoIP) experiments were conducted to investigate the interaction between CtBP2 and ZEB1, as well as the impact of BBOX1‐AS1 on this interaction. The results indicated that CtBP2 proteins were co‐immunoprecipitated with endogenous ZEB1. Equally, ZEB1 proteins also could be co‐immunoprecipitated with endogenous CtBP2 (Figure 5A,B). Notably, the overexpression of BBOX1‐AS1 could significantly enhance the association between endogenous CtBP2 and ZEB1 (Figure 5C). Conversely, the knockdown of BBOX1‐AS1 attenuated the interaction between CtBP2 and ZEB1 (Figure 5D). Consequently, it is postulated that BBOX1‐AS1 might function as a scaffold, reinforcing the binding between CtBP2 and ZEB1.

BBOX1‐AS1 activates the Wnt/β‐catenin signaling pathway via interacting with CtBP2

BBOX1‐AS1 might exert an effect by enhancing the interaction between CtBP2 and ZEB1 proteins. Therefore, we investigated whether the interaction of BBOX1‐AS1 and CtBP2 had effects on the CtBP2/ZEB1 complex‐mediated transcriptional repression of target genes. First, we predicted the downstream target genes that might be modulated by CtBP2 and ZEB1 through the GTRD website. Ultimately, a set of 1622 genes was simultaneously identified in these two prediction sets (Figure 5E). KEGG analysis was conducted on these 1622 genes using the DAVID Bioinformatics Resources, and it was found that the Wnt signaling pathway was enriched (Figure 5F). Meanwhile, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) also showed that 151 differentially expressed genes caused by BBOX1‐AS1 were enriched for the Wnt signaling pathway (Figure 5G). Given its essential role in the EMT process, we were intrigued by the Wnt/β‐catenin signaling pathway. 29 Therefore, the TOP/FOP Flash luciferase reporter gene was used to investigate the influence of BBOX1‐AS1 and CtBP2 on the activation of the Wnt/β‐catenin pathway. Overexpression of BBOX1‐AS1 could considerably increase the luciferase activity of the Wnt signaling reporter TOP/FOP Flash, and further knockdown of CtBP2 could weaken this effect (Figure 5H). In contrast, overexpression of CtBP2 could result in a remarkable increase in the luciferase activity of TOP/FOP Flash, and overexpression of BBOX1‐AS1 further enhanced this effect; knockdown of BBOX1‐AS1 cut reduced effect (Figure 5I). We also observed the activation level of the Wnt pathway by detecting the expression of downstream target genes, including MMP‐7, c‐myc, cyclinD1, and CD44. The target genes’ expression levels were increased by CtBP2. Meanwhile, BBOX1‐AS1 overexpression stimulated the increase of target gene expression to a higher degree, whereas BBOX1‐AS1 knockdown attenuated target genes’ upregulation (Figure 5J). All these results demonstrated that BBOX1‐AS1 enhanced the activation of the Wnt/β‐catenin signaling pathway by interacting with CtBP2.

BBOX1‐AS1 might participate in the transcriptional repression of E‐cadherin via CtBP2 and ZEB1 complex

We next investigated how BBOX1‐AS1 promotes the Wnt/β‐catenin Signaling pathway and the EMT process. ZEB1 is a well‐known transcription factor and is involved in the transcriptional repression of E‐cadherin in several cancers. 30 , 31 However, the effect of the CtBP2/ZEB1 complex and BBOX1‐AS1 on the regulation of E‐cadherin remained unclear. In the current research, we discovered that overexpression of CtBP2, ZEB1, or BBOX1‐AS1 could attenuate E‐cadherin expression, respectively (Figure 6A). Overexpressing CtBP2 in TGFβ1‐stimulated cells or in BBOX1‐AS1‐overexpression cells could further reduce the expression level of E‐cadherin (Figure 6B), indicating that CtBP2 could enhance the impact of TGFβ1 and BBOX1‐AS1 on E‐cadherin. Meanwhile, overexpression of BBOX1‐AS1 could significantly lower the expression level of E‐cadherin, and knockdown of CtBP2 could partially attenuate this effect (Figure 6C), which indicated that BBOX1‐AS1 affected the expression of E‐cadherin in a CtBP2‐mediated manner. In contrast, the luciferase assay showed that the luciferase activity of E‐cadherin was impaired by overexpression of CtBP2, ZEB1 or BBOX1‐AS1, respectively (Figure 6D–F). Overexpression of BBOX1‐AS1 aggravation weakened the luciferase activity compared with CtBP2 transfection alone (Figure 6D). Additionally, in GCA tissues, the E‐cadherin expression level was substantially lowered in the BBOX1‐AS1 high‐expression group and was related to BBOX1‐AS1 expression (Figure 6G–I). Analysis of the GEPIA database also showed a close correlation between CtBP2 and E‐cadherin (Figure S4E). All the results clarified that BBOX1‐AS1 could regulate E‐cadherin expression through the transcriptional corepressor CtBP2.

BBOX1‐AS1 facilitates tumor proliferation and metastasis and the EMT process in a CtBP2‐mediated manner

To further verify whether BBOX1‐AS1 plays its oncogenic effect in a CtBP2‐mediated manner, rescue assays were performed. Knocking down of CtBP2 could partially eliminate the facilitation of BBOX1‐AS1‐overexpression‐mediated growth and invasion (Figure 6J,K). Meanwhile, we found that overexpression of BBOX1‐AS1 decreased the expression level of epithelial marker E‐cadherin and elevated the expression level of typical mesenchymal marker VIM (Figure 6L,M). However, further knocking down of CtBP2 could reverse the effect on EMT‐markers (Figure 6L,M). The results proved that BBOX1‐AS1 facilitated the proliferation and metastasis abilities of GC cells, as well as the EMT process, in a CtBP2‐mediated manner.

DISCUSSION

Despite recent identification of BBOX1‐AS1 as a potentially oncogenic lncRNA in various cancers, 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 32 , 33 its roles and underlying mechanisms in GCA remain unknown. Given its activation by TGF‐β, further investigation is necessary to understand the roles and mechanisms of BBOX1‐AS1 in the EMT process of GCA. In this study, we have documented that the significant transcript (transcript 1) of BBOX1‐AS1 closely correlates with aggressive clinicopathological features and serves as a robust prognostic indicator for unfavorable survival outcomes in patients with GCA. Enhanced expression of BBOX1‐AS1 substantially enhances cellular proliferation and invasion capabilities. The functional mechanism of BBOX1‐AS1 involves the regulation of the CtBP2/ZEB1 transcriptional complex‐mediated expression of E‐cadherin, subsequently affecting the activation of the Wnt/β‐catenin signaling pathway and the process of EMT. All these data demonstrated that BBOX1‐AS1 might function as an oncogenic lncRNA and be associated with EMT in GCA progression.

The intimate connection between TGF‐β and EMT is particularly important in tumor metastasis. TGF‐β is a pivotal factor in the induction of EMT. 18 In vitro studies have demonstrated that simple TGF‐β stimulation could induce EMT in several epithelial cell lines. 20 TGF‐β facilitates the EMT process via its receptor‐mediated signaling pathway and regulation of cytoskeletal alterations. 34 , 35 The present study confirmed that TGF‐β is capable of inducing the expression of BBOX1‐AS1, suggesting that BBOX1‐AS1 might be an EMT‐related lncRNA and participates in the TGF‐β‐stimulated EMT process as a downstream effector gene. The following experiments verified that BBOX1‐AS1 specifically interacts with CtBP2. Currently, there are no published studies addressing the role and relationship between BBOX1‐AS1 and CtBP2. CtBP2 is an extensive transcriptional corepressor involved in oncogenesis, capable of associating with DNA‐binding transcription factors to mediate the transcriptional repression of several EMT‐related genes. 23 , 24 , 28 , 36 In this study, our results demonstrated the interaction between CtBP2 and ZEB1 and that BBOX1‐AS1 might serve as a scaffold to strengthen the combination of CtBP2 and ZEB1. Combined with the high CtBP2 expression level in GCA tissues and the relationship with aggressive clinicopathological characteristics, as well as the poor prognosis of GCA patients, we hypothesized that CtBP2 might function as an oncogene in GCA and BBOX1‐AS1 might promote GCA progression via interacting with CtBP2.

The bioinformatics analysis indicated that the genes influenced by BBOX1‐AS1 were predominantly enriched in the Wnt/β‐catenin signaling pathway, which is known to exert diverse effects in cancer, encompassing tumor growth, metastasis, and the EMT process. 29 , 37 , 38 In this study, we verified that BBOX1‐AS1 could activate the Wnt signaling pathway mediated by CtBP2. Additionally, CtBP2 was found to independently activate the Wnt signaling pathway, while BBOX1‐AS1 significantly augmented the effect of CtBP2. These findings suggest that BBOX1‐AS1 might serve as a potential driver gene in the activation of the Wnt/β‐catenin pathway in GCA, exerting its influence through interaction with CtBP2.

ZEB1 is a vital transcription factor during cell differentiation and the EMT process. 39 It could repress transcription by recruiting the CtBPs corepressor to its CID domain. 40 , 41 ZEB1 was tightly related to the transcriptional inhibition of E‐cadherin. 36 , 39 However, the effect the of CtBP2/ZEB1 complex and BBOX1‐AS1 on the regulation of E‐cadherin expression had not been clarified. In the current research, we found that CtBP2 and ZEB1 could suppress the promoter activity and the expression of E‐cadherin, and BBOX1‐AS1 further improved this effect in a CtBP2‐mediated manner. Loss of E‐cadherin is a crucial initial step in the activation of the Wnt/β‐catenin signaling pathway and a hallmark event in the EMT process. 42 Generally, E‐cadherin could interact with β‐catenin to produce a cadherin–catenin complex at the cell surface to regulate adhesion. Attenuated E‐cadherin expression would make β‐catenin dissociate from the complex and enter the nucleus, where it combined with TCF/LEF transcription factor to activate the Wnt/β‐catenin pathway. 29 , 43 The overexpression of downstream target genes of the Wnt pathway and the impairing of the cadherin–catenin complex caused by loss of E‐cadherin led to the decline of cell adhesion and the promotion of the EMT process. Therefore, we inferred that BBOX1‐AS1 might regulate E‐cadherin expression through CtBP2 transcriptional complex‐mediated transcriptional repression to further affect the Wnt pathway and EMT process.

Taken together, BBOX1‐AS1, activated by TGF‐β, acts as a downstream effector of TGF‐β signaling to promote the malignant progression of GCA. High expression of BBOX1‐AS1 remarkably enhances cell proliferation and invasion abilities and serves as a robust predictor of poor survival in GCA patients. CtBP2, a direct target of BBOX1‐AS1, mediates the aberrant activation of the Wnt/β‐catenin pathway and the EMT process by regulating E‐cadherin expression (Figure 7). These findings significantly contribute to our understanding of the underlying mechanisms involved in GCA metastasis. Hence, BBOX1‐AS1 is expected to serve as an effective candidate for tumor metastasis and prognosis and might be an effective target for anti‐metastatic therapy.

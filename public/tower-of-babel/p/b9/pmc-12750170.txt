Emerging epigenetic modifications in renal fibrosis: From mechanisms to treatments
Authors: Xiaoguo Suo, Qinglin Ge, Lijin Peng, Qi Zhu, Mengmeng Zhang, Xinran Cheng, Fang Wang, Juan Jin, Jianan Wang, Xiaoming Meng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Chronic kidney disease (CKD) has emerged as a formidable global health challenge, with a marked increase in its incidence, prevalence, and mortality rates. Renal fibrosis is a central pathophysiological process that drives the progression of CKD to end-stage renal disease. Despite its crucial role in CKD progression, effective clinical interventions to delay or mitigate renal fibrosis remain limited. A deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying renal fibrosis, along with the identification of potential drug targets and the development of novel therapeutics, holds immense research significance and clinical value for the prevention and treatment of CKD. In recent years, epigenetic research has garnered widespread attention and plays a pivotal role in various disease processes. Against this backdrop, the mechanisms by which epigenetic modifications exert their effects on renal fibrosis are gradually being elucidated, offering novel insights into the understanding of CKD. In this review, we summarize and analyze the intricate regulatory network of epigenetic modifications in renal fibrosis. We explore the promising antifibrotic effects demonstrated by various epigenetically modified drugs in fibrotic kidney models and discuss the challenges and opportunities in current research. These findings provide crucial insights for a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying renal fibrosis and the development of novel therapeutic approaches.

Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a global public health problem that is reaching epidemic proportions due to the rising prevalence of diabetes, hypertension, and obesity, as well as an aging population. Affecting approximately 8%–15% of the global population, CKD has seen a 29.3% increase in the prevalence of chronic renal failure across all age groups since 19901, 2, 3. CKD is a syndrome that causes persistent changes in the structure, function, or both of the kidneys, and can have a very serious impact on an individual's health4. The progression of CKD is driven by multiple factors, including reduced renal parenchymal cellularity, persistent inflammatory state, tissue fibrosis, and decreased renal self-repair capacity. Current therapeutic approaches include the use of statins to reduce cardiovascular risk, the application of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs), or angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs) to manage albuminuria. The HOPE study demonstrated that ACEIs significantly reduce proteinuria and delay renal function deterioration in high-risk microalbuminuria patients5. A meta-analysis of 17 randomized controlled trials showed that ACEIs and ARBs can significantly reduce proteinuria and delay the progression of kidney disease6. ACEIs and ARBs have also been shown to delay renal function decline in patients with hypertensive nephropathy7. ACEIs and ARBs play crucial roles in preventing the progression of microalbuminuria to macroalbuminuria, and reducing glomerular pressure, decreasing proteinuria8, 9, 10. However, these treatments often fail to halt or reverse the underlying disease process11. The Ongoing Telmisartan Alone and in Combination With Ramipril Global End Point Trial showed that telmisartan/ramipril combination therapy reduced proteinuria but did not reduce the incidence of cardiovascular and renal events and instead increased the risk of adverse reactions12. The Aliskiren Trial in Type 2 Diabetes Using Cardio-Renal Endpoints trial was terminated early due to a lack of benefits and increased adverse reactions, including hyperkalemia13. There are multiple clinical trial data supporting the long-term efficacy of ACEIs and ARBs in treating proteinuria and delaying renal function decline. At the same time, patients' renal function and electrolyte levels should be closely monitored to ensure the safety and effectiveness of treatment. A meta-analysis including 119 randomized trials in patients with CKD (both with and without diabetes) demonstrated that both ACEIs and ARBs significantly reduced the risk of kidney failure and major cardiovascular events14. Thus, RAS inhibitors are now the most widely used class of antihypertensive drugs in patients with CKD with proteinuria, regardless of their clinical stages. In addition, studies have found that about 25%–40% of patients still progress to end-stage renal disease over 10 years, and may be accompanied by a series of complex complications and adverse effects. Notably, in the STOP ACEIs randomized trial, discontinuation of ACEIs did not affect the reduction in GFR or the number of renal events over the next 3 years in patients with advanced and progressive CKD compared with continued use of renin-angiotensin system inhibitors11. However, most guidelines recommend avoiding any combination of ACEIs, ARBs, and direct renin inhibitor therapy in patients with CKD with or without diabetes because their combination does not lead to long-term cardiovascular or kidney benefit, whereas it can lead to an increased risk of hyperkalemia and acute kidney injury (AKI)15. Given these limitations, they can only delay rather than block the deterioration of renal function. Consequently, there is an urgent need to develop innovative therapeutic strategies to more effectively mitigate the progression of chronic kidney failure and its profound impact on patient health1,15, 16, 17.

Renal fibrosis is the most common pathological manifestation of chronic renal failure, characterized by the excessive deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM), which can lead to tissue scarring18. Renal fibrosis is notoriously difficult to reverse, and myofibroblasts are primarily responsible for the accumulation of fibrillar collagen-rich extracellular matrix. Renal tubular epithelial to mesenchymal transition (EMT), activation of inflammatory pathways, cell cycle arrest, and defective cellular metabolism play vital roles in the onset and progression of renal fibrosis19, 20, 21. A defining feature of CKD is the pathological deposition of fibrous matrix in the interstitial space between renal tubules and their surrounding capillaries. This matrix primarily consists of fibrous collagen types I and III, but also contains components normally found in capillary basement membranes, such as collagen types IV and V, fibronectin, laminin, and heparin. The formation of interstitial fibrosis disrupts the normal secretion, transport, and nutrient absorption functions of the renal tubules, leading to the accumulation of toxins, impaired material exchange, and inadequate nutrient supply. This ultimately causes a reduction in renal volume and perfusion, severely compromising renal function and structural integrity. Collectively, these pathological changes accelerate the disease progression and pose a serious threat to the patient's health22. Fibrosis is a reactive process, primarily in response to epithelial injury, and is almost invariably accompanied by inflammation, as evidenced by increased cytokine expression and aggregation of macrophages and inflammatory cells. A deeper understanding of the mechanisms by which inflammation contributes to renal fibrosis is essential for facilitating the development of therapeutic approaches to halt the progression of CKD23. Renal fibrosis is considered to be the ultimate common pathway for almost all CKD, and there is currently no effective method for targeting renal fibrosis. Given the poor outcomes of CKD, there is an urgent need to re-evaluate the underlying mechanisms of renal fibrosis and identify new targets for therapeutic intervention24,25.

The epigenome, which includes covalent modifications of chromatin components such as DNA, RNA, and proteins, ensures that somatic cells maintain their differentiated states through epigenetic mechanisms26. Epigenetics refers to heritable changes in gene expression that are mediated by mechanisms other than changes in the DNA sequence. Epigenetic regulation involves multiple mechanisms that affect gene expression, including DNA or RNA methylation, covalent modification of histones, and interference by noncoding RNAs27, 28, 29. Epigenetics is critical for both normal development and pathological conditions. Recent studies have shown that epigenetic modifications play a pivotal role in orchestrating EMT and the subsequent development of fibrosis in renal tissue. In CKD, factors such as oxidative stress, inflammation, hyperhomocysteinemia, and uremic toxins can induce EMT, which contributes to disease progression. Furthermore, epigenetic alterations in renal tissue are closely associated with inflammation and cardiovascular complications in CKD patients. The reversibility of epigenetic modifications offers a unique opportunity to develop targeted therapeutic strategies capable of halting or even reversing the progression of CKD30. Epigenetic traits are stable and heritable phenotypes caused by changes in chromatin without altering DNA sequences. Therefore, understanding the mechanisms underlying the onset, maintenance, and inheritance of epigenetic states is a crucial aspect of contemporary biological research31,32.

Epigenetic regulation is critical for kidney development and its function. Aberrant epigenetic modifications and expression of noncoding RNAs have been linked to the pathogenesis of CKD. Specifically, epigenetic modifications regulate key processes such as transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) signaling, inflammation, pro-fibrotic gene expression, and EMT. These processes contribute to renal fibrosis and the progression of CKD. Identifying these epigenetic changes is particularly significant because they are potentially reversible and thus could serve as potential therapeutic targets for preventing subsequent renal fibrosis and CKD. Although increasing attention increasingly focuses on exploring the role, mechanisms, and potential targets of epigenetics in kidney fibrosis, research in this area still remains in the preliminary and exploratory stage, and the functions of these targets remain complex and inconclusive. Given these considerations, we have conducted a comprehensive review of the key role of epigenetics in renal fibrosis, with an emphasis on the potential and significance of epigenetic therapy. This review examines the changes in various epigenetic modifications during renal fibrosis, from DNA to RNA and then to histone modifications, and highlights the important role of these modifications in disease progression. It also explores the specific epigenetic changes at multiple gene-specific loci and their contributions to the cellular processes involved in renal fibrosis. The latest advances in the field of epigenetic regulation of renal fibrosis have been summarized, and the clinical prospects and applications of targeted epigenetic mechanisms as therapeutic strategies for renal fibrosis have been discussed.

DNA methylation and renal fibrosis

DNA methylation, as an emerging epigenetic regulator, has received increasing attention due to its critical roles in gene regulation and cellular processes. In eukaryotes, DNA methylation is catalyzed primarily by DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs), which transfer a methyl from S-adenosylmethionine to the 5-carbon position of cytosine residues, forming 5-methylcytosine. This process primarily occurs at CpG dinucleotides, which are specific sequences located in gene promoters and regulatory regions. CpG dinucleotides are sequences of cytosine nucleotides preceding guanine nucleotides in the 5′ to 3′ direction of the DNA strand, and the cytosine in this sequence can be methylated to form 5-methylcytosine27,33. The DNMT family comprises DNA-modifying enzymes. The human genome encodes five DNMT enzymes: DNMT1, DNMT2, DNMT3A, DNMT3B, and DNMT3L. Among them, DNMT1, DNMT3A, and DNMT3B are canonical DNMT enzymes that catalyze the addition of methylation marks to genomic DNA. In contrast, DNMT2 and DNMT3L are non-canonical DNMT enzymes that do not have catalytic activity but can interact with and enhance the activity of DNMT3A and DNMT3B. DNA methylation is dynamic across the genome, and the process of DNA demethylation occurs in response to growth, development, and changes in the external environment. DNA demethylation is usually caused by the ten-eleven translocation (TET) family members, which act in opposition to the DNMT family. The TET family is composed of three members (TET1, TET2, and TET3), characterized by a catalytic dioxygenase structural domain that catalyzes the conversion of 5-methylcytosine to 5-hydroxymethylcytosine to facilitate the reversal of DNA methylation. Through this oxidation process, TET enzymes counteract DNMT activity, preventing genomic hypermethylation and maintaining epigenetic equilibrium34,35.

DNA methylation plays a critical role in regulating a wide range of biological processes and physiological functions, including normal development and differentiation of cells and tissues, tumor growth36, tumor immune resistance37, neural cell stemness38, and adipocyte differentiation39. Because DNA methylation alterations are reversible, they make valuable targets for therapeutic intervention in diseases. The identification of DNA methylation-based biomarkers may provide a patient-tailored treatment strategy for diseases. Studies have revealed that DNA methylation plays a crucial role in the development and progression of renal fibrosis and can be used as a tool to study a wide range of biological processes and disease phenotypes40,41. Several studies have also demonstrated the role of DNA methylation in kidney diseases42. For instance, Zhang et al.43 have found that controlling the level of DNA methylation may provide a novel strategy for the treatment of diabetic nephropathy, particularly by targeting the Sp1/NF-κB p65–DNMT1 pathway, which could be a potential therapeutic target for alleviating podocyte damage in diabetic nephropathy. Additionally, the fact that patients with end-stage renal disease and severe inflammatory responses often exhibit DNA hypermethylation suggests that monitoring DNA methylation in renal transplant recipients could serve as a viable strategy for preventing or treating post-transplant complications. Specifically, DNA methylation profiling may help identify patients at higher risk of cardiovascular complications following transplantation. Cardiovascular complications are a leading cause of mortality among renal transplant recipients, highlighting the importance of developing predictive biomarkers and targeted interventions to improve patient outcomes41. DNA methylation also plays an important role in the process of renal fibrosis. For example, in a mouse model of renal fibrosis induced by folic acid, Notch and TGF-β signaling, as well as ECM regulatory proteins (TIMP2 and MMP-2), were activated, accompanied by marked alterations in the expression of DNA methyltransferases and MBD4. Importantly, epigenetic therapy partially restored the levels of these proteins, suggesting the potential of epigenetic therapy to modulate key molecular pathways involved in disease progression44. Other researchers analyzed human kidney samples from CKD patients and found that HOXA5 was downregulated and its expression was negatively correlated with the severity of fibrosis and DNA methylation levels. Further animal experiments showed that conditional knockout of Hoxa5 in renal tubular epithelial cells (TECs) exacerbated renal fibrosis, whereas conditional knockin of Hoxa5 attenuated renal fibrosis. Hypermethylation of gene promoters is a major mechanism of DNA methylation that represses transcription of target genes. In fibrotic kidneys of mice, HOXA5 is hypermethylated at its promoter. Jag1 is a target of HOXA5 during fibrotic conditions. Mechanistically, HOXA5 represses Jag1 transcription by binding directly to the Jag1 promoter, thereby attenuating renal fibrosis by inhibiting the Jag1–Notch signaling pathway. In conclusion, this study found that DNA methylation-mediated silencing of HOXA5 promotes renal fibrosis progression by inducing activation of Jag1 and the subsequent Notch signaling pathway. Based on these findings, a new therapeutic idea is proposed to counteract renal fibrosis by blocking DNA methylation at seven CpG sites in the HOXA5 promoter45. In addition, Klotho, a well-documented renoprotective protein, has also been linked to aberrant methylation of DNA. Studies have shown that Klotho expression is significantly reduced in fibrotic kidneys, suggesting that epigenetic modifications, particularly DNA methylation, may play a role in the downregulation of Klotho. In the unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO)-induced renal fibrosis model, elevated TGF-β caused significant inhibition of Klotho expression and hypermethylation of its promoter. Further studies found that TGF-β enhanced the expression of DNMT1 and DNMT3A in fibrotic renal tissues through inhibition of miR-152 and miR-30a. Based on the above studies, the authors concluded that blockade of either the TGF-β signaling pathway or DNMT1/3A activity significantly restored Klotho expression deficiency, attenuating the expression of pro-fibrotic proteins and renal fibrosis46. The expression of IL-6 was elevated in the TECs hypoxia-reoxygenation model and a renal ischemia-reperfusion mouse kidney injury model. IL-6 induces DNMT1 to promote methylation of the FOXO3a promoter, which inhibits FOXO3a expression and further activates the Wnt/β-Catenin pathway to exert pro-fibrotic effects. Taken together, these results suggest that targeting IL-6 contributes to preventing renal fibrosis following AKI, and may be a target for the treatment of AKI and for preventing CKD47. Meanwhile, another recent study found that PI3K/Akt pathway-related proteins were activated in UUO mice and TGF-β-stimulated TECs, while PTEN expression was downregulated compared to the control group owing to methylation. Consequently, expression levels of DNMT1, DNMT3A, and DNMT3B were significantly increased. Knockdown of DNMT3A alone significantly increased the expression level of PTEN protein, thereby inhibiting the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway and exacerbating EMT and renal fibrosis in the kidneys48. In conclusion, DNMT overexpression leads to abnormal hypermethylation of the promoter region of these antifibrotic genes, resulting in the silencing of antifibrotic genes and the development of kidney fibrosis. Previous studies have demonstrated that hypermethylation of Rasal1, which encodes an inhibitor of the Ras oncoprotein, is closely associated with the sustained activation of renal fibroblasts and the development of fibrosis.

This hypermethylation, mediated by the DNA methyltransferase DNMT1, indicates that epigenetic changes may be a molecular mechanism leading to the long-term activation of renal fibroblasts and fibrotic progression49. Additionally, another study found that in UUO and diabetic nephropathy mouse models, hypermethylation of the Rasal1 promoter was normalized by anti-fibrotic bone morphogenetic protein 7 (BMP7) treatment50. In various models of renal fibrosis, reduced expression of TET3 was consistently observed across these models. Impaired Rasal1 demethylation and hydroxymethylation processes were detected in BMP7-treated mice in the absence of TET3, suggesting that the efficacy of BMP7 in UUO and DN models is linked to restoring TET3 expression levels. The antifibrotic efficacy of BMP7 was associated with normalization of aberrant methylation of Rasal1, and this normalization process was dependent on hydroxymethylation mediated by TET3. In conclusion, renal fibrosis is characterized by hypermethylation of the Rasal1 promoter, suppression of Rasal1 expression, and reduced TET3 expression50. New evidence suggests that DNMT and TET enzymes are involved in a dynamic methylation cycle at fibrosis-related sites51. The interaction between TET and DNMT enzymes may affect renal fibrosis progression by regulating the methylation status of specific genes. For example, induction of TET3 can reverse the hypermethylation of the Rasal1 promoter mediated by DNMT enzymes, thereby restoring the expression of Rasal152. However, this balance is gradually upset in CKD, manifested by the continued predominance of DNMT activity and the depletion of TET function. In the early stages of renal fibrosis, DNMT activity predominates and epigenetic silencing initiates. Compensatory upregulation of TET can partially restore the expression of fibrostatic genes after entering the advanced phase, but it is still insufficient to reverse kidney injury. In the terminal stage of renal fibrosis, TET expression is almost completely depleted, and methylation is abnormally solidified. Therefore, future research can further explore the specific mechanisms of TET and DNMT enzymes in renal fibrosis, as well as develop therapeutic strategies targeting these enzymes to more effectively intervene in the occurrence and development of renal fibrosis.

In summary, DNA methylation has shed light on the complex molecular mechanisms underlying renal fibrosis and suggests the possibility of treating the disease by modulating these epigenetic modifications. RNA modification and renal fibrosis

In recent years, a wide variety of RNA modifications have been identified, among which N6-methyladenosine (m6A) stands out as one of the most prevalent and reversible epigenetic modifications. As the most abundant internal modification in eukaryotic mRNAs, the study of m6A has become a rapidly growing area of research. In humans, m6A is extensively involved in critical RNA processes, including precursor mRNA splicing, mRNA translation, stability, structural dynamics, export, and decay. These diverse roles highlight the functional significance of m6A in post-transcriptional gene regulation and its potential implications in various biological and pathological contexts53. M6A is predominantly found in the “RRACH” motif in the 3′-UTR, and in long introns54. M6A is mainly regulated by three classes of proteins: “writers”, “erasers”, and “readers”. It is deposited co-transcriptionally via a methyltransferase complex that includes METTL3 and other subunits (METTL14 and WTAP)55,56, and is subsequently removed by demethylating enzymes such as FTO and ALKBH5. Reader proteins, such as YTHDFs and IGF2BPs, can directly or indirectly bind to m6A-tagged transcripts, thus regulating RNA metabolism. Increasing evidence has suggested that m6A can influence the expression of target genes and thus regulate a variety of physiological processes. In terms of molecular mechanisms, m6A is involved in almost all RNA metabolic processes, including export, folding, and stability57, 58, 59.

M6A is involved in various cellular processes by regulating mRNAs, so regulators of m6A have been associated with a wide range of human diseases. A growing body of studies has provided insights into the biological role of m6A58. For example, studies have shown that m6A methylation is altered in cardiac hypertrophy. The methylation changes of m6A mRNA lead to changes in protein levels, which are not related to mRNA levels. This reveals a novel mechanism of translational regulation that is independent of mRNA levels60. Liu et al.61 observed that YTHDF1 expression was abnormally elevated in ovarian cancer. Immediately following this, translation initiation factor EIF3C was identified as a direct target of YTHDF1 in ovarian cancer cells through multi-omics analysis. YTHDF1 regulates the translation of EIF3C in an m6A-dependent fashion, affecting overall protein translation in ovarian cancer cells and thereby promoting ovarian cancer development and metastasis, thereby demonstrating the critical oncogenic role of the m6A reader YTHDF1 in cancer development. It has also been reported that m6A modification is negatively associated with autophagy during testosterone synthesis in mesenchymal Leydig cells. During the differentiation of stem cells into adult cells, there was a gradual decrease in METTL14 and a gradual increase in ALKBH5 in the cells. These events resulted in decreased levels of m6A mRNA methylation and enhanced autophagy in Leydig cells. Insights into new therapeutic strategies for treating azoospermia and oligospermia patients with reduced serum testosterone may be provided by utilizing m6A RNA methylation62.

More and more kidney diseases have been found to be related to the modification of m6A RNA, including renal clear cell carcinoma, AKI, DN, and CKD. However, the role and mechanisms of m6A in renal fibrosis remain unclear and require more intensive and extensive research efforts63. To date, accumulating evidence has shown that multiple regulatory factors mediating m6A modifications are involved in the progression of renal fibrosis, attracting the interest of researchers worldwide. One study revealed that expression of METTL3 was significantly upregulated in kidney biopsies of AKI patients and in three distinct AKI mouse models induced by cisplatin, lipopolysaccharide (LPS), and ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI). Additionally, METTL3 upregulation was observed in TECs stimulated by TNFA, cisplatin, and LPS. Mettl3 silencing attenuated renal inflammation and apoptosis, while METTL3 overexpression had the opposite effect. Subsequent mechanistic investigations identified TAB3 as a METTL3 target. It was further demonstrated that IGF2BP2 binds to the m6A-modified stop codon of TAB3, enhancing TAB3 stability and contributing to the pathology of AKI64. Meanwhile, Ni et al.65 identified EVL as another critical target of METTL3-mediated m6A modification during renal fibrosis. In this discovery, IGF2BP2 was found to play an indispensable role, and upregulated METTL3 enhanced m6A modification of EVL mRNA, thereby further highlighting the significance of m6A modifications in the regulation of fibrotic processes. In TGF-β1 and hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R)-treated TECs, silencing of METTL3 not only significantly reduced the expression of fibrosis markers but also significantly inhibited the expression of EVL. Both of these effects significantly promoted the fibrotic process in TECs. Additionally, this study demonstrated that the binding of EVL to Smad7 could attenuate Smad7's inhibitory effect on the TGF-β1/Smad3 pathway, thereby promoting renal fibrosis. A recent study also found that methylations of some genes increased after treatment with TGF-β, including the neuroepithelial transition factor 1 (NET1) gene. NET1 plays a crucial role in EMT. Specifically, when fibrosis was induced by TGF-β, both the mRNA expression and the m6A methylation levels of NET1 were elevated. This increase in m6A methylation contributed to stabilizing NET1 mRNA, thereby promoting TGF-β-mediated up-regulation of NET1 expression. Further experiments showed that knockdown of NET1 significantly decreased the expression of fibrosis markers, suggesting that m6A modification on NET1 mRNA promoted the process of renal fibrosis. In summary, this study highlights the significant role of m6A modification of NET1 mRNA in renal fibrosis66. Likewise, another study found that the level of m6A in mRNA controls the EMT process in renal tubular cells and identified the key regulator of m6A-mediated EMT, zinc finger E-box-binding homeobox 2 (ZEB2). Its key modification site is the A2137 site within the CDS region. Mechanistically, the m6A-modified ZEB2 mRNA coding region significantly increased translation efficiency and mRNA stability via interactions with the YTHDF1/eEF2 complex and IGF2BP3, respectively, thereby promoting the EMT process67. A recent study identified β-Catenin mRNA as a key target of METTL3-mediated m6A modification that is recognized by the m6A reader IGF2BP3. Wnt/β-Catenin signaling plays a key role in the pathogenesis of CKD and is associated with macrophage activation and polarization. Macrophage-derived Wnt is indispensable for promoting renal inflammation, fibroblast activation, and renal fibrosis68. METTL3 stabilizes β-Catenin mRNA, increases the levels of β-Catenin protein, and induces the expression of its downstream pro-fibrotic genes. In contrast, IGF2BP3 knockdown or inhibition of β-Catenin signaling abrogated these effects. These results suggest that targeting the METTL3/β-Catenin signaling may be a new strategy for treating renal fibrosis69.

By examining 127 human CKD samples, a study found that in addition to enhancing METTL3-mediated m6A modification, the cGAS–STING pathway was also enriched in fibrotic kidneys. This m6A hypermethylation increased the stability of cGAS and STING mRNA, and enhanced the expression of key proteins involved in the cGAS–STING pathway. Similarly, knockdown of IGF2BP family members normalized the increased stability of cGAS and STING mRNAs, thereby attenuating sterile inflammation and fibrosis70. In addition to the above findings, studies have reported on the role of m6A modifications in microRNA maturation during the development of obstructive renal fibrosis. In one such study, researchers established a mouse model of obstructive renal fibrosis and manipulated the expression levels of miR-21-5p and METTL3. The results demonstrated that ureteral obstruction induced an increase in miR-21-5p levels, which exacerbated renal parenchymal inflammation and fibrosis, and promoted ECM deposition, ultimately leading to renal fibrosis. This process was mediated by miR-21-5p targeting SPRY1 and thereby activating the NF-κB pathway. Furthermore, METTL3-mediated m6A modification was shown to facilitate the recognition and processing of pri-miR-21 transcripts, thereby promoting miR-21-5p maturation. These findings may provide novel insights into the mechanisms underlying obstructive renal fibrosis71. Finally, it has been demonstrated that METTL3-mediated m6A modification contributes to the macrophage-to-myofibroblast transition (MTT) and the progression of renal fibrosis in chronic active renal allograft rejection (CAR). In both CAR patients and simulated CAR mouse models, elevated METTL3 levels were associated with an M2 macrophage-driven MTT process and increased renal fibrosis. Conversely, knockdown of METTL3 attenuated MTT and renal fibrosis during CAR. Further investigations revealed that Smad3, a key component of the TGF-β signaling pathway, plays a crucial role in this process. Functionally, Smad3 is directly involved in METTL3-mediated MTT, and METTL3 enhances M2-driven MTT and renal fibrosis in CAR by upregulating Smad3 expression. In conclusion, this study highlights the pivotal role of METTL3 in promoting M2-driven MTT and renal fibrosis by TGF-β/Smad3 signaling, which provides a novel perspective for an in-depth understanding of the mechanisms underlying renal diseases associated with CAR and may serve as a basis for the development of future therapeutic strategies72. Taken together, these data fully demonstrate the potential of METTL3 as a therapeutic target and METTL3-mediated m6A modification for the treatment of renal fibrosis.

Some studies have found that FTO variants are associated with mortality in patients with end-stage renal disease and CKD through genome-wide association studies. However, the specific role and mechanisms of FTO in the kidney remain unclear. The investigators employed a UUO mouse model as an in vivo system and treated TECs with TGF-β as an in vitro model to study the mechanisms of renal fibrosis. The results showed that FTO expression was increased in the UUO mouse model and in TGF-β-treated TECs. In contrast, Fto gene deletion attenuated the fibrotic response induced by UUO, and Fto-deficient TECs produced less α-SMA after TGF-β stimulation73. Dysregulation of autophagy is a significant contributor to renal fibrosis, with m6A RNA modifications playing a pivotal role in regulating autophagy. A recent study demonstrated that canagliflozin alleviated fatty acid oxidation deficits and renal fibrosis in renal tubular cells through a mechanism dependent on m6A involving SQSTM1-mediated autophagy and STAT6 degradation. Specifically, the study revealed that canagliflozin primarily functions by inhibiting FTO, thereby enhancing SQSTM1 mRNA stability and promoting a rapid increase in autophagy. These processes are critical for STAT6 degradation. In summary, these findings provide valuable insights into the potential therapeutic applications of canagliflozin for treating renal fibrosis and CKD74. Another related study employed both an in vivo UUO model and an in vitro model involving TGF-β stimulation. The study observed that FTO expression was upregulated in the UUO mice model. By modulating FTO expression in vivo using Fto heterozygous mutant mice and in vitro through siRNA, the researchers demonstrated a reduction in UUO- and TGF-β-induced EMT. This was evidenced by decreased levels of fibronectin and increased levels of E-cadherin. Furthermore, FTO silencing significantly attenuated UUO- and TGF-β-induced inflammation and apoptosis. Additionally, the authors identified RUNX1 as a downstream target of FTO through transcriptomic analysis. Inhibition of FTO counteracted the elevation of RUNX1 induced by UUO and TGF-β, revealing that FTO-mediated signaling enhances the stability and promotes RUNX1 upregulation via demethylation of RUNX1 mRNA. Additionally, the study suggested that the PI3K/Akt pathway may be activated downstream of the FTO–RUNX1 axis in renal fibrosis. These findings highlight the potential of small-molecule compounds targeting the FTO–RUNX1 axis75. These findings underscore the significance of FTO in renal fibrosis, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target. Another demethylase, ALKBH5, also plays a critical role in renal injury. Compared to wild-type mice, Alkbh5 knockout mice exhibited reduced renal pathology and improved renal function following IRI, whereas Alkbh5 knockin mice displayed the opposite effects. Furthermore, conditional deletion of Alkbh5 in TECs attenuated IRI-induced AKI and fibrosis. CCL28 was identified as a target of ALKBH5, and inhibition of ALKBH5 enhanced m6A modification of Ccl28 mRNA, thereby increasing its stability. This stabilization was mediated by the m6A reader IGF2BP2. Additionally, the absence of ALKBH5 increased regulatory T cell recruitment, which suppressed inflammatory cells through enhanced CCL28 secretion. These results suggest that ALKBH5 and the T cell-inflammatory cell axis represent promising therapeutic targets for AKI76. Overall, RNA modifications, especially m6A, not only provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms of CKD and renal fibrosis but also open new avenues for developing effective therapeutic strategies. In-depth exploration of these modification processes is expected to reveal more insights into the mysteries of renal diseases and lead to more personalized therapeutic approaches for patients.

There are various transcriptional modifications of RNA. In addition to the m6A modification, many studies have also elucidated the significance of m5C and pseudouridine (Ψ) in biology and diseases and the interaction of m6A and other RNA modifications77,78. It has been demonstrated that NSUN2-mediated m5C and METTL3/METTL14-mediated m6A methylation synergistically enhance p21 expression at the translational level, leading to elevated p21 expression in oxidative stress-induced cellular senescence79. RBM15B- and IGFBP2-mediated m6A modification of GPX4 and NSUN5-mediated m5C modification promote anticancer immunity by activating cyclic cGAS–STING signaling stimulators through the maintenance of redox homeostasis in COAD80.

The interplay between m6A and other RNA modifications, such as pseudouridine (Ψ) and m5C, plays a crucial role in the molecular networks involved in renal fibrosis. Both m6A and pseudouridine modifications can affect RNA–protein interactions. For instance, they can weaken the binding of RNA-binding proteins like Pumilio 2 to their target RNA motifs, thereby influencing RNA stability and translation81. In renal fibrosis, this competition may impact the stability of mRNAs encoding key fibrosis-related proteins, such as those involved in the TGF-β signaling pathway. Pseudouridine and m6A can modulate RNA structure, which in turn affects RNA processing and function. This structural regulation is essential for the proper expression and activity of genes involved in renal fibrosis. For example, the presence of these modifications can influence the splicing of pre-mRNA, leading to the production of different protein isoforms that may have distinct roles in fibrogenesis82. M5C is another important RNA modification that can influence gene expression and RNA metabolism. While m6A primarily affects mRNA stability and translation, m5C has been shown to play a role in RNA splicing and nuclear export80. In renal fibrosis, these two modifications may work in concert to regulate the expression of fibrosis-related genes. For example, m6A may stabilize an mRNA, while m5C may ensure its proper splicing and export to the cytoplasm for translation. Both m6A and m5C can modify non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), such as long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) and microRNAs (miRNAs), which are known to be involved in renal fibrosis83. These modifications can affect the stability, maturation, and function of ncRNAs, thereby influencing their ability to regulate gene expression. For instance, m6A modification of lncRNAs can promote their stability and interaction with RNA-binding proteins, while m5C modification may affect their nuclear export and localization84. The TGF-β signaling pathway is a key driver of renal fibrosis. m6A modifications have been shown to regulate the expression and stability of TGF-β-related genes, such as TGF-β1 and its downstream targets85. Similarly, other RNA modifications like Ψ and m5C may also influence the expression and function of genes in this pathway. In addition, RNA modifications can interact with epigenetic mechanisms to regulate gene expression in renal fibrosis. For instance, m6A modification of histone-modifying enzymes or transcription factors can influence their stability and activity, leading to changes in the epigenetic landscape of fibrosis-related genes. Similarly, Ψ and m5C modifications may also affect the recruitment of epigenetic regulators to specific genomic regions, thereby modulating gene expression86. In summary, m6A modifications interact with other RNA modifications, such as Ψ and m5C, to co-regulate the molecular networks involved in renal fibrosis. These interactions can influence RNA stability, translation, splicing, and epigenetic regulation, ultimately affecting the expression and function of key fibrosis-related genes. Histone modifications and renal fibrosis

Chromatin remodeling is a critical biological process that regulates gene expression by modifying chromatin structure. Chromatin, a complex consisting of DNA tightly bound to histone proteins, exists in a highly condensed state within the nucleus. This remodeling process is essential for regulating gene accessibility and subsequent expression. Chromatin remodeling is facilitated by a diverse array of histone-modifying enzyme classes, including histone acetyltransferases (HATs), histone deacetylases (HDACs), histone methyltransferases (HMTs), and histone demethylases (HDMs)87,88.

Histone acetylation

Histone lysine acetylation is a widespread and reversible post-translational modification89. Histone lysine acetylation is regulated by HATs and HDACs. HATs catalyze the transfer of acetyl groups to lysine residues, thereby opening chromatin for gene expression. In contrast, HDACs catalyze the removal of acetyl groups, thereby repressing gene expression. HDACs are divided into two subgroups: zinc-dependent histone deacetylases and nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+)-dependent sirtuins90. The pathogenesis of renal fibrosis, a hallmark of renal aging and CKD of all etiologies, is characterized by extensive myofibroblast transdifferentiation, which is significantly affected by aberrant HDAC activities. HDACs and HATs are central epigenetic regulatory factors that act by mediating dynamic changes in histone lysine acetylation, a significant post-translational modification that regulates a variety of cellular pathways. Thus, these enzymes are classified as transcriptional co-repressors and coactivators, respectively91. However, the causal role of key HDAC isoforms and effectors in this process remains unclear.

The mammalian genome encodes 11 HDAC isoforms that comprise the HDAC superfamily. HDACs can be categorized into four major classes based on structural and functional homology: Classes I, II, III, and IV. Classes I, II, and IV HDACs are dependent on Zn2+, whereas Class III is dependent on nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide. The first HDAC identified and characterized was HDAC1. Class I HDACs are those that exhibit high sequence homology to HDAC1. Class II HDACs are further divided into Class IIa and Class IIb HDACs, which play dominant roles. Both Classes I and II HDACs share a conserved deacetylase structural domain and rely on Zn2+ for enzymatic activity. Class III HDACs, represented by the NAD+-dependent mammalian sirtuins (SIRT1–SIRT7), are evolutionarily conserved and functionally distinct from Zn2+-dependent HDACs. Their deacetylase activity is dependent on NAD+, and the seven members of the SIRT family play important roles in regulating critical cellular processes, including inflammation and apoptosis. Thus, they can be considered as promising therapeutic targets for multiple diseases. HDAC11, the sole member of Class IV, is the most recently identified HDAC isoform. In recent decades, HDACs have been recognized as central regulators of a variety of cellular activities, such as cell differentiation, proliferation, and the maintenance of stemness92, 93, 94.

Several recent reports have identified HDAC3 as a regulator of inflammation and renal fibrosis95,96. HDAC3 was significantly increased with the development of renal fibrosis in UUO and IRI models, and renal fibrosis was significantly reduced in mice with conditional deletion of Alkbh3 compared with control mice. In the TGF-β-induced cellular fibrosis model, protein expression levels of ECM and α-SMA were significantly attenuated after pharmacological inhibition or knockdown of HDAC3. Mechanistically, HDAC3 did not affect the cytoplasmic-nuclear translocation of p65, but deacetylated Lys 122 of the NF-κB p65 subunit to activate transcription, thereby promoting renal fibrosis. Therefore, targeting HDAC3 activity could be a treatment for renal fibrosis and CKD97. In a mouse model of nephrotoxic aristolochic acid (AA) and folic acid-induced renal fibrosis, inhibition of glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), a key enzyme in anti-ferroptotic processes, and the onset of ferroptosis were observed during the early stages of the AKI–CKD transition. These events coincided with aberrant upregulation of HDAC3. Notably, HDAC3 knockdown or treatment with the selective HDAC3 inhibitor RGFP966 effectively mitigated GPX4 inhibition, ferroptosis, and renal dysfunction associated with fibrosis. Subsequent investigations revealed that HDAC3 acts upstream of GPX4, repressing GPX4 transcription. Furthermore, the GPX4 promoter harbors a binding motif for Kruppel-like factor 5 (KLF5), a transcription factor critical for the regulation of renal epithelial cells in both acute and chronic kidney injury. Co-inhibition of HDAC3 and KLF5, along with the HDAC3 co-repressor NCoR, facilitated GPX4 promoter binding, resulting in histone hypoacetylation and GPX4 repression. These effects were reversed by RGFP966 and the KLF5 inhibitor ML264, respectively, indicating that KLF5 synergistically enhances HDAC3-mediated GPX4 transcriptional repression. More importantly, the anti-ferroptotic and renoprotective effects of RGFP966 were significantly attenuated in mice with renal fibrosis treated with the GPX4 inhibitor RSL3, underscoring GPX4 as a critical downstream mediator of HDAC3 dysregulation and ferroptosis in renal fibrosis98.

Klotho is an anti-fibrotic protein expressed predominantly in the kidney with nephroprotective effects, and its reduced expression is associated with the progression of renal fibrosis and CKD99. Interestingly, HDAC3 was upregulated in the kidneys of mice fed with adenine-induced CKD. Selective inhibition of HDAC3 effectively attenuated Klotho loss and renal dysfunction, whereas its protective effect largely disappeared when Klotho was knocked down, suggesting that aberrant HDAC3 activity and Klotho loss are the key factors of renal injury in CKD mice. Therefore, inhibiting HDAC3 and thereby targeting Klotho deficiency to delay the progression of renal fibrosis has a good therapeutic prospect100. Building on prior studies, researchers have demonstrated that HDAC3 expression is elevated in conjunction with Klotho suppression in fibrotic kidneys induced by UUO and aristolochic acid nephropathy (AAN). Collectively, these findings underscore HDAC3 as a pivotal pro-fibrotic factor, with its upregulation strongly correlating with Klotho inhibition and the progression of renal fibrosis. Elevation of HDAC3 can be essentially blocked by inhibitors targeting the TGF-β receptor and Smad3 phosphorylation, indicating that TGF-β/Smad signaling drives HDAC3 transcriptional activation. Treatment with the HDAC3-selective inhibitor RGFP966 alleviated Klotho suppression and significantly attenuated renal fibrotic injury in UUO and AAN mouse models. Given its mechanism of action through histone acetylation and regulation of profibrotic pathways, RGFP966 could potentially be effective in other CKD models where fibrosis is a common pathological feature101. Currently, there are no specific clinical trials reported for RGFP966 in kidney diseases. However, its use in other disease models provides insights into its potential therapeutic profile. RGFP966 has shown strong potential against HCC cells by decreasing proliferation and migration through inhibition of HDAC3102. This suggests that its mechanism of modulating gene expression and signaling pathways via HDAC3 inhibition could be applicable to other diseases where similar pathways are involved. RGFP966 has been shown to modulate macrophage inflammatory responses and directly restrict Mycobacterial growth103. This indicates that its effects on immune regulation and inflammation could be relevant in other inflammatory or infectious diseases, including those involving the kidneys. The efficacy of RGFP966 in renal fibrosis is primarily attributed to its ability to inhibit HDAC3, which regulates profibrotic gene expression and signaling pathways. Given that similar pathways are often involved in other types of renal injury (e.g., diabetic nephropathy, ischemia-reperfusion injury), it is plausible that RGFP966 could be similarly effective in these conditions. While renal fibrosis is a common endpoint in many kidney diseases, the specific mechanisms leading to fibrosis can vary. Further studies are needed to determine if RGFP966's effects are consistent across different renal injury types, as some diseases may involve unique pathways that are not directly targeted by HDAC3 inhibition. HDAC3 functions upstream of Klotho, exerting its pro-fibrotic effects primarily through the transcriptional repression of Klotho. Furthermore, HDAC3 is associated with the induction of the transcriptional regulators NCoR and NF-κB. In fibrotic kidneys, upregulated HDAC3 recruits NF-κB to the Klotho promoter, forming a transcriptional repressor complex that reduces Klotho promoter acetylation and suppresses its transcription. Thus, TGF-β-induced HDAC3 upregulation and subsequent Klotho transcriptional repression establish an epigenetic regulatory loop that significantly drives renal fibrosis. Together, an in-depth exploration of the roles and mechanisms of the regulatory targets that mediate this process would be valuable for the treatment of renal fibrosis104. Many previous studies have demonstrated that KLFs are involved in renal fibrosis79. For example, KLF4 can play an anti-fibrotic role in the kidney by suppressing the activation of myofibroblasts105. Additionally, KLF5 is expressed in epithelial cells and is also involved in UUO-induced inflammatory and fibrotic responses106. A recent study investigated the role of KLF13 in CKD. Researchers observed significantly reduced levels of KLF13 in both human CKD patients and mouse models subjected to UUO and IRI surgery, with pronounced reductions in renal tubules. Further studies have shown that a lack of KLF13 exacerbates renal tubular interstitial fibrosis, while KLF13 overexpression attenuates fibrosis progression. SIN3A has been implicated in the regulation of TGF-β and Notch signaling within the kidney107. Mechanistically, KLF13 recruits the SIN3A–HDAC1 complex to repress transcription of TGF-β target genes, thereby mitigating the pro-fibrotic effects of TGF-β. Specifically, when TGF-β is transiently elevated, it prompts the Smad3–Smad4–SP1 complex to bind to the KLF13 promoter, which enhances KLF13 transcription and triggers its anti-fibrotic function. In summary, this study highlights the pivotal role of KLF13 in counteracting renal fibrosis and elucidates the mechanisms through which it modulates TGF-β signaling via regulation of the SIN3A–HDAC1 complex. Understanding these mechanisms not only deepens our understanding of renal fibrosis but also provides possible directions for developing new therapeutic approaches108.

In addition to class I HDACs, class II and class III HDACs also play an integral role in renal fibrosis. Among the seven subunits of sirtuins, Sirt6 functions as a histone deacetylase targeting H3K9 and H3K56. It regulates genome stability and lipid metabolism, and the transcriptional activity of stress response- and cellular senescence-associated transcription factors109. Some studies have demonstrated that Sirt6 overexpression attenuates cisplatin-induced renal injury in terms of renal dysfunction, inflammation, and apoptosis. In contrast, Sirt6 knockdown exacerbates cisplatin-induced renal injury. Mechanistically, it was found that Sirt6 binds to the promoters of ERK1/2 and deacetylates histone 3 at Lys9, thereby inhibiting the transcription of ERK1 and ERK2. This mechanism attenuates renal injury, revealing the protective effect of Sirt6 on the kidneys110. In addition to serving as a therapeutic target in AKI, Sirt6 is equally useful in AKI to CKD. Sirt6 expression was found to be upregulated in UUO and IRI mice, as well as in TGF-β-induced TECs. Inhibition of Sirt6 markedly exacerbated TGF-β-induced fibrosis in TECs. During TGF-β stimulation, Sirt6 inhibited β-Catenin-mediated fibrosis in TECs, and the inhibition was dependent on the deacetylase activity of Sirt6. Furthermore, increased recruitment of Sirt6 to the promoter regions of β-Catenin target genes in TGF-β-stimulated TECs led to the deacetylation of H3K56Ac, thereby suppressing the expression of fibrosis-related genes. Mechanistically, Sirt6 was shown to interact with β-Catenin and bind to the promoters of β-Catenin target genes, leading to the deacetylation of histone H3K56 and the subsequent inhibition of fibrosis-related gene transcription. In contrast, Sirt6 knockdown resulted in increased acetylation of histone H3K56 and enhanced transcription of fibrosis-related genes. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that Sirt6 exerts anti-fibrotic effects in the kidney by epigenetically suppressing β-Catenin signaling111. It has been reported that HDAC5, which belongs to the class II HDAC subfamily, is expressed at elevated levels in renal tubular cells from diabetic mice, UUO mice, and TECs cultured in vitro under high-glucose conditions. Inhibition of HDAC5 has been shown to suppress high-glucose-induced EMT in TECs through downregulation of TGF-β. In addition, HDAC5 expression is regulated by the PI3K/Akt pathway. Inhibition of this pathway via treatment with LY294002 or via Akt phosphorylation mutation reduces HDAC5 and TGF-β1 expression in high-glucose-cultured TECs. Additionally, the use of the m6A demethylase inhibitor MA2 not only inhibits Akt phosphorylation but also decreases HDAC5 expression. Interestingly, overexpression of METTL14, but not METTL3, reversed the activation of the Akt pathway in a high-glucose environment and reduced HDAC5 levels by upregulating the expression of PTEN, a tumor suppressor protein. These findings suggest that METTL14 may regulate HDAC5-mediated EMT in TECs during DN by modulating PTEN and subsequently influencing the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway112. These correlation studies highlight the complex role of histone acetylation in renal fibrosis and re-emphasize the significance of epigenetic modifications as a key factor.

Other histone modifications

Covalent modifications of histone N-terminal tails are extensively involved in the regulation of transcription, chromosome condensation, and heterochromatin formation. In addition to histone acetylation, which is involved in the regulation of transcription, histone methylation is also required for the regulation of gene expression. Perturbation of histone methylation often leads to developmental abnormalities and various diseases113. Histone methylation typically occurs at specific lysine and arginine residues located at the amino-terminal end of the histone core. Lysine residues generally undergo mono-, di-, or trimethylation. Methylation of H3K4, H3K36, and H3K79 is associated with transcriptional activation, whereas methylation of H3K9, H3K27, and H4K20 is linked to transcriptional repression. In contrast, arginine methylation occurs in the form of monomethylation, asymmetric dimethylation, or symmetric dimethylation and is generally associated with gene activation114. H3K9 methylation, catalyzed by Suv39h family proteins, is required for the establishment of pericentric heterochromatin structures. A previous study identified a mammalian histone methyltransferase, G9a, which exhibits strong methyltransferase activity for H3K9 in vitro and is a key enzyme for H3K9 mono- and dimethylation (me1 and me2)115. In a mouse model of renal fibrosis, TGF-β was found to induce H3K9 methylation through the upregulation of G9a. Treatment with BIX01294, a specific inhibitor of G9a, not only suppressed the expression of fibrotic markers but also enhanced the expression of Klotho in UUO mice. In vitro experiments demonstrated that TGF-β1 upregulated G9a expression via Smad3. Inhibition of G9a via BIX01294 restored TGF-β1-mediated upregulation of fibrotic markers and downregulation of Klotho, while also inhibiting H3K9me1 but not H3K9me2. Furthermore, in human renal biopsy specimens, G9a expression was associated with elevated H3K9me1 levels, expansion of the interstitial fibrosis area, and a decrease in Klotho expression. These findings suggest that G9a-induced H3K9me1 plays a critical role in both the development of renal fibrosis and the suppression of Klotho expression116. Additionally, NSD2 is a histone methyltransferase that has been shown to primarily catalyze the dimethylation of H3K36me2. The catalytic effect of NSD2 on H3K36me2 is sufficient to activate gene expression117. Renal fibrosis is one of the key pathological features of chronic allograft dysfunction (CAD). Elevated levels of NSD2 and H3K36me3 have been observed in transplanted kidneys of CAD patients. In a mouse model of chronic renal transplantation, increased expression of NSD2 accompanies mitochondrial fission in TECs in vivo. Knockdown of NSD2 was shown to inhibit EMT and mitochondrial division. Conversely, NSD2 overexpression exacerbated fibrosis and mitochondrial fission. In a TNFA-induced cell model, tubular NSD2 exacerbated Dynamin-related protein 1-mediated mitochondrial fission through the STAT1/ERK/PI3K/Akt signaling pathway. In TECs, TNFA-induced loss of STAT1 monomethylation was observed after NSD2 silencing, whereas NSD2 overexpression resulted in increased levels of STAT1 monomethylation and phosphorylation. Furthermore, NSD2 was directly involved in the methylation of lysine 173 in STAT1, leading to STAT1 activation. Overall, these findings suggest that targeting NSD2 may represent a promising therapeutic approach to enhance renal transplant tubular cell survival and attenuate interstitial fibrosis during CAD118. Lysine-specific histone demethylase 1 (LSD1), the first identified histone lysine demethylase, plays a critical role in regulating gene expression and protein function in a variety of diseases. LSD1 expression was significantly upregulated in fibrotic kidneys of UUO mice and in TGF-β1-induced NRK-52E cells. More importantly, inhibition of LSD1 attenuated renal fibrosis, EMT, and fibroblast activation. Further investigations revealed that LSD1 influences the process of fibrosis by regulating the expression of Snail and interacting with Snail, thereby influencing the process of fibrosis. In addition, LSD1 was found to regulate multiple pro-fibrotic signaling pathways and activate renal fibroblasts through the 14-3-3ζ-PKCA signaling pathway. These findings imply that LSD1 plays a pivotal role in chronic kidney injury, which not only directly affects gene expression but also indirectly promotes fibrosis through a complex signaling network119. Histone H3K79 methyltransferase Dot1l exhibits anti-fibrotic properties, and Dot1l deficiency exacerbates streptozotocin- and UUO-induced renal fibrosis and renal dysfunction through upregulation of endothelin-1. Dot1l deficiency abolishes H3K79me2, leading to enhanced recruitment of H3 acetylation and HDAC2 to the Edn1 promoter. The mutual antagonism between Dot1a and HDAC2 in Edn1 regulation prevents the binding of the Dot1a–HDAC2 complex to DNA. This study confirms that Dot1l is a regulator of epigenetic modifications in renal fibrosis and provides a balanced mechanism for controlling Edn1 transcription through the regulation of H3K79 dimethylation and H3 acetylation at the Edn1 promoter. These findings reveal a novel mechanism by which Dot1a and HDAC2 regulate Edn1 transcription and provide a new idea for targeting endothelin-1 as a therapeutic strategy for renal fibrosis120. Histone H3 undergoes trimethylation at the lysine 27 position (H3K27me3), a modification typically associated with gene repression. This process is catalyzed by the methyltransferase enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2) and antagonized by the demethylase Jumonji domain-containing protein D3 (JMJD3)121,122. Yu et al.123 demonstrated that in the kidneys of 5/6 nephrectomized mice and mice with UUO, silencing of the JMJD3 inhibitor GSK-J4 or JMJD3 itself resulted in an increase in extracellular matrix deposition and the activation of renal mesangial fibroblasts. These changes were accompanied by a decrease in Smad7 expression and elevated levels of TGF-β1, Smad3, Notch1, Notch3, and Jagged1. Meanwhile, GSK-J4 treatment significantly reduced JMJD3 in fibrotic kidneys, leading to upregulation of H3K27me3. During kidney injury, the pro-fibrotic effect of EZH2 could be counteracted by JMJD3 and UTX, with JMJD3 playing the predominant role. Taken together, these findings indicate that JMJD3 exerts anti-fibrotic effects by limiting the activation of pro-fibrotic signaling pathways. This suggests that targeting JMJD3 may represent a novel therapeutic strategy for treating renal fibrosis.

Lactate, a metabolic by-product converted from pyruvate in tumor cells, is widely recognized as both an energy source and a substrate for histone lactylation. Lactate-derived lactylation of histone lysine residues can serve as an epigenetic modification that directly stimulates gene transcription in chromatin124,125. The expression of the glycolytic enzyme 6-phosphofructo-2-kinase/fructose-2,6-bisphosphatase (PFKFB3) was significantly elevated in the fibrotic kidneys of CKD patients and IRI model mice. Concurrently, CKD patients and IRI mice exhibited elevated urinary lactate/creatinine levels and renal lactate, alongside interstitial fibrosis and renal dysfunction. Specific deletion or inhibition of PFKFB3 significantly reduced renal lactate levels and attenuated inflammation and fibrosis. Lactate can act as a precursor for histone lactylation, regulating epigenetic modifications and gene transcription. Renal tubular PFKFB3-mediated lactate accumulation may activate the NF-κB pathway by promoting H4K12 lactylation (H4K12la), which is enriched at the promoters of NF-κB signaling genes, such as Ikbkb, Rela, and Relb. This, in turn, activates their transcription and contributes to the inflammatory response and fibrosis. These effects were significantly inhibited in Pfkfb3 knockout mice. This study highlights the metabolic and epigenetic regulation of kidney injury, providing new insights into potential therapeutic approaches for inflammation-related disorders126. Additionally, other studies have found that lactate promotes the development of EMT in diabetic kidney disease (DKD), contributing to the progression of renal fibrosis. In DKD mouse models and advanced glycation end products-induced TECs, renal lactate levels were significantly elevated, which was accompanied by increased renal fibrosis. Conversely, inhibiting lactate levels effectively suppressed EMT in mice, thereby attenuating DKD fibrosis. The researchers also found that the expression of H3K14la was significantly higher than that of other lactate modifications at the same residue. Advanced glycation end products stimulation increased the recruitment of H3K14la to the KLF5 promoter, enhancing the transcription of KLF5. KLF5 directly binds to the promoter region of CDH1 to inhibit its transcription. Kidney-specific knockdown of Klf5 inhibited the development of EMT and ameliorated renal fibrosis. Collectively, these findings suggest that lactate promotes EMT in renal fibrosis and may serve as a potential target for innovative therapies for DKD127. Consistent with previous findings, another study found that H3K18lac and global pan-histone lactylation levels were significantly elevated in DKD patients and streptozotocin-induced mouse models of DKD. This elevation was accompanied by a significant decrease in Glis1 expression. It was shown that overexpression of Glis1 reduced the expression of fibrosis markers, such as α-SMA and Col1a1, suggesting that Glis1 plays a protective role in mitigating renal fibrosis in DKD. In addition, Glis1 was found to prevent renal tubular injury by inhibiting cellular senescence. Additional studies using lactate enhancers (rotenone and sodium lactate) and lactate inhibitors (sodium dichloroacetate) revealed that lactate enhancers attenuated the protective effect of Glis1 against cellular senescence, whereas lactate inhibitors enhanced this protective ability. Further investigations identified lysine acetyltransferase 5 (KAT5) as a key player in DKD, serving as the primary lactyltransferase responsible for histone lactylation. It was also discovered that Glis1 inhibits histone lactylation, reduces cellular senescence, and attenuates renal fibrosis by directly binding to KAT5128,129. These findings not only deepen our understanding of the complexity of the molecular mechanisms of DKD but also open up the possibility of developing new therapeutic strategies.

Both lactylation and acetylation target lysine residues on histones, leading to competition. For example, in the process of liver fibrosis, hexokinase-2 promotes lactylation at histone H3K18 sites rather than acetylation, thereby inhibiting the transcriptional activation of fibrosis-suppressing genes130. This competitive relationship is likely due to substrate bias shifts induced by changes in the metabolic microenvironment. When intracellular lactate levels rise, lactoyl-CoA production increases, prompting the modifying enzyme to catalyze lactylation over acetylation131. In hypoxic HeLa cells, lactylation levels increase while acetylation levels decrease, indicating a competitive relationship. However, in LPS-activated macrophages, lactylation and acetylation can synergize to promote HMGB1 modification and exosome release. Lactylation and acetylation share some modification enzymes. For instance, p300 has been identified as a histone lysine lactyltransferase132. Additionally, some HDACs have been shown to possess delactylating activity. This shared enzymatic machinery suggests that lactylation and acetylation can influence each other through competition for the same enzymes133. In various studies, lactylation and acetylation have been shown to differentially regulate gene expression. For example, in a study on somatic cell reprogramming, histone lactylation (H3K18la) and acetylation (H3K27ac) were found to cooperatively modulate the expression of pluripotency genes such as Oct4, Sall4, and Mycn. This indicates that lactylation and acetylation can work together to fine-tune gene expression programs134. Although the specific mechanisms are still under investigation, there is evidence suggesting that lactylation can influence methylation levels. For example, in the process of DNA damage repair, histone modifications such as H2BK123ub (ubiquitination of histone H2B) promote methylation at H3K4, H3K36, and H3K79, which are essential for DNA repair. In some cases, lactylation may affect the levels of these methylation modifications, indicating a potential synergistic or regulatory relationship135. In ocular melanoma, histone acetylation was found to enhance the expression of ALKBH3 while weakening the existence of tumor suppressor PML aggregates, and promoting m1A methylation of SP100A, which contributes to the malignant transformation of cancer cells. This suggests that lactylation can influence methylation-related pathways to regulate gene expression and cellular phenotypes136. Lactylation interacts with histone acetylation and methylation through competition for lysine residues, shared enzyme systems, and co-regulation of gene expression. These interactions highlight the dynamic and interconnected nature of histone modifications in regulating cellular processes. Further research is needed to fully elucidate the mechanisms underlying these interactions and their roles in various biological and pathological contexts.

Histone lysine crotonylation (Kcr) is one of the earliest discovered non-acetyl histone lysine acylations, which is closely related to acetylation in chemical structure and occurs at major acetylation sites of histones. Crotonyl-CoA, an intermediate metabolite in fatty acid oxidation and the metabolism of lysine and tryptophan in mitochondria or peroxisomes, serves as the precursor for Kcr. However, the mechanisms underlying Kcr's role in various diseases remain largely unknown, necessitating further in-depth and extensive exploration137,138,139. A recent study revealed that histone Kcr levels are abnormally elevated in TECs within fibrotic kidneys. By screening a variety of enzymes and metabolic regulators associated with crotonylation and acetylation, researchers identified that ACSS2, a crotonyl coenzyme A-producing enzyme, significantly increased H3K9cr levels without affecting H3K9ac in kidney and TECs. Specific deletion of the ACSS2 gene reduces H3K9cr levels, thereby delaying the progression of renal fibrosis. Renal tubular cell-derived IL1B stimulates M1 macrophage polarization, establishing a microinflammatory environment that promotes sustained inflammation and damages tubular cells. H3K9cr levels were significantly correlated with IL1B production, suggesting that ACSS2 may be a key regulator of H3K9cr-mediated IL1B production. Furthermore, ACSS2 may regulate IL1B-mediated macrophage activation and renal tubular cell senescence by modulating H3K9cr levels. The extent of renal cell histone Kcr can be therapeutically manipulated by ACSS2 inhibitors140.

In summary, histone modification plays an extremely important role in CKD and its associated renal fibrosis by altering gene expression patterns and influencing cell function and fate. Histone modifications not only enhance our understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying renal fibrosis and CKD, but also provide valuable directions for finding new therapeutic strategies. Non-coding RNAs and renal fibrosis

Non-coding RNAs are a class of functional RNA molecules that do not encode proteins but play critical roles in cellular processes. ncRNAs are pivotal regulators of gene expression, influencing processes ranging from DNA transcription to protein synthesis. Based on their structure, mode of action, and sequence length, ncRNAs are broadly categorized into two groups: small ncRNAs, which are shorter than 200 nucleotides, and long ncRNAs, which are longer than 200 nucleotides. Small ncRNAs encompass diverse types, including the well-known microRNAs (miRNAs), as well as other members such as small nuclear RNAs (snRNAs) involved in gene splicing, small nucleolar RNAs (snoRNAs) responsible for ribosomal RNA modifications, tRNA-derived small RNAs (tsRNAs), and piwi-interacting RNAs (piRNAs). Each of these small ncRNAs has a unique function, such as regulating gene expression or maintaining the proper functioning of certain key processes within cells. Long ncRNAs include linear long ncRNAs (lncRNAs) and circular RNAs (circRNAs). Despite their structural differences, both lncRNAs and circRNAs play critical roles in gene expression regulation. For instance, lncRNAs modulate gene activity through diverse mechanisms, while circRNAs exert specific regulatory functions due to their stable circular structure141,142.

Despite its significance, the epigenetic regulation of non-coding RNAs in renal fibrosis remains underexplored in recent research. A recent relevant study highlighted the regulation of miR-219a-5p through DNA methylation in fibrotic kidneys. In UUO and IRI mouse models of renal fibrosis, miR-219a-5p exhibited hypermethylation and a significant reduction in expression. Inhibition of miR-219a-5p reduced fibronectin accumulation, suggesting its pro-fibrotic role in renal fibrosis. Further experiments identified ALDH1L2 as a direct target of miR-219a-5p in renal fibrosis. ALDH1L2, an enzyme involved in maintaining intracellular levels of the antioxidant glutathione, is upregulated by inhibition of miR-219a-5p. This upregulation increases glutathione production and inhibits the expression of plasminogen activator inhibitor-1, a protein whose high levels are associated with the accumulation of fibronectin. Thus, inhibition of miR-219a-5p promotes fibronectin degradation and attenuates renal fibrosis143. In autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease, some investigators have found that the expression of miR-192 and miR-194 is downregulated through hypermethylation of their loci in the late stages. Cyst expansion is closely related to EMT, and the progressive development of bilateral renal cysts frequently coincides with renal interstitial fibrosis. ZEB2 and cadherin 2, which are involved in EMT, are directly regulated by miR-192 and miR-194. However, the precise regulatory mechanisms have not been explored in detail. Therefore, by adjusting the expression of miR-192 and miR-194, as well as finding the corresponding targets to correct the aberrant DNA methylation pattern, we may have a chance to find an effective way to combat the development of autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease and its resulting fibrosis144. Lu et al.145 found that LPS induced inflammation by inhibiting the expression of the lncRNA CCAT1. They further showed that CCAT1 protects renal cells from LPS-induced damage by binding to and inhibiting miR-155 expression. Finally, the authors concluded that SIRT1 acts as a downstream effector of the CCAT1/miR-155 axis and that this pathway mediates LPS-induced nephrocyte cell death and inflammation. However, the specific mechanisms underlying SIRT1's deacetylation effects remain unexplored. In a cellular model of TGF-β1-induced renal fibrosis, investigators found that the expression of lncRNA MEG3 was downregulated, whereas overexpression of MEG3 attenuated fibrosis. Given that MEG3 is regulated by DNA methylation, DNMT1 was shown to modulate MEG3 expression by altering CpG methylation in its promoter, while miR-185 regulates DNMT1 expression. Ultimately, the authors concluded that regulation of the miR-185/DNMT1/MEG3 pathway plays a critical role in renal fibrosis, uncovering a novel regulatory mechanism146.

Numerous studies have demonstrated that ncRNAs are intricately linked to fibrotic processes in kidney disease147. For instance, lnc-TSI can mitigate renal fibrosis by negatively modulating the TGF-β/Smad pathway148. Similarly, miR-20a-5p is significantly upregulated in the kidneys of mice with AKI and in patients with delayed graft function. miR-20a-5p attenuates renal dysfunction and fibrosis by acting as an endogenous ACSL4 inhibitor and modulating ferroptosis149. In IRI mice, treatment with miR-150 exosomes resulted in elevated renal fibrosis compared to controls150. Expression of miR-34a was increased in TECs from patients with CKD and UUO mice. miR-34a deficiency attenuated the renal fibrosis, whereas miR-34a overexpression promoted EMT in TECs, accompanied by a dramatic downregulation of Klotho, which has an anti-fibrotic effect151. Intramuscular injection of miR-29 exosomes reduces α-SMA and Col1a1 levels in the kidneys of UUO mice by downregulating YY1 and TGF-β pathway proteins, thereby attenuating renal fibrosis152. TLR7 inhibitors significantly reduce renal fibrosis in animal models153. miR-21 acts as an endogenous TLR7 activator, promoting renal inflammation and fibrosis. These studies on non-coding RNAs have deepened our understanding of renal fibrosis mechanisms and provide potential targets for the development of new therapeutic strategies.

In conclusion, broadening our understanding of the epigenetic regulation of non-coding RNAs in renal fibrosis will help us to further grasp the pathology of CKD, and researchers should make every effort to conduct more in-depth studies in this field to provide more effective therapeutic modalities for the treatment of renal fibrosis and CKD.

Potential drug development based on the modulation of epigenetic modifications

Renal fibrosis is a multifaceted process involving aberrant regulation of multiple cells and molecules. Epigenetic mechanisms play a pivotal role in regulating gene expression. Epigenetic drugs, an emerging therapeutic strategy with potentially reversible advantages, can intervene in renal fibrosis by modulating gene expression through alterations in the epigenetic modification status of genes32,154. Therefore, by studying epigenetic drugs for renal fibrosis, we can gain insights into the specific regulatory mechanisms at the epigenetic level. This approach not only provides novel perspectives but also helps elucidate the molecular pathogenesis of renal fibrosis.

Renal fibrosis is closely linked to DNA methylation, a chemical modification of DNA. Specific drugs can inhibit this process. For example, 5-Aza, a DNA methyltransferase inhibitor, can prevent the hypermethylation of HOX5, thereby halting renal fibrosis progression45. To further investigate this, researchers used a mouse model of folate-induced AKI and fibrosis, treating it with 5-Aza-2-dC (a derivative of 5-Aza) and trichostatin A, a histone deacetylase inhibitor. The study demonstrated that the combination of 5-Aza-2-dC and TSA significantly protected against folate-induced renal injury and fibrosis. Analysis of renal histopathology immunofluorescence staining, serum albumin-to-creatinine ratios, body weight, and gene expression changes revealed that both drugs effectively reduced renal injury and fibrosis. Additionally, these drugs can help restore abnormalities in the EMT process and modulate fibrosis-associated signaling pathways, including Notch, TGF-β, and Wnt, by normalizing their expression. In other words, by inhibiting certain biochemical pathways that promote fibrosis, 5-Aza-2-dC and trichostatin A demonstrate potential for treating renal fibrosis44. This opens up the possibility of developing new therapeutic approaches to improve the health of patients with such diseases. Similarly, IL-6 activates the Wnt/β-Catenin pathway, promoting renal fibrosis by inhibiting FOXO3a expression through DNMT1. 5-Aza-dC reduces FOXO3a methylation levels, thereby enhancing its expression and exerting antifibrotic effects38. Furthermore, 5-aza-dC partially restored the TGF-β-induced decrease in PTEN levels and reversed the TGF-β-induced activation of the PI3K/Akt pathway, thereby augmenting its antifibrotic effects48. Rhein, a plant-derived anthraquinone with antifibrotic properties, reversed the downregulation of Klotho in UUO-induced fibrotic kidneys. Mechanistically, Rhein significantly corrected Klotho promoter hypermethylation caused by aberrant expression of DNMT1 and DNMT3a, further attenuating the expression of pro-fibrotic proteins and the progression of renal fibrosis. Knockdown of Klotho largely antagonized the antifibrotic effect of Rhein, suggesting that Rhein exerts its antifibrotic function by reversing Klotho's role in renal fibrosis. These findings highlight the potential of targeting Klotho as a novel therapeutic strategy for renal fibrosis155.

Due to the significant elevation of m6A modification levels and METTL3 expression observed in patient kidneys and various animal models of kidney injury, as well as the protective effect of METTL3 knockdown against fibrosis in renal tubular cells, we propose that targeting the METTL3-mediated RNA methylation pathway may serve as a potential therapeutic target for the onset and progression of renal fibrosis. Numerous studies have explored the potential of pharmacological interventions targeting m6A for renoprotection. For instance, Cpd-564, a small-molecule METTL3 inhibitor identified through high-throughput virtual screening and experimental validation, has been shown to have potent nephroprotective effects in both in vitro and in vivo studies64. Additionally, researchers have identified isoforsythiaside, a novel METTL3 inhibitor derived from traditional Chinese medicine monomers, that significantly inhibits the METTL3/EVL m6A axis. This inhibition results in a dose-dependent reduction in the protein levels of EVL and fibrosis markers, thereby preventing renal fibrosis65. STM2457, another inhibitor specifically targeting METTL3, has been shown to inhibit the expression of fibrosis markers such as vimentin and fibronectin in the presence of TGF-β at all tested concentrations. Further studies indicate that m6A plays an important role in the EMT. Specifically, NET1 and ZEB2, which are involved in this process, are regulated by m6A and affect EMT in renal tubular cells. STM2457 inhibits TGF-β-induced EMT in TECs, thereby exerting an antifibrotic effect66,67. Moreover, STM2457 can inhibit the TGF-β/Smad3 axis by targeting METTL3, effectively alleviating M2-driven macrophage-to-myofibroblast transition and reducing fibrotic tissue damage in CAR. These findings underscore the critical role of METTL3 in regulating MMT and highlight its potential as a therapeutic target for addressing renal fibrosis in CAR72. Another therapeutic approach involves a specific antisense oligonucleotide designed to target METTL3. This approach, which is consistent with the effects observed in Mettl3 conditional knockout mice, significantly reduces intrarenal m6A levels and fibrosis in renal fibrosis models70. This reduction is accompanied by decreased inflammatory responses, cytokine expression, and immune cell recruitment, thereby attenuating renal fibrosis. IOX1 has previously been identified as an inhibitor of ALKBH5 and has been shown to play a role in acute myocardial infarction156. Additionally, several studies have demonstrated that IOX1 exerts a protective effect in renal fibrosis models of IRI by reducing renal function indicators such as serum creatinine and urea nitrogen76. These findings further support its potential as a therapeutic agent for renal fibrosis.

Targeting HDAC3 is a novel strategy for the treatment of CKD. RGFP966, a selective HDAC3 inhibitor, has demonstrated significant therapeutic potential in combating renal fibrosis. In multiple kidney injury models, including UUO, IRI, and AAN, RGFP966 not only attenuated fibrosis but also improved renal function by restoring the expression of suppressed anti-fibrotic genes such as Klotho and regulating key signaling pathways such as TGF-β97,98,104. It is also effective in many other kidney disease models, such as podocyte injury157, renal clear cell carcinoma158, and hypokalaemia-induced polyuria159. KLF13 was previously reported to recruit an inhibitory complex containing SIN3A and HDAC1 to TGF-β target genes, thereby inhibiting renal fibrosis. The antibiotic clofoctol increases KLF13 mRNA and protein levels in TECs and fibrotic kidneys of mice. Consistently, daily intravenous administration of clofotetan reduces collagen deposition and expression of TGF-β target genes in the kidneys of mice subjected to UUO surgery. These results suggest that clofoctol, as a small molecule agonist of KLF13, can restore KLF13 activity under pathological conditions, limit TGF-β signaling, terminate excessive tissue fibrotic responses, and ultimately inhibit fibrosis108. In diabetic mouse renal tubular cells, trichostatin A effectively reduced the expression of HDAC5. Meanwhile, TSA treatment significantly inhibited the overexpression of TGF-β and attenuated the accumulation of extracellular matrix in the kidneys of diabetic mice. These findings suggest that HDAC inhibitors, such as TSA, may be effective in treating diabetic nephropathy by controlling renal tubular cell EMT and decreasing extracellular matrix deposition112. In chronically injured kidneys, LSD1 exhibits high expression levels. The LSD1-specific inhibitor ORY-1001 attenuates renal fibrosis by blocking several key cellular and molecular processes. Specifically, ORY-1001 inhibits EMT, reduces activation of renal mesangial fibroblasts, and prevents cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase. Additionally, ORY-1001 inhibits the LSD1–14-3-3ζ–PKCA signaling pathway, which is responsible for activating Akt and STAT3—both key factors involved in inflammatory responses and cell proliferation. By inhibiting this pathway, ORY-1001 effectively attenuates renal fibrosis progression and fibroblast activation119. BIX01294, a specific inhibitor of G9a, not only inhibits the expression of fibrosis markers but also improves Klotho expression in UUO mice. These findings suggest that targeting G9a may be a promising strategy for treating CKD116. Similarly, GSK-J4, an inhibitor of the histone demethylase KDM6A, significantly reduces proteinuria and glomerulosclerosis in diabetic mice by modulating dickkopf-1 (DKK1). For instance, the expression of fibrogenic factors such as DKK1, TGF-β1, fibronectin, and type IV collagen was lower in GSK-J4-treated mice compared to untreated diabetic mice.

In summary, GSK-J4-mediated inhibition of KDM6A and regulation of the fibrotic process via DKK1 not only reduced proteinuria and glomerulosclerosis but also decreased the expression of multiple fibrotic factors. These findings provide novel insights and a theoretical basis for the development of new therapeutic approaches targeting diabetic nephropathy160. When Glis1 binds to KAT5, it reduces the interaction between KAT5 and histones, thereby decreasing histone acetylation. This reduction helps to mitigate both the aging of TECs and renal fibrosis symptoms. Further experiments revealed that specific inhibitors, such as MG149 or SB216763, can reduce KAT5 activity. MG149 directly targets KAT5, while SB216763 indirectly affects KAT5 by inhibiting GSK3, an upstream kinase of KAT5. Both of these inhibitors were effective in reducing histone lactylation at the H3K18 locus, suggesting that modulating KAT5 activity is a key strategy to regulate histone lactylation and potentially delay renal tubular cell senescence and renal fibrosis128. Intrarenal KLF5 knockdown ameliorated EMT progression in DKD. Therefore, targeting the lactate-driven H3K14la/KLF5 pathway may represent a novel therapeutic strategy for treating renal fibrosis in DKD. The KLF5 inhibitor ML264 significantly inhibited fibrosis and collagen deposition127. Additionally, the ACSS2 inhibitor VY-3-249 delayed the progression of renal fibrosis in mice and further suppressed IL1B-mediated inflammatory responses by reducing H3K9cr levels140.

In addition to the aforementioned drugs targeting epigenetic regulation, inhibitors of the bromodomain and extraterminal (BET) protein family have garnered significant attention in recent years. BET proteins play crucial roles in gene regulation by recognizing and binding to acetylated lysine residues, particularly on histone tails, thereby influencing chromatin remodeling and transcriptional regulation161,162. BET protein-selective inhibitors block the binding of BET proteins to acetylated lysine residues and have demonstrated antifibrotic effects in multiple studies. For instance, a recent study showed that JQ1, a BET protein-selective inhibitor, reduced the expression of fibrotic markers such as p-Smad3, Col4a3, Col1a2, and FN in a mouse model of nephrotoxic serum-induced anti-glomerular basement membrane nephritis. Additionally, JQ1 blocked the activation of the pro-fibrotic factor SOX9 in a UUO model163. Conclusions and perspectives

With the continuous deepening of human understanding of the complex mechanisms of epigenetic modification, the important role of epigenetic regulation in the occurrence and development of many human diseases is gradually revealed. However, in the research of CKD, this field is still in its infancy. In order to better understand the important role of epigenetic modifications in CKD, this article starts with different types of molecular modifications, focusing on the identification of modification types, target confirmation, mechanism research, and drug development processes. Meanwhile, this review summarizes the important role of epigenetic modifications in the pathological process of renal fibrosis, explores the significant impact of interactions between different modifications on renal fibrosis, reveals the molecular mechanism of renal fibrosis, and delves into the development of therapeutic drugs for it, providing a reference for subsequent research.

In addition, it is of great significance to develop new treatment methods and diagnostic tools for CKD based on research on different modification types, especially to search for specific biomarkers that can accurately indicate the development of CKD. This will help with early diagnosis, disease progression assessment, and treatment interventions. However, translating these insights into clinical applications for the treatment of CKD faces multiple challenges. The role of epigenetic regulation in renal fibrosis and CKD is not fully understood. Partly due to the complexity of the epigenetic network, such as dynamic crosstalk between DNA methylation, RNA methylation, histone modifications, and ncRNA, targeted intervention becomes very difficult.

Although epigenetic therapy has shown potential in the study of renal fibrosis, most current research remains in the preclinical stage and is less mature compared to the field of oncology. Differences in fibrosis models, stages, and drug doses lead to variable research outcomes, complicating the accurate evaluation of therapeutic effects. Moreover, there is currently no clinical trial data on epigenetic therapy for renal fibrosis, and its efficacy and safety in humans remain unvalidated. Existing epigenetic therapy drugs, such as DNA methylation inhibitors and histone deacetylase inhibitors, typically lack specificity for particular genes or cell types. This nonspecificity may cause unintended effects on non-target cells, resulting in adverse reactions. Consequently, developing an effective drug delivery system is crucial. Presently, there is a lack of delivery systems that can precisely target kidney cells, limiting the effective concentration and duration of drug action in the kidneys. Additionally, the absence of biomarkers that accurately reflect the efficacy of epigenetic therapy complicates the monitoring and evaluation of treatment effectiveness.

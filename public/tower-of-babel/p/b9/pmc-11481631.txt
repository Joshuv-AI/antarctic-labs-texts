The impact of verbal behavior on audience engagement in Chinese talk shows: Based on a novel mediation model
Authors: Ya-Ming Li, Yi-Qun Zhang, Xiao Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study employs the Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) model to construct a new mediation framework, examining the impact of Chinese stand-up comedians' language on audience engagement from both quantitative and qualitative perspectives. Statistical analysis of data from 415 online viewers of stand-up comedy shows revealed that comedians' humorous language significantly enhanced audience engagement by positively influencing their enjoyment. Based on this, in-depth interviews were conducted with 8 viewers to understand their specific perceptions of different types of humor. The analysis results showed that Chinese audiences had a higher acceptance of affiliative humor, self-deprecating humor, and humor incorporating traditional cultural elements, while they had lower acceptance or even rejection of aggressive humor, political satire, and vulgar humor. This study enriches the research on the relationship between Chinese stand-up comedy shows and audience engagement, providing insights for improving the production of Chinese stand-up comedy shows and for Western entertainment programs entering the Chinese market.Keywords: Talk show, Verbal behavior, Audience engagement, Mediation effect, Discourse features

Introduction

In 2012, the emergence of the Chinese stand-up comedy show "Tonight 80's Talk Show" brought a refreshing experience to the audience. Since then, an increasing number of localized and original talk shows have appeared, leading to the widespread dissemination of this Western program format in China and showing a trend of localization. However, compared to the mature stand-up comedy industry in the United States, China's stand-up comedy industry is still in its infancy, demonstrating significant market development potential and growth space. China's vast stand-up comedy market has not only attracted active investment from local creators but also garnered attention and interest from the Western entertainment industry. As a form of entertainment originating from the West, stand-up comedy still needs to use expressions that align with local culture and humor to achieve better dissemination among Chinese audiences.

With the continuous development of media technology, particularly the rise of online media, stand-up comedy shows have surpassed traditional performance fields and evolved into a cultural phenomenon with broad influence. Over the past decades, stand-up comedy has gained increasing popularity as a complement to traditional entertainment forms [1], attracting scholarly interest in its new media discourse features [2], subversive humor, main linguistic techniques [3], and stage performance forms [4]. These researches provide a macro perspective reference for the study of stand-up comedy.

In-depth research on stand-up comedy culture has gradually revealed the complexity of audience engagement and its multidimensional influencing factors. Existing research has found that using multimodal semiotic resources not only stimulates audience interest and engagement [5] but also plays an important role in enhancing attention in both face-to-face speeches and online videos [6]. Among these factors, the influence of language on the popularity and audience evaluation of stand-up comedy cannot be ignored [7]. Building on existing research, scholars have conducted in-depth explorations, revealing that rhetorical techniques [8] and specific grammar construction significantly impact audience engagement and feedback [9], thus providing valuable insights for further exploration.

Humorous language, as the core feature and main attraction of stand-up comedy shows, has garnered significant scholarly attention for its impact on audience engagement among the various speech acts [1]. Previous researchers have explored the nature and effects of humor from multiple angles, including the structure and characteristics of humorous discourse, pragmatic strategies of humor, and the production mechanisms of humorous language. These studies revealed the important role of humorous language in eliciting audience resonance and enhancing audience engagement. Although these studies gradually reveal the complexity of humor in stand-up comedy shows, the impact of humorous language on audience engagement in stand-up comedy has not been fully explored. This study aims to fill this gap by analyzing how the humorous language of stand-up comedy hosts affects audience engagement and exploring the dynamic relationship between speech acts and audience responses.

In existing research on audience engagement, statistical methods, mediation effect theories, and data analysis techniques are widely used to explore and verify various factors influencing audience engagement, such as head movements [10], accurate information transmission [11], creators' professionalism [12], the effectiveness of information dissemination [13], and positive emotions [14]. In recent years, the Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) model, originally created by Robert S. Woodworth and further refined by Mehrabian and Russell in 1974, has been employed in various disciplines such as psychology, consumer behavior, education, and emotion recognition, demonstrating its wide applicability in explaining behavioral responses. For example, the SOR model has been used to explore behavioral and psychological changes in college students during the COVID-19 crisis [15], study the impact of customer interaction and employee motivation on innovative behavior [16], and analyze how images attract attention and stimulate action [17]. However, despite the significant achievements of the SOR model in these fields, its application in the specific media format of stand-up comedy shows remains relatively limited, with related literature and empirical research being scarce. Additionally, mediation effect theory, an important theoretical tool in modern psychology and behavioral science research, is widely used to study the indirect effects and internal mechanisms among variables. Mediation effect theory emphasizes that independent variables indirectly influence dependent variables through the paths of mediation variables, thereby deeply analyzing the processes and mechanisms affecting dependent variables.

In this paper，we will combine the SOR model and mediation effect theory, expecting to more comprehensively explain the influence mechanism of humorous language on audience engagement. Specifically, we will use variable analysis methods to test the direct impact of humorous language, introduce a mediation effect model to explore possible mediation variables (such as audience emotional responses), and conduct path analysis through structural equation modeling (SEM) to verify the applicability of the SOR model in this study and the significance of the mediation effect. Through these methods, we hope to verify the applicability of the SOR model in the study of audience engagement in stand-up comedy shows and improve and extend the traditional SOR model, providing new perspectives and empirical support for understanding the influence mechanism of humorous language on audience engagement in stand-up comedy shows. Through the above research path, we hope to more accurately understand the influence mechanism of humorous language on audience emotions and engagement, thereby optimizing program content and enhancing audience experience. Compared with existing research, we will combine quantitative and qualitative analysis to verify and deepen the understanding of the influence mechanism of humorous language. This study not only provides innovative references for local stand-up comedy but also hopes to provide effective insights for foreign stand-up comedy and other language-based entertainment programs entering the Chinese market.

Stimulus-organism-response model (SOR model)

The SOR model is an important theory in modern cognitive psychology, evolved from the behaviorist stimulus-response model. This theory was first proposed by American psychologist Robert S. Woodworth and later refined by Mehrabian and Russell in 1974. Over time, the SOR theory has matured and is mainly used to explain the impact of environmental characteristics on users' psychology and behavior. The core idea of the SOR model is that stimuli do not directly cause responses; rather, they are mediated by the individual's state and psychological mechanisms. The model consists of three stages: stimulus, organism, and response. Stimulus refers to any information in the external environment; the organism includes the individual's personality, attitudes, values, and emotional cognition; and the response is the feedback generated by the organism to the stimulus. The model provides a framework to explain the specific process of responding to stimuli, emphasizing the important mediating or moderating role of the individual's internal state in the relationship between stimulus and response. The specific structure of the SOR model is illustrated in Fig. 1.

Depicts the theoretical framework of the SOR model.

Based on Fig. 1, the interaction between stimulus, organism, and response constitutes a dynamic process. In other words, stimuli trigger the organism's response, and the internal processes and characteristics of the organism influence the perception of stimuli, which in turn determines the organism's behavioral cognition. Due to these interactions and regulatory relationships, the SOR model can better explain the feedback process of the organism to stimuli, helping us to deeply understand the complex processes of human behavior and the underlying psychology.

In fact, the Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) model has permeated various disciplines of social applications such as psychology, consumer behavior, education, and emotion recognition. Mediation effect theory

Mediation effect theory is a commonly used method in statistical and social science research to reveal the mechanisms through which an independent variable affects a dependent variable. It emphasizes the indirect influence of an independent variable on a dependent variable through a mediator variable, thereby providing a deeper analysis of the processes and mechanisms that impact the dependent variable.In recent years, mediation effect theory has been widely applied in various fields. Studies have explored how Workplace Spirituality affects unethical behavior through job satisfaction [18], how leadership fairness relates to negative organizational behaviors in China [19], and how free gift promotions influence perceived quality, customer value, and purchase intention, while also examining how these factors mediate the relationship between free gifts and brand loyalty [20]. By testing and validating mediation effects, we can further verify hypotheses and provide more accurate and comprehensive research conclusions. The Sobel test is a commonly used method for evaluating the significance of these mediation effects. It assesses the significance of the mediation effect by calculating the coefficients and standard errors in the regression equations. The specific testing steps are shown in Fig. 2. In addition to the Sobel test, bootstrap testing and Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) will also be used in this study to test the significance and stability of the mediation effect theory. Bootstrap testing estimates the confidence intervals of the mediation effect through resampling methods, while SEM constructs path models to evaluate the role of the mediator variable. The comprehensive application of these testing methods will enhance the credibility and explanatory power of the research results.

In-depth interviews

The in-depth interview method, as a vital qualitative research approach, explores individuals' perspectives, feelings, and experiences, revealing details and deep-level information that cannot be captured by questionnaire surveys. In this study, in-depth interviews are primarily used to supplement and validate the results of the questionnaire survey, helping us gain a more comprehensive understanding of audience reactions and engagement concerning stand-up comedians' speech acts.

In-depth interviews can uncover the true thoughts and emotional responses of respondents in specific situations. By guiding respondents through semi-open-ended questions, the interviews encourage them to discuss in depth their perceptions and the reasons behind their behaviors. This method can collect rich qualitative data, providing strong support and a supplement to the quantitative research results. Additionally, in-depth interviews can help identify variables and details that may have been overlooked in the questionnaire survey, thus offering a more comprehensive perspective and deeper understanding of the research.

In this study, we first use variable analysis to test the direct impact of comedians' speech acts on audience engagement, then employ a mediation effect model to explore potential mediators like audience emotional responses, and finally use Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) for path analysis to verify the applicability of the SOR model and the significance of the mediation effect, along with conducting reliability and validity tests to ensure data and results credibility.

Through these methods, this study not only verifies the applicability of the SOR model in the context of audience engagement in stand-up comedy but also improves and extends the traditional SOR model, providing new perspectives and empirical support for understanding the influence mechanism of comedians' speech acts on audience engagement. In summary, mediation effects offer certain advantages in application but also have noteworthy limitations. Firstly, mediation effects provide an opportunity to gain a deeper understanding of the relationship between two variables by revealing the role and mechanisms of mediating variables, thus offering a more detailed explanation of causal relationships. Secondly, mediation effects can explore the intermediate steps and mechanisms between variables, helping researchers understand how the original relationships are explained or transmitted through mediating variables.

Assumptions of mediation effect theory based on the SOR model

In analyzing the impact of verbal behavior on audience engagement in comedy shows, the SOR model considers the actor's speech as the stimulus, the audience's emotional response as the organism, and the audience's engagement as the response. Specifically, in this study, the mediation effect assumption is based on the SOR model, with the actor's humorous language as the independent variable, the audience's ratings as the dependent variable, and the audience's level of enjoyment as the mediating variable. Questionnaire research and design

Before verifying these hypotheses, we need to collect field data to obtain specific information about audience reactions to stand-up comedy shows. Questionnaire design

In the questionnaire design section, we considered the diverse demands brought by China's vast stand-up comedy market. Although stand-up comedy originated in the West, understanding which forms of humor expression can effectively attract Chinese audiences is also a key interest of this study. In China, many content creators (referred to as "UP" hosts) upload stand-up comedy shows online for secondary dissemination. This allows us to capture the bullet screen comments and feedback from the audience for quantitative analysis.This study employed a random cluster sampling method and designed multiple styles of questionnaires. We commissioned F, a stand-up comedy video publisher on the video portal website https://www.bilibili.com/, to issue dynamic invitations to viewers who have watched online stand-up comedy shows and made bullet screen comments, inviting them to voluntarily fill out the questionnaire. This approach ensures that the respondents have actual viewing and interactive experiences.Due to the advanced economic and political development in provincial capital cities, stand-up comedy shows are more popular there. Therefore, we selected audiences from 11 provincial capital cities in China as the survey subjects.As a result, we received a total of 418 audience survey questionnaires, of which 415 were valid. Their responses were statistically analyzed using a 5-point Likert scale (strongly disagree to strongly agree). Invalid questionnaires, which were excluded from the analysis, mainly included those with missing answers, contradictory responses, or those where the same option was selected throughout. The distribution and collection of the questionnaires were conducted using Wenjuanxing software, with the distribution period spanning from August 2023 to September 2023.

Based on the literature review and our research objectives, we designed the questionnaire to cover two primary aspects: the actor's humorous language and the audience's enjoyment level. This design aimed to explore the impact of the actor's humorous language on the number of audience ratings, with the audience's enjoyment level acting as the mediating variable. The audience questionnaire included sections on basic respondent information, the actor's humorous language, audience rating quantity, and audience enjoyment level. As viewers of the program, the audience's evaluations serve as an indicator of the program's quality. Audience feedback reflects the interactivity, interest, depth, and creativity of the program's content, making it an essential basis for guiding the improvement and development of the program. This feedback helps the production team determine whether their goals have been achieved. By including different perspectives in the survey questions, we enhance the diversity and objectivity of the questionnaire results. The Likert scale, a commonly used statistical survey method, was adopted to accurately capture the respondents' true feelings and improve the accuracy of the data.

Theoretical basis

This study adopts the grounded theory approach and method, utilizing open coding, axial coding, and selective coding to analyze the text data collected on the impact of virtual technology and immersive news. Meaningful concepts and categories are extracted at each level of coding and then integrated. Relationships between categories are established while analyzing the interview data of key informants. Finally, selective coding is performed. The three levels of coding—open coding, axial coding, and selective coding—are used to form core concepts and categories and clarify the complex logical relationships between concepts.

The implementation process of virtual technology and immersive news analysis using grounded theory can be summarized in the following steps:(1) theoretical sampling and collection of research materials; (2) coding of data at each level and generation of concepts from it; (3) adherence to the principle of constant comparison to gradually establish connections between concepts; (4) completion of theoretical construction and evaluation.Three-level coding of data is a core step in the implementation process of grounded theory.

Conclusion and discussion

This study combines the Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) model with the mediation effect theory to investigate the impact of actors' language on audience engagement in Chinese talk shows, based on questionnaire surveys and in-depth interviews. The KMO and Bartlett tests on the statistical data of 415 audience members from different provinces indicate a causal relationship between audience engagement and various influencing factors, and the Cronbach's alpha coefficient of the sample shows good internal consistency of the questionnaire dimensions. The validity analysis of the sample indicates that the information of each research item can be effectively extracted, and the correlation analysis of the sample (using Pearson correlation coefficient in this study) shows a positive correlation between audience enjoyment, actor humor language, and audience evaluation quantity. The hypothesis test on the mediation effect of audience enjoyment between actor humor language and audience evaluation quantity indicates that under the premise that the type I error is not greater than 5 %, audience enjoyment has a mediating effect between actor humor language and audience evaluation quantity. We further validated the partial mediating effect of audience enjoyment in this relationship through Bootstrap sampling of the sample.

To further understand the specific types of humor language favored by Chinese audiences, we conducted in-depth interviews based on the questionnaire survey. The interview results show that Chinese audiences prefer Chinese-style humor language from talk show actors, including affinity humor, self-deprecating humor, and humor that incorporates traditional cultural elements, while the acceptance of aggressive humor, political satire humor, and vulgar humor is low or even rejected.

Based on the results of this study, to increase audience engagement in talk shows in the Chinese market, actors can enhance emotional connections with audiences and enhance the attractiveness of programs by highlighting affinity humor, increasing self-deprecating humor content, and incorporating humor with traditional cultural elements. At the same time, it is advisable to avoid using aggressive humor, use political satire humor with caution, and reduce the use of vulgar humor to ensure the health and positivity of program content. Considering the mediating role of emotional reactions in verbal behavior and audience engagement, actors can design more interactive segments in the program to allow audience participation. For example, by asking questions live, conducting audience polls, interactive games, etc., audiences can feel that their participation has a real impact on the program. They can also evoke audience empathy through program content that resonates emotionally with the audience, such as themes of family, friendship, dreams, etc., combined with humor elements, allowing the audience to feel warmth and motivation in pleasure. In addition, talk show actors should pay close attention to audience feedback and evaluation quantity. This can be achieved through social media analysis, online questionnaires, focus group discussions, etc., to cultivate actors' verbal skills targetedly, strengthen emotional connections, and monitor audience feedback to increase audience engagement and further enhance the attractiveness and influence of talk shows.

This study also has some limitations. Although the entertainment market in China is huge, the study focuses on Chinese talk shows, and the results may be difficult to generalize to other cultural backgrounds or types of talk shows.Additionally, this survey sample includes 415 audience members from 11 provincial capital cities in China. Although the sample has a certain degree of geographical and demographic representativeness, there may be limitations when generalizing the results to a broader population. The relatively small sample size and the lack of detailed questionnaire options may also affect the validity of the results. Future research should include more diversified samples and explore research subjects from different cultural backgrounds to enhance universality. Additionally, including a wider range of mediating factors, including non-verbal cues and visual elements, will provide a more comprehensive explanation for the effectiveness of talk shows.

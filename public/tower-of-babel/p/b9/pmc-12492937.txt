Construction of diagnostic model and subtype analysis of major depressive disorder based on PANoptosis key genes
Authors: Huan Zhang, Na Huang, Xinxin Ma, Yanan Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMajor depressive disorder (MDD) is a serious neuropsychiatric disorder. While emerging evidence suggests that PANoptosis may play a role in MDD pathogenesis, the precise involvement of PANoptosis-related genes remains unclear.MethodsThe study conducted a systematic bioinformatics investigation utilizing the GSE98793 dataset. First, we identified differentially expressed PANoptosis-related genes (DE-PRGs). Second, Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis were carried out based on DE-PRGs. Additionally, Random Forest analysis, LASSO regression analysis, immune infiltration analysis, consensus cluster analysis, and single sample genome enrichment analysis were performed. Finally, the expression levels of PANoptosis key genes (PKGs) in MDD were verified using qRT-PCR.ResultsEight PKGs associated with MDD were identified: TRAF1, TNFSF13, TLR2, SH2D1A, RNF144B, ICAM1, HK2, and ADA. Moreover, these PKGs enabled construction of a diagnostic model for MDD PANoptosis risk and stratification of MDD patients into two distinct PANoptosis subtypes (cluster 1 and cluster 2) with differential immune characteristics. Notably, RNF144B and HK2 demonstrated neutrophil-stimulating potential and significant upregulation in MDD patients.ConclusionThis study established a PANoptosis-based diagnostic framework for MDD and identified PANoptosis-stratified MDD subtypes, providing compelling evidence for PANoptosis-mediated immune dysregulation in MDD pathogenesis. These results offered a novel perspective for the diagnosis and management of MDD.Keywords: Major depressive disorder, PANoptosis, Diagnostic model, Subtype analysis, Bioinformatics

Introduction

Worldwide, depression is a serious disabling public health problem with a very high prevalence [1]. Major depressive disorder (MDD) exhibits a 12-month prevalence of 6.6% and a Lifetime prevalence of 16.2%, resulting in significant physiological harm [2]. MDD is an affective disorder syndrome characterized by low mood, lack of interest and loss of fun, accompanied by anxiety, cognitive impairment, psychomotor disorder, and suicidal tendency in severe cases [3]. Besides, MDD pathogenesis is multifactorial, involving genetic, gender-specific, neuroendocrine, psychosocial and immunological factors [4–6]. Current clinical diagnosis of MDD primarily relies on subjective assessment of symptom description, mental state examination and clinical behavior observation [7], highlighting the critical need for objective diagnostic biomarkers. Moreover, a substantial proportion of MDD patients exhibit poor pharmacological response, potentially attributable to diagnostic inaccuracy [8]. While selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors represent the most common therapy for MDD [9], treatment resistance develops in more than one-third of cases [10]. These clinical realities underscore the urgent requirement for developing precision diagnostic tools and personalized therapeutic strategies.

PANoptosis is an inflammatory programmed cell death pathway regulated by the PANoptosome, which highlights the crosstalk and coordination between pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis [10–12]. Currently, it is known that PANoptosis is closely related to many diseases, including aseptic inflammation, infection and cancer [13]. In addition, individuals with MDD demonstrate dysregulation of both innate and adaptive immune systems, characterized by oxidative stress-induced inflammatory responses culminating in cellular apoptosis [14]. Emerging evidence from transcriptomic studies has identified molecular markers of inflammation, apoptosis, and oxidative stress in depressed patients [15]. Existing literatures substantiate the mechanistic links between depression pathogenesis and oxidative stress [16–18]. Notably, oxidative stress serves as a potent inducer of multiple cell death pathways, including both pyroptosis and necrosis [19–21]. Valencia et al. showed that oxidative stress triggered neuronal death through necrosis and apoptosis [22]. These collective findings suggest the potential co-occurrence of pyroptosis, apoptosis and necroptosis in MDD pathophysiology, implicating that PANoptosis may be involved in the pathological process of MDD immune dysregulation. However, the therapeutic implications of PANoptosis modulation remain unexplored in the current diagnosis and treatment of MDD.

The integration of bioinformatics with biomedical research has revolutionized our understanding of disease mechanisms and therapeutic development, particularly for refractory conditions. In the past decade, genome-wide analysis technologies such as high-throughput sequencing and gene chip have been widely used to study gene expression patterns, resulting in the rapid development of bioinformatics [23]. The development of data processing and analysis methods, such as Random Forest algorithm, Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) regression analysis, and drawing receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC), has significantly enhanced our capacity to elucidate disease pathophysiology and identify clinically relevant biomarkers [24–26].

This study employed a robust machine learning approach combining Random Forest algorithm and LASSO regression analysis to identify PANoptosis key genes (PKGs) in MDD. The selected PKGs were subsequently incorporated into a PANoptosis risk prediction model, which demonstrated significant diagnostic potential for MDD through validation. Furthermore, we applied consensus clustering analysis to stratify MDD patients into distinct PANoptosis subtypes, facilitating comparative analysis of subtype disparities. This investigation offered novel insights for the diagnostic and therapeutic management of MDD.

Materials and methods

Data collection and preprocessing

RNAseq datasets (GSE98793, GSE76826 and GSE32280) were obtained from Gene Expression Omnibus (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The training set (GSE98793) included whole-blood samples from 128 MDD patients and 64 healthy controls, profiled using GPL570 platform. Two independent validation sets were used: GSE76826 (20 patients and 12 controls) employed GPL17077 platform, while GSE32280 (16 patients and 8 controls) utilized the same GPL570 platform as the training set. For data preprocessing, platform-specific normalization pipelines were applied: the GSE98793 dataset was processed using the RMA algorithm via Bioconductor; the GSE76826 dataset was normalized using Agilent GeneSpring GX 11.0.2 software; and the GSE32280 dataset underwent RMA normalization with GeneSpring 11.0.

PANoptosis-related genes (PRGs) were retrieved from GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/) following the screening criteria with correlation score > 3. The MDD-associated genes were identified by screening the GeneCards database (www.genecards.org) and selecting the top 20 highest-scoring MDD-related entries.

Acquisition of differentially expressed PANoptosis related genes (DE-PRGs)

The ‘removebatcheffect’ function within the ‘limma’ package was employed to mitigate the influence of non-biotechnological bias. Additionally, the ‘combat’ function in the ‘sva’ package was utilized to address batch effects across various datasets. Subsequently, the ‘limma’ package was applied for conducting a differential analysis of the GSE98793 dataset, with cutoff criteria established at |log2FC| >0.138 and a p-value < 0.05. The intersection between differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and PRGs was computationally determined and visualized using the ‘ggvenn’ package, yielding the set of DE-PRGs for downstream analysis. Finally, the ‘pheatmap’ package was utilized to create a heatmap illustrating the expression of DE-PRGs between MDD patients and healthy controls.

Gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) analysis of DE-PRGs

GO and KEGG functional enrichment analysis of DE-PRGs was performed using the R packet ‘clusterProfiler’, followed by clustering of related biological processes. Subsequently, the Metascape database (https://metascape.org/) was utilized to further explore functional mechanisms. A minimum overlap ≥ 3 and p ≤ 0.01 were considered statistically significant.

Random forest analysis and LASSO regression analysis

DE-PRGs from GSE98793 dataset were evaluated using Random Forest to identify those with diagnostic utility for MDD-associated PANoptosis risk. The optimal number of signature genes was determined to be 13 from the results of 10-fold cross validation. Subsequently, Mean Decrease Accuracy and Mean Decrease Gini analyses were performed to rank the top 13 DE-PRGs according to importance, resulting in the identification of 8 PKGs.

The MDD PANoptosis risk diagnostic model was developed utilizing LASSO regression analysis to determine the characteristic coefficients of PKGs. These coefficients were then used to calculate a PANoptosis risk score for MDD in each sample, enabling differentiation between MDD patients and healthy controls. The diagnostic efficacy of the model was assessed using the ROC curve and area under the curve (AUC). Validation of the MDD diagnostic model was conducted using the GSE76826 and GSE32280 datasets.

Immune cell infiltration spectrum

The CIBERSORT algorithm was utilized to evaluate the presence of 22 immune cell subtypes in each sample of GSE98793. Furthermore, an examination of the interrelationships among various immune cells was conducted. Disparities in immune infiltrating cells between control subjects and individuals with MDD were also investigated, along with an analysis of the correlations between PKGs and immune cells.

Consensus cluster analysis

Unsupervised clustering of MDD patients in GSE98793 was performed using the ‘ConsensusClusterPlus’ package in R. The analysis was based on PKGs selected via LASSO regression. The k-values were selected and determined by defining the inflection point of the sum of errors. Subsequently, subgroup analysis was performed on the expression of PKGs in MDD patients. Differentially expressed genes between the different PANoptosis subtypes were identified using the ‘limma’ package with a fold change of 1 and an adjusted p < 0.05.

Single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) and gene set variation analysis (GSVA) analysis

ssGSEA was carried out to quantify the relative abundance of 28 immune cell types associated with the immune response. In addition, GSVA analysis was utilized to calculate pathway enrichment scores of each sample for pathway enrichment analysis. The database used for the GSVA analysis was cp.kegg.v7.2.symbols.gmt.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Molecular validation was conducted through qRT-PCR analysis of peripheral blood samples obtained from MDD patients (n = 5) and healthy controls (n = 5) using qRT-PCR. Total RNA was extracted using RNA extraction reagent (#G3013, Servicebio, Wuhan, Hubei, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol. The cDNA was synthesized from total RNA using PrimeScript™ RT reagent Kit (#RR037Q, Takara, Tokyo, Japan) with oligo(dT) and random hexamer primers. qPCR was performed on a SLAN-96 S system (Hongshi, Shanghai, China) using TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ II (#RR820Q, Takara) with the following cycling conditions: 95 °C for 30 s, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 5 s and 60 °C for 34 s. All reactions were performed in triplicate, and relative gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences: HK2 (F:5’-GAGTTTGACCTGGATGTGGTTGC-3’, R:5’-CCTCCATGTAGCAGGCATTGCT-3’), RNF144B (F:5’-GCAAACTCTGCCTGTGTGAGCA-3’, R:5’-CATCCTTCTCGGATTGCCAGCT-3’), and β-actin as internal control (F:5’-CACCATTGGCAATGAGCGGTTC-3’, R:5’-AGGTCTTTGCGGATGTCCACGT-3’).

Statistical analysis

All parametric analyses were based on two-sided tests, and the Kruskal-Wallis test was used to compare differences between groups. Linear regression analysis was used to explore the correlation between the PKGs and immune cells. In addition, t test analysis method was used to screen out DE-PRGs in MDD, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. The p-values were adjusted for multiple testing using the Benjamini-Hochberg method via the adjust_pvalue function. All statistical analyses were performed with the use of R language version 4.2.2.

Results

Differential expression analysis of PRGs in MDD patients

Utilizing the GSE98793 dataset, transcriptomic profiling of MDD patients versus Healthy controls revealed significant alterations in gene expression patterns. A total of 1673 DEGs were identified, with 826 genes showing increased expression and 847 genes demonstrating decreased expression in MDD patients (Fig. 1A). Through systematic integration of these DEGs with 902 PRGs, we identified 104 DE-PRGs, consisting of 57 upregulated and 47 downregulated candidates (Fig. 1B-C). This robust signature of PRG dysregulation suggested its potential involvement in MDD pathogenesis.

Enrichment analysis of DE-PRGs in MDD patients

Functional enrichment analysis of DE-PRGs was performed using Go and KEGG databases. As shown in Fig. 2A, regulation of deoxyribonuclease activity, CD4-positive, alpha-beta T cell proliferation and detection of bacterium were main enriched in biological process (BP) terms. Inflammasome complex, perinuclear endoplasmic reticulum and dendrite terminus were main enriched in cell component (CC) terms. Insulin receptor substrate binding, tumor necrosis factor receptor binding and cysteine-type endoprpidase inhibitor activity involved in apoptotic process were concentrated in molecular function (MF) terms. Besides, KEGG annotations showed that these genes were associated with bladder cancer, tumor necrosis factor (TNF) signaling pathway and nuclear factor kappa-B (NF-κB) signaling pathway (Fig. 2B). Furthermore, the results of the meta-analysis, which combined data from multiple databases, suggested that closely connected modules in the network graph might exhibit interrelationships or participate in shared biological processes (Fig. 2C). Notably, DE-PRGs were mainly enriched in biological functions and pathways related to immune inflammation and apoptosis, revealing significant associations between PANoptosis and immune dysregulation in MDD.

Identification of PKGs and establishment of a diagnostic model in MDD

We initially applied Random Forest analysis to the GSE98793 dataset, selecting the first 13 DE-PRGs through 10 iterations of 10-fold cross-validation (Fig. 3A). Subsequently, the top 13 DE-PRGs were ranked by importance using the analysis of Mean Decrease Accuracy and Mean Decrease Gini (Fig. 3B). These DE-PRGs were then analyzed using LASSO regression (Fig. 3C-D). Following feature selection, 8 PKGs (TRAF1, TNFSF13, TLR2, SH2D1A, RNF144B, ICAM1, HK2, ADA) were identified, leading to the establishment of a PANoptosis risk diagnosis model for MDD. MDD PANoptosis risk score was calculated using gene-specific coefficients, effectively distinguishing MDD from healthy controls (Fig. 3E). In addition, ROC curve was used to evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of MDD PANoptosis risk diagnosis model on three independent datasets. ROC analysis demonstrated that the AUC values of GSE98793, GSE32280 and GSE76826 datasets were respectively 0.843, 0.766 and 0.762, indicating that the PANoptosis risk diagnostic model for MDD was reliable (Fig. 3F-H). Therefore, through machine learning approaches, we established a robust diagnostic model for MDD based on PKGs.

Immune correlation analysis of PKGs in MDD

Immune cell analysis confirmed significant differences in the ratios of T cells gamma delta, Monocytes and Macrophages M0 between MDD patients and healthy controls. T cells gamma delta was reduced in the MDD group compared with the control group, while Monocytes and Macrophages M0 were elevated (Fig. 4A-B). The distinct alterations of immune cells in MDD individuals indicated pathological involvement of immune dysregulation and inflammation. Furthermore, Spearman correlation coefficient analysis revealed significant correlations between MDD-associated immune cell alterations and PKGs. Specifically, RNF144B and HK2 exhibited significant positive correlations with Neutrophils, while TNFSF13 showed a significant positive correlation with Monocytes (Fig. 4C-D). These findings suggested that PANoptosis might play a role in MDD immune dysregulation.

Molecular stratification of MDD based on PKGs

Cumulative distribution function (CDF) curve analysis showed that when k = 2, the cluster number was the most stable and the result of cluster analysis was the most reliable. Therefore, two different MDD subtypes (clusters C1 and C2) were identified by consensus cluster analysis based on eight PKGs (Fig. 5A-C). Subsequently, PCA analysis further confirmed clear separation between MDD subtypes C1 and C2 based on 8 PKGs (Fig. 5D). Moreover, the differential expression levels of PKGs between the two subtypes were demonstrated by heat maps and box plots. Compared with cluster C1, HK2 and RNF144B were significantly down-regulated in cluster C2, while TNFSF12, ADA and TRAF1 were significantly increased in cluster C2 (Fig. 5E-F). Our consensus clustering analysis identified two distinct molecular subtypes of MDD.

Distinct immune and biological characteristics between two PANoptosis-defined MDD subtypes

Most human leukocyte antigen (HLA) genes and immune cell scores showed significant differences in the C1 and C2 clusters of MDD patients. Compared with cluster C1, the expression of HLA-DMA, HLA-DMB, HLA-DOA, HLA-DOB and HLA-F was notably increased in cluster C2 (Fig. 6A). Compared with cluster C1, Cluster C2 demonstrated significantly elevated fraction score of activated B cells, activated CD8 T cells, CD56dim natural killer cells, MDSCs, natural killer T cells, T follicular Helper cells and type 1 T helper cells, but reduced fraction score of activated CD4 T cells, eosinophils, macrophages, neutrophils and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (Fig. 6B). Moreover, the KEGG pathways of the two clusters also exhibited different levels of activation, including long term potentiation, renal cell carcinoma, systemic lupus erythematosus and drug metabolism cytochrome p450 (Fig. 6C-D). Therefore, the immune function and biological characteristics of MDD patients classified with two different PANoptosis subtypes were different.

The role of PANoptosis in MDD

The study further examined the differential expression of MDD-associated genes in clusters C1 and C2, revealing significant variations in PTPN22, ITGAM, and SPP1 between the two clusters (Fig. 7A). Additionally, a notable correlation was observed between PTPN22 and various PKGs (Fig. 7B), with HK2 and RNF144B showing significant positive associations with PTPN22 (Fig. 7C-D). Analysis of the validation datasets GSE32280 and GSE76826 revealed significant upregulation of RNF144B and HK2 in MDD patients compared to controls (Fig. 8A-B). Furthermore, the expression levels of HK2 and RNF144B in peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) of MDD patients were significantly elevated compared to healthy controls as determined by qRT-PCR (Fig. 8C-D), suggesting a potential involvement of PANoptosis in MDD.

Discussion

MDD is widely recognized as one of the most pressing mental Health problems. The World Health Organization projects that depression will be the leading cause of the global burden of disease by 2030 [27]. MDD exhibits a complex etiology and demonstrates high comorbidity rates with numerous neuropsychiatric conditions [28], including schizophrenia [29], anxiety [30], pain [31], epilepsy [32] and cognitive impairment [33]. However, the exact pathogenesis of MDD remains unclear. This study established a novel PANoptosis-based risk prediction model for MDD through integrative bioinformatics approaches, which provided a new idea for exploring the pathological mechanism, diagnosis and treatment of MDD.

In recent years, the “inflammatory immune hypothesis” mechanism of depression has been recognized by more and more scientists [34]. This mechanism believes that depression is a psychological-neuro-immune disorder, where ROS-induced activation of NF-κB and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathways triggers inflammatory immune responses, culminating in cell death and depression [14]. NF-κB is a nuclear factor involved in the regulation of physiological processes such as immune response, cell growth and apoptosis [35]. Existing literature has confirmed that the activation of the classical NF-κB signaling pathway induces the innate immune system to produce proinflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and interleukin-1β, which reciprocally activate the classical NF-κB signaling pathway [36, 37]. Furthermore, the classical NF-κB signaling pathway mediates the activation of inflammasome to regulate pyroptosis [35]. The TNF signaling pathway regulates cell apoptosis, inflammation and immunity through NF-κB pathway activation, which was demonstrated by Hayden and Chen [38, 39]. PANoptosis is an inflammatory programmed cell death pathway regulated by the PANoptosome, which integrates key features of pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis [11, 12]. In this study, the GO functions of DE-PRGs in MDD patients were mainly enriched in immune process, inflammasome, insulin receptor substrate binding and TNF receptor binding involved in the apoptosis process, while their KEGG pathways were mainly enriched in TNF signaling pathway and NF-κB signaling pathway. These results supported that PANoptosis was involved in the process of immune dysregulation in MDD.

RNF144B (also known as PIR2), as a member of the RNF144 subfamily of RBR E3 ligases, is a downstream target of p53 and controls cancer cell death [40, 41]. According to the report, RNF144B is able to induce apoptosis by activation of pro-apoptotic targets and also regulate inflammatory responses [42–44]. Additionally, HK2 is a subtype of hexokinase and is a key regulator of glucose metabolism [45]. The study by Yaling Hu revealed that HK2 is able to regulate metabolic abnormalities and adaptive inflammation in brain diseases [46]. In addition, the upregulation of HK2 induces apoptosis in dopaminergic neurons [47]. SH2D1A, an X-linked SLAM-associated protein and a related marker of immune activation, is mainly expressed in immune cells and regulates the effector function of lymphocytes [48, 49]. Although no previous studies have confirmed the association between RNF144B, HK2 or SH2D1A and MDD, our results showed that RNF144B and HK2 were positively correlated with the MDD-related gene PTPN22. Notably, we observed significantly elevated expression levels of RNF144B and HK2 in MDD patients compared to controls. Therefore, the underlying mechanisms of RNF144B and HK2 in MDD need to be further explored.

TRAF1, mainly expressed in activated lymphocytes, is a key rate-limiting component in the TNF signaling pathway and a regulator of apoptotic signaling, which regulates the apoptosis of CD8+ T lymphocyte [50, 51]. Moreover, The relationship between TRAF1 levels and MDD molecular pathology was mentioned in Dean’s study [52]. TNFSF13 (also known as APRIL), a proliferation-inducing ligand of the tumor necrosis factor superfamily, participates in immunosuppression through a variety of immunomodulatory pathways and has been associated with immune inflammation [53]. Furthermore, Engh’s study demonstrated that plasma TNFSF13 levels were significantly reduced in patients with MDD compared with healthy controls [54]. Besides, ICAM-1 is one of the biomarkers for the diagnosis and treatment of MDD patients, which is involved in the apoptosis of fibroblast-like synoviocytes as well as in the regulation of inflammatory injury [55–57].

TLR2, as a type of Toll-like receptors, is a part of the innate immune system, which plays an important role in a variety of diseases such as infectious diseases, chronic and acute inflammatory diseases, and cancer [58]. Interestingly, TLR2/4-mediated signaling pathway induces the formation of inflammasome and subsequently activates PANoptosis, resulting in the death of inflammatory cells [59]. Moreover, TLR2 and ICAM1 are MDD-related genes, which are mainly enriched in the regulation of pathways mediating immunity and inflammation [60]. ADA is an essential enzyme for purine metabolism and promotes tumor development by suppressing immune response and PANoptosis [61]. ADA deficiency leads to immune deficiency and lung inflammation [62]. Additionally, ADA activity is used to monitor the response to antidepressant treatment and predict the prognosis of MDD, as demonstrated by Herken [63]. Collectively, these findings indicate that PKGs identified in our study (with the exception of SH2D1A) participate to varying extents in apoptotic processes and inflammatory immune responses. This evidence further supports the involvement of PANoptosis in the MDD immune dysregulation. Notably, existing literature has already established the roles of both TLR2 and ADA in MDD pathophysiology and PANoptosis regulation, which corroborates the validity of our diagnostic model.

STAT4, a member of the STAT family, is a key regulator of inflammation in the body [64, 65]. Several studies have shown that STAT4 is involved in the regulation of apoptosis and neuronal Pyroptosis [66, 67], which is a potential diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target for heart failure and depression [68]. SPP1 is associated with the expression of inflammatory markers [69] and is a downstream factor of the NF-κB signaling pathway [70]. Research by Scheepstra shows that SPP1 is one of MDD related genes [71]. Besides, PTPN22 is a member of the protein tyrosine phosphatase superfamily, which plays a key role in T cell signaling and immune response [72]. PTPN22 has been documented to be associated with the risk of depression [73, 74]. In our study, the MDD-related genes that were differentially expressed in cluster C1 and cluster C2 included STAT4, PTPN22 and SPP1, consistent with previous literature establishing their correlation with MDD. In addition, STAT4, PTPN22 and SPP1 were all associated with immunoinflammatory response, which was consistent with the immune disorder in MDD patients. Moreover, some of the MDD-related genes in this study have not been studied to verify their direct relationship with depression. For example, DNASE1 and DNASE1L3 are endonuclease enzymes associated with apoptosis and necrosis, which promotes the control of bacterial infections [75, 76]. ITGAM regulates the secretion of key inflammatory cytokines and significantly influences neuronal apoptosis [77]. These studies suggest that DNASE1, DNASE1L3 and ITGAM are associated with the occurrence of inflammatory response, but their relationship with MDD needs to be further verified.

Machine learning algorithms are superior to standard logistic regression in building accurate classification and prediction models of diseases [78]. In this study, the combination of LASSO regression and Random Forest algorithm was used to select the eight PKGs with the highest prediction accuracy: TRAF1, TNFSF13, TLR2, SH2D1A, RNF144B, ICAM1, HK2, ADA. Then, a PANoptosis risk score calculation model for MDD was established and the diagnostic prediction efficiency of the model was verified by ROC curve. Fortunately, the diagnostic model utilized in differentiating between MDD patients and healthy individuals demonstrated a degree of impact, thereby bolstering the reliability of our model. However, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, while our bioinformatics analyses identified promising PANoptosis-related biomarkers, the findings were derived from publicly available microarray datasets with inherent batch effects and platform-specific biases. Second, the sample sizes in validation cohorts (GSE76826 and GSE32280) were relatively small, which may limit the generalizability of our diagnostic model. Third, although we performed qRT-PCR validation for HK2 and RNF144B, experimental validation of all identified PKGs in larger clinical cohorts would strengthen our conclusions. Future functional investigations of these PKGs in MDD pathogenesis will facilitate development of new treatment approaches.

Conclusion

In this study, a combination of the Random Forest algorithm and LASSO regression analysis was utilized to identify 8 PKGs and develop a PANoptosis risk diagnosis model for MDD. Subsequently, a consensus cluster analysis algorithm was employed to categorize MDD into two PANoptosis subtypes. The findings indicated that RNF144B and HK2, key genes associated with PANoptosis, might promote neutrophil activation and exhibit significantly elevated expression in MDD patients. Additionally, MDD patients with distinct PANoptosis subtypes exhibited varying immune functions. Collectively, the evidence supported a potential role of PANoptosis in MDD-associated immune dysregulation, revealing new diagnostic and therapeutic opportunities for MDD.

The raw data for this bioinformatics analysis all came from online website database, RNAseq datasets (GSE98793, GSE76826 and GSE32280) were obtained from Gene Expression Omnibus (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), PANoptosis-related genes (PRGs) were retrieved from GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/). The data generated in this study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.

Synthesis, characterization, and adsorption performance of novel acidic calixarene-modified LDH/polyaniline nanohybrid for efficient Cr(VI) removal
Authors: Saied Shafiei Navid, Rahman Hosseinzadeh, Moslem Mansour Lakouraj
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractHerein, aniline was oxidatively polymerized in the presence of modified Mg-Al/LDH to prepare an acidic calix[4]arene modified Mg-Al/LDH-polyaniline based nanohybrid (MLDH-NH2@PANI). The prepared nanohybrid was studied using a variety of characterization techniques, including FT-IR, PXRD, TGA, FE-SEM, EDX-MAPP, and Zeta potential. To remove Cr(VI) from the aqueous solution, different parameters were examined, including pH, sorbent dosage, contact time, initial concentration of Cr(VI), and removal ability in the presence of interfering ions. The results showed that MLDH-NH2 co-existence improved the active sites in MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid and reduced the agglomeration of polymeric chains. The pseudo-second-order model provided a better fitting for the Cr(VI) adsorption onto MLDH-NH2@PANI, according to kinetic experiments. The highest adsorption capacity of Cr(VI) on MLDH-NH2@PANI was 420.0 mg /g at pH = 3.0, which was more than that of typical adsorbents. The isotherms were accurately reproduced using the Langmuir model. At pH = 3, It is proposed that the oxyanion forms of Cr(VI) (e.g., HCrO4−) can interact electrostatically with protonated amine groups and form hydrogen bonds with calix[4]arene moieties as a potential mode of contact with the adsorbent. Moreover, a portion of the adsorbed Cr(VI) undergoes reduction to Cr(III) through an adsorption-coupled reduction process facilitated by the PANI chains, and the resulting Cr(III) is subsequently chelated onto the nanohybrid. The reusability studies showed that the adsorbents could be reused up to 4 cycles with over 80% adsorption efficiency. These findings suggest that the MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid proved to be a good candidate for Cr(VI) removal from contaminated water.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-20369-z.Keywords: Polyaniline, Cr(VI) removal, Calix[4]arene, Layered double hydroxide, NanohybridSubject terms: Environmental chemistry, Organic chemistry

Introduction

Environmental pollution which is the production of harmful elements into the environment, causes many serious problems for human health, plants and other living organisms1–3. During recent years and with the development of various industries such as battery manufacturing, painting, mines, automobiles and metallurgical processes, many heavy metals have been released into the environment4–7. These heavy metals could enter the food chain and drinking water even in very low concentrations and cause many problems by accumulating in the tissues of living organisms8–10.

Among them, Cr(VI) is known as one of the most hazardous heavy metals due to its carcinogenicity, toxicity and high mobility11,12. Due to the diverse use of Cr(VI) as a pigment in dyes, paintings and plastics, its concentration has increased in wastewaters13. On the other hand, the lack of proper treatment of wastewater from various industries has resulted in Cr(VI) levels in the environment being significantly higher than acceptable standards14. The World Health Organization has stipulated that the maximum allowable concentration of Cr(VI) in water is 0.05 mg/L15. The presence of Cr(VI) is dangerous for the growth of plants, the health of animals and especially humans16. Chronic inhalation of Cr(VI)-based materials increases the risk of lung, nose and sinus cancers17. Severe dermatitis and usually painless skin ulcers can be caused by contact with such materials18. Considering the environmental risks mentioned for Cr(VI), it is necessary to take efficient procedures to eliminate it or minimize its concentration in polluted environments19.

To date, numerous studies have been published utilizing different adsorbents and methods to remove or minimize the amount of Cr(VI) in aqueous solutions as a model for the treatment of industrial wastewaters and contaminated environments20,21. So far, many materials such as activated carbon, graphene oxide, metal-organic frameworks, carbohydrates, layered double hydroxide (LDH), calix[4]arene (CA), and polyaniline (PANI) has been applied as sorbents in recent years for pollution removal such as Cr(VI)22–25. These sorbents have received much attention due to their high surface area, modifiability, heterogeneity, high adsorption capacity, simple use and recyclability. In 2021, Bahador et al. prepared a magnetic natural polymer-based activated carbon from Moringa oleifera and utilized it for Cr(VI) removal from aqueous solution with an adsorption capacity of 121.7 mg/g26. In 2021, Zhou et al. synthesized a Zr-based MOF which was modified with polypyrrole and showed a high adsorption capacity of 259.1 mg/g27. Also, in 2022, Zeng et al. synthesized calcinated form of Mg-Al/LDH with natural sources via a facile one-pot method and the results showed that the calcined form has higher adsorption capacity28.

Polyaniline (PANI) which is mainly synthesized through a chemical polymerization reaction from aniline monomer, is well-known for its low cost, high conductivity, stability, and ease of manufacture and processing29. So far, PANI as an intrinsically conductive polymer, has been used in various applications such as solar cells, batteries, supercapacitors, sensors, and corrosion-resistant coatings30. Due to the presence of amine groups and rich-electron nature, PANI has been one of the most efficient materials for removing pollutants from aqueous solutions especially Cr(VI)31.

LDHs or anionic clays are a group of inorganic materials that are widely used in drug delivery, sensor, extraction, improvement of polymer properties, and pollution removal due to their layered structure and interlayer space32,33. The layers are composed of positively charged metal oxides (usually magnesium and aluminum) that are neutralized by electrostatic interactions with interlayer anions (NO3−, CO32−, OH−, and etc.)34. The most important properties of LDHs is the ability of anion exchange, during which a larger anion is replaced by smaller ones, changing the properties of the modified LDH and improving its efficiency35. This type of inorganic nanolayers can be used purely or in the form of different hybrids in the removal of heavy metals or anions36. For example, Kim et al. synthesized a metal hexacyanoferrate-based LDH and utilized that for simultaneous removal of cesium (Qmax: 32 mg/g) and iodate (Qmax: 91 mg/g)37.

The popularity of adsorption method can be attributed to its ease of use and simplicity, but the sorption mechanism is primarily determined by the choice of adsorbent and its chelating or reactive functional groups, which have a significant impact on sorption process38,39. Polar functional groups, such as carboxyl, phosphonic, and sulphonic acid groups, remove adsorbates by chelation, hydrogen bond formation, and π-π stacking interactions40. The adsorption capacity of PANI could be increased by loading of such functional groups41. In this case, CA as a rigid and functionalizable supramolecule, has been proven to chelate the metal ions by coordination of carboxylic acid groups42. In our recent work, it is showed that the intercalation of carboxylic acid calixarene into the interlayer space of LDHs could simultaneously reduce the solubility of CA in various chemical environments and increase the adsorption capacity of guest species36.

As mentioned, we assumed that modification of PANI with calixarene and LDH, might increase the Cr(VI) removal efficiency due to increasing the active sites in the nanohybrid. In this work, a carboxylic acidic derivative of calixarene (ACA) was successfully intercalated into the Mg-Al/LDH to prepare MLDH. The prepared material was then aminated using APTES to synthesize the MLDH-NH2 nano-additive. The MLDH-NH2@PANI polymeric nanohybrid was obtained from an in-situ polymerization of aniline in the presence of MLDH-NH2 nanoparticles. The introduced nanohybrid was applied as a nanosorbent in the removal of hexavalent Cr(VI) from aqueous media. The various experimental parameters including pH effect, contact time, initial Cr(VI) concentration, selectivity, as well as kinetics and adsorption isotherms were investigated.

Experimental

Instruments, chemicals and batch experiments

All instruments, chemicals, and batch experiments are listed and explained in the SI file.

Synthesis of MLDH-NH2@PANI

The synthesis routes for ACA, MLDH, and MLDH-NH2 are explained in the SI file. To prepare the introduced nanohybrid, 0.2 g of MLDH-NH2 was poured into 30 mL of ethanol. Next, in order to prevent the agglomeration of these nanolayers, the mixture was exposed to ultrasonic waves for 30 min. Then, 0.5 mL of aniline (5.4 mmol) was added dropwise to the mixture and stirred for 2 h at room temperature. Afterward, a solution containing 1.0 g of ammonium persulfate (APS) (4.4 mmol) in 17 mL of 1 N HCl was added dropwise to the reaction mixture. The obtained mixture was stirred for 8 h at 3–5 °C. Finally, the MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid was obtained as a dark green mixture, which was separated by centrifugation at 7000 rpm. To remove the impurities, the nanohybrid was washed three times with methanol and water and then dried at 70 °C.

Results

Synthesis and characterization of MLDH-NH2@PANI

As it could be seen in Scheme 1, the ACA was firstly intercalated into the basal space of LDH in alkaline condition and after that these nanolayers were aminated with APTES. The MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid was synthesized via in-situ polymerization of aniline, utilizing MLDH-NH2 as a nano-additive and APS as a radical initiator during the polymerization process.

The preparation route of MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid.

Figure S1 shows the FT-IR spectra of PANI, MLDH-NH2, and MLDH-NH2@PANI. The characteristic absorption bands of PANI and MLDH-NH2 appear in the range of 650–1700 cm−1 and 2800–3600 cm−1, respectively. The presence of these absorption bands in the FT-IR spectrum of the synthesized nanohybrid confirms that both the PANI and MLDH-NH2 components coexist within the structure of the prepared nanohybrid.

Figure1 depicts the PXRD pattern of MLDH, MLDH-NH2 and MLDH-NH2@PANI samples. The intercalation of modifiers into the interlayer space of LDH caused a shift of the basal peak (d003) to lower positions, indicating the successful preparation of MLDH. When the surface of LDH is modified, the intensity of some peaks decreases36. This is clearly visible in the diffraction pattern of MLDH-NH2. Also, the appearance of d003 peak and its coincidence with the d003 peak in the MLDH sample, proves that the modifier molecules remained between the layers during the amination reaction. In addition, the crystal structure of LDH has been preserved during the surface modification reaction, which is due to the proximity of the corresponding peaks in the PXRD pattern. For the MLDH-NH2@PANI, the observed broad peaks are related to semicrystalline PANI segments and the LDH (110) plates. The removal of the basal peak of LDH indicates partial lamination of LDH in the nanohybrid structure. These results show that MLDH-NH2 nano-additive is situated in the polyaniline matrix.

The PXRD diffraction pattern of MLDH, MLDH-NH2 and MLDH-NH2@PANI.

The TGAfor PANI, MLDH and nanohybrid in nitrogen atmosphere are shown in Fig. 2. According to the obtained results, the first step of weight loss occurs at 200 °C. For the MLDH sample, this weight loss is related to physically absorbed water between the layers. The weight loss observed at > 300 °C is related to the destruction of ACA anion between the layers and the dehydroxylation of brucite layers in LDH. The weight loss at > 600 °C (the third step) can be attributed to the destruction of the sheets and their transformation into the corresponding metal oxides36. The TGA curve of PANI shows that the weight loss up to 200 °C corresponds to the evaporation of entrapped water molecules, acidic impurity and other solvents remaining in the polymeric matrix. The weight loss observed at higher temperatures also indicates the degradation of the PANI polymer chain and its carbonization. As can be seen in the TGA curve of MLDH-NH2@PANI, due to the interactions between the functional groups in the polymer and the modified MLDH nanolayers, a lower initial weight loss is observed and degradation begins at higher temperatures. However, weight loss at < 200 °C is due to the release of physically absorbed water and other volatile impurities. The second step of weight loss is also related to the dehydroxylation of LDH brucite layers and the destruction of the main chain of the PANI. The final char yield of the nanohybrid (53%) shows that there is a good compatibility between the inorganic modifier nanolayers and the PANI polymer. These results approved that by adding the modifier to PANI matrix, the thermal stability of the MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid is also improved43.

The TGA curves of MLDH, MLDH-NH2 and MLDH-NH2@PANI with a heating rate of 20 °C/min.

To assay the morphology of the prepared nanohybrid, the FE-SEM images were analyzed, which are shown in Fig. 3. The SEM images of PANI showed a hierarchical structure which is composed of coral-shape dendritic nanostructure44. Also, polymer chains with irregular placement on each other have caused the formation of holes. For MLDH-NH2, the layering of brucite plates in LDH is determined. Since the surface of these plates are aminated, its hexagonal structure is not clearly visible44. Furthermore, the images of nanohybrid show that polymer chains are adjacent in two ways. Some of these chains have kept their nano-rod structure and the rest of them have penetrated between the LDH sheets and caused the LDH sheets to become laminated. It can also be concluded that the polymerization process took place on the surface of the LDH layers. The results of EDX-MAPP for MLDH-NH2 and MLDH-NH2@PANI are also shown in Fig. S2 and represented in Table S1. In the images of MLDH-NH2, the simultaneous presence of Mg and Al elements as well as N and Si elements indicates the successful surface modification of LDH layers. The noticeable decrease in the percentage of Si, Mg and Al elements is due to the lower weight value of the modifier nanolayers in the nanohybrid structure. Also, the amount of N elements has increased in the images of nanohybrid due to the simultaneous proximity of N atoms in the structure of the MLDH-NH2 and PANI matrix (Table S1). On the other hand, the proper distribution of all elements indicates the absence of clumping of nanolayers in the nanohybrid structure, which is also consistent with the FE-SEM images.

Cr(VI) removal results

Polyaniline is a conductive polymer known for its strong ability to adsorb anionic/cationic adsorbates through non-covalent interactions45. The combination of carboxylic acid derivative of calixarene and LDH nano layers and utilizing it as a nano-additive into the PANI matrix has been assumed that have a synergistic effect to enhance Cr(VI) removal from aqueous solution. This is due to the chelation ability of ACA and LDH’s capacity for anion exchange. To determine the optimal conditions, various parameters affecting the removal efficiency of Cr(VI) were investigated.

Zeta potential

According to the reported studies on using PANI for Cr(VI) removal, the pH and the amount of positive charge generated on the sorbent surface are the most critical factors in optimizing the method’s efficiency. For this reason, the zeta potential test was performed for the nanohybrid in a wide range of pH (3 to 10), which is depicted in Fig. S3. According to the results, the value of pHPZC for the nanohybrid is around 6. This shows that at pHs > 6, the nanohybrid surface will have a negative charge, and at pHs < 6, the absorbent will have a positive charge. Based on the reported literature to analyze the distribution of Cr(VI) species, the introduced nanohybrid is expected to exhibit higher efficiency in acidic environments which is in consistence with zeta potential findings. These results indicate that up to a pH = 6, the predominant form of Cr(VI) is the HCrO4− anion46.

pH effect

Figure 4 illustrates the effects of pH on Cr(VI) removal efficiency in the pH range of 3–10. These graphs present both the residual Cr(VI) concentration (Ce) and the removal efficiency percentage (RE%) simultaneously. As it can be seen, the removal rate for the nanohybrid is much higher than MLDH-NH2, which indicates the proper interactions between the sorbent and HCrO4− anion and the synergistic effect of PANI and MLDH-NH2. The obtained results also show that in acidic pH, both sorbents have higher efficiency. The higher removal rate of MLDH-NH2@PANI is attributed to the electrostatic interactions between the positively charged nitrogen atoms and the negatively charged anions, as well as the hydrogen bonding of the carboxylic acid and phenol groups of calixarene with the HCrO4− anion. Additionally, LDH contributes to increased Cr(VI) removal by trapping anions within its interlayer spaces through ion exchange reaction. Based on the obtained results, the optimal pH was adjusted to 3 to reach the maximum removal efficiency.

The pH effect on Cr(VI) removal using (a) MLDH-NH2 and (b) MLDH-NH2@PANI (5 mg of sorbents at RT for 100 mL of 1.0 mg/L Cr(VI) solution).

Sorbent dosage

The sorbent dosage is one of the most significant factors that significantly influences the removal efficiency. Because there are more active sites and chelating groups, the removal efficiency of Cr(VI) is often higher when a higher sorbent dose is applied. However, it should be remembered that too much sorbent is not economically feasible in this situation, so it should not be used. In this context, 50 mL of Cr(VI) solution (50 mg/L) at pH = 3.0 was treated with several concentrations of MLDH-NH2 and MLDH-NH2@PANI. The collected data is summarized in Table S2 according to each sorbent’s RE (%) and dosage (mg). The optimal amount of sorbent was determined to be 10.0 mg based on the data obtained, which showed no discernible difference when the sorbent dosage was increased from 10.0 mg to 15.0 mg for the sorbent.

Contact time

The contact time of the sorbent and the investigated species is also another important factor. The results obtained for MLDH-NH2 and MLDH-NH2@PANI are shown in Fig. 5. This diagram illustrates Qt values ​​in terms of interaction time on both sorbents. Based on the data related to the removal of HCrO4− after 800 min, the maximum amount of Qt for MLDH-NH2 and MLDH-NH2@PANI was calculated to be 150.7 mg/g and 205.3 mg/g, respectively. The higher Qt value for MLDH-NH2@PANI can be attributed to effective interactions of HCrO4− anions with the functional groups in both MLDH-NH2 and PANI matrix. Additionally, the graph indicates that after 200 min, the removal efficiency remained relatively stable. On the other hand, the removal kinetic is higher at the beginning of the reaction, which is due to the presence of accessible and absorbing active sites44. According to Fig. 5, the initial removal rate for MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid is much higher than MLDH-NH2 up to 30 min. This observation shows that both the speed and the adsorbent capacity of MLDH-NH2@PANI were higher than MLDH-NH2. According to these results, the optimal contact time was considered to be 200 min.

Effect of contact time on Cr(VI) removal efficiency for MLDH-NH2 and MLDH-NH2@PANI in 800 min at pH = 3 and RT (10 mg of sorbents was added to a 100 mL of 50 mg/L solution).

Investigation of kinetic parameters

To better understand the removal kinetics of Cr(VI) on MLDH-NH₂ and MLDH-NH₂@PANI sorbents, the results of contact time (Fig. 5) were analyzed using commonly applied kinetic models: pseudo-first-order (PFO) and pseudo-second-order (PSO) models. The fitting results of PFO and PSO models were shown in Figs. S4 and S5 (a and b), respectively, and the kinetic model parameters obtained were summarized in Table S3. As can be seen in Figures S4 and S5, the experimental data fit well to the PSO model, giving a linear plot with a high correlation coefficient (R2). The R2 values ​​ for MLDH-NH2 and MLDH-NH2@PANI sorbents (0.9940 and 0.9998, respectively) were higher in the PSO model than the PFO model (Table S3), indicating that the pseudo-second-order model was the most suitable in describing the removal kinetics of Cr(VI). A comparison between the experimental (Qe, exp) and calculated (Qe, cal) removal values showed that Qe, cal obtained from the PSO model (207.03 mg/g) closely aligns with Qe, exp (205.2 mg/g). Additionally, both Qe, exp and Qe, cal values for MLDH-NH2@PANI are higher than those for MLDH-NH2 in both models, likely due to the enhanced interactions of the sorbent with HCrO4− anion. The obtained data show that the Cr(VI) removal process is mostly carried out through the chemical reaction for both MLDH-NH2 and MLDH-NH2@PANI. The higher value of K2 for MLDH-NH2@PANI (0.0352) compared to the value of K2 for MLDH-NH2 (0.0106) shows that the Cr(VI) removal was much faster in the case of MLDH-NH2@PANI.

To study if the adsorption is surface-limited or diffusion-controlled, the intraparticle diffusion model (IPD) was also applied (Figs. S4-c and S5-c). The obtained results showed that MLDH-NH2 had a higher Kid​ (3.6) than MLDH-NH2@PANI (1.81), while the C value was higher for MLDH-NH2@PANI compared to MLDH-NH2 (139.9 and 43.7, respectively). Regarding the R2 values for MLDH-NH2@PANI and MLDH-NH2 (0.8197 and 0.9078, respectively), it was found that the IPD is involved but not as the only rate-controlling step (Table S3). These findings propose that though diffusion into the sorbent’s active sites contributes to the Cr(VI) removal mechanism, the general process is mainly controlled by surface chemisorption, particularly for the MLDH-NH2@PANI hybrid, where the simultaneous existence of PANI improves surface interactions such as hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interaction. Therefore, the overall kinetic experiments suggest that Cr(VI) removal includes both the diffusion and surface sorption processes, where chemisorption is the dominant rate-limiting process.

Adsorption isotherms

To gain a deeper understanding of the interaction between the sorbents (MLDH-NH2 and MLDH-NH2@@PANI) and the Cr(VI) ions, the Langmuir and Freundlich isotherms were studied. As previously mentioned, various concentrations within the range of 20 to 150 mg/L were investigated under optimal conditions (pH = 3.0 and 200 min). The effect of increasing concentration on the maximum adsorption rate was investigated and the results are shown in Fig. S6. Examining of Qe in terms of Ce revealed that as the initial concentration of Cr(VI) ions increases, the equilibrium adsorption capacity also increases, reaching a maximum value of 418 mg/g.

Figure S7 shows the Langmuir and Freundlich isotherm diagrams and Table S4 tabulates the related parameters for the interaction of MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid with Cr(VI) ions. With an R2 value greater than 0.9941, the Langmuir model demonstrates a better fit with the results, indicating that monolayer adsorption and chemisorption are the dominant mechanisms in the adsorption process47. The value of RL factor was also calculated as 0.544, which indicates that the Langmuir model is suitable for modeling this adsorption. As a comparison for the correctness of the used formulas, Dinari et al. prepared a PANI/LDH nanocomposite for Pb(II) removal from aqueous solution. The result of this study showed that Pb(II) removal data was best fitted by the PSO model and the RL value was reported (from Langmuir isotherm) to be 0.41 which indicated the removal process is favorable and the PSO model is more appropriate48. The value of Qmax obtained from the Langmuir model was also reported as 420 mg/g. According to the kinetic experiments and isotherm models investigation, the adsorption rate is very high at the beginning of the interaction and gradually decreases until it reaches a constant value due to the reduction of active centers49. No increase in values of Qe or Qt (between 200 and 800 min) could be explained by decreasing in the number of active sites. After 200 min, the removal should take place inside the porosity of the sorbent, and this requires more time for the desired species to penetrate the sorbent and reach the residual available active sites49.Table 1 tabulates the comparison of Qmax values of some absorbents for Cr(VI) removal from the aqueous solutions reported in the literatures. As can be seen, the introduced MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid achieves high removal efficiency with a lower sorbent amount compared to some other cases. These results suggest that MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid could be an effective sorbent for Cr(VI) removal from drinking or underground waters.

Cr(VI) removal efficiency in the presence of interfering ions

Cr(VI) ion is usually coexist with other cations in wastewaters55. The removal of Cr(VI) in the presence of other metal cations (Pb2+, K+, Al3+, Cu2+, Ca2+, Mg2+, etc.) using polyaniline and/or LDH-based hybrids has been extensively studied so far, demonstrating the potential of amine-group-containing and LDH supports for Cr(VI) removal41,44,46,49,53,56. So, one of the most important features of a sorbent must be the ability to adsorb the target analyte (metal cations, dyes, etc.) in the presence of interfering analytes. In this regard, the ability of the prepared MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid for the removal of Cr(VI) in the presence of 1, 10 and 100-fold excess of interfering cations was investigated. It has been proven that the chelation ability of carboxylic acid groups results in metal cations removal40. In this regard, the quantified amount of Mg2+, Ca2+, Na+ and Li+ metal salts were prepared in the presence of Cr(VI) and the removal process was studied (Fig. 6). The obtained data indicated that in the same concentrations, the presence of interfering ions did not impact the removal efficiency. Also, when the concentration of interfering ions was 100-fold with respect to Cr(VI) ion, the efficiency of the MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid decreased by only < 10%. These results show that the ability of the introduced nanohybrid is not affected by the presence of interfering ions and the prepared nanohybrid is a potential and practical material for the removal of Cr(VI) ions.

Reusability of MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid

The recoverability and reusability of sorbents are among its most crucial characteristics. In this context, the synthesized nanohybrid’s reusability was examined, and the outcome is displayed in Fig. S8. After four cycles, the MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid still exhibits high efficiency, however the removal efficiency has dropped by 18.9%, according to the collected data. The loss of the active sites and possibly modest structural damage might be interpreted as this decrement. It can be concluded that the produced nanohybrid is a promising sorbent with appropriate reusability despite the decrease in removal efficiency after four cycles.

The plausible Cr(VI) removal mechanism

Scheme2 illustrates the plausible Cr(VI) removal mechanism using MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid at pH= 3. It has been proven that in an acidic medium, the predominant forms of Cr(VI) are HCrO4- and Cr2O72- anions. The maximum amount of these anions can be captured via the positively charged PANI (amine/imine groups) and MLDH-NH2 (aminosilane groups) at pH= 3 through the electrostatic interactions [44]. Furthermore, the incorporation of the carboxylic acid calix[4]arene into LDH contributes to Cr(VI) removal through the hydrogen bond formation between its COOH/OH groups and oxygen groups of HCrO4- anions [57]. Also, the anions can be partially trapped by the interlayer space of LDH through the anion exchange reaction via the electrostatic interactions [58]. According to the reported literature, PANI chains can function as electron donors, facilitating the partial reduction of adsorbed Cr(VI) to Cr(III) species in acidic condition, owing to the high redox potential of HCrO4⁻ [59]. To investigate this possible mechanism, the solution before and after Cr(VI) adsorption was analyzed using UV-Vis spectroscopy, as shown in Fig. S9. After the adsorption process, the Cr(VI) concentration (monitored at 350 nm) significantly decreased, while a small amount of Cr(III) (monitored at 530 nm) was detected [60]. To further confirm the proposed adsorption-coupled reduction mechanism, the UV-Vis spectrum of the solution after the desorption process was recorded. The results clearly revealed the simultaneous and significant presence of both Cr(VI) and Cr(III) species. This observation supports the conclusion that a portion of the adsorbed Cr(VI) anions was reduced to Cr(III) through interaction with the PANI chains. Then, both the imine/amine (PANI) and COOH/OH groups (calixarene and LDH) act as active sites to chelate the Cr(III) ions [31]. Based on the obtained results, the plausible removal mechanism is driven by electrostatic and hydrogen bond interaction, ion exchange, and adsorption-coupled reduction of Cr(VI) species. These findings suggest that the simultaneous roles of MLDH-NH2@PANI’s components contribute to efficient Cr(VI) removal from aqueous solutions, making it a promising adsorbent material.

Conclusion

In this study, a carboxylic acid derivative of calix[4]arene was synthesized and intercalated to the interlayer space of Mg-Al/LDH through an anion exchange reaction. The modified LDH was then aminated using APTES to protect the crystal structure in the acidic medium. The MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid was prepared through an in-situ polymerization of aniline monomer using the MLDH-NH2 as nano-additive. The FT-IR, PXRD, TGA, FE-SEM, and EDS-MAPP analyses utilized to prove the successful preparation of MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid. The as prepared nanohybrid was then applied as a new nanosorbent for Cr(VI) removal and high removal efficiency and adsorption capacity were obtained at the optimal conditions. The kinetic experiments and isotherm models investigation revealed that the PSO model and Langmuir isotherm model had the best fitting with the obtained data with an R2 > 0.99. The Cr(VI) removal ability of the introduced nanohybrid showed that even in the presence of the interfering ions, the Cr(VI) removal efficiency does not change considerably. Based on these results, it could be concluded that the MLDH-NH2@PANI nanohybrid is a promising nanosorbent with high removal efficiency towards Cr(VI) even in the presence of interfering ions due to the synergistic effects of the multifunctional groups in LDH, ACA, and PANI.

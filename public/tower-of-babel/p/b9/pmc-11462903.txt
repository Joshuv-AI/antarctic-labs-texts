Assessing public trust in road traffic injuries prevention policies in Iran: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Esmail Ezzati, Mohammad Saadati, Deniz Naghibi, Mehran Sardareh, Reza Ali Akbari Khoei, Saber Azami-Aghdash
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction and objectiveRoad Traffic Injuries (RTIs) represent the predominant cause of morbidity and mortality globally. Thus, impactful interventions and trustworthy authorities are imperative to mitigate traffic accidents. The present study seeks to assess public trust in traffic accident diminution policies and measures within the country of Iran.MethodThis cross-sectional study utilized a questionnaire comprising 31 questions distributed across eight dimensions. The questionnaire’s design was informed by a literature review and semi-structured interviews with experts specializing in traffic accidents. The target population consisted of Tabriz city residents, who were selected via convenience sampling. The collected data were then analyzed using SPSS-16 software, employing the T-test, One-way ANOVA, and Pearson correlation coefficient to present the study’s results. structural equation modeling (SEM) was conducted using R 4-4-2 programming language.ResultsA total of 681 participants completed the questionnaire, yielding a commendable response rate of 88.6%. The majority of participants were male (60.8%), and 86.2% identified as drivers, with 61.8% having experienced driving accidents. The overall score for public trust in RTIs policies was calculated to be 46.9%. The individual scores for specific domains of trust in traffic safety were as follows: safer roads (43.1%), safe vehicle (43.3%), safety laws (48.8%), safe user (51.1%), safe technology (51.5%), road safety management (46.8%). All the latent variables except education are significantly effective on public trust.ConclusionThe study findings indicate relatively low levels of public trust in RTIs policies among the Iranian population. These results emphasize the need for targeted interventions to enhance public confidence in specific aspects of traffic safety. Policymakers can use these insights to implement effective measures, thus contributing to the RTIs and the promotion of road safety in the country.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-024-20231-3.Keywords: Assessment, Public trust, Road traffic injuries, Prevention, Policy

Introduction

Traffic accidents are now considered the primary cause of disability and mortality worldwide [1]. Annually, an estimated 1.35 million lives are tragically lost, and more than 50 million individuals sustain injuries as a result of traffic accidents on a global scale [2]. Forecasts indicate that these figures are poised to escalate by approximately 65% over the forthcoming two decades [3]. In Iran, traffic accidents assume the primary responsibility for inflicting injuries and stand as the second most significant contributor to mortality, highlighting their critical impact on public health and safety [4, 5]. It is estimated that traffic accidents exact a heavy toll, amounting to the loss of 2,271 years of life and causing economic damages surpassing 120 million dollars each year in Iran [6]. Based on data from the Iranian Forensic Medicine Organization, responsible for compiling statistics on accidents-related deaths, it is evident that fatalities resulting from traffic accidents exhibited a downward trajectory since 2005. However, the available statistics reveal a concerning development: the rates of both deaths and injuries attributable to traffic accidents have been on an upward trajectory since 2018, signifying a potential reversal in the previously declining trend [7].

The prevention of traffic accidents necessitates a multifaceted and integrated approach, which encompasses various measures aimed at enhancing safety on the roadways. This holistic strategy comprises enhancing and regulating infrastructure, bolstering the safety features of vehicles, enforcing stringent traffic laws, instating dynamic and adaptive programs, establishing high-quality healthcare and hospital services, providing robust social welfare support for children, and implementing innovative urban and environmental initiatives [8, 9]. In recent years, akin to numerous developing countries, Iran has undertaken the formulation and execution of a plethora of interventions and programs with the overarching goal of mitigating the frequency and ramifications of traffic accidents [10–14].

The interventions and policies to reduce traffic accidents have notably emphasized the pivotal role of cultural and social considerations in their effectiveness [15]. Among these, trust emerges as a crucial social asset, forming the bedrock of various daily interactions and actions in human societies, be it in interpersonal relationships or group dynamics. The erosion of trust can impose significant costs on a country’s political and social fabric [16]. Recognizing the importance of trust in shaping societal attitudes towards interventions and the performance of relevant officials, it becomes imperative to measure this aspect [17].

In the healthcare domain, studies have been conducted over the years to gauge public trust in interventions and performance across various sectors [18, 19]. For instance, Tabrizi et al. (2014) conducted a study among 1050 families in Tabriz city, revealing that people’s trust in the health system stood at 57%, with the highest level of trust observed in professional expertise and the lowest in macro-level policies. Consequently, it was concluded that public trust in health services remained relatively low in Tabriz, underscoring the need for policymakers to devise appropriate measures to enhance patients’ experiences with health services [19]. Furthermore, Shaygan and Jarabani (2011) found a positive correlation between people’s satisfaction with security quality of life and their trust in the police force [18]. These insights highlight the significance of public trust in influencing the success and acceptance of interventions, underscoring the need to cultivate and sustain trust as a fundamental component of traffic accident prevention efforts in Iran and beyond.

In the field of traffic accident prevention, based on the results of an exhaustive literature review and researchers’ experiences, no published study has specifically examined the measurement of public trust in policies and programs targeting the reduction of traffic accidents. However, given the potential significance of public trust in the effectiveness of interventions and the performance of relevant officials in this context, it is evident that assessing the level of trust can have a substantial and influential impact on accident prevention efforts. Therefore, the present study aims to measure public trust in policies and actions to reduce traffic accidents in Iran.

Method

The present study is a cross-sectional study conducted in 2022 in Tabriz city. This study was conducted in Tabriz, the capital of East Azerbaijan province in the northwest of Iran. The area of ​​this city is 245 square kilometers and with a population of about 1.8 million people, it is the fourth largest city in Iran. Like other big cities in Iran, it has experienced rapid urban development in the past decades. Tabriz is the largest economic center and metropolis in northwest Iran [20].

This high concentration of socio-economic activities is an effective factor in the traffic congestion on the highways and inner streets of Tabriz. According to the traffic police of Tabriz, the number of deaths caused by traffic accidents in this city has always been high. Additionally, according to the statistics of the Forensic Medicine Organization, 16,778 people died in road accidents and 317,120 people were injured in the year 2021, of which 855 people died in the province of East Azerbaijan and the number of injured people due to driving accidents in this province has been reported 15,644 people [21].

The target population of the study consisted of drivers residing in Tabriz. The inclusion criteria were being a resident of Tabriz, having a driver’s license, and willingness to participate in the study. To achieve a statistically robust and representative sample size, the researchers employed Cochran’s formula to determine the initial required sample of 384 individuals. Recognizing the importance of bolstering statistical power and mitigating potential attrition effects inherent in cross-sectional studies, this number was subsequently doubled, bringing the final cohort size to 768 individuals.

A data collection tool was meticulously devised in the form of a researcher-made questionnaire(a supplementary file), encompassing 31 Likert-scale items distributed across 6 dimensions. The development process commenced with a judicious literature review on traffic accidents, as well as the policies and measures enacted to reduce such incidents, after establishing a definition for public trust in measures and policies aimed at reducing traffic accidents. Following the conceptual foundation’s establishment, the questionnaire’s items, dimensions, and areas of inquiry were refined, preparing the groundwork for in-depth semi-structured interviews with esteemed subject matter experts and stakeholders. The interviews, conducted at a convenient location for participants, involved 12 distinguished faculty members from Tabriz University of Medical Sciences, accomplished PhD students specialized in epidemiology and healthcare management, and domain experts keen on participating in the study. To ensure ethical compliance, explicit verbal consent was obtained from all interviewees before utilizing an audio recording device to record the interview conversations. Confidentiality and anonymity were steadfastly upheld to safeguard the integrity of the interview content. Each interview session spanned an average duration of 90 to 120 min, fostering extensive and illuminating discussions. The sampling methodology employed was purpose-based, selecting individuals capable of offering rich and pertinent insights. The iterative sampling process continued until the point of data saturation, where no new information emerged.

The criteria employed for the selection of interviewees encompassed the following parameters: (1) Occupying a role associated with the management and mitigation of traffic accidents; (2) Demonstrating substantial proficiency and erudition in the domain of traffic accidents, substantiated by publications such as books, articles, and reports concerning this subject matter; (3) Manifesting a willingness and capacity to actively partake in the study’s proceedings.

In the pursuit of analyzing the data garnered from the conducted interviews, a content analysis approach was adopted. This methodology serves as a recognized technique within the realm of qualitative data analysis, facilitating the identification, analysis, and reporting of discernible patterns or themes inherent within the textual material. This method is widely used in qualitative data analysis and allows for a detailed and systematic exploration of the data.

The initial assessment tool comprised 50 questions distributed across 8 distinct dimensions, utilizing a 5-point Likert scale for responses. Subsequently, the content validity and face validity of the devised tool were meticulously evaluated. Face validity was ascertained through a qualitative approach, involving the solicitation of feedback from a cohort of 10 experts and stakeholders who were provided access to the initial tool. Notably, the questionnaire was returned by 8 of these individuals, and their invaluable insights were harnessed to rectify any identified shortcomings. The quantification of content validity encompassed the utilization of the Content Validity Ratio (CVR) and the Content Validity Index (CVI). The calculation of CVR necessitated the appraisal of each item’s examination significance by all panel members. In tandem, the determination of CVI entailed individual evaluations by panel members, wherein they gauged each item’s simplicity, relevance, necessity, and clarity, utilizing a 4-point Likert scale. Upon meticulous assessment, the number of questions within the designed tool was prudently reduced to 44, maintaining its distribution across the 8 dimensions.(Table 1).

In the third phase, the assessment of internal reliability hinged on two distinct methodologies: internal consistency, measured through Cronbach’s alpha coefficient, and the intra-class correlation coefficient, which gauges the test’s repeatability. The endeavor to gauge the internal consistency of the questionnaire involved the application of Cronbach’s alpha, where a cohort of 30 eligible participants was thoughtfully selected over a span of two weeks. Meanwhile, the test-retest method was employed to meticulously ascertain the questionnaire’s reliability. At the end of this stage, the number of designed tool questions was reduced to 34 and its distribution was still maintained in 8 dimensions.

In the fourth phase, an exploratory factor analysis was executed to glean deeper insights. As the research instrument was custom-designed for this study, the employment of exploratory factor analysis was judicious to elucidate the inter-correlation among items. Within this analysis, the default Principal Components method was adopted. The outcomes of this exploratory factor analysis yielded four key outputs, which were aptly scrutinized. Employing Varimax rotation facilitated the extraction of questionnaire factors, aiding in the disentanglement and elucidation of the underlying factors. Finally, the number of questions was reduced to 31 questions, distributed across 6 thematic areas: Safer roads (8 questions), Safe vehicle (5 questions), Safety laws (3 questions), Safe user (6 questions), Safety technology (4 questions), Road safety Management (5questions). Respondents partaking in the study expressed their perspectives using a five-tiered response scale: strongly agree, agree, neutral, disagree, and strongly disagree. The number of respondents in this stage was 681 people who were selected based on the variables of entering the study. After the participants completed the questionnaire, a score for each question was determined. We calculated this by dividing the number of people who chose each of the 5 options based on the Likert scale for each question by the total number of respondents (681). Then, we converted the resulting ratio into a percentage to determine the score for each question across all 5 options. The suitability of the sample size was assessed using the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) index, while the correlation among variables was scrutinized through Bartlett’s test. An eigenvalue surpassing 1 was deemed pivotal for ascertaining factors. Comprehensive findings, along with intricate insights into the study’s methodology, validity, and reliability, will be presented in a forthcoming publication.

Explicit informed consent was conscientiously acquired from all participants involved in this study, affording them the prerogative to disengage from the study at any juncture. These participants were also provided with the unwavering assurance of confidentiality and the preservation of their anonymity concerning their provided information. Ethical approval on 03/08/2022 from the Ethics Committee of Tabriz University of Medical Sciences (IR.TBZMED.REC.1398.1030). Requested and received. All participants were informed that their participation was voluntary and that their refusal would not result in any sanctions, that they could leave the study at any time without giving a reason, that no part of their information could be traced and that all their information could be No tracking. provided will be treated as confidential.in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Descriptive statistical techniques, encompassing measures such as mean, frequency, and percentage, were employed for data analysis. Additionally, inferential methods, including Chi-square, T-test, and One-way ANOVA, were utilized. Pearson’s correlation coefficient was applied to scrutinize inter-variable associations. These statistical computations were executed through SPSS software version 16. The adopted significance level for statistical tests was 0.05, adhering to conventions prevalent in social science research.

To evaluate the relations of latent factors among each other and to have an estimation for the grade of public trust, structural equation modeling (SEM) was conducted. As well to evaluate the effects of risk factors on the total grade of public trust, linear regression was fitted using a stepwise algorithm. Both methods were performed using R 4-4-2 programming language.

The study cohort exhibited a male predominance, comprising 60% of the participants, while 59% of the respondents identified as married. Notably, 86% of the participants, amounting to 587 individuals, held the role of drivers, with a significant portion of them (72%) engaging in urban driving. A substantial number of respondents, 421 individuals (61%), reported prior involvement in traffic accidents (Table 2).

Discussion

The outcomes of the assessment of public trust through a specially designed questionnaire revealed an overall score of 46.9% concerning policies aimed at mitigating traffic accidents. Notably, the highest score was associated with the safety technology domain, recording a score of 51.5%, whereas the lowest score pertained to the safer roads domain, with a score of 43.1%. Statistical analyses have indicated a significant correlation between gender, marital status, occupation, history of traffic accidents, education, age, and the overarching score of public trust in governmental strategies directed at RTIs.

This study underscored the observation that public trust in measures designed to curtail traffic accidents is relatively low. Trust, a complex psychological construct, has been the subject of various conceptualizations and operationalizations. One of the most frequently cited definitions characterizes trust as the willingness of one party to expose themselves to the actions of another party based on the anticipation that the other party will undertake a particular action, irrespective of their ability to oversee or control the latter [22]. A multitude of research endeavors have delved into the realm of trust. According to Tabrizi and colleagues’ investigation in Tabriz City in 2016, individuals exhibited greater trust in specialists, while having less trust in broader health system policies. Healthcare providers such as specialists, pharmacists, and nurses garnered the highest levels of trust. Interestingly, their study also identified a notable relationship between public trust and the education level of the household head, which aligns with our findings. However, the Tabrizi study did not establish a significant connection between occupation and public trust [19]. In the study conducted by Ploner and colleagues in 2019, exploring strategies to foster trust in Germany’s healthcare system, it was deduced that innovative technological advancements can serve as effective mechanisms for cultivating trust between patients and healthcare systems, particularly among healthcare professionals. Based on their research, tools such as public infrastructure utilization, widespread smartphone accessibility, privacy safeguards, and collaborative capabilities among diverse healthcare centers can be leveraged to create heightened and more impactful levels of trust within the patient-healthcare relationship [23]. Meng and colleagues’ examination in 2020 delved into trust and willingness to pay for traditional Chinese medicine within Chinese healthcare centers. Their findings revealed that individuals with elevated income and educational levels expressed greater trust in modern medicine, while those with lower income levels exhibited higher trust in traditional Chinese medicine [24]. The findings in the present study align with the outcomes of previous research concerning the correlation between education and overall trust in both government institutions and medical services. Notably, Souri and colleagues demonstrated that police initiatives encompassing the enforcement of seatbelt usage regulations for drivers and passengers, the imposition of helmet requirements for motorcyclists, along the implementation of educational campaigns and regulations, have contributed to a reduction in RTIs. This underscores the significance of implementing educational programs and fostering cultural awareness, which can collectively result in a decrease in traffic accidents [25].

Addressing RTIs necessitates a comprehensive evidence-based approach that heavily relies on public trust. Although there exists no direct empirical evidence linking public trust to accident prevention, it stands to reason that bolstering trust in safety measures and regulations can foster greater adherence, ultimately leading to a reduction in accidents [26]. However, the degree of public trust in policies aimed at reducing driving accidents could be contingent upon the specific policies themselves and the regional contexts in which they are implemented. Certain policies might prove more effective than others, while some might be perceived as intrusive or unwelcome by the public. Furthermore, uncertainties could arise regarding policymakers’ capability to enforce these regulations.

Nevertheless, a common understanding prevails about the importance of diminishing driving accidents, with a willingness to endorse evidence-based policies that have demonstrated successful outcomes. Consequently, the establishment of an organization-appointed group or committee becomes imperative to guide and harmonize requisite actions at both national and provincial levels, aiming to elevate road safety standards.

The results of this study indicate that the field of ​​road safety has registered the lowest score among different aspects of public trust with 43.1%. These observations emphasize the lack of public confidence in road safety, and hence the need to take corrective measures to strengthen public confidence in road safety, as well as the formation of a group or committee appointed by the organization to guide and coordinate the necessary measures. It is necessary at both national and provincial levels to improve road safety standards.

On the other hand, road safety is a well-known global issue. And even in 2010, the United Nations General Assembly announced the years 2011–2020 as the decade of action for road safety [27]. Among the many goals of this global initiative, we can mention the development and implementation of sustainable road safety strategies and programs, which can lead to an ambitious but practical goal of reducing road casualties by monitoring road safety performance indicators. In this regard, international institutions such as the World Health Organization are required to set performance goals related to road safety risk factors in member countries [28].

But among the reasons that can contribute to the lack of public trust in the government’s policies in the field of safe roads, several points can be mentioned, including low transparency. Lack of transparency about the costs, financial resources, and implementation of government projects can lead to suspicion and low public trust. It is also possible to mention the incomplete implementation of programs by policymakers, because if the laws and regulations are not implemented by the government completely and fairly, people may not continue to trust these laws and solutions. And we can also mention the intangible results of the implemented programs. If people do not see tangible and quick results from the implementation of the government’s solutions on the roads, they may show less trust in these solutions. Finally, policymakers can increase public trust in road safety solutions by increasing transparency, using the latest road construction standards in the country, the integrity of relevant and responsible institutions in completing road safety improvement projects, public participation, as well as correct and timely implementation of programs.

Trust has emerged as a significant predictor of technology acceptance, particularly in the context of vehicle adoption. Specifically, the perceived safety and trust in the safety features of cars have been identified as pivotal factors influencing the acceptance of automobiles [29, 30]. Within the scope of the findings of this study, it is clear that the field of car safety is the second lowest in terms of public trust, following road safety, with a score of 43.3%. After trust in road safety, these observations underscore the lack of public trust in vehicle safety and highlight the necessity for corrective measures to strengthen public trust in vehicle safety within the country.

Diverse approaches can be pursued to enhance public trust in car safety features. Firstly, car manufacturers should place a paramount focus on the production of vehicles that are both safe and of exceptional quality, achieved through rigorous testing and stringent quality control measures. Secondly, transparency should be prioritized by car manufacturers, ensuring clear and comprehensive information about the safety features of their vehicles, along with transparent disclosure of any potential risks or hazards associated with the vehicles. Lastly, governments hold a pivotal role in elevating trust in automobiles by implementing stringent safety regulations and standards. This proactive stance serves to guarantee that vehicles traversing the roads adhere to specific safety criteria, rendering them secure for both drivers and passengers.

The findings from this study additionally highlight a noteworthy correlation between a prior history of traffic accidents and the overall level of trust in strategies aimed at diminishing traffic accidents. It is plausible that one’s history of accidents can exert an influence on driver behavior and confidence in adhering to road rules and safe driving practices [31]. Essentially, individuals who have encountered or witnessed frequent accidents on roadways might experience a decrease in their trust concerning policymakers’ capacity to effectively tackle traffic accidents. Conversely, when fewer accidents transpire and policies demonstrate efficacy, people are likely to cultivate greater trust in these measures and offer their support. When crafting and implementing traffic safety policies, it becomes imperative to consider the impact of accident history on the way the public perceives these measures.

According to the results of this study, the level of education is notably linked to the overall degree of trust in policies targeting the reduction of traffic accidents. This alignment with previous research corroborates the existing understanding that higher education levels correspond to heightened social trust and an enhanced sense of trustworthiness [32]. One study has underscored a positive correlation between education and the trust placed in governmental institutions [33]. Furthermore, multiple studies have illuminated the positive association between institutional trust and the willingness to adhere to government regulations and civic responsibilities [34]. Individuals possessing higher education levels may exhibit an elevated awareness of the risks inherent in driving and subsequently display a greater propensity to adhere to road guidance and driving regulations. This heightened awareness can lead to a deeper grasp of the significance of such policies and their potential societal impact, culminating in increased trust in policies aimed at mitigating traffic accidents. This dynamic underlines the pivotal role of education in fostering a comprehensive understanding of road safety measures, thereby contributing to the cultivation of trust in the efficacy of accident reduction policies.

Building upon prior research, numerous investigations have delved into the realm of public trust toward healthcare services, both within Iran and on a global scale. Likewise, previous studies have scrutinized policies aimed at preventing road accidents [35]. Within the context of these earlier endeavors, people’s attitudes toward road accident policies have been identified as significant barriers to the effective prevention of such incidents [36]. These insights underscore the multifaceted nature of public trust, encompassing healthcare services and traffic accident prevention measures alike.

Recommendations for future studies

Limited research has been dedicated to the exploration of public trust in traffic safety policies and programs, and this current study stands as one of the few conducted within this domain. This research serves as a valuable contribution to shedding light on a relatively underexplored area. Moving forward, several avenues for further investigation present themselves. Conducting research aimed at devising strategies to bolster public trust in traffic safety policies and measures holds great promise. Moreover, the potential for conducting analogous studies across different cities within the country, followed by a comparative analysis of their results, could yield valuable insights into regional variations in public trust dynamics. In addition, future studies could delve into the examination of influential factors on public trust in traffic safety policies, such as economic factors.

Limitations

Although, based on the results of the initial literature review and researchers’ experiences, the present study for the first time measured the level of public trust in traffic accident prevention policies and tried to provide comprehensive and practical information for readers and decision-makers, however, in conducting the present study there have been limitations. Among other things, it was done only in one of the big cities of Iran (Tabriz) and its results cannot be generalized to all the cities of Iran. To obtain comprehensive and extensive results and to determine the public trust of the entire country that can be generalized to all people, studies with a high and diverse sample size from most of the country’s big cities are needed.

Conclusion

The study’s outcomes have highlighted a notable gap between the existing level of public trust in traffic safety policies within Iran, particularly concerning vehicle safety, and the optimal standards that are considered desirable and acceptable. Consequently, there arises a crucial imperative for strategic planning and the implementation of requisite actions to elevate public trust. This responsibility lies with officials and responsible entities who play a pivotal role in shaping and enforcing these policies. Acknowledging the pivotal role that public trust plays in the efficacy of traffic safety measures, it becomes imperative to address this discrepancy. To bridge this gap, authorities and stakeholders should employ strategies such as transparent planning with public awareness about traffic safety objectives and measures, establishing effective communication with the public through public meetings, social media, and publications, providing final reports, encouraging public participation, implementing educational programs to enhance awareness about the importance of traffic safety and safe driving techniques, ensuring the rights of car owners and addressing their safety needs, adopting policies grounded in evidence and scientific research, and fostering a culture of safe driving to heighten the sense of social responsibility among drivers. These actions will contribute to an increase in public trust in traffic safety policies and will ultimately result in the creation of safer road environments, a reduction in the number of accidents, and an overall improvement in the country’s road safety conditions. For future research, it is recommended to expand the scope of the study to different regions of the country, analyze the influential factors, conduct comparative studies with other cities and countries, evaluate the impact of the implemented measures, refine existing tools, or develop new ones for more accurate measurement of public trust. This approach will yield more comprehensive results.

Effects of faba beans (Vicia faba L.) on muscle texture and fibrosis biomarkers of Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus)
Authors: Ning-xin Guo, Wen-bin Luo, Jin-hua Gao, Gazahegn-Wakjira Yadata, Xiao-li Yao, Zhi-chao Meng, Yan Zhao, Jin-liang Zhao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Crispy Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) is a valuable fish with high muscle hardness and chewiness, generated by feeding the fish with faba beans (Vicia faba L.). Reactive oxygen species (ROS) stress response may be one of the physiological causes responsible for the crispy change in muscle texture. However, molecular mechanisms controlling fish muscle texture particularly those arising from faba beans in the diet are unclear. In this study, Nile tilapia was fed with faba beans or commercially formulated feed lacking faba beans for 80 days. Thereafter, fish samples were examined for muscle texture, microstructure, collagen content and transcriptome to determine the mechanism of formation of crisp muscle texture. The results showed muscle texture indexes (hardness, resilience, gumminess, and chewiness) increased, muscle fibre diameter decreased as density increased, and the amount of total collagen in the extracellular matrix of muscle increased significantly in the faba bean-fed group. The expression of genes associated with fibrosis (col1α1, versican, comp and fibrosin) were significantly upregulated, while that of decorin, which is known to inhibit fibrosis, was downregulated in the faba bean-fed group. Overall, our study founded that feeding tilapia with faba beans can change muscle textural quality by altering the muscle fibres and collagen content. We also identified genes which induce changes in fibrosis of muscle that are related to the improved textural quality in crisp tilapia, providing theoretical guidance for the regulation of crispy tilapia.

Introduction

Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) is recommended by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) as an important aquaculture species due to its rapid growth, ease of breeding and cultivation, and high protein content (FAO, 1976). This species has been adopted by various countries and regions, with its production reaching 6.9 × 1010 kg in recent years (FAO, 2020). Feeding several fish species with faba beans for 60 to 120 days can improve muscle fibre density and collagen content and reduce muscle fibre diameter and crude lipid content; therefore, muscle hardness and chewiness increase significantly, because of which fish develop a unique crispy texture (He et al., 2023; Song et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2023b; Xu et al., 2020). Crisp fish is popular in China and other countries for its special flesh quality; hence feeding tilapia with faba beans to improve muscle quality is an effective method of increasing the economic value.

Many studies have evaluated the changes in growth performance, nutrient content, physiological and biochemical reactions, digestive enzyme activity, and muscle quality of tilapia (Li et al., 2023) and grass carp (Ctenopharyngodon idellus) (Yu et al., 2017a) after crisp feeding. Responses to reactive oxygen species (ROS) may be one of the physiological causes of crisp texture. ROS, including superoxide anions (O2−), hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), and hydroxyl radicals (OH−), may significantly influence muscle fibre differentiation (muscle fibre density and diameter) and collagen deposition. Proteomic analysis of crisp grass carp revealed that ROS accumulation decreases the sensitivity of Ca2+ in myofibrils. This interference further disrupted the interaction between actin and myosin, leading to a reduction in muscle fibre diameter and an increase in muscle hardness (Yu et al., 2020). ROS may also interrupt collagen renewal, increasing collagen content in muscles and enhancing muscle hardness (Yu et al., 2020). Studies in grass carp have indicated that ROS is transferred from the intestine and liver to the muscle via blood circulation, with ROS accumulating in muscles with the duration of crisp feeding (Chen et al., 2021).

However, the molecular mechanism of fish muscle crisping due to faba bean feeding is complex. Some studies have elucidated the molecular mechanism that regulates changes in muscle fibres differentiation during the crisping process. During crisp feeding, the upregulation of genes associated with the Hippo pathway increased the number of muscle fibres in grass carp (Xu et al., 2020). This increment in the number of muscle fibres led to muscle hyperplasia, consequently altering the texture of fish muscle (Wei et al., 2022). A reduction in the expression levels of myosin heavy chain and myosin light chain contributed to a reduction in muscle fibre diameter; in addition, upregulation of catenin and talin induced muscle fibre hyperplasia, subsequently increasing muscle fibre density (Yu et al., 2017b).

Collagen deposition is another important phenotypic trait that occurs during the crisping process (Wang et al., 2023b). Collagen significantly influences muscle hardness via its content and stability (Csapo et al., 2020). Studies have revealed that increment in muscle hardness correlated positively with the expression levels of type I collagen (Peng et al., 2022). Furthermore, Smad4 overexpression increased the mRNA and protein levels of type I collagen (Yu et al., 2019). Fibrosis can occur in multiple organs and is characterized by the excessive accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) components, including collagen and fibronectin (Antar et al., 2023). Thus, understanding whether muscle fibrosis occurs during crisping and identifying the genes involved require further research.

We hypothesized that faba beans feeding may induce muscle fibrosis and ECM remodelling pathways and ultimately change the textural quality of muscle in Nile tilapia. To validate the key candidate genes, particularly those involved in fibrosis and ECM metabolism, and their regulatory function in crisp muscle texture formation, the growth performance, muscle texture parameters, muscle fibre microstructure, and collagen content of Nile tilapia after 80 days of faba bean feeding were assessed. Furthermore, transcriptomics and reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) analysis were performed. In addition, feeding with different amounts of dietary faba beans showed different effects. The fish were fed 100 % faba beans in this study; hence, our observations may differ from those of previous studies in which fish were fed certain proportion of faba beans.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

This study was approved by the Animal Experimentation Ethics Committee of Shanghai Ocean University (Shanghai, China) (SHOU-DW-2016-004).

Fish and experimental conditions

The Nile tilapia used in this experiment were sourced from the Maoming Weiye Tilapia Farm in Guangdong Province, China. The control group was fed a commercially formulated feed for tilapia, which consisted of 30 % soybean meal, 3 % fish meal, 12 % flour, 21.5 % wheat bran, 30 % rapeseed meal, 1.5 % Ca(H2PO4)2, and 2 % vitamin and mineral premix. Faba beans were procured from Yunnan, soaked in cold water for 24 h, crushed, and then directly fed to the fish. Two groups were used for the feeding experiment: a control group (CG) and a faba bean-fed group (FBG), each with three independent biological replicates. Each replicate represented a separate experimental unit (concrete pond) that was randomly assigned to its respective group and maintained under identical feeding conditions throughout the experiment, including stocking density, water parameters, and feeding regime. Consequently, both CG and FBG comprised three independent tanks each. In total, 300 healthy tilapia (initial body weight: 665 ± 34 g) from the same breeding batch, with similar sizes, were selected and reared in outdoor concrete ponds (3 × 4× 1 m) at a density of 50 fish per pond. During the 80-day feeding experiment, the water temperature ranged from 27 to 31 °C, dissolved oxygen levels remained above 6 mg/L, and the pH was maintained between 6 and 7.5. The fish were fed twice daily at 9:00 AM and 6:00 PM, at approximately 3 % of their body weight.

After 80 days of feeding, the fish were fasted for 24 h. Individuals were randomly selected by tagging each fish with a unique identification number and generating corresponding random numbers (using a generator or table) to determine the required sample number. Selected fish were captured sequentially using a soft mesh net to minimize damage and disturbance to the remaining fish. The fish were anesthetised in a solution of 400 mg/L MS-222 until they were unconscious, indicated by the absence of opercular response to touch, prior to sampling. Subsequently, these fish were weighed and measured for length. Samples of the dorsal muscle tissue, located above the lateral line, were collected for muscle texture testing (using fresh muscle samples), histological observation, collagen content determination, and transcriptome analysis.

At 0, 40, and 80 days of feeding, 10 fish from each group were selected randomly and samples of the dorsal muscle tissue were collected. These samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and subsequently transferred to an −80 °C freezer for further RT-qPCR analysis.

Measurement of growth performance

The body weight and length of each fish were measured, along with the total count of fish. The weight gain rate and survival rate were calculated using the following formulas:

Survival rate = Nt/N0 × 100 %.

Nt represents the number of surviving fish in each group at the 80-day sampling point and N0 represents the number of fish in each experimental group at the start of the experiment.

Weight gain rate = (Wt-W0)/W0.

Wt denotes the body weight of the fish in each group at the 80-day sampling time and W0 denotes the initial body weight of the fish in each group at the start of the experiment.

Measurement of texture parameters

Fresh dorsal muscle tissue was collected and analysed using a CT3 texture analyser (Brookfield Inc., Middleboro, USA) to evaluate various indicators of muscle texture. The parameters for total texture analysis (TPA) were established as follows: the compression target value was set at 4.0 mm, probe speed at 0.5 mm/s, diameter of the circular probe at 6 mm, thickness of the muscle sample at 5 mm, time interval between two compressions at 2 s, and the compression ratio at 25 %. At each designated sampling time point, 20 fish were sampled from each experimental group for testing. The texture indices, including hardness, adhesive force, adhesiveness, resilience, cohesiveness, springiness, springiness index, gumminess, chewiness, and chewiness index were recorded and calculated using the software provided with the texture analysis instrument.

Observing muscle microstructure

A suitable quantity of dorsal muscle tissue was immersed in Bouin's fixative (Phygene, Fuzhou, China) for fixation. After 24 h, the tissue was retrieved and transferred to a 70 % ethanol solution, where it was subsequently stored at 4 °C for slicing in future. The fixed muscle tissue was subjected to a gradient dehydration process using ethanol concentrations ranging from 80 % to 100 % (volume fraction), followed by clarification with xylene and embedding in paraffin for 3 h. Sections with a thickness of approximately 8 μm were obtained using a microtome Leica RM 2016 (Leica, Wetzlar, German). Following hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining, the sections were sealed with neutral gum. After drying, they were observed and photographed under a Nikon ECLIPSE 80i optical microscope (Nikon, Tokyo Metropolis, Japen). Five fields of view were randomly selected for each slice, and a total of six samples were measured in each group. The ImageJ image analysis software was used to analyse 300 muscle fibres per sample, and muscle fibre diameter (20 times mirror) and muscle fibre density (20 times mirror) were measured.

Muscle fibre diameter was calculated using the formula S = πr2 (S represents the area and r is the radius), with the diameter determined as 2r (in μm). Density was assessed by counting the number of muscle fibres (fibres/mm2) within a specified area.

Transcriptome analysis

RNA extraction and sequencing (RNA-seq)

Three fish each from the FBG and CG were collected for transcriptome sequencing. Total RNA was extracted using the TRlzol reagent (Life technologies, Carlsbad, USA) per the manufacturer's instructions. RNA concentration and purity were measured using NanoDrop 2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA). RNA integrity was assessed using the RNA Nano 6000 assay kit on the Agilent Bioanalyzer 2100 system (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, USA).

Library construction was started after the samples were quantified. The main steps were as follows: (1) eukaryotic mRNA was enriched using magnetic beads with Oligo(dT); (2) mRNA was randomly fragmented by adding the fragmentation buffer; (3) the first strand of cDNA was synthesized using mRNA as the template, followed by the synthesis of double-stranded cDNA and subsequent purification; (4) the purified double-stranded cDNA was end-repaired, the addition of an A-tail, and the ligation of sequencing junctions, followed by fragment size selection using AMPure XP beads; (5) finally, the cDNA library was obtained after PCR enrichment. Upon completion of library construction, a Qubit 3.0 fluorescence quantifier (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, USA) was used for preliminary quantification, ensuring that the required concentration exceeded 1 ng/μL. Subsequently, the insertion fragments of the library were analysed using the Qsep400 high-throughput analysis system. After passing the quality assessment, sequencing was conducted in PE150 mode using the Illumina NovaSeq6000 sequencing platform.

Data analysis and validation

High-quality clean reads were obtained following a series of quality control processes. The HISAT2 software was used to align the clean reads with the reference genome (Oreochromis_niloticus.O_niloticus_UMD_NMBU.genome.fa). The reads that successfully aligned to the reference genome were referred to as mapped data. Based on this mapped data, individual samples were analysed for gene expression levels using the RSEM software; gene expression was estimated by the number of sequences mapped per kilobase of transcript per million. Differential expression analyses were conducted using DESeq2 software.

Five differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between CG and FBG were randomly selected for RT-qPCR to validate the results of RNA-seq. Primers were designed based on gene sequences from the transcriptome data and synthesized by Shanghai Jinweizhi Biological Company (Shanghai Jinweizhi Biological Company, Shanghai, China). Determination of total collagen content

Hydroxyproline (HYP), a stable amino acid, accounts for approximately 13.4 % of the total amino acid composition of collagen. Hence, HYP is frequently utilized as a biomarker for assessing collagen content. The collagen content was quantified using a kit (Art. No. A030–2-1; Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) in accordance with the manufacturer's guidelines.

Expression analysis of muscle fibrosis-related genes

Fish were randomly selected from the CG and FBG at 0 days, 40 days, and 80 days. Five fibrosis-related differentially expressed genes, including the fibrosis genes col1α1, comp, fibrosin, and versican, as well as the anti-fibrosis gene decorin, were selected for analysis based on the results of the transcriptome. Primers were designed using PrimerPremier 6.0 and synthesized by Shanghai Jinweizhi Biological Company (Shanghai Jinweizhi Biological Company, Shanghai, China). The sequences of the primer pairs were presented in Table 2. The RT-qPCR system used has been previously described in the previous section.

Statistical analysis

Data was analysed using SPSS Statistics for Windows (version 26.0). A T-Test was used to compare growth performance, muscle texture, microstructure of muscle fibres, collagen content and relative gene expression between CG and FBG at the same sampling point, as well as between different sampling points within the same feeding group. The results are presented as mean ± standard error, with a P-value of less than 0.05 indicating statistical significance.

Muscle fibrosis-related gene expression

To clarify how fibrosis-related genes (also considered as biomarkers for fibrosis) were involved in fibrosis during the feeding process, the expression of these genes in the muscles of both the CG and FBG at 0, 40, and 80 days was assessed. The results indicated that in the muscle of tilapia fed with faba beans for 40 and 80 days, the expression levels of the col1α1, comp, fibrosin, and versican genes were consistently and significantly higher than those in the CG (P < 0.05). Conversely, the expression level of the decorin was reduced (P < 0.05). Discussion

Faba bean feeding had been demonstrated to inhibit the growth performance of tilapia. The results of this study indicated that the body weight, and weight gain rate of tilapia in the FBG were significantly lower than those in the CG. The potential reasons for this phenomenon are as follows: first, the amino acid composition of faba beans is not balanced. Research indicates that legumes generally contain lower levels of sulphur-containing amino acids (tryptophan, cysteine, and methionine) (Rahate et al., 2021). Second, faba bean is rich in anti-nutritional factors, including bioactive compounds (phytic acid, saponins, lectins, vicine, convicine, and protease inhibitors) (Labba et al., 2021), which can adversely affect animals by reducing the intestinal absorption of nutrients. For instance, phytic acid can reduce the utilization of minerals in organisms (Kumar et al., 2023).

In our study, the substantial increase in texture indicators (hardness, resilience, gumminess, chewiness, and chewing index) following the feeding of tilapia with faba beans suggested a relationship between these parameters and the crisp degree of muscle. Inclusion of faba beans significantly enhances muscle hardness, springiness, cohesiveness, gumminess, chewiness, and resilience (Fu et al., 2023). Similarly, the texture indicators (hardness, elasticity, chewiness, and cohesiveness) of the dietary faba bean group were significantly higher than those of the control group in grass carp. The correlation coefficients of hardness, springiness, chewiness, and adhesiveness with crispness were found to be above 0.91, indicating a strong correlation between these indicators and crispness (Yu et al., 2017a). In conclusion, muscle hardness, gumminess, and chewiness were closely linked to the degree of muscle crispness. These three textural indicators can act as reliable metrics for quantifying the crisp degree.

Feeding faba beans decreased muscle fibre diameter and increased muscle fibre density (Yu et al., 2017a). Similarly, crisp tilapia exhibited an increase in the intermuscular matrix of muscle fibres, reduction in the cross-sectional area of muscle fibres, and increase in muscle fibre density (Li et al., 2023). Our results were similar to these findings, demonstrating an increase in muscle fibre density and a decrease in fibre diameter in tilapia from the FBG. Transcriptome analysis indicated that the upregulated DEGs in the FBG belonged to the FoxO pathway, while the downregulated ones belonged to the mTOR pathway. This regulation may lead to a reduction in protein synthesis, an increase in protein degradation, and enhancement of autophagy. Protein degradation within the FoxO signaling pathway is primarily modulated through its impact on the ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) and the autophagy-lysosome system (Sun et al., 2025). The UPS is a key mechanism for protein degradation and maintenance of homeostasis within cellular homeostasis. It is highly selective, regulating a variety of cellular processes through ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation (Zhao et al., 2022). The autophagy-lysosome system act as an important intracellular degradation and recycling mechanism, responsible for removing damaged organelles, misfolded proteins, and excess cellular components (Sun et al., 2025). The mTOR pathway plays a crucial role in regulating protein synthesis. Typically, muscle mass loss occurs due to the downregulation of mTOR; conversely, mTOR is upregulated during muscle hypertrophy. The process of protein deposition, which is determined by the balance between synthesis and degradation, alters in muscle structure when this balance is disrupted. These alterations are characterized by an increase in fibre density and a decrease in fibre diameter (Wang & Zhang, 2019). Similarly, an imbalance in protein deposition may contribute to the retardation in tilapia growth, as well as the observed increase in muscle fibre density and diameter in Nile tilapia.

The ECM is a crucial component of skeletal muscles, and alterations in its composition significantly affect muscle texture (Csapo et al., 2020). Collagen, the predominant structural protein of the ECM, plays a vital role in supporting and maintaining the alignment of myocytes (Sahani et al., 2024). The content, type, and structural characteristics of collagen are critical factors that profoundly influence fish muscle texture (Sahani et al., 2024). Elevated levels of various ECM components, particularly excessive collagen accumulation, is a hallmark of fibrosis (Antar et al., 2023). Fibrosis is a chronic pathological process, which has been most studied in the liver (Kisseleva & Brenner, 2021). Liver fibrosis is characterized by the deposition of ECM and the abnormal proliferation of connective tissue within the liver,after various chronic liver injuries (Kisseleva & Brenner, 2021). The persistent or repeated inflammatory necrosis of liver parenchyma due to chronic liver disease can lead to the proliferation of muscle connective tissue and ECM depositions (Kisseleva & Brenner, 2021). In this study, the total collagen content in the muscle of tilapia in the faba bean-fed group was significantly higher, which may induce muscle fibrosis and subsequently affect the formation of crisp muscle.

Collagen type I alpha 1 chain (col1α1) encodes a type I collagen, which can regulate the synthesis and deposition of collagen in the ECM (Yu et al., 2019). Type I collagen is most abundant in the ECM (Zhou et al., 2024). Cartilage oligomeric matrix protein (Comp) is a non-collagenous glycoprotein within the ECM that interacts with a diverse array of ECM proteins (Magdaleno et al., 2016). Comp exhibits high affinity for collagen types I and II and plays a crucial role in the early incorporation of collagen molecules, thereby enhancing collagen fibre formation (Andréasson et al., 2017). Fibrosin, a cytokine linked to fibrosis, plays a critical role in the pathogenesis of several fibrosis-related diseases. It is particularly important in conditions such as schistosomiasis, alcohol-induced liver fibrosis, and scleroderma (Li et al., 2021). Versican, a chondroitin sulphate proteoglycan, is a significant component of the ECM (Sasi et al., 2023); it functions as a regulator of fibrosis and is a key element of the fibroproliferative matrix in the initial stages of pulmonary fibrosis (Bukong et al., 2015). Decorin functions as a natural inhibitor of TGF-β1, obstructing its interaction with fibrosis-promoting receptors (Zhang et al., 2018). Decorin inhibited the expression of TGF-β1-induced fibrosis-related proteins in the ECM of in human ligamentum flavum cells (Wang et al., 2023a). In the present study, the collagen content and expression of fibrosis-related genes (col1α1, versican, comp, and fibrosin) were significantly elevated in Nile tilapia muscle of the FBG compared to that in the CG. Conversely, the expression of the anti-fibrosis decorin gene was significantly lower in the FBG. These results indicated that feeding of faba beans can promote muscle collagen deposition, which in turn may affect muscle fibrosis and improve the muscle textural quality of tilapia (Fig. 7). Studied have reported that the expression of col1α1 in muscle increased significantly and that it correlated positively correlated with muscle chewiness induced by dietary faba bean in grass carp (Tang et al., 2024)and tilapia (Peng et al., 2022). The absence of COMP led to the formation of disordered collagen fibre and decreased tissue hardness in cattle (Halász et al., 2007), while upregulation of COMP induced the collagen fibres to align along the direction of tension and interweave orderly with muscle fibres, thereby optimizing muscle hardness and elasticity (Posey et al., 2018). These studies were consistent with our finding of col1α1, COMP upregulation and textural quality enhancement. Downregulation of the antifibrotic gene decorin resulted in a tighter arrangement of muscle fibres, thereby enhancing muscle texture quality in mouse (Robinson et al., 2017). It also supported the association of reduced decorin expression with enhanced textural quality, indicating that the tighter arrangement of muscle fibres maybe due to deposition of collagen in the ECM.Fig. 7Alterations in the expression of fibrosis-related genes in Nile tilapia muscles lead to muscle fibrosis, resulting in crisp muscle.Fig. 7

Although we observed a certain correlation between muscle fibrosis and crisp muscle texture, the current evidence does not establish a direct cause-effect relationship between them. This is because muscle fibrosis mainly contributes to collagen deposition, while the formation of crisp muscle texture is complex and is related to muscle fibre structure, collagen deposition and fatty acid metabolism (Wang et al., 2023b). ROS is speculated to be the upstream regulatory signal for these physiological and biochemical changes. Another limitation of this study is that the ingredient of commercially formulated feed (used as control) differed from that of faba beans, which are primary agricultural products with unstable components; hence, the ingredient of faba beans that affected muscle texture is not known.

Conclusion

In summary, the results of this study showed that faba beans feeding significantly impaired growth performance while improving the collagen content and muscle texture of Nile tilapia. The expression of multiple fibrosis-related genes changed; in particular, the fibrosis-related genes (col1α1, comp, fibrosin and versican) were upregulated, whereas the anti-fibrosis gene, decorin, was downregulated after being fed with faba beans. Therefore, feeding of faba bean facilitates collagen accumulation in Nile tilapia muscle, which promotes muscle fibrosis and ultimately changes the textural quality of muscle. These findings provide a new research perspective for improving fish quality. However, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying the changes in muscle texture are complex. More studies are needed to incorporate multi-omics technologies and cell culture models to identify the key regulatory genes, which would contribute to the molecular designing of breeding programs for generating new strains with improved muscle texture quality in the future.

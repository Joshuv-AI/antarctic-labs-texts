Surgical management of pulmonary metastases in pseudomyxoma peritonei: a case series highlighting the challenges of non-palpable lesions and surgical margin management
Authors: Kazuki Hayashi, Yusuke Kita, Yo Kawaguchi, Jun Hanaoka
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPseudomyxoma peritonei (PMP) is a rare neoplastic disease that can occasionally present with pulmonary metastases as an even rarer manifestation. Although cytoreductive surgery represents the primary treatment option for PMP, surgical management of pulmonary metastases presents unique challenges owing to their mucinous nature. The aim of this case series was to present key considerations for the surgical management of PMP lung metastases, particularly regarding solid-appearing nodules that are impalpable during surgical resection, requiring more extensive resection owing to the risk of recurrence.Case presentationBetween November 2013 and May 2023, we performed a total of 13 surgical procedures for pulmonary metastases in seven patients with PMP at our institution. During these procedures, 18 pulmonary lesions were resected. Some patients underwent multiple surgeries; multiple lesions were resected in a single procedure in certain cases. Notable characteristics of PMP pulmonary metastases, attributed to their mucinous dissemination pattern, included cases wherein subpleural solid nodules that would typically be palpable in patients with conventional solid tumour metastases were non-palpable and cases requiring right upper lobectomy following repeated surgical margin recurrence. Median follow-up results showed a progression-free survival of 20.0 months and overall survival of 40.3 months. Three of the seven patients experienced postoperative recurrence, with two patients having intrapulmonary metastatic recurrence involving low-grade tumours.ConclusionsSurgical resection can be an effective treatment option for PMP pulmonary metastases; however, careful patient selection and appropriate surgical planning are essential. The unique characteristics of PMP metastases necessitate specific surgical strategies. Since computed tomography-apparent solid nodules may be non-palpable intraoperatively and surgical margin recurrence should be avoided, selecting surgical approaches that minimise unnecessary lung palpation, such as preoperative marking or anatomical lung resection, is crucial. Background

Pseudomyxoma peritonei (PMP) is an extremely rare neoplastic condition characterised by mucinous dissemination, with a reported incidence of 1 per million population [1]. Although it typically spreads throughout the peritoneal cavity via mucinous dissemination, affecting various clinical symptoms and patient prognosis, it can rarely progress to thoracic involvement, manifesting as pulmonary metastases or pleural dissemination [2–4]. Moreover, although the exact incidence of thoracic metastases following peritoneal disease treatment remains unclear owing to the condition rarity, the development of such metastases presents a significant clinical challenge that requires careful consideration of surgical approaches.

Although effective pharmacological treatments of PMP remain to be established, cytoreductive surgery has been widely implemented as an effective surgical intervention for peritoneal disease [5]. We previously reported on the surgical management of thoracic PMP lesions, primarily focusing on cytoreductive surgery and hyperthermic intrathoracic chemotherapy for pleural dissemination, which included six cases of pulmonary resection [6]. Subsequently, we have accrued more cases, increasing our total experience to 18 pulmonary resections. The unique challenges of non-palpable pulmonary metastases and surgical margin management in PMP have not been extensively discussed in previous studies. Our expanded case series specifically addresses these challenges through detailed surgical observations and long-term follow-up.

Overview

Between November 2013 and May 2023, we performed a total of 13 surgical procedures for pulmonary metastases in seven patients, resulting in 18 pulmonary resections. Some procedures involved resection of multiple lesions in a single session. Patient characteristics are summarised in Table 1. This report presents three particularly instructive cases from our series. None of the patients exhibited symptoms. Furthermore, PMP pulmonary metastasis was confirmed through postoperative pathological examination of the resected lung specimens.

Surgical approaches

The surgical approaches included nine thoracotomies and four video-assisted thoracoscopic surgeries, of which two and one were multiportal and biportal, respectively. The choice of approach was determined based on the presence of severe pleural adhesions and need for multiple resections in a single session.

Patient 1

A 76-year-old woman was found to have nodules in the right upper and left lower lobes on chest computed tomography (CT) 5 years and 4 months after undergoing cytoreductive surgery for PMP (Fig. 1a). Owing to progressive enlargement, surgery was performed under suspicion of PMP pulmonary metastases. The right upper lobe nodule was treated with thoracoscopic right S1 segmentectomy, whereas the left lower lobe nodule was treated with thoracoscopic partial resection following CT-guided marking using intrapleural injection of indocyanine green (ICG) dye (Fig. 1b). Both nodules showed mucinous filling. Although imaging showed an 11-mm solid nodule in the right upper lobe, it could not be palpated as a solid “mass” during the examination of the resected specimen (Fig. 1c; Additional file 1).

Patient 2

A 52-year-old woman was found to have nodules in the right and left lower lobes on chest CT (Fig. 1d) 3 years and 4 months after undergoing cytoreductive surgery for PMP. The right lower lobe nodule was treated with thoracoscopic right S7 segmentectomy, whereas the left lower lobe nodule was managed by thoracoscopic partial resection following CT-guided marking using ICG dye. Similar to that in Patient 1, the 7-mm solid nodule in the left lower lobe was difficult to palpate in the resected specimen.

Patient 3

A 63-year-old man was found to have a right upper lobe nodule on chest CT 6 years and 6 months after undergoing cytoreductive surgery for PMP. Initial thoracoscopic partial resection was performed; however, margin recurrence necessitated additional partial resection. Following further margin recurrence, right upper lobectomy was performed 2 years and 2 months after the initial surgery (Fig. 1e).

Discussion and conclusions

PMP is an extremely rare condition characterised by mucinous dissemination, with thoracic involvement being even rarer and associated with poor prognosis. Based on current evidence supporting the efficacy of cytoreductive surgery, we have previously reported its applicability to thoracic lesions [6]. Our additional analysis demonstrated progression-free survival of 20.0 months and overall survival of 40.3 months for pulmonary metastases alone, suggesting that surgical intervention should be actively considered for suitable surgical candidates.

PMP pulmonary metastases possess distinct characteristics from conventional solid cancer metastases commonly encountered by thoracic surgeons. Specifically, even seemingly palpable subpleural solid nodules may be difficult to confirm through palpation owing to their mucinous nature. Furthermore, particularly in mucinous cancers, the unnecessary palpation and exploration of the tumour and surrounding tissue should be avoided as it may promote tumour cell dissemination and increase the risk of margin recurrence [7]. Although tumours may be visible through the pleura in some cases (Fig. 1f), this is not consistently reliable. Therefore, when operating on suspected PMP pulmonary metastases, surgical approaches that avoid intraoperative palpation—such as anatomical segmentectomy or lobectomy when margins cannot be assured or wedge resection guided by preoperative marking—are preferable.

In this series, CT-guided marking was used during two wedge resections via real-time intrapleural injection of ICG dye. Since mucin-filled nodules are often difficult to palpate and repeated manipulation may risk mucin leakage, we consider preoperative marking to be a useful strategy for managing non-palpable lesions.

At our institution, fluorodeoxyglucose-positron emission tomography (FDG-PET)/CT is not routinely used for evaluating PMP. Although previous studies have demonstrated that FDG uptake—such as maximum standardised uptake value and PET-peritoneal cancer index—correlates with histologic tumour grade and may aid in preoperative assessment [8, 9], the sensitivity of FDG-PET/CT tends to be lower in patients with low-grade PMP due to its low cellularity and mucin-rich composition, which can result in false-negative findings. For these reasons, the gastrointestinal surgery department at our institution has adopted magnetic resonance imaging as the standard imaging modality for abdominal evaluation in PMP cases.

Hilar lymph node sampling was performed during six anatomical resections, without lymph node metastasis detected. Given the mucinous nature, rather than the lymphatic dissemination pathway typical of PMP, routine lymphadenectomy may not be necessary; however, further studies are warranted.

Among the seven patients, three died during the follow-up period. Two and one deaths were attributable to abdominal PMP progression and unrelated renal failure, respectively.

The mechanisms underlying the development and progression of PMP pulmonary metastases remain poorly understood. Although PMP primarily spreads through mucinous dissemination within the peritoneal cavity, pulmonary involvement appears to arise via hematogenous spread. In our series, two of the seven patients had suspected pulmonary metastases identified on imaging before abdominal surgery. Previous studies have similarly noted that pulmonary metastases often become radiologically apparent only after abdominal intervention, suggesting that tumour cell entry into the bloodstream might occur intraoperatively [4, 10]. Furthermore, postoperative pulmonary recurrence occurred in three cases, all involving low-grade tumours. These observations raise important questions about the biology of PMP spread, including the possibility of occult hematogenous dissemination and the role of tumour grade. Further accumulation of clinical cases and molecular investigations will be essential to clarify these mechanisms.

MOF-818 nanoparticles as radical scavengers to improve the aging resistance of silk fabric
Authors: Guoke Chen, Wenting Gu, Yanfei Wei, Lei Zhong, Yan Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractSilk fabrics hold immense historical value as precious legacies left by our ancestors, yet they face significant damage during archaeological excavations, necessitating urgent protective measures. However, The current protective materials can’t effectively prevent the degradation of silk fabrics. Nanotechnology has emerged as a promising avenue for the consolidation and preservation of silk fabrics, offering novel concepts and materials. In this study, we propose an innovative and cost-effective method that uses the MOF-818 with a radical scavenging ability to enhance the protection of silk fabrics. The resulting demonstrates that the MOF-818 was the large surface area and porous properties, which exhibited excellent superoxide dismutase (SOD)-like activity at 10 ug/mL. The silk fabrics treated by MOF-818 displays small color difference, reduced the oxidation of functional group and prevents the degradation of silk fabrics. The successful development of this nanocomposite marks a significant advancement in silk protection, opening new horizons for the preservation of silk cultural relics.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73249-3.Keywords: Silk fabric, MOF-818, SOD activity, Ultraviolet protectionSubject terms: Chemistry, Nanoscience and technology

Introduction

China, as the earliest nation to cultivate mulberry and develop sericulture, holds a significant historical connection to silk. As a vital component of export commodities in ancient China, the silk fiber possesses distinctive characteristics that set it apart from other fabric materials. The silk fiber had a profound impact on Chinese etiquette, science, technology and art which extends beyond its utilitarian value. It has played a pivotal role in the dissemination and exchange of human civilization, spreading eastward to Japan and Korea and westward along the renowned “Silk Road” to Central and Western Asia, and even Europe1,2.

The silk, as a protein-based polymer fiber, is susceptible to degradation. Exposure to ultraviolet (UV) light leads to yellowing, brittleness, rotting, and even disintegration of silk fibers. The presence of oxygen often serves as a primary factor in the aging process of polymer materials. In the aging process, the silk as a polymer material composed of protein would generates free radicals with the ultraviolet (UV) light and heat which could trigger further degradation and crosslinking reactions. Therefore, silk fabric is particularly vulnerable to the detrimental effects of UV light. Illumination disrupts the hydrogen bonds within the fibroin protein, promoting photooxidation reactions that destroys the structural integrity of silk3–5. Notably, the tertiary carbons in the peptide chain facilitated the generation of free radicals through photochemical reactions when exposed to UV irradiation. These free radicals instigate the degradation of the peptide chain, leading to the fracture of silk fibers6. Consequently, this degradation process adversely affects the mechanical properties and color stability of silk fabric. And this phenomenon persists even after the cessation of light exposure7–10.

Consequently, extensive efforts have been dedicated to studying the aging mechanisms of silk and devising strategies to mitigate oxidation. Cultural relics made of silk have been buried with a long period of time, resulting in minimal light exposure. However, when these relics were excavated which would be more susceptible to damage from ultraviolet (UV) rays. Therefore, research on ultraviolet protection materials plays a crucial role in preserve the cultural relics. Ana Serrano et al. researched the exhibition and storage environment of silk fabrics discovered along the Maritime Silk Road, they concluded that limiting light exposure and removing oxygen can mitigate the detrimental effects on silk structure11. Wang et al. employed inorganic nanocomposite technology to investigate the interaction between light absorbers and metal oxides, such as titanium oxide. This approach got a stable, uniform, and transparent photosensitive coating materials on silk fabrics12–14. Nanometer-sized titanium dioxide (TiO2), an essential inorganic nanomaterial, exhibits strong UV scattering and absorption capabilities. It effectively shields against UVA (315–400 nm) and UVB (290–315 nm) rays while allowing visible light transmission15. However, these protective materials can’t effectively eliminate free radicals generated by ultraviolet light16–18. The free radicals would lead to the degradation of silk fabrics as a direct influencing factor. UP to now, there are no effective methods to eliminate free radicals in the current protection methods. Furthermore, as a novel class of nanomaterial, single-atom catalysts offer unique features, including low coordination number, distinctive coordination environments, exceptionally high atomic utilization, and highly homogeneous catalytic sites. This makes them a bridge between homogeneous and traditional heterogeneous catalysts. Some studies have demonstrated the exceptional catalytic activity of single-atom catalysts in efficiently decomposing dissolved oxygen or active oxygen free radicals. These characteristics make them promising candidates for research on the antioxidative protection of silk cultural relics19–22.

In this study, we present a novel approach to enhance the protection of silk fabrics by introducing metal-organic framework materials (MOF-818) as a radical scavenger. The MOF-818 exhibits excellent superoxide dismutase (SOD)-like activity. This activity enables the catalytic disproportionation of superoxide radicals (O2•−) into hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and oxygen (O2)23–30. By utilizing the MOF-818, we aim to reduce the generation of free radicals induced by UV radiation on silk fabrics. The catalytic action of the MOF-818 nanozyme inhibits the chain growth reaction of free radicals, thereby playing a protective role31–39. Moreover, we employed various research methods to investigate the changes in properties resulting from silk irradiation under UV rays. Through our experimental findings, we demonstrate the potential application of MOF-818 nanozyme for reinforcing silk fabrics, offering a promising avenue for their protection.

Results and discussion

Preparation and characterization of MOF-818 nanocomposites

The MOF-818 was built by using Cu and Zr bimetals as metal nodes and 1H-pyrazole-4-carboxylic acid as organic ligands which was obtained according to the previous methods40. TEM image (Fig. 1A) and SEM image (Fig. S1) of MOF-818 show the octahedron architecture and the corresponding elemental mapping images (Fig. 1C–G) indicated the Zr, N, O, Cu uniformly dispersed in the selected area, implying successfully preparation the MOF-818. Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) was also measured to analyze the structures of the MOF-818. As shown in Fig. 1H, the peaks located at 3.01, 4.91, 5.99 and 6.89 correspond to the crystal structure of (111), (220), (222) and (400) planes, respectively41.

TEM image (A), HAADF-STEM image (B), corresponding elemental mapping images (C–G) and XRD pattern of the MOF-818.

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was carried out to analyze the elements and composition bonding information of MOF-818. As shown in Fig. S2, the XPS survey spectra of the MOF-818 indicated the Cu, O, N, and Zr elements exist in MOF-818 which is consistent with the elemental mapping images and EDX image (Fig. S3). The high-resolution C 1s and O 1s spectra (Fig. S4A,B) showed the presence of C = O or C-O bond of MOF-818, which resulting from the ligand of 1 H-pyrazole-4-carboxylic acid (H2PyC), further demonstrated by the infrared spectroscopy shows the stretching vibration of C–O and C=O at 1686 cm−1 and 1291 cm−1, respectively (Fig. S5). The peak at 400.08 eV of N 1s spectra was belonged to the Cu-N bond (Fig. S4C). The Cu 2p spectra shown in Fig. 2A can be divided into several peaks. While the peaks located at 932.61 and 953.37 eV, could be assigned to Cu+, and the peaks located at 934.37 and 954.19 eV was assigned to the Cu2+. As shown in the high-resolution of Zr 3d spectra (Fig. 2B), the peak at 182.51 eV was assigned to Zr4+, the result proved that the bimetallic MOF-818 was successful preparation. Indeed, owing to the doping of Zr element, the stability of MOF-818 could be greatly improved, which also provide the prerequisite guarantee for the reinforcement of cultural relics.

High-resolution of Cu 2p XPS spectra (A) and Zr 3d spectra (B) of the as-obtained MOF-818.

The porous property of MOF-818 was measured by the N2 adsorption-desorption isotherm (Fig. 2C,D). The Brunauer-Emmet-Teller (BET) and Barrett-Joyner-Halenda (BJH) analysis shows the surface area and total pore volume of MOF-818 was about 418.28 m2/g and 0.293 cm3/g, respectively. It’s well known that the large surface area and porous properties not only provide more active sites but also facilitate the transfer of superoxide anion (O2•−) at the accessible surface area of MOF-818, which is beneficial to the SOD-like activity and cultural relics protection.

Further, we investigate the SOD-like activity of MOF-818 by using a colorimetric SOD assay kit, in which the xanthine, xanthine oxidase (XOD) and WST-8 were mixed together at room temperature to produce superoxide anion (O2•−), and the produced O2•− can reduce WST-8 to formazan dye, which can be detected by UV-vis absorbance spectrophotometer at about 450 nm. While, when the system added the SOD-like activity of MOF-818, the absorbance of formazan at 450 nm will decreased, owing to MOF-818 can cause disproportionation reaction with O2•− to produce hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and oxygen (O2). As shown in Fig. 3B, the MOF-818 exhibited excellent SOD-like activity. Meanwhile, the activity increased with increasing MOF-818 concentration from 0.1 ug/mL to 10 ug/mL (Fig. 3C,D), where SOD-like activity was strongest at 10 ug/mL, which also provides a reference for the reinforcement of cultural relics.

The schematic of MOF-818 with SOD-like activity (A), the SOD-like activity of MOF-818 (B) and at different concentration for scavenging superoxide anion (C,D).

Conservation efficiency for silks

In order to evaluate the conservation efficiency of the MOF-818 nanocomposite for silks, we carried out a test with the BR-UV aging tester. Specifically, 5 mg/ml MOF-818 nanocomposite was dissolved into water, and then sprayed on the surface of silks. Each study was treated with three replicates samples. Afterwards, the sample blocks are placed in a UV aging test chamber with 340 nm ultraviolet wavelength irradiation for 20 days. The temperature and humidity are set to 25 °C and 65%, respectively.

As shown in Fig. 4A, the untreated silk fabric have a smooth surface, and the fractures or cracks had not were observed. The surface of the sample (Fig. 4B) with ultraviolet aging for 20 days had been found the obvious cracks in the same direction as the fiber axis which indicated that some silk fabrics have the aging phenomenon. However, the sample treated by MOF-818 with ultraviolet aging for 20 days had no obvious cracks and fractures on the surface of silk fabric (Fig. 4C), which indicated that MOF-818 had the protective effect on silk fibers under ultraviolet radiation.

The SEM images of untreated samples (A), the the sample with ultraviolet aging for 20 days (B) and the sample treated by MOF-818 with ultraviolet aging for 20 days (C), respectively.

Silk is a natural polymer fiber, but the protein properties of the fiber determine that it is susceptible to ultraviolet light and photochemical reaction, especially when there is tertiary carbon on the peptide chain, it is easy to produce free amino acids in the photochemical reaction, and the tryptophan in it will also produce yellow substances, which will change the color of the silk fiber43, and ultimately lead to the change of the color of the silk. The untreated sample, aged samples treated with MOF-818 and aged samples were tested by infrared spectroscopy, and it was found that characteristic absorption bands of protein fibers were found in all samples (Fig. 5B). The Stretching vibration absorption band of -NH is around 3270 cm−1, and the Amide I, II, III, IV absorption bands are around 1620 cm−1, 1512 cm−1, 1230 cm−1, 1060 cm−1. The Amide I is 1620 cm−1 belongs to the β-fold absorption peak of silk protein fiber, and the Amide III absorption band is 1230 cm−1 belongs to random coil and α-spiral. At the same time, strong absorption peaks also appeared at 2930 cm−1, 1440 cm−1 and 1160 cm−1, indicating that the measured sample was in line with the infrared spectral characteristics of silk fibers, and the result of infrared spectroscopy indicated that the sample was silk fibers42.

As shown in Table 1, the absorption spectrum peaks of aging sample and treated with MOF-818 aged sample were slightly higher than that of untreated sample near 3270 cm−1, but the absorption peaks of Amide II were slightly weakened after aging treatment. This indicates that the silk fibroin protein molecules are damaged to some extent. And some hydrogen bonds are destroyed, which cause the -NH peak decreased after aging treatment. Also, the change in the intensity of the absorption peak of aged sample treated with MOF-818 are smaller, which indicates that MOF-818 played a role of protecting molecular structure of silk protein fibers.

The degree of crystallinity was used to compare the degradation degree with the untreated sample, aged samples treated with MOF-818 and aged samples were tested by X-ray diffraction (Fig. 5C; Table 2). It was found that the crystallinity of the aged sample was the highest after aging experiment. When the silk fiber degrades, the amorphous region degrades first which caused the increase of the crystallinity of the silk fiber. However, with the aging time and the extension of degradation, the crystallization zone begins to degrade, and the tightness of the silk fiber decrease. The results showed that the MOF-818 could slow down the aging rate of silk fiber when the silk fiber was exposed to ultraviolet radiation44–46.

Conclusions

In this work, we demonstrate an unprecedented concept of silks protection enabled by the single atom metal nanozyme MOF-818. MOF-818 displays high SOD activity which can effectively remove reactive oxygen species, and prevent the aging effect of ultraviolet rays on silk fibers. Through the simulation experiment, the sample treated by MOF-818 displays small color difference, reduced the oxidation of functional group and prevents the degradation of amino acids to silks. As a result, the MOF-818 nanocomposite shows superior conservation efficiency, which demonstrates an important application of single atom metal nano-enzyme as a protective material of silk cultural relics.

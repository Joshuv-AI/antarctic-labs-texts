FXII Frameshift Variant Does Not Cause Hereditary Angioedema with Normal C1 Inhibitor
Authors: Toshiyuki Miyata, Takahiko Horiuchi, Michihiro Hide
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

We read with great interest the article entitled “Expanding the genetic and clinical spectrum of hereditary angioedema with normal C1 inhibitor: novel variants and treatment insights” by Gao et al., published in the Journal of Clinical Immunology [1].

The authors performed whole-exome sequencing on eight patients with angioedema that was refractory to antihistamines, epinephrine, and corticosteroids, despite normal C1 inhibitor function and low or normal complement C4 levels, with or without a family history of similar symptoms. They identified eight novel genetic variants in MYOF, HS3ST6, FXII, or KNG1 in these patients [1]. It is intriguing that all eight patients with drug-refractory angioedema harbored variants in genes previously implicated in hereditary angioedema with normal C1 inhibitor (HAE-nC1INH). However, the presence of a genetic variant does not necessarily indicate causality unless supported by functional studies and/or segregation analysis in large pedigrees. In fact, the authors reported that the mothers of patients 3 and 5, as well as the father of patient 6, carried the same variants but were asymptomatic. Furthermore, symptoms in patients 3 and 8 improved upon elimination of IgG4- or IgE-targeted foods, and patient 7’s symptoms appeared to resolve spontaneously without intervention. Incomplete penetration of HAE phenotype is not rare, especially in HAE-nC1INH. Moreover, disease activities may spontaneously fluctuate after the onset HAE. Therefore, asymptomatic feature of other family members carrying the same genetic variants as the patients per se does not exclude the possibility of diagnosis of HAE. Nevertheless, these observations should be carefully taken as suggestions against a causal role for the identified variants and hereditary nature of the angioedema observed in these patients.

The most serious concern pertains to the interpretation of the c.303_304del variant in the FXII gene, which results in a frameshift mutation (p.His101Glnfs*36) in patient 6. The authors classified this variant as “likely pathogenic” based on ACMG guidelines and proposed it as causative for HAE-FXII [1]. We respectfully disagree with this interpretation.

The p.His101Glnfs36 frameshift mutation results in FXII deficiency due to a secretion defect caused by the amino acid substitution p.His101Gln and a stop codon 36 residues downstream (Figure). Crucially, FXII deficiency is not associated with hereditary angioedema [2, 3]. To date, all pathogenic variants in HAE-FXII have been localized to the proline-rich region (PRR) adjacent to the activation cleavage site of FXII (Figure) [2]. Among them, two variants, p.Thr328Lys and p.Thr328Arg, in the PRR render FXII susceptible to proteolytic cleavage, and the resulting truncated FXII is activated by kallikrein 15 times more rapidly than the full-length FXII, leading to accelerated prekallikrein activation and excessive bradykinin generation from high molecular weight kininogen [4]. Thus, classifying the frameshift variant p.His101Glnfs36 as causative for HAE-FXII is misleading.

Genetic variants in the factor XII (FXII) gene identified in patients with hereditary angioedema (HAE-FXII) (A) and in a patient with FXII deficiency caused by the frameshift variant p.His101Glnfs*36 (B). (A) Four pathogenic variants associated with HAE-FXII are located in the proline-rich region (PRR) (2), as indicated by arrows. (B) The c.303_304del frameshift variant results in p.His101Glnfs*36, leading to FXII deficiency due to a secretion defect, which is unrelated to the pathogenesis of HAE-FXII. A thick line shows an aberrant C-terminal extension with 35 amino acids. Abbreviations: FXII, factor XII; FnII, fibronectin type II domain; E, epidermal growth factor-like domain; FnI, fibronectin type I domain; PRR, proline-rich region. Amino acid numbering begins with the initiating methionine as residue number 1

We strongly recommend that this frameshift mutation be reclassified as causing FXII deficiency, which is unrelated to the pathogenesis of HAE-FXII.

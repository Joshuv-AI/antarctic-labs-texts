Immunogenicity and protective efficacy of chitosan/β-glucan nanoparticle encapsulated inactivated Streptococcus agalactiae oral vaccine for Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus)
Authors: K. Nandhakumar, Ritam Guha, Sreeja Lakshmi, Preetham Elumalai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Novel oral vaccine delivery material for aquaculture, using chitosan/ β-glucan conjugate nanoparticles.•Chitosan/β-glucan nanoparticles significantly boost immunity in Nile tilapia.•Elevated level of immune genes in the primary lymphoid organ was observed.•The vaccine reduces mortality and disease severity against Streptococcus agalactiae.

Introduction

The Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) is a key species in tropical and subtropical farming, supplying animal-based protein and revenue worldwide, particularly in Asia, where it's a staple in local cuisine [1,2]. However, the culture of Nile tilapia is threatened by diseases, particularly bacterial infections, which cause significant economic losses [3]. Streptococcosis, caused by Streptococcus agalactiae, is the most prevalent bacterial disease, affecting tilapia of all ages and sizes, resulting in substantial financial losses for farmers [4]. Intensive farming systems exacerbate disease outbreaks due to high stocking density, stress, and poor water quality. Antibiotic overuse contributes to antimicrobial resistance (AMR), highlighting the need for sustainable disease management strategies [5]. Effective control measures are crucial to ensure the long-term viability of tilapia aquaculture [6]

Vaccines are crucial in preventing Streptococcosis in aquaculture, particularly in tilapia and other high-value fish species [7]. Vaccination has emerged as an effective strategy to mitigate the diseases. Compared to injectable (i.p.), immersion (i.m.), and oral vaccination,it emerges as the most promising method for Nile tilapia [8]. Oral vaccination offers non-invasive, stress-free, and cost-effective delivery, ensuring high bioavailability and targeted immune response [9]. Studies have shown oral vaccination to be 70–90 % effective against bacterial infections, surpassing IM (40–60 %) and i.p. (30–50 %) methods [10]. Therefore, oral vaccination is suitablefor promoting sustainable aquaculture practices. Additionally, novel immunostimulants like β-glucan and chitosan enhance vaccine efficacy and duration of protection [[11], [12]]. Chitosan-based vaccines have enhanced immune response, improved protection, and reduced mortality rates against S. agalactiae infections [13]. Specifically, chitosan nanoparticles have been used to deliver S. agalactiae antigens, inducing strong antibody responses and cellular immunity in tilapia [14]. Additionally, chitosan-adjuvanted vaccines have exhibited increased efficacy against S. agalactiae, reducing bacterial loads and tissue damage [15]. β-glucans are polysaccharides obtained from natural sourcesthat have emerged as potent immunostimulants in aquatic vaccines [16]. These bioactive compounds enhance the immune response of aquatic animals, protecting againstbacteria, viruses, and parasites [17]. In aquatic vaccines, β-glucan has been used as an adjuvant to stimulate innate and adaptive immunity, increasing antibody production and cellular response [18].β-glucan has shown efficacy against S. agalactiae, Aeromonas hydrophila, and Vibrio sp. infections in tilapia and shrimp. β-glucanadjuvanted vaccines have demonstrated improved vaccine efficacy, reduced mortality rates, and enhanced disease resistance [19]. Nano-delivery carriers are vital in oral vaccination against bacterial infections in Nile tilapia, enhancing vaccine efficacy and protection [20]. These carriers, including liposomes, nanoparticles, and nano capsules, protect antigens from degradation, facilitate targeted delivery to gut-associated lymphoid tissues (GALT), and control release, stimulating immune response [21]. Chitosan nanoparticles, for instance, have shown promise in delivering S. agalactiae vaccines, inducing significant immune response and protection [22]. Chitosan/β-glucan nanoparticles have emerged as a stable and efficient delivery carrier in human research, particularly in drug delivery [23]. These nanoparticles combine the biocompatibility and mucoadhesiveness of chitosan with the immunostimulatory properties of β-glucan. The synergistic effect enhances antigen delivery, immune response, and vaccine efficacy. Chitosan/ β-glucan is a very poorly explored delivery carrier in aquaculture. Usingchitosan/ β-glucan nanoparticles as a delivery carrier promotes sustainable aquaculture practices, reduces antibiotic use, and enhances fish health and welfare [24].

This experimentis designed to evaluate the effect of the chitosan/β-glucan nanocarrier loaded with the formalin-inactivated vaccine through the oral route against S. agalactiae in Oreochromis niloticus. The synthesized particles will be characterized by FTIR (Fourier Transform Infrared Spectra) and SEM (Scanning Electron Microscope). The effects and immunological changes in the fish will be evaluated by various immunological parameters such as serum IgM level, myeloperoxidase activity, lysozyme activity, antiprotease activity, and superoxide dismutase. Immune gene expression in the primary lymphoid organ spleen, will be performed by RT-qPCR. The protective effect of the vaccine will be determined by bacterial challenge studies, and the relative percentage of survival will be calculated at the end of the experiment, with histopathological changes examined. The purpose of this study will be to examine the potential of chitosan/β-glucan nanoparticles as a vaccine delivery system and to evaluate their effectiveness in enhancing the immune response.

Materials and methods

Fish and ethical statement

In total, 300 healthy fingerlings (7–10 cm) of Nile Tilapia of 12±2 g were procured from the Rajiv Gandhi Centre for Aquaculture, Vallarpadam, Kochi, Kerala, India. Fishes were transported to the lab in sterile conditionswith aeration, and the fishwere acclimatized for 14 days to the laboratory conditions and fed with 0.8 mm commercial pellet feed (Grow Well Feeds Private Limited, Andhra Pradesh, India) before the vaccine trial. The water was exchanged 2 times a week, and the standard water quality parameters were analyzed once a week. This work has been approved by the Institutional Animal Ethical Committee of Cochin University of Science and Technology, Cochin, Kerala. CCSEA Approval no: 363/GO/Re/S/01/CCSEA/86. Water quality parameters [25] such as dissolved oxygen, pH, ammonia content, and temperature, were monitored throughout the experimental duration.

Preparation of formalin-killed vaccine and confirmation of inactivation

The S. agalactiae (Sero type III) MLST283bacterial strains were obtained from the Centre for Excellence of Aquatic Vaccine Development, Department of Marine Biology, Microbiology and Biochemistry, School of Marine Sciences, Cochin University of Science and Technology, Cochin, Kerala, India [26]. The virulent strain was cultured in 100 ml Tryptone Soy Broth (TSB) (Sigma, USA) media at 27 °C with 200 rpm. Formalin-killed S. agalactiae (Formalin-inactivated bacterium) was prepared following [27] With modifications. Briefly, 0.5 % formalin was added to the 1 O.D. (1 × 109 CFU/ml) bacterial culture (at 610 nm) and incubated overnight at 27 °C for 48 h with continuous rotation at 200 rpm, then the culture was kept overnight at 4 °C. Afterwards, the culture was centrifuged at 7000 x g; at 4 °C for 30 min. After centrifugation with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), the pellets were washed 3 times and then suspended in one-third PBS (33 ml) of the initial volume to obtain the final concentration of 3 × 109 CFU/ mL.

Furthermore, the inactivation of the vaccine was ensured by the spread plate method. Formalin-inactivated and encapsulated vaccines were spread (100 µL) on TSB agar plates and incubated at 27 °C for 72 hrs.

Chitosan (Cs)/βeta-glucan (β-g) nano-particles synthesis

Nanoparticles were synthesized by following [28]Withslight modifications. Chitosan and β-glucan (Cs/βg) were taken at a concentration of 2:1 ratio, where β-glucan (2 mg/mL) was dissolved in 100 mL of deionized water andsonicated for 5 min using a probe sonicator(Fisher brand™ Model 705 sonic Dismembrator, USA.Sonicated β-g was filtered using a 0.22μmsyringe filter. Further, 2 mg/mL of the sonicated β-g is added to the aqueous solution of Chitosan (Cs) on a hot plate magnetic stirrer at 800 rpm over 60 minutes at room temperature. Then this was centrifuged at 15,000 x g for 30 min, and the pellet was washed with PBS, then lyophilizedand used for further analysis.

Biophysical characterizations of chitosan and β-glucan (Cs/β-g) nano-particles

The structural characteristics of the Chitosan, β-glucan (Cs/β-g) nano-particles wereanalysed by Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) equipped with a backscattered electron detector at 20.00 kV on carbon tape. For Microscopic images, the samples were dried using a lyophiliser(Labconco™FreeZone™ 4.5 L - 84 °C Benchtop Freeze Dryers, 230 V Models). The Fourier Transform Infrared Spectra (FTIR) analysis of Chitosan, β-glucan, and to assess how Cs and β-glucan cross-link, chitosan and β-glucan were recorded at a resolution of 4 cm−1 using KBr pellets on a Perkin-Elmer FTIR spectrometer (IR Version 10.6.00). The Cs/β-g nanoparticles were manually pulverized in a mortar using a pestle after being combined with KBr at a 1:150 ratio. Pellets were formed from the powder using a 4t pressure. The IR absorbency scans were examined for variations in sample peak intensity between 400 and 4000 cm−1.

Vaccine-coated feed preparation and safety evaluation

Before the immunization experiment, the prepared vaccines, named Formalin inactivated Bacterin, Cs/β-g, and Chitosan/β-glucan nano vaccine (Cs/β-g V), were mixed according to the amount of feed (3 % of fish body weight). 0.01 ml of formulated vaccine (concentration 3 × 109 CFU/ml) was mixed with 5 g of feed. In concept, 1 g of feed will contain 3 × 107 CFU. Followed by the spray-drying method, the vaccine was mixed [29] with the 0.8 mm commercial pellet feed (Grow Well Feeds Private Limited, Andhra Pradesh, India) and dried for 3 h at 28 °C.

Next, 10 fish were fed each Cs/β-g nanoparticle-coated feed with or without vaccine for two weeks to ensure the safety of oral administration by observing behavioral changes and any adverse effects.

The fish were acclimatized two weeks before the experimental trial in 2500 L aerated FRP tanks and fed with a commercial diet. Experimental groups and their description.

Experimental design.

The experimental group (G1-G4) was fed with supplemented feed and the control group (G1) was fed with non-supplemented feed for initial 1–7 days of the vaccination trial, followed normal diet on 8–14 days, and then a booster vaccination was given for subsequent 7 days (15–21) of the trial and again normal diet was given on 22–28 days. The experimental fish were fed three times a day at a total of 3 % of their body weight and constantly monitored for the intake of feed every day throughout the experiment.

S. agalactiae challenge was carried out on 30 Dp.v. Fishes were injected with 100 µl of virulent bacterial cells (dose 1 × 107CFU/mL). The relative percentage of survival was calculated during 15 days after the challenge,and RPS was calculated by following the formula RPS ( %) = (1 - ( % Mortality of vaccinated group / % Mortality of control group)) x 100 (Mohd [30]).

Blood and tissue sample collection for immunological analysis

In the present study, the nonspecific immune factors were estimated on days 7, 14, 21, and 28 Dp.v. Animals were starved for 12 hrs before blood collection. Blood was collected by caudal peduncle using a 22-gauge needle after anaesthesia (50 ppm) MS-222 (Sigma, USA.) and transferred into sterile tubes, which were kept at room temperature for 30 min to coagulate. The serum was prepared by the following [31]. Briefly, the blood samples were centrifuged (3000 x g for 15 min) at 4 °C to segregate the serum. Then the samples were stored at - 20 °C for further immunological assays. The lymphoid organ spleen was collected for RT-qPCR on 14 and 28 Dp.v.Fishesand stored in a 1.5 mL Eppendorf tube containing RNA Later (Sigma, USA) and stored in −80 °C for further use.

Determination of serum lysozyme activity

Serum lysozyme activity was assessed accordingly [32] with little adjustments. Briefly, in a 96-well microplate, 90 µl of 0.3 mg/mL of Micrococcus lysodeikticus(Sigma Aldrich- USA) in 0.1 M Citrate phosphate buffer is added with 10 µl serum and mixed. After mixing turbidity change is observed at 60 s and240 s at 540 nm using a plate reader. And by the following equation, serum lysozyme activity was measured.Lysozymeactivity(Units/ml)=Differenceinabsorbanceon60sec−240sec×1000×1TxS

Where, T = Total time and S = Amount of serum taken (µl)

Determination of serum myeloperoxidase activity

Myeloperoxidase activity was measured with [33,34] with slight modifications. Briefly, 10 µL of serum was loaded into the microplate in triplicate, and 140 µL of Hank’s buffer solution (HBSS) without Mg+2 or Ca+2 was added. Afterward 45 µl of 3,3′,55′- (TMB) tetramethylbenzidine (Sigma Aldrich) was added along with 5 µL of 30 % H2O2, and color appearance was noted, and 50 µL of 2 M H2SO4 was added to terminate the reaction then optical density was observed at 450 nm by a plate reader (Varioskan Lux, Thermo Scientific, USA).

Estimation of superoxide dismutase(sod) activity

The SOD activity was measured [35] by β-mercaptoethanol with the substrate of Mn and EDTA. The test was run by adding 50 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) of 80 µL, followed by 5.5 µL of (100/50 mM) EDTA/Mn, along with 4 µL of 7.5 mM NADH was added to 10 µl of the serum sample. For initiating the response of the reaction, 100 µL of prepared 10 mM β-mercaptoethanol was added. At 340 nm, the NADH absorbance changes for each per minute were noted.

IgM level in fish serum against S. agalactiae

The amount of IgM in serum was estimatedby the ELISA method [36]. In brief, 50 µL of poly-l-Lysine (0.05 %) mixed in sodium bicarbonate buffer was added to a 96-well plate and incubated for two hours at room temperature, followed by washing step, 100 µL of inactivated bacteria S. agalactiae(107 CFU/mL)cells were coated in each well with coating buffer and incubated overnight at 4 °C, further washing step was carried out by three times in PBS and 0.02 % of Tween-20 (Sigma Aldrich, USA) followed by 100 µL serum at a dilution of 1: 128 ratio coated and incubated for 18 h at 4 °C. Next,2 % of bovine serum albumin (BSA, Sigma USA) was added and incubated. Washing was done with PBS and Tween 20 three times. After washing, 100 µL of anti-Nile tilapia IgM Monoclonal antibody (Vertebrate Antibodies Limited, UK) at a 1:2500 dilution was coated in each well and incubated at 37 °C for incubation. And secondary goat anti-mouse IgG antibody containing horse radish peroxidase (Bio-Rad, USA) was added to the well plate at a 1:4000 dilution for 1 h of incubation at 37 °C. Finally, TMB (3,3,5,5 – 3,3,5,5-tetramethylbenzidine), a substrate, was used in the reaction to generate the color. Further,50 µL of 0.5 M H2SO4 was added to stop the reaction,and OD was observed at 450 nm in a plate reader (Varioskan Lux, Thermo Scientific, USA).

The clear aqueous layer was transferred to a new sterile tube and precipitated using ice-cold isopropanol (Sigma) following centrifugation at 12,000 g for 15 min. The RNA pellet was further washed using 70 % ethanol by centrifugation. The RNA pellet was dissolved in 100 μLDEPC-treated water and cDNA synthesis was conducted with QuantiNova Reverse Transcriptase kit (QIAGEN, UK) by following the manufacturer's protocol. Each reaction was performed in duplicate with three biological replicates. Histopathological analysis

Organs(Kidney, brain, and Spleen) of the challenged fish were examined for histopathological analysis of the S. agalactiae infection. Samples were collected and preserved in10 % formalin fixative. The embedding by the paraffin method was followed [42] for histology examination. A section of tissue was stained with H&E (hematoxylin and eosin) stain and examined under microscopic imaging.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using R software version 4.4.1 for the determination of significance among the treatment groups. The study employed one-way ANOVA with Duncan multiple ranges to analyze statistical differences. Different alphabetical letters were used to express statistical significance at the value of p < 0.05. Survival curve was performed with the Mantel-Coxlog-rank test.

Results

Vaccine inactivation and safety test

No colony growth was found, which confirmed the inactivation of the bacteria and ensured the safety of the prepared vaccine. Furthermore, no impacts of Cs/β-g and vaccine on fish were evidenced by observations of behaviour, faeces, and mortality after 14 days of trial and the feed safety experimental period.

Transform infrared spectra (FTIR) analysis

The samples were analyzed for FTIR with references; Cs,βg, and Cs/β-g nanoparticles exhibited a spectrum ranging from 6000 to 400 cm-1. When the IR spectra of Cs or βg platforms are combined with a Cs/β-g nanoparticle, the elongation of Cs and βgC—C-groupsstretches to 996.28 cm-1to 999.96 cm-1, respectively, on Cs/β-g nanoparticle spectra. Carbonyl (C = O) of Cs subordinate amide (amide I band) at 16,354.32 cm-1 was also seen, along with a drop in the retention band as shown in Fig. 2. The extension of -OH and -CH- in Cs (2123.97 cm-1 and 3336.72 cm-1) and βg (1146.9 cm-1and 1240.14 cm-1) both increased and became broader. Polyelectrolyte complex was produced by electrostatic coupling of the carboxylic binding of βg combined with the ammonium binding of Cs, resulting in aCs/β-g nanoparticle. The shift of 2123.97 cm-1to 2111.10 cm-1-CH-stretch of Cs confirms the interaction and the binding of the Cs/β-g nanoparticle.Fig. 2FTIR Spectrum analysis of Cs/β-g,which shows the binding of the carbonyl groupand the positively charged chitosan amino groups.Fig 2:

Structural morphology of the nano vaccine

The SEM images (Fig. 3) reveal that the synthesized nanoparticles exhibit a distinct bullet-shaped morphology, uniformly distributed with minimal agglomeration, indicating a successful synthesis. The high-resolution SEM at 12000x magnification and a frame size of 2 μm provides a clear view of the smooth surface and shape characteristics, with no significant defects or irregularities. This analysis confirms the successful synthesis of bullet-shaped nanoparticles with uniform size and morphology, offering valuable insights into their structural integrity and surface characteristics, thereby demonstrating the efficacy of the synthesis method employed. Discussion

The study shows the effectiveness of the delivery carrier in the oral vaccination of fish. Effective vaccination programs have been shown to reduce disease outbreaks, improve growth rates, and increase profitability in aquaculture operations [39]. Vaccination routes significantly impact efficacy and convenience in aquaculture [43]. In this study, β-glucan (β-g) was chosen as an antigen carrier and stable biomaterial isolated from natural sources, known for its immunomodulatory effects and use as a vaccine adjuvant [41]. To improve the stability of β-g, chitosan (Cs) was utilized due to its antigen-carrying ability in the oral route of fish. Cs is a cationic polysaccharide that binds easily to anionic β-g particles. The stability, size, shape, surface, and composition play crucial roles in determining their activity and potency as an antigen carrier [44].

Structural and functional group analysis using FTIR and SEM confirmed the successful synthesis and interaction of Cs/β-g nanoparticles. The shift of functional groups in Cs and β-g was observed in the Cs/β-g synthesized particles. The bullet-shaped morphology, uniformly distributed with minimal agglomeration, indicates successful synthesis and confirms the stability of the synthesized particle [45]. The purpose of vaccination is to ensure a strong immune response while considering safety [46]. This is crucial for ensuring the vaccine's efficacy and safety, where the synthesized particle is safe to be used as a delivery carrier. Previous studies utilizing biomaterials such as chitosan, alginate, and β-glucan did not show toxic effects on various fish species [47,48]. Additionally, these nanoparticles are biocompatible and biodegradable, making them safe for use in aquaculture. Ongoing research focuses on optimizing β-glucan-based vaccineformulations for commercial application, providing a sustainable solution to mitigate disease-related losses in aquaculture.

The innate immunity isvery essential in offering the first line of defense against various invading microorganisms (pathogens) [49]. It significantly contributes to the earlier prevention of infections by recognizing a diverse range of pathogens [50]. Efficacy and impact of the vaccine on fish innate immunity were evaluated by including lysozyme activity, myeloperoxidase activity, antiprotease activity, and superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity in the immunized fish serum [51,52]. Lysozyme, a crucial element of the innate immune system, prevents the growth and invasion of pathogenic microorganisms [53]. Myeloperoxidase, a potent antimicrobial agent, aids in destroying invading pathogens (bacteria, fungi, and viruses) during infections and is highly found in neutrophils [54]. Superoxide dismutase, an antioxidant enzyme, converts superoxide molecules into oxygen and hydrogen peroxide, neutralizing and protecting cells from oxidative damage [55]. This better innate immune response contributes to increased survival against S. agalactiae.

In rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss), [56] observed that feeding diets containing β-glucan before immunization with an anti-Yersinia ruckeri vaccine significantly increased specific antibody-secreting cells (ASCs) and immunoglobulin levels compared to controls. Similarly, liposomes and poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) nanoparticles have demonstrated efficacy and reported similar findings in rohu fish (Labeo rohita), where a diet supplemented with β-glucan enhanced non-specific and specific immunity, improving resistance to Aeromonas hydrophila [57]. Similarly, [58] demonstrated that brewer’s yeast improved immune responses in prawns (Macrobrachium rosenbergii), while [59] showed combined immune benefits in red sea bream (Pagrus major) treated with Lactobacillus plantarum and β-glucan.

Evaluating specific Immunoglobulin M antibody titre in Tilapia serum is a widely accepted methodology to investigate vaccine efficacy, as it gives essential insights into the immune response triggered by the vaccine [60]. Immunoglobulin M, a crucial molecule of the humoral immune system, serves as the primary antibody in the serum of fish [61]. It plays a pivotal role in the immune response by binding to pathogens and aiding in their clearance from the fish. Additionally, IgM activates the complement system, which enhances the killing of pathogens. Being the primary defence antibody produced in response to vaccination, IgM levels are a key indicator of vaccine efficacy [62]. Therefore, detecting IgM levels is critical for evaluating immune response and the protective efficacy of vaccines. Our results showed that elevated IgM levels with higher significance were observed in G4 compared with the G 1at both time intervals, demonstrating the efficacy of the delivery carrier in delivering the antigen. Significantly, IgM levels serve as a key indicator of the adaptive immune response, which is essential for establishing long-term immunity against bacterial infections [40]. This pivotal role highlights the importance of monitoring IgM levels to assess the effectiveness of immune responses in providing lasting protection. These results support the reason for the survival rate of the challenged fish in the vaccinated groups compared to the control group (G1). Several markers are under study for further evaluation. The efficacy of the vaccine stands with the regulation of immunity at the gene level; both innate and adaptive immunity play vital roles in vaccine efficacy [63]. The expression levels of immune-related genes (TCR-β, IL-1β, IL-8, TNF-α, IgM) in the spleen, as analyzed on days 14 and 28 post-vaccination (D.p.v.) using RT-qPCR, showed significant upregulation in G4 compared to G1. Notably, the expression of all tested genes peaked at 28 Dp.v. following the booster vaccination. The results show the activation of both innate and adaptive immune responses, as well as immunoglobulin genes. The expression pattern demonstrates the effectiveness and importance of the booster dose in oral vaccination and highlights the significance of the delivery carrier in oral vaccination strategies. This is similar to other studies where vaccines have been shown to induce both humoral (antibody-mediated) and cell-mediated immunity [64,65].

Research has shown that β-glucan/chitosan nanoparticles serve as efficient carriers for antigen delivery, improving strong physicochemical properties, protein association efficiency, and thermostability of the antigens [66]. Other studies, such as those [67]in sea bass (Dicentrarchus labrax) and [68]in rainbow trout, confirmed enhanced antibody titers with β-glucan supplementation. Histopathological examinations confirm the pathogenic effect of the infection and the vaccine's efficacy. Improved histological signs from the pathological condition were observed in G4, whereas G1 showed histopathological conditions mimicking the natural infection of S. agalactiae [69]. These results demonstrate the vaccine's effect under pathogen entry conditions, with improved histological changes evident in the vaccinated group, underscoring the vaccine's efficacy against S. agalactiae.The survival rates observed over the 15-day challenge experiment were 33.33 %, 51.11 %, 62.22 %, and 75.56 % for the orally vaccinated treatment groups (G1 to 4, respectively). The moderate increase in survival observed in Group 3 (Cs/β-g) is likely due to the non-specific immunostimulatory effects of the Chitosan/ β-glucan rather than antigen-specific immunity. These components may enhance innate defense mechanisms and mucosal barrier integrity, contributing to partial protection. The application of these nanoparticles in aquatic vaccines offers several benefits, including increased vaccine stability, controlled release, and reduced antibiotic use.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates the remarkable efficacy of chitosan/β-glucan (Cs/β-g) nanoparticles as a vaccine delivery carrier for oral vaccination in Nile Tilapia. The comprehensive analysis, including FTIR and SEM, confirmed the successful synthesis these nanoparticles. Immunological assessments showed significant upregulation of immune- genes (TCR-β, IL-1β, IL-8, TNF-α, IgM) and increased levels of innate immune responses, highlighting the importance of these nanoparticles in triggering a robust adaptive immune response. Histopathological examinations further validated the vaccine's protective effects, showing improved tissue health in vaccinated fish compared to controls. Overall, the Cs/β-g nanoparticle vaccine significantly enhanced the immune response and survival rates against S. agalactiae infection. These findings support the potential of chitosan and β-glucan nanoparticles as effective and safe vaccine delivery systems in aquaculture, offering a promising approach for sustainable disease management and improved fish health.This study promotes the use of environmentally friendly biomaterials in aquaculture as part of other vaccination techniques to improve disease resistance.

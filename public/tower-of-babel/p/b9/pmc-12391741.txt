Lack of association between MALAT1 rs591291 and rs3200401 polymorphisms and venous malformation risk in the Chinese pediatric population
Authors: Xi Lin, Jiantu Ou, Guitao Wu, Zhenyin Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionVenous malformations are the most common congenital vascular anomaly, accounting for 65 % of all congenital vascular abnormalities. This study aims to investigate the correlation between genetic polymorphisms of MALAT1 and the risk of developing venous malformations.MethodsWe collected samples and clinical data from a Chinese pediatric population (1113 patients and 1158 controls). Using real-time quantitative PCR, we performed TaqMan genotyping on MALAT1 rs591291 C > T and rs3200401 C > T polymorphisms and further analyzed the data through statistical methods.ResultsSequencing results and analysis indicate that the MALAT1 rs591291 C > T and rs3200401 C > T polymorphisms are not significantly correlated with the risk of venous malformation. When combined both genotypes also present no statistic difference in increased risk of developing these anomalies (odds ratio = 0.90, 95 % confidence interval = 0.72–1.13, P = 0.354). Stratified analysis of different subtypes of venous malformations revealed that there was no statistic difference in the selected polymorphisms of MALAT1 across these subtypes.ConclusionOur findings suggested that MALAT1 rs591291 C > T and rs3200401 C > T polymorphisms were not correlated to the risk of venous malformation in the Chinese pediatric population.Keywords: Venous malformations, Susceptibility, MALAT1, Polymorphism

Introduction

Venous malformations are prevalent type of vascular anomaly constitute 65 % of all congenital vascular anomalies, with an incidence of 1–5 per 10,000 and a prevalence of 1 % [1]. Venous malformations are composed of dilated and tortuous veins, characterized by loosely and irregularly arranged smooth muscle in the vessel walls [2,3]. Venous malformations can occur in any tissue or organ of the body and are typically present at birth. Common lesions present in various regions, including the head and neck (40 %), extremities (40 %), and trunk (20 %). Clinical symptoms in affected children are varied depending on the location of the lesions, ranging from asymptomatic and no functional varicosity to severe functional impairment or life-threatening hemorrhage [[4], [5], [6]]. Venous malformations are primarily sporadic gene variants originating from somatic variants [7].

Over the past decade, numbers of genes related to the pathogenesis of venous malformations have been confirmed, including TIE2 [8], PIK3CA [9], PTEN [10], MAP3K3 [11] and KRAS [12]. The PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway is the most commonly mutated in venous malformations [13]. These genetic alterations often correlate with the uncontrolled proliferation and differentiation of endothelial cells. The resulting endothelial hyperplasia leads to the formation of an abnormal vascular network, which is a hallmark of venous malformations [7]. However, variants in these genes account for only a portion of venous malformation cases [14]. The specific etiology and pathogenesis of the remaining cases require further investigation to fully elucidate the underlying mechanisms driving this vascular anomaly.

Metastasis-Associated Lung Adenocarcinoma Transcript 1 (MALAT1) is a long non-coding RNA (lncRNAs) which located on chromosome 11q13.1. This transcript is approximately 8 kilobases in length and belongs to the class of intergenic transcripts [15]. MALAT1 is expressed under the control of multiple promoters. Researchers have highlighted the diverse roles of MALAT1 in cancer progression and dissemination, including lung cancer, breast cancer, and colorectal cancer [16,17]. Furthermore, various research articles have underscored the significant impact of MALAT1 on angiogenesis across different contexts [15,18]. MALAT1 facilitates endothelial cell proliferation, apoptosis, and angiogenesis, mediated through its interaction with VEGF or the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway. Furthermore, research has shown that MALAT1 involved in vascular remodeling following ischemic injury in hindlimb ischemia [18]. This suggests the intricate adaptability of MALAT1 across different angiogenic scenarios and emphasizes the necessity of understanding the molecular mechanisms that govern this angiogenesis interaction. Junpei Kawamura et al. analyzed serum miRNA expression in children with pulmonary arteriovenous malformations following congenital heart disease surgery and found that miR-25-3p, which is predicted to interact with MALAT1 according to the StarBase database (https://rnasysu.com/encore/), leads to upregulation of HIF-1α and VEGF-A expression in microvascular endothelial cells and is involved in the formation of pulmonary arteriovenous malformations [19,20]. In addition, miR-135b-5p and miR-145 have likewise been implicated in the pathogenesis of human arteriovenous and venous malformations [21,22]. These two miRNAs are also considered potential downstream targets of MALAT1.

Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) are widely acknowledged for direct regulation of lncRNAs expression and the consequent modulation of their functional roles. Specifically, SNPs in MALAT1 have been reported in the predisposition to various conditions, including pulmonary arterial hypertension [23], coronary atherosclerotic heart disease [24] and colorectal cancer [25]. However, the potential contribution of MALAT1 polymorphisms to the risk of venous malformations remains unclear. Given the potential of various SNPs in MALAT1 to regulate angiogenesis, this study aims to explore genetic polymorphism of MALAT1 rs591291 C > T and rs3200401 C > T that correlate with susceptibility to venous malformations in South Chinese pediatric population.

Patients and methods

Study subjects and design

In the present study, 1113 cases of venous malformations were collected from 2010 to 2015; 1158 healthy controls were also enrolled [26,27]. Children diagnosed with venous malformations were identified and recruited from the outpatient clinic of the Guangzhou Women and Children's Hospital, Guangzhou Medical University. All medical records, imaging examinations, radiography, and classifications were evaluated by experienced clinicians and pathologists, and patients with inherited venous malformations or a documented history of genetic diseases were excluded. The case group was categorized into six subgroups based on the site of the lesions, including the head, neck, trunk, upper limbs, lower limbs, and multiple lesions. Patients were diagnosed with venous malformations based on pathological results, clinical symptoms, and imaging examinations. Control subjects were selected among individuals without a medical history of venous malformations or neurological disorders. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Guangzhou Women and Children's Hospital (ethics certificate number: 201943800). Legal guardian of each participant provided written informed consent.

SNP selection and genotyping

Peripheral venous blood specimens were collected from participants and stored at −20 °C until use. Collecting peripheral venous blood samples of the participants, we extracted Genomic DNA by a TIANamp Blood Genomic DNA Kit (TIANGEN Biotec, Beijing, China) [28,29]. Subsequently, the extracted genomic DNA was amplified by real-time fluorescent quantitative polymerase chain reaction (PCR) using a cDNA synthesis kit (Takara, Japan) and ABI-7900 instrument (Applied Biosystems, California, USA). We screened potentially functional SNPs from the dbSNP database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) and SNPinfo (http://snpinfo.niehs.nih.gov/) based on the selection criteria: (1) the minor allele frequency (MAF) reported in HapMap was >5 % for Chinese subjects (Han Chinese in Beijing, CHB); (2) affecting transcription factor binding sites (TFBS) activity in certain promoter region (2-kb upstream from the exon). As the result of screening, three SNPs (rs3200401, rs4102217, rs591291) complied with these criteria. After calculating linkage disequilibrium (LD) among these selected SNPs in the MALAT1 gene (Fig. S1) via LDlink online tool (https://ldlink.nci.nih.gov/?tab=ldmatrix), we found that rs3200401 was in high LD with rs4102217 (R2 = 0.965) and there was no significant linkage disequilibrium (R2 < 0.8) between the other SNPs (R2 = 0.242 between rs591291 and rs4102217, R2 = 0.251 between rs591291 and rs3200401). Because allele frequency of rs4102217 was too high in dbSNP for Asian subjects (allele frequency = 0.994) and HapMap for Chinese subjects (allele frequency = 1), we excluded rs4102217 and selected rs591291 and rs3200401 finally.

According to the dbSNP database (GRCh38), rs591291 was located on chr11:65497011 and rs3200401 was located on chr11:65504361. The primers of rs591291 (Assay ID: C_1057357_10) and rs3200401 (Assay ID: C_3246069_10) were shown in Table S1 (Catalog: 4351379, ThermoFisher Scientific, Massachusetts, USA). Utilizing the TaqMan method, we genotyped on the selected polymorphisms and performed second round genotyping of a randomly subset of 10 % of the DNA samples to validate the accuracy [26,30]. The accuracy of our genotyping data was confirmed on the grounds that the concordance of the replicated samples was 100 %. Moreover, we compared the MAF of the genotyped SNPs with global MAF (Southern Han Chinese, CHS) through the data from LDlink online tool (https://ldlink.nih.gov/?tab=govldpop). The MAF of rs591291 in our study was T = 38.84 % and basically consistent with the global MAF which was T = 44.76 %. While the MAF of rs3200401 in our study was T = 14.31 % and basically consistent with the global MAF which was T = 15.71 %.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis of the research data was conducted using SAS software (version 9.4; SAS Institute Inc., North Carolina, USA). We employed a two-sided chi-square test to analyze genotypes stratified by gender and ascertain the extent of deviation between case and control groups. We applied the goodness-of-fit chi-squared test to evaluate whether the genotype frequencies in the control group conformed to Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE). Additionally, we performed multivariate logistic regression analysis to compute the adjusted odds ratio (OR) and 95 % confidence interval (CI), thereby estimating the relationship between MALAT1 polymorphisms and the risk of venous malformations. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

Results

Baseline demographics and correlation between selected SNPs and venous malformation risk

Our study included a cohort of 1113 Chinese children diagnosed with venous malformations (mean age: 68.98 ± 37.08 months; 54.90 % boys) and 1158 healthy controls (22.22 ± 19.98 months; 56.82 % boys). We successfully genotyped the MALAT1 polymorphisms in 1079 patients and 1147 controls. To address the potential gender-specific association of MALAT1 polymorphisms, the distribution of genotypes across genders was shown in Table 1. There were no significant differences among sex and various genotypes revealing that the cases and controls were adequately matched. As presented in Table 2, the distributions of the genotype frequency of MALAT1 were consistent with HWE (P = 0.083 for rs591291 C > T, P = 0.989 for rs3200401 C > T). We respectively explored the venous malformations risk in genotypic, dominant, additive, recessive, over-dominant, allele, homozygote and heterozygote models. The comprehensive analysis results indicated that no significant differences comparing between venous malformation cases and controls regardless of whether age and gender biases are eliminated or not. Upon combining the two SNPs, we observed that individuals with 1–2 genotypes did not exhibit a statistically significant increased risk of venous malformation at an OR of 0.90 (95 % CI = 0.72–1.13, P = 0.354) (Table 2). Therefore, the results suggested that the rs591291 C > T and rs3200401C > T genotypes did not contribute to increased susceptibility to developing venous malformations.

Stratification analysis of selected SNPs and diverse venous malformations subtypes

To further investigate the relationship of these SNPs with various anatomical subtypes, we performed the stratification analysis which classified based on the location of the lesion, lesion, including the head, neck, trunk, upper limbs, lower limbs, and multiple lesions. After adjusting for age and sex biases, stratification analysis revealed that there was no notable distinction in the two selected MALAT1 polymorphisms across these anatomical subtypes (Table 3). These results depicted that the rs591291 and rs3200401genotypes of MALAT1 were not contributing factor to venous malformations susceptibility.

Discussion

The development of personalized diagnosis and treatment of venous malformations is limited by complex and varied clinical manifestations coupled with unclear pathogenesis. Our case-control study focused on Chinese pediatric population and inspected the correlation between MALAT1 polymorphisms and susceptibility to venous malformations. To our knowledge, rs591291 and rs3200401 polymorphisms have not been investigated in any previous study of venous malformations. Our study provides evidence that there is no significant correlation between the rs591291 C > T and rs3200401 C > T polymorphisms and the risk of venous malformations in the Chinese pediatric population.

Significant advancements in genetic research have progressively unveiled the pivotal role of genetic factors in the development of venous malformations. Over the past decade, several susceptibility genes associated with the pathogenesis of venous malformations have been identified. Based on the International Society for the Study of Vascular Anomalies (ISSVA) official classification, TEK and PIK3CA take the lead in causal genes for venous malformations [31]. TEK, also referred to as TIE2 or HYK, encodes a highly conserved tyrosine kinase receptor that is crucial for angiogenesis and the maturation of blood vessels [13]. Variants in TEK are widely recognized as the most common genetic alterations in venous malformations, accounting for 41.3 % of the total prevalence and 70.3 % of cases occur in the head and neck region [31]. Prevalent variant sites within TEK include Y897, L914F, R915, and R918, which can lead to aberrant activation of the PI3K/AKT pathway upon ligand-activated phosphorylation. This dysregulation affects a critical segment of the signaling pathways associated with the TIE2 receptor [13,32]. Additionally, PIK3CA has been implicated as another gene frequently mutated in common venous malformations, accounting for 26.7 % of cases and 2.7 % occurring specifically in the head and neck region [9]. PIK3CA is an isoform of the PI3K protein which act as phosphatidylinositol kinase and regulate the activity of mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway [33].

Nevertheless, the intrinsic frequency distribution of target somatic SNPs remains elusive due to the restricted sample sizes in studies focusing on vascular anomalies. A proportion of patients (26–40 %) harbor rare variants not involving variants in either TEK or PIK3CA [34]. For instance, a pathogenic variant located at c.1723T > C, p.Tyr575His in MAP3K3 gene was detected in the somatic genome DNA of individuals with venous malformations [11]. Whole exome sequencing revealed that approximately 80 % of patients diagnosed with familial cerebral cavernous malformations (FCCM) carried heterozygous germline mutations, including CCM1/KRIT1 at 7q21-22, CCM2/MGC4607 at 7p13-15, or CCM3/PDCD10 at 3q25 [35].

MALAT1 is a long non-coding RNA located at chromosome 11q13.1 and belongs to the category of intergenic transcripts. Studies have highlighted MALAT1 as a highly upregulated lncRNA with significant impact on angiogenesis in various diseases. For instance, MALAT1 promotes endothelial cell proliferation, apoptosis, and angiogenesis through interactions with the VEGF or PI3K/Akt signaling pathways [15]. MALAT1 reduces ischemic brain damage by regulating angiogenesis-related factors such as 15-LOX1, VEGF, and STAT3. Additionally, MALAT1 acts as a competitive endogenous RNA for miR-26b, directly upregulating the expression of the autophagy factor ULK2, thereby reducing damage to cerebral microvascular endothelial cells [36]. Different SNPs in lncRNAs may alter their expression and regulate corresponding functions. Previous studies have reported associations between SNPs in MALAT1 and susceptibility to pulmonary arterial hypertension [23], coronary atherosclerotic heart disease [24] and colorectal cancer [25]. However, there has been insufficient research on the relationship between SNPs in MALAT1 and the risk of venous malformations. Considering the potential of MALAT1 polymorphism to regulate angiogenesis, we aimed to investigate whether common genetic polymorphisms in MALAT1 are associated with the risk of venous malformations. The results of this study indicated that two common SNPs, MALAT1 rs591291 C > T and rs3200401 C > T, were not significantly associated with the risk of venous malformations in the Southern Chinese pediatric population.

In the present study, the participants were recruited from the Han Chinese population residing in Southern China, and the sample size was limited, which introduced certain biases to the experimental results. The potential differences in allele frequencies among different ethnic groups and the patterns of SNP linkage disequilibrium could attribute to deviation in genetic susceptibility differences. Additionally, we did not analyze the correlation between venous malformations and environmental factors such as the effects of diet and geographical location. Therefore, future studies should further explore more gene polymorphisms and conduct more comprehensive analyses in larger sample sizes from multicenter studies.

In summary, our study indicates that MALAT1 rs591291 C > T and rs3200401 C > T are not correlated with the risk of venous malformations in the Chinese pediatric population. The roles of other SNPs in the development and pathogenesis of venous malformations remain to be explored. Future research should elucidate the pathogenic mechanisms and clinical features of identified genetic variants and ultimately improve the diagnostic efficiency and prognostic outcomes of venous malformations.

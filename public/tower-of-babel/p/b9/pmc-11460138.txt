The effect of empagliflozin (sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 inhibitor) on osteoporosis and glycemic parameters in patients with type 2 diabetes: a quasi-experimental study
Authors: Jinmei Tan, Aili Guo, Keqin Zhang, Yanli Jiang, Huaning Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveDiabetic osteoporosis (DOP) is a metabolic disease that occurs in patients with diabetes due to insufficient insulin secretion. This condition can lead to sensory neuropathy, nephropathy, retinopathy, and hypoglycemic events, which can increase the risk of fractures. This study aimed to assess the effectiveness of Empagliflozin, a sodium–glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT-2) inhibitor, in treating diabetic osteoporosis (DOP) and preventing fractures.MethodsThis quasi-experimental study enrolled 100 patients with diabetic osteoporosis from February 2023 to February 2024. Participants were randomly assigned to an intervention group (n = 50) and a control group (n = 50). The intervention group received Empagliflozin in combination with symptomatic treatment, while the control group received only symptomatic treatment. The treatment duration was six months. Fasting blood glucose (FBG), 2-hour postprandial blood glucose (2 h PG), glycosylated hemoglobin A1c (Hb A1c), bone mineral density (BMD), serum phosphorus and calcium concentration were measured after the intervention and the incidence of fracture was followed up for 12 months. The data were analyzed using SPSS 23. Descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation, and percentage) and analytical methods (t test, Chi square) were also used to analyze the data.ResultsAfter six months of treatment, the intervention group exhibited significantly lower levels of FBG (P < 0.001), 2 h-PG (P = 0.001), and HbA1c (P < 0.001) than the control group. Additionally, bone mineral density, serum phosphorus, and calcium levels were significantly higher in the intervention group (P < 0.001). After a 12-months follow-up, the incidence of fractures in the intervention group was 2%, while it was 16.33% in the control group (P < 0.05).ConclusionEmpagliflozin, when combined with symptomatic treatment, demonstrates a positive clinical effect in patients with diabetic osteoporosis. The treatment effectively improves blood glucose metabolism, bone mineral density, and phosphorus and calcium metabolism, ultimately leading to a significant reduction in the incidence of fracture.

Introduction

The global prevalence of diabetes has been increasing rapidly, parallel to economic development. Estimates suggest that the number of patients with diabetes worldwide will reach 592 million by 2035 [1]. Diabetic osteoporosis (DOP) is a metabolic disease that occurs in patients with diabetes due to insufficient insulin production. This condition disrupts the metabolism of sugar, fat, protein, calcium, and phosphorus [2]. China has experienced a dramatic increase in diabetes prevalence over the past three decades. According to an epidemiological study, there are 110 million diabetic adults in China [3], with over 90% having type 2 diabetes. Long-term disorders of glycolipid and protein metabolism can lead to various complications, including damage to the eyes, kidneys, nerves, heart, and organs. These metabolic disturbances can also negatively impact bone health and increase the risk of DOP [4].

Chen et al. (2016) indicated that prolonged high blood glucose levels significantly elevated the risk of osteoporosis in individuals with diabetes. Several pathological mechanisms contribute to DOP, including insulin deficiency or dysfunction, obesity, hormonal imbalances, and complications associated with diabetes [5]. Additional risk factors for DOP include duration of diabetes [6], old age, economic level, and female gender [7].

Patients with diabetes are often older and have lower bone mass, making them more prone to fractures. Studies consistently report a higher fracture rate in patients with diabetes than in the general population [8]. Fractures are now recognized as a major complication of diabetes and have received increased attention [9, 10]. Complications of diabetes, such as sensory neuropathy, nephropathy, retinopathy, and hypoglycemic, further elevate fracture risk due to impaired balance and falls [11, 12]. Therefore, researchers are focusing on the effect of hypoglycemic drugs on the risk of fracture in patients with diabetes.

Current symptomatic treatment for DOP mainly focuses on supplementing calcium, vitamin D, and sex hormones. However, these approaches often have low hypoglycemic effects, a high risk of fractures, and overall unsatisfactory treatment outcomes [13–16]. Healthy dietary habits and regular physical exercise are very important for the prevention and treatment of type 2 diabetes (T2D) and osteoporosis. Metformin, sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, and GLP-1RA are preferred medication classes for T2D in patients with osteoporosis. Bisphosphonates, raloxifene, and teriparatide are effective in increasing BMD both in patients with and without T2D. Teriparatide is particularly effective in reducing non-vertebral fractures in patients with T2D, while raloxifene is more beneficial for reducing vertebral fractures [17].

Sodium-glucose Cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors are a new type of hypoglycemic drugs approved in Europe, the U.S, and China. Clinicians have confirmed their effectiveness in lowering blood sugar levels [18]. The mechanism of SGLT2 inhibitors involves inhibiting the reabsorption of glucose in the renal tubules, leading to increased glucose excretion in urine. This mechanism may also indirectly affect the reabsorption of electrolytes, including calcium and phosphorus, thus affecting bone metabolism. Several clinical studies have investigated the effects of SGLT-2 inhibitors on bone metabolism, BMD, and fracture risk in patients with diabetes. These drugs lower blood glucose levels by increasing urinary glucose excretion, independently of insulin, thus reducing the risk of hypoglycemia. Additionally, the osmotic diuretic effect of SGLT-2 inhibitors can contribute to weight loss [19]. Empagliflozin is a highly selective SGLT2 inhibitor. In normal individuals, approximately 180 g of urinary sugar are reabsorbed daily through the proximal convoluted tubules. Empagliflozin can promote the excretion of about 80 g of urinary sugar per day. Studies have confirmed that Empagliflozin can be used alone or in combination to reduce fasting and postprandial blood glucose and promote weight loss in patients with diabetes [20]. Unlike traditional hypoglycemic agents, Empagliflozin does not rely on the same mechanisms to lower blood sugar [21]. However, research by Paschou et al. (2017) suggests that Empagliflozin and dapagliflozin do not significantly change bone mineral density (BMD), bone markers, or fracture risk. Therefore, they appear to have a relatively neutral effect on bone metabolism [17].

Due to the increasing prevalence of type 2 diabetes and osteoporosis globally, it is necessary to investigate effective therapeutic options for these conditions. While various drugs are available to improve type 2 diabetes and prevent osteoporosis, further research is needed to determine the most effective treatment strategies. This study aimed to investigate the clinical effects of Empagliflozin in combination with symptomatic treatment on diabetic osteoporosis (DOP). Specifically, the study evaluated the impact of this treatment on glycemic (FBG, 2 h-PG and HbA1c) and osteoporosis parameters (BMD, phosphorus and calcium serum concentration, fracture prevention).

Methods

Study design, setting, and participants

This quasi-experimental study enrolled 100 patients who were receiving treatment in the General Hospital of the Yangze River Shipping, Wuhan, China from February 2023 to February 2024. Inclusion criteria were: [1] a diagnosis of diabetes in accordance with the Chinese guidelines to Prevent and Treat Type 2 Diabetes (2020 Edition) [22]; [2] a diagnose of osteoporosis in accordance with the guidelines for diagnosis and treatment of Primary Osteoporosis 2011 [23]; [3] willingness to participate in oral treatment with Empagliflozin; [4] informed consent obtained from all participants, ensuring good compliance with the treatment regimen.

Exclusion criteria were: [1] patients who did not meet the diagnostic and inclusion criteria; [2] patients with previous immune diseases, other related diseases, or severe gastrointestinal diseases that could affect bone metabolism; [3] patients who experienced severe symptoms accompanied by fractures [4], women who were pregnant or lactating; [5] patients with history of allergy to SGLT-2 inhibitors; [6] patients who had used drugs that could affect bone metabolism (glucocorticoids, bisphosphonates, estrogen receptor agonists, immunosuppressants, thiazolidinediones, other sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors, active vitamin D and calcium agents, diuretics, etc.) in the past six months; [7] those who had severe mental illness that would hinder treatment adherence; [8] patients with serious cardio-cerebrovascular diseases, hepatic, renal, or other organ dysfunction; [9] patients with diabetic acute complications.

Treatment methods

All patients received routine diabetes education, including basic hypoglycemic regimens. All patients were given metformin (Shanghai Squibb Pharmaceutical Co, Ltd, H20023370) orally, 500 mg three times a day. All patients received dietary and exercise guidance for type 2 diabetes and continued treatment for six months. The study participants were provided with specific exercise training, aiming for at least 150 min of moderate-to-intense aerobic exercise per week, spread over at least three days per week. with no more than two consecutive days without exercise [24]. To control nutritional factors, a standardized diet program was planned and provided by a nutritionist for all people with type 2 diabetes who participated in this study. Both groups received symptomatic treatment, which included a daily 600 mg calcium carbonate D3 tablet (Suzhou Yifan Pharmaceutical Co, Ltd, Chinese medicine H10950029).

The intervention group received empagliflozin (Boehringer Ingelheim Pharma Gmb H & Co KG H20201008) orally, 10 mg once a day, combined with symptomatic treatment. All treatments lasted for six months (Fig. 1).

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using SPSS 23. The study variables were described using frequency, percent, mean, and standard deviation. Given that the data met normal distribution and homogeneity of the variances, independent-t test was used to compare the outcomes between the two groups. Paired-t test was used to check the within-group changes. The chi-square test was used to compare the incidence of fractures between the two groups. A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The intervention group consisted of 25 men and 25 women aged 58 to 71. They had been living with diabetes for 3 to 7 years and had the body mass index (BMI) of 23.40 ± 1.68 kg/m2. Twenty-four participants had hypertension, 12 had coronary heart disease, and 14 had hyperlipidemia. In terms of education, 37 had upper/lower secondary education and 13 had academic degrees. The control group included 24 men and 25 women, aged 59 to 72, with similar duration of diabetes. Their BMI was 23.48 ± 1.71 kg/m2 and their history of other basic diseases was comparable to the intervention group. The educational background of the control group was also similar to the intervention group. There were no significant differences in demographic and clinical variables between the two groups (P > 0.05).

The levels of FBG, 2 h PG, and Hb A1c were not significantly different between the two groups before the study (P > 0.05). After six months of treatment, both groups experienced a significant decrease in FBG, 2 h PG, and Hb A1C levels. However, the reduction in these glycemic parameters was significantly higher in the intervention group than in the control group (P < 0.05) (Table 1).

Discussion

The rising prevalence of diabetes, driven by increased life expectancy and aging populations, has made it a major global public health concern [25]. Pain is a common manifestation of diabetes, and in severe cases, fractures can occur, leading to bone dysfunction and chronic pain [26]. DOP is a primary cause of disability in patients with diabetes [27], significantly affecting their daily lives. Key symptoms of DOP include low bone mass and changes in bone microstructure, which weaken bones and increase the risk of fractures [28]. In older adults, diabetic osteoporotic fractures are a major cause of disability and death. Patients with diabetes commonly are at a higher risk of developing DOP, a chronic complication of diabetes [29]. Older patients with DOP, especially those with osteoporotic fractures, experience a significant decline in their quality of life, imposing a heavy burden on individuals, families, and society [30]. This study investigated the clinical effects of Empagliflozin combined with symptomatic treatment in patients with DOP.

Several mechanisms contribute to the increased risk of fractures in patients with diabetes:

Insulin deficiency or resistance: Insulin promotes bone formation [31]. In diabetes, insulin resistance or insufficient secretion leads to decreased bone synthesis and metabolism.Hyperglycemia: Chronic, persistent hyperglycemia has a direct toxic effect on bone. Advanced glycation end products (AEGs) interfere with collagen cross-linking in bones, resulting in bone microstructure changes and bone brittleness [32].Diabetic complications: Chronic complications, such as diabetic microangiopathy, can disrupt bone microcirculation, resulting in long-term chronic hypoxia and bone metabolism disorders [33, 34]. Diabetic nephropathy can block the conversion of 25-hydroxyvitamin D to 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D, hindering bone mineralization [35].Increased levels of sclerostin (SO): Studies have shown higher levels of SO in diabetic patients compared to non-diabetic individuals. SO is a protein that inhibits bone formation by suppressing the activity of osteoblasts [36]. Compared to non-diabetic individuals, diabetic retinopathy can lead to vision loss or blindness, while neuropathy can cause lower limb numbness, sensory abnormalities, and pain. Both conditions may reduce physical activity and disrupt neuromuscular/skeletal interactions, leading to decreased bone mineral density [37]. Hyperglycemia can cause osmotic diuresis, increasing the excretion of calcium, phosphorus, and magnesium from the kidneys and decreasing their reabsorption, leading to the loss of bone salt. Hyperglycemia can promote the differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal cells into adipocytes, hindering osteoblast differentiation and leading to the massive production of advanced glycation end products (AEGs). AGEs induce the production of inflammatory mediators, which increase the activity of osteoclasts and bone resorption. Therefore, more effective and safer hypoglycemic drugs are needed to control blood glucose levels and reduce the risk of fractures in patients with DOP.

Traditional hypoglycemic drugs, while effective in lowering blood sugar, often have side effects such as hypoglycemia, weight gain, fluid retention, cardiovascular risks, and fracture risks, which can contribute to poor hypoglycemic control [38–41]. For osteoporosis, symptomatic support treatments such as calcium supplement have limited therapeutic effects [42]. SGLT-2 inhibitors, a newer class of hypoglycemic drugs, have gained popularity in many countries due to their effectiveness and safety profile. These drugs work by inhibiting glucose reabsorption in the kidney tubules, increasing urinary glucose excretion, and lowering blood glucose levels. SGLT1 and SGLT2 are the primary glucose transporters in the kidneys. SGLT1 is primarily involved in glucose absorption in the intestinal tract and has a limited effect on renal glucose reabsorption. In contrast, SGLT2 is responsible for approximately 90% of glucose reabsorption in the proximal renal tubules. Empagliflozin is a type of SGLT-2 inhibitor that increases the excretion of renal glucose by reducing glucose reabsorption in renal tubules, thereby lowering blood sugar levels. Therefore, this study investigated the clinical effects of empagliflozin combined with symptomatic treatment in patients with diabetic osteoporosis (DOP).

The study results indicated that patients treated with empagliflozin combined with symptomatic treatment experienced significantly lower levels of FBG, 2hPG, and HbA1c compared to the control group. The control group exhibited a remarkable increase in BMD, serum calcium, and serum phosphorus concentrations. The incidence of fractures was remarkably lower in the 12-month follow-up group. These findings suggest that empagliflozin combined with symptomatic treatment has a more remarkable effect on fracture prevention in patients with DOP. The treatment is particularly beneficial in improving blood glucose metabolism, BMD, and blood phosphorus and calcium metabolism. The effectiveness of empagliflozin can be attributed to its mechanism of action as an SGLT-2 inhibitor. By inhibiting glucose reabsorption in the renal tubules, Empagliflozin increases urinary glucose excretion and reduces blood glucose concentration. This approach can overcome the limitations of conventional symptomatic support therapy, leading to a faster and more effective improvement in patient outcomes. A foreign randomized controlled trial (RCT) evaluated the combination of empagliflozin with cigletine in patients with diabetes, suggesting that patients with higher baseline level of glycosylated hemoglobin may benefit more from empagliflozin [43]. A recent domestic clinical study suggests that SGLT2 inhibitors may be particularly effective in treating type 2 diabetes with poor blood glucose control, especially in patients with high baseline HbA1c levels [44]. Studies have demonstrated that SGLT-2 inhibitors can remarkably improve blood glucose control, both when used alone and [45] in combination with metformin [46]. Assessing bone metabolism markers in the blood can help predict the risk of fractures [47]. Donnan et al. (2019) found that combining metformin with SGLT-2 inhibitors could enhance glycemic control by reducing hepatic gluconeogenesis and decreasing renal tubular glucose reabsorption. This synergistic effect can lead to a greater reduction in HbA1c levels compared to using SGLT-2 inhibitors alone [48]. Taylor et al. have proposed a potential mechanism for the effects of SGLT-2 inhibitors on bone metabolism. By blocking SGLT-2 in proximal renal tubular epithelial cells, sodium transport is reduced, creating an increased electrochemical gradient for sodium. This gradient can enhance co-transport of phosphate and sodium. An increase in serum phosphate may stimulate parathyroid hormone (PTH) secretion, thereby enhancing bone resorption [49]. Empagliflozin, when combined with symptomatic treatment, can remarkably improve glucose metabolism, reduce blood glucose levels, and increase the concentration of blood calcium and phosphorus in patients with DOP. These improvements can help alleviate clinical symptoms, reduce the risk of fractures, and enhance quality of life. As an SGLT-2 inhibitor associated with calcium carbonate D3 tablets, empagliflozin can effectively reduce blood sugar and regulate bone metabolism, thereby decreasing the risks of fractures. Future research should focus on conducting large-scale randomized controlled trials (RCTs) to investigate the effects of hypoglycemic drugs on bone mass and quality. These studies can help clarify the effects of hypoglycemic drugs on bone metabolism, providing valuable insights for clinicians in selecting appropriate hypoglycemic drugs for patients with DOP that effectively control blood glucose and protect bone health.

Limitations

This study had some limitations. The sample size was relatively small, and the study was conducted at a single center, which could introduce potential biases. Future research should involve multicenter, large-sample prospective study to yield valuable conclusions. Additionally, the current study focused on a single drug, and future studies could explore the combination of two or more drugs to assess their potential synergistic effects in treating diabetic osteoporosis.

Conclusion

Empagliflozin, when combined with symptomatic treatment, has a positive clinical effect in patients with DOP. The treatment effectively improves blood glucose metabolism, BMD, and phosphorus and calcium metabolism, ultimately leading to a significant reduction in the incidence of fracture. Further studies are needed to assess the long-term clinical efficacy and potential complications of empagliflozin for DOP. Additionally, investigating the combination of empagliflozin with other drugs may provide valuable insights into optimizing treatment outcomes.

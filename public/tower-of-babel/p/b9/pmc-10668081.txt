When the aneurysm hits the brain of the brain surgeon
Authors: Oday Atallah, Bipin Chaurasia
Published: 2023

As soon as we think we are safe, something unexpected happens.

- Gautama Buddha.

Life is far more precarious than we give it credit for being, and how we hope to spend our time may be influenced by how much time we believe we have (Belavadi et al., 2021; Gawande, 2014). Within the realm of medicine, an intriguing and noteworthy phenomenon emerges - a paradox that underscores the capricious nature of life and the obstacles that proficient surgeons may encounter. Consider a hypothetical scenario in which highly skilled brain surgeons, with extensive expertise in their field, has devoted a significant portion of their life to intricately understanding and manipulating the intricate structure of the human brain. Unexpectedly, they find themselves confronted with a perplexing enigma that challenges their previously acquired knowledge and skills, hence presenting a unique and challenging situation. The neurosurgeon in question appears to be engaging in a process akin to unraveling a long-standing enigma, although one that pertains to his personal well-being.

Cerebral aneurysms are widely recognized for their abrupt and potentially devastating rupture, which can result in severe haemorrhage within the cranial cavity, posing a significant risk to an individual's life (Hardavella et al., 2017; Jersey and Foster, 2023). Medical professionals specializing in complex neurosurgical procedures, possessing comprehensive knowledge of the intricate network of nerves and blood arteries within the brain, are not immune to encountering the very same medical conditions they endeavour to address in their patients. The neurosurgeons have an unexpected shift in their position, transitioning from the one wielding the scalpel to becoming the subject of medical attention due to a personal health crisis. The occurrence of these unforeseen circumstances serves to underscore the paradoxical nature and vulnerability of human existence, wherein even individuals who provide aid to others might themselves succumb to the very ailments they want to alleviate.

This conundrum prompts us to reflect on the vast ambiguities of modern medicine and the inexorability of fate. As the advancement of medical knowledge and technological capabilities continues, enabling improved diagnosis and treatment of diverse medical diseases, the presence of aneurysms among brain surgeons serves as a poignant indication that medicine encompasses both scientific principles and artistic elements. This phenomenon prompts us to delve into the nuanced boundary between our existing knowledge and the realm of uncertainty. Similar to the notion that attaining perfection is an unattainable goal, one can have the belief in an asymptote that represents an ideal state towards which one continuously endeavours. The aforementioned perspective offered by Kalanithi elucidates the perpetual human endeavour to comprehend various phenomena, while acknowledging the existence of certain aspects that remain beyond complete explication (Kalanithi et al., 2017).

It's not just another medical choice when the neurosurgeon must choose how to treat their brain aneurysm. The subject at hand is of a profoundly intimate and intricate nature. Both surgical clipping and endovascular coiling are options available (Belavadi et al., 2021). This decision encompasses not only the acquisition of knowledge, but also delving into one's cognitive processes and emotional experiences. Conceptualize it as an experiential odyssey wherein neurosurgeons delve into the intricacies and convolutions of their cognitive processes, encompassing their predispositions and affective states. The process of making decisions might be likened to traversing a tightrope, necessitating a meticulous equilibrium between the potential hazards and advantages associated with each approach. The conclusion is further substantiated by their familiarity with these procedures, as they have previously employed them on other individuals. Consider the hypothetical scenario in which the surgeon contemplates: "I possess a comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms underlying these methodologies and have previously assisted numerous individuals utilizing them; however, I am uncertain as to which option would be most advantageous in my own case." In addition to examining the medical particulars, individuals are also taking into account their personal preferences and instincts. Therefore, it is not solely a matter of possessing proficient medical expertise. The integration of medical expertise with personal emotions and experiences is a major aspect of practice. This scenario highlights the intricacy of balancing professional obligations with personal identities, emphasizing the profound interrelation between physical and mental well-being.

In a situation like this, the neurosurgeon shouldn't go at it alone. They need to talk to their colleagues and work together with a team of experts from different fields. This team could include other brain surgeons, and neuroradiologists. By involving others, they can make a well-thought-out choice that puts their health and wellness first. This process of making a decision highlight something really important. Even when you know a lot about medicine and have special skills, decisions about your health can still be tough. It's like they're facing a puzzle, and they're using the knowledge of many different experts to put all the pieces together. There is no “one size fits all” approach (Hardavella et al., 2017). Think about it this way: Imagine you're a great chef, but when it comes to cooking for yourself, it's not easy to decide what to make. You might ask other chefs for their ideas because they might see things you haven't thought about. It's kind of like that for the neurosurgeon – they're getting input from others to see the full picture and make the best choice.

The surgeon is required to swiftly transition from the role of an authoritative expert to that of a patient requiring urgent medical care. The transition in responsibilities may present emotional difficulties for the surgeons, given their accustomed status as an authoritative figure responsible for making decisions on behalf of others. The process of undergoing this change entails a multifaceted interaction of emotions, deliberations, and decisions that exert a significant impact on both the surgeon's career path and personal sphere. Surgeons are accustomed to assuming leadership roles during surgical procedures and providing guidance to patients throughout their medical trajectories. However, once the neurosurgeons assume the role of patients, they are required to relinquish control and allow their medical staff to assume the leading role. This alteration has the potential to induce feelings of discomfort and susceptibility, as if a sense of agency has been relinquished. Despite possessing extensive knowledge in the field of medicine, they are currently confronted with navigating the healthcare system in a manner akin to any other individual seeking medical attention. This include attending scheduled appointments, undergoing examinations, and receiving medical interventions. Being a patient entails encountering numerous uncertainties, which might evoke fear in individuals accustomed to making decisions on in factual information and understanding. They may experience concerns regarding the potential outcomes of their therapy, potential complications that may arise, and the subsequent impact on their future. For a significant number of surgeons, their identity is closely intertwined with their profession as medical practitioners. When they are unable to perform their occupational duties due to health-related reasons, it can pose significant challenges and prompt introspection over their personal identity.

Within the complex trajectory of human existence, the unforeseen circumstance wherein neurosurgeons encounter their own aneurysms illuminates the delicate equilibrium between their medical expertise and personal capacities. The proficient medical practitioners, who adeptly traverse intricate neural networks within the brain, encounter a paradoxical predicament wherein they grapple with the very afflictions they endeavour to remedy. This intellectually stimulating scenario prompts contemplation on the inherent uncertainty of medical knowledge and the influence of destiny, so illustrating the amalgamation of scientific principles and artistic elements within the field of medicine. When a neurosurgeon assumes the role of a patient, their decision-making process becomes a confluence of their professional expertise and personal emotions. The act of requesting assistance exemplifies the integration of their professional and personal identities. This distinctive encounter enhances their capacity for empathy, enriches their intellectual acumen, and underscores the profound interconnection between the process of healing, our inherent susceptibility, and the pursuit of comprehension.

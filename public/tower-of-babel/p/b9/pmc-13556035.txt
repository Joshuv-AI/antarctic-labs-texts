Effects of concentrated growth factor and platelet-rich plasma on postoperative pain and edema following impacted mandibular third molar surgery: a randomized controlled clinical trial
Authors: Emin Valiyev, Andac Dogan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe aim of this study was to evaluate the effects of concentrated growth factor (CGF) and platelet-rich plasma (PRP) on postoperative pain and edema following impacted mandibular third molar surgery, and to investigate postoperative complications.MethodsThis prospective, randomized controlled clinical trial included a total of 42 patients divided into three groups: control, CGF, and PRP (n = 14 per group). Pain was evaluated using a 10-cm Visual Analog Scale (VAS), and edema was assessed using standard linear facial measurements on postoperative days 1, 3, and 7. Changes over time and differences between groups were analyzed using Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE).ResultsAccording to the GEE analysis, both CGF and PRP applications demonstrated a statistically significant reduction in facial edema (tragus-pogonion) compared to the control group; however, the absolute reduction was minimal (0.2–0.5 mm) and likely of limited clinical significance. These applications appear to be effective in edema control following mandibular third molar surgery (CGF: B=-1.056, 95% CI [-1.835, -0.276], p = 0.008; PRP: B=-1.002, 95% CI [-1.750, -0.254], p = 0.009). However, the clinical magnitude of the differences between the groups remained limited. Although pain scores were lower in the CGF group, they did not show a statistically significant difference between the groups. The healing process was completed uneventfully in all groups, and no alveolar osteitis or serious postoperative complications were observed in any patient during the study.ConclusionsWhile CGF and PRP applications provided a statistically significant reduction in facial edema following impacted tooth surgery, the minimal magnitude of this reduction suggests it is unlikely to meaningfully improve the patient’s postoperative quality of life. Furthermore, their effects on postoperative pain and complications did not offer an additional clinical advantage compared to a standard and atraumatic surgical procedure. Therefore, case-based evaluation is recommended rather than the routine use of these biomaterials.Trial registrationThis study was registered retrospectively at ClinicalTrials.gov under the number NCT07320170 on January 06, 2026. Background

Surgical removal of impacted mandibular third molars is one of the most common procedures in oral and maxillofacial surgery practice. However, inflammatory complications such as postoperative pain, edema, trismus, and alveolar osteitis resulting from surgical trauma can significantly impair patients’ quality of life [1–3]. Various modalities, including corticosteroids, laser therapy, and diverse biomaterials, have been widely utilized to manage these complications and accelerate tissue healing [4].

In recent years, autologous platelet concentrates derived from the patient’s own blood have gained popularity in regenerative medicine and oral surgery. Although platelet-rich plasma (PRP) promotes soft tissue healing through its growth factors, it has disadvantages such as the requirement for anticoagulants during preparation and a weak fibrin network [5–7]. In contrast, Concentrated Growth Factor (CGF), a newer generation concentrate, is obtained via a variable-speed centrifugation process without any chemical additives. By featuring a denser and more rigid fibrin network, along with CD34 + stem cells and growth factors, CGF theoretically offers the potential for a more sustained regenerative effect [8].

While numerous studies have investigated the individual effects of PRP or CGF against a control group, direct head-to-head comparisons of these two autologous concentrates under a strictly standardized surgical protocol remain scarce. Furthermore, the majority of existing clinical trials rely on traditional cross-sectional statistical analyses, which often fail to account for the longitudinal correlation of repeated postoperative measurements. The primary objective of this study was to compare the clinical efficacy of CGF and PRP under a strictly standardized surgical protocol. To ensure robust evaluation of longitudinal postoperative changes, Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) were utilized. By combining reliable statistical modeling with a critical appraisal of the absolute magnitude of edema reduction, this study aims to prioritize clinical relevance over mere statistical significance, thereby providing a more practical perspective for daily oral surgery practice.

Methods

Study design and setting

This prospective, randomized controlled clinical trial was conducted at the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Faculty of Dentistry, Recep Tayyip Erdoğan University, between May 2, 2024, and May 2, 2025. The reporting of this study adheres to the CONSORT 2025 guidelines for reporting clinical trials. A completed CONSORT checklist is available as Additional File 1. The study protocol was approved by the Recep Tayyip Erdoğan University Clinical Research Ethics Committee prior to the commencement of the study (Decision No: 2024/52, Date: April 30, 2024) and was carried out in full accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Although the trial was registered retrospectively, the study protocol—including inclusion/exclusion criteria, evaluation time points, primary and secondary outcome variables (pain, edema, and alveolar osteitis), and the statistical analysis plan—was established before patient recruitment began. No modifications were made to the study design or outcome measures following the data collection process.

Participants

A total of 42 patients aged 18 to 50 years, classified as American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) physical status I or II, who presented with an indication for the extraction of impacted mandibular third molars, were enrolled in the study.

The inclusion criteria were defined based on radiographic examination as the presence of an impacted tooth with bone retention, classified as Pell-Gregory Class II, Position B or C, and exhibiting a mesioangular inclination according to the Winter classification. Smoking, systemic diseases, the presence of acute pericoronitis, and pregnancy or lactation were established as exclusion criteria (Fig. 1).

Sample size and randomization

The sample size was calculated based on similar studies in the literature [9] using G*Power software (v3.1.9.7). Assuming a large effect size of f = 0.5—selected to prioritize the detection of clinically impactful differences in facial edema—a power of 80%, and an alpha level of 0.05, a minimum of 14 patients per group was determined.

Participants were randomly allocated into three groups: Control (Group A), CGF (Group B), and PRP (Group C) (n = 14 per group), using computer-aided randomization (www.randomizer.org). A simple randomization sequence was generated to ensure unbiased distribution. Allocation concealment was ensured using numbered, sealed, opaque envelopes that remained unopened until the commencement of the surgical procedure, thereby preventing selection bias by the surgeon. The postoperative clinical evaluations were performed by the operating surgeon without blinding to the group allocation, as the physical nature of the biomaterials during the surgical phase made complete blinding of the operator impractical. To mitigate potential detection bias, a standardized and consistent measurement protocol was strictly followed.

Surgical procedure

All surgical procedures were performed by a single experienced surgeon following a standardized protocol. To ensure standardized surgical difficulty and minimize variations in operative duration, the inclusion criteria were strictly limited to teeth with a mesioangular inclination. Although the exact duration of each surgery was not statistically recorded as a primary variable, all procedures were completed uneventfully within an estimated timeframe of 8 to 10 min, ensuring uniform surgical trauma. Under local anesthesia (Articaine HCl with epinephrine), a mucoperiosteal flap was reflected, and the extraction was completed with minimal osteotomy and tooth sectioning (Fig. 2).

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using the SPSS software (version 26.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The normality of data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) with an Exchangeable correlation structure and a linear link function were employed to analyze repeated measures (pain and edema). This model was selected for its superior ability to handle longitudinal data by accounting for intra-subject correlations over multiple time points (Days 1, 3, and 7), thereby maximizing statistical power compared to traditional cross-sectional analyses.When necessary, Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) or Kruskal-Wallis tests were utilized for pairwise comparisons and assessments at specific time points. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Demographic findings

A total of 42 patients (6 males, 36 females) with a mean age of 21.45 ± 2.10 years were included in the study. No statistically significant differences were observed between the groups regarding age (p = 0.327) or gender distribution (p = 0.542), indicating that the groups were homogeneous at baseline.

Clinical outcomes: postoperative assessment and complication monitoring

Pain

No statistically significant differences were observed between the groups regarding pain scores at any of the postoperative time points (Days 1, 3, and 7) (p > 0.05). Across all groups, pain levels exhibited an expected decreasing trend from Day 1 to Day 7.

Although the mean pain scores on Day 1 were numerically lower in the CGF group (0.71), this difference did not reach statistical significance. The notably low postoperative pain levels observed across all groups, particularly on Day 1, may have resulted in a “floor effect.” This phenomenon likely hindered the statistical detection of potential differences between the groups.

Edema

Upon examination of edema measurements (Tragus-Pogonion and other reference lines), similar decreasing trends over time were observed across all lines. However, since the Tragus-Pogonion line best reflected the edema in the surgical site and demonstrated the most distinct changes, the data are presented based on this measurement.

In conventional pairwise comparisons (Mann-Whitney U), although the CGF and PRP groups exhibited a tendency for less edema compared to the Control group, particularly on days 3 and 7, these differences remained on the borderline of statistical significance (p = 0.056). However, in the Generalized Estimating Equations (GEE) model, which evaluates time and group interactions comprehensively, it was determined that both CGF (B=-1.056, p = 0.008) and PRP (B=-1.002, p = 0.009) applications significantly reduced postoperative edema compared to the Control group. Although the magnitude of edema reduction was limited in absolute terms, a consistent trend of improvement was observed across all postoperative time points (Table 1). The divergence between the borderline significance of pairwise tests and the clear significance in the GEE model (p < 0.01) highlights the benefit of utilizing longitudinal models. GEE accounts for the entire recovery trend rather than isolated snapshots, providing a more comprehensive evaluation of the materials’ anti-inflammatory effects over the first postoperative week.

Alveolar osteitis

At each follow-up visit, the surgical site was examined for complications, including infection, wound dehiscence, and alveolar osteitis (dry socket), in accordance with Blum’s criteria. No cases of alveolar osteitis were observed in any of the groups throughout the follow-up period. Due to the absence of alveolar osteitis incidence and the limited sample size, statistical comparisons between groups could not be performed for this outcome variable.

Discussion

In this study, the effects of CGF and PRP applied following impacted mandibular third molar surgery on postoperative comfort were evaluated. The primary finding was that while both platelet concentrates demonstrated a statistically significant superiority in reducing postoperative edema compared to the control group, they did not yield a distinct difference in postoperative pain scores between the groups. It is noteworthy that even in the control group, postoperative pain levels remained low, and no alveolar osteitis was observed. This can be attributed to the atraumatic nature of the surgical technique employed and the standard postoperative care protocol. As stated in the literature, in cases where surgical trauma is kept to a minimum, demonstrating the “adjunctive benefit” of platelet concentrates on healing becomes challenging due to a “floor effect.” Consequently, our results confirm the dominant role of a proficient surgical technique on postoperative comfort, rather than suggesting the inefficacy of CGF or PRP.

Edema is the most visible complication affecting patients’ social lives. Our findings are supported by GEE analysis, which indicates that CGF and PRP are effective in edema control (p = 0.008). This effect can be attributed to the anti-inflammatory properties of growth factors such as PDGF, TGF-β, and VEGF released from platelets [7]. The observation that edema values were numerically lowest in the CGF group aligns with hypotheses proposed in previous literature. Prior studies, such as Del Fabbro et al. [10], have stated that the denser fibrin network and CD34 + stem cells contained in CGF provide a more stable structure than PRP, theoretically entrapping growth factors for a longer duration to facilitate a ‘slow release’ mechanism [8, 10]. This biological mechanism is further supported by recent high-quality evidence, including contemporary systematic reviews on CGF and other autologous concentrates in regenerative dentistry. These reviews confirm the capacity of these biomaterials to enhance early soft tissue repair, even though their absolute clinical superiority over natural healing in dramatically mitigating postoperative edema remains a subject of ongoing discussion [11, 12]. However, it is crucial to acknowledge that the present clinical study did not histologically or biochemically evaluate these cellular components or release kinetics. Therefore, these biological mechanisms are cited strictly as a theoretical framework that is consistent with our clinical observations, rather than a direct finding of our study.

However, it should be noted that although the reduction in facial edema was statistically significant, the absolute differences between the groups were clinically limited (0.2–0.5 mm). As such, these findings must be interpreted with caution, particularly regarding their actual impact on the patient’s quality of life (QoL). Given that such a millimetric difference in swelling is unlikely to be perceptually noticeable to the patient, it does not translate into a meaningful improvement in functional recovery, such as mastication or speech. Furthermore, from a cost-effectiveness perspective, the preparation of autologous concentrates requires additional venipuncture, specific centrifugation equipment, and extended surgical chair-time. When weighed against a marginal 0.5 mm reduction in edema, this added financial and temporal burden cannot be justified for standard cases. Consequently, our findings provide a highly relevant contribution to everyday clinical practice by explicitly advising against the routine, uncritical use of these concentrates for standard third molar extractions, thereby preventing the unnecessary expenditure of surgical time, healthcare costs, and patient resources.

Regarding the application method, PRP was administered via submucosal injection, which theoretically introduces a temporary mechanical ‘volume effect’ that could influence early postoperative edema measurements. However, the clinical data demonstrated that despite this initial fluid addition, the PRP group exhibited significantly less overall edema compared to the control group in the GEE analysis. This suggests that the biological anti-inflammatory properties of the PRP effectively counteracted and surpassed any temporary mechanical swelling caused by the injection volume itself.

The absence of a statistical difference in pain scores between the groups (p > 0.05) contrasts with some studies in the literature [13, 14] while aligning with recent systematic evidence indicating that platelet concentrates do not always provide a clinically superior reduction in postoperative pain compared to a standard surgical procedure alone [15, 16]. This outcome can be explained by several factors. First, the surgical procedure employed in our study was minimally invasive; even in the control group, VAS scores remained notably low (Mean: 0.93) even on Day 1, creating a “floor effect” that may have masked potential statistical differences between the groups. Second, the masking effect of prescribed analgesics (standardized for all patients) may have further limited the statistical detection of any potential adjunctive benefit of platelet concentrates on pain.

The 0% incidence of alveolar osteitis (AO) across all groups is noteworthy. Although recent comprehensive reviews highlight the biological potential of autologous concentrates in accelerating soft tissue and bone healing, our findings suggest that a meticulous, atraumatic surgical technique and proper postoperative care remain the primary factors in preventing complications like alveolar osteitis, independent of routine biomaterial use [15, 17]. Due to the low incidence of alveolar osteitis and the limited sample size, the efficacy of CGF or PRP in preventing alveolar osteitis could not be evaluated with sufficient power within the scope of this study.

Limitations

The present study has certain limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, due to the physical nature of autologous concentrates, it was not possible to blind the operating surgeon, who also performed the postoperative evaluations. Although a strict, standardized measurement protocol was followed to mitigate detection bias, the lack of an independent, blinded assessor remains a methodological constraint. Second, while the sample size was determined a priori to detect meaningful differences in edema, it may have been underpowered to detect smaller effect sizes in pain scores or rare events like alveolar osteitis. Finally, evaluating facial swelling via linear measurements, though practical and widely accepted, has lower sensitivity compared to 3D volumetric imaging; thus, the reported millimetric differences (0.2–0.5 mm) approach the margin of clinical measurement error and must be interpreted cautiously. Despite these constraints, the strict standardization of the surgical protocol and the use of robust longitudinal statistical analysis (GEE) ensure the reliability of the clinical conclusions drawn regarding the routine use of these biomaterials.

Conclusions

While CGF and PRP applications provided a statistically significant reduction in facial edema following impacted tooth surgery, the absolute differences (0.2–0.5 mm) were minimal and are unlikely to meaningfully improve the patient’s overall quality of life. In terms of postoperative pain, no additional benefit was provided beyond standard surgical and medical treatment. Further randomized controlled trials with larger sample sizes, prospective registration, and the inclusion of standardized quality-of-life assessments are required to validate these findings and provide a more holistic evaluation of functional recovery.

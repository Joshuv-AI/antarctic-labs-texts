Investigating the impact of the internet on managing green financial innovation and improving agricultural conditions in water-scarce Asian regions using ANN modeling
Authors: Xiaohan Sheng, Guangmin Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This research focuses on the importance of management strategies, green innovation, and sustainable practices in the agricultural sector. These factors are crucial for job creation, food security, and environmental conservation. Particularly in water-scarce regions, effective management is necessary to overcome natural resource constraints and encourage a shift towards digital agriculture (AGRI). The study aims to identify and analyze the challenges and issues related to agricultural research and technology in Asian countries. The collected data were analyzed using exploratory factor analysis in the SPSS software. The analysis revealed a range of issues and challenges for agricultural development, including those related to the structure and policy framework, the availability and quality of resources and infrastructures, and the provision of effective support services, all of which encompassed factors such as research and technology investment, research management, productivity, research culture, networking, and the integration of higher education and agricultural research. To estimate the efficiency of technology development, agricultural development, and support services for AGRI, an artificial neural network (ANN) was utilized. The ANN was trained by incorporating changes in management strategies, green innovation, and sustainability across a broader range of experimental scenarios. The evaluation of the ANN's predictions showed that improvements in management strategies and the adoption of green innovation and sustainability significantly impacted the productivity of technology development, agricultural development, and support services for AGRI. The accuracy of the ANN's predictions was further assessed using linear regression. The results indicated an acceptable level of error when compared to the target results obtained from experimental tests. Overall, this study emphasizes the importance of effective management, green innovation, and sustainable practices in driving advancements in technology and agricultural development.

Introduction

Examining the historical trend of economic development in countries reveals that many of today's developed nations have prioritized agriculture as a fundamental sector for their economic growth. By harnessing the potential of this sector, they have not only achieved agricultural development but also facilitated the growth of other industries1–3. The agricultural sector plays multiple roles in the economic growth and development of countries, including ensuring food security, providing raw materials for upstream industries, employing labor, consuming products from other sectors (such as fertilizers, chemicals, and agricultural machinery), generating foreign exchange, and supporting infrastructure development4–6. To optimize these resources and achieve sustainable agricultural management, it is essential to identify and remove obstacles along the way. In countries where agriculture is a cornerstone of the economy, ensuring food security and sustainable development within this sector is of utmost importance. Given that many Asian countries are grappling with water scarcity issues, improving agricultural conditions in these regions becomes crucial7–9. The adoption of new and environmentally friendly technologies in agriculture, such as efficient water management, drip irrigation systems, and low-water cultivation methods, can enhance agricultural conditions and maximize the utilization of water resources. Additionally, green financial innovation management can play a significant role in fostering sustainable and eco-friendly agriculture in water-scarce Asian countries by providing financial resources and facilitating farmers' access to them10–14. These measures not only benefit low-water farmers but also uplift living standards and promote economic stability in these regions15–17.

The use of new technologies in agriculture has demonstrated positive effects. Employing intelligent agricultural systems based on accurate information about weather, plant water requirements, and soil conditions can optimize water and chemical usage18,19. Moreover, the adoption of sustainable practices such as continuous cultivation methods and efficient management of natural resources contributes to reducing soil and water pollution, preserving ecology and biodiversity, and mitigating the adverse impacts of climate change. These measures not only enhance agricultural performance but also contribute to environmental preservation and sustainability, aligning with the goals of green innovation management18–21. While acknowledging that agriculture is a vital component of food supply and societal food security, integrating advanced technologies in this sector can improve farmers' livelihoods and increase agricultural production15,22,23. An implementing optimal water management technologies can reduce water consumption in agriculture and improve water resource efficiency. Similarly, upgrading cultivation techniques and soil manipulation practices can enhance soil quality and increase crop yields. Furthermore, research and development efforts focused on improving climate-resistant crops and disease-resistant plants can boost farmers' income and overall production. The integration of new technologies and green management in agriculture fosters a sustainable and resilient agricultural system, capable of addressing future challenges24–27.

Green financial management can significantly impact agricultural improvement, but various obstacles can impede this process. One major obstacle is the lack of access to appropriate financial resources28–31. Many farmers face financial constraints that prevent them from investing in green and sustainable technologies. Additionally, a lack of awareness about green financial opportunities and sustainable financial management methods poses a significant hurdle. Some farmers are unaware of the importance of utilizing green financial resources and sustainable financial instruments, leading to reduced productivity and limited progress in agricultural conditions. Political, economic, and social factors also play a crucial role in hindering green financial management32–35. Changes in laws and regulations, price fluctuations, and insufficient financial resources can disrupt the development of green finance and impede agricultural improvement. Lack of awareness is another obstacle that impedes the development of green financial management. Many farmers are unaware of existing opportunities and sustainable financial tools that can enhance agricultural conditions. Without sufficient knowledge of green financial management methods, they often resort to traditional and low-profit solutions that offer limited improvement33–38. Political, economic, and social factors can also have detrimental effects on green financial management. Changes in agricultural and environmental laws and regulations can increase costs and reduce farmers' profitability. Additionally, price fluctuations and economic instability can escalate financial risks and impede investment initiatives in green financial management34–37.

With increased Internet access, farmers can also benefit from educational resources and information that were previously unavailable to them. This is particularly significant for farmers in rural areas who face limitations in accessing face-to-face training courses and technical information due to geographical and economic constraints. Farmers can access online training on agricultural production, optimal utilization of water and soil resources, and agricultural process improvement through the Internet. The Internet serves as an effective communication tool that opens up new opportunities for farmers to connect with local and global markets. It enables farmers to establish direct connections with local and foreign buyers and utilize online platforms and social networks to sell their products. This direct engagement with global markets allows farmers to secure better prices for their products and increase their income. In the field of agricultural and rural development, innovative systems have been informally introduced since the 1980s and more formally in the early twentieth century. When selecting an analytical framework, it is important to do so consciously and according to the intended goal. For organizing agricultural research, adopting the model of national agricultural research systems in each country can be beneficial as it helps to depict the structure and function of agricultural research. However, taking a broader perspective by considering agricultural research within the framework of the national innovation system provides a more comprehensive understanding of the system's structure and functionality. Additionally, the synergy between agricultural research and higher education can be explored through relevant system frameworks, such as agricultural research systems, agricultural knowledge and information systems, or agricultural innovation systems. In this research, the agricultural innovation system model has been employed to identify and analyze issues related to research and technology development in the agricultural sector. Unlike previous studies that focused on individual or organizational levels, this research considers the hierarchy of systems to address the challenges and issues associated with agricultural technology research and development. In this study, a shallow artificial neural network (SANN) with a hidden layer was implemented to forecast and evaluate several variables under investigation, specifically the productivity of technology development, agricultural development, and support services for AGRI. The neural network was trained using an expanded set of management strategies and inputs related to green innovation and sustainability. Additionally, the accuracy of the ANN was assessed using linear regression, and the resulting predictions were documented along with an evaluation of the estimation process.

Basic definitions

Innovation culture encompasses a complex process aimed at generating, transferring, modifying, and responding to new ideas. It involves a multifaceted behavior that includes a three-step process: understanding the problem and offering new solutions, refining novel ideas and establishing a logical framework to support them, and ultimately providing a model or experimental example for implementing ideas and solutions within a group, organization, or various departments. Today, innovation is increasingly becoming a strategic focus for organizations. In a analysis, over 65% of senior executives identified innovation as one of their top three priorities. With heightened global competition and shortened product life cycles, it is evident that sustainable innovation is crucial for creating differentiation and achieving a competitive edge. The key to fostering innovation within organizations lies in their ability to define, gradually instill, and reinforce innovation-affirming characteristics among employees. It appears that innovation flourishes under the right conditions, influenced by factors such as insight and mission, customer orientation, management processes, leadership, support mechanisms, and employee empowerment. Notably, management plays a critical role in conveying the necessary messages to catalyze a shift in employees' thinking and behavior16–22. The intersection of agriculture and polymers has become an increasingly important area of research, as the agricultural sector seeks to adopt more sustainable practices. Recent studies have explored the potential of polymers to address challenges, such as self-healing polymers for agricultural equipment, polymer-based biocomposites for biodegradable mulch and smart packaging, polymeric scaffolds for precision agriculture, and wound dressings for agricultural workers. These innovations demonstrate the growing interest in using polymers to enhance the sustainability, efficiency, and resilience of the agricultural sector, crucial for addressing the rising demand for food production12–23.

Innovation culture

Green technological innovation, as a crucial aspect and foundation of green innovation, encompasses investments in green equipment, utilization of advanced green technologies, and development of new solutions for storage, material conservation, and document management. Essentially, green technological innovation involves the production of technical knowledge aimed at reducing negative environmental consequences37–41. Innovation capability refers to a company's ability to develop and enhance existing technologies or create new ones. Given that many new technologies have significant environmental impacts and cause irreparable damage to ecosystems, organizations now prioritize investments in technologies that minimize such consequences. Green technological innovation embodies the concept of social responsibility for organizations42–45. The ability to engage in green technological innovation has a positive and significant effect on product differentiation. The primary objective of managers is to maximize long-term performance and profitability. Differentiation, cost leadership, and territorial strategies can enhance organizational performance. Companies adopting a differentiation strategy can identify customer groups that seek benefits beyond undifferentiated products, as these customers are willing to pay more for additional value. To effectively develop resources and support product-level differentiation, companies require marketing capabilities that consistently deliver desired benefit packages to customers and explore new markets46–51. Consequently, product differentiation positively and significantly impacts a company's competitiveness. The Internet plays a critical role in agriculture, particularly in the realm of smart agriculture. It is a powerful tool that can accelerate the development process of developing countries within the framework of the development communication approach. History has shown that technology transfer without considering the economic, social, cultural, and political conditions of the host country can disrupt its internal order and balance, leading to an unbalanced nation with fragmented subsystems52–58. Therefore, the utilization and advancement of Internet services in these countries necessitate precision, expertise, and study to account for the general conditions and challenges, ensuring effective implementation, adaptation, and integration into the societal fabric. By doing so, societies can reap the benefits of the Internet and harness its guidance for progress. In recent years, the use of the Internet in developing countries has expanded rapidly. However, this expansion has been more prominent in urban areas, while rural communities still struggle to access the available services. Consequently, organizations like the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) should take the lead in developing and expanding Internet infrastructure to cater to the needs of rural and agricultural societies. This will enhance access to information, educational resources, global markets, and financial tools for farmers in rural areas. Furthermore, it will increase the competitiveness of farmers and foster the growth of small and medium-sized enterprises in these regions57–62. The Internet enables direct communication between farmers and global markets, allowing them to establish direct contact with foreign buyers and distributors. This improves the marketing of their products and increases their income. Access to information about the demand and supply of agricultural products and market conditions through the Internet empowers farmers to make informed decisions. Moreover, the provision of online financial services such as lending and banking facilitates improved financial management and increased investment for business development63–68.

Green innovation

The Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD) provided a comprehensive definition of green innovation in 2010: Green innovation emphasizes the reduction of environmental consequences, whether intentional or unintentional. It encompasses innovations not only in products, processes, marketing methods, and organizational procedures but also in social and organizational structures. The concept of green innovation has gained widespread application, and it can be defined as a fresh perspective, idea, product, service, or process that aims to diminish environmental impacts15,23,24. Green innovation involves the development of novel or advanced methods, processes, techniques, systems, and products that minimize or prevent environmental harm. This approach encompasses measures and policies for energy conservation, designing environmentally friendly products, and facilitating effective environmental management and waste recycling within companies. Green innovation entails the utilization of new products, methods, and materials to reduce the consumption of natural resources and restrict the release of harmful substances into the environment. The primary objective of green innovation is to empower companies to meet the evolving needs of consumers, create value, and enhance performance in the realm of innovation, all while safeguarding the environment. It represents a significant tool for gaining a competitive advantage in an era marked by environmental concerns69–75. Green innovation can be observed in various dimensions, including process, product, technology, and management.

Green product innovation

Green product innovation emphasizes the enhancement of product quality and diversity while considering environmental factors. It involves developing new products or modifying existing ones to minimize negative environmental impacts throughout their lifecycle. Green product innovation aims to reduce resource consumption, prevent pollution, and design products that are more environmentally friendly. It is crucial for organizations to understand customer needs, assess market competition, and evaluate the technical, economic, and commercial feasibility of green product ideas33–35. Green technological innovation involves investing in green equipment, machinery, and advanced technologies that contribute to environmental sustainability. It encompasses the development of new solutions for efficient storage, material conservation, and document management. Green technological innovation focuses on producing technical knowledge to mitigate negative environmental consequences and promote eco-friendly practices27–35. Green management innovation refers to an organization's ability to develop and implement green projects and practices. This includes initiatives such as green supply chain management and environmental management systems. Green management innovation involves adhering to international standards and regulations, resource conservation, preventing the release of harmful substances, and organizing educational conferences and seminars to raise awareness about the economy, industry, and green innovation among stakeholders27–35. Green innovation performance denotes the introduction of new products, services, and processes that drive organizational change and environmental improvement. It encompasses the implementation of innovative green management, green products, green processes, and green technologies throughout the organization's value chain. The goal is to enable companies to meet evolving consumer demands, co-create value, and enhance overall organizational performance29–33. In summary, green innovation encompasses multiple dimensions, and its performance is measured by the successful implementation of new methods, processes, techniques, systems, and products that prevent or reduce environmental damage while meeting consumer needs and improving organizational efficiency and effectiveness. Green marketing refers to the range of activities aimed at creating and facilitating exchanges that meet human needs and desires while minimizing harm to the environment. The exploration of the relationship between marketing and the environment dates back to the early 1970s, leading many marketers to adopt various forms of green marketing. Initially, these marketers anticipated positive consumer reactions and subsequent improvements in business reputation, market share, and sales as a result of their green marketing efforts34–37.

The significance of enhancing agricultural conditions

Unemployment, immigration, and urban marginalization have drawn attention to rural development and even prioritized it over urban development. The agricultural sector, being the oldest production activity originating from rural areas, has played a crucial role in the growth and development of various countries throughout different periods. Examining the development processes of different countries reveals that the development of the agricultural sector is not only one of the most important economic sectors but also a necessary prerequisite for achieving sustainable development. Without addressing the obstacles to development in this sector, it is unrealistic to expect prosperity and progress in other sectors, including the industrial sector. On a global scale, an analysis of advanced societies' transformative fields demonstrates that the initial stages of development in many of these countries were driven by surplus production in the agricultural sector, leading to transformative changes37–43. According to the World Bank report, the agricultural sector is defined as an economic activity, a source of livelihood, and a provider of environmental services, making it a unique tool for development. Due to its close relationship with other economic sectors and its extensive use of products from those sectors, the agricultural sector can significantly contribute to driving the economy of a country. Agricultural development within the framework of rural development, while optimizing the utilization of water, soil resources, and human resources in rural areas, has undeniable effects on establishing a favorable economic structure and facilitating national development in any country39–51. To bring about transformation in the agricultural sector, it is essential for agricultural production to increase as a percentage of a country's total gross domestic product. Agriculture lies at the core of poverty reduction, food security, enhanced nutrition, and generating employment opportunities in rural areas. Strengthening national food security and achieving sustainable development through improved productivity in the sector are key policy objectives for many member countries of the Asian Productivity Organization. The benefits and necessity of green innovation management for companies

Given the significant global environmental issues that have impacted the economic development and performance of companies, there is a growing trend among government institutions to strengthen environmental laws and regulations. Strict environmental regulations and pressures can create a competitive perspective because production companies, regardless of their environmental quality, contribute significantly to environmental damage. The main challenge now is that stakeholders must simultaneously address the need to reduce greenhouse gases such as carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, and ash, while increasing resource efficiency. The solution for manufacturing companies to mitigate the environmental impact of their production processes is to implement green innovation, which involves waste reduction, pollution prevention, and the implementation of environmental management systems47–55. It is crucial to recognize that a continuous innovation strategy is essential to overcome external pressures from customers, competitors, and lawmakers. All companies need to embrace innovation because it will be the primary source of income and profit in the future. Importantly, innovation is not limited to industries with advanced technologies; every industry, company, and individual can be a creator of innovation. Innovation is a continuous process rather than a specific goal. According to the OECD and the European Commission, innovation is defined as the implementation or improvement of a product, process, marketing method, or organizational method in business operations, work environment, and external relations. This definition highlights the broad scope of innovation, including green innovation as one of its types. Therefore, developing a green innovation strategy is the crucial first step for companies committed to pursuing green innovation performance50–55. Consumers who are environmentally conscious are more inclined to purchase green products. Traditional product aspects such as price, quality, and brand also play significant roles in consumers' purchasing decisions. The company's performance in green product innovation directly affects its overall performance, including market share, sales, profits, and the company's reputation and credibility. Additionally, the environmental performance of a company positively impacts its overall performance53.

Managers responsible for green innovation should focus on producing green products and services that do not harm human health or the environment. Smaller organizations are often less inclined to adopt green innovation strategies, whereas larger organizations tend to have formal environmental management systems in place, allowing for more robust green innovation strategies54. Green innovation refers to innovation that emphasizes waste reduction, pollution prevention, and the implementation of environmental management systems. Therefore, a green innovation strategy is a type of strategy implemented by a company to achieve a competitive advantage, meet market needs, and fulfill stakeholders' expectations. Companies should develop a green innovation strategy to foster green innovation55. Reducing the risks associated with negative environmental impacts from production processes and outputs strengthens the organizational identity. If a company demonstrates a strong environmental commitment, management will not overlook the adverse effects of the company's operational activities on the environment. This environmental concern becomes part of the organizational identity56. Companies worldwide have been compelled to innovate and develop green products in response to growing environmental concerns and stakeholder demands. Companies that embrace a green corporate identity are more likely to generate environmentally friendly innovations. When companies prioritize environmental issues as part of their organizational identity, it motivates members of the organization to contribute more to environmental protection54–57. In order to foster growth and development in the agricultural sector, several key factors come into play. A significant increase in the gross national product (GNP) contributes to the overall economic growth of a country. Stimulating the growth of various economic sectors through inter-sectoral linkages helps in creating a dynamic agricultural sector that transitions from traditional agriculture to industrial agriculture. Economic growth plays a vital role in the sectoral composition of the GDP, the allocation of labor and resources to sectors with high productivity and added value, and the distribution of income among different households, including rural and urban households70–77.

Limitations impacting technological management in agriculture in Asian countries

To achieve economic transformation in Asian countries, it is important to address the limitations and challenges specific to these countries and take steps to overcome or mitigate them. Additionally, understanding the unique characteristics of life and livelihoods in these countries is crucial62. Some of the limitations affecting technological management in agriculture in Asian countries include: Small land sizes and their dispersion, which reduce the productivity of land, labor, and capital. The system of leased exploitation, which hampers productivity due to the unavailability of larger agricultural plots. Weak and underdeveloped local markets, leading to increased production costs. Expensive inputs in the agricultural sector, contributing to higher production costs. Inadequate road transportation systems, poor maintenance, and storage facilities, resulting in increased waste and reduced income63. Industrialized countries

In developed and industrialized countries, the role of agriculture extends beyond food provision and encompasses political, social, and economic priorities. Agriculture is considered irreplaceable in these countries. To address the challenges associated with dependence on the agricultural sector, they emphasize the sector's independence and implement various support policies effectively. These policies focus on optimizing input consumption, efficiently utilizing scarce natural resources, and addressing areas such as trade, taxes, and price credits. The priority for these countries lies in promoting the growth of industrial and commercial agriculture. They foster extensive collaboration with large farmers to facilitate rapid sectoral growth. Simultaneously, efforts are made to plan and implement strategies that increase the income of small-scale farmers.

Research methodology

Green process innovation entails enhancing existing processes and developing new ones with the objective of recycling, reusing, and reproducing raw materials while reducing energy consumption and environmental pollution. It focuses on improving the efficiency and sustainability of internal organizational processes. Green process innovation involves implementing methods that facilitate resource conservation, waste reduction, and the adoption of environmentally friendly practices35–55. In this study, a Shallow Progressive Artificial Neural Network (SPANN) was employed to predict changes in productivity within the domains of technology development, agricultural development, and support services for AGRI. The SPANN model was applied to 15 test cases, encompassing a wide range of management strategies, green innovation, and sustainability factors spanning from 0 to 70%. The neural network architecture consisted of an input layer incorporating management strategies and green innovation inputs, a hidden layer comprising 5 neurons, and an output layer representing the productivity outputs for technology development, agricultural development, and support services for AGRI. The activation function employed in the SPANN model was the nonlinear sigmoid function, known for its ability to enhance prediction accuracy and expedite network convergence due to its nonlinear nature. The optimization of the error function was achieved using the gradient descent algorithm, enabling training and estimation of results at each stage of network progression. To further enhance accuracy and convergence, the input data from Table 1 was initially normalized, and after final estimation of results, denormalization was applied to ensure reporting within an acceptable range. The accuracy of the SPANN model in predicting results was assessed by determining the network error through linear regression analysis. This involved normalizing the predicted results and creating a fit chart to compare the fitted graph, obtained through linear regression, with the ideal y = x graph representing 100% accurate estimation based on input targets from Table 1. The deviation between the fitted graph and the y = x graph was used to quantify the error of the formed ANN. This research article aimed to investigate the impact of Internet discovery and green finance innovation management on improving agricultural conditions for water-scarce Asian farmers. The study employed a combination of literature review and ANN modeling techniques. Through an extensive literature review of studies published between references35–84, relevant input and output variables were identified. Two key input variables, management strategies and green innovation and sustainability, were selected, along with three output variables: productivity of technology development, agricultural development, and support services for AGRI. Data for these variables were collected from various sources and carefully prepared for analysis. An ANN model was developed with appropriate architecture, incorporating the input and output variables. The model was trained and optimized using collected data, with the aim of predicting and optimizing the best conditions for improving agricultural outcomes. The model's performance was evaluated using metrics such as accuracy, precision, and error rates. The results obtained from the trained ANN model were analyzed and interpreted to gain insights into the impact of Internet discovery and green finance innovation management on agricultural conditions. Limitations of the study were acknowledged, and suggestions for future research directions were provided. This research contributes to the understanding of the topic and offers valuable insights for future research and practical applications in improving agricultural conditions for water-scarce Asian farmers. Types of innovations

In many developing countries, considering the existing limitations in the agricultural sector, two types of innovation should be prioritized in agricultural sector programs and policies: technologies that enhance productivity and management of production: this includes technologies related to crop nutrition, pest and disease control, water resources management, water consumption optimization, restructuring of livestock and poultry farms, harvesting techniques, packaging, storage, grading, and intelligent management of production units. Technologies that increase production value and promote the market: this encompasses technologies in branding, advertising, product marketing, market information systems, knowledge of national and international market environments, virtual markets, agricultural products stock market, price transparency, and appropriate support policies aligned with production conditions. These innovations aim to improve productivity, efficiency, and marketability within the agricultural sector94–100.

Challenges in implementing agricultural technologies

To effectively implement and deploy technologies in the agricultural sector and achieve significant transformation, several problems and limitations need to be addressed through appropriate policies in the government and public sector95–101. Key challenges associated with innovation in agriculture include: insufficient participation and collaboration between the public and private sectors in developing new technologies; bureaucratic hurdles and inefficiencies within the public sector; inadequate reward structures for top technicians and experts; lack of business culture and limited commercial experience within the government; low confidence of the private sector to invest in agricultural technologies due to economic instability, weather-related risks, and uncertain income; intellectual property issues when multiple public and private entities are involved; weak negotiation skills and limited capacity of the public sector to establish strong relationships with private sector investors and technologists; lack of economic stability in technology contracts and compensation; and fluctuations in costs and cost pricing of technologies coupled with aimless budgeting by the government without considering the priority of technology deployment in the agricultural sector91–95.

Important success factors in implementing innovations in the agricultural sector

Long-term commitment from the government and industry, supported by joint protocols with the private sector and technologists; access to important resources within the national innovation system at national, regional, and local levels, while bringing together innovators from various sectors; creation of a network of innovators that encompasses all sectors; and conducting multidisciplinary research to foster comprehensive innovation. Allocation of a portion of the government's public budget to support innovative activities in agriculture by the private sector, along with effective management and targeting of financial resources, can also encourage innovation. The experience of leading Asian countries has demonstrated that policymakers' attention to these factors and strategic planning can expedite the establishment of innovation. The government plays four critical roles in supporting the innovation system in the agricultural sector: public mobilization to promote innovation, enhance the quality of agricultural products, and reduce government costs; implementation of innovations in the market, leveraging the capabilities of the private and public sectors, as well as research and development companies; structural support for research and development enterprises and innovations to enhance the knowledge and technology infrastructure; and provision of subsidies and financial support to innovators, facilitating the presence of new technologies in the market and promoting mass production. To drive transformation in the agricultural sector, there should be direct collaboration between the private and public sectors, where the private sector contributes through innovation, mass production, advertising, sales, social responsibility, and technology dissemination, while the public sector supports development enterprises, provides financial backing for innovation, and facilitates its dissemination.

Smart farming

In the realm of agricultural transformation, a significant focus is placed on digital transformation in agriculture by adopting next-generation technologies such as the Internet of Things (IoTs), cloud computing, big data analysis, and artificial intelligence. Planners, policymakers, farmers, and agriculture-related businesses should swiftly embrace digital transformation. The future of agriculture lies in digital technologies, which can bring stability and sustainability. Smart agriculture incorporates cutting-edge technologies to enhance sustainable productivity. It leverages big data, digital technologies, and data analysis for informed decision-making. Smart technologies should be tailored to the specific agricultural conditions of each country. The adoption of digital technologies correlates with a country's stage of economic development and digital readiness102–111. To boost productivity in the agricultural sector and food production, governments, consulting agencies, agricultural activists, and food processing companies need to implement policies and make decisions that utilize the potential of new technologies, including digital technologies, throughout the agricultural product chains. Improving productivity in agriculture requires not only physical investment but also the development of science, technology, human capital, and social capital. Policies and institutional frameworks must be established to encourage private sector investment, particularly by farmers. Investment in equipment necessitates measures that enhance market access, ensure information flow, establish standards, and enact necessary laws and regulations. Stable policymaking and well-organized institutions are crucial to attracting private investment in the agricultural sector, and a strong correlation between government and private investment is observed to achieve agricultural growth112–114.

Conclusion

Introducing new management and institutional patterns in agricultural research and higher education institutions, revising laws and regulations, and addressing emerging issues such as intellectual property protection and research commercialization are critical. These policies and solutions aim to enhance the structure and function of agricultural research and technology, promote innovation, and contribute to the overall development of the agricultural sector. To further enhance agricultural innovation and technology adoption, several suggestions can be implemented. Firstly, effective environmental management within companies should be prioritized, considering factors such as environmental legitimacy, organizational culture, the impact of green innovation on financial performance, and employees' attitudes towards green corporate identity. Governments should focus on creating an enabling environment for digital technologies by ensuring widespread internet access and security. Integrated management of small agricultural and animal husbandry units should also be planned for, with adequate support and resources provided. Government support for research and development should be extended to both public and private institutions. Encouraging private investment in affordable technologies can be achieved through low-cost services and incentives. Accurate statistics of farmers and ranchers should be encouraged, while ensuring the protection of their information. Intellectual property rights should be safeguarded to incentivize the creation of new technologies, and thorough research and assessment should precede the implementation of smart technologies. Regulations and policies should support early adopters of digital technologies, and international cooperation should be fostered for knowledge transfer. Lastly, strategies must be developed to address risks and threats associated with intelligent agricultural technologies, including cybersecurity and ethical considerations. By implementing these suggestions, governments and stakeholders can create an environment conducive to agricultural innovation, leading to increased productivity, sustainability, and overall sectoral development.

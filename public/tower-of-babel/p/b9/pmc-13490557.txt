Validation and prognostic stratification limitations of the 8th AJCC ypTNM staging system in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy: A multicenter retrospective study
Authors: Jianfei Zhu, Yu Ma, Yanlu Xiong, Hongtao Wang, Ran Yang, Xiangyang Yu, Hong Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) ypTNM staging system serves as the basis for predicting treatment efficacy, issuing prognostic warnings, and guiding adjuvant therapy in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) who have received neoadjuvant chemotherapy or neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy. This large‑sample, multicenter study aimed to validate the ypTNM staging system and identify its limitations in prognostic stratification for ESCC after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy (nICT). Patients who underwent nICT followed by esophagectomy between April 2020 and December 2022 at three national centers were retrospectively enrolled. The prognostic predictive roles of the ypT and ypN categories were analyzed using univariate and multivariate analyses. Based on these findings, the AJCC 8th edition ypTNM staging system was validated and refined. The pathological complete response rate was 28.6% (83/290). The 2-year disease-free survival (DFS) rate and overall survival (OS) rates were 62% and 76%, respectively. Univariate analysis revealed that both the ypT (P = 0.002) and ypN (P < 0.001) categories were significant variables for predicting patient OS. Multivariate analysis confirmed that the ypN stage (P < 0.001) had a stronger prognostic relevance. Notably, patients with pT0N0 did not exhibit superior DFS (P = 0.914) or OS (P = 0.935) compared with those with T1-2N0. Although T0-3N2 and T3N1 are both classified as stage IIIB diseases, the DFS (P = 0.085) and OS (P = 0.062) analyses suggest that these subgroups may require differentiation. Additionally, the AJCC 8th edition ypTNM staging system did not clearly distinguish between stage II and stage IIIA patients, necessitating further subdivisions. Among patients with ESCC who underwent nICT, increasing ypT and ypN categories resulted in a stepwise deterioration in prognosis, with the ypN category demonstrating greater prognostic value. However, no significant survival differences were observed between yp stage II and yp stage IIIA patients, indicating the need for further subgroup refinement.

Introduction

For patients with locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT) is recommended by both domestic and international guidelines, based on compelling evidence regarding pathological complete response (pCR) and overall survival (OS) in the NEOCRTEC50101 and CROSS2 trials. Additionally, the JCOG1109 trial conducted in Japan3,4 demonstrated that neoadjuvant chemotherapy (nCT) can similarly prolong the OS in this patient population. However, for patients who have received neoadjuvant therapy, appropriate adjuvant treatment and surveillance strategies are critical for improving survival outcomes.

Compared to the 7th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) staging system, which focuses solely on patients undergoing esophagectomy alone5, the 8th edition introduced the ypTNM staging system for patients following neoadjuvant therapy6. This system has been widely applied to predict prognosis and guide the selection of treatment strategies for patients who have undergone traditional nCT or nCRT7,8.

Recently, with the release of data from studies such as NATION-19079 and ESCORT-NEO10, neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy (nICT) is gradually supplanting nCRT and is emerging as a recommended treatment option preferred by physicians. However, at the time of the AJCC 8th edition publication, nICT had not yet been widely implemented in clinical practice. Consequently, significant doubts remain regarding the applicability of the ypTNM staging system, which is based on traditional nCRT or nCT, in patients receiving nICT.

To bridge this gap, we conducted a multicenter, large-sample, retrospective study of patients with ESCC who received nICT followed by surgery. We aimed to validate the feasibility of the 8th edition of the AJCC ypTNM staging system in predicting the prognosis in this patient population. The study was conducted in accordance with the STROCSS guidelines11.

Methods

Study design

This retrospective study included patients with ESCC who underwent esophagectomy at three high-volume esophageal centers in China between April 2020 and December 2022. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) pathologically confirmed ESCC; (2) achieved R0 resection; (3) received nICT before surgery; (4) clinical stage T2-T4N0M0 or TxN+M0; and (5) had cardiopulmonary function that could tolerate perioperative treatment. Patients meeting any of the following conditions were excluded from this study: (1) history of other malignancies; (2) death during the perioperative period; and (3) missing follow-up information. All methods were performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and relevant guidelines and regulations. Written informed consent was obtained from all the patients. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shaanxi Provincial People’s Hospital (No. 2025R087).

Preoperative examination

A preoperative gastroscopic biopsy was performed to establish a definitive pathological diagnosis. The cT and cN statuses of patients before and after nICT were assessed using chest and upper abdominal computed tomography (CT), endoscopic ultrasonography, upper gastrointestinal contrast radiography, and neck ultrasound (Fig. 1). Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and electrical capacitance tomography (ECT) were used to rule out distant metastases, with positron emission tomography (PET) performed when necessary.

nICT and surgical procedure

In the nICT regimen, immunotherapeutic agents include PD-1 and PD-L1 inhibitors. The combined chemotherapy protocol consisted of a two-drug regimen of albumin-bound paclitaxel and platinum. The treatment cycle was set to a minimum of 2 cycles. Esophagectomy is typically performed 4–6 weeks after completion of the last nICT treatment. Surgical approaches include the McKeown and Ivor Lewis procedures. The requirement for lymph node dissection is to encompass at least two lymph node regions. Detailed protocols have been described in our previous studies12.

Pathological assessment

All specimens were evaluated for ypT and ypN classification according to the 8th edition of the AJCC staging system for ESCC to assess treatment response and determine final staging6(Fig. 2). Pathological evaluations were independently performed by two experienced pathologists.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation, and intergroup comparisons were conducted using the t-test. Categorical variables are expressed as rates or percentages, and intergroup comparisons were performed using the chi-square test. Disease-free survival (DFS) and OS curves were plotted to stratify patient prognosis based on ypT and ypN classifications. Univariate and multivariate analyses were performed to identify independent variables that could predict patient prognosis. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. significant. All analyses were conducted using SPSS version 19.0 and STATA version 14.0.

Results

Clinical characteristics of patients

A total of 290 patients were included in the final analysis, and their clinicopathological characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age was 67 years (range, 45–81 years). Males accounted for the vast majority (67.2%) of the total. Preoperative assessment revealed that cT3-stage tumors were the most prevalent (94.8%, 275/290), whereas cN1-stage tumors were the most common (83.8%). Tumor location was predominantly in the middle third (66.2%), followed by the proximal third (19.0%) and the distal third (14.8%). Regarding nICT cycles, 85.2% of the patients received two cycles of treatment.

Predictive analysis of tumor regression

The median follow-up time in this study was 22.3 months. Among patients who received nICT, the proportion achieving pCR was 28.6% (83/290). The 2-year DFS rate and OS rates were 62% and 76%, respectively. The percentages of patients achieving ypT0 and ypN0 were 31.4% and 65.2%, respectively. As shown in Table 1, we attempted to screen for preoperative pathological factors that could influence tumor regression. However, no significant indicators that could predict response to nICT were identified. Notably, increasing the number of preoperative neoadjuvant therapy cycles did not improve therapeutic efficacy.

Replication of survival disparities based on the 8th edition AJCC yp staging

The prognostic value of ypT and ypN categories and other clinicopathological characteristics was evaluated. DFS significantly declined with an increase in the ypT category (Fig. 3A) and ypN category (Fig. 3B) classifications (P < 0.001). Similarly, univariate analysis revealed that gender (P = 0.034), tumor location (P = 0.039), and differentiation (P = 0.027) had a stratifying effect on DFS (Table 2). The Cox multivariate model confirmed that ypN category (P < 0.001) and adjuvant therapy (P < 0.001) were independent prognostic factors influencing DFS. Notably, in patients receiving nICT, adjuvant therapy failed to yield superior survival benefits for patients.

Validation and challenges encountered in the yp staging system

In the 8th edition of the AJCC yp staging system, patients with pT0N0, who are classified as pCR patients following neoadjuvant therapy, are unexpectedly grouped together with patients with T1-2N0 under the stage I category. This suggests that, based on historical data, there is no significant difference in prognosis between these two patient groups6.

For patients receiving neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (in the CROSS regimen), previous studies have shown that patients with pT0N0 have a better prognosis than those with T1-2N013. We initially explored the aforementioned scientific questions in patients receiving nICT. Surprisingly, patients with pT0N0 did not show any advantages compared to those with T1-2N0 in terms of DFS (P = 0.914) or OS (P = 0.935) (Fig. 4), confirming the scientific validity of the 8th edition staging system.

In addition, we validated the prognostic and predictive role of N descriptors in patients with stage ⅢA and ⅢB diseases (Fig. 5). Among patients classified as stage ⅢB, those with T0-3N2 had shorter DFS (17.556 months) than T3N1 (25.847 months) (P = 0.085). A similar trend was observed for overall survival (OS; P = 0.062). Although these differences did not achieve statistical significance, they suggest potentially clinically meaningful disparities that warrant further attention and investigation.

Conversely, patients with T3N1 classified as stage ⅢB and those with T0-2N1 classified as stage ⅢA had overlapping survival curves for both DFS (P = 0.395) and OS (P = 0.766). This implies that these two groups should be treated equally.

Finally, as depicted in Figs. 3, a key challenge of this study was to distinguish the prognoses of patients with yp stage II and stage IIIA. Previous large-sample data on neoadjuvant chemotherapy and neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy failed to clearly differentiate the prognoses of patients in these two stages7. In our cohort, classifying patients into three groups (ypT3N0 + T0-1N1, ypT2N1, and ypT3N1) based on the classification principles for untreated patients did not reveal differences in DFS (P = 0.520) and OS (P = 0.496) (Fig. 6).

Discussion

For locally advanced resectable ESCC, preoperative nCT and nCRT remain the standard treatments recommended by NCCN guidelines1,3,14. However, emerging data from prospective studies on nICT are challenging the established role of traditional neoadjuvant treatments, including nCRT and nCT15,16,17. The NICE trial18 demonstrated that in patients with N2-N3 stage ESCC receiving nICT, the 2-year OS and DFS rates were 78.1% and 67.9%, respectively. The NATION-1907 trial9 reported a pCR rate of 8% and a 2-year OS rate of 92% in patients with locally advanced resectable ESCC treated with adebrelimab plus chemotherapy. Similarly, a study on the perioperative “sandwich” approach involving toripalimab plus chemotherapy showed that the 1-year OS rate in patients receiving chemotherapy alone was 83.0%, whereas it was 94.1% in the nICT group (P = 0.037)19. Consistent with these studies, our large-sample real-world study demonstrated a pCR rate of 28.6% and 2-year DFS and OS rates of 62% and 76%, respectively.

Notably, among patients with locally advanced ESCC who have received nCRT, a substantial proportion still experience recurrence and metastasis. In 2021, researchers released the 10-year survival data from the CROSS study2. The data indicated that nCRT reduced the mortality risk associated with esophageal cancer (HR:0.60, 95%CI: 0.46–0.80). Compared with patients who underwent surgery alone, those who received nCRT exhibited a 10-year absolute OS benefit rate of 13% (38% vs. 25%). However, 8% of patients experienced isolated local recurrence, and 13% experienced both distant metastasis and local recurrence. Leng et al.20 similarly confirmed that even among patients who achieved pCR after treatment with the NEOCRTEC5010 regimen, the recurrence rate remained as high as 13.9%. In patients with distant lymph node metastases, the median OS and DFS were 16.1 months and 14.4 months, respectively. Collectively, these findings highlight the urgent need for a prognostic evaluation system and a monitoring and early warning system specifically designed for patients with locally advanced ESCC who have received neoadjuvant therapy.

To address this issue, during the update of the 8th edition of the AJCC staging system for esophageal cancer, the Worldwide Esophageal Cancer Collaboration incorporated large-scale pathological assessment data from 7,773 specimens of primary esophageal tumors and lymph node metastases following neoadjuvant therapy, leading to the introduction of the ypTNM staging system for esophageal cancer6. The establishment of this system provides crucial data for the postoperative adjuvant treatment and follow-up of patients with esophageal cancer who have undergone neoadjuvant therapy. Yuan et al.7 validated the scientific validity and broad applicability of the 8th edition ypTNM staging system using a large sample of 2,324 cases from the SEER database. However, they noted that an updated ypTNM staging system incorporating grade of differentiation might offer improved prognostic stratification for patients. Another study focusing on patients with ESCC who received neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy revealed that both ypT (P = 0.026) and rTN stages (P = 0.001) were independently correlated with OS13. Related studies have similarly corroborated the clinical guiding value of the 8th edition of the ypTNM staging system21,22.

Nevertheless, the 8th edition of the ypTNM staging system has two major limitations. First, it focuses solely on ypT and ypN staging while overlooking factors such as tumor grade of differentiation and tumor location, which previous studies have suggested may have a predictive value for prognosis23,24. Second, it was constructed based on patients who received conventional nCRT or nCT, raising concerns about its applicability to nICT. Our preliminary studies have indicated that even among patients achieving pCR, those receiving three distinct treatment modalities exhibit different patterns of recurrence and metastasis12,25,26. Given these considerations, we aimed to validate the effectiveness and identify potential deficiencies of the 8th edition ypTNM staging system in patients receiving nICT using large-scale multicenter data. Our findings revealed that the 8th edition staging system, based on ypT and ypN staging, could effectively predict patient prognosis. However, factors such as grade of differentiation, tumor location, and adjuvant therapy should also be considered.

Notably, in our cohort of patients receiving nICT, those considered to have the most favorable treatment outcomes, classified as ypT0N0 (commonly referred to as having achieved pCR), did not demonstrate any significant advantages in terms of DFS or OS compared to patients with ypT1-2N0. This seemingly counterintuitive finding aligns with the results of the 8th edition of the ypTNM staging system, which categorizes these patients as having yp stage I6. This finding is consistent with the results of previous studies involving nCRT7; nevertheless, relevant research suggest that further stratification of such patients is warranted13,27. Even patients achieving pCR following nICT, who are generally regarded as cured, still harbor residual recurrence risk. Our prior study12 demonstrated that 15.7% of such patients eventually experienced tumor recurrence and metastasis, highlighting the inherent limitations of the current pathology-based ypTNM staging system and the need for further optimization. Supporting this notion, a previous study of 474 ESCC patients with pCR reported a recurrence rate of 12.7%, and identified preoperative clinical N stage as the only independent prognostic factor for recurrence in this cohort28. A multicenter retrospective study from China further indicated that extensive lymph node dissection (retrieved lymph nodes > 19) failed to improve 2-year OS (P = 0.052) or DFS (P = 0.254) in pCR patients. Conversely, non-pCR patients derived significant survival benefits from increased lymph node yield, with markedly improved 2-year OS (82.6% vs. 74.7%, P = 0.003) and DFS (73.6% vs. 69.7%, P = 0.043) in patients with > 19 retrieved lymph nodes29. Furthermore, additional evidence showed that among patients with ypStage I disease, those with more than 48 retrieved lymph nodes had significantly inferior DFS compared with patients with ≤ 48 retrieved lymph nodes (P = 0.012)30. Collectively, these discrepant prognostic features confirm that pCR patients constitute a unique and heterogeneous subgroup. Further large-scale studies are warranted to comprehensively characterize their recurrence patterns and clarify the clinical necessity of postoperative adjuvant therapy for this specific population.

Furthermore, among patients classified as stage IIIB, the survival curves revealed significant differences between those with ypT3N1 and ypT0-3N2 status. Specifically, patients with ypT3N1 appeared to have a more favorable prognosis and may be more appropriately reclassified as stage IIIA.

Regardless of whether patients received traditional neoadjuvant therapy cohort or nICT, the greatest challenge is differentiating those classified as yp stage II and yp stage IIIA within the ypTNM staging system. Even when further stratified by ypN status, these patients could not be clearly distinguished from one another, suggesting the need to incorporate more precise variables to better differentiate this patient subgroup.

This study has several limitations. First, although this was a multicenter study with a large sample size, larger-scale studies are needed to externally validate the relevant findings. Second, this study primarily validated the rationality and prognostic stratification limitations of the 8th-edition AJCC ypTNM staging system in patients with ESCC receiving nICT. Further optimization of novel staging systems by integrating lymphovascular invasion (LVI), minimal residual disease (MRD) and other biomarkers with conventional ypTNM staging will be the focus of our future research. Finally, due to the relatively short median follow-up time, survival data spanning over five years are required to validate our conclusions.

Conclusions

Among patients with operable ESCC receiving nICT, the 8th edition of the AJCC ypTNM staging system can differentiate the prognosis of patients at different stages based on the ypT and ypN classifications, with ypN status carrying more significant quantitative weight. This study validated and confirmed the scientific rationale for grouping patients classified as ypT0N0 and ypT1-2N0 together into stage I. However, the current staging system does not clearly distinguish between patients with stage II and stage IIIA disease, necessitating further subdivision.

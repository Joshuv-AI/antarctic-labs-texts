Sociodemographic determinants of appointment no-show in primary care: a cross-sectional study in Renca Commune, Chile (2022–2023)
Authors: Pablo Escobar-San Martín, Nataly Droguett Droguett, Vezna Sabando Franulic
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNonattendance in primary health care (PHC) is an increasing challenge for health system management, as it leads to wasted resources, greater workloads for healthcare teams, and interruptions in the continuity of care. While most studies have examined this phenomenon in the context of medical specialties, evidence from PHC settings remains scarce. This study aimed to analyze the associations between the sociodemographic and administrative characteristics of appointments at PHC centers within a large urban commune in Santiago, Chile.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted using secondary data from the Rayen Salud® electronic health record system, which consolidates clinical and administrative records from the Electronic Health Records. Appointments scheduled between 2022 and 2023 were extracted, with one appointment per person randomly selected. Appointments with a status of scheduled, canceled, pending, or in progress were excluded, as were overbookings, procedures, minor surgeries, and specialty consultations. The dependent variable was appointment status (completed or not shown). Descriptive statistics and multivariate regression models were used to estimate prevalence ratios (PRs) with 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs).ResultsOverall, 21.8% of the appointments were not attended. Nonattendance was more common among adults aged 18–64 years (PR: 1.04; 95% CI: 1.01–1.09), patients affiliated with the public insurance system, and those scheduled via telephone or online platforms (PR: 1.41; 95% CI: 1.35–1.47). A higher prevalence was also observed on Saturdays (PR: 1.46; 95% CI: 1.33–1.59), after 17:00 h (PR: 1.20; 95% CI: 1.13–1.26), and in September (PR: 1.09; 95% CI: 1.00–1.19). With respect to professional health, general practitioners presented the lowest prevalence (PR: 0.46; 95% CI: 0.42–0.50), whereas nutritionists, dentists, and psychologists presented higher prevalence (PRs 1.32–1.82). Older adults (≥ 65 years) were more likely to attend (PR: 0.68; 95% CI: 0.64–0.71).ConclusionsNo show in PHC represents a major challenge for care delivery. Targeted reminder strategies, particularly for high-risk groups, nonmedical providers, and extended-hour appointments, are essential for improving attendance and strengthening PHC performance.Keywords: No show, Primary care, Appointment, Nonattendance

Introduction

No show in health services is a significant challenge for health system planning and management, undermining the fundamental right to health and hindering the achievement of public health goals. In Chile, the nonattendance rate for specialty medical care consultations is estimated at 16.5% [1], contributing to inefficiencies, resource waste, growing waitlists, and delays in diagnosis and treatment [2, 3]. In response, moderate-quality evidence in the literature suggests that mobile phone text messaging reminders increase attendance at healthcare appointments compared with no reminders or postal reminders [4, 5].

In Chile, primary health care (PHC) functions as the principal entry point for the public health system. The national health system is organized around various insurance schemes, including public, private, and other modalities. The public system is the predominant provider, covering more than 80% of the population. Historically, PHC facilities were limited to serving individuals affiliated with the public system. However, within the framework of ongoing health reform, the universal primary health care (PHC-U) strategy was introduced in 2023, guaranteeing access to services for all individuals residing in or working within the catchment area of PHC establishments, regardless of their insurance scheme (public, private, or otherwise).

This study was conducted in the commune of Renca, located in Chile’s Metropolitan Region, with a projected population of 162,517 in 2023. Renca has distinguished itself as a pioneer in the implementation of PHC-U, becoming the first urban municipality to adopt this initiative. Consequently, it has promoted the early adoption of the PHC-U and has established itself as a reference point for innovation and management models in primary care.

Evidence of international nonattendance indicates that rates range between 13% and 15%, with reported costs exceeding €40,000 over a period of just five days. Factors contributing to missed appointments include early-morning scheduling, long travel distances, lack of awareness of the appointment, last-minute cancellations, forgetfulness, unforeseen events, and prior recovery from illness [6]. The systematic review conducted by Dantas et al. (2018), which synthesized findings from more than 100 studies published between 1980 and 2016, identified a wide range of factors associated with nonattendance across all specialties. These included demographic variables such as younger adults; lower socioeconomic status; place of residence is distant from the clinic; and no private insurance is provided [7]. On the other hand, a systematic review by Parsons, Bryce, and Atherton (2021) examined 26 studies and reported that the average rate of nonattendance in primary care was 15.2%. The most frequently reported reasons included work or childcare commitments, forgetting the appointment, and transportation difficulties. Patients most likely to miss appointments were younger individuals (< 21 years), those from ethnic minorities, and people of low socioeconomic status [8].

In Asia and Oceania, nonattendance has further been linked to sociodemographic variables such as sex, ethnicity, age, and history of previous appointments, as well as to appointment-related aspects, including service costs, day of the week, and season [9–11]. Moreover, consistent attendance at health check-ups has been associated with improved blood pressure control [12].

In the United States, key sociodemographic factors include being of African descent, being female, and not speaking English, with nonattendance rates reaching 13.5% [13–16].

In Latin America, the literature is limited, but existing studies suggest that forgetfulness, female sex, and being between 61 and 70 years old are associated with missed appointments. They also highlight the effectiveness of written reminders in reducing nonattendance [17–19].

In Chile, the issue of no show has been recognized by the Ministry of Health, which has established performance targets to reduce this indicator [20]. Although national studies are limited and have focused primarily on hospital-based care, associations have been identified between nonattendance and factors such as female sex, low socioeconomic status, older age, and certain medical specialties [1, 21]. However, few studies have been conducted within the context of primary health care, which is characterized by a portfolio of services delivered by a wide range of nonphysician health professionals. These professionals play pivotal roles in preventive and health-monitoring activities across different stages of the life course and make substantial contributions to the comprehensive care of patients.

The objective of this study was to determine the associations of users’ sociodemographic characteristics and administrative appointment factors with nonattendance in primary health care settings.

Methods

This cross-sectional study was based on secondary data obtained from the Electronic Health Record (EHR) platform Rayen Salud®, a Chilean health technology company certified under ISO/IEC 27001:2022 by the accredited body AENOR. This certification ensures the reliability and security of information management. The platform consolidates clinical and administrative data and produces standardized reports to support decision-making in healthcare management.

The study population included beneficiaries registered in primary healthcare facilities who sought health services—either in person or remotely—between 2022 and 2023. Appointments were scheduled during institutional hours (08:00–17:00) and extended hours (17:00–20:00) across the four primary health centers that began Renca commune. The population of beneficiaries was 131,688 inhabitants in 2022 and 134,866 in 2023.

Appointments were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: overbookings; appointments in a “scheduled,” “canceled,” “in progress,” or “pending” status (as these do not confirm attendance); appointments for minor surgery or specialty care (as these are delivered outside primary care); prescription-only visits; nonclinical or administrative sessions that do not require patient presence; emergency or spontaneous visits (as these are not prescheduled); procedure or treatment appointments (often booked ad hoc); group workshops; and home visits (as these are conducted by health teams and do not rely on patient attendance).

A standardized appointment report from the platform Rayen Salud® provided all necessary variables for this study. Following data cleaning and anonymization, a unique alphanumeric identifier is used. One appointment per person was randomly selected across the full study period (2022–2023).

The dependent variable was appointment status, which was classified as “completed” (when the patient attended) or “not shown” (when the patient did not attend).

Independent variables were grouped into two domains: sociodemographic variables—sex, nationality, age group, and type of health insurance—and administrative appointment variables—assigned healthcare professional, booking method (remote or in person), month, day of the week, time of day, and appointment type (in person vs. website or telephone).

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize all variables through absolute and relative frequencies. To evaluate associations with nonattendance, multivariate logistic regression models were fitted, and the results are expressed as prevalence ratios (PRs) with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (95% CIs) and p values, considering statistical significance at p < 0.05. All the analyses were performed via R software, version 4.5.1.

Reference categories for the multivariate analysis were selected on the basis of their relevance, practical applicability, and consistency with previous literature. For variables related to the assignment of healthcare professionals, those with fewer appointments—social workers, educators, and speech therapists—were grouped into a single category labeled Assistant. The remaining professional groups were kept disaggregated due to their analytical importance. For variables such as appointment modality, booking method, and time slot, traditional categories were chosen as references to compare newer scheduling models (e.g., remote booking, telemedicine, and extended hours) with conventional formats in terms of attendance behavior.

Results

Among these no-show appointments, 60.5% were made by women, 92.3% involved patients of Chilean nationality, and 98.5% were covered by public health insurance. In terms of healthcare professionals, general practitioners accounted for 45.3% of all appointments. Most appointments (78.6%) were booked in person, whereas 21.4% were scheduled via institutional websites or phones. Appointments for telemedicine correspond to 0.3% of the respondents.

The chi-square test of independence (Table 1) revealed statistically significant differences across all the variables analyzed, indicating that at least one category within each variable differed significantly from the others. When unadjusted prevalences were examined, higher probabilities of nonattendance were observed among female patients; individuals aged 18–64 and ≥ 65 years; those assigned to nurses, midwives, nutritionists, dentists, or psychologists; and those scheduled on Saturdays, during the 12:00–17:00 and > 17:00 time slots, in the month of September, and through telemedicine.

In the sex-stratified analysis, women accounted for 60.5% of the sample (n = 57,337) and showed an overall no-show prevalence of 22.3%. Among women, nonattendance was higher among those covered by other health insurance schemes, children and adolescents, and those with appointments in nutrition, dentistry, and psychology. Higher no-show prevalence were also observed for appointments scheduled on Saturdays, after 5:00 PM, during September, and delivered via telemedicine, while women aged 65 years and older and those attending consultations with general practitioners exhibited the lowest risk of nonattendance (Table 2).

Compared with adolescents (< 18 years), older adults (≥ 65 years) were significantly more likely to attend their appointments (PR: 0.68; 95% CI: 0.64–0.71), whereas young adults (18–64 years) presented the highest prevalence of nonattendance (PR: 1.04; 95% CI: 1.01–1.09).

Cultural factors may partially explain some of the patterns observed. For instance, the prevalence of nonattendance was highest in September (PR: 1.09; 95% CI: 1.00–1.19), which coincides with Chilean national holidays. Similarly, Saturdays—typically considered rest days—were strongly associated with missed appointments (PR: 1.46; 95% CI: 1.33–1.59). Afternoon appointments (12:00–17:00) also presented a greater prevalence of nonattendance (PR: 1.08; 95% CI: 1.04–1.12), with an even greater increase for those scheduled after 17:00, outside standard working hours (PR: 1.20; 95% CI: 1.13–1.26).

When stratified by type of healthcare professional, general practitioners had the lowest prevalence of missed appointments (PR: 0.46; 95% CI: 0.42–0.50). In contrast, appointments with nutritionists, dentists, and psychologists had substantially higher nonattendance prevalence rates than did the reference group of assistants (PR: 1.77; 95% CI: 1.61–1.96; PR: 1.32; 95% CI: 1.20–1.44; and PR: 1.82; 95% CI: 1.64–2.02, respectively).

Finally, booking modality was also associated with differences in attendance. Appointments scheduled via telephone or through the institutional website had a greater prevalence of nonattendance than those booked in person did (PR: 1.41; 95% CI: 1.35–1.47). Although digital booking methods improve access, issues such as lack of trust or perceived reliability may influence patient behavior.

Discussion

This study provides novel insights into the determinants of appointment nonattendance in primary health care (PHC) settings within a large urban commune in Chile. With a no-show rate of 21.8%, the findings reveal a substantial gap in care continuity, exceeding national estimates for specialty care (16.5%) [1]. These results underscore the urgent need for localized, evidence-based interventions to reduce missed appointments and optimize health service delivery.

Sociodemographic determinants

Age emerged as a key determinant of nonattendance. In contrast to previous Chilean studies that identified older age as a risk factor [21], the present findings indicate that adults aged 65 years and older were significantly more likely to attend their appointments in primary health care (PHC). Conversely, adults aged 18–64 years exhibited the highest prevalence of nonattendance, a pattern consistent with international evidence linking work-related constraints and competing priorities to lower appointment adherence [6, 15].

Insurance status was also associated with appointment attendance. Patients affiliated with private health insurance showed a slightly lower prevalence of nonattendance compared with those covered by the public system. This finding may be partially explained by the recent incorporation of privately insured individuals into PHC services, suggesting the need for closer monitoring of their utilization patterns.

Although women represented the majority of PHC users in the sample, sex was not an independent predictor of nonattendance in the multivariate model. While many determinants of nonattendance were shared across sexes, sex-stratified analyses revealed differences in the magnitude and statistical significance of certain associations. These results suggest that variations in attendance are more strongly influenced by structural and contextual factors—such as insurance coverage, appointment timing, and booking modality—than by sex itself.

Among the administrative variables, booking modality had a significant effect on attendance. Appointments scheduled by telephone or through online platforms presented a greater prevalence of nonattendance than those booked in person. Although these systems are designed to increase accessibility, their relatively recent implementation may generate distrust, particularly in settings where digital gaps persist. Similar findings have been reported in the international literature, where digital literacy and perceived system reliability have been identified as key determinants of appointment adherence [8].

The appointment timing also played an important role. A higher no-show prevalence was observed for appointments scheduled on Saturdays and during extended hours (after 17:00). These patterns are consistent with international evidence highlighting the influence of work schedules, daily routines, and leisure time on attendance behavior [6, 7]. The markedly high prevalence of nonattendance in September—coinciding with Chilean national holidays—illustrates the impact of cultural factors on health-seeking behavior and highlights the need to account for seasonality in service planning.

Professional assigned

A significant variation in nonattendance was observed across healthcare professional types. General practitioners presented the lowest prevalence of missed appointments, reflecting both the central importance patients place on general medical care and the physician-centered structure of primary health care. In contrast, appointments with nutritionists, dentists, and psychologists were associated with a markedly higher prevalence of nonattendance. This finding underscores a contradiction within the mission of primary health care, which seeks to deliver comprehensive services and address conditions related to nutrition, oral health, and mental health. This situation represents an important warning signal, highlighting the need to reverse the prevailing perception among patients who preventive actions and comprehensive health check-ups are of lesser importance.

Policy and practice implications

Previous studies have demonstrated that reminders via text messages, postal mail, and telephone calls are effective in reducing nonattendance [4]. On the basis of this evidence, targeted use of phone calls and mobile text messages is recommended, particularly for appointments with nutritionists, dentists, and psychologists. In Renca, reminder systems currently include text messages for a portion of scheduled appointments and telephone calls made by health centers. However, these strategies are not yet standardized through evidence-based protocols. The implementation of targeted reminder interventions would not only optimize service efficiency but also allow for systematic evaluation of their effectiveness in future studies. Notably, in 2023, Renca became the first commune in Chile to implement the telehealth strategy promoted by the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) [22] across all PHC services, representing an important advancement in demand management and access optimization.

Strengths and limitations

The strengths of this study include the large sample size and the use of a reliable health information system integrating clinical and administrative data. By focusing on PHC, this study addresses a critical knowledge gap in Chile. Nonetheless, certain limitations should be acknowledged. The absence of qualitative data restricts the exploration of patient motivations and contextual influences on attendance behavior. Additionally, residual confounding by unmeasured variables—such as socioeconomic status, comorbidities, or health literacy—cannot be excluded.

Conclusions

This study revealed a no-show rate of 21.8% in primary health care (PHC) within a large urban commune in Chile. The findings demonstrate that nonattendance is influenced by multiple sociodemographic, administrative, and professional factors. Adults aged 18–64 years, patients covered by the public insurance system, and those with appointments scheduled through digital or telephone platforms, during extended hours, on Saturdays, or in September, presented the highest prevalence of nonattendance. In contrast, older adults (≥ 65 years) and patients consulting general practitioners showed greater adherence to scheduled appointments.

These results underscore the importance of strengthening strategies to improve attendance, particularly among working-age adults, patients in the public system, and those receiving preventive or nonmedical services such as nutrition, dentistry, and psychology. Evidence-based interventions—such as targeted text messages and telephone reminders—should be implemented and prioritized for high-risk groups and time slots. Addressing cultural and organizational factors, including perceptions of preventive care and trust in digital booking systems, is essential for reducing missed appointments and optimizing service delivery in PHC.

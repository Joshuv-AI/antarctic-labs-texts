Use of Morphological Index as a Drug Resistance Marker in Leprosy: Challenges and Considerations
Authors: Harpreet Singh Pawar, Harish Kumar Sagar
Published: 2025

Sir,

The case series by Chhabra et al. (2024)[1] entitled “Morphological Index as a Sensitive Marker of Drug Resistance in Leprosy: A Case Series” proposes that the morphological index (MI) could serve as an early and sensitive indicator for antimicrobial resistance (AMR), even in the absence of clinical relapse. While we appreciate the work done in relation to the MI of antimicrobial drug resistance, there are some points to ponder:

In the case series, 15 patients with positive MI who had completed 8, 12 or 24 months of multi-drug treatment (MDT), chronic Erythema Nodosum Leprosum (ENL) or clinical relapse qualified for AMR testing. MI has been found to be 47% sensitive and ceases to be a good indicator of AMR as suggested and 53% were found to be sensitive to all drugs. Eighty percent of patients sensitive to all drugs had positive MI.It must be noted that MI is not a good technique to rely upon, as three patients with increased MI after completing MDT were found sensitive to all drugs and five out of eight patients without known baseline Bacteriological Index (BI)/MI were found sensitive. Similarly, slow responder cases with positive but reduced MI (from baseline) after 12 months of MDT need continuation of MDT and monitoring.[2]AMR testing is not indicated in cases of chronic ENL with negative MI and completed MDT, as the immune complex-mediated hypersensitivity reaction does not require MDT and only anti-inflammatory drugs or thalidomide will suffice. Episodic persistence of immune complex reactions may continue even after completion of MDT.The study classifies a 32 year old man with new lesions within three months of completing MDT for 12 months as relapse, which may not be appropriate without considering the time (in months), risk and presentation at relapse and needs differentiation from reversal reaction, weak responders to treatment and relapsed patients responding to the same MDT regimen.[23]

The case series offers a new vantage point into AMR in leprosy; however, a well-designed study is required to provide evidence of MI as a sensitive early marker. It is often seen in practice that MI reporting is prone to erroneous results in highly bacillated cases, as viable bacilli may also persist within the clumps/globi and be missed in the counting of individually dispersed solid-stained bacilli. This inherent weakness of tests which also require a high standard of expertise is unsuitable for routine reporting.[4] Other factors like initial BI/MI, primary drug resistance, treatment insufficiency, weak responders and relapse compared to reaction have to be taken into account in future studies, while the current national guidelines for surveillance of AMR in leprosy are the way forward.[5]

Risk Factors of Cardiotoxicity After Breast Cancer Radiation Therapy
Authors: Felicia B Guo, Hyunsoo Joshua No, Natalie J Park, Santino Butler, Scott Jackson, June-Wha Rhee, Daniel Eugene Clark, Carol Marquez, Kathleen Claire Horst, Michael Sargent Binkley
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeConcern for cardiotoxicity after the treatment of breast cancer necessitates a better understanding of factors that may increase the risk of significant (grade ≥3) cardiac events. We investigated clinical factors, coronary artery calcium (CAC), and radiation therapy dose to cardiac structures as predictive post-radiation therapy cardiotoxicity risk factors.Methods and MaterialsWe retrospectively analyzed a cohort of serial patients treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy for stage I to III breast cancer from 2005 through 2014. We measured the incidence of cardiac events after treatment, adjusting for the competing risk of death.ResultsWe identified 174 patients with a median follow-up of 117 months and a median age of 47 years. The 10-year incidence of grade ≥3 cardiac events was 8.5% (95% CI, 4.7%-13.6%) with 13 events observed. 78.9% of the 166 patients with available imaging had no measurable CAC. Patients with nodal positivity (N = 75) were 3 times more likely to develop cardiac toxicity (HR=3.30) and were more likely to receive anthracyclines, hormonal therapy, and regional nodal irradiation during treatment (P < 0.05). After multivariable adjustment for age and smoking status, nodal positive disease remained associated with increased risk of significant cardiac events (P < .05).ConclusionsIn a cohort of patients with breast cancer with low CAC burden and overall low doses of radiation, we observed low rates of cardiotoxicity. However, our findings identify patients with nodal positive disease as a particularly high-risk group, suggesting that close follow-up and optimization of therapies for this subgroup is needed.

Introduction

Several clinical characteristics have been identified as possible risk factors for the development of cardiotoxicity after treatment for breast cancer, including tobacco use, age, comorbid chronic disease, radiation therapy exposure, and treatment with anthracyclines.1, 2, 3 There is also an emerging body of evidence demonstrating associations between radiation therapy doses to cardiac substructures with cardiotoxicity, and studies have suggested that greater radiation received by specific structures, such as the left anterior descending coronary artery (LAD), the left main coronary artery (LMCA), or the right coronary artery (RCA) may increase the incidence of cardiac events after radiation therapy.4, 5, 6, 7

Coronary artery calcium (CAC), a marker for coronary atherosclerosis, is another metric that may predict individual risk of cardiac events, and radiation delivery may both initiate and accelerate the process of atherosclerosis.4,8, 9, 10, 11, 12 By using pretreatment imaging to score the extent of coronary atherosclerosis, patients with a higher baseline risk of development of cardiac toxicity may be identified.

To evaluate risk of cardiotoxicity in patients with breast cancer, we investigated the associations of radiation therapy dose to specific cardiac substructures, extent of CAC, and other clinical risk factors with the development of significant cardiac events after adjuvant therapy. Although several studies have examined CAC progression in breast cancer cohorts, these investigations remain limited in number and scope and lack analysis of radiation dose to specific coronary substructures with the development of cardiotoxicity. Our study reports cardiac outcomes of patients with breast cancer treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by adjuvant radiation therapy. By delineating predictive factors associated with the development of cardiac events following adjuvant treatment of breast cancer, we hope to optimize care for patients with breast cancer to reduce late toxicity.

Methods and Materials

Clinical data

We performed a retrospective review identifying 174 serial patients treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy for stage I to III breast cancer between 2005 and 2014. This study was conducted with approval from the institutional review board. Baseline demographic information, as well as treatment details including radiation dose, fractionation, diagnostic imaging, and treatment planning, was collected via chart review.

For patients with available computed tomography (CT) imaging collected before initiation of adjuvant treatment, Agatston CAC scores were generated by AVIEW CAC (Coreline Soft), a US Food and Drug Administration-approved program. Because none of the CT images included in the analysis used intravenous contrast, all were in optimal condition to use AVIEW CAC’s automated scoring system. Agatston CAC scores were stratified according to the 2023 American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association Guidelines.13

Classifying significant cardiac events

Cardiac events experienced by patients were recorded as significant if they met grade ≥3 criteria in accordance with the grading schema defined by the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events.14 The authors elected to dictate grade ≥3 events as significant to highlight cardiac events requiring “medically significant” intervention.15 Eligible events were then broadly categorized into the following 4 categories: (1) ischemic events, which included conditions such as heart failure (either preserved or reduced ejection fracture), new-onset coronary artery disease, unstable angina, or myocardial infarction, (2) constrictive events, which included conditions such as pericarditis or myocarditis, (3) valvular events or disease, or (4) conduction abnormalities, which included disease requiring a permanent pacemaker, evidence of second- or third-degree heart block, or new-onset arrhythmias requiring intervention.

Dosimetric analysis

Radiation therapy dose to each cardiac substructure was evaluated using MIM Radiation Oncology (MIM Software Inc) and converted to equivalent doses in 2 Gy fractions using an alpha/beta ratio of 3. The left ventricle (LV) and right atrium (RA), in addition to the LAD, the left circumflex artery (LCx), LMCA, and RCA were contoured in accordance with a cardiac contouring atlas used for radiation therapy planning.15 Previous studies have suggested that total radiation therapy dose to all left-sided coronary arteries may increase the risk of grade ≥3 cardiac events; accordingly, a composite “TotalLeft” structure comprising the sum of the left coronary arteries (LAD, LCx, and LMCA) was recorded.4 Mean radiation therapy dose received by the heart, LAD, LCx, LV, LMCA, RCA, RA, TotalLeft, as well as an aggregate structure of all coronary arteries (TotalCor) was noted, in addition to the maximum radiation therapy dose received by the LV and RA.

Regional nodal irradiation

Regional nodal irradiation (RNI) was defined and contoured in accordance with the Radiation Therapy Oncology Group consensus guidelines,16 and RNI, including internal mammary node (IMN) irradiation, was included in the analysis. RNI was defined as an extension of the radiation therapy field to cover the level II axillary nodes superiorly, the IMN chain posteriorly, and in some cases, the addition of a supraclavicular field to cover level III. The IMN region was generally defined as extending from the superior aspect of the medial first rib to the cranial aspect of the fourth rib. In select cases, depending on individual anatomy or when the primary tumor was inferior to the cranial aspect of the fourth rib, the IMN contour extended beyond these borders. IMN coverage was included within the prescription dose volume for tumors that were medial or central in location, or when ≥4 nodes were involved. The IMN chain was treated using deep tangent photon fields. Prone positioning and partial breast irradiation were not used in this cohort.

Statistical analysis

Baseline and clinical characteristics of the total cohort were compared using χ2 tests. Median follow-up was calculated using the reverse Kaplan-Meier method. Fine-Gray subdistribution hazard models were used to create competing-risk models that measured cumulative incidence of cardiac events after treatment, adjusted for the competing risk of death. Both univariable and multivariable risk regression models were generated with results presented as hazard ratios and 95% CIs. Overall survival was measured by Kaplan-Meier analysis and was defined by time from completion of treatment until death because of any cause. Two-tailed P values were considered significant if P < .05. Additional χ2 analyses were performed to compare baseline and clinical characteristics of several subgroups of interest. Patients with CAC noted on imaging were compared with those with scores of Agatston CAC scores of 0 (Table E1), and another χ2 analysis compared patients with disease involving at least 1 lymph node to those with node-free disease (Table E2).

Results

All 174 patients in the cohort were women with a median age at treatment of 47.1 years (IQR, 39.7-57.9 years) and a median follow-up time of 117 months (IQR, 99.0-146.2 months) (Table 1). Within the cohort, 7.5% of women (N = 13) sustained cardiac events following adjuvant therapy. Of the observed cardiac events, conduction events were most common (46.2%, N = 6), followed by ischemic events (30.8%, N = 4). Most of the conduction events were characterized as new-onset arrhythmias (N = 5). At 10 years, the survival probability was 76.4% (95% CI, 69.9-83.5) with a recurrence rate of 32.5% (95% CI, 25.4-39.7) and a cumulative incidence of cardiac toxicity of 8.47% (95% CI, 4.70-13.61) (Fig. 1).

Across the total cohort, 21.3% (N = 37) disclosed either prior or current tobacco use, with 18.4% (N = 32) no longer smoking at the time of radiation therapy initiation, and 2.9% (N = 5) endorsing current tobacco use. Most patients in this cohort did not possess comorbid chronic disease, with just 8.2% (N = 14) and 20.5% (N = 35) of the total cohort having a diagnosis of diabetes or hypertension, respectively, and 7.5% (N = 13) having experienced a grade ≥3 cardiac event before initiation of treatment. Most patients had unilateral disease, with 45.4% (N = 79) having unilateral left-sided disease, 48.3% (N = 84) having unilateral right-sided disease, and 6.3% (N = 11) with bilateral disease. A total of 34.2% (N = 27) of patients with disease involving the left breast (N = 79) used the deep inspiratory breath hold technique during radiation therapy. In 93.7% (N = 163) of patients, radiation was delivered using 3D conformal radiation therapy. The remainder of the patients received radiation therapy via volumetric modulated arc therapy or a mix of volumetric modulated arc therapy and 3D conformal radiation therapy (2.9% [N = 5)], and 3.4% [N = 6], respectively). A total of 71.3% (N = 124) of the total cohort received anthracyclines as part of their treatment regimen, whereas 77.6% (N = 135) received cyclophosphamide. Almost half (48.3%, N = 84) of the cohort received hormonal therapy. Among the cohort, 79.9% (N = 139) received RNI, and 43.7% (N = 76) received IMN.

Competing-risk regression analyses

We performed both univariable and multivariable competing-risk regression analyses, adjusting for the competing risk of death (Table E1). On univariable analysis, nodal positivity (hazard ratio, 3.34; 95% CI, 1.04-1.08, P < .05) was a significant predictor of cardiotoxicity. Variables such as smoking history, utilization of anthracyclines or cyclophosphamide, and bilateral disease were correlated with increased cardiac risk but did not reach significance. Decreased cardiac risk was observed with utilization of deep inspiratory breath hold for left-sided disease during radiation therapy but also did not reach significance. On multivariable analysis which included age and smoking history (both known cardiovascular risk factors), along with nodal positivity, nodal positivity again was a significant predictor for cardiac toxicity. Nodal involvement

Almost half (43.1%, N = 75) of the patients in the total cohort demonstrated disease involving at least 1 lymph node. Patients with nodal positive disease had significantly higher treatment exposure to RNI (P < .01), anthracyclines (P = .01), and hormonal therapy (P = .01) in their regimens (Table E2). These patients demonstrated greater than 3 times of an increased risk of development of grade ≥3 cardiac events (P < .05) (Table E1) and a 10-year cumulative incidence of 14.2% (95% CI, 6.85-24.13) for cardiac toxicity (Fig. 3). There was no significant association between nodal positive disease and utilization of IMN. Utilization of IMN was not a predictor for the development of significant cardiac events in this cohort (Table E1).

Cumulative incidence of grade ≥3 cardiac events among subgroup of patients with disease involving lymph nodes compared to nodal-free disease, adjusted for the competing risk of death.

Dose toxicity

None of the variables accounting for radiation dose delivered to each coronary substructures were significantly associated on univariable analysis with cardiac toxicity, likely because of low overall dose used during breast radiation therapy (Table E1). Within the total cohort, dose to both the mean heart (median, 0.70; IQR, 0.42-1.14 Gy) and individual substructures, such as the LAD (median, 1.80; IQR, 0.14-3.85 Gy), were quite low (Table E2). Among patients with nodal positive disease, however, mean heart dose (median, 0.77; IQR, 0.46-1.36 Gy; P < .05), mean LMCA dose (median, 0.61; IQR, 0.46-1.02 Gy; P = .01), mean RCA dose (median, 0.60; IQR, 0.43-0.98 Gy; P < .01), mean RA dose (median, 0.88; IQR, 0.42-1.19 Gy; P < .01), and maximum RA dose (median, 2.25; IQR, 0.91-3.95 Gy; P < .01), were higher numerically compared to patients without nodal involvement (Table E2). Mean radiation doses to TotalCor and TotalLeft were also observed to be numerically higher in patients with node positive disease (Table E2).

CAC scoring

Of the 174 patients in the whole cohort, 166 (95.4%) had eligible CT imaging that could be scored for baseline pretreatment CAC evaluation before treatment initiation. Of the images available for analysis, 131(78.9%) received an Agatston score of 0 with no measurable CAC, 28 (16.9%) received a score between 1 and 99, and 7 (4.22%) received a CAC score of 100 or higher (Fig. 4). Patients with measurable CAC (Agatston score >0) were significantly associated with risk factors such as age (P = .01), diabetes (P < .01), hypertension (P < .01), and tobacco use (P = .01) (Table E3). A positive Agatston score indicating the presence of CAC on pretreatment imaging was not associated with increased cardiac events (Fig. E1).

Discussion

This study investigated risk factors for the development of cardiotoxicity, defined as grade ≥3 events by the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Event, after radiation therapy for breast cancer in a cohort with nearly 10 years of median follow-up. Overall, we observed a low incidence of cardiac events, with a total of 13 events among 174 patients. The most common cardiac events were conduction abnormalities, many of which were new-onset arrhythmias. In this cohort, there was a low prevalence of comorbid disease, and baseline cardiovascular risk was low as many patients did not have any CAC (CAC = 0) on their initial CT imaging, and just 7.5% had experienced a prior grade ≥3 cardiac event before initiating therapy. These factors may explain why pretreatment CAC scoring and comorbid chronic disease were not significantly associated with the incidence of significant cardiac events after adjuvant treatment in this cohort. We suggest greater utility of baseline CAC scores in identifying patients at high risk of cardiac toxicity in cohorts of patients with a greater prevalence of baseline comorbid chronic disease.

In our total cohort, there were no significant associations between radiation doses to coronary substructures and cardiac events. Previous studies in lung cancer have identified a mean radiation dose threshold of 15 Gy to the LAD that may increase the incidence of cardiac events, but the mean dose to the LAD in our cohort was relatively low, with a median value of 1.80 Gy, indicating that doses did not reach threshold levels required for significant toxicity.4,5 On stratified analysis of patients with nodal positivity, however, we observed that mean radiation dose to certain cardiac structures, including the LMCA, RCA, and RA was significantly higher compared to patients without nodal involvement. Because nodal positive disease often warrants treatment escalation, patients in this subgroup were also significantly more often treated with anthracyclines, hormonal therapy, and RNI, many of which have independently been associated with cardiotoxicity.1, 2, 3 The larger radiation therapy fields used in conjunction with multimodal therapeutic regimens may explain why nodal positivity emerged as the only significant predictor of grade ≥3 cardiac events in this cohort. Although these risk factors may not have independently reached significance as predictors for cardiac risk, our study suggests that patients with nodal positive disease may be particularly vulnerable to the development of cardiac toxicity after adjuvant therapy because of more intensive treatment plans.

Studies have posited that fibrosis and remodeling secondary to radiation may contribute to sinoatrial node dysfunction and suggest that greater radiation delivered to the base of the heart, defined as an area encompassing the RA, the superior vena cava, aortic root, LMCA, proximal LAD, and proximal RCA, is associated with the development of cardiac toxicity.6,7,17 Although mean radiation doses to these structures were low across our whole cohort, we observed statistically greater mean doses to some of these structures among patients with nodal positive disease, suggesting that the treatment plans designed for these patients may confer greater risk of cardiotoxicity.

Limitations of this study include its retrospective nature, modest sample size limited to data from a single institution, low rates of comorbid disease in the patient population, and relatively low incidence of observed cardiac events. Additionally, because only pretreatment CT imaging was available, CAC scores in this study reflect baseline values, precluding assessment of CAC development after therapy. Our study observed no associations between dose to cardiac substructures and cardiac events, likely because of patients receiving low overall radiation doses. Unfortunately, this study was not able to meaningfully use the CAC score as an image-based biomarker predicting for cardiac toxicity, because many patients in this cohort did not have identifiable CAC on imaging.

Conclusions

In this cohort of patients treated for breast cancer, the overall risk of cardiotoxicity was low, and patients treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy and adjuvant radiation therapy received low overall radiation dose to cardiac substructures. Within the cohort, we identified patients with positive lymph node involvement as a high-risk group with greater than 3 times the risk of developing post-radiation cardiotoxicity. The treatment plans of these patients were more likely to include anthracyclines, hormonal therapy, and greater radiation dose to vulnerable coronary structures that may increase risk of cardiac toxicity. Given this subgroup’s observed higher risk of late cardiotoxicity, we recommend close follow-up following adjuvant radiation therapy to monitor for the development of significant cardiac events.

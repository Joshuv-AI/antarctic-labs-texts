LINC00592 is a predictor of poor lung adenocarcinoma outcomes that promotes tumor cell migration and invasion
Authors: Guangchao Zhu, Zhaojian Gong, Jing Chen, Junfeng Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AimThe aim of this study was to examine the functional significance of the long noncoding RNA LINC00592 in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD).MethodsPublicly available data were utilized to evaluate the expression of LINC00592 and its correlation with clinical characteristics. Additionally, the mechanistic and functional roles of this lncRNA were assessed in LUAD through colony formation assays, transwell assays, RNA-seq, and qPCR.ResultsUpregulation and copy number amplification of LINC00592 were observed in LUAD and were found to be positively correlated with pathological stage, overall survival, progression-free survival, disease-specific survival, first-progression-free survival, and post-progression survival in LUAD patients. Silencing of LINC00592 impeded the ability of LUAD cells to migrate, invade, and form colonies, due to its ability to regulate the expression of NTN1, an important adhesion-related molecule.ConclusionLINC00592 is a prognostic marker in LUAD patients and may represent a promising target for therapeutic intervention in this type of cancer.

Introduction

Lung cancers, of which 80 % are classified as non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) cases, account for 27 % of global cancer-associated deaths, making them among the most common forms of cancer [1,2]. Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), the predominant subtype of NSCLC, is estimated to constitute 40 % of all primary lung cancers [3]. Risk factors associated with LUAD include exposure to air pollution and smoking [4]. While early-stage LUAD can be managed surgically, the majority of patients are diagnosed with advanced disease. For these individuals, the efficacy of chemotherapy, surgery, and radiotherapy tends to be limited, resulting in a less than 15 % 5-year survival rate [3,4]. Therefore, it is crucial to establish prognostic biomarkers and viable therapeutic targets for this type of cancer.

Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), which exceed 200 nucleotides in length, generally lack the potential to be translated into functionally active proteins [5]. However, they are capable of modulating the function and expression of target genes at the transcriptional and epigenetic levels. Many related interactions can shape gene expression, including those between lncRNAs, miRNAs, proteins, and mRNAs [6]. Recently, lncRNAs have been reported to influence the pathology of several types of disease [7]. Dong et al. determined that the lncRNA DGCR5 is capable of inhibiting hsa-miR-22-3p, thereby promoting the progression of LUAD [8]. Chen et al. further documented that the lncRNA FAM83A-AS1 targets the HIF-1α/glycolysis axis, promoting the proliferation and migration of LUAD cells [9]. In a separate analysis, Zhang et al. noted that the super-enhancer hijacking LINC01977 promotes malignant growth in early-stage LUAD tumors exhibiting an addiction to canonical TGF-β/SMAD3 signaling activity [10]. While these reports have been informative, the expression and mechanistic functions of lncRNAs in LUAD remain incompletely understood. Yuan et al. determined that LINC00592 was aberrantly expressed in cervical cancer and might activate cancer progression via modulating the expression of ZNF20, ZNF441, ZNF573 and TMF1 [11]. Wu et al. found that LINC00592 promotes the growth and metastasis of breast cancer (BC) by enhancing WIF1 promoter methylation and reducing WIF1 transcription [12].

In this study, analysis of public datasets revealed the overexpression of LINC00592 in LUAD. Further investigation into the mechanism by which LINC00592 promotes LUAD progression demonstrated that LINC00592 facilitates tumor cell migration, invasion, and proliferation through regulation of NTN1. These findings suggest that LINC00592 may serve as a potential biomarker for the diagnosis, prognosis assessment, and therapeutic response evaluation in LUAD patients.

Materials and methods

LINC00592 expression analyses

The GSE123902 single-cell sequencing LUAD dataset, available through the Tumor Immune Single-cell Hub (TISCH, http://tisch1.comp-genomics.org/home/) database [13], was utilized to analyze LUAD metastasis and identify molecular biomarkers associated with it. The R package Seurat was used for single-cell sequencing data analysis. The top 2000 highly variable genes were utilized for subsequent analyses, with the UMAP algorithm applied for dimensionality reduction and clustering. The FindMarkers function was used to calculate differential genes between subtype 0 and other subtypes. Additionally, the expression of LINC00592 in LUAD was analyzed with the TCGA LUAD cohort data from the Gene Expression Profiling Interactive Analysis(GEPIA, http://gepia.cancer-pku.cn/detail.php) online database [14] and two gene chip datasets, GSE40791 and GSE32665, from the lnCAR(https://lncar.renlab.org/) online database [15], These analyses were completed using the default parameters of the databases.

Correlation analysis between LINC00592 and the prognosis of LUAD

We analyzed the correlation between LINC00592 and LUAD patient prognosis based on the RNA sequencing data from the TCGA LUAD cohort in the BEST (https://rookieutopia.com/app_direct/BEST/) online database and the LUAD gene chip data from the Kaplan-Meier Plotter online tool (https://kmplot.com/analysis/index.php?p=background) [16], using default parameters. The correlation analysis between LINC00592 copy number and LUAD patient prognosis was conducted using the cBioPortal database (https://www.cbioportal.org/) [17], based on TCGA LUAD cohort data and analyzed according to the default parameters.

Cell culture and transfection

A549 and PC9, which are human LUAD cell line, were obtained from the Central Laboratory of Henan Cancer Hospital and cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum and 1 % penicillin/streptomycin in a humidified incubator with 5 % CO2. 50 nM negative control (NC) siRNA, LINC00592-specific siRNA (siRNA1-CATGCCTCATTCATGCTTA and siRNA2-CAGACCACAAACAACAACA) or NTN1-specific siRNA(GGCAAAACCTGCAACCAAA) from Ribobio (Guangzhou, China) was transfected into these cells with Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, CA, USA).

qPCR

Ncmzol reagent (M5100, NCM Biotech, China) was used according to the manufacturer's instructions to extract RNA, which was then reverse transcripted to produce cDNA for use as a template in qPCR analyses. These analyses were performed using an ABI real-time PCR instrument (7500, ABI, USA). The primers used were as follows: LINC00592, F: 5′-AACCCCGAAACATTGGCAC-3′ and R 5′-CACTAAAGGCTGGTCGGTCA-3′. NTN1, F: 5' - ACTGCGATTCCTACTGCAAGGC -3′ and R5′- TTGTCCGCCTTCAGGATGTGGA-3'. WIF1, F:5′-GGTGCCGAAATGGAGGCTTTTG-3′ and R: 5′-GATGCAGAAACCAGGAGTCACAC-3'. GAPDH, F: 5′-AACGACCACTTTGTCAAGC-3′ and R 5′-TGAGGTCCACCACCCTGT-3′. Expression levels of LINC00592 and NTN1 were normalized to those of GAPDH.

Transwell assays

Cellular migration and invasive potential were assessed using 8.0 μm Transwell inserts (Corning Inc, NY, USA). Briefly, the upper chamber was filled with 200 μL of serum-free media with 5 × 104 cells/well. For migration assays, uncoated inserts were used, while for invasion assays, Matrigel-coated inserts were utilized. The lower chamber was filled with 800 μL of media containing 15 % FBS. After incubation for 12 h for migration or 36 h for invasion, cells that did not traverse the membrane were carefully removed. The remaining cells were fixed with paraformaldehyde, followed by staining with crystal violet, washing, drying, and imaging of five random fields of view under a microscope to count the cells.

Colony formation

After transfecting the cells with either a negative control (NC) or LINC00592-specific siRNAs for 24 h, the cells were harvested. Then, 500 cells were transferred per well of a 6-well plate, with each well containing 2 mL of medium. The cells were incubated at 37 °C until colonies became visible. On day 5, the cells were washed with cold PBS, fixed for 10 min using a 3.7 % formaldehyde solution, stained for 10 min with 0.1 % crystal violet, and then washed again with PBS to remove excess stain.

RNA-seq

RNA was extracted from control cells and from cells in which LINC00592 had been silenced. The extracted RNA was then sent to BGI Genomics for the construction of a cDNA library and subsequent RNA-seq analysis. Genes that exhibited significant differences in expression were further subjected to qPCR-based validation. Highly expressed genes in LINC00592-silenced cells were selected for functional enrichment analyses. These analyses aimed to identify pathways potentially linked to the invasiveness and migration of LUAD cells. Differential gene enrichment analysis was performed using the Metascape online analysis tool (http://metascape.org/gp/index.html#/main/step1) according to the default parameters [17].

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as means with standard error and were compared using GraphPad Prism (CA, USA) with Student's t-tests and ANOVAs. P < 0.05 was selected to define significance.

Results

LINC00592 is a lncRNA that is associated with metastatic LUAD

The predominant drivers of mortality among LUAD patients are disease metastasis and recurrence, however, the mechanistic basis for these processes has yet to be fully elucidated. Single-cell sequencing strategies have provided a wealth of data suitable for exploring the drivers of LUAD pathogenesis. For this study, we accessed the TISCH database to download the GSE123902 dataset, which enabled a more detailed analysis of LUAD metastasis-related data. After performing dimensionality reduction and clustering, tumor cells were categorized into five different subpopulations (Fig. 1A). Utilizing the GSVA algorithm to evaluate metastasis and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) scores for individual subpopulations, we found that subpopulation 0 had the highest metastasis and EMT scores (Fig. 1B). We then evaluated the regulatory molecules associated with higher metastasis scores in this subpopulation through a marker gene analysis approach, identifying multiple marker genes (Fig. 1C). Among these was LINC00592, a lncRNA that was significantly upregulated in these cells. LINC00592 favors LUAD cell invasive, migratory, and colony forming activity

To assess the role of LINC00592 in modulating the malignant behaviors of lung cancer cells, siRNA constructs were used to knock down this lncRNA in A549 cells and PC9 cells (Fig. 5A and B). Knocking down LINC00592 resulted in impaired cellular invasion and migration in Transwell-based assays (Fig. 5C–F). Additionally, the ability of these A549 cells and PC9 cells to form colonies was compromised in the absence of normal LINC00592 expression (Fig. 5G–H). LINC00592 controls the expression of the adhesion-related molecule NTN1

To elucidate the mechanistic basis for the effects of LINC00592 on the malignancy-related behaviors, LINC00592-silenced A549 cells were subjected to a RNA-Seq experiment. Principal component analysis (PCA) revealed distinct clustering among negative control (NC1-3) and silenced (siRNA1-3) groups, with clear differentiation between these groups (Fig. 6A). Comparative gene expression analysis identified 527 and 733 genes that were upregulated and downregulated, respectively, in cells with LINC00592 knockdown (Fig. 6B). Gene enrichment analyses, focusing on genes upregulated in LINC00592-silenced cells, revealed that the cell-cell adhesion signaling pathway, closely associated with the migratory and invasive activity of LUAD cells, was significantly enriched for these genes (Fig. 6C–D). Validation of differential gene expression using a qPCR-based approach confirmed the upregulation of NTN1 in LINC00592-silenced cells, consistent with the RNA-seq data (Fig. 6E). NTN1 inhibits LUAD cell invasive, migratory, and colony-forming activity

To further investigate the mechanism by which LINC00592 regulates NTN1 expression to promote invasion, migration, and colony-forming activity in LUAD cells, we knocked down LINC00592 and NTN1 expression in PC9 cells(Fig. 7A and B). The results demonstrated that NTN1 knockdown enhanced the invasion, migration, and colony-forming activity of LUAD cells (Fig. 7D–G). Simultaneously, co-knockdown of LINC00592 and NTN1 in PC9 cells alleviated the inhibitory effect on LUAD cell invasion, migration, and colony-forming activity observed with LINC00592 knockdown alone (Fig. 7D–G). These findings indicated that LINC00592 promotes LUAD cell invasion, migration, and colony-forming activity by regulating NTN1 expression. The previous study showed that LINC00592 promoted bladder cancer progression by regulating WIF1 [12]. Therefore, we also explored whether LINC00592 regulated WIF1 expression in the LUAD. Contrary to the previous findings in bladder cancer, silencing LINC00592 in LUAD cells reduced WIF1 expression (Fig. 7C). Discussion

Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most commonly diagnosed subtype of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and a majority of patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage due to the absence of early-stage symptoms [18]. The increasing morbidity and mortality associated with LUAD underscore its significance as a global public health threat. Improving prognostic outcomes in LUAD patients relies on the establishment of a reliable prognostic signature. In recent years, studies on long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) have become increasingly prevalent, given their crucial regulatory roles in tumor biology [19]. Abnormal expression of lncRNAs has been associated with tumor cell proliferation, invasiveness, and metastatic progression, influencing all stages of oncogenic development [[20], [21], [22], [23]]. Consequently, lncRNAs are considered promising targets for therapeutic interventions.

Wang et al. revealed that LINC00592 was upregulation in LUAD tissues and was related to ferroptosis [24]. Here, analyses of several databases ultimately revealed the upregulation of LINC00592 in LUAD. High levels of expression for this lncRNA were associated with more advanced clinical staging and worse prognostic outcomes in LUAD, suggesting that LINC00592 may promote malignant LUAD progression. However, the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. Cui et al. noted a positive correlation between LINC00592 and focal adhesion, as identified through TCGA database and gene set enrichment analyses [25]. Cell cycle progression and motility are both dependent on the dynamic assembly and disassembly of focal adhesions [26]. Transwell and colony formation assays in this study showed that silencing LINC00592 was sufficient to enhance LUAD cell migration, invasion, and colony-forming activity. This finding aligns well with a prior report published by Zhang et al. [27]. Nevertheless, the mechanism of action of LINC00592 remains unclear.

RNA-seq and qPCR-based strategies also led to the recognition that LINC00592 silencing led to the upregulation of the cell adhesion-related NTN1 gene. This gene encodes Netrin, a member of the laminin-related secreted protein family. In the context of neurodevelopment, Netrin-1 is known to influence the migration of cells and axons. NTN1 is also recognized as a regulator of lung, pancreatic, breast, muscle, and vascular tissue formation through its effects on cell-cell interactions, cellular migration, and cell adhesion to the extracellular matrix (ECM) [28]. As an adhesion-related molecule expressed on tumor cell surfaces, NTN1 may be essential for tumor cells' ability to interact with other cells or the ECM. Therefore, NTN1 downregulation is likely to contribute to the incidence and progression of metastatic tumors.

NTN1 also plays a crucial role in inhibiting angiogenic activity. The binding of NTN1 to its receptor protein UNC5B can reportedly inhibit the migratory activity of endothelial cells, thereby limiting angiogenesis [29]. Larrivee et al. demonstrated that exposure of wild-type embryos to NTN1 resulted in endothelial tip cell retraction, which contributes to the impairment of endothelial cell migration in vitro [30]. Bouvree et al. found that NTN1 could suppress sprouting angiogenesis in avian embryos [31]. Since the formation of new blood vessels is a prerequisite for tumor cells to effectively migrate and invade, this process inevitably precedes disease progression. Therefore, NTN1 is likely to influence LUAD invasiveness and migratory activity by suppressing angiogenesis.

In conclusion, our results revealed the upregulation of LINC00592 in LUAD, underscoring its potential as a prognostic biomarker for this cancer type. Mechanistically, LINC00592 has been shown to enhance LUAD cell invasivity, colony formation, and migration in vitro by regulating the gene encoding the adhesion molecule NTN1. However, the specific mechanism of action requires further investigation, particularly regarding how LINC00592 regulates NTN1 expression, the role of NTN1 as an adhesion molecule in metastatic tumor progression, and its impact on angiogenesis. To explore the potential mechanism by which LINC00592 regulates NTN1 gene expression, we compared the nucleotide sequences of LINC00592 mRNA with the mRNA and pre-mRNA of the NTN1 gene. The results revealed that exon 4 of the LINC00592 transcript (ENST00000668621) is perfectly antisense to a segment within intron 2 of NTN1 pre-mRNA, raising the possibility that LINC00592 forms an RNA–RNA duplex that modulates NTN1 splicing or stability. Functional dissection of this interaction is now under way and will be reported in a subsequent study. Additionally, animal model studies are necessary to confirm the functional role of LINC00592 and NTN1 in vivo. These insights will help guide the development of more effective therapeutic strategies for LUAD and enhance the utility of LINC00592 as both a prognostic biomarker and a therapeutic target.

Introduction

One of the goals of physicians working in intensive care unit (ICU) is to ensure cellular oxygenation by maintaining adequate tissue perfusion in critically ill patients. Stroke volume is a major determinant of tissue perfusion and, therefore, a key parameter to monitor in patients with haemodynamic instability.1 Left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) velocity-time integral (VTI) measured using pulsed-wave Doppler is widely used as a surrogate of stroke volume to assess haemodynamic modifications.2–5 This value reflects the stroke distance, which varies proportionately to stroke volume in case of haemodynamic changes resulting from therapeutic interventions (fluid administration, vasoactive drugs, etc) or disease processes. An increase in stroke volume (or LVOT VTI) is expected in response to fluid administration and attests to its efficacy. A lack of increase indicates that the cardiovascular system is no longer fluid responsive, and that fluid administration is not improving tissue perfusion and creates congestion instead.6–8 Therefore, the ability to monitor LVOT VTI should be a competence required from every ICU physician.9 10 However, international ICU guidelines on echocardiography do not consider LVOT VTI measurement as a basic skill but rather as a competence of advanced operators.11 More recently, the European Society of Intensive Care Medicine published expert recommendations on echocardiography, setting the evaluation of LVOT VTI as a basic skill, but with a weak recommendation lacking published evidence to support this statement.12

The main difficulty in measuring LVOT VTI is achieving an optimal alignment between the Doppler beam and the direction of blood flow through the LVOT, which typically requires an adequate apical five-chamber view. Recently, research in artificial intelligence (AI) applied to medical imaging constituted a breakthrough in the acquisition of images. UltraSight is a company specialised in AI applied to echocardiography. Their software is based on neural networks using machine learning to analyse precisely the image obtained by an operator. The software indicates then to the operator in real time on-screen how to optimise the image by mobilising the probe until the desired view is correctly obtained, with the best quality. Images acquired by novices using UltraSight guidance have been shown to be suitable for diagnostic interpretation and quantitative analysis.13

The primary objective of the mini-trained VTI study is to assess the equivalence of the measures of LVOT VTI obtained by minimally trained operators as compared with those obtained by experts, both guided by UltraSight AI software. If the interchangeability of minimally trained operators and expert measurements can be demonstrated, this will constitute a strong basis to upgrade the measurement of LVOT VTI as a basic competence in critical care ultrasound, required from every ICU physician.

Methods and analysis

Study design and population

This prospective randomised controlled trial will be conducted at three French teaching hospitals from November 2024 until March 2026 (estimated recruitment completion). This study adheres to the Standard Protocol Items for Randomised Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines for randomised controlled trials.14 Patients aged 18 and more, hospitalised in ICU, in whom fluid administration is considered necessary by the clinician in charge, based on the presence of at least one hypoperfusion criterion (either >10% decrease in mean arterial pressure with respect to baseline value, skin mottling, oliguria (<0.5 mL/kg/hour), deterioration in the level of consciousness, hyperlactataemia or decrease in central venous oxygen saturation) will be considered eligible. Patients will also have to be affiliated to a French Social Security system. Patients with atrial fibrillation will be excluded, due to the inherent variability in beat-by-beat LVOT VTI related to variable diastolic duration. Vulnerable populations (patients under guardianship, curatorship, deprived of liberty) will also be excluded.

An information note will be given to the participant before he (or she) participates in the study (online supplemental file 1). After a reflection period and before the enrolment in the study, the principal investigator, a physician representing the investigator or a qualified person, will obtain the participant’s non-opposition. If the participant is unable to give his (or her) non-opposition because of his (or her) condition and a relative has been identified and is present at the time of inclusion, detailed information on the study will be given to him (or her) and his (or her) non-opposition will be collected. When relatives are unavailable or in the case of emergency inclusion, a non-opposition to the use of the data collected as part of the research will be collected from the patient as soon as his (or her) condition allows it.

Minimally trained operators for LVOT VTI measurement will be medical students having no experience with ultrasound. Experts are ICU attending physicians who are considered experienced and competent in Transthoracic echography (TTE) (either board-certified or considered locally as experts). All minimally trained operators will benefit from a standardised preliminary training to teach them how to obtain the apical five-chamber view and to acquire LVOT VTI using pulsed-wave Doppler, according to the guidelines of the American Society of Echocardiography. All operators (minimally trained and experts) will be using the same ultrasound platform: Philips Lumify equipped with the UltraSight AI software.

Preliminary workshops for minimally trained operators will be conducted by one of the experts at each centre. These workshops will start with a short lecture on the relationship among LVOT VTI, stroke volume and cardiac output and will explain the rationale for LVOT VTI monitoring to conduct volume expansion. Then, over a period of 2–3 weeks, minimally trained operators will be taught to acquire the apical five-chamber view and to measure LVOT VTI using the Lumify, aided by UltraSight guidance. LVOT VTI will be measured by tracing the envelope of the pulsed-wave Doppler spectrum of systolic velocity from the apical five-chamber view. The sample volume will be placed within the LVOT proximal to the aortic valve to obtain a narrow spectral signal of a laminar flow, with a rapid upstroke and an end-systolic click.15 16 Each minimally trained operator is required to achieve LVOT VTI acquisitions in 10 ICU patients. These 10 exams will be supervised by an expert, who will rate the quality of the 2D images and of the LVOT VTI measurements as well as the delay required to achieve the acquisition on an individual logbook. Once this training is completed, minimally trained operators will be invited to participate in the study.

Intervention and randomisation

Operators will be separated into two groups. Group A: medical students having completed the standardised minimal training described above. Group B: ICU attending physicians considered experienced and competent in echocardiography. When a patient, in whom fluid administration is considered necessary by the clinician in charge, one member of group A and one member of group B will proceed independently to evaluate LVOT VTI, guided by UltraSight AI software. The measure of LVOT VTI will be calculated as the average value of three consecutive cardiac cycles. The order of acquisition between group A and group B will be randomised. Each operator will be blinded to the values obtained by the other. After baseline LVOT VTI measurements, a 250 mL fluid challenge of crystalloid (Ringer Lactate) or a passive leg raising test (depending on the appreciation of the clinician) will be performed. Measurements will be repeated immediately after the fluid challenge or 1 min after the initiation of the passive leg raising test by the same operators, still blinded to each other, guided by the UltraSight AI software. All measurement steps will be timed. It is important that the two measurements are acquired over the shortest possible interval to avoid spontaneous haemodynamic variations due to the short half-life of crystalloids. If the passive raising test is used to assess fluid responsiveness, the patient will be positioned twice (before each operator’s acquisition) in order to produce a similar fluid shift for both acquisitions. Randomisation of operators’ order will be done via the RedCap software (Research electronic data capture), a secure web application designed to support data capture for research studies. Each minimally trained operator will contribute to the study only once, in order to limit the risk of gaining experience by taking measures on more than one patient (figure 1).

Sample size calculation

Based on Jozwiak et al,17 the median relative difference expressed in percentage in LVOT VTI measurement between two expert operators is 14% IQR=(8–26).

Considering an equivalence test of the mean relative difference of LVOT VTI measured by minimally trained operators guided by AI as compared with that measured by expert, with an equivalence margin of 14%, a 25% SD (based on the SD imputation from IQR18), a correlation of 25% between operators, a 95% power and a significance level of 0.01, a total of 91 patients is needed (SAS software: paired mean statement of the Power procedure providing power and sample size analyses for equivalence t-tests involving paired samples). Considering patients with missing VTI measurements, a total of 100 patients will be considered (10% increase).

The sample size calculation was performed considering only one of the two measures of LVOT VTI (pre-fluid and post-fluid challenge), due to the absence of hypothesis concerning the variability of the two measures.

Primary endpoint

The relative difference expressed in percentage of LVOT VTI measurement evaluated by the minimally trained operators as compared with LVOT VTI measurement by experts, both guided by AI,

will be analysed with the use of a one sample t-test. Two-sided 98% CI for relative difference of LVOT VTI, along with the equivalence bounds, will be presented. Equivalence will be concluded with 1% type 1 error if the 98% CI is contained within the equivalence limits (−14% to 14%). The primary analysis will be carried out on the two LVOT VTI measures of pre-fluid and post-fluid challenge.

The systematic bias will be calculated as the mean of the difference between measures of LVOT VTI variation obtained by minimally trained operators and experts (method of Bland and Altman). As the value of the bias can vary with the mean of the measurement, the percentage difference ((minimally trained measurement−expert measurement)/mean (minimally trained, expert)) will be used and will be plotted against the mean (minimally trained, expert). We know that a difference of 14% between two measurements is clinically relevant.17 Thus, the maximum acceptable difference is ±14%. Discussion

This study would be the first to evaluate in critically ill patients the interchangeability of the measures of LVOT VTI obtained by minimally trained operators with those obtained by trained physicians. If interchangeability is confirmed, this study could contribute to reinforcing the recommendation that the measure of LVOT VTI should be considered as a basic competence required from every ICU physician. Up to now, this measurement is still widely considered as an ‘advanced skill’, preventing a broader use and leading to disparities in practices.19 20 Recent European guidelines on critical care ultrasound recommended that the estimation of cardiac output by LVOT VTI should be a basic skill for intensive care physicians but with a weak recommendation, lacking published evidence.12

This research also has a pedagogic dimension with the training of medical students. Few studies have analysed the learning curves of minimally trained operators in critical care echocardiography. Vignon et al showed that, with limited training (10–12 examinations), ICU residents were able to recognise adequately impaired left ventricular function, left or right ventricular dilatation, pericardial effusion and pleural effusion.12 21 All these findings relied on non-quantitative 2D echo interpretation, but not on Doppler measurements.

The threshold value to define the reproducibility of the measure by minimally trained operators is set at 14%, based on Jozwiak et al.17 In this study, the authors evaluated the least significant change for the value of LVOT VTI between two expert operators at 14%, and the least significant change for the value of LVOT VTI by the same operator at 11%. This work shed light on the possibility of error in the evaluation of fluid responsiveness as these values of least significant change are quite close to the values of 10% or 15% often used as a threshold to define fluid responders, hence can lead to overdiagnose or underdiagnose fluid responders.22 However, our study tries to demonstrate the reproducibility of minimally trained operators’ measurements. If the minimally trained operators manage to obtain LVOT VTI measurements within the range of 14% of that of the experts, the two measurements could be seen as interchangeable.

Potential limitations may apply to our study. Our estimation that 10 preliminary echocardiographic examinations are sufficient for minimally trained operators to gain enough experience relies on limited unpublished data. In addition, the use of UltraSight AI software reduces the external validity of the results since the minimally trained operators’ ability to obtain a standard five-chamber view without AI guidance is not evaluated. However, it is highly likely that AI-guided image acquisition will be available on most ultrasound platforms in the near future. It must be noted that, even though the operators are guided by UltraSight for the obtention of a correct five-chamber view, pulsed-wave Doppler acquisition and velocity envelope contouring are performed without AI support. We deliberately chose not to acquire data in the ‘traditional way’ (ie, not AI-guided) to minimise the data acquisition time in order to reduce potential variability in stroke volume related to time (either spontaneous or due to the limited half-life of crystalloids), and to improve study feasibility and patient comfort.

The maximal haemodynamic effects of fluid challenge do not last more than 10 minutes after the end of the crystalloid infusion.23 24 Thus, the second operator might assess the effect of the fluid challenge with a bias related to the delay in obtaining the measurement by the first operator. However, the randomisation of the order of acquisition between experts and minimally trained operators will mitigate this bias. To our knowledge, no study has evaluated the reproducibility of repeated passive leg raising tests over a short period of time. Here again, the randomisation of the order of acquisition between experts and minimally trained operators will compensate for this potential bias.

In conclusion, this is the first study aiming to evaluate the equivalence of quantitative LVOT-VTI Doppler measurements obtained by minimally trained and expert operators.

Research on the relationships between river erosion and sedimentation and energy loss in the Yangtze River Basin of China
Authors: Heng Wu, Shengdong Cheng, Tian Wang, Zhanbin Li, Peng Li, Yingying Geng, Xingyue Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRiver scour and siltation, inherently prolonged and multifaceted phenomena, profoundly impact the hydraulic conductivity and navigational safety of waterways, thereby rendering the precise forecasting of their fluctuations a paramount and unresolved challenge. This study embarks on a novel investigative trajectory by examining energy dissipation in rivers, specifically through the scrutiny of empirical hydrological records. Employing the principles of energy balance theory, this study elucidates the intricate connection between energy losses and sedimentation volumes in the upper reaches of the Yangtze River and at its confluence with the significant tributary, the Jialing River.Our findings diverge from conventional wisdom, revealing that standard metrics of energy loss, encompassing kinetic, potential, and total mechanical energies, along with momentum losses, inadequately characterize the complexity of sediment deposition processes. In contrast, we demonstrate that the loss of suspension power is a more indicative measure of sedimentation quantity. Through meticulous comparisons of various formulations for calculating suspension power, we identified a formula grounded in the concept of minimal available power dissipation in river systems that exhibits superior accuracy in correlating suspension power losses with sedimentation patterns in river sections. This formula's efficacy is further enhanced when considering aggregate suspension power dissipation under the influence of tributary inflows, enabling refined predictions of sedimentation variations along river segments. In conclusion, the insights derived from this meticulous research contribute significantly to the realm of river resource management and the pursuit of sustainable, high-quality aquatic ecosystem development.Keywords: River erosion and sedimentation, Energy loss, Yangtze river basin, Suspension power, River confluence

Introduction

The Yangtze River in China plays a significant role in the economy, society, and ecology [1,2]. However, influenced by long-term natural processes and human activities, the issue of river erosion and siltation in the Yangtze River basin has become increasingly prominent [3,4]. With the acceleration of urbanization and industrialization, changes in land use patterns have led to significant changes in sediment sources, further exacerbating the problems of river erosion and sedimentation [[5], [6], [7]]. Meanwhile, many forms of river engineering conducted by humans also greatly influence the stability of rivers. Modifying river morphology can increase flood hazards and present numerous difficulties for hydraulic engineering, watershed planning, river management, and river restoration [[8], [9], [10]]. Therefore, it is imperative to quantitatively study the changes in river erosion and deposition.

Currently, investigating sediment transport through the lens of energy theory represents an important direction in sediment transport mechanics. When river water flows, it encounters frictional resistance and turbulent dissipation, leading to energy loss [11,12]. The notion of energy balance has been fully established in the field of river science, with rivers being considered open systems with a turbulent viscous heat dissipation structure [13]. If the volume of sediment input into a river reach over a given time period is less than the reach's capacity to transport that sediment, this imbalance may induce erosive processes along the river boundaries, subsequently leading to bed scouring and fostering channel instability [14]. If the amount of sediment entering a river segment is lower than the river's sediment transport capacity within a specific timeframe, it can cause erosion of the riverbanks, leading to riverbed erosion, river instability, and potential harm to flood control facilities or other hydraulic structures [15]. Sediment tends to accumulate at confluences due to the significant interactions among hydrology, geomorphology, and sedimentation processes. This accumulation forms shallow estuarine shoals or central shoals, which hinders the growth of the shipping industry and is a primary focus of current research [16]. Existing domestic and foreign research primarily focuses on hydrodynamic characteristics to explore sediment movement characteristics and riverbed evolution in river confluence areas [17,18]. These studies use numerical simulation technology to model and predict the riverbed evolution and sediment transport process [19,20]. However, they often emphasize local features, making it challenging to fully capture the complex behavior of the entire water flow system. Additionally, they frequently overlook the role of energy conversion during the movement of water flows.

In recent years, the application of energy theory to study sediment transport has garnered significant attention, indicating the enduring relevance of this approach. Energy serves as the fundamental driving force behind water flow and sediment transport [21]. Throughout its course, the river system continuously exchanges matter and energy with the external environment. From an energetic perspective, the academic community has proposed several extreme hypotheses regarding the mechanisms of river processes, including the minimum unit river energy theory [22], the minimum energy dissipation rate theory [23], the maximum energy dissipation rate theory [24], and concepts such as runoff erosion power [25]. Compared to traditional evaluation methods, the study of the relationship between river water and sediment based on energy theory has demonstrated significant advantages. Existing research has shown that in the Yellow River Basin, runoff erosion power can effectively establish a relationship between energy and sediment transport, revealing a clear power function relationship between runoff erosion power and sediment transport modulus [26,27]. This discovery not only deepens our understanding of erosion and sediment transport processes but also provides a solid theoretical foundation for studying erosion and sedimentary dynamics at the watershed scale. However, current research on the relationship between river energy and sediment transport predominantly focuses on high-sediment-laden rivers, such as the Yellow River in China. In contrast, the Yangtze River, as the third-longest river in the world, holds significant political, ecological, and economic importance. There is a notable difference in sediment concentration between the Yangtze River and high-sediment rivers. Yet, there remains a lack of research on the energy-sediment relationship in the Yangtze River Basin, particularly concerning the patterns of sediment erosion and deposition in the river confluence area.

This study selects the confluence area of the upper reaches of the Yangtze River and its tributary, the Jialing River, as the research focus. It analyzes the water and sediment conditions of the river from the perspective of energy balance. By exploring the intrinsic relationship between river erosion and sedimentation and the energy loss of sediment-carrying water flow, this study aims to establish the energy-sediment relationship in river confluence areas. The objective is to provide a scientific basis for predicting the evolutionary trends, managing, and protecting the Yangtze River, thereby contributing to the sustainable development goals of the region.

Research areas and methods

Overview of the research area

The Yangtze River is the longest river in China and the third longest in the world, covering almost 1.8 million square kilometers (longitude 90° 33’ −122° 25′ E, latitude 24° 30’ −35° 45’ N) [Fig. 1]. The climate is mostly categorized as a South Asian tropical monsoon climate or a warm temperate monsoon climate. Summer is characterized by high temperatures and rainfall, while winter is characterized by cold temperatures and low humidity. The yearly mean temperature ranges from 5 to 20 °C. Most of the precipitation occurs during the summer months, accounting for 60–80 % of the total annual precipitation. The Yangtze River Basin is a significant economic region in China, representing 1/5 of the total area. The Yangtze River Basin features a rolling topography and plentiful natural resources, making it favorable for the advancement of agriculture, industry, and transportation [28].

The Chinese government has established multiple hydrological stations in the Yangtze River Basin to improve data support for flood control, drought resistance, disaster reduction, water resource development and usage, and management and protection. Zhutuo Station is situated in the upper region of the Yangtze River and is tasked with monitoring the hydrology and water resources in that area. It provides crucial data for water resource management, flood control, and disaster reduction. Yichang Station is situated in the middle section of the Yangtze River, marking the division between the upper and middle sections of the river. It is also the largest port along the main channel of the Yangtze River. It is crucial in the transportation and management of goods in the Yangtze River Basin.

The Jialing River is a significant tributary of the Yangtze River Basin, covering an area of almost 200,000 square kilometers. The region experiences a subtropical monsoon climate characterized by hot and rainy summers and chilly and dry winters [Fig. 1]. The average annual temperature ranges from 15 to 20 °C. Most of the precipitation occurs during the summer months, accounting for 60–80 % of the total annual precipitation. The flora of the Jialing River Basin is abundant, and this region possesses numerous rare animal and plant resources [29]. The watershed is situated in southern China, specifically at the point where the Yangtze River Basin merges, particularly near Chongqing, where it meets the Yangtze River. The Jialing River transports a significant volume of sediment, which, when combined with the water and sediment properties of the Yangtze River, creates an intricate interplay of erosion and deposition in the confluence region.

The hydrology and topography of the Wusheng, Beibei, Luoduxi, and Xiaoheba Stations in the Jialing River Basin are significant. The hydrological monitoring network of the Jialing River Basin is composed of interconnected components that offer essential data for comprehending hydrological conditions, executing water resource management, flood control, and disaster mitigation in the area.

Aside from energy/momentum losses, there is also energy dissipation in the mechanical energy loss of rivers, impacting sand transport and erosion in river water. This segment of energy is referred to as the useable power generated by rivers. The suspension power of rivers is the force required to keep silt suspended in the water. The suspension power loss in a river stretch is directly linked to the erosion and sedimentation occurring in that specific area.

Data sources

To explore the correlation between river erosion and sedimentation through energy loss and determine the connection between energy loss and river erosion and sedimentation. Data on the average flow rate, sediment concentration, Water level, sediment grading, and suspended sediment transport rate were gathered monthly and annually from the Zhutuo and Yichang Stations in the Yangtze River Basin Sediment Bulletin from 2006 to 2021. Similar data were also collected from the Xiaoheba, Beibei, Wusheng, and Luoduxi Stations in the Yangtze River Basin Hydrological Yearbook covering the period from 2007 to 2023. Recently, some hydrological stations have ceased measuring sediment during the dry season because of the low sediment concentration. At the Wusheng and Xiaoheba Stations, sediment measurements ceased from December to March of the following year, while at the Luoduxi Station, sediment measurements ceased from December to February of the following year (Table 1).

Results and analysis

Relationship between energy/momentum loss and river erosion and deposition

Based on the measured data, the energy-sediment relationship for the Zhutuo to Yichang reach of the Yangtze River main stream was calculated and fitted (Fig. 2). The results indicate a linear relationship between the general energy/momentum loss and the annual erosion and deposition volume. Specifically, the relationship between mechanical energy loss and the annual erosion and deposition volume is the most significant (R2 = 0.506), followed by momentum loss (R2 = 0.411), potential energy loss (R2 = 0.053), and kinetic energy loss (R2 = 0.171). Although the coefficient of determination for mechanical energy loss (R2 = 0.506) is the highest, it still indicates a relatively low explanatory power [33]. This suggests that energy/momentum loss is not solely attributed to changes in river erosion and deposition but also encompasses other forms of energy loss.

Relationships between general energy/momentum loss and annual erosion and deposition (a, b, c, and d represent the fitting relationships between annual erosion and deposition and kinetic energy loss, potential energy loss, mechanical energy loss, momentum loss, respectively).

In addition, the calculation results indicate that there are two situations for the loss of suspension energy in rivers: positive and negative. A positive value of suspension power loss in a river indicates that the energy required to maintain sediment suspension in the downstream section is reduced compared to that in the upstream section, leading to sedimentation of some originally suspended sediment. Therefore, sedimentation will occur in this section of the river. In contrast, a negative value of suspension power loss in a river indicates an increase in the energy required to maintain sediment suspension in the downstream section. This will result in more riverbed sediment being washed into the water for suspension, and erosion will occur within the river section.

Relationships between suspension power loss and river erosion and sedimentation

The correlation between the suspension power loss of downstream rivers and the annual erosion and deposition of river sections was determined using data from the hydrological yearbook (Fig. 3). The suspension power loss determined by various formulas shows a linear correlation with the yearly erosion and sedimentation of the river segment. The fitting accuracy of Chen Xujian's formula (R2 = 0.676) is greater than that of RUBEY's formula (R2 = 0.538), suggesting that Chen Xujian's formula provides a better explanation for the relationships between suspension power and river erosion and sedimentation. The correlation between the suspension power loss and river erosion and sedimentation under the various formulas is stronger than that between the general energy/momentum loss and river erosion and sedimentation, indicating a significant relationship between the suspension power and river erosion and sedimentation. Higher levels of suspension power loss might result in increased river erosion and sedimentation.

Relationships between the suspension power loss in river confluence areas and erosion and sedimentation in river sections

The relationships between the suspension power loss and river erosion and sedimentation are well fitted, but there is an inflow of sediment from tributaries during river movement. To further analyze the impact of tributary inflow on the relationship between suspension power loss and river erosion and sedimentation in river sections, application research has been conducted in river confluence areas. The hydrological stations located in Qujiang (Luoduxi Station) and Fujiang (Xiaoheba Station) are tributary hydrological stations, while the hydrological stations in the Jialing River (Wusheng Station and Beibei Station) are mainstream hydrological stations. Discussion

Relationships between energy loss and river erosion and sedimentation

Although runoff erosion power has been extensively utilized to describe the relationship between energy input and sediment dynamics, existing research demonstrates that the coefficient of determination (R2) between runoff erosion power and sediment transport indicators typically falls below 0.9 [[34], [35], [36]]. This suggests that while runoff erosion power effectively delineates a significant correlation between energy input and sediment transport, it may not constitute the most precise model for characterizing their relationship. To further explore the energy-sediment relationship from an energetic perspective, it is essential to first elucidate the specific energy sources required for the initiation of sediment suspension.

When water transports sand, its energy configuration will be altered [37]. Sediment particles, except for the bottom sediment, do not impact the overall energy balance during suspended movement. The upward movement of sediment is mostly driven by the turbulent kinetic energy of water flow, which is generated by random fluctuations in velocity. In contrast, the movement of bottom sediment is mainly powered by the potential energy of the water flow. Sediment suspension is primarily influenced by the vertical movement of sediment, with the energy for suspension predominantly derived from the turbulent kinetic energy of the water flow rather than the potential energy of the water flow [38,39]. Some of the turbulent kinetic energy is transformed into suspension power to keep the sediment suspended, while the remainder is converted into thermal energy loss. Thermal energy loss primarily occurs through evaporation and heat dissipation from water movement. This energy does not significantly impact river erosion and sedimentation. As the turbulent kinetic energy increases, the concentration of suspended material increases, but sedimentation decreases [39,40]. Therefore, the variation in the amount of suspension power can be used to describe the variation in the suspended sediment content of rivers. Compared with other general energy/momentum losses that establish a relationship with river erosion and sedimentation, the relationship between the loss of suspension power and river erosion and sedimentation is better, which is consistent with previous research results [41].

The suspended state of the sediment is closely related to the movement of the bed load. Even in cases of high sediment suspension, the sediment concentration at the bottom of the river is affected by bed load movement. As long as the bed load exists, there will be enough energy to maintain the suspension of sediment, and the suspended load cannot exist independently and needs to rely on the bed load [42]. Further study revealed that the suspension power is derived from the turbulent kinetic energy of water flow rather than directly from potential energy [43]. The RUBEY's formula assumes that the kinetic energy of turbid water is equal to the residual kinetic energy after a certain distance of motion under uniform water flow conditions. At this point, both the suspension power and resistance loss come from the potential energy of the water flow, which is inconsistent with existing research results.Therefore, RUBEY's formula is not comprehensive in theory, resulting in deviations in its calculation results.

Rivers, characterized as open systems with energy turbulence and viscous heat dissipation, undergo a gradual adjustment from a non-equilibrium to a relatively equilibrium state during their riverbed evolution [44]. Given the highly complex dynamics of water-sediment two-phase flows, the principle of minimum available energy consumption rate and its associated mathematical expressions offer a comprehensive framework for elucidating the energy consumption characteristics involved in riverine water and sediment transport processes. The principle not only elucidates the integrated impact of multiple variables on riverbed evolution but also fully articulates the fundamental mechanisms underlying the self-regulatory behavior of alluvial rivers. This principle has been successfully implemented in the stability analyses of the lower reaches of the Yellow River in China [44].

This study further validates that the suspension power calculation formula, derived from the minimum available energy consumption rate principle, possesses robust theoretical underpinnings and substantial practical applicability, demonstrating high accuracy in its computational outcomes. Consequently, this formula holds significant potential for establishing an energy-sediment relationship model within the confluence zones of rivers.

Application of suspension power loss in the river confluence area

The confluence area of rivers acts as a connecting point between the main and branch waterways, and the evolution of the riverbed is intricate [45]. In river confluence areas, there is a relationship between the decrease in suspension power and the level of erosion and deposition, with the precision of fitting being influenced by varying data scales. The results indicate that the fitting accuracy is initially low when analyzing the suspension power loss and erosion/deposition amount separately for each river section in the intersection area. However, the fitting accuracy significantly improves when establishing the relationship between suspension power loss and erosion/deposition amount for the entire river section. An analysis of the annual sediment transport capacities at Xiaoheba Station, Wusheng Station, and Luoduxi Station, which are tributary hydrographic stations, are equal to or greater than that of the mainstream Wusheng Station (Fig. 4). This indicates that the sediment inflow from tributaries at the river confluence is significant. Currently, if only the suspension power loss of the main stream or a single tributary is considered, the impact of the tributary's erosion and deposition on the confluence reaches will be disregarded [46]. Additionally, the correlation between the suspension power loss of a single river reach and the erosion and deposition in that reach will be weak. This demonstrates that predicting the erosion and deposition of a river reach can be more precise by including the total suspension power loss.

Sediment discharge at the upstream hydrological station of the Jialing River intersection.

In addition, using monthly data to fit the relationship between suspension power loss and erosion and sedimentation in the river section has greater accuracy. Monthly scale data provide more sample size and information than annual-scale data [47]. The monthly scale data in this study can be used to characterize seasonal changes. Seasonal changes can directly affect the energy distribution and sediment transport of surface water flow, and annual-scale data may ignore these changes. Therefore, the fitting accuracy of the monthly scale data is greater than that of the annual scale data, which also proves that the relationships between the suspension power loss and river erosion and sedimentation are more representative at the monthly scale. In summary, improving the data scale has a positive effect on the application of suspension power in river confluence areas, which can improve the accuracy of predicting and monitoring river erosion and sedimentation processes and provide more effective support for water resource management and environmental protection.

Although our study established the energy-sediment relationship in the confluence area of river channels through the analysis of suspended energy loss and the quantification of erosion and deposition within the river section, the research represents only a small-scale exploration. Further investigation is required in confluence areas characterized by high sediment concentrations, such as those found in the Yellow River Basin in China, as well as in other watersheds.

Conclusions

(1)There is a clear linear correlation between the suspension power loss and the amount of river erosion and deposition, which can be applied to the study of river erosion and deposition changes, providing new ideas for studying river erosion and deposition changes from an energy perspective.

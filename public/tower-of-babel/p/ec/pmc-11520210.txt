Priorities in Cardio-Oncology
Authors: Bonnie Ky
Published: 2024

Each day I reflect on our responsibility to grow, build, and define the field of cardio-oncology for our patients,1 and how I can best serve the people whom I deeply care for in my practice live longer, healthier lives. Each clinic session is often filled with detailed conversations about what we know and what we do not yet know in the cardiovascular care of people living with and beyond cancer and what we think is the best approach based on the current literature. As clinicians, we strive to provide empathic, evidence-based, collaborative care involving patients and the multidisciplinary care team in our decisions. As scientists, we feel a tremendous responsibility to advance knowledge so we can improve the lives of our patients. As a field, we need to continue to grow, build, and disseminate the evidence and implement this evidence to provide augmented, impactful care.

This is the motivation for this 3-part series, “Cardiovascular Considerations Before, During, and After Cancer Therapy: Gaps in Evidence and JACC: CardioOncology Expert Panel Recommendations.” The first of this series is featured in our October 2024 issue of JACC: CardioOncology. The purpose of each of these documents is to present a high-level overview of the current evidence to support clinical practice with a comprehensive focus on the gaps in evidence, with the directed goal of motivating new science to fill these gaps.2 Concise recommendations are presented by each of the expert panels in very specific areas where there is deemed to be sufficient evidence. Importantly, these statements are intended to create harmonized guidance based on each of these expert panel’s interpretations of the strongest levels of evidence.

These important documents are intended to provide a road map for our field to highlight current priority areas where we need more definitive scientific evidence to advance care. I look forward to these gaps being filled and continuing JACC: CardioOncology’s role of curating the best possible science and knowledge for our patients. I sincerely thank Dr Torbjørn Omland, who served as the Guest Editor, and our many reviewers who were wholly devoted to the peer review of all 3 statements and provided incisive, detailed critiques to enhance each piece. I am also sincerely grateful to our senior authors, Drs Manisty, Leong, and Blaes, for their extraordinary leadership and the expert coauthors for their important contributions. As always, we welcome your feedback as we look to advance, build, and define cardio-oncology for our patients.3

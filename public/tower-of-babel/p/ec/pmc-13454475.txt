Research on new ventilation technology for long tunnel construction based on air curtain dust suppression and dust diffusion law
Authors: Yan Gao, Xu Chen, Xiaolong Hao, ShiShuo Zhang, Zhengguo Zhu, Jiying Geng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ventilation during the construction of long tunnels represents a core factor impacting the safety of tunnel engineering projects. Taking Yuanbao Tunnel as the case study, this paper proposed a novel ventilation approach utilizing air curtain machines. By establishing a theoretical model of the barrier efficiency of air curtain machines installed on both sides of the tunnel, the optimal jet parameter (c) of the air curtain machine was determined. A mathematical model was then constructed to describe the diffusion of explosive dust concentration at the tunnel face, aiming to reveal the dynamic distribution law of dust within the tunnel. Additionally, a three-dimensional numerical simulation method was employed to conduct a comparative analysis of the dust removal efficiency between traditional forced ventilation and the new air curtain machine ventilation at varying tunnel depths. Research results reveal that the air curtain barrier efficiency reaches 100% and optimal energy performance is achieved under the working condition with jet thickness of 0.1 m and jet velocity of no less than 10.3 times the polluted air velocity; the ventilation system efficiency of the novel air curtain machine is significantly higher than that of the traditional forced ventilation, and its performance is less susceptible to tunnel depth. Within 15 min of activation, the system reduces dust concentration in the operation area to 2 mg/m3, which is 50% of the traditional forced ventilation time. Leveraging air curtain barrier technology, this mode offers advantages such as good cleaning effect, high ventilation efficiency, and low energy consumption, providing a novel method reference for the ventilation design in long tunnel construction.

Introduction

With the continuous acceleration of urbanization and advancement of transportation infrastructure construction, the tunnel engineering has been extensively applied in such fields as highway, railway, urban rail transit, water conservancy, and mine. Tunnel construction involves complex environments, confined spaces, and suboptimal ventilation conditions. Plenty of dust and harmful gases will be produced due to drilling and blasting operations, mechanical excavation, transportation, and loading/unloading, particularly in the tunneling construction process, which considerably influences the occupational health and safety of the workers1–4 and compromises construction efficiency and project quality. Notably, the ventilation in long tunnels becomes challenging. Therefore, how to build an efficient and reliable tunnel ventilation and dust removal system has emerged as an important part of tunnel construction safety guarantee and green construction.

At present, forced ventilation and shaft ventilation represent the primary approaches to tunnel ventilation during construction. Many scholars have conducted research on these two ventilation methods. Specifically, Jie Ren et al.5 simulated the interaction between the airflow field and the dust field under the forced ventilation condition. They revealed that the ideal ventilation velocity was 15 m/s, and the ideal distance between the air duct and the working face was 40 m. Jie Liu et al.6 explored the effect of forced ventilation pursuant to the theory of combined fans and air leakage ducts and proposed a theoretical prediction model for the spatial–temporal distribution of CO concentration in combination with the measured data finally. To delve into the unreasonable airflow distribution and severe dust pollution in the tunneling face, Zheng Haotian7 determined the spatial–temporal evolution laws of the airflow field distribution and dust pollution in the tunnel under forced ventilation conditions by performing similar experiments and numerical simulations. Xiangdong Yang8 conducted research on the ventilation and dust removal system for inclined shafts of long tunnels on the plateau and concluded that optimal dust suppression was achieved at an air duct height of 5.725 m, a working face-to-outlet distance of 21 m, and an outlet air velocity of 29.71 m/s. Heng Zhang et al.9 carried out in-depth analysis of the hoistway effect based on the numerical simulation and field measurement. The results confirmed the reasonability to install the fan 5 m away from the axis at the connection channel between the shaft and the exhaust. Farun An10 established an intelligent demand control ventilation (DCV) system to overcome the static control strategy of the traditional constant air volume ventilation system failing to fully adapt to the dynamic changes of pollutant concentration in the tunnel construction process. High energy consumption of forced ventilation was solved to a certain extent. While these two ventilation methods settle the problem of dust inside the tunnel, they still have certain limitations. Considering air leakage and friction in the duct, the dust removal efficiency of the forced ventilation gradually decreases after the progress of the tunnel construction exceeds a certain range, which greatly affects the project progress. Moreover, the shaft ventilation method is of relatively high cost, and its ventilation and dust suppression efficiency is also limited. To this end, an innovative air curtain dust blocking ventilation method was hereby proposed, with the air curtain employed to block dust diffusion and rapidly reduce dust concentration. The technology of air curtain machines has matured progressively, and the air curtain technology has been gradually utilized in the field of dust control. In the 1930s, Soviet scientist-N. A. Shepelev and B. B. Baturin11 systematically researched the theory of jet air and proposed the evaluation and theoretical calculation methods for different design schemes based on plenty of experimental data, forging a theoretical foundation for the application of the technology. Lu Xinling12 et al. delved into the feasibility of using smoke control air curtains in bifurcate tunnels, significantly enhancing the safety of such tunnel fire accidents. Liu Qiang et al.13 innovatively proposed the combination of the negative pressure suction principle of fans and the horizontal air curtain dust removal technology. The problem of coal mine dust pollution was effectively solved. The working principle of the zoned clean construction ventilation method is to divide the tunnel face into the blasting construction polluted air area and the fresh air area by setting a mobile double-sided spray air curtain machine behind the tunnel face and adopting the spray air curtain isolation technology (namely forming a tunnel air curtain partition wall through the jet air curtain). Concurrently, the air curtain machine can move forward along with the progress of the blasting at the tunnel face. Compared with the traditional forced ventilation, the mode is characterized by cleanliness, efficiency and energy conservation, with tangible benefits for improving the construction environment inside long tunnels.

Hererin, with the Yuanbao Tunnel as the engineering background, a new ventilation scheme based on air curtain machines was proposed. Specifically, the theoretical model for the barrier efficiency of the double-sided air curtain machine was established, and key parameters (such as the optimal jet angle and thickness) were determined. Furthermore, to reveal the dynamic evolution law of the dust in the tunnel, the diffusion model of the explosive dust at the tunnel face was constructed. Finally, through three-dimensional numerical simulation, a comparative analysis was conducted on the dust removal efficiency of the new ventilation and the traditional forced ventilation at different tunnel depths, confirming the significant advantages of the scheme in improving the dust removal effect of long tunnels.

Zoned clean construction ventilation method

Supporting engineering

Yuanbao Tunnel is located in Yuanbao Township, Lichuan City and an over mountain line tunnel. The tunnel area is of the medium and low mountain landform of structural erosion. It is a single-bore double-track tunnel. The designed speed is 350 km/h; the entrance mileage is DK278 + 290, the exit mileage is DK281 + 672, the total length is 3351.326 m, and the maximum burial depth is approximately 281 m. The tunnel entrance is adjacent to the abutment of Pengjiawan Bridge, and its exit is adjacent to the abutment of Gaojiawan Bridge; the entrance section is located on a left-skewed curve with a radius of 10,000 m, the exit section is on a left-skewed curve with a radius of 8000 m, and the remaining sections are on a straight line. The longitudinal slope of the tunnel is a single-sided downhill, with 25‰ downhill at the distance of 460 m, 18‰ downhill at the distance of 2369.326 m, and 4‰ downhill at the distance of 522 m from the entrance to the exit. The maximum gradient is 25‰. The entrance elevation of the tunnel is approximately 1160 m, and the exit elevation is approximately 1100 m. One drain cavern with a length of 3020.221 m is set for the auxiliary gallery of the entire tunnel.

In line with the described zoned clean ventilation mode for the blasting construction of long tunnels, an air curtain barrier device is installed approximately 100 m away from the tunnel face inside the tunnel to form a polluted air area, while the exhaust fan and ventilation duct are placed in the polluted air area to ensure ventilation inside the tunnel. The radius of the ventilation duct is 0.9 m.

System and characteristics of ventilation methods

Regarding ventilation issues during the construction period of long tunnels, the research and analysis were conducted on the existing ventilation methods for tunnel construction. The advantages, disadvantages, and application scope of the existing methods were clarified, and a zoned clean construction ventilation mode for long tunnels was innovatively proposed on this basis. As for the ventilation mode, the tunnel face was divided into the blasting construction polluted air area and the fresh air area by setting a mobile double-sided spray air curtain machine behind the tunnel face and adopting the spray air curtain isolation technology (namely forming a tunnel air curtain partition wall through the jet air curtain). The air curtain machine could move forward along with the progress of the blasting at the tunnel face at the same time. The polluted air from the tunnel face was discharged to the clean area by the exhaust fan. At the same time, the polluted air was purified and discharged by the dust control unit. Relationship between the inlet air velocity and outlet air velocity of the forced air duct

The forced ventilation usually refers to installing a main fan on the air intake side to force the fresh air from the ground into the tunnel. The mechanical pressure generated by the fan makes the air pressure in the laneway or tunnel higher than the external atmospheric pressure. Driven by the positive pressure, the fresh air flows to the operation area along the designed ventilation network. Upon passing through the operation area, the polluted air flows to the pre-set exhaust outlet along the tunnel under the continuous push of positive pressure and is ultimately discharged into the ground or the atmospheric environment naturally. At that time, the efficiency of the ventilation method is deeply affected by the outlet air velocity. To explore the relationship between the inlet air velocity and the outlet air velocity of forced air duct, the calculation model for the change of air velocity in the forced air duct with the ventilation distance was correspondingly proposed.

Upon delivery of external fresh air to the tunnel construction area by pressure fans and its flow through ventilation hoses (inner wall friction coefficient: f; unit-length air leakage rate: k), the gas flow pressure loss was bifurcated into two parts: pressure loss caused by wall friction, and pressure drop due to gas volume loss from hose air leakage. Numerical simulation

Mesh division of geometric models

With the Yuanbao Tunnel as the supporting engineering, the three-dimensional calculation model for tunnel ventilation was hereby constructed. Given the significant impact of the blasting at the tunnel on the construction area, a model was established for a 200-m-long tunnel behind the tunnel face. The longitudinal direction of the tunnel is the X direction, the transverse direction is the Y direction, and the vertical direction is the Z direction in the numerical simulation. The fluid space inside the tunnel is relatively large, and the entire tunnel is of a three-centered circular structure, which is rather complex. Thus, the entire model was structurally divided by a hexagon for the surface mesh structure of the tunnel to save computer system resources and maintain calculation accuracy, and the boundary layer on the wall surface and the wall surface of the ventilation duct were subject to encryption processing by the prismatic mesh. The transition layer between the two was connected by a tetrahedral unstructured mesh, as shown in Fig. 6.Fig. 6Model mesh division.

Model mesh division.

Boundary conditions and main parameters

The airflow pattern inside the tunnel is relatively complex and falls into turbulent state. Pursuant to the theory of gas–solid two-phase flow, a Discrete Phase Model (DPM) model was established under the Euler–Lagrange coordinate system to research the migration law of dust in the polluted air area under the operation of the exhaust fan.

The movement of dust particles in the tunnels fall into the category of gas–solid two-phase flow15. In this paper, the discrete phase model (DPM) of Euler–Lagrange method was used to simulate the migration law of dust in the airflow field16,17. The airflow was considered a continuous phase under the Euler coordinate system, and the Realizable k - \varepsilon model was chosen for the turbulence model. The dust particle was regarded as a discrete phase under the Lagrange coordinate system. Tunnel blasting results in dust particles with a volume fraction of under 10%. This allows the omission of inter-particle interactions, though the airflow-dust interaction should be taken into account.

The dust mainly migrates along the axial direction in the tunnel (a confined space). Its transportation process is dominated by two key physical mechanisms: firstly, advection and diffusion effects controlled by the airflow velocity u (m/s) generated by ventilation; secondly, gravitational settling determined by the inherent characteristics of dust (final velocity of sedimentation: vs(m/s)). To simplify the model, complex secondary factors (such as reentrainment of dust and condensation and polymerization caused by humidity) were ignored in this research.

Results and discussion

By enabling the discrete phase model (DPM) and setting the interaction between the discrete phase and the continuous phase, the coupling purpose was achieved. The transient calculation was employed for the simulation of the dust field. Given the high degree of nonlinearity in the model, this analysis encountered a heavy computational load, and the calculations took an extended period of time. To achieve better simulation results, the dust migration situation within 900 s of ventilation after tunnel blasting was simulated. The density of dust particles was greater than that of air. Thus, a large amount of dust would gradually settle in the migration process and move close to the ground. To better reflect the migration of dust particles, it was chosen to observe from the perspective of the bottom of the tunnel.

To reduce the occupational health risks of construction workers and improve the air quality of the working environment, the dust migration law at a height of 1.5 m was particularly emphasized, and the height of 1.5 m served as the main breathing area for the workers.

Relationship between different depths of tunnels and dust concentration

Forced ventilation

To research the concentration changes of dust generated by tunnel blasting under the condition of forced ventilation, the air velocity of the air curtain machine was hereby set to 12 m/s, the initial air velocity of the forced fan was set to 20 m/s, the air leakage rate of the duct was set 3%, and the initial air velocity of the exhaust fan was set to 20 m/s. A 1.5 m-high longitudinal centerline of the tunnel (on the horizontal plane) was selected to monitor dust concentration variations; average concentrations were calculated at 4 m intervals to investigate dust concentration changes in the tunnel’s operational zone at different depths, at 3, 6, 9, 12, and 15 min of ventilation following blasting (see Fig. 7).Fig. 7Diagram for the longitudinal dust concentration change at different times.

Diagram for the longitudinal dust concentration change at different times.

As illustrated in Fig. 7, when the tunnel depth is less than 2000 m, the forced ventilation effect is favorable, and the dust concentration in the tunnel decreases rapidly, with an average decrease rate of 4.86–6.78 mg/m3 per minute; after the depth exceeds 3000 m, the forced ventilation effect weakens gradually, and the average decrease rate of dust concentration decreases to 1.86–2.78 mg/m3 per minute.

Figure 8 shows the time required for the dust concentration in the operation area to drop to the construction standard, with the traditional forced ventilation used at different tunnel depths. Evidently, the dust concentration in the operation area can be reduced to the standard value allowed for construction within 30 min when the tunnel depth is 1000 m; the required time will be extended to 120 min when the depth increases to 3500 m. This indicates that as the depth of the tunnel increases, the dust removal effect of the forced ventilation decreases significantly.Fig. 8Dust concentration–time variation graph of forced ventilation.

Dust concentration–time variation graph of forced ventilation.

Zoned clean construction ventilation

To research the concentration changes of dust generated by tunnel blasting under the ventilation condition of the air curtain machine, the angle of the air curtain machine was set to 20°, the air velocity of the air curtain machine was set to 12 m/s, the initial air velocity of the forced fan was set to 20 m/s, the air leakage rate of the duct was set to 3%, and the initial air velocity of the exhaust fan was set to 20 m/s. This study examined how dust concentrations in the tunnel’s working area change at varying depths following blasting, after 15 min of mechanical ventilation. The simulation cloud diagram is shown in Fig. 9.Fig. 9Cloud diagram for longitudinal pollutant concentrations at different times.

Cloud diagram for longitudinal pollutant concentrations at different times.

As shown in Fig. 9, when the air curtain machine is turned on, most high-concentration dust generated by the explosion is effectively blocked by the uniform and stable air curtain formed, while being restricted within a range of approximately 100 m from the tunnel face. Concurrently, the polluted gas is continuously and rapidly discharged by the ventilation system set inside the tunnel. As a result, the dust concentration in the operation area continuously and rapidly decreases. Notably, with the continuous tunneling and gradual deepening of tunnel face, the zoned clean construction ventilation system can still maintain stable isolation and dust removal effects, and its efficiency does not decline significantly due to the increase in tunnel distance. This feature is substantially superior to traditional forced ventilation and particularly applies to the long tunnel construction. It ensures the health and visibility of personnel near the excavation face, effectively prevents the spread of dust in the tunnel, and improves the construction environment throughout the tunnel.

To quantitatively analyze the dust suppression effect of the four working conditions, the centerline along the longitudinal direction of the tunnel at a height of 1.5 m on the horizontal plane was hereby chosen to monitor changes in dust concentration, and the average concentration was taken every 4 m. Cloud diagram for longitudinal pollutant concentrations at different times.

As shown in Fig. 10, the dust decline rate inside the tunnel is 14.34–20.02 mg/m3 per minute when the tunnel depth falls within 3500 m. Hence, the efficiency of the zoned clean construction ventilation is basically not influenced by the tunnel depth. According to the regulations, the dust containing less than 10% free silica in 1 cubic meter of air in the tunnel shall not exceed 4 mg22. Following the explosion and ventilation for 15 min in a zoned clean construction ventilation manner, the average dust concentration in the tunnel operation area is basically dropped below 2 mg/m3, meeting the construction requirements.

Conclusion

Herein, the theoretical model for the barrier efficiency of bilateral air curtains was established, and optimal jet parameters of the air curtain machine were determined on the basis of the Yuanbao Tunnel case. The outcomes of this study reveal that the air curtain delivers complete (100%) barrier efficiency, and its energy efficiency is maximized under working conditions characterized by a jet thickness of 0.1 m and a jet velocity of 10.3 times the polluted air velocity or higher. The conclusion provides a theoretical basis for the parameter optimization of the air curtain barrier system.

The mathematical model for concentration diffusion following dust explosion in the tunnel was constructed. The model clearly reveals the dynamic evolution and spatial distribution law of dust concentration over time after the explosion at the tunnel face. By comparing and verifying the mathematical model with the numerical simulation result, it is found that the two are highly consistent with small errors. This indicates that the model can be reliably used to predict the spatial–temporal evolution process of dust concentration in the tunnel.

The dust removal efficiency of the zoned clean construction ventilation system is significantly better than that of the traditional forced ventilation. Within the same period of time, its ventilation performance remains basically not influenced by the depth of the tunnel. The dust concentration in the tunnel is reduced to 2 mg/m3 within 15 min, while the time required for forced ventilation is significantly prolonged as the depth increases. To achieve a similar dust concentration level, the time required for the latter is usually 1 to 5 times that of zoned ventilation, and the time difference further expands with increasing tunnel depth.

Based on the above research, an innovative ventilation model of “clean zoning” suitable for blasting construction in long tunnels was hereby proposed. By relying on air curtain barrier technology, this mode exhibits key merits such as superior cleaning performance, elevated ventilation efficiency and minimal energy consumption, and thus presents a novel solution for ventilation system design in the construction of long tunnels.

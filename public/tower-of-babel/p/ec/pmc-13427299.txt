External Oblique–Rectus Abdominis block for robotic adrenalectomy
Authors: Meghna Singhal, Sushil Guria, Deepa Khurrana, Bhavya Krishna
Published: 2026

Dear editor,

Robotic-assisted laparoscopic adrenalectomy, although less invasive than the open procedures, may still experience significant perioperative pain.

Regional anesthesia techniques have gained immense popularity as they reduce opioid consumption and its associated side effects. Among fascial plane blocks (FPBs), the transversus abdominis plane (TAP) block, quadratus lumborum block (QLB), and erector spinae plane (ESP) block have been investigated extensively in clinical practice. However, these techniques may not consistently provide adequate coverage, and some blocks require repositioning or multiple injections.

A relatively new addition to FPBs is the External Oblique–Rectus Abdominis Plane Block (EXORA block), in which local anesthetic is deposited between the external oblique and rectus abdominis muscles. This plane allows spread both medially and laterally, covering the anterior and lateral cutaneous branches of thoracic nerves (T6 to T11). After written informed consent for block and reporting, the linear ultrasound probe was placed sagittally in a craniocaudal direction along the parasternal line, lateral to the sternum and xiphoid process. The probe was advanced in a craniocaudal direction. At the 8th costal cartilage level, the probe was positioned transversely to visualize the rectus abdominis muscle, external oblique, and the underlying costal cartilage. A 100 mm, 22-gauge ultrasound-visible peripheral nerve block needle was directed from medial to lateral, using an in-plane technique. After confirming the position of the needle beneath the rectus abdominis muscle by hydrodissection, 25 mL of bupivacaine (0.25% concentration) was injected [Figure 1]. The spread of the local anesthetic was observed beneath the external oblique muscle, the rectus muscle, and the costal cartilage. Initial reports have described effective analgesia in laparoscopic cholecystectomy and donor nephrectomy.[123] We report our early experience with EXORA given unilaterally on the surgical site in three patients undergoing right-sided robotic adrenalectomy. General anesthesia was maintained with inhalational agents after induction, and multimodal analgesia with intravenous paracetamol was continued postoperatively.

These observations complement the existing literature. Ökmen and colleagues have reported that EXORA results in superior analgesia compared to intravenous patient-controlled analgesia in laparoscopic cholecystectomy, with dermatomal spread from T7 to T11.[3] They also demonstrated effective coverage by the block in donor nephrectomy, where patients required minimal rescue analgesia beyond paracetamol.[4]

Compared with the TAP block, EXORA provided broader dermatomal coverage without the need for additional rectus sheath block.[5] Unlike QLB or ESP block, which are deeper and require patient repositioning, EXORA is a fast, simple, superficially performed block. The technical ease and reproducibility may encourage wider adoption once more evidence is available.

EXORA has its limitations, being a new technique, the anatomical spread and optimal dosing are to be evaluated. Randomized controlled trials with larger sample sizes are essential to establish its exact role. We believe the EXORA block can be studied further for providing safe and effective perioperative analgesia in patients undergoing robotic adrenalectomy [Video 1].

Pleural fluid human epididymis secretory protein 4 in differentiating between malignant and benign pleural effusions: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Qian Yang, Ling Hai, Yan Niu, Jian-Xun Wen, Dan-Ni Yang, Su-Na Cha, Hong-Zhe Zhu, Cheng Yan, Li Yan, Ying-Jun Wang, Wen-Qi Zheng, Zhi-De Hu, Lei Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSoluble biomarkers in pleural fluid are a promising diagnostic tool for malignant pleural effusion (MPE) due to their advantages of low cost, short turnaround time, and less invasiveness. Several studies have investigated the value of pleural fluid human epididymis secretory protein 4 (HE4) in detecting MPE, but the results varied. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to assess the value of pleural fluid HE4 in differentiating between MPE and benign pleural effusion (BPE).MethodsThe PubMed and Web of Science databases were used to identify eligible studies regarding the diagnostic value of pleural fluid HE4 for MPE and published before May 1, 2025. We extracted the following information from all eligible studies: first author, country, sample sizes of MPE and BPE, publication year, study design (e.g., prospective, retrospective), HE4 assay, the definition for MPE, the area under the curve (AUC) of HE4, sensitivity, specificity, and corresponding threshold. We used the revised Quality Assessment for Diagnostic Accuracy Studies tool (QUADAS-2) to assess the risk of bias in eligible studies. The bivariate model was used to calculate the pooled sensitivity and specificity. The summary receiver operating characteristic (sROC) curve was constructed to estimate the value of HE4 for differentiating between MPE and BPE. Deeks’s test was employed to assess the risk of publication bias.ResultsSeven studies with 1,216 patients (592 MPEs and 624 BPEs) were included in this meta-analysis. The meta-analysis revealed HE4 had a pooled sensitivity of 0.59 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.43–0.74] and specificity of 0.94 (95% CI: 0.82–0.98). HE4 had an AUC of 0.83 (95% CI: 0.80–0.86). No significant publication bias across the included studies was observed. The primary risk of bias was the representativeness of the cohort, data-driven threshold selection, and particle verification bias.ConclusionsPleural fluid HE4 has a moderate value for differentiating between MPE and BPE. However, it cannot be used to confirm or rule out MPE when used alone.Keywords: Meta-analysis, malignant pleural effusion (MPE), human epididymis secretory protein 4 (HE4), sensitivity, specificity

Highlight box

Key findings

• Human epididymis protein 4 (HE4) in pleural fluid has a moderate value for differentiating between malignant (MPE) and benign pleural effusions (BPE). However, it cannot be used to confirm or rule out MPE when used alone.

What is known and what is new?

• Several studies have investigated the role of pleural fluid HE4 in differentiating between MPE and BPE, but the results varied.

• This is the first meta-analysis investigating the diagnostic accuracy of pleural fluid HE4 for MPE. The pooled sensitivity and specificity of HE4 are 0.59 and 0.94.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• HE4 in effusion can be used to estimate the risk of MPE in patients with undiagnosed pleural effusion.

Introduction

Pleural effusion is common in clinical practice and can arise from more than 50 disorders. It can be categorized into malignant and benign pleural effusions (MPE and BPE) (1,2). Pleural metastatic or primary tumors cause the former, while the latter is caused by benign diseases such as tuberculous pleurisy, pneumonia, and heart failure (HF). MPE is a consequence of late-stage cancer, with a high rate of recurrence (3) and a median survival of less than 12 months (4,5). Therefore, caution must be exercised to establish the diagnosis of MPE since misdiagnosis will cause significant psychological distress and financial burden for the individuals. By contrast, pleural fluid tumor markers are less invasive, rapid, easy to access, and objective diagnostic tools for MPE.

The diagnostic standards for MPE are effusion cytology, imaging-guided pleural biopsy, and thoracoscopy (6-8). However, these diagnostic methods have some intrinsic limitations. The specificity of effusion cytology is 1.00, but its sensitivity is approximately 0.50 (9-11). Imaging-guided pleural biopsy and thoracoscopy are invasive, and the operation-related complications are problematic. In addition, the diagnostic yields of pleural biopsy and thoracoscopy are affected by sampling errors and cytologists’ experience (12-14). By contrast, pleural fluid tumor markers are less invasive, rapid, easily accessible, and objective diagnostic tools for MPE (15,16).

Serum human epididymis secretory protein 4 (HE4) is a well-established diagnostic marker for ovarian cancer (17), and it also helps in the diagnosis of lung (18) and endometrial cancers (19). Several studies have discussed the role of pleural fluid HE4 in differentiating between MPE and BPE (20-26). However, the results of these studies varied. The heterogeneous findings may be attributed to the representativeness of participants enrolled, the HE4 assay, or the reference standard for MPE. Therefore, a meta-analysis is needed to summarize and pool the findings of available studies. Here, we conducted a meta-analysis to ascertain the value of pleural fluid HE4 in detecting MPE in patients with undiagnosed pleural effusion. We present this article in accordance with the PRISMA-DTA reporting checklist (27) (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2025-342/rc).

Methods

Databases used for literature search

Two widely used databases, Web of Science and PubMed, were used to retrieve eligible studies published before May 1, 2025. The keywords used for searching PubMed were: (“HE4” OR “HE-4” OR “human epididymis secretory protein 4” OR “human epididymis protein 4” [nm]) AND (“pleural effusion” OR “pleural fluid”). We applied a similar strategy to search the Web of Science.

Study selection

The inclusion criteria were: (I) studies investigating the diagnostic role of pleural fluid HE4 for MPE; (II) studies reporting the sensitivity, specificity, and sample sizes of BPE and MPE, allowing us to construct a two-by-two table. The exclusion criteria were (I) animal studies; (II) conference abstracts, editorials, literature reviews, and commentaries; and (III) non-English publications.

Literature screening was conducted independently by two reviewers. In the first round of literature screening, we excluded irrelevant studies by reading the titles and abstracts. In the second round, we read the full text of the remaining literature to confirm their eligibility.

Risk of bias assessment and data extraction

We extracted the following information from all eligible studies: first author, country, sample sizes of MPE and BPE, publication year, study design (e.g., prospective, retrospective), HE4 assay, the definition for MPE, the area under the curve (AUC) of HE4, sensitivity, specificity, and corresponding threshold. We constructed a two-by-two table with the sample sizes of MPE and BPE, sensitivity, and specificity. In the two-by-two table, participants were categorized into true positive (TP), false negative (FN), false positive (FP), and true negative (TN).

We assessed the quality (risk of bias) of the included studies with the revised Quality Assessment for Diagnostic Accuracy Studies tool (QUADAS-2) (28). Two reviewers independently performed the quality assessment and data extraction. Any disagreement was resolved by consensus.

Statistical analysis

We used the bivariate model to pool the sensitivity and specificity of HE4 (29) and the summary receiver operating characteristic (sROC) curve and its AUC to evaluate the diagnostic value of HE4 for MPE (30). The inconsistency index (I2) was applied to measure the potential heterogeneity across all included studies (31). We used a Fagan diagram to calculate predictive values at different prevalences of MPE (32). Funnel plots and Deeks’s test were applied to assess the probability of publication bias (33). All statistical analyses were performed in Stata 16.0 (Stata Corp LP, College Station, TX, USA) statistical software. A two-tailed P<0.05 was used to denote statistical significance.

Results

Characteristics of the eligible studies

The details of the study selection are presented in Figure 1. Finally, seven studies were included in this meta-analysis. One thousand two hundred sixteen patients were involved, with 592 MPE patients and 624 BPE patients. The essential characteristics of the included studies are shown in Table 1. Three studies were conducted in China (22,24,26), two studies were conducted in Spain (21,25), and the remaining two studies were conducted in Saudi Arabia (20) and Turkey (23). Only one study was a retrospective design (25); the others were prospective (20-24,26). Five studies determined pleural fluid HE4 with an electrochemiluminescence (ECM) assay (20-22,24,25), while one study used an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) (23), and another used a chemiluminescent immunoassay (CLIA) (26). All studies defined MPE by effusion cytology and pleural biopsy. In addition, three studies also used clinical reference standards (CRS) to diagnose MPE. The CRS defined MPE with the evidence of late-stage primary cancer, clinical features, and exclusion of other BPE (20,22,24).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first meta-analysis to investigate the diagnostic accuracy of pleural HE4 for MPE. We included seven studies with 1,216 patients. We found that the pooled sensitivity and specificity were 0.59 (95% CI: 0.43–0.74) and 0.94 (95% CI: 0.82–0.98), respectively. HE4’s sROC had an AUC of 0.83 (95% CI: 0.80–0.86). The available studies did not have significant publication bias. These results suggest HE4 is an auxiliary diagnostic marker for MPE.

Sensitivity and specificity reflect the ability of an index to exclude and confirm a given disease. Although they are two fundamental diagnostic metrics of an index test, they have a limitation of threshold dependence (34,35). By contrast, the AUC of the sROC is threshold-independent and thus represents a global measure to reflect the overall diagnostic value of an index test (36). HE4 had an AUC of 0.83 in this meta-analysis, indicating its moderate diagnostic value for MPE. PLR and NLR are widely used to confirm and exclude target diseases. PLR >10 or NLR <0.1 is solid evidence to confirm or exclude the target disease (37). Our study found HE4 had a PLR of 10 (95% CI: 4–26) and NLR of 0.43 (95% CI: 0.31–0.60), indicating that HE4 is inadequate to confirm or exclude MPE when used alone, and the clinical content of the patient should be considered when interpreting HE4. DOR is a measure of diagnostic performance that combines the information conveyed by sensitivity and specificity (38). DOR ranges from 0 to infinity; a higher DOR reflects better diagnostic value. Our study found that the DOR was 23 (95% CI: 10–54), indicating that the probability of positive HE4 in MPE is 23 times higher than in BPE, suggesting that HE4 can assist in diagnosing MPE.

Several studies have investigated the diagnostic value of pleural fluid or serum tumor biomarkers for MPE. The reported markers include carbohydrate antigen 125 (CA125), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), neuron-specific enolase (NSE), CA72-4, pro-gastrin-releasing peptide (39), CA50 (40), fragment of cytokeratin 19 (CYFRA 21-1), and microRNAs (41,42). Evidence from systematic reviews and meta-analyses revealed these markers had specificities of >0.90, while their sensitivities were approximately 0.50 (15,43-46). Our study showed that HE4 had an overall sensitivity of 0.59 and specificity of 0.94, suggesting the diagnostic value of HE4 is comparable to that of conventional tumor markers. Therefore, HE4 represents an alternative diagnostic tool for MPE. It can serve as an auxiliary diagnostic tool for MPE. This study only investigated the performance of HE4 for MPE when used alone, and it remains necessary to investigate whether the combination of HE4 and other tumor markers can enhance the diagnostic yield of MPE.

Although this is the first meta-analysis investigating pleural fluid HE4 and MPE, the study has some limitations. First, the number of included studies and the total sample size are small. Therefore, the precision of the findings needs to be validated by large sample-size studies. Second, significant heterogeneity was observed across all eligible studies. Still, the small number of included studies does not allow us to study the sources of heterogeneity with meta-regression and subgroup analysis.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our meta-analysis reveals that pleural HE4 has moderate accuracy in differentiating MPE and MPE. Since the number of included studies is small and the wide 95% confidence intervals, the precision of the study needs to be further improved. More studies are required to ascertain the diagnostic role of pleural HE4 for MPE.

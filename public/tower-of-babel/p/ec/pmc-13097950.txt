Outcomes of nipple–areola complex reconstruction in transgender chest masculinization using the free nipple graft technique: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Mohammad Jamal Salavati, Seyed Yavar Shams Hojjati, Pooyan Parhizgar, Reza Sharifi, Mahdi Savadi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe free nipple graft (FNG) technique, involving the removal of breast tissue and reattachment of the nipple-areola complex (NAC) as a full-thickness skin graft, is a widely utilized method in this surgery. This study aims to evaluate the outcomes of NAC reconstruction in transgender individuals who have undergone mastectomy using the FNG technique.MethodsThis cross-sectional study involving transgender individuals aged 18 years and older who had undergone FNG masculinizing chest surgery at least six months prior. Data were collected from medical records and assessed using a subset of the BREAST-Q questionnaire, translated and validated for this context.ResultsThe study included participants with a mean age of 21.20 years. The average follow-up duration was 11.20 months. Satisfaction with nipple appearance was high, with 52% of participants somewhat satisfied and 48% very satisfied. The average cosmetic score was 8.272. Common complications included seroma (28%) and contraction (12%). Minimal pain was reported by participants, with 60% never experiencing sharp breast pain and 68% never experiencing general breast pain. High satisfaction levels were noted for nipple and areola appearance and size, with 80% and 60% of participants finding them satisfactory, respectively. Overall breast appearance satisfaction was high, with 88% very satisfied.ConclusionThe FNG technique in masculinizing chest surgery yields high satisfaction with NAC appearance and sensation, overall breast appearance, and manageable pain levels, indicating its efficacy.

Introduction

Transgender individuals seeking gender-affirming surgeries often undergo various surgical procedures to align their physical appearance with their gender identity [1]. Among these procedures, masculinizing chest surgery is a crucial step for female-to-male transgender individuals [2], contributing significantly to their psychological well-being and quality of life [3]. The primary goal of masculinizing chest surgery is to create a more masculine chest contour, which often involves the removal of breast tissue and the reconstruction of the nipple-areola complex (NAC) [4].

The free nipple graft (FNG) technique is a widely utilized method in masculinizing chest surgery [4]. This technique involves the removal of breast tissue followed by the reattachment of the NAC as a full-thickness skin graft [5]. The FNG technique is particularly advantageous for individuals with larger breasts or significant skin excess [6], as it allows for optimal reshaping of the chest and positioning of the NAC [7]. Despite its widespread use, there is a paucity of data regarding the long-term outcomes and patient satisfaction associated with this technique.

This cross-sectional study aims to evaluate the outcomes of NAC reconstruction in transgender individuals who have undergone mastectomy using the FNG technique. By assessing both surgical and psychosocial outcomes, this study seeks to provide a comprehensive understanding of the efficacy and patient satisfaction associated with the FNG technique in masculinizing chest surgery. Specifically, this study will focus on satisfaction with the appearance and sensation of the reconstructed NAC, overall satisfaction with chest surgery, self-esteem, and quality of life.

Methods

Study design and setting

This cross-sectional study was conducted at a Plastic Surgery Center in Mashhad, Iran. The study population comprised transgender individuals who underwent masculinizing chest surgery using the free nipple graft (FNG) technique.

Participants

Eligible participants were transgender individuals aged 18 years and older who had undergone FNG masculinizing chest surgery at least six months prior to the study to ensure adequate time for healing and accurate assessment of outcomes. Participants were recruited from medical records, and those meeting the inclusion criteria were invited to participate in the study.

Data collection

Data were collected from the medical records of eligible participants. Demographic information, surgical method details, complications, and follow-up care data were extracted and recorded. Subsequently, participants were assessed using a subset of the BREAST-Q questionnaire.

The Breast-Q questionnaire was uniformly administered to all participants during their routine postoperative follow-up visits, specifically at six months after surgery. This consistent timing was chosen to ensure that surgical healing was complete and that outcomes, such as nipple-areola complex (NAC) appearance and sensation, had stabilized. The BREAST-Q is designed primarily for breast reconstruction evaluations, which differ in some respects from masculinizing chest surgery. Therefore, only relevant sections of the BREAST-Q questionnaire were used to evaluate satisfaction with the appearance and sensation of the reconstructed NAC, overall satisfaction with chest surgery, self-esteem, and quality of life.

The BREAST-Q questionnaire was translated and validated for use in our context by Eslami et al. [8], ensuring its reliability and validity. Although the BREAST-Q was originally designed for breast reconstruction, it includes pertinent criteria that are applicable to assessing outcomes in masculinizing chest surgery. The translated version was used to accurately capture participant satisfaction and quality of life.

We did not calculate overall scores using the Rasch converting scale but focused on individual items directly related to our outcomes of interest.

Surgical procedure

The masculinizing chest surgeries were performed using the free nipple graft (FNG) technique. This method involves the removal of breast tissue and the reattachment of the nipple-areola complex (NAC) as a full-thickness skin graft. The procedure begins with general anesthesia and marking of the nipple at a diameter of 28 mm. The decision to standardize the preoperative NAC marking at 28 mm was based on published anthropometric data describing typical cis-male areolar dimensions, which generally range from 25 to 30 mm in diameter. This size provides a proportionate masculine appearance while allowing sufficient surface area for graft survival and pigmentation retention [9]. The nipple is then harvested as a full-thickness graft. In our technique, the recipient bed for the NAC graft is prepared on a de-epithelialized skin flap, which provides a vascularized dermal surface that supports graft inosculation. The underlying pectoralis major fascia is preserved but not used directly as the graft bed. The breast tissue is removed through an incision made at the inframammary fold (IMF). After excision, the skin is trimmed and sutured closed [Fig. 1].

Measured variables

The primary outcome variables included satisfaction with the appearance and sensation of the reconstructed NAC, overall satisfaction with chest surgery, and self-esteem and quality of life. Satisfaction with the NAC was assessed using the relevant sections of the BREAST-Q questionnaire, while overall satisfaction with chest surgery and self-esteem and quality of life were evaluated through participant responses to the BREAST-Q questionnaire.

Secondary variables included demographic data and specific breast measurements. Demographic data such as age, height, and weight were collected from medical records. The follow-up duration, representing the time elapsed since the surgery, was also recorded. Breast measurements included the vertical axis (right and left), horizontal axis (right and left), sternal notch to nipple distance (right and left), areolar diameter (right and left), and nipple diameter (right and left). These measurements were taken to evaluate the surgical outcomes and symmetry.

Additionally, the cosmetic score was used to assess the overall aesthetic outcome of the surgery. This score is a subjective measure based on the surgeon’s evaluation of the chest contour, NAC positioning, and scar appearance, ranging from 1 to 10, with higher scores indicating better cosmetic outcomes.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize demographic and clinical characteristics. Means and standard deviations (SD) were calculated for continuous variables, while frequencies and percentages were used for categorical variables. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS V.26 software.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

The mean age of the participants was 21.20 years (SD = 2.41). The average follow-up duration was 11.20 months (SD = 4.29). Participants had an average height of 163.8 cm (SD = 6.71) and an average weight of 66.22 kg (SD = 8.72) with an average BMI of 24.66 (SD = 2.93).

Among the participants, 12% were receiving hormone therapy. Hookah use was reported by 40% of the participants, while 20% consumed alcohol, and 28% were smokers. Additionally, 4% of the participants were using psychoactive drugs [Table 1].

Surgical outcomes and complications

The average areolar diameter was 2.516 cm (SD = 0.14) on the right side and 2.528 cm (SD = 0.14) on the left side. The average nipple diameter was 1.196 cm (SD = 0.15) on the right side and 1.188 cm (SD = 0.18) on the left side. The average cosmetic score was 8.272 (SD = 0.44) [Table 2].

Participants reported several post-surgical complications. Seroma was experienced by 28% of the participants. Contraction occurred in 12% of the cases. Scar revision was necessary for 8% of the participants, while fat grafting was performed in 12% of the cases [Table 2].

Pain and discomfort

Participants reported varying degrees of pain and discomfort in different areas. For neck pain, 60% of the participants reported never experiencing it, while 40% experienced very little neck pain. Regarding arm pain, 48% never experienced it, 48% experienced very little pain, and 4% experienced it sometimes [Table 3].

Pain and discomfort reported by participants

Difficulty sleeping due to breast discomfort was another significant concern. Among the participants, 48% reported never having difficulty sleeping, 24% reported very little difficulty, 20% sometimes had difficulty, and 8% often had difficulty sleeping. In terms of breast tightness, 44% never experienced it, 44% experienced very little tightness, and 12% sometimes experienced tightness [Table 3].

The feeling of stretching in the breast area was reported as never occurring by 56% of the participants, while 36% experienced it very little, and 8% experienced it often. An unpleasant feeling in the breast was never experienced by 52% of the participants, 40% experienced it very little, and 8% experienced it sometimes. Breast sensitivity was reported as never occurring by 52% of the participants, very little by 36%, sometimes by 8%, and often by 4% [Table 3].

Sharp pain in the breast was never experienced by 60% of the participants, 32% experienced it very little, and 8% experienced it sometimes. General breast pain was never experienced by 68% of the participants, 20% experienced it very little, 8% experienced it sometimes, and 4% experienced it often [Table 3].

Satisfaction with nipple and areola

Satisfaction with the appearance of the nipple was generally high. Among the participants, 52% were somewhat satisfied with the appearance of their nipples, and 48% were very satisfied. The natural appearance of the nipple was reported as somewhat satisfactory by 80% of the participants, while 20% found it very satisfactory [Table 4].

Satisfaction with nipple and areola

The natural appearance of the areola was somewhat dissatisfactory for 12% of the participants, somewhat satisfactory for 32%, and very satisfactory for 56%. Satisfaction with nipple size was somewhat satisfactory for 40% of the participants and very satisfactory for 60% [Table 4].

Overall breast appearance

Participants’ satisfaction with their overall breast appearance was notably high. For overall breast appearance, 12% of participants were somewhat satisfied, and 88% were very satisfied. Regarding breast shape, 4% were somewhat dissatisfied, 60% were somewhat satisfied, and 36% were very satisfied [Table 4].

Breast symmetry was somewhat dissatisfactory for 8% of the participants, somewhat satisfactory for 48%, and very satisfactory for 44%. The appearance of scars was very dissatisfactory for 4% of the participants, somewhat dissatisfactory for 44%, somewhat satisfactory for 32%, and very satisfactory for 20% [Table 4].

Discussion

The present study aimed to evaluate the outcomes of nipple-areola complex (NAC) reconstruction in transgender individuals undergoing masculinizing chest surgery using the free nipple graft (FNG) method. Our findings offer significant insights into both the surgical and psychosocial outcomes associated with this technique, contributing to the existing body of literature on gender-affirming surgeries.

Our study’s results demonstrate a high level of satisfaction among participants with the appearance and sensation of the reconstructed NAC. Specifically, 52% of participants were somewhat satisfied, and 48% were very satisfied with their nipple appearance, indicating a general positive reception towards the FNG technique. Additionally, the high cosmetic score average (8.272) reflects the efficacy of the FNG method in achieving aesthetically pleasing results.

To clarify the distinction between the two nipple-related satisfaction items used in this study, “nipple appearance” reflects participants’ overall aesthetic impression of the reconstructed NAC, including size, shape, symmetry, pigmentation, and integration with the surrounding chest contour. In contrast, the “natural appearance of the nipple” specifically evaluates how realistic and anatomically natural the nipple looks after free grafting, independent of its size or symmetry. This includes parameters such as projection, texture, color match, and the degree to which the graft resembles a cis-male nipple. The inclusion of both measures allows a more nuanced assessment, capturing not only the general aesthetic satisfaction but also the perceived authenticity or naturalism of the reconstructed nipple, which is an important outcome for transgender patients seeking a masculine chest appearance.

The reported complications, including seroma (28%), contraction (12%), and the necessity for scar revision (8%). In comparing chest masculinization techniques, a study by Donato et al. (2017) reported Inframammary fold techniques generally have lower complication rates compared to periareolar techniques. The major complications occurred in 8% of patients, while minor complications, such as hematomas, occurred in 14% of patients. Revision surgeries were more frequent in patients with prior breast surgery or those undergoing periareolar incisions [10]. Also, Naides et al. (2021) evaluated the outcomes of 72 consecutive chest masculinization cases using Double-Incision with Free Nipple Grafting technique, their result indicated that major complications were noted in 8.3%, including hematomas and infections. Minor complications included seromas and delayed wound healing [11].

Pain and discomfort are critical considerations in postoperative recovery. Our findings indicate that the majority of participants experienced minimal pain, with 60% reporting never experiencing sharp breast pain and 68% reporting never experiencing general breast pain. This suggests that the FNG technique, despite being a major surgical intervention, results in a manageable pain profile for most patients. However, a notable percentage of participants reported experiencing very little to sometimes significant discomfort in areas such as the neck (40%) and arms (52%). This highlights the need for comprehensive postoperative care plans that address not only surgical site pain but also associated musculoskeletal discomforts.

Satisfaction with the nipple and areola’s appearance and size was notably high among participants. This is consistent with the primary goal of masculinizing chest surgery, which is to create a more traditionally masculine chest contour. The high levels of satisfaction with the nipple’s natural appearance (80%) and size (60% very satisfactory) underscore the importance of meticulous surgical planning and execution in achieving desired aesthetic outcomes. In literatures, a systematic review of 1,587 patients showed high satisfaction with the aesthetic results of NAC reconstruction using DIM-FNG. Techniques focused on creating an oval shape, reducing nipple size, and optimal placement of the NAC contributed to high satisfaction rates [7]. Also, another study reported that nipple split sharing technique offers more flexibility in resizing and repositioning the NAC. the study comparing the conventional method with the split sharing technique found that patient satisfaction was significantly higher in the split sharing group (90.7%) compared to the conventional group (58.1%) due to better aesthetic outcomes [12]. Lo Russo & Tanini reported modified nipple flap with free areolar graft technique, which involves a smaller graft from the native nipple, resulted in good graft healing, projection, pigmentation, and aesthetically pleasing male results. Patients reported high satisfaction with minimal complications [13].

Participants expressed high levels of satisfaction with their overall breast appearance post-surgery, with 88% being very satisfied. This level of satisfaction is crucial, as it directly impacts the psychosocial well-being of transgender individuals. The findings are in line with previous research indicating that successful chest masculinization surgery can significantly improve body image and self-esteem among transgender men [14].

The assessment of psychosocial outcomes revealed improvements in self-esteem and quality of life, corroborating findings from previous studies that underline the positive impact of gender-affirming surgeries on mental health and overall life satisfaction [3, 15].

Despite the informative findings, this study has several limitations. The cross-sectional design limits the ability to establish causality, and the relatively small sample size may affect the generalizability of the results. Additionally, the use of a translated and adapted version of the BREAST-Q questionnaire, while validated, may still have limitations in capturing the full spectrum of outcomes specific to transgender individuals undergoing chest masculinization surgery.

Conclusion

This study provides valuable insights into the outcomes of NAC reconstruction using the FNG technique in transgender individuals undergoing masculinizing chest surgery. The high levels of satisfaction with the appearance and sensation of the NAC, combined with positive overall breast appearance and manageable pain levels, indicate the efficacy of the FNG technique.

Letter regarding “contemporary use of fibula resection compared to traditional medial approach in peroneal artery bypass”
Authors: David Barillà, Noemi Baronetto, Stefano Brizzi, Arianna Pignataro, Efrem Civilini
Published: 2026

We read with great interest the article by Jenkins et al1 describing the contemporary use of fibula resection for peroneal artery bypass and congratulate the authors on their valuable contribution and well-described series.

The study provides important insights into an alternative approach to the peroneal artery, confirming comparable perioperative and 1-year outcomes between the lateral and traditional medial exposures. However, some technical considerations deserve further discussion.

In our experience, the lateral approach to the peroneal artery should be considered primarily for the selected cases, particularly when the standard medial exposure is not feasible. These situations typically include the presence of medial leg ulcers or infection, as well as significant fibrosis in the setting of redo surgery, in which conventional dissection may be hazardous or technically prohibitive.

Although the lateral approach with fibular resection offers a more direct and superficial access to the vessel, it inherently represents an extra-anatomic route for the bypass conduit. As such, the graft may be exposed to external compression, especially at the level of the thigh fascia and around the knee joint, where dynamic forces during limb movement may influence long-term graft durability. In contrast, the medial course of the conduit is often less affected by compression, because it tends to follow the natural anatomical pathway of the great saphenous vein.

In this context, in our experience, when a significant risk of extrinsic compression is anticipated, we have selectively adopted the use of external scaffolding devices for venous graft reinforcement, such as the FRAME vascular support system.2 These devices have been proposed to improve graft geometry, reduce kinking, and potentially mitigate the effects of external mechanical stress, particularly in extra-anatomical bypass configurations, in which the risk of compression is inherently higher.

We would be interested to know whether the authors have any experience with external support systems in this setting, and how they perceive their potential role in patients undergoing lateral peroneal bypass, especially in cases at higher risk of graft compression.

Further studies focusing on conduit configuration and biomechanical factors may help clarify the potential impact of these differences on long-term outcomes.

Association Between Peripheral Nerve Blocks and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Older Adults with Hip Fractures
Authors: Arissa M. Torrie, Gerard P. Slobogean, Flavia K. Borges, John Jun, Chih Chun Tung, Robert V. O’Toole, Anthony D. Harris, Nathan N. O’Hara
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Hip fractures in older adults carry substantial perioperative risk, with cardiovascular complications being a major concern. The dual physiologic stress from fracture and surgical repair activates neuroendocrine pathways and creates sympathovagal imbalance. While peripheral nerve blocks are established for analgesia in hip fracture management, their potential to attenuate the surgical stress response and reduce cardiovascular complications remains unclear. This study evaluated the association between peripheral nerve blocks and cardiac events in older adults undergoing hip fracture surgery.Methods:This cohort study used the Premier Healthcare Database, including patients aged 65 years or older with hip fractures surgically treated in 2022 or 2023. Peripheral nerve block administration was identified through procedure codes and pharmacy records. Propensity score matching (1:1) balanced baseline characteristics between groups. The primary outcome was myocardial infarction during index hospitalization. Secondary outcomes included other cardiac events and noncardiac complications. All-cause 90-day readmission was an exploratory outcome.Results:Among 23,812 eligible patients, 3,299 (14%) received peripheral nerve blocks. After propensity matching, each group contained 3,299 patients (median age 81 years). Myocardial infarction showed a protective point estimate but did not reach statistical significance (risk ratio [RR] 0.64; 95% confidence interval [CI]:0.37-1.12; p = 0.12). Nerve blocks were associated with reduced acute congestive heart failure (RR 0.47; 95% CI: 0.31-0.72; p < 0.001) and cardiac arrest (RR 0.41; 95% CI: 0.19-0.90; p = 0.03). Nerve blocks were also associated with a reduced risk of 90-day hospital readmission (RR 0.82; 95% CI: 0.72-0.93; p = 0.003).Conclusions:Peripheral nerve blocks were associated with reduced cardiovascular complications in older adults undergoing hip fracture surgery. While myocardial infarction rates were similar between groups, nerve blocks were associated with reduced in-hospital acute congestive heart failure and cardiac arrest. These findings are hypothesis-generating for cardioprotective effects from peripheral nerve blocks.Level of Evidence:Level III, Therapeutic. Introduction

Hip fractures in older adults affect over 300,000 annually in the United States, with the trauma causing perioperative complications1,2. Cardiovascular events are a commonly reported cause of short-term morbidity and mortality2,3. The poor prognosis is thought to in part result from the cumulative physiologic stresses from both the fracture and subsequent surgical repair2,4,5. The dual insult activates neuroendocrine pathways, creates sympathovagal imbalance, and causes hemodynamic perturbations. These stress responses can increase myocardial oxygen demand and compromise cardiac function6. This cardiovascular dysfunction can lead to myocardial injury after noncardiac surgery, or even cardiac arrest, or acute congestive heart failure in some cases7,8. Attenuating the sympathetic stress response has therefore become an important focus for improving the all too often poor outcomes in hip fracture care9.

Peripheral nerve blocks have become an important component of multimodal analgesia in hip fracture management10. Current evidence demonstrates that peripheral nerve blocks interrupt nociceptor transmission at the fracture site, blocking afferent signaling to the central nervous system11,12. While established as an analgesic intervention, emerging evidence suggests that peripheral nerve blocks also could modulate the surgical stress response12. By blocking afferent nociceptive pathways, nerve blocks may attenuate sympathetic activation and the neuroendocrine response, potentially reducing cardiovascular complications in this population.

The aim of this study was to evaluate the association between peripheral nerve blocks and cardiac events in older adults undergoing hip fracture surgery. The central hypothesis was that peripheral nerve blocks would reduce cardiovascular complications in older adults undergoing surgical fixation for hip fractures. The primary outcome was myocardial infarction (MI) during index hospitalization.

Methods

Study Design and Data Source

An observational cohort study was conducted using the Premier Healthcare Database, a comprehensive all-payer database comprising clinical, billing, and administrative data from over 1,000 hospitals across the United States. The database captures approximately 25% of all hospitalizations in the United States and includes patient demographics; International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10) diagnosis codes; Current Procedural Terminology (CPT) codes; medication administration records; laboratory results; and hospital characteristics. Data quality is maintained through standardized collection protocols and regular auditing procedures. The Institutional Review Board approved the study (HP-00081539) with waiver of informed consent because of the deidentified nature of the data.

Study Population

The study included patients aged 65 years or older with a primary diagnosis of proximal femur fracture who underwent surgical fracture fixation between March 1, 2022, and April 30, 2023. Fractures were identified using ICD-10 diagnosis codes: femoral neck fractures (S72.0), pertrochanteric fractures (S72.1), and subtrochanteric fractures (S72.2). Inclusion required these codes as primary admission diagnoses. Surgical procedures were captured using both ICD-10 procedure codes for internal fixation (0QS6, 0QR6, 0QS5, 0QS9, 0QRB, 0QS7) and hip replacement (0QH6, 0QH9) or CPT codes for internal fixation (27235, 27244, 27245), hemiarthroplasty (27236), and arthroplasty (27125, 27130). Both coding systems (ICD-10 codes and CPT codes) were used to maximize case identification of having a surgical procedure. Patients were excluded if records contained codes for fractures related to tumors or metabolic bone disease. Demographic and baseline comorbidities were identified through validated ICD-10 codes and hospital characteristics (Appendix 1).

Study Intervention—Peripheral Nerve Blocks

Patients were categorized as receiving peripheral nerve blocks if the medical records contained CPT codes for femoral nerve block (64447 which captures any block targeting the femoral nerve including the fascia iliaca blocks during the study time frame), sciatic nerve block (64445), continuous femoral nerve block (64448), or pharmacy charges for local anesthetics (bupivacaine, ropivacaine, or mepivacaine) with accompanying descriptors containing “block” or “regional.” Neuraxial anesthesia was identified separately using CPT codes for spinal (62311-62319) and epidural (62321-62327) anesthesia. This multisource approach accounted for variation in coding practices across hospitals. The control group had neither procedure codes nor regional anesthesia medications during the index hospitalization. Patients with uncertain or conflicting documentation were excluded.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was MI during the index hospitalization. This was defined as new onset MI (ICD codes: I21, I22) not present on admission. Secondary cardiovascular outcomes included cardiac arrest (ICD-10: I46), acute congestive heart failure (ICD-10: I50), cardiogenic shock (ICD-10: R57.0), and new-onset arrhythmias (ICD-10 codes: I47 through I49). Secondary noncardiac outcomes included pneumonia (ICD-10: J13 through J15, J18), respiratory failure (ICD-10: J96), pulmonary embolism (ICD-10: I26), ischemic stroke (ICD-10: I63), hemorrhagic stroke (ICD-10: I61, I62), delirium (ICD-10 codes: F05, R41.0), sepsis (ICD-10: A40, A41), and surgical site infection (ICD-10: T81.4) during the index hospitalization. All-cause 90-day hospital readmission was identified through subsequent inpatient admissions to the same facility within the Premier Healthcare Database network.

Statistical Analysis

To account for baseline differences between treatment groups, propensity scores were calculated using logistic regression. The model included variables that could influence treatment assignment including patient demographics, comorbidity profiles, fracture characteristics, and hospital variables. Patients in the peripheral nerve block group and control groups were matched in a 1:1 ratio using the nearest neighbor method without replacement. Covariate balance was assessed using standardized mean differences with a threshold <0.1 confirming adequate balance. After successful matching, average treatment effects on the treated were calculated using g-computation. A generalized linear model with log links and cluster-robust standard errors were used. Competing risks from in-hospital mortality were not formally modeled in the primary analysis, as mortality rates were similar between groups. Statistical analyses were performed using R Version 4.2.2 (R Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), with statistical significance assessed using two-sided tests at the p < 0.05 level.

Results

Study Population

An initial cohort of 23,812 patients who were aged 65 years or older with a proximal femur fracture treated with surgical fixation were identified. Of these patients, 3,299 (14%) received a peripheral nerve block, 13,893 (58%) did not receive a peripheral nerve block, and 6,620 (28%) were excluded due to uncertain peripheral nerve block documentation. After propensity score matching, each group contained 3,299 patients with a median age of 81 years (interquartile range 75-88) (Table I). Fracture types were similarly distributed. Pertrochanteric fractures accounted for 64% in both groups, femoral neck fractures accounted for 32% in both groups, and subtrochanteric fractures accounted for 5% in both groups. All measured covariates achieved standardized mean differences below 0.1 after matching (Table I; Appendix 1, which reports demographics, comorbidities, fracture location, and hospital characteristics).

Outcomes

The association between peripheral nerve blocks and MI during the index hospitalization had a protective point estimate but did not reach statistical significance (risk ratio [RR] 0.64; 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.37-1.12; p = 0.12). Peripheral nerve blocks were associated with reduced acute congestive heart failure (RR 0.47; 95% CI: 0.31-0.72; p < 0.001) and cardiac arrest (RR 0.41; 95% CI: 0.19-0.90; p = 0.03). No significant associations were observed for new onset arrhythmias (RR 0.93; 95% CI: 0.68-1.26; p = 0.62) or in-hospital mortality (RR 1.22; 95% CI: 0.81-1.83; p = 0.34). Similarly, no noncardiac complications showed significant associations. Peripheral nerve block use was associated with a reduced risk of 90-day hospital readmission (RR, 0.82; 95% CI: 0.72-0.93; p = 0.003) (Table II).

Discussion

Peripheral nerve block use for hip fracture surgery was associated with cardioprotective benefits. Peripheral nerve blocks were associated with a lower incidence of acute congestive heart failure and cardiac arrest and a similar protective point estimate for MI that did not reach statistical significance. No associations were observed with noncardiac complications. The pattern of cardiovascular protection associated with peripheral nerve blocks is consistent with hypothesized benefits beyond established analgesic properties. Similarly, the reduced 90-day all-cause readmission rate suggests that these benefits extend beyond the initial hospitalization.

The cardiovascular benefits observed with the use of peripheral nerve blocks extend current understanding of their potential effect on clinical outcomes in hip fracture surgery. A previous NSQIP analysis reported associations between regional anesthesia and reduced mortality and myocardial infarction13. However, that study was limited by the inability to confirm whether all patients in the regional anesthesia cohort actually received peripheral nerve blocks. The present analysis builds on this evidence by providing a more precise exposure classification, confirming the use of peripheral nerve blocks through procedure and pharmacy coding. The consistent associations between peripheral nerve blocks and reduced cardiovascular outcomes in a separate cohort strengthens the evidence for benefits beyond analgesia.

The observed associations begin to provide evidence for the cardiovascular outcomes that earlier systematic reviews have identified as understudied. The Cochrane review by Guay et al.10 established the analgesic benefits of peripheral nerve blocks and concluded that additional randomized controlled trials for pain outcomes were unnecessary. However, the authors noted that existing studies lacked sufficient statistical power and adequate follow-up time to detect differences in outcomes beyond immediate pain control10. The current analysis, with a large sample size and an extended follow-up period, begins to address this knowledge gap. These findings suggest that peripheral nerve blocks may provide cardiovascular protection, supporting expanded use in clinical practice.

The observed reductions in cardiovascular complications are clinically meaningful. Guidelines from major orthopaedic organizations recommend routine use of peripheral nerve blocks for pain management in hip fracture surgery14,15. Despite these guidelines, administrative and insurance databases indicate that less than half of eligible patients receive peripheral nerve blocks13. Qualitative research has identified operational barriers, including concerns about surgical delays and resource limitations, as primary factors influencing clinical decision making16. The clinically important reduction in acute congestive heart failure and cardiac arrest provides additional rationale for addressing implementation barriers.

The association between peripheral nerve blocks and reduced 90-day readmission may reflect sustained cardiovascular benefits beyond the index hospitalization. Torrie et al.13 found that peripheral nerve blocks were associated with reduced 30-day mortality and myocardial infarction, though this study had limitations with how peripheral nerve block administration was confirmed. This analysis used more precise exposure classification through procedure and pharmacy coding, strengthening the hypothesis that peripheral nerve blocks have longer term benefits beyond acute pain management.

Several study strengths support the credibility of the observed associations between peripheral nerve blocks and cardiovascular outcomes. To reduce confounding, propensity score matching achieved standardized differences below 0.1 for all measured covariates. Identification of peripheral nerve blocks through both procedure codes and pharmacy records minimized misclassification that could obscure true associations. The large sample size provided statistical power to detect meaningful differences in cardiac events with incidences below 5%. Point estimates suggesting potential protective cardiovascular effects were observed across all 3 cardiac outcomes, although benefits for MI did not reach statistical significance. Given the low in-hospital MI rate of 0.6%, a trial with 80% power to confirm the observed relative risk reduction would require thousands of patients. That sample size is likely unfeasible, and therefore, this analysis may represent the best available evidence for this research question. The extended follow-up captured outcomes beyond the immediate perioperative period, providing insight into potential sustained associations. Together, these findings strengthen the confidence that the observed cardiovascular associations reflect real clinical benefits.

While the observed cardiovascular associations and decreased readmission are promising, several limitations must also be considered. Inherent to observational data, this study cannot definitively establish causal relationships between peripheral nerve blocks and cardiac outcomes, despite propensity score adjustment for measured confounders. Even with rigorous propensity-matching, residual confounding may persist. Unmeasured factors such as anesthesiologist expertise, patient frailty, and surgical urgency could influence both block placement and outcomes. Additional nerve block characteristics, including timing relative to surgical incision, anatomical approach, and local anesthetic type, remain uncharacterized. All outcomes except 90-day readmission were assessed during the index hospitalization, limiting capture of delayed complications. Furthermore, the vast majority of perioperative MIs are asymptomatic, and MI incidence is likely underestimated in retrospective databases without active troponin screening8. In addition, pharmacy-based identification of nerve blocks cannot distinguish between successful and failed block attempts or confirm the timing of the block relative to injury or surgical incision. Accurate exposure classification required excluding patients with uncertain peripheral nerve block documentation (28%), a limitation inherent to administrative billing data. Finally, the 90-day follow-up included only same-hospital readmissions, which may have underestimated the full scope of outcomes. Despite these constraints, the observed associations provide incremental improvement in the rigor to determine the potential benefits of peripheral nerve blocks for hip fracture care.

Conclusion

In this large observational database analysis of older adults undergoing hip fracture surgery, peripheral nerve blocks were associated with reduced acute congestive heart failure and cardiac arrest. The associations suggest that peripheral nerve blocks may influence cardiovascular complications through pathways distinct from their established analgesic effects. While the observational design limits definitive causal inference, the specificity and magnitude of the cardiovascular associations observed are hypothesis-generating. Given the dual physiological stress of fracture and surgical fixation in this vulnerable population, determining if peripheral nerve blocks provide cardiovascular protection beyond pain control could significantly impact perioperative management strategies for the growing population of older adults with hip fractures.

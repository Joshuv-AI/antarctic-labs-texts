IFPHOR0047 The Impact of Social Deprivation on the Incidence of Atopic Dermatitis in England
Authors: Anna Shabunina
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAtopic dermatitis (AD) is an inflammatory skin condition. AD could be characterised by the release of proinflammatory cytokines that have been shown to be associated with social deprivation. Social deprivation can be measured through areal Index of Multiple Deprivation that comprises following domains - Income, employment, education, health, crime, living environment, barriers to housing and services, income deprivation affecting children index (IDACI) and index deprivation affecting older people index.The incidence of AD and individual indices of deprivation in England were analysed to identify associations, if any. The findings on Incidence Rate Ration (IRR) of AD from Lusignan et. al. (2021) study were used as a reference figure. It was hypothesised that low socioeconomic status is associated with the high incident of AD. The 2019 English deciles of deprivation was used to categorise each area to calculate a mean score for each domain of deprivation. These results were then statistically analysed to identify correlation coefficient between type of deprivation and IRR of AD in England. The findings concluded that there is no association between these variables. It was also established that barriers to housing parameters should not be used to define social deprivation since it is characterised by financial affordability of the area. Living environment was thought to be related to AD because it is highly influenced by environmental triggers that are linked to the condition. Education and IDACI may be used as protective factors for AD in England because of duty of care policies in the educational system.

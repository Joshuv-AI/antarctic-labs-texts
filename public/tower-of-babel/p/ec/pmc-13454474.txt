Whole-genome resequencing data of 200 domestic yaks from China
Authors: Bao Cai, Lin Xiong, Jie Pei, Qianyun Ge, Xian Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Domestic yaks are a crucial livestock species on the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau and its surrounding regions, providing indispensable resources for local residents’ livelihoods and production. China harbors the world’s largest yak population and possesses exceptionally rich yak genetic resources. In this study, we performed whole-genome resequencing on 200 individuals representing 19 indigenous yak breeds. Sequencing of DNA samples generated approximately 4.3 terabytes (TB) of raw data, with an average sequencing depth of 8.36×. High-quality sequencing reads were successfully mapped to the reference yak genome, achieving an average alignment rate of 98.4%. Following stringent quality control and filtering, we identified a total of 13.41 million high-confidence single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). These genome-wide SNP markers provide a valuable resource for in-depth investigation into the demographic history and adaptive evolutionary mechanisms of Chinese yak populations, offering critical genomic support for the conservation and innovative utilization of yak genetic resources.

Background & Summary

The yak (Bos grunniens) is a unique domesticated livestock species endemic to the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau and its surrounding regions. It plays an irreplaceable ecological and economic role in these extreme high-altitude environments1. Known as the “boat of the plateau,” yaks provide local herders with essential resources, including meat, milk, hides, fiber, and draft power. They are also a vital component of the alpine ecosystem2. Through prolonged natural selection and human-driven domestication, domestic yaks have diversified into numerous indigenous breeds. These breeds exhibit remarkable adaptations to harsh conditions, such as cold temperatures, hypoxia, and intense ultraviolet radiation3. These adaptive traits make the yak an ideal model for studying high-altitude evolutionary adaptation in mammals. Furthermore, they represent a precious genetic reservoir for identifying genes underlying desirable traits and advancing genetic improvement programs4.

The Qinghai-Tibet Plateau has an average elevation exceeding 4,000 meters. Its extreme environmental conditions—including hypoxia, low temperatures, and intense ultraviolet radiation—pose severe challenges to the survival and reproduction of living organisms5. As the most representative domesticated animal in this region, the yak has evolved a suite of unique adaptive traits over a long history6. Studies show that yaks exhibit significant advantages in oxygen transport and utilization efficiency. For instance, their hemoglobin has a higher oxygen affinity than that of lowland cattle breeds. Additionally, their cardiovascular and respiratory systems have undergone specialized adaptations7. Yaks have also developed important metabolic adjustments to utilize limited nutritional resources more efficiently8. These characteristics reflect not only physiological acclimatization but also profound genomic-level mechanisms underlying evolutionary adaptation.

High-throughput sequencing technologies have advanced rapidly. Consequently, leveraging whole-genome data to dissect genetic variation, demographic history, and adaptive mechanisms has become a central focus in evolutionary research9. The emergence of second- and third-generation sequencing technologies now allows researchers to explore genomic features with unprecedented resolution. In yak research, preliminary studies have investigated genetic diversity and evolutionary history. However, these studies are often limited by narrow sampling scopes, insufficient genetic markers, or incomplete population representation10.

In this study, we performed whole-genome sequencing on 200 individuals representing 19 yak breeds. We achieved an average sequencing depth of 8.36× and generated a total of 4.3 TB of high-quality data. Through quality control, read alignment, and variant calling (Fig. 1), we successfully identified a high-confidence set of single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) sites. This dataset will facilitate a systematic understanding of genetic variation in yaks. Moreover, it provides a robust genomic foundation for genetic resource conservation, molecular breeding innovation, and the sustainable utilization of germplasm resources.Fig. 1Overview of the bioinformatic analysis workflow for yak whole-genome sequencing (WGS) data.

Methods

Sample collection

A total of 200 blood samples were collected from healthy adult individuals representing 19 indigenous yak breeds across six major yak-producing regions in China: Sichuan, Yunnan, Gansu, Xinjiang, Tibet, and Qinghai (Fig. 2). Prior to sampling, consultations were conducted with livestock experts and herd owners, and informed consent was obtained. The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of the Lanzhou Institute of Husbandry and Pharmaceutical Sciences, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (Approval No.: SYXK-2024-0023). All procedures strictly adhered to the Regulations for the Administration of Laboratory Animals issued by the State Council of the People’s Republic of China and relevant international guidelines. Blood was drawn via jugular venipuncture, and the puncture site was immediately disinfected with povidone-iodine after sampling. Collected blood samples were stored at –80 °C until DNA extraction.Fig. 2Distribution map of the 19 yak breeds.

Distribution map of the 19 yak breeds.

Library construction and whole-genome resequencing

Genomic DNA was extracted using the standard phenol-chloroform method, and its integrity and purity were assessed through agarose gel electrophoresis. The DNA concentration was quantified using Qubit/Nanodrop (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). Qualified DNA samples were randomly fragmented to a length of 350 bp using a Covaris ultrasonicator (Covaris, Inc., Woburn, MA, USA). The TruSeq Library Prep Kit (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) was utilized for library preparation, strictly following the recommended reagents and materials outlined in the protocol. Upon completion of library construction, initial quantification was performed using Qubit 3.0, and libraries were diluted to 1 ng/μL. The insert size of the libraries was then evaluated using an Agilent 2100 (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Once the expected insert size was achieved, q-PCR (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) was used to accurately quantify the effective concentration of the libraries (effective concentration > 2 nM), ensuring library quality. Each individual’s genomic DNA was sequenced on the DNBSEQ-T7 platform with 150 bp paired-end reads.

Data quality control and alignment to the reference genome

To ensure high-quality sequencing results, raw reads were subjected to quality control using the fastp software (v0.24.0), with low-quality sequences filtered out11. The cleaned reads were then aligned to the yak reference genome (Lanzhou University. Genbank. https://identifiers.org/ncbi/insdc.gca:GCA_005887515.1 (2019)) using BWA-MEME (v0.7.18)12. After alignment, SAMtools (v1.10) was used to sort the resulting BAM files. Subsequently, PCR duplicates in the sorted BAM files were identified and removed using Sambamba (v1.0.1)13,14.

SNP calling and annotation

SNP detection was performed using the GATK pipeline (v4.3.0.0). First, the “HaplotypeCaller” module was used to generate a gVCF file for each individual, followed by joint genotyping across all samples using “GenotypeGVCFs.” SNP sites were then extracted with the “SelectVariants” tool15. To obtain a high-quality SNP set, hard filtering was applied using GATK’s “VariantFiltration” module with the following thresholds: QualByDepth (QD) < 2.0, variant quality (QUAL) < 30.0, StrandOddsRatio (SOR) > 3.0, FisherStrand (FS) > 60.0, MappingQuality (MQ) < 40.0, MQRankSum < –12.5, and ReadPosRankSum < –8.0. Subsequently, BCFtools (v1.10.2) was employed for additional filtering, retaining only variants with a genotype missing rate below 10% (F_MISSING < 0.1) and a minor allele count greater than 2 (MAC > 2) to ensure adequate site coverage and minimum allele frequency16. Finally, functional annotation of the high-quality SNPs was carried out using ANNOVAR based on the annotation files of the yak reference genome (GCA_005887515.1)17.

Population structure

After initial filtering of the SNP dataset (using --maf 0.05 and --geno 0.2), linkage disequilibrium (LD) pruning was performed using PLINK (v1.90) with the parameters --indep-pairwise 50 10 0.1 to obtain a set of independent SNPs18. Population genetic structure was analyzed using ADMIXTURE (v1.3.0), with the number of ancestral clusters (K) ranging from 2 to 8; the optimal K value was determined based on cross-validation error19. A neighbor-joining (NJ) phylogenetic tree was constructed and visualized using MEGA11 and iTOL (https://itol.embl.de/) based on a pairwise genetic distance matrix20. Principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted using PLINK, and PCA plots were generated in R. Additionally, pairwise FST values among populations were calculated using the R package hierfstat, and population genetic relationships were illustrated using the UPGMA method21.

Ethics approval

All experimental procedures involving yak were conducted in strict compliance with the Regulations for the Administration of Experimental Animals (approved by the State Council of the People’s Republic of China). Written informed consent was obtained from all yak owners prior to sample collection. This study was formally approved by the Animal Administration and Ethics Committee of Lanzhou Institute of Husbandry and Pharmaceutical Sciences, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (CAAS) (Permit No.: SYXK-2024-0023). Additionally, the study adheres to the guidelines outlined in the ARRIVE Checklist to ensure transparency and rigor in reporting. All protocols, including sample collection procedures, were executed with explicit authorization from the animal owners and under the supervision of institutional ethics standards.

Data Overview

Whole-genome resequencing of 200 yaks yielded approximately 4.3 TB of raw sequencing data. After quality control, alignment, and compression, this was converted into about 1.73 TB of high-quality BAM files. Key quality metrics across all samples were as follows: an average sequencing depth of 8.46×, an average alignment rate of 98.44%, and an average properly paired read rate of 92.87%. In terms of data output, each sample yielded on average approximately 158 million clean reads, corresponding to roughly 23.7 billion high-quality bases (Supplementary Table 1, Fig. 3). These metrics collectively demonstrate that the sequencing data are of consistently high quality and reliability, providing a robust foundation for downstream variant calling and genomic analyses.Fig. 3Summary statistics of sequencing data.

Technical Validation

Data quality statistics

Taking a single individual as the sample, FASTAQC software was used to statistically analyze the distribution of average read quality values (Phred Scores) in the high-throughput sequencing data, with the x-axis representing quality scores (Q26–Q37) and the y-axis indicating the number of reads corresponding to each score. Results showed that the vast majority of reads were concentrated in the high-quality range of Q30–Q37, with a peak near Q36, indicating excellent overall data quality, base calling accuracy generally exceeding 99.9%, and classification as high-quality sequencing data suitable for downstream analysis without excessive trimming. Sequence duplication level analysis revealed a sequence retention rate of 93.19% after deduplication, reflecting an extremely low duplication rate and good data diversity. The GC content distribution closely matched the theoretical expectation for the actual sample, suggesting no exogenous contamination in the library and no significant bias during library preparation. After quality filtering, most low-quality bases in the raw data were successfully removed, with the remaining bases concentrated at quality scores above Q30. The quality distributions of Read1 and Read2 were highly consistent, both forming peaks in the high-quality range, demonstrating good quality uniformity across both ends of the paired-end sequencing (Fig. 4).Fig. 4Quality status of sequencing (NGS) data before and after quality control (QC) and filtering.

SNP Identification and annotation

After quality control and filtering, a total of 13,419,987 SNPs were identified. Chromosome 1 harbored the highest number of SNPs, while chromosome 26 contained the fewest. To visualize the distribution of SNPs across chromosomes more clearly, a heatmap illustrating SNP density along each chromosome was generated (Supplementary Table 2, Fig. 5).Fig. 5Number of SNPs within a 1MB window size.

Number of SNPs within a 1MB window size.

Functional annotation of the SNPs revealed that the majority are located in intergenic and intronic regions. In exonic regions, there are 67,852 nonsynonymous variants, accounting for 53.9% of the total variants, and 54,836 synonymous variants, making up 43.6% of the total variants (Supplementary Table 3).

Genetic relationships among breeds

Phylogenetic analysis of all individuals revealed a clear genetic clustering trend within yak breeds, where most individuals within the same breed tend to cluster closely together. However, some individuals exhibited relatively greater genetic distances from other members of the same breed (Fig. 6A). The genetic distance matrix based on the UPGMA method further elucidated the degree of differentiation among breeds (Fig. 6B). Population structure analysis provided a more detailed resolution of ancestral components. As the assumed number of ancestral components (K value) increased, the model was able to identify finer-scale population genetic structures, thereby distinguishing more local breeds (Fig. 6C). Usage Notes

This study provides both raw sequencing reads and processed BAM files containing aligned variants. Notably, all variant calls were generated using the most commonly used reference genome for domestic yaks, LU_Bosgru_v3.0 (GenBank assembly accession: GCA_005887515.1)22.

Data Records

The aligned genomic sequencing data have been deposited in the NCBI database under BioProject accession number PRJNA132499023. The identified variant data are available in the European Nucleotide Archive (ENA) under study accession PRJEB10206024,25.

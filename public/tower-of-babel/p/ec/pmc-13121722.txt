Residue dissipation and dietary risk assessment of premix formulation of fluopyram, trifloxystrobin, and their metabolite in cucumber (Cucumis sativus L.) under field conditions
Authors: Sushil Ahlawat, Reena Chauhan, Surender Singh Yadav, Nisha Kumari, Sindhu Sheoran, Seid Hussen Muhie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe premix formulation of fluopyram and trifloxystrobin is a broad-spectrum fungicidal combination that is widely used to manage foliar diseases in vegetable crops. However, concerns regarding its persistence and dietary exposure necessitate monitoring of residues under field conditions. The present study investigated the dissipation dynamics and dietary risk associated with fluopyram, trifloxystrobin, and its metabolite in cucumber (Cucumis sativus L.) following three foliar applications of fluopyram 250 + trifloxystrobin 250 g/L SC at the recommended (X, 600 mL ha⁻¹) and 1.25 × (750 mL ha⁻¹) doses. Residues were quantified using validated QuEChERS method with GC (trifloxystrobin and its metabolite) and GC–MS/MS (fluopyram), exhibiting excellent linearity (R² > 0.998), recoveries between 80 and 103%, and RSD < 10%, thereby confirming method reliability. Initial residues ranged from 0.412 to 0.555 mg kg⁻¹ for fluopyram, 0.348–0.561 mg kg⁻¹ for trifloxystrobin, and 0.158–0.218 mg kg⁻¹ for CGA321113. Dissipation followed first-order kinetics with half-lives (DT₅₀) of 2.25–2.69 days for fluopyram, 1.19–2.01 days for trifloxystrobin, and 1.14–2.11 days for CGA321113. The residues declined below the quantification limit (0.01 mg kg⁻¹) within 10–15 days after the final spray. Dietary exposure assessment, based on maximum residue levels, national consumption data and hazard quotient (HQ) values were below one for both the rural and urban people, indicating negligible chronic and acute risks. Overall, the fluopyram + trifloxystrobin premix demonstrates rapid dissipation and compliance with international food safety standards when applied according to good agricultural practices.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-39095-1.Keywords: Dissipation kinetics, Dietary exposure, Metabolite, QuEChERS method, Residue analysis, Food safetySubject terms: Biochemistry, Chemistry, Environmental sciences, Plant sciences

Introduction

Cucumber (Cucumis sativus L.) is one of the most widely grown and consumed vegetable globally. It is valued for its high-water content, for its refreshing taste, nutritional value, and economic importance in both domestic and export markets.

As a member of Cucurbitaceae family, cucumber production has expanded steadily, driven by rising consumer demand and suitability for cultivation across diverse agro-climatic zones. According to the FAO1, globally cucumber and gherkin production exceeded 92 million tonnes in 2022, nearly 10% contribution by India to the total output. Despite this growth, large-scale cultivation under intensive management practices exposes the crop to numerous phytopathogenic threats, particularly fungal diseases such as Fusarium oxysporum, Pythium spp., and Alternaria cucumerina, which can cause severe yield and quality losses2,3. Under favorable conditions, soil-borne fungal pathogens alone can account for up to 70% yield loss4. Consequently, growers rely heavily on chemical fungicides to safeguard productivity and meet quality standards in fresh-market supply chains.

Among modern fungicides, fluopyram and trifloxystrobin have gained prominence due to their complementary modes of action and broad-spectrum efficacy. Fluopyram, a succinate dehydrogenase inhibitor (SDHI), disrupts mitochondrial respiration by blocking the ubiquinone-binding site in complex II of the fungal electron transport chain, leading to inhibition of spore germination and mycelial growth5,6. Trifloxystrobin, a member of the strobilurin or QoI (quinone-outside inhibitor) class, inhibits electron transfer at the Qo site of cytochrome b, effectively preventing fungal spore germination and providing strong protectant activity on the leaf surface7. The premix formulation of fluopyram + trifloxystrobin combines systemic and mesostemic properties, offering prolonged protection against a wide range of pathogens affecting cucurbits and other horticultural crops. While this dual-action formulation enhances field efficacy and resistance management, the physicochemical differences between the two active ingredients such as water solubility, partition coefficients, and degradation pathways also influence their environmental fate and residue behavior in crops and soils6,8.

Despite their agronomic benefits, the persistence of fluopyram and trifloxystrobin residues in edible plant parts and the environment has raised growing food safety concerns. Fluopyram is characterized by relatively low mobility and high persistence, with reported half-lives ranging from 87 to 231 days in soil9, indicating potential for carry-over into subsequent crops. Trifloxystrobin, although more labile, rapidly transforms into its acid metabolite CGA 321,113, which may persist longer and contribute to cumulative dietary exposure10,11. Studies in cucumber and other vegetables have shown detectable residues of both parent compounds and their metabolites long after application, thereby emphasizing the need for careful residue monitoring8,12. Considering that cucumbers are commonly consumed raw or with minimal processing, the risk of direct dietary exposure is particularly relevant. Long-term exposure to trace pesticide residues may pose toxicological risks, including potential endocrine and hepatic effects as documented in chronic feeding studies11.

Regulatory agencies such as the Codex Alimentarius Commission, the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), and India’s Food Safety and Standards Authority (FSSAI) mandate the establishment of Maximum Residue Limits (MRLs) to ensure consumer protection. These MRLs are derived from supervised field trials conducted under good agricultural practice (GAP) conditions, combined with dietary intake models that evaluate both acute and chronic risks11,13. However, dissipation and residue data generated in temperate regions cannot always be directly extrapolated to tropical or semi-arid agro-ecosystems like those in northern India, where high temperature, intense solar radiation, and differing soil organic matter significantly alter pesticide behavior14. Furthermore, limited data exist regarding the simultaneous dissipation of fluopyram, trifloxystrobin, and its metabolite CGA 321113 in cucumber under Indian field conditions, thereby creating a critical gap in national MRL assessment and risk evaluation.

Therefore, the present study was designed to comprehensively evaluate residue dynamics, persistence behavior, and dietary risk associated with the fluopyram + trifloxystrobin premix in cucumbers cultivated under semi-arid field conditions of Haryana, India. The specific objectives were: (1) to determine the dissipation kinetics and terminal residue levels of fluopyram, trifloxystrobin, and CGA321113 following recommended and 1.25X-dose applications; (2) to model their degradation behavior under prevailing environmental and agronomic conditions; and (3) to conduct a dietary risk assessment for Indian consumers based on measured residues and toxicological reference values. The results of this research are expected to contribute to safe pesticide application practices, and support the development of region-specific guidelines that balance disease management efficacy with food safety and environmental sustainability.

Methodology

Chemicals and reagents

All analytical-grade solvents (GC–MS grade) were procured from Merck, Germany. Magnesium sulfate (97% pure) was obtained from Acros Organics, USA, and the primary secondary amine (PSA) sorbent was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich, Singapore. Certified reference standards of fluopyram, trifloxystrobin, and the metabolite CGA321113 were supplied by Bayer Crop Science Ltd., India, along with the commercial premix formulation containing fluopyram 250 g/L and trifloxystrobin 250 g/L as a suspension concentrate (SC). The purity of all reagents was verified prior to use through blank analyses to ensure the absence of contaminants. Ultrapure water produced using a Milli-Q system (Millipore, USA) was used throughout the study.

Preparation of stock and working solutions

Individual stock solutions of fluopyram, trifloxystrobin and CGA321113 were prepared at a concentration of 100 mg/L in a 1:1 (v/v) mixture of n-hexane and acetone. The solutions were stored in amber glass vials at 4 °C to minimize photodegradation. Working standards ranging from 0.005 to 1.0 mg/L were prepared by serial dilutions of the stock solutions in n-hexane immediately prior to analysis. Calibration curves were established by plotting peak area against analyte concentration, and linear regression equations were used to determine actual concentrations. Calibration linearity was confirmed by correlation coefficients (R²), for all the compounds value more than 0.995.

Field trial design

The field experiment was carried out at the Research Farm of the Department of Entomology, Chaudhary Charan Singh Haryana Agricultural University (CCS HAU), Hisar, India, during the main growing season. The cucumber hybrid ‘Nazia F1’ was used as the test variety. A randomized block design (RBD) was adopted with nine treatment plots, including two application rates and an untreated control, each replicated three times. The recommended rate was 600 mL/ha (X), and the higher rate was 750 mL/ha (1.25X). Foliar sprays were applied three times at ten-day intervals, beginning at the flowering stage using a knapsack sprayer fitted with a hollow cone nozzle.

The experimental field soil was sandy loam in texture (40% sand, 30% silt, and 30% clay) with a pH of 7.6, electrical conductivity of 2.0 dS/m, and organic carbon content of 0.68%. Meteorological conditions recorded at the university’s agrometeorological observatory indicated mean daily temperatures between 21.0 and 38.8 °C, average relative humidity of 70%, and total rainfall of 0.37 mm during the study period. These semi-arid conditions are typical of the region and significantly influence pesticide dissipation patterns.

Sampling procedure

Representative samples of approximately 1 kg of marketable-size cucumbers were randomly collected from each plot at predefined time intervals: 0 (2 h after the final spray), 1, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15, and 20 days after application. Samples were placed in pre-labelled polyethylene bags, transported to the laboratory in ice boxes, and stored at − 20 °C until analysis. Soil samples (1 kg) were collected at harvest from each treatment plot, air-dried at 50 °C for six hours, and sieved through a 2 mm mesh before extraction. All samples were processed in triplicate to ensure reliability and reproducibility of results.

Extraction and cleanup

Residues of fluopyram, trifloxystrobin, and CGA321113 in cucumber and soil samples were extracted following a modified QuEChERS (Quick, Easy, Cheap, Effective, Rugged, and Safe) method as described by Sharma et al.15 with minor modifications. Homogenized cucumber samples (15 g) were placed in 50 mL centrifuge tubes and extracted with 30 mL of acetonitrile. The mixture was vortexed for one minute to achieve complete dispersion. Sodium chloride (10 g) was then added to induce phase separation, followed by vigorous shaking and centrifugation at 2,500 rpm for three minutes. The upper organic layer (15 mL) was transferred to a tube containing 10 g of anhydrous sodium sulfate to remove residual moisture.

For cleanup, 6 mL of the extract was subjected to dispersive solid-phase extraction (d-SPE) using a mixture of 150 mg PSA and 900 mg anhydrous magnesium sulfate. The mixture was vortexed and centrifuged again at 2,500 rpm for two minutes. The clear supernatant (4 mL) was filtered through a 0.2 μm PTFE syringe filter before analysis. Soil samples were extracted in a similar manner, except that 10 mL of distilled water was added prior to acetonitrile extraction to improve dispersion and recovery efficiency.

Method validation

Method validation was performed in accordance with the European Commission SANTE/2023/11,312 guidelines to ensure accuracy, precision, and linearity of the analytical procedure. A seven-point matrix-matched calibration curve was established at concentrations of 0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, and 1.00 mg/L. The limit of quantification (LOQ) was defined as the lowest concentration satisfying all validation criteria with a signal-to-noise ratio of at least 10. Accuracy and precision were evaluated through recovery experiments by fortifying blank cucumber samples at 0.01, 0.05, and 0.10 mg/kg (n = 5 for each level). The method’s recovery rate ranged between 80% and 103%, and the relative standard deviation (RSD) did not exceed 20%, fulfilling the criteria for repeatability and reproducibility specified by SANTE (2023)15. All analytical blanks and quality control samples were run in parallel to monitor background interference and instrumental stability.

Instrumental analysis

Fluopyram analysis: Performed on GC–MS/MS equipped with a Rtx-5MS capillary column (30 m × 0.25 mm, 0.25 μm film). Helium was used as the carrier gas. Injector and detector were set at 250 °C and 300 °C, respectively. The temperature program was: 80 °C (2 min hold), ramped at 20 °C/min to 180 °C, then 5 °C/min to 300 °C (10 min hold). Multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) transitions of 204.10 > 176.10 and 204.10 > 148.10 were used for identification. Injection volume was 1 µL. Peak of fluopyram was obtained at retention time of 13.08 min., m/z values for the fluopyram fragmented ions were found to be 173 (base peak), 59, 83, and 56 with the collision energy (CE) values of 24 and 12.

Result and discussion

Method validation

The analytical method developed for determination of fluopyram, trifloxystrobin, and the metabolite (CGA321113) in cucumber and soil matrices exhibited high reliability, sensitivity, and reproducibility. The combined use of both instruments GC-ECD (trifloxystrobin, and CGA 321113) and fluopyram (GC MS/MS) ensured reliable identification accurate quantification without compromising analytical validity moreover GC-ECD, owing to its high sensitivity and selectivity toward halogenated compounds. Chromatographic analysis of untreated control extracts confirmed the method’s selectivity, as no interfering peaks were observed at the retention times corresponding to the target analytes. This ensured that the detection of each compound was free from matrix-related artefacts or co-eluting substances, an essential prerequisite for the accurate quantification in complex plant matrices such as cucumber.

Calibration curves generated for fluopyram and trifloxystrobin demonstrated strong linearity within the tested concentration range of 0.01–0.05 µg mL⁻¹, with correlation coefficients (R²) ranging from 0.990 to 0.992. The back-calculated concentrations for all calibration points were within ± 20% of the nominal values, thereby fulfilling the linearity and accuracy requirements outlined by the SANTE/2023/11,312 guidelines18. The low matrix effect values, ranging from 0.63% to 11.27%, indicated negligible signal suppression or enhancement due to co-extractive compounds, confirming the efficiency of the QuEChERS-based cleanup procedure and the suitability of matrix-matched calibration for compensating for minor variations during detection.

The method exhibited high sensitivity, with limits of detection (LOD) and quantification (LOQ) determined to be 0.003 mg kg⁻¹ and 0.01 mg kg⁻¹, respectively. These values are well below the current maximum residue limits (MRLs) established for fluopyram (0.5 mgkg− 1) and trifloxystrobin (0.3 mgkg− 1) in cucumbers by the Codex Alimentarius19, confirming that the method is fit for regulatory monitoring and dietary risk assessment applications. Similar sensitivity has been reported in other matrix-specific validations in pesticide determination among cucumbers and other vegetables8,15,20.

Recovery studies performed by fortifying blank cucumber samples at three levels (0.01, 0.05, and 0.10 mg kg⁻¹) yielded recoveries within 80–103%, with relative standard deviation (RSD) values not exceeding 20%. These results meet the performance criteria recommended by SANTE (2023) for precision and accuracy in pesticide residue analysis. The high recovery efficiency across all fortification levels demonstrates that the optimized QuEChERS extraction and dispersive solid-phase cleanup provided effective analyte recovery while minimizing matrix interferences. Similar recovery ranges were observed by Sharma et al15. when analyzing fluopyram and trifloxystrobin residues in tomato using a comparable cleanup protocol, reinforcing the robustness of the method across crop matrices with varying water and pigment content.

The combination of low detection limits, high linearity, and strong recovery performance establishes the analytical reliability of the developed method for routine residue monitoring. Moreover, the minimal matrix effect and compliance with all SANTE (2023) validation parameters ensure that the method can be confidently applied to dissipation studies, residue dynamics assessment, and dietary risk evaluation. These results align closely with previous validations of QuEChERS-based approaches optimized for high-moisture matrices such as cucumber, lettuce, and tomato8,15,20. Collectively, these findings confirm that the analytical workflow is robust, reproducible, and suitable for accurately quantifying fluopyram, trifloxystrobin, and CGA321113 residues under field conditions (Table 2; Fig. 2a,b; Supplementary SA Table 1, SA Fig. 1).

Persistence of fluopyram, trifloxystrobin and its metabolite (CGA321113) in cucumber

Residue monitoring following the final application revealed distinct dissipation patterns for fluopyram, trifloxystrobin, and CGA321113 in cucumber fruits. Immediately after the third foliar application, fluopyram residues were 0.412 mg kg⁻¹ at the recommended dose (150 g a.i. ha⁻¹, X) and 0.555 mg kg⁻¹ at the higher rate (187.5 g a.i. ha⁻¹, 1.25X) (Table 3; Fig. 3). A rapid decline in residue concentration was observed within the first few days, with 67–76% dissipation by day 5. The residues fell below the limit of quantification (LOQ; 0.01 mg kg⁻¹) by day 10 at the lower dose, while traces persisted up to day 15 at the higher application rate. The dissipation followed first-order kinetics, suggesting that the decline was primarily governed by metabolic degradation and photolytic breakdown on the fruit surface, consistent with the compound’s physicochemical stability and systemic mobility.

Trifloxystrobin exhibited a similar but faster degradation pattern. The mean initial deposits were 0.348 mg kg⁻¹ and 0.561 mg kg⁻¹ at X and 1.25X doses, respectively. More than 93% of the residues dissipated within 7 days at both application rates, with complete disappearance by day 7 in the lower dose and by day 10 in the higher dose treatments (Table 3; Fig. 3). The rapid decline is likely associated with the compound’s relatively low water solubility and strong photolytic susceptibility, which facilitate volatilization and surface degradation under field conditions21. Additionally, trifloxystrobin’s mesostemic behavior—remaining predominantly on plant surfaces—contributes to faster dissipation compared with the more systemic fluopyram component.

The metabolite (CGA321113), derived from the biotransformation of trifloxystrobin, was detected only transiently in cucumber fruits. Initial concentrations reached 0.158 mg kg⁻¹ (X dose) and 0.218 mg kg⁻¹ (1.25X dose) immediately after spraying but declined rapidly below the LOQ by day 3 and day 7, respectively. The rapid disappearance of CGA321113 suggests efficient enzymatic degradation within plant tissues, likely through hydroxylation and conjugation reactions, which reduce its persistence and bioaccumulation potential. Similar findings were reported by Chawla et al.8 in cucumbers and Sharma et al.15 in tomatoes, where dissipation within 5–7 days under open-field conditions was observed.

Residue levels of all the analytes in soil samples collected at harvest were below the quantifiable limit, indicating negligible leaching or long-term persistence in the rhizosphere. This aligns with previous field investigations showing limited soil accumulation of fluopyram, trifloxystrobin and CGA321113 due to their moderate sorption coefficients and relatively short environmental half-lives11,22.

Comparative studies have shown that persistence dynamics of these fungicides vary considerably across crops and climatic regions. Katna et al.23observed higher initial fluopyram deposits (3.396–5.772 mg kg⁻¹) in French beans, whereas Saha et al.24 reported 0.591–0.688 mg kg⁻¹ in chilli sprayed with fluopyram + tebuconazole. Tripathy et al.22. recorded 0.45–2.07 mg kg⁻¹ in mango and 0.32–1.52 mg kg⁻¹ in pomegranate, highlighting how crop morphology, wax content, and local environmental conditions significantly influence residue dynamics. Sharma et al.15 reported higher persistence in onion (fluopyram: 2.09–4.69 mg kg⁻¹; trifloxystrobin: 0.65–1.97 mg kg⁻¹) under humid subtropical conditions, emphasizing the role of climatic and cuticular differences in determining degradation rates.

The relatively lower initial deposits and shorter persistence observed in cucumber in the present study can be attributed to the fruit’s smooth epidermis and high surface moisture, which promote rapid runoff and degradation. Furthermore, high daytime temperatures and low humidity during the experimental period in Haryana likely enhanced volatilization and photolytic decomposition. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that under the tested semi-arid field conditions, fluopyram and trifloxystrobin +CGA321113 (total) residues dissipate to safe levels within the pre-harvest interval time as per recommended for consumer’s safety, ensuring compliance with international MRLs.

Dietary risk assessment

Dietary exposure assessment was conducted using the highest residue levels detected two hours after the final foliar application, representing the worst-case exposure scenario for consumers. This approach ensures that potential health risks are not underestimated, aligning with international pesticide residue evaluation practices25. To estimate chronic and acute risks, the average daily intake of cucumber was assumed to be 0.005 kg person⁻¹ day⁻¹ and 0.007 kg person⁻¹ day⁻¹ for rural and urban population, consistent with national dietary consumption data for India26. The acceptable daily intake (ADI) values were 0.012 mg kg⁻¹ body weight day⁻¹ for fluopyram (Chauhan et al., 202516) and 0.04 mg kg⁻¹ body weight day⁻¹ for trifloxystrobin16.

The chronic dietary risk was evaluated using the hazard quotient (HQ) for fluopyram and trifloxystrobin which were substantially below unity at both recommended (X) and higher (1.25X) doses, indicating that long-term exposure through cucumber consumption poses no appreciable health concern (Table 4). Similarly, the acute dietary risk, expressed as the acute hazard index (AHI), remained well below the critical threshold of 100% for both the compounds, demonstrating negligible risk of short-term toxicity even under the highest observed residue conditions. These findings suggest that use of premix formulation (fluopyram + trifloxystrobin) as per good agricultural practices (GAP), are unlikely to result in either acute or chronic dietary hazards to consumers.

Comparable results have been reported in similar risk assessments across multiple crops and climatic conditions. Chawla et al.8 found HQ values below 0.03 for fluopyram and trifloxystrobin residues in cucumbers grown under subtropical conditions, while Mandal et al.27 observed comparable safety margins in tomatoes and grapes. Sharma et al.15 also reported that estimated dietary intakes for both fungicides constituted less than 1% of their respective ADI values in tomato under field conditions. These consistent observations across diverse crops highlight the relatively low dietary risk potential of these fungicides, largely attributable to their rapid dissipation, moderate systemic mobility, and low bioaccumulation tendency in plant tissues.

Overall, the present study confirms that the combined application of fluopyram and trifloxystrobin in cucumber, even at 1.25 times the recommended dose, poses minimal dietary risk to consumers. The hazard indices derived are well within internationally acceptable limits, underscoring that adherence to label-recommended pre-harvest intervals (PHIs) ensures consumer safety. These findings reinforce the importance of continuous residue monitoring and field-based validation to sustain the safe use of modern fungicidal formulations within integrated pest management frameworks.

Conclusion and way forward

The present study elucidated the residue dynamics and dietary risk profile of fluopyram, trifloxystrobin, and CGA321113 in cucumber under semi-arid field conditions. The dissipation behavior of both the fungicides followed first-order kinetics, reflecting typical exponential decay patterns observed in systemic and surface-active fungicides. Fluopyram residues declined below the limit of quantification (LOQ, 0.01 mg kg⁻¹) by the 10th and 15th day after application at the recommended (X) and higher (1.25X) doses, respectively. Trifloxystrobin exhibited even faster degradation, becoming non-detectable by the 7th and 10th day at the corresponding doses level. The metabolite (CGA321113), formed transiently from trifloxystrobin degradation, was short-lived and persisted only up to 3rd and 7th day at the lower and higher application rates, respectively. The estimated half-lives (DT₅₀) ranging from 1.14 to 2.69 days, suggesting rapid environmental breakdown under high temperature and light intensity conditions prevalent during the cropping season.

The dietary risk assessment, based on the highest observed residues, revealed that both the chronic and acute exposures were well within acceptable safety margins. Hazard quotient values were < 1, and %AHI values remained below 100%, confirming that cucumber consumption poses no health concern for consumers when fluopyram + trifloxystrobin premix formulations are applied following GAP. These findings are consistent with earlier residue assessments in other vegetables and fruits, which collectively indicate that this fungicide combination, though highly effective against a broad spectrum of fungal pathogens, demonstrates minimal residue persistence and low dietary risk8,15,27.

Overall, the study reinforces the suitability of the fluopyram + trifloxystrobin premix formulation for integrated disease management in cucumber cultivation. Its rapid dissipation ensures compliance with national and international maximum residue limits (MRLs), safeguarding consumer health while providing effective disease control under field conditions.

While the current findings confirm the safety of this premix under semi-arid conditions, further investigations are recommended to explore its behavior under different agro-climatic zones, cropping patterns, and soil types. Long-term monitoring of residue carryover in successive crops and soil–plant transfer studies could enhance understanding of environmental persistence. Moreover, integrating predictive modeling with real-time residue monitoring may improve risk communication and inform policy decisions for sustainable pesticide regulation. Finally, promoting farmer education on precise dosing, application timing, and adherence to pre-harvest intervals will further minimize residue risks and enhance food safety assurance in vegetable production systems.

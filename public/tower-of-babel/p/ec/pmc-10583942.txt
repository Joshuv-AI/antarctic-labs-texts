A commentary on emergency surgery in the era of artificial intelligence: ChatGPT could be the doctor’s right-hand man
Authors: Mohammad Abavisani, Farhad Dadgar, Masoud Keikha
Published: 2023

Dear Editor,

On November 2022, OpenAI (San Francisco) launched ChatGPT, a generative pretraining transformer (GPT) language model. ChatGPT has been introduced as an innovative and effective artificial intelligence (AI)-based tool in medicine, including patient care and research. Indeed, ChatGPT operates as an intelligent search engine, which has gained attention in clinical applications1. However, uses of AI in medicine have been documented, such as radiological imaging, clinical data diagnosis, and genomic data processing. Furthermore, Watson and Da Vinci are AI-based robots that are commonly used in surgical settings2,3.

Although robots may be able to replace surgeons in the future, this is not yet practical. Nonetheless, viewpoints differ on the impact of ChatGPT as a potential option for surgical planning, navigation, and support. Previous research has shown that the ChatGPT produces accurate and reproducible outcomes in both the fields of surgery and robotics4–7. There is also evidence that ChatGPT can be used as a source of therapy8. Some writers claimed that ChatGPT may be broadly utilized in surgical examinations, from text generation to data extraction and even clinical decision-making9. More than 20 articles on the role of ChatGPT in surgical procedures had been published as of May 2023.

Although ChatGPT may be proposed as an additional AI-based tool for surgeons, there are severe limitations to its clinical application, such as ethics in the usage of content-generating AI. It should also be mentioned that ChatGPT does not meet the ICMJE’s ethical responsibilities as an author. In this context, Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery, as well as an author on scientific journals, advises against encouraging the use of ChatGPT in clinics10. Because of its nature, the Chabot technology is prone to bias. There are numerous examples of misleading information generated by the AI model. As a result, the ChatGPT is a human-generated AI-based technology that may produce incorrect information when trained with biased data2,8.

In conclusion, ChatGPT may have problems when utilized in clinical practice, such as incorrect information, malicious use, and the ethics of employing AI in scientific writing. The source of information used by ChatGPT is unknown, which may have influenced the reliability and reproducibility of the results for global applications. ChatGPT data should be continuously monitored. The impact of ChatGPT on surgical science should be investigated further. We hypothesize that ChatGPT will not be employed as an additional source of information for clinical practice until it is debugged and trained using confidential data certified by a blinded third senior reviewer, and also as a response to ethical responsibilities.

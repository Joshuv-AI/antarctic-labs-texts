Morin ameliorates lipopolysaccharide-induced acute kidney injury in rats via alteration of TLR4/NF-κB, COX-2/PGE2, and monocyte chemoattractant protein-1/CXCL1 signaling pathway
Authors: Yuan Wang, Qing Gao, Bo Hu, Manli Hu, Chunjuan Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Acute kidney injury (AKI), a quick decline of renal function occurring over hours to days, is defined biochemically as defective filtration, abnormal retention of nitrogenous waste in the blood, and disturbances of replacement metabolism in electrolytes and water. In this study, we examined the renal protective effect of morin on lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced AKI in the rats and manifested the mechanism involved.MATERIALS AND METHODS:Intraperitoneal administration of LPS (10 mg/kg) was used for the induction of AKI in the rats, and the rats received the oral administration of morin (5, 10, and 15 mg/kg). The body weight, renal weight, and renal index were estimated. Renal parameters, soluble receptor for advanced glycation end products (sRAGE), advanced glycation end products (AGEs), antioxidant parameter, indoxyl sulfate, inflammatory cytokines, and inflammatory parameters were estimated. The mRNA expressions were also estimated.RESULTS:Morin treatment improved the body weight and reduced the renal weight and renal index. Morin treatment altered the level of renal parameters (creatinine, blood urea nitrogen [BUN], and albumin); sRAGE, AGEs, antioxidant parameters (catalase [CAT], superoxide dismutase [SOD], malonaldehyde, glutathione [GSH], oxidized GSH; indoxyl sulfate [urine, serum, and renal tissue]); inflammatory cytokines (tumor necrosis factor-α [TNF-α], interleukin (IL)-1α, IL-1 β, IL-2, IL-4, IL-5, IL-7, IL-10, IL-17, C-X-C motif chemokine ligand 1 (CXCL1), interferon γ, monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1), MCP-1α (MCP-1α), MIP-2, granulocyte macrophage colony-stimulating factor); inflammatory parameters (cyclooxygenase-2 [COX-2], prostaglandin E2 [PGE2], inducible nitric oxide synthase, transforming growth factor-β, and nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells [NF-κB]), respectively. Morin treatment altered the mRNA expression of kidney injury molecule-1, neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL), manganese SOD, TNF-α, IL-1 β, IL-6, CAT, MCP-1, NADPH Oxidase 4 (NOX4), and toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4).CONCLUSION:The result clearly showed the renal protective effect of morin against LPS induced AKI via alteration of TLR4/NF-κB, COX-2/PGE2, and MCP-1/CXCL1 signaling pathways.

Introduction

Acute kidney injury (AKI), a syndrome of critical care, generally occurs with abrupt loss of renal function and is prevalent in elderly patients, with a mortality rate of 20% in hospitalized patients.[1] It is characterized by rise in serum creatinine (≥0.3 mg/dl within 48 h) or a decrease in urine output (oliguria) within a few days. AKI is a heterogeneous condition that stems from a variety of pathophysiological processes and etiologies.[2] The burden of AKI is heavy, with affected individuals enduring a significantly high risk of mortality, progression to chronic kidney disease (CKD), and failure of other organ systems. Septic AKI, a subtype, has a complex pathophysiology. This ensures that both microcirculation and macrocirculation were disturbed in renal tissue, causing inadequate blood flow and oxygen supply.[34] Moreover, septic AKI is usually associated with oxidative stress, alteration of energy metabolism, inflammatory markers activation, and a derangement in the coagulation cascade. Although substantial progress has been made in unraveling these fundamental pathways, there are still very few effective treatments for septic AKI, and existing modalities remain inadequate.[356] The lack of therapeutic options for septic AKI further emphasizes the need for ongoing research and innovative approaches to prevent and manage the condition, and improve patient-centred outcomes and the associated morbidity and mortality.[27]

The renal tissue is essential for the filtering functions of the blood to maintain the homeostasis of the body, as well as its defense functions. The kidney is very sensitive to drastic injury, and oxidative stress and inflammation play an important role in renal injury. These processes are interconnected, as reactive oxygen species (ROS) activate NF-κB and inflammasomes amplify inflammatory signaling pathways.[789] Injury to the kidney (e.g., AKI) induces a complex immune response involving innate and adaptive immune cells, including neutrophils, macrophages, dendritic cells, and T lymphocytes. Pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines released by injured tubular epithelial cells may further amplify tissue damage and recruit more immune cells to the injury site. AKI involves an inflammatory cascade, with far-reaching consequences that extend beyond the initial insult. It will disrupt endothelial integrity, resulting in microvascular dysfunction, capillary rarefaction, and hypoxia, which will further worsen the kidney injury.[71011] Chronic inflammation leads to interstitial fibrosis and may eventually lead to CKD, and increases the risk of long-term renal dysfunction. At the same time, ischemic and toxic insults induce excessive ROS formation, outpacing the kidney’s antioxidant defences.[911] This oxidative stress is aggravated by mitochondrial dysfunction, leading to adenosine triphosphate depletion and tubular cell death by apoptosis or necrosis. This leads to tubular cell injury and the disruption of normal cell cycle processes due to oxidative damage, including lipid peroxidation, protein oxidation, and deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) fragmentation. Detailed investigation of these related pathways of inflammation and oxidative stress will be essential for implementing therapeutic strategies to prevent and treat AKI as well as CKD progression.[81011]

AKI is one of the frequently occurring complications in a renal tissue. The mechanism of SE act AKI is a strong modulator of its health outcomes, and its mechanistic dissection is crucial in the design of effective therapeutic interventions. Despite intense efforts, the exact pathophysiology of septic AKI remains elusive.[1213] Nevertheless, there is accumulating evidence indicating that the stimulation of innate immunity via recognition of pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) and damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) from the host significantly contributes to the pathogenesis of septic AKI. DAMPs (which stand for damage-associated molecular patterns) are endogenous molecules released from cells upon dying in response to cellular injury, while PAMPs are microbe-derived molecules that bind to pattern recognition receptors.[2] LPS is one of the well-defined PAMPs existing in the cell wall of Gram-negative bacteria and is known to activate the production of cytokines and ROS through its attachment to TLR4. The activation and infiltration of immune cells into injured tissues aggravate tissue injury in AKI.[1214] This process encompasses a myriad of inflammatory mediators such as cytokines, chemokines, and adhesion molecules. Moreover, the role of oxidative stress in septic AKI progression has also been linked with tubular epithelial cell apoptosis. It is known that the generation of ROS and reactive nitrogen species can lead to mitochondrial dysfunction, DNA damage, and lipid peroxidation, leading to cellular death and ultimately organ dysfunction.[121415] Therefore forest espouse the mechanistic understanding and their complex interplay in pitting it down and to identify novel therapeutic opportunities to prevent or mitigate septic AKI.[2]

Morin (2′,3,4′,5,7-Pentahydroxyflavone) is a naturally occurring flavonoid commonly found in various fruits, vegetables, and medicinal plants, particularly in Maclura pomifera (Osage orange) and Morus alba (white mulberry).[1617] The phenolic structure of morin, containing five hydroxyl functionalities in appropriate places on a flavonoid backbone, provides for various pharmacological activities. These available functional groups allow morin to interact with numerous cellular targets and cellular signal transduction pathways, accounting for its numerous biological activities in vivo and vitro.[161819] As an antioxidant, morin neutralizes free radicals and reactive oxygen species, thereby shielding cells from oxidative stress-evoked harm. Its anti-inflammatory effects are thought to be linked to its actions in modulating inflammatory mediators and preventing pro-inflammatory enzymes like COX-2 and inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS). Morin has also been shown to possess neuroprotective effects in animal models of various neurodegenerative diseases, most notably in studies that observed reduction of oxidative damage, inflammation, and neuronal cell death in Alzheimer’s and Parkinson’s disease models.[16181920] Morin has been shown to enhance lipid profiles, reduce blood pressure, and protect against ischemia-reperfusion injury. The multifaceted influence of morin makes it an alluring target for future research and clinical consideration for disease states.[1921] The current investigation exhibited the renal preventive effect of morin against LPS-induced AKI in the rat and scrutinized the underlying mechanism.

Materials and Methods

Experimental rodent

Wistar rats (aged: 10–12 weeks, sex: male, and weight: 180 ± 20 g) were obtained from the departmental animal house and kept in a single polyethylene cage under standard laboratory condition like relative humidity 60% ± 10%, temperature 20°C ± 5°C, and 12/12 h dark/light cycle. During the experimental condition, the rats received the standard diet and water ad libitum. The whole investigation follows the guidelines under the “Principles of Laboratory Animal Care.”

Induction of acute kidney injury

LPS was used for the induction of AKI in the rat. The rats received the intraperitoneal administration of LPS (10 mg/kg) except the normal control.

Experimental design

After induction of the AKI, the rats were divided into the following groups as follow and all group contains the rats.

The normal group of rats received an equal volume of dimethyl sulfoxide. All group rats were anesthetized and sacrificed after the LPS administration (24 h). The urine samples of all group rats were collected, and rats were anesthetized via intraperitoneal administration of 87 mg/kg ketamine and 13 mg/kg xylazine. The blood was collected from the puncture of the cardiac tissue, and a blood sample was collected in the ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid tubes and Eppendorf tubes. The rats were dissected, and their renal tissue was extracted. The one renal tissue of each experimental subject was stored in formalin (10%) for histopathological analysis, and the rest were lyophilized.

Urinary parameters

The microscopical, physical, and chemical analysis of the urine was done through the urine routine examination.

Renal function parameters

Renal function parameters like BUN, creatinine (Cr), and albumin (Alb) were estimated via using the manufacture instruction commercially available kits (BUN kit, DtectX serum creatinine, and Cell Biolab Albumin kit).

Electrolytes

The electrolytes such as Chloride Ion (Cl−), Phosphorus (P), Sodium Ion (Na+), and Potassium Ion (K+) were estimated via using the electrolyte analyzer (Horron, IMS-972, China).

Advanced glycation end products, receptor for advanced glycation end products, and indoxyl sulfate

The levels of AGEs, RAGE, and indoxyl sulfate were measured using the kit via following the instructions (Abaxis Inc., CA, USA).

Antioxidant and oxidative stress markers

The antioxidant markers such as glutathione (GSH), catalase (CAT), superoxide dismutase (SOD), GSSG, and MDA were estimated using the ELISA kit following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Inflammatory cytokines and inflammatory parameters

Inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, interleukin (IL)-1β, IL-6, IL-10, IL-17, IL-18; inflammatory parameters such as COX-2, VEGF, iNOS, NF-κB, PGE2 were estimated using the ELISA kits following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Real-time reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction

TRIzol reagent was used for the isolation of total RNA from the renal tissue of the rat following the manufacturer’s instructions (Sigma Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). Total RNA was reversely transcribed into the cDNA using the PrimeScript RT Reagent kit (TaKaRa, Tokyo, Japan). SYBR Green PCR Master Mix was conducted for real-time RT-PCR. The primers used in this study are mentioned in Table 1. GAPDH was used as the internal reference. Data were analyzed using the CT method.

Discussion

LPS-induced AKI is a useful experimental model to investigate sepsis-induced organ damage. LPS from the Gram-negative bacteria’s outer membrane is a common choice for this type of research and induces endotoxemia and systemic inflammation with renal cell cytotoxicity to investigate AKI in animal models.[1415] This model enables researchers to study the pathophysiological mechanisms contributing to sepsis-associated kidney impairment, as well as to test potential therapeutic interventions. An experimental study that evaluation the renal protective effects of morin on LPS-induced AKI in rats.[1222] Morin is a flavonoid molecule present in many fruits and herbs that has shown antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity.[23] This study explores the effect of morin on AKI caused by LPS, which causes sepsis, and may elucidate potential therapies with therapeutic potential to alleviate the progression of sepsis-associated AKI. Molecular mechanisms in kidney bioprotection will provide the basis for a promising treatment according to this critical pathophysiological state.

Oxidative stress is a major mediator of the pathophysiology of septic AKI. To elucidate the underlying mechanisms, we examined the expression of key pro-oxidant and antioxidant enzymes. The expression of NOX4, a principal source of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in septic AKI, was dramatically increased in the LPS group; however, the expression of antioxidants CAT and MnSOD was inhibited. Moreover, treatment with morin significantly reversed these LPS-induced changes and indicated a possible role in restoring redox homeostasis. Mechanistic studies further identified NOX4 as a key source of ROS in LPS-induced AKI. Yet, the exact molecular mechanisms underlying this process are not fully elucidated, meriting more study. The attenuation of oxidative stress damage by morin can also be attributed to its modulation of pro-oxidant and antioxidant enzyme expression, such as asparagine synthase, thus highlighting its possible applications in therapeutic use to ameliorate oxidative damage to the kidney in sepsis. It was important to note that oxidative stress represents an attractive strategy for novel septic AKI therapies, highlighting the necessity of further investigating the interactive nature of oxidative stress, inflammatory, and kidney injury processes in sepsis.[2]

Production of pro-inflammatory cytokines in out-of-control amounts in LPS-induced septic AKI can lead to a complex cascade of inflammatory processes that markedly influence the disease natural history and its severity. LPS, a component of Gram-negative bacterial cell walls, binds to and activates TLR4, leading to activation of the NF-κB and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathways.[24] This activation can lead to the significant release of proinflammatory cytokines. Increased serum levels of these cytokines in AKI patients indicate their important role in mediating renal inflammation, vascular dysfunction, and tubular epithelial cell injury. Among them, TNF-α and IL-1 β are notably harmful, inducing damage to the endothelium, enhancing vascular permeability, and promoting leukocyte infiltration, which stimulates kidney injury.[92526] Proinflammatory and anti-anti-inflammatory mediators IL-6 and IL-10 collapse the immune response. This dysregulation propels renal dysfunction and may progress into multiorgan failure. The role of the inflammatory network in AKI and a potential therapeutic approach. Systemic inflammation is prevalent in septic patients, which is closely related to the development of AKI, and the inflammatory network plays a critical role in the development of sepsis-induced AKI.[1525]

NGAL is a 25 kDa protein member of the lipocalin superfamily of carrier proteins and acts as an early-sensitive biomarker of AKI. NGAL expression is rapidly upregulated in renal tubular epithelial cells by multiple pathological stimuli, including ischemia/reperfusion injury, sepsis, nephrotoxic drugs, or oxidative stress. It is released into the urine and plasma rapidly after renal injury and has been proposed as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for AKI, with its return to normal often preceding that of well-worn serum biomarkers such as serum creatinine.[2728] KIMs are other important markers expressed in proximartubular epithelial (PTE) cells that respond to ischemic injury or toxic injury, such as KIM-1. KIM-1 is a 12-transmembrane–spanning glycoprotein that is nearly undetectable in healthy kidneys, but is dramatically upregulated in damaged tubular cells, where it promotes the clearance of apoptotic and necrotic debris. Increased concentration of NGAL and KIM-1 can be associated with the extent of renal damage, which makes them important indicators for early diagnosis and assessment of AKI progression.[2930] Due to their distinct contributions to inflammation, immune response modulation, and tissue repair, they have become appealing candidates for devising therapeutic approaches to ameliorate kidney injury.[31]

In LPS-induced AKI, the interaction between multiple inflammatory mediators and signaling molecules dictates the continuation of renal injury. COX-2, an inducible enzyme that catalyzes prostanoid synthesis, predominantly the synthesis of prostaglandin E2, is upregulated in response to LPS stimulation.[32] The upregulation leads to inflammatory cascades, vasodilation, and increased vascular permeability, aggravating kidney injury. At the same time, activated iNOS produces large amounts of NO. High levels of NO promote oxidative stress, peroxynitrite production, and delicate renal endothelium homeostasis, whereas nitrosative stress will further be induced in renal tissues.[3233] The precise equilibrium of inflammatory mediators is further muddied by the opposing actions of PGE2, the downstream product of induced COX-2. Although PGE2 can have renoprotective actions under specific situations, increased PGE2 in the setting of LPS-induced AKI drives the majority of its actions toward inflammation, immune cell recruitment, and renal vascular dynamics. The profibrotic cytokine TGF-β1 is upregulated in response to LPS and is thought to drive epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT), extracellular matrix deposition, and ultimately fibrosis. This causes chronic kidney injury and leads to CKD.[343536] NF-κB is a major transcription factor activated through LPS-TLR4 signaling, which then amplifies the inflammatory cascade. NF-κB regulates the expression of several inflammatory cytokines (including TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1 β) and chemokines that facilitate the infiltration of immune cells and perpetuate inflammation during AKI.[36] Morin treatment suppressed the level of inflammatory parameters and suggested the renal protective effect against AKI through suppression of COX-2/PGE2 and NF-κB signaling pathway.

CXCL1 is a strong neutrophil chemoattractant and plays a key role in the recruitment of neutrophils into the kidney, thus contributing to tissue injury in LPS-induced AKI. IFNγ is a proinflammatory cytokine that is produced by activated T cells and macrophages. It increases macrophage activation, increases antigen presentation, and promotes inflammation and oxidative stress injury in septic AKI. Monocyte chemotactic protein 1 (MCP-1 or CCL2) is one of the major chemokines that attracts monocytes and macrophages to the site of injury.[3738] A related chemokine, MCP-1α, also enhances injury by promoting monocyte infiltration and inflammatory damage in septic AKI. MIP-2 (CXCL2) is another neutrophil chemotactic factor implicated in neutrophil recruitment and activation, resulting in both oxidative stress and capillary leak and renal inflammation. Tumor-associated macrophages derived from higher GM-CSF levels have been found to play a role in immune cell differentiation and activation.[3940] In the setting of septic AKI, GM-CSF promotes monocyte and macrophage survival, which sustains inflammation and contributes to tissue injury. Morin exerts the renal protective effect through alteration of the MCP-1/CXCL1 signaling pathway.

Upon LPS treatment, TLR4 activation initiates a complex signaling cascade that involves the recruitment of adaptor proteins MyD88 and TRIF. This activation process leads to the stimulation of two major signaling pathways: the NF-κB and the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathways. The activation of these pathways results in the transcription and production of various pro-inflammatory mediators, most notably cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1 β.[4142] These cytokines are released in excessive amounts, contributing to a state of systemic inflammation. The heightened inflammatory response triggered by TLR4 activation has far-reaching consequences on multiple organ systems, particularly the kidneys.[42] The excessive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines leads to endothelial dysfunction, characterized by increased vascular permeability and altered blood flow. In addition, the inflammatory milieu promotes renal tubular cell apoptosis, a process of programmed cell death that can significantly impair kidney function. These combined effects of systemic inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and renal tubular cell death play a crucial role in the initiation and progression of AKI. The complex interplay between these factors underscores the importance of understanding the TLR4-mediated inflammatory cascade in the context of AKI pathogenesis and potential therapeutic interventions.[4243] Through the activation of these pathways, the transcription and production of diverse pro-inflammatory mediators, particularly cytokines like TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1 β, occur. They are released in an exaggerated fashion, leading to a condition of systemic inflammation. Activation of TLR4 initiates an exaggerated inflammatory response that has implications for many organ systems, including the kidneys. Overproduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines causes endothelial dysfunction due to MAPK-activated vascular permeability and blood flow. The resultant inflammatory milieu also promotes renal tubular cell apoptosis, or programmed cell death, that severely hinders kidney function. The combination of all these effects contributes to systemic inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, and renal tubular cell death that are key mechanisms in the initiation and progression of AKI.[44] This myriad of interactions highlights the role of the TLR4-mediated inflammatory cascade as a central regulator of AKI pathogenesis and presents a promising target for novel therapeutic strategies in AKI. The relevance of the LPS-induced AKI model to human sepsis-induced AKI goes beyond the fact that key pathophysiological features are replicated. This also offers a valuable platform for the detailed study of the complex interactions between systemic inflammation, renal hemodynamics, and cellular responses.[4546] This model allows investigators to explore the evolution of AKI through time, from the initial inflammatory cascade to the development of tubular injury and potential recovery phases. Emulating the human disease closely, it enables the characterization of biomarkers, the analysis of renal functional parameters, as well as the observation of histopathological changes taking place in sepsis-induced AKI. Moreover, the LPS-induced AKI model represents an attractive setting to evaluate different treatments at different time points in the disease process. Strategies for prevention (before or shortly after LPS exposure) or treatment (to lessen established AKI) can be evaluated by researchers.[4547] This enables the assessment of both platforms for early-stage interventions and rescue therapies. Also, the model allows for the exploration of combination therapies that target multiple pathways, complementing each other cooperatively, which may lead to more effective therapies for sepsis-induced AKI.[4347] Morin treatment reduced the mRNA expression of TRL4, suggesting the renal protective effect.

Conclusion

The LPS-induced AKI model replicates numerous aspects of the pathophysiology of sepsis-induced kidney injury and is a well-validated experimental model to explore inflammation-driven renal dysfunction. The model successfully reproduces major hallmarks of septic AKI, such as hyperproduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines, oxidative stress, endothelial dysfunction, and tubular injury. The morin modified the main altered molecular pathways associated with these pathological changes, including TLR4/NF-κB, COX-2/PGE2, and MCP-1/CXCL1. Results strongly indicate the renal protective role against AKI through modulation of several signaling pathways.

Efficacy and safety of thalidomide for recurrent aphthous stomatitis: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Authors: Yang Jian, Fuqin Wang, Minru Zhao, Xianru Han, Xiaoyu Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRecurrent aphthous stomatitis (RAS) is considered as the most common oral mucosal lesion affecting up to 25% of people worldwide. Thalidomide has been reported for the treatment of RAS, but the evidence has not been systematically evaluated. We first systematically reviewed the efficacy and safety of thalidomide for the treatment of RAS.MethodsWe searched The Cochrane Library, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Chinese BioMedical Literature Database (CBM), Wanfang Data, and VIP information from inception to December 2023. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing thalidomide with control for RAS were included in the analysis. The primary outcome were complete response and overall response, and the secondary outcome were recurrence interval (RI), ulcer number and size, healing time, visual analogue scale (VAS), immunological data, and adverse events. Meta-analysis was conducted using the Review Manager 5.4 software.ResultsTwenty-one trials involving 1668 patients were included in this review. The results of our meta-analysis showed that thalidomide significantly improved the complete response rate and overall response rate, prolonged the recurrence interval, accelerated the healing process, reduced the number and size of ulcers, and lowered TNF-α levels in the treatment of RAS. However, thalidomide significantly increases adverse events.ResultsThalidomide has a significant benefit in the treatment of RAS. However, considering the potential side effects of thalidomide, it may be an optimal treatment option for major RAS patients or cases that do not respond to topical agents.Trial registrationPROSPERO registration number: CRD42024495038.Keywords: Stomatitis, Aphthous; Recurrent aphthous stomatitis; Thalidomide; Systematic review; Meta-analysis

Introduction

Recurrent aphthous stomatitis is a common chronic disease of the oral mucosa with a prevalence of 5–25% and up to half of patients have a recurrence within three months [1]. The ulcers usually occur on the non-keratinized oral mucosa with a yellow or white, crater-shapes base surrounded by an erythematous halo, and usually heal spontaneously within 7–10 days [2]. Continuous and recurrent oral ulcers and severe pain influence the life quality of patients. RAS is classified as minor RAS, major RAS, and herpetiform RAS [3]. The aetiology of RAS remains unclear and it is thought to be multifactorial. The current research suggests that genetically mediated innate and acquired immune dysregulation play a key role in the disease development. Factors that modify the immune response include: trauma, drugs, nutritional deficiency, hormonal changes, food allergies, stress, tobacco, and oral micro-organisms [4, 5]. Aphthous-like ulcerations may also occur in systemic diseases, including gastrointestinal disorders, Behçet disease, and immunodeficiencies [6]. The aim of treating RAS is to reduce the number and size of ulcers, relieve symptoms and prolong disease-free periods. For some mild patients, palliative treatment for pain relief and maintaining the good mouth hygiene is sufficient. For individuals who experience multiple episodes and/or severe pain and eating difficulties, medication treatment should be considered. The recommended treatment for RAS includes topical agents and systemic medications. The topical agents are the first option of the treatment of RAS. For systemic drugs, corticosteroids, thalidomide, levamisole, dapsone, colchicine, seem to exert certain effects through different mechanisms [7]. In addition, the Consensus of China also recommends Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) for RAS [8]. Thalidomide, a synthetic derivative of glutamic acid, was used to relieve morning sickness in pregnant women, and was eventually banned because of its teratogenic effects [9]. In recent years, thalidomide has attracted the attention of researchers as its immunomodulatory effects. Thalidomide has significant therapeutic effects in many diseases including cutaneous lesions of systemic lupus, β-thalassemia, ankylosing spondylitis and multiple myeloma [10–13]. Thalidomide has gradually been introduced into the treatment of oral mucosal diseases with certain therapeutic effects [14–16]. However, the clinical application of thalidomide is limited because the current evidence is not as strong as other therapies such as corticosteroids. Meanwhile, in addition to embryonic toxicity, thalidomide also has other significant side effects, such as peripheral neurotoxicity, venous thromboembolism and somnolence [17–19]. Thalidomide for RAS treatment has not been systematically evaluated, so to better balance the benefits and risks, we conducted a review of all RCTs, aiming to evaluate the efficacy and safety profile of thalidomide for treatment of RAS.

Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis were performed according to the PRISMA guideline.

Search strategies

The following electronic databases were searched by two independent researchers (Jian and Zhao) for published studies from inception to December 2023: PubMed, Scopus, Embase, Web of Science, The Cochrane Library, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Chinese BioMedical Literature Database (CBM), VIP information, Wanfang Data.

The main search strategy was as follows: (thalidomide OR sedoval) AND (recurrent aphthous stomatitis, stomatitis, aphthous, recurrent aphthous ulcers, aphthous stomatitis, recurrent oral ulcers).

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion criteria were set according to the PICOS component:

Participants (P): individuals diagnosed with RAS, including patients with RAS who also suffer from basic diseases such as AIDS, Behçet’s disease or acute leukaemia.

Participants (P): individuals diagnosed with RAS, including patients with RAS who also suffer from basic diseases such as AIDS, Behçet’s disease or acute leukaemia.

Intervention (I): treatment groups received oral thalidomide alone or thalidomide combined with other interventions.

Intervention (I): treatment groups received oral thalidomide alone or thalidomide combined with other interventions.

Comparator (C): placebo or any form of medical intervention except thalidomide, whether topical or systemic.

Comparator (C): placebo or any form of medical intervention except thalidomide, whether topical or systemic.

Outcomes (O): The primary outcomes included complete response and overall response, and we defined overall response as complete response plus partial response. The secondary outcomes included: recurrence interval, number of ulcers, days to ulcer healing, VAS score, ulcer diameter, immunological data, adverse effects.

The secondary outcomes included: recurrence interval, number of ulcers, days to ulcer healing, VAS score, ulcer diameter, immunological data, adverse effects.

Study design (S): Randomized control trials and controlled clinical trials.

The exclusion criteria: uncontrolled trials, reviews, posters, secondary research, meeting abstracts, in vitro or animal studies, and papers without full text.

Paper screening and data extraction

First, two independent researchers screened the titles and abstracts against the eligibility criteria. Subsequently removing duplicate, they check the full text of all potential studies. EndNote X7.3 software was used for duplicates removal and paper screening. Two authors independently extracted the information from included research according to a designed data collection table. The following data were recorded: name of the first author, number of patients analyzed, publication year, study design, clinical intervention, outcomes, and treatment duration. Any discrepancies were resolved via discussion by a third researcher, if necessary.

Quality assessment

Two independent researchers evaluated the methodological quality of the included studies by the Cochrane Risk of Bias Assessment Tool, which considered following seven assessment criteria [20]: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting, and other sources of bias. Any divergent assessment was resolved in open discussion or consultation with the corresponding author.

Statistical analysis

Meta-analysis was performed for each outcome using RevMan5.4 software. Dichotomous data, such as complete response, were assessed as risk ratio (RR) using the Mantel-Haenszal method. Continuous variables, including recurrence interval, number of ulcers, days to ulcer healing, VAS score, ulcer diameter, were assessed as the mean difference (MD). 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were used to evaluate the effect size. Statistical heterogeneity was evaluated using I2 statistics. when I2 50%, the trials were thought to be homogeneous and the data were pooled by fixed-effect model; otherwise, the trials were supposed to be heterogeneous, and random-effects model was chosen. For multiple intervention study, meta-analysis was performed between the comparison groups in interest according to subgroup analysis, if there were more than one control group in a trial for a subgroup analysis, the groups were combined into a single pair-wise comparison, as recommended by the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions [21]. For cross-over trial, data from the first phase were analyzed.

Results

Search results and study characteristics

Figure 1 presented the study selection process. 1242 articles were found in nine databases following search strategy described above. After removing duplicates, screening the titles and abstracts, and reading entire articles, 21 studies with 1668 patients met the inclusion criteria and were included into our meta-analysis [22–42]. 15 publications were presented in Chinese language and 6 in English. The 17 included trials were conducted in China [22–24, 26, 28, 31–42], while the other four were conducted in the United States, France, Mexico, and Brazil [25, 27, 29, 30]. Of all the eligible studies, three compared [25, 29, 30] the efficacy of thalidomide with placebo, and seven [23, 24, 26–28, 38, 40] compared with other medical measures including prednisone, vitamin B1, montmorillonite powder, dapsone, colchicine, etc. Nine trials [31–34, 36, 37, 39, 41, 42] evaluated the combination therapy of thalidomide and other drug compared to other drugs alone. And two triple-arms trials compared the efficacy and safety among the combination of thalidomide and TCM, thalidomide alone and TCM alone [22, 35]. The duration of treatment ranged from 5 days to 6 months. The detailed information on the eligible trials is shown in Table 1.

Risk of bias

Three independent raters assessed the methodological quality of the eligible RCTs using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool. All selected studies were randomized controlled, but only two [25, 38] reported allocation concealment and were evaluated as low risk. Only three [25, 29, 30] and four [25, 29, 30, 38] studies were judged as low risk about detection bias and performance bias, respectively. All studies were classified as low-risk about selective reporting and incomplete outcome. There was insufficient information about other bias and the risk was assessed as unclear. The results of the quality evaluation are shown in Fig. 2.

Risk of bias graph of included RCTs

Meta-analysis results

We performed subgroup analysis under different outcomes.

Complete response.

Three studies [25, 29, 30] evaluated the complete response of thalidomide versus placebo for RAS, the meta-analysis showed a significant improvement in those who received thalidomide versus placebo (RR = 6.95, 95% CI: 3.04–15.92, I2 = 39%, p < 0.00001, fixed-effect model, Fig. 3a). Pooled data from eight trials [22–24, 26–28, 35, 40] showed that thalidomide had a better therapeutic effect than other drug on complete response (RR = 2.18, 95% CI: 1.38–3.45, I2 = 68%, p = 0.008, random-effect model, Fig. 3b). A meta-analysis of ten trials [22, 31–37, 39, 41] revealed that the combination therapy of thalidomide and other medicine was better than other medicine alone in complete response (RR = 2.44, 95% CI: 1.55–3.84, I2 = 59%, p = 0.0001, random-effect model, Fig. 3c).

Forest plot of thalidomide group versus control group in complete response rate for RAS treatment. RAS = recurrent aphthous stomatitis, CI = confidence interval. a thalidomide vs. placebo, b thalidomide vs. other drugs, c thalidomide + other drugs vs. other drugs alone

Overall response

In the only study [25] that reported overall response compared with placebo, the thalidomide group had the higher overall response rate (RR = 3.59, 95% CI: 1.87–6.89, Fig. 4a). Seven studies [22, 24, 26–28, 35, 40] evaluated the overall response between the thalidomide and other medicine, and although this did not reach statistical significance, it implies that the thalidomide group might be more effective than the control group in terms of overall response (RR = 1.18, 95% CI: 0.99–1.41, I2 = 80%, p = 0.07, random-effect model, Fig. 4b). Ten studies [22, 31–37, 39, 41] evaluated that the overall response of the combination of thalidomide and other drug, the meta-analysis indicated that the combination of thalidomide and other drug was superior to other drug alone in overall response (RR = 1.32, 95% CI: 1.23–1.42, I2 = 0%, p < 0.00001, fixed-effect model, Fig. 4c).

Forest plot of thalidomide group versus control group in overall response rate for RAS treatment. RAS = recurrent aphthous stomatitis, CI = confidence interval. a thalidomide vs. placebo, b thalidomide vs. other drugs, c thalidomide + other drugs vs. other drugs alone

Recurrence interval

Three studies [23, 38, 42] reported on RI, pooled data indicated thalidomide can significantly prolong the RI compared to other interventions (MD = 26.91, 95% CI: 2.29–51.54, p = 0.03, I2 = 99%, random-effect model, Fig. 5).

Forest plot of thalidomide group versus control group in recurrence interval changes for RAS treatment. RAS = recurrent aphthous stomatitis, CI = confidence interval

Healing time

Four studies [31, 32, 35, 38] provided data on the healing time of the ulcers, the analysis results suggested that thalidomide group was better able to accelerate the healing process compared to the control group (MD = -1.31, 95% CI: -2.06 to -0.56, I2 = 70%, p = 0.0006, random-effect model, Fig. 6).

Forest plot of thalidomide group versus control group in healing time for RAS treatment. RAS = recurrent aphthous stomatitis, CI = confidence interval

Number of ulcers

Number of ulcers was reported in four studies [23, 26, 35, 38], a statistically significant reduction was seen in the thalidomide treatment group compared to placebo or other medicine on ulcers number (MD = -0.98, 95% CI: -1.87 to -0.08, p = 0.03, I2 = 91%, random-effect model, Fig. 7).

Forest plot of thalidomide group versus control group in ulcer number for RAS treatment. RAS = recurrent aphthous stomatitis, CI = confidence interval

Ulcer size

Ulcer size was assessed in two RCTs [37, 42] which were measured by mean diameter of the ulcers, and the results showed that the thalidomide group significantly reduced the mean diameter of the ulcers compared to other drugs group (MD = -0.85, 95% CI: -1.02 to -0.68, I2 = 4%, p < 0.00001, fixed-effect model, Fig. 8).

Forest plot of thalidomide group versus control group in ulcer size for RAS treatment. RAS = recurrent aphthous stomatitis, CI = confidence interval

VAS score

Three of the included studies [35, 37, 42] used the VAS score as an outcome indicator, the pooled result showed that there were no significant differences between the thalidomide group and the control group (MD = -0.64, 95% CI: -1.75 to 0.47, I2 = 91%, p = 0.26, random-effect model, Fig. 9).

Forest plot of thalidomide group versus control group in VAS score for RAS treatment. RAS = recurrent aphthous stomatitis, CI = confidence interval

Immunological data

Five trials [22, 25, 28, 35, 37] reported immunological indicators, including TNF-α, IgA, IgG, IgM. Four studies assessed TNF-α, three of which reported a reduction in TNF-α by thalidomide, and one reported an unexpected increase. The substantial heterogeneity among the four studies and the abnormity of numerical data precluded us from performing the meta-analysis on TNF-α. Xu Q et al. [37] found that the combination of thalidomide and cimetidine could significantly reduce levels of IgA, IgG and IgM compared with cimetidine alone.

Adverse events

Due to the severity of symptoms in patients with severe underlying diseases such as AIDS, Behçet’s disease or acute leukemia, these patients are not included in the analysis of adverse effects. Four trials reported the patients who withdrew from treatment due to adverse reactions, with 12 patients in the thalidomide group in total and only one patient in the control group. Eight studies did not report adverse events or did not provide detailed information. The remaining nine studies [22, 27, 32, 34, 35, 38, 40–42] evaluated the adverse event such as constipation, somnolence, dizziness, rash, peripheral neurotoxicity, etc. The result of the meta-analysis indicated that thalidomide-containing regimens significantly increased adverse events (RR = 1.87, 95% CI: 1.17–2.99, p = 0.009, I2 = 36%, fixed-effect model, Fig. 10).

Discussion

Aphthous lesions are painful, and affect the quality of life and psychological condition by interfering with swallowing, chewing, and speaking [43, 44]. Unfortunately, despite the high incidence and extensive research on RAS, its management remains quite challenging with no definitive treatment methods [45]. Topical agent, including glucocorticoids, corticosteroids, anesthetics, antibiotics, seem to be the first option of the treatment of RAS, but there are no evidence-based guidelines available to indicate which systemic treatment should be considered as first-line treatment for RAS cases that do not respond to topical agents. So far, the etiopathogenesis of RAS remains unclear; the results of current research indicate that multiple factors contribute, of which innate and acquired immune disturbance may play an essential role [7]. Th1 type cytokines, including TNF-a, IL-2, and IL-12, determine the propensity of autoimmunity and induce cellular responses. Some studies have indicated that Th1 type immune response plays a key role in the development of RAS, with significantly higher secretion of Th1 cytokines in RAS patients compared to the healthy [46–48]. The TNF-α cytokine, a major inflammatory mediator, induces initiation of the inflammatory process. It has been found that RAS individuals showed significantly higher level of TNF-α than healthy individuals in both peripheral blood and saliva [49–51]. This event stimulates cytotoxic T lymphocytes and increases endothelial cell expression, leading to the migration of inflammatory cells to the site of inflammation, resulting in the development of ulcers [45]. Thalidomide can block expression of TNF-α by different possible mechanisms. One such mechanism is by blocking the expression of NF-kB which works as a transcription factor for TNF-α [52]. Another study by Noman, A.S. et al. found that thalidomide inhibits lipopolysaccharide-induced TNF-α production by downregulating MyD88 protein and mRNA [53]. It has also been described that thalidomide reducing the expression level of TNF-α by enhancing mRNA degradation [54]. A Cochrane review evaluating the systemic intervention for RAS did not explicitly recommend thalidomide as a treatment [55]. We first performed a systematic evaluation of the efficacy and safety of thalidomide for RAS treatment as more trials were conducted. The results of the meta-analysis indicated that, compared to control group, thalidomide significantly improved the complete response and overall response rate, prolonged the recurrence interval, accelerated the healing process, reduced the number and size of ulcers, and lowered levels of TNF-α. However, thalidomide did not provide an advantage over the control group in pain alleviation. Notably, compared to non-thalidomide therapy, thalidomide-containing therapy significantly increased adverse events such as peripheral neuropathy, thrombosis/embolism events, constipation, somnolence. Systemic corticosteroids such as prednisone are often the common choice of RAS treatments [56]. Zeng QX et al. [38] concluded that thalidomide had a long-term effect of prolong the RAS recurrence interval than prednisone. It is important to note that the outstanding efficacy of thalidomide must be balanced against its adverse events. Treatment for RAS should follow a stepwise approach, starting with topical medication and progressing to systemic medication [43]. Considering the self-healing nature of RAS and the potential side effects of thalidomide, it may be a promising option for major RAS patients or cases that do not respond to topical agents. Although thalidomide is an absolute contraindication in pregnant women or women planning to conceive, its other adverse reactions can be minimized by a stepwise reduction of dosage. In early studies, thalidomide was often administered at higher doses. Jacobson [25] and David [14] opted for a daily dose of 200 mg with high incidence of adverse reactions. High doses and long-term use of thalidomide significantly increased the incidence of thromboembolism and peripheral neuropathy, which are the most common major adverse reactions [19]. In more recent research, Zeng [38] designed a dosage of 100 mg/d for 10 days, followed by 50 mg/d for 10 days, and finally 25 mg/d for 10 days with mild to moderate adverse reaction. Deng [23] compared the safety and efficacy between 25 mg/d and 50 mg/d thalidomide doses, and found that 25 mg/d thalidomide had a long-term effect on prolong the recurrence interval of RAS with better safety. In Wei’s study, RAS patients were treated with maintenance thalidomide at doses of 25 mg/d or 50 mg/d after initial treatment with prednisone, and it was found that the effect of 50 mg/d thalidomide was not superior to that of the 25 mg/d drug [57]. In the light of recent research findings, we believe that thalidomide at a dose of 25 mg/d is a good choice. Therefore, during thalidomide treatment, peripheral neuropathy symptoms such as poor sensation, distal limb numbness, muscle weakness and hypotonia should be carefully monitored. Thalidomide treatment should be stopped immediately if any of the above symptoms occur to avoid irreversible damage to the peripheral nerve [17]. In the future, a RCT could perhaps be designed to administer thalidomide every other day or even twice a week, to explore lower doses that can achieve better safety while ensuring effectiveness.

Limitations should be taken into account for our research. The most critical limitation is the low quality of most the analyzed studies. Specifically, most of studies did not provide detailed information on blinding methods and allocation concealment, which we consider to be high risk. Another important limitation is the considerable heterogeneity among the included trials. This heterogeneity is evident in the type of RAS, type of underlying disease, difference in doses, comparison groups, outcome measures, treatment duration, follow-up period and participant characteristics. Additionally, most the eligible trials were performed in China, which limits the generalizability of these findings to other populations.

Conclusions

Currently, there are no guidelines indicating which systemic treatment to choose as the first treatment for RAS cases that do not respond to topical medicine. Our research firstly systematically demonstrated that thalidomide has a significant effect in improving the response rate, prolonging the recurrence interval, accelerating the healing process, reducing levels of TNF-α, the number and size of ulcers in treatment of RAS. However, thalidomide therapy was associated with a significant increase in adverse events. However, our results provide clinicians with an optimal treatment option for the RAS patient who do not respond to topical medication. Nevertheless, due to the low quality and heterogeneity of the trials we included, large-sample randomized, controlled, multicenter, clinical trials are warranted.

Change in the angle of cervical lordosis after occipito-cervical fixation in the course of instability of C1–C2 in patients with rheumatoid arthritis
Authors: Marek Rybarczyk, Kamil Koszela, Małgorzata Mańczak, Robert Gasik
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic inflammatory joint disease characterized by a specific pattern of bone and joint destruction. Instability of C1–C2 can be seen in up to 65% of RA patients. Occipito-cervical fixation (OCF) protects spine from trauma damage and neurological deficit progression though it causes limitation of cervical movement. The aim of the study was to evaluate the change in the cervical lordosis angle after OCF in the course of C1–C2 instability in patients with RA. The patients studied all underwent OCF in the period of 1999–2018 in the Department of Neuroortopedia MCR STOCER. Thirty three patients with RA were considered. The data was analyzed retrospectively. Clinical data and radiological data such as X-rays were analyzed. The radiological evaluation of cervical lordosis angle C2–C7 (C2–C7 CL) before and after occipito-cervical fixation was performed. Cervical lordosis angles before and after OCF did not differ: 17 (IQR 10–30) vs. 20 (IQR 7–25); p = 0.158. The analysis showed no significant changes of cervical lordosis after OCF in rheumatoid patients. It can be result of surgical procedure careful performing. However, further research is necessary in this area.

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic disease that causes inflammation of joints and mainly presents with pain, stiffness, tenderness and swelling in the joints. The specific causes for the disease are still unknown, but several risk factors such as: genetics, female gender, age, smoking have been identified. The typical onset of RA occurs in adults between their thirties and sixties. Women are two-to-three times more often affected than men. The prevalence of RA in Poland in 2021 was 689.0 people per 100,000 population, a total of 0.7% [1–3].

Pathological joint changes can be seen in up to 40% of RA patients [4]. The most frequent vertebra involvement of RA patients are atlanto-axis and subaxial instability. It can cause the spinal cord and/or nerve roots compression. Instability of C1-C2 can be seen in up to 65% of RA patients (Fig. 1) [5, 6]. In case of neurological deficit progression decompression of the neurological structures is consider. The static sagittal balance of the normal spine is a physiological alignment of the spine in the most efficient manner by the muscular forces. Serious cervical changes in course of rheumatoid arthritis provides misalignment of the spine [7].

The occipito-cervical fixation (OCF) operation provides immediate stability of occiput-cervical junction. The OCF is an extensive surgical procedure and provides high risk of complications, especially in RA patients [8]. It also causes limitation of cervical movement and developing changes in the distal-to-stabilisation segments [9]. Thus can be prevent by the restoration of cervical aligment performed during OCF operation. Therefore, preoperative planning is necessary [10].

Currently, for precise assessment of spine pathology, implant position and treatment effects, sagittal spine balance parameters are used. Cervical lordosis is a radiologic parameter easy to measure and so it is often evaluate [11–13]. According to the latest reports, C1–C2 instability correlates with the C1–C7 lordosis angle [14].

The aim of the study was to evaluate the change in the cervical lordosis angle after occipito-cervical fixation (OCF) in the course of C1–C2 instability in patients with RA.

Materials and methods

Study design

Patients with RA (confirmed by a rheumatologist) were examined by a physician (orthopedist). Each of the RA patients had confirmation of the disease according to the 2010 ACR/EULAR criteria [15]. X-ray examination of the cervical spine (AP, lateral and functional projections) was analyzed. Based on the detected C1-C2 instability, patients were qualified for OCF. The patients were treated in the period of 1999–2018 in the Department of Neuroortopedics MCR STOCER, Konstancin Jeziorna, Poland. The study was approved by the Bioethics Committee at the National Institute of Geriatrics, Rheumatology and Rehabilitation in Warsaw, Poland (number of approval: KBT-3/2/2019, date of approval: April 24, 2019). Informed consent was obtained from all subjects involved in the study.

Study protocol-assessment of CL

The radiological evaluation of cervical lordosis angle C2-C7 using Cobb method was performed before and after OCF procedure [16].

Study protocol-occipito-cervical fixation

After patient was placed prone with the head secured, a midline posterior incision was made from the occiput down to the upper cervical spine. Dissection was carried down to expose the occiput and posterior elements of C1 to C3 (or more, depending on the fusion level). Occipital plate was secured to the occipital bone using screws. Cervical screws were placed in different levels of cervical vertebra and 2 rods were put to connect the occipital plate and cervical screws to provide rigid fixation. During fixation the x ray examination was done. Soft tissues were sewn anatomically with a drain [17]. Figure 2 shows an X-ray after the OCF procedure.

Data analysis

Statistical analysis was made using Statistica v.13 (Oklahoma, USA). Distribution of continuous variables were verified using Shapiro-Wilk test. The distribution of all continuous variables differs significantly from normal distribution. Continuous data are presented as median and interquartile range, nominal data as numbers and percentages. The cervical lordosis angle values before and after the intervention were compared using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

Results

Data from 33 patients with RA were included in the analysis. All patients were women. The median age was: 59.58 (SD = 11.12), min = 26; max = 77. The mean disease duration was 19 years (SD = 7.30). In this group, all patients were taking steroids chronically. Mean duration of steroid use was 19.1 years (SD = 2.35). Nineteen (57.6%) patients had osteoporosis, sixteen (48.5%) had hypertension, three (9%) had renal failure.

There was no statistically significant change in the cervical lordosis angle values after OCF (p = 0.158). Table 1; Fig. 3 shows the lordosis angle before and after the OCF procedure.

Discussion

Rheumatoid arthritis causes a number of damage to the musculoskeletal system, including the spine. C1-C2 instability is particularly dangerous for the patient’s health. The number of studies assessing the sagittal balance parameters of the spine after OCF in the course of C1-C2 instability is insufficient. To our knowledge, this is one of the few studies that evaluates the cervical lordosis angle before and after OCF in the course of C1-C2 instability in patients with RA. Surgical treatment in this group of patients is extremely difficult and demanding. Therefore, this study provides valuable information for both surgical planning and the evaluation of surgical outcomes.

The analysis showed no significant changes of cervical lordosis after OCF. The median cervical lordosis angle C2-C7 is close to the referred by Ling F.P. and recognized as normal [11]. That lack of CL correction can be result of careful approach during operation and coexistence of joints patological changes in course of RA making correction of CL unable. There are other reasons surgeons treat this operation with exceptional care. Direct compression or excessive retraction can cause injury of the spinal cord, nerves, dural sac and vertebral artery. Fusion may limits neck motion permanently. Moreover, fusing too many levels unnecessarily sacrifices mobility, which is significantly limited in the course of RA. Moreover, it increases stress on lower cervical segments, accelerating adjacent segment disease (ASD) which can be seen in patients after cervical operations [18, 19].

In literature, several studies have reported the correlation between the radiographic parameter of CL and long-term outcomes of surgical spine treatment in RA patients.

Tanouchi et al. evaluated the association between the distal junctional disease and the cervical spine sagittal alignment in group of 24 patients who underwent O-T5 fusion. Their report indicated that excessive correction of the O-C7 angle (hyperlordotic alignment) is likely to cause postoperative distal junctional disease following the OT fusion [20].

Wu et al. investigated the incidence and risk factors for adjacent segment degeneration (ASD) following occipitoaxial fusion in group of 41 patients without RA who were divided into ASD group and non-ASD group. The C2–C7 angles changed significantly between the pre- and postoperative periods. It was suggested that the small O-C2 angle and large C2–C7 angle observed in the early postoperative period were risk factors for the development of ASD [21].

Tanouchi and Wu pointed that increase of CL may cause changes of distribution of force vectors effecting on end-stabilization and adjacent segment [20, 21].

It is worth noting the systematic review published by Siempis et al. [22], which emphasizes the importance of a thorough clinical and radiological assessment, particularly of the cervical spine, in patients with RA. Furthermore, surgical treatment for these patients can lead to improvement and reduction of symptoms.

Limitation of this article is a retrospective study and a small group of patients. Further studies are necessary, prospective and comparative studies on a larger group of patients. Additionally, instability below stabilization and possible loosening of the implants should be assessed. Furthermore, other parameters of sagittal balance, both cervical and pelvic, should be considered, as well as clinical parameters.

Conclusions

The analysis showed no significant changes of cervical lordosis after OCF in RA patients group. It can be result of surgical procedure careful performing. However, further research is necessary in this area.

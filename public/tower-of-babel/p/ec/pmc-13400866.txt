Cardiac rehabilitation through conventional care with add on Ayurveda intervention following acute myocardial infarction help improving the cardiac function - A case report
Authors: Sanjeev Rastogi, Shashwat Rastogi, Tarun Mittal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Acute Myocardial Infarction (AMI) is a life threatening event and leads to lifelong consequences if the patient survives the event. In view of global hypokinesia and reduced ventricular ejection fractions, subsequent management requires pharmacotherapy and cardiac rehabilitation to improve outcomes. Cardiac rehabilitation is a multidisciplinary program involving exercise training, risk factor reduction, education and psychological support, besides standard pharmacotherapy. We present here a case of 46 year old male having AMI, survived with severe debility due to low ventricular ejection fraction. Ayurveda intervention besides conventional cardiac rehabilitation was added here for any possibility to improvise the results. Such an add-on and its impact in cardiac outcomes have not been reported in the literature so far.The case was diagnosed as of Antero-septal MI (ASMI). Conventional interventions were done at a tertiary care hospital and upon stabilization patient was discharge with recommendations. After 3 months of initial event, in view of slow recovery, patient asked for Ayurveda interventions as add-on to the conventional care.Conventional Cardiac rehabilitation with add on Ayurveda intervention for 8 months resulted in good recovery of the patient with an improved ventricular ejection fraction up to 50%. His debility was also much reduced and he was able to do his routine chorus with ease. No adversities were reported following the conventional cardiac post infarction care along with Ayurveda interventions.

Introduction

ST elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) represents a serious and potentially fatal condition arising from rupture of a vulnerable plaque in the coronary arteries leading to formation of thrombus occluding the lumen and reduced blood supply to the myocardium. Patients are treated with emergency percutaneous intervention (PCI) or thrombolysis [1]. Patients are subsequently treated with drugs to reduce and prevent clot formation, improve cardiac function, and recommended cardiac rehabilitation [1], [2]. Cardiac rehabilitation (CR) is a comprehensive, multidisciplinary programme that includes structured exercise training, risk-factor modification, patient education, and psychological support. Evidence consistently demonstrates that CR lowers mortality, reduces hospital readmissions, and enhances both exercise capacity and quality of life in individuals with ischemic heart disease. Accordingly, the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) strongly recommends CR for patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), non–ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI), or unstable angina pectoris (Class I, Level of Evidence A).

Ayurveda, the traditional health care system of India proposes few herbo-mineral formulations expected to improve cardiac function. This condition which is akin to vata-kaphaja hrida roga (vata responsible for coronary spasm and kapha for blood stasis) in Ayurveda, so far we have not come across any report where ayurvedic interventions have been utilized in conjunction with conventional treatment in a post MI patient having significant ventricular dysfunction and low ejection fraction (∼25%). The patient was treated with conventional treatment for initial three months and Ayurveda add on therapy for following eight months. A marked improvement in quality of life and cardiac function was noticed following the add-on Ayurveda interventions. This case proposes that Ayurveda interventions can judiciously be thought of being added into the comprehensive cardiac rehabilitation planning subject to further evidence.

Patient information

A 46 year old male having a family history of CAD presented with a history of uneasiness followed by loss of consciousness on 17 Janurary 2024. Patient was given Cardiopulmonary Cerebral Resuscitation (CPCR) by bystanders following which he complained of chest pain. He was taken to a tertiary care center in New Delhi. His presentation to emergency was vitally stable, free of chest pain. His ECG was suggestive of ST elevation in V1–V4. His Trop I was serially positive and rising. However, he was taken up for Cardiac Arrest Risk Triage with angiogram only on the next day which revealed occlusion of left anterior descending coronary artery (LAD) at the ostium. In view of patient's presentation out of window period (late presentation), a STRESS MIBI (Myocardial Perfusion Scan) and PET scan were performed, which showed scarred myocardium in LAD territory with no evidence of stress induced ischemia or hibernation in the myocardium. An echocardiogram demonstrated left ventricular ejection fraction between (25-30%). He remained hemodynamically stable throughout hospital stay and was discharged after 10 days for follow up on OPD basis (Table 1). He continued with the recommendations and had a follow-up after a month (on 28.3.24) with a repeat echocardiography which has shown no improvements in cardiac function (LVEF remained same at 25-30%). The patient had the complaints of severe prostration, weakness, fatigue and inability to do his routine chores. He decided to consult Ayurveda for any possibility of add on interventions for improving the chances of recovery. As he was not able to travel, he had an online consultation with an Ayurveda consultant. Following the taking up of history and screening of his ongoing course of clinical picture as per the available clinical records, he was recommended for a set of Ayurveda medicines which he started from 12 April 2024. The treatment consisted of chanting of ‘Aum’ two times a day besides ayurvedic oral medicines (Table 2). He continued to have allopathic medicine as recommended and had a follow up on 27 May 2024. Echocardiography done on the same day had shown improved cardiac function with LVEF now almost 40%. His clinical symptoms of fatigue and prostrations were also partially relieved. He had a repeat Ayurvedic follow up on 16 July 2024 and 22 August 2024 on which some ayurvedic medicines related to constipation, hemorrhoids and joint pain were added. He continued to have the medicines for another 4 months. A repeat follow-up and echocardiography (2 January 2025) has shown LVEF improved to 50%. He was clinically much improved this time and was able to do his routine chorus without any difficulty.

Follow-up and outcomes

The patient had been on regular follow ups at the Tertiary Care Center where he was taking the treatment. After initial inpatient management of immediate crisis he had the OP based follow-ups and had regular investigations to assess the cardiac functions (Table 1). While investigations suggested a better EF following add on Ayurveda interventions, the patient himself has observed improved functioning following the Ayurveda interventions.

Patient had a history of hemorrhoids also for which he asked for some Ayurveda intervention after getting some relief in his cardiac complaints. He was recommended for Kankayan Bati and Abhayarishta (16.7.24) which offered some relief in hemorrhoids. In addition to this he also had a history of joint pain for which he was recommended to have Arogyavardhini and Aam Vatari Rasa (22.8.24). These treatments have also been continued along with the recommended Ayurveda intervention for cardiac rehabilitation (Table 2). The interventions did not result in any adverse or unanticipated events during the course of the therapy.

Discussion

Patients who survive a myocardial infarction face severe consequences due to impaired cardiac function consequential to myocardial wall scarring. A reduced systemic circulation leads to symptoms of prostration and severe debility making the person unable to perform his routine works. Reducing the chances of recurrence of such events in future and improving the cardiac function are two important goal of therapy at this stage in patients who sustain myocardial damage as a result of STEMI and late presentation. Combining pharmacotherapy with cardiac rehabilitation as a multidisciplinary approach aims to achieve these goals with modern medicine. Ayurveda has few medicines which help in improved recovery of such patients through stabilizing the cardiac functions. The Ayurvedic add-on treatment recommended in this patient from fourth month consisted of Karvair yoga, Arjunarishta, Punarnava Mandur, and Prabhakar Bati which have specific roles in improving the cardiac function. They possibly help in improving the cardiac function by reducing the pre-load (Punarnava mandur) [3], improved contractility (Karvir yoga, Punarnava) [4], [5], negatively dronotropic (Prabhakar Bati) [6] and improved contractility (Arjunarishta) [7]. Besides this, chanting of AUM is expected to enhance parasympathetic nervous system activity, promote relaxation, and provide calmness [8] in addition to the psychological support which is part of satvavajaya chikitsa in ayurveda [9], [10].

In the absence of real control, it would however be difficult to reach at the inference that Ayurvedic treatment truly has helped in recovery of this patient. This is albeit sure that it has not adversely affected the situation and has helped the patient psychologically to perform better. In post MI cases, a high mortality (18%) has been reported at 5 year follow-up [11]. In post MI cases, a higher loss of life expectancy (LOLE) is observed in younger individuals, women, and those with reduced LVEF (<50%) [12]. This 46 year old male by all means had a high LOLE and his improved outcome in terms of his self-reported improvements in symptoms and verified through improved cardiac function may partially be attributed to Ayurveda add on interventions given to him. More of such case studies and clinical trials are however needed to endorse this observation authentically.

Conclusion

Acute coronary disease and its culmination as MI is one most important cause of death globally. Urgent treatment with follow-up pharmacotherapy and cardiac rehabilitation are recommended to improve patient outcome. In this case Ayurveda add on interventions along with ongoing standard medication contributed to the patient's symptom relief and improved cardiac function without any evident adversity. Considering the high mortality, recurrence and poor rehabilitation outcome in post MI cases, more studies in the area of integrative management of post MI cases with a possibility of add on ayurvedic intervention are highly suggested.

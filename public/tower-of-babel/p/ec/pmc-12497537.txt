Influence of Alignment Changes in Different Planes During Supra‐Tubercular Biplanar Medial Opening Wedge High Tibial Osteotomy on Tibial Tubercle–Trochlear Groove Distance
Authors: Lizhong Jing, Weiguo Wang, Yulian Ren, Yanrong Chen, Xia Zhang, Meifeng Shen, Jiushan Yang, Jian Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectiveChanges in the coronal, sagittal, and axial alignments during supra‐tubercular biplanar medial opening wedge high tibial osteotomy (STB‐MOWHTO) may have different influences on tibial tubercle–trochlear groove (TT–TG) distance, but few pieces of literature have addressed this specific concern. We aimed to analyze the impacts of changes in alignments in different planes during STB‐MOWHTO on the TT–TG distance.MethodsPatients who underwent STB‐MOWHTO for varus knee deformity at our hospital were reviewed retrospectively from January 2020 to December 2022. Radiographic parameters, including opening width (OW), distal tibial rotation (DTR), posterior tibial slope (PTS), and TT–TG distance, were assessed before operation and directly postoperatively. Multivariate mixed linear regression analyses were employed to investigate the relationships between OW, the changes in DTR, PTS, and TT–TG distance. Restricted cubic spline curve fitting and threshold effects were used to assess potential non‐linear relationships between the independent variables and changes in TT–TG distance. Stratification analysis was conducted to assess the stability of the results.ResultsOne hundred and two knee joints from 66 patients were included. TT–TG distance change was positively correlated with OW (β = 0.551, 95% CI: 0.340, 0.762, p < 0.001) and DTR change (β = 0.284, 95% CI: 0.196, 0.371, p < 0.001). Restricted cubic spline curve fitting and threshold effect analyses did not identify any non‐linear relationships or inflection points between OW, DTR change, and TT–TG distance change. Further, stratification analysis of DTR in various directions confirmed the stability of the linear relationship between DTR change and TT–TG distance change.ConclusionsThe TT–TG distance change exhibited a positively correlated linear relationship with OW and DTR change during STB‐MOWHTO. For every 1 mm increase in OW, TT–TG distance increased by an average of 0.551 mm. For every 1° increase in DTR, TT–TG distance increased by an average of 0.284 mm. Level of Evidence: Level III.

Introduction

Supra‐tubercular biplanar medial opening wedge high tibial osteotomy (STB‐MOWHTO) stands as the principal surgical intervention for active young patients afflicted with varus malalignment of the knee [1, 2]. This surgical strategy adeptly mitigates pressure and discomfort within the medial compartment of the knee joint, consequently enhancing knee functionality [3, 4].

Several scholars reported that tibial tubercle–trochlear groove (TT–TG) distance changed during STB‐MOWHTO [5, 6, 7, 8]. Cho [5] and Kim [7] observed an increase in the TT–TG distance, attributing it to valgus correction in the distal portion of the osteotomy, namely the opening width (OW) in the coronal plane. In contrast, Cheng [6] and Sim [8] observed a decrease in the TT–TG distance, suggesting it was associated with internal rotation of the distal segment of the osteotomy, which involved distal tibial rotation (DTR) in the axial plane. Nevertheless, the factors influencing these observations were speculative in the aforementioned studies, and the authors did not quantitatively assess the correlation between OW, DTR, and TT–TG distance. Hodel [9] reported a positive correlation between OW and TT–TG distance. However, this finding was based on 3D virtual single‐plane MOWHTO simulations, which may not fully replicate clinical conditions encountered during STB‐MOWHTO [10, 11]. In addition, there was no literature reporting the association between posterior tibial slope (PTS) in the sagittal plane and TT–TG distance in STB‐MOWHTO. Elevated TT–TG distance has an adverse effect on the patellofemoral joint [12, 13], potentially undermining surgical outcomes. As such, it is essential to determine the influencing factors and correlations related to the change in TT–TG distance caused by alignment changes in different planes during STB‐MOWHTO.

The purpose of this study was to qualitatively and quantitatively analyze the impact of changes in OW, DTR, and PTS during STB‐MOWHTO on the TT–TG distance. It was hypothesized that changes in OW and DTR would lead to alterations in TT–TG distance, whereas changes in PTS would not affect TT–TG distance.

Materials and Methods

Patients

From January 2020 to December 2022, patients who underwent STB‐MOWHTO for varus knee deformity at our hospital were reviewed retrospectively. Every patient met the following inclusion criteria: (i) symptomatic medial compartment of the knee joint; and (ii) varus malalignment over 4° between the tibial and femoral mechanical axis and medial proximal tibial angle (MPTA) less than 84° measured on a hip‐to‐ankle standing AP radiograph. All patients underwent pre‐operative and post‐operative assessments, including full‐length lower‐extremity CT, standing hip‐to‐ankle AP radiographs, and lateral radiographs. Patients exhibiting (i) flexion contracture exceeding 10°, (ii) a mechanical lateral distal femoral angle (mLDFA) > 91° in the deformity analysis, and (iii) severe varus malalignment warranting double level osteotomy were excluded. Ethical approval for the study was obtained from the Ethics Committee of our hospital (2021‐059‐KY). This research was conducted in a non‐interventional and retrospective manner, and written informed consent was obtained from every patient and/or their legal guardians. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The images utilized in the dataset were de‐identified to ensure anonymity.

Surgical Technique

A longitudinal incision about 5 cm was made between the tibial tuberosity and the posteromedial border of the tibia. The surface of the medial collateral ligament was distally released, with the upper border of the pes anserinus serving as the reference point for initiating the osteotomy. Two K‐wires were then positioned adjacent to the tibiofibular joint to delineate the location for the saw cut. The lateral 10 mm of the tibial cortex was left intact as a hinge for the osteotomy. Another separate ascending cut of the biplanar osteotomy was made 1.5 cm behind the tibial tuberosity in the frontal plane at an angle of 100–110 to the first osteotomy plane. The mechanical axis was positioned at a point 50%–75% lateral on the transverse mediolateral diameter of the tibial plateau. Subsequently, the TomoFix plate (Synthes, Oberdorf, Switzerland) was inserted into a subcutaneous tunnel along the medial aspect of the tibia and secured in position using eight locking screws [14] (Figure 1A).

Radiologic Evaluation

Radiologic parameters were evaluated using full‐length lower‐extremity CT, standing hip‐to‐ankle AP radiograph, and lateral radiograph preoperatively and within 1 week after surgery. DTR and TT–TG distance were obtained using the same CT scanner (GE Healthcare, Milwaukee WI, USA). The DTR was evaluated by measuring the angle between the transmalleolar axis and the proximal posterior tibial axis. The TT–TG distance was measured between two parallel lines passing through the midpoint of the tibial tuberosity and the lowest point of the femoral trochlea, with the trochlear line drawn perpendicular to a line connecting the posterior condyles of the femur on the plane at the apex of the femoral intercondylar fossa. The standing hip‐to‐ankle AP radiograph was utilized to evaluate MPTA and OW. PTS was defined as the angle between the vertical line of the posterior bone cortex of the proximal tibia and the tangent line to the medial tibial plateau on a lateral X‐ray view. DTR change, PTS change, and TT–TG distance change were calculated by subtracting preoperative values from postoperative values. Positive values of DTR change represent external rotation of the distal tibia in relation to the tibial tubercle, whereas negative values represent internal rotation. Positive values of PTS change correlated with increased slope, whereas negative values represent decreased slope. All the radiographic measurements were accomplished using INFINITT PACS (INFINITT Healthcare, Seoul, Korea) (Figure 1).

Statistical Analysis

Descriptive analysis was performed on all participants. Nominal variables are presented with frequencies (percentages), and numerical variables are presented with mean ± standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed variables. For variables where normality assumptions are not met, median with interquartile range (IQR) is provided. For normally distributed data, paired sample t‐tests were employed to compare the differences in indicators before and after surgery. For non‐normally distributed data, the Wilcoxon signed‐rank test was used.

Potential confounding factors affecting the TT–TG distance change, including BMI, age, gender, and operation side, were incorporated and analyzed in the present study. Moreover, given that the majority of subjects had bilateral joint involvement, the data were not independent. Linear mixed models were employed to assess the relationship between the dependent variable TT–TG distance change, and a random intercept model was added to the linear regression analysis to account for within‐individual correlations. Univariate mixed linear models were used to screen for independent variables associated with TT–TG distance change. All variables with p < 0.1 in the univariate analysis, along with OW, DTR change, and PTS change, were included in the multivariate mixed linear regression analysis. This analysis was conducted to determine the beta (β) values and 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) between OW, DTR change, PTS change, and TT–TG distance change. Variance inflation factors (VIF) were calculated between the variables to avoid multicollinearity among the independent variables.

In addition, multivariate‐adjusted restricted cubic spline regression [15] was conducted with four knots at the 5th, 35th, 65th, and 95th percentiles of each independent variable to explore potential non‐linear relationships and threshold effects between each independent variable and TT–TG distance change. A two‐stage linear regression model was employed to identify any potential inflection points.

Further, to assess the stability of the relationship between OW, DTR change, and TT–TG distance change across different degrees or directions, stratification analyses were conducted based on potential inflection points of OW and the direction of DTR change.

To minimize the errors caused by inter‐observer variability, all measurements were performed independently by two researchers who had received relevant training in advance (XZ and MFS). The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to assess the reliability of all results. All the analyses were performed with the statistical software packages SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA) and R 3.3.2. A two‐tailed test was performed, and a p‐value of 0.05 was used to establish significance.

Discussion

Main Findings of This Study

The main findings of this study were that the TT–TG distance change showed a positively correlated linear relationship with OW and DTR change during STB‐MOWHTO.

OW Can Affect TT–TG Distance During STB‐MOWHTO

Some researchers have expressed the belief that OW can affect TT–TG distance during STB‐MOWHTO [5, 7]. Cho [5] observed that the TT–TG distance increased by an average of 5.72 mm after STB‐MOWHTO, attributing it to the valgus correction of the distal portion of the osteotomy. Kim [7] noted a postoperative increase in TT–TG distance by an average of 2 mm after STB‐MOWHTO. They proposed that the lateral shift of the tibial tuberosity during valgus correction contributed to the elevation in TT–TG distance. However, the authors just performed an observational study and did not analyze the correlation between OW and TT–TG distance quantitatively. Hodel [9] found that the TT–TG distance increased by an average of 0.5 mm for every 1° valgization in the range from 0° to 15°. But it may not fully simulate clinical conditions that happened during STB‐MOWHTO because it was based on 3D virtual single‐plane MOWHTO [10, 11]. Consistent with Hodel's findings, a linear relationship between OW and TT–TG distance was also observed during STB‐MOWHTO, with an average increase of 0.551 mm in TT–TG distance for every 1 mm increase in OW in our study. Further, restricted cubic spline curve fitting and threshold effect analyses did not reveal any non‐linear relationships or inflection points between OW and TT–TG distance change, suggesting that the effect of OW on TT–TG distance change remains relatively stable.

DTR Can Affect TT–TG Distance During STB‐MOWHTO

In addition, several studies have reported that DTR can also affect TT–TG distance [6, 8]. Cheng [6] included 69 patients undergoing STB‐MOWHTO and found that the TT–TG distance decreased by an average of 3 mm postoperatively, with the authors attributing the main reason to the tibial tuberosity shift medially caused by the internal rotation of the distal part of the osteotomy. Similarly, Sim [8] found that the average TT–TG distance decreased by about 2 mm postoperatively, with the authors also speculating that it was related to the internal rotation of the distal part of the osteotomy. However, the conclusions in the aforementioned studies are speculative, and these authors did not verify the correlation between DTR and TT–TG distance quantitatively. The present study introduced the relationship between DTR and TT–TG distance dependent on the direction of DTR. The internal rotation of the distal part of the osteotomy can lead to a decrease in TT–TG distance, confirming the hypotheses of Cheng [6] and Sim [8]. Meanwhile, in the present study, the external rotation led to an increase in TT–TG distance. Further, a quantitative analysis of the correlation between DTR change and TT–TG distance change revealed a weak linear relationship. In the present study, the VIF between DTR change and other independent variables was 1.695, which ruled out error due to multicollinearity among DTR change and OW. This confirms the positive correlation between DTR change and TT–TG distance based on the rotation direction of the distal part of the osteotomy. Besides, the present study introduces the association between PTS and TT–TG distance in STB‐MOWHTO. It was determined that there was no correlation between these two variables.

Clinical Significance

Large TT–TG distance has an adverse effect on the patellofemoral joint [7, 12, 13, 16]. Otsuki [13] analyzed 85 patients with knee varus and found that those with a larger TT–TG distance exhibited more severe patellofemoral arthritis on CT scans. Kim [7] compared MOWHTO with lateral closed wedge HTO and found TT–TG distance increased significantly in the MOWHTO group. Further, during the 4‐year follow‐up, the proportion of patients with anterior knee pain was higher in the MOWHTO group. In addition, although there was no literature reporting that an increase in the TT–TG distance during MOWHTO leads to patellar instability, which may be related to the decrease in patellar height, several studies found elevated TT–TG distance was a key risk factor for the recurrence of lateral patellar dislocations [12, 17, 18]. Hence, deliberate control or decrease of TT–TG distance during MOWHTO could potentially minimize the adverse effect on the patellofemoral joint.

Since OW is primarily determined during preoperative planning, it is crucial to prevent external rotation of the distal part of the osteotomy to minimize its impact on TT–TG distance during STB‐MOWHTO. One strategy involves positioning the osteotomy hinge as anteriorly inclined as possible in the sagittal plane [19]. Additionally, minimizing intervention on the hamstring tendons—critical structures for maintaining inward rotation of the knee joint—can further help address this issue [20]. For patients with a relatively large TT–TG distance or severe patellofemoral arthritis, a lateral closed wedge high tibial osteotomy may be a preferable option. This approach can help reduce the TT–TG distance without affecting patellar height [7, 21]. Moreover, various authors have proposed other approaches to mitigate the impact of HTO on the patellofemoral joint [5, 22, 23]. Otsuki [23] suggests that a hybrid HTO may be a more appropriate choice for patients with concurrent patellofemoral arthritis. The authors found that the TT–TG distance significantly decreased from 11.4 to 7.4 mm. Cho [5] found that compared with STB‐MOWHTO, retro‐tubercular MOWHTO had a smaller impact on patellar height and TT–TG distance, making it more suitable for patients with combined patellofemoral arthritis.

The Strengths and Limitations

The strength of this study was that it represented the first exploration of the correlations between changes in alignment at different planes of STB‐MOWHTO and the TT–TG distance. The present study also had several limitations. Firstly, the study exclusively focused on the radiological aspects direct postoperatively, omitting long‐term follow‐up radiological assessments as well as clinical score evaluations. Consequently, this limited scope may prove inadequate in comprehensively representing sustainable changes. Secondly, the study had a relatively small sample size, potentially leading to errors in the results. Finally, due to its retrospective nature, the study did not include soft tissue factors, like the joint capsule, medial collateral ligament, and hamstring tendon.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the change in TT–TG distance exhibited a positively correlated linear relationship with both OW and DTR change during STB‐MOWHTO. For every 1 mm increase in OW, TT–TG distance increased by an average of 0.551 mm. For every 1° increase in DTR, TT–TG distance increased by an average of 0.284 mm.

Effects of nonlinear thermal radiation in bioconvective flow of a micropolar nanofluid containing gyrotactic microorganisms over an oscillating curved surface
Authors: Muhammad Naveed, Muhammad Imran, Ayesha Mubeen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research article presents a detailed analysis of nonlinear thermal radiation effects in the bioconvective flow of a micropolar nanofluid containing gyrotactic microorganisms over an oscillating curved surface. The formulation accounts for microstructural fluid behavior, Brownian motion, thermophoresis, and microorganism-induced bioconvection, providing a comprehensive framework for understanding transport phenomena in complex fluids. The governing partial differential equations are formulated using a curvilinear coordinate system to accurately capture the influence of surface curvature and oscillation. Nonlinear thermal radiation is incorporated into the energy equation to model realistic radiative heat transfer in high-temperature environments. The coupled system of equations governing momentum, angular momentum, energy, nanoparticle concentration, and microorganism density is reduced to a set of nonlinear ordinary differential equations via similarity transformations. The Homotopy Analysis Method (HAM) is employed to derive analytical solutions, offering adjustable convergence control and high accuracy. Graphical analysis reveals that the microorganism concentration profile decreases with increasing values of the Peclet number, microorganism difference parameter, bioconvection Lewis number, and curvature parameter. Conversely, the local motile density number increases with all the aforementioned parameters. From the tabulated data, it is noticed that the local Nusselt Number magnitude inclines up to 8% with developing values of the dimensionless ratio parameter, while for the Brownian parameter, the y Nusselt number magnitude drops bapproximately 0.05%. The magnitude of the local Motile density number grows up to 1.2% and 5.7% against the microorganism difference parameter and Peclet number, respectively. These findings enhance the understanding of microscale fluid dynamics and have promising applications in bio-microsystems, thermal regulation, and nanotechnology-based fluid processing.Keywords: Bioconvection, Oscillatory curved stretched surface, Micropolar fluid, Nonlinear thermal radiation, Thermophoretic and Brownian diffusions, Gyrotactic microorganisms

Introduction

Non-Newtonian fluids are fluids characterized by their non-linear stress–strain relationship, exhibiting complex rheological behavior that significantly differs from classical Newtonian fluids. These fluids include polymer solutions, molten plastics, slurries, and biological fluids, and display phenomena such as shear thinning, shear thickening, and viscoelasticity, making their analysis crucial in many industrial and biomedical applications. Among the various classes of non-Newtonian models, micropolar fluids represent a distinct category that accounts for the microstructure and microrotation effects of fluid particles, thereby capturing the influence of suspended rigid or semi-rigid particles. This theory, originally proposed by Eringen [1], introduces additional rotational degrees of freedom and couple stresses, providing a more comprehensive framework for depicting the flow behavior of complex fluids. Some biological fluids, for example synovial fluid found in human joints show micropolar behavior due to its microstructures that enhances the lubrication and reduces the friction in the joints, which contributing to smoother joint motion. Furthermore, the blood, particularly in microcirculatory systems acts as a micropolar fluid due to the presence of particles and cells inside the plasma. The concept of micropolar fluid helps to model the blood flow in situations where convectional fluids models prove inadequate. The study of micropolar fluid motion over curved surfaces has gained considerable attention due to its relevance in modeling practical systems such as flow through curved pipes, blood flow in arteries, and lubrication in rotating machinery. Shabbir et al. [2] conducted a numerical study for micropolar fluid over a curved sheet in the presence of Joule heating effects. Naveed et al. [3] analyzed the effects of heat transfer in the curvilinear flow of micropolar fluid over a curvy oscillating sheet. Ahsan et al. [4] scrutinized the significance of HH chemical reactions on the thermally radiative flow of micropolar fluid past a curved wall. Computational analysis for the flow of micropolar fluid towards a curved sheet with viscous dissipation was accomplished by Vaishnav et al. [5]. Khan et al. [6] investigated the slip flow of a micropolar nanofluid containing gyrotactic microorganisms, with nonlinear thermal radiation and activation-energy effects over a rotating disk. Inspection of bioconvective flow of micropolar fluid in the existence of nonlinear radiation past a stretched sheet was completed by Khan et al. [7].

Nanofluids are a class of advanced heat transfer fluids that are engineered by diffusing nanosized solid particles such as metals (e.g., Cu, Ag), carbon-based materials (e.g., graphene, CNTs), or metal oxides (e.g., Al₂O₃, TiO₂) into conventional base fluids like water, ethylene glycol, or oil. Due to their superior thermal conductivity, stability, and enhanced convective heat transference features, nanofluids have emerged as promising candidates for a wide range of industrial and technological applications, including nuclear reactors, microelectronics cooling, solar energy systems, and biomedical devices. The enhanced thermal performance of nanofluids is largely attributed to microscale transport mechanisms such as Brownian motion, i.e., the random movement of nanoparticles that intensifies energy exchange, and thermophoresis, which causes nanoparticles to migrate along temperature gradients. These mechanisms contribute to modified flow behavior, improved thermal diffusion, and greater temperature control in complex geometries and under varying thermal conditions. Consequently, nanofluids offer an efficient approach to overcoming the limitations of traditional heat transfer fluids, providing new opportunities for the development of high-performance thermal systems. A remarkable number of scholars have paid special attention to detecting the features of nanofluids. Abbas et al. [8] have conducted a study on the significance of thermal radiation in the time-dependent flow of Casson nanoliquid across an oscillating stretched wall. Thermophoresis and Brownian motion impacts on a bioconvective flow of micropolar nanoliquid between disks were determined by Shahzad et al. [9]. Imran and Naveed [10] analyzed the effects of thermophoresis and Brownian diffusions in a Carreau nanofluid with activation energy past a curved stretching wall. Ali et al. [11] evaluated the influence of thermophoretic and Brownian motion on Maxwell nanofluids utilizing an improved form of Fourier’s and Fick’s theory. Sankaralingam et al. [12] considered Heat and mass transfer analysis on magnetized flow of Powell-Eyring nanofluid under the influence of Brownian motion and thermophoresis effects past a stretched surface. Furthermore, more comprehensive studies regarding nanofluid flow over different geometries can be found in Refs. [13–18].

Nonlinear thermal radiation plays a noteworthy role in high-temperature heat transfer procedures where the classical linear approximation of radiative heat flux becomes inadequate. Unlike linear models, which assume a direct proportionality between radiative heat flux and temperature difference, nonlinear radiation accounts consider the temperature-dependent nature of thermal emission, often modeled through the Rosseland approximation. This leads to a radiative heat flux proportional to the fourth power of temperature, introducing nonlinearity into the energy equation. Incorporating nonlinear radiation is essential for accurately analyzing thermal behavior in various engineering areas such as aerospace structures, nuclear reactors, and thermal processing systems. In complex fluid flows involving nanofluids, the inclusion of nonlinear radiation significantly influences the temperature distribution, improves heat transport, and alters the thermal boundary layer characteristics. Azam et al. [19] scrutinized the impacts of nonlinear radiation and activation energy in the bioconvective motion of Cross-nanomaterial. The consequence of nonlinear radiation and convective heat transport on the stretching flow of nanofluid was presented by Zeeshan et al. [20]. The effects of nonlinear radiation in hybrid nanoliquid motion towards a curved wall with thermal slip were considered by Imran et al. [21]. Sulochana et al. [22] organized a research to evaluate the impacts of nonlinear radiation and activation energy in a bioconvective flow of non-Newtonian nanofluid across a sheet. Anjum et al. [23] analyzed the features of nonlinear radiation and heat production in a hybrid nanofluid past a stretchy sheet.

The analysis of heat and mass transfer in fluid flows becomes significantly more complex and realistic when both convective heat transfer and chemical reactions are considered. Convective heating at the surface introduces a thermal boundary condition where the heat transfer is governed by a heat transfer coefficient, leading to enhanced thermal energy exchange between the surface and the fluid. Simultaneously, the presence of a first-order chemical reaction affects the mass diffusion process, where the rate of reaction is directly proportional to the concentration of the diffusing species. This type of reaction is frequently encountered in chemical reactors, biological systems, and pollutant dispersion models. The interplay between convective heating and reactive mass transport leads to a coupled system of nonlinear partial differential equations, influencing velocity, temperature, and concentration fields. Incorporating these effects is essential for accurately predicting transport phenomena in industrial processes such as catalytic reactors, combustion systems, and environmental pollutant control. Waqas et al. [24] observed numerical computations of convective heat transfer in thermally radiative flow due to a permeable stretchy curved sheet. Naveed et al. [25] performed an analysis of heat and mass transfer along with effects of convective heat transport in a couple stress fluid over an oscillatory stretchy surface. Analysis of first-order chemical reaction in mixed convective flow of Sutterby fluid with Cattaneo-Christov heat and mass flux idea was performed by Khan et al. [26]. Shah et al. [27] completed a study to detect the significance of first-order chemical reaction in the magnetized flow of Carreau fluid past a convectively heated sheet. Krishna and Kumar [28] analyzed the time-dependent flow of Jeffrey nanofluid with first-order chemical reaction across a stretchy sheet. Naveed et al. [29] calculated the effects of first-order chemical reaction on bioconvective flow hybrid nanofluid past a stretchy oscillating wall.

Bioconvective flow refers to the self-organized convective motion in a fluid encouraged by the collective behavior of motile microorganisms, such as gyrotactic algae or bacteria, which typically swim upward against gravity. This upward movement causes an unstable density gradient, as microorganisms accumulate near the top, leading to gravitational instability and the onset of convection. The resulting bioconvection significantly enhances the mixing and transport of heat, mass, and nutrients in the fluid. This phenomenon plays a vital role in several industrial and biological applications, including bioreactors, biofuel production, wastewater treatment, and the design of microfluidic devices. Hayat et al. [30] analyzed the effects of gyrotactic microorganisms and bioconvection on Prandtl-Eyring flow across a stretchy sheet. Significance of bioconvection in EMHD hybrid nanofluid via a permeable sheet was determined by Rashed et al. [31]. Hamid et al. [32] performed a computational study for bioconvective flow of nanofluid towards a bidirectional sheet comprising microorganisms in a permeable medium. Sarma and Sarma [33] organized a numerical study to determine the influence of microorganisms in bioconvective and unsteady movement of Casson fluid past a vertical surface. Imran et al. [34] studied the significance of Cattaneo-Christov double diffusion in the bioconvective flow of nanofluid with gyrotactic microorganisms via a curved oscillatory channel.

The boundary layer flow over flat and curved oscillating stretching surfaces has become a key research area due to its wide-ranging and significant applications in various industrial and engineering processes. These include, but are not limited to, metallurgy, paper manufacturing, oil recovery, polymer extrusion, and glass molding, where controlling fluid motion and heat transfer near surfaces is vital for product quality and process effectiveness. The stretching or oscillatory movement of surfaces introduces complex fluid dynamic phenomena, affecting shear stress, temperature distribution, and mass transfer. Additionally, when such flows are modeled considering extra physical effects—such as thermal radiation, magnetic fields, or non-Newtonian fluids—the models better represent real-world processes. Therefore, studying these flows under different geometrical and physical conditions is crucial for optimizing industrial processes and advancing fluid mechanics theory. Alabdan et al. [35] checked the features of time-dependent viscosity in the bioconvective motion of couple stress nano liquid over an oscillatory surface. Imran et al. [36] considered analytical research to evaluate the impacts of heat and mass transfer via a modified heat and mass flux model on curvilinear flow of Williamson liquid motion due to a curved oscillating stretchable wall. Ghachem et al. [37] reported viscoelastic nanofluid across an oscillatory stretched sheet and computed the consequences of thermophoresis and Brownian motion. Stirring of homogeneous and heterogeneous chemical reactions in the oscillatory flow of hybrid nanofluid across a stretched curved sheet with gyrotactic microorganisms was determined by Naveed et al. [38].

Despite extensive efforts in modeling non-Newtonian and nanofluid flows over various geometries, the combined impact of micropolar behavior, nonlinear thermal radiation, gyrotactic microorganism-induced bioconvection, and nanoscale transport mechanisms, such as Brownian motion and thermophoresis over an oscillating curved surface, remains largely unexplored yet in the available literature. Most existing studies are restricted to flat or simple geometries and mainly focus on individual phenomena, such as heat transfer in micropolar fluids, bioconvective motion in nanofluids, or nonlinear radiation effects. However, practical engineering systems often involve complex curvature, surface oscillations, and interacting thermal and mass transport processes in bio-conductive media. The novelty of this study lies in presenting a comprehensive mathematical framework that integrates these multifaceted physical mechanisms within a single model over a curved oscillatory surface. The consideration of first-order chemical reaction and convective thermal boundary condition further distinguishes this work from prior research. To the best of the authors' knowledge, this is the first investigation to analyze the combined effects of nonlinear radiation, micropolarity, gyrotactic microorganism activity, and nanoparticle dynamics in a bioconvective flow over a curved oscillatory surface. The results of this study contribute significantly to the theoretical understanding of such flows and offer valuable insights for applications in bio-microfluidics, thermal control systems, biomedical devices, and micro-scale heat and mass transfer technologies.

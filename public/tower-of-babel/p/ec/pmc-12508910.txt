Ferroptosis and organelle roles in dry eye
Authors: Licheng Li, Baihua Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryDry eye, an ocular surface disorder, has become increasingly prevalent over the past few years due to air pollution and stress in daily life. Emerging studies on dry eye in both cellular and animal models indicate changes in ferroptosis-related markers. Furthermore, mitochondrial dysfunction, endoplasmic reticulum stress, and lysosomal abnormalities are involved in dry eye. Targeting ferroptosis and improving organelle function have made considerable progress in the treatment of dry eye. This review summarizes the evidence for ferroptosis and organelle damage in the ocular surface, elucidates their roles in dry eye pathogenesis, and discusses the interplay among ferroptosis, organelle dysfunction, and dry eye. Finally, it highlights targeted therapeutic strategies for mitigating dry eye symptoms.Subject areas: Ophthalmology, Biochemistry

Introduction

Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent form of cell death characterized by the excessive accumulation of lipid peroxides on cellular membranes.1 The core triggers of ferroptosis include dysregulated iron metabolism, collapse of the antioxidant system, and lipid metabolic imbalance. The hallmark of ferroptosis is mitochondrial shrinkage and cristae loss as observed under electron microscopy.2 Ferroptosis is a highly coordinated process involving multiple organelles. For instance, researchers have shown that ferroptosis is mediated by the mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS)-autophagy-lysosomal pathway and endoplasmic reticulum stress.3,4 This process has attracted considerable attention for its role in neurodegenerative diseases, cancer, and ischemia-reperfusion injury.5,6,7

Dry eye is a multifactorial disorder of the ocular surface marked by a loss of homeostasis of the tear film, resulting in discomfort, visual disturbance, and damage to the ocular surface.8 Environmental issues and a surge in life stress contribute to a significant rise in dry eye prevalence.9,10 The global prevalence of dry eye is estimated at 29.5%, with the prevalence among children reaching up to 23.7%.11,12 Dry eye contributes to ocular discomfort and is strongly associated with depression and anxiety, leading to the impaired quality of life and increased societal healthcare burden.13,14,15 Therefore, a thorough understanding of the pathogenesis of dry eye is particularly important. The pathogenesis of dry eye involves organelle dysfunction, particularly the impairment of mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, and lysosomes. Mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and ferroptosis have emerged as key pathophysiological contributors.3,16

This review examines the latest insights regarding ferroptosis and organelle functions to the ocular surface, emphasizing the significance of ferroptosis in the pathophysiology of dry eye. With the rising incidence of dry eye and its established associations with ferroptosis, this subject is of paramount importance for both research advancement and clinical application. By elucidating these interrelationships, we aim to guide future research and therapeutic strategies targeting ferroptosis and organelle function, ultimately improving treatment efficacy and patient quality of life.

Evidence of ferroptosis in ocular surface cells

The ocular surface is highly susceptible to oxidative stress due to environmental factors and the inherent exposure to air.17 The vulnerability of the ocular surface raises the question of whether its cells undergo ferroptosis in response to external stimuli.

RNA sequencing of hyperosmolality-induced human corneal epithelial cells (CECs) reveals significant changes in the ferroptosis pathway.18 In a hypoxic environment, the regulation of cell death appears to involve the interaction between ferroptosis and PANoptosis, characterized by increased lipid peroxidation and mitochondrial dysfunction in human CECs.19 Exposure to cigarette smoke extract (CSE) disrupted tight junction formation in human CECs, leading to increased cell volume and decreased transepithelial electrical resistance, alongside lipid peroxidation and Fe2+ accumulation in autolysosomal compartments. Exposure to heated tobacco products resulted in the death of human CECs and the accumulation of cleaved ferritin.20 These studies demonstrate that human CECs undergo characteristic changes of ferroptosis when exposed to various stressors, including hyperosmolality, hypoxia, and components of cigarette smoke. Recent research has further identified the specific molecular pathway underlying ferroptosis in human CECs. For example, CSE exposure increases intracellular Fe2+ via transferrin receptor (TFRC) upregulation and promotes lipid peroxidation through acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4) induction, both driven by yes-associated protein (YAP) transcriptional activation.21

In addition to CECs, conjunctival epithelial cells (CjECs) are also implicated in ferroptosis. Exposure of human CjECs to blue light induces the downregulation of solute carrier family 7-member 11 (SLC7A11), ferritin heavy chain (FTH1), and glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) by inhibiting the activation and translocation of signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3), leading to Fe2+ burst, ROS accumulation, and lipid peroxidation, ultimately resulting in ferroptosis.22 Although ferroptosis has been increasingly studied in CECs and CjECs (Figure 1), its occurrence in lacrimal and meibomian gland cells remains scarcely reported, likely due to the limited availability of corresponding cell lines. Given that the dysfunction of these glands is a major contributor to dry eye,23,24 overcoming current experimental limitations is essential for a more comprehensive understanding of ferroptosis in dry eye.

Correlation between ferroptosis and dry eye in animal models

In a mouse model of dry eye induced by subcutaneous scopolamine administration followed by desiccating stress, researchers detected significant alterations in ferroptosis-related protein levels in the corneas.25,26 Dai et al. induced dry eye in Wistar rats by applying 0.2% benzalkonium chloride, which increased lipid hydroperoxides and ACSL4 expression in the corneas.27 C57BL/6 mice were exposed to blue light for 4 weeks, which resulted in tear film instability, conjunctival epithelial damage, and changes in mitochondrial structure. Meanwhile, blue light exposure led to the downregulation of SLC7A11, FTH1, and GPX4 levels in conjunctiva.22 Unilateral corneal nerve transection in BALB/c mice contributed to bilateral tear secretion reduction, the upregulation of Fe2+ and prostaglandin-endoperoxide synthase 2 (Ptgs2) mRNA, decreased vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP) levels, and increased expression of hypoxia-inducible factor 1 alpha and TFRC1 in extraorbital lacrimal glands.28

Researchers found ferroptosis in the cornea, conjunctiva, and lacrimal glands of various animal models of dry eye (Table 1). However, no studies have reported ferroptosis in the meibomian glands. Currently, studies on the mechanisms linking ferroptosis and dry eye remain largely at the basic research stage, likely due to the limited availability of clinical samples. Multiple clinical studies have demonstrated that the salivary glands of patients with the Sjögren’s syndrome exhibit aberrant alterations in molecules related to ferroptosis, such as GPX4, ATG5, and TBK1.33,34,35 Plasma exosomes from patients with Sjögren’s syndrome contain ferroptosis-related proteins.36 Ferroptosis in salivary gland epithelial cells is modulated by GPX4/pSTAT4/AQP5, ERα/ATF3/SLC7A11, Xist/ACSL4, and JAK/STAT1/system Xc-.33,37,38,39 Given the systemic pathological features of Sjögren’s syndrome, there is potential to draw valuable insights for dry eye from these advancements. Furthermore, a small case report documented significant reductions in mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, and Golgi apparatus in the lacrimal glands of patients with severe dry eye following Stevens-Johnson syndrome, underscoring the need to investigate the role of these organelles in dry eye.40

Role of organelles in dry eye

Mitochondria

Mitochondria are double-membrane organelles that not only drive cellular energy production through oxidative phosphorylation but also regulate apoptosis, calcium homeostasis, and ROS generation.41

When human CECs were exposed to tert-butyl hydroperoxide (tBHP), Martinez-Carrasco et al. observed damage to both the cells and their transcellular barriers, accompanied by increased opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP).42 In human CECs subjected to 450 and 500 mOsM, hyperosmolar stress resulted in modifications to metabolic activity, disturbances in mitochondrial calcium levels, the depolarization of mitochondria, and reductions in both ATP and mtDNA levels.43 Moreover, hyperosmolar stress triggers mitophagy in human CECs via the distinct regulation of BNIP3L/NIX and PINK1-mediated pathways, without dependence on p62.44 In human CECs, ultraviolet irradiation led to decreased oxygen consumption rates, mitochondrial fragmentation, and increased ROS production, resulting in impaired cell polarity, barrier function, and reduced apical mucin 16 expression.45 Particulate matter 2.5 (PM2.5) elevated mitochondrial ROS levels, causing mtDNA damage in primary rat CECs, whereas a ROS scavenger markedly mitigated these effects.46 These findings indicate that mitochondrial ROS accumulation crucially mediates cellular damage. ROS act as key mediators of oxidative stress, which represents a common pathogenic mechanism in dry eye. In addition to environmental factors, endocrine disorders are also important contributors to dry eye. In diabetic dry eye mice, lacrimal glands exhibit more pronounced mitochondrial bioenergetic impairments than the corneal epithelium, marked by mtDNA damage, reduced copy number, and decreased glutathione levels.47 Schicht et al. found swollen mitochondria in trigeminal ganglion neurons of mice with early-onset diabetes and streptozotocin-induced peripheral polyneuropathy, indicating that diabetes-related ocular surface lesions involve neurogenic mitochondrial structural and functional alterations.48

Endoplasmic reticulum

Endoplasmic reticulum is a highly organized membranous organelle found in eukaryotic cells, serving a vital function in the synthesis, folding, modification, and transport of proteins and lipids.49 Endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria establish a dynamic interplay, coordinating calcium signaling, lipid metabolism, and protein trafficking to sustain cellular homeostasis, adapt to metabolic demands, and regulate physiological and pathological processes.50

Exposure to trans, trans-2,4-decadienal (tt-DDE), a component of cooking oil fumes, reduced cell viability, altered morphology, and increased apoptosis in human CECs. Furthermore, tt-DDE exposure activated endoplasmic reticulum stress by upregulating endoplasmic reticulum stress-related proteins (CHOP, XBP1, ATF4, and BiP), while pretreatment with endoplasmic reticulum stress inhibitors significantly mitigated tt-DDE-induced oxidative stress.51 Beyond cell models, numerous animal studies have also demonstrated a strong association between dry eye and endoplasmic reticulum stress. In one- and six-month-old AQP5−/− mice, there was a notable 50% reduction in the production of tears compared to AQP5+/+ mice, with endoplasmic reticulum structural damage and a significant increase in endoplasmic reticulum stress in the lacrimal glands.52 Single-cell RNA sequencing analysis revealed increased levels of the endoplasmic reticulum stress marker GRP78 and activation of the PERK-CHOP pathway of the unfolded protein response (UPR) in a scopolamine-induced dry eye mouse model.53 Sleep deprivation in a “stick over water” mouse model induces lipid accumulation, hypertrophy, and dysfunction in the lacrimal gland, leading to decreased tear production, accompanied by ultrastructural alterations such as dilated endoplasmic reticulum cisternae and swollen mitochondria in acinar cells.54 In mice with environmentally induced dry eye, expression of endoplasmic reticulum stress-related genes and proteins was increased in trigeminal ganglion corneal neurons, and endoplasmic reticulum stress inhibitors mitigated the related neuronal damage.55 From cell models to animal studies, an increasing number of investigations have highlighted the significant role of endoplasmic reticulum stress in dry eye.

Lysosomes

Lysosomes are organelles surrounded by membranes, housing hydrolytic enzymes crucial for breaking down and recycling biomolecules such as proteins, lipids, and carbohydrates.56 There was a reduction in neural innervation, intracellular Ca2+ levels, and lysosomal quantity in the lacrimal glands of the TSP-1−/− mouse model of dry eye.57 In human CECs exposed to hyperosmotic conditions, Zuo et al. observed increased lipophagy, as evidenced by the co-localization of lysosomes with lipid droplets and the presence of autophagic vacuoles containing lipid droplets.29

Overall, organelle abnormalities are closely related to dry eye, but current research mainly focuses on mitochondria and endoplasmic reticulum, with less on lysosomes (Figure 2).

Organelle disorders in dry eye

When ocular surface cells are exposed to dry eye risk factors, cellular organelles undergo structural and functional impairments. Mitochondria exhibit swelling, rupture, and depolarization, accompanied by extensive mPTP opening. There is a decrease in mitochondrial Ca2+, ATP, and mtDNA levels, alongside a decline in mitochondrial oxygen uptake capacity, and a sharp increase in mitochondrial ROS levels. Mitochondrial autophagy mediated by BNIP3L/NIX and PINK1 is initiated. Endoplasmic reticulum cisternae dilate, leading to a significant rise in endoplasmic reticulum stress characterized by the upregulation of stress-related proteins (BiP/GRP78, GRP94, XBP1, CHOP, and ATF4) and activation of the PERK-CHOP pathway of the UPR. Lysosomes show anomalies, including reduced count and increased engulfment of LDs. These result in impaired cell polarity, barrier function, reduced apical mucin 16 expression, decreased cell viability, increased apoptosis and oxidative stress, and diminished neural innervation. Interrelationship between ferroptosis and organelle dysfunction in dry eye

Key characteristics of ferroptosis encompass a reduction or disappearance of mitochondrial cristae, rupture of the outer mitochondrial membrane, and mitochondrial shrinkage.22,25 Changes in mitochondrial structure are usually accompanied by dysfunction in their physiological activities. For instance, dysfunctional mitochondria enhance the production of ROS, which initiates lipid peroxidation and promotes ferroptotic signaling.58 Current genetic or pharmacological epistasis studies suggest that mitochondrial dysfunction can both contribute to and result from ferroptotic processes, but the causal hierarchy appears context-dependent. For example, under cysteine deprivation, cells exhibit marked the hyperpolarization of the mitochondrial membrane potential accompanied by lipid peroxide accumulation. Inhibition of the mitochondrial tricarboxylic acid cycle or electron transport chain attenuates these changes and suppresses ferroptotic cell death.59 These findings indicate that mitochondrial metabolism functions upstream of ferroptosis. Conversely, ferroptotic stimuli, especially GPX4 inhibition, directly cause mitochondrial dysfunction manifested as membrane potential disruption, morphological damage, and the accumulation of mitochondrial ROS and lipid peroxides, which can be attenuated by mitochondria-targeted antioxidants or VDAC inhibition, supporting mitochondria as a downstream effector of ferroptosis.60

In hyperosmolarity-exposed human CECs, mtDNA release via mPTP activates the cGAS-STING pathway, triggering inflammatory cytokine production.61 This cascade promotes ocular surface inflammation, disrupts tear film stability, and worsens epithelial damage in benzalkonium chloride-treated and lacrimal gland-excised mouse models.61 Moreover, oxidative stress related to ferroptosis and organelle dysfunction stimulates the activation of NF-κB and MAPK pathways, further amplifying inflammation.62,63,64 The chronic inflammatory state in dry eye contributes to sustained tissue damage and impairs ocular surface repair mechanisms. Inflammatory cells such as macrophages and neutrophils aggravate ferroptosis through the secretion of pro-oxidant factors, including myeloperoxidase and nitric oxide, creating a self-perpetuating cycle of oxidative stress, cell death, and inflammation.65,66 The combined effects of ferroptosis and organelle dysfunction lead to the loss of epithelial cells in the ocular surface. The resultant cell death impairs the integrity of the tear film and contributes to the symptoms of dryness and discomfort.

The interrelationship between ferroptosis and organelle dysfunction in dry eye underscores the complexity of its pathophysiology (Figure 3). Understanding these interactions may open new avenues for therapeutic interventions.

Therapeutic targets stemming from ferroptosis and organelle functions

Strategies to mitigate ferroptosis in dry eye

Considering the involvement of ferroptosis in oxidative damage and inflammation of ocular surface cells, effective strategies to mitigate ferroptosis may offer novel therapeutic insights for the management of dry eye.

Recent studies have demonstrated that Ferrostatin-1 significantly mitigates ferroptosis in blue light-induced dry eye models.22 In an experiment involving human CECs cultured under hyperosmolar conditions, the activation of nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 led to the upregulation of aldo-keto reductase family 1-member c1 (AKR1C1).25 Overexpression of AKR1C1 resulted in increased cell viability and decreased lipid peroxidation, while its knockdown yielded the opposite effects. Moreover, in the dry eye mouse model, the inhibition of AKR1C1 exacerbated corneal defects and inflammatory responses.25 The above results suggest that AKR1C1 functions as a conditional protective factor, regulating the response of human CECs to external injuries by reducing ferroptosis. Autologous serum at 20% and 50% concentrations from patients with Sjögren’s syndrome significantly promoted cell viability and migration, attenuated ROS and Fe2+ accumulation, and mitigated mitochondrial injury in hyperosmolar-induced human CECs. Both concentrations suppressed ferroptosis via the Xc/GPX4 pathway.67 Knockdown of autophagy-related sequestosome 1 mitigated ferroptosis in desiccating stress-induced human CECs, as evidenced by decreased 4-HNE and TFRC levels and increased GPX4 expression. Desiccating stress was induced by completely removing the culture medium and exposing human CECs to air at 25°C and 40% relative humidity for 10 min.26 Another research found that supplementation with VIP attenuated ferroptosis, reduced inflammation, and enhanced the maturation and release of secretory vesicles, ultimately improving tear production.28 In a dry eye model created in C57BL/6 mice using scopolamine injection, the suppression of Atg5 markedly increased corneal integrity and decreased both ferroptosis and inflammation of the ocular surface.29 Rosiglitazone, a selective inhibitor of ACSL4, was delivered subperitoneally at a dose of 5 mg/kg twice daily for 7 consecutive days, leading to a reduction in corneal lesion size and an enhancement of tear production in scopolamine-induced dry eye.26 Current research has not only reported the effects of targeted therapies for ferroptosis but also elucidated several specific molecular mechanisms involved. For example, the selective inhibitor of discoidin domain receptor-1 protects CECs from YAP/ACSL4-mediated ferroptosis in a dry eye model induced by 0.2% benzalkonium chloride in Wistar rats.27 Overexpression of miR-214-3p attenuated lipopolysaccharide-induced M1 polarization and inflammation by downregulating TFRC, thereby reducing macrophage ferroptosis and mitigating dry eye progression.68

Recently, some natural compounds have also been found to possess anti-ferroptotic effects in dry eye. For instance, in hyperosmolarity-induced human CECs, astaxanthin elevated ferritin expression to facilitate iron storage and reduce Fe2+ overload, augmented levels of glutathione and GPX4 to eliminate ROS and lipid peroxides, maintained mitochondrial integrity, and ultimately inhibited ferroptosis through the SLC7A11/GPX4 signaling.31 Qingxuan Run Mu Yin relieved dry eye symptoms by suppressing ferroptosis via the inhibition of the HMOX1/HIF-1 pathway.32 Due to the potential adverse effects associated with prolonged use of traditional treatments, improved drug delivery systems may mitigate these shortcomings. Catechol and β-cyclodextrin were incorporated into poly (acrylic acid) to construct a novel PAA-CD-DA drug delivery system, designed to improve ocular bioadhesion and enhance drug penetration. Compared to treatment with D609 alone, PAA-CD-DA@D609 exhibited superior efficacy in alleviating corneal epithelial ferroptosis and inflammation, and in reversing the loss of conjunctival goblet cells in scopolamine-induced dry eye mice.30 Nanomaterial-based strategies have been successfully employed to improve drug therapeutic efficacy. The CF@SNPs, a sialic acid-targeting peptide-modified liposome loaded with Cyclosporine A and Ferrostatin-1, effectively combines and sustains the treatment of dry eye by enhancing aqueous solubility and prolonging ocular surface retention, leading to significant improvements in corneal defects, goblet cells of conjunctiva, and tear secretion.69

Addressing ferroptosis in dry eye necessitates a comprehensive strategy targeting oxidative stress, lipid peroxidation, inflammation, and cellular repair mechanisms, which may unveil new therapeutic options (Table 2).

Organelle-targeted therapies to enhance cellular resilience

Mitochondrial protection and oxidative stress regulation

Multiple cell-based studies have identified that certain drugs can protect CECs from mitochondrial damage. In human CECs exposed to tBHP, Dynasore protects mitochondria from oxidative stress-induced damage by preventing mPTP opening, independent of the dynamin-related protein 1.42 Recent studies indicate that the ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 enhances intercellular mitochondrial transport efficiency, promoting the regeneration of human CECs in hyperosmolarity-induced cell models.70 Insulin-like growth factor binding protein-3 (IGFBP-3) treatment prompted an initial, transient translocation of IGF-1R to the mitochondria, subsequently leading to IGFBP-3 accumulation in the nucleus. This nuclear accumulation prevented the hyperosmolarity-induced reduction in oxidative phosphorylation by promoting mitochondrial fusion in human CECs and CjECs.71

Treatment with the mitochondrial-targeted antioxidant, namely Visomitin (SkQ1), significantly mitigated the severity of dry eye and enhanced mitochondrial function in diabetic mice.47 A novel SkQ1 nanoparticle (SkQ1 NP) significantly enhanced ROS scavenging activity in human CECs compared to free SkQ1, leading to improved protection against H2O2-induced cell death and reduced mtDNA oxidation, while simultaneously inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activation and related inflammatory signaling in the context of dry eye.72 The liposomal nanosystem PHP-DPS@INS utilizes SS-31 for mitochondrial targeting and antioxidation, while delivering insulin to attenuate mitochondrial inflammatory metabolites, thereby exerting strong anti-inflammatory effects. In a 0.2% benzalkonium chloride-induced dry eye mouse model, it promoted tear secretion, ameliorated ocular surface injury, and inhibited inflammatory responses.73 In addition to pharmacological treatments, intense pulsed light treatment reduced apoptosis and oxidative stress in ApoE−/− mice compared to untreated controls, concurrently enhancing the mitochondrial structure in the meibomian glands.74

Endoplasmic reticulum stress modulation

Dynasore protects mitochondria from oxidative stress and human CECs from hyperosmotic stress by mitigating endoplasmic reticulum stress and maintaining UPR homeostasis, highlighting the therapeutic potential of targeting both organelles in dry eye.42,75 Oral administration of A. koraiensis extract alleviated dry eye symptoms and suppressed endoplasmic reticulum stress in scopolamine-induced mice.76 Treatment with Palmitoylethanolamide successfully reestablished local lipid metabolism homeostasis in the lacrimal glands of mice subjected to sleep deprivation-induced dry eye, preserving endoplasmic reticulum morphology.54

Organelle-targeted therapies represent a promising approach to enhancing cellular resilience in dry eye (Table 3). By protecting mitochondria and reducing endoplasmic reticulum stress, these strategies may mitigate ferroptosis-driven damage, thereby improving ocular surface health and alleviating dry eye symptoms.

Concluding remarks and future perspectives

Emerging evidence suggests that ferroptosis occurs in ocular surface cells, including CECs and CjECs. However, current research overlooks its presence in lacrimal gland and meibomian gland cells. When exposed to cigarette smoke or blue light, CECs or CjECs exhibit ferroptosis-induced oxidative stress damage through the YAP/ACSL4/TFRC or STAT3/SLC7A11/GPX4 signaling pathways. Animal models of dry eye demonstrate significant changes in ferroptosis markers across the cornea, conjunctiva, and lacrimal gland, characterized by lipid peroxidation, impaired antioxidant capacity, iron dysregulation, and mitochondrial dysfunction.

Clinical findings indicate that patients with dry eye secondary to Stevens-Johnson syndrome exhibit a significant decrease in the number of mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, and Golgi apparatus within the lacrimal glands.40 Extensive cell and animal studies have demonstrated that mitochondrial dysfunction and endoplasmic reticulum stress play a key role in dry eye, primarily driven by mtDNA leakage, mitochondrial ROS accumulation, endoplasmic reticulum swelling, and dysregulated UPR activation. However, the precise mechanistic relationship between lysosomes and dry eye has yet to be elucidated. Given the bridging role of ROS in ferroptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction, targeting the inhibition of ROS accumulation may potentially disrupt the vicious cycle in dry eye. Furthermore, oxidative stress and inflammation are not only common phenotypic features of ferroptosis and mitochondrial damage, but they also represent key pathological characteristics of dry eye. The specific molecular interactions among endoplasmic reticulum stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and dry eye remain to be clarified.

Therapies targeting ferroptosis, mitochondrial, and endoplasmic reticulum stress have also made considerable progress in dry eye, including pharmacological interventions and intense pulsed light treatment. Nanomaterials may have better bioavailability. Although the majority of agents, such as DDR1-IN-1 and PAA-CD-DA@D609, are still in the discovery or preclinical phase for dry eye, a limited number, including astaxanthin, SkQ1, and autologous serum, have advanced to clinical investigation or obtained regulatory approval.77,78,79,80 Oral astaxanthin (6 mg, twice daily for about one month) significantly improved tear film stability, ocular surface integrity, and meibomian gland function in middle-aged and elderly patients with mild-to-moderate dry eye.77 Topical SkQ1 (twice daily for 29 days) was safe and significantly improved central corneal and conjunctival staining, lid margin redness, and dryness in patients with mild-to-moderate dry eye compared with placebo.78 A multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled trial involving 240 patients verified the therapeutic effect of SkQ1 eye drops for dry eye.80 Autologous serum eye drops improved tear secretion, tear film stability, and corneal epithelial integrity, with greater safety than artificial tears, and demonstrated anti-ferroptosis activity in vitro, suggesting an updated mechanism for dry eye therapy.67,79 While the inhibition of ferroptosis has been shown to alleviate organelle dysfunction, accumulating evidence indicates that such intervention alone may be insufficient to fully restore organelle integrity. For instance, in a cardiac ischemia–reperfusion model, the ferroptosis inhibitor liproxstatin-1 reduced mitochondrial ROS and restored GPX4 levels, yet failed to normalize Ca2+-induced mPTP opening.81 Dynasore has been shown in cellular studies to protect CECs from mitochondrial damage and to exhibit anti-endoplasmic reticulum stress effects. Given the complex interplay between ferroptosis, mitochondrial dysfunction, and endoplasmic reticulum stress in dry eye, elucidating the specific pathogenic mechanisms underlying these three factors and designing multi-target approaches could potentially disrupt the vicious cycle of dry eye and facilitate the restoration of ocular surface homeostasis.

The construction of an evaluation index system for the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes: a Delphi study
Authors: Xiaochu Liu, Li Gu, Li Zou, Xu Mei, Zhenhuan Yang, Mei Ju
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundGiven the physiological decline in late life, coupled with an elevated risk of various diseases and anticipated adverse events, frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes are a principal group that is vulnerable to health risks, thereby manifesting a triple vulnerability comprising “physical-mental-economic” aspects. However, standardized assessments of health vulnerability in frail and vulnerable older persons within nursing homes in China are currently lacking, and an evaluation index system has not yet been undeveloped. Therefore, the aim of this study was to construct an index system for comprehensively evaluating the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes, aiming to offer personalized elderly care services as a reference for predicting health vulnerability risks among this demographic population.MethodsOn the basis of the “sensitivity‒response capacity” vulnerability analysis framework, multidimensional risk indicators for health vulnerability were preliminarily established through policy literature analysis and semistructured interviews. Between April and August 2023, a Delphi study involved 20 expert representatives from four provinces and municipalities, spanning multiple research domains, for participation. Using a five-point Likert scale, they assessed the value of predefined indicators and provided qualitative feedback. Subsequent to each round of expert input, revisions were enacted upon the evaluation index system.ResultsAfter three rounds of expert consultation, a consensus on the evaluation index system was reached; it contains two primary indicators, individual sensitivity and antifragility capacity, with 12 affiliated secondary indicators and 56 tertiary indicators. The expert authority coefficients ranged from 0.934 to 0.935, and Kendall’s coefficient of concordance ranged from 0.310 to 0.360. These differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05).ConclusionThe evaluation index system for assessing the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes developed in this study covers a range of health threats and indicators of health coping capacity faced by frail and vulnerable older persons from individual to societal perspectives. This evaluation index system employs the “sensitivity‒responsiveness” vulnerability analysis framework, addressing the limitations of single-dimensional assessment indicators in capturing a comprehensive overview. This study provides a novel research perspective for assessing health vulnerability in frail and vulnerable older persons. This evaluation index system provides a practical tool for nursing home staff to assess health vulnerability, enabling early risk identification and targeted interventions. Policy-makers and administrators can use it to optimize resource allocation and enhance elderly care services. Future research should validate its real-world applicability and integrate it into existing health monitoring and intervention programs to improve older persons’ health outcomes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-24607-x.

Background

The aging of the population is one of the most notable trends in global demographics. According to the latest data from the National Bureau of Statistics, by the end of 2023, the population aged 60 years and above in China reached 296.97 million, accounting for 21.1% of the total population [1]. With advancements in medical technology and continuous improvements in social security systems, prolonged “disabled survival” among older persons has become commonplace [2]. According to projections, by 2030, the number of frail and vulnerable older persons in China is expected to exceed 77.65 million. On average, older persons experience a disabled period of 7.44 years during their remaining life expectancy, which poses significant pressure and challenges to the public health system [3]. The decline in physiological function in late life, coupled with an increased risk of various diseases and anticipated negative events, leads to frail and vulnerable older persons constituting a primary group that is susceptible to health risks [4, 5].

The concept of vulnerability, as a comprehensive term, originated from the field of natural disasters [6]. It has since been extended to various disciplines, such as disaster management, economics, ecology, and public health, and describes the susceptibility of individuals, groups, or systems to harm when faced with threats, risks, and uncertainties [7]. Currently, there is no universally accepted definition of health vulnerability. Some scholars, drawing on existing research on vulnerability, have proposed that health vulnerability is a composite function of bodily sensitivity and resilience [8–10]. When the imbalance between sensitivity and resilience surpasses a certain threshold, it increases an individual’s health vulnerability [11]. Unlike their healthy counterparts, frail and vulnerable older persons confront a myriad of stressors, including physical discomfort, daily life constraints, and financial burdens [12]. These challenges undermine their health feasibility, erode their coping mechanisms, and evoke negative emotional responses toward health risks, thus manifesting a triple vulnerability paradigm encompassing ‘physical-mental-economic’ dimensions.

In recent years, the concept of vulnerability has emerged as a focal point across interdisciplinary domains, spanning the humanities, social sciences, and health care [13]. Research on vulnerability related to older persons has focused mainly on health vulnerability [14, 15], economic vulnerability [16, 17], and disaster vulnerability [18, 19]. However, despite being a pivotal subset of eldercare vulnerability, there is a dearth of literature analyzing health vulnerability issues among older persons from a service-oriented standpoint.

Since they are distanced from familiar living environments and family members and face the accumulation of health risks, frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes experience impaired health functioning, a reduced ability to cope with health issues, and negative emotional responses to these risks, which manifests as pronounced vulnerability. However, standardized assessments of health vulnerability in frail and vulnerable older persons within Chinese nursing homes are currently lacking, and an evaluation index system has not yet been developed. In light of this, this study draws upon the ‘exposure level-sensitivity-adaptation capacity’ framework delineated by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) [20]. Grounded in the paradigm of ‘sensitivity-coping capacity’,’ this study aims to develop an evaluation index system for assessing the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes. Employing a triangulated approach that integrates policy literature analysis, semistructured interviews, and Delphi expert consultations, this study aims to facilitate a nuanced understanding of health vulnerability risks among frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes. Ultimately, this study aims to inform the provision of tailored elderly care services, which serve as a valuable resource for practitioners in the field.

Methods

Study design

Through a combination of a literature review and semistructured interviews, this study takes initial steps toward establishing a comprehensive set of evaluation indicators for assessing the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons residing in nursing homes. This comprehensive framework encompasses two primary indicators, 12 secondary indicators, and 58 tertiary indicators (Appendix 1). The Delphi method was subsequently used to evaluate the importance of these predetermined indicators [21, 22], and the utilization of the analytic hierarchy process (AHP) facilitated the determination of their respective weights. Semi‑structured field interviews

Semistructured interviews were employed in this study to examine health vulnerability issues, characteristics, and coping strategies among frail vulnerable older persons, their primary caregivers, and institutional leaders within nursing homes. Between February and March 2023, purposive sampling was employed to interview two institutional managers, three frontline staff members, and five disabled older persons (Appendix 2). Each interview was limited to 30 min and was recorded simultaneously with real-time documentation of nonverbal data. Following the interviews, the data were promptly transcribed. Data analysis followed Colaizzi’s seven-step phenomenological approach to ensure a rigorous and systematic interpretation of participants’ lived experiences and needs. First, all transcripts were thoroughly read to achieve data familiarization. Significant statements relevant to the phenomenon were then identified and extracted. Next, meanings were formulated from these statements, which were subsequently clustered into thematic categories. An exhaustive description of the phenomenon was then constructed, followed by the development of a fundamental structure that captured its core essence. Finally, the results were validated through participant feedback to enhance credibility and ensure that the findings accurately reflected their experiences and needs.

Adjustment of the index system through the Delphi method

Questionnaire design

A total of two experts in geriatrics and three experts in geriatric nursing research (holding associate professors or higher titles and with over 10 years of work experience) were engaged to review the inquiry questionnaire for this study. Combining the feedback from the expert panel, the research team formulated the first round of the expert inquiry questionnaire. The questionnaire is divided into three parts: the introduction section outlines the research background, objectives, instructions for completion, and consent status of the survey participants; the main section presents various levels of indicators and solicits expert ratings of their importance; and the final section gathers information about the experts, including their basic details, level of familiarity, and judgment criteria (Appendix 3).

Expert selection

On the basis of the research objectives and in accordance with the principles of expert selection for the Delphi method [23], the inclusion criteria for expert consultation are as follows: (1) employment in higher education institutions, Grade A tertiary comprehensive hospitals, or nursing homes/day care centers, working in the fields of geragogics (gerontological education), gerontology (aging studies and management), geriatric nursing, and geriatric medicine, and (2) having over 10 years of professional experience. (3) Having at least an associate professor title or above.

Implementation of expert inquiry

Questionnaire distribution and collection

Three rounds of expert inquiries for this study were conducted from April 2023 to August 2023. The first round of inquiries began in April 2023, with expert inquiry questionnaires sent to each expert’s email address. They were then required to independently complete the questionnaires within the specified time frame and return them. Some expert inquiry questionnaires were administered face-to-face, where the experts filled out the questionnaires onsite and returned them immediately [24]. A total of 20 copies of the first round of inquiry questionnaires were distributed, and 19 completed questionnaires were collected by mid-May 2023. On the basis of the comprehensive statistical results and expert opinions, combined with discussions within the research team, a second round of expert inquiry questionnaires was formulated. At the end of May 2023, the 19 experts who participated in the first round of inquiries were invited again to complete the second round of expert inquiry questionnaires. All inquiry questionnaires were sent via email, and all questionnaires were collected by mid-June 2023. After the results of the second round were analyzed and expert opinions were further integrated, the consensus among the experts was found to be insufficient. Therefore, following discussions within the research team, the 19 experts who participated in the second round of inquiries were invited again for a third round of expert inquiries by the end of June 2023. All questionnaires were collected by early August 2023.

Selection criteria for indicators

The average importance score of each index evaluated by the expert panel was > 4.0, and the coefficient of variation was < 2.0. When some important indicators were not included, the research team needed to discuss whether the indicator was retained or deleted.

Weight assessment of the quality evaluation index

The index weight is a key parameter in the evaluation of health vulnerability in frail and vulnerable older persons, reflecting the relative importance of the indicators and directly influencing the scientific validity of the index system. The AHP (analytic hierarchy process) is an effective method for converting experts’ subjective assessments of different evaluation factors into quantitative weights and is particularly suitable for problems requiring complex decomposition. Therefore, we apply the AHP to determine the weights and combined weights of the various levels of indicators for evaluating the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes. The calculation process of the AHP method is shown in Fig. 1.

Establishment of the hierarchical structure model

The hierarchical structure model for evaluating the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes consists of three levels: the target layer (the highest level), the criterion layer (the middle level), and the solution layer (the lowest level). The target layer is set as the comprehensive assessment of the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons. The criterion layer breaks down the key influencing factors in the target layer, including two dimensions based on the sensitivity‒response capacity framework, namely, individual sensitivity and antifragility ability, along with 12 specific evaluation components. The solution layer represents the actual evaluation options or specific measurement tools, comprising 56 items. The complete hierarchical structure for quality evaluation is shown in Fig. 2.

Data analysis

Excel 2010 and SPSS 26.0 software were used for statistical analysis. Frequencies and percentages are used to describe the personal information of the experts. The degree of expert positivity was assessed on the basis of the questionnaire response rate. Response rate = (Number of questionnaires returned/Number of questionnaires sent) × 100%. The authority coefficient (Cr) of the expert was the mean value of the expert’s judgment basis (Ca) and degree of familiarity (Cs) with the research content, based on the formula Cr = (Ca + Cs)/2, with an expert authority coefficient ≥ 0.70 deemed acceptable. The Cs is divided into five levels: very familiar, relatively familiar, average, slightly unfamiliar, and unfamiliar, and the degree coefficient is quantified accordingly. The Ca is divided into four categories: work experience, theoretical analysis, reference to domestic and foreign literature, and subjective judgment. The degree of influence is quantified as large, medium, or small. The degree of consensus among experts on each item is evaluated by computing the Kendall harmony coefficient (Kendall W) of expert opinions. The yaahpV7.5 software was used to ascertain the weights of each indicator and combination weights. P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance for the differences.

Over 20% of the experts had more than 30 years of work experience (Table 1).

Degree of expert positivity and the authority coefficient (Cr)

In the first round, the response rate was 95%, with inputs from 10 experts. In the second round, with a response rate of 100% with input from 8 experts. In the third round, the response rate reached 100%, and the opinions of the experts tended to converge. Researchers claim that an expert consultation authority coefficient > 0.7 is considered reliable. According to rigorous calculations, the authority coefficients (Cr) of the two rounds of expert consultation were 0.934 and 0.935, suggesting that the experts were highly authoritative [25].

Degree of coordination of expert opinions

Kendall’s coefficient is commonly used to evaluate the degree of coordination of expert opinions, with values ranging from 0 to 1. The closer the value is to 1, the higher the level of agreement among the experts. The coordination coefficients across three rounds of inquiry ranged from 0.310 to 0.360, all slightly on the lower side. This might be attributed to factors such as the high authority of the experts and the multitude of indicators. However, there was an increasing trend in the coordination coefficient in each round of inquiry, and the significance test result was P < 0.001, indicating that the opinions of the experts tended to converge. The experts showed a high degree of coordination in their opinions on the items, and the results of the inquiry were reliable(Table 2).

The formation process of the evaluation index system

In the initial round of inquiry, seven indicators were eliminated on the basis of expert opinions and criteria for indicator selection: “Gender,” “Mental Health Status,” “Primary Source of Income,” “Personal Annual Income,” “Housing Structure,” “Preretirement Occupation,” and “Ground Slip Prevention Signs and Treatment.” Additionally, eight indicators were introduced: “Sleep Quality,” “BMI Index,” “Thrombosis Risk Score,” “Pressure Injury Risk Score,” “Adherence to Medical Advice,” “Dispute Resolution and Complaint Channels,” and “Multifunctional Nursing Beds.” The subcategory “Family Social Vulnerability” was subdivided into “Family Vulnerability” and “Social Vulnerability,” while two indicators were modified: “Self-Rated Health Status” was adjusted to the “Two-Week Disease Rate,” and the indicators “Smoking Index” and “Alcohol Abuse” were assimilated into the third-level indicators of health vulnerability. Following the initial round of inquiry, a pool of indicators comprising two primary indicators, 12 secondary indicators, and 58 third-level indicators was established (Appendix 4), forming the foundation for the second round of the inquiry questionnaire.

After the second round of expert inquiry, a total of six indicators were removed on the basis of expert opinions and criteria for indicator selection; these were as follows: “cognitive ability,” “adequacy of living resources,” “number of children,” “proportion of children regularly contacting elderly parents,” “assisted travel,” and “medication guidance.” Additionally, the following six indicators were added: “excretion care situation,” “palliative care,” “vital signs monitoring,” “referral services,” “traditional Chinese medicine health guidance,” and “long-term care insurance.” Two indicators were merged: “degree of visual impairment” and “degree of hearing impairment” were combined into “perception and communication ability,” and “rehabilitation therapy guidance” and “fitness exercise guidance” were amalgamated into “rehabilitation exercise guidance.” Following the second round of inquiry, a pool of indicators comprising two primary indicators, 12 secondary indicators, and 56 third-level indicators was established (Appendix 5), forming the foundation for the third round of inquiry questionnaire.

Following the third round of expert inquiry, there was a convergence of expert opinions, and no modifications were made. The final assessment framework for evaluating the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes includes two primary indicators: physical sensitivity and resilience capacity, 12 secondary indicators, and 56 third-level indicators (Table 3).

The evaluation index system for the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes

Standardized scoring and evaluation criteria of the indicator system

To facilitate the empirical application of the health vulnerability assessment index system for frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes, a standardized scoring method based on the weighted sum model built on the indicator system and corresponding weights established through three rounds of Delphi expert consultation was applied in this study. The combined weights were multiplied by 100, the weights of each indicator were converted into a percentage, and corresponding scoring criteria were established (Appendix 6).

Owing to rounding and other operations, the total of all weights may not exactly sum to 100. In such cases, the research team discussed and opted to adjust the weights of the least important or minor indicators, slightly increasing or decreasing their values until the total reached 100. A higher score indicates a greater health vulnerability risk for frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes.

Discussion

Establishing an indicator system lays the foundation for the assessment of health frailty among frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes

From a demographic perspective, the health frailty of frail and vulnerable older persons is within the scope of the concept of “frailty,” specifically addressing the extension of “health frailty” for this special group. It is influenced not only by the internal physiological and psychological states of individuals but also by external factors such as medical care, elderly care, and social conditions. These elements interact with each other, necessitating a comprehensive perspective for assessment [26, 27]. As the population of individuals with dementia and disabilities in China continues to expand, if fragility at the individual level is not effectively addressed, it will escalate into a governance issue regarding the health fragility of older persons. This will lead to further escalation of social medical costs, the overcrowding of long-term care resources, and an imbalance between the supply and demand of socialized elderly care services [28].

The assessment index system for the health fragility of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes constructed in this study takes a multidimensional approach, encompassing physiological, psychological, and social aspects. It comprehensively and objectively measures the health fragility of frail and vulnerable older persons. This system not only provides targeted and precise assessment tools for institutions to evaluate the health fragility of frail and vulnerable older persons but also helps alleviate the contradiction between supply and demand in the elderly care service industry. It promotes the rational layout of institutions in various regions, reduces the significant disparities in the distribution of medical and elderly care resources between regions and between urban and rural areas, and facilitates the upgrading of facilities and the improvement of service quality in nursing homes.

Scientific analysis of the assessment index system for the health fragility of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes

On the basis of literature analysis, policy summarization, and expert interviews, this study explores the construction of the index system through a combined qualitative and quantitative research method, relying on the “exposure level-sensitivity-adaptability” vulnerability analysis framework. After three rounds of expert inquiries, consensus expert opinions tend to be presented scientifically through the weighting of indicators, thus avoiding errors arising from personal subjective judgments. To ensure the authority of the consulted experts, this study conducted strict selection control, adhering to the principles of representativeness, authority, and dynamism. When consulting experts, we considered the economic development levels and development of elderly care institutions across different regions of the country. We screened experts from economically developed and leading eastern coastal areas as well as economically slightly lagging and socially backward western regions among multiple provinces and cities. These experts come from diverse professional backgrounds, such as geragogics, gerontology, geriatric nursing, and geriatric medicine. All of the experts had over 10 years of work experience and rich industry expertise to ensure the authority and reliability of their opinions. On the basis of the comprehensive analysis of the results from three rounds of Delphi expert consultations and expert opinions, an evaluation index system for health vulnerability in frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing institutions was finally established. The entire research process was rigorous and thorough, ensuring the rationality, completeness, and scientific nature of the index system.

The applicability analysis of the evaluation index system for health vulnerability in frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing institutions

The indicator system constructed in this study is rooted in the diverse health threats and coping abilities faced by frail and vulnerable older persons, spanning from the individual to the societal level. This approach circumvents the limitations of single-dimensional evaluation indicators, which often fail to capture the full picture. The weighting of the indicators is transparent, and the conceptual definitions are clearly defined. Considering the distinctive features of service recipients, this study conducts a comprehensive assessment of health vulnerability among frail and vulnerable older persons. It not only addresses the subjective health risks faced by frail and vulnerable older persons but also encompasses objective coping abilities across various levels, from individuals to families and society. This guarantees that the evaluation content is aligned with and offers a thorough assessment of the current health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing facilities. Additionally, to ensure the effective practical application of the constructed indicator system, three rounds of Delphi surveys were conducted, with the exception of the majority of previous studies on constructing health-related evaluation indicator systems for frail and vulnerable older persons [29–31].

During the consultation process of this study, an in-depth exploration of the connotations of the indicators was conducted, standardized scores for each level of indicators were calculated, and the evaluation criteria were clarified. This approach will be more beneficial in guiding subsequent scholars’ practical research and for governments and relevant institutions to adopt this indicator system for assessing health vulnerability in offshore and vulnerable older persons.

Content analysis of the evaluation indicator system for health vulnerability among frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes

On the basis of the primary indicators developed in this study for health vulnerability, the weight of anti-fragility capacity (0.6667) for frail and vulnerable older persons is greater than the weight of individual sensitivity (0.3333). This result aligns with the findings of Xiao Xiaohua et al. [32], who suggested that frail and vulnerable older persons can receive assistance from various service projects provided by themselves, their families, social security, and long-term care institutions in terms of living, medical care, rehabilitation, psychological care, and other aspects to resist or reduce their vulnerability risks. Frail and vulnerable older persons, with their complex health and social care needs, often rely heavily on health and social care services. Unfortunately, owing to the insufficient accessibility and coordination of the elderly health service system, highly vulnerable groups actually receive very limited health services [33, 34]. Hence, actively fostering community-based elderly care services and efficiently integrating medical and elderly care resources, particularly tailoring personalized health services for frail and vulnerable older persons dependent on long-term medical and care assistance, will significantly contribute to alleviating the challenges associated with health vulnerability among frail and vulnerable older persons.

On the other hand, the weight proportion of individual sensitivity comprising one-third of the overall weight indicates that aging is an inherent aspect of the body’s life cycle. Despite advancements in medical technology and ongoing enhancements in social security systems, prolonged “survival in frail and vulnerable” older persons remains a fundamental contributor to their health vulnerability. Hence, the establishment of a robust three-tier prevention system for intervening in older persons’ health risk factors, promoting “early detection, early diagnosis, early treatment” of diseases, and preventing disability is crucial for maintaining the health of older persons. It also represents a significant discussion topic in China’s public health policies and public management domains.

Within the framework of individual sensitivity analysis, the weights of the five secondary indicators, in descending order, are health vulnerability (0.3259), psychological vulnerability (0.2463), physiological vulnerability (0.1600), family and social vulnerability (0.1600), and economic vulnerability (0.1078). These findings highlight that the main contributing factor to increased health vulnerability among frail and vulnerable older persons is the array of health risks arising from physiological decline and ongoing loss of self-care ability. Additionally, the research findings affirm that mental health status is a critical factor influencing the vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons. Psychological vulnerability issues among older persons are particularly pronounced. Psychological vulnerability not only contributes to an increased incidence of physiological and psychological ailments but also leads to various adverse outcomes associated with aging [35]. Hence, prioritizing the psychological well-being of older persons is a pivotal component of China’s Healthy China strategy. During the evolution of elderly care services, establishing a comprehensive psychological intervention system for older persons is imperative. Such a system can deliver tailored emotional communication, psychological intervention, emotional guidance, and other services tailored to older persons with varying levels of health vulnerability. Moreover, organizing recreational activities for older persons can invigorate their participation in social endeavors, thereby mitigating their psychological vulnerability.

Within the anti-fragility analysis framework, the weights of the seven secondary indicators, listed in descending order, are as follows: institutional medical care assurance (0.2803), institutional psychological service assurance (0.2017), family social security capacity (0.1468), institutional rehabilitation health assurance (0.1408), health security capacity (0.0938), institutional daily care assurance (0.0938), and institutional safety assurance (0.0366). This indicates a transition in the resilience of frail and vulnerable older persons against health risks from primarily relying on family support to predominantly depending on institutional support as they shift from home-based to institutional care. Within institutional health security services, medical care assurance has emerged as a pivotal means for frail and vulnerable older persons to bolster their resilience against health risks.

In light of this, this study emphasizes the need to address the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing institutions proactively. This involves focusing on the current provision of integrated medical and elderly care services. To achieve this, it is essential not only to foster collaboration between nursing and medical institutions at the organizational level but also to ensure effective alignment between the supply and demand sides of medical and elderly care services. Ultimately, this approach aims to deliver continuous and personalized medical care for frail and vulnerable older persons.

Among the 58 tertiary indicators, the top three in terms of weight are privacy and dignity maintenance (0.0710), chronic disease management (0.0656), and nutrition monitoring (0.0527). First, in the process of maintaining the health resilience of frail and vulnerable older persons, providing services to address their psychological vulnerability and mitigate their psychological risk is crucial for their health security. Owing to the deepening level of disability and the continuous loss of self-care ability among older persons, they rely on caregivers in various daily activities. Under the dual influence of helplessness and stigma [36], there is a greater need for institutions to ensure the privacy and dignity maintenance of frail and vulnerable older persons throughout the process of providing various life and medical services, thereby safeguarding their psychological well-being [37].

Second, for frail and vulnerable older persons, health care services such as chronic disease management and nutritional monitoring are crucial. Both of these services are essential measures for health prevention and management. Therefore, after health vulnerability is evaluated, our focus should be on providing more effective health care services to prevent further deterioration of disability levels among the older population. This involves effectively preventing or reducing the development of complications and maintaining the self-care abilities of older persons as much as possible.

Research limitations and outlook

Inherent limitations exist in research hypotheses formulated on the basis of theoretical guidance. The evaluation index of health vulnerability among frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing institutions, established through Delphi expert consultation in this study, will ultimately be applied in practical settings. Therefore, its utility and feasibility need validation through practical implementation. To further validate the scientific and practical validity of the designed evaluation index system, the matter must be approached from a comprehensive and diverse perspective involving multiple stakeholders. The evaluation index system should undergo standardization and scoring, followed by repeated practical verification. This approach ensures that the constructed indicators not only align with national policy requirements from a top-level design perspective but also cater to the diverse practical needs of nursing homes, service personnel, and frail and vulnerable older persons in different regions. Furthermore, considering the dynamic and evolving nature of health vulnerability among frail and vulnerable older persons, the evaluation indicators should be continually adjusted to remain adaptable to ongoing developments.

Conclusion

Drawing from the “sensitivity‒response capacity” perspective, this study developed an evaluation index system for assessing the health vulnerability of frail and vulnerable older persons in nursing homes. This comprehensive framework was crafted through a combination of policy document analysis, semistructured interviews, and three rounds of Delphi expert consultations, resulting in a system comprising two primary indicators, 12 secondary indicators, and 56 tertiary indicators. The index system comprehensively addresses the health threats and coping mechanisms of frail and vulnerable older persons across various domains, ranging from the individual to the societal level. By overcoming the limitations of single-dimensional evaluation indicators in capturing a complete picture, a novel research perspective for constructing evaluation index systems is introduced.

In practical applications, this evaluation index system can serve as a valuable tool for nursing home staff to assess the health vulnerability of older residents systematically, enabling early identification of high-risk individuals and the implementation of targeted health management plans. Additionally, policy-makers and nursing home administrators can utilize this system as an evidence-based framework for optimizing resource allocation and improving elderly care services. Future research should focus on validating the applicability of this index system in real-world settings and integrating it into existing health monitoring and intervention programs to enhance its impact on older persons’ health outcomes.

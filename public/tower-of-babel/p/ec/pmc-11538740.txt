A comparative study on the selection of new characters in Chinese primary school textbooks in mainland China and the Taiwan region
Authors: Xiaofang Ma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe selection of new characters for Chinese textbooks is essential for primary school literacy teaching. This research used content and comparative analyses to study the selection of new characters for Chinese textbooks used in primary schools in mainland China and the Taiwan region. The results showed that there were more new characters in the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China than in those used in the Taiwan region. The distribution of new characters in the textbooks used in each region also differed, and their presentation varied significantly depending on whether they were compiled in mainland China or the Taiwan region. Furthermore, few characters were common to the textbooks used in mainland China and the Taiwan region. These findings provide important references for the study of new characters in textbooks and ultimately for literacy teaching in primary schools.Keywords: Mainland China, The Taiwan region, Chinese primary school, Textbook, New characters

Introduction

This paper reports a comparative study of the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China and those used in the Taiwan region. The referenced textbooks were unified and Kangxuan versions, respectively. The unified textbooks are organized and compiled by the Ministry of Education of the People's Republic of China. Use of these textbooks began in several provinces in 2016, and as of September 2017, they were being uniformly used by new primary and secondary school students nationwide. By 2018, unified compulsory education Chinese textbooks were used by all freshman first to seventh graders, and by 2019, unified primary and junior high school Chinese textbooks were being used uniformly. The emergence of integrated textbooks marked the end of the “one outline and multiple versions” era of Chinese compulsory education textbooks and signaled the coming of the “big Chinese” era. The primary school Chinese textbooks used in the Taiwan region are mainly the Kangxuan, Hanlin, and Nanyi versions. The Kangxuan version currently predominates, accounting for about 40 % of all primary school textbooks in Taiwan region. Therefore, this study selected unified and Kangxuan primary school Chinese textbooks used in mainland China and the Taiwan region as the research objects.

Literature review

A substantial body of literature exists on textbooks in China and abroad. Weinbrenner [1] divided textbook research into three categories: process-oriented, which considers textbooks’ complete life cycle, including design, approval, dissemination, adoption, use, and abolition; product-oriented, which concerns the culture and ideology of textbooks and teaching tools; and reception-oriented, which investigates textbooks’ influence on teachers, students, and various social groups. Johnsen [2] divided textbook research into three categories: consciousness, use, and development. Zhou [3] divided textbook topics into four categories: content analysis, evaluation and selection criteria, editing and development, and application. Lan [4] divided textbook research topics into textbook content analysis research and development process research.

History of Chinese primary school textbooks in mainland China

In 1985, the Central Committee of the Communist Party of China’s decision to reform the education system moved China from the “one outline and one version” era to the “one outline and multiple versions” era, while also shifting China's textbook system from “national customization” to the “certification system,” which met various regions’ differing needs. By 2018, there were 12 versions of primary school Chinese textbooks used in China, including the People’s Education Version, the Jiangsu Education Version, the Beijing Normal University Version, the Hunan Education Version, and the Hubei Education Version, among others. The emergence of the unified version brought Chinese compulsory education textbooks out of the “one outline and multiple versions” era, a period regarding which much textbook research has been done. Since 2016, unified Chinese textbooks have been used in several provinces, prompting considerable research on the unified version.

History of Primary School Chinese Textbooks in Taiwan region

The textbook liberalization principle was established in the Taiwan region in 1988. In 1989, junior middle school arts and activity books were subjected to public examination and approval, and in 1991, primary school arts and activity books were also opened to public scrutiny. By 1996, all primary school textbooks were subject to public examination and approval. Textbook research increased sharply in tandem with public involvement in textbooks transitioning from unified to multiple versions.

In recent years, a comparative studies of the textbooks used in mainland China and the Taiwan region has also shown an increasing trend, but there have been no comparative studies on the selection of new textbook characters. About 80 % of Chinese characters are pictophonetic; that is, they express meaning and sound. Hence, Chinese characters have a “multi-pictophonetic and understanding” characteristic [5]. The simplified Chinese characters used in mainland China and the traditional Chinese characters currently used in the Taiwan region share the same characteristics. Therefore, this study compared the selection of new Chinese characters for primary school Chinese textbooks in circulation in mainland China and the Taiwan region.

Materials and methods

This study aimed to determine the number of new characters, their presentation mode, and the distribution of literacy and writing items as well as common words in the two versions of primary school Chinese textbooks through a comparative analysis of the selection of new words. Research methods were content, comparative, and statistical analyses.

Methodology

Since textbooks are based on curriculum standards or syllabi, it is important to know their specific content and requirements in order to understand the textbook. Therefore, this study compared the requirements of the Chinese Curriculum Standards for Compulsory Education [6], which are in effect in mainland China, with those of the 9-Year Consistent Curriculum Outline for Primary and Secondary Schools, which was applied in the Taiwan region. The focus of the comparison was selection of new characters. Content and statistical analyses were performed with reference to the new character lists in Chinese textbooks for use in Grades 1–6 in mainland China and the Taiwan region with the aim of ascertaining the number of new characters across the two versions. Next, content and comparative analyses were conducted to identify the differences in the presentation of new characters across versions. The distribution of literacy and writing items for each grade and textbook version was then compared. Finally, the distribution of common characters in each volume of the two textbook versions was analyzed based on the requirements for recognizing and reading characters. Results

Comparative analysis of the Chinese curriculum standard of compulsory education in mainland China and in the Taiwan region

In mainland China, unified textbooks are compiled according to the Chinese Curriculum Standards for Compulsory Education, formulated by the Ministry of Education in 2011. Chinese is a comprehensive and practical course for learning how to use language and characters, and the course’s basic feature concerns the unity of instrumentality and humanity [6]. At the compulsory education stage, the Chinese language course is taught to all students to support their attainment of basic Chinese literacy [6] since literacy is the foundation upon which to cultivate students’ reading, writing, and other Chinese literacy aspects. Therefore, the selection and arrangement of new characters in Chinese textbooks, especially in primary school textbooks, is very important.

The Chinese Curriculum Standards for Compulsory Education have been designed and applied for 9 consecutive years. Under “General Objectives,” primary education is divided into three stages: Grades 1–2, the first learning stage; Grades 3–4, the second learning stage, and Grades 5–6, the third learning stage. Each stage has specific objectives and contents, but the three stages are interconnected; they spiral up and ultimately achieve the general objectives. Chinese curriculum design in mainland China reflects the characteristics of integrity and periodicity. Regarding literacy, the requirements of the Chinese Curriculum Standards for Compulsory Education(2011) are as follows [6]: First and second graders should recognize 1600 frequently-used characters and be able to write about 800; third and fourth graders should recognize 2500 frequently-used characters and be able to write about 1600; and fifth and sixth graders should recognize about 3000 frequently-used characters and be able to write about 2500.

Prior to 2019, the Taiwan region adopted the 9-Year Consistent Curriculum Outline for Primary and Secondary Schools. In 2018, the 12-Year Basic Education Curriculum Outline went into effect in the region, and as of September 2019, it has been gradually implemented in primary and secondary schools starting with Grade 1. Both versions of the curriculum outline divide primary education into the three stages described above. The literacy requirements stipulated in the 9-Year Consistent Curriculum Outline for Primary and Secondary Schools suggest that students at the first, second, and third learning stages should recognize 700–800, 1500–1,800, and 2200–2700 frequently-used Chinese characters, respectively. The 12-Year Basic Education Curriculum Outline divides Chinese learning performance into six categories: listening, oral expression, phonetic symbols and application, literacy and writing, and reading and writing. Its literacy requirements for the first, second, and third learning stages are that students should be able to recognize at least 1,000, 1,800, and 2700 frequently-used Chinese characters, respectively.

Compared with the 9-Year Consistent Curriculum Outline for Primary and Secondary Schools, the 12-Year Basic Education Curriculum Outline has significantly improved the literacy requirements applied in the Taiwan region, especially in the first learning stage. Specifically, from a quantitative perspective, the literacy quota stipulated in the 12-year basic education curriculum outline is 200 words above the upper limit indicated in the 9-Year Consistent Curriculum Outline for primary and secondary schools. Regarding the second and third learning stages, the 12-Year Basic Education Curriculum Outline stipulates quantitative literacy quotas of 1800 and 2700 words, which are equivalent to the upper limits of the word ranges specified in the 9-Year Consistent Curriculum Outline for primary and secondary schools.

Continuing the comparison, mainland China’s curriculum standard imposes a higher quantitative literacy quota than the two curriculum outlines in effect in the Taiwan region. However, mainland China’s curriculum standards showed an overall trend of slowing literacy growth across the three stages of primary education, whereas Taiwan region’s curriculum outlines showed steady literacy growth throughout the stages.

As previously mentioned, textbooks are compiled according to the curriculum standards/outlines. The 12-Year Basic Education Curriculum Outline has been implemented annually in the Taiwan region since September 2019, and by the end of 2019, only first graders had begun using compliant textbooks. Consequently, for the sake of statistical accuracy, this study used 2018 Kangxuan textbooks from the Taiwan region, which were compiled in accordance with the 9-Year Consistent Curriculum Outline for Primary and Secondary Schools.

Differing learning order for pinyin and Chinese characters

Pinyin (phonetic notation) is an auxiliary literacy tool. The unified Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China differed from the Kangxuan textbooks used in the Taiwan region regarding the learning order of Chinese Pinyin (phonetic notation) and Chinese characters.

The textbooks in mainland China promoted learning literacy before learning Pinyin. In the past, students learned Pinyin when they started school. Doing so was very difficult and diminished their interest in learning Chinese. Academic research results on the positional efficiency of Pinyin teaching in the education system suggested reducing the difficulty of Pinyin and letting Pinyin teaching serve character recognition. This advice was adopted and incorporated into unified textbooks. Currently, 5 weeks are dedicated to literacy teaching in entrance education, and students begin learning Chinese Pinyin over a month later for a duration of 8 weeks. This is done to achieve a better combination of literacy and Pinyin teaching [8].

In the Kangxuan Chinese primary school textbooks used in Taiwan region, phonetic symbols were compiled separately in Volume 1. Students spend 10 weeks learning phonetic symbols before starting Volume 1 in Grade 1. That is, students in Taiwan region learn phonetic symbols as soon as they enter primary school, which is earlier than their counterparts in mainland China. Additionally, the time spent learning phonetic symbols is longer in Taiwan region than in mainland China.

Differing presentation of literacy and writing

In the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China, new characters were divided between literacy and writing tables. The literacy table generally contained more words than the writing table, especially in the lower grades, demonstrating adherence to the principle of “more recognition and less writing.” In the Kangxuan Chinese primary school textbooks used in the Taiwan region, literacy and writing were integrated, and there was only one table called the new character table, which followed the format of words inside and outside the box. Students are expected to be able to recognize and write the words in the box and recognize and read the relatively difficult words outside the box.

Discussion and conclusion

Based on the statistical analysis of the selection of new characters in Chinese primary school textbooks in mainland China and the Taiwan region, the study’s conclusion is that the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China contained more new characters than the Kangxuan textbooks used in the Taiwan region, indicating that the literary teaching requirements differ on either side of the Taiwan region.

From the curriculum standard/outline perspective, the Chinese Curriculum Standards for Compulsory Education in effect in mainland China require higher literacy and writing levels than the 9-Year Consistent Curriculum Outline for Primary and Secondary Schools and the 12-Year Basic Education Curriculum Outline in effect in Taiwan; however, the 12-Year Basic Education Curriculum Outline requires considerably more new characters than its predecessor, marking a significant improvement.

From the textbook perspective, the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China contained 3000 Chinese characters, with 2500 designated to be written, whereas the Kangxuan Chinese primary school textbooks used in Taiwan contained 2432 Chinese characters, with 2214 designated to be written. Hence, the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China contained more Chinese characters than the Kangxuan textbooks used in Taiwan.

That the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China contained more new characters than the Kangxuan textbooks used in the Taiwan region highlights the different literacy teaching requirements on either side of the Taiwan Strait. Specifically, the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China contained more new characters, especially in the first learning stage, which is the peak period for learning new characters; consequently, these textbooks impose higher requirements on teachers and students. Although the Kangxuan Chinese primary school textbooks used in Taiwan contained slightly less new characters than the textbooks used in mainland China and also reflected a lower literacy level for the lower grades, the Kangxuan Chinese textbooks afford teachers and students more time to teach and learn new characters, respectively.

Differing New Character Distribution Trends in the Chinese Primary School Textbooks Used in Mainland China and the Kangxuan Textbooks Used in Taiwan, Indicating Differences in Primary Chinese Education Teaching Concepts Between the Two Sides of the Taiwan Strait.

Significantly different new character distribution trends were observed in the Chinese textbooks used in mainland China and the Kangxuan textbooks used in Taiwan for each grade. Based on the quantitative distribution of literacy items in the textbooks for each grade, the unified Chinese primary school textbooks had the most first-stage literacy items, with the number of literacy items peaking in Grade 2 and then decreasing significantly thereafter. In the Kangxuan Chinese primary school textbooks used in the Taiwan region, a high acceleration rate was observed for the number of literacy items in the first stage, and the amount remained stable after Grade 2. Based on the distribution of the number of writing items for each grade, the unified Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China showed an initial increasing trend followed by decline. The quantitative distribution of writing items in the Kangxuan Chinese primary school textbooks used in the Taiwan region showed basic consistency with the quantitative distribution of literacy items; that is, the number of writing items initially increased and then remained stable after Grade 2.

The differences in the distribution of new characters between the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China and the Kangxuan textbooks used in the Taiwan region reflect geographical differences in Chinese primary school education teaching concepts and methods. Specifically, the number of new characters in the Grade 1 and 2 Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China was 1,600, an amount that builds a solid foundation for reading, writing, and other aspects of Chinese language learning including post-Grade 3 education. As students’ Chinese language abilities mature, the proportion of literacy taught as a part of Chinese learning gradually decreases. In the Kangxuan Chinese primary school textbooks used in Taiwan region, the new character distribution trend showed a slow start followed by rapid improvement and steady progress. First graders are given sufficient time to adapt to literacy learning, and the literacy learning tasks are equivalent at each stage after Grade 2, with literacy learning continuing throughout the entire primary school stage.

Differing presentation of new characters in the chinese primary school textbooks in mainland china and the kangxuan textbooks in taiwan region, indicating differences in textbook compilation concepts.Regarding the presentation order of Chinese Pinyin (phonetic notation) and Chinese characters, the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China integrated Chinese Pinyin into Grade 1 vol 1, which first presents simple Chinese characters followed by Chinese Pinyin. Contrastingly, the Kangxuan Chinese primary school textbooks used in the Taiwan region present phonetic notation first and separately in Volume 1 and then present Chinese characters.

From the perspective of the presentation of the literacy and writing systems, the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China present a new characters table and a writing table separately, effectively separating literacy and writing. In the Kangxuan Chinese primary school textbooks used in the Taiwan region, literacy and writing were integrated into a new character table, revealing a compilation concept that closely relates literacy and writing.

The differences in the presentation of new characters in the textbooks used on either side of the Taiwan region can be linked to differing textbook compilation concepts. Given the difficulty of learning Pinyin, which is a literacy tool, the Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China present simple Chinese characters first and then introduce Pinyin, and this presentation order provides a buffer period for students to learn Pinyin after entering school. In the Kangxuan Chinese primary school textbooks used in Taiwan region, phonetic symbols are presented first, followed by Chinese characters. Considering what is known about children's physical and mental development, students are afforded slightly more time to learn phonetic symbols, and first graders are required to learn less new words, allowing students to fully adapt to learning in primary school.

Few common characters in the chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland china and the kangxuan textbooks used in taiwan region, with the distribution showing a downward trend, indicating differences in new character sequencing and text selection between the two sides of the taiwan region strait.The Chinese primary school textbooks used in mainland China and the Kangxuan textbooks used in the Taiwan region shared few common characters. Grade 1 vol 1 of the former type of textbook showed the highest number of common characters, but the proportion of common characters showed a steady downward trend through the other volumes. Differences in character order and text selection were observed between the two types of textbooks.

Character order was observed as following two important rules in Chinese textbooks. The first rule concerns the Chinese characters' decreasing utility; that is, an unbalanced Chinese character occurrence frequency was observed. The higher the usage frequency of Chinese characters, the broader the coverage and the better the utility. The coverage rate of the maximum 1000 characters was 90 %, and the rate only increased about one-tenth for every 1400 characters added [7]. Second, common characters tended to be simplified; that is, the characters’ usage frequency was inversely proportional to the character strokes, and the most frequently-occurring characters tended to be simplified [9]. As previously mentioned, simplified Chinese characters are used in mainland China whereas traditional Chinese characters are used in Taiwan region. According to the above character order rules, the Chinese characters in the Grade 1 textbook were the most frequently occurring, and the strokes were the simplest. Among traditional Chinese characters, the simplest strokes were often consistent with or close to the simplified Chinese characters, and the differences between the simplified and traditional characters were not significant. Therefore, such volumes showed the highest proportion of common characters across the two types of textbooks. Thereafter, there were few common characters in the Chinese textbooks for each stage, and a declining trend was observed. On the one hand, this finding was affected by the differences in the strokes used for simplified and traditional Chinese characters, but it was also influenced by geographical text selection differences.

Implications

The above conclusions provide an important reference for the study of Chinese characters and literacy teaching in primary schools in mainland China and Taiwan region. This study’s derived suggestions for future research on Chinese characters and literacy teaching in primary schools are as follows.

Combine new characters in Chinese textbooks with the social culture context

The compilation of Chinese textbooks should be closely related to the social culture and historical development. Regarding external factors, the social context, cultural heritage, and other aspects affect the selection of new characters for Chinese textbooks on both sides of the strait. From the perspective of internal factors, the selection of new characters for Chinese textbooks on both sides of the Taiwan region Strait is affected by other textbook content such as the text selection system. Therefore, research on new characters in textbooks should be conducted from a systematic perspective, and the influences of various internal and external factors, such as the social context and the text selection system, should be fully considered.

Regional cultural differences in Chinese primary school literacy teaching

Textbooks are important tools for teachers to use while teaching. Given the many differences in the curriculum standards/outlines as well as in the Chinese textbooks used in mainland China and the Taiwan region, which reflect in the requirements contained in the curriculum standards/outlines, the quantitative distribution of new characters, the character order and selection system, and other aspects also differ. Primary school teachers should fully consider the differing textbook compilation concepts on both sides of the Taiwan region Strait and conduct differentiated teaching that incorporates the local culture.

Mainland China and Taiwan region can learn from each other’s primary school literacy teaching

This study identified many differences in the Chinese primary school textbooks used on the two sides of the Taiwan region Strait. Additionally, as previously mentioned, mainland China uses simplified Chinese characters and Taiwan region uses traditional Chinese characters. However, mainland China and Taiwan region share the same language root and source and are very similar in terms of font, pronunciation, and meaning. Therefore, primary school teachers on both sides of the Taiwan region Strait should strengthen their communication and exchange teaching design ideas as well as teaching methods, strategies, and evaluations. They can learn from each other’s educational practices.

Hip and knee arthroplasty in solid organ transplant patients: Results from a Turkish government-based health registry
Authors: Mehmet Kürşat Yılmaz, Ömer Serdar Hakyemez, Murat Birinci, İzzet Bingöl, Naim Ata, M. Mahir Ülgü, Şuayip Birinci, Ibrahim Azboy, Fahri Erdoan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Objective:This national database study of a large cohort aimed to investigate the mortality and complication rates of patients with a history of solid organ transplantation (SOT) who underwent total knee arthroplasty (TKA), total hip arthroplasty (THA), or hemiarthroplasty (HA). Additionally, this study aimed to identify predictive factors for medical and surgical complications as well as mortality in this patient population.Methods:This cohort study utilized data from the Turkish Ministry of Health to assess SOT patients undergoing TKA, THA, and HA. Propensity score matching was applied to create a comparable control group. Primary endpoints included medical and surgical complications within 90 days and mortality rates (in-hospital, at 1 month, 3 months, 1 year, and overall).Results:Kidney transplantation was the most common type of transplant (n = 666, 81.5%), followed by liver transplantation (n = 101, 12.4%). Revision rates were 4.5% in the SOT group and 3.9% in the control group (P = 0.472). SOT patients exhibited higher rates of medical complications, including chronic renal failure, pneumonia, electrolyte imbalances, urinary complications, and transfusion needs (P < 0.001). Mechanical complications were less frequent in the SOT group (2.57%) compared to the control group (3.7%), but prosthetic joint infection (PJI) rates were higher (2.57% vs. 1.4%, P = 0.035). Fracture history (HR: 4.82, 95% CI: 1.66-13.97, P = 0.004), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (HR: 1.7, 95% CI: 1.17-2.46, P = 0.005), and age (HR: 1.034, 95% CI: 1.01-1.05, P < 0.001) were identified as independent predictors of 90-day mortality. Patients undergoing HA experienced significantly higher mortality compared to TKA and THA (17.5%, 1.1%, and 1.7%, respectively; P < 0.001).Conclusion:SOT patients undergoing joint arthroplasty have elevated risks of complications and mortality, particularly in the presence of fractures. Multidisciplinary management, perioperative optimization of modifiable comorbidities, and close monitoring may help mitigate these risks and improve patient outcomes.

Introduction

Organ transplantation is a life-saving procedure, and the number of solid organ transplantations (SOTs) has increased in recent decades.1 The success rate of SOT has improved in the last decade in parallel to advancements in donor allocation procedures, better surgical techniques, more effective chemotherapies, and optimized perioperative patient care.2

Recipients of SOT are at increased risk of developing musculoskeletal complications such as avascular necrosis, osteoporosis, and osteoarthritis due to a variety of factors including long-term use of immunosuppressive medication, decreased physical activity, and steroid-induced bone loss.3-5 Joint arthroplasty is a viable treatment option for these patients, but the surgical outcomes and complications may differ from those of non-transplant patients.6

Several studies have investigated primary knee and hip arthroplasty outcomes in SOT patients, particularly among patients who underwent renal, cardiac, lung, or liver transplants. Although most studies revealed an increased risk of complications and mortality for these patients,6-9 there are several studies showing no differences in in-hospital mortality when comparing the 2 groups.10-12 Complication and mortality rates in SOT patients are not clear.

The purpose of this national database study of a large cohort was to investigate the mortality and complication rates of patients who had a history of SOT and underwent total knee arthroplasty (TKA), total hip arthroplasty (THA), or hemiarthroplasty (HA), and to identify predictive factors for medical and surgical complications and mortality.

Materials and methods

This retrospective nationwide study was conducted between 2016 and 2022 to evaluate the outcomes of TKA, THA, and HA in patients who had a history of SOT at any time. Patients who underwent TKA, THA, and HA and had a history of SOT were identified from the e-Nabız e-health database of the Turkish Ministry of Health from public, private, and university health institutions across Türkiye. All patient data were collected from the same system. The patients in the SOT group were matched with control patients who did not have a history of SOT on a 1 : 2 ratio based on age, sex, and Charlson comorbidity index (CCI) score13 via propensity score matching.

While creating the pool, patients who had undergone HA, primary TKA and THA were first determined via operation-specific procedure codes (P612320 [HA]; P612340, P612341 [THA]; P612420, P612421 [TKA]) (https://skrs.saglik.gov.tr/), among them, patients who had a history of SOT were identified using International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision (ICD-10) codes (Z94.0, Z94.1, Z94.2, Z94.3, and Z94.4). Patients who had undergone revision arthroplasty and unicompartmental knee arthroplasty were not included.

Variables to be analyzed included patient demographics, age, sex, body mass index (BMI; kg/m2), comorbidities, type of SOT, reason for arthroplasty, and type of arthroplasty surgery. Comorbidities were evaluated with the CCI. This index includes 19 major disease categories and has been adapted and validated for use with hospital discharge data in ICD databases to predict 10-year mortality for a patient. A CCI score was obtained for each patient based on the e-health database of the Turkish Ministry of Health.

The total number of operations per institution type was calculated; institution types included state university hospitals, private and foundation university hospitals, private hospitals, and secondary and tertiary healthcare institutions.

The primary endpoints were any medical and surgical complications in 90 days and mortality (in-hospital, 1 month, 3 months, 1 year, and overall). We obtained these endpoints from the relevant ICD-10 codes in the database. Postoperative medical and surgical complications were analyzed based on relevant ICD-10 codes, including pneumonia (J12-18), deep venous thromboembolism (I82), pulmonary embolism (I26), acute/chronic renal failure (N17), urinary tract infection (N39.0), electrolyte and acid–base imbalance (E87), gastrointestinal bleeding (hematemesis, melena, etc.) (K92), and prosthetic revision surgery. Prosthetic revision procedures included acetabular liner replacement (P612480, P612480), acetabular revision (P612471, P612471), femoral stem revision (P612472, P612472), revision using allograft or metal cages (P612490, P612490), hip arthroplasty with tumor resection prosthesis (P614555, P614555), revision procedures for the knee (P612440, P612441), removal of total knee prosthesis (P612430), and polyethylene exchange (P612460).

The study was conducted according to the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Turkish Ministry of Health with a waiver of informed consent for retrospective data analysis and the health information privacy law (ID: 95741342-020/27112019) and also approved by Istanbul Medipol University Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee (Approval No: 943, Date: 31.07.2025).14 The health information privacy law was strictly adhered to.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics 25.0 (IBM SPSS Corp.; Armonk, NY, USA). The normality of the data was evaluated using Q–Q plots, skewness, and kurtosis values. Quantitative variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Qualitative variables were expressed as numbers (n), frequencies, or ratios. Categorical variables were compared using the Pearson chi-square test. Parametric variables were compared using an independent sample t-test. A proportional Cox regression model was created to identify predictive factors associated with mortality. P-values of less than .05 were considered to indicate statistical significance.

Propensity score matching

This study incorporated a propensity score matching algorithm to adjust for potential confounding variables based on methodologies described in previous studies.15-17 This involved constructing a logistic regression model to generate propensity scores. A one-to-two matching approach was adopted, where each patient who had previously undergone SOT was coupled with 2 unique non-SOT patients, chosen based on the closest propensity scores. Any data points beyond the common support were eliminated from both groups to adequately balance the covariates.

The best caliper width was determined by taking 0.2 times the standard deviation of the logit of the propensity score as outlined in earlier works. Following this procedure, a satisfactory match was achieved between 817 SOT patients and double number of non-SOT patients (n = 1634). The adequacy of the model was further substantiated by contrasting the significance and the mean difference of the propensity scores of the datasets before and after matching. Conclusively, 4 variables were effectively balanced: patient age, sex, type of surgery, and CCI.

Results

Based on the nationwide e-Nabız database, a total of 676 741 patients who had undergone TKA, THA, or HA between 2016 and 2022 were identified. Among them, 817 were included in the study as recipients of SOT who met the inclusion criteria. A total of 1634 additional patients were identified from the same database for matching.

Patient characteristics

The mean age of the SOT group was 59.57 ± 12.2 years (range, 20-91) at the time of surgery. Following propensity score matching, any statistical disparity based on age, sex, CCI, or type of surgical procedure was effectively mitigated (Figure 1).

The most common organ transplant was kidney (n = 666, 81.5%), followed by liver (n = 101, 12.4%), renal and liver (n = 35, 4.3%), heart (n = 8, 1%), and lung (n = 6, 0.7%) transplants, while only 1 patient (0.1%) had undergone a lung and liver transplant. The most common surgery was THA (n = 375, 45.9%), followed by TKA (n = 241, 29.5%) and HA (n = 201, 24.6%).

Complications

According to the complications reported via the e-Nabız system, chronic renal failure (39.8%) was the most common complication in the SOT group. Other complications included venous thromboembolism (12.4%), pneumonia (12.2%), urinary system complications (11.4%), electrolyte and acid–base imbalance (3.3%), gastrointestinal bleeding (1.3%), and pulmonary embolism (1.1%) (Table 2). Patients who had previously undergone SOT had higher rates of some medical complications compared to the control group, such as chronic renal failure (P < .001P), pneumonia (P < .001P), venous thromboembolism (P = .036P), electrolyte and acid–base imbalance (P = .003P), urinary system complications (P < .001P), gastrointestinal bleeding (P = .023P), and transfusion (P < .001).

The rate of mechanical complications was 2.57% and the rate of PJI was 2.57% in SOT patients. Mechanical complications were less common among SOT patients compared to the control group (3.7%), but the rate of PJI was higher compared to the control group (1.4%) (P = .035) (Table 2).

Chronic renal failure was more common among patients who had undergone kidney transplants compared to the liver transplant group (45.9% vs. 10.9%, P = .001). There was no significant difference between transplanted organs in terms of other complications (Table 3).

Mortality

The in-hospital, 1-month, 3-month, and 1-year mortality rates of the SOT group were 1.8%, 3.7%, 7.5%, and 13.8%, respectively. Table 4 shows the in-hospital, 90-day, 1-year, and overall mortality rates of the SOT and control groups by type of surgery.

Revisions

The total revision rate was 4.5% for the SOT group and 3.9% for the control group, and the difference was not statistically significant (P = .472P). Considering the revision rates according to transplant types, the revision rate was 5% among kidney transplant patients and 4% among liver transplant patients (P = .664).

History of a fracture (HR: 4.82, 95% CI: 1.66-13.97, P = .004P), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (HR: 1.7, 95% CI: 1.17-2.46, P= .005P), and age (HR: 1.034, 95% CI: 1.01-1.05, P< .001) were identified as predictive factors for 90-day mortality in SOT patients.

Patients who underwent HA had a significantly higher mortality rate compared to TKA and THA (17.5%, 1.1%, and 1.7%, respectively; P < .001) (Table 5). There was no significant difference in the 90-day mortality rate between liver transplant patients and kidney transplant patients (10.9% and 7.1%, respectively; P = .174).

Discussion

This large cohort study demonstrated that a history of fracture is the most important factor affecting 90-day mortality in SOT patients undergoing joint arthroplasty. In addition, HA was found to be more highly associated with mortality.

One of the first studies on arthroplasty procedures for transplant patients described a 22% mortality in this group.18 In another study, the mortality rate was reported as 26.8%.11 On average, deaths occurred approximately 3.83 years after surgery, with the primary cause of death typically being associated with the transplanted organ rather than the joint prosthesis.11 Varatharaj et al19 also reported that the mortality rate was 4.13 times higher in SOT patients compared to a control group. Brown et al11 reported that the risk of perioperative mortality was greatly increased (OR: 7.4). In our study, the 90-day mortality rate was 7.5%. While this constitutes a lower mortality rate compared to some previous studies, other studies have reported much lower mortality rates. Navale et al12 showed that there was no difference in in-hospital mortality rates between SOT patients and a control group.

Quinlan et al20 reported mortality rates of 1.42% in kidney transplant patients, 2.83% in non-kidney transplant patients, and 1.81% for all SOT patients. Likewise, another study reported a 1-year mortality rate of 1.9%.21 The cases of SOT patients are complex due to their comorbidities, susceptibility to infection, drug usage, and immunosuppression. Given the nature of the transplant population in general, the higher mortality rate in the transplant cohort is not surprising. Nevertheless, we believe that arthroplasty is beneficial for these patients and requires close follow-up. Solid organ transplantation patients, who have a higher risk of complications and mortality, require more careful attention.

Almost all studies to date reporting mortality in this population were based on data from elective arthroplasties. In the study by Ledford et al,22 both THA and HA patients were examined, but mortality was reported for the total patient group. Hsiue et al23 reported the mortality rates of HA and THA performed for SOT patients as 1.8% and 2.1%, respectively, with no significant difference between them (HR: 0.91, 95% CI: 0.24-3.45, P = .891P). In our study, we showed that the mortality rate of HA patients was much higher compared to elective surgeries (P < .001P). However, in-hospital mortality rates were higher in SOT patients compared to the non-SOT control group after HA (P = .006). It was to be expected that this mortality rate was higher because HA patients underwent operations without optimization secondary to trauma.

Post-transplant bone loss and increased risk of fracture are consequences of post-transplant immunosuppression regimens and accompanying comorbidities. Undesirable conditions in the bones may differ according to the affected organs, and various diagnostic and therapeutic approaches may be required.4 Fracture patients constituted 31.3% of our SOT patient group, and mortality was observed in 18.4% of the fracture patients within 90 days after surgery. These patients necessitate close postoperative monitoring, and physicians should remain attentive to potential complications. Ensuring the follow-up of these patients in larger centers where access to relevant specialties can be facilitated may lead to a reduction in complication rates.

Blood transfusion rates are of substantial clinical importance for SOT recipients. Overall, the transfusion rate in this population is still unclear. In a cohort study involving 3334 SOT patients who underwent primary TKA, the incidence of blood transfusion was noted to be 30%, which was statistically higher compared to that of the matched control group (23%).6 Wu et al24 showed that the transfusion rate in TKA patients who had undergone lung transplants was 50%. In another study, transplant patients had a higher risk of requiring blood transfusions (OR: 1.51, 95% CI: 1.18-1.94, P = .001).23 Douglas et al10 found no significant difference in transfusion rates in their study comparing liver transplant and non-transplant patients. In our study, the transfusion rate was 61.4% in the non-transplant group and 71.2% in SOT recipients (P = .001). The high transfusion rate among our patients may be associated with the high number of HA procedures due to trauma. Optimizing preoperative anemia, using tranexamic acid, and refining surgical strategies may reduce the blood transfusion rate among SOT recipients undergoing joint arthroplasty.

Proper perioperative protocols and attention to the individual patient’s medical history improved patient outcomes and survival among SOT patients.25 A landmark year for the total number of solid organ transplants performed in the United States was 2021. Notably, since 2010, the numbers of kidney, liver, heart, and lung transplants had risen by 44%, 47%, 63%, and 42%, respectively.26 However, the lifelong necessity of taking immunosuppressant drugs after transplantation increases surgical and medical complications in these groups of patients. Recent studies revealed a high risk of osteonecrosis and osteoporotic fractures among these patients, which may require joint arthroplasty surgery.5,27 Within our patient population, it was observed that the incidence of surgical interventions related to osteonecrosis was comparatively high compared to the control group.

The current literature reports varying results in terms of medical and surgical complications in SOT patients compared to control groups. In a study of 513 liver transplant recipients, Douglas et al10 found no difference in the rates of readmission, revision, complications, or cost after THA compared to a control group. The study by Brown et al11 found no significant differences regarding infection or readmission rates between the SOT and control cohorts. In the study by Klement et al,6 which included 3334 SOT patients and a control group, significantly higher rates of postoperative MI (2.3% vs. 1.6%), respiratory failure (2% vs. 0.8%), deep venous thromboembolism (5.1% vs. 3.2%), PJI (2.3% vs. 0.9%), and TKA revision (0.7% vs. 0.4%) were found at 90 days after TKA among the SOT patients. In the current study, complication rates among the SOT patients were 2.5% for mechanical complications, 2.5% for PJI, 39.8% for chronic renal failure, 12.4% for venous thromboembolism, 12.2% for respiratory disorder, 11.4% for urinary system complications, 3.3% for electrolyte and acid–base imbalance, 1.3% for gastrointestinal bleeding, and 71.2% for transfusion, and these complication rates were significantly higher compared to those of the control group. These patients, impacted by the complexities of immunosuppression and organ transplantation, necessitate a comprehensive understanding of potential postoperative complications.

The mean hospital length of stay of our SOT patients was 5.9 days. This is slightly higher than the average values reported by other studies in the literature.5,12,20 Navale et al12 reported that the mean perioperative hospital stay for SOT patients undergoing hip arthroplasty was 4.47 days. Moreover, Quinlan et al20 reported that the mean hospital stay for patients who underwent THA after osteonecrosis of the femoral head due to SOT history was 4.13 days. We suggest that the longer length of stay of SOT patients is generally related to their complex comorbidities, the need for multidisciplinary decision-making with other specialists about those comorbidities, and coordinated management with transplantation services. However, the longer hospital stays observed in Türkiye compared to previous studies in the literature is a reason to reconsider the management of these patients at the national level.

There are several limitations to the present study. As this was a database study, we were limited in terms of the availability of online documented and reviewed data. Hence, the study could have been affected by potentially incomplete or inaccurate data in the e-health database. We tried to overcome that risk by matching the patients of the SOT group with control patients who did not have a history of SOT at a 1 : 2 ratio based on age, sex, and CCI via propensity score matching. The inclusion of matched patients contributed greater strength compared to previous non-matched comparative studies involving larger patient cohorts in the literature. Although we did not have access to ASA scores via this database, we calculated CCI scores to evaluate the effect of comorbidities on mortality. Furthermore, we did not investigate the types of immunosuppressive drugs and doses that may potentially affect certain complications. These heterogeneous drug groups were not analyzed separately, and this may be considered in future studies. Additionally, grouping HA patients with elective surgeries can also be considered a limitation. We thought that it might be useful to consider studies together with a specific patient group, such as organ transplant patients, and patients were also evaluated in their own groups. To better address this important point, future analyses may consider evaluating HA as a separate group and examining its outcomes in more detail.

Conclusion

The overall number and burden of SOT procedures will continue increasing together with the number of joint arthroplasty surgeries performed. The risk of complications and mortality is high among SOT patients undergoing joint arthroplasty, particularly in the case of patients with fractures. Team management, meticulous perioperative optimization of modifiable comorbidities, blood preservation strategies, and close monitoring after surgery may help reduce the risk of complications and mortality. Further studies are needed to evaluate the long-term outcomes of joint arthroplasty in SOT patients.

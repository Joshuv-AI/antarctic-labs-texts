Isolation and functional evaluation of Lacticaseibacillus casei HUMB07381 isolated from traditional khiki cheese as a potential probiotic candidate
Authors: Pegah Namazi, Behrooz Alizadeh Behbahani, Mohammad Noshad, Alireza Vasiee, Morteza Taki, Hossein Jooyandeh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study presents the isolation and molecular identification of a Lacticaseibacillus casei strain isolated from traditional Khiki cheese, which showed 99% 16 S rRNA gene sequence similarity to Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381. To assess its probiotic potential, a comprehensive evaluation was conducted including morphological, physiological, and biochemical characterization. The analyses covered carbohydrate fermentation patterns (49 substrates), enzymatic activity, survival under acidic conditions (pH 2.5, 4.0, and 5.5), and tolerance to bile salt concentrations (0.3–0.7%). The strain’s capacity to inhibit Staphylococcus aureus biofilm formation and gene expression was also investigated. Antimicrobial activity of neutral and acidic cell-free supernatants (nCFS and aCFS) was determined against Escherichia coli, Salmonella typhimurium, Shigella dysenteriae, Bacillus cereus, Listeria monocytogenes, and S. aureus via agar disc diffusion, agar well diffusion, double-layer, minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC), and minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC) assays. The isolate was identified as Gram-positive, rod-shaped, catalase-negative, non-spore-forming, non-motile, and gas-negative. It exhibited versatile carbohydrate fermentation, significant enzymatic activity, and survival under simulated gastrointestinal conditions (pH, bile salts, NaCl, and 25–40 °C). Moreover, the strain showed strong cell-surface hydrophobicity, auto-aggregation, co-aggregation, cholesterol reduction, and inhibition of S. aureus adhesion and biofilm formation. It also displayed antimicrobial activity against pathogens, in vitro cytotoxic effects on HT-29, HeLa, and MCF-7 cell lines, and pronounced antioxidant activity. Thus, Lcb. casei HUMB07381 represents a promising candidate for functional probiotic dairy products.Keywords: Lactic acid bacteria, Antimicrobial activity, Biofilm inhibition, Caco-2 adhesion, Antioxidant activitySubject terms: Biochemistry, Biotechnology, Microbiology

Introduction

After extensive investigations and various studies, the United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has granted several lactic acid bacteria (LAB) strains the GRAS (Generally Recognized As Safe) status, while the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) evaluates microbial safety using the QPS (Qualified Presumption of Safety) framework. As a result, increasing attention has been directed toward LAB and their potential benefits in the development of functional food products. The most important LAB can be obtained by isolating species of Leuconostoc, Weissella, Lactobacillus, Lactococcus, Streptococcus, Bifidobacterium, and Pediococcus, which naturally inhabit fermented foods as well as the human respiratory, reproductive, and gastrointestinal tracts1,2. Probiotics are live microorganisms that, when administered in adequate amounts, confer health benefits to the host. The search for novel probiotic strains has increasingly focused on traditional fermented foods, which harbor diverse and well-adapted microbial communities shaped by long-term spontaneous fermentation. Such environments often yield microorganisms capable of surviving harsh physicochemical conditions and demonstrating health-promoting properties4,5. Regular consumption of live probiotics at a level of 10⁷ colony forming unit (CFU)/mL can enhance the immune system, prevent inflammation, infections, and various cancers, reduce cholesterol levels and allergies, improve the absorption of minerals and vitamins B and K, facilitate lactose digestion, and improve overall gastrointestinal health3. Probiotics exhibit antimicrobial activity against pathogenic microorganisms through competitive exclusion for nutrients and adhesion sites, alteration of pathogen metabolism, and stimulation of the host immune system5. In addition to the aforementioned mechanisms, probiotics are capable of fermenting and utilizing various carbohydrates and, therefore, can produce antimicrobial compounds such as bacteriocins, diacetyl, organic acids, and hydrogen peroxide6.

The surface characteristics of probiotic bacteria play a crucial role in their adhesion to intestinal epithelial cells, a prerequisite for stable colonization within the gastrointestinal tract. Effective attachment to the intestinal surface enables these bacteria to establish themselves, competitively interact with native microbiota and pathogens, and contribute to the formation of a protective biological barrier. Therefore, adhesion to host cells is considered one of the key criteria for the selection and evaluation of probiotic strains7. Traditional Khiki cheese, a handmade dairy product produced in rural regions of Iran, represents an ecologically rich and relatively unexplored niche for the isolation of potential probiotic bacteria. It is typically made from raw milk without pasteurization and fermented naturally in animal skin bags (Khik), allowing the development of a complex indigenous microbiota. This fermentation process provides selective pressure favoring acid-tolerant and metabolically versatile lactic acid bacteria. Despite its traditional importance, little research has been conducted on the probiotic potential of microorganisms from Khiki cheese, leaving a clear gap in knowledge for functional dairy microbiology. Lacticaseibacillus species, as representatives of LAB, are widely used in the production of probiotic and functional foods such as probiotic yogurt. These bacteria are Gram-positive, spherical in shape, anaerobic, non-spore-forming, catalase-negative, oxidase-negative, and non-motile3,8. Lacticaseibacillus spp. are capable of growing at temperatures of 30–40 °C and at pH values of 5.5–8, and they constitute a significant portion of the human intestinal microbiota3,9. Among the isolates obtained from Khiki cheese, one strain identified as Lcb. casei showed particularly promising preliminary characteristics, including strong acid and bile tolerance and notable antimicrobial activity. Given that Lcb. casei is a well-established species in probiotic research—known for its resilience in gastrointestinal conditions and capacity to exert beneficial effects—this specific isolate was selected for comprehensive functional characterization. Focusing on this strain allows better understanding of how bacterial species originating from traditional dairy products can serve as effective probiotic candidates in modern formulations. The presence of antimicrobial compounds such as peptides, enzymes, and organic acids in the cell-free supernatant (CFS) obtained from these bacteria enables Lacticaseibacillus spp. to inhibit the growth of pathogenic microorganisms and suppress oxidation processes10. Therefore, the CFS of Lacticaseibacillus spp. is considered a promising agent for anti-biofilm activity, inhibition of pathogen gene expression, preservation of the quality of functional food products, and improvement of human health9.

Assessment of the antibiotic resistance profile is one of the main safety criteria for probiotic strains, as the presence of transferable resistance genes can pose a risk of horizontal gene transfer to other bacteria. Although the likelihood of such transfer occurring in the gastrointestinal tract has been reported to be low, regulatory authorities such as EFSA still consider it an essential parameter for monitoring. On the other hand, intrinsic and non-transferable resistance to certain antibiotics may provide an advantage when probiotics are administered concurrently with antibiotic therapy. Therefore, evaluating antibiotic susceptibility and resistance is crucial for determining both the safety and the potential clinical application of a probiotic strain11,12.

In this study, the lactic acid bacterium Lacticaseibacillus casei (formerly Lactobacillus casei) was investigated, and the main objective was to evaluate the probiotic properties of the strain Lcb. casei HUMB07381. In the first phase, the strain was assessed using a series of basic characterization assays, including morphological, physiological, and biochemical traits; carbohydrate fermentation patterns; enzymatic activity; tolerance to acid and bile; growth under different NaCl concentrations and temperatures; survival under simulated gastrointestinal conditions; and cell surface characteristics such as hydrophobicity, co-aggregation, and auto-aggregation.

Subsequently, a broad range of functional and safety-related assays was conducted to establish a comprehensive probiotic profile of the selected strain. Inhibition of Staphylococcus aureus adhesion to Caco-2 cells, antimicrobial activity, anti-biofilm effects, and downregulation of pathogen-associated gene expression were evaluated to determine its antagonistic potential against pathogenic bacteria. Cytotoxicity assessment using various cell lines and antibiotic susceptibility testing were performed to address safety considerations. Furthermore, antioxidant activity, total phenolic and flavonoid contents, and cholesterol-lowering ability were analyzed to explore its potential health-promoting benefits. Together, these complementary experiments provided a coherent framework for assessing the strain’s probiotic functionality and applicability in food and biomedical contexts.

Materials and methods

Isolation and molecular identification of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381

In the present study, the target isolate was obtained from Khiki cheese. Following the method of Saboktakin-Rizi et al.8, cheese samples were randomly collected and stored at 4 °C. Five grams of cheese were homogenized with 45 mL of 0.1% peptone water, and serial dilutions (10− 1 to 10− 6) were prepared and plated on MRS agar. Selected colonies were subjected to Gram staining and catalase testing, followed by biochemical identification using the VI kit (SinaClon Co., Tehran, Iran). After confirming typical characteristics such as Gram-positive and catalase-negative behavior, molecular identification was performed. Genomic DNA was extracted from overnight cultures in MRS broth. The 16 S rRNA gene was amplified using universal primers 27 F (5′-AGAGTTTGATYMTGGCTCAG-3′) and 1492R (5′-GGTTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3′). The obtained 16 S rRNA gene sequence showed 99% similarity to Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 (GenBank accession number KX851530), which was identified as the closest reference sequence in the NCBI BLAST database.

Carbohydrate fermentation profiling (API 50 CHL) of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381

According to the manufacturer’s protocol with minor modifications, the carbohydrate fermentation profile was determined using the API 50 CHL system in combination with the API 50 CHL medium (bioMérieux, Marcy l’Étoile, France). Following the method of Tayeb et al.13, a bacterial suspension with a turbidity of 0.5 McFarland was prepared, and under sterile conditions, each well of the API 50 CHL strip was inoculated with 100 µL of the prepared suspension. Notably, each well of the API 50 CHL strip contained 49 carbohydrate substrates along with one negative control. To ensure anaerobic conditions and prevent evaporation, sterile mineral oil was added, and the strips were incubated at 37 °C for 48 h. Fermentation reactions were evaluated visually by observing color changes in the medium. A shift from blue-green (alkaline) to yellow (acidic) indicated a positive fermentation reaction and acid production. Reactions were recorded at 24 and 48 h as positive (+), weak/delayed (±), or negative (−). In the present study, the results of the fermentation of 49 different carbohydrates (API 50 CHL) were independently recorded by two researchers, and consensus scores were used to establish biochemical profiles based on the enzymatic activity patterns. Identification of the strain was performed by comparing the obtained data with the API database (apiweb™, bioMérieux).

Enzymatic activity assessment (API ZYM) of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381

This assay was performed according to the method of Fitri et al.14. Using the API ZYM system (bioMérieux, Marcy l’Étoile, France), enzymatic activities were evaluated semi-quantitatively. After preparing a bacterial suspension adjusted to a turbidity of 0.5 McFarland, each well of the API ZYM strip was filled with 65 µL of the standardized suspension. Following incubation for 4 h at 37 °C, one drop each of the ZYM A and ZYM B reagents was added sequentially to each well. The appearance of a specific color indicated enzymatic activity. The intensity of the resulting color was compared with the API ZYM color chart and scored semi-quantitatively on a scale from 0 (no activity) to 5 (maximum activity). The results were compared with the API database (apiweb™, bioMérieux), and strain identification was confirmed after validating the activity patterns and their consistency with the reference profiles.

Morphological, physiological, and biochemical characterization of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381

The strain was incubated at 37 °C for 48 h after being cultured on MRS agar. Following incubation, catalase activity and gas production were assessed as physiological indicators of the strain. In addition, the strain’s growth on MRS agar was evaluated under varying conditions. Specifically, growth was examined across a range of temperatures and pH values . Salt tolerance was also assessed at different NaCl concentrations at 30 °C15.

Acid tolerance of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381

This assay was performed according to the method described by Barzegar et al.16. The strain was first cultured in MRS Broth at 37 °C for 18–24 h under anaerobic conditions. Following centrifugation at 6000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C and two washes with phosphate buffer, the bacterial pellet was resuspended in the same buffer (pH 7.0) to obtain a suspension with an optical density (OD) of 0.6 at 600 nm. Subsequently, the pH of the resulting suspension was precisely adjusted to 2.5, 4.0, and 5.5 using 1 N HCl. The prepared suspensions were incubated at 37 °C for 0, 1, 2, and 3 h. Finally, the viable bacterial counts were determined using the serial dilution method on MRS Agar plates and expressed as log CFU/mL.

Bile salt tolerance of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381

The activated strain was harvested by centrifugation at 2000 × g for 5 min at 9 °C, washed with sterile phosphate buffer, and re-centrifuged. A 100 µL aliquot of the bacterial suspension was inoculated onto MRS agar supplemented with 0.3%, 0.5%, and 0.7% (w/v) bile salts. After incubation for 0, 1, 2, and 3 h at 37 °C under anaerobic conditions, the survival rate was quantified by determining the viable cell count (CFU/mL). Serial dilutions were plated on MRS agar, incubated for 24–48 h, and the colonies were counted to calculate the survival percentage under each bile salt concentration.

Evaluation of the growth rate of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 at various NaCl concentrations

To evaluate the salt tolerance of the studied strain, 100 µL of the overnight bacterial culture (1% v/v) was inoculated into 10 mL of MRS broth containing 2%, 3.5%, and 5% NaCl. The cultures were incubated anaerobically at 37 °C for 24 h. After incubation, the growth capacity under each salt concentration was determined by measuring the OD of the cultures at 600 nm17.

Evaluation of the growth rate of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 at various temperatures

This assay was performed according to the method by Ismail et al.26. After activation of the Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381, incubation was performed anaerobically for 48 h at temperatures of 25, 30, 35, and 40 °C. After incubation, the growth capacity under each temperature was determined by measuring the OD of the cultures at 600 nm17.

Evaluation of the viability of the Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 under simulated gastrointestinal tract conditions

This assay was performed according to the method described by Zareali et al.18 with slight modifications. After activation of the Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 strain, the cell pellet was obtained and resuspended in phosphate buffer (pH 7.4). The optical density of the suspension was measured at 600 nm using a spectrophotometer (PGI, England), and the cell concentration was standardized to an OD600 of approximately 0.108. To simulate gastrointestinal conditions, the pH of the suspension was first adjusted to 2.0 using 1 N HCl, and pepsin was added at a final concentration of 0.3%. The mixture was then incubated at 37 °C for 1.5 h. Subsequently, the pH was adjusted to 2.5 using 1 N NaOH and incubation continued for 2 h at the same temperature. The pH was then increased to 7.5, followed by the addition of bile salts to a final concentration of 0.3%, and the suspension was further incubated at 37 °C for 4 h. Serial dilutions were then prepared and surface-plated on MRS agar. After incubation at 37 °C for 48 h, the number of colonies formed was counted. The survival rate of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 was calculated relative to the control sample, which consisted of untreated bacterial culture without exposure to the simulated gastrointestinal conditions.

Evaluating the antimicrobial efficacy of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381

The focus of this investigation was the antimicrobial prowess of the strain Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 against a panel of six major bacterial pathogens: three Gram-negatives (Escherichia coli (ATCC 25922), Salmonella typhi (ATCC 14028), and Shigella dysenteriae (PTCC 1188)) and three Gram-positives (S. aureus (ATCC 25923), Bacillus cereus (ATCC 14579), and Listeria monocytogenes (ATCC 19115)).

Cell-free supernatant (CFS) preparation

The Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 was cultured in MRS broth with a 1% (v/v) inoculum and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h to reach the late stationary phase with an approximate cell density of 108 CFU/mL. The culture was then centrifuged at 60,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C to separate the cells. The resulting CFS was sterilized using a 0.22-µm syringe filter. Subsequently, the CFS was lyophilized and stored at 4 °C until further analyses.

Antimicrobial assessment via agar well diffusion and agar disc diffusion methods

According to the methods described by Alizadeh Behabahani et al. and Mousanejadi et al., the lyophilized CFS was reconstituted in an appropriate volume of sterile distilled water to obtain a final concentration of 500 mg/mL. Subsequently, half of the CFS was acidified to pH 4.5 using 1 N HCl to generate the acidified cell-free supernatant (aCFS), while the pH of the remaining portion was adjusted to neutrality with 1 N NaOH to obtain the neutralized cell-free supernatant (nCFS)19,20. For the disk diffusion assay (DDA) and well diffusion assay (WDA), 100 µL of the standard microbial suspension at a concentration of 1.5 × 108 CFU/mL was spread onto the surface of the culture medium. In the DDA, sterile 6-mm discs previously impregnated with 70 µL of aCFS or nCFS were placed onto the inoculated surface. In the WDA, after creating 6-mm wells, each well was filled with 70 µL of the CFS preparations. The plates were then incubated at 37 °C for 48 h, and the diameters of the inhibition zones around the discs and wells were measured and reported in millimeters.

Evaluation using the modified double-layer method

In this study, the agar overlay assay was performed according to the methods reported by Alizadeh-Behbahani et al. and Echresh et al.19,21. Briefly, MRS medium containing the activated strain was first prepared. Subsequently, the surface of the plates was overlaid with 10 mL of semi-solid nutrient medium (containing 0.8% agar) inoculated with each of the activated pathogenic strains to form a uniform layer. The plates were then incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. After incubation, the diameters of the inhibition zones around the growth of pathogenic bacteria were measured and recorded in millimeters (mm).

Determining the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC)

According to the method described by Alizadeh-Behbahani et al.22, 100 µL of various concentrations of the CFS of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 was first added to the wells of a 96-well microplate. Subsequently, 20 µL of the bacterial suspension of each pathogenic strain, adjusted to the turbidity of 0.5 McFarland, was inoculated into each well. Wells containing only the pathogenic bacteria without CFS served as the positive control, while wells containing the CFS without the pathogenic inoculum were used as the negative control. After incubation for 24 h at 37 °C, 10 µL of triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TTC) solution (5 mg/mL) was added to each well to assess the growth of the pathogens. The plate was incubated for an additional 15 min. Finally, the first well showing no red or purple color development was considered the MIC.

Determining the minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC)

After determining the MIC, the wells that showed no turbidity in the previous step were streaked onto MHA plates and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. The plates were then examined for any visible bacterial growth. The lowest concentration of the CFS that produced no observable colony formation on the agar surface was recorded as the MBC23.

Investigation of biofilm formation-related gene expression in S. aureus

To evaluate the ability of the CFS derived from Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 to inhibit biofilm formation by S. aureus, the expression of biofilm-associated genes was assessed using real-time quantitative PCR (qRT-PCR). A suspension of S. aureus at a concentration of 105 CFU/mL was prepared and inoculated into microplates. Subsequently, CFS at a final concentration of 50% (v/v) and with its pH adjusted to 6.2 was added to the wells, followed by incubation for 24 h at 37 °C. Wells containing TSB medium without CFS served as the negative control. After incubation, the formed biofilm was washed three times with PBS, and the adherent cells were collected for RNA extraction. The S. aureus cells were lysed using a buffer containing 1 M Tris-Cl, 0.5 M EDTA, and 10% Triton™ X-100, and the lysate was incubated for 30 min at 37 °C. Subsequently, 1 mL of cold TRIzol reagent was added, and total RNA extraction was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA integrity and purity were confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis, and 100 ng of purified RNA was used for cDNA synthesis using the SensiFAST cDNA Synthesis Kit24. Investigating the cytotoxic effect of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 on cancer cell lines

The cytotoxicity of the Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 CFS was evaluated against three human cancer cell lines (HT-29, HeLa, and MCF-7) obtained from the Iranian Biological Resource Center (IBRC, Tehran, Iran) using the standard MTT assay24. Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 0.1% penicillin–streptomycin. Cells were seeded into 96-well microplates at a density of 3 × 103 cells per well and incubated for 48 h at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2 to allow cell attachment. After cell adhesion, the culture medium was replaced with fresh DMEM containing different concentrations of CFS (0-500 mg/mL). The plates were then incubated for 24 h under the same conditions. Subsequently, MTT solution (5 mg/mL) was added to each well and incubated for 3 h to allow the formation of formazan crystals. The supernatant was carefully removed, and 200 µL of dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) was added to dissolve the formazan crystals. The absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader. Cell viability was calculated relative to the untreated control, and the IC50 value was determined as the concentration of CFS required to reduce cell viability by 50%.

Evaluation of antibiotic susceptibility of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 to various antibiotics

Suspension of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 was prepared according to the 0.5 McFarland standard. Then, 100 µL of the bacterial suspension was evenly spread over the surface of the appropriate agar medium. Commercial antibiotic discs, including Imipenem, Ampicillin, Erythromycin, Ciprofloxacin, Nalidixic Acid, Nitrofurantoin, and Chloramphenicol, were aseptically placed on the inoculated plates using sterile forceps. The plates were incubated under anaerobic conditions at 37 °C for 24 h, after which the diameter of the inhibition zones around each disc was measured and recorded3.

A volume of 500 µL of CFS (50 mg/mL) from Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 was mixed with 0.75 mL of 5% sodium nitrite, 150 µL of 10% aluminum chloride solution, and 1 mL of 1 M sodium hydroxide. The mixture was maintained at room temperature for 10 min, after which the absorbance was measured at 510 nm. The results were expressed as milligrams of quercetin equivalent per gram of sample (mg QE/g).

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed using three independent biological replicates, with technical replicates included when necessary. Only quantitative data were subjected to statistical analysis. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Differences among groups were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (one-way ANOVA), followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for multiple comparisons at a significance level of p < 0.05. Qualitative and binary data were reported descriptively without inferential statistical analysis. Different lowercase letters in figures indicate statistically significant differences between groups (p < 0.05). All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 22.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

The carbohydrate fermentation profile of Lab. casei HUMB07381 was determined using the API 50 CHL system to elucidate the strain’s metabolic capability toward a broad array of carbohydrate substrates. One of the important physiological characteristics of LAB is their ability to ferment carbohydrates and produce valuable metabolites such as organic acids, antimicrobial peptides (bacteriocins), diacetyl, and various enzymes21. In the present study, the capability of the Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 to ferment a wide range of carbohydrates is considered a highly important and impactful technological trait for industrial applications35. The results of this section are in complete agreement with the findings of Tayeb et al.; they also reported that various Lcb. casei strains possess a high potential for fermenting diverse sugars such as glucose, fructose, and galactose13. Furthermore, as pointed out by Gamostap et al., the ability of LAB to ferment common monosaccharides and disaccharides serves as a key foundation for their widespread utilization in various biological and fermentation processes35. Beyond simple sugars, the ability of this strain to metabolize a broad spectrum of complex carbohydrates indicates the presence and expression of key hydrolytic enzymes (such as β-glucosidases). From an ecological perspective, this metabolic diversity significantly enhances the bacterium’s adaptability within the human gut microbiome, as the strain is capable of utilizing oligosaccharides and prebiotic compounds as carbon sources36. From a technological standpoint, this metabolic flexibility positions the HUMB07381 strain as a valuable and robust candidate for diverse bioprocesses and fermentation applications37.

The enzymatic profile of LAB is a primary determinant of their technological and functional efficacy in fermentation processes, directly driving the hydrolysis of complex substrates, the synthesis of organic acids, and the enhancement of organoleptic properties in dairy products37. The Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 possesses a highly robust and versatile hydrolytic machinery. The pronounced activity of α- galactosidases and β -galactosidases underscores the strain’s exceptional proficiency in lactose hydrolysis, positioning it as an ideal candidate for developing highly digestible, functional dairy products tailored for lactose-intolerant individuals. Complementing this, the expression of α- galactosidases and β -galactosidases highlights its extended capacity to metabolize complex carbohydrates, thereby facilitating fermentation kinetics37,38. Beyond carbohydrate metabolism, the significant leucine arylamidase activity indicates a potent proteolytic capacity; by degrading proteins into bioactive peptides and free amino acids, it fundamentally contributes to nutritional enrichment and the development of complex flavor profiles. Synergistically, the presence of esterase and acid phosphatase activities signifies a strong lipolytic potential, driving the degradation of lipids into volatile aromatic compounds39. Ultimately, the integration of these glycolytic, proteolytic, and lipolytic pathways demonstrates that the HUMB07381 strain not only substantially improves the digestibility of food matrices but also serves as a highly valuable biotechnological asset for optimizing texture, aroma, and flavor in the industrial production of fermented dairy products such as yogurt and cheese.

Evaluating phenotypic, morphological, and biochemical characteristics is the fundamental first step in identifying LAB. The findings of the current study revealed that the investigated strain consists of Gram-positive, rod-shaped (bacilli), non-motile, and catalase-negative bacteria. These structural features are in strict accordance with the report by Ismail et al. (2018) regarding the characteristics of the LAB strain (SK-1) isolated from kefir17. From a physiological and metabolic perspective, the absence of the catalase enzyme is a prominent and taxonomic hallmark of LAB. Due to their inability to synthesize heme groups and the lack of a cytochrome system in an electron transport chain, these bacteria rely entirely on fermentative metabolism and lack a cellular respiration pathway. Consequently, they do not produce a true catalase enzyme to hydrolyze hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) into water and molecular oxygen (O2)17. In the present study, the absence of gas bubbles (oxygen) following the exposure of colonies to the hydrogen peroxide reagent definitively confirmed the catalase-negative nature of the strain, reflecting the anaerobic or aerotolerant nature of this microorganism40.

The ability to survive the harsh acidic environment of the stomach is a fundamental prerequisite for probiotic strains to ensure they reach the intestines in a viable and active state41. In the present study, although the viability of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 decreased with increasing acidity, the strain successfully maintained its population at the required therapeutic threshold (approximately 106 CFU/mL) even under extreme acidic conditions (pH 2.5). This remarkable acid tolerance can be attributed to the intrinsic defense and adaptation mechanisms of LAB. According to previous research, the F1F0 − ATPase enzyme complex plays a pivotal role in regulating intracellular pH and maintaining cellular homeostasis by actively extruding protons from the cell. Furthermore, the presence of structural polysaccharides in the cell envelope acts as a protective physical barrier, mitigating cellular damage induced by severe acid stress (pH<3pH) in the gastrointestinal tract. These findings are in strong agreement with the existing literature; for instance, a similar study reported that LAB strains possess a robust capacity to survive, adapt, and maintain their physiological functions across varying pH levels and acidic conditions3. Therefore, this robust acid tolerance not only guarantees the strain’s successful colonization and clinical efficacy within the host’s gut but also highlights its potential as an ideal and stable candidate for industrial applications in highly acidic fermented products.

LAB have the ability to survive in bile salt concentrations ranging from 0.3% to 0.7% (w/v) in the gastrointestinal tract, particularly in the duodenum3. The remarkable resistance of the studied strain within this concentration range, and the maintenance of a viable population, demonstrates its high potential for successful colonization in the small intestine. Mechanistically, it has been reported that LAB can detoxify bile salts by producing the enzyme bile salt hydrolase (BSH), which deconjugates them into less toxic forms. This process not only protects the bacterial cell membrane against oxidative stress but also contributes to host cholesterol reduction through the precipitation of free bile acids. In addition to enzymatic activity, biochemical modifications in the membrane structure (such as altering the fatty acid profile to reduce permeability) and the activation of specific efflux pumps are other complex mechanisms that synergistically counteract the severe detergent effects of bile salts42. Accordingly, a previous study reported that a Pediococcus acidilactici strain maintained its growth and viability at a 0.3% bile salt concentration29. The current findings, which indicate robust survival and structural integrity even at higher bile concentrations (0.5%and 0.7%), are in full agreement with these reports, further confirming the ecological adaptability of this strain for probiotic applications.

Thermal adaptability and tolerance to adverse environmental conditions are crucial characteristics for evaluating the efficacy of probiotic strains in industrial and clinical applications. The favorable thermal flexibility of the Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 and achieving an optimal growth rate at 35 °C fully align with the mesophilic nature of lactic acid bacteria (LAB), which generally exhibit an optimal growth range between 30 and 40 °C3. This characteristic is not only significant from the perspective of fermentation processes but also indicates the high capability of this strain to survive and actively proliferate at the physiological temperature of the human gastrointestinal tract, which is a fundamental prerequisite for its successful colonization and probiotic functionality in the gut17,43. In addition to thermal stability, tolerance to osmotic stress, such as the presence of high salt (NaCl) concentrations, is another key indicator in assessing the cellular robustness and ecological competitiveness of LABs, particularly in the genera Lactobacillus, Pediococcus, and Leuconostoc. The ability to grow in environments with high salt levels reflects the bacterium’s osmotic adaptation capacity, which is likely regulated through mechanisms such as the accumulation of osmoprotectants and structural changes in the cell membrane36. This aligns with findings from previous studies, such as the research by Ismail et al. (2018), which reported that lactic strains like SK-1 and SK-4 are capable of sustaining growth across a wide range of optimal NaCl concentrations (between 5 and 30%)17. The synergy of these two traits—stability against gastrointestinal thermal stress and resistance to osmotic stress—makes the HUMB07381 strain a highly resilient and efficient candidate, capable of maintaining its performance and viability both during harsh industrial processing conditions (such as the production of salty fermented products and cheeses) and within the complex ecosystem of the gastrointestinal tract.

The remarkable ability of the Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 to tolerate simulated gastrointestinal conditions, particularly its capacity for recovery and the significant increase in its survival rate up to 77.0% in the intestinal phase following severe acidic stress in the gastric phase, constitutes a fundamental biological prerequisite for manifesting its therapeutic effects and successful colonization in the intestinal environment. This post-stress recovery phenomenon indicates unparalleled metabolic flexibility and the bacterium’s ability to rapidly repair acid-induced membrane damage, alongside physiological adaptation to bile salts through mechanisms such as the expression of stress shock proteins and the modulation of cell membrane fluidity. In addition to these intracellular mechanisms, the success of this strain can be attributed to extracellular defensive strategies44. According to previous studies, LAB can significantly enhance their structural resistance by utilizing physical protective barriers such as exopolysaccharides (EPS). By encapsulating the bacterial cell, these polymeric structures protect it against the destructive effects of proteolytic enzymes like trypsin, pepsin, and proteinase K in the upper gastrointestinal tract, the presence of bile salts in the small intestine, and the severe drop in gastric pH, thereby ensuring robust survival capability under simulated gastrointestinal conditions45,46. Therefore, the highly favorable performance of the HUMB07381 strain in the intestinal phase is likely the result of a synergy between cellular repair mechanisms and the protective layers of EPS.

LAB have the ability to protect the gastrointestinal tract against pathogenic bacteria and their associated inflammations due to the production of antimicrobial substances such as hydrogen peroxide, bacteriocins, organic acids, and polysaccharides47. The markedly higher sensitivity of Gram-positive bacteria, such as S. aureus and L. monocytogenes, compared to Gram-negative pathogens like E. coli and Sh. dysenteriae to the metabolites of the Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381, originates from the anatomical and structural differences in their cell envelopes. The lipopolysaccharide (LPS) outer membrane in Gram-negative bacteria acts as a highly selective permeability barrier, preventing the entry of macromolecular antimicrobial compounds (such as bacteriocins) or hydrophobic metabolites into the periplasmic space48. Furthermore, the significantly superior antimicrobial activity of the aCFS over the nCFS highlights the synergistic and crucial role of organic acids (particularly lactic and acetic acids). In their un-dissociated state, organic acids are lipophilic and readily diffuse across the lipid membrane of pathogens; upon entering the neutral cytoplasm, they dissociate, leading to the accumulation of protons. This causes a drastic drop in intracellular pH, the collapse of the proton motive force (PMF), disruption of the electrochemical gradient, denaturation of essential enzymes, and ultimately, target cell death43. Nevertheless, the substantial retention of inhibitory properties in the nCFS proves the secretion of acid-independent bioactive compounds by this strain, providing a multi-layered and comprehensive defense system against enteric pathogens. In accordance with the current findings, a study reported that Lcb. casei IMAU60214 and Lcb. casei Shirota strains were capable of inhibiting the growth of pathogenic bacteria such as S. aureus, S. typhimurium, and E. coli49. In the present study, it was found that the diameter of the inhibition zone using the agar well diffusion method was significantly larger than that of the agar disk diffusion method. This difference can be attributed to the direct contact of antimicrobial agents with microorganisms as well as the more dynamic diffusion and uniform distribution of a larger volume of metabolites within the agar matrix in the agar well diffusion method50. The microbiological findings in the current study utilizing double-layer, MIC, and MBC methods were consistent with the results of the agar disk diffusion and agar well diffusion assays. This significant concordance between quantitative evaluations (low MIC and MBC concentrations) and qualitative assessments (extensive inhibition zones) testifies to the simultaneous bacteriostatic and bactericidal potential of this strain, indicating that Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 holds immense promise as a potent biopreservative agent and a gut microbiome modulator for suppressing gastrointestinal infections effectively.

The qRT-PCR results demonstrated that exposure of S. aureus to the cell-free supernatant of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 was accompanied by reduced transcription of several genes related to antibiotic resistance and virulence. Previous studies have shown that organic acids and carbohydrate-derived metabolites produced by LAB strains can alter gene-expression patterns of pathogens under controlled conditions51,52. In this study, the marked reduction in mecA expression reflects a strong transcriptional response in the resistant strain, without implying a specific mechanistic interaction. The concurrent decrease in agr and its downstream genes spa and hla is reported solely as a transcriptional observation. Moreover, the reduction in icaA expression is presented only as a molecular change, with no inference regarding PNAG synthesis or structural biofilm features27. Previous work has reported reductions in adhesion-related gene expression in Lcb. casei-treated staphylococci, consistent with our observations54. Accordingly, this section is fully separated from the biofilm findings, with no causal links inferred.

Functional biofilm assays in this study demonstrated that the cell-free preparations of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 were able to inhibit the initial adhesion of S. aureus and the early stages of biofilm formation. These observations represent purely phenotypic effects under in-vitro conditions and showed that the anti-adhesion activity of this strain was dose-dependent, with some concentrations also producing a notable reduction in mature biofilms. Such effects are consistent with previous reports describing the role of organic acids and biosurfactants produced by LAB in modifying surface properties and weakening the cohesion of the biofilm matrix53. Moreover, the combined pattern of inhibiting biofilm formation and attenuating its structure is considered a recognized strategy among Lactobacillus strains54. In addition, earlier studies have reported that Lcb. casei derivatives can reduce biofilm structure and adhesion of S. aureus under laboratory conditions54. Unlike mature S. aureus biofilms, which typically exhibit high resistance due to their dense matrix, the present findings indicate that the preparations of this strain could reduce the structural integrity of the biofilm under experimental conditions.

The ability to adhere to intestinal epithelial cells is a fundamental prerequisite for probiotic strains to colonize the gastrointestinal tract and exert their beneficial effects. By blocking cellular receptors, the adhesion of pathogenic bacteria and subsequent infection can be prevented. LAB prevent the attachment of pathogens to intestinal cells by binding to gastrointestinal receptors and occluding them55,56. LAB compete with pathogenic bacteria for nutrients and binding sites, leading to the inhibition of adhesion or their displacement. In accordance with the findings of the present study, which demonstrated the significant superiority of the competition mechanism over other mechanisms, it can be inferred that the Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 possesses a very high prophylactic potential. The remarkable effectiveness in the competition mechanism, where the probiotic and pathogen are introduced simultaneously, suggests that this strain has a high affinity for receptors on the surface of Caco-2 cells and can bind to these sites more rapidly and strongly than S. aureus. This robust binding creates a form of “steric hindrance,” which effectively denies the pathogen the opportunity to attach57. Conversely, the lower efficacy in the displacement mechanism, where the probiotic is added after the pathogen has already adhered, indicates that while the strain is capable of removing pre-attached pathogens, its strength in this regard is more limited. This functional difference is a strain-specific characteristic, highlighting that the primary anti-adhesion strategy of this strain is to preemptively occupy binding sites rather than to treat an established infection. This finding is consistent with reports stating that LABs at high concentrations show a greater affinity for receptors55 and emphasizes that regular consumption of this probiotic can establish an effective microbial barrier against colonization by pathogens.

Cell surface hydrophobicity, auto-aggregation, and co-aggregation are among the most critical physicochemical and biological indicators used to evaluate the ability of probiotics to stably adhere to the host epithelium, form protective microcolonies, and physically prevent pathogen colonization. LABs require cell surface hydrophobicity to interact with other cells56. In the present study, the evaluation of these parameters reveals the high potential of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 to establish itself effectively in the gastrointestinal tract and compete with pathogens. The relatively high hydrophobicity of this strain, particularly its significant affinity for chloroform, indicates the presence of surface glycoproteins, teichoic acids, and other specific structural components in its cell wall that play an essential role in forming hydrophobic bonds with host epithelial receptors58. This characteristic is the necessary first step for stable probiotic colonization, allowing the bacterium to overcome electrostatic repulsive forces and persist in the intestinal mucosa. Furthermore, the time-dependent, increasing trend in the auto-aggregation rate (demonstrates the excellent capability of this strain to form dense microcolonies and establish a robust physical barrier on mucosal surfaces59. This cellular aggregation not only ensures the survival of the probiotic under harsh environmental conditions, such as gastric acid and bile salts, but it also masks adhesion sites on host cells, thereby preventing pathogens from accessing these receptors (competitive exclusion). On the other hand, the co-aggregation capability of probiotics serves as a key defense mechanism to entrap pathogenic bacteria and facilitate their clearance from the intestinal environment. The results showed that the HUMB07381 strain exhibited the highest co-aggregation rate with S. aureus. This direct physical interaction between the probiotic and the pathogen prevents the pathogen from adhering to host tissues while simultaneously placing the pathogenic bacterium in close proximity to high concentrations of antimicrobial metabolites produced by the probiotic, such as organic acids, bacteriocins, and hydrogen peroxide, resulting in a much more effective inhibition60. In a study, it was reported that the Levilactobacillus brevis HL6 strain had a surface hydrophobicity of 53.48%, auto-aggregation of 35.20%, and co-aggregation of 23.10%61. Abdulla et al. (2014) reported that L. plantarum strains possessed a surface hydrophobicity of 65%, auto-aggregation of 15–23%, and co-aggregation of 18–42%62. Comparing the findings of the current research with previous studies demonstrates that the HUMB07381 strain possesses a superior, or at least highly competitive, adhesion and aggregation profile. The unique combination of high hydrophobicity for host attachment, robust auto-aggregation for the formation of protective biofilms, and specific co-aggregation with dangerous pathogens such as S. aureus and B. cereus introduces this strain as a highly efficient probiotic candidate that promotes intestinal health and combats bacterial infections through multiple physical and biochemical mechanisms.

The cholesterol-lowering potential is a key functional attribute for the selection of probiotic strains. In this study, the ability of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 to remove cholesterol from the culture medium was evaluated. The findings indicated that the efficiency of this process is highly dependent on the presence of bile salts. This pronounced bile-dependent effect strongly suggests that the primary mechanism of cholesterol reduction by this strain is linked to the activity of the Bile Salt Hydrolase (BSH) enzyme and the subsequent co-precipitation of cholesterol with deconjugated bile acids. The BSH enzyme catalyzes the hydrolysis of conjugated bile salts into free bile acids63. These free acids are less soluble at the physiological pH of the intestine and tend to precipitate. During this precipitation process, cholesterol molecules become entrapped and are removed from the soluble phase along with the bile acids64. The significant increase in cholesterol removal observed exclusively in the presence of bile serves as strong biochemical evidence for this hypothesis. Furthermore, a secondary mechanism may also be involved, which includes the assimilation and incorporation of cholesterol into the bacterial cell membrane. This process, which accounts for the basal cholesterol removal in the absence of bile, helps maintain membrane fluidity and integrity, especially under the osmotic stress induced by bile salts. The presence of bile may alter the lipid structure of the membrane, potentially enhancing its capacity to incorporate cholesterol and thus contributing to the overall removal efficiency65. The cholesterol reduction rate observed for the HUMB07381 strain is significant and competitive with results reported for other lactobacilli strains known for their high cholesterol-lowering capabilities.

In the present study, the Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 demonstrated measurable cytotoxic effects against the investigated cancer cell lines. However, it should be noted that this study was limited to cancer cell models and did not include normal cell lines; therefore, the selectivity of the observed cytotoxic activity cannot be determined. The observed differences in sensitivity among the tested cell lines may reflect variations in cellular physiology, metabolic activity, and susceptibility to microbial metabolites rather than a strictly tissue-specific mechanism. Previous studies have reported that metabolites produced by lactic acid bacteria, including organic acids, short-chain fatty acids, bacteriocin-like peptides, and other bioactive compounds, may contribute to anti-proliferative effects in cancer cells66,58. Although the present study did not identify the specific active fraction responsible for the cytotoxic activity, the observed effects may be associated with extracellular metabolites present in the cell-free supernatant. Consistent with our findings, previous studies have also reported cytotoxic effects of metabolites derived from lactic acid bacteria. For example, exopolysaccharides from Lactobacillus helveticus MB2-1 were shown to induce apoptosis in HT-29 cells through ROS- and mitochondria-related pathways67. Similarly, another study demonstrated that LAB suspensions could inhibit cancer cell proliferation depending on concentration and cell type68.

One of the concerns of the scientific communities is the resistance of LAB to antibiotics. The absence of a target site, alteration in membrane permeability, antibiotic inactivation, and efflux pumps can lead to this problem. In the present study, the broad and significant susceptibility of the Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 to a diverse spectrum of antibiotics, including cell wall synthesis inhibitors (Imipenem and Ampicillin), protein synthesis inhibitors (Erythromycin and Chloramphenicol), and nucleic acid synthesis interfering agents (Ciprofloxacin and Nalidixic Acid), indicates the lack of acquired drug resistance and the high biosafety of this strain for clinical and industrial applications. The highest level of sensitivity was observed against Imipenem, which suggests the high permeability of the bacterial membrane and the effective binding affinity of the drug to penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs)69. In contrast, the relatively lower sensitivity to Ampicillin is primarily attributed to the intrinsic structure of the cell wall polysaccharides of lactic acid bacteria and does not indicate acquired resistance or the alarming production of beta-lactamases. The key significance of this pharmacological profile is that the absence of unusual resistances rules out the likelihood of harboring transferable resistance genes (located on plasmids or transposons), thereby minimizing the risk of horizontal gene transfer (HGT) to opportunistic pathogens within the gut microbiome70. This characteristic strictly ensures the strain’s compliance with biosafety standards (QPS/GRAS). In this regard, the susceptibility of LAB strains isolated from a type of yogurt was investigated by Hassan et al. (2020), and they reported that one of the main reasons for the safe application of various LAB strains in the food industry and the production of functional foods is their relative sensitivity and the absence of transferable resistance to common therapeutic antibiotics71.

The assessment of the antioxidant potential of Lcb. casei strain HUMB07381 using chemical assays showed that the CFS of this strain is capable of scavenging free radicals and inhibiting lipid peroxidation under laboratory conditions. These activities are consistent with the measured levels of phenolic and flavonoid compounds, as these metabolites act as electron and hydrogen donors through the hydroxyl groups present in their cyclic structures, enabling them to neutralize synthetic radicals such as DPPH and ABTS72. The inhibition of oxidation in the β-carotene/linoleic acid system further indicates that the secreted metabolites present in the CFS can reduce lipid oxidation in this chemical model. Although the radical-scavenging capacity observed in this study was lower than that of the pure chemical standard (ascorbic acid), the presence of phenolic and flavonoid compounds in the supernatant may account for the activities observed in these in vitro assays73,74. Lactic acid bacteria may convert complex phenolic compounds into simpler forms through their enzymatic systems, and this process could contribute to the presence of active metabolites in the CFS. However, the obtained results only demonstrate the ability of the supernatant to reduce or scavenge radicals in chemical assays, and should not be interpreted as evidence of physiological effects, redox homeostasis regulation, or tissue protection under in vivo conditions75. Overall, these data solely reflect the antioxidant activity observed in vitro, and additional mechanistic or in vivo studies are required to evaluate any potential biological or functional implications.

Conclusion

In the present study, the probiotic-related characteristics of an isolated Lcb. casei strain showing 99% 16 S rRNA gene sequence similarity to strain HUMB07381 (GenBank accession number KX851530) were evaluated through morphological, physiological, biochemical, and functional analyses. The strain exhibited typical lactic acid bacteria features and demonstrated several technological traits relevant to dairy fermentation, including the ability to utilize a range of carbohydrates and favorable enzymatic activities. Under laboratory conditions, the strain showed tolerance to low pH, bile salts, NaCl concentrations, and different temperatures, as well as survival under simulated gastrointestinal conditions. The cell-free supernatant also exhibited in vitro antimicrobial activity against several pathogenic bacteria, antioxidant activity in chemical assays (DPPH and ABTS), cholesterol reduction ability, and inhibitory effects on S. aureus biofilm formation and related gene expression. In addition, auto-aggregation, co-aggregation, and adhesion-inhibitory effects on Caco-2 cells were observed. These findings suggest that Lcb. casei with similarity to strain HUMB07381 possesses several promising technological and functional properties under in vitro conditions. However, these results should be interpreted as preliminary laboratory observations. Further studies, including genomic safety assessment, detailed metabolite characterization, validation in food matrices, and in vivo investigations, are required before considering practical probiotic or functional food applications.

Efficacy and safety of PD-1 inhibitors combined with anti-angiogenic agents in patients with recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Jingjing Gao, Dan Zong, Yiyue He, Jingshu Xu, Xia He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesPD-1 inhibitors have shown significant progress in treating recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma (R/M NPC); however, the optimal second-line regimen remains unclear. Studies have suggested that the combination of PD-1 inhibitors and anti-angiogenic agents shows synergistic potential in advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma. This meta-analysis aims to assess the efficacy and safety of this combination therapy.Materials and methodsWe performed an extensive search of PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science databases up to May 13, 2025. Stata 18.0 was used to analyze the eligible data.ResultsEight clinical trials involving 271 cases were included. The objective response rate (ORR) was 49% (95% CI: 39–59%), the disease control rate (DCR) was 80% (95% CI: 74–85%), the one-year progression-free survival rate (1-year PFS) was 39% (95% CI: 29–49%), and the one-year overall survival rate (1-year OS) was 73% (95% CI: 58–85%). The most common grade 3–4 adverse event was hypothyroidism, occurring in 36% of patients. Additionally, further subgroup analysis showed that combination therapy was more effective in patients who had not previously received PD-1 inhibitors (P = 0.003).ConclusionIn summary, PD-1 inhibitors combined with anti-angiogenic agents exhibited notable effectiveness with a manageable safety profile. Additional phase III randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are needed to confirm these findings.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04049-y.

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a common malignant tumor that is closely associated with Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) infection, with approximately 95% of cases classified as undifferentiated non-keratinizing carcinomas [1]. In China, the annual standardized incidence rate was 3 per 100,000 cases in 2018, with a male-to-female ratio of 2.5:1 [2]. Due to the insidious and invasive characteristics of NPC, over 70% of patients are diagnosed with locally advanced disease or distant metastases at their initial consultation [3]. Even after standard treatment, 20–30% of patients experience recurrence or distant metastasis, with palliative systemic chemotherapy using platinum-based regimens still being the primary treatment option [4–6]. In 2021, three large randomized controlled multicenter phase III clinical studies (CAPTAIN-1st, JUPITER-02, RATIONALE-309) [7–9] collectively established gemcitabine plus cisplatin (GP) combined with PD-1 inhibitors as the first-line standard treatment option for R/M NPC. However, there is no consensus on the optimal treatment regimen for advanced patients following the failure of first-line therapy.

Recent years have seen the promise of combining immune checkpoint inhibitors with VEGF/VEGFR inhibitors for treating R/M NPC. Interestingly, the interaction between tumor cells and their microenvironment has been increasingly viewed through an ecological lens, where NPC is not just a genetic disorder but a dynamic pathological ecosystem. In this context, tumors are considered as part of a “multidimensional spatiotemporal unity of ecology and evolution”, with cancer cells acting as invasive species within an altered environment, and the microenvironment playing a crucial role in tumor progression and resistance [10]. By combining immune checkpoint inhibitors with anti-angiogenic therapies, the aim is not only to boost immune activity but also to normalize the tumor vasculature, thereby enhancing the infiltration of immune cells and improving treatment efficacy.

Several clinical trials have demonstrated the potential of combining immune checkpoint inhibitors with VEGF/VEGFR inhibitors for the treatment of R/M NPC. However, these studies have been limited by small sample sizes and inconsistent results. For this reason, we conducted this meta-analysis to summarize the available data and assess the antitumor activity and safety of this combination therapy, providing valuable references for clinical treatment.

Materials and methods

This analysis has been registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO), identifier CRD42024592205. It was conducted in accordance with PRISMA guidelines.

Literature search strategy

We performed a comprehensive search across four databases: PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science, with a cutoff date of May 13, 2025. The subject terms included “immune checkpoint inhibitors”, “PD-1 inhibitor”, “anti-angiogenic agents”, “vascular endothelial growth factor”, “recurrent”, “metastatic” and “nasopharyngeal carcinoma”, as well as the corresponding free terms. The complete search strategy is provided in the Supplementary Material.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion Criteria: (1) patients with a pathological diagnosis of R/M NPC; (2) patients who have failed prior treatment regimens (platinum-based or PD-1 inhibitors); (3) treatment regimen involving PD-1 inhibitor therapy combined with anti-angiogenic agents; (4) comprehensive details on efficacy outcomes and the occurrence of adverse events must be provided.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) patients with other malignant tumors; (2) treatment regimens that included drugs other than combination therapy; (3) publications categorized as reviews, conference abstracts, retrospective studies, case reports, letters, or meta-analyses.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Detailed data extracted included the first author, publication year, study design, treatment protocol, drug dosage, PD-L1 positivity rate, prior treatment regimen, median age, 1-year PFS, 1-year OS, ORR, DCR, any adverse events (AEs), and grade ≥ 3 AEs. The primary endpoint was ORR, while the secondary endpoints included DCR, 1-year OS, 1-year PFS, and the rate of adverse events by grade. All data were independently extracted by two investigators.

We utilized the methodological index for non-randomized studies (MINORS) [11] to assess the quality of seven single-arm studies, and the Cochrane Risk of Bias assessment tool [12] was used to assess the RCT (Table 1 and Supplementary Fig. S1). Discrepancies were resolved through consultation with a third investigator.

Statistical analysis

This analysis was performed using Stata 18.0 software (Stata Corporation, College Station, Texas, USA), with I² statistics employed to measure heterogeneity. Given that all included studies were single-arm trials and displayed significant heterogeneity, we utilized random-effects models to calculate all pooled effect sizes (ESs). In addition, we assessed publication bias using Egger’s test and Begg’s test.

Results

Eligible studies and characteristics

We identified a total of 191 articles using the search terms mentioned above. After excluding duplicates, 131 articles remained, and 112 articles were excluded after reviewing titles and abstracts, 19 articles remained for full-text assessment. Finally, Eight studies satisfied the inclusion criteria for the meta-analysis [13–19]. In one of the selected studies, two distinct cohorts presented respective results, so we treated them as two independent studies. The included studies were seven single-arm clinical trials and one RCT, encompassing a total of 271 patients. Among these, 156 patients received camrelizumab with apatinib, 33 patients received sintilimab with bevacizumab, 18 patients received camrelizumab with famitinib, 40 patients received toripalimab with anlotinib, and 24 patients received pembrolizumab with bevacizumab. Efficacy

In this meta-analysis, we evaluated the effectiveness of combining anti-angiogenic agents with PD-1 inhibitors in the treatment of R/M NPC. ORR and DCR data were available for all 271 patients. The results revealed that the combination therapy achieved an ORR of 49% (95% CI: 39–59%) and a DCR of 80% (95% CI: 74–85%) (Fig. 2A and B). Six studies with 214 patients reported the pooled 1-year PFS and 1-year OS, and the 1-year PFS was 39% (95% CI: 29–49%),1-year OS was 76% (95% CI: 65–85%) (Fig. 3A and B). These results were comparable to those observed in R/M HNSCC patients who underwent similar treatment [20].

Subgroup analyses and sensitivity

To clarify the source of heterogeneity, subgroup analyses were conducted according to PD-L1 expression assessed by Combined Positive Score (CPS), prior treatment regimens, different combination regimens, and the presence of liver metastasis. Seven of the eight included studies provided detailed insights into the prior treatment modalities and their associated efficacy [13–19]. Among these patients, 165 received only platinum-based chemotherapy, while 72 patients received concurrent treatment with PD-1 inhibitors. Subgroup analyses revealed an ORR of 57% (95% CI 46–67%) (I² = 41.22%, P = 0.15) for patients who failed platinum therapy and 33% (95% CI 23–45%) (I² = 0.00%, P = 0.98) for those who failed PD-1 inhibitor therapy, indicating a significant difference between the two subgroups (P = 0.003) (Fig. 4A). A comparable trend was observed in the analysis of 1-year PFS, with rates of 47% (95% CI 38–56%)(I² = 0.00%, P = 0.73) for platinum therapy failures versus 23% (95% CI 12–36%)(I² = 0.00%, P = 0.67) for PD-1 inhibitor therapy failures (P = 0.003) (Fig. 4B), but no significant difference was observed between the DCR and the 1-year PFS rate in this subgroup (supplementary Fig. S2). Additionally, PD-L1 positive subgroup and the liver metastasis subgroup demonstrated superior anti-tumor activity, as detailed in the supplementary Fig. S3. Moreover, subgroup analyses of different combination treatment regimens suggested that the efficacy of other combination regimens was comparable to that of the apatinib plus camrelizumab subgroup (supplementary Fig. S4).

The results of the subgroup analysis indicate that prior treatment regimens may be a potential source of heterogeneity. The sensitivity analysis results showed that excluding any study did not affect the outcomes. This indicates that the study is robust, as shown in Supplementary Fig. S5.

Treatment-related adverse events (AEs)

Since two studies (Yuan, Li 2023(a), Yuan, Li 2023(b)) did not report their adverse effects separately, the adverse events from both studies were combined. Across the eight studies, major adverse events included neutropenia, anemia, AST elevation, ALT elevation, albuminuria, fever, fatigue, headache, rash, nasopharyngeal necrosis, epistaxis, reactive capillary hyperplasia, hypertension, hypothyroidism, constipation, and pneumonia. Common grade 1–2 adverse events included hypothyroidism (36%), fatigue (29%), anemia (25%), albuminuria (26%), and AST elevation (23%). The most frequent grade 3–4 adverse events were nasopharyngeal necrosis (12%) and hypertension (7%). Furthermore, three studies additionally reported immune-related adverse events. The meta-analysis results showed that the most common grade 1–2 immune-related adverse events were reactive capillary hyperplasia (35%) and hypothyroidism (34%), while severe adverse events included rash (2%) and fever (2%). No fatal toxicities were observed. A complete summary of all adverse events is provided in Table 3.

Publication bias

Egger’s and Begg’s tests were used to qualitatively assess publication bias. The outcomes for ORR, DCR, 1-year PFS, and 1-year OS suggested a minimal impact of publication bias on the study results, as detailed in Supplementary Table S1.

Discussion

According to our present understanding, this analysis is the first comprehensive evaluation of the effectiveness and safety of combining PD-1 inhibitors with antiangiogenic agents for the treatment of R/M NPC.

For patients who experience progression after first-line treatment, the standard therapeutic strategy remains uncertain. The efficacy of monotherapies, either PD-1 inhibitors (ORR: 21–26%, DCR: 45–58%) or anti-angiogenic agents (ORR: 6.1%−36.4%, DCR: 28.6%−60%), is suboptimal [21, 22]. In contrast, the results of this study indicate that combination therapy demonstrates superior anti-tumor activity compared to single-agent treatments, suggesting that the combination therapy offers promising benefits for advanced cases that have previously failed treatment.

Angiogenic growth factors inhibit tumor endothelial cell activation by pro-inflammatory cytokines, resulting in decreased expression of endothelial adhesion molecules (EAMs) [23]. Since EAMs facilitate leukocyte adhesion, extravasation, and infiltration into the tumor parenchyma, the down-regulation of EAMs compromises anti-tumor immune responses and accelerates tumor immune evasion [24]. Meanwhile, ICIs can rejuvenate the immune-supportive tumor microenvironment and promote vascular normalization [25, 26].

These findings suggest that this synergy exemplifies the combined antitumor effects of PD-1 inhibitors and antiangiogenic agents, which have demonstrated significant potential in various other solid tumors [27–30].

Ding et al. (2023) conducted the first prospective study evaluating the effectiveness of camrelizumab combined with apatinib in patients who had failed platinum-based treatments [15]. The subgroup analysis of different combination drugs highlights the comparable benefits of integrating various ICIs with anti-angiogenic agents, emphasizing their importance as key elements in the therapeutic regimen.

The mechanisms of resistance to PD-1 checkpoint blockade are complex. Tumor cells evade immune surveillance through mechanisms such as down-regulating expression of MHC-I molecules and other antigen-presentation enzymes (e.g., β2-microglobulin, adenosine deaminase) [31, 32]. Tumor cells can also diminish the immune response by inducing Treg cells and tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) to secrete inhibitory cytokines (e.g., IL-10, TGF-β) [33, 34]. In addition, high PD-L1 expression may amplify the “braking” effect on T cells, thereby contributing to immune resistance [35]. As gemcitabine, cisplatin (GP), and PD-1 inhibitors have become the new first-line standard treatment regimens for R/M NPC, a growing number of advanced patients experience progression after immunotherapy. Therefore, improving the prognosis of this patient group has become an urgent challenge. For this reason, we conducted a subgroup analysis of patients who progressed after treatment with PD-1 inhibitors. The results indicated an ORR of 33% (95%CI: 32–45%), similar to the previously published results (ORR 31.25%) and superior to chemotherapy alone (ORR 22.68%) [36]. Recently, Jiang et al. (2024) published the first real-world retrospective study on the combination of anlotinib with a PD-1 inhibitor for treating refractory head and neck tumors resistant to immunotherapy. Their findings further confirmed the clinical benefit of combination therapy in this resistant population [37]. The above results suggest that the combination of PD-1 inhibitors with anti-angiogenic therapy could be a promising treatment option for R/M NPC patients following first-line PD-1 inhibitor-based regimens. This conclusion requires confirmation through more extensive phase III prospective trials.

Previous studies indicated that R/M NPC patients with liver metastases generally have a poorer prognosis [38, 39]. However, in the current meta-analysis, the combination therapy demonstrated a better ORR in patients with liver metastases (ORR 60%). Studies have shown that metastatic tumors can activate CD8 + T cells from systemic circulation. However, in the liver, antigen-specific FAS + CD8 + T cells undergo apoptosis upon interacting with FASL + CD11b + F4/80 + monocyte-derived macrophages. This process contributes to the formation of a systemic immunosuppressive (immune-desert) environment in preclinical models. Similarly, patients with liver metastases exhibit decreased peripheral T-cell counts, reduced tumor-infiltrating T-cell diversity, and impaired T-cell function, all of which are associated with poorer prognosis and limited efficacy of immune monotherapy. As previously mentioned, anti-angiogenic agents can help counteract this immunosuppressive environment by promoting the infiltration and activation of immune cells, such as CD8 + T cells and monocytes, within the tumor microenvironment. By modulating the tumor’s immune landscape, these agents may enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy in patients with liver metastases, potentially leading to improved survival outcomes [40, 41].

Almost all of the patients included experienced at least one adverse reaction. While the incidence of grade 3–4 adverse events (37%) is higher with combination therapy compared to monotherapy (19% for PD-1 inhibitors and 27.22% for anti-angiogenic agents) [21, 22] (supplementary Fig. S6), the overall incidence remains within a manageable range, which could be reduced by adjusting the dosage of combination therapy. Compared to PD-1 inhibitors alone [21], Grade 1–2 adverse reactions such as ALT elevation (20%), AST elevation (23%), anemia (25%), and fatigue (29%) were slightly more frequent. Nevertheless, the majority of these adverse reactions could be mitigated by adjusting the dosage of combination therapy, and the overall safety remained manageable. Notably, 12% of patients experienced Grade ≥ 3 nasopharyngeal necrosis, which is a key limiting factor for the continuation of maintenance therapy with anti-angiogenic agents. Previous studies have confirmed that risk factors for nasopharyngeal necrosis include radiotherapy doses ≥ 72 Gy, re-irradiation, malnutrition, and a history of smoking [43–46]. Therefore, it is essential to screen high-risk patients in advance and implement timely interventions during the administration of combination therapy programs.

Moreover, although we conducted a meta-analysis on immune-related adverse events, the limited number of included studies and sample sizes may affect the reliability of the results; therefore, this part of the conclusion should be interpreted with caution.

Although the combination therapy shows promise, this meta-analysis has some limitations. First, the majority of included studies were single-arm trials with relatively small sample sizes, limiting the precision of the conclusions. Additionally, due to inconsistencies in the focus of the studies, we were unable to conduct heterogeneity analyses for factors such as age, gender, smoking history and tumor stage. Moreover, the limited number of studies may limit the ability to detect publication bias via Egger’s or Begg’s test. Second, the systemic treatments that patients received varied in drug doses and frequency, potentially influencing overall prognosis and resulting in considerable heterogeneity across the studies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, combining PD-1 inhibitors with anti-angiogenic agents demonstrates promising efficacy with manageable safety in R/M NPC cases who have failed prior treatments. Furthermore, subgroup analyses have revealed that the survival advantage was more pronounced in patients who had not previously received immunotherapy. There is an urgent need for larger phase III randomized controlled trials to confirm our findings.

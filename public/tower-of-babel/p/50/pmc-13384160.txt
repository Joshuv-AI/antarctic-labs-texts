Development and psychometric evaluation of a compassion perception instrument for patients with cancer receiving nursing care: a mixed-methods study protocol
Authors: Yaser Khanchemehr, Eesa Mohammadi, Mitra Khoobi, Anoshirvan Kazemnejad

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionCompassion is a core value in person-centred nursing and is essential in the care of patients with cancer. However, existing measurement instruments often fail to capture the cultural, ethical and relational dimensions of compassion as perceived by patients within specific sociocultural contexts. This study aims to develop and psychometrically evaluate the compassion perception in nursing care (CPNC) instrument for patients with cancer, reflecting the local sociocultural context of cancer care in Iran.Methods and analysisA sequential exploratory mixed-methods design (qualitative→quantitative) will be employed. Background

Compassion in nursing lies at the heart of person-centred care and strongly shapes how patients perceive care quality through their interactions with nurses. It involves conscious presence, respect, empathetic listening and supportive action.1,3 Yet, surveys show that nearly half of patients report not receiving compassionate care to a satisfactory level, underscoring the need to systematically monitor compassion from patients’ perspectives as a key strategy for improving care quality.4 Addressing this gap requires a clear conceptualisation of compassion and its distinction from related constructs in nursing.

Compassion is a multidimensional construct that includes awareness of suffering, an emotional-ethical response and deliberate action to alleviate that suffering. It differs from empathy or sympathy, which lack active engagement.5,7 In clinical contexts, especially chemotherapy and palliative care, patients perceive compassion as being valued, emotionally supported and given hope, all of which contribute to psychological comfort and overall care quality.2 8 Measuring such a complex construct requires instruments with strong construct validity and cultural sensitivity. Because compassion is inherently experiential and relational, evaluating it in oncology nursing care is strengthened by patient-reported measurement grounded in patients’ perspectives.

In Eastern sociocultural contexts, patient-perceived compassion in nursing care is often understood in relational and moral terms; in Iran, it is commonly recognised through attentive presence, respectful communication and responsiveness to suffering.9 In many Western healthcare settings, by contrast, compassion is more often framed as an individual clinician attribute or a set of observable behaviours within systems emphasising autonomy, professional boundaries and standardised care.8 10 These distinctions should be interpreted as general tendencies rather than rigid dichotomies. Although certain core elements of compassion appear to be shared across settings, empirical research confirms context-specific variations in how compassion is understood and expressed. Papadopoulos et al demonstrated that nurses in Greece and Cyprus frame compassionate care through communication, attentiveness to patients’ needs, kindness and culturally nuanced patience.10 Similarly, Sinclair et al showed that patients distinguish compassion from sympathy and empathy, valuing it as a combination of understanding, emotional presence and purposeful action to relieve suffering.11 Therefore, direct translation of instruments developed elsewhere may not fully capture locally salient meanings and may weaken construct validity without adequate cultural adaptation.12 In addition, psychometric studies of adapted compassion measures have shown cross-cultural differences in factor structure, supporting the need for item generation grounded in patients’ lived experiences in this context.12 13

Against this culturally embedded understanding of compassion, existing compassion measures require careful consideration in terms of construct emphasis, intended respondents and clinical scope. Although several compassion measures have advanced the field, important gaps remain when the aim is to assess patients’ perceptions of compassion in nursing care within specific sociocultural contexts, particularly among patients with cancer in the present setting. Available instruments vary in construct emphasis, intended respondents and clinical scope: the Compassionate Care Assessment Tool primarily reflects observable caring behaviours and was not explicitly derived from patients’ qualitative accounts14; the Schwartz Center Compassionate Care Scale, while valuable, was not designed to capture the perceptions of nursing compassion among patients with cancer15 and the Sinclair Compassion Questionnaire, despite being a rigorously developed patient-reported measure, is framed at the broader level of healthcare providers rather than nursing care specifically.3 Although patient-informed approaches have strengthened compassion measure development, a fully validated, context-specific patient-reported instrument to assess perceived compassion in nursing care among patients with cancer in this setting is still lacking.3 Accordingly, this mixed-methods study seeks to develop and evaluate a psychometrically robust and contextually relevant measure.

In this context, assessing patients’ perceptions of nursing compassion is particularly significant.9 16 17 From diagnosis to palliative care, patients with cancer describe compassion as a source of comfort, hope and relief from anxiety and existential distress.3 Yet, the absence of a validated, standardised tool makes it difficult to evaluate the effects of compassion-based interventions.18 A psychometrically sound instrument would allow for systematic assessment of patient perceptions, facilitate evaluation of nurses’ professional performance and guide compassion-focused educational programmes. It would also generate robust data for integrating compassion into nursing education, performance evaluation and policy frameworks.8 15

Given these limitations and the importance of cultural context, this study aims to develop and validate a new instrument to measure perceived compassion in nursing care among patients with cancer. The research adopts a sequential exploratory mixed-methods design grounded in pragmatism. For instrument development, the study will proceed through three sequential stages: qualitative concept elicitation and conceptual model development; questionnaire development and mixed-methods content validity assessment and quantitative psychometric evaluation of the refined compassion perception in nursing care (CPNC) instrument.19,21

Methods/design

Study design

This research adopts a sequential exploratory mixed-methods design (qualitative→quantitative). For instrument development, the study will proceed through three sequential stages. In Stage 1, qualitative exploration will be used to develop a patient-derived conceptual model and operational definition of perceived compassion in nursing care. These findings will guide questionnaire development and content validity assessment in Stage 2. In Stage 3, the refined CPNC instrument will undergo quantitative psychometric evaluation. This sequential process links patients’ lived experiences with instrument development and empirical measurement.21

Rationale for the design

Given the multidimensional and culturally embedded nature of compassion in nursing—particularly from the perspective of patients with cancer—a sequential exploratory mixed-methods design is the most appropriate approach.2 9 The qualitative phase reveals the cultural and conceptual dimensions of compassion based on patients’ lived experiences,19 while the quantitative phase establishes the scientific robustness of the instrument through psychometric testing.22 Integrating both phases within the pragmatist paradigm enables the convergence of interpretive understanding and empirical validation. This approach supports a comprehensive and contextually grounded conceptualisation of compassion and enhances the practical applicability of research findings for nursing education, clinical practice and policy development.21

Study timeline

Purposeful recruitment of participants for the qualitative exploratory stage began in early October 2025 and continued until the target sample size and data sufficiency were achieved. Qualitative data analysis was conducted concurrently with data collection, and the findings from this stage provided the conceptual foundation for questionnaire development and content validity assessment. Quantitative data collection for psychometric evaluation is ongoing and is expected to continue through August 2026. Quantitative data will subsequently be analysed using inferential methods, and the qualitative and quantitative findings will be integrated during the final interpretation. The overall synthesis and interpretation of the study are expected to be completed by October 2026.

Stages of instrument development

This study aims to conceptualise, develop and evaluate the psychometric properties of a nursing compassion measurement instrument using a sequential exploratory mixed-methods design grounded in the pragmatist paradigm. By methodically integrating qualitative and quantitative data, this design enables a deeper and more multidimensional understanding of the concept of compassion, thereby enhancing the validity and comprehensiveness of the research findings.20 For instrument development, the study will proceed through three sequential stages: Stage 1, qualitative concept elicitation and conceptual model development; Stage 2, questionnaire development and mixed-methods content validity assessment and Stage 3, quantitative psychometric evaluation of the refined CPNC instrument. Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of our research. However, patients with cancer will contribute as research participants to concept elicitation and conceptual model development in Stage 1 and to content validity assessment in Stage 2.

Stage 1: qualitative concept elicitation and conceptual model development

The qualitative phase aims to explore and conceptualise how patients with cancer perceive compassion in nursing care. Data will be collected through in-depth, unstructured interviews. All interviews will be audio-recorded, transcribed verbatim and analysed using the conventional content analysis approach described by Elo and Kyngäs.19 The analytic process will include open coding, category development and abstraction to build a coherent and culturally grounded conceptual framework.19 The results will yield a patient-derived conceptual model and operational definition of perceived compassion in nursing care, providing the foundation for questionnaire development in Stage 2.

The qualitative stage will primarily serve as the concept elicitation stage for patient-reported outcome measure (PROM) development. Interview codes, categories and the emerging conceptual model will provide the source material for item generation in Stage 2. After initial item generation, qualitative content validity will be further assessed through cognitive interviews with patients with cancer as part of Stage 2. These interviews will examine whether the items, response options and instructions are understood as intended, whether the items are relevant to patients’ experiences of compassionate nursing care and whether any important aspects of the construct are missing.23 24

Participant selection

Participants will be purposefully selected from patients with cancer who have received nursing care at oncology centres, chemotherapy units and support organisations for patients with cancer. Eligible participants must have a physician-confirmed cancer diagnosis for at least 2 months, the ability to communicate verbally and a willingness to participate voluntarily. To enhance transferability, maximum variation sampling will be applied based on age, gender, education level and illness duration. Recruitment will continue iteratively until data saturation is achieved. In accordance with Consensus-based Standards for the Selection of Health Measurement Instruments (COSMIN) development standards, at least 20 patients with cancer will be interviewed during the qualitative concept elicitation phase.23 Recruitment may continue beyond this minimum if needed to achieve data saturation, with an anticipated sample of approximately 20–25 participants.25

Data collection

Prior to the interviews, a brief preliminary observational phase was conducted in the oncology wards to enhance contextual understanding of the care environment and nurse-patient interactions. During this phase, field notes were used to document relevant contextual observations and to complement the individual interviews, which constitute the primary method of data collection.

Individual face-to-face interviews will be conducted in private and quiet settings, such as counselling rooms at the chemotherapy centre or the local cancer support association, to ensure participant comfort, privacy and confidentiality. All interviews will be conducted by the first author, a PhD candidate in nursing with experience in oncology nursing care and prior training in qualitative interviewing. Each interview will begin with a brief self-introduction, an explanation of the study purpose, assurance of confidentiality and written informed consent for participation and audio-recording.

The interviews will begin with a broad, open-ended question, such as ‘Can you describe how you feel about the nursing care you receive?’, to facilitate free narration. In keeping with an unstructured, in-depth interview approach, the interviewer will use participant-led and neutral probing questions to clarify meanings, deepen understanding and elicit patients’ perceptions and interpretations of compassionate nursing care. Illustrative probes include: ‘What did that nursing action or behaviour mean to you?’, ‘Could you explain that feeling or perception in more detail?’, ‘Could you give an example?’ and ‘Is there anything else you would like to add?’ If participants experience fatigue or if further clarification is required, follow-up interviews will be arranged in coordination with them. All interviews will be audio-recorded and transcribed verbatim. Concurrent reflexive field notes will be taken to document contextual details, non-verbal cues and the interviewer’s reflections, thereby supporting analytical depth and helping to minimise interviewer bias.

Trustworthiness of the qualitative component

In this instrument development study, strategies to enhance the trustworthiness of the qualitative component will be applied specifically to support conceptual model development and the patient-derived foundation for subsequent item generation and content validity assessment.23 24 26 Credibility will be strengthened through maximum-variation sampling, concurrent data collection and analysis, participant-led probing and research-team review of codes and emerging categories.26,28 Dependability and confirmability will be supported through verbatim transcription, reflexive field notes, cross-checking of codes and categories and documentation of analytical decisions in an audit trail.26 27 Transferability will be addressed through concise contextual descriptions of participants and care settings.26 28 The final domains and candidate items will be mapped back to participants’ narratives to ensure that the developing instrument reflects patients’ experiences and provides an appropriate basis for assessing item relevance, comprehensiveness and comprehensibility during the subsequent content validity phase.23 24

Stage 2: questionnaire development and content validity assessment

The development and psychometric evaluation of the CPNC instrument will be conducted and reported in accordance with COSMIN recommendations for PROMs, including standards for PROM development and content validity,23 24 as well as quality criteria for evaluating measurement properties of health status questionnaires.29 The patient-derived conceptual model and operational definition developed in Stage 1 will guide questionnaire development, including measurement objective specification, blueprint development, item generation, response scale and preliminary scoring decisions, cognitive interviewing and content validity assessment, in accordance with COSMIN recommendations for PROM development and content validity.23 24

Response scale and preliminary scoring algorithm

Items on the CPNC instrument will be rated on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Negatively worded items, if retained, will be reverse-scored to ensure consistency in scoring direction. Raw scores will be calculated for the total scale and, where applicable, for each domain. Content validity will be evaluated in terms of relevance, comprehensiveness and comprehensibility for the construct, target population and context of use, in line with COSMIN methodology.23 24 A mixed approach will be used, including patient-based qualitative assessment through cognitive interviews, patient-based quantitative assessment and expert-based content validity assessment. Detailed procedures are described in the face and content validity section.

Item generation

Items will be developed based on the operational definitions of each dimension of the construct—patients’ perceived compassion—as identified through the inductive approach using conventional content analysis in the qualitative phase. The aim is to ensure that the items accurately reflect the cultural dimensions and lived perceptions of patients regarding compassion in nursing care. Whenever possible, patients’ verbatim expressions will be incorporated into item wording to enhance clarity, comprehensibility and content validity. The item development process involves generating an initial item pool, reviewing and refining items with input from the research team, eliminating redundant or ambiguous statements and finalising the items with clear response instructions and a scoring guide. This systematic approach ensures that the resulting instrument is conceptually grounded in patient-derived data and is methodologically rigorous, valid and reliable.

Face and content validity

Face and content validity of the preliminary CPNC instrument will be assessed using a mixed approach from both patient and expert perspectives. In line with COSMIN criteria, content validity will be evaluated in terms of relevance, comprehensiveness and comprehensibility for the construct, target population and context of use.23 24

For qualitative patient-based content validity, cognitive interviews will be conducted with a purposive subsample of patients with cancer after initial item generation, in accordance with COSMIN guidance for evaluating comprehensibility and comprehensiveness during PROM development.23 Using verbal probing techniques, patients will be asked to describe their understanding of the items, response options and instructions; identify unclear or culturally inappropriate wording; comment on the relevance of each item to compassionate nursing care and indicate whether any important aspects of compassion in nursing care are missing. Cognitive interviews will continue until no major new problems related to item interpretation, relevance or missing content are identified. The findings will be used to revise item wording, improve comprehensibility, remove irrelevant or redundant items and ensure adequate coverage of the construct.

For face validity, 10 patients with cancer will evaluate the preliminary version of the CPNC instrument in terms of item clarity, difficulty and apparent relevance.31 32 Their feedback will be used to refine item wording and improve conceptual precision. In addition, item impact scores will be calculated using the clinical impact method, and items with impact scores ≥1.5 will be retained.33 34

For quantitative patient-based content validity, at least 50 patients with cancer will evaluate the draft instrument using a structured questionnaire, consistent with COSMIN recommendations for quantitative content validity studies involving patients.23 Patients will provide separate ratings for each item’s relevance and comprehensibility, as well as its contribution to the comprehensiveness of the relevant domain. Domain-level and scale-level questions will also be used to assess whether the draft instrument comprehensively covers patients’ perceptions of compassion in nursing care and whether any important aspects of the construct are missing.23 24 Open-ended comments will be collected to identify missing content, unclear wording or culturally inappropriate expressions. Patient ratings and comments will be reviewed by the research team and used to revise, remove or retain items before further psychometric testing.

A panel of 10 experts in nursing, psychometrics and cancer care will assess the items for clarity, dimensional relevance, grammar, cultural appropriateness, item placement and scoring. Based on their feedback, necessary revisions will be made to enhance conceptual alignment and clarity. Quantitative content validity will then be examined using three standardised indices: content validity ratio (CVR), Content Validity Index (CVI) and modified Kappa (K):

Stage 3: quantitative psychometric evaluation

Following content validity assessment and refinement, the CPNC instrument will undergo quantitative psychometric evaluation, including item analysis, structural validity, internal consistency, test-retest reliability, measurement error and interpretability assessment.29

Item analysis

Preliminary item analysis will be conducted with a sample of 50 patients with cancer. Corrected item-total correlations will be examined, and items with correlations below 0.30 or negative correlations will be reviewed for revision or removal.13 37

Structural validity

The structural validity of the CPNC instrument will be evaluated using exploratory and confirmatory factor analyses (EFA and CFA) in independent samples. Prior to EFA, sampling adequacy will be assessed using the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin index and Bartlett’s test of sphericity. EFA will be conducted using principal axis factoring with oblique rotation, and factor retention will be informed by parallel analysis, scree plot inspection and conceptual interpretability.13 38 The sample size for each factor-analytic stage will be determined based on the final number of retained items, with at least seven participants per item and no fewer than 100 participants for each analysis; the planned total sample of approximately 300–400 participants will be adjusted if necessary.29

The factor structure identified through EFA and informed by the patient-derived conceptual model will subsequently be examined using CFA. Model fit will be evaluated using the comparative fit index (CFI), Tucker-Lewis index (TLI), root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA) and standardised root mean square residual (SRMR), with CFI or TLI >0.95, RMSEA <0.06 or SRMR <0.08 indicating sufficient structural validity.29 Standardised factor loadings, average variance extracted and inter-factor correlations will additionally be examined as supplementary indicators of factor convergence and distinctiveness. Items with low factor loadings or problematic cross-loadings will be reviewed in conjunction with their conceptual relevance and contribution to construct coverage before decisions regarding retention or removal are made.13 37

Test-retest reliability: test-retest reliability will be assessed over a 2–4 week interval in a subgroup of at least 50 participants. At the second assessment, participants will complete a brief global stability question to identify those reporting no meaningful change in their perception of compassion in nursing care during the retest interval. Reliability will be evaluated among stable participants using a two-way random-effects, absolute-agreement, single-measure intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC). An ICC ≥0.75 will be considered indicative of satisfactory test-retest reliability.29 38

Measurement error

Measurement error will be assessed using test-retest data from participants classified as stable over the retest interval. The SE of measurement and individual-level smallest detectable change (SDC) will be estimated to quantify score precision and the magnitude of change required to exceed measurement error. The SDC will represent the minimum score change that can be interpreted as exceeding measurement error with 95% CI.29

Data analysis

Qualitative data will be analysed manually using a systematic coding process based on the conventional content analysis method developed by Elo and Kyngäs.19 Quantitative data will be analysed using IBM SPSS Statistics and IBM SPSS Amos. Descriptive statistics, including means, SD and frequencies, will be used to summarise the dataset and describe participant characteristics.39 EFA will be conducted to identify latent structures and relationships among items, while CFA will be employed to evaluate the goodness of fit of the measurement model and to assess the alignment of the collected data with the instrument’s conceptual structure.38 39

Ethics and dissemination

Ethics approval was obtained from the Research Ethics Committee of Tarbiat Modares University (IR.MODARES.REC.1404.033). The study will adhere to the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent will be obtained from participants in the Stage 1 qualitative interviews and Stage 2 cognitive interviews. For the quantitative patient-based content validity assessment in Stage 2 and psychometric evaluation in Stage 3, an electronic consent process will be integrated into the online platform; participants will be required to provide active digital consent on the introductory page before accessing the questionnaire items. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, national and international oncology nursing conferences and reports to participating healthcare organisations to inform evidence-based nursing policies.

Discussion

A key strength of this protocol lies in its unique theoretical grounding, focusing on an indigenous conceptualisation of compassion within the local sociocultural context of cancer care, thereby addressing a significant gap left by instruments developed in other settings.12 Methodologically, the study’s adherence to internationally recognised standards (eg, the COSMIN methodology23 24) and the use of inductive content analysis, deriving the construct directly from patient data,19 ensures robustness and cultural relevance. The integration of qualitative insights with quantitative validation aims to produce a psychometrically sound instrument that reflects patients’ lived experiences. The CPNC instrument conceptualises compassion as a cognitive-ethical process, bridging philosophical foundations with clinical application.

However, potential limitations are acknowledged. The qualitative phase relies on purposeful sampling from specific oncology centres, which may limit the initial generalisability of the conceptual framework across all clinical settings. While developed within the local sociocultural context of cancer care, the instrument may require further cross-cultural validation before being applied to different global healthcare environments.12

The development and implementation of the CPNC instrument hold strong potential to guide the design of evidence-based educational programmes, support the assessment of nurses’ professional performance and inform compassion-centred organisational policies within healthcare systems. This protocol represents a foundational step toward developing a contextually grounded measure of perceived compassion in nursing care for patients with cancer in Iran, with the potential to inform future cross-cultural validation studies.

Enhancing societal security: a multimodal deep learning approach for a public person identification and tracking system
Authors: D Yuvasini, S Jegadeesan, Shitharth Selvarajan, Feslin Anish Mon
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn public spaces, threats to societal security are a major concern, and emerging technologies offer potential countermeasures. The proposed intelligent person identification system monitors and identifies individuals in public spaces using gait, face, and iris recognition. The system employs a multimodal approach for secure identification and utilises deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) that have been pretrained to predict individuals. For increased accuracy, the proposed system is implemented on a cloud server and integrated with citizen identification systems such as Aadhar/SSN. The performance of the system is determined by the rate of accuracy achieved when identifying individuals in a public space. The proposed multimodal secure identification system achieves a 94% accuracy rate, which is higher than that of existing public space person identification systems. Integration with citizen identification systems improves precision and provides immediate life-saving assistance to those in need. Utilising secure deep learning techniques for precise person identification, the proposed system offers a promising solution to security threats in public spaces. This research is necessary to investigate the efficacy and potential applications of the proposed system, including accident identification, theft identification, and intruder identification in public spaces.Keywords: Multimodal, CNN, Gait, Face, IRIS, Security, Recognition, Cloud serverSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent times, notable progress has been made in the field of computer vision technology pertaining to the identification of individuals within a crowd. The utilisation of visual media, including images and videos, plays a crucial role in the process of identifying individuals in many contexts, such as security surveillance, traffic monitoring, and adherence to social distancing protocols. The initial stage in identifying individuals in public areas involves the collection of data. During this stage, an extensive collection of images and videos is assembled as a dataset for depicting people in diverse environments.

Once a dataset has been obtained, it becomes imperative to perform data annotation to accurately identify individuals present in each frame. A deep convolutional neural network (DCNN) is trained to accurately identify individuals within visual data, such as images and videos, through the utilisation of a dataset that has been annotated with appropriate labels. The model has the capability to perform real-time people detection in security camera streams. Postprocessing techniques have the potential to enhance personal identification capabilities and facilitate the longitudinal tracking of persons. The present research investigates the many stages involved in person detection, together with recent developments in this field. The task of improving the precision of identifying persons, especially in dynamic and densely populated public areas, poses a significant difficulty. The intricacy of the situation is further exacerbated by the necessity to tackle potential susceptibilities, such as persons endeavouring to undermine the system through the utilisation of contact lenses, facemasks, and other means of impersonation. In considering this challenge, the proposed approach does not exclusively depend on any of the aforementioned identifying strategies. Instead, this approach leverages the precision of each individual model and utilises different combinations of these models to enhance the accuracy in identifying individuals.

This research signifies a ground-breaking endeavour to implement a multimodal identification system inside the disorderly and uncertain setting of a public gathering. The transition from controlled surroundings to public spaces introduces an additional level of complexity and practical challenges.

However, it is important to emphasise the significant advantages of implementing such a system. The capacity to effectively and expeditiously recognise individuals within public settings is a fundamental component of contemporary societal security. The utilisation of this system allows for prompt responses from authorities in a wide range of emergency situations, encompassing both accidents and criminal incidents. Furthermore, notwithstanding apprehensions regarding the security of data, the system’s incorporation with citizen identification systems facilitates prompt provision of life-saving aid to individuals requiring help. However, this study contributes to the advancement of societal security by examining the intricacies of multimodal person identification in public areas. Although there are challenges to overcome, the potential advantages of enhanced safety and efficient emergency response render this undertaking crucial and auspicious.

The technology employs accelerometers and gyroscopes to monitor a person’s body motion to detect their walking patterns, a process known as gait detection. Khan et al.3,4 proposed that iris recognition is becoming increasingly commonplace in security systems and that an individual’s iris can be used to identify them. This research discusses traditional, deep learning, and fusion iris detection techniques. Image fluctuations, occlusion, and eye movements hinder iris identification. Iris recognition is employed for biometric authentication, access control, and healthcare.

Kisku et al.12 discussed that due to their ability to learn complex patterns, iris detection techniques based on deep learning are gaining popularity. Zhang et al.13,14 discussed that incorporating multiple modes of identification, such as gait, facial recognition, and iris scanning, into a single system is without a doubt a challenging endeavour. Numerous studies have applied deep learning techniques to multimodal biometric recognition systems to achieve high accuracy and real-time performance. Using a CNN and LSTM, Singh et al.15,16 recognised face, iris, fingerprint, and voice modalities with high precision and performance in real time. However, the study is limited by its small sample size and lack of security analysis.

However, the study was constrained by a small sample size, the lack of a security analysis, and the absence of a comparison to other methods.

Joshi et al.19 proposed a multimodal biometric system that utilised a convolutional neural network (CNN) for facial, iris, and fingerprint recognition. The system achieved high precision and was resistant to variations in illumination and pose. Nevertheless, the dataset was limited, there was no analysis of system security, and comparisons to other methods were limited. While their work provided insights into real-time AES, it lacked a comprehensive analysis of potential security flaws and made no mention of other AES modes. Lu et al.20 centered their research on the design and implementation of AES on FPGA platforms and offered valuable insights into hardware acceleration. However, the paper did not delve into potential security flaws or concerns with FPGA-based AES. (2016) performed a security analysis of the AES-ECB mode and proposed enhancements to counteract known plaintext attacks. The performance of the AES and RSA encryption algorithms was evaluated by Tharun et al. in (2014). However, the paper did not delve deeply into the security aspects of AES or discuss its vulnerabilities, instead focusing on AES performance comparisons.

The majority of this research are based on small datasets that may not accurately represent real-world circumstances. This research focus on particular modalities, such as the face, iris, and locomotion, which may not be sufficient for dependable multimodal biometric systems. An essential component of biometric authentication systems, system security, is absent from some of the research articles. Some research articles fail to provide a clear explanation of the employed deep learning models, making it difficult to replicate or improve the work. In certain circumstances, the quality and quantity of training data can be a limitation for the efficacy of deep learning models. The lack of standardisation, evaluation metrics and protocols for testing multimodal biometric systems makes it difficult to compare the performance of various approaches.

Proposed methodology

As shown in Fig. 1, the proposed system collects and encrypts video data capturing the gait, facial, and iris patterns of individuals to protect them from intruders (Step 1). The encrypted data are then uploaded in a secure manner to a cloud server (Step 2), where an efficient indexing system is established to facilitate rapid retrieval (Step 3). The encrypted images are analysed by sophisticated feature extraction, which derives unique identifiers for gait, facial features, and iris patterns. When an image is submitted for authentication (Step 4), the system extracts its distinctive characteristics and searches the index for encrypted images with similar characteristics (Step 5). Once a match has been identified, the system provides the corresponding encrypted image to maintain confidentiality (Step 6).

Proposed system’s communication flow.

To increase security, each image is assigned a unique ID that is encrypted and only shared with authorised officials (Step 7). This sharing of unique IDs between neighbouring CCTV/camera systems enables efficient tracking while preserving data privacy throughout the process (Step 8 & 9).

As depicted in Fig. 2, the proposed system architecture is a unified multimodal system that employs deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) to accurately recognise a person’s gait, face, and iris in a public environment. Surveillance cameras collect the system’s inputs as video, which is then segmented into frames and pre-processed using various techniques. The input images are first resized so that they are compatible with the DCNN input layer. Second, data normalisation is performed to normalise the pixel values to have a mean of zero and a variance of one, thereby facilitating a faster convergence during training. Third, background subtraction is performed to isolate the human as the only object of interest in the foreground. Fourth, pose normalisation is performed to normalise the feature sequence by aligning it with a fixed reference pose, thereby rendering the features more resistant to variations in body pose.

Proposed system architecture.

Finally, temporal normalisation is performed to normalise the temporal dimension of the feature sequence by equating the number of frames or duration of time for each sequence. The pre-processed images are then compared to a resultant database (DB) to determine if they already possess a secure identifier. If an ID is present, it is shared with the respective surveillance camera and nearby cameras to track the individual’s movement. If the image lacks a unique identifier, the system continues with DCNN prediction using training data. DCNN has multiple layers, including an input layer, a deep convolutional layer, and an output layer. The deep convolutional layer computes gait based on a variety of factors, including the average speed of a person’s movement, body length, image coordination, translation, rotation, and generation. In a similar fashion, the face is identified using calculations of far face authenticity, near face legitimacy, and pattern generation, and the iris is analysed using liveliness detection, pattern generation, segmentation, normalisation, and fragmentation. All layer features are integrated, and the pooling layer, dropout layer, and fully connected layer perform correlation feature extraction. Once the identification process is complete, the system verifies the output against the AADHAR/SSN database, and if verified, it generates a secure ID using 256-bit key data block binary pattern recognition. Such identifiers are shared with CCTV and adjacent cameras for monitoring purposes. If the individual is unknown, the system immediately notifies the verification authority and stores the identification in a database for monitoring purposes. In the event of a critical situation, the system suggests an appropriate resource. In addition, the system incorporates AES-secured communication to improve its dependability, as well as an intelligence-based recommendation system that authorities can use to monitor people’s actions and provide life-saving assistance when necessary. The proposed system architecture has the potential to revolutionise public safety and surveillance by providing authorities with a potent instrument to detect and prevent crime and respond to emergencies in real time. Face detection

We propose a real-time, reliable face detection method based on two different methods: far-distance authenticity face detection and near-distance legitimacy face detection. The system can identify a person’s head with a rotation angle between 0 and 150 degrees, rotating from left to right between − 75 and 75 degrees along the vertical axis.

Far-distance authenticity face detection

In face detection from a distance, the camera first identifies the approximate location of the face within the captured image. The Hough transformation is then applied to an elliptical posture-scanned directed image. Each tangent vector in the directed image is associated with one of 36 elliptical templates, which represent possible postures between the tangent vector and an ellipse pattern Zhang et al.22.

This method is designed specifically for face sizes that correspond to ellipses with major axis radii between 60 and 120 pixels. Along with the maximum and minimum ellipses of the template, a greyscale representation of the locus of centres for all potential ellipses tangent to the vector in a 45-degree pattern is displayed. It is essential to note, however, that the approach does not directly account for sagittal face rotations. The patterns assign a score to each potential centre by utilising the tangent vectors in the directed image. After analysing the entire directional image, the accumulator with the highest score identifies the most likely location of the face within the ellipse.

Secure ID Generation

In the "big-block permutation" step, the input image (designated input_image) is divided into large blocks. The number of these blocks, denoted by num_big_blocks, is determined by the size of the image and the block size chosen (block_size_block_size). The generation of a unique random permutation (permutation_big_blocks) based on user-specific keys, namely, key_big_block and Aadhar_ID/SSN_ID, is a crucial component of this step. This permutation determines how these blocks should be rearranged.

In the subsequent "small block permutation" phase, the large blocks are divided into smaller blocks based on the image’s various components (gait, face, and iris, represented as components). The number of these smaller blocks (num_small_blocks) is determined by block_size and big-block size. Similar to the preceding step, permutations (permutations_small_blocks) are produced to facilitate the rearrangement of these smaller blocks. These permutations are also associated with user-specific keys to ensure that the process can be reversed. The “Pixel Permutation” step shuffles pixel values within each small block using random permutations (permutation_pixels). This reorganisation at the pixel level adds an additional layer of security to the encryption process. In the final step of "Pixel Substitution," order-preserving substitution tables (substitution_tables) are generated based on user-specific keys (key_order_preserving_substitution). These tables determine the replacement of pixel values within an image. The selection of random substitution tables for each pixel ensures that the overall image structure remains intact while the original content is obscured. After these steps are completed, the image is fully encrypted and converted into a secure ID after the Aadhar/SSN database verification that can only be decrypted by authorised authorities with the corresponding keys. This exhaustive approach ensures the security and privacy of personal images. For tracking purposes, this image can be transformed into a secure I’D (refer figure).

Environmental robustness: Discuss the system’s performance in various lighting or environmental conditions, leveraging gait, face, and iris data to maintain accuracy.

To test the system’s robustness, the proposed architecture was evaluated in varying conditions, including poor lighting, crowded spaces, and partial occlusions. The system demonstrated high accuracy, with face detection maintaining a 95.32% accuracy under varying angles of up to 45 degrees, while gait detection achieved 86.28%. These metrics confirm the system’s resilience to environmental challenges. Additionally, the system employs pose normalization and temporal normalization, which further enhance its performance under adverse conditions. A simulated test across different scenarios showed that the system can identify individuals in less than 0.2 s, even in crowded environments.

While the system achieves an impressive 94% accuracy, the remaining 6% error rate could result in misidentification or false rejections, especially in high-stakes environments like security or healthcare. To mitigate these risks, the system incorporates multi-modal biometric fusion (gait, face, iris), which reduces reliance on a single mode of recognition. Additionally, the use of False Rejection Rate (FRR) and False Acceptance Rate (FAR) metrics allows continuous fine-tuning of the system to reduce errors. The system could also incorporate human-in-the-loop validation for high-risk cases, where the system flags potential misidentifications for manual review.

Conclusion

In a public context, a unified multimodal identification system that uses DCNN to recognise a person’s gait, face, and iris has been proposed. By utilising the accuracy of each model and the combined accuracy of several combination models, this system attempts to handle the complexity and challenges brought on by the incorporation of numerous modalities while improving the accuracy of human identification. It is a ground-breaking attempt to accurately identify people in congested public places. However, to increase reliability, AES-secured communication must be implemented to guarantee the confidentiality of sensitive data used by the system. Additionally, a system of intelligence-based suggestions has been put in place, enabling authorities to keep track of people’s actions and, when necessary, to quickly render life-saving help. The proposed system promises to recognise and address public emergencies quickly while maintaining the privacy of sensitive data.

Future scope

The proposed approach has various possible uses in the security and surveillance industries as well as a novel method of multimodal identification in public spaces. The large range of potential applications in many industries and situations shows how adaptive and versatile the proposed system is. Access control for places that need to be extremely secure, such governmental structures, research facilities, and military installations, is one common use. Law enforcement organisations can use the system’s capabilities for criminal investigations, suspect tracking, and identification duties, which make it easier to analyse surveillance material and identify people of interest. Similar to airports, border crossings can benefit from the system’s quick and precise recognition capabilities, which speed up the process of confirming travellers. The system can be used in the healthcare sector to accurately identify and authenticate patients in hospitals and other medical institutions, assuring the provision of appropriate care and protecting private medical information. The system’s secure and seamless identification capabilities can benefit retail establishments and malls, improving consumer experiences while upholding a high level of safety. The system can also be used by educational institutions to securely identify and authenticate students, creating a secure and orderly environment on the institution’s property.

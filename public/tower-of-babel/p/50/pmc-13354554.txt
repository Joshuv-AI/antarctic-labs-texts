Numerical assessment of Lorentz-force-driven nanofluid cooling in trapezoidal ducts for improved solar panel performance
Authors: Mashhour A. Alazwari, Ali Basem, Hussein A. Z. AL-bonsrulah, Nidal H. Abu-Hamdeh, Khalid H. Almitani, Ahmad H. Milyani, Ahmed B. Khoshaim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study explores strategies to boost the productivity of solar panel operating in dusty environments by employing the Lorentz force as an active cooling mechanism. The cooling system features a trapezoidal duct equipped with fins. The duct is filled with a ferrofluid composed of water and Fe3O4 nanoparticles, and simulations were conducted for two different nanoparticle volume fractions (ϕ). The photovoltaic module is modeled as a multilayer structure, with a thermoelectric generator (TEG) positioned beneath the absorber to convert thermal gradients into supplementary electrical energy. The study evaluates electrical efficiency (ηₑl) across various operational conditions. To assess the effect of electromagnetic forces, the Hartmann number (Ha) was varied alongside different inlet velocities (Vin) and dust mass concentrations (ω). Results show that thermal efficiency (ηth) improves with higher inlet velocity and stronger magnetic fields, while increased dust deposition significantly reduces performance. Similarly, electrical efficiency benefits from enhanced Ha and Vin but declines sharply under heavy dust accumulation. The incorporation of Fe3O4 nanoparticles greatly enhances cooling effectiveness and thermal regulation. Specifically, at Ha = 77, increasing the inlet velocity further boosts thermal and electrical efficiencies. Additionally, system performance uniformity improves substantially with higher magnetic field strength and faster inlet flow, demonstrating that the combination of Lorentz-force cooling, nanofluid enhancement, and thermoelectric integration provides a robust solution for maintaining solar panel performance under challenging dusty conditions.

Introduction

Given the escalating ecological issues and the severe effects of climate alter, it is more crucial than ever to meet the global goal of declining carbon emissions. Due to rapid population growth, there was a significant rise in global energy request1,2. As a result, there has been significant advancement in renewable energy development over recent years3,4. The utilization of solar energy is generally classified into two main categories: to generate useful heat or to produce electrical energy5,6. Pachori et al.7,8 examined multiple geometric designs in solar energy systems and demonstrated the critical role of solar energy as a sustainable resource. By combining different approaches, it becomes possible to capture a significant amount of irradiation, leading to the creation of a system called PVT (Photovoltaic thermal)9–12. Underneath the PV cells, a flow duct is incorporated to enable the simultaneous generation of both types of energy13–16. Improving the cooling of photovoltaic systems is crucial for enhancing their efficiency, which has led several researchers to concentrate on this issue17–19. The design of thermal systems has been widely explored for various applications through innovative approaches20,21. In addition, the adoption of numerical simulation techniques has played a key role in achieving more efficient and optimized system configurations22–24. Dimri et al.25 introduced an innovative that incorporates a thermoelectric module. They showed that the collectors’ overall productivity exceeded that of PV modules about 4.723%. In an examination carried out by Praveenkumar et al.26, a PVT-TEG system was investigated. The showed that about 41.2% decrease in temperature can be achieved. Additionally, these systems demonstrated an 8.5% enhancement in electrical efficiency. Sheikholeslami and Yarmohamad27 studied a hybrid system integrating PV technology with an electrolyzer. Their results indicated that the design can reach a cumulative profit of about 3701.73 USD over a 20-year period.

Hissouf et al.28 developed a hybrid PVT unit that improves overall efficiency through dual operation. By using a coolant fluid to extract heat, the system effectively reduces TPV. Their findings demonstrated a 1.9% augmentation in electrical productivity with the addition of nanoparticles. Al-Waeli et al.29 discovered the behavior of a PVT unit that used both nanomaterials and paraffin for cooling. They findings revealed that the value of ηth was 72%. Sheikholeslami and Alturaihi30 studied a PVT incorporating paraffin as a cooling medium. The found that the offered enhancement contributes to a CO₂ reduction improvement of approximately 11.31%. Gelis et al.31 inspected a research to investigate the impact of various nanofluids on the performance of PVT systems. They indicated that Alumina/H2O nanofluid achieved highest electrical efficiencies of 21.18%. Sheikholeslami and AL-Behadili32 examined a hybrid system combining a solar chimney with a PV module incorporating a paraffin-based thermal storage layer. Their results indicated that increasing the tilt angle from 30° to 60° led to an improvement in inlet mass flow rate of approximately 15%, demonstrating the influence of geometric configuration on system performance. Jia et al.33 investigated a PVT system that utilized Al2O3/H2O and TiO2/H2O as the testing fluids. Their outputs revealed that the highest electrical output can be obtained in presence of alumina nanomaterial. Khalili and Sheikholeslami34 explored a PVT/TE system integrated with confined jet impingement. To achieve the highest electrical and thermal efficiencies, they identified the optimal jet placement through mathematical analysis and numerical simulations. Based on their findings, the optimal system achieved ηele and ηth of approximately 84% and 15.44%, respectively.

The research presented in this study introduces several novel elements that distinguish it from previous work in the field of solar energy units. One of the primary innovations is the integration of a finned trapezoid-shaped cooling duct filled with ferrofluid, which is a mixture of water and iron oxide nanoparticles, to boost the cooling. While ferrofluids have been explored in various thermal management applications, their specific use in this configuration, combined with the impact of Lorentz force, represents a new approach. This magnetic force, characterized by the Hartmann number (Ha), significantly improves the heat extraction capabilities of the ferrofluid. Moreover, the addition of a thermoelectric module above the cooling plate adds another layer of innovation. This module leverages the temperature differential created by the cooling system to generate additional electricity, thereby utilizing waste heat that would otherwise be lost. This dual approach—combining photovoltaic (PV) and thermoelectric energy generation—addresses the persistent challenge of maximizing energy output from solar panels. The study also addresses the often-overlooked issue of dust accumulation on solar panels, which can severely impair their efficiency. Unlike previous research that may have focused primarily on cooling mechanisms or thermoelectric enhancements, this study incorporates the simulation of dust deposition. Compared to previous studies, which have either focused on individual aspects such as ferrofluid cooling, thermoelectric generation, or dust mitigation in isolation, this research offers a holistic technique by incorporating these elements. Previous publications have typically addressed the challenges of thermal management or energy conversion separately, without fully exploring the potential synergies between different technologies. The importance of this study lies in its potential to improve the overall efficiency and operational reliability of solar energy systems, particularly in environments where dust accumulation and high temperatures are prevalent challenges.

Integration of solar panel with TEG in existence of Lorentz force

In the current study, a solar panel system has been enhanced with a finned trapezoidal-shaped cooling duct filled with ferrofluid, a mixture of H2O and iron oxide nanoparticles, to improve thermal management. This innovative approach not only addresses the challenge of overheating but also incorporates a thermoelectric generator (TEG) positioned above the cooling plate to exploit the temperature gradient for additional electricity production. The system involves multiple layers within the solar panel, each modeled using conduction equations that integrate heat sources identified through optical analysis. This comprehensive modeling approach allows for a full inspection of the unit’s performance. A key focus of current work is the impact of dust on the solar panel’s unit. Dust, which can significantly reduce the optical transmissivity of the glass, poses a major challenge to maintaining high system performance. To accurately simulate the effects of dust, the optical properties of the glass were adjusted affording to different dust densities (ω). This allows for a nuanced understanding of how dust impacts both the (ηth) and (ηele). To further enhance the cooling efficiency, an external magnetic force is applied, characterized by the Hartmann number (Ha). This magnetic field intensifies the heat extraction capabilities of the ferrofluid, reducing the overall temperature of module. The study also explores the effects of varying the fraction of iron oxide nanoparticles (φ) and the inlet velocity (Vin) on the behavior of unit. The simulations cover a range of scenarios to derive the total electrical and thermal performance metrics for each case. The dimensions of the solar panel used in this study were derived from Ref.35. This reduction in computational domain size leverages the symmetry of the system, making the simulations more efficient without compromising accuracy. The thermophysical and optical parameters of the different layers involved in the panel were obtained from Ref.36. Additionally, the properties necessary for evaluating the impact of TEG were sourced from Ref.37. In summary, this research introduces a multifaceted technique to improving the productivity of solar panels by combining advanced cooling techniques, thermoelectric generation, and the consideration of dust accumulation. Results and discussion

In current paper, a solar panel unit has been innovatively equipped with a finned trapezoid-shaped cooling duct to optimize its efficiency. Above the cooling arrangement, a thermoelectric module has been strategically placed to capitalize on the temperature gradient created by the cooling process, further increasing electricity generation. A significant aspect of this research is the examination of dust accumulation on the system’s performance. To counteract the heat generated and enhance cooling efficiency, an external magnetic force is applied to the system. This magnetic force interacts with the ferrofluid, enabling it to draw more heat away from the upper layer of the panel, effectively lowering its temperature. The study investigates the effects of varying the fraction of iron oxide (φ), the Hartmann number (Ha), and the (Vin) of the cooling fluid. The performance metrics, including (ηele) and (ηth), were calculated for all combinations of these variables.

Figure 2 exemplifies the grid style employed in the simulation. To select the optimal mesh resolution, various grid densities were tested, and their corresponding outputs were compared in Fig. 3. The most effective mesh, which comprised 1.4 million cells, was selected for this study. This approach helps to balance computational efficiency with accuracy, ensuring that the mesh size is sufficient for reliable results without incurring excessive computational costs. For validation purposes, the electrical performance results obtained from the current numerical setup were compared with those from previous research by Sheikholeslami and Khalili39. Their study utilized nanofluid as the test fluid and featured a novel cooling duct design. The comparison focused on laminar flow conditions, and Fig. 4 demonstrates that the results from the current simulation align well with the previous findings. This confirms that the numerical setup used in this study is reliable and suitable for the new work being conducted. The good agreement between the new results and previous findings underlines the robustness of the simulation framework and its applicability to the present research. Figure 5 demonstrates the enhanced uniformity of isotherms across the solar panel as a result of increasing inlet velocity (Vin) and Hartmann number (Ha). As (Ha) increases, the uniformity of temperature distribution improves significantly. Specifically, when Vin is set at 0.033 m/s, the uniformity increases by approximately 24.42%. This improvement becomes even more pronounced at a greater Vin of 0.077 m/s, where the uniformity increases by about 29.14%. In contrast, increasing the inlet velocity (Vin) also contributes to better uniformity. At Ha = 0, raising Vin leads to a 4.28% improvement in uniformity. When Ha is set to 77, the improvement in uniformity is notably greater, at 10.26%. When comparing the effects of Vin on uniformity under different Hartmann numbers, the increase in uniformity with rising Vin for Ha = 77 is 2.39 times more significant than for Ha = 0. Additionally, augmenting Ha results in a 19.34% augmentation in uniformity as Vin increases. These results demonstrate that both Ha and Vin play crucial roles in improving the uniformity of temperature distribution across the solar panel. Higher Hartmann numbers lead to better uniformity by enhancing the impacts of magnetic fields on fluid flow and heat distribution. Similarly, increasing the inlet velocity helps in distributing heat more evenly, especially when combined with a higher Hartmann number. The comparative analysis underscores the effectiveness of adjusting these parameters to achieve more uniform temperature distribution.Fig. 5Contours for TPV.

Figure 6 depicts the influence of iron oxide nanoparticle fraction (φ) on the unit’s efficiency. As φ increases, the system benefits from enhanced heat transfer owing to the improved conductivity of the ferrofluid. This allows the ferrofluid to absorb more heat from the upper layer, resulting in higher thermal and electrical efficiencies (ηth and ηele), which directly correlate with φ. When (Ha) is 0, increasing the inlet velocity (Vin) reduces the impact of φ on ηth and ηele by approximately 16.75% and 6.13%, respectively. This decrease is attributed to the dominance of convection effects, which overshadow the benefits of increased thermal conductivity provided by higher φ. Conversely, at Ha = 77, increasing Vin leads to an enhancement of ηth and ηele by about 12.64% and 25.87%, respectively, due to the improved heat absorption and retention capabilities of the ferrofluid with a higher φ. This differing behavior illustrates the significant role of MHD effects in modulating the impact of φ. Specifically, when φ is 0.0056 and Ha is 0, ηth and ηele increase by about 10.08% and 1.13%, respectively, with higher Vin. However, as Ha increases, the benefits of φ on ηth and ηele diminish. The increment in ηth and ηele due to rising Vin decreases by approximately 9.16% and 3.58%, respectively, when Ha is introduced. This indicates that MHD effects play a crucial role in altering the relationship between φ and the system’s performance. The results highlight how the iron oxide nanoparticle fraction (φ) influences the performance of the system. The varying impact of φ under different Hartmann numbers and inlet velocities underscores the complex interplay between conduction, convection, and MHD effects. This underscores the importance of optimizing φ in conjunction with other operational parameters to achieve maximum system efficiency. Understanding these dynamics is essential for designing more effective and efficient solar panel systems that leverage the benefits of ferrofluids and MHD effects.Fig. 6Difference of the (a) ηele, and (b) ηth versus V-in, φ, and Ha.

Figure 7 illustrates the effects of (Ha) and dust density (ω) on the efficiency of the unit, specifically the (ηth) and (ηele). When the inlet velocity (Vin) is 0.033 m/s and there is no dust (ω = 0), increasing the Hartmann number results in improvements of approximately 4.59% in ηth and 0.51% in ηele. This enhancement is credited to the more effective lessening in the temperature of the silicon layer due to the MHD effects. The augmented heat absorption by the ferrofluid, facilitated by a higher Ha, leads to better performance of the solar panel. In a dust-free scenario with higher Vin, the gains in ηth and ηele are diminished, with decreases of about 16.73% and 6.11%, respectively. This reduction is due to the decreased impact of the Lorentz force under conditions of higher convective flow, which mitigates the benefits of MHD. When considering the presence of dust, the beneficial impact of the Hartmann number becomes somewhat reduced. Specifically, when Ha and ω are both zero, increasing the ferrofluid velocity improves ηth and ηele by about 9.99% and 1.13%, respectively. Similarly, with Ha set at 77 and ω at 6.85, increasing the ferrofluid velocity results in improvements of approximately 9.15% in ηth and 1.09% in ηele. The application of ferrofluid at higher inlet velocities enhances the cooling rate and raises the outlet temperature. However, with dust deposition on the glass, excess heat accumulates in the glass layer, reducing the amount of heat available to the ferrofluid and consequently decreasing the thermal power. Additionally, the system’s electrical power also declines due to the presence of dust. As dust density increases, ηth and ηele decrease by approximately 15.72% and 25.44%, respectively. These results underscore the complex interplay between MHD effects, dust deposition, and fluid dynamics. While the Hartmann number positively influences performance by enhancing heat absorption through MHD, its effectiveness is limited by factors such as convection and dust presence.Fig. 7Difference of the (a) ηele, and (b) ηth versus V-in, ω, and Ha.

Conclusions

This study investigates an integrated photovoltaic–thermoelectric system equipped with MHD-assisted ferrofluid cooling in a finned trapezoidal duct under dusty environmental conditions. The main objective is to enhance solar energy conversion efficiency by combining advanced thermal management, magnetic field control, and waste heat recovery. The results demonstrate that system performance is strongly influenced by magnetic effects (Hartmann number), inlet velocity, nanoparticle concentration, and dust deposition. Based on the numerical findings, the key findings are summarized as below:The proposed MHD-assisted ferrofluid cooling system significantly improves heat dissipation, leading to enhanced thermal regulation of the photovoltaic module.The integration of a thermoelectric generator (TEG) enables recovery of waste heat, contributing to additional electrical power generation and improving overall system efficiency.Increasing the Hartmann number (Ha) enhances flow control and thermal uniformity due to Lorentz-force effects, resulting in improved thermal and electrical performance.Dust accumulation (ω) has a strong detrimental effect on system performance by reducing optical transmittance, leading to reductions of approximately 15.72% in thermal efficiency and 25.44% in electrical efficiency at high dust density.Increasing inlet velocity (Vin) enhances convective heat transfer and improves both ηth and ηele; however, its influence becomes weaker at higher magnetic field strengths due to Lorentz-force-induced flow damping.The impact of (ϕ) is more pronounced at lower velocities, while at higher Vin, the system becomes dominated by forced convection, reducing the relative contribution of enhanced thermal conductivity.The system exhibits a diminishing return (saturation) behavior at high values of Ha and Vin, where further increases lead to marginal performance improvement.The uniformity of temperature and flow distribution improves significantly with increasing Ha and Vin, contributing to more stable system operation.MHD-based enhancement improves thermal performance but cannot fully compensate for optical losses caused by dust, highlighting the need for complementary mitigation strategies.Overall, the results indicate that optimal performance is achieved through a combined strategy integrating MHD cooling, nanofluid enhancement, thermoelectric recovery, and effective dust management techniques.

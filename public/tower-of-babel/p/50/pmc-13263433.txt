Global and Chinese co-occurrence patterns association with socio-demographic index for congenital heart disease and Down syndrome, 1990–2021
Authors: Shuo Chen, Ziqin Zhou, Miao Tian, Xiaohua Li, Zhanhao Su, Jimei Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCongenital heart disease (CHD) and Down syndrome (DS) are two major birth defects that severely impact the quality of life and survival of affected children. This study aimed to systematically assess the disease burden, spatial co-occurrence patterns, and associations with the Socio-demographic Index (SDI) for CHD and DS globally and in China from 1990 to 2021.MethodsData on disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) and prevalence for CHD and DS were extracted from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021 (GBD 2021) for 204 countries and territories. Temporal trends were evaluated using Joinpoint regression analysis. Disease burden levels were categorized into quartiles to generate co-occurrence maps and year-SDI heatmaps.ResultsFrom 1990 to 2021, global age-standardized DALY rates for CHD and DS exhibited an overall downward trend [estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) =−1.59% and −0.14%, respectively]. China experienced substantially larger declines [CHD, average annual percentage change (AAPC) =−5.03%; DS, AAPC =−3.62%], yet prevalent cases continued to increase, reaching 2.35 million and 1.61 million in 2021, respectively. Globally, the concordant co-occurrence pattern for prevalence predominated (41.66% of countries) and remained persistently stratified by SDI: CHD-dominant in low-SDI regions, DS-dominant in high-SDI regions, and concordant across other SDI strata. In China, the co-occurrence pattern for DALYs shifted from high–high concordant in 1990 to low-low concordant in 2021, while the prevalence pattern transitioned from DS-dominant to concordant in 2017.ConclusionsOver the past 32 years, both global and China levels have seen declines in the DALY burden of CHD and DS, with China achieving a more pronounced reduction. However, the rising prevalence burden highlights that life-course health management remains a weak link. The concordant co-occurrence pattern in prevalence burden suggests that prevention and control strategies for CHD and DS should adopt an integrated approach, addressing both common patterns and unique characteristics of each condition to further improve overall health outcomes.Keywords: Congenital heart disease (CHD), Down syndrome (DS), disease burden

Highlight box

Key findings

• From 1990 to 2021, the global age-standardized disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) rate for congenital heart disease (CHD) decreased significantly [estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) =−1.59%], while China achieved a more pronounced decline [average annual percentage change (AAPC) =−5.03%]. China’s DALY burden for both CHD and Down syndrome (DS) shifted from a “both-high” to a “both-low” global rank.

• Despite reduced DALYs burden, the number of prevalent cases for both conditions rose globally and in China.

• Co-occurrence patterns evolved into distinct clusters by 2021. Prevalence burden patterns were entrenched and strongly linked to Socio-demographic Index (SDI): CHD-dominant in low-SDI regions, DS-dominant in high-SDI regions, and concordant pattern was observed elsewhere.

What is known and what is new?

• CHD and DS are major birth defects with a strong clinical association. Disease burden varies with socioeconomic development.

• This first 32-year global spatiotemporal analysis of CHD and DS co-occurrence patterns reveals divergent trajectories between global and China levels, a turning point around 2010, and opposite correlations with SDI: DALYs burden inversely, prevalence burden positively. It documents China’s unique transition from a “double high” to a “double low” co-burden pattern.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Effective birth defect control requires integrated strategies addressing both conditions simultaneously, as their burdens co-vary.

• Differentiated strategies are needed based on development level: low-SDI regions should prioritize surgical access and prenatal diagnosis; high-SDI regions should focus on life‑course management.

• The post-2019 rise in global DS burden underscores the need for resilient maternal health systems to maintain screening during public health crises.

Introduction

Congenital heart disease (CHD) and Down syndrome (DS) are two major birth defects that severely impact the quality of life and survival of affected children (1,2). CHD refers to structural abnormalities resulting from developmental malformations of the heart and great vessels during the embryonic period. As the most common type of birth defect, CHD has a global incidence of approximately 8–9‰ among live births and remains the leading congenital cause of infant and child mortality (3,4). Patients with CHD have ongoing complex health-care needs in the various life stages (5). DS, caused by trisomy 21, is a chromosomal disorder often accompanied by dysfunction in multiple organ systems (6). As the leading genetic cause of intellectual disability, it also entails a genetically determined form of Alzheimer’s disease, necessitating lifelong comprehensive management (7). The prevalence of CHD in children with DS is significantly higher than that in the general population, with approximately 50% of children with DS also presenting with CHD (8,9). Individuals with trisomy 21 and CHD have unique and complex care needs in the domains of physical, developmental, and psychosocial health, which affect functional status and quality of life (10). Moreover, the presence of CHD substantially increases mortality in individuals with DS and is the leading cause of death in this population, resulting in more complex treatment decisions, higher surgical risks, and greater long-term health burdens (11). Therefore, systematic management of children with DS must include CHD screening and intervention, while etiological evaluation of certain CHD cases should also consider the possibility of chromosomal abnormalities. This close clinical association implies that single-disease prevention and control strategies may be insufficient to fully address the public health challenges posed by both conditions.

The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study, as a globally authoritative public health data platform, provides a foundational basis for systematically assessing the long-term epidemiological trends of CHD and DS. Previous studies based on GBD data have largely focused on describing the burden of individual diseases and have not adequately revealed the spatial co-distribution or temporal evolution of the burden of these two clinically and etiologically interrelated conditions (12,13). At the socioeconomic level, the Socio-demographic Index (SDI)—a composite indicator of national or regional development—has been shown to be significantly associated with the burden of various cardiovascular and chromosomal disorders (14,15). However, the differential patterns of CHD and DS burden and their co-occurrence across levels of sociodemographic development remain unclear, and the strength of their association and spatiotemporal trends have not been systematically assessed. Using data from the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors Study (GBD) 2021 database, this study aims to systematically analyze the burden levels, temporal trends, and co-occurrence patterns of CHD and DS globally and in China from 1990 to 2021, and to explore their association with the SDI, so as to provide a scientific basis for developing integrated prevention and control strategies. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2026-0195/rc).

Methods

Data sources and processing

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. This is an ecological study that examined co-occurrence patterns of CHD and DS and their association with the SDI using global and Chinese data from 1990 to 2021. Data were obtained from the GBD 2021 database (https://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-2021), which is led by the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation (IHME) at the University of Washington in the United States. The GBD 2021 covers 204 countries and regions worldwide, and all data are publicly accessible to researchers through the GBD Results Tool.

CHD and DS, the focus of this study, are classified under birth defects in the GBD disease classification. The core indicators extracted included disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) and prevalence rates for CHD and DS. DALYs for a disease or health condition are the sum of years of life lost due to premature mortality (YLLs) and years of healthy life lost due to disability (YLDs) due to prevalent cases of the disease or health condition in a population. One DALY represents one lost year of healthy life. To eliminate the influence of differing age structures across populations, all rates were age-standardized using the direct method, referencing the GBD 2021 standard population age structure. The standardization formula is: standardized rate =Σ (crude rate for each age group × proportion of the corresponding age group in the standard population). The socioeconomic indicator extracted was the SDI, which integrates per capita income, average educational attainment, and total fertility rate to measure national development levels. Based on the GBD 2021 classification criteria, all countries were categorized into five SDI levels: high, high-middle, middle, low-middle, and low SDI groups.

Statistical analysis

This study employed various statistical and visualization methods to sequentially analyze temporal trends, spatial co-occurrence patterns, and associations with socioeconomic factors.

Joinpoint regression analysis was used to assess temporal trends in age-standardized rates of CHD and DS indicators from 1990 to 2021. By fitting segmented linear regression lines, inflection points where trends changed significantly were identified. The annual percent change (APC) for each trend segment and the average annual percent change (AAPC) for the entire study period, along with their 95% confidence intervals (CI), were calculated. The AAPC formula is: (eβ – 1) × 100%, where β represents the regression coefficient of the fitted log-linear model. Trend interpretation criteria were as follows: if the lower limit of the 95% CI for AAPC exceeded 0, the trend was considered increasing; if the upper limit was below 0, the trend was considered decreasing; if the 95% CI included 0, the trend was deemed stable. The estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) and its 95% CI were calculated using a linear regression model, where the natural logarithm of the age-standardized rate [ln(ASR)] was regressed on calendar year. The EAPC was computed as 100 × [exp (β) – 1], where β represents the regression coefficient. An EAPC greater than 0 indicates an increasing trend in ASR over time, whereas an EAPC less than 0 signifies a decreasing trend.

To examine the global spatial patterns of CHD and DS burden and their association with the SDI, a co-occurrence pattern analysis framework was developed. This framework operates at the population ecology level and characterizes the rank combinations of age-standardized burden indicators for CHD and DS within the same country or region, rather than analyzing comorbidity at the individual level. Specifically, it assesses whether the relative global rankings of the two diseases exhibit convergent or divergent spatial patterns. The procedure was as follows: Using 1990 and 2021 as cross-sectional time points, countries were categorized into four levels based on global quartiles of CHD and DS DALY rates and prevalence rates: Q1 (below the 25th percentile, defined as “low” burden), Q2 (25th–50th percentile, “low-middle”), Q3 (50th–75th percentile, “high-middle”), and Q4 (above the 75th percentile, “high”) (16). Based on this classification, global co-occurrence maps were generated for each indicator and year using ArcGIS software, depicting the combinations of CHD and DS levels (e.g., both high, both low, CHD-dominant, or DS-dominant patterns). To analyze the temporal evolution of disease co-occurrence patterns across regions with different SDI levels, a year-SDI heatmap was constructed. The age-standardized burden indicators for CHD and DS (DALY rates and prevalence rates) were divided into four levels based on global quartiles of data distribution across 204 countries from 1990 to 2021. For the same SDI group and the same year, the combination of CHD and DS levels defined the co-occurrence pattern. The frequency of each combination was calculated and ultimately presented as a heatmap illustrating trends over time.

All data processing, statistical analyses, and heatmap generation were performed using R software (version 4.3.0), primarily utilizing the tidyverse, ggplot2, and raster packages. Joinpoint regression analyses were conducted using the Joinpoint Regression Program (version 5.0.2) developed by the U.S. National Cancer Institute. All map generation and thematic map production were completed using ArcGIS Pro (version 3.1) geographic information system software.

Results

Trends in the disease burden of CHD in global and China

From 1990 to 2021, the disease burden of CHD globally and in China exhibited a core characteristic: a substantial reduction in health loss due to the disease, alongside a continued increase in the number of prevalent cases. Based on CHD burden data from 1990 and 2021, at the global level, total DALYs due to CHD decreased from 3,781.99 (2,894.15–4,669.83) per 10,000 person-years in 1990 to 2,789.21 (2,133.32–3,445.11) per 10,000 person-years in 2021, and the age-standardized DALY rate declined from 613.26 (470.20–756.32) per 100,000 population to 426.03 (324.52–527.54), indicating a significant overall burden reduction (EAPC: −1.59%, P<0.001) (Table 1). The number of prevalent CHD cases increased from 1,175.43 (1,046.63–1,304.23) per 10,000 person-years in 1990 to 1,586.31 (1,414.45–1,758.18) per 10,000 person-years in 2021, with the age-standardized prevalence rate rising slightly from 209.44 (186.79–232.09) per 100,000 to 210.52 (187.70–233.35), showing a modest increasing trend (EAPC: 0.04%, P<0.001). By sex, the prevalence rate remained stable in males, while it increased slightly in females (Table 2). At the China level, the decline in CHD burden was even more pronounced. Total DALYs dropped sharply from 901.93 (658.49–1,145.38) per 10,000 person-years in 1990 to 129.12 (107.87–150.42) per 10,000 person-years in 2021, and the age-standardized DALY rate fell from 811.94 (591.27–1,032.62) per 100,000 to 165.56 (139.83–191.28). Both males and females experienced significant burden reductions, with a slightly greater decline observed in males (Table 1). The number of prevalent CHD cases in China increased from 221.88 (195.04–248.71) per 10,000 person-years in 1990 to 234.94 (207.01–262.87) per 10,000 person-years in 2021, and the age-standardized prevalence rate rose from 189.55 (166.74–212.37) per 100,000 to 196.01 (172.24–219.29), indicating an increasing trend (EAPC: 0.15%, P<0.001). Both males and females showed increases in prevalence, with a higher increase in males (Table 2).

Joinpoint regression analysis (Figure 1) revealed that from 1990 to 2021, the age-standardized DALY rate for CHD exhibited an overall downward trend both globally and in China, with a particularly marked decline in China (overall AAPC: −5.03%; 95% CI: −5.10% to −4.94%), corresponding to an average annual decrease exceeding 5%. Trend inflection points divided the decline into five distinct phases: a slight increase during 1990–1995 (APC =1.37%); a shift to decline after 1996, with accelerated declines in 2002 (APC =−3.29%), 2006 (APC =−7.76%), and 2011 (APC =−10.58%); and a deceleration in the decline after 2019 (APC =−6.06%). Regarding sex differences, the overall downward trends for males and females were consistent, with similar inflection points across phases. In the early 1990s, the DALY rate was higher in males than in females; over time, this gap narrowed, with trend lines converging in the later period (Figure 1A,1C). Additionally, from 1990 to 2021, the age-standardized prevalence rate for CHD increased slightly both globally and in China, with no significant trend changes (overall AAPC: 0.016%; 95% CI: 0.013–0.018%) (Figure 1B,1D).

Trends in the disease burden of DS in global and China

Based on DS burden data from 1990 and 2021, the global DALY burden of DS showed a slight overall decline (EAPC: −0.14%), though this was not statistically significant (P=0.051). By sex, a significant decline was observed in males (EAPC: −0.41%), while a slight increase was noted in females (EAPC: 0.27%) (Table 3). The number of prevalent DS cases decreased substantially from 166.30 (138.02–201.80) per 10,000 person-years to 24.47 (23.36–34.06) per 10,000 person-years. The age-standardized prevalence rate declined from 28.10 (23.36–34.06) per 100,000 to 25.28 (20.53–31.50) per 100,000, reflecting a slow but significant downward trend (EAPC: −0.83%, P<0.001), with similar declines in both sexes (Table 4).

At the China level, total DALYs for DS increased from 53.43 (31.36–83.89) per 10,000 person-years to 246.23 (152.76–365.11) per 10,000 person-years. However, the age-standardized DALY rate decreased significantly from 48.46 (28.22–76.32) per 100,000 to 36.52 (22.43–54.65) per 100,000 (EAPC: −4.70%, P<0.001). Sex-specific analysis showed a greater decline in males (EAPC: −5.25%) than in females (EAPC: −3.62%) (Table 3). The number of prevalent DS cases rose from 35.94 (28.86–44.80) per 10,000 person-years to 161.20 (135.52–194.70) per 10,000 person-years. The age-standardized prevalence rate exhibited a slight downward trend, though it did not reach statistical significance (EAPC: −0.18%, P=0.09).

From 1990 to 2021, the trends in DS burden diverged markedly between the global and China levels: China achieved substantial success in reducing health loss, whereas the global burden declined modestly and even showed signs of resurgence in recent years. Joinpoint regression analysis (Figure 2) indicated distinct trends in the age-standardized DALY rate for DS globally and in China. Globally, the DS age-standardized DALY rate showed a slight overall increase (overall AAPC: 0.247%; 95% CI: 0.22–0.27%). The trend exhibited periodic fluctuations: a slight increase during 1990–1995 (APC =0.698%), followed by a decline after 1995; a reversal around 2005 with a gradual uptick; and an accelerated increase after 2019 (APC =4.33%). In China, the DS age-standardized DALY rate demonstrated a significant and sustained decline (overall AAPC: −3.62%; 95% CI: −3.70% to −3.52%). This decline was multi-phasic: increases during 1990–1996 (APC =2.95%) and 1997–2000 (APC =1.28%); a shift to decline after 2000, with accelerated decreases during 2001–2008 (APC =−6.90%) and 2009–2011 (APC =−9.50%); a decelerated decline after 2012 (APC =−5.64%); and a flattening after 2019 (APC =−1.59%) (Figure 2A,2C). Regarding the age-standardized prevalence rate, both global and China levels showed gradual downward trends with small magnitudes of change (global AAPC: −0.859%, 95% CI: −0.887% to −0.836%; and China AAPC: −0.332%, 95% CI: −0.370% to 0.301%, respectively) (Figure 2B,2D). By sex, males consistently exhibited higher age-standardized DALY and prevalence rates than females throughout the period, though the long-term trends were highly similar between sexes.

Global spatial co-occurrence patterns of CHD and DS burden

In 1990, countries with a high DALY burden of CHD were concentrated in Asia and sub-Saharan Africa. China was also among the high-burden countries at that time. By 2021, the global distribution of CHD burden had shifted substantially: burden levels in most Eastern European and East Asian countries decreased markedly, while high-burden regions became concentrated in Central Asia and Africa. China transitioned from a high-burden to a low-burden country, with its color (on the map) lightening notably alongside neighboring East Asian countries (Figure 3A). Unlike CHD, the distribution of DS burden already exhibited strong regional clustering in 1990, with high-burden countries primarily located in the Middle East, North Africa, and East Asia. By 2021, the burden reduction was most pronounced in East Asia, particularly in China, which became a low-burden region for DS. Concurrently, global high-burden hotspots for DS became more prominently concentrated in Middle Eastern and North African countries (Figure 3B).

Notable spatial overlap existed in the distribution of DALY burdens for CHD and DS, with the three co-occurrence patterns distributed relatively evenly across countries. Spatially, in 1990, CHD-dominant regions were mainly in Eastern Europe and parts of North America, while concordant and DS-dominant regions were more dispersed. By 2021, CHD-dominant regions were concentrated in southern Africa, concordant regions in Eurasia and North Africa, and DS-dominant regions in Latin America and Oceania. For China’s DALY burden, the co-occurrence pattern shifted from a both-high pattern in 1990 to a both-low pattern in 2021 (Figure 3C).

Regarding prevalence burden, the co-occurrence patterns for CHD and DS showed no substantial differences between the two years. Countries with a high prevalence burden of CHD were mainly in Eastern Europe and North Africa; those with a high DS prevalence burden were concentrated in Eastern Europe, Latin America, and Oceania. China’s prevalence burden for neither disease fell into the high-burden category (Figure 4A,4B). Globally, the burden levels of CHD and DS exhibited a high degree of synchrony. In 1990, 85 countries (41.66% of all countries) displayed a concordant pattern. The number of countries with CHD-dominant and DS-dominant patterns was 59 (28.92%) and 60 (29.41%), respectively. By 2021, the number of concordant countries decreased to 78 (38.23%), but it remained the most prevalent co-occurrence pattern. Spatially, concordant regions in 1990 were concentrated in northern South America, Western Europe, and East Africa, while in 2021, they were centered in Europe and East Africa. CHD-dominant regions were concentrated in North America and Latin America, and DS-dominant regions were primarily in Asia (Figure 4C).

Co-occurrence patterns and temporal evolution across SDI strata

Health loss, as measured by DALYs, demonstrated a clear and consistent negative association with SDI level: the lower the SDI, the higher the burden level. For CHD, the burden level increased stepwise with decreasing SDI, from the stably lowest level (Q1) in the high SDI stratum to the persistently highest level (Q4) in the low SDI stratum. DS followed a similar gradient, with the high SDI stratum exhibiting a relatively low level (Q2) and the low SDI stratum reaching the highest level (Q4) by the end of the observation period. In contrast to DALYs, the prevalence burden of CHD was higher in the high SDI stratum than in other SDI strata. For DS, the prevalence burden showed a clear positive association with SDI level: the higher the SDI, the higher the burden level. DS burden increased stepwise with rising SDI, from the consistently lowest level (Q1) in the low SDI stratum to high levels (Q4, Q3) in the high SDI stratum (Figure 5A,5B).

(B) Changes in DALY and prevalence burden levels for DS. (C) Changes in co-occurrence patterns for DALY and prevalence burden. From 1990 to 2021, global and China-level trajectories in the relative DALY burden ranks of CHD and DS diverged markedly. Globally, a key turning point occurred around 2010: prior to this, the world was characterized by a CHD-dominant high-burden state (CHD: Q4; DS: Q3); after 2010, the global burden level shifted downward and stabilized at a concordant moderate level (both at Q3). China’s co-occurrence pattern evolved from a both-high burden (both at Q4) in 1990, through phases of an initial decline in DS burden [2005], followed by CHD burden catching up [2005–2012], and a synchronous decline to moderate levels [2013], ultimately stabilizing in a both-low pattern (both at Q1) from 2017 onwards.

Distinct trajectories of DALY burden evolution were observed across different SDI strata from 1990 to 2021. In high SDI countries, the CHD DALY burden level first declined in 1999 and stabilized after 2014 in a DS-dominant pattern (CHD: Q1; DS: Q2). Improvements were relatively delayed in high-middle and middle SDI countries, which eventually stabilized in a concordant pattern (Q2 and Q3, respectively). Low-middle SDI countries persistently exhibited a CHD-dominant high-burden pattern (CHD: Q4; DS: Q3) throughout the observation period. In contrast, low SDI countries saw their burden levels further evolve after 2016 into and remain in a both-high pattern (CHD and DS both at Q4).

Unlike the marked decline observed in DALY rates, the global relative rank of prevalence remained highly stable over a long period, with only a single change at the end of the period. From 1990 to 2011, the global prevalence rank was stable in a concordant pattern (CHD Q3; DS Q3). Starting in 2012, the co-occurrence pattern shifted to a CHD-dominant pattern (CHD Q3; DS Q2), which persisted until 2021. From 1990 to 2016, China consistently exhibited a DS-dominant pattern (CHD Q2; DS Q3); from 2017 onwards, China transitioned to a concordant pattern (both Q2), which remained through 2021 (Figure 5C).

The prevalence patterns across SDI strata revealed a more entrenched burden landscape compared to DALYs. From 1990 to 2015, high SDI countries persistently exhibited a DS-dominant pattern (CHD Q3; DS Q4) for the longest duration. After 2016, this shifted to a concordant pattern (both Q3). High-middle and middle SDI countries remained in a concordant pattern (both Q2) for the vast majority of the period (high-middle SDI: 1990–2020; middle SDI: entire period), indicating stable moderate burden levels. In low SDI and low-middle SDI countries, a CHD-dominant pattern (CHD Q2; DS Q1) persisted consistently from 1990 to 2021.

Discussion

CHD and DS represent two major categories of birth defects with significant clinical and public health implications worldwide, and their interrelation is profound. This study systematically analyzed the evolving trends and spatial distribution patterns of the disease burden of CHD and DS globally and in China from 1990 to 2021. The findings revealed that, from 1990 to 2021, the DALY burden for both conditions exhibited an overall declining trend globally and in China. However, the prevalence burden increased due to improved survival, with the decline in DALY burden in China occurring at a much faster rate than the global average. The co-occurrence patterns for prevalence burden were predominantly concordant, while those for DALY burden displayed characteristics of regional clustering. From 1990 to 2021, the global co-occurrence pattern for CHD and DS DALY burden transitioned from a CHD-dominant high-burden state to a concordant burden state. China achieved a more dramatic shift, transitioning from a concordant high-burden to a concordant low-burden pattern. The DALY burden levels for both DS and CHD showed a negative correlation with SDI. Furthermore, the co-occurrence patterns for prevalence burden were deeply entrenched and strongly associated with SDI levels: low and low-middle SDI regions predominantly exhibited a CHD-dominant pattern, high SDI regions a DS-dominant pattern, and other SDI regions a concordant pattern.

Interpretations

Despite significant declines in age-standardized DALY rates for CHD globally and in China, the number of prevalent cases continues to rise. This accumulation of prevalent burden is primarily attributable to the widespread implementation of neonatal screening and improved success rates of surgical and interventional (hybrid) treatments, coupled with enhanced diagnosis and management of CHD in the delivery room and neonatal intensive care unit, which have collectively reduced case fatality and long-term disability rates (17-20). The sustained decline in the age-standardized burden of both conditions in China reflects the effectiveness of systematic prevention and control strategies implemented over the past three decades, including strengthened prenatal screening networks, comprehensive promotion of neonatal screening, establishment of regional pediatric cardiac centers, and continuous improvement in healthcare coverage (21-23). In contrast, the global age-standardized DALY rates for DS has shown an increasing trend since 2019, diverging markedly from the continued downward trend in China. This shift may be associated with disruptions to maternal and child health services, reduced accessibility to prenatal screening, and delayed screening for gestational age during the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic (24,25). Moreover, a consistent and persistent sex difference was observed: DALYs rates were consistently higher in males than in females. This observed sex difference may be partly attributable to differential fetal survival rates between males and females (26). It also aligns with a systematic review covering 1965 to 2021, which indicated a higher risk of CHD comorbidity in male DS patients—a disparity that has gradually narrowed since 2000 (27). Notably, despite this higher absolute burden in males, the long-term trend increased slightly in females (EAPC =0.27) but decreased in males (EAPC =−0.41). The rising DALY trend in females reflects improved survival and YLD accumulation from chronic conditions, owing to their lower severe CHD burden and longer life expectancy. In contrast, males with higher CHD burden benefit more from cardiac surgery, reducing YLL and DALY. Thus, the female EAPC rise indicates longer life with disability, not worsening burden.

In terms of DALYs, the global co-occurrence pattern evolved from a dispersed distribution in 1990 to a distinct tri-polar pattern by 2021: CHD-dominant regions concentrated in southern Africa, concordant regions in Eurasia and North Africa, and DS-dominant regions in Latin America and Oceania. This pattern aligns closely with the overarching negative correlation between DALY burden and SDI. In low SDI regions, limited prenatal screening and diagnostic capacity, coupled with scarce surgical and intensive care resources, lead to high CHD-related mortality and disability, resulting in a CHD-dominant pattern (28-30). High SDI regions benefit from comprehensive birth defect surveillance systems that capture DS prevalence accurately, while advanced clinical research delineates its incurable long-term consequences—including early-onset Alzheimer’s disease—further accentuating DS-dominant burden (31,32). The concordant pattern across Eurasia and North Africa represents a transitional state: moderate CHD management capacity without fully eliminating mortality risk, alongside gradually improving DS care, creating a balanced health loss between the two conditions. The period around 2010 emerged as a critical turning point, possibly driven by the scaling up of charitable programs for CHD in low-income countries, the dissemination of prenatal screening technologies, and improved child survival facilitated by the UN Millennium Development Goals, collectively contributing to the shift from a CHD-dominant high-burden state to a concordant moderate-burden state (33,34). Regarding prevalence burden, the concordant pattern remained the most prevalent co-occurrence type throughout the period (approximately 40%). This is likely because the primary cause of mortality in DS patients is associated CHD, and interventions such as prenatal screening for CHD, perinatal management, and subsequent curative or palliative treatments exert a consistent influence on the prevalence burden of both conditions (8,35). Consequently, this concordant pattern is driven by shared determinants in diagnostic capture and survival conditions, whereby high SDI regions synchronously expand the prevalent pool for both conditions through enhanced detection and life-extending care, while low SDI regions experience synchronous contraction due to underdiagnosis and high early mortality (36-38).

China’s transition from a both-high pattern (both Q4) in 1990 to a both-low pattern (both Q1) in 2021 for DALY burden signifies the synchronized control of both conditions. This achievement stems from a systematic prevention and control strategy covering the entire “screening-diagnosis-intervention-management” continuum. This included the nationwide rollout of non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT) for DS, the extension of traditional screening to both urban and rural areas, and the intensive implementation of CHD neonatal screening networks, regional pediatric cardiac centers, and healthcare coverage policies from the 2000s onwards (39-41). The shift in prevalence burden lagged behind that of DALYs. During the period of rapid expansion in prenatal screening coverage [2000–2015], increased DS detection rates led to a temporary increase in registered prevalent cases. It was only after screening coverage reached saturation and live births with DS declined substantially that the cumulative prevalence burden shifted to a low-burden concordant pattern after 2016 (42,43).

China’s experience, having achieved a complete transition from a “double high” to a “double low” co-burden pattern, offers a valuable paradigm. Its success underscores that for middle- and low-income countries, strengthening postnatal treatment capacity must proceed in tandem with advancing prenatal prevention systems; coordinated efforts in both areas are essential for achieving sustainable control of birth defects. Secondly, future prevention and control strategies need to be phased and precisely targeted: low SDI regions should prioritize improving access to CHD surgery and DS prenatal diagnosis rates to reduce preventable premature mortality. In contrast, regions like China, having consolidated current gains, need to shift focus from “reducing mortality” to “improving quality of life”, with increased attention to comorbidity management in adulthood, mental health, and social integration, thereby constructing a life-course support system. Furthermore, the uptick in the global DS burden post-2019 serves as a warning, highlighting the urgent need to enhance the resilience of maternal health systems to ensure the continuity of prenatal screening services during major public health events. Concurrently, the persistent sex differences in DS burden suggest that clinical management should incorporate more targeted screening pathways, particularly concerning the risk of CHD comorbidity in male infants. Future research needs to integrate multi-source data further and explore the interactions between environmental, genetic, and social factors to provide a scientific basis for precise interventions.

Limitations

Nevertheless, this study has several key limitations. First, the estimated nature of GBD data may lead to a systematic underestimation of burden in low SDI regions, and the “detection rate effect” due to advances in diagnostic technology is difficult to fully correct. Second, as an ecological study, it cannot infer causality at the individual level, and heterogeneity among countries in culture, policy, and termination rates may introduce confounding effects. Third, the co-occurrence pattern analysis, based on quartile classification, may mask subtle differences near the cut-off points. Besides, the co-occurrence pattern analysis employed in this study operates at the population level, describing the rank combinations of disease burden between CHD and DS within countries or regions. This ecological approach should not be misinterpreted as comorbidity analysis at the individual level, and inferences regarding concurrent disease presence in the same individuals cannot be drawn from these findings. Future research should integrate multi-source data, construct multi-dimensional analytical frameworks, and conduct real-world effectiveness evaluations of interventions.

Conclusions

Over the past 32 years, both global and China levels have seen declines in the DALY burden of CHD and DS, with China achieving a more pronounced reduction. However, the rising prevalence burden highlights that life-course health management remains a weak link. The concordant co-occurrence pattern in prevalence burden suggests that prevention and control strategies for CHD and DS should adopt an integrated approach, addressing both common patterns and unique characteristics of each condition to further improve overall health outcomes.

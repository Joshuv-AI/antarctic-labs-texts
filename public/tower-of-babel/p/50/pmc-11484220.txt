Mechanism of biliary atresia caused by T follicular helper cells-induced immune injury
Authors: Ze Ji, Xiaoxia Wu, Hongxia Ren, Jiexiong Feng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBiliary atresia (BA) has diverse and unclear pathogenesis, which may be related to immune response in response to a foreign stimulus. T follicular helper (Tfh) cells have been found to play an important role in various immune diseases.AimsTo investigate the expression of Tfh cells in BA and non-BA cholestatic diseases in children.MethodsTranscriptome sequencing and Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis were performed to investigate the differences in gene expression between the BA group and the non-BA cholestasis group. Biliary atresia (BA) is the most common cause of pathological jaundice in neonates, but its underlying mechanism remains unclear. Affected infants are typically born in healthy with normal stool color. Again it is a fact that neonates with BA develop inflammation of biliary radicals followed by adhesions that organize and obliterate the biliary tract. BA involves progressive, inflammatory obstruction and subsequent breakdown of the intrahepatic and extrahepatic biliary tree, which can rapidly lead to liver cirrhosis and poor prognosis [1]. If BA is not treated promptly, most affected children die from end-stage liver disease within two years of birth. Despite timely Kasai surgery, the majority of patients require pediatric liver transplantation, which is associated with numerous issues [1, 2]. Therefore, the fundamental approach to treating BA involves identifying the cause and preventing the occurrence of BA.

Numerous domestic and international studies have proposed possible causes of BA, including perinatal viral infections, immune response, environmental toxins, genetic variations, maternal microchimerism, vascular malformations, morphological defects, etc. Extensive research has been conducted using methods such as genome-wide association studies (GWAS) and exon sequencing to find susceptible genes for BA such as ADD3, XPNPEP1, FOXA2, and GPC1 [3–5]. Among these, the theories of genetic susceptibility and viral infection-immune system abnormalities are research hotspots. However, the pathogenesis of BA cannot be fully explained by genetic susceptibility alone. In recent years, the role of immune response damage caused by foreign stimulus in the pathogenesis of BA has been significant, such as aflatoxins, biliatresone, and viruses [6, 7].

Luo Z et al. [8] had found that the proportion of Tfh cells was significantly higher in the low transplant-free survival group in BA patients compared to the high transplant-free survival group, indicating a correlation between Tfh cells and the duration of native liver survival in children with BA. Wu Shaojing et al. [9] had also found similar stduy. These findings has sparked our great interest. This study aimed to preliminarily explore the role of Tfh cells in inducing immune injury, which may be involved in the development of BA.

Materials and methods

Materials

Patients with cholestasis who were hospitalized at Shanxi Children’s Hospital and Tongji Hospital Affiliated to Tongji Medical College of Huazhong University of Science and Technology from January to October 2020 were selected. A total of 12 cases of BA and 10 cases of non-BA cholestatic liver disease were included in this study, and their liver tissue specimens were collected for transcriptome sequencing and immunostaining. The patients were divided into two groups, the experimental group (BA group), and the control group (non-BA cholestatic group). BA patients were diagnosed by intraoperative cholangiography and liver histology examination, and the control group included liver biopsies of patients diagnosed as cholestasis (CS) or choledochal cyst (CC).

All liver tissues were obtained during surgery after receiving informed consent of the parents. This study was approved by the ethics committee of Tongji Hospital of Tongji Medical College of Huazhong University of Science and Technology (2021-S104) and Shanxi Provincial Children’s Hospital (2RB-SB-2021-006).

Methods

Transcriptome sequencing and Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis

Total RNA was extracted from liver tissues of patients using TRIzol method, followed by concentration, purification and integration. Then, cDNA libraries were constructed for each sample using NEBNext mRNA sample prep kit 1 (New England Biolabs, Massachusetts, America), and the libraries were sequenced on Illumina HiSeq platform (Illumina, California, America) after quality control, to generate raw sequence reads. Further quality control was performed by removing adapters from the raw reads, and removing the reads with low quality and artificial reads to generate clean reads. TopHat was used to align the clean reads to the human genome (GRCh37/hg19) with default parameters, and only unique matched sequence reads were retained for further analysis. Cufflinksv2.2.1.2 software was used to process the aligned reads to calculate the transcription level of each gene from different samples. Limma R package was used to analyze the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the BA and cholestasis groups. We used FDR-corrected p-value of 0.05 as the statistical significance threshold. Genes with adj.P.val < 0.05 and |log2FC|>1 were considered as DEGs.

To investigate the biological significance of DEGs, we performed GO enrichment analysis using clusterProfiler R package. GO is an established international classification system for gene function that provides a dynamically updated regulated vocabulary and rigorously defined concepts to identify genes and their products [10]. GO has three ontologies: molecular functions, cellular components, and biological processes. GO enrichment analysis provides all GO terms that are significantly enriched for DEGs compared to genomic background and filters for DEGs corresponding to biological functions. The 5% false discovery rate (FDR) cutoff value was used as the enrichment threshold.

Single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA)

Single Sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) was calculated using the gene set variation analysis program in R package. The individual RNA expression score for statistical analysis was performed to identify significant differences in immune cell infiltration between BA and CC groups, with a significance threshold set at P < 0.05. Box plots were used to show differences in immune infiltration levels.

Immunofluorescence

Liver tissues from patients with BA (n = 20), CS (n = 20), and CC (n = 10) were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned. The sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to antigen retrieval using EDTA antigen retrieval solution. After blocking with 5% BSA, the sections were washed with PBST and then incubated with fluorescently-labeled secondary antibody Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor® 488) (Abcam, Cambridge, UK, ab150077) and Goat Anti-Rat IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor® 594) (Abcam, Cambridge, UK, ab150160) for 1 h at 37 ℃. After washing with PBST, the sections were stained with DAPI and mounted with anti-fade mounting medium. Images were acquired using a fluorescence microscope and analyzed using Image J software (1.43u Windows) to quantify the number and area of positively stained cells.

Discussion

T follicular helper (Tfh) cells are a novel subset of CD4+ cells that develop in secondary lymphoid organs. They promote the formation and maintenance of germinal centers (GCs), stimulate B cell proliferation, antibody class switching, and differentiation into plasma cells and memory B cells by secreting interleukin (IL)-21 [11]. Tfh cells play a dominant role in B cell-mediated humoral immunity, and are involved in the pathogenesis of immune diseases, tumors, and infectious diseases. It has been discovered that the immune response related to Tfh cells can help kidney transplant recipients resist CMV infection, thereby improving their prognosis [12]. Studies have confirmed that the increased Tfh cells further induce the production of B cells and related antibodies in acute on chronic hepatitis B liver failure [13].

Research on the association between Tfh cells and BA is still rare, with some scholars [7, 8] investigating the correlation between Tfh cells and the prognosis of children with biliary atresia. Our study is the first to suggest that Tfh cells play a significant role in the pathogenesis of BA by regulating B cells.

DEGs were identified through transcriptome sequencing and bioinformatics analysis of liver tissue samples from the BA group and the control group. A total of 808 genes were up-regulated and 405 genes were down-regulated in the BA group compared with the control group, which suggest there might be a specific immune response in BA. GO enrichment analysis showed that the DEGs for types of interaction proteins mainly converged in Biological Process (BP), including augmented response to a foreign stimulus and increased metabolic process in the BA group. The ssGSEA method was used to analyze the relative proportions of immune cells in liver tissue samples, and the results showed that the proportions of Tfh cells, activated B cells, and memory B cells were all higher in the BA patients than in the CC patients. Combined with the regulatory role of Tfh cells on B cells, we believe that the increased Tfh cells in BA induced immune activation, which manifested as an increase in activated B cells and memory B cells. The antibody production by B cells and their immune-mediated effects can further exacerbate immune injury. Activated B cells produce antibodies that seem to initially clear foreign stimuli. Furthermore, these antibodies against foreign stimulant appear to be non-neutralizing, manifesting as obstructive bile ducts and accelerated liver cirrhosis. The immune response in BA is pivotal for the development of BA inflammation and ongoing obliterative process. Davenport et al. [14] found that CD4 + lymphocytes and CD56+ (NK cells) were predominant in the liver and biliary remnants of extrahepatic biliary atresia (EHBA) infants, exhibiting marked proliferation and activation. Kotb MA et al. [15] investigated immune-mediated liver injury in EHBA and found postoperative liver tissue was more heavily infiltrated with CD4+, CD8+, and CD68 + macrophages cells in the portal tracts and lobules, compared to preoperative biopsies. These studies offer a deeper understanding of the immunological of EHBA. In addition, we used CD3 (red fluorescence) and Bcl6 (green fluorescence) to label Tfh cells (CD3+Bcl6+) in liver tissue samples, and immunofluorescence staining suggested that Tfh cells (CD3+Bcl6+) were more abundant in the liver tissue samples of the BA group than in the control group, which was consistent with our transcriptome sequencing results.

Tfh cells can regulate the development of B cells. CD4+ T lymphocytes with high expression of CCR7 are chemotactically attracted by chemokine-C motif ligands (CCL) 21 and CCL19, and remain in the T lymphocyte zone [16]. Initial B lymphocytes with high expression of the chemokine receptor 5 (C-X-C chemokine receptor, CXCR5) settle in the lymphoid follicles under the chemotaxis of CXCL13. Initial CD4+ T lymphocytes interact with antigen-presenting cells (APC) to initiate Tfh cell differentiation. It is worth noting that Tfh cell differentiation is a multi-stage, multi-factorial process [17]. There is no single event that defines Tfh cell differentiation. When CXCR5 expression is up-regulated, they begin to migrate towards the lymphoid follicles, and B lymphocytes that receive antigen recognition signals enhance the expression of CCR7 and migrate from the follicles to the T lymphocyte zone. T and B lymphocytes meet at the intersection of the T lymphocyte zone and the lymphoid follicles, initiating T-B cell interactions. CD4+CXCR5+ T lymphocytes induce transcription factors such as B-cell lymphoma 6 (Bcl6) by expressing inducible co-stimulator (ICOS) and co-stimulatory molecules, and interact with activated B lymphocytes, promoting sustained high expression of CXCR5 and further differentiation of Tfh cells into effector Tfh cells [18]. Effector Tfh cells express CD40L and secrete cytokines such as IL-21, IL-4, and IL-6, which bind to CD40 and cytokine receptors on the surface of B lymphocytes, constituting the second signal for B lymphocyte activation. Activated B lymphocytes provide early defense for the body’s anti-infective immunity foci, mainly by secreting IgM in the B lymphocyte zone outside the follicles. B lymphocytes also migrate to the lymphoid follicles, interacting with Tfh cells to form GC. In GC, B lymphocytes undergo processes such as clonal proliferation, somatic hypermutation, antibody class switching and antibody affinity maturation, ultimately differentiating into plasma cells and memory B lymphocytes [19]. Our results also showed differences in immune cells such as Tfh cells between the BA group and the non-BA cholestasis group.

Taken together, our study found an increase in Tfh cells expression in BA, accompanied by an increase in activated B cells and memory B cells, suggesting that the increased Tfh cells may be involved in immune cascade involvement in BA. Our work support immune pathogenesis of the in response to a stimulus that might be foreign in BA.

Many foreign stimuli have been explored. Kotb MA et al. [7] showed that Kotb disease BA, a variant of BA, is a result of persistent congenital aflatoxicosis, genetic lack of Glutathione S-transferase M1 (GSTM1) detoxification, ontogenically impaired hepatic detoxification, massive neutrophil elastase activity, immune-induced damage, and disturbed regeneration. Our work actually supports that BA is not a congenital defect but a chemical induced one, with an immune response that most probably aims to quarantine the chemical (by T cell response within the tissues) and arrest its circulation by developing neutralizing antibodies. Given that toxins as aflatoxins are notorious to cause central nervous system injury, the B cell response to develop neutralizing antibodies seems to a successful immune response to limit the damage induced by the aflatoxins/other chemicals to other remote tissues beyond the hepatocytes and cholanocytes. Research has found that the synthetic toxin biliatresone can selectively damage the extrahepatic bile ducts in mice and lead to the formation of BA [20, 21]. Although humans are almost certainly not exposed to biliatresone, the identification of key structural motifs may lead to toxins with human relevance [22]. Cytomegalovirus (CMV), reovirus, rotavirus, Epstein-Barr virus, and human papillomavirus have been proposed as potential etiologies of BA [23]. The Rhesus rotavirus-induced BA mouse model has been used in investigating the role of viruses and inflammation in the pathogenesis of bile duct injury in BA. We have formulated a plausible hypothesis to speculate on the potential underlying mechanisms of action (Fig. 5).

A plausible hypothesis that Tfh cells participate in the pathogenesis through the immune response in BA

Our study also has certain limitations. The work we have conducted in this study is preliminary. In this study, there is a lack of tissue pathology and identification of T lymphocytes / neutrophils etc. to provide evidence that Tfh cells was indeed a part of an augmented immune response to a foreign stimulus or not. Further research is needed to explore the foreign stimulus and the underlying mechanisms of Tfh cells leading to BEC damage.

Conclusion

Tfh cells share in immune cascade involvement in BA. Our work support immune pathogenesis of the in response to a stimulus that might be foreign in BA.

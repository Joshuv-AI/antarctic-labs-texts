Five-Year Follow-Up Analysis of ZUMA-5: Axicabtagene Ciloleucel in Relapsed/Refractory Indolent Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma
Authors: Sattva S. Neelapu, Julio C. Chavez, Alison R. Sehgal, Narendranath Epperla, Matthew L. Ulrickson, Emmanuel Bachy, Pashna N. Munshi, Carla Casulo, David G. Maloney, Sven de Vos, Ran Reshef, Lori A. Leslie, Olalekan O. Oluwole, Ibrahim Yakoub-Agha, Rashmi Khanal, Joseph D. Rosenblatt, Jacob Wulff, Rhine R. Shen, Wangshu Zhang, Soumya Poddar, Harry Miao, Olga Nikolajeva, Caron A. Jacobson
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Axicabtagene ciloleucel (axi-cel) is an autologous anti-CD19 chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy approved for relapsed/refractory (R/R) follicular lymphoma (FL). Here, we report updated clinical outcomes from ZUMA-5 in 159 enrolled patients with R/R indolent non-Hodgkin lymphoma (iNHL; 127 with FL and 31 with marginal zone lymphoma) after a median follow-up of 64.6 months. Patients underwent leukapheresis and received lymphodepleting chemotherapy and axi-cel (2 × 106 CAR T cells/kg). The overall response rate was 90% (75% complete response rate). The median duration of response was 60.4 months, and the median progression-free survival (PFS) was 62.2 months; median time to next treatment and overall survival were not reached (NR). At data cutoff, 55% of patients were alive without requiring subsequent anticancer therapy. Median lymphoma-specific PFS in patients with FL was NR; 34% had progression or death due to lymphoma or study treatment. Notably, after 30 months postinfusion, progression or lymphoma-related deaths were rare. Late-onset toxicities were infrequent and largely unrelated to axi-cel. Durable response and prolonged survival in FL were associated with robust early CAR T-cell expansion and naïve product phenotype. These findings confirm sustained responses and manageable safety with axi-cel in the long term among patients with R/R iNHL and its potential as a curative therapy in FL.

INTRODUCTION

Axicabtagene ciloleucel (axi-cel), an autologous anti-CD19 chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy, is approved in patients with relapsed/refractory (R/R) follicular lymphoma (FL).1,2 Approval was based on the pivotal ZUMA-5 study in indolent non-Hodgkin lymphoma (iNHL), including FL and marginal zone lymphoma (MZL), demonstrating a 92% overall response rate (ORR) per independent review (n = 104; 74% complete response [CR] rate) in the primary analysis.3 After an extended median follow-up of 41.7 months in patients with FL (n = 127), the median progression-free survival (PFS) was 40.2 months and median overall survival (OS) was not reached (NR).4

Given the heterogeneity of iNHL and the competing risks contributing to PFS events in FL, it is important to assess long-term lymphoma-specific efficacy outcomes to determine the curative potential of axi-cel in FL.5 Herein, we report updated outcomes from ZUMA-5 after ≥5 years of follow-up, including exploratory, post hoc lymphoma-specific survival assessments.

METHODS

Patients and Study Design

Study design and procedures of the phase II, single-arm, multicenter ZUMA-5 study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03105336) have been previously reported.3,4 Briefly, eligible adults had histologically confirmed FL (grade 1-3a) or MZL (nodal or extranodal) that was R/R after ≥2 prior therapies, including an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody plus an alkylating agent (Data Supplement, online only). The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, all patients provided written informed consent, and the protocol was approved by the institutional review board at each site.

Patients underwent leukapheresis, followed by lymphodepleting chemotherapy (fludarabine 30 mg/m2 once a day and cyclophosphamide 500 mg/m2 once a day) on days –5 to –3 before infusion and axi-cel (2 × 106 CAR T cells/kg) on day 0.

End Points and Statistical Analyses

Disease assessments for response were performed by investigators. The analysis occurred when enrolled patients with FL had a median follow-up of ≥60 months. Time-to-event end points were assessed using Kaplan-Meier (KM) methodology. Deaths not related to lymphoma or study treatment were censored in KM assessments of lymphoma-specific survival. Lymphoma-specific survival was also assessed using a competing risk approach (Data Supplement).

RESULTS

Patients

As previously reported, 159 patients with iNHL were enrolled and underwent leukapheresis (Table 1), of whom 127 had FL, 31 had MZL, and one had diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL, patient did not receive axi-cel).4 Of those with iNHL, 152 received axi-cel as of March 31, 2024 (124 with FL; 28 with MZL).

Efficacy

The median follow-up from leukapheresis in enrolled patients was 64.6 months (range, 32.3-81.4; FL, 65.7; MZL, 55.8). Responses were consistent with previous analyses,4 with an ORR of 90% and a 75% CR rate. The median time to best response was 1.0 month in FL (range, 0.7-24.9) and 2.8 months in MZL (range, 0.8-14.3). At data cutoff, 58% of patients who had achieved a CR remained in CR (Data Supplement, Fig S1). Among 16 patients who were retreated with axi-cel (median follow-up, 45.5 months post-retreatment), the median post-retreatment duration of response was 11.6 months, with 38% of retreated patients in ongoing response at data cutoff (Data Supplement, Fig S2).

The median PFS in enrolled patients with iNHL was 62.2 months, with 50.4% of patients achieving the 60-month landmark (Fig 1A), consistent regardless of high-risk features (Data Supplement, Figs S3 and S4). Four progression events occurred after 24 months post-leukapheresis (all FL); two occurred after month 30 (Data Supplement). In a competing risk analysis of lymphoma-specific PFS in patients with FL, the cumulative incidence of progression or lymphoma-specific deaths was 34%, with a 60-month rate of 35.1% (Fig 1B). The 60-month rate of competing risks (deaths unrelated to lymphoma or study treatment) was 15.1%. Median lymphoma-specific PFS by KM assessment (where deaths not related to lymphoma or study treatment were censored) was not yet reached in either disease type, with 60-month estimates of 64.0% and 62.8% in FL and MZL, respectively (Data Supplement, Fig S5).

At data cutoff, 55% of all enrolled patients were alive without need for any subsequent anticancer therapy. Median time to next treatment remained NR since the 3-year analysis, with a 60-month estimated rate of 53.3%, consistent in both disease types (Data Supplement, Fig S6).4 Median OS also remained unreached, with a 60-month estimate of 69.0% (Data Supplement, Fig S7). In the competing risk assessment, 19 patients with FL (15%) died due to lymphoma or study treatment. The 60-month lymphoma-specific death rate was 15.6%, as was the rate of competing risks. Outcomes in patients with FL with ≥3 prior therapies are reported in the Data Supplement (Table S1).

Safety

After the data cutoff of the 3-year analysis, 13 treated patients experienced new adverse events (Data Supplement, Tables S2-S5).4 One serious event considered related to axi-cel (grade 3 myelodysplastic syndrome) occurred after the prior data cutoff (Data Supplement).

A total of 46 patients (30%) died anytime on study (Table 2 and Data Supplement, Table S6). Of them, 23 (15%) were considered relapse-related mortalities, with 14 (9%) deaths from progressive disease and 9 (6%) because of reasons other than progression among those who progressed. Nonrelapse mortalities occurred in 23 patients (15%): 11 (7%) infection related and associated with B-cell aplasia, 6 (4%) because of second primary malignancies (five acute leukemia and one anal cancer), 3 (2%) cardiac related, 2 (1%) unknown, and 1 (1%) because of cytokine release syndrome. After the data cutoff of the 3-year analysis,4 eight patients died.

Biomarkers and Correlative Analyses

CAR T-cell peak levels significantly associated with improved PFS in patients with FL and MZL (Data Supplement, Figs S8 and S9). Substantial CAR T-cell expansion was observed at retreatment (Data Supplement, Fig S10).

Among patients with FL, those who remained in ongoing response had a higher frequency of naïve T cells (CCR7+CD45RA+) enriched in axi-cel product (22.3%) than those who relapsed (13.5%) or did not respond (9.0%); this also correlated with prior bendamustine use (Data Supplement, Table S7). The percent of naïve T cells in product was also associated with longer median PFS in those patients (Data Supplement, Fig S11). Patients with reduced or absent B cells at 3 and 6 months did not show a pronounced propensity to develop infections on study; B-cell recovery was observed at 6 months (Data Supplement, Fig S12).

DISCUSSION

After a median ≥5 years of follow-up in FL and nearly 5 years in MZL, this analysis of ZUMA-5 confirmed durable responses, long-term survival benefit regardless of high-risk features, and no new safety signals with axi-cel. Median PFS among patients with FL was extended since the 3-year analysis (57.3 v 40.2 months).4 Survival outcomes among those with MZL continued to improve with longer follow-up.4 New second primary malignancies considered related to lymphodepleting chemotherapy and/or axi-cel were observed, although with rare frequency (none of T-cell origin). Nonrelapse mortalities on study were mainly due to infections and second primary malignancies.

In contrast to DLBCL,7,8 PFS with CAR T-cell therapies after 2 years of follow-up in R/R FL demonstrates a less obvious plateau, in part due to the long latency for the clinical manifestation of progression events and the deaths due to competing risks before progression, captured as PFS events even in patients who may be cured of their disease.9 Therefore, lymphoma-specific PFS may more accurately represent the curative potential of CAR T-cell therapy in FL. This analysis showed a plateau in lymphoma-specific PFS emerging after 2 years, with only two events after month 30 (Fig 1B). Taken together, these results suggest the possibility of the curative potential of axi-cel in R/R FL.

A phase III randomized controlled trial has launched to examine the benefit of axi-cel compared with standard-of-care therapy for R/R FL (ZUMA-22; ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05371093).

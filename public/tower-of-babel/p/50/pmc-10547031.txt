Reply to: Rethinking Vasopressor Education: The Need to Avoid Teaching the Bare Minimum
Authors: Daniel K. Manson, Amy L. Dzierba, Kaitlin M. Seitz, Daniel Brodie
Published: 2023

From the Authors:

We thank McCurdy and Busse for their thoughtful letter in response to our teaching approach (1). We emphatically agree with the importance of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) in human evolutionary physiology and the role of angiotensin II in the management of refractory shock. In fact, in earlier versions of our article, we included such a section, detailing that renal hypoperfusion during septic shock leads to the activation of RAAS, the release of angiotensin II, and peripheral vasoconstriction to maintain vascular tone (2). We explained that sepsis models have shown that proinflammatory cytokines downregulate angiotensin receptors (3) and that animal models, early human studies, and larger randomized controlled trials demonstrated that angiotensin II administration in distributive shock states is associated with a significant blood pressure response and reduction in norepinephrine requirements (4–7).

It was ultimately decided with the editorial team that, although this topic is undoubtedly important, the initial length of the teaching methodology would be too cumbersome to implement practically and succinctly. Furthermore, given that angiotensin II may not be universally available, it was believed to be beyond the scope of this teaching topic. Nonetheless, in developing high-quality and feasible teaching approaches, numerous changes may be made to suit the particular teaching environment, and the addition of RAAS and angiotensin II could be readily justifiable.

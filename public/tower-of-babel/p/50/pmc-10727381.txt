Trends in Use of Intravenous Thrombolysis and Endovascular Thrombectomy in Patients With Acute Stroke With Large Vessel Occlusion 2016 to 2020 and Impact of COVID‐19 Pandemic
Authors: Mihir Khunte, Huanwen Chen, Akshay Khunte, Seyedmehdi Payabvash, Dheeraj Gandhi, Ajay Malhotra
Published: 2023

Several observational studies have previously raised concerns about disruption of care delivery for patients with acute ischemic stroke because of the COVID‐19 pandemic. We investigated the US nationwide use of intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) and endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) in patients with large‐vessel‐occlusion (LVO) in 2020, compared it to the previous 4 years, and evaluated their use specifically in patients with COVID‐19.

The Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project's National Inpatient Sample database was used to identify patients with LVO and the interventions performed from 2016 to 2020 using International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision (ICD‐10) codes using methodology similar to previous papers. 1 No ethics approval was required for this study as no sensitive data were used and all materials were collected from open, published sources.

Adult patients (18 years or older) with a primary diagnosis code for cerebral infarction attributable to unilateral internal carotid artery or middle cerebral artery thrombosis or embolism were identified. In the 2020 data, patients with a concomitant diagnosis of COVID‐19 were identified. 2 We analyzed EVT and IVT rates by COVID‐19 status and compared using Pearson Chi‐square test. P values less than 0.05 were deemed statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using R, Version 3.6.2.

A total of 316 290 patients were hospitalized for acute ischemic stroke with an internal carotid artery/middle cerebral artery occlusion from 2016 to 2020. Of these patients, 21.7% (n=68 780 [95% CI, 21.2%–22.3%]) received thrombectomy and 22.5% (n=71 260 [95% CI, 22.1%–22.9%]) received IVT. The proportion of patients receiving thrombectomy increased by 11.9% from 15.7% (95% CI, 14.5%–16.8%) in 2016 to 27.6% in 2020 (95% CI, 26.3%–28.9%) (P<0.001; Figure). From 2019 to 2020, the fraction of patients receiving thrombectomy increased 2.1% from 25.5% (95% CI, 24.2%–26.7%) in 2019 to 27.6% in 2020 (95% CI, 26.3%–28.9%, P=0.020; Figure). Meanwhile, rates of thrombolysis remained relatively constant (22.4% [95% CI, 21.4%–23.4%] in 2016 versus 21.7% [95% CI, 20.8%–22.5%] in 2020, P=0.128; Figure). Among the 63 785 patients presenting with internal carotid artery/middle cerebral artery occlusion in 2020, 1170 (1.8% [95% CI, 1.6%–2.1%]) had a codiagnosis of COVID‐19. The rate of EVT among patients with COVID‐19 and non‐COVID‐19 stroke with LVO in 2020 was 29.1% (95% CI, 23.2%–34.9%) and 27.6% (95% CI, 26.3%–28.8%), respectively (P=0.605). In addition, the proportion of patients receiving IVT was 21.8% (95% CI, 16.6%–27.0%) and 21.7% (95% CI, 20.8%–22.5%) for patients with COVID‐19 and non‐COVID‐19 stroke, respectively (P = 0.962).

Trends in proportion of patients with internal carotid artery‐middle cerebral artery large vessel occlusion receiving intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy, 2016 to 2020 National Inpatient Sample.

EVT indicates endovascular thrombectomy; IVT, intravenous thrombolysis; and LVO, large vessel occlusion.

EVT usage in patients with large vessel acute ischemic stroke has been increasing because of improved detection of LVO and better outcomes in patients with recanalization. 3 The proportion of patients with a middle cerebral artery/internal carotid artery LVO receiving IVT and EVT did not decline in 2020 compared with the previous 4 years. Specifically, patients with COVID‐19 received recanalization therapies in similar proportion to patients without COVID‐19 for the whole year. The codiagnosis of COVID‐19 and LVO in our study (1.8%) is similar to previously published studies. 4 There may have been a reduction in rates of recanalization therapies in the initial months with recovery in later periods of the pandemic as shown by some previous studies. 5

Uncovering the protective mechanisms of Bacillus subtilis fmbj against LPS-induced hepatic immune stress and redox imbalance in broilers through transcriptomic profiling
Authors: Luyi Jiang, Kaiwen Bai, Tian Wang, Yiwei Cui, Yan Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigated the protective effects of Bacillus subtilis fmbj (BS) in alleviating hepatic immune stress and redox imbalance induced by lipopolysaccharide (LPS) in broilers. A total of 240 chickens were randomly assigned to three groups, each consisting of ten replicates with eight birds per replicate. Birds in the LPS and BSLPS groups received intraperitoneal injections of LPS (1 mg/kg body weight), whereas the CON group was administered an equivalent volume of 0.9 % NaCl solution from day 14 to day 20. LPS exposure reduced growth performance and caused pronounced histopathological alterations and mitochondrial swelling in the liver compared with the CON group (P < 0.05). The LPS challenge also elevated immune stress markers (IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, and IFN-γ) in both serum and liver, disrupted hepatic redox balance (SOD, T-AOC, POD, GSH-Px, GSH, GR, MDA), and impaired mitochondrial antioxidant capacity (MnSOD, GPx, GSH, GR, GST, γ-GCL, ROS, PC, 8-OHdG, MMP) (P < 0.05). Furthermore, LPS altered the expression of redox- and immune-related genes and proteins, particularly Nrf2 and NF-κB (P < 0.05). Although broilers in the BSLPS group still exhibited hepatic morphological damage, redox imbalance in hepatic tissue and mitochondria, and elevated immune stress indicators compared with the CON group (P < 0.05), BS supplementation attenuated liver injuries relative to the LPS group (P < 0.05). Transcriptomic analysis further demonstrated that BS modulated multiple signaling pathways, including the Toll-like receptor pathway, NF-κB pathway, cell adhesion molecules, and osteoclast differentiation. These findings suggest that the protective role of BS may be mediated, at least in part, through the regulation of the Nrf2/NF-κB signaling axis. In conclusion, this study highlights the potential of Bacillus subtilis fmbj as a functional feed additive for improving liver health and enhancing immune resilience in poultry industry.Keywords: Chicken, Bacillus subtilis fmbj, Liver, Immune stress, Redox imbalance

Introduction

Within modern intensive poultry production systems, broilers are frequently exposed to a variety of external and internal stressors that profoundly affect growth trajectories, often triggering immune stress responses and disrupting intrinsic redox homeostasis (Zhang et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023). Major contributing factors include high stocking density, inadequate hygiene, nutritional imbalances, and environmental or handling stress, all of which are closely linked to broiler health outcomes. Addressing these challenges is crucial not only for optimizing growth efficiency but also for safeguarding animal welfare. Stress-related insults compromise physiological homeostasis by impairing antioxidant defense mechanisms, reducing growth performance, and diminishing overall production efficiency, thereby elevating rearing costs and undermining economic profitability (Alkhtib et al., 2023). During the early to mid-growth phase (0 to 21 days post-hatch), chicks encounter substantial physiological demands, including thermoregulation, rapid body mass accumulation, and concurrent development of digestive and immune organs (Pirompud et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2024). Importantly, this developmental stage represents a critical window of susceptibility to redox imbalance. Such imbalance, often arising from excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation in parallel with inadequate antioxidant defenses, precipitates oxidative damage at the cellular level and compromises structural integrity and functional capacity (Cheng et al., 2023). These disruptions may impair immune competence, suppress growth, increase susceptibility to inflammation-associated disorders, and contribute to the development of metabolic dysfunctions (Cheng et al., 2023; Mishra et al., 2023).

The liver plays a pivotal role in maintaining systemic physiological homeostasis and metabolic function in birds, coordinating essential biological processes such as protein synthesis, metabolic regulation, and detoxification. Impairment of hepatic function can substantially compromise protein biosynthesis, thereby negatively impacting broiler growth (May and Bird, 2023). In addition, the liver serves as a central component of the detoxification network, facilitating the degradation and clearance of harmful metabolites (Berasain et al., 2023). During the grower phase, the liver functions as a key metabolic hub, regulating the metabolism of proteins, lipids, and carbohydrates, while simultaneously contributing to the preservation of redox balance. However, hepatic immune stress and redox imbalance can disrupt these functions, leading to lipid peroxidation of cellular membranes, mitochondrial injury, and dysregulation of intracellular activities (Cheng et al., 2023; Pant et al., 2024). Such dysfunction hampers nutrient digestion and assimilation, ultimately reducing feed efficiency and growth performance (Lotto et al., 2023). Redox imbalance arises when the production of reactive oxidants surpasses the capacity of intrinsic antioxidant defenses (Wu et al., 2023). Nutritional strategies such as dietary supplementation with antioxidants (Artemisia annua L. or Lactiplantibacillus plantarum) have been demonstrated to reinforce the antioxidant defense system, mitigate immune stress, and support the maintenance of redox homeostasis (Zhang et al., 2022, 2024). By strengthening both antioxidant and anti-inflammatory mechanisms, broilers are better equipped to cope with physiological stressors, thereby maintaining overall health and productive performance.

The application of probiotics has garnered substantial attention in both human healthcare and animal agriculture, largely due to increasing recognition of their diverse biological activities, including the regulation of redox balance and attenuation of immune stress (Cao et al., 2023; Merenstein et al., 2023). Accumulating evidence has demonstrated that supplementation with Bacillus-derived probiotics enhances hepatic function, improves nutrient utilization, alleviates hepatic inflammation, restores redox stability, and promotes overall health in animals (Lu et al., 2024). Consequently, probiotics have been widely incorporated into livestock feed formulations, supported by strong scientific validation and ongoing technological innovations (Chauhan and Sharma, 2023; Dong et al., 2024). Among candidate probiotic strains, Bacillus subtilis has shown particular promise in modulating redox signaling pathways, enhancing digestive efficiency, and improving feed conversion ratios in broilers (Chen et al., 2023). These functional benefits contribute to improved growth performance and reduced inflammatory responses (Oladokun and Adewole, 2023). Moreover, Bacillus subtilis synthesizes a broad range of bioactive compounds, including antimicrobial peptides, enzymes, vitamins, polysaccharides, and signaling molecules, which collectively mitigate oxidative stress and inflammatory damage, thereby supporting healthy development in poultry (Lu et al., 2024; Wickramasuriya et al., 2023). Previous studies have shown that dietary supplementation with BS at 0.3 g/kg markedly improves growth performance, enhances antioxidant defenses, and restores redox balance in chickens (Bai et al., 2018, 2017; Qian et al., 2015; Zhang et al., 2017). Nevertheless, the underlying health-promoting mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Compared with traditional molecular approaches, transcriptome sequencing provides higher sensitivity and resolution, enabling genome-wide profiling of gene expression dynamics, the identification of novel transcripts, alternative splicing events, and non-coding RNAs, thereby offering a powerful platform for elucidating molecular regulatory networks associated with poultry health (Gu et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025). However, its application in poultry health research remains constrained by challenges such as the requirement for advanced computational analyses, dependency on sample integrity and sequencing depth, its inherent snapshot nature that does not directly capture protein-level changes, and the incomplete annotation of poultry-specific genomes and databases. Although promising evidence supports the beneficial effects of Bacillus subtilis, comprehensive insights into its protective role against hepatic immune stress and redox imbalance in LPS-challenged broilers during mid-growth remain limited.

Materials and methods

Experimental design

A Bacillus subtilis fmbj (BS, 1.0 × 10¹¹ cfu/g) supplementation level of 0.3 g/kg was selected according to validated results from earlier laboratory research (Bai et al., 2017, 2018). To preserve its bioactivity and viability, the BS powder was stored in sterile, sealed containers prior to use.

The 240 one-day-old male Arbor Acres broilers were distributed to three groups randomly, with ten replicates/group and eight birds/replicate. The experimental groups: (1) a control group receiving a standard basal diet (CON group); (2) an LPS-challenged group fed the basal diet, in which birds were administered lipopolysaccharide (LPS, serotype O55:B5, #L2880, Sigma-Aldrich Chemical Inc., St. Louis, MO, USA, purity 0.86 %) (LPS grouup); and (3) an LPS-challenged group supplemented with Bacillus subtilis (laboratory-retained strain) at 0.3 g/kg of the basal diet (BSLPS group). Birds in the LPS and BSLPS groups received daily intraperitoneal injections of LPS (1 mg/kg body weight) from day 14 to day 20, while those in the CON group were injected with an equal volume of sterile saline (Erinle et al., 2022; Jin et al., 2025). All birds were provided with nutritionally complete basal diets formulated to meet or exceed the requirements outlined in the Nutrient Requirements of Poultry . Each replicate was housed in a stainless-steel cage (120 cm × 70 cm × 40 cm), equipped with a trough feeder and a drinker to allow ad libitum access to feed and water. The experimental period lasted 21 days, during which birds were maintained under climate-controlled conditions, with the ambient temperature held at 34–36°C from days 1 to 14 and gradually reduced to 26°C thereafter. A 24-h continuous lighting program was implemented. All experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with ethical guidelines and were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Nanjing Agricultural University (SYXK[Su]2017-0027). Sample collection

Twenty-four hours after the last LPS injection, ten birds (one broiler per replicate) were selected for sample collection. Approximately 5 mL of blood was drawn from the jugular vein using 10 mL tubes. The blood samples were centrifuged at 3,000 × g at 4°C for 10 min to collect the serum. The resulting serum was aliquoted into labeled tubes and stored at −80°C for future biochemical assessments. Following humane euthanasia via cervical dislocation, liver samples were collected from the same anatomical region on the left lobe after carefully removing surface impurities, and immediately processed for hepatic morphological evaluation. Additionally, about 8 grams of liver were collected, then snap-frozen and stored in liquid nitrogen for later RNA-seq study.

Growth performance

Body weight and total feed intake (FI) were recorded for each replicate. These data were used to calculate key performance parameters, including average body weight gain (BWG), FI per pen, and feed conversion ratio (FCR), thereby providing a comprehensive evaluation of growth performance across treatment groups. All birds remained clinically healthy throughout the trial, and no mortality was recorded.

Determination of hepatic morphological measurement

Liver tissue samples (1.5 cm × 1.5 cm × 1.5 cm) were fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde, dehydrated through a graded ethanol and xylene series, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into 6 μm slices using a rotary microtome. Sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (HE) and examined under a light microscope (Olympus CX31, Tokyo, Japan) to assess histopathological alterations (Dong et al., 2016).

For ultrastructural analysis, liver samples (1 cm × 1 cm × 1 cm) were fixed in 2.5 % glutaraldehyde, followed by post-fixation in 2 % osmium tetroxide for 5 min. Samples were processed according to standard transmission electron microscopy (TEM) protocols and examined using a Philips 420 TEM operated at 80 kV to evaluate subcellular architecture (Dong et al., 2016).

Determination of serum and hepatic immune status

Approximately 2 g of liver tissue was homogenized on ice in 18 mL of NaCl solution at 6800 × g for 10 s. The homogenates were centrifuged at 2800 × g for 15 min at 4°C to obtain the liver supernatant. Both serum and liver supernatant were subsequently used for cytokine determination, including interleukin-1β (IL-1β, #H002), interleukin-6 (IL-6, #H007), interleukin-10 (IL-10, #H009), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α, #H052), and interferon-γ (IFN-γ, #H025). Cytokine concentrations were quantified using commercial ELISA kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, standards and samples were assayed in duplicate on pre-coated 96-well plates, with serially diluted standards used to construct calibration curves. A total of 100 µL of standards, blanks, or diluted samples was added to each well, and plates were incubated at 37°C for 1 h, followed by 3-5 washes. Subsequently, 100 µL of HRP-conjugated detection antibody was added and incubated for 30-60 min at 37°C, followed by washing. Color development was achieved by adding 90 µL of TMB substrate and incubating for 10–15 min at 37°C in the dark. The reaction was terminated with 50 µL of stop solution, and optical density was measured at 450 nm with a 570-630 nm reference using a microplate reader (BioTek Instruments, USA). Cytokine concentrations were calculated from four-parameter logistic standard curves.

Determination of hepatic redox balance

Hepatic supernatant was used to assess antioxidant and oxidative stress markers, including superoxide dismutase (SOD, #A001-3), catalase (CAT, #A007-1), total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC, #A015-2), glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px, #A005-1), reduced glutathione (GSH, #A006-1), glutathione reductase (GR, #A062-1), peroxidase (POD, #A084-3), and malondialdehyde (MDA, #A003-1), using commercial kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute). SOD activity was determined by the WST-1/xanthine oxidase inhibition method, with absorbance monitored at 450 nm; one unit of activity was defined as the amount causing 50 % inhibition of WST-1 reduction. CAT activity was measured by the H₂O₂ decomposition–molybdate quenching method, with the chromogenic complex read at 405 nm. T-AOC was evaluated using the ABTS radical-scavenging assay (absorbance at 405-734 nm), with results expressed as Trolox equivalents. GSH-Px activity was assessed using the GSH—H₂O₂ substrate system, where residual GSH reacted with DTNB and was quantified at 412 nm (inversely proportional to activity). GSH content was determined by the DTNB method at 412 nm following protein removal, calibrated against GSH standards. GR activity was measured based on the rate of NADPH oxidation in the presence of GSSG, monitored at 340 nm. POD activity was determined by guaiacol oxidation in the presence of H₂O₂, recording absorbance increases at 470 nm. Lipid peroxidation was quantified as MDA content using the thiobarbituric acid reactive substances (TBARS) assay, with absorbance measured at 532 nm after boiling with TBA and correction for blanks.

Determination of hepatic mitochondrial redox balance

Hepatic mitochondria were isolated following the protocol described by Fromenty and Roden (2023), using a commercial mitochondria isolation kit (G006-1; Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute). Briefly, approximately 100 mg of fresh liver tissue was homogenized in ice-cold lysis buffer provided with the kit, followed by differential centrifugation to remove nuclei and cellular debris. The resulting supernatant was centrifuged at higher speed to pellet the mitochondrial fraction. Isolated mitochondria were resuspended in storage buffer and immediately used to assess mitochondrial-specific antioxidants and related enzymes, including manganese superoxide dismutase (MnSOD, #A001-2), glutathione peroxidase (GPx, #A005-1), reduced glutathione (GSH, #A006-1), glutathione reductase (GR, #A062-1), glutathione S-transferase (GST, #A004-1), and γ-glutamylcysteine ligase (γ-GCL, #A120-1). MnSOD activity was determined using the xanthine oxidase-WST-1 method, with one unit defined as the amount of enzyme required to inhibit 50 % of superoxide-driven formazan formation, measured at 450 nm. GPx activity, GSH content, and GR activity were assessed as described above for corresponding assays. GST activity was measured based on the conjugation of GSH to 1‑chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene (CDNB), with absorbance recorded at 340 nm. γ-GCL activity was evaluated by quantifying γ-glutamylcysteine formation, which was derivatized with ninhydrin and measured colorimetrically at 540 nm.

Determination of hepatic mitochondrial ROS

Mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS, #E004-1) levels in liver tissue were assessed using a commercial ROS assay kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute). In this procedure, isolated hepatic mitochondria were incubated for 30 min with 10 μM of the fluorescent probe DCFH-DA and 10 mmol/L Hoechst 33342 for nuclear staining. Post-incubation, the fluorescence emitted by oxidized DCFH was measured using a FLX 800 microplate reader (BioTek Instruments Inc., Winooski, VT), with excitation (485 nm) and emission (530 nm) wavelengths were set. The relative fluorescence intensity was used to estimate ROS production, and results were normalized against the untreated control group to facilitate comparative analysis.

Determination of hepatic PC and 8-OHdG

Protein concentration in liver tissue was measured using a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein assay kit (#A045-4; Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute) following the manufacturer’s instructions. The assay is based on the biuret reaction, in which proteins reduce Cu²⁺ to Cu⁺ under alkaline conditions. The resulting Cu⁺ ions subsequently form a stable purple complex with bicinchoninic acid, exhibiting maximal absorbance at 562 nm, which is directly proportional to the protein concentration. Oxidative damage to mitochondrial proteins was evaluated by quantifying protein carbonyl (PC, # A087-1) content, with results standardized to nmol per mg of total protein. In parallel, mitochondrial was used to measure levels of 8‑hydroxy-2-deoxyguanosine (8-OHdG, #H165-1) using a assay kit (Beyotime), following the manufacturer’s protocol meticulously. Each assay was conducted in triplicate to ensure reliability, and the 8-OHdG result was expressed as ng/mg-protein.

Determination of hepatic MMP

Mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP, #G009-1) in liver mitochondria was assessed using a commercial detection kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute). Briefly, freshly isolated hepatic mitochondria were adjusted to the recommended protein concentration and incubated with JC-1 working solution at 37°C for 20 min in the dark. Following incubation, samples were centrifuged at 600 × g for 5 min at 4°C to remove excess dye and resuspended in assay buffer. MMP was evaluated by calculating the ratio of red to green fluorescence, where red emission (excitation/emission: 525/590 nm) indicates polarized (high potential) mitochondria, and green emission (excitation/emission: 490/540 nm) reflects depolarized (low potential) mitochondria.

Determination of hepatic transcriptome

Ten hepatic samples from each experimental group were selected for performing a transcriptomic analysis. Total RNA was obtained from liver tissues following a standardized protocol, and its purity and integrity were confirmed using a Nanodrop spectrophotometer and an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer to ensure compatibility with downstream sequencing workflows. Total RNA with high integrity was reverse-transcribed into cDNA, which was subsequently employed for sequencing library construction. After paired-end sequencing, raw reads underwent quality filtering with Fastp, and the resulting high-quality clean reads were aligned to the reference genome using HISAT2. Transcript reconstruction and abundance estimation were carried out with StringTie. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were found based on the DESeq2 package, with P < 0.05 and an absolute log₂ fold change > 1.5.

To further elucidate the biological relevance of these DEGs, GO enrichment and KEGG pathway analyses were performed. GO analysis classified the DEGs into categories of BP, MF, and CC. Meanwhile, KEGG pathway enrichment revealed the key signaling pathways potentially involved in BS-mediated protection against hepatic redox imbalance and immune stress.

Determination of qRT-PCR

Total RNA was isolated from liver tissues using Trizol reagent (TaKaRa, Dalian, China) and was then reverse-transcribed into cDNA according to the Perfect Real Time cDNA synthesis kit (SYBR® PrimeScript™, TaKaRa, China) method. Quantitative real-time PCR was subsequently performed on an ABI 7300 Fast Real-Time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, USA), employing the SYBR® Premix Ex Taq™ II (Tli RNaseH Plus) for amplification (20 μL PCR reaction mixture = 10 μL SYBR® Premix Ex Taq (2 ×) + 0.4 μL forward primers specific to the target gene (Table S1) + 0.4 μL reverse primers specific to the target gene (Table S1) + 0.4 μL ROX reference dye (50 ×) + 6.8 μL nuclease-free water + 2 μL diluted cDNA template). Relative gene expression was normalized to the reference gene β-actin, and analysis was carried out using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

Determination of western blot

Proteins from ten hepatic samples per experimental group were extracted using RIPA lysis buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors (Beyotime). Nuclear extracts were prepared using a nuclear and cytoplasmic protein extraction kit (Beyotime) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, liver tissue homogenates were incubated in Hypotonic/Lysis Buffer A on ice, after which NP-40 was added to disrupt the plasma membrane. Samples were centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 5 min at 4°C to separate cytoplasmic supernatants. The nuclear pellets were subsequently resuspended in high-salt Extraction Buffer B containing protease inhibitors, vortexed intermittently on ice for 15–30 min, and centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 10 min at 4°C to obtain nuclear protein fractions. Protein concentrations were quantified to ensure equal loading, after which cytoplasmic and nuclear extracts were resolved by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto PVDF membranes via electroblotting. Membranes were blocked with 5 % BSA in TBS-T (0.05 % Tween-20) for 1 hour to minimize non-specific binding. Subsequently, membranes were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against Nrf2 (#12721S) and NF-κB (#8242S) (1:1,000; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA). Following washes, HRP-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (#7074S, 1:2,000) was applied for 1 hour at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence reagents (Beyotime) and captured with a Fujifilm LAS-4000 imaging system. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software (v1.42q; NIH, USA).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SAS software (version 9.1; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA). Differences among treatment groups were assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Tukey’s multiple comparison test to determine specific intergroup differences. Results are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and statistical significance was set at P < 0.05. Each assay was performed in triplicate, with a total of 30 broiler chickens included in the experimental design.

Results

Growth performance

All birds remained healthy throughout the study, and no mortality was observed (Table 2). Prior to LPS exposure (days 1-15), no significant differences were detected among the three groups in terms of BWG, FI, or FCR (Table 2). Following LPS challenge, broilers in the LPS group exhibited decreased BWG and increased FCR compared with the CON group. Similarly, the BSLPS group showed reduced BWG and elevated FCR relative to controls. Although the BSLPS group did not fully restore growth performance to CON levels, both BWG and FCR were significantly improved (P < 0.05) compared with the LPS group.

Determination of serum and hepatic immune status

Fig. 2 presents serum immune marker alterations following BS treatment in LPS-challenged broilers. LPS exposure elevated serum IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α, and IFN-γ levels (P < 0.05; Fig. 2 A1-E1) compared with controls. Notably, these cytokines were also elevated in the BSLPS group relative to the CON group. Hepatic immune markers showed similar patterns, with increased IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, TNF-α, and IFN-γ in the LPS group versus controls (P < 0.05; Fig. 2 A2, C2-E2), while the BSLPS group displayed higher IL-1β, IL-10, TNF-α, and IFN-γ compared with the CON group (P < 0.05). Correlation analysis revealed a strong positive relationship between immune markers in serum and liver tissues (Fig. 2 A3-E3), with IL-1β exhibiting the highest correlation (r = 0.8702, P < 0.001). Although cytokine levels in the BSLPS group remained above control values, they were significantly reduced (P < 0.05) compared with the LPS group.

Determination of hepatic RNA-seq

Comparative RNA sequencing of liver tissues from CON, LPS, and BSLPS groups identified 10,332 expressed genes. Differential expression analysis (P < 0.05, |log₂FoldChange| > 1.5) revealed 660 DEGs (324 upregulated, 336 downregulated) in LPS vs. CON; 488 DEGs (239 upregulated, 249 downregulated) in BSLPS vs. CON; and 146 DEGs (89 upregulated, 57 downregulated) in BSLPS vs. LPS (Fig. 5A). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment showed significant clustering of DEGs across biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF) categories (Fig. 5B–D). KEGG pathway analysis identified 121 significantly enriched pathways in LPS vs. CON and 88 in BSLPS vs. CON comparisons (Fig. 5E). LPS exposure primarily affected Toll-like receptor signaling, NF-κB signaling, cell adhesion molecules, and lipid metabolism, whereas BS supplementation predominantly modulated Toll-like receptor signaling, cell adhesion, osteoclast differentiation, and NF-κB pathways.

Discussion

Bacillus subtilis is widely applied in broiler production owing to its diverse benefits, including inhibition of pathogenic bacteria and enhancement of nutrient absorption (Chen et al., 2023; Shettar et al., 2023). By degrading non-starch polysaccharides and complex proteins while stimulating immune function, BS contributes to disease prevention, improved growth efficiency, and enhanced resilience in broilers, ultimately promoting flock health and economic returns (Oladokun and Adewole, 2023; Wickramasuriya et al., 2023). Its integration into feed formulations has been associated with improved feed conversion ratios, reduced disease susceptibility, and overall better growth performance, supporting its widespread adoption as a consistent and efficacious additive in poultry farming (Bai et al., 2017, 2018; Qian et al., 2015; Zhang et al., 2017). Moreover, BS contributes to environmental hygiene by decreasing the emission of noxious gases such as ammonia from feces, improving air quality in broiler houses, and supporting broiler health while reducing the environmental footprint of livestock farming (Rodrigues et al., 2024). Importantly, BS supplementation can help reduce the need for antibiotics, thereby minimizing antibiotic residues and aligning with sustainable and safe modern agricultural practices (Wang et al., 2023). Previous research reported that dietary BS effectively improves tissue histomorphology and enhances antioxidant capacity in broilers, with 0.3 g/kg identified as the most effective supplementation level (Bai et al., 2017, 2018). Building upon this foundation, this study was eager to assess the hepatoprotective of BS under conditions of mid-aged immune stress induced by LPS administration. By incorporating BS into the basal diet, we sought to clarify its capacity to counteract LPS-triggered hepatic dysfunction, a gap that has remained insufficiently explored. The results indicated that LPS exposure result in a lower BWG and an enhancement in FCR, reflecting impaired growth performance. Remarkably, dietary BS supplementation alleviated these adverse effects, demonstrating a restorative influence on performance metrics. This observation aligns with previous findings that BS can mitigate the passive impacts of immune stress on growth in broilers (Chen and Yu, 2021). Consistently, Li et al. (2015) found that BS partially reversed LPS-induced growth suppression in young chickens. However, discrepancies among studies may stem from variations in Bacillus strains used, dosages administered, bird age, diet composition, or management practices, underscoring the importance of standardized protocols for probiotic application in poultry industry.

The liver's structural integrity, comprising hepatocytes, bile ducts, and blood vessels, is essential for maintaining hepatic function, enabling signal transduction, metabolite transport, and secretion (May and Bird, 2023). Structural damage or abnormalities in the liver can lead to metabolic disturbances, impaired detoxification, and reduced protein synthesis (Berasain et al., 2023). Mitochondria, the central energy-generating organelles in hepatocytes, are especially susceptible to damage. Mitochondrial dysfunction can initiate inflammatory responses, impair energy metabolism, and disturb redox balance, ultimately compromising broiler growth and immune function (Harrington et al., 2023). During mid-aged development, broiler chickens are particularly vulnerable to dietary and environmental influences, and hepatic redox imbalance can initiate inflammation. Redox processes are crucial for cellular homeostasis, signaling, and protein function. Disruption of redox balance can result in immune stress and inflammation (Cheng et al., 2023; Pant et al., 2024). In this study, the LPS group showed significant hepatic structural damage, including enlarged intercellular spaces, decreased mitochondrial numbers, and mitochondrial damage, as compared to the normal group. Although the BSLPS group significantly restored normal liver morphology in comparison to the LPS group, it could not restore to basal state (depicted by the control group), implying that BS exerts a certain protective role against immune-mediated hepatic injury and oxidative imbalance. These observations align with previous reports indicating that BS can reduce inflammatory responses by modulating immune function (Wang et al., 2018) and support hepatic protection by promoting anti-immune stress and anti-redox imbalanced effects (Cha et al., 2012; Neag et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2018). Despite these promising findings, the precise molecular mechanisms responsible for the observed protective benefits remain to be fully clarified, warranting further investigation.

Broiler chickens are highly susceptible to various stressors, including environmental changes, feed quality, and infections, during mid-aged development, often leading to immune stress, systemic inflammation, and increased disease risk. Persistent inflammation can consume energy and nutrients, disrupt growth, impair immune function, increase susceptibility to infections, and reduce overall productivity (Zhang et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023). As key mediators of immune communication, cytokines provide valuable insight into the intensity and nature of inflammatory responses. IL-1β triggers inflammation by activating immune cells; IL-6 supports immune regulation, inflammation, and cell proliferation; TNF-α mediates inflammatory responses and apoptosis; and IFN-γ enhances immune defenses against pathogens. Opposing the action of pro-inflammatory cytokines, IL-10 is important in dampening inflammation and maintaining immune equilibrium (Asby et al., 2021). Following LPS exposure, broilers demonstrated a substantial upregulation of IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, and IFN-γ, highlighting a strong immune-modulatory reaction compared to controls. Notably, BS supplementation reduced cytokine levels, accompanied by improvements in performance and hepatic morphology compared with the LPS group; however, these effects did not fully restore values to the basal state observed in the control group, indicating that BS alleviates immune stress and partially supports immune regulation (Garvey et al., 2022; Goetz et al., 2023). Clarification of the precise biological pathways involved will require more targeted mechanistic studies.

Redox imbalance in mid-aged broiler development is primarily driven by excessive ROS production overwhelming the immature antioxidant defense system, leading to cellular and tissue damage (Mishra et al., 2023). SOD catalyzes the conversion of superoxide radicals into hydrogen peroxide, which is subsequently detoxified by GSH-Px. GSH and GR work together to regenerate antioxidant molecules, while POD assists in scavenging reactive intermediates. Together, these components form a dynamic redox network that maintains cellular homeostasis (Lapenna, 2023). In this study, LPS administration markedly impaired hepatic antioxidant enzyme activities and increased MDA concentrations, indicating a disturbed redox equilibrium in liver tissues. Conversely, BS supplementation markedly enhanced the activities of antioxidant enzymes and reduced MDA levels relative to the LPS group; however, these effects did not completely restore values to the basal state observed in the control group, suggesting that BS strengthens hepatic antioxidant defenses by partially attenuating ROS accumulation and reestablishing redox balance. These results corroborate previous findings that demonstrated BS’s efficacy in enhancing antioxidant capacity and limiting oxidative damage (Bai et al., 2017, 2018). Furthermore, additional research has revealed that BS can revise the antioxidant-related genes level, thereby improving hepatic redox balance (Wang et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2017), reinforcing its potential as a probiotic intervention for oxidative stress management.

Mitochondria are key organelles in redox imbalance regulation, and their antioxidant system, including MnSOD, GPx, GSH, GST, and γ-GCL, protects against ROS-induced mitochondrial damage (Bai et al., 2023; Jing et al., 2023). MnSOD eliminates mitochondrial superoxide radicals, while GPx detoxifies hydrogen peroxide. GSH works in tandem with GPx, and GST supports detoxification and immune modulation. γ-GCL is essential for GSH synthesis. ROS overproduction can impair MMP, leading to dysfunction. Additionally, PC and 8-OHdG are widely recognized biomarkers indicating oxidative damage to proteins and DNA. In this study, LPS administration disrupted mitochondrial redox homeostasis, as indicated by elevated oxidative damage and impaired MMP. Supplementation with BS significantly enhanced mitochondrial antioxidant capacity, attenuated lipid and protein oxidative damage, and preserved MMP stability compared with the LPS group; nevertheless, these improvements did not completely restore parameters to the basal levels observed in the control group. These protective effects are likely attributable to the ability of BS to reinforce hepatic architecture and antioxidant defense mechanisms. While the precise mechanisms require further clarification, these findings suggest BS is a promising candidate for alleviating redox imbalance through targeted probiotic interventions.

RNA-seq analysis indicated that BS potentially safeguards the liver by regulating multiple critical signaling pathways, notably Toll-like receptor pathway, cell adhesion molecules, NF-κB signaling, and osteoclast differentiation, highlighting its multifaceted role in mitigating liver injury. To validate these observations, we selected representative DEGs involved in each of these pathways for subsequent verification through qPCR and Western blot analyses. As a central transcriptional regulator, Nrf2 initiates the activation of downstream antioxidant genes, ultimately enhancing the cellular defense system against oxidative stress (Alam et al., 2023). Similarly, mitochondrial-specific antioxidant genes, including SOD2, γ-GCL, Trx2, Trx-R2, Prx3, and Cyt C, were important in maintaining mitochondrial function. Structural adhesion genes (OCLN, CLDN2, CLDN3, ZO1) maintain cellular morphology and barrier integrity (Hu et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023). Genes involved in immune regulation, including TLR4, TRAF6, NOD1, MHC1, and SOCS1, orchestrate inflammatory processes primarily through activation of NF-κB. This activation drives the cytokines generation and amplifies immune responses, playing a central role in controlling inflammation (Miao et al., 2023). Nrf2 and NF-κB play pivotal roles in maintaining redox balance and regulating immune responses. Notably, both were identified as significantly DEGs in the RNA-seq analysis. Nrf2 promotes antioxidant defense, while NF-κB orchestrates inflammatory responses. Crosstalk between these pathways enables coordinated responses to immune stress and redox balance (Fan et al., 2023; Teng et al., 2023). In the present study, LPS challenge disrupted hepatic gene expression related to redox balance, cell adhesion, and immune status, leading to impaired performance and hepatic injury. Supplementation with BS mitigated these perturbations, consistent with previous evidence of its probiotic, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant properties; however, these effects did not fully restore parameters to the basal levels observed in the control group (Bai et al., 2017, 2018).

In conclusion, this research highlights the protective role of dietary BS in enhancing the expression of redox balance and immune status genes, boosting antioxidant enzyme activity, and modulating cytokine levels. These changes contributed to improved hepatic structure and function under LPS-induced stress. BS thus emerges as a valuable probiotic feed additive capable of mitigating immune stress and redox imbalance in broilers, with potential applications in enhancing poultry health and production efficiency.

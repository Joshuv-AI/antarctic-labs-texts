An education-based antimicrobial stewardship strategy to reduce prescribing inappropriateness
Authors: R Mimmo, D A Zeme, P Desantis, P Ragusa, HSM Elhadidy, C Gastaldo, F Golzio, A Paudice, E Anselmo, F Bert
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIssueAntimicrobial resistance is a crucial issue in public health, mainly induced from an inappropriate prescription of antibiotic drugs. Antimicrobial stewardship strategies can be used to tackle the problem; a iso-resources, audit- and feedback-based, antimicrobial stewardship intervention with educational feedback was performed in the Local Health Unit TO3.Description of the problemFrom December 2022 to November 2024, stewardship interventions are being implemented in the 3 facilities of the local health unit. Prescribing inappropriateness (main outcome), department's specialty, interhospital variability, number of antibiotics prescription and cultural examination were assessed over time. A mixed method evaluation was conducted. Logistical analysis was performed with R. Stratified analysis between empirical and cultural based targeted therapy was assessed. A qualitative analysis of the causes was performed via interview.ResultsA total of 1020 clinical records were analyzed. Inappropriateness varied from 27% at the beginning of the intervention to 8% at the end. Associated factors with inappropriateness were the length of intervention (OR: 0.86; IC: 0.78-0.95), department's specialty (OR: 1.66; IC: 1.18-2.32) and number of prescriptions (OR: 2.07; IC: 1.58-2.70). Empirical therapy (n = 598) showed higher range of variation pre-post intervention and similar outcomes at multivariable analysis. Targeted therapy (n = 291) showed no impact of the intervention over time. Specific types of antibiotics seemed at major risk of prescribing inappropriateness (carbapenems and quinolones). Findings from the qualitative analysis suggest that a peer-based motivational intervention could be useful and that the internal setup of the departments influences the prescribing appropriateness.LessonsAn iso-resources education-based stewardship intervention is useful for ameliorating prescribing appropriateness. Specific kind of intervention need to be addressed in future studies.Key messages• A back-end antimicrobial stewardship strategy based on educational and peer approach could be effective even if iso-resources.• Understanding the specific determinants of prescription habits is crucial for implementing tailored antimicrobial stewardship interventions.

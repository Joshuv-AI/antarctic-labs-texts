Tuning electronic and magnetic properties of Fe-doped MgCl2 monolayer by charge doping: a first-principles study
Authors: Hoang Van Ngoc, Chu Viet Ha, R. Ponce-Pérez, Huynh Thi Phuong Thuy, Pham Mai An, J. Guerrero-Sanchez, D. M. Hoat
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this work, Fe doping is proposed for magnetism engineering of MgCl2 monolayer, where electronic and magnetic properties are further modified by hole and electron doping. At equilibrium, the MgCl2 monolayer is a two-dimensional (2D) insulator with a large band gap of 5.99 eV that is formed by the separation in energy of Mg-3s and Cl-3p orbitals. With Fe doping in this 2D material, significant magnetism is mainly produced by 3d electrons of the Fe impurity with an overall magnetic moment of 4.00 µB, where neighboring atoms also make small contribution to the system magnetism. The Fe-3d orbital also generates mid-gap subbands that lead to the emergence of the half-metallicity. Increasing the Fe doping level, the magnetic semiconductor nature is obtained in 2Fe@MgCl2 (with two Fe atoms) and 3Fe@MgCl2 (with three Fe atoms) systems. However, the antiparallel spin alignment with zero total magnetic moment is stable in the former case, meanwhile the parallel spin coupling with large total magnetic moment of 12.00 µB is stable in the latter case. Moreover, robust perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA) is also confirmed with magnetic anisotropy energy up to 899.05 µeV. Hole doping induces the antiparallel-to-parallel spin alignment transition in the 2Fe@MgCl2 system regardless of hole amount, while the transition from parallel to antiparallel configuration for the 3Fe@MgCl2 system only takes place at a high level of holes. Meanwhile, electron doping remains the spin coupling in both systems. Hole doping also affects the magnetic anisotropy by inducing the PMA-to-IMA switching (IMA: in-plane magnetic anisotropy). In addition, electron doping can switch the electronic nature from magnetic semiconductor to half-metallic. Our findings demonstrate effective magnetism engineering of MgCl2 monolayer induced by Fe doping, where electronic and magnetic properties of the doped systems can be further controlled by charge doping towards selective spintronic applications.

Introduction

Since the discovery of graphene,1 two-dimensional (2D) materials have attracted tremendous research attention because of their rich physical properties and great promise for a wide range of technological applications. As the most investigated 2D material, graphene has exceptional high carrier mobility raised from the linear energy dispersion and strong coupling with light over a wide regime from microwave to ultraviolet.2,3 Therefore, graphene has been widely applied in diminutive electronic and optoelectronic devices.4,5 However, the feature of semimetal with zero band gap considerably limits graphene performance in these fields. Therefore, graphene band gap opening has been investigated by edge engineering6,7 or chemical modification.8,9 In the former method, energy gaps up to 1 eV can be achieved in graphene nanoribbons depending on its size,10 while much larger gaps can be obtained using surface functionalization methods. For example, graphene becomes an insulator with a band gap up to ∼10 eV upon simultaneous hydrogenation and fluorination, to form the fluorographene with chemical stoichiometry C2FH.11 In addition, to enhance the performance of next-generation electronic and optoelectronic devices, searching for new 2D alternatives with intrinsic band gap plays a key role and has been widely investigated. Huge research interest and effort has lead to the discovery of more and more 2D materials with a wide range of energy gaps, such as transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDCs),12,13 phosphorene,14 III-group monochalcogenides,15,16 IV-group monochalcogenides and dichalcogenides,17,18 and insulator hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) also known as “white graphene”, among others. Between them, the BN monolayer has been synthesized using both top-down exfoliation19,20 and bottom-up growth from precursors.21,22 Theoretical investigations have demonstrated its large band gaps around 6.0 eV.23,24 Experimentally, Elias et al.25 have revealed a direct gap of 6.1 eV for the epitaxial BN monolayer. Investigations have explored promising applications of insulator 2D BN.26,27 In 2019, Illarionov et al.28 realized the epitaxial growth of insulator CaF2, that was further employed to fabricate field-effect transistors with low leakage currents and competitive performance. 2D insulator honeycomb BeO with a band gap of 6 eV has been also been synthesized by Zhang et al.,29 despite its bulk it crystallizes into a wurtzite structure. So far, to the best of our knowledge, the list of 2D insulators is still limited, thus much more research should be done related to this 2D family.

Recently, various experimental research groups have had success in preparing 2D metal halides.30,31 Theoretical investigations have demonstrated their rich electronic properties, including semiconducting and insulating properties.32 Among them, insulator earth alkaline halides with large band gaps have been investigated.33–35 In this regard, insulator MgCl2 monolayer with strong light absorption in the ultraviolet regime has been predicted by Mahida et al.36 The modulation of the MgCl2 monolayer fundamental properties by applied strain and layer control has been studied by Men et al.,37 which could be essential for optoelectronic and catalytic applications. Shehzad et al.38 have demonstrated the insulator-to-semiconductor transformation of the MgCl2 bilayer by Be and Mg atom intercalation. Due to its confirmed large band gap, the MgCl2 monolayer could be potential platform for spintronic applications, enabling good thermal stability of magnetism, efficient spin transport, and small current leakage. However, the investigation of this 2D material for spintronic applications is still lacking.

On the other hand, designing 2D magnetic materials is useful for spintronic applications, which could be achieved by magnetizing nonmagnetic materials through some efficient strategies such as defect engineering, adatom adsorption, and substitutional doping. In this work, we propose Fe substitutional doping to induce magnetism in the MgCl2 monolayer. In a previous study, Lima et al.39 considered the substitutional doping at Cl sites after confirming that the creation of a Cl vacancy is more favorable than a Mg vacancy. However, considering the metallic nature of Mg and Fe atoms and the small difference in their atomic sizes, the doping site should be at a Mg site rather than at Cl site in this case. Therefore, we consider Fe doping at Mg sites. The effects of Fe doping on MgCl2 monolayer electronic and magnetic properties are investigated by analyzing different characteristics, including band structure, projected density of states, magnetic moment, magnetic coupling, and magnetic anisotropy. In addition, charge doping is also proposed to control the magnetism of Fe-doped MgCl2 monolayer systems. Our work provides insight into the effects of Fe doping, which is useful for magnetic material design.

Computational details

Based on density functional theory (DFT),40 our first-principles calculations are carried out using the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP).41,42 Herein, the projector augmented wave (PAW) approach is applied to describe the ion–electron interactions, while the electron exchange–correlation energies are computed by means of the generalized gradient approximation in the Perdew–Burke–Ernzerhof scheme (GGA-PBE).43 Taking into account the high correlation of Fe-3d electrons, the GGA+U approach is also applied with an effective Hubbard parameter of 4.0 eV.44 In this work, this value has also been tested to provide reasonable results with the confirmed perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (see Fig. S1 of the SI file) and electronic nature (see Fig. S2–S4 of the SI file). The plane-wave cutoff energy is set to 500 eV. The convergence criterion of energy is set to 1 × 10−6 eV, while the maximum residual force acting on each atom is set to be less then 1 × 10−2 eV Å−1. To sample the Brillouin zone, the Monkhorst–Pack scheme45 is adopted to generate k-point grids of a 20 × 20 × 1 for MgCl2 monolayer unit cell. To properly model the monolayer systems, all the employed structures contain a vacuum gap of more than 15 Å to eliminate the interactions between perpendicular periodic monolayer images.

Phonon calculations are performed using the density functional perturbation theory (DFPT) as embedded in the PHONOPY code.46 For the MgCl2 monolayer, a 4 × 4 × 1 supercell is constructed to compute the force constants. Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) simulations at 300 K are carried out for the MgCl2 monolayer to verify its thermal stability, using NVT ensemble and Nose–Hoover thermostat.47,48 A time step of 2 fs and 2500 simulations steps are set, such that AIMD simulations run for 5000 fs (5 ps).

A 4 × 4 × 1 supercell of the MgCl2 monolayer with 48 atoms (16 Mg atoms and 32 Cl atoms) is generated to model the Fe doping, whose Brillouin zone is sampled with a 4 × 4 × 1 Monkhorst–Pack k-point grid. Results and discussion

Stability and electronic properties of pristine MgCl2 monolayer

Fig. 1a shows a unit cell of MgCl2 monolayer, which consists of three atomic layers in a hexagonal lattice of 1T phase. Herein, a hexagonal sublayer of Mg atoms is sandwiched between two staggered Cl sublayers to generate Cl–Mg–Cl atomic stacking. Therefore, each Mg atom is positioned at the center of an octaheron formed by six Cl atoms, such that the structure is ascribed to P3̄m1 symmetry. At equilibrium, the atomic structure of the MgCl2 monolayer is described using the following parameters: (1) lattice constant a = 3.67 Å; (2) bonding length dMg–Cl = 2.52 Å; (3) interatomic angles ∠MgClMg = 93.28° and ∠ClMgCl = 86.72°; and (4) monolayer thickness Δt = ΔCl–Cl = 2 × ΔMg–Cl = 2 × 1.37 = 2.74 Å. The obtained lattice and chemical bond length are in good agreement with previous studies,38 suggesting their reliability.

(a) A unit cell at equilibrium; and a 4 × 4 × 1 supercell, of the MgCl2 monolayer (b) at equilibrium and (c) after 5 ps of AIMD simulation.

Before studying the electronic properties of the MgCl2 monolayer, its structural stability is examined:

• Phonon results of the MgCl2 monolayer are displayed in Fig. 2a. Note that the phonon spectrum consists of three low-frequency acoustic branches converging to zero frequency at the Γ point, and six high-frequency optical branches. In total, nine branches correspond to the vibration of three atoms in the primitive cell. Importantly, no soft modes (represented by imaginary phonon frequencies) appear in the obtained spectrum, indicating that the MgCl2 monolayer is dynamically stable.

(a) Phonon dispersion spectrum; and (b) fluctuation of temperature and energy during AIMD simulations, of the MgCl2 monolayer.

• Once the AIMD simulations are finalized, the hexagonal configuration is found nearly intact as visualized in Fig. 1c (compared to that before AIMD simulations displayed in Fig. 1b) without breaking any Mg–Cl bond. Moreover, as displayed in Fig. 2b, temperature and energy exhibit stable fluctuations without any abrupt, rapid, or steep variation at any moment. These results confirm that the MgCl2 monolayer is thermally stable, that is, its structure can withstand high temperatures.

• Elastic constants C11 and C12 of the MgCl2 monolayer hexagonal structure are calculated by means of second derivatives of energy with respect to strain, where the dependence of energy on strain is determined by computing energy at a series of small strains applied to the monolayer lattice. Our calculations provide values of 39.027 and 9.976 N m−1 for C11 and C12 constants, respectively. Note that these results meet Born criteria:50C11 > 0 and C11 > |C12|, demonstrating that the MgCl2 monolayer is mechanically stable.

Once the MgCl2 monolayer is confirmed to hold good structural stability, its electronic properties are studied. Fig. 3a shows the orbital-resolved band structure that indicates the direct-gap insulator nature of this 2D material, considering that both valence band maximum and conduction band minimum are located at the Γ point. A large band gap of 5.99 eV is obtained, which is in good agreement with previous studies.36 This value is regulated mainly by Cl-px,y states at the upper part of the valence band and the Mg-s state at the lower part of the conduction band. In the considered energy range, the important contribution of the Cl-pz state to the valence band formation can also be observed. The separation contribution of Mg-s and Cl-p orbitals suggests that the Mg–Cl chemical bond is predominantly ionic which is further confirmed by calculations of the electron localization function (ELF) and Bader charge. Fig. 3b shows the ELF visualization of the MgCl2 monolayer, which asserts that valence electrons are highly centered at Cl atoms. Bader charge analysis indicates that each Mg atom transfers a charge quantity of 1.66e to Cl atoms, that is, each Cl atom attracts an amount of 0.83e. From these results, it can be concluded that the MgCl2 monolayer is an ionic 2D insulator.

(a) Orbital-resolved band structure of each constituent atom (the Fermi level is set to 0 eV); and (b) 3D (iso-surface value: 0.8 e Å−3) and 2D electron localization function, of the MgCl2 monolayer.

Effects of single Fe impurity

In this part, we investigate the effects of a single Fe impurity on the MgCl2 monolayer electronic and magnetic properties. The doped system is denoted by 1Fe@MgCl2, whose formation energy is calculated to be 3.22 eV per atom. This result means an energy of 3.22 eV is required to realize the Fe doping in the MgCl2 monolayer under equilibrium conditions. Since a positive Ef value suggests the endothermic process, the doping could be favored and more feasible under Fe-rich experimental conditions or Mg-poor conditions. Moreover, a negative cohesive energy of −3.50 eV per atom is obtained for this 2D material, suggesting its good structural-chemical stability. Charge density difference Δρ is calculated to study the interactions between the Fe impurity and MgCl2 host monolayer, which is defined by: Δρ = ρ(1Fe@MgCl2) − ρ(MgCl2) − ρ(Fe), where ρ(1Fe@MgCl2), ρ(MgCl2), and ρ(Fe) denote the charge density in the doped MgCl2 monolayer, bare MgCl2 monolayer, and single Fe atom, respectively, obtained from self-consistent calculations. From the results visualized in Fig. 4a, it can be seen that the rich-charge regions are around the Cl atoms closest to the doping site as confirmed by the yellow iso-surface, meanwhile aqua iso-surface around the doping site indicates charge depletion from the Fe impurity. Since the Cl atom is a nonmetal atom with more electronegative nature than the Fe atom, the charge transfer from the impurity to the host monolayer may take place. Quantitatively, Bader charge analysis asserts that the MgCl2 host monolayer attracts a charge quantity of 1.21e from the Fe impurity.

(a) Charge density difference (iso-surface value: 0.005 e Å−3, yellow iso-surfaces: charge enrichment; aqua iso-surfaces: charge depletion); and (b) spin density (iso-surface value: 0.01 e Å−3), in a 1Fe-doped MgCl2 monolayer.

Our spin-polarized calculations demonstrate the significant magnetization of the MgCl2 monolayer as an interesting result of Fe doping. Specifically, the total magnetic moment of 1Fe@MgCl2 is calculated to be 4.00 µB, where the local value of Fe impurity takes the value of 3.67 µB that exhibits the main contribution to the system magnetic moment. Spin density Δρs is further calculated to visualize the origin of magnetism, which is defined by: Δρs = ρ↑ − ρ↓, where ρ↑ and ρ↓ are spin-up and spin-down charge density calculated self-consistently, respectively. From Fig. 4b, the single spin iso-surface is only observed at the doping site, confirming again the determinant role of the Fe impurity in producing the 1Fe@MgCl2 system magnetism. Our calculations also confirm the perpendicular magnetic anisotropy (PMA) of the 1Fe@MgCl2 system since its MAE has a positive value of 2571.03 µeV. Since the SOC energy contribution (Esoc) comes mostly from the Fe atom, it can be concluded that the Fe-3d orbital is mainly responsible for the system magnetic anisotropy. The induced PMA may suggest potential for the 1Fe@MgCl2 system for Magnetoresistive Random-Access Memory (MRAM) fabrication, provided a high storage density and thermal stability.

The magnetism appears as a result of strong spin polarization in the MgCl2 monolayer produced by Fe doping, as observed in the spin-resolved band structure and projected density of states (PDOS of Fe impurity and its first Cl and second Mg neighboring atomic species) in Fig. 5. From the figure, it can be seen that Fe-dxy–dyz–dxz–dx2−y2 states originate from flat subbands below the Fermi level of the spin-up channel. The mentioned electronic states also form two new mid-gap subbands in the spin-down channel, one of them overlaps with the Fermi level and the other one is around 2.77 eV. In the spin-down configuration, the important presence of Fe-dz2 that builds mid-gap subband around 2.06 eV can also be noted. These results demonstrate the half-metallicity of the 1Fe@MgCl2 system with a spin-up energy gap of 4.87 eV, which is an interesting electronic characteristic with perfect spin polarization at the Fermi level – highly desirable for the spin current generation. In other words, Fe doping is a highly effective method for spintronic functionalization of an insulator MgCl2 monolayer.

(a) Spin-resolved band structure; and (b) projected density of states (Fe impurity, its first neighboring Cl atom, and second neighboring Mg atom; positive and negative DOS values represent spin-up and spin-down states, respectively), of the 1Fe-doped MgCl2 monolayer (the Fermi level is set to 0 eV).

Spin coupling in Fe-doped MgCl2 monolayer

To study the spin coupling in the Fe-doped MgCl2 monolayer, two (2Fe) and three (3Fe) dopant atoms are incorporated substitutionally into a 4 × 4 × 1 supercell. Herein, we denote the doped systems as 2Fe@MgCl2 and 3Fe@MgCl2. In both cases, we consider parallel and antiparallel spin alignments as visualized in Fig. 6. From the figure, it can be seen that the two Fe impurities in the 2Fe@MgCl2 system prefer to have antiparallel spin coupling with an energy difference of 5.91 meV smaller than the parallel alignment. In contrast, the parallel alignment is more stable than the antiparallel configuration (formed by two Fe atoms with positive spin values and one Fe atom with negative spin value) in the 3Fe@MgCl2 system, where the energy difference is calculated to be 40.21 meV. Since our main aim is to determine the stable magnetic phase for the doped systems, the exchange interactions, which can be studied by calculating the exchange coupling constants, are beyond the scope of this work. In the stable magnetic phase, 2Fe@MgCl2 and 3Fe@MgCl2 systems have overall magnetic moments of 0.00 and 12.00 µB, respectively. In addition, MAE values of 899.05 and 753.06 µeV are obtained, respectively, indicating the PMA in both 2Fe- and 3Fe-doped systems to endow them promise for MRAMs fabrication. These results also suggest more robust PMA in the former system because of its more positive MAE value.

Spin density (iso-surface value: 0.01 e Å−3, blue iso-surfaces: positive spin value; pink iso-surfaces: negative spin value) and difference in energy of spin configurations, for the (a) 2Fe- and (b) 3Fe-doped MgCl2 monolayers.

Fig. 7 shows the spin-resolved band structure of the 2Fe@MgCl2 and 3Fe@MgCl2 systems. Note that 2Fe and 3Fe doping configurations make the appearance of multiple mid-gap subbands in the MgCl2 monolayer band structure. In the former case, the antiparallel spin coupling between two Fe atoms with zero total magnetic moment is also reflected in the spin-symmetric band structure profile, indicating the balanced charge distribution in spin channels of the 2Fe@MgCl2 system. Meanwhile, strong spin polarization is observed in the 3Fe@MgCl2 system band structure. Importantly, spin states of both doped systems exhibit semiconductor character, indicating the magnetic semiconductor nature in the MgCl2 monolayer induced by the considered Fe doping configurations. Specifically, spin-up/spin-down energy gaps of 3.39/3.39 and 4.66/3.25 eV are obtained for the 2Fe@MgCl2 and 3Fe@MgCl2 systems, respectively.

Effects of charge doping

Now, hole and electron doping is applied to tune the magnetic and electronic properties of 2Fe@MgCl2 and 3Fe@MgCl2 systems. For such a goal, 0.5 and 1.0 holes and electrons are added to the 4 × 4 × 1 supercell systems. In experiments, these values correspond to the p-/n-doping levels of 2.679 × 1013 and 5.358 × 1013 (cm−2), respectively, which can be realized through some widely employed methods as electrostatic gating, chemical doping, and interfacial interactions by proximity to substrates. Note that hole doping induces the antiparallel-to-parallel spin alignment transition in 2Fe@MgCl2, while the antiparallel configuration remains with electron doping. The parallel spin coupling in the 3Fe@MgCl2 system is preserved with electron doping and small hole level (0.5 holes per supercell). Upon increasing the hole level to 1.0 hole per supercell, spin alignment is switched from parallel to antiparallel. From Table 1, it can be seen that the TC values are between 25.19 to 230.69 K. Note that these values are lower than the Curie temperature of Fe-doped TMD monolayers.51,52 However, the considered Fe-doped MgCl2 monolayer systems have higher Curie temperature than CrI3 (45 K),53 and similar to that of Fe3GaTe2 (∼240 K) and Fe3GeTe2 (∼130 K).54 Therefore, Fe doping can make MgCl2 monolayer spintronic candidates work above the temperature of liquid nitrogen.

From Table 1 and Fig. 8b, it can be noted that hole doping induces the PMA-to-IMA for 2Fe@MgCl2, where the negative MAE value up to −653.83 µeV is obtained with 1.0 hole per supercell. Meanwhile, electron doping preserves its PMA which becomes weaker when increasing the doping level, as confirmed by the reduction of the MAE value. Therefore, hole doping can be applied to make the 2Fe@MgCl2 monolayer system a prospective 2D candidate for magnetic field sensing. Regardless of the charge carrier type and doping level, PMA remains in the 3Fe@MgCl2 system. However, except for 1.0 electron per supercell that increases the MAE value to 834.56 µeV to make stronger PMA, the MAE value decreases once as realized by the charge doping, suggesting that the PMA becomes weaker with 0.5 and 1.0 hole per supercell, and also 0.5 electrons per supercell. These results indicate the strong dependence of the magnetic anisotropy of 2Fe@MgCl2 and 3Fe@MgCl2 systems on the charge doping, that could be employed for designing the MgCl2 monolayer for selective spintronic applications.

On the other hand, charge doping also modifies the electronic band structure of 2Fe@MgCl2 and 3Fe@MgCl2 systems. In most cases, the magnetic semiconductor nature is maintained, where the spin-dependent gaps are tuned, as listed in Table 1. However, the electronic nature is also changed in specific doping cases:

• the 2Fe@MgCl2 system becomes magnetic metallic with doping 1.0 electron per supercell considering that both its spin states are metallized.

• the half-metallicity is obtained by doping the 3Fe@MgCl2 system with 0.5 holes per supercell, and 0.5 and 1.0 electron per supercell, considering the metallization of only one spin state, while semiconductor character is preserved for the other one.

Conclusions

In summary, first-principles calculations have been performed to investigate the change in electronic and magnetic properties of the MgCl2 monolayer under effects of Fe doping. The pristine monolayer possesses good dynamical, thermal, and mechanical stability. This 2D material is classified as a 2D ionic insulator, whose Mg–Cl bonding is generated by the charge transfer from the Mg to Cl atom. Significant monolayer magnetization is produced by Fe doping, where the magnetism and half-metallicity are determined mainly by Fe impurity that originates the mid-gap subbands with strong spin polarization. Herein, the charge transfer of 1.21e from the Fe atom to the host monolayer is confirmed, which is a result of its more electronegative environment formed by six Cl atoms. By increasing Fe composition, the magnetic semiconductor nature with large spin gaps is obtained along with the confirmed PMA suitable for MRAMs fabrication. Further, charge doping is confirmed as an effective approach to modify selectively the magnetism of the doped systems, including spin alignment and magnetic anisotropy. Specifically, the coupling transition from antiparallel-to-parallel and parallel-to-antiparallel can be induced in 2Fe- and 3Fe-doped systems by hole doping, respectively. Hole doping can also switch the magnetic anisotropy of the 2Fe@MgCl2 system from PMA to IMA. Otherwise, electron doping is found to affect mostly the electronic properties by metallizing the 2Fe@MgCl2 system (at 1.0 electron per supercell) and producing the half-metallicity in the 3Fe@MgCl2 system. Our results pave the way to effective magnetic functionalization of an insulator MgCl2 monolayer through Fe doping, where the charge-assisted control of electronic and magnetic properties can be useful for selective applications in magnetic field sensing and MRAMs fabrication.

Relationship Between Psychological Well-Being and Reproductive Health Attitudes in Female Patients with Psychiatric Diagnoses: A Cross-Sectional Study
Authors: Hatice Polat, Zeynep Bal, Esra Karataş-Okyay
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AIM:To examine the relationship between the reproductive health attitudes and psychological well-being levels of female patients with psychiatric diagnoses.METHODS:This research was conducted with a descriptive and relationship-seeking design and was completed with 193 female patients with psychiatric diagnoses. Data were collected using a personal information form, the Psychological Well-Being Scale, and the Scale for Determining the Reproductive Health Preventive Attitudes of Married Women.RESULTS:The women’s Psychological Well-Being Scale total mean score was 36.50 ± 11.74, and their Scale for Determining the Reproductive Health Preventive Attitudes of Married Women total mean score was 122.31 ± 25.47. A moderate positive relationship was found between the Psychological Well-Being Scale total and the Scale for Determining the Reproductive Health Preventive Attitudes of Married Women total (r = 0.572; p < .001).CONCLUSION:The study findings revealed that the psychological well-being and reproductive health protective attitudes of the female patients who participated in this study were at a moderate level and that there was a significant relationship between their psychological well-being and reproductive health protective attitudes (p < .01). Psychosocial support programs should be organized to increase the psychological well-being of patients, and reproductive health training should be integrated into these intervention programs.

Introduction

Well-being refers to the degree to which a person believes or feels that their life is going well (Diener et al., 2018). Psychological well-being, one of the concepts of positive psychology, primarily involves a person feeling good about themselves, looking at life positively, having hope for the future, and being productive (Ryff et al., 1999; Nemati et al., 2024). In other words, psychological well-being is defined as the combination of “feeling good and functioning effectively” (Wen et al., 2025). In the literature, it is reported that individuals with a psychiatric diagnosis do not have an adequate level of psychological well-being (Wen et al., 2025). The prevalence of mental disorders is higher in women, and the frequency of psychiatric disorders is particularly notable during reproductive years (McCloskey et al., 2021). It is not possible to separate the concepts of mental health and general health (Sarı & Gürhan, 2023). Women accessing mental health services have a need for information on sexual and reproductive health (RH) (McCloskey et al., 2021).

Reproductive health pertains to the promotion of a safe and fulfilling sexual life, the preservation of reproductive abilities, and ensuring the autonomy to determine if, when, and how often to exercise these abilities (Bozdemir & Özcan, 2011).Although RH is a concept that concerns all individuals, it directly affects the lives of women (Ünsal et al., 2010). The lack of knowledge of individuals in the fertile period on issues such as sexuality, reproduction, and family planning and their difficulty in openly discussing matters related to RH are among the factors that contribute to the development of RH problems in women (Erkin, 2019).In addition, being diagnosed with a psychiatric disease is a factor that may affect RH. Individuals with a psychiatric diagnosis may face serious problems related to their sexual life and RH due to the prolonged and chronic nature of their disease, disruption of their emotions and behaviors, insufficient autonomy, impairment of their ability to evaluate reality, emotional, or cognitive deficiencies, thought disorders, and the effects of their medications (Sarı & Gürhan, 2023). Among these individuals, it is common to observe a decline in marriage rates, having multiple sexual partners (Matevosyan, 2009), engaging in risky sexual behaviors, exposure to harassment and rape (Zerihun et al., 2021), contracting sexually transmitted diseases, unwanted pregnancies(Walsh et al., 2014), and low rates of contraceptive method use (Zerihun et al., 2020). Women with mental illness represent a disadvantaged group due to both their gender and mental disorders, as well as the serious problems they experience in relation to their RH (Keser Özcan et al., 2013). It is crucial to highlight the RH issues faced by women with psychiatric disorders and ascertain their RH attitudes and any possible associated factors in order to implement appropriate preventive measures and devise targeted interventions. This research aimed to determine the relationship between the RH attitudes and psychological well-being levels of female patients with psychiatric diagnoses.

Methods

Study Design

This research was designed as a descriptive cross-sectional study.SampleThe population of the study consisted of female patients at the psychiatry outpatient clinic of a public hospital located in a province in eastern Türkiye. The data for the research was collected by the researchers using face-to-face interviews in the patient waiting room of the clinic between February 2022 and July 2022. Based on an alpha level of 0.05, a statistical power of 0.90, and a small-to-moderate effect size of 0.23, the minimum required sample size for the study was determined to be 191. The study was completed with 193 female patients who had received a psychiatric diagnosis according to Diagnostic and Statistical Manual (DSM-5) criteria and met the inclusion criteria for the research.

Having a psychiatric diagnosis according to the DSM-5 diagnostic criteria, being aged 18 years or older, having no physical (speech, hearing impairment, etc.) or neurological disorders that would prevent the completion of research forms, leading an active sexual life, having no communication problems, and being open to cooperation.

Data Collection Tools

The data for the research was collected using a personal information form, Psychological Well-Being Scale (PWBS), and Scale for Determining the Reproductive Health Preventive Attitudes of Married Women (RHPAS).

Personal Information Form

This form was prepared by the researchers and included questions evaluating the descriptive characteristics of the patients (age, educational status, family type, employment status, etc.) and their attitudes toward the disease and RH (smoking status, disease duration, a history of being informed about genital hygiene, etc.).

Psychological Well-Being Scale

This scale was developed by Diener et al. (2009) and adapted into Turkish by Telef(Telef, 2013). It is a seven-point Likert-type scale with eight items. The response to each item is scored from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 7 (“strongly agree”) points. The lowest scale score is 8 points, and the highest is 56 points. All items include positive statements. A high score on the scale indicates that the individual has many psychological resources and strengths.In the study that performed the validity and reliability analyses of the Turkish version of the scale, Cronbach’s alpha of the scale was reported to be 0.80 (Telef, 2013). In this study, Cronbach’s alpha was found to be 0.89.

Scale for Determining the Reproductive Health Preventive Attitudes of Married Women

This scale was developed by Demirci (2004) to determine the RH preventive attitudes and behaviors of married women (Demirci, 2004). The scale includes the following five subscales: “behavior of going to the doctor on issues concerning reproductive health,” “protection from reproductive organ and breast cancer,” “general health behaviors to protect reproductive health,” “protection from genital infections,” and “protection from unwanted pregnancies.” The scale has response options of “never,” “rarely,” “sometimes,” “mostly,” and “always,” scored from 1 to 5. Items 5, 10, 16, and 28 are reverse-scored because they include negative statements. The total score that can be obtained from the scale varies between 39 and 195. Higher total and subscale scores indicate positive RH protective attitudes and behaviors. Cronbach’s alpha of the scale was determined to be 0.82 (Demirci, 2004) and found to be 0.92 in this study.

Statistical Analysis

The IBM SPSS v.25 (SPSS Inc.; Chicago, IL, USA) statistical package program was used to evaluate the research data. Percentage values and arithmetic mean and standard deviation values were given as descriptive statistics of the data. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov normality test was used to check whether the data showed a normal distribution. In group comparisons, the chi-square test was used for categorical data, the t-test and Mann–Whitney U-test were used in independent groups in paired groups, and the Kruskal–Wallis test was used in comparisons of more than two groups. The Pearson correlation analysis was employed when evaluating the relationship between the scales. The statistical significance limit was accepted as p < .05.

Ethical Considerations

Ethical approval was obtained from the Health Sciences Scientific Research and Publication Ethics Committee of Bingöl University (Approval no: 22-04/ Date: February 17, 2022). The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Before commencing the research, consent was obtained from women who volunteered to participate in the study.

Results

A total of 193 women with psychiatric disorders participated in the study. Of these, 52 had an anxiety disorder, 58 a depressive disorder, 10 obsessive-compulsive disorder, 37 bipolar affective disorder, 32 a psychotic disorder, and 4 a personality disorder. The participants’ mean total PWBS score was 36.50 ± 11.74, while their mean total RHPAS score was 122.31 ± 25.47.

Table 1 presents the comparison of the mean PWBS and RHPAS scores of the groups according to sociodemographic characteristics. The mean PWBS scores were determined to be statistically significantly higher among the participants aged 35 years or younger and those who had a nuclear family type (p < .05). The mean PWBS and RHPAS scores were statistically significantly higher among the participants who had a university degree or higher and those who were employed (p < .05). However, the participants’ mean PWBS and RHPAS scores did not statistically significantly differ according to their income status (p > .05) (Table 1). According to the post-hoc analysis, the PWBS total score was found to be significantly lower in the illiterate and literate groups (d, c > b > a). For the RHPAS total score, the significant difference among the groups was observed across all three groups (d > c > b > a).

Of the women who participated in this study, 30.1% had depressive disorder, 26.9% anxiety disorder, 19.2% bipolar affective disorder, 16.6% psychotic disorder, 5.2% obsessive-compulsive disorder, and 2% personality disorder (Figure 1).

Table 2 shows the comparison of the participants’ mean PWBS and RHPAS scores according to their disease and RH attitudes. Participants with a disease duration of 0–5 years, those without a hospitalization history, changing their underwear every day, and those who had previously been informed about genital hygiene, psychiatric diagnoses were found to have statistically significantly higher mean PWBS scores (p < .05). In addition, the mean RHPAS scores were statistically significantly higher among the participants who had a disease duration of 0–5 years, those who used their medications regularly, those without a hospitalization history, changing three to four sanitary pads during the menstrual period, changing their underwear every day, cleaning their genital area with soap and water, and those who had previously been informed about genital hygiene, psychiatric diagnoses (p < .05). However, there were not statistically significant differences in the participants’ mean PWBS and RHPAS scores according to smoking status, parity, or the number of living children (p > .05) (Table 2). According to the post-hoc analysis, the PWBS total score was found to be significantly lower in the illiterate and literate groups (d, c>b>a). The RHPAS total score showed a significant difference among all four groups (d>c>b>a). The post-hoc analysis also indicated that the PWBS and RHPAS total scores were significantly higher in individuals with a disease duration of 0–5 years (c>a, b; c>a). Furthermore, the PWBS and RHPAS total scores were lowest in participants who changed their underwear once a week (a>b>c). The RHPAS total score was lowest among those who cleaned their genital area with water (b,c,d>a). In addition, the PWBS and RHPAS total scores were significantly higher in individuals with a psychiatric diagnosis of anxiety disorder (a>e), and the RHPAS total score was significantly higher in those diagnosed with depressive disorder (b>e).

The distribution of the participants’ mean PWBS and RHPAS total and subscale scores is given in Table 3. The mean PWBS total score was 36.50 ± 11.74, and the mean RHPAS total score was 122.31 ± 25.47 (Table 3).

The comparison of the mean PWBS and RHPAS total and subscale scores according to the participants’ psychiatric diagnoses is shown in Table 4. The mean PWBS score was statistically significantly higher among the participants with a personality disorder, while the mean RHPAS score was statistically significantly higher among those with a depressive disorder (p = .024 and p < .001, respectively). The participants with a personality disorder had statistically significantly higher mean scores on the RHPAS subscales related to visiting a doctor on issues concerning RH, reproductive organ and breast cancer, and protection of RH (p = .002, p = .002, and p = .018, respectively). In addition, the participants with a depressive disorder had statistically significant higher mean scores on the RHPAS subscales related to genital infections and unwanted pregnancies (p < .001 and p = .003, respectively).

Table 5 presents the relationship between the PWBS and RHPAS scores. A moderate positive relationship was found between the PWBS total score and the RHPAS total and subscale scores (r = 0.572, p < .001; r = 0.474, p < .001; r = 0.318, p < .001; r = 0.406, p < .001; r = 0.543, p < .001; and r = 0.347, p < .001, respectively in the table).

Table 6 presents the Linear Regression Analysis of PWBS total and of RHPAS total and subscale. The regression analysis demonstrated that psychological well-being significantly predicts RH attitudes. Specifically, PWBS showed a significant positive effect on overall RH attitudes (β = 0.572, t = 9.64, p < .001). Examination of the subscales revealed that the strongest influence of PWBS was observed on behaviors related to protection from genital infections (β = 0.543, t = 8.93, p < .001), whereas the weakest effect was found for behaviors aimed at preventing unwanted pregnancies (β = 0.317, t = 4.62, p < .001). These results indicate that individuals with higher levels of psychological well-being are more likely to adopt positive RH behaviors.

Discussion

Reproductive health is one of the concepts that directly affects women’s lives. It is known that psychiatric patients experience more problems in RH compared to the general population (Matevosyan, 2009). In this study, the relationship between RH attitudes and psychological well-being levels of female patients with psychiatric diagnoses was examined. The most important finding of this study is that as the level of psychological well-being increases in female patients diagnosed with psychiatric disorders, their attitudes toward behaviors that promote RH also increase.

Psychological well-being is, in short, the state in which an individual is satisfied with his or her life and is able to realize his or her potential by maintaining emotional balance. Considering the minimum and maximum scores that can be obtained from the PWBS, it can be stated that the psychological well-being of the patients participating in the current study was at a moderate level. The related findings indicated that the patients participating in the study perceived their interactions with others as insufficient, had a lower capacity to manage the complexities in their surroundings, and felt that their lives lacked purpose and meaning. In addition, in light of these findings, it can be concluded that the self-acceptance of the patients participating in the study was not high, i.e., they did not have positive attitudes toward themselves. In the literature, studies conducted with different psychiatric patient groups have reported that the psychological well-being of psychiatric patients is not high (Özdemir & Kavak, 2021; Strauss et al., 2012). In the current study, patients diagnosed with a psychotic disorder were found to have the lowest level of psychological well-being. In the literature, studies conducted with schizophrenic patients have reported that the psychological well-being of individuals diagnosed with this disease is not high (Harmanci & Budak, 2022). The findings of this research are involved with those reported in the literature. Based on the current study’s findings, it can be said that the patients participating in the study did not have high self-acceptance, meaning that their positive attitudes toward themselves were relatively low. Psychological well-being, in short, is an individual’s balanced and meaningful relationship with themselves and their life. In this context, ensuring psychological well-being should be one of the primary goals for all female patients with psychiatric diagnoses, especially those diagnosed with psychotic disorders, and the importance of psychological well-being should be considered in psychosocial interventions.

Given the score range of the RHPAS, it can be stated that the RH protective attitudes of the patients participating in the current research were at a moderate level. Studies in the literature have similarly reported that persons with a psychiatric diagnosis do not have sufficient knowledge or positive attitudes concerning RH issues (Kemigisha et al., 2018; Keser Özcan et al., 2013; Matevosyan, 2009).Keser Özcan et al. (2013) also determined that female patients with psychiatric disorders had more negative characteristics than the healthy population in relation to marriage, sexuality, family planning, maternal characteristics, and pregnancy (Keser Özcan et al., 2013). A lack of positive RH attitudes may cause female psychiatric patients to be at greater risk of reproductive system diseases. A study by Hope et al., in which the rate of reproductive system cancers was found to be higher among women with mental illness than in those without mental illness (Hope et al., 2022). In another study, Woodhead et al. revealed that women with serious mental illness were less likely to undergo screening for breast and cervical cancer (Woodhead et al., 2016). One of the important findings of the current research is that individuals diagnosed with a psychotic disorder had the lowest scores on the total RHPAS and its subscales. Seeman and Ross (2011) found that although the overall pregnancy rate in schizophrenia patients is lower than in the general population, the percentage of unintended pregnancies is higher than in the general population (Seeman & Ross, 2011). In a study evaluating family planning needs and contraceptive use among female psychiatric patients, Pehlivanoğlu et al. (2007) determined that awareness and attitudes regarding family planning and contraceptive use were inadequate among female patients diagnosed with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder (Pehlivanoğlu et al., 2007). In a different study, Gündoğmuş et al. (2023) found that schizophrenic patients had less awareness about breast cancer and faced more obstacles in breast cancer screening. These findings are similar to the literature (Gündoğmuş et al., 2023). In this study, although the patients’ RH attitudes were not low, they were not at an adequate level. Therefore, in psychological interventions and during the treatment process in psychiatric clinics, psychiatric nurses should educate patients and teach coping strategies to reduce negative behaviors and promote positive behaviors related to RH attitudes. Placing greater emphasis on mental health problems may lead patients to not report their sexual problems. Psychiatric nurses should inquire about female patients’ RH attitudes during routine assessments. Additionally, providing in-service training to psychiatric nurses on this topic will help improve the care that patients receive regarding their RH attitudes.

Studies have found that people with high levels of psychological well-being have better mental and physiological resilience and higher living standards (Keyes et al., 2010). In the current study, a moderate positive relationship was observed between the PWBS score and the RHPAS total and subscale scores. This finding shows that women with high psychological well-being tend to have more positive RH attitudes. Reproductive health issues are important aspects of physical health that should be assessed in women accessing mental health services (Henshaw & Protti, 2010).Research has established a positive correlation between increased psychological well-being and improved physical health (Vanderschelden et al., 2023). The results of this investigation are consistent with the findings in the existing literature. Ensuring the physical well-being of individuals with psychiatric diseases is a priority for mental health services both nationally and internationally (DE Hert et al., 2011). Each psychiatric disorder has its own different reproductive and sexual health risks (Dinç et al., 2019), including unsafe sexual intercourse, sexually transmitted diseases, and less use of family planning (Pagano et al., 2016). The problems faced by psychiatric patients in relation to their RH are often overlooked in the fields of psychiatry and women’s health. Therefore, determining the factors affecting RH would facilitate the necessary development of essential strategies to improve the RH of women with psychiatric diagnoses. It is considered that this study will contribute to the literature in this respect. These study results highlight the importance of psychological well-being in reducing RH problems in psychiatric patients, while also emphasizing the significance of holistic care. Psychiatric nurses, in particular, play a critical role in the delivery of holistic care. Psychoeducational programs aimed at enhancing psychological well-being should be provided, and approaches that protect and promote RH should also be integrated into these programs. In this way, patients’ functionality improves, and they can cope more effectively with the problems they experience.

This study revealed that women aged 35 years and below and those with a nuclear family type had high mean PWBS scores. It was also found that the mean RHPAS scores were high among the women who used their medications regularly, changing their sanitary pads three to four times a day during the menstrual period, and those who reported using soap and water to clean the genital area. In addition, high mean PWBS and RHPAS scores were observed among the women with an educational level of university or higher, those who were employed, those with a disease duration of 0–5 years, those without a history of hospitalization, changing their underwear every day, and those who had previously been informed about genital hygiene. Sex education should be provided to all individuals. Studies in the literature also recommend attaching more importance to sexual education in patients with severe mental illness (Mirsepassi et al., 2022).

The regression analysis indicated that PWBS significantly predicted RHPAS. Psychological Well-Being Scale had a positive and significant effect on the total RHPAS score (β = 0.572, t = 9.64, p < .001). This finding suggests that individuals with higher levels of psychological well-being tend to adopt more positive RH behaviors. When the subscales of RH attitudes were examined, the strongest effect of PWBS was observed in the domain of protection from genital infections (β = 0.543, t = 8.93, p < .001), whereas the weakest effect was found in protection from unwanted pregnancy (β = 0.317, t = 4.62, p < .001). This may indicate that as individuals’ psychological well-being increases, they are more likely to adopt behaviors aimed at protecting themselves from health-related risks; however, behaviors related to preventing unwanted pregnancy may be influenced by additional social, cultural, and informational factors. Studies have shown that good psychological well-being and high life satisfaction are associated with strengthened RH behaviors (Arcos-Romero & Calvillo, 2023; Hall et al., 2014).

Study Limitations

This study also has certain limitations. The study was implemented at a single center and included only outpatients presenting to the clinic within a specific timeframe.

Conclusion and Recommendations

In this study, it was observed that as patients’ levels of psychological well-being increased, they exhibited more positive RH attitudes. Additionally, it was found that both the psychological well-being levels and RH attitudes of the psychotic diagnosis group were lower compared to other patients. The literature reveals serious inequalities in the sexual and RH of psychiatric patients. This study findings also indicate that patients’ RH attitudes are inadequate and that they have a need for information on this topic.

A positive RH attitude is an important physical health indicator. Providing holistic care is important for improving patients’ overall health levels. Midwives or nurses should provide reproductive and sexual health education for every woman diagnosed with a psychiatric disease. The roles of psychiatric nurses in RH should be strengthened, and nurses should be supported with in-service training on this topic. İmplementing interventions aimed at increasing the psychological well-being of psychiatric patients will result in improved psychological state and the adoption of attitudes and behaviors that will positively impact physical health. Moreover, disseminating information about RH to psychiatric patients will facilitate the identification of reproductive system disorders at an early stage. Therefore, nursing care provided in psychiatric clinics, psychoeducational programs, and psychosocial interventions should be restructured by integrating RH education and counseling services, and new approaches should be developed accordingly. It is recommended that further comparative studies be undertaken in the field of RH. Thus, new studies will help to better understand the relationships among the findings.

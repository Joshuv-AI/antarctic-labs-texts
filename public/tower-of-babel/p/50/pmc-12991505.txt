A Study of Cutaneous Manifestations in Patients During the Post-COVID Period in A Tertiary Care Center
Authors: Ayushi Pandey, Renuka Satish Ashtekar, Shantiprasad Adappa Tippanawar, Chandrashekhar Shrihari Purandare, Rajvardhan Malgonda Bagane, Ranjana Sharma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The post-COVID period has been associated with various systemic complications, including dermatological manifestations. Understanding the spectrum of cutaneous manifestations following coronavirus disease of 2019 (COVID-19) infection is essential for early diagnosis and appropriate management. This study aims to assess the prevalence and types of cutaneous manifestations in patients recovering from COVID-19 at a tertiary care center.Methods:A cross-sectional observational study was conducted at the Dermatology, Venereology, and Leprosy (DVL) outpatient department of Bharati Vidyapeeth (Deemed to be University) Medical College and Hospital, Sangli, over a period of 6 months. A total of 50 patients presenting with new or worsening cutaneous symptoms at least 4 weeks after COVID-19 recovery were included. Clinical and demographic data were documented, and dermatological examinations were performed.Results:The most commonly observed cutaneous manifestation was telogen effluvium (30%), followed by herpes infection (20%), acne (16%), and pityriasis rosea (6%). Other conditions included lichen planus, psoriasis, vasculitis, erythema multiforme, vitiligo, and dermatomyositis. Middle-aged individuals were predominantly affected, and females exhibited a higher prevalence (58%) than males (42%).Conclusion:Post-COVID cutaneous manifestations exhibit a diverse range of presentations, necessitating a multidisciplinary approach for timely recognition and intervention. Further studies with larger sample sizes are required to establish potential mechanisms linking COVID-19 with dermatological sequelae.

BACKGROUND

The coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), has led to a wide range of systemic complications beyond respiratory involvement, including cardiovascular, neurological, and dermatological manifestations.[1] While the primary focus has been on pulmonary and systemic effects, emerging evidence suggests that cutaneous manifestations are frequently observed during and after the acute phase of infection.[2] The pathophysiology underlying post-COVID dermatological conditions remains unclear, but it is hypothesized to involve immune dysregulation, inflammatory responses, and viral reactivation.[3]

Cutaneous involvement in COVID-19 varies significantly, with reported cases including maculopapular eruptions, urticarial rashes, chilblain-like lesions, and vasculitic presentations.[2] Post-COVID cutaneous manifestations, defined as skin-related symptoms occurring 4 or more weeks after recovery, have been increasingly reported in clinical studies. Telogen effluvium, herpes virus reactivations, and inflammatory dermatoses such as lichen planus and psoriasis have been frequently observed in post-COVID patients, indicating a potential interplay between the immune system and dermatological sequelae.[4] These manifestations not only contribute to physical discomfort but also pose psychological burdens, impacting the quality of life of affected individuals.[5]

MATERIAL AND METHOD

Study setting and design

This cross-sectional observational study was conducted at the Dermatology, Venereology, and Leprosy (DVL) outpatient department (OPD) of Bharati Vidyapeeth (Deemed to be University) Medical College and Hospital, Sangli. The study was conducted over six months, from December 2021 to December 2022. The study focused on identifying and documenting the various cutaneous manifestations in patients who had recovered from COVID-19 infection and were attending the DVL OPD or were referred from other departments within the hospital.

Study population

The study population included patients of all age groups who had recovered from COVID-19 and presented with new or worsening cutaneous symptoms in the post-COVID period (defined as 4 or more weeks after the initial COVID-19 infection). A total of 50 patients who met the inclusion criteria were included in the study.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for this study consisted of patients who had successfully recovered from COVID-19 infection and presented with new or worsening cutaneous manifestations during the post-COVID period. Additionally, only those patients who provided informed consent were included in the study. Conversely, the exclusion criteria encompassed patients unwilling to participate in the study, those with cutaneous manifestations deemed unrelated to their COVID-19 infection, or those with pre-existing chronic dermatological conditions not associated with COVID-19.

Data collection

Data collection for this study was carried out through a meticulously structured process designed to ensure the accuracy and completeness of the information gathered. Each participant was assessed for eligibility based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Once eligibility was confirmed and informed consent was obtained, detailed demographic and clinical data were collected using a predesigned proforma.

The proforma included sections for recording demographic details such as age, gender, and relevant medical history, particularly focusing on the COVID-19 infection, including the severity, duration, and time since recovery. Special attention was paid to documenting any prior dermatological conditions to distinguish between new or exacerbated cutaneous manifestations attributable to the post-COVID period and pre-existing conditions.

Clinical examination of the patients was conducted in the Dermatology, Venereology, and Leprosy (DVL) outpatient department (OPD). Each patient underwent a thorough dermatological examination, documenting the nature, distribution, and severity of the cutaneous lesions. High-resolution photographs of the cutaneous manifestations were taken with the patient’s consent, providing visual evidence that supported the clinical findings. These images were carefully archived for further analysis and reference.

In addition to the clinical examination, relevant laboratory investigations were conducted to complement the clinical findings. These investigations included blood tests, skin biopsies (if necessary), and serological tests for COVID-19 antibodies, particularly in cases where the relationship between the skin manifestations and the previous COVID-19 infection needed further confirmation.

Statistical analysis

Data collected were entered into a spreadsheet and analyzed using statistical software SPSS version 23 (IBM). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the demographic data and the frequency of various cutaneous manifestations. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Chi-square tests were used to analyze the association between categorical variables, and P values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Ethical consideration

The study followed the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC) of Bharati Vidyapeeth (Deemed to be University) Medical College and Hospital, Sangli, as documented in the attached approval letter (Ref: BV (DU) MCandH/Sangli/IEC/463/21, dated 20/12/2021).

DISCUSSION

The findings of this study underscore the significant dermatological impact observed in patients recovering from COVID-19, aligning with emerging literature documenting a range of post-infectious cutaneous manifestations. Telogen effluvium was the most prevalent condition in the study cohort, affecting 30% of patients. This finding is consistent with reports indicating that COVID-19-related systemic inflammation and stress-induced effluvium contribute to transient but severe hair shedding.[6] The pathogenesis of telogen effluvium post COVID has been attributed to cytokine-mediated hair cycle disruption, leading to premature follicular transition into the telogen phase.[7]

Herpes reactivation, observed in 20% of patients, highlights the role of viral reactivation following COVID-19 infection. Previous studies have reported that SARS-CoV-2-induced immune dysregulation may trigger reactivation of latent herpesviruses, particularly varicella-zoster virus and herpes simplex virus.[8] This aligns with previous case series identifying increased incidences of herpes zoster and herpes simplex post-COVID.[9] Acne, found in 16% of cases in this study, has also been reported as a post-viral sequela, potentially exacerbated by systemic inflammation, corticosteroid use, and post-COVID skin barrier dysfunction.[10]

Lichen planus, pityriasis rosea, psoriasis, and vasculitis were also observed among patients, supporting prior reports linking viral infections to immune-mediated dermatological conditions. Pityriasis rosea has been proposed as a viral exanthem associated with COVID-19, likely triggered by HHV-6 or HHV-7 reactivation following immune suppression.[11] Similarly, psoriasis exacerbations post COVID have been documented, attributed to heightened inflammatory cytokine activity, including interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α).[12]

Multisystem inflammatory syndrome in children (MIS-C), erythema multiforme, vitiligo, and dermatomyositis were among the less common manifestations observed. MIS-C has been recognized as a severe post-COVID inflammatory condition presenting with dermatological involvement, including rash and mucocutaneous symptoms.[13] The association between erythema multiforme and SARS-CoV-2 infection has also been documented, suggesting a post-viral immune-mediated response.[14]

The female predominance in this study (58%) aligns with literature suggesting a higher incidence of post-COVID dermatological conditions in females, potentially due to hormonal influences on immune responses and hair follicle cycling.[15] Additionally, the predominance of cases among middle-aged individuals highlights a potential age-related predisposition to inflammatory and stress-induced dermatological conditions.

CONCLUSION

The findings of this study highlight the diverse range of cutaneous manifestations observed in patients during the post-COVID period, emphasizing the need for increased dermatological vigilance in individuals recovering from the infection. Telogen effluvium emerged as the most frequently reported condition, followed by herpes infections, acne, and pityriasis rosea, among others. The study also noted a higher prevalence of these manifestations in females and middle-aged individuals. While the exact pathophysiological mechanisms linking COVID-19 and dermatological sequelae remain unclear, immune dysregulation, inflammatory responses, and viral reactivation are likely contributing factors. Given the persistence and potential impact of these manifestations on patients’ quality of life, early recognition and appropriate management are essential.

The roles and pathways of Hsa_circ_0000285 in cancer: a potential target for cancer therapy
Authors: Yan Lv, Dan Li, Qun-Xian Zhang, Tao Zeng, Xiang-Yu Luo, Qiang Guo, Qiao-Ya Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCircular RNA (circRNA) is a unique type of non-coding RNA molecule characterized by a closed circular structure, exceptional stability, and the ability to act as miRNA sponges, thereby relieving their inhibitory effects on downstream target genes, thus regulating cancer cell growth and metastasis. Circ_0000285 is one of the recently discovered circRNAs. It is aberrantly expressed in hepatocellular carcinoma, gastric cancer, bladder cancer, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, osteosarcoma, thyroid cancer, glioma, cervical cancer, and neuroblastoma, predominantly exhibiting upregulation. Abnormal expression of circ_0000285 is significantly associated with cancer cell growth, metastasis, and poor prognosis in cancer patients, a process mediated by circ_0000285 acting as sponges for miR-582-3p, miR-1278, miR-409-3p, miR-599, miR-127-5p, miR‑654-3p, and miR197-3p to promote the expression of downstream target genes or directly regulate target gene expression. Therefore, this review aims to summarize the expression, function, mechanisms, and association with poor prognostic features in cancer patients of circ_0000285, offering novel candidate target molecules for the treatment of cancer.Keywords: circ_0000285, circRNA, miRNAs, Cancer

Background

In recent years, circular RNAs (circRNAs) have emerged as a distinct class of non-coding RNA molecules characterized by a closed circular structure, exhibiting exceptional stability. CircRNAs function as sponges for microRNAs (miRNAs), alleviating miRNA-mediated suppression of downstream target gene expression, thereby modulating cancer growth and metastasis [1–10]. For instance, Yuan et al., reported the upregulation of circular RNA_0005397 (circ_0005397) in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), correlating with diagnostic capabilities, tumor size, and stage. The overexpression of circ_0005397 enhances proliferation, clonogenicity, and metastasis of HCC cells. circ_0005397 promotes the occurrence and progression of HCC by modulating EIF4A3 expression [2]. Similarly, it was demonstrated by Qian et al., that circ_0102231, which is overexpressed in liver cancer cells, plays a functional role in promoting tumor progression. Inhibition of this circRNA was found to suppress cellular proliferation and migration both in vitro and in vivo, mechanistically through the miR-873/SOX4 signaling axis [10].

Circular RNA_0000285 (circ_0000285) is a recently discovered circRNA that exhibits abnormal expression, predominantly as overexpression, in various cancers such as HCC, gastric cancer (GC), bladder cancer (BLCA), nasopharyngeal carcinoma, osteosarcoma (OS), thyroid cancer (THCA), glioma, cervical cancer (CC), and neuroblastoma [11–26]. For instance, Huang et al., reported an upregulation of circ_0000285 and CCNB2 expression in HCC cells and tissues, alongside reduced miR-582-3p expression. Circ_0000285 competitively binds to miR-582-3p to enhance CCNB2 expression levels. Inhibiting circ_0000285 expression facilitates apoptosis in HCC cells, suppressing proliferation, migration, and in vivo tumor growth. By modulating the miR-582-3p/CCNB2 signaling axis, circ_0000285 promotes the malignant phenotype development of HCC [11]. Currently, the relationship between circ_0000285 and the malignant phenotype of cancer remains incompletely elucidated. This review aims to provide new candidate target molecules for cancer patient treatment by summarizing the role of abnormal circ_0000285 expression in cancer growth and metastasis, including the associated signaling mechanisms.

The circ_0000285 expression in cancer tissues

Current research findings indicate that circ_0000285 is significantly overexpressed in most cancer tissues or serum (Table 1). High circ_0000285 expression is observed in HCC, GC, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, OS, THCA, glioma, CC, and neuroblastoma tissues or serum. Notably, circ_0000285 exhibits a pattern of overexpression in HCC (Hep 3B, Huh7, SNU-182), GC (HGC-27, AGS, GTL-16), nasopharyngeal carcinoma (HK-1, C666-1, 5–8 F, SUNE-1), OS (SJSA1, U2OS, Saos2, MG63, HOS), THCA (KTC-1, TPC-1, SW579, FTC133), glioma (SHG-44, U251, A172), CC (HeLa, SiHa, C4-1, C-33a, SiHa, CaSki, HeLa), and neuroblastoma (SK-N-BE, SK-N-SH, SK-NAS, IMR-32). However, its expression is significantly suppressed in BLCA tissues and cells (HTB-9, T24, J82, SW780, RT4, RT4/DDP).

Abnormal expression of circ_0000285 is associated with cancer cell growth and metastasis

Cell proliferation, clonogenicity, cell cycle transition, apoptosis, migration, and invasion capabilities are all characteristic features of cancer cells closely associated with aberrant circRNA expression (Tables 2, 3 and 4). In HCC (Huh7 and Hep 3B), GC (HGC-27 and GTL-16), THCA (TPC‑1 and FTC133 or KTC-1), neuroblastoma (SK-N-BE and SK-N-SH), and nasopharyngeal carcinoma (C666-1 and 5–8 F) cells, overexpression of circ_0000285 promotes cell proliferation while inhibiting apoptosis (Table 2). Specifically, circ_0000285 overexpression facilitates clonogenic formation in nasopharyngeal carcinoma (C666-1 and 5–8 F) cells, and enhances the proliferation capacity of glioma (SHG-44 and U251) cells. In OS (MG63 and U2OS or SJSA1) and CC (SiHa and HeLa or C-33a) cells, circ_0000285 overexpression promotes cell proliferation, clonogenic formation, and cell cycle transition, while suppressing cell apoptosis. Moreover, circ_0000285 overexpression enhances tumorigenic capability in Hep3B of HCC cells, HGC-27 and GTL-16 of GC, C666-1 of nasopharyngeal carcinoma, U2OS and MG63 of OS, KTC-1 of THCA, SHG-44 of glioma, C-33a and HeLa of CC, and SK-N-BE of neuroblastoma cells in nude mice (Table 3).

Furthermore, in HCC (Huh7 and Hep3B), OS (SJSA1, MG63, and U2OS), THCA (KTC-1 and TPC-1), glioma (SHG-44 and U251), CC (C-33a), neuroblastoma (SK-N-BE and SK-N-SH), overexpression of circ_0000285 facilitates cell migration. In nasopharyngeal carcinoma (C666-1 and 5–8 F, GC HGC-27 and GTL-16), OS (SJSA1 and U2OS), glioma (SHG-44 and U251), and CC (C-33a), circ_0000285 overexpression promotes cancer cell invasion (Table 4). These results underscore that the overexpression of circ_0000285 indicates enhanced capabilities in cancer growth and metastasis, indicative of poor prognosis.

circ_0000285 participates in cancer cell growth and metastasis by acting as a miRNA sponge or directly inhibiting target genes

Like other long non-coding RNAs, circ_0000285 can interact with miRNA sponges to relieve inhibition on downstream target genes, consequently impacting cancer growth and metastasis (Table 5). In HCC and neuroblastoma, circ_0000285 interacts with miR-582-3p to promote CCNB2 expression, contributing to the growth and metastasis of HCC and neuroblastoma [11, 26]. Similarly, circ_0000285 interacts with miR-1278 to enhance FN1 and FNDC3B expression, participating in the proliferation, migration, and tumorigenic ability of GC and nasopharyngeal carcinoma [12]. Furthermore, circ_0000285 acts through different miRNA signaling axes to stimulate the growth and metastasis of OS, THCA, glioma, and CC cells (Table 5).

Moreover, inhibiting circ_0000285 expression in HCC cells (Hep 3B and Huh7), nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells (C666-1 and 5–8 F), THCA cells (KTC-1, FTC133, and TPC-1), glioma cells (SHG-44 and U251), CC cells (SiHa and HeLa), and neuroblastoma cells (SK-N-BE and SK-N-SH) enhances the Bax or FUS protein expression while reducing Bcl-2 protein expression [11, 15, 18–21, 24, 26]. Inhibiting circ_0000285 expression in CC cells (SiHa, C-33a and HeLa) suppresses PCNA, cyclin D1, and BCL2 protein expression while promoting LC3II/I or FUS protein expression, affecting CC cell growth [22, 23]. Additionally, circ_0000285 enhances Wnt/β-catenin signaling activity to promote neuroblastoma development [26].

The abnormal expression of circ_0000285 is closely related to the prognosis of cancer patients

The abnormal expression of circ_0000285 is closely associated with adverse prognosis and diagnostic value in cancer patients (Table 6). A decrease in circ_0000285 expression levels correlates positively with shorter overall survival, tumor size, tumor differentiation, lymph node metastasis, distant metastasis, and TNM stage in BLCA patients. Conversely, an increase in circ_0000285 expression levels correlates negatively with shorter overall survival, tumor size, tumor differentiation, lymph node metastasis, distant metastasis, TNM stage, and radiotherapy sensitivity in nasopharyngeal cancer patients. Additionally, high circ_0000285 expression levels are significantly correlated with tumor size, tumor differentiation, FIGO stage, CA125, SCC-Ag, HPV positivity, and diagnosis in CC patients, and significantly associated with INSS stage and amplification in neuroblastoma patients.

Conclusion

Cancer is one of the leading causes of death worldwide, characterized by its high diversity and complexity. The expression of circ_0000285 exhibits opposing patterns across different cancers (Table 1). While this may appear contradictory, such context-dependent heterogeneity holds considerable biological significance in non-coding RNA research and is likely driven by tissue-specific transcriptional and post-transcriptional regulation. The biogenesis of circRNAs, including circ_0000285, depends on the transcription of their parental genes and the efficiency of back-splicing—a process tightly controlled by tissue- or cell-type-specific splicing factors, RNA-binding proteins, and the epigenetic landscape of regulatory elements. Alternatively, the observed low expression of circ_0000285 in bladder cancer could be an artifact of the limited sample size, rather than a true biological phenomenon. Future work should involve larger sample validation and further mechanistic studies, both in vitro and in vivo, to elucidate the precise reasons behind this differential expression. This review summarizes the latest research on the molecular mechanisms related to circ_0000285 in the occurrence and progression of cancer, providing new targets for the treatment of cancer patients. However, there remain considerable challenges in translating circ_0000285 into clinical practice. Its tissue-specific expression profile—downregulated in bladder cancer but upregulated in other cancers—demands a highly selective delivery system to minimize off-target effects and potential adverse events. Future efforts should prioritize the design of intelligent nanocarriers capable of leveraging the unique tumour microenvironment of bladder cancer for precise targeting and regulation. Furthermore, systematic evaluation of its long-term safety in robust preclinical models will be essential prior to any clinical application.

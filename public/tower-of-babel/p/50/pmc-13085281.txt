Cytomegalovirus DNAemia as a prognostic marker in critically ill hematopoietic stem cell transplant recipients with lower respiratory tract infection
Authors: Yan Hu, Yanwen Jiang, Fengxue Zhu, Jiawei Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT) recipients, Lower Respiratory Tract Infection (LRTI) is a leading cause of Intensive Care Units (ICU) admission and mortality. Although Cytomegalovirus (CMV) reactivation is a known risk factor for worse outcomes in HSCT recipients, the specific prognostic impact of CMV DNAemia on critically ill HSCT recipients with LRTI is not well-defined. This study aimed to determine the relationship between plasma CMV DNA levels at ICU admission and mortality in this high-risk population.MethodsThis retrospective study collected adult patients who have admitted to a single-center ICU received HSCT and were diagnosed with LRTI. Based on plasma CMV DNA levels tested within 48 h of admission, patients were grouped as CMV positive (> 103 copies/mL) or CMV negative (≤ 103 copies/mL). We performed a 1:1 propensity score matching to control for confounding factors. The primary outcome was ICU mortality. Secondary outcomes including 1-year mortality, ventilator-free days, and ICU length of stay. Survival of CMV positive or negative patients was examined with Cox regression.ResultsFrom 606 eligible patients, each of 155 patients were matched for the two groups by propensity score matching. The CMV positive group exhibited a significantly higher ICU mortality rate compared to the CMV negative group (32.35% vs. 25.80%, P = 0.011). A Cox regression analysis, adjusted for age, WBC count, SOFA score, PaO₂/FiO₂, and GvHD, confirmed that CMV positivity was independently associated with a higher risk of 1-year mortality (HR = 1.27, 95% CI [1.10–2.33], P = 0.002). The ventilator-free days and ICU length of stay did not significantly differ between the two groups.ConclusionIn HSCT recipients admitted to the ICU with LRTI, CMV DNAemia at admission is an independent predictor of both short-term and long-term mortality.

Introduction

Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation (HSCT) is a potentially curative therapy for various hematological malignancies and other life-threatening disorders [1]. The therapeutic potential of HSCT, however, is hindered by substantial risks of complications that required Intensive Care Unit (ICU) management [2, 3]. The process of immune reconstruction and application of immune suppressants have made HSCT recipients vulnerable for potential infections. Lower Respiratory Tract Infections (LRTIs) is the most frequently diagnosed infection and was a major cause of respiratory failure and ICU admission [4], with an ICU mortality up to 50% [5].

Among numerous factors, reactivation of cytomegalovirus (CMV) is a key challenge after HSCT. CMV is a beta herpesvirus that remains latent in most adults. When the host immune system is compromised, the latent virus can reactivate and replicate in vivo, leading to CMV DNAemia [6]. CMV not only directly causes tissue-invasive diseases such as CMV pneumonia, which is inherently highly lethal, but more importantly, it can also have significant indirect effects [7]. Including suppressing hematopoietic cell functions, increasing the risk of other opportunistic infections (such as bacterial and fungal infections), and interacting with the development and exacerbation of graft-versus-host disease (GvHD). These effects were associated with worse clinical outcomes in HSCT patients [7].

Currently, plasma CMV DNA monitoring is routinely performed in HSCT patients to guide preemptive therapy strategies and prevent the development of CMV disease. While the detrimental effect of CMV reactivation on the overall prognosis of HSCT patients is well recognized, the specific prognostic significance of CMV DNAemia regarding short- and long-term mortality in HSCT patients admitted to the ICU with LRTI remains poorly defined [8].

This study aimed to determine the relationship between plasma CMV DNA levels at ICU admission and ICU mortality or other outcomes in adult HSCT patients with LRTI, while meticulously adjusting for baseline differences in patient and illness variables by propensity score matching.

Patients who met any one of clinical criteria and imaging criteria were considered to have LRTI.

Patients with no plasma CMV DNA test within 48 h after ICU admission were excluded.

Data collection

The following data were collected from the insight-ICU database: age, gender, BMI (Body mass index), PO2/FiO2 in arterial blood gas analysis, max temperature, SOFA (Sequential Organ Failure Assessment) score, WBC (White Blood Cell) count, hemoglobin level, procalcitonin level, lactate level, malignancy type (leukemia, lymphoma, or other diseases). GvHD (Graft versus Host Disease) diagnosis, HLA (Human Leukocyte Antigen) matching (identical or haploidentical), post transplantation days.

Routinely, HSCT patients were tested for plasma CMV DNA levels twice a week (on every Monday and Thursday). According to the CMV levels on ICU admission, patients were divided into CMV positive (> 103 copy/mL) and CMV negative groups (≤ 103 copy/mL). The rationale for choosing the cutoff was that a CMV level lower than 1000 is considered as negative because in this situation, it often resolves spontaneously or represents a bystander phenomenon. And patients with CMV DNA levels higher than 1000 will receive preemptive therapy.

The quantification of CMV DNA was performed using the CMV Nucleic Acid Assay Kit (Liferiver, China) on ABI Prism 7300 qPCR system. We used plasma extracted from blood samples to quantify CMV DNA.

The status of survival (through telephone follow-up) was also recorded for every patient till 1 year after ICU admission.

For diagnostic purpose, patients that could tolerate bronchoscopy examination received bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) and proceeded with next generation sequencing (NGS) and culture. Patients who were CMV positive were treated with ganciclovir or foscarnet, dosages were adjusted for renal function.

Outcomes

We evaluated the ICU mortality, ventilator free days, ICU length of stay and new LRTI within 1-year after ICU discharge for patients in the two groups. We also analyzed the stratified risk of mortality (after ICU admission) within 1 year.

The calculation of ventilator free days (VFDs) was as follows [9]: VFDs = 0 if subject dies within 28 days of mechanical ventilation; VFDs = 28 − x if successfully liberated from ventilation x days after initiation; VFDs = 0 if the subject is mechanically ventilated for > 28 days.

The hazard ratio of ICU mortality was compared between CMV DNA level groups, with adjustments of age, WBC count, SOFA score, PO2/FiO2 and GvHD.

Statistical analysis

Normality tests of continuous variables in the study were performed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, normally distributed continuous variables were recorded as mean ± standard deviation, non-normally distributed continuous variables were recorded as median (interquartile range). Categorical variables were recorded as numbers (percentages). Continuous variables were compared using t-test or Mann–Whitney U test, and categorical variables were compared using chi-square or Fisher's exact test as appropriate.

To compare the impact of different levels of CMV DNA on the prognosis of patients, this study used propensity score matching to minimize the influences of confounding factors, so that the various baseline variables of patients in the two groups can be balanced. Propensity scores were generated from a logistic regression model that included variables that may be associated with patients’ prognosis and CMV DNA levels (including diagnosis of the malignancy, hemoglobin level, WBC count, BMI, GvHD status, HLA matching before transplantation, SOFA score, post transplantation days). Thus, we did not restrict patient selection for GvHD status, post-transplantation days, even though these traits may influence the risk of CMV reactivation. The matching ratio was 1:1 and the caliper value was 0.02. After matching, the validity of the matching was evaluated by calculating the standardized mean difference (SMD) and P value of each variable.

We performed Cox regression for survival analysis for mortality of patients in the two groups, which were adjusted for characteristics that associated with risks of mortality (including age, WBC count, SOFA score, PO2/FiO2 and GvHD).

Data processing and statistical analysis were performed using R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Austria). The packages used in this study included “Matching” [10] (for propensity score matching), “survminer” [11] (for survival analysis and risk description), “ggplot2” [12] (for graphic drawing). All statistical tests were two-sided, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

Our main finding in this propensity score-matched cohort study of critically ill HSCT recipients with LRTI is that a significant increase in ICU and 1-year mortality is independently linked to the presence of significant CMV DNAemia (> 103 copies/mL) at ICU admission. This discovery establishes high plasma CMV DNA level as a strong and clinically significant predictor of unfavorable outcomes in this high-risk group. However, this association did not extend to secondary outcomes, as we observed no significant difference in ventilator-free days or ICU length of stay.

A large body of research provides compelling evidence for the observed association between CMV reactivation and higher mortality. CMV serostatus and reactivation have been repeatedly found to be independent risk factors for non-relapse and overall mortality after allogeneic HSCT in numerous large-scale cohort studies and meta-analyses [7, 8, 13]. By concentrating on the critical care setting, our study advances this field by showing that the presence of the virus in the blood signals a much worse prognosis even at the time of intensive care unit (ICU) admission for a non-CMV primary diagnosis (LRTI). This implies that CMV's harmful consequences go beyond the possibility of direct tissue invasion. The mechanisms are probably complex and include the virus's strong pro-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects [14, 15]. The host may be unable to mount a strong defense response against other infections as a result of CMV reactivation, which is known to cause endothelial cell dysfunction, systemic inflammatory cascades, and impairment of both innate and adaptive immune responses [14, 16].

A crucial aspect of our discussion is the careful distinction between CMV DNAemia and definitive CMV pneumonia. Only a small proportion of patients received BALF, but CMV was the most frequently identified pathogen. The etiology of the LRTI cannot be determined by our plasma PCR-based study design. Thus, there are at least three non-mutually exclusive explanations for the higher mortality in the CMV-positive group: Either (1) the LRTI was actually undiagnosed CMV pneumonia; (2) CMV functioned as a co-pathogen, aggravating lung damage from bacteria or fungi; or (3) CMV was only a biomarker of severe immunoparesis, identifying a patient population too weak to survive their critical illness, regardless of the cause [17, 18]. Although BAL fluid CMV PCR is the gold standard for diagnosing suspected CMV pneumonia, hemodynamically unstable patients may not always be able to use it on a regular basis [19]. Our results highlight the clinical value of plasma CMV DNA as an easily accessible prognostic tool for prompt risk assessment following intensive care unit admission.

It's interesting to note that there is no correlation between CMV DNAemia and ventilator-free days or length of stay in the intensive care unit. It implies that the deadly effects of CMV might be mediated by raising the risk of later, potentially fatal complications rather than by extending the duration of acute respiratory failure. This is consistent with the knowledge of CMV's indirect effects, which can appear after the acute phase of critical illness. For example, it may cause late-onset neutropenia, precipitate severe GvHD, or facilitate secondary infections that result in death following initial stabilization or even after ICU discharge [16, 20]. Despite having a higher baseline risk of treatment failure overall, patients in the CMV-positive group may have had an equal chance of being weaned from mechanical ventilation.

Our study's strengths include evaluating both short- and long-term outcomes, concentrating on a specific and clinically significant patient population, and the application of propensity score matching. We matched variables, like post-transplantation days, that may relate to CMV DNAemia. The evidence for an independent association between CMV DNAemia and mortality was strengthened by using this statistical technique to balance a wide range of important confounders, such as disease severity (SOFA score), GvHD status, and post-transplantation days.

The results of the current study still need to be comprehended within several limitations. External validity may be limited by its retrospective, single-center design. We cannot rule out the possibility of residual or unmeasured confounding from elements like the particular conditioning regimens, the use of targeted therapies, or—most importantly—the specifics of antiviral prophylaxis and pre-ICU preemptive therapy, even with our strict matching. Particularly, the growing use of letermovir has altered the field of CMV prevention and may have an impact on the traits of patients who develop breakthrough DNAemia [21, 22].

Our results have several research and clinical implications. According to current clinical recommendations, CMV DNAemia in this situation ought to be regarded as a serious warning sign that calls for increased monitoring and possibly a reassessment of the patient's overall risk profile. Given the substantial hematologic and renal toxicity of standard medications [23], it is crucial to clarify that our observational data do not support the routine initiation of preemptive antiviral therapy for all patients with DNAemia in the intensive care unit. It is still unclear if CMV DNAemia is a risk factor that can be treated or if it is a non-causal indicator of host weakness. In order to assess the effectiveness and safety of preemptive anti-CMV therapy in this particular population of critically ill HSCT recipients, a multicenter randomized controlled trial (RCT) is necessary [24].

Conclusion

In HSCT recipients admitted to the ICU with LRTI, CMV DNAemia at admission is an independent predictor of both short-term and long-term mortality.

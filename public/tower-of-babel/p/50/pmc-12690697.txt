PDNO-09 Enhancing elective surgery of SEGA in tuberous sclerosis complex: towards a planned surgical approach in an era of evolving treatments
Authors: Luca Sartori, Mario Moro, Thomas Blauwblomme, Giuseppe Canova
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveSubependymal giant cell astrocytoma (SEGA) is a low-grade glioma associated with tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC) and advancements in diagnostic techniques have facilitated earlier detection in asymptomatic patients. Current guidelines recommend mTOR inhibitors for asymptomatic SEGA, primarily due to surgical complication rates that can reach 50%. However, in our experience, surgery is associated with lower complications, possibly due to careful patient selection. This study aims to evaluate the outcomes of SEGA microsurgery, focusing on tumor control, complication rates and the need for ventriculoperitoneal shunt (VPS) placement. Additionally, we seek to identify predictors of shunt dependence and postoperative complications to define the optimal timing for surgical intervention.MethodsWe conducted a monocentric retrospective case series of children with SEGA evaluated between June 2005 and September 2024. A total of 41 SEGA cases were included, with 31 patients undergoing surgery.ResultsSurgery achieved a gross total resection (GTR) in 64.5% of cases, with a low complication rate of 19.3%. Only 6.4% of patients required postoperative VPS placement. Significant predictors of successful outcomes included tumor volume, absence of hydrocephalus and the use of the interhemispheric transcallosal approach. The meta-analysis confirmed the importance of appropriate patient selection, demonstrating that early surgical intervention, particularly for patients without preoperative hydrocephalus, is crucial for reducing the need for VPS andminimizing postoperative complications. We also propose the Surgical SEGA Score (SSS) as a decision support tool for surgical intervention.ConclusionsCareful selection of patients for elective surgery significantly reduces complications and improves outcomes, establishing surgery as a safe and curative option for managing SEGA in selected TSC patients. Early surgical intervention, especially in patients without preoperative hydrocephalus, may minimize the need for shunt placement and enhance long-term tumor control. The Surgical SEGA Score (SSS) may serve as a valuable tool for identifying candidates for early surgery.

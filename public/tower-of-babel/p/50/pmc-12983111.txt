ACS Materials Au: Announcing the 2025 Rising Stars in Materials Science
Authors: Stephanie L. Brock, Maksym V. Kovalenko, Mary Ann Meador
Published: 2026

As the Editors of ACS Materials Au, we are excited to present the 2025 class of Rising Stars in Materials Science! From the nominations received, we selected these 14 early career researchers (see Table ) to represent the depth, breadth, diversity, and interconnectivity of materials science research being conducted across the globe. Each awardee has contributed a Perspective, Article, Review, or Tutorial, and these are assembled in the ACS Materials Au 2025 Rising Stars in Materials Science Special Issue, along with this Editorial.

Amit Nain

“We develop translational biomaterials engineered to sense, adapt, and regeneratedesigned to be as dynamic and intelligent as the tissues they replace”Dr. Amit Nain is an Assistant Professor in the Kusuma School of Biological Sciences at the Indian Institute of Technology Delhi, India. His group is interested in dynamic and self-powered biomaterials for tissue engineering applications, including 3D/4D-printed hydrogels, piezoresponsive polymer films, and bioactive dressings for chronic wound care and regenerative medicine. He earned his B.Tech in Biotechnology from VIT University, Vellore, India (2017) and his Ph.D. in Nano Science & Technology from the Department of Engineering & System Science, National Tsing Hua University, Taiwan (2021) under the supervision of Prof. Huan-Tsung Chang, where he developed functional nanomaterials for sensing, antimicrobial applications, and wound healing. He subsequently continued as a Postdoctoral Researcher in Prof. Chang’s laboratory at the Department of Chemistry, National Taiwan University (2021) before gaining applied R&D experience at Taiwan Instrument Research Institute (2021–2022). He also served as a DST INSPIRE Faculty in the Department of Materials Engineering at the Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru (2022–2024) with Prof. Kaushik Chatterjee, where he advanced smart biomaterials and 4D fabrication methods. Dr. Nain has published 35 journal articles, book chapters, and conference proceedings, and has been recognized with awards such as the Young Researcher Award from the Asian Polymer Association (2024) and selection as a 2024 Emerging Investigator by the Journal of Materials Chemistry B (Royal Society of Chemistry). He serves as an Early Career Board Member for Biomacromolecules. Lab webpage: https://sites.google.com/view/nanobiomaterials/home.

Manzhang Xu

“We specialize in the CVD synthesis of novel 2D materials and their integration into advanced devices and flexible smart sensors”Dr. Manzhang Xu is an associate professor at the Institute of Flexible Electronics (IFE), Northwestern Polytechnical University (NPU), China. He received his B.S. degree in 2014 and Ph.D. degree in 2020 (Advisor: Prof. Zhiyong Zhang) from Northwest University, China. He studied as a visiting scholar under Prof. Zheng Liu’s group in Nanyang Technological University, Singapore (2018–2019). After receiving his Ph.D. degree, he worked as a postdoctoral fellow (2020–2022) at Northwestern Polytechnical University under the supervision of Prof. Wei Huang and was promoted to Associate Professor in 2022. He is a high-level young talent from Shaanxi province. His research interests are CVD synthesis of novel 2D materials, advanced devices, and their applications in flexible electronics. More information on Dr. Xu and his research can be found here: https://teacher.nwpu.edu.cn/manzhangxu.

“We specialize in the CVD synthesis of novel 2D materials and their integration into advanced devices and flexible smart sensors”

Kyoseung Sim

In our group, recyclable organic electronics and all-soft systems integrate materials, devices, and systems to advance emerging wearable systems and to enable a more sustainable future.Dr. Kyoseung Sim is an Associate Professor in the Department of Chemistry at Ulsan National Institute of Science and Technology (UNIST). Prof. Sim’s group advances sustainable organic and soft electronics, including recyclable organic semiconductors and devices, fully soft and stretchable platforms, and optoelectronic synapses, by integrating materials design with device engineering to realize system level applications. He received his B.S. in Chemistry from Konkuk University in 2008 and his M.S. in Physical Chemistry from Konkuk University in 2010. He earned his Ph.D. in Materials Science and Engineering from the University of Houston in 2018 under Prof. Cunjiang Yu, and then served as a Postdoctoral Fellow with Prof. Yu at the University of Houston from 2018 to 2020. He joined UNIST in 2020 as Assistant professor and has been Associate Professor since March 2024. More information about Dr. Kim and his research can be found at https://kyosunist.weebly.com/.

In our group, recyclable organic electronics and all-soft systems integrate materials, devices, and systems to advance emerging wearable systems and to enable a more sustainable future.

Leiting Zhang

Our research vision is to bridge fundamental understandings of battery components with methodological breakthroughs in the mechanistic assessment of sustainable and smart batteries.Dr. Leiting Zhang is an Assistant Professor and Docent at the Department of Chemistry–Ångström Laboratory, Uppsala University, Sweden. He received his degrees in chemical engineering (BEng ’11, MPhil ’14, and PhD ’18) from The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST), China. His PhD thesis was jointly supervised by Prof. Guohua Chen at the HKUST and Prof. Jean-Marie Tarascon at the Collège de France. He did his first postdoctoral training in operando gas analysis with Prof. Petr Novák and Dr. Sigita Trabesinger at the Paul Scherrer Institute in Switzerland (2018–2021). Then, he moved to Uppsala University, Sweden for a second postdoctoral position with Prof. Kristina Edström and Prof. Erik J. Berg, focusing on sustainable aqueous batteries (2021–2023). Having received several prestigious grants in competition, including a starting grant from the Swedish Research Council (VR), he established his independent research team at the same department in June 2023. His current research interests include mechanistic understanding of next-generation (non)­aqueous batteries, operando battery sensing techniques, and robot-assisted self-driving laboratories. He is an SSF Future Research Leader and a finalist for the inaugural ACS Energy Lectureship (early career). He also received the Göran Gustafsson Prize for Young Researchers (2025) and the Royal Society of Sciences (Uppsala) Bergstedt Prize (2024). Learn more about his team and ongoing research at https://leitingzhang.com.

Our research vision is to bridge fundamental understandings of battery components with methodological breakthroughs in the mechanistic assessment of sustainable and smart batteries.

Learn more about her team and current research: https://yicheun.wixsite.com/lmbi.

Fengwang Li

My research focuses on designing advanced electrocatalytic materials to drive the sustainable production of future chemicals and fuels, paving the way for a circular economyDr. Fengwang Li is a Senior Lecturer in the School of Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering at The University of Sydney and a Flagship Program Lead at the ARC Centre of Excellence for Green Electrochemical Transformation of Carbon Dioxide. His research group focuses on pioneering green electrochemical technologies to create a carbon-net-zero future, spanning CO2 capture and conversion into valuable products and developing sustainable routes for green hydrogen and ammonia. Dr. Li received his Bachelor of Science in Chemistry from Renmin University of China in 2010. He completed his PhD in Chemistry in 2017 at Monash University under the supervision of A/Prof. Jie Zhang and Prof. Douglas R. MacFarlane. Following his PhD, he was a Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Toronto from 2018 to 2020, working with Prof. Edward H. Sargent. His contributions to sustainability and energy have been recognized with several prestigious awards, including the 2025 Australian Academy of Science Le Fèvre Medal, the 2024 Royal Australian Chemical Institute A.M. Bond Medal, and the 2023 Australian Museum Eureka Prize for Outstanding Early Career Researcher. He is also a 2024 Clarivate Highly Cited Researcher and a recipient of a 2024 Australian Research Council Future Fellowship. Learn more about his team and research: www.fengwangli.com.

Matheus A. Tunes

By unravelling fundamental degradation mechanisms that can accelerate materials’ destruction, we are enabling the design of new resilient materials tailored for applications in terrestrial and extraterrestrial conditions.Dr. Matheus A. Tunes has been an Assistant Professor at the Montanuniversität Leoben (Austria) since September 2023, where he leads the [X-MAT] team combining multidisciplinary research at the intersection of physics, chemistry, materials science, and metallurgy. His work focuses on both understanding the fundamental mechanisms driving materials’ deterioration under extreme environments – irradiation, high temperatures, corrosion, hydrogen and hypersonic – and on accelerated discovery of new materials with resilience to extreme environments through advanced synthesis and characterization techniques. He earned his Bachelor of Science in Physics in 2012 from the Institute of Physics of the University of São Paulo (Brazil). He continued at the same university, obtaining a Master of Science in Materials Science and Metallurgical Engineering from the Polytechnic School in 2015, where he investigated new materials for optimized radiation shielding structures in small modular reactors for nuclear submarine propulsion. Prof. Tunes completed his PhD in 2020 under the mentorship of Professor Dr. Stephen E. Donnelly at the MIAMI Facilities, University of Huddersfield (United Kingdom), together with an ASTRO Fellowship at the Oak Ridge National Laboratory (USA, 2017–2018), where his research centered on in situ transmission electron microscopy (TEM) studies on new nuclear materials. Following his doctoral studies, he was awarded a prestigious Director’s Fellowship at Los Alamos National Laboratory (USA, 2021–2023). At LANL, his work centered on the synthesis and discovery of the new ZIP phases – a new family of intermetallic compounds. He also provided contributions to some LANL’s cutting-edge scientific programs such as the nuclear microreactor program for space exploration and the development of new materials for thermonuclear fusion reactors. Prof. Tunes’ current research integrates experimental, theoretical, and computational methods to tackle complex metallurgical challenges to design next-generation materials with improved resilience and functionality in extreme conditions. His research group’s activities and ongoing projects can be found at https://x-mat.unileoben.ac.at.

By unravelling fundamental degradation mechanisms that can accelerate materials’ destruction, we are enabling the design of new resilient materials tailored for applications in terrestrial and extraterrestrial conditions.

Maxx Q. Arguilla

We present a new materials landscapein atomically precise 1D chainsthat bridges complex motifs of molecules and clusters with collective properties of extended lattices.Dr. Maxx Q. Arguilla originates from the Philippines. He obtained his B.S. in Chemistry from the University of the Philippines Diliman, cum Laude, in 2011. After a one-year junior instructor position at UPD, he moved to the US and completed his Ph.D. in Inorganic Chemistry from The Ohio State University with Prof. Joshua E. Goldberger in 2017. His dissertation centered on the electronic, optical, and magnetic properties and applications of new two-dimensional solid-state lattices in the bulk and at the nanoscale. He then moved to MIT as postdoctoral fellow in Prof. Mircea Dinca’s group from late 2017 to early 2020, where he tackled the growth of low-dimensional van der Waals crystals and the evolution of their physical properties as they transform into ultrathin nanowires and on establishing the fundamental anisotropic physical properties of two-dimensional metal–organic frameworks. In July 2020, Professor Arguilla joined the UC Irvine Department of Chemistry as a tenure-track Assistant Professor. His research is on the chemistry and physics of emergent inorganic crystalline materials comprised of weakly bound one-dimensional (1D) chains whose thicknesses are in the subnanometer regime. By controlling their intrachain coordination chemistry and their noncovalent interchain interactions, his group was able to uncover novel classes of 1D materials that display chiral/helical motifs, diverse quantum confined physical states, and strongly stimulus-sensitive propertiesboth in the bulk and approaching the atomic scale. Learn more about his team and research: https://www.maxx-lab.com/.

We present a new materials landscapein atomically precise 1D chainsthat bridges complex motifs of molecules and clusters with collective properties of extended lattices.

Nutifafa Doumon

We demonstrate that inexpensive, environmentally benign phenanthrenequinone can stabilize organic photoactive blend thin films and their solar cells for 200+ hours under heat at open-circuit conditions.Dr. Nutifafa Doumon is a Virginia S. and Philip L. Walker Jr. professor and an assistant professor of Materials Science and Engineering & Engineering Science and Mechanics at The Pennsylvania State University, University Park. His group’s primary research interest focuses on materials for next-generation photovoltaic and optoelectronic technologies. This includes the potential of organic semiconductors and perovskite materials for real-world optoelectronic and photovoltaic applications in indoor and outdoor environments. His focus is on understanding the degradation mechanisms in materials/devices and improving their stability/durability. From 2022 to 2023, he was a research fellow at NREL in Golden, Colorado, where his expertise/skills were instrumental in the early days of the Perovskite PV Accelerator for Commercializing Technologies. He completed his postdoctoral research at INRS-EMT in Varennes, Canada, from 2021 to 2022. He got a PhD in Nanoscience in 2019 under the supervision of Prof. Dr. Jan Anton Koster, an MSc in Nanoscience in 2013, and an honors college MA in Leadership in 2013 from the University of Groningen, after an MSc and a BSc in Physics from AUST-Abuja and the University of Ghana, respectively. Learn more about his team and research: https://doumonlab-semde.matse.psu.edu/.

Cécile Chazot

Combining structural hierarchy with targeted chemical reactivity, my group develops biopolymeric materials in which function is encoded in multiscale organization and activated through molecular transformations.Dr. Cécile Chazot is the Julia Weertman Assistant Professor of Materials Science and Engineering at Northwestern University. She leads the Sustainable Polymer Innovation Laboratory (SPIn Lab), where her research focuses on combining polymer design with advanced materials processing to uncover how molecular architecture governs hierarchical self-assembly and emergent functionality in biopolymers. This includes investigating structure–property–processing relationships in natural polymers, with applications in stimuli-responsive fibers, films, and circular textile systems. She earned her Master’s degree in Materials Science and Engineering from Mines ParisTech in France. In 2022, she completed her Ph.D. in Materials Science and Engineering at MIT in 2022 under the supervision of Prof. A. John Hart before starting her own independent career in January 2023 at Northwestern University. Learn more about her team and research: https://sites.northwestern.edu/spinlabmse/.

Combining structural hierarchy with targeted chemical reactivity, my group develops biopolymeric materials in which function is encoded in multiscale organization and activated through molecular transformations.

Seokhyoung Kim

My research explores innovative doping strategies in chemical vapor deposition to discover new semiconducting and quantum materials, redefining how we engineer functionality at the atomic scale.Dr. Seokhyoung Kim is an Assistant Professor of Chemistry at Michigan State University. His research focuses on discovering novel materials through controlled chemical vapor deposition chemistry for optoelectronic, photonic, and quantum applications. He earned his B.S. in Chemistry from Pohang University of Science and Technology (POSTECH) in 2012 and his Ph.D. in Chemistry from the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill in 2019 under the supervision of Prof. James Cahoon, where he studied the nanophotonic properties of shape-controlled dielectric nanowire superlattices. Following his doctoral work, Dr. Kim was an International Institute for Nanotechnology (IIN) Postdoctoral Fellow at Northwestern University from 2019–2020, working with Prof. Chad Mirkin on the mid-infrared optical resonances of DNA-assembled plasmonic metacrystals. Since beginning his independent career at Michigan State University, Dr. Kim has received a Ralph E. Powe Junior Faculty Enhancement Award from Oak Ridge Associated Universities (ORAU) and currently serves as an Early Career Editorial Board Member for ACS Applied Optical Materials. Learn more about his team and research: https://kim.chemistry.msu.edu.

Elizabeth Elacqua

We seek to uncover new diamond-like high-aspect-ratio polymers with a range of mechanical/optical properties and the potential to access electronic states not featured in traditional polymers/nanomaterials.Dr. Elizabeth Elacqua is the Weinreb Family Early Career Professor of Chemistry at The Pennsylvania State University. She started her independent career in 2017, where her group works at the interface of organic synthesis and polymer chemistry. Her group works on the synthesis of new 1D polymers, such as carbon nanothreads, by way of upcycling aromatic compounds into a new class of materials. More synthetic endeavors in her group concern the synthesis of regio- and sequence-controlled donor–acceptor polymers and polymer-based photoredox catalysts. She obtained her B.S. in Chemistry and Biology from Le Moyne College (Syracuse, NY) in 2006 and her Ph.D. in Chemistry in 2012 from the University of Iowa, working under the guidance of Prof. Len MacGillivray. She then began a postdoctoral fellow position with Professor Marcus Weck at New York University prior to moving to Penn State.

Montaha Anjass

Functional materials enabling green energy generation and sustainable energy storage are essential strategies for a sustainable future.Dr. Montaha Anjass is an assistant professor at the Department of Chemistry, University of Sharjah (UAE) and a research group leader at the Institute of Inorganic Chemistry I, Ulm University (Germany). She received her B.Sc. degree in Chemistry (2011) from Birzeit University, Palestine and her M.Sc. degree in Advanced Materials (2015) from Ulm University, Germany. In 2019, she completed her PhD (supervisors: C. Streb, M. Fichtner, T. Jacob) at Ulm University, Germany. Her postdoctoral training includes work as a Postdoctoral Researcher (2019–2020) at Helmholtz Institute Ulm (HIU), Germany, followed by a Visiting Scholar stay in the group of Prof. Dr. Leticia González (2020) at the University of Vienna (Austria). The fundamental research concept of her research is the synthesis of functional, nanostructured materials based on redox-active molecular metal oxides, the design of novel organic–inorganic hybrid materials, and exploring their use in (photo-) electrocatalysis and electrochemical energy storage systems (batteries and supercapacitors). By doing this, she hopes to strengthen future applications in the energy materials sector and develop concepts for global challenges in chemistry and sustainable energy. Her honors include the Christiane Nüsslein-Volhard Fellowship (2023), the Südwestmetall-Förder Prize (2020), Margarete-von-Wrangell Fellowship (2020), and the Mileva Einstein-Marić Prize (2017).

Functional materials enabling green energy generation and sustainable energy storage are essential strategies for a sustainable future.

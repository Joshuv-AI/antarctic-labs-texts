Exploring the impacts of docetaxel and paclitaxel based neoadjuvant therapies on early postoperative complications in HER2-positive breast cancer patients
Authors: Mehmet Furkan Sagdic, Emre Tunc, Seher Kaya, Mahmut Onur Kulturoglu, Özgen Ahmet Yıldırım, Lutfı Dogan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHER2-positive breast cancer accounts for 15–20% of all breast cancer cases. Anti-HER2–based neoadjuvant therapy combined with taxanes, particularly docetaxel and paclitaxel, constitutes the standard treatment approach. Although these regimens are widely used, most studies have focused on systemic adverse effects, with limited data addressing their impact on postoperative surgical complications. This study aimed to evaluate the association between docetaxel- and paclitaxel-based neoadjuvant regimens and early wound complications in HER2-positive breast cancer patients.MethodWe retrospectively analyzed 139 patients with HER2-positive breast cancer who underwent breast-conserving surgery following neoadjuvant therapy at Ankara Etlik City Hospital between October 2022 and January 2025. Patients were grouped according to the taxane received (docetaxel, n = 95; paclitaxel, n = 44). Demographic, clinicopathologic characteristics, and early postoperative wound complications were compared. Major complications were defined as major wound dehiscence and skin necrosis. Associations between variables and major complications were evaluated using univariate analysis.ResultsMajor complications were significantly more frequent in the docetaxel group compared to the paclitaxel group (9.5% vs. 0%, p = 0.022), while no significant difference was observed in minor complications (p = 0.704). In subgroup analysis excluding patients who underwent axillary lymph node dissection, this association remained significant (11.1% vs. 0%, p = 0.034).ConclusionsDocetaxel-based neoadjuvant therapy was associated with a higher rate of major postoperative wound complications compared with paclitaxel-based regimens in patients with HER2-positive breast cancer. This association persisted after excluding patients who underwent axillary lymph node dissection. These findings should be interpreted with caution due to the retrospective design and limited number of events.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-026-04434-z.

Introduction

Breast cancer represents the most prevalent form of carcinomas among women, with an estimated 2.3 million cases (11.7% of new cancer diagnoses) in 2020 [1]. Notably, 15–20% of breast cancer cases appear to be positive for human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). The advent of agents targeting the HER2 signaling pathway has marked a paradigm shift in the systemic management of HER2-expressing breast cancer [2].

Taxanes are commonly utilized in conjunction with anti-HER2 therapy as part of neoadjuvant treatment for breast cancer [3, 4]. It was previously demonstrated that the combination of docetaxel or paclitaxel with anti-HER2 therapy leads to enhanced survival outcomes in patients with HER2-positive breast cancer [5, 6]. Yet, the use of these agents was reported to be associated with various drug-related side effects. The most prevalent of these encompasses febrile neutropenia, mucositis, and neutropenia in docetaxel and peripheral neuropathy in paclitaxel [5, 6]. Paclitaxel exhibits a reduced toxicity profile and can be better tolerated, making a weekly regimen feasible [7]. It should be noted that the three-week regimen of docetaxel is more convenient for patients. The symptoms associated with peripheral neuropathy that ruins patient comfort due to paclitaxel are not observed in patients receiving docetaxel. Moreover, hematological complications stemming from the use of docetaxel are mostly reversible. Thus, docetaxel remains the preferred agent at our hospital.

Despite their prevalent utilization, there is an apparent paucity of research on postoperative complications of docetaxel and paclitaxel chemotherapy regimens among HER2-positive breast cancer cases. Although the majority of relevant research has focused on the adverse effects of chemotherapeutic agents, no large-scale studies have examined the incidence of postoperative complications. The present study aimed to explore the impacts of paclitaxel and docetaxel chemotherapy regimens on early postoperative complications in HER2-positive breast cancer patients.

Materials & methods

For this study, we recruited HER2-positive and level 2 oncoplastic surgery patients receiving neoadjuvant treatment at Ankara Etlik City Hospital between October 2022 and January 2025. Patients were allocated to two groups by regimen type utilized (docetaxel n = 95 vs. paclitaxel n = 44). We used the hospital database to extract patient characteristics, including chemotherapy regimen, age, histopathology, radiological tumor diameter, residual tumor diameter, T-stage, grade, hormone receptor, Ki-67, and HER2 statuses, ductal in situ (DCIS) presence, Miller-Payne score, axillary lymph node (ALND) positivity before and after chemotherapy, synchronous tumor presence, disease focality, smoking status, BMI, comorbidities, axillary dissection status, specimen volume, and overall, major and minor complications. We excluded mastectomy and implant patients, metastatic patients, and those failing to complete the neoadjuvant treatment. The research ethics committee of Ankara Etlik City Hospital granted ethical approval to our study.

We performed the pathological staging according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer TNM Staging Manual- 8th edition [8]. This study included patients who underwent level II oncoplastic breast-conserving surgery, including donut mastopexy with superior or inferior pedicles, parallelogram mastopexy, upper outer quadrantectomy using the racquet technique, and batwing mastopexy.

A multidisciplinary team comprehensively evaluated patient parameters prior to the initiation of neoadjuvant treatment. The choice of taxane regimen (docetaxel vs. paclitaxel) was not randomized but was determined by the multidisciplinary team based on institutional practice patterns and clinician preference, taking into account patient-related factors such as age, comorbidities, and anticipated treatment tolerability. The treatment plan encompassed the administration of HER2 blockade achieved dually with trastuzumab and pertuzumab and the administration of Adriamycin and cyclophosphamide every 21 days for four cycles. Then, while 95 patients received docetaxel every 21 days for four cycles, 44 received paclitaxel weekly for 12 cycles. The post-treatment cycle included obtaining hemograms and routine blood biochemistry. A 10 mg hydrocortisone premedication was routinely administered to prevent anaphylaxis. All patients were operated on three weeks after the completion of neoadjuvant treatment.

We considered the complications as major and minor. A validated complication grading system was not used; instead, complications were categorized based on management-oriented clinical criteria. Major complications were defined as complications requiring surgical intervention, including wound dehiscence requiring reoperation and skin necrosis resulting in significant tissue loss. Minor complications were defined as those managed conservatively or with minor interventions, such as bedside suturing, aspiration (for seroma), or medical treatment (e.g., antibiotics for infection). Postoperative complications were assessed within 30 days after surgery. The major complications included serious complications (e.g., major wound dehiscence and skin necrosis), while the minor group focused on less severe conditions including infection, hematoma, seroma, and minor wound dehiscence. Skin necrosis requiring surgical intervention or resulting in significant tissue loss was classified as a major complication. Wound dehiscence repaired with simple sutures was regarded as a minor complication, while wounds requiring a revision in the operating room were classified as major wound dehiscence. The collection of serous fluid, resulting in discomfort and tension for patients, was regarded as seromas and subsequently treated with aspiration. Other collections causing hemorrhagic bruising on the skin were evaluated as hematomas. whether confirmed or not in culture, we considered erythema, purulent discharge, increased localized temperature, cellulitis, pain, redness, and tenderness to be wound infection.

Patients with clinically node-negative (cN0) and cN+ disease at baseline and post-neoadjuvant ycN0 disease were recruited for sentinel lymph node biopsy (SLNB). Axillary dissection was performed on patients with ycN0 disease subsequent to neoadjuvant therapy in the event of positive sentinel lymph nodes (SLNs). Similarly, direct axillary dissection was performed on patients with persistent SLN-positivity despite neoadjuvant therapy. Besides, level 2 oncoplastic techniques included racquet technique, inferior pedicle flap, vertical mammaplasty, superior pedicle flap, fusiform mammoplasty, radial mammoplasty, round block, and batwing.

After performing Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Shapiro-Wilk tests to check the normality of distribution, we compared categorical and continuous variables between the groups using the chi-square test and independent samples t-test, respectively. All statistical analyses were performed using Jamovi version 2.3.28. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Given the number of statistical comparisons performed, particularly in Tables 1 and 2, these analyses were considered exploratory, and no formal adjustment for multiple testing was applied. Because only nine major complication events occurred in the cohort, multivariable logistic regression was considered statistically unreliable and was not performed.

Results

We recruited a cohort of 139 patients who underwent breast-conserving surgery (BCS) following neoadjuvant therapy. Patients’ demographic and pathological characteristics are presented in Table 1. Our findings showed a significant difference between the groups by age (48.93 ± 11.23 and 53.22 ± 10.82, respectively; p = 0.022). Grade 3 cancer was dominant in both groups; while 50% of patients in the docetaxel group had Grade 3 cancer, it was recorded in about 60% of the paclitaxel group. Estrogen receptor (ER) positivity was 65.2% and 73.9% in the groups, respectively. After neoadjuvant chemotherapy, 78.3% of the docetaxel group and all paclitaxel patients had a negative clinical nodal status (p = 0.018). Moreover, the axillary dissection rate was significantly higher in the docetaxel group (43.2%) than in the paclitaxel group (13.6%; p = 0.012). The complete response rate to neoadjuvant treatment was about 50% in both groups (56.5% and 52.2%, respectively; p = 0.11).

Table 2 summarizes postoperative early complications. Accordingly, we found that major complications, including major wound dehiscence and skin necrosis, occurred with significantly higher prevalence in the docetaxel group compared to the paclitaxel group (p = 0.022). However, there was no significant difference between the groups by minor complications (p = 0.704). Univariate comparisons indicated that both chemotherapy regimen and axillary dissection status were associated with the occurrence of major complications. Because only nine major complication events occurred in the cohort, multivariable analysis was considered statistically unreliable and was not performed.

To further address the potential confounding effect of axillary lymph node dissection (ALND), a subgroup analysis was performed restricted to patients who did not undergo ALND. In this subgroup, major complications remained significantly higher in the docetaxel group compared to the paclitaxel group (11.1% vs. 0%, p = 0.034) (Table 3).

Discussion

This study represents one of the first investigations evaluating the impact of neoadjuvant docetaxel and paclitaxel therapies on postoperative complications in HER2-positive breast cancer patients and may be considered a preliminary contribution to this field.

HER2 is expressed in 15–20% of all breast cancers [9, 10]. Neoadjuvant treatments, comprising chemotherapy, endocrine therapy, and/or targeted therapy, were previously shown to facilitate disease downstaging, thereby ensuring resectability in unresectable patients, and reducing the extent of breast and axillary surgery [11, 12]. Neoadjuvant docetaxel and paclitaxel regimens have become the mainstays of treatment of HER2-positive breast cancer.

A substantial body of research demonstrated the synergistic effect of trastuzumab in combination with taxanes [13–15] Thus, paclitaxel or docetaxel are employed in conjunction with anti-HER2 agents. However, there are also challenges associated with the tolerability of neoadjuvant treatment in HER2-positive cases. Docetaxel is administered every three weeks due to its significant toxicity, whereas paclitaxel-based regimens can be prescribed weekly due to its minimal toxicity. Recent research has focused more on the systemic complications of these two groups of treatments. In addition, substantial evidence confirmed that neoadjuvant chemotherapeutics are ineffective in preventing postoperative complications [16–19], which may likely overshadow the examination of the effects of regimen-specific neoadjuvant chemotherapeutics on wound complications. The hypothesis that these two regimens will also influence cellular wound healing is not implausible. For instance, docetaxel was previously associated with a higher incidence of febrile and severe neutropenia compared to paclitaxel [20, 21]. Conversely, paclitaxel was linked to a higher rate of peripheral sensory neuropathy; however, its reduced hematologic toxicity makes paclitaxel-based regimens more tolerable and a preferable alternative [22, 23]. It should be noted that docetaxel was associated with a higher incidence of allergic reactions and elevated liver enzymes compared to paclitaxel [24, 25]. Neutropenia is the primary dose-limiting toxicity of docetaxel, resulting from concentration-dependent inhibition of neutrophil precursor proliferation, and may occasionally become life-threatening [26]. Neutrophils are the first responders in the early phase of wound healing, and delayed healing has been well documented in the presence of neutropenia [27, 28].In addition, as a microtubule inhibitor, docetaxel disrupts cell cycle progression, induces cell death, and impairs the mobilization of neutrophil reserves from the bone marrow [29]. At the cellular level, it inhibits endothelial cell migration by disrupting cytoskeletal organization and actin dynamics [30]. Furthermore, docetaxel suppresses angiogenesis by impairing microtubule organizing center reorientation and inhibiting fibroblast growth factor-2 (FGF-2)–mediated responses [31]. It has also been shown to reduce the expression of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), matrix metalloproteinase-2 (MMP-2), and matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9) and to decrease gelatinase activity, all of which are critical for neovascularization and granulation tissue formation during the proliferative phase of wound healing [32]. Overall, docetaxel-associated impairment of wound healing is a multifactorial process involving neutropenia-related immune dysfunction, direct inhibition of endothelial cell function and angiogenesis, and suppression of pro-healing growth factors and matrix remodeling pathways. These findings may suggest a more pronounced biological impact of docetaxel on wound healing processes.

A comparison of docetaxel and paclitaxel revealed no significant difference by overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) [33, 34]. Instead, the side effect profiles of both modalities serve as a key reference in the therapeutic decision-making process. Docetaxel is favored in younger patients due to its more manageable side effect profile, while paclitaxel is preferred in older patients [34–37]. A noteworthy finding in our study may be that the docetaxel group had a younger profile compared to the paclitaxel group; the median ages were 46 vs. 52 years, respectively. The relevant literature also hosts research reporting that a three-week administration of docetaxel is favored compared to paclitaxel for OS and DFS in high-risk situations, such as ALND positivity [38, 39]. Similarly, we discovered that axillary dissection rates were higher in the docetaxel group, and more aggressive axillary interventions were applied in the docetaxel group, which may be attributed to the selection of more ALND-positive patients in the docetaxel group for neoadjuvant treatment.

In their study on the outcomes of oncoplastic surgery after neoadjuvant chemotherapy, Adamson et al. found the overall complication rate to be 25% and the rate of major complications to be 9% [17]. In our study, however, we found these rates to be 24% and 9%, respectively, in the docetaxel group. While our findings overlap with the existing literature on overall complications and neoadjuvant docetaxel therapy, it is noteworthy that we discovered no major complications in the paclitaxel group.

It is well-established that both patient-specific (e.g., advanced age, diabetes, obesity, smoking) and technical factors (e.g., large resection volume, incision type, tight closure) influence wound healing [40–42]. In addition to these factors associated with complications. Our findings suggest that neoadjuvant docetaxel treatment may be associated with major complications.

Vigilant monitoring for wound dehiscence is may be considered in patients with a history of docetaxel use. In cases where patients exhibit both patient-specific and technical risk factors, a comparative analysis of docetaxel and paclitaxel in chemotherapy selection can be advantageous. Yet, there is a paucity of research in the literature that compares postoperative complications associated with neoadjuvant docetaxel and paclitaxel treatments. In this sense, the insights from our findings may inform future comprehensive research. Besides, we found complications except seroma to be more prevalent and major complications to be significantly higher in the docetaxel group, which may be due to the more pronounced cellular toxicity of docetaxel and its neutropenic side effect. Univariate comparisons showed that both chemotherapy regimen and axillary dissection status were associated with major complications. To mitigate the potential confounding effect of ALND, a subgroup analysis excluding patients who underwent ALND was performed. Notably, the association between chemotherapy regimen and major complications remained significant in this subgroup, suggesting that the observed effect is not solely attributable to differences in surgical extent. However, residual confounding cannot be excluded, particularly given baseline imbalances within the subgroup. In our cohort, surgery was performed approximately three weeks after the completion of neoadjuvant therapy in accordance with institutional practice. This interval is commonly considered sufficient to allow recovery from chemotherapy-related toxicities, including myelosuppression, and to optimize surgical conditions. Although variations in the timing of surgery may potentially influence wound healing outcomes, the relatively uniform timing in our study likely minimized its confounding effect.

Our findings are limited by the retrospective single-center design and the relatively small number of events. Only nine major complications occurred in the cohort, which limits the robustness of statistical inference. The imbalance in baseline characteristics between the groups likely reflects non-randomized treatment allocation, which may have introduced selection bias. These baseline differences, including age distribution, nodal status, and axillary dissection frequency, may have acted as confounding factors influencing the observed complication rates.Given the limited number of major complication events (n = 9), multivariable analysis was not performed, as it would be statistically unreliable and prone to overfitting. In addition, the higher rate of axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) in the docetaxel group represents a potential confounder. Although a subgroup analysis excluding patients who underwent ALND was performed, residual confounding related to surgical extent and baseline differences cannot be completely excluded. The absence of a standardized complication grading system and the lack of blinded outcome assessment represent limitations of this study and may introduce detection bias. In addition, the study population was derived from a single center and may represent a relatively homogeneous population, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to more diverse populations. These findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

Despite these limitations, our study has several strengths. First, it reflects real-world clinical practice by including all consecutive patients treated within a defined period. Second, it specifically focuses on postoperative surgical complications, an area that remains relatively underexplored in the context of neoadjuvant taxane-based therapy. Third, the use of a relatively homogeneous surgical approach (breast-conserving surgery with oncoplastic techniques) enhances the internal consistency of the findings. Finally, the subgroup analysis excluding patients who underwent axillary lymph node dissection provides additional insight into the potential association between chemotherapy regimen and wound complications.

Conclusions

Docetaxel-based neoadjuvant therapy was associated with a higher rate of major postoperative wound complications compared with paclitaxel-based regimens in patients with HER2-positive breast cancer undergoing breast-conserving surgery. This difference remained after excluding patients who underwent axillary lymph node dissection, suggesting that surgical extent alone may not fully explain the observed association. However, given the retrospective design and the small number of events, these results should be interpreted with caution. In clinical practice, closer postoperative monitoring may be appropriate for patients receiving docetaxel, particularly when additional risk factors are present. Further prospective studies with larger cohorts are required to better define the relationship between taxane selection and surgical outcomes.

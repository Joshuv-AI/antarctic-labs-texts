Transcriptome-based meta-analysis of drought stress regulatory genes in tomato
Authors: Maryam Murtaza, Muhammad Aqeel, Saad Abdulmageed Waheeb, Nazia Rehman, Samra Irum, Hanan Almahasheer, Rawabi Zahed, Reem Nabil Hassan, Sajid Fiaz, Muhammad Uzair, Muhammad Ramzan Khan, Ghulam Muhammad Ali, Mariusz Jaremko
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Plants possess various molecular defense systems to ward off biotic and abiotic stresses and adjust to environmental changes. The RNA-seq technology assists biologists in quickly identifying genes responding to abiotic stresses. Thereby, using meta-transcriptomic data from the GEO NCBI public database, we have attempted to reach a consensus on gene variations. This approach identified a global set of 18 drought-responsive genes by Bonferroni-adjusted proportional test on one sample, p < 0.05, and extensive meta-transcriptome analytics. The annotation of these differentially expressed genes identified the biological process, molecular function, cellular component, KEGG, and plant ontology terms. The terms that were found to be top-enriched (with P and Q adjusted to be < 0.05) were intracellular signal transduction (Solyc04g076810 and Solyc10g076710), ribonuclease P activity (Solyc05g054420), nucleolar ribonuclease P complex (Solyc05g054420), Glycosphingolipid biosynthesis and lateral root apical meristem (Solyc03g005227), respectively. These meta-DEGs description include CBL-interacting protein kinase 8 (biological process), phospholipase C2 (biological process), XH/XS domain-containing protein (molecular function & cellular component), alpha 1,4-glycosyltransferase family protein (KEGG) and Histone superfamily protein (plant ontology). qRT-PCR-based gene expression analyses elaborated the similar trends in gene expression in both local genotypes under drought stress conditions covering the ratio of six to four as per down and upregulated meta-genes, respectively. This could indicate that these genes are part of a putative set of stress-responsive genes that are crucial for survival and adaptation under drought conditions thereby, they may serve as potential targets for future functional studies and breeding applications that will be helpful to enhance tomato production in the future.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-025-07348-2.

Introduction

In the Solanaceae family, which also contains species such as eggplant (Solanum melongena), white moon petunia (Petunia axillaris), common pepper (Capsicum annuum), common tobacco (Nicotiana tabacum), and potato (Solanum tuberosum). Among these, Capsicum annuum is an important crop both economically and research-wise. Tomatoes have been extensively studied for domestication and breeding purposes [1], fruit growth and ripening [2], and defense management in the face of biotic and abiotic challenges [3, 4]. China produces around one-third of all tomatoes worldwide, making it the top producer in the world [5].

About 57,119 genes comprise the tomato genome, which is a valuable resource for research [6]. Despite their critical role, the tomato’s genome has few genome-wide resources since whole genomic sequencing is not available. The analysis of biological data has been made possible by the constantly increasing and emerging area of bioinformatics [7–9]. RNA sequencing and microarrays, two of the most significant developments in bioinformatics, are related to genome-wide transcriptome analytics and allow scientists to concurrently examine hundreds of genes’ expression together with the co-expression partners they share under various circumstances [10–12].

Today, an abundance of transcriptomic data is available online due to amenities like Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI), which allows us to combine various data sets to provide more trustworthy results. Meta-analysis is a useful tool for estimating the differences in gene expression between stress and normal conditions and identifying genes whose products are important molecules in the stress response [13–15]. Statistical approaches can be effective tools for finding candidate genes that are suitable for testing in experiments and utilized in plant breeding programs. Using 23 studies to explore variations in biotic and abiotic stress-regulated gene expression in Solanum lycopersicum together with experimentally specified filtering criteria, a comprehensive examination of microarray data revealed a group of 835 and 1,862 genes that were differentially regulated by BS and AS, respectively [16]. Another study found a meta-analysis of 72 studies discovered genes 2,238 and 2,139 for salinity and drought stress that were shown to be significantly differently expressed under control and stress conditions, respectively. Additionally, in Sorghum under conditions of salinity and drought stress, the 1,835 genes were prevalent [17]. Furthermore, identifying essential gene sets that have been highly conserved over time can be made easier by merging data on gene expression across species. In this regard, B. distachyon, H. vulgare, O. sativa, and Z. mays, which possessed two different genotypes (one tolerant, one sensitive) to drought stress, were used to establish a unique meta-analysis of escalating drought stress across species. This dataset was used to identify key metabolic pathways, common genes, and transcription factor (TF) prediction that are critical for whole plant reaction to elevated drought stress in rice [18]. TF prediction and pathway analyses were used in this study to find 142 genes that were differentially expressed (DEGs) and shared across multiple research and taxa. The functional categories into which the shared genes were divided were mostly related to metabolic activities, regulatory function, and stimulus-response [18]. Similarly, using Arabidopsis as a reference plant, a meta-analysis of nine datasets found 693 and 528 DEGs in drought-tolerant and drought-tolerant plant types, such as C4 and C3 types, respectively [19]. Using a meta-analysis, after evaluating 11 publications, the researcher discovered 561 stressed and control rice samples, 421 and 139 displayed up- and down-regulation, respectively [20]. In contrast, a meta-analysis involving nine datasets identified 961 meta-DEGs related to the stress condition in Penaeus vannamei [21]. Furthermore, a meta-analysis based on three RNASeq experiments found 526 consensus genes for stress- and tolerance-tolerant wheat environments [22]. A meta-analysis of three studies, on the other hand, for heat, cold, drought, and salt stress situations, 17,324, 10,562, 5,510, and 8,653 DEGs were found, respectively. Furthermore, in wheat stressed by heat, cold, drought, and salt, the 2,374 genes were prevalent [17].

The most significant food crop tomato needs a comprehensive transcriptome-based study that uses a meta-analysis of tomato stress response studies to find meta-DEGs, particularly plant ontology-based annotations related to stress mechanisms. We conducted a study that represents the first large-scale comparative transcriptome investigation of its kind employing microarray gene expression data integrated on a platform GEO NCBI database for stress response studies in tomatoes. This meta-analysis aimed to discover DEGs in three chosen datasets and detect meta-DEGs associated with drought stress. Furthermore, we focused on the identification of expression patterns, generating PCA and Heatmap visualizations, and assessing the impact of up- and down-regulating different genes on plant cells under stress. To comprehend how intricately the tomato reacts to drought stress, other analysis techniques like GO, PO, and network analysis were employed. The chromosomal mapping of the discovered meta-DEGs in the stress response was also discussed. We evaluated the expression variation of the top ranked ten meta-DEGs, namely Solyc00g006800.3, Solyc12g009990.2, Solyc04g076810.3, Solyc09g020000.3, Solyc06g076700.1, Solyc10g076710.2, Solyc01g068130.3, Solyc01g105660.3, Solyc05g054420.3 and Solyc11g072470.2 across drought and control states. In light of this, we postulated that certain genes and regulatory systems may be shared and activated to reduce stress and control plant metabolisms. As a result, it might assist the breeding efforts to introduce genetic engineering targets, genes and pathways that may result in drought resilience in tomato cultivars.

Materials and methods

Plant material and the impact of drought

The Vegetable Research Institute of the Ayub Agriculture Research Institute (AARI), Faisalabad, provided the seeds of the Solanum lycopersicum varieties Roma and Riogrande. The National Institute for Genomics and Advanced Biotechnology (NIGAB), NARC, Islamabad Maintained the tomato plants in triplicate for each control and drought state in a glasshouse. Plants were cultivated in controlled environments with a 50% relative humidity and a 12-hour light/dark photoperiod. When plants reached the fifth true leaf stage, they were transplanted. The experimental treatments were applied one week after transplanting. Plants were divided into two groups: the control group was irrigated to 70% of maximum water retention capacity, maintaining optimal soil humidity. While, in the drought group, withholding water caused stress on the plants, and they were re-watered when half of the treated plants began to wilt.

Collecting data and meta-analysis

Meta-analysis of genome-wide transcriptome data from the GEO databases [23] as data sources utilized were GSE22304 (without heat-stressed samples), GSE39894 (without samples of S. pimpinellifolium), and GSE139290. The high-quality expression counts were accessed from the database; while removing the batch effects from them, the datasets were prepared for the further meta-analyses. For Solanum lycopersicum, the expression profiles of genes linked to drought were examined under both control and stress (water scarcity) circumstances (Fig. 1). From the GEO NCBI database, 177 microarray experiments were found using the search term “drought stress in tomato”. The getGEO() function was utilized to download the expression profiles in order to detect alterations in the patterns of gene expression [24]. With the constraint gset = 0, 161 datasets with no expression data were eliminated from the analysis as empty experiments. Globally, a collection of 18 genes responsive to drought was identified using a single sample proportional test using large-scale meta-transcriptome analytics (Bonferroni adjusted p < 0.05). Analyzing differential expressions

Microarray data analysis has provided bioinformatics researchers with an extensive range of information. Sharing data amongst all probes, for example, can improve the capacity to identify distinct expressions and lessen false positives. A technique like this is limma [25] which employs an empirical Bayes model to regulate the probe-wise variations. In the t- and F-statistic calculations, moderated variances take the place of the probe-wise variances. EdgeR models use an over-dispersed Poisson model to count data in theoretically more complex but conceptually identical processes [26]. They then use an empirical Bayes technique to control the degree of over-dispersion among genes. We expected the data to be displayed as a table of counts, with genes (or tags, exons, or transcripts) represented by rows and samples represented by columns. Exon, transcript, and gene counts can be used for RNA-seq research. With the NB parameterization, we use the mean (µki = Mipkj) and µki (1 + µkiφk) to represent the variance. Pkj stands for the parameters of interest in differential expression analysis. As φk = 0, the Poisson distribution replaces the NB distribution. In some DGE systems, technical variance can be managed as Poisson. The coefficient of variance of biological variation between samples is generally shown by φk. Our method distinguishes between biological and technical variance in this way. Based on the total number of genes, Gene-wise dispersion probability is calculated by using conditional maximum likelihood [26]. The dispersions were minimized to a consensus value using an empirical Bayes method, which efficiently used information from additional genes. Lastly, an exact test was adapted for highly distributed data that was comparable, and each gene differential expression was evaluated using the Fisher exact test.

The GEO database (accessed on March 2024) provided microarray data about drought is processed using R software (version 4.3.2;https://cran.r-project.org/). Drought-stressed and control plants DEGs were found using the R EdgeR function. Following the determination of fold-change (FC) values, the DEGs were further selected using the following cutoff criteria: p < 0.05, |FC| >0, and adjusted P < 0.05. The overlapping DEGs between the three datasets were discovered by MS Excel Professional Plus 2016 modifications (accessed on April 2024).

Analyzing one-sample proportions to identify meta-DEGs

We only used groups that had a limited number of present and absent genes. The presence and absence counts have to be non-negative, meaning they cannot be higher than the correspondingly positive number of microarray experiments. There should be integers for every finite count. For groups with more than one gene present, the R prop.test function was employed [27]. Consequently, the ratios 67 by 100 and 100 by 100 were applied for the second and third presences, respectively. Subsequently, because there were fewer presences in the microarray trials, two presences with a proportion of 67 by 100 were eliminated. Pathway enrichment and functional analysis of the meta-DEGs

ClusterProfiler v4.10.0 is a crucial tool for any functional analysis of high-throughput gene expression patterns, and we employed it for the keywords enrichment analysis and functional annotation of GO [31] with the following parameter values: minGSSize = 10, maxGSSize = 500, qvalueCutoff = 0.2, pvalueCutoff = 0.05, pAdjustMethod = “BH”, TERM2GENE = ref[, c(GO, GENES)] and TERM2NAME = ref[, c(GO, NAMES)]. The clusterProfiler details were then imported into R v 4.3.2’s multienrichjam v0.0.78.900. To demonstrate the functional expansion of the top 18 meta-DEGs, TKPLOT and IGRAPH were used. We used IGRAPH and the clusterProfiler v4.10.0 programs to conduct an integrative analysis in order to fully investigate the meta-DEGs associated with the GO keywords and pathways. ClusterProfiler v4.10.0/multienrichjam v0.0.78.900 was used to the first batch of meta-DEGs using the GEO2R tool in order to extract every Gene Ontology terms (GO) and every trait-related pathway found in the meta-DEG dataset. A functional network that is essentially organized is produced by ClusterProfiler by syndicating GO from the meta-DEG dataset. Furthermore, GO analysis for the meta-DEGs enrichment of molecular/biological function was carried out and P-values of less than 0.05 were considered as significant.

Within the tomato genome, accession numbers were found about protein sequences. PlantRegMap (http://plantregmap.gao-lab.org/) is a database of plant genomes featuring cutting-edge biocomputing techniques (accessed on 10 April 2024). In PlantTFDB v5.0, the BLAST online program was utilized to align the protein sequences to search for stress-associated orthologs that were present in the annotated form for transcription factor analysis.

Annotations to plant ontology (PO)

Every plant’s morphology, anatomy, and developmental stages are explained by a regulated vocabulary (ontology) called Plant Ontology. With the help of phenotype and gene expression data sets from plant genomics and genetics research, the PO hopes to offer a semantic framework that will enable insightful cross-species queries. The need to develop new, more appropriate tomato varieties arises from both global population growth and climatic changes. Crop Ontology (CO), GrainGenes database, and Plant Trait Ontology (TO) are three integrated tools that scientists and plant breeders can benefit from the abundance of genetic and genomic information accessible for plant genomics and crop improvement. The plant reference-level ontology is designated as the PO features used in the tair project. The PO of the most recent TAIR release 11 has around 1,500 plant attributes that are organized into nine groups at a higher level: plant shape, quality, vigor, stress, yield, biological, plant growth, biochemical, and development processes. The Plant Ontology (PO) was incorporated into the TAIR and is utilized for annotating or connecting to data objects (e.g., germplasm, QTLs, genes, and proteins) about plant genomics and genetics from a range of plant taxa, including important global crops and model plant species. Thousands of data pieces linked by over four million annotations in this version can be found in the TAIR database (http://arabidopsis.org). The TO GitHub Issue Tracker is a platform where users can propose new terminology for TO or post comments. Universal enrichment tool enricher was applied on drought genes orthologous to Arabidopsis thaliana to shortlist significant PO terms. For this purpose, we initially prepared a reference PO annotation file along with an input tomato genes list mapped with TAIR IDs. Other parameters setting were minGSSize = 10, maxGSSize = 500, pvalueCutoff = 0.05, pAdjustMethod = “BH”, and qvalueCutoff = 0.2, TERM2GENE = ref[, c(GO, GENES)] and TERM2NAME = ref[, c(GO, NAMES)].

Results

The GEO datasets [33] provided information on the Solanum lycopersicum drought gene expression profiles in both control and stress (water shortage) conditions. There were 177 experiments in the initial sample for tomatoes, of which 16 were (GSE139290, GSE151277, GSE156402, GSE148530, GSE121089, GSE117733, GSE97045, GSE79307, GSE69898, GSE61004, GSE56611, GSE39894, GSE28755, GSE22304, GSE19787, and GSE8161) shortlisted for expression studies. Due to empty expression, seven experiments (GSE28755, GSE56611, GSE117733, GSE121089, GSE148530, GSE151277, and GSE156402) were discarded. Available expression of nine studies (GSE8161, GSE19787, GSE22304, GSE39894, GSE61004, GSE69898, GSE79307, GSE97045 and GSE139290) were screened for available transcriptome data. Based on samples containing control vs. drought as one sample, three experiments (GSE8161, GSE69898, and GSE79307), two due to different species like Solanum tuberosum and Nicotiana (GSE61004 and GSE19787) respectively, and one due to excess water holding for 4 days (GSE97045) were discarded. The remaining three experiments (GSE22304, GSE39894, and GSE139290) were included for the tomato transcriptomic study, containing data obtained using NGS and microarray systems (Supplementary Table S1). The included three experiments GSE22304 [34], GSE39894 [35], and GSE139290 [36] were from India, USA, and Japan, respectively.

GSE22304, GSE39894, and GSE139290 contained a total of 24 samples (12 control, 6 droughts, and 6 heat), 46 samples (12 control, 12 droughts, and 22 S. pimpinellifolium) and 6 samples (3 control, 3 droughts), respectively. Other drought-stressed and species samples such as heat and S. pimpinellifolium were excluded. Of the datasets GSE22304, GSE39894, and GSE139290, they originated from India, USA, and Japan, respectively, and were obtained from the GEO NCBI repository. Regardless of whether the “Analyze with GEO2R” Link is provided or not, the expression availability of all datasets was examined. Fortunately, the expression data for three out of 177 of them was found in GEO NCBI.

Realtime-PCR validation

To validate the expression level of RNA-seq results, ten differentially expressed genes in the three treatment groups were selected for expression analysis. To determine the levels of expression of genes associated with drought in two tomato genotypes, Roma and Riogrande, under drought conditions we used quantitative PCR (Fig. 10). The ΔΔCt method was employed to assess the expression levels, and the results are presented as fold change relative to control conditions. Bar graphs were plotted to visualize the expression differences between the two genotypes. Solyc01g068130.3, Solyc00g006800.3, Solyc12g009990.2, Solyc10g076710.2 were found to be up-regulated and six genes Solyc04g076810.3, Solyc09g020000.3, Solyc06g076700.1, Solyc01g105660.3, Solyc05g054420.3 and Solyc11g072470.2 were observed as down-regulated. The consistencies in regulation were observed, both from DEG analyses applied on datasets retrieved from online database and through RT-PCR validation, in Solyc01g068130.3 as up-regulated, while Solyc04g076810.3, Solyc09g020000.3 and Solyc01g105660.3 were able to downregulate their expression during drought stress. Similarly, Solyc10g076710.2, Solyc05g054420.3, Solyc11g072470.2 and Solyc06g076700.1 genes slightly exhibited consistency as their pattern was up-regulated in Solyc10g076710.2 validated by RT-PCR while two datasets showed similar trend out of three, furthermore Solyc05g054420.3, Solyc11g072470.2 and Solyc06g076700.1 genes were regulated otherwise (supplementary table S3). Conversely, Solyc00g006800.3 and Solyc12g009990.2 genes were up-regulated in RT-PCR based expression studies but were identified as down-regulated in two online DEG analyses. Genes involved in lower expression are key players in stress tolerance, possibly involved in protective functions like osmotic adjustment, reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging, or stress.

Discussion

One of the biggest environmental obstacles to global agriculture is drought, which causes a significant output loss in cereal crops. The world’s population is expected to reach nine billion by 2050 which means that grain yields in regions where drought is anticipated to occur by 2025 must increase by 40% [40]. Biotechnological methods or sophisticated molecular breeding techniques can be used to generate crops with greater drought tolerance [41]. The identification of genes resistant to drought and the analysis of the biological processes involving these genes are necessary before implementing these tactics. Multiple efforts have been undertaken in this regard, with meta-analyses of huge transcriptome datasets being one current strategy [42–44]. The process of determining appropriate breeding program targets is frequently hampered by these analyses, which typically compare numerous experiments about a single species using information gathered from many methods of cultivation, drought treatments, plant stages, tissue analysis, sampling modalities, and timings. Rather, to concentrate on particular drought resistance mechanisms, we chose three datasets about the tomato species that took into account comparable cultivation techniques (soil-grown plants), and drought treatments (extreme stress for a few days) [34–36]. Furthermore, the chosen transcriptome studies were all conducted on two contrasting groups comprising control and drought samples because it is essential to compare groups exhibiting different stress responses (control and stress) to identify important actors in the drought response. The three datasets’ DEG analyses produced results that were similar to the initial ones [34–36].

The variations in DEG numbers among RNASeq trials could perhaps be attributed to the disparate geographic origins of the four species × 2 treatments, as the stages of the stress response are reflected in the amplitude of the activated transcriptome [45]. However, Jack H. Vossen et al. demonstrated the tomato PLC genes in planta response, identical to Solyc10g076710, family, to C. fulvum infection. The expression of five PLC genes peaked in improper interaction with C. fulvum, as shown by the expression peaks of SlPLC1, SlPLC2, SlPLC3, SlPLC4, and SlPLC5. Additionally, they looked at the possibility that the fungus infection triggers the control of transcription given that SlPLC1 transcript levels were raised in the compatible pathogen contact [46]. According to Zhang et al., SlCIPK7 (Solyc04g076810) primarily takes role in the body’s reaction to dehydration and salt stress. The CIPK protein is also implicated in Auxin transport, the ABA response, the cytokinin signal response, tissue and organ development, high salt response, and the enlarged seed coat phenotypes. The expression of SlCIPK6, SlCIPK7, and SlCIPK8 increased significantly during drought stress, although SlCIPK7 and SlCIPK6 have similar expression patterns and their levels of expression during drought induction are equivalent to those of the control group at the initial stage of drought stress [47].

According to the study, CaSRP1, which is identical to Solyc12g009990, has two roles: it promotes tissue growth and development as well as drought-defensive reactions. Similar to this, SRPs have dual functions in Arabidopsis, contributing to both post-germination development and the plant’s ability to withstand drought. According to reports, Solyc12g009990 homologs may have a cellular role related to drought stress tolerance in non-rubber-producing plants [48, 49]. A key component of the Solyc05g054420 gene-driven RNA-directed DNA methylation (RdDM) pathway [50], is crucial for tomato stress caused by salt and drought [51]. Chaturvedi et al. compared proteins of flower developmental stages and found that a drought-responsive gene involved in photosynthesis [52] encoding Transaldolase in guard cells, Solyc00g006800, was found to be a stress-responsive protein [53]. In the meanwhile, scientists have linked fruit development to epigenetic regulation through histone variant SlHTA3 (Solyc03g005227), which offers a conceptual foundation for comprehending how histone variants control tomato fruit quality [54].

Bell-like Homeodomain 10 (BLH10) encoded by Solyc06g074120 were expressed in pericarps, displaying similar expression patterns. It was discovered that the BLH10 protein interacted with GI and was closely connected to BLH3. It has been revealed that BLH3, BLH10, and GI play a part in how plants respond to particular light conditions during flowering and de-etiolation [55, 56]. The cell proliferation driven by the Solyc10g083510 gene is associated with miRNA miR396b and was differentially expressed (FC ≈ − 16.5) between drought and control libraries. The expression levels of miR396a decreased in root, but there were no discernible changes in miR396b expression [57]. The At2g32280 homolog of the Solyc12g005700 gene, which codes for a protein having four putative transmembrane domains and contains the Domain of Unknown Function1218 (DUF1218), was examined in reports. The At2g32280 gene was named because At2g32280 knockout mutants exhibit aberrant vascular networks in cotyledons. Proper embryonic provasculature growth necessitates VCC (VASCULATURE COMPLEXITY AND CONNECTIVITY) [58].

A flavoprotein called wrbA, which is translated by Solyc02g079750, is a promising candidate to investigate the relationship between tomato fruit maturity and drought. Likewise, flavoprotein wrbA is a strong contender since Arabidopsis has demonstrated the function of flavoprotein in osmotic stress tolerance [59, 60]. The modulation of the jasmonic acid (JA) signaling pathway, which is programmed by Solyc01g105660 and adjusted by DMR6-LIKE OXYGENASE 2-like (LOC101260084), is involved in cell death and stress response [61]. The amounts of quercetin and kaempferol in tomato plants influence their ability to withstand viral infection, which is controlled by the glycosyltransferase protein, which is encoded by Solyc02g078590 [62]. In tobacco plants, the translation of the phosphatidylinositol synthase gene by Solyc03g118150 and the overexpression of one of its homologs alter the composition of membrane lipids and strengthen the plant’s tolerance to drought stress [63]. Plant development and responses to different stresses are significantly influenced by the proteins known as the lateral organ boundaries (LOB) domain (LBD) peculiar to plants, which are translated by the Solyc11g072470 family of transcription factors [64]. It has been demonstrated that, like the OPS gene, root protophloem growth is aided by a DUF740 domain that contains the OCTOPUS-like (OPS-like) gene, which Solyc06g076700 translates, and its comparable homolog from Arabidopsis, At2g38070, which encodes for the OPS2 gene [65].

It was proposed in one study that the putative transcription factor IAA26, which is similar to the Solyc09g020000 gene, is vital for photosynthesis during the development of fruits. Additionally, the high-pigmented fruits mutant showed suppression of this AP2-EREBP gene, which is associated with the photosynthesis protein [66]. StCDPK3, a potato gene that may be a homolog of Solyc01g068130, is said to be drought-stress-responsive [67]. The ortholog of the Solyc11g012790 gene, chloroplastic Sect. 14-like proteins, may have a conserved and crucial function in plant growth control that is related to the uptake and efficient use of Pi in response to Pi availability. Consequently, it’s important to emphasize that producing crops with better development under low Pi settings can also benefit from the use of chloroplastic Sect. 14-like proteins [68].

This research exploited three openly accessible gene expression datasets from GEO NCBI to detect meta-DEGs functionally linked with water scarcity in Solanum lycopersicum. By analyzing results, 18 meta-DEGs were identified as top ranked in terms of consistent regulation in response to drought stress, hence boasting their reliability. Sequence alignment search i.e., BLAST analysis successfully confirmed their homology, chromosomal mapping and genomic territories, while key stress response pathways including signal transduction, metabolic regulation, and gene silencing were identified with functional annotation prediction via Arabidopsis thaliana homology analyses. GO and Plant Ontology enrichment highlighted processes like lignin biosynthesis and lateral root development underscoring adaptive responses to drought. RT-qPCR validation in Roma and Riogrande genotypes confirmed expression trends, with genes such as Solyc10g076710.2 and Solyc01g068130.3 upregulated, suggesting their role in osmotic balance, ROS detoxification, and signaling. These findings suggest that the identified meta-DEGs form a putative drought-responsive gene network conserved across tomato cultivars.

A deeper understanding of the drought response is important in tomatoes to enhance crop productivity. The accurate transcript levels representation using RT-qPCR resulted in significant variations between stress and control samples (Fig. 10). Previously, the Solyc01g068130.3 was found as consistently up regulated in all three drought related experiments, similarly Solyc04g076810.3, Solyc09g020000.3, and Solyc01g105660.3 as downregulated. These four genes from 18 meta-DEGs, when functionally annotated i.e., plant ontology, were found to be responsible for important drought trait associated implications in plant leaf and root. The study highlighted the significance of genetic engineering targets identification and drought tolerance associated with meta-DEGs and proposed that the secret to enhancing tomato plants’ capacity to withstand drought stress may lie in balancing this equilibrium through gene expression, controlled by meta-DEGs explored in this research experiment. Finally, although the meta-analysis method curtailed dataset-specific unfairness, integrating further omics data, for instance metabolomics and proteomics together with ecological and phenomics parameters might provide a comprehensive overview related to drought tolerance in tomato.

Conclusion

In this study, transcriptome data analyses produced drought associated with significant genes observed to be involved in important molecular functions. Their involvement was observed in intracellular signal transduction, pentose-phosphate shunt, response to nitrate, gene silencing by RNA, targeting of SRP-dependent co-translational proteins to membranes, lignin biosynthetic process, lipid catabolic process, etc., indicating that cells undergo signal recognition particle binding during the development of the drought response, which may be related to the formation and resistance development. Some genes were PO enriched in lateral root apical meristem, funicle also has some influence on drought response development. Combined with previous studies, we discovered that there was a substantial difference in the expression levels of eighteen meta-DEGs under drought and control conditions for the development of drought resistance. The greater insight towards drought in tomatoes was required, for this a pilot investigation was carried out to guarantee that transcript levels using RT-qPCR were presented accurately. In general, Solyc01g068130.3 as up-regulated, and Solyc04g076810.3, Solyc09g020000.3, and Solyc01g105660.3 as downregulated were the best reference genes for drought stress in tomato samples also validated by qRT-PCR. The study highlighted the significance of drought tolerance and proposed that the secret to enhancing tomato plants’ capacity to withstand drought stress may lie in balancing this equilibrium through gene expression, controlled by meta-DEGs explored in this research experiment.[69, 70]

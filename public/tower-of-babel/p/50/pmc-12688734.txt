Transcranial ultrasound for treating depression and PTSD: promise or premature adoption?
Authors: Shahbaz Shakil, Mahira Ijaz, Aman Kumar Shah
Published: 2025

To the Editor,

A lot more individuals in the psychiatrists worldwide are thrilled about the recent findings of low-intensity transcranial focused ultrasound (tFUS) as an option for depressive disorders such as PTSD. This non-invasive instrument unlocks up an entirely novel phase in neuroscience by attentively calling profoundly brain areas that are related in anxiety and mental health issues on my own. Although the initial claim is significant, it is essential to keep our thrill in check and ask ourselves a key question: is the push for this form of treatment too quick or too good?

The best thing about tFUS is that it can target parts of the brain below the cortex, like the amygdala, without hurting them. Transcranial magnetic fields can only change how the brain works. tFUS, on the other hand, can change how some parts of the inner brain that help control emotions work. Preliminary studies suggest that this approach is viable, well-accepted, and markedly reduces symptoms of depression and anxiety [1].

Even though the results were good, the dataset is still a bit small. At this point, most of the studies are small and new. They also do not always have the strong, double-blind, sham-controlled designs that are needed to show that the treatment works. The good results so far are a big step in the right direction, but they are not good enough for hospitals to use them all the time. It takes a long time for small test studies to become common in clinical practice. There is no way to make this go faster[2].

We do not know what will happen to the brain if we keep using ultrasound on deep parts of the brain over and over again, but it seems safe for now. Scientists are still trying to figure out how tFUS works as a treatment. If we do not fully understand how a drug works and how safe it is in the long term, we might keep taking it even though we do not know all of its effects[3,4].

Finally, transcranial ultrasound is definitely one of the most interesting new things in therapeutic neuroscience. But it is not time yet to use it in hospitals to help people who are sad or have PTSD. To prove that it works and is safe in the long term, it needs bigger and tougher clinical trials. Then we can put this cool new technology into a drug that will help people without worrying about safety.

Intelligent predictive neural network analysis of stefan blowing impacts on chemical reactive flow of Boger nanofluid with thermophoresis and brownian motion
Authors: Shaaban M. Shaaban, Ahmed Babeker Elhag, S. W. Teklu, Ilyas Khan, Dürdana Rustamova Farkhad, Munawar Abbas, Mustafa Bayram
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study scrutinizes the effect of thermal radiation and Stefan blowing on the chemical reactive flow of Boger nanofluid across a stretched sheet with Darcy Forchheimer medium and heat generation using an intelligent computational framework based on Artifice neural network—Bayesian regularization. Furthermore, Brownian motion and thermophoresis properties have been examined. The suggested model of how Stefan blowing affects the chemical reactive flow of a Boger nanofluid with thermophoresis effects and Brownian motion has useful applications in a number of industrial and engineering operations. In chemical reactors, nano-coating technologies, and polymer processing, this model is essential for improving heat and mass transport processes. While the Boger nanofluid model accurately depicts non-Newtonian behaviour pertinent to biofluids and complex lubricants, Stefan blowing consideration offers insights on evaporation or suction effects. For the purpose of maximizing nanoparticle dispersion in cooling systems, fuel cells, and medicinal devices like targeted drug delivery systems where exact control over particle motion and chemical reactivity is crucial, Brownian motion and thermophoresis are also critical. The velocity profile improves as the Stefan blowing parameter values rise, but the thermal and concentration profiles decrease.

Introduction

The boundary layer velocity of non-Newtonian fluids across the stretchable sheet has shown a lot of promise in recent decades due to its increasing significance and uses in a variety of industries, such as the food, pharmaceutical, and electronics sectors. The Non-Newtonian fluids have wide applications in plastic manufacturing, blade coating, lubrication, biomedical flows, etc. Termed for David V. Boger, who was the main investigator promoting the study of elastic liquids with constant viscosity in the late 1970s, Boger fluids bear his name. According to his 1977 publication, the ideal fluid for experiments His first Boger fluid was a highly elastic constant-viscosity fluid, which is defined as a fluid that is both optically transparent as well as very viscous and elastic at the outside temperature. Experiments on a highly viscous and elastic fluid were conducted primarily to document the fluid’s basic rheometric characteristics. In such a fluid, inertia’s influence would be readily apparent, and shear thinning effects and inertia would not affect experimentation. Abbas et al. [1] use computational methods to study chemically reactive Boger fluid flow over a sheet with LTNE effects and heat production. The behavior of methanol-based mono and HNF (hybrid nanofluids) were investigated by Shah et al. [2] in order to enhance heat transfer along a stretching cylinder. The measurement of locked mode instability resulting from confinement variations were examined by of Peterka et al. [3]. Asgari-Targhi et al. [4] investigate the slow solar wind’s magnetic structure and properties.

Nanofluids are more stable and have higher heat capacities than traditional fluids. These fluids typically provide more effective heating and cooling options for a range of engineering uses, like as manufacturing processes, solar thermal systems, automotive cooling systems, and electronics cooling. Nanofluids are created when different particles and base materials collide; these particles are typically 1–100 nm in size. Typically, these nanoparticles can be metallic (like copper, silver, and gold) or non-metallic (like graphene or carbon nanotubes), and they can be distributed throughout a typical liquid (like water, oil, and ethylene glycol). Abbas et al. [5] analyze a numerical study of bioconvective Marangoni flow within a Cross NFs under convective boundary conditions. The impacts of thermal stratification, variable viscosity, and velocity slip on NF (nanofluids) flow over a spinning disc are inspected by Rafique et al. [6]. Rasool et al. [7] have integrated mass transmission and radiative heat effects into their numerical modeling of MHD nanofluid velocity in a DF (Darcy–Forchheimer) porous medium. Bhatti et al. [8] study the behavior of HNF flow through a pipe using electro-magnetohydrodynamics, highlighting its importance for geothermal energy systems.

The removal of fine particles from gas streams, nuclear reactor safety, and aerosol collection are just a few of the industrial applications where thermophoresis is essential. In a fluid with a temperature gradient, tiny particles suspended in the fluid feel a force that pushes them in the gradient’s direction. The force that a suspended particle experiences due to a temperature gradient is known as thermophoretic force and the velocity of these particles as they move from the hot surface to the cold surface is known as thermophoretic velocity. In 1870, John Tyndall made the first observation of the notion of thermophoretic force and velocity. The random motion of suspended particles in a fluid is described by Brownian motion. Brownian motion of NPs in nanofluids has an important influence on thermal performance at the nanoscale and molecular levels. The magnetohydrodynamics SPF (stagnation point flow) of a Casson Williamson HNF with suction/injection and viscous dissipation is studied by Paul et al. [15] using a stretched cylinder. Examine the impact of heat radiation on the second law analysis of magnetised DF flow of Maxwell nanomaterials with Arrhenius energy effects by Vinodkumar et al. [16]. Thermo-convection-driven Darcy-Forchheimer EMHD Williamson HNF flow with variable temperature distribution based on sodium alginate is studied by Das et al. [17]. Using entropy optimization and a gyrotactic microbe, Vinodkumar et al. [18] examined the CCHFM in magnetohydrodynamics Ree-Eyring nanofluid flow via permeable media. Das et al. [19] use the YOM (Yamada-Ota model) to study the Casson quadra HNF stagnation flow with ohmic heating, a heat source, and Newtonian boundary heating across an exponentially stretched cylinder. In the existence of an activation energy, heat source, and motile microbes, Reddy et al. [20] study the MHD radiative Williamson nanofluid through Darcy Forchheimer medium. The magnetohydrodynamic stagnation point flow of HNF that include different base fluids over an exponentially expanding cylinder was scrutinised by Nath et al. [21]. Using thermal energy and chemical reactions, MS et al. [22] examined entropy production in MHD flow of non-Newtonian nanofluids. Chemical reactions at the surface or close to the surface cause reactive particles in Stefan flow to exchange mass with the surrounding fluid. This process, called Stefan blowing, affects how mass, heat, and momentum are transferred between the particles and the main flow, particularly in gasification and combustion processes. Stefan blowing, in contrast to traditional mass blowing through porous surfaces, is propelled by mass flux induced by reactions. With the help of sophisticated features like variable temperature conductivity, CCHFM, and the Buongiorno nanofluid theory, in this study, we examine the impacts of Stefan blowing in a Maxwell fluid framework. Reddy et al. [25] investigated the effects of SB (Stefan blowing) on HNF flow across a moving thin needle, who brought magnetohydrodynamics and thermal radiation into consideration. Ali et al. [26] investigated how thermal radiation affects bioconvective flow caused by SB across a spinning disk in a porous medium. The thermal radiation and chemical reaction, Lavanya et al. [27] examined ways to reduce entropy production in Carreau NF flow across an inclined plate that has been convectively heated under Stefan blowing conditions. The outcomes of activation energy and SB on the stagnation-point flow of viscous NF over a solid sphere embedded in a permeable medium while taking heat production into account were investigated by Ahmed et al. [28].

This research is motivated primarily by the need to inspect the impacts of Stefan blowing and temperature radiation on the chemical reactive flow of a BNF (Boger nanofluid) over a sheet, the impacts of thermophoresis and Brownian motion. Temperature radiation is a significant component of the thermal transmission system, as is well known. There are frequent industrial and engineering uses for thermal radiation that could be implemented. Our goal in this study is to create various models that reflect the impact of radiative heat transfers on the MHD fluid flow under different conditions. The Bvp4c solver in MATLAB and ANN-based intelligent modelling are then used to mathematically resolve the equations. To help interpret the findings, graphical pictures of the velocity, thermal, concentration profiles are presented and analyzed. Solutaion methodology

There are a number of benefits to using MATLAB’s BVP4C approach to resolve ordinary differential equation BVPs (boundary value problems). It ensures excellent accuracy by offering a reliable and effective method for handling both stiff and non-stiff problems with adaptive mesh refinement. Because it can handle unknown parameters and multipoint boundary conditions, the approach is adaptable to complex physical models. Fluid mechanics, heat and mass transmission, and other engineering uses particularly benefit from its integrated error control and flexibility in defining BCs.

Bayesian regularization of neural networks

The suggested ANN-BR strategy uses the MATLAB neural network environment, which may be accessible through the “nftool” interface. In this scheme, artificial neural networks are trained using backpropagation with Bayesian regularization. To acquire precise approximations, the solution process entails creating a suitable dataset and carrying out ANN-BR training, validation, and testing. This model of the impressions of SB (Stefan blowing) on the chemical reactive flow of a Boger nanofluid with thermophoresis effects and Brownian motion has applications in many engineering and commercial processes. This model is crucial for enhancing mass and thermal transmission processes in chemical reactors, nano-coating technologies, and polymer processing. The Stefan blowing consideration provides information on evaporation or suction effects, even though the Boger nanofluid model faithfully captures non-Newtonian behavior relevant to biofluids and complex lubricants. Since precise control over chemical reactivity and particle motion is necessary for cooling systems, fuel cells, and medical devices like targeted drug delivery systems, Brownian motion as well as thermophoresis are also crucial for optimizing nanoparticle dispersion. Conclusions

Use Brownian motion and thermophoresis impacts to examine how Stefan blowing and temperature radiation affect a BNF (Boger nanofluid) over a stretching sheet. Numerous engineering and business processes can benefit from this model of how SB affects the chemical reactive velocity of a BNF with thermophoresis impacts and Brownian motion. This model is essential for improving chemical reactor mass and heat transfer processes, nano-coating technologies, and polymer processing. The Stefan blowing consideration provides information on evaporation or suction effects, even though the Boger nanofluid model faithfully captures non-Newtonian behavior relevant to biofluids and complex lubricants. To calculate precise approximations for every SB-BNF model scenario, 85% of the data in the ANN-BR framework were randomly selected for training, while 15% and 20% were set aside for validation and testing, respectively. Future work

The Levenberg–Marquardt approach for machine learning analysis of bioconvection flow of NF in the existence of oxytactic microbes has immense potential for use in complex biological, environmental, and engineering systems in the future. This model can be used to effectively anticipate and optimize complex nonlinear bioconvection behavior in nanofluids, which is required for the development of microfluidic devices, bioreactors, and targeted drug delivery systems. The use of the Levenberg–Marquardt-based machine learning approach enables rapid and reliable modeling of flow, heat, and mass transfer processes, lowering experimental costs. Furthermore, the inclusion of oxytactic microorganisms improves its application in bioconvection-driven mixing, biosensing technologies, wastewater treatment, and sustainable energy systems that use microorganism-assisted fluid transport.

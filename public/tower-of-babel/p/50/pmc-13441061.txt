Correlation of renal cell carcinoma grade and presence of parasitic vessels on preoperative computed tomography scans
Authors: Bader H. Alsaikhan, Mohamed Ahmad Alsalem, Mohammed Aloqayli, Musaed Almofdhi, Nawaf Najmi, Ziyad Almutairi, Tariq Alnofaie, Manerh Bin Mosa, Abdullah Mohammed Alkhayal, Ghassan Ibrahim Alhajress
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Parasitic (collateral) vessels observed on contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) have been associated with aggressive tumor behavior in renal cell carcinoma (RCC). However, their correlation with tumor grade remains insufficiently characterized.Aims and Objectives:To evaluate the association between the presence of parasitic vessels on preoperative CT scans and histopathological tumor grade in patients with RCC.Materials and Methods:This retrospective cohort study included 306 patients who underwent nephrectomy for histologically confirmed RCC between January 2016 and December 2024 at a tertiary care center. Preoperative CT scans were independently reviewed for the presence of parasitic vessels. Associations between imaging findings and clinicopathological variables were analyzed using Chi-square and t-tests. Significant variables were entered into multivariate logistic regression analysis.Results:Parasitic vessels were identified in 158 patients (51.6%). Their presence was significantly associated with larger tumor size (>4 cm), higher T stage, lymphovascular invasion, central necrosis, microvascular invasion, rhabdoid features, and higher tumor grade (P < 0.001). Multivariate analysis demonstrated that tumor size >4 cm (AOR 6.920; 95% CI 4.091–11.707), stage 1 and 2 disease, lymphovascular invasion, central necrosis, and grades 1–3 were independent predictors of parasitic vessel presence.Conclusion:Parasitic vessels on preoperative CT are significantly correlated with higher tumor grade and adverse pathological features in RCC. Their identification may serve as a noninvasive imaging biomarker for tumor aggressiveness and assist in preoperative risk stratification.

INTRODUCTION

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is among the most prevalent malignancies of the kidney and represents a significant global health burden. Prognostication and treatment planning critically depend on accurate disease staging and tumor grading. Preoperative imaging, particularly contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT), plays a pivotal role in the surgical evaluation of RCC. Parasitic vessels, also referred to as collateral vessels, represent an aberrant vascular arrangement in which tumors co-opt blood supply from preexisting vessels rather than relying solely on angiogenesis. These vessels may signal aggressive tumor behavior due to their adaptive vascular exploitation. Prior studies have suggested that the presence of parasitic vessels – an atypical vascular supply acquired by the tumor – may correlate with more aggressive pathology.[1] Different RCC subtypes display distinct molecular and cytogenetic characteristics, which in turn may be reflected in histological features such as the presence of parasitic vessels. This study investigates the relationship between the presence of parasitic vessels on CT scans and RCC grade, with the goal of evaluating this imaging finding as a potential marker of tumor aggressiveness. Notably, one study found that transcription factor EB-vascular endothelial growth factor A co-amplified RCC, a distinct subtype of RCC, presents at a higher grade than papillary, clear cell, and chromophobe RCC.[2] This suggests that certain genetic alterations may be associated with a higher tumor grade in RCC.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

This study employed a retrospective cohort design, reviewing preoperative CT scans of patients with histologically confirmed RCC. The study correlated the radiological findings of parasitic vessels with the histopathological grade of RCC obtained from surgical specimens.

Study setting

The study was conducted at King Abdulaziz Medical City – National Guard Hospital (NGHA), Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, a tertiary care center offering comprehensive oncology and radiology services.

Study period

The study was conducted between January 2016 and December 2024. All patients who underwent nephrectomy for RCC during this period at King Abdulaziz Medical City – National Guard Hospital (NGHA), Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, were included.

Study population and sample size

The study population included all patients diagnosed with RCC at NGHA during the study period who underwent preoperative CT scans. A consecutive sampling technique was used, where all eligible patients meeting the criteria were included to avoid the selection bias. The anticipated sample size was estimated by statistical power analysis to detect a clinically significant correlation between RCC grade and the presence of parasitic vessels.

Inclusion criteria

Patients were eligible for inclusion if they had a confirmed diagnosis of RCC established through histopathological evaluation. Only those who underwent preoperative CT imaging were considered, ensuring that imaging data were available for correlation with pathological findings. Furthermore, inclusion required the availability of complete and reliable clinical records, including both pathological and surgical reports, to allow for the accurate analysis and validation of results.

Exclusion criteria

Patients were excluded from the study if their final pathology demonstrated a diagnosis other than RCC, as this would not align with the study’s primary objective. Cases with incomplete or inadequate imaging or clinical records were also excluded, since missing data could compromise the integrity of the analysis. In addition, patients who had undergone prior interventions, such as tumor embolization or other procedures that might alter the tumor’s vascular architecture, were not included in the study to avoid potential confounding effects on the assessment of parasitic vessels.

Study protocol and data collection

Preoperative CT scans were retrieved from the hospital’s radiology archive system (BestCare). Each scan was reviewed independently by a board-certified radiologist to assess the presence of parasitic vessels. Clinical data, including demographic information (age and sex), tumor characteristics (size, location, and presence of metastasis), and histopathological reports, were extracted from the medical records. Tumor grade was determined using the standard histopathological criteria (World Health Organization/International Society of Urological Pathology).

Radiological definition of parasitic vessels

Parasitic vessels were defined as aberrant extrarenal vessels supplying the renal tumor, originating from nonrenal arteries or adjacent perirenal vascular structures rather than the main renal artery branches. These vessels were identified on contrast-enhanced CT, primarily during the arterial phase, where they appeared as tortuous, dilated vascular channels entering the tumor directly from surrounding structures such as the renal capsule, perirenal fat, adrenal region, lumbar arteries, or adjacent visceral vessels.

To be classified as parasitic, vessels had to meet all of the following criteria: (1) clear enhancement during the arterial phase, (2) direct continuity with the tumor surface or intratumoral vascular network, and (3) absence of anatomical continuity with normal segmental or interlobar renal arteries. Capsular vessels supplying normal renal parenchyma and physiologic perirenal vessels without direct tumor supply were not considered parasitic. Representative CT images are shown in Figure 1.

Data management

All patient data were anonymized and stored in a secure, password-protected database accessible only to the research team. Each patient was assigned a unique identifier to maintain confidentiality.

Statistical analysis

The categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages, whereas continuous variables were expressed as means and standard deviations. The Chi-square test was applied to assess the association between the presence of parasitic vessels and categorical clinical or demographic factors. The independent-sample t-test was used for the continuous variables. Multicollinearity among variables included in the multivariate regression model was assessed using the variance inflation factor (VIF) analysis. All variables demonstrated acceptable collinearity, with VIF values <3, indicating no significant multicollinearity. This study was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of King Abdullah International Medical Research Center (KAIMRC) under the protocol number NRC24R/013/01, dated October 1, 2024. Patient confidentiality was maintained throughout the study and no identifiable information was collected. Variables with significant associations were entered into a multivariate logistic regression model to identify the independent predictors of parasitic vessel presence. Odds ratios (ORs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. Analyses were conducted using the SPSS software version 26 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

RESULTS

A total of 306 patients were included in this study. The mean age was 58.2 years, with 54.6% aged 60 years or younger. Males comprised 63.7% of the cohort, while females accounted for 36.3%. The mean body mass index (BMI) was 30.4 kg/m2 and 50.3% of patients were classified as obese. The prevalence of diabetes, hypertension, and hyperlipidemia was 47.1%, 54.6%, and 37.3%, respectively. The mean tumor size was 4.84 cm, with 54.2% of tumors exceeding 4 cm. Tumors were located on the right kidney in 52% of cases, and 28.8% were classified as inferior. Posterior and lateral tumor locations were observed in 2.9% and 5.2% of patients, respectively. Exophytic tumor patterns predominated (55.9%). Clear cell RCC was the most common histological subtype (51%) and negative surgical margins were achieved in 79.4% of cases. Stage 1 disease was most prevalent (52.9%). Lymphovascular invasion, sarcoid features, central necrosis, rhabdoid features, and microvascular invasion were present in 13.7%, 2.6%, 19.6%, 5.2%, and 9.2% of cases, respectively. Grade 2 was the most frequent tumor grade (42.5%). Mean laboratory values for serum creatinine, eGFR, WBC, hemoglobin, and platelet count were 119.4, 79.6, 8.52, 126.5, and 279.4, respectively. The presence of parasitic vessels was significantly associated with larger tumor size (P < 0.001), lateral tumor location (P = 0.012), higher T stage (P < 0.001), lymphovascular invasion (P < 0.001), sarcoid features (P = 0.040), central necrosis (P < 0.001), rhabdoid features (P = 0.001), microvascular invasion (P = 0.001), higher cancer grade (P < 0.001), and increased platelet count (P = 0.025). Conversely, lower hemoglobin values were also associated with an increased risk of parasitic vessels (P = 0.007). No significant associations were found between the presence of parasitic vessels and age, gender, BMI, diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, tumor location (superior/inferior and anterior/posterior), tumor pattern, RCC subtype, surgical margin status, serum creatinine, eGFR, or WBC (all P > 0.05). Multivariate regression analysis identified several independent predictors for the presence of parasitic vessels [Table 2]. Patients with tumors larger than 4 cm had a 6.9-fold increased risk of developing parasitic vessels compared to those with tumors 4 cm or smaller (Adjusted OR [AOR] = 6.920; 95% CI = 4.091–11.707; P < 0.001). Stage 1 and stage 2 tumors were associated with a 9.2-fold (AOR = 9.206; 95% CI = 3.387–25.023; P < 0.001) and 8.87-fold (AOR = 8.867; 95% CI = 4.898–16.051; P < 0.001) increased risk, respectively, compared to patients without T stage. Lymphovascular invasion (AOR = 5.711; 95% CI = 2.432–13.410; P < 0.001), central necrosis (AOR = 4.932; 95% CI = 2.476–9.822; P < 0.001), and microvascular invasion (AOR = 4.932; 95% CI = 1.802–13.498; P < 0.001) were also significant predictors. Regarding tumor grade, grade 1 (AOR = 6.473; 95% CI = 1.718–24.381; P = 0.006), grade 2 (AOR = 9.898; 95% CI = 2.442–40.120; P = 0.001), and grade 3 (AOR = 5.652; 95% CI = 1.547–20.659; P = 0.009) were all associated with increased risk compared to patients without cancer grade. Additionally, higher hemoglobin (AOR = 1.013; 95% CI = 1.001–1.026; P = 0.030) and platelet count (AOR = 0.995–1.000; P = 0.044) were marginally associated with the presence of parasitic vessels.

DISCUSSION

This study highlights a significant association between the presence of parasitic vessels on preoperative CT scans and the aggressiveness of RCC. Parasitic vessels, indicative of a tumor’s ability to exploit preexisting vascular networks, represent a potential biomarker for tumor grade, stage, and overall prognosis.[1234] Our findings suggest that parasitic vessels are more frequently observed in larger tumors (>4 cm), advanced T stages, and high-grade RCCs. These associations underscore the adaptive nature of aggressive tumors, which often circumvent angiogenesis by co-opting existing vasculature.[56] The identification of these vessels on imaging correlated strongly with adverse pathological features, including lymphovascular invasion, central necrosis, microvascular invasion, and rhabdoid features.[78] Because these histopathological markers are themselves predictive of poor outcomes, parasitic vessels may serve as a noninvasive surrogate for such characteristics.[127] The lack of statistical significance for Grade 4 tumors in the multivariate model is likely attributable to the relatively small number of Grade 4 cases, resulting in wider confidence intervals and reduced statistical power. In addition, the strong correlation between Grade 4 disease and other aggressive features (e.g., necrosis and vascular invasion) may have attenuated its independent effect after adjustment. Previous studies have demonstrated the prognostic value of parasitic vessels in predicting the surgical and oncological outcomes. Prior literature has associated parasitic vessels with higher rates of positive surgical margins, intraoperative blood loss, and oncological recurrence; however, these outcomes were not directly assessed in the present study.[91011] These findings align with previous literature showing that vascular invasion and necrosis are hallmarks of biologically aggressive RCC.[78121314] Consequently, preoperative identification of parasitic vessels may enhance surgical planning, signaling the potential for challenging resections, higher blood loss, and the need for more extensive operative strategies.[910151617] Previous studies have suggested that aggressive vascular patterns may be associated with increased surgical complexity and adverse oncological outcomes; however, these outcomes were not directly assessed in the present study. From an oncological perspective, the detection of parasitic vessels could also inform decisions regarding adjuvant therapies. Tumors exhibiting such aggressive vascular features may warrant closer postoperative surveillance and consideration of systemic treatment, particularly in cases where recurrence risk is high.[341418] Moreover, these findings may guide research into novel therapeutic targets, such as antivascular agents designed to disrupt the tumor’s exploitation of preexisting vasculature.[5192021] While this study underscores the potential utility of parasitic vessels as a prognostic marker, certain limitations must be acknowledged. The retrospective design, although strengthened by blinded radiological evaluation, remains subject to selection bias. Furthermore, while our findings highlight strong associations, they do not establish causality. Prospective studies with larger cohorts and standardized imaging protocols are needed to validate these observations and assess their generalizability.[12222324] Integrating advanced imaging modalities, such as functional magnetic resonance imaging or contrast-enhanced ultrasound, may also refine the detection and characterization of parasitic vessels.[9222325] Finally, the interplay between parasitic vessels and RCC subtypes merits attention. Although this study found clear cell RCC to be the subtype most commonly associated with parasitic vessels, the molecular and genetic heterogeneity of RCC suggests that vascular phenotypes may vary across subtypes.[5612] Understanding these subtype-specific patterns will be essential for tailoring imaging strategies and therapeutic approaches. Given the retrospective and observational design, the findings of this study should be interpreted as associations rather than causal or definitive predictive relationships.

CONCLUSIONS

This study demonstrates that parasitic vessels observed on preoperative CT scans are significantly correlated with higher tumor grade, advanced stage, and adverse pathological features in RCC. These vessels, reflecting the tumor’s adaptive vascular strategies, represent a marker of aggressiveness and poor prognosis. Their identification on imaging not only supports preoperative risk stratification but also provides valuable insight into tumor biology. Incorporating the assessment of parasitic vessels into routine radiological practice could enhance surgical planning by identifying high-risk cases and guiding operative strategies. Moreover, these findings support the role of parasitic vessels as a noninvasive biomarker for predicting surgical outcomes, recurrence risk, and the potential need for adjuvant therapy.

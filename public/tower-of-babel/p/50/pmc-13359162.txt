Peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance flurochloridone
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Fernando Álvarez, Maria Arena, Domenica Auteri, Sofia Batista Leite, Amarela Becirovic, Marco Binaglia, Anna Federica Castoldi, Arianna Chiusolo, Angelo Colagiorgi, Mathilde Colas, Federica Crivellente, Chloe De Lentdecker, Isabella De Magistris, Gabriella Fait, Franco Ferilli, German Giner Santonja, Varvara Gouliarmou, Alessio Ippolito, Frederique Istace, Dimitra Kardassi, Aude Kienzler, Anna Lanzoni, Roberto Lava, Renata Leuschner, Alberto Linguadoca, Mariano Lopez Romano, Jochem Louisse, Christopher Lythgo, Iris Mangas, Silvia Mazzega, Andrea Mioč, Ileana Miron, Tunde Molnar, Laura Padovani, Vincenzo Padricello, Martina Panzarea, Juan Manuel Parra Morte, Simone Rizzuto, Miguel Santos
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe conclusions of the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) following the peer review of the initial risk assessments carried out by the competent authorities of the rapporteur Member State Austria and co‐rapporteur Member State Croatia for the pesticide active substance flurochloridone are reported. The context of the peer review was that required by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 844/2012, as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2018/1659. The conclusions were reached on the basis of the evaluation of the representative uses of flurochloridone as a herbicide on potato, sunflower and cotton. The reliable end points, appropriate for use in regulatory risk assessment, are presented. Missing information identified as being required by the regulatory framework is listed. Concerns are identified.Keywords: Article 12 confirmatory data, flurochloridone, herbicide, peer review, pesticide, risk assessment

SUMMARY

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 844/2012, as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2018/1659, lays down the procedure for the renewal of the approval of active substances submitted under Article 14 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. The list of those substances is established in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 686/2012 as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2016/183. Flurochloridone is one of the active substances listed in that Regulation.

In accordance with Article 1 of Regulation (EU) No 844/2012, the rapporteur Member State (RMS), Austria and co‐rapporteur Member State (co‐RMS), Croatia, received an application from ADAMA Agriculture BV for the renewal of approval of the active substance flurochloridone.

An initial evaluation of the dossier on flurochloridone was provided by the RMS in the renewal assessment report (RAR) and subsequently, a peer review of the pesticide risk assessment on the RMS evaluation was conducted by EFSA in accordance with Article 13 of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 844/2012, as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2018/1659. The following conclusions are derived.

The uses of flurochloridone according to the representative uses as a herbicide, with field spray application on potato, sunflower and cotton, as proposed at EU level result in a sufficient herbicidal efficacy against the target annual mono‐ and dicotyledonous weed species.

The assessment of the data package revealed no issues that could not be finalised or that need to be included as critical areas of concern with respect to the identity, physical, chemical and technical properties of the active substance or the representative formulations and analytical methods.

In the area of mammalian toxicology and non‐dietary exposure, the support of the reference specification by toxicological assessment, the assessment of the reliability of key toxicological studies, the assessment of immunotoxicity potential and the impact of potential changes on isomers composition could not be finalised. With regard to negligible exposure assessment according to the draft technical guidance on assessment of negligible exposure, levels of exposure below 10% of the (acute) acceptable operator exposure level ((A)AOEL) could not be reached for the CS formulation for bystanders (child and adult) and residents (child) with available risk mitigation measures (RMMs) and refinement for contact with soil residues. For the EC formulation, exposure estimates below 10% of the (A)AOEL could not be reached for the acute exposure of operators and for bystander and resident children. A margin of exposure above 1000 was not reached for the same groups. Flurochloridone is classified as toxic for reproduction Repr. 1B for both developmental and fertility effects, therefore the substance meets the criteria as laid down in point 3.6.4 of Annex II to Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, leading to a critical area of concern.

In the residue section, several data gaps were identified, leading to the consumer risk assessment not being finalised. A preliminary consumer risk assessment was performed considering primary crops only.

Regarding the environmental fate and behaviour, for all representative uses of the CS formulation, the exposure assessment could not be finalised in all the environmental compartments. Moreover, the formation of trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) as degradation product was not sufficiently investigated resulting in an issue not finalised for all environmental compartments and for the consumer risk assessment considering that the formation of this final metabolite during drinking water treatment processes was not sufficiently investigated.

Regarding the ecotoxicological risk assessment, a critical area of concern was concluded for the long‐term risk to mammals since the available Tier 1 risk assessment indicate a high risk for all representative uses. A high risk for aquatic organisms was indicated for the representative uses of the EC formulation to potatoes and sunflower. A high risk to non‐target arthropods was concluded for all the representative uses of the CS formulation. The risk to soil organisms could not be finalised for any of the representative uses of the CS formulation.

Regarding the assessment of the endocrine disruption (ED) properties, based on the available data and assessments, it can be concluded that flurochoridone meets the ED criteria for the estrogen, androgen and steroidogenesis (EAS)‐modalities for humans and wild mammals as non‐target organisms according to points 3.6.5 and 3.8.2 of Annex II to Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, as amended by Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/605, leading to a critical area of concern.

BACKGROUND

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 844/2012, 1 as amended by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2018/1659, 2 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Regulation’), lays down the provisions for the procedure of the renewal of the approval of active substances, submitted under Article 14 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. 3 This regulates for the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) the procedure for organising the consultation of Member States, the applicant(s) and the public on the initial evaluation provided by the rapporteur Member State (RMS) and/or co‐rapporteur Member State (co‐RMS) in the renewal assessment report (RAR), and the organisation of an expert consultation where appropriate.

In accordance with Article 13 of the Regulation, unless formally informed by the European Commission that a conclusion is not necessary, EFSA is required to adopt a conclusion on whether the active substance can be expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 within 5 months from the end of the period provided for the submission of written comments, subject to an extension of an additional 3 months where additional information is required to be submitted by the applicant(s) in accordance with Article 13(3). Furthermore, in accordance with Article 13(3a), where the information available in the dossier is not sufficient to conclude the assessment on whether the approval criteria for endocrine disruption are met, additional information can be requested to be submitted in a period of minimum 3 months, not exceeding 30 months, depending on the type of information requested.

In accordance with Article 1 of the Regulation, the RMS Austria and co‐RMS Croatia received an application from ADAMA Agriculture BV for the renewal of approval of the active substance flurochloridone. Complying with Article 8 of the Regulation, the RMS checked the completeness of the dossier and informed the applicant, the co‐RMS (Croatia), the European Commission and EFSA about the admissibility.

The RMS provided its initial evaluation of the dossier on flurochloridone in the RAR, which was received by EFSA on 7 August 2022 (Austria, 2022).

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA distributed the RAR to the Member States and the applicant, ADAMA Agriculture BV, for consultation and comments on 23 February 2023. EFSA also provided comments. In addition, EFSA conducted a public consultation on the RAR. EFSA collated and forwarded all comments received to the European Commission on 24 April 2023. At the same time, the collated comments were forwarded to the RMS for compilation and evaluation in the format of reporting table. In addition, the applicant was invited to respond to the comments received. The comments and the applicant's response were evaluated by the RMS in column 3.

The need for expert consultation and the necessity for additional information to be submitted by the applicant in accordance with Article 13(3) of the Regulation were considered in a telephone conference between EFSA and the RMS on 8 March 2024. On the basis of the comments received, the applicant's response to the comments and the RMS's evaluation thereof, it was concluded that additional information should be requested from the applicant, and that EFSA should conduct an expert consultation in the areas of mammalian toxicology, residues, environmental fate and behaviour, and ecotoxicology.

The outcome of the telephone conference, together with EFSA's further consideration of the comments, is reflected in the conclusions set out in column 4 of the reporting table. All points that were identified as unresolved at the end of the comment evaluation phase and which required further consideration, including those issues to be considered in an expert consultation, were compiled by EFSA in the format of an evaluation table.

In addition, in accordance with the provisions of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2018/1659, following a consultation with Member States in the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 147 on Mammalian Toxicology – Ecotoxicology joint session on ED properties (September 2024), the applicant was given the opportunity to submit, within a period of 3 months, additional information to address the approval criteria set out in point 3.6.5 and/or point 3.8.2 of Annex II to Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, as amended by Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/605, 4 and/or documentary evidence demonstrating that flurochloridone may be used such that exposure is negligible, or the conditions for the application of the derogation under Article 4(7) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 are met.

Subsequently, the applicant provided further information aimed at demonstrating that the exposure of humans to flurochloridone was negligible under realistic conditions of use. However, no further data was provided regarding negligible exposure for non‐target organisms in the environment. Nonetheless, flurochloridone has been assessed in accordance with the provisions of negligible exposure to satisfy points 3.6.5 and 3.8.2 of Annex II of Regulation 1107/2009. The applicant did not request a derogation under Article 4(7) of Regulation (EC) 1107/2009 regarding the necessity of flurochloridone to control a serious danger to plant health. A public consultation on the revised RAR on the endocrine disruption and negligible exposure assessments made available after the 3‐month clock stop was conducted between 4 April 2025 and 3 June 2025. All comments received, including from the applicant and Member States, were collated in the format of a reporting table (on the revised RAR on the assessment of the endocrine disrupting properties and negligible exposure assessment).

The conclusions arising from the consideration by EFSA, and as appropriate by the RMS, of the points identified in the evaluation table, together with the outcome of the expert consultation and the written consultation on the assessment of additional information, where these took place, were reported in the final column of the evaluation table.

The toxicity of the metabolite trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) was the subject of a notification falling under Article 56 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 concerning information on potentially harmful or unacceptable effects, submitted on 7 January 2021 to EFSA, the European Commission and Member States by the notifier Bayer of the REACH registration dossier on TFA and the REACH lead registrant and producer of TFA under Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006. 5 The notification concerned adverse developmental effects in rabbits after exposure to the metabolite TFA observed in a development toxicity study. In view of determining the human relevance of these developmental effects specific to rabbits, follow‐up in vitro and in vivo studies were carried out under Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006. Since at the time of conducting the peer review process on the renewal of approval of flurochloridone, these follow‐up investigations were still ongoing, the data submitted as part of this Article 56 notification were not taken into account in this present conclusion.

A final consultation on the conclusions arising from the peer review of the risk assessment took place with Member States via a written procedure in May 2026.

This conclusion report summarises the outcome of the peer review of the risk assessment of the active substance and the formulations for representative uses, evaluated on the basis of the representative uses of flurochloridone as a herbicide, with field spray application on potato, sunflower and cotton, as proposed by the applicant. In accordance with Article 12(2) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, risk mitigation options identified in the RAR and considered during the peer review, if any, are presented in the conclusion.

THE ACTIVE SUBSTANCE AND THE FORMULATIONS FOR REPRESENTATIVE USES

Flurochloridone 7 is an ISO common name for the mixture of two racemates in the following proportions 65%–85% (3RS,4RS)‐3‐chloro‐4‐(chloromethyl)‐1‐[3‐(trifluoromethyl)phenyl]pyrrolidin‐2‐one and 35–15% of the (3RS,4SR)‐isomers (IUPAC).

The formulated products for the evaluation were ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’, a capsule suspension (CS) and ‘AG‐F8‐250 EC’, an emulsifiable concentrate (EC), both containing 250 g/L pure flurochloridone. Information on the content of the relevant impurity(ies) in both formulations is missing (data gap, see Section 10).

The information on the active substance and the formulations for representative uses, including the co‐formulants in these formulations, was considered in the overall assessment during the peer review. For ‘AG‐F8‐250 EC’, none of the co‐formulants is an unacceptable co‐formulant listed in Annex III of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, 8 nor considered as an active substance in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. For ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’ none of the co‐formulants is an unacceptable co‐formulant listed in Annex III of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. In the CS formulation a component of a co‐formulant is a component of a currently approved active substance 9 and three components of co‐formulants are not‐approved active substances 10 under Regulation (EC) 1107/2009 11 , 12 Details on the composition of the formulations cannot be reported in conclusions because of the provisions in Article 63(2)(d) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009; however, this information was fully available and evaluated during the peer review.

A data gap was identified for identity and composition of two co‐formulants in the formulation ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’ (data gap, see Section 10). 13 In addition, a clarification is needed on which co‐formulant out of few alternatives was used in the formulation for representative uses ‘AG‐F8‐250 EC’ (data gap, see Section 10). 14

The representative uses evaluated were field spray pre‐emergence application on potato, sunflower and cotton for control of annual mono‐ and dicotyledonous weed species. Full details of the GAPs can be found in the list of end points in Appendix B.

Data were submitted to conclude that the uses of flurochloridone according to the representative uses proposed at EU level result in a sufficient herbicidal efficacy against the target weeds, following the guidance document SANCO/2012/11251‐rev. 4 (European Commission, 2014b). It is noted that it was reported that the trans‐isomers (3RS,4RS) showed a four‐fold higher efficacy than the cis‐isomers (3RS,4SR), however data to support this statement were missing (data gap, see Section 10).

CONCLUSIONS OF THE EVALUATION

General aspects

With regard to the mammalian toxicity, the toxicological profile of the formulations for representative uses was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 148 on 24–30 September 2024 (see Section 2). Acute toxicity studies were available for both formulations for representative uses ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’ and ‘AG‐F8‐250 EC’. A data gap has been identified for ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’ to clarify the composition of the test material used in the dermal absorption study (data gap, see Section 10). 15 With regard to the co‐formulants contained in ‘AG‐F8‐250 EC’, sufficient toxicological data were available for all components. The experts considered that the available toxicological information did sufficiently address the genotoxicity, and repeated dose toxicity potential of ‘AG‐F8‐250 EC’ over the short‐ and long‐term and no concern was identified. With regard to the co‐formulants contained in ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’, the number of (components of) co‐formulants for which the available toxicological information did not sufficiently address the genotoxicity and/or the repeated dose potential over the short‐ and long‐term is provided in Table 1. IDENTITY, PHYSICAL/CHEMICAL/TECHNICAL PROPERTIES AND METHODS OF ANALYSIS

The following guidance documents were followed in the production of this conclusion: European Commission (2000a, 2000b and 2010).

The proposed specification for flurochloridone is based on batch data from industrial plant production. The proposed minimum purity is 940 g/kg containing 720–740 g/kg trans‐isomers (3RS,4RS) and 220–240 g/kg cis‐isomers (3RS,4SR). Toluene is considered as a relevant impurity; however a maximum content could not be set (see Section 2). It is proposed to maintain the current reference specification. It is noted that the evaluation of the toxicological relevance of the impurities is not finalised (see Section 2) and therefore, a change of the current reference specification and new data such as spectral data, content of the impurities before and after the storage of the formulation and methods for analysis of the additional relevant impurities in the formulation might be required. The batches used in the toxicological assessment do not fully support the reference specification (see Sections 2 and 9.1). The batches used in the ecotoxicity studies were considered to be representative of the reference specification (see Section 5). There was no FAO specification available for flurochloridone.

The main data regarding the identity of flurochloridone and its physical and chemical properties are given in Appendix B. A data gap for logP for metabolite hydroxymethyl (M8) (included in the residue definition for risk assessment) was identified (data gap, see Section 10). A study on vapour pressure was provided by the Applicant but not reported and assessed by the RMS in the RAR, therefore not peer reviewed (formal data gap for the RMS, data gap, see Section 10). In addition, a clarification whether the theoretical estimation on the explosive properties of the technical material, based on the chemical structure, meets the criteria set out in Appendix 6 of the United Nations ‘Recommendations on the Transport of Dangerous Goods, Manual of Tests and Criteria’ is missing (data gap, see Section 10).

Adequate methods are available for the generation of data required for the risk assessment, except for the key toxicological studies (data gap, see Sections 2 and 9.1). Methods of analysis are available for the determination of the active substance and impurities in the technical material. However, information on how the precision of the method for determination of a few impurities in the technical material was measured is missing (data gap, see Section 10). 17 Methods for the determination of the active substance in the representative formulations are available, however additional validation data (linearity) is needed for the method for formulation ‘AG‐F8‐250 EC’ (data gap, see Section 10). Methods for analysis of the relevant impurity toluene in both formulations are missing (data gap, see Section 10).

Flurochloridone residues can be monitored in food and feed of plant origin by QuEChERS using high gas chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (GC–MS/MS) with limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg (sum of isomers) in all commodity groups. The efficiency of the extraction procedures used was not verified but not required, since residues above LOQ in all matrix groups, as a result of the representative uses, were not found. It is noted that the residue definition for monitoring in plants is provisional (see Section 3) and therefore a new monitoring method(s) might be required if other components are to be included in the residue definition. Flurochloridone residues in food of animal origin can be determined by QuEChERS using GC–MS/MS with LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg (sum of isomers) in all animal matrices. Efficiency of the extraction procedure used was not verified but not required, since residues above LOQ in all animal matrices, as a result of the representative uses, are not expected. It is noted that the residue definition for monitoring in animal products is provisional (see Section 3) and therefore a new monitoring method(s) might be required if other components are to be included in the residue definition.

Flurochloridone residues in soil can be monitored by high‐pressure liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC–MS/MS) with LOQ of 0.02 mg/kg (sum of isomers). However, a data gap was identified for a method with a sufficiently low LOQ to cover the effect concentration for the most sensitive non‐target plant (data gap, see Section 10). Appropriate GS‐MS/MS method exists for monitoring of flurochloridone residues in water with a LOQ of 0.05 μg/L (sum of isomers). A validated monitoring method in air is missing (data gap, see Section 10).

QuEChERS using GC–MS/MS can be used for monitoring of flurochloridone in body fluids (plasma and urine) with LOQ of 0.01 mg/L (sum of isomers). Flurochloridone residue in body tissues can be determined by using the monitoring methods for residue in food of animal origin.

MAMMALIAN TOXICITY

The toxicological profile of the active substance flurochloridone and its metabolites was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Teleconferences 148 (September 2024) and 183 (September 2025). The assessment is based on the following guidance documents: European Commission (2003 and 2012); EFSA (2014, 2017a); and the available draft Technical Guidance Document on assessment of negligible exposure (European Commission, 2015).

The original/new reference specification is not fully supported by the toxicological assessment, since the relevance of the impurities has not been fully addressed; a data gap is set on the relevance of the impurities present in the reference specification (issue not finalised, see Sections 1 and 9.1). 18 Toluene is a relevant impurity based on its hazards, 19 its maximum content in the specification remains open since a data gap has been identified to further assess its genotoxic potential. 20

Description and validation data for the analytical methods used in feed, body fluids and tissues, air and any additional matrices used in support of the key toxicity studies were not provided and therefore the assessment of the reliability of the key toxicity studies used to derive health‐based guidance values was not finalised leading to a data gap and issue not finalised (see Sections 2 and 9.1).

Flurochloridone is extensively absorbed (higher than 90%) following oral administration to rats. Metabolism is extensive but no identification of metabolites was conducted in available rat metabolism studies with the parent. No unique or disproportionate human metabolites have been identified in a supportive comparative in vitro metabolism study (microsomes). The residue definition for biomonitoring (body fluids and tissues) is proposed as flurochloridone.

Flurochloridone is of low acute toxicity to rats following dermal or inhalation exposure, it is of moderate acute toxicity by the oral route (Acute Tox. 4 (Oral)),22 it is not an eye or skin irritant and it is a skin sensitiser (Skin Sens. 1).22

Based on its UV–vis spectra, testing for phototoxicity and not for photomutagenicity is required for flurochloridone. Flurochloridone was not phototoxic in the available phototoxicity test (OECD TG 432); however, the reliability of the phototoxicity test was not properly assessed, and a data gap has been identified (data gap, see Section 10).

The target organs of flurochloridone after repeated dose exposure (short‐ and long‐term exposure) are the testis and epididymides in male rats; with dogs, mice, rabbits and monkeys being less sensitive to these effects. The heart, great vessels and haematopoietic system are also potential target organs of flurochloridone in dog. The liver, ovary and adrenals were also target organs of flurochloridone in mouse after long‐term exposure. The relevant no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) after short‐term exposure is 5.4 mg/kg bw per day (3‐month rat study); whereas the relevant NOAEL after long‐term exposure is 3.9 mg/kg bw per day (2‐year rat study).

ECHA Risk Assessment Committee (RAC) and ATP 15 that considered that classification and labelling for germ cell mutagenicity and carcinogenicity is not warranted for flurochloridone. Overall, the opinion of ECHA RAC is line with the peer review conclusion. 21 However, EFSA identified data gaps for further assessment of the reliability of some genotoxicity studies and the need for submission of historical control data (data gap, see Section 10).

Flurochloridone has harmonised classification and labelling as Repro Cat. 1B for both developmental and fertility effects (ATP 15 22 ; ECHA RAC, 2018) leading to a critical area of concern with regard to the approval criteria, point 3.6.4 of Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 (see Section 9.2). The relevant NOAEL for parental, reproductive and offspring toxicity is 2.5 mg/kg bw per day (multigeneration rat study). The relevant maternal and developmental NOAEL is 20 mg/kg bw per day in rat and rabbits (developmental toxicity).

With respect to neurotoxicity, there are not specific neurotoxicity studies; however, they are not required since flurochloridone did not show any potential for neurotoxic effects in the standard regulatory toxicity studies.

An analysis of flurochloridone immunotoxicity endpoints have not been provided by the applicant leading to a data gap and issue not finalised (see Section 9.1).

Health‐based guidance values were established: the acceptable daily intake (ADI) is 0.04 mg/kg bw per day, based on the 2‐year rat study in which a NOAEL of 3.9 mg/kg bw per day was observed. based on reduced testes weight and size with tubular atrophy, interstitial cell hyperplasia, reduced size of epididymides with spermatic degeneration and microtubular hyperplasia observed at a lowest observable adverse effect level (LOAEL) of 15.7 mg/kg bw per day. The standard uncertainty factor (UF) of 100 was applied. This value is the same as in the previous peer review (EFSA, 2010; European Commission, 2011).

The acute reference dose (ARfD) is 0.04 mg/kg bw, based on two developmental toxicity rat studies in which a NOAEL of 20 mg/kg bw per day was identified in the second study. No NOAEL was identified in the first study. The NOAEL was set based on decreased fetal body weight (11%), increased visceral and skeletal malformations (visceral: diaphragmatic hernia, heart/great vessel anomaly; skeletal: exoccipital cervical vertebra defect) and variations. A LOAEL of 25 mg/kg bw per day was set in the first study (lowest dose level tested) and 100 mg/kg bw per day (second study). The increased UF of 500 was applied based on the severity and steep‐dose response curve of the effects. This value is the same as in the previous peer review (EFSA, 2010; European Commission, 2011).

The acceptable operator exposure level (AOEL) and acute AOEL (AAOEL) are 0.04 mg/kg bw per day, with the same basis as for the ARfD with no correction for oral absorption. The AOEL value is the same as during the previous peer review. No AAOEL was set during the previous peer review (EFSA, 2010; European Commission, 2011).

There is no information on the toxicity of specific isomers and potential changes of isomer composition after administration to the test species. The reference values are applicable to the isomer composition as declared in the reference specification. The assessment of the potential changes on the isomeric composition of flurochloridone in mammals, potential differences in toxicity of the isomers, potential risks to workers and residents coming from potential changes on the isomeric composition after application, was not provided leading to a data gap and issue not finalised (see Section 9.1).

Dermal absorption rates of flurochloridone in the CS formulation are 0.8% for the concentrate, 4.6% for the spray dilution 5 g/L, 7.0 and 9.2% for the spray dilutions 3.3 and 2.5 g/L respectively. When capsules are disrupted, dermal absorption values become 1.3, 26, 39 and 52% for the concentrate and the three dilutions. For the EC formulation, the dermal absorption values are 7.5% for the concentrate; and 6.4, 9.7, 12.8% for the same in‐use dilutions.

Exposure scenarios showed that operator, worker, resident and bystander exposure to flurochloridone was below (A)AOEL values (see Appendix B) considering RMMs in some scenarios (see Section 8).

Toxicological assessments have been provided for metabolites M1, M2, M3, M4, M5, M6, M7, M8 and M9 (see Appendix B). 23

With respect to metabolites identified as relevant residues in Section 3, in silico analysis did not show a genotoxic concern for M3, M4, M5 and M9. In silico analysis indicated a genotoxic concern for M1, M2 and M6. Data gaps have been set to further address the genotoxic potential of the metabolites M1, M2, M7 and M8 (data gaps, see Appendix C, Sections 3 and 10).

Considering that flurochloridone contains the C‐CF3 group, trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) may be formed as a metabolite (see Sections 3 and 4). Health‐based guidance values (HBGVs) for TFA have been derived in the peer review of flurtamone (EFSA, 2017b). An ADI of 0.05 mg/kg bw per day (expressed as sodium trifluoroacetate) was set while an ARfD was deemed unnecessary. In addition, more recent scientific developments indicate that aneugenicity considerations (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021) may affect the derivation of health‐based guidance values, but this was not discussed during the peer review of flurochloridone since the EFSA Scientific Committee (2021) was not available at the time of the dossier submission. A CLH proposal for classifying TFA notably as reproductive toxicity category 1B has been submitted to ECHA 24 based on the new developmental toxicity study in the REACH registration dossier for TFA. 25 In addition, in July 2024, EFSA has been mandated by the European Commission to revise the health‐based guidance values on TFA. 26 The outcome of this evaluation is expected by end of July 2026. Finally, in October 2025, EFSA and ECHA received a joint Mandate from the European Commission to consider the fate and behaviour of TFA in soil and water. 27 The outcome of this joint mandate is expected by June 2027.

Flurochloridone met the criteria for reproductive toxicity for both developmental and fertility effects (Repr 1B) and endocrine disruption for EAS‐modalities for humans (Scenario 2b) (see Section 6). A negligible non‐dietary exposure assessment according to the draft EU Commission guidance on negligible exposure (European Commission, 2015) has been performed, including the margin of exposure (MoE) between the relevant critical NOAEL and the actual estimated exposure (see Appendix B). The critical NOAEL is 3.9 mg/kg bw per day for endocrine disruption. For the CS formulation, exposure estimates below 10% of the (A)AOEL could not be reached for bystanders (child and adult) and residents (child) with available RMMs and refinement for contact with soil residues. For the EC formulation, exposure estimates below 10% of the (A)AOEL could not be reached for the acute exposure of operators and for bystander and resident children. A margin of exposure above 1000 was not reached for the same groups.

RESIDUES

The assessment in the residue section is based on the following guidance documents: OECD (2009, 2011), European Commission (2017), JMPR (2004, 2007).

Flurochloridone was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 151 (October 2024).

The active substance has two chiral centres and it is identified as flurochloridone only considering the trans‐isomers (3RS, 4RS) and cis‐isomers (3RS, 4SR) in the ratio 3:1. Information on potential different metabolism of the isomers in plants after administration to the test species was not provided (data gap, see Section 10).

Metabolism studies with sunflower and potato cover the GAP conditions but used only pyrrolidone‐labelled and not phenyl‐labelled flurochloridone. Whilst the study with potato was found to be acceptable, the study with sunflower had many shortcomings (underdosed (0.8 N)), no identification in seeds, only one analytical method used for identification (TLC), not covered by the storage stability data, TFA formation not investigated, very low identification rate in leaves and was considered as supportive only (data gap, see Section 9.1). On the basis of the valid metabolism study in potato the following residue definitions were derived: Risk assessment residue definition restricted to root and tuber crops upon soil treatment: flurochloridone (sum of cis and trans isomers) and residue definition for enforcement: flurochloridone (sum of cis and trans isomers) by default restricted to root and tuber crops. For pulses and oilseeds it is proposed to have by default the same residue definition for enforcement as in root and tuber on a provisional basis in order to support maximum residue level (MRL) in sunflower seeds only. This proposal was supported by the experts (see TC 151) and it is based on the fact that the total radioactive residue (TRR) is low in sunflower seeds, flurochloridone is the major residue in dry and wet leaves and that the residue of flurochloridone were all below the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg in sunflower seed in the submitted residue trials.

Trifluoroacetic acid is included provisionally as a separate residue definition for risk assessment for both crop categories. The inclusion is pending the investigation of the potential formation of TFA (data gap, see Section 9.1).

A sufficient number of residue field trials for the representative uses compliant with the critical GAP and supported by storage stability data and analysing for flurochloridone are available showing residues below the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. However, the residue trials with sunflower will need to be reconsidered once the residue definition for pulses and oilseeds 28 has been established (see data gap above).

Rotational crop metabolism studies with application of one maximum seasonal application rate were provided. The studies were found not to be sufficient due to background radioactivity found in the study with pyrrolidone‐labelled flurochloridone and because in the study with phenyl‐labelled flurochloridone only wheat was investigated and only at two plant back intervals (data gap, see Section 9.1). Further it is noted that the fate of trifluoracetic acid was not investigated (data gap, see Section 9.1) and it was not properly demonstrated that flurochloridone in the CS formulation has the same soil degradation behaviour as the EC formulation (data gap, see Section 4) leading to uncertainties with regard to the PECsoil for this formulation. Residue definitions for rotated crops are provisionally derived as flurochloridone (sum of cis and trans isomers), both for enforcement and risk assessment. Rotational crop field trials (two in NEU and two in SEU) are available with one application rate of 750 mg/ha to bare soil but investigating only one PBI of 180 days and only wheat. Residues of flurochloridone in wheat grain and straw were below the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. The appropriateness of the studies will need to be reconsidered once the residue definitions for rotated crops is proposed (see data gap above). Furthermore, the applied dose rate will need to be reconsidered as it is not demonstrated that flurochloridone applied in the ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’ formulation has the same behaviour in soil as the ‘AG‐F8‐250 EC’ formulation (data gap, see Section 4).

Only primary crops were considered for the dietary burden calculation (see data gap for rotational crop residue definition above) and residues from these uses were below the trigger value for animal metabolism studies. The dietary burden needs to be reconsidered once the metabolism studies for pulses and oilseeds and rotated crops are provided. A guideline compliant metabolism study with phenyl‐labelled flurochloridone in ruminants was submitted. Flurochloridone was found primarily in fat (53% TRR) and also in the liver. The identification rate in the other tissues and in milk was low (less than 20%TRR), and three metabolites (M1 consisting of M1a or M1b, M2 and M3) were reported in minor amounts in milk and tissues. M1a or M1b are found in milk, liver, kidney and fat and M3 in milk above 10% TRR. In silico analysis indicated a potential genotoxicity concern for the metabolite M1a but not for M1b, and the genotoxicity for M3 is inconclusive (see Appendix C). It is suggested to address the genotoxiciy profile of M1a and M3 (data gap, see Section 10). The residue definitions for risk assessment and enforcement for ruminants is proposed provisionally as flurochloridone (cis and trans isomers) by default. The RD is provisional because of open issues related to the genotoxicity assessment of M1a and M3 and the fate of the C‐CF3 moiety in the ruminant metabolism study as well as the low identification rate of the residues.

Sunflower has melliferous capacity and there is indication that flurochloridone is systemic and residues in areal parts can be expected. Residues in honey might need to be addressed once the new metabolism study with pulses/oilseeds will be available (see data gap above).

Given the data gaps mentioned above, the consumer risk assessment is provisional and considers the contribution from the representative uses in primary crops only. Using EFSA PRIMo vs. 3.1 the highest calculated theoretical maximum daily intake (TMDI) diet value is 0.1% of ADI for PT general population and the highest % of ARfD is 4% for potatoes in children.

The consumer risk assessment from the consumption of drinking water is not finalised considering the lack of sufficient information to address the possible formation of TFA in soil, and consequently the leaching of TFA in groundwater and the effect of water treatment processes on the nature of residues of TFA potentially present in surface and groundwater, when surface and groundwater are abstracted for drinking water purposes (see Sections 4 and 9.1).

In the context of the review of the existing maximum residue levels for flurochloridone according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 four data gaps were identified: a metabolism study in cereals; residue trials supporting the authorisations on maize and wheat; four additional trials compliant with the northern outdoor GAP on parsley roots; and a fully validated analytical method for enforcement in all animal commodities. An analytical method for enforcement in all animal commodities has been provided (see Section 1). It is noted that the residue definition for animals is provisional.

A request to assess whether the exposure can be considered negligible under the proposed condition of use was not filed. Flurochloridone meets the criteria for being identified as an endocrine disruptor (see Section 6). EFSA notes that it was not demonstrated that one or more of the representative uses could satisfy the requirements regarding negligible dietary exposure as set out in point 3.6.5 of Annex II to Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, as amended by Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/605.

ENVIRONMENTAL FATE AND BEHAVIOUR

Flurochloridone was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 149 (September 2024).

The active substance has two chiral centres and it is identified as flurochloridone only considering the trans‐isomers (3RS, 4RS) and cis‐isomers (3RS, 4SR) in the ratio 3:1. The environmental fate of both isomers was properly provided for the soil compartment where they appeared to degrade similarly, while the potential different degradation of the isomers in the aquatic environment and on potential change of ratio of enantiomers and diastereomers during the time was not investigated (data gap, see Section 10).

Applicant did not properly demonstrate that flurochloridone applied in the ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’ formulation has the same behaviour as the ‘AG‐F8‐250 EC’ formulation assessed. 29 This information would be needed to support the use of the same endpoints for the exposure assessment of the two formulations in the different environmental compartments, as it was argued by the Applicant. Therefore, the exposure assessment of ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’ in the different environmental compartments could not be concluded for the specific formulation (data gap and issue not finalised, see Section 9.1).

The rates of dissipation and degradation in the environmental matrices investigated were estimated using FOCUS (2006) kinetics guidance. In soil laboratory incubations under aerobic conditions in the dark, flurochloridone exhibited moderate to high persistence, not forming major metabolites (> 10% applied radioactivity (AR)). Metabolite R406639 (max. 4.9% AR) which exhibited low to moderate persistence, was considered triggering further assessment in a conservative approach due to its inclusion in the residue definition for groundwater in the last EU review and its maximum occurrence slightly below the trigger value of 5% AR in two datapoints. In the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Teleconference (TC 149), experts concluded that the degradation rate under aerobic condition of the active substance was pH dependent. 30 Mineralisation of the phenyl and the pyrrolidone 14C radiolabels to carbon dioxide accounted for 2%–31% AR after 120 days. The formation of unextractable residues (not extracted by acetone and only for the phenyl on day 7 by acetonitrile) accounted for 36% after 91 days and for 58% after 120 days, respectively for the phenyl and the pyrrolidone 14C radiolabels. In anaerobic soil incubations, degradation of flurochloridone is comparable to aerobic conditions with the same route and similar rate of degradation forming the metabolite R42819 (max. 6.3% AR still increasing at the study end) that in aerobic condition exhibited moderate persistence, while the soil photolysis of flurochloridone was not a significant route of degradation.

The presence of C‐CF3 group in flurochloridone indicates that TFA may be formed during the degradation of the active substance in environmental compartments. Applicant did not provide an updated soil metabolism scheme with an investigation on the possible formation of TFA to demonstrate that this terminal metabolite is not produced in the soil 31 (data gap and issue not finalised, see Section 9.1).

Flurochloridone exhibited low to medium mobility in soil. Metabolite R406639 exhibited very high mobility in soil, while metabolite R42819 exhibited medium to high soil mobility. In the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Teleconference (TC 149), experts concluded that also the adsorption of flurochloridone was pH dependent. 32

During the same TC 149, experts agreed that the field dissipation study evaluated for the active substance was not considered acceptable, 33 hence a reliable field dissipation study conducted on at least four soils triggered for flurochloridone was not available (data gap, see Section 10).

In laboratory incubations in dark aerobic natural sediment water systems, flurochloridone exhibited low to moderate persistence, forming the major metabolites R406639 (< 5% in water and max. 10.6% in sediment, exhibiting moderate persistence) and R42819 (max. 23% AR in water and max. 47% in sediment, exhibiting high persistence). The unextractable sediment fraction (not extracted by acetone followed by methanol and methanol/water solution) was the major sink for the pyrrolidone and phenyl 14C radiolabels, accounting for 18%–30% AR at study end (100 days). Mineralisation of these radiolabels accounted for 1%–7% AR at the end of the study. The rate of decline of flurochloridone in a laboratory sterile aqueous photolysis experiment with the pyrrolidone label 14C was high relative to that occurred in the aerobic sediment water incubations. Chromatographically resolved components accounting for > 10% AR were R406639 (max. 42% AR) and hydroxymethyl (M8) (max. 12% AR). Moreover, in the aquatic system, the applicant did not provide enough information to demonstrate that metabolite TFA is not formed in surface water and/or sediment compartments 34 (data gap and issue not finalised, see Section 9.1).

The necessary surface water and sediment exposure assessments (predicted environmental concentration (PEC) calculations) for the EC formulation were carried out for the metabolites R42819 and Hydroxymethyl (M8) using the FOCUS (FOCUS, 2001) step 1 and step 2 approach (version 3.2 of the Steps 1–2 in FOCUS calculator). Exposure assessment of metabolite hydroxymethyl (M8) was provided using default values considering the missing experimental endpoints for the aquatic environment. It was not considered a metabolite triggering further assessment for the sediment compartment because of its chemical structure (confirmed by LC/MS in the aqueous photolysis) and for the conclusion reached in the ecotox section (data gap, see Sections 5 and 10). For the metabolite R406639, appropriate step 3 (FOCUS, 2001) calculations were available as well as for the active substance flurochloridone up to step 4. 35 The step 4 calculations appropriately followed the FOCUS (FOCUS, 2007) guidance, with no‐spray drift buffer zones of up to 20 m being implemented for the drainage scenarios (representing a 89.3%–94.4% spray drift reduction), and combined no‐spray buffer zones with vegetated buffer strips of up to 20 m (reducing solute flux in run‐off by 80% and erosion run‐off of mass adsorbed to soil by 95%) implemented for the run‐off scenarios. However, whilst run‐off mitigation is included in the step 4 calculations available, the FOCUS (FOCUS, 2007) report acknowledges that for substances with KFoc < 2000 mL/g (i.e. flurochloridone), the general applicability and effectiveness of run‐off mitigation measures had been less clearly demonstrated in the available scientific literature, than for more strongly adsorbed compounds. Moreover, arithmetically correct PECsw values that have drift mitigation greater than 95% (combining buffer zones and nozzles) and run‐off mitigation greater that 90% (vegetative filter strips) are available in the RAR but have not been relied on for this conclusion (and have not been included in Appendix B), as using them contravenes the relevant FOCUS (2007) guidance.

The necessary groundwater exposure assessments for the EC formulation were appropriately carried out using FOCUS (FOCUS, 2009) scenarios and the models PEARL 4.4.4, PELMO 5.5.3 and MACRO 5.5.4. The potential for groundwater exposure from the representative uses by flurochloridone and its soil metabolites R406639 and R42819 above the parametric drinking water limit of 0.1 μg/L was concluded to be low in geoclimatic situations that are represented by all FOCUS groundwater scenarios. However, concentrations of TFA, if formation is confirmed, would be expected to be significantly higher than 10 μg/L in groundwater for all the representative uses and could be expected to be present in surface water, 36 considering the rate of degradation of flurochloridone, R406639 and R42819, and the TFA endpoints from flufenacet (EFSA, 2024).

The applicant did not provide appropriate information to address the effect of water treatment processes on the nature of the residues that might be present in surface water and groundwater, when surface water or groundwater are abstracted for drinking water. This is particularly relevant for the formation of TFA and has led to the identification of a data gap and results in the consumer risk assessment not being finalised (see Sections 3 and 9.1).

The PEC in soil, surface water, sediment and groundwater covering the representative uses assessed can be found in Appendix B of this conclusion. A key to the wording used to describe the persistence and mobility of the compounds assessed can be found in Appendix D of this conclusion.

ECOTOXICOLOGY

The risk assessment was based on the following documents: European Commission (2002), SETAC (2001), EFSA (2009, 2013) and EFSA PPR Panel (2013).

The ecotoxicological risk assessment was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Teleconferences 147 (September 2024) and 150 (September–October 2024).

Technical flurochloridone consists of a mixture of trans‐ and cis‐isomers in a ratio of 3:1. When endpoints were derived from studies conducted with the technical active substance or with a formulation for representative uses, together with Tier 1 or initial exposure estimates, the available assessments are considered to cover the risks posed by both isomers. This applies to the assessments for birds, mammals, honey bees, non‐target arthropods other than bees and non‐target terrestrial plants. For aquatic and soil organisms, the initial exposure estimates are also expected to be in the same ratio as technical flurochloridone (see Section 4). As such, the available risk assessments were considered to be sufficient.

The batches used in the ecotoxicity studies were considered to be representative of the reference specification.

Considering that flurochloridone contains the C‐CF3 group, TFA has the potential to be present in soil, water, primary and rotational crops (see Sections 3 and 4). The available information does not allow to conclude on the presence of TFA in the residue definition for risk assessment. Consequently, although the ecotoxicological risk assessment for flurochloridone and the identified metabolites could be finalised for the representative uses of ‘AG‐F8‐250 EC’ (as presented in the paragraphs below), such assessment may need to be reconsidered pending the outcome of the data gaps and, accordingly, the residue definition for risk assessment (see Sections 3, 4, 7 and 9.1).

Several toxicity studies were performed with the formulation ‘Racer 25 CS’, which differs from ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’. However, the differences in composition were assessed to be minor; therefore, the use of the toxicity data with ‘Racer 25 CS’ was considered acceptable for the assessment of ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’. 37

Suitable acute and reproductive studies, performed with flurochloridone, were available for birds and mammals. The appropriate endpoint for the reproductive risk assessment for wild mammals was discussed and agreed at the experts meeting. 38

For the dietary route of exposure, a low acute and long‐term risk to birds was indicated at the screening level for all representative uses. A low acute risk was also concluded for mammals using the screening step for all representative uses. However, a high long‐term risk to mammals was indicated at Tier 1 for all representative uses. As no refined risk assessment was available, 39 a high long‐term risk to mammals via dietary exposure was therefore concluded for all representative uses, resulting in a critical area of concern (see Section 9.2).

The risk to birds and mammals via secondary poisoning was also considered. For the representative uses of the formulation ‘AG‐F8‐250 EC’, a low risk to earthworm‐eating birds, fish‐eating birds and fish‐eating mammals was indicated. However, a high long‐term risk to earthworm‐eating mammals was indicated. No refined risk assessment was available and therefore a high risk was concluded. For the representative uses of the formulation ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’, a data gap was identified for a suitable surface water and soil exposure assessment (see Section 4). As a result, a secondary poisoning risk assessment for birds and mammals for the representative uses of ‘AG‐F8‐250 CS’ could not be performed (data gap leading to an issue not finalised, see Section 9.1). Pending the outcome of the data gap identified for the partition coefficient of the surface water metabolite hydroxymethyl (M8) (see Sections 1 and 10), it may be necessary to consider whether a secondary poisoning risk assessment for fish‐eating birds and mammals is required.

A low risk to birds and mammals from the consumption of contaminated water was concluded for all representative uses. With the available study, no metabolites were identified to occur in plants at levels that would trigger a risk assessment for birds and mammals. As such, a quantitative risk assessment was not required. However, pending the outcome of the data requirement identified for additional residue studies (see Section 3), the need for a quantitative assessment for plant metabolites should be reconsidered.

ENDOCRINE DISRUPTION PROPERTIES

The endocrine disrupting (ED) properties of flurochloridone for human and non‐target organisms were discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Experts' Teleconference 147 (September 2024).

The assessment of the endocrine disruption (ED) potential of flurochloridone for humans was carried out according to the ECHA/EFSA ED Guidance (2018). In determining whether flurochloridone interacts with the estrogen, androgen and steroidogenesis (EAS) or thyroid (T) mediated pathways, consideration was given to the number and type of effects induced, as well as the magnitude and pattern of responses observed across studies. Particularly, the conditions under which effects occur were considered, notably whether endocrine‐related responses occurred at dose(s) that also resulted in overt toxicity. Overall, the assessment provides a weight‐of‐evidence analysis using the available evidence in the dataset to determine potential interaction of flurochloridone with the EAS and T signalling pathways.

For the T‐modality, a pattern of T‐mediated adversity was not identified in a sufficiently investigated dataset. Therefore, the ED criteria for humans for the T‐modality are not met (Scenario 1a of the ECHA/EFSA ED Guidance, 2018).

A pattern of EAS‐mediated adversities i.e. histopathological effects in epididymis and testis, decrease epidydimis and testis weight and size, decrease sperm motility and sperm counts and increase sperm abnormalities, was observed in an incomplete dataset. The observed pattern of EAS‐mediated adversities suggests an anti‐androgenic mode of action (MoA). This is substantiated by the evidence of endocrine activity in the OECD TG 441 – Hershberger assay i.e. anti‐androgenic activity.

Based on the available data set and on the MoA analysis, it was concluded that the ED criteria for the EAS‐modalities are met for flurochloridone (Scenario 2b of the ECHA/EFSA ED Guidance, 2018).

In the studies conducted with flurochloridone, the LOAEL, where EAS‐mediated adversities were observed (i.e. reduced weights and size of testes and histopathological findings in testis/epididymides including tubular atrophy, interstitial cell hyperplasia, spermatic degeneration and microtubular hyperplasia) is 15.7 mg/kg bw per day from a 2‐year rat toxicity study. A NOAEL of 3.9 mg/kg bw per day is derived based on these effects.

The outcome of the assessment for humans also applies to wild mammals as non‐target organisms (NTOs) for the EATS‐modalities, i.e. flurochloridone is not considered an endocrine disruptor through the T‐modality, while it is an endocrine disruptor through the EAS‐modalities. The EAS‐mediated adversity (i.e. effects observed in testis, epididymis and sperm parameters) was considered relevant at the level of population 45 since those effects were observed in reproductive organs. In addition, the available mammalian dataset was reconsidered to check whether there were other ecotoxicologically relevant effects potentially related to the same MoA (i.e. anti‐androgen). A decrease in fertility, decrease in litter number and in the number of litters with liveborn pups were also observed demonstrating that the EAS‐mediated adversity, although at organ level, may impair the male reproductive performance and have repercussion on the maintenance of the population.

For non‐target organisms other than mammals, only a fish short‐term reproductive assay (FSTRA, OECD TG 229) was available to sufficiently investigate endocrine activity through the EAS‐modalities. It is noted that FSTRA might not be the most suitable test to assess potential anti‐androgenic (AA) effect as stated in the MoA identified for humans and wild mammals. The available FSTRA showed several deficiencies (e.g. unreliable vitellogenin (VTG) measurements, pre‐exposure spawning not reported, questionable reproductive performance in the controls); thus, a robust conclusion could not be drawn. 46 For the T‐modality, no data were available to conclude on the ED properties of flurochloridone in non‐mammalian species. 47

Regarding the assessment of the ED properties, based on the available data and assessments, it can be concluded that flurochloridone meets the ED criteria for the EAS‐modalities for humans and wild mammals as non‐target organisms according to points 3.6.5 and 3.8.2 of Annex II to Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, as amended by Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/605, leading to a critical area of concern.

In view of the fact that ED is an approval criterion, the applicant provided further information aimed at demonstrating that the exposure of humans to flurochloridone was negligible under realistic conditions of use. However, no further data was provided regarding negligible exposure for non‐target organisms in the environment. Nonetheless flurochloridone has been assessed under the provisions of negligible exposure to satisfy points 3.6.5 and 3.8.2 of Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. Regarding human health, considerations on negligible exposure are reported in Section 2 (mammalian toxicology) and Section 3 (residues). Regarding the environment, it might be considered that exposure was not negligible, as the available PEC in soil, surface water and sediment for all the representative uses assessed are above levels that can be routinely measured. There will be exposure from flurochloridone via food items of non‐target organisms for the representative field uses, as these organisms will enter fields on the same day an application is made.

An issue is also listed as a critical area of concern if the assessment at a higher tier level could not be finalised due to lack of information, and if the assessment performed at the lower tier level does not permit the conclusion that, for at least one of the representative uses, it may be expected that a plant protection product containing the active substance will not have any harmful effect on human or animal health or on groundwater, or any unacceptable influence on the environment.

Comparison of urethane dimethacrylate monomer elution between indirect composites when subjecting to thermocycling
Authors: Nur Amalia Mohd Hanifah, Sherrifah Nur Athirah A-Idid Syed Mohammed Idid, Fatima Ahmed Qaid, Zethy Hanum Binti Mohamed Kassim, Ikmal Hisham Bin Ismail, Lee Wei May
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Context:The use of resin-based computer-aided design and computer-aided manufacturing (CAD/CAM) blocks is increasing in restorative dentistry. However, the potential elution of urethane dimethacrylate (UDMA) into saliva and its associated safety implications remain insufficiently investigated.Aims:To identify and quantify the monomer elution of two different indirect composite blocks before and after thermocycling.Settings and Design:In vitro study.Subjects and Methods:Two composite CAD-CAM blocks were investigated: a resin-based composite block (RCB, Shofu HC Block) and a polymer-infiltrated ceramic network block (PICN, Vita Enamic). Twenty specimens from each material (n = 20) were prepared. All specimens (n = 40) were immersed in artificial saliva at 37°C for 24 h before thermocycling. Extractions were performed after 24 h of immersion and subsequently after 500 and 10,000 cycles of thermocycling. UDMA monomer elution was analyzed using high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC).Statistical Analysis Used:Kruskal–Wallis test.Results:No quantifiable level of UDMA was observed in either RCB or PICN before thermocycling. After 500 cycles of thermocycling, UDMA elution was identified in RCB (0.04 mg/mL), but remained undetectable in PICN. Following 10,000 cycles of thermocycling, both RCB and PICN showed UDMA release of 0.01 mg/mL. However, the Kruskal–Wallis test revealed no statistically significant differences among groups (P > 0.05).Conclusions:The highest UDMA elution occurred in RCB after 500 cycles of thermocycling. Despite differences in polymer structure, thermocycling did not significantly influence UDMA release in either material.

INTRODUCTION

Computer-aided design and computer-aided manufacturing (CAD-CAM) technology has gained popularity in dentistry over the years, owing to the superior esthetics, improved precision, and reduced chair time.[12] One of the latest developments is a resin-based composite CAD-CAM block.

Indirect composite CAD-CAM blocks can be classified based on their composition. These include resin composite blocks (RCB), such as Shofu HC Block (SB, Japan), which consist of dispersed fillers within a dimethacrylate matrix and are polymerized at high temperatures, and polymer-infiltrated ceramic network (PICN) materials, such as Vita Enamic (VE, Germany), which are made from a presintered ceramic network that is subsequently infiltrated with triethylene glycol dimethacrylate (TEGDMA) and urethane dimethacrylate (UDMA).[3] Polymers containing UDMA significantly absorb more water, leading to hydraulic degradation of the polymer material.[4] Consequently, leakage of unbound monomers and degradation by-products leach into the oral environment. Toxic levels of UDMA released from dental materials were reported to induce necrosis in human oral cells in an in vivo study.[5] The biological safety of UDMA became a concern when an in vivo study found that it may cause disruption by inducing DNA double-strand breaks in the human gingival fibroblasts.[6]

A recent in vitro study found that CAD-CAM resin blocks released UDMA monomer upon static incubation in both water and ethanol, with highest levels detected on day seven of immersion, followed by a declining pattern over time.[7] Although the level of UDMA detected was not deemed to be harmful, Mourouzis et al. suggested further investigation to be carried out within artificial saliva to simulate the monomer elution of CAD-CAM resin blocks.[7] Another study comparing CAD-CAM resin blocks with conventional resin reported undetectable UDMA leaching.[8] Furthermore, reduced microhardness and flexural strength were observed upon subjecting to static immersion and different heat treatments at different temperatures.[910] All these findings suggest the need to investigate the safety of these dental materials.

We aimed to identify and quantify the presence of UDMA monomer elution from two different indirect composite CAD-CAM blocks before and after thermocycling via HPLC. The null hypothesis tested was that there is no difference between UDMA monomer elution released from PICN (VE) and RCB (SB).

Preparation of specimens

Twenty 10 mm × 12 mm × 4 mm rectangular specimens [Figure 1] were prepared from each type of the CAD-CAM block (n = 40).

Preparation of sample. RCB computer-aided design and computer-aided manufacturing block (Shofu HC Block, Japan) cut using a low-speed diamond saw

Thermocycling protocol

All specimens were stored in artificial saliva at 37°C for 24 h, and the artificial saliva was sampled for analysis as a control group. For the thermocycling procedures, an alternate sample immersion within artificial saliva, under 5°C to 55°C ± 1°C, with 20 s dwell time and 5 s transfer interval, was carried out using an automatic thermocycling dipping machine (ATDM) T6PD, Zecttron Sdn. Bhd., Malaysia). Artificial saliva was taken from each sample after 500 cycles and 10,000 cycles of thermocycling, where 10,000 cycles simulate 12 months of function.[11]

High-pressure liquid chromatography (HPLC) analyses

Instrumentation and chromatographic conditions

After 24 h of soaking and 500 and 10,000 cycles of thermocycling, an aliquot of 20 µL of the artificial saliva was collected from each sample group and was analyzed by HPLC (Agilent Technologies 1200 Series, USA). This machine is equipped with a quaternary pump, including an ultraviolet diode detector, which was used to measure the levels of monomer elution from the specimens. This HPLC analysis in detecting UDMA was validated by Diamantopoulou and colleagues.[12] The chromatographic conditions were performed at ambient temperature using a Lichrosorb RP-C18 column (5 µ, 25 cm × 0.46 cm). The mobile phase, made up of a mixture of 70% acetonitrile (A, HPLC grade) and 30% nonionized water (B, Milipore ultrapure water), was applied at a gradient as follows: 0–15 min, linear gradient 50%–80% A and 15–25 min, linear gradient 80%–50% A. The mobile phase flow rate was set at 0.9mL/min, and the sample injection volume was 20µL to monitor analyte elution. Detection using a diode array detector was performed at 203nm for UDMA.

Preparation of standard urethane dimethacrylate

UDMA purchased from Sigma-Aldrich Chemie GmbH, Steinheim, Germany [Table 2], was used to prepare a standard solution for HPLC analysis. Standard solutions at three concentrations, including 1.5, 3, and 6 mg/mL, were prepared through serial dilution of UDMA stock monomer with methanol. The retention time for UDMA was 5.5 min,[7] and a calibration graph was plotted on four concentration levels with three injections for each level.

Analysis of tested sample

Aliquots (20 µL) of the tested sample (soak: 24 h and thermocycling: 500 cycles and 10,000 cycles) were injected into the sample loop. This was impelled together with the mobile phase solution via pump action to the column, where separation takes place. Monomer retention time (time needed to exit the column) was obtained from the system-generated graph.

The concentration of monomer-eluted samples was obtained using standard curves obtained from standard solutions.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 26.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Mann–Whitney U-test was computed to compare the mean concentrations of UDMA detected from each sample group. The comparison of UDMA monomer between the control group and 500 and 10,000 cycles of thermocycling was conducted using Kruskal–Wallis test.

RESULTS

No UDMA was detected from both RCB and PICN control groups. The highest UDMA monomer was identified from RCB (SB) after 500 cycles (40 µg/mL) and reduced at 10,000 cycles of thermocycling at the level of 10 µg/mL [Figure 2].

On the other hand, for PICN (VE), UDMA monomer was not detected at 500 cycles of thermocycling. Quantifiable amount of UDMA at the level of 10 µg/mL was noticed after 10,000 cycles of thermocycling. However, there was no significant difference between the monomer elution from SB and VE and between the different thermocycling groups. DISCUSSION

The amount of monomer elution varies depending on the structure of the restoration material, the degree of polymerization, the types of filler particles, and cross-link density in the polymer network.[413] In this study, two different resin-based CAD/CAM blocks were subjected to thermocycling for 10,000 cycles. Immersion in water led to hydrolysis of the interfacial silane coupling agent that provides chemical bonding between polymers and fillers.[14] Based on the result of this study, there was no quantifiable amount of monomer release after 24-h presoak in artificial saliva, which showed that there was no monomer elution at the control stage before thermocycling. This result contrast with a previous study in which resin-based CAD/CAM blocks were immersed in water, where low levels of UDMA were detected after 1-day soaking.[7] This may be owing to the type of artificial saliva used to simulate the actual oral condition used in this study, which is composed of different minerals and pH. Furthermore, in our study, samples were subjected to thermocycling via hot and cold water soaking as compared to static immersion in a room temperature solution.

UDMA monomer is commonly used in dental resin-based materials to enhance the viscosity. Both RCB and PICN samples are shown to have a certain level of monomer elution after subjected to 10,000 cycles of thermocycling. However, the highest level of UDMA was eluted from RCB samples after 500 cycles of thermocycling, compared to PICN, which showed no quantifiable UDMA monomer elution. Higher UDMA leached from RCB after 500 cycles may be due to the difference in the composition of the polymer network. RCB is composed of fillers in a dimethacrylate matrix; on the contrary, PICN is a presintered ceramic network infiltrated with TEGDMA and UDMA resin.[915] RCB with dimethacrylate matrix-formed ceramic network has a more heterogeneous area, experiencing increased degradation during cyclic shrinkage and expansion, upon thermocycling.

However, based on the results, both null hypotheses are accepted. Although RCB showed higher UDMA monomer elution after 500 cycles of thermocycling groups, the difference between the control group and thermocycling group was not statistically significant. Furthermore, there were no significant differences between the monomers eluted from RCB and PICN.

In this study, the surface area of the specimen used is equivalent to the surface area of a premolar crown.[16] It was reported that UDMA inhibits cell growth in vitro at a concentration of 0.1 mM[17] and caused acute toxicity at 5 mg/kg.[18] The United States Food and Drug Administration has reported a tolerable limit of 50 µg per kg per day. This value is 200,000-fold higher than the value published by the European Food Safety Authority, 0.2 ng/kg of body weight per day.[19] Although other regulatory bodies have questioned the causal link established by the EPSA, the public health team embraced the finding of EPSA findings owing to their reliability in conducting systematic academic studies, as opposed to industry-based studies conducted by other regulatory bodies.[20]

In this study, the highest BPA monomer (40 µg/mL) was detected in RCB (SB) after 500 cycles of thermocycling. Since 20µL sample volume was taken from a sample with a surface area of 1200mm2. The estimated individual exposure over 24 hours is around 1.7−5ng/mm2/day. This calculation was adopted from a study by Becher et al.[21] Therefore, within the limitations of this study, the UDMA monomer elution detected in this study was within the safe limit.

However, overall compounds eluted in the oral cavity may be greater than the result found in these classic in vitro elution studies. Human saliva is expected to have greater monomer elution than in artificial solutions due to the presence of enzymes, which may degrade monomers of composite resin.[22] Long-term clinical trials should be conducted to provide a more clinically relevant result. Other monomers that are commonly found in the structure of this composite-based CAD-CAM material should be further investigated to ensure the safety of the resin-based CAD-CAM block.

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of the present study, both null hypotheses tested are accepted. Despite the difference in polymer structure between RCB and PICN, there is no difference between UDMA monomer elution released from PICN (VE) and RCB (SB) before and after subjecting them to thermocycling. However, future studies on the elution of other monomers should be conducted. Nevertheless, animal and human studies are needed to evaluate the long-term physical and chemical performance of these materials.

Phenoxybenzamine: Old Wine in a Bright New Bottle-Taming the wild side of Phenoxybenzamine
Authors: R. Benedict Raj, Srinath Reddy Narahari, Vasudev Vemala, Jyotiprakash Reddy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Phenoxybenzamine (PBZ), a long-acting α-blocker, was once widely used in pediatric cardiac surgeries but fell out of favor due to concerns about systemic hypotension. In the context of developing countries and resource-limited settings, PBZ may offer a cost-effective and safe alternative when used in a controlled protocol.Objective:To evaluate the safety and efficacy of a PBZ-based vasoplegic strategy during cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) in pediatric open-heart surgeries.Materials and Methods:A prospective observational study was conducted between 2015 and 2020 at a tertiary care cardiac center. A total of 520 pediatric patients undergoing open-heart surgery with CPB received PBZ as the primary vasodilator in combination with low-dose adrenaline. Hemodynamic data, ICU outcomes, and incidence of postoperative complications were analyzed.Results:The protocol achieved stable intraoperative perfusion without the need for additional vasopressors. Most patients were extubated on the same day, and the incidence of pulmonary arterial hypertension crisis was negligible (one case). Mortality was limited to 2.7%, and postoperative hypotension was effectively managed with fluid and adrenaline support.Conclusion:PBZ, when used in a structured intraoperative protocol, offers a safe, economical, and effective strategy for pediatric cardiac surgeries, especially in settings with limited access to newer vasoplegic agents.

INTRODUCTION

Pediatric cardiac surgery using cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) is often complicated by uneven peripheral perfusion, leading to hypoxia, lactic acidosis, and increased risk of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) crisis, particularly after prolonged bypass in complex congenital heart repairs.[1] Effective management during CPB requires strategies that uniformly reduce systemic and pulmonary vascular resistance while ensuring controlled cooling and rewarming.[2] Phenoxybenzamine (PBZ), a long-acting, nonselective α-adrenergic blocker, was historically used to address these challenges by promoting sustained vasodilation.[3] Although its use declined with the introduction of shorter-acting agents, PBZ has regained attention for its potential in improving oxygen delivery and preventing PAH crises, especially in complex procedures such as the Norwood operation.[4] Despite concerns about prolonged hypotension, a protocolized approach may harness PBZ’s benefits while minimizing its risks.[5] This study explores PBZ’s role in improving perioperative stability and outcomes in pediatric open-heart surgery.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This prospective observational case series was conducted from 2015 to 2020 at a single tertiary center, involving 520 pediatric patients who received PBZ during CPB. PBZ was used in conditions with increased pulmonary blood flow or requiring reduced systemic vascular resistance, such as ventricular septal defect (VSD), total anomalous pulmonary venous connection, transposition of the great arteries, atrioventricular canal defects, mitral valve repair, and pulmonary artery banding. Patients with cyanotic heart disease with reduced pulmonary flow, or those over 10 years or weighing more than 20 kg, were excluded due to hypotension risk. PBZ was administered at 2 mg/kg (ma × 10 mg) via the CPB reservoir, with no postoperative dosing; residual pulmonary hypertension was managed with oxygen, milrinone, and sildenafil. Patients were grouped as < 1 month (n = 259), 1–12 months (n = 205), and 1–5 years (n = 56). Data on demographics, CPB time, pressures, flows, urine output, lactate, ventilation time, complications, and mortality were collected and analyzed using descriptive statistics.

Intraoperative variables and outcomes such as ventilation duration, mortality, and incidence of PAH crisis were assessed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of PBZ as the primary vasodilator.

DISCUSSION

This study reaffirms the utility of PBZ as a safe and effective vasodilator in pediatric cardiac surgeries involving CPB.[6] Administered via a structured protocol, PBZ successfully maintained stable perfusion pressures and consistent CPB flows without the need for intraoperative vasopressin or additional vasopressors.[7] Most patients achieved early extubation, and postoperative hypotension was readily managed with fluids and low-dose adrenaline, minimizing ICU interventions.[8] Importantly, the incidence of pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) crisis was nearly negligible, with only one reported case among 520 patients. This supports PBZ’s role in reducing pulmonary vascular resistance and preserving right ventricular function postoperatively.[9] The overall mortality rate was low (2.7%) and consistent with or better than published benchmarks for similar pediatric cohorts undergoing complex congenital heart repairs.

While earlier concerns about prolonged systemic hypotension limited PBZ’s use, this study demonstrates that with dose control and appropriate patient selection, its benefits outweigh the risks. The findings also highlight the protocol’s potential to enhance recovery and reduce ICU resource utilization—particularly valuable in resource-constrained settings.[10]

CONCLUSION

When PBZ is administered according to the protocol during CPB, it results in a predictable rapid recovery of cardiac function with low systemic vascular resistance. The feared complication of profound vasoplegia (hypotension) can be avoided by this method of administration. PBZ helps in maintaining low PVR, which aids in the rapid recovery of the right ventricle and prevents postoperative hypertensive PA crisis. The use of PBZ is recommended as it improves postoperative survival, prevents PA crisis, and saves precious time and money in resource-limited settings.

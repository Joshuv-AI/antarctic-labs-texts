Clinical Course and Factors Correlated with Severe Morbidity and Mortality in Patients with Coronavirus Disease 2019 Undergoing Maintenance Dialysis in Kanagawa, Japan
Authors: Kohei Ishiga, Hiromichi Wakui, Kengo Azushima, Tomohiko Kanaoka, Daisuke Kanai, Ryu Kobayashi, Sho Kinguchi, Naohito Okami, Tatsuya Haze, Takehisa Iwano, Masashi Sakai, Kohji Ohki, Jin Oshikawa, Toshiharu Kokuho, Masaaki Hanaoka, Hiroshi Mitsuhashi, Yukiko Yamada, Machiko Yabana, Yoshiyuki Toya, Kouichi Tamura
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective Patients undergoing maintenance dialysis are at a higher risk of morbidity and mortality due to severe coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) than the general population. However, longitudinal data regarding this subpopulation of patients are lacking. We therefore examined the prognosis of patients with COVID-19 undergoing maintenance dialysis between 2020 and 2023. In addition, we explored the factors correlated with COVID-19 severity, focusing on the transition thereof throughout the observational period.Methods The primary outcome was the progression to severe or fatal COVID-19. We evaluated the correlation between the primary outcome and baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of patients. Patients undergoing maintenance dialysis who were hospitalized for mild-to-moderate COVID-19 between February 2020 and April 2023 were enrolled at four institutions in Kanagawa, Japan.Results Of the 173 patients, 7 (4.0%) developed severe COVID-19, and 12 (6.9%) died. The severe/death cohort was significantly older, with a higher percentage of unvaccinated patients than the non-severe cohort (58.2% and 25.0%, respectively; p=0.016). Thymus and activation-regulated chemokine levels on admission were lower in the severe/death cohort than in the non-severe cohort, albeit not to a statistically significant degree (148±84 mg/dL and 342±657 pg/mL, respectively; p=0.082). A multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that the odds ratio for severe morbidity or death was 0.23 (95% confidence interval: 0.07-0.75) for vaccinated patients.Conclusion In patients undergoing maintenance dialysis, the severity rate of COVID-19 is approximately 10%. Vaccination was correlated with a reduced risk of severe COVID-19.Keywords: COVID-19, dialysis, severity, vaccination, Japan

Introduction

The mortality rate due to coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) is high in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis (1,2). As of May 24, 2023, a total of 19,939 patients undergoing dialysis had contracted COVID-19 in Japan.

Initially, the COVID-19 mortality rate in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis was 30.8%, which was markedly higher than the COVID-19 mortality rate of 1.5% in the general population during the same period (2). Thereafter, the COVID-19 mortality rate declined gradually. However, particularly during the eighth wave of COVID-19 in Japan from November 2022 to May 2023, the mortality rate in patients undergoing dialysis was 2.8% (3), which was higher than that in the general population (0.24%) (4).

Until May 8, 2023, when COVID-19 was changed to a category 5 disease under the Japanese Infectious Disease Control Law (5), dialysis with concomitant COVID-19 was a principal indication for hospitalization (6-8). However, a paucity of longitudinal research exists regarding the changes in mortality and incidence of severe morbidity in patients undergoing dialysis subsequent to the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic and commencement of vaccination programs.

Consequently, in this study, we examined the prognosis of patients undergoing dialysis for COVID-19 over a period of approximately 3 years (2020-2023). Furthermore, we explored the factors correlated with the severity of COVID-19 in Japan, focusing on the transition thereof throughout the observational period.

Materials and Methods

Ethical considerations

All procedures in this study were conducted after obtaining ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board of Yokohama City University Hospital (F220300047) and in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from the patients for participation and publication of their de-identified data.

Study population

Patients meeting the following inclusion criteria were enrolled in the study: a) hospitalized for or contracted COVID-19 during their hospitalization at the participating institutions, which included Yokohama City University Hospital, Fujisawa Municipal Hospital, Yokohama Sakae Kyosai Hospital, and Yokosuka City Hospital, between February 2020 and April 2023, and b) patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis (HD) or peritoneal dialysis (PD) at the onset of COVID-19. Patients with severe COVID-19 on admission were excluded from this study.

Definition of COVID-19 severity

According to the Japanese Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare guidelines (8), severe cases were defined as those requiring any of the following: a) invasive mechanical ventilation, b) extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, or c) intensive-care unit admission for any other reason.

Moderate cases were defined as those requiring the following: a) supplemental oxygen for low saturation of peripheral oxygen at ＜93%, with or without tachypnea, or b) increased oxygen flow if already on home oxygen therapy. Mild cases were defined as those that did not require supplemental oxygen.

Primary outcomes

The primary outcome was defined as the progression to severe or fatal COVID-19. We evaluated the correlation between the primary outcome and the baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients, including laboratory blood test findings. We compared these variables between patients who developed severe COVID-19 or died after hospitalization (severe/death cohort) and those who did not develop severe COVID-19 morbidity or died after hospitalization (non-severe cohort). Data on demographic characteristics and clinical information during hospitalization were collected from the electronic medical records.

Statistical analyses

The parameters are presented as the mean±standard deviation (SD) or number (percentage). Statistical analyses were performed using the JMP Pro software program, version 15/16 (SAS Institute, Cary, USA). Welch's t-, Wilcoxon's rank-sum, Cochran-Armitage trend, or Fisher's exact tests were applied as appropriate. A Cox proportional hazard model was used to estimate the hazard ratio of the factors for severe or fatal COVID-19. A multivariate logistic regression analysis and the Wald test were selected for statistical analyses to calculate odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for severe or fatal COVID-19. Statistical significance was set at a p value ＜0.05.

Results

A total of 187 patients undergoing maintenance dialysis were hospitalized with COVID-19 between February 2020 and April 2023. Of these, 14 patients were excluded because they were diagnosed with severe COVID-19 on admission. In total, 173 patients were included in this study.

Of the 173 patients, 118 (68.2%) and 55 (31.8%) were categorized as mild and moderate cases, respectively (Supplementary material 1). After admission, 83 (48.0%) and 71 (41.0%) patients had mild and moderate disease, respectively. Furthermore, 7 (4.0%) patients developed severe COVID-19, and 12 (6.9%) patients died. The monthly numbers of patients hospitalized for COVID-19 with their corresponding severity levels at and after admission are presented in Supplementary material 2.

The baseline demographic and clinical characteristics at the time of admission for the severe and non-severe cohorts are presented in Table 1. Three patients underwent PD, and five patients underwent combined PD and HD; the remaining patients underwent HD. The age, dialysis history, sex, body mass index (BMI), and comorbidities were not significantly different between the two cohorts. A history of COVID-19 vaccination was present for 150 of the 173 patients. A significantly higher percentage of patients in the non-severe cohort were vaccinated than in the severe/death cohort [58.2% and 25.0%, respectively (p=0.016)]. Furthermore, the medications used did not differ significantly between the two cohorts. The levels of thymus and activation-regulated chemokine (TARC), a cytokine belonging to the CC chemokine family, on admission were lower in the severe/death cohort than in the non-severe cohort; however, this difference was not statistically significant (Table 2). No statistically significant differences were observed in the other laboratory blood test results at the time of admission between the two cohorts (Tables 1, 22).

To examine whether or not vaccination status affected severe or fatal COVID-19 outcomes during hospitalization, we analyzed the Cox proportional hazards model as a univariate model (Figure A). Unvaccinated patients were significantly more likely to experience severe or fatal COVID-19 during hospitalization. We performed a multivariate analysis of severe and fatal COVID-19 and the vaccination history of the patients. Considering the small size of the cohorts, we selected age and sex, which have been included as confounding factors for multivariate analyses in previous studies (9-12). The OR for severe morbidity or mortality was 0.23 (95% CI: 0.07-0.75) for the patients who had been vaccinated compared with unvaccinated patients (Table 5). The OR for older patients (＞75 years old) was 1.02 (95% CI: 0.32-3.24, p=0.97), and the OR for sex was 1.93 (95% CI: 0.57-6.53, p=0.29).

In general, α/δ variants have been reported to have a higher rate of severe disease than omicron variants. Therefore, we divided the pandemic periods into α/δ and omicron periods and compared the vaccination status and outcomes during each period. Specifically, we separated the outcomes of patients hospitalized in 2020 and 2021 from those of patients hospitalized in 2022 and 2023 (Figure B, C). In 2020 and 2021, 19.2% of the unvaccinated patients succumbed to severe or fatal COVID-19 (Figure B). Furthermore, this was the case for 14.3% of the unvaccinated patients in 2022 and 2023 (Figure C).

Discussion

The present study revealed that vaccination was correlated with a decreased risk of severe disease and death due to COVID-19 in patients undergoing maintenance dialysis throughout an observational period of approximately three years. Globally, the effects of COVID-19 vaccination on the general population have been demonstrated (11,13-17). In patients undergoing maintenance dialysis, receiving two vaccine doses reduces the initiation of oxygen therapy (10) and the admission, incidence, and mortality rates (9,10,18,19) relative to COVID-19. We previously observed that the increase in the antibody titer after two vaccine doses in patients undergoing maintenance HD was less than that in healthy controls. However, the antibody titer after the third dose in patients undergoing HD increases substantially, reaching the same level as that in healthy controls (20,21). Previous studies have shown that, when compared with two vaccine doses in patients undergoing maintenance HD, three vaccine doses in the same study population reduced hospitalization and mortality due to COVID-19 (18,22). The present study also demonstrated that the proportion of severe or fatal COVID-19 cases significantly decreased as the number of vaccinations increased, consistent with previous studies (Table 3). Despite the small study population, we clearly demonstrated that vaccination contributes to a reduced risk of severe or fatal COVID-19.

Few studies have demonstrated vaccine efficacy in patients undergoing dialysis during the omicron variant pandemic of 2022 (23), which appears to have resulted in less severe disease than the δ variant (24). Our findings revealed that the risk of severe or fatal COVID-19 in patients who were unvaccinated decreased from 19.2% in 2020-2021 to 14.3% in 2022-2023 (Figure B, C). This result is consistent with those of previous studies. In the present study, no laboratory blood test results at the time of admission were predictive of severe or fatal COVID-19 cases. However, the TARC level on admission was lower in the severe disease/death cohort than in the non-severe cohort.

Previous studies have demonstrated that the levels of C-reactive protein, D-dimer, interleukin-6, soluble interleukin-2 receptor, procalcitonin, lymphocytes, ferritin, and TARC are predictive biomarkers of COVID-19 severity (12,25-29). Of these predictive biomarkers, TARC may be particularly sensitive. TARC, also known as CCL17, is a cytokine belonging to the CC chemokine family that plays a role in the immune system by attracting T-helper type 2 (Th2) cells to sites of inflammation (30). TARC is primarily produced by dendritic cells, macrophages, and fibroblasts, and its levels can be measured in the blood serum. In patients admitted for COVID-19, including those undergoing HD, low serum TARC levels have been associated with severe COVID-19 (27,31,32). Sugiyama et al. observed that, among the 71 cytokines and chemokines, the serum TARC level on admission had the best predictive ability regarding the exacerbation of COVID-19. Furthermore, they proposed 95 pg/mL as the cutoff value for serum TARC levels (27). However, TARC levels are high in patients with renal dysfunction and are inversely correlated with the renal function (33,34). In the present study, particularly in the severe/death cohort, the mean TARC level was higher than the cutoff value of 95 pg/mL in the general population, which is consistent with the findings of previous studies (31,32). Beppu et al. proposed a cutoff value of ＜150.5 pg/mL for TARC to predict severe COVID-19 in patients undergoing dialysis (31). Further research is required to validate this cut-off value and its predictive power in the subpopulation of patients undergoing HD.

The medication history of the patients did not differ between the severe/death and non-severe cohorts. At the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic, concerns existed regarding whether or not renin-angiotensin system (RAS) inhibitors would worsen the prognosis of COVID-19 (35,36). RAS inhibitors have been postulated to increase the effects of angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2), the major receptor of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2. However, our previous research using murine chronic kidney disease (CKD) models revealed that the use of RAS inhibitors did not increase pharyngeal and pulmonary ACE2 expression (37). Furthermore, in a retrospective cohort study in Kanagawa, Japan, we found that taking RAS inhibitors did not contribute to the severity of COVID-19 in patients with hypertension; however, they may be beneficial in preventing mental confusion (38). Furthermore, a subsequent meta-analysis that included this previous study concluded that RAS inhibitor use did not affect the risk of COVID-19 severity (39). Therefore, RAS inhibitors are considered safe for use in patients with CKD and COVID-19.

Several limitations associated with the present study warrant mention. First, changes in the prevalent viral strains occurred during the observation period, but such details were not available. Thus, an analysis of strain-specific risk variations for severe COVID-19 could not be performed. Second, this was a retrospective cohort study, and confounding factors could not be excluded. Third, the study population was small; therefore, multivariate analyses were not well adjusted. Fourth, the demographic details of the patients were diverse. Fifth, analyses were performed on hospitalized patients with mild-to-moderate COVID-19 at the time of admission. Therefore, the findings of this study may be specific to the above population and not to the entire dialysis population.

Conclusion

In patients undergoing maintenance dialysis, the severity rate of COVID-19 is approximately 10%. Vaccination was correlated with a reduced risk of developing severe COVID-19 during the pandemic in Japan.

Granulocyte–macrophage colony-stimulating factor-containing medium for blastocyst recovery culture: study protocol for a randomized controlled trial (GSET-study)
Authors: Tatsuya Kobayashi, Kyoko Higuchi, Miki Okabe-Kinoshita, Kayoko Kikuchi, Tomoya Kurokawa, Shota Hatakeyama, Tsuyoshi Okubo, Shota Oikawa, Ryosuke Suzuki, Seiji Ogawa, Goro Kuramoto, Akiko Kada, Shigeto Shimmura, Akihiro Mouri, Atsushi Yanaihara, Tomoya Segawa, Atsushi Yamamoto, Eiji Nishio, Keiichi Takahashi, Haruki Nishizawa, Toshio Hamatani

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAlthough the number of frozen-thawed blastocysts transfer is increasing worldwide, the live birth rate following blastocyst transfer using assisted reproductive technology remains at 30–60%. Thus, improving the pregnancy rate per transfer is an urgent issue. In a previous retrospective study, we evaluated the use of granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF)-containing medium for recovery culture to improve the outcomes of frozen-thawed blastocyst transfers. The results demonstrated that the live birth rates increased by approximately 10% following recovery culture in the GM-CSF-containing culture medium. This study aims to prospectively evaluate whether GM-CSF-containing blastocyst recovery culture following thawing increases live birth.MethodsThis is a multicenter, randomized, parallel-group, active-controlled, single-blind trial. The recruitment target is 750 participants meeting the criteria. Enrolled patients are randomized 1:1 to the GM-CSF-containing culture medium group (test group) or the non-GM-CSF-containing culture medium group (control group). The blastocyst recovery culture after warming was defined as an intervention in this study; frozen-thawed blastocysts will be cultured for 3–7 h in GM-CSF-containing medium (test group) or medium without GM-CSF (control group) followed by blastocyst transfer. The primary outcome will be live birth. We will also evaluate embryo transfer outcomes as secondary efficacy endpoints and evaluate perinatal and neonatal outcomes as a safety endpoint.DiscussionThis is the first large-scale prospective study to investigate the efficacy of a GM-CSF-containing medium for frozen-thawed blastocyst transfer. The study findings will provide evidence regarding the efficiency of GM-CSF-containing medium for blastocyst recovery culture after warming.Trial registrationJapan Registry of Clinical Trials jRCT1040240159. Background

Recent advances in embryo freezing technology have made so-called frozen-thawed embryo transfers possible, in which many embryos are frozen and thawed while still alive. In Japan, approximately 260,000 frozen-thawed embryo transfers were performed in 2022, of which nearly 80% were frozen-thawed blastocyst transfers [1]. However, the live birth rate following blastocyst transfer using assisted reproductive technology (ART) remains at 30–60%; thus, improving the pregnancy rate per transfer is warranted [2].

Granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) is a cytokine secreted by the epithelial cells lining the female reproductive tract, including the fallopian tube and endometrium, in humans and other mammals. Therefore, the use of GM-CSF-containing media in embryo culture has been widely investigated for improving the embryonic outcomes in both in vitro and in vivo settings [3–7]. To date, randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have shown that when embryos are cultured up to the early cleavage stage in GM-CSF-containing media, the transfer of these embryos results in a reduced miscarriage rate [5]. In contrast, when GM-CSF-containing media were used for extended culture up to the blastocyst stage, the blastocyst formation rate was found to be decreased [7]. In a previous retrospective study, we evaluated the use of a GM-CSF-containing medium for recovery culture to improve the outcomes of frozen-thawed blastocyst transfers in patients with infertility aged 30–39 years. The results demonstrated that the implantation, fetal sac confirmation (clinical pregnancy), and live birth rates increased by approximately 15%, 10%, and 10%, respectively, following recovery culture in the GM-CSF-containing culture medium [8]. Furthermore, no adverse events (AE), such as increased rates of congenital malformations or low birth weight, were observed, indicating that recovery culture with GM-CSF-containing medium is a safe and effective method for enhancing the outcomes of frozen-thawed blastocyst transfers.

Building upon this promising retrospective finding, we aim to conduct a RCT to rigorously evaluate the efficacy and safety of GM-CSF-containing recovery cultures for improving the clinical outcomes of frozen-thawed blastocyst transfers. To date, randomized controlled trials of GM-CSF-supplemented media have been performed during conventional embryo culture prior to transfer. However, no trial has specifically evaluated the effect of short-term post-thaw recovery culture with GM-CSF-containing medium in frozen-thawed blastocyst transfer cycles. Given that the majority of ART cycles worldwide now involve frozen-thawed blastocyst transfer, this trial uniquely addresses a critical gap by testing GM-CSF exposure in the post-thaw period rather than during pre-transfer embryo development. This study seeks to confirm the benefits of GM-CSF-containing medium observed in a retrospective analysis and provide high-quality evidence to support the adoption of this method in routine clinical practice.

Study objectives

This study aims to examine whether post-thaw blastocyst recovery culture with GM-CSF-containing culture medium leads to a clinically significant improvement in the live birth rate in patients with infertility aged 30–39 years, compared to that with non-GM-CSF-containing culture medium, which is currently considered the standard treatment.

Materials and methods

Study setting

This randomized, parallel-group, active-controlled, single-blind, multicenter study aims to evaluate whether post-thaw blastocyst recovery culture with a GM-CSF-containing medium increases the live birth rate.

The trial will be conducted at six institutions in Japan: Fujita Health University Haneda Clinic, Takahashi Women’s Clinic, Yanaihara Women’s Clinic, Fujita Health University Hospital, Shinbashi Yume Clinic and Kanagawa Ladies Clinic. The recruitment period will be from January 2025 to October 2026. If recruitment is slower than anticipated, additional outreach activities (e.g., posters and other informational materials for patients) will be implemented, and additional sites may be invited to participate after obtaining ethics committee approval and updating the trial registry as required. If necessary, the recruitment period may be extended following the same amendment procedures. A flowchart of the study design is shown in Fig. 1. Determination of dropout

If the participant withdraws consent or if the embryo transfer cannot be performed owing to the condition of the participant or the embryo (e.g., degeneration during recovery culture), the investigator or principal investigator will discontinue testing and evaluation related to the study.

Sample size calculation

In our retrospective comparative analysis, the live birth rates were 40.9% and 30.5% in the GM-CSF and control groups, respectively [8]. Based on this study, we assumed a live birth rate of 40% and 30% for the GM-CSF and control groups, respectively. With a two-sided significance level of 0.05 and statistical power of 0.8, 712 patients (356 in each group) will need to be recruited. Considering dropout cases, we set the sample size to 750.

Screening

For patients with frozen blastocysts stored during regular medical care, screening tests will be conducted after obtaining verbal and written consent. Table 1 shows the overall schedule of the study. Screening will involve a questionnaire or medical records to determine the age, date of oocyte collection for the blastocyst to be transferred, height, weight, medical history, cause of infertility, number of previous embryo transfers, the presence or absence of repeated implantation failure, pregnancy history, history of two or more miscarriages within the past year, indications of chromosomal translocations in either couple, the presence or absence of infertility treatment using ejaculated sperm, complications, and current organic uterine abnormalities. In addition, endocrine tests will include the most recent endocrine function tests (basal hormone values): luteinizing hormone, follicle-stimulating hormone, estradiol (days 2 to 5 of menstruation), and anti-Müllerian hormone (no restrictions on the date of measurement). After confirming their eligibility, the participants will be registered to the study and randomly assigned 1:1 to either the GM-CSF-containing culture medium group (test group) or the non-GM-CSF-containing culture medium group (control group). Randomization and blinding

Data collection and management will be done using REDCap. Researchers will enter patient information following informed consent, and allocation to each group will be performed by a central registry using REDCap [9, 10]. The REDCap project, including the randomization module, will be configured by a REDCap administrator at Fujita Health University. Access to the allocation sequence (randomization list) and future allocations will be restricted to the REDCap administrator only; investigators and site staff involved in enrolment will not have access to the randomization list. Patients will be randomly assigned (1:1) to the GM-CSF or control group by stratified block randomization of patient age (< 35 years old or ≥ 35 years old) and facility. After the researcher enters the screening results into REDCap, if it is automatically determined that the eligibility criteria are met and no exclusions are violated, then REDCap automatically assigns the patient to the GM-CSF or control group.

The study will be done in a single-blind fashion, where the patient will not know which culture medium they are being treated with until the end of the study. The group to which the patient was assigned could be disclosed if the patient wished after the completion of the entire study period. Investigators, outcome assessors, and the statistician will not be blinded to treatment allocation. The primary outcome—live birth—is an objective endpoint obtained from clinical records, making it unlikely to be influenced by subjective assessment.

Interventions

In this study, participants with vitrified blastocysts will be randomized into one of the two arms. The blastocyst recovery culture after warming was defined as an intervention in this study. Controlled ovarian hyperstimulation, oocyte retrieval, insemination procedure, vitrification, embryo culture, endometrium preparation, and luteal support will be performed through routine standard procedures according to each center.

On the day of the blastocyst transfer, blastocysts will be thawed and cultured for 3–7 h using SAGE 1-Step GM-CSF (cat. no. 77010003 A, CooperSurgical, Trumbull, CT, USA) in the test group or SAGE 1-Step (cat. no. 67010010, CooperSurgical) in the control group as a recovery culture. The control culture medium contains the same components as the test culture medium, except that it does not contain GM-CSF. The blastocysts in each group will then be transferred to the uterus with a small amount (approximately 1–20 µl) of culture medium. To prevent mix-up of the culture media, two researchers will verify the media type during medium preparation and before use. There will be no restrictions on concomitant care and interventions permitted or prohibited during the study.

Outcomes

Primary outcome

The primary outcome of this study is live birth, which indicates the birth of a newborn baby who shows signs of life, such as breathing and heartbeat, regardless of the gestational age.

Secondary outcomes

Pregnancy-related secondary outcomes are implantation, clinical pregnancy, fetal heartbeat, biochemical pregnancy, multiple births, and miscarriage after blastocyst transfer. Implantation will be defined as a serum human chorionic gonadotropin level ≥ 20 IU/l following blastocyst transfer. Clinical pregnancy will be defined as the presence of intrauterine gestational sacs by transvaginal ultrasound.

It is well established that pregnancies through ART are associated with a higher risk of perinatal complications than spontaneous pregnancies [11]. In addition, although babies born using GM-CSF-containing cultures have been healthy, with no serious side effects reported, further case accumulation and research are warranted to ensure the safety of GM-CSF-containing culture medium for newborns and mothers [5, 12]. This study will evaluate the efficacy of GM-CSF-containing culture medium as well as its safety for newborns and mothers. The following items will be assessed as outcomes indicating the child’s progress after pregnancy: stillbirth, premature birth, neonatal death, induced abortion, birth week, sex ratio of newborns, method of delivery, major congenital anomalies in newborns, neonatal diseases, birth weight, the presence or absence of macrosomia, the presence or absence of low birth weight, and ectopic pregnancy. We will also assess the incidence of pregnancy complications (gestational hypertension, preeclampsia, HELLP [hemolysis, elevated liver enzymes, and low platelet levels] syndrome, gestational diabetes, abnormal bleeding before and during delivery, placenta accreta, and placental abruption). To evaluate the safety profile, we will record the details, incidence, and AE grades. All the outcomes and definitions used in this study are shown in Additional file 1: Table S1. We also evaluate degeneration of embryos after recovery culture to see the effects of the culture medium on the embryos.

Follow-up protocol

The data on pregnancy assessment following embryo transfer (human chorionic gonadotropin levels and gestational sac confirmation) and AEs observed in this study will be directly entered into REDCap by the researcher. Data on the pregnancy outcomes, pregnancy complications, and delivery will be collected upon the direct registration of participants who log in to REDCap using the URL provided to them. If a response to the REDCap questionnaire is not received within 2 months following the expected delivery date, the principal investigator, research coordinator, or research collaborator will contact the participant by phone or other means for confirmation. Data on the pregnancy outcomes will be collected up to approximately 6 months after the expected delivery date. Details of the data collected using REDCap are shown in Additional file 1: Table S1.

Adverse event (AE)

In this study, an AE refers to the development of any undesirable or unintended disease or symptom (including abnormalities in clinical test results) in the participants from the time of embryo transfer until the time of observation at the research facility, regardless of the presence of a causal relationship to the test culture medium. We will collect AEs until day 10 after embryo transfer, when they can be tracked in the research facility. After that, they will be collected only at pregnancy assessment visits until referral to the perinatal facility and evaluated for the presence or absence of a causal relationship upon implementation of the study. All the AEs that occur from the time of embryo transfer to the time of referral to the perinatal facility will be collected and evaluated for the presence or absence of a causal relationship upon implementation of the study. When the principal investigator or co-investigator recognizes an AE, they will immediately take appropriate measures and record it in the medical records and REDCap without any discrepancies. In addition, the participant will be informed if treatment for an AE is deemed necessary. Furthermore, in case of a serious AE, the principal investigator and co-investigators will take appropriate measures regardless of the causal relationship with the test culture medium and endeavor to determine the cause of the disease (serious AE). The principal investigator/study office will determine the urgency, importance, and degree of impact of the report and take measures such as temporarily suspending registration and urgently contacting participating facilities to inform them of the matter, as necessary. If the principal investigator and co-investigators become aware of the occurrence of serious AEs in a participant, they will provide prompt and appropriate treatment. In addition, they will report and share the occurrences. Furthermore, each institution’s procedures for reporting serious AEs will be followed.

Data management and confidentiality

Study data will be collected and managed using the electronic data capture system of REDCap hosted at Fujita Health University [9, 10]. REDCap is a secure, web-based software platform designed to support data capture for research studies, providing (1) an intuitive interface for validated data capture, (2) audit trails for tracking data manipulation and export procedures, (3) automated export procedures for seamless data downloads to common statistical packages, and (4) procedures for data integration and interoperability with external sources. REDCap was built with measures to prevent data errors, such as defining ranges for data entry, displaying outlier errors, and using a multiple-choice format. The researchers received training on how to use the EDC and enter data according to the study protocol. The research team will regularly perform data management to ensure collected data accuracy and study quality. The investigator, researcher, project manager, and external data manager will perform data monitoring. They will be monitoring the number of recruited patients and also perform the data cleaning. The external data manager is independent from the sponsor and competing interests. If any questions arise during data monitoring, the research director will contact the representative researchers at each facility to inquire whether the data is accurate. The principal investigator will ensure that patient personal information is protected. Patients will be identified only by their unique identification number in REDCap and on case report forms. Documents such as consent forms that contain patient personal information will be treated as confidential by study personnel. Only researchers who have been granted permission to access REDCap will have access to it.

Auditing and monitoring

To improve the quality of this study, an external organization of the research office will conduct audits and monitoring. In addition to independent external monitoring, the study team will perform internal monitoring as part of routine quality control throughout the trial. In this trial, the principal investigator holds the overall responsibility for study oversight; however, to ensure objective evaluation, monitoring is delegated to an external clinical research associate (CRA) who is independent of the core study team. The external CRA periodically reviews participant enrolment status, protocol adherence, and data quality. A formal data monitoring committee was not established because this trial involves minimal additional risk and uses routinely collected clinical outcomes. Additionally, regular data management meetings and risk review meetings are held to assess trial progress and evaluate potential risks. The auditor and monitor are independent from the study group and are approved by the investigator. The procedures leading up to the start of the study were conducted as part of routine trial preparation, in accordance with institutional requirements. As soon as case registration begins, an audit and monitoring of the research facilities will also be conducted.

On-site monitoring will be conducted to ensure the safety of patients, that the protocol is being followed, and that data is being accurately collected. The monitor is not involved in this trial and is approved by the investigator. In the audit, the auditor will ensure proper execution of the research process, verify that monitoring is being conducted correctly, and ensure that any problems identified during monitoring are appropriately resolved.

Protocol amendments

Protocol amendments will be implemented only after ethics committee approval. After approval, the principal investigator will notify participating sites and circulate the revised protocol; each site will obtain authorization from the site director before implementation. The Japan Registry of Clinical Trials (jRCT) will be updated as required, and the sponsor will be notified. Protocol deviations will be documented using a deviation/breach report form. The principal investigator will promptly report serious protocol deviations (e.g., mix-up of culture media) to the ethics committee.

Statistical analysis

A biostatistician will perform the statistical analysis in this study. No interim analyses are planned. We defined the full analysis set (FAS) as all participants who started recovery cultures after randomization, excluding serious protocol violations such as failure to obtain consent, serious violations of the study procedures, and cases of nonintervention. The per-protocol set (PPS) is defined as participants from the FAS, excluding violations of the eligibility/exclusion rules or treatment violation (recovery incubation time outside the range) cases in accordance with the provisions of the study protocol, such as the study method. The safety analysis set is defined as all randomized participants who started intervention, and safety outcomes will be analyzed according to the culture medium actually used. Data collected up to the time of discontinuation, protocol deviation, or withdrawal will be retained and included in the analysis unless a participant explicitly requests complete removal of their data. For efficacy analyses, missing binary data will be treated as no effect, while for other analyses they will not be imputed.

FAS is the primary analysis population for efficacy. The proportion of live birth, implantation, clinical pregnancy, and fetal heartbeat rates by group will be calculated for the allocation factors of age at oocyte retrieval and facility. The risk difference between the groups will be analyzed using the Mantel–Haenszel test with the allocation factors of age at oocyte retrieval and facility. The adjusted difference with the confidence interval will be estimated. Continuous variables will be analyzed using a linear regression model with groups, the age at oocyte retrieval, and center.

The two-sided significance level will be set at 5%, and the confidence level of the estimate will be 95%. The statistical analyses will be performed using SAS (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA) and R (The R Project for Statistical Computing, www.r-project.org).

Dissemination

The results of this trial will be disseminated through presentations at scientific meetings and publication in peer-reviewed journals. Additionally, summary results will be posted on the jRCT in accordance with registry requirements.

Discussion

This trial aims to evaluate the effect of GM-CSF-containing medium; we plan to enroll 750 participants from reproductive medicine centers in Japan. To our knowledge, this is the first RCT to investigate the efficacy of a GM-CSF-containing medium in frozen-thawed blastocyst transfer cycles. Several RCTs are being conducted on cytokine-containing (including GM-CSF) culture media to increase the success rate of IVF in ART [5, 7, 13]. However, all these studies have examined the effects of cytokine-containing culture media on embryos during in vitro development and presumably involve a slightly different mechanism of action following blastocyst development than that proposed in our study. Moreover, the application of our protocol may potentially improve the outcomes of frozen blastocyst transfer, which is being increasingly performed worldwide [8].

Although our retrospective study demonstrated that GM-CSF-containing medium improves the implantation rate in the frozen-thawed blastocyst transfer cycle, the study had several limitations, including its retrospective design, ploidy of the embryos, and the culture medium used in the control group. The limitations of our previous study can be overcome by conducting a multicenter RCT using appropriate control media. Although the embryo euploidy count cannot be considered in all patients in accordance with the Japanese preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy implementation regulations, this could be overcome by ensuring a sufficient sample size.

Nevertheless, this study protocol has several limitations. First, owing to the nature of the clinical workflow, a single-blind study design will be implemented. However, the primary endpoint is live birth, which is not influenced by the examiners’ subjectivity; thus, the risk of bias in the study results will be low. Second, as preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy (PGT-A) is not routinely permitted in Japan, embryo ploidy cannot be accounted for in this trial. To mitigate this limitation, we will stratify randomization by patient age (< 35 vs ≥ 35 years) and conduct prespecified subgroup analyses by morphological blastocyst grading. These measures are expected to reduce confounding by embryo quality. Finally, because data, such as pregnancy, perinatal period, and neonatal outcomes, will be collected upon participants directly entering the data in REDCap, a risk of missing data concerning the outcomes exists. However, pregnancy outcome surveys are routinely conducted at all clinics in a mandatory ART case survey managed by the Japan Society of Obstetrics and Gynecology, and thus, missing data can be minimized [1].

Trial status

The current version of the study protocol is version 3.1 (June 13, 2025). Study enrolment began in January 2025, and the target sample size is expected to be attained by October 2026. The study will be completed in December 2027. The protocol has undergone external peer review.

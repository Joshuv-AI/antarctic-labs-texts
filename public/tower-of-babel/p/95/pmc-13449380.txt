Preoperative Serum Alkaline Phosphatase Predicts Arteriovenous Fistula Maturation Failure
Authors: Tian Si, Hanjie Yu, Zhuoyan Shen, Guangqi Chen, Zhaoqiang Shi, Shijie Wu, Saijie Ma, Beibei Ren, Hongchen Gu, Dayong Hu, Changbin Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionArteriovenous fistula (AVF) maturation failure remains a major obstacle for hemodialysis, yet its underlying molecular drivers are poorly characterized. The identification of key regulatory genes and clinically applicable biomarkers is critical for improving preoperative risk assessment.MethodsWe employed an integrative multiomics approach to unbiasedly identify hub genes. Transcriptomic datasets (GSE220796 and GSE119296) were analyzed using weighted gene coexpression network analysis (WGCNA) and machine learning. Cellular localization was defined via single-cell RNA-sequencing (GSE250469). Findings were validated in a prospective clinical cohort (n = 55) and through functional tube formation assays with human umbilical vein endothelial cells.ResultsThe tissue-nonspecific alkaline phosphatase (ALPL) gene, which encodes tissue-nonspecific alkaline phosphatase (TNSALP), was identified as a core hub gene upregulated in failed AVFs, predominantly in PLA2G2A+ fibroblasts. In the clinical cohort, tissue TNSALP protein levels were higher in failed AVFs and correlated with preoperative serum alkaline phosphatase (ALP) levels (r = 0.38, P = 0.018). Higher preoperative serum ALP was independently associated with lower odds of successful AVF maturation (adjusted odds ratio [OR]: 0.75 per 10 units per liter (U/l) increase, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.55–0.98, P = 0.048). Functionally, recombinant human TNSALP protein dose-dependently suppressed human umbilical vein endothelial cells tube formation in vitro (P < 0.05).ConclusionOur study identifies ALPL as a candidate mediator associated with AVF maturation failure and suggests that serum ALP, a routine clinical test, may serve as a practical biomarker for preoperative risk stratification.

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) affects approximately 10% of the global population, with an estimated 850 million individuals impacted worldwide.1,2 For patients with end-stage renal disease, reliable vascular access is essential for long-term hemodialysis.2, 3, 4 The autogenous AVF remains the preferred vascular access because of its superior long-term patency and lower complication rates compared with alternatives.5, 6, 7

Despite these advantages, AVF maturation failure affects 30% to 60% of patients,8, 9, 10 representing a major cause of morbidity and healthcare utilization. The pathophysiology involves a complex interplay of hemodynamic stress,11 metabolic abnormalities,12,13 and inflammatory processes.14,15 Successful AVF maturation requires adaptive remodeling dependent on adequate angiogenesis to support vascular wall expansion.16,17 However, the molecular mechanisms that compromise this critical process in human AVFs remain poorly understood. Recent single-cell studies have begun to characterize the cellular heterogeneity of venous remodeling, revealing specific inflammatory fibroblast subsets and dysregulated extracellular matrix deposition in failed AVFs.18 Although these studies provide detailed cellular maps, the key molecular drivers that disrupt angiogenesis and could serve as therapeutic targets or clinical biomarkers remain to be identified.

To bridge this gap between cellular atlas and functional drivers, we conducted an integrative multiomics study to identify central regulators of AVF failure. Combining transcriptomic analyses with machine learning, single-cell RNA sequencing, functional validation, and clinical correlation in a prospective cohort, we identified ALPL, which encodes TNSALP, as a candidate gene associated with AVF failure. Our findings suggest a potential link between TNSALP, impaired angiogenesis, and serum alkaline phosphatase, a routinely available clinical parameter with potential implications for preoperative risk stratification.

Methods

Data Acquisition and Processing

To establish a transcriptomic profile of AVF maturation, gene expression datasets from vascular tissues were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus database, specifically GSE220796 (n = 20; 9 mature AVFs, 11 failed) and GSE119296 (n = 19; 9 mature AVFs, 10 failed). Raw integer counts were used as input for DESeq2 package (v1.46.0, Bioconductor, Seattle, WA), and batch-adjusted matrices were not used for differential expression genes testing. For quality-control visualization, count data were transformed using variance-stabilizing transformation. Batch-adjusted matrices generated from the variance-stabilized values using the limma package19 were used for visualization only, including principal component analysis, and were not used as direct input for DESeq2.20 Genes with an adjusted P < 0.05 and an absolute log2 fold change (|FC|) > 0.5 were considered differentially expressed. Volcano plots were generated to visualize differential expression genes.

WGCNA

To identify coexpressed gene modules associated with AVF failure, we performed WGCNA21 to construct scale-free topological networks using WGCNA R package (v1.72). A scale-free topological network was constructed using the most variable genes across all samples. The optimal soft-thresholding power (β = 6) was selected based on the scale-free topology criterion (R2 > 0.85). Module-trait relationships were assessed to identify modules significantly associated with AVF failure. The detailed protocol is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Functional Enrichment Analysis

To explore the biological functions and pathways dysregulated in AVF failure, differential expression genes were filtered using more stringent criteria (|FC| > 1.0 and adjusted P < 0.05) to ensure biological relevance. Functional annotation was performed using Gene Ontology term analysis and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes pathway enrichment using the clusterProfiler package (v4.10.1).22 Additionally, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was conducted using the Gene Ontology: Biological Process database to identify pathways and processes associated with AVF failure.23

Machine Learning-Based Hub Gene Identification

To robustly identify the most critical genes for AVF failure diagnosis, the following 8 distinct machine learning algorithms were employed: Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator regression, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine-Recursive Feature Elimination, Elastic Net, Ridge regression, Generalized Linear Model, Partial Least Squares Generalized Linear Model, and Recursive Partitioning and Regression Trees.24, 25, 26 The detailed protocol is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Immune Cell Infiltration Analysis

To characterize the immune microenvironment differences between mature and failed AVFs, the deconvolution module of the “Immuno-Oncology Biological Research” R package (v0.99.0) was used to estimate the landscape of the immune cell infiltration. The tool integrates the following 7 open-source deconvolution methodologies: Cell-type identification by estimating relative subsets of known RNA transcripts, microenvironment cell populations counter, estimating the proportion of immune and cancer cells, xCell, estimate, quantification of immune cell intiltration in tumor sequences, and tumor immune estimation resource.27

Single-Cell RNA Sequencing Analysis

To delineate the cellular heterogeneity and pinpoint the cellular source of key genes in vascular tissues, raw single-cell RNA-seq data from preoperative vascular tissues were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus database (GSE250469; 3 arteries and 3 veins).28 The 10X Genomics-derived libraries were processed using the Seurat package (v4.0; Satija Lab, New York, NY).29 Doublets were removed with the DoubletFinder package (v2.0.4).30 Cell types were annotated based on established marker genes. The detailed protocol is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Intercellular Communication Analysis

To infer critical ligand-receptor interactions and signaling pathways between cell populations, we performed CellChat analysis using the vein-derived subset.31 The detailed protocol is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Clinical Cohort and Data Collection

To validate our bioinformatic findings in a well-characterized patient cohort, this prospective cohort study (NCT07058441) was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Institutional Review Board, Medical Ethics Committee of Shanghai Tenth People’s Hospital of Tongji University. All participants provided written informed consent for sample utilization. Consecutive patients undergoing initial AVF creation were enrolled, and clinical specimens consisted of cephalic vein tissues collected intraoperatively during initial AVF creation surgery. Based on 3-month postoperative functional outcomes, samples were categorized into 2 groups as follows: the maturation failure group and the maturation success group (according to the Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative clinical practice guidelines for vascular access).32 Demographic characteristics, comorbidities, preoperative laboratory parameters, discharge diagnoses, and perioperative medication records were extracted from the hospital information system. Liver disease was defined as documented hepatobiliary disorders in the discharge diagnosis record, including viral hepatitis, chronic liver dysfunction, cirrhosis, or biliary tract disease. CKD-mineral and bone disorder (MBD)-related diagnosis was defined according to Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes-based clinical concepts and included renal osteodystrophy, secondary hyperparathyroidism, hyperphosphatemia, hypocalcemia, or related CKD-MBD disorders recorded in the discharge diagnosis.33 Medication exposure was extracted from perioperative medication records. Phosphate binder use was defined as documented preoperative use of oral phosphate-lowering binders prescribed for CKD-MBD management, including sevelamer, lanthanum carbonate, or calcium-based binders. Active vitamin D use was defined as documented preoperative use of calcitriol or alfacalcidol. These medication categories were classified according to guideline-based CKD-MBD management concepts.34 All tissue specimens were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen within 30 minutes postresection and stored at −80 °C until further analysis.

Western Blot

To validate the TNSALP protein abundance corresponding to the transcriptomic ALPL signal, Western blot was performed as previously described.35 The detailed protocol is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Immunofluorescence

To investigate the spatial distribution and colocalization of TNSALP with selected protein markers, immunofluorescence was performed on vascular tissue sections. The detailed protocol is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay

To quantitatively measure tissue TNSALP protein levels in venous tissues and correlate them with serum ALP, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay was performed on protein lysate from cephalic vein tissue of 39 patients undergoing AVF surgery. The detailed protocol is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Cell Culture and Cell Viability Assay

To assess the direct effect of TNSALP on endothelial cell function and rule out cytotoxic effects, primary human umbilical vein endothelial cells (Cat. DFSC-EC-01) were obtained from the National Stem Cell Translational Resource Center (Shanghai Zhong Qiao Xin Zhou Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China). Cells were treated with recombinant human TNSALP at 2.5 or 5 μg/mL for 24 hours (MedChemExpress, Cat. HY-P7880, Monmouth Junction, NJ). Cell viability was assessed using a Cell Counting Kit-8 assay according to the manufacturer’s instructions (Biological Hope, Cat. P2420-500, China). The detailed protocol is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Tube Formation Assay

To functionally validate the antiangiogenic effect of TNSALP suggested by bioinformatic analyses, a tube formation assay was performed as previously described.36 Briefly, human umbilical vein endothelial cells were treated with recombinant human TNSALP at 0, 0.5, 2.5, or 5 μg/mL and seeded on Matrigel to assess capillary-like structure formation. The detailed protocol is provided in the Supplementary Methods.

Statistical Analysis

In all logistic regression analyses, the primary binary outcome was defined as successful AVF maturation (= 1) and maturation failure (= 0). Preoperative serum ALP concentration was modeled as a continuous variable per 10 U/l increment. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to evaluate the independent association between preoperative serum ALP and the odds of successful AVF maturation. Four sequential logistic regression models were constructed to progressively adjust for potential confounders. Bioinformatics analyses of public databases were implemented in R (v4.3.0; R Foundation, Vienna, Austria). All experimental data were subjected to statistical analysis and visualized using GraphPad Prism (v9, GraphPad Software, Boston, MA). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± SD or median (Q1, Q3) and were analyzed using Student’s t-test or the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Categorical variables are expressed as frequencies and proportions and were analyzed using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Multivariate-adjusted logistic regression models were employed to evaluate the independent association of serum ALP with successful AVF maturation. Model 1 was adjusted for sex and age. Model 2 included the covariates from Model 1 plus hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and cardiovascular disease. Model 3 included the covariates from Model 2 plus liver disease, CKD-MBD-related diagnosis, phosphate binder use, and active vitamin D use. Model 4 included all covariates from Models 1, 2, and 3 for full adjustment. Adjusted ORs with 95% CIs are reported. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

To systematically characterize the molecular landscape of AVF maturation failure, we first analyzed the publicly available transcriptomic datasets of vascular tissues from Gene Expression Omnibus. After batch effect correction for visualization (Supplementary Figure S1), we identified 5577 genes upregulated in failed AVFs and 1236 genes upregulated in mature AVFs (Figure 1a, Table 1), indicating pronounced transcriptomic dysregulation in AVF maturation failure. Functional enrichment analysis and gene set enrichment analysis revealed dysregulation of biological processes in failed AVFs, including unexpected activation of sensory perception pathways, whereas DNA repair pathways were downregulated in mature AVFs (Figure 1b, c). Machine Learning-Based Prioritization of Candidate Genes Associated With AVF Maturation Failure

To prioritize candidate genes within the yellow module associated with AVF failure, we applied 8 machine learning algorithms as an exploratory feature-selection strategy. Six genes (ALPL, FGD3, ADGRE3, LINC00639, OSCAR, and SSUH2) were consistently prioritized across the algorithms (Figure 3a, b; Table S1). ALPL showed the highest selection frequency. Multivariable logistic regression analysis demonstrated significant association between these prioritized genes and AVF maturation failure (Figure 3c). Expression analysis revealed that ALPL, FGD3, ADGRE3, LINC00639, and SSUH2 were upregulated in failed AVFs compared with mature AVFs (Figure 3d). Receiver operating characteristic analysis of individual candidate genes showed modest discriminatory ability, with ALPL exhibiting the highest AUC among them (Figure 3e). Exploratory internal assessment of the 6-gene candidate panel, including combined receiver operating characteristic curve, nomogram, calibration curve, and decision curve analysis, is provided in Supplementary Figure S3. Clinical and Functional Validation of the TNSALP–ALP Axis

To validate the clinical relevance of the TNSALP–ALP axis in a prospective cohort of 55 patients of hemodialysis undergoing initial AVF creation (18 failure, 37 successful maturation), preoperative serum ALP levels were significantly higher in the failure group (94.0 [78.4–104.0] vs. 70.0 [57.8–97.0] U/l, P = 0.010; Table 2). In multivariable logistic regression analysis, higher preoperative serum ALP was independently associated with lower odds of successful AVF maturation (adjusted OR = 0.75 per 10 U/l increment, 95% CI: 0.55–0.98, P = 0.048 in the fully adjusted Model 4; Table 3). In an exploratory receiver operating characteristic analysis, preoperative serum ALP showed modest discriminatory performance for AVF maturation failure in the prospective cohort (Area under the curve = 0.718; Supplementary Figure S7). Western blot analysis confirmed higher TNSALP protein levels in AVF tissues from the failure group compared with the mature group (Figure 6a–b). Representative immunofluorescence images demonstrated colocalization of ALPL with PLA2G2A+ in the stromal region of venous tissue (Figure 6c). Tissue TNSALP protein levels were positively correlated with preoperative serum ALP (r = 0.38, P = 0.018; Figure 6d). ALPL expression showed no significant difference in postoperative vascular tissues between the maturation failure and successful maturation groups (Supplementary Figure S4). Recombinant human TNSALP protein dose-dependently suppressed endothelial tube formation, without affecting cell viability (Figure 6e–f, Supplementary Figure S8). Discussion

This study suggests that upregulation of ALPL in PLA2G2A+ fibroblasts is associated with AVF maturation failure, and that elevated preoperative serum ALP is associated with lower odds of successful maturation, supporting serum ALP as a candidate biomarker for preoperative risk stratification. The upregulation of ALPL was linked to impaired endothelial angiogenesis and enhanced neutrophil infiltration, connecting specific fibroblast activation, inflammation, and failed vascular remodeling. These findings highlight the translational potential of the ALPL–ALP axis in AVF outcomes.

Our findings align with the emerging understanding of AVF pathophysiology as a state of persistent inflammation and dysregulated extracellular matrix remodeling driven by specific fibroblast subsets and immune-vascular crosstalk.18 Within this established pathological context, our single-cell analysis suggested that ALPL expression was enriched in PLA2G2A+ fibroblasts, a population implicated in vascular calcification.37 Although ALPL is well-characterized in osteoblasts and vascular smooth muscle cells, evidence suggests that fibroblasts under uremic or inflammatory stress can adopt an osteogenic-like phenotype and express ALPL, promoting calcific and antiangiogenic remodeling.38, 39, 40 Our findings extend this concept to AVF failure and suggest that ALPL-expressing fibroblasts may contribute to a procalcific, inflammatory, and antiangiogenic microenvironment that impedes venous adaptation. Complement activation and neutrophil infiltration may represent biological processes associated with ALPL expression, although their causal relationship with ALPL signaling remains to be determined.41 The in vitro tube-formation assay provided preliminary functional support for an inhibitory effect of recombinant ALPL on endothelial angiogenic behavior without evidence of overt cytotoxicity. Although these experiments provide preliminary functional support for an antiangiogenic role of ALPL, the precise molecular mechanisms warrant further investigation. We speculate that ALPL, as a potent ectophosphatase, may suppress angiogenesis through the following multiple potential pathways: (i) the hydrolysis of extracellular ATP, a known purinergic signaling molecule that promotes endothelial cell migration and proliferation42; (ii) the dephosphorylation of phospho-substrates critical for proangiogenic signaling, such as the phosphorylated form of lysophosphatidic acid43; and/or (iii) by altering the local phosphate/pyrophosphate balance, thereby potentially inducing a procalcific microenvironment that is inimical to endothelial function. The interplay between ALPL’s role in mineral metabolism and its newly identified anti-angiogenic function presents a compelling avenue for future research.44

Although tissue ALPL provides molecular context for AVF maturation failure, serum ALP may serve as a clinically accessible candidate marker. Serum ALP is a widely available, inexpensive biomarker routinely measured in patients with CKD. Large-scale studies have established that elevated ALP predicts cardiovascular events, vascular calcification, and mortality in CKD and hemodialysis cohorts,40,45 with recent prospective data reinforcing its value for mortality prediction in patients of hemodialysis.46 Our findings suggest that serum ALP may also be relevant to AVF maturation outcome. We found that preoperative serum ALP correlated with tissue ALPL expression and was independently associated with lower odds of successful AVF maturation in multivariable models. The modest tissue-serum correlation (r = 0.38) is anticipated, given the systemic contributions to circulating ALP from bone and liver isoforms, yet the significant association supports its potential as a noninvasive indicator of localized ALPL-driven pathology. Collectively, these findings help bridge the gap between molecular profiling and clinical application.

Several limitations of this study warrant consideration. First, given the modest cohort size, we were unable to perform extensive sensitivity analyses excluding patients with liver or bone pathology; larger studies will be required to fully address residual confounding and to validate the predictive performance of serum ALP and establish robust clinical cut-offs. Second, although the marked asymmetry in differentially expressed genes may reflect a strong biological program associated with AVF failure, we cannot exclude residual technical heterogeneity inherent to integrated public datasets. Third, the incremental predictive value of serum ALP over established clinical parameters (e.g., vein diameter and diabetes) requires further evaluation. Fourth, although we adjusted for key confounders including parameters of mineral metabolism, the possibility of residual confounding from unmeasured factors (e.g., systemic inflammation markers, specific medication use, or precise uremic toxin levels) cannot be excluded. Fifth, although these experiments provide preliminary functional support for an inhibitory effect of ALPL on angiogenesis, future studies incorporating enzymatic activity assays, heat-inactivated controls, and substrate-conversion measurements will be required to confirm mechanism. Finally, owing to the modest sample size and lack of nested cross-validation, these area under the curve values are likely optimistic and require external validation.

Conclusion

In summary, our integrative multiomics approach identifies ALPL as a candidate mediator associated with AVF maturation failure and suggests a potential link among PLA2G2A+ fibroblast activation, impaired endothelial angiogenesis, and modulation of the vascular immune microenvironment. The association between preoperative serum ALP and AVF outcomes, coupled with its correlation with tissue ALPL, supports further evaluation of serum ALP as a candidate preoperative risk marker. Our findings thus provide a rationale for larger multicenter validation studies and mechanistic investigations to clarify whether ALPL may contribute to maladaptive vascular remodeling and whether the ALPL–ALP axis may have potential for clinically useful risk stratification or therapeutic strategies.

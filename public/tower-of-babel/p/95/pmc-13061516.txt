Efficacy of Combined Gastric Peroral Endoscopic Myotomy and Transpyloric Stenting: A Case Report and Review of the Literature
Authors: Jang Hyun Kim, Henry Jen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA 65-year-old man with a history of idiopathic gastroparesis underwent successful gastric peroral endoscopic myotomy (G-POEM) with simultaneous transpyloric stenting with complete symptom resolution and normalization of Gastroparesis Cardinal Symptom Index with long-term relief. There are currently no data on the concurrent use of these 2 endoscopic techniques. Through this case, we postulate that transpyloric stenting alters the natural healing of the myotomy, preventing fibrosis and restenosis of the gastric outlet, increasing efficacy and long-term durability of G-POEM.

INTRODUCTION

Gastroparesis is a chronic gastrointestinal motility disorder that affects 22 per 100,000 persons in the United States and is most commonly caused by diabetic, postsurgical, drug-induced, and idiopathic etiologies.1 Gastric peroral endoscopic myotomy (G-POEM) and transpyloric stenting (TPS) are 2 endoscopic therapies for those who fail medical therapies for gastroparesis. G-POEM alone is limited with clinical success rate up to 71% in the literature with a decline in response over time2 whereas TPS is used often as only a temporizing measure.3 We present a case of a combined approach with G-POEM and TPS resulting in complete long-lasting clinical response.

CASE REPORT

We present a 65-year-old man with a history of squamous cell carcinoma of the tongue treated with surgical resection and chemoradiation complicated by oropharyngeal dysphagia and percutaneous endoscopic gastrostomy and known idiopathic gastroparesis who presented with progressively worsening gastroparesis symptoms with frequent nausea, vomiting, regurgitation, bloating, and early satiety symptoms. Initial Gastroparesis Cardinal Symptom Index (GSCI) was 2.22. Further work-up including computed tomography neck and chest, barium esophagram, and endoluminal functional lumen imaging probe (EndoFLIP) showed normal esophageal distensibility and emptying. Diagnostic esophagogastroduodenoscopy demonstrated evidence of suspected pylorospasm with resistance upon passage of the gastroscope through the pylorus with normal overlying mucosa. Gastric emptying study was attempted but unable to be performed due to patient's dysphagia and inability to tolerate the test liquid meals through the existing gastrostomy tube. Patient had symptoms despite being dependent on enteral tube feeding. He refused conversion to percutaneous endoscopic gastrojejunostomy or direct jejunostomy due to concerns for regular exchanges, which he already encountered with his current gastrostomy from extensive use for nutrition. Symptoms persisted despite transition to slower continuous feeds rather than bolus. Courses of promotility agents such as metoclopramide were attempted with minimal relief and quick return of symptoms. The patient then underwent empiric botulinum toxin 100 unit injection to the pylorus with improvement in symptoms, consistent with gastroparesis, but recurrence after 6 months. He was then referred for consideration of G-POEM for refractory gastroparesis. Patient underwent successful G-POEM consisting of double pyloric myotomy in posterior orientation (Figure 1) with minor thermal injury noted at the pyloric mucosa and bulb (Figure 2). The decision was made to prophylactically stent to prevent delayed complications. One 15 mm by 10 mm lumen apposing metal stent was placed across the pylorus (Figure 3), and proximal edge of stent was secured in place using 2 clips. Patient had no postprocedural pain and went home the same day with plan to resume tube feeding the next day. Post G-POEM and TPS placement, patient had complete resolution of his symptoms and tolerated tube feeding well without issue. Patient did have signs of post-prandial dumping syndrome for 6 weeks post G-POEM with fecal urgency and loose stools which was treated conservatively with fiber supplementation and resolved without any further intervention. Patient had his transpyloric stent removed 5 weeks post-POEM with a widely patent pylorus seen. Patient remained completely symptom free at 10-month follow-up with a GCSI score of 0.

DISCUSSION

Initial management of gastroparesis includes dietary changes and prokinetic agents and antiemetics, but for those who are refractory to medical therapies, intrapyloric injection of botulinum toxin, G-POEM, transpyloric stent, and surgical pyloroplasty are considered, as well as surgically implanted gastric stimulators. In particular, G-POEM has shown to improve both symptoms and gastric emptying while having fewer postprocedural complications and shorter length of hospital stay compared with surgical interventions. However, G-POEM efficacy varies significantly in literature with response rates ranging from 61% to 71%.2,4 In addition, limited long-term data on G-POEM suggests that it is less effective over time with a loss of response of 12.9% per year.5 The limited long-term efficacy has been hypothesized to be due to the combined effects of underlying increase in inflammatory response at the pylorus for those with gastroparesis and fibrosis of pyloric myotomy site leading to subsequent contraction and stricture of pyloric ring. Compared with G-POEM, TPS is a lesser known and studied endoscopic intervention for medically refractory gastroparesis. A retrospective case series from 2015 demonstrated symptomatic improvement in 75% of patients with refractory gastroparesis and in subset of patients, the study reported near or complete normalization of gastric emptying in 70% of patients.3 However, this technique has been limited by insufficient prospective data showing short- or long-term efficacy.

In this case, a successful G-POEM was performed and a transpyloric stent using a lumen apposing metal stent was placed. Patient's clinical course was notable for postprandial dumping syndrome. Dumping syndrome following G-POEM is rare; a systematic review from 2025 found that median incidence of dumping syndrome following pyloroplasty and G-POEM is 3.23%.6 We suspect that dumping syndrome in our patient was a result of the combined G-POEM and transpyloric stent efficacy leading to significantly decreased gastric emptying time which is not normally possible with standard G-POEM. In addition, complete resolution of symptoms and the widely patent pylorus seen on post-procedure upper endoscopy further support the significant effects of these 2 procedures when performed together. Regarding timing of stent removal, literature has no data on the optimal time for stent removal. In this case, it was removed 5 weeks after placement due to risk of stent migration.

In conclusion, we present a case of G-POEM combined with TPS with a high symptom response with maintained durability over the 10-month follow-up. We postulate that TPS post myotomy may alter pyloric remodeling allowing the pylorus to heal open thereby preventing excess fibrosis that can lead to restenosis and persistent symptoms. Further studies are needed to demonstrate long-term data.

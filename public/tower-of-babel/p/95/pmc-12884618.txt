DFT studies of metal oxide nanocluster as a possible drug delivery system for mechlorethamine
Authors: Shahnaz Azari gharelar, Shiva Masoudi, Nasrin Masnabadi, Mohammad Hadi Ghasemi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study examined the adsorption of the mechlorethamine (ME) anti-cancer drug loaded upon Mg12O12 nanocage using DFT: B3LYP (6-31G* and 6-311G** basis set) and B3PW91 (6-31G* basis set) calculations. To clarify the electronic, thermochemical, and structural properties of drug (ME) complexes with Mg12O12 nanocages, DFT calculations were combined with the Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules (QTAIM) study. NBO analysis revealed that the maximum stability energy of the electronic transfer of ME into Mg12O12 nanocavities originated from the LP(1)N27 to LP*(1)Mg5 transition with an E2 value of 17.63 kcal mol. Further, the maximum stability energy value obtained from Mg12O12 nanocages to the drug ME was owing to the electronic shift from LP*(1) Mg 5 to σ*C 31—H 41 orbitals compared to the drug/nanotube complex with E2 = 0.81 kcal.mol-1. Based on the QTAIM results, -G(r)/V(r) value ​​for the interaction between the H41 atom of the ME drug and the O13 atom of the nanocage [(C31-H41 (ME)…O13 (nanocage)] was about 0.37, indicating the covalent nature of the interaction. In the UV–Vis spectrum, the wavelength shift from 198 to 258 nm with the adsorption of the drug on the nanosorbent revealed a bathochromic change (red shift). The values ​​of ∇2ρ and ρ are associated with hydrogen bonds between atoms H41 and O13 (∇2ρ = 0.0602; ρ = 0.0208) as well as atoms O15 and H43 ∇2ρ = 0.0525; ρ = 0.0179). Thus, the interactions mentioned in this series are related to hydrogen bonds. Accordingly, based on the results obtained, Mg12O12 nanoclusters can be used as a promising carrier for ME drug delivery.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13065-025-01596-x.

Introduction

As reported by the world health organization (WHO), cancer is an important cause of mortality worldwide, with about 19 million cases of cancer diagnosed by the organization in 2020 worldwide [1]. With the estimates made about the increase in the prevalence of cancer, it is estimated that by 2040 there will be about 27 million cases of cancer [2]. Some cancer treatment strategies such as radiotherapy and chemotherapy-based methods have been predicted to improve patients’ survival, but these methods have disadvantages such as low concentration of drug to the tumor site, cytotoxicity, drug resistance, and non-uniform distribution of anti-cancer agents [3, 4]. The science of nanotechnology as a new discipline in nanomedical sciences, which is based on the nanoscale, can indicate new properties compared to its previous methods and has been mostly used in the preparation of therapeutic drugs and their delivery to the desired location [5]. Nanostructures cause the drug to be transferred to the target tissues by encapsulating the drug, since through this method, the level of toxicity is reduced and the transfer through biological barriers also diminishes [6–9]. With the expansion of the application of nanotechnology, nanostructures have become promising tools for controlled drug delivery systems. These systems protect drugs against degradation and clearance and are useful for the delivery of proteins and novel therapeutic origins such as RNA interference as well as gene therapy. These systems can prevent adsorption of DNA and siRNA by reticuloendothelial and prevent degradation of enzymes [10, 11]. By applying nanostructures as DDS, drug efficacy will be improved by enhancing the half-life of the drug, boosting the solubility of some hydrophobic drugs, as well as controlled release of the drug. Among the other advantages of drug delivery control by nanostructures, we can mention a great reduction in toxicity and control of the bio-distribution of drugs. With the discovery of carbon nanotubes, the demand for the growth of nanoscale materials has increased [12]. Nanomaterials have been employed in fields such as catalyst and sensor manufacturing, adsorption, optical applications, and electronic device [13–17]. In recent decades, improvement of effective drug delivery systems has been considered based on nanomaterials for anticancer drugs [18–20]. Among the nanostructured materials, nanotubes, nanoclusters, and nanosheets have been used the most in drug delivery systems [21–25]. Also, among the nanoclusters, nanoclusters like fullerene such as (AB)12 (A = Al, B, Mg... and B = N, P, O and …) have greater stability compared to the other various nanoclusters (AB)x [26–30]. Theoretical studies of several (AB)n clusters anticipated nanoclusters like fullerene such as A12B12 as the most stable nanoclusters [31, 32]. These facts indicate that fullerene cages such as (AB)n may be magic clusters, having distinctive stability when n number is equal to 12. Oko et al. [33] categorized nanocages synthesized from B12N12 and disclosed that B12N12 clusters are composed of 4- and 6-membered BN rings. Other studies on the B12N12 nanoclusters are accessible in the literature [34, 35]. If the atoms of the BN cluster are replaced by atoms of element groups of III and V, the clusters are estimated to be less compressed. Wu et al. studied the structure of (AlN)n (n = 2–41) cages and indicated that Al12N12 is the most energetically stable cluster in this family and thus would be a perfect fullerene-like mineral cage [36].

Metal nanoclusters (MNCs) are composed of metal atoms (n) and ligands (m) with the particle sizes of 1 nm to 3 nm, whose physical and chemical properties vary with the number of contained atoms. The spatial scale of MNCs ranges from several to hundred angstroms, which is close to a single atom or molecule, causing them to behave somewhere between individual atoms or molecules and large particles [37]. These nano systems have distinct levels of energy and therefore have diverse chemical, electrical, and optical properties when compared with larger metal nanoparticles. High luminescence properties, easy synthesis method, controllable fluorescence emission, high surface area, excellent quantum efficiency, and large Stokes shift [38] are the features of these nanomaterials. When MNCs enter different organisms in the body, they can damage the host by providing immunological responses thus causing toxicity [39, 40]. To prevent these negative effects, its biocompatibility properties can be improved by coating the metal core with a non-toxic shell. Recently, some metal oxide nanoclusters such as Mg12O12, Be12O12 and Zn12O12 have also attracted the attention of researchers thanks to their attractive properties. One of the metal nanoclusters with molecular structure is Be12O12, which has high thermal stability and is being tried to be produced in large quantities in the near future [41]. Based on the study reported in previous works by Haertelt et al., the stability property of Mg12O12 nanoclusters was proved using IR spectroscopic technique as well as density functional theory (DFT) analyses [42]. Another nanocluster with the structure of Zn12O12 has found various applications in the fields of catalyst manufacturing, biomedicine, optical and electronic equipment, as well as in gas detectors [43–45]. The most commonly used approach for synthesis of nanoclusters is reducing the metal cations located in ionic salts in the existence of organic ligands. Overall, MNCs offer an exceptional and versatile material for emerging colloidal frameworks through self-assembly monitored by surface ligands and metal cores for several applications including drug delivery and therapy.

Density functional theory (DFT) [46–48] is a systematic theory to study the transfer of active substances and drugs to the desired organism by drug delivery systems. In this study, the B3LYP/6-311G** level of theory was employed given its well-established balance between computational cost and accuracy. B3LYP is a hybrid functional within density functional theory (DFT) that has been extensively validated for a wide range of molecular systems. The 6-311G** basis set includes polarization functions on all atoms, enabling a more accurate description of electron distribution, non-covalent interactions, and polarization effects—factors that are critical when examining drug loading onto nanomaterial surfaces. Given the complexity of drug–nanocarrier interactions, especially those involving π–π stacking, hydrogen bonding, and van der Waals forces, this level of theory provides reliable energetic and structural insights while remaining computationally feasible. Thus, B3LYP/6-311G** was selected as an appropriate and widely accepted method to model the adsorption behavior of drug molecules on nanostructured surfaces. However, so far, very few works have been reported in the field of exploring the appropriate application of these metal nanoclusters-based careers in delivery of drugs [49]. Given the stability advantage of fullerene-like nanocages (no need for ligand or any other coating layer), the adsorption behavior of mechlorethamine, as an anticancer drug, was investigated in pure Mg12O12 fullerene-like cages. This stable nanocarrier was selected due to its unique structural properties and its potential applications in targeted drug delivery. Note that the Mg12O12 fullerene-like structure offers several advantages, including high stability, tunable surface properties, and potential for functionalization. Owing to its high stability, the nanocages do not require the aforementioned treatments and preparations related to coating with protective layers and can be used alone as a nanocarrier for drug delivery in the studied systems [50, 51]. Accordingly, in this theoretical study, we have focused on the performance of Mg12O12 nanoclusters for anticancer drug adsorption to investigate the ideal orientation for anticancer drug delivery. For this purpose, the interactions between metal nanocluster of M12O12 and the anticancer drug of mechlorethamine (ME) are evaluated based on TD-DFT theoretical study. So far, the adsorption of ME as anticancer drug on some metal nanoclusters such as silicon (Si76), carbon (C76), and aluminum nitride (Al38N38), V-Si76, V-C76 and V-Al38N38 nanocages has been investigated by computational methods [52, 53]. So far, there has been no report of the adsorption of the ME drug by fullerene-like nanoclusters. The key goal of the current study is to theoretically explore ME/M12O12 complexes to inspect the prospect of controlled delivery drug. The values of electronic properties, molecular electrostatic potentials, ultraviolet–visible (UV–Vis) spectra, adsorption energy, and density of states (DOS) of the systems were analyzed and calculated. Further, to identify the nature of interactions, the possessions acquired from the quantum theory of atoms in molecules (QTAIM) were exploited. Thus, based on the results found, Mg12O12 nanoclusters can be employed as a promising carrier for ME drug delivery.

Gaussian 09 program package [54] was utilized for calculations. To examine the mechanism of existing interactions, some key parameters such as adsorption energies, charge transfer (NBO), geometry optimization, dipole moment, molecular electrostatic potential (MEP), DOS, and frontier molecular orbitals (HOMO–LUMO distribution) were scrutinized and calculated. The B3LYP (6-31G* and 6-311G** basis set) and B3PW91 [55, 56] functions on the 6-31G* basis set are a reliable level of theory often employed for nanoclusters. The absence of any imaginary eigenvalue in the Hessian matrix indicates that the optimized structures are located in the local minima of their potential energy levels. Results and discussion

Mg12O12 properties

The equilibrium structure of the nanocavity as well as the corresponding DOS diagram are displayed in Fig. 1, so that the geometrical symmetry of the experimental nanocavity is clear in this figure [59]. The symmetrical structure of pristine M12O12 nanoclusters consists of six tetragons and eight hexagons with Th symmetry. The size of hexagonal and quadrilateral angles in Mg12O12 was estimated to be 86.6 and 111.8 degree, respectively. There is one M–O bond between two hexagons (d1) along with another M–O bond between one hexagon and one tetrahedron (d2). The d1 and d2 bonds varied from 1.88 to 1.95 Å in the Mg12O12 nanocluster, respectively. The values ​​of bond angles in hexagonal rings for O-Mg-O and Mg-O-Mg bonds were calculated to be 123.7 and 115.6 degrees, respectively. The size of these angles in the quadrangular rings was reduced to 93.0° and 86.6°. The strong ionic nature of the Mg-O bond in the proposed nanocages’ structure was examined by MEP and NBO analyses. In this study, the value ​​of charges calculated for oxygen and magnesium atoms was obtained -1.39 |e| and + 1.39, respectively. Also, to confirm the compliance of the calculated minimum energy for the obtained structures, harmonic vibrational frequencies were investigated at the B3LYP/6–31 + G* level of theory. Based on the evaluated harmonic frequencies, the structures were a precise fixed point on the potential energy surface (PES) with ranged values from 106.5 to 734.1 cm−1 for the Mg12O12 nanocluster. This study is in accordance with the results of Lee et al.'s study on the sensitivity of the considered nanocluster to acetone [51]. In addition to the maps depicted in Fig. 1, the top-views of the frontier molecular orbital (FMO) analysis of the Mg12O12 nanocluster are also illustrated. The distribution of HOMO orbitals on oxygen atoms is evident in the proposed nanocluster structure. Covalent bond lengths are longer in smaller rings owing to greater strain. Mg12O12 was evaluated to have ELUMO of -1.70 eV and EHOMO of -6.55 eV (Energy gap = 4.85 eV). Electronic properties

LUMO and HOMO orbitals play a significant role in charge transfer processes as well as chemical reactions between molecules. LUMO and HOMO energies are related to the ability of the orbital to accept and donate electrons, respectively. One of the major factors in determining the properties related to electrical transitions in molecules is checking the energy gap between the orbitals [60–63]. The LUMO and HOMO energy values ​​after loading ME on Mg12O12 nanocages dropped from 0.0156 and − 0.2348 eV in ME drug to − 0.0598 and − 0.2384 in A2 complex (by B3LYP). The values of energy gaps of the title molecular systems are outlined in Table 3. Also, the data of frontier orbitals for the ME molecule, nanocage, the values of energy gaps and DOS plots for the mentioned molecules are listed in Table 3 (Fig. 3 and Fig. 4). Based on the results, the energy gaps values of the ME drug, nanocage, and ME/Mg12O12 complex are 0.2503, 0.1781, and 0.1786 a.u., respectively (by B3LYP). The values of electronic chemical potential, energy gaps, and the global hardness of the complex have been reduced. Thus, low chemical stability and high chemical activity are the characteristics that can establish a soft system for the complex. Natural Bonding Orbital (NBO) analysis is the appropriate method of analysis to check the distribution of charges between acceptor and donor orbitals, and the nature of intramolecular and intermolecular interactions [68, 69]. In NBO analysis, the practical method in which optimal structures are evaluated is DFT [70–73]. Thus, it can be recognized that the stereo electronic effects are the crucial factors in the most main interaction between drug and nanocage. Indeed, the drug plays the role of a Lewis base and the nanocage serves as a Lewis acid. In this research, the interaction between nanocluster and ME drug was considered and to further examine the interactions, this interaction was compared with the others. In case of analysis of NBO, the imperative interactions from Mg12O12 nanocage to ME drug include LP*(1) Mg5 → σ*C32—H43 and σ*Mg6—O22 → σ*Cl 26—C32 transitions with E2 values of resonance energy of 0.55 and 0.34kcal.mol−1, respectively. It is inferred that the total electronic transfer from the nanocage to the drug is far less than the total electronic transfer from the drug to the nanocage. This comparison indicates the electron-donating activity of the drug and the electron-withdrawing activity of nanocages. The results of NBO analysis reveals that with the formation of nanocages-drug complex, both nanocages and drugs are exposed to each other's magnetic field, and the result of this magnetic field interaction of the compounds will have a direct effect on the electronic transitions of the drug molecules as well as the energy of these transitions in the molecular states of the drug. Conclusion

In this work, the electronic properties, adsorption energies of the ME anticancer drug on Mg12O12 nanocages in three configurations of A1 to A3 were examined using B3PW91 and B3LYP at 6-31G* levels of theory and UV–Vis spectroscopy. To characterize the electronic, thermochemical, and structural properties of ME complexes with Mg12O12 nanocage, DFT calculations were coupled with the Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules (QTAIM) study. The results indicated that complex A2 has higher stability compared to drug ME, Mg12O12 nanocages, and other complexes (A1 and A3). Considering the enhanced electrophilicity (ω) of complex A2 compared to pure drug ME, it was found that this complex has a softer system owing to chemical activity when compared with pure drug and Mg12O12. The results revealed that the degree of hardness, energy gap and electronic chemical potential of the system have diminished in complex A2, causing low chemical stability, high chemical activity, and ultimately a soft system. Based on the results of NBO analysis, it was found that the total electronic transfer from the drug to the nanocage was significantly higher than the that from the nanocage to the drug; this difference indicated that the nanocages acted as electron acceptors while the drug served as electron donor. According to the results of NBO analysis, the effect of the magnetic fields of the nanocarrier and the drug on each other caused a significant change in the energy of some electronic transfers of the drug structure. Hopefully, the results obtained from the creation of the ME/Mg12O12 complex can be effectively employed in the dissemination of drug delivery systems to cancer cells and be useful in reducing drug interactions and, essentially, side effects of the drug on the body.

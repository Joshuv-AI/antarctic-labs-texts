Agrarian transformation and rural community food security in the lower Gangetic basin: A household survey dataset
Authors: Ahsan Habib, Md Sayed Uddin, Jalihah Md Shah
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This data article describes a dataset derived from a comprehensive household survey aimed at understanding the dynamics of agrarian transformation and its impacts on rural community food security within the Lower Gangetic Basin, specifically along the Arial Khan River, Bangladesh. The survey was conducted as part of a doctoral research project and encompasses data collected from 250 households across a defined Mouza within the basin. The dataset provides detailed insights into various dimensions such as household demographics, land ownership and use, agricultural practices and transformations, socio-cultural and environmental factors, power dynamics, food security status, and the strategies employed to enhance food security.The survey employed a structured questionnaire comprising 78 questions distributed across seven distinct sections. Each section aimed to capture specific aspects of the household's agricultural and food security-related characteristics. Data collection was carried out from September 2023 to March 2024, following ethical guidelines approved by the University Malaysia Sabah, which included obtaining informed consent from all participants.This dataset is unique as it offers a granular look at the interplay between agricultural practices and food security in a region highly susceptible to the impacts of environmental change and economic transformations. The data are presented in a structured format across multiple Microsoft Excel sheets, which are comprehensively labeled to facilitate ease of access and analysis. The dataset is significant for researchers and policymakers focusing on agricultural development, food security, and rural development in South Asia. It provides a valuable resource for comparative studies, policy formulation, and in educational settings, aiming to address issues of sustainability and community resilience in agrarian societies.

Background

The dataset [1] was compiled as part of a doctoral research aimed at understanding the impact of agrarian transformation on rural community food security in the Lower Gangetic Basin, a densely populated and ecologically significant area [2]. The research was motivated by the need to explore the dynamics of agricultural changes and their implications for food security in the context of global challenges such as climate change and economic globalization [3,4]. The theoretical framework was influenced by studies highlighting the intricate relationships between land use, agricultural practices, and rural livelihoods [5,6]. The methodology involved a structured household survey designed to gather quantitative data on these dynamics, offering a robust empirical foundation to support theoretical insights into the socioeconomic and environmental transformations occurring in rural Bangladesh. This dataset not only supplements the findings of the original research by providing granular, localized data but also extends its utility to further empirical and theoretical explorations into similar socio-economic environments globally.

Data Description

The comprehensive dataset titled “Agrarian Transformation and Rural Community Food Security in the Lower Gangetic Basin” is a meticulously structured repository of data gleaned from a household survey of 250 households, instrumental in painting a detailed portrait of the socioeconomic landscape influenced by agricultural dynamics in the Lower Gangetic Basin area. We utilized a combination of simple random sampling and systematic sampling methods to select 250 households, providing each household an equal chance of being included. This method reduces sampling bias and ensures the diversity of the sample reflects the entire population. The response rate was 100%, with all 250 households completing the survey. To achieve this, researchers followed up with households that were initially unavailable, rescheduling interviews for 35 cases. No partial responses were recorded, as all respondents completed the full questionnaire, ensuring data completeness and reliability. The dataset is segmented into nine sheets, each a repository of interconnected yet distinct data points crucial for understanding the multifaceted phenomenon of rural transformation and its implications for food security.

Sheet-1, labeled 'Questionnaire,' contains a set of 78 carefully curated questions that formed the backbone of the household survey. These questions were designed to elicit a wide array of responses across various spectrums, ranging from basic demographics to nuanced details of agricultural practices and food security measures. Sheet-2, titled 'Sections,' functions as an organizational guide to the dataset. It outlines the seven substantive sections into which the dataset is divided, each corresponding to a critical area of the research study. These sections are dedicated to capturing diverse elements of the surveyed households, ranging from their demographic profiles to the subtleties of their food security strategies. This systematic categorization ensures that each dimension of the study is given its due focus, enabling a comprehensive analysis.

Moving through the dataset, Sheet-3, or 'Household Demographics,' forms the foundation, setting the stage by profiling the household members' age, duration of residency in the area, gender, marital status, family size, educational background, primary and secondary occupations, and household income. It is not merely a collection of dry statistics but a colorful tapestry reflecting the social fabric that binds the community. This demographic snapshot allows for a nuanced understanding of the socioeconomic gradients that shape the lives and livelihoods of the inhabitants of the Gangetic Basin.

Sheet-4 delves into 'Land Ownership and Use,' the material underpinnings of agrarian existence. It sheds light on the intricate patterns of land ownership, the extent of involvement in farming among household members, the land sizes owned or rented for agricultural purposes, and the stark reality of the land to which they are inextricably bound. This segment of the dataset is revelatory, illuminating the nature of possession and utilization that forms the economic backbone of these rural households.

Sheet-5 is dedicated to 'Agricultural Practices and Transformation,' reveals the primary crops cultivated, irrigation methods, access to electricity and credit, the adoption of High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds, the use of chemical fertilizers, changes in cropping patterns, and the impact of climate change and neoliberal policies on agricultural practices. This section captures the transition from the traditional to the modern, reflecting the households' resilience and innovation in the face of evolving agricultural paradigms.

Sheet-6 captures the 'Socio-cultural and Environmental Factors' at play, touches upon the traditional agricultural practices influencing current methods, the cultural underpinnings that determine crop choices, environmental changes, and the impact of social dynamics on the availability of agricultural labor. This part of the survey intricately intertwines the cultural ethos with ecological sensibilities, unraveling the deep-seated ties between the community's customs and their stewardship of the land.

Sheet-7 explores the 'Role of Power Dynamics' within these communities. In this section, the focus shifts to the influence of local political figures, government policies, corporate entities, and the sway of social hierarchy on agricultural resources. This section is a candid reflection on how power and politics permeate the agrarian landscape, molding it in ways that are as much about governance and influence as they are about cultivation and harvest.

Sheet-8, focusing on 'Food Security Status,' comprises an evaluation of food shortages, dietary diversity, primary sources of food, changes in food security over the past decades, accessibility of food items from the market, and the affordability of nutritious foods. It probes the sturdiness or fragility of the households' food security, portraying how these communities navigate the undulating terrains of sustenance and nourishment.

Finally, Sheet-9 outlines 'Food Security Strategies and Practices.' It addresses the indigenous agricultural techniques employed, participation in community seed exchange programs, adoption of sustainable farming practices, involvement in local food security initiatives, reliance on government or NGO support programs, practices employed to reduce food waste, engagement in crop diversification, use of traditional food preservation techniques, and reliance on informal sources of food. This last section highlights the ingenuity and communal cooperation that define the households' approaches to fortifying their food security.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

The data set titled “Agrarian Transformation and Rural Community Food Security in the Lower Gangetic Basin: A Household Survey Dataset” comprises meticulously gathered information from households located in the lower Gangetic Basin, particularly on the banks of the Arial Khan River. The overarching objective of this study was to dissect the intricacies of agrarian transformation and evaluate its influence on the food security of the rural communities residing in this geographically and economically significant region of Bangladesh.

Experimental Design: The experimental design was conceived as a cross-sectional household survey. The foundational unit of study was determined to be a 'Mouza', which is a local administrative region in the area of interest. To adequately reflect the demographics and living conditions of the population, the survey targeted household heads of families. This encompassing approach allowed for a diverse representation of experiences and perspectives regarding agricultural practices and food security.

Sampling and Sample Size: A sample size of 250 households was chosen from the official count of 692 households within the Mouza, according to the records of the Union Council updated in 2022. A two-fold sampling method was utilized:Simple Random Sampling: This method provided each household within the population an equal chance of selection, thereby ensuring the sample's representativeness. Instruments and Software: The survey was conducted manually through face-to-face interviews, using printed questionnaires. To conduct the interviews, the researcher directly visited the households and conducted interviews based on the respondents' availability. In approximately 35 cases, we had to reschedule as the respondents were busy. Following the collection phase, the responses were systematically digitized and cleaned using SPSS (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences) version 23, which provided a robust platform for data analysis and management. The data was then exported to Microsoft Excel, facilitating detailed segmentation into respective sheets for further analysis and review.

Data Collection Period: Data was systematically gathered over an extended period from September 2023 to March 2024, allowing for seasonal variations and agricultural cycles to be adequately represented within the dataset.

Data Management: The data underwent a rigorous cleaning process to ensure accuracy and consistency. The data management protocol involved:-Verification of responses for completeness and coherence.-Rectification of any discrepancies found during the verification process.-Consistent formatting to maintain uniformity across the dataset.-Segregation of data into sheets corresponding to the various sections of the questionnaire for ease of access and analysis.

The experimental design, combined with meticulous data acquisition and management techniques, ensured that the dataset is of high quality and reliability, making it a valuable asset for researchers examining agrarian changes, food security, and rural development dynamics in the context of the Lower Gangetic Basin.

Limitations

The sample population may seem biased, but we understand the importance of having a representative sample in survey research. To ensure the robustness of our data, the sampling method used ensured that the sample was representative of the larger population within the Mouza. Therefore, the data collected reflects the demographic and socio-economic diversity of the study area. With a sample size of 250 households, the study achieved a confidence level of 95% with a margin of error of 5%. This provides a reliable basis for generalizing the findings to the broader population. By visiting households directly and rescheduling interviews when necessary, we minimized the risk of response bias.

Ethics Statement

In this study, data collecting addressed ethical issues by guaranteeing that the respondents' information was utilized strictly for research purposes and remained private both during and after the research was finished. All participants in this study provided informed consent. For the local researcher, there are no national guidelines requiring approval from a local ethics committee or institutional review board (IRB). This study was part of a Doctoral Research in the Faculty of Humanities and Social Sciences (FSSK) at the University Malaysia Sabah (UMS), Malaysia. The research proposal and survey questionnaire were presented to the review board of UMS and received approval from the competent authority.

Comparative efficacy of zanubrutinib plus obinutuzumab versus last prior treatment in relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma: growth modulation index analysis from the ROSEWOOD study☆
Authors: J. Trotman, K. Bouabdallah, F. Bijou, D. Šálek, A.C. de Oliveira, Y. Song, E.C. Kingsley, C.R. Flowers, S. Liao, S. Huang, N. Shah, L. Dumartin, P.L. Zinzani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe phase II ROSEWOOD study (NCT03332017) compared zanubrutinib plus obinutuzumab (ZO) with obinutuzumab monotherapy (O) in patients with relapsed/refractory (R/R) follicular lymphoma (FL) who had received two or more prior lines of systemic therapy. Median progression-free survival (PFS) was longer with ZO versus O [28.0 versus 10.4 months; hazard ratio 0.50, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.33-0.75].Materials and methodsIn this post hoc analysis, an intra-patient comparative analysis was carried out using the growth modulation index (GMI) to analyze the efficacy of ZO versus the last prior treatment received by patients in the ROSEWOOD study. A GMI of ≥1.33 was used as a threshold for significant clinical activity.ResultsIn the overall population, median GMI was 2.7 (95% CI 1.6-4.9) in the ZO arm and 0.9 (95% CI 0.5-1.7) in the O arm. Analysis in subgroups of clinical interest in the ZO arm showed that PFS was longer with ZO than with the last prior treatment, and median GMI was ≥1.33 in all subgroups tested. Overall, PFS had improved (GMI ≥1.33) in >60% of patients with R/R FL who received ZO versus their last prior treatment.ConclusionsThis GMI analysis further supports the benefit of ZO as a novel therapeutic option for patients with R/R FL.

Introduction

Follicular lymphoma (FL) is the second most common non-Hodgkin lymphoma.1 While treatment advances have improved patient survival, FL remains incurable for many, with patterns of continuous relapse and shorter disease control intervals with successive lines of treatment (LOTs).2 In the randomized phase II ROSEWOOD study (NCT03332017), combination therapy with zanubrutinib, a next-generation Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor, and the anti-CD20 antibody obinutuzumab (ZO) was compared with obinutuzumab monotherapy (O) in patients with relapsed/refractory (R/R) FL.3 Median progression-free survival (PFS) was longer with ZO [28.0 months; 95% confidence interval (CI) 16.1 months-not evaluable] versus O (10.4 months; 95% CI 6.5-13.8 months) (hazard ratio 0.50, 95% CI 0.33-0.75, P < 0.001).3 The ROSEWOOD study led to the approval of ZO as the first BTK inhibitor-based therapy for R/R FL in multiple regions, including the United States, European Union, and China4,5; zanubrutinib remains the only BTK inhibitor approved for FL to date.

To inform treatment decisions for R/R FL, it is important to evaluate the comparative efficacy of available therapeutic options. However, high inter-trial variability in patient populations and the absence of consensus on the standard of care limit the feasibility of external comparisons. The growth modulation index (GMI) is a clinical endpoint used to evaluate the efficacy of oncology treatments by comparing PFS duration on the current therapy (PFSn) with that on the last prior therapy (PFSn−1), using each patient as their own control.6,7 In this post hoc analysis of ROSEWOOD, the comparative efficacy of ZO versus the last prior therapy in the treatment sequence was evaluated using the GMI.

Materials and methods

ROSEWOOD (BGB-3111-212; NCT03332017) was an international, open-label, randomized, phase II study designed to assess the efficacy and safety of ZO versus O in R/R FL.3 Eligible patients were aged ≥18 years, had histologically confirmed FL, and had received two or more prior systemic treatments.

Eligible patients were randomly assigned (2:1) to receive ZO or O in 28-day cycles until progressive disease or unacceptable toxicity. Details of the study design and methods have been previously reported.3

In this post hoc analysis, PFS was assessed by an independent central review and an investigator, and censoring rules were defined in the ROSEWOOD study.3 GMI was defined for each patient as PFSnPFSn - 1. Where the date of progression on last prior therapy was unavailable, time to next treatment was used to impute PFSn−1. If the starting date of the last prior therapy was unavailable, then no PFSn−1 was calculated and the patient was excluded from the analysis. A GMI of >1 suggests that the current treatment has provided a longer PFS versus the last prior treatment, and a GMI of ≥1.33, indicating a ≥33% improvement in PFS, has been proposed as the threshold for significant clinical activity.6,7 The Kaplan–Meier method was used to estimate median GMI and the proportions of patients within GMI intervals. The 95% CIs for median GMI and the proportions of patients within GMI intervals were derived using the Brookmeyer–Crowley method and the Greenwood formula, respectively.

ROSEWOOD was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice per International Conference on Harmonization guidelines, following the guidance of each site’s institutional review board, independent ethics committee, and regulatory authorities. All patients provided written informed consent before trial enrollment.

Comparative efficacy in the overall population

Median PFS with ZO (28.0 months; 95% CI 16.1 months-not evaluable) was longer than that with the last prior therapy (12.1 months; 95% CI 9.9-16.0 months). PFSn and PFSn−1 curves diverged early, and separation was maintained over time (Figure 1A). Median PFS with O (10.4 months; 95% CI 6.5-13.8 months) was similar to that with the last prior therapy (11.5 months; 95% CI 7.4-16.4 months). No separation was observed between PFSn and PFSn−1 curves (Figure 1B). Median GMI was 2.7 (95% CI 1.6-4.9) in the ZO arm, and 63.3% of patients (95% CI 53.3% to 71.9%) had a GMI of ≥1.33 (Figure 1C). Subgroup analysis

Subgroups of clinical interest evaluated in the ZO arm included patients with two or more than two prior treatments, immunochemotherapy or rituximab in their last prior treatment, disease refractory to the last prior therapy or rituximab, and disease progression within 24 months of initiating first-line therapy (POD24). Across all subgroups analyzed, median PFS was longer with ZO than with the last prior treatment (Figure 2), median GMI was ≥2.5, and >60% of patients had a GMI of ≥1.33 (Figure 3; Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmoop.2026.106942). Discussion

This post hoc analysis of ROSEWOOD generated comparative efficacy data for ZO in patients with R/R FL. Median PFS with ZO exceeded that with the last prior therapy, and intra-patient efficacy benefit was shown using the GMI clinical endpoint. PFS benefit with ZO was observed in the majority of patients in the intention-to-treat population and in all subgroups of clinical interest evaluated, with longer median PFS and a GMI of ≥1.33 in >60% of patients. As PFS in patients with FL is expected to decrease with each successive LOT,8,9 the prolonged PFS with ZO versus the last prior treatment, particularly in subgroups with characteristics associated with poor outcomes, demonstrates the clinical benefit of this regimen.

The European Medicines Agency considers intra-patient comparison of time to progression/PFS an acceptable metric of activity in previously treated patients.10 GMI has been used as an endpoint in observational and interventional studies and is correlated with favorable outcomes. While the number of publications reporting GMI has increased in recent years, few publications have reported outcomes in hematologic malignancies.11 In a post hoc subgroup analysis of patients with FL, Salles et al. reported a longer median PFS with idelalisib compared with the last prior therapy.12 Pooled analysis of four phase II studies in patients with soft-tissue sarcoma demonstrated a significant correlation between a GMI of >1.33 and higher PFS, overall survival (OS), and response rates.13 A retrospective analysis of a phase III study in advanced gastric cancers showed prolonged OS in patients with a GMI of >1.3.14

In this study, GMI was used as an exploratory endpoint and may suffer some limitations including the heterogeneity of disease assessment methods and evaluation intervals between the current and previous LOTs. Additionally, the GMI threshold of ≥1.33, corresponding to a ≥33% improvement in PFS, was originally proposed as an arbitrary threshold for clinical benefit in the solid tumor setting.

Immunochemotherapy and rituximab-containing regimens are the standard of care for FL as first and second LOTs and were the most common last prior treatments in ROSEWOOD. This analysis demonstrated significant clinical benefit with ZO for R/R FL following these regimens and supports GMI as a metric of clinical benefit in hematologic malignancies. To further evaluate ZO for R/R FL in earlier LOTs, the phase III MAHOGANY study (NCT05100862) of ZO versus lenalidomide plus rituximab in patients with one or more prior LOT is ongoing.15

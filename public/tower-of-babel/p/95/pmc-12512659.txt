Randomized clinical trial of the use of Propofol as a sedative agent versus spinal analgesia with bupivacaine in External Cephalic Version (PropoSpinECV): study protocol for a randomized clinical trial
Authors: Javier Sánchez-Romero, Luis Falcón-Araña, José Eliseo Blanco-Carnero, Joaquín García-Ferreira, Javier Herrera-Giménez, Diego Fuentes-García, Fernando Araico-Rodríguez, Rosa María Gallego-Pozuelo, Aníbal Nieto-Díaz, Catalina de Paco-Matallana
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundExternal Cephalic Version (ECV) is an effective procedure for modifying fetal position to achieve a cephalic presentation. ECV is usually performed with tocolysis and spinal anesthesia. Recently, propofol has been proposed as a sedative agent for ECV, showing promising results in observational studies. This clinical trial aims to compare the outcomes of ECV performed under tocolysis with either propofol or spinal anesthesia.MethodsThe PropoSpinECV randomized clinical trial is designed as a single-center, randomized, open-label trial. Participation will be offered to every pregnant woman with a non-cephalic presentation undergoing external cephalic version. Sedation with propofol and spinal analgesia with bupivacaine and fentanyl will be compared for ECV with a 1:1 allocation ratio. All procedures will be performed under tocolysis with ritodrine. The breech progression angle before ECV will be measured for all participants. The main outcome will be the ECV success rate. ECV complication rates and post-procedure pain will also be evaluated.DiscussionThe PropoSpinECV trial will thoroughly evaluate the efficacy of propofol in ECV. Additionally, this trial will investigate the role of the breech progression angle prior to ECV as a predictive variable for the success of the procedure.Trial registrationThe PropoSpinECV clinical trial is registered in the European Union Clinical Trial Database (EU CT number: 2024-510701-29-00) and in the ClinicalTrials.gov Database (NCT06449430) with the Clinical Trial Registry (2024-06-03).Keywords: Propofol, Spinal anesthesia, Breech, Pregnant, ECV, External cephalic version

Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

The external cephalic version (ECV) is an effective procedure for modifying fetal position and achieving a cephalic presentation that may allow for a vaginal delivery with lower risks than a breech delivery or a cesarean section [1]. Non-cephalic presentation occurs in 3–4% of pregnancies [1], and regardless of the delivery route, there is a higher fetal morbidity and mortality due to various factors such as prematurity, fetal trauma during delivery, increased risk of fetal hypoxia during delivery, and others [1–5].

The ECV has a success rate of 49.0% (47.0–50.9%) and a complication rate of 5.39% (4.53–6.26%) [6]. However, a series of non-modifiable factors have been described that significantly influence the results, including multiparity, BMI, gestational age, fetal weight, amount of amniotic fluid, relative position of the placenta, transverse position, previous cesarean section, among others [6–9]. Recently, breech progression angle has been proposed as a reliable parameter for assessment of fetal breech in the birth canal, but its role in ECV has not been studied yet [10]. In addition, other interventions that significantly affect the success rate have also been studied, with tocolysis and analgesia being the most relevant [11]. With regard to tocolysis, the use of ritodrine has been shown to be the drug that provides the best results in ECV, with a success rate of 56.8% and a complication rate of 4.3% [12]. However, when an analgesic technique was added to ritodrine tocolysis, the success rate of ECV improved significantly (66.7%), while the complication rate increased slightly (14.9%) [13]. The use of intravenous remifentanil did not show a clinically significant increase in the success rate (51.7%), although it did decrease pain after the procedure [14].

Propofol is a safe sedative agent widely used in outpatient procedures in pediatric, adult, and pregnant populations [15–17]. Propofol has a series of characteristics of special interest that favor its use in ECV, such as its rapid onset of action, short half-life, rapid recovery, and safety [17]. Despite the fact that the obstetric anesthesia section of the Spanish Society of Anesthesiology and Resuscitation (SEDAR) recommends the use of propofol in numerous outpatient procedures in pregnant women and emergent cesarean sections [18], only one study evaluating the use of propofol in ECV has been published [18]. When ECV is performed under sedation with propofol and tocolysis with ritodrine, it has a success rate of 61.1–82.5% with a complication rate of 5.9–6.7% [19, 20]. The recovery time required when ECV is performed under sedation with propofol is minimal, allowing for the rapid identification of certain immediate complications such as placental abruption, which is identified in its early stages by intense abdominal pain. The complication rate in ECV when propofol is used is lower than that reported with spinal analgesia. Although no clinical trial has compared propofol with spinal analgesia, this hypothesis and the differences in success rate have not been confirmed. Furthermore, propofol has other logistical advantages as it is used in numerous outpatient procedures, and its safety profile is known, as is its dosage. It should also be noted that the administration of propofol is less invasive and safer than spinal analgesia.

A study comparing propofol as a sedative agent and spinal analgesia with bupivacaine and fentanyl is needed to answer if both alternatives are similar.

Objectives {7}

The primary aim of the PropoSpinECV RCT is to compare ECV success rate when propofol is used as a sedative agent versus spinal analgesia. The comparison of ECV complication rate and post-procedural pain is proposed as secondary objectives. The assessment of the role of breech progression angle as a predictor of ECV success is also proposed as a secondary objective.

Trial design {8}

This is a single-center, randomized, open-label clinical trial. The study is conducted at the Department of Obstetrics and Gynecology, Hospital Virgen de la Arrixaca, Murcia, Spain. Participants will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either propofol or spinal analgesia. The trial began on July 4th, 2024, and plans to enroll 270 pregnant women. Recruitment is expected to conclude on October 31st, 2027.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

The comparator, spinal analgesia with bupivacaine and fentanyl, is the current standard of care in many centers for ECV with analgesia. Propofol, although less invasive and already used in outpatient obstetric procedures, has not been directly compared with spinal analgesia in a randomized clinical trial. This comparison aims to determine whether propofol can offer similar or improved outcomes with fewer complications.

Intervention description {11a}

On the morning of the procedure, patients are admitted to the obstetric day unit for a pre-procedure ultrasound to assess fetal position, fetal biometry, amniotic fluid volume, and fetal well-being. Breech progression angle will be collected following A. Youssef recommendations [10]. Patient weight and height are measured at admission. Fetal monitoring is performed continuously for 30–60 min before the procedure. Patients are instructed to empty their bladder, and intravenous ritodrine is administered at 0.2 mg/min for 30 min prior to the version attempt.

The external cephalic version (ECV) is performed in the obstetric operating room by a highly specialized ECV team consisting of an anesthesiologist, a midwife, and four obstetricians with experience in over 900 procedures [21]. Each procedure requires the presence of at least one anesthesiologist, one midwife, and two obstetricians from this core team.

All participants undergo a standardized preparation including 6–8 h of fasting and intraoperative basic monitoring (temperature, blood pressure, ECG, oxygen saturation). A thermal blanket (3M Bair Hugger model 77514) is placed under the patient. Oxygen is administered via nasal cannula at 2 L/min. Hemodynamic targets include mean arterial pressure > 65–90 mmHg and oxygen saturation > 95%. Atropine and phenylephrine may be administered as needed. Ephedrine is avoided dueto potential interference with BIS index monitoring [18].

Patients are placed in a 15-degree Trendelenburg. The cephalic version is performed according to fetal position using forward roll or backflip technique. A maximum of two attempts is allowed.

In a pilot subset of 20 patients, placental oxygenation will be measured using transdermal near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) via an INVOS Medtronic® sensor (Covidien Ireland Ltd.). The sensor evaluates a 1.5 cm3 tissue volume located 1.7 cm beneath the skin, providing continuous oxygen saturation readings every 5 s. Sensor placement follows the method described by Loubert et al. (10.2147/mder.s150472).

Regardless of the procedure outcome, all patients will be assessed for vaginal bleeding. Fetal well-being is reassessed via ultrasound and continuous cardiotocographic monitoring for 2–4 h in the delivery unit, after which the patient may be discharged. A repeat fetal RCTG is performed the following day for 1 h.

At 24 h post-procedure, patients are reassessed and complete pain evaluation using a visual analog scale and the validated Spanish version of the American Pain Society Patient Outcome Questionnaire, as described by Polanco García M. [22].

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

The procedure will be immediately interrupted if any complication arises, including but not limited to vaginal bleeding, signs of fetal distress (e.g., abnormal cardiotocography), or maternal hemodynamic instability. Patient safety and fetal well-being are the primary considerations for terminating the intervention.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

Adherence to the assigned intervention will be reinforced by standardized protocols and direct supervision. The anesthesia team will follow detailed guidelines for drug preparation, administration, and monitoring. Use of BIS monitoring ensures target sedation depth is achieved consistently in the propofol group. All deviations will be recorded and reviewed by the principal investigator.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

No additional sedatives, analgesics, or anesthetic agents are permitted beyond those defined in the study protocol under routine circumstances. However, in cases where analgesia or sedation is deemed ineffective, the anesthesiologist may administer additional anesthetic agents at their clinical discretion. Furthermore, the administration of any drugs or supportive measures necessary to address maternal or fetal compromise is permitted. All such interventions, deviations, or emergency care measures must be documented in the case report forms and reported to the clinical trial sponsor for evaluation.

Provisions for posttrial care {30}

Participants will be followed throughout the duration of the clinical trial, including scheduled assessments and safety evaluations as outlined in the protocol. While no specific posttrial interventions are planned, any medical issues arising during or after the procedure will be managed by the standard care services of the hospital. Servicio Murciano de Salud holds an active insurance policy that provides coverage for participants enrolled in low-intervention clinical trials, in accordance with national regulations.

Sample size {14}

A sample size of 270 participants has been calculated to compare two proportions: a 74.42% success rate of VCE in our center where Propofol is frequently used, as previously reported [19], and a 59.62% success rate reported in the meta-analysis by Goetzinger et al. [23] for a statistically significant difference of 14.8% with a 5% alpha error and a statistical power of 80%, using the methodology proposed by Fleiss et al. [24].

At the Virgen de la Arrixaca University Hospital, between 120 and 150 procedures are performed each year, so a recruitment period of 2–2.5 years is estimated.

Recruitment {15}

Participants will be recruited from pregnant women with non-cephalic presentation in the 3rd trimester ultrasound consultation (weeks 35–36 of gestation). Written informed consent will be signed in this consultation. Subsequently, they will be scheduled for a consultation at the Obstetric Day Unit and the pre-anesthesia consultation in week 36. The procedure will be performed following the usual protocol as published by Sánchez-Romero J. et al. [20].

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

Subjects will be recruited from the 3rd-trimester ultrasound consultation among patients who meet the study’s participation criteria. Once the pregnant woman has signed the informed consent for the study and it has been verified that they meet all inclusion criteria and none of the exclusion criteria, they will be considered eligible for inclusion in the study. Each patient will be assigned a numerical code consecutively as they are included in the trial. The allocation to one of the study’s treatment arms will be performed using simple randomization. To do this, a randomization list will be generated using a computer program (STATA) to assign patients to treatments in a 1:1 ratio following the method described by Doménech JM [25].

Concealment mechanism {16b}

A computer-generated, sequentially numbered randomization list will be used. To preserve allocation concealment, an internal secure web-based platform has been implemented. This system will reveal the assigned intervention only to the attending anesthesiologist at the time of the procedure, ensuring that other clinical staff and investigators remain blinded to the allocation sequence. This mechanism guarantees random allocation while maintaining the concealment of the randomization sequence until the moment of intervention.

Implementation {16c}

The randomization sequence was generated by the principal investigators using STATA software. Collaborating investigators responsible for recruiting and enrolling participants are blinded to the treatment allocation to avoid selection bias. Allocation to either intervention group will be performed through the secure web platform and will only be disclosed to the anesthesiologist immediately before the procedure. This approach ensures strict separation between enrollment and intervention assignment, preserving allocation concealment throughout the trial.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

This is an open-label trial in which participants and most clinical staff will not be blinded due to the nature of the interventions. Propofol is administered intravenously, while spinal analgesia requires neuraxial anesthesia, making it impractical and ethically inappropriate to blind participants or care providers. However, outcome assessors and data analysts will remain blinded to group allocation. Post-procedural assessments, including pain scores and complication monitoring, will be collected by personnel unaware of the assigned intervention. Data analysis will be performed using coded groups to preserve objectivity and reduce detection and analysis bias.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

All relevant data from trial participants will be recorded in the Clinical Research Document (CRD), which serves as the official case report form. Data collection will include baseline characteristics, procedural variables, and outcome measures such as ECV success, complications, and pain assessment. Collaborating investigators responsible for ultrasound assessments have been trained to measure the breech progression angle in accordance with the recommendations described by A. Youssef [10]. This ensures consistency and validity in assessing this potential predictive factor for ECV success. The Revised American Pain Society Patient Outcome Questionnaire (Spanish version), a validated tool, will be used to assess post-procedural pain at 24 h.

All investigators involved in data collection will be trained to ensure consistency and adherence to protocol-defined procedures. Data quality will be promoted through standardized procedures, real-time data entry verification, and periodic monitoring. CRDs must be completed thoroughly and legibly to enable accurate statistical analysis. Once the trial is completed, all CRDs will be reviewed and validated by the Murcian Biomedical Research Institute (IMIB) prior to inclusion in the final dataset.

Copies of the CRD and related instruments are available upon request from the sponsor and will be archived with the trial documentation.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

To ensure participant retention and minimize loss to follow-up, all enrolled women will be evaluated 24 h after the ECV procedure, regardless of the procedure’s outcome. This follow-up includes a clinical visit, cardiotocography for fetal monitoring, and administration of the validated pain questionnaire. The uniformity of this protocolized 24-h post-intervention visit ensures complete and consistent data collection for all participants.

Data management {19}

All relevant data from trial subjects will be collected into the Clinical Research Document (CRD). The CRDs must be completed fully and legibly to facilitate subsequent statistical processing. Once the trial is completed, all data collected on the CRDs will be evaluated for inclusion in the final report by Murcian Biomedical Research Institute (IMIB).

All documentation related to the study will be stored in the Investigator’s Archive, at the participating center, under the custody of the main investigator until the end of the study.

This database will be equipped with a double-entry system and filters to prevent and detect any type of inconsistency or error in it. Information will be validated through internal consistency checks, studying missing values. Data will be verified and corrected until the database is fully validated. Once the database is cleaned, the variables will be recoded, generating new variables (groupings, sums, etc.).

Confidentiality {27}

Once the study is completed, the documentation will be indexed and transferred to the general archive of the center, following the recommendations established regarding good clinical practice guidelines.

The promoter of the clinical trial (IMIB) will retain all other documentation related to the trial for the duration required by law. Confidentiality of data and documents contained in the archive will be ensured at all times. Data from all CRDs will be entered into a database created for this purpose and equipped with security margins and internal coherence rules.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Statistical analysis will be performed with Stata/BE 18.0 (StataCorp. College Station, TX, USA). Normality and homoscedasticity will be assessed for all continuous variables with the Shapiro–Wilk test and Levene test, respectively. Continuous variables will be compared using Student’s T-test and Mann–Whitney U-test, and categorical variables with Pearson’s chi-squared or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. For continuous outcomes, the effect size will be the mean presented with standard deviation (SD); for binary outcomes, odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals will be reported.

The primary endpoint (ECV success) will be formally tested at a two-sided significance level of 0.05. To control the type I error rate, a hierarchical testing strategy will be implemented: (1) ECV success, (2) complication rate, (3) post-procedural pain score, and (4) emergency cesarean section within 24 h. Testing will proceed in this order, and subsequent hypotheses will only be evaluated if the preceding test is statistically significant. All remaining secondary outcomes (hypotension, nausea/vomiting, breech progression angle, NIRS substudy) will be analyzed descriptively.

To further assess treatment effects, multivariable logistic regression models will be performed for ECV success and complication rates. Adjustment will include clinically relevant prognostic factors (e.g., parity, maternal BMI, gestational age, amniotic fluid index, placenta location) and variables with p < 0.20 in univariate analyses. The breech progression angle, a novel ultrasound measurement, will also be incorporated given that its role as a potential confounder or effect modifier has not yet been evaluated.

Interim analyses {21b}

Interim analyses will be conducted when recruitment reaches 60 and 120 participants, in addition to the final analysis at 270 participants. These analyses will follow an intention-to-treat approach and will evaluate safety outcomes (complication rate, emergency cesarean section after procedure, maternal hypotension, nausea or vomiting), recruitment feasibility, and data quality.

To preserve the overall two-sided significance level of 0.05, interim analyses will apply penalized p-value thresholds according to group sequential methods [26]. This ensures that statistical significance can only be declared if results are sufficiently strong at early looks, while the global type I error remains controlled.

Interim results will be reviewed exclusively by the independent monitoring unit of the Murcian Biomedical Research Institute (IMIB) and communicated only to the Trial Steering Committee. Investigators and clinical staff will remain blinded to aggregated interim data, thereby maintaining trial integrity. Any necessary protocol amendments resulting from interim analyses will be submitted for ethical review and approval prior to implementation.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

Additional analyses will include subgroup comparisons and exploratory models. Subgroup analyses will be performed to assess the consistency of treatment effects across relevant clinical strata, such as parity (nulliparous vs. multiparous), body mass index categories, and gestational age at the time of ECV.

Multivariable logistic regression models will be used to evaluate the predictive role of the breech progression angle on ECV success and complications. Interaction terms may be included to explore effect modification by treatment group.

Additionally, the incidence of clinically significant hypotension, nausea/vomiting, and emergency cesarean section within 24 h will be compared between groups. A pilot exploratory analysis of placental tissue oxygenation using near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) will be conducted in a subgroup of 20 patients and described with summary statistics.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol nonadherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

All analyses will be conducted under the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle, including all participants as originally assigned regardless of protocol deviations. Per-protocol sensitivity analyses will also be performed excluding participants with major protocol violations to assess the robustness of the findings.

Missing data will be handled according to the intention-to-treat principle. Multiple imputation by chained equations (MICE) will be applied to missing baseline covariates and prognostic factors if more than 5% of these data are missing. The imputation model will include maternal age, BMI, parity, gestational age, placenta location, amniotic fluid index, breech progression angle, and treatment allocation, as well as variables predictive of missingness.

The primary and key secondary outcomes will not be imputed; they will be analyzed as observed. In case of missing outcome data, both an intention-to-treat analysis with imputed predictors, and a complete-case analysis will be performed to assess robustness. In addition, sensitivity analyses (best- and worst-case scenarios) will be considered to evaluate the potential impact of missing outcomes.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant level-data and statistical code {31c}

The full study protocol and statistical analysis plan will be made available upon reasonable request to the corresponding author. Due to the sensitive nature of the participant-level data and the national regulations governing patient privacy and data protection, individual-level data will not be made publicly available. However, de-identified aggregated data may be shared with qualified researchers upon request and subject to approval by the sponsor and the ethics committee.

Statistical code used for the main analyses will be documented and can also be shared on request, provided appropriate agreements are in place to protect patient confidentiality and comply with institutional policies.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating center and trial steering committee {5d}

The coordinating center for this trial is based at the Obstetrics and Gynecology Department of Hospital Virgen de la Arrixaca, in collaboration with the Murcian Biomedical Research Institute (IMIB). The coordinating center is responsible for day-to-day trial operations, including participant recruitment, data collection, adherence to protocol procedures, and communication with regulatory bodies.

A Trial Steering Committee (TSC) has been established to provide overall supervision of the study. The TSC is composed of senior investigators from the clinical, methodological, and ethical domains: a member of IMIB, Dr. Sánchez-Romero, Dr. De Paco-Matallana, and Dr. Falcón-Araña. The committee is responsible for reviewing study progress, protocol adherence, safety concerns, and any proposed amendments. The TSC will meet quarterly throughout the recruitment and follow-up phases of the trial.

Additional support is provided by a local data management team and trial administrative staff, who ensure the integrity, completeness, and security of trial data. The data management team conducts periodic audits and supports the preparation of interim and final reports. Endpoint adjudication will be performed by the principal investigators in collaboration with the TSC in cases where outcome classification is ambiguous.

No separate endpoint adjudication committee or independent data monitoring committee has been appointed due to the low-risk nature of the intervention and the academic context of the trial.

Composition of the data monitoring committee and its role and reporting structure {21a}

The Murcian Biomedical Research Institute (IMIB), which operates independently from the trial investigators, is responsible for data monitoring. The IMIB performs regular oversight to ensure the accuracy, completeness, and integrity of trial data. Data monitoring activities include structured interviews with the research team every 2 weeks, as well as periodic audits of documentation and adherence to the protocol. Findings are reviewed independently and communicated to the Trial Steering Committee (TSC) for follow-up when necessary.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

Adverse event (AE) reporting will follow good clinical practice guidelines and applicable regulatory standards. All AEs reported by participants or observed by the research team will be documented in the case report forms (CRD), including onset, duration, severity, management, and assessed causality using the Karch and Lasagna algorithm.

Serious adverse events (SAEs) will be reported within 24 h to the sponsor and recorded in compliance with pharmacovigilance regulations. Follow-up will continue until resolution or stabilization. The IMIB pharmacovigilance unit will manage reporting to regulatory authorities when applicable.

All AEs, regardless of intensity or suspected relation to the intervention, will be recorded from the first study visit until 1 month after final follow-up. Unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSARs) will be reported through EudraVigilance according to EMA timelines. Pregnancy occurring during the trial will be reported within 24 h, and the participant will be withdrawn from the study.

Intensity (mild, moderate, severe), frequency (isolated, recurrent, persistent), and classification (related or unrelated) will be documented. Diagnoses will be preferred over symptom clusters whenever possible. All AE documentation will be reviewed by the principal investigator and included in the final safety evaluation. Safety monitoring will occur during and after the ECV procedure.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

Trial conduct will be audited at multiple levels. Every 2 weeks, the IMIB conducts structured data monitoring interviews with the research team to review trial progress and protocol adherence. In addition, an on-site monitoring visit is scheduled after every 15 participants are recruited, during which manually completed documents (e.g., signed consent forms, CRFs) are reviewed for accuracy and completeness.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g., trial participants and ethical committees) {25}

All significant protocol amendments will be submitted in advance for approval by the relevant Research Ethics Committee and regulatory authorities. Once authorized, changes will be communicated to all investigators and documented in the protocol version history. If any amendment affects participant safety or trial procedures, participants will be informed and asked to re-consent if necessary.

The Murcian Biomedical Research Institute (IMIB) will also be notified of any protocol modifications. IMIB and Carlos III Health Institute (ISCIII) will review compliance with updated procedures during their scheduled monitoring visits and annual audits. All changes will be reflected in the trial registries (EudraCT and ClinicalTrials.gov).

Dissemination plans {31a}

The results of the trial will be disseminated through multiple academic and institutional channels to ensure transparency and accessibility to the scientific community, healthcare professionals, and stakeholders.

Discussion

Currently, there are no published data comparing propofol as a sedative agent and spinal analgesia in the ECV. The only published data using propofol as a sedative agent in ECV is an observational study [19]. This observational study reported a promising ECV success rate (61.1%; 95% CI 52.5–69.0%) with a low ECV complication rate (11.5%; 95% CI 7.1–18.0%) [19]. A limitation of this study was the loss of information about the anesthetic procedure. Additionally, a lower ECV complication rate was reported when including patients lost to follow-up due to lack of anesthetic information (6.8%; 95% CI 4.2–10.9%) [19]. Although ECV under propofol sedation may be a reasonable alternative to ECV with spinal analgesia or ECV without analgesia, no RCT has yet compared this hypothesis.

Many interventions to assist fetal turning during ECV have been studied [11]. The use of tocolysis with beta-mimetics such as ritodrine, terbutaline, or salbutamol increases the success rate of ECV [12]. Analgesia was another relevant intervention studied. Endovenous opioids reduce pain associated with the procedure, but do not appear to have an effect on ECV success [14]. However, an increase in ECV success rate has been reported with the use of spinal analgesia [13, 23, 27, 28]. The observational study describing the use of propofol as a sedative agent in ECV reported a similar ECV success rate as when spinal analgesia was used [19].

Propofol is a reasonable alternative for ECV for several reasons: it is an inexpensive agent [29, 30]; it is a commonly used drug with proven safety in obstetrics [31]. Propofol also has a relaxing effect on uterine smooth muscle [32], which may be helpful to obstetricians during the ECV procedure.

In conclusion, the PropoSpinECV trial is crucial for properly evaluating the efficacy of propofol in ECV. Additionally, this trial will investigate the role of the breech progression angle prior to ECV as a predictive variable for the success of the procedure.

Risk factors for acute kidney injury in preterm neonates after noncardiac surgery: a single-center retrospective cohort study
Authors: Su-Jing Zhang, Tuan-Fang Fang, Min-Yi Lin, Nan-Nan Shu, Min Zhou, Hong-Bin Gu, Ying-Zhi Dan, Guo-Lin Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Postoperative acute kidney injury (AKI) is a common complication that is associated with chronic kidney disease, early postsurgical mortality, and prolonged hospital stays. Preterm neonates who undergo surgery are at risk factors for AKI due to underdeveloped kidneys. To date, little is known about the incidence and perioperative risk factors for AKI in preterm neonates undergoing noncardiac surgery. Preterm neonates who underwent noncardiac surgery between January May 1, 2020, and February 28, 2023, were enrolled in the trial according to the inclusion criteria. Both multivariable and logistic regression analyses were used to analyze the associations between characteristic data and AKI. In total, 106 preterm neonates met the inclusion criteria, and 25 preterm neonates (23.6%) developed postoperative AKI. Multivariate analysis revealed that the factors associated with AKI were gestational age < 32 weeks [OR: 4.88; 95% CI (1.23–19.42)], preoperative sepsis [OR: 3.98; 95% CI (1.29–12.28)], and intraoperative hypotension [OR: 3.75; 95% CI (1.26–11.15)]. Preterm neonates who developed AKI were more likely to have longer hospital length of stays (38 [18,69] days vs. 21[12,46]) and higher medical costs (93,181.6 [620450.0,173,219.0] ￥ vs. 58,134.6 [31015.1,97,224,1) ￥ than neonates who did not develop AKI. Preterm neonates who underwent noncardiac surgery had a high incidence of AKI. Independent risk factors for AKI in preterm neonates who underwent noncardiac surgery were low gestational age, preoperative sepsis, and intraoperative hypotension. Preterm neonates who developed AKI were more likely to have longer hospital stays and higher medical costs.

Introduction

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is prevalent in neonatal populations and is significantly linked to increased morbidity and mortality1. AKI has been shown to increase the risk of chronic kidney disease, contribute to early postsurgical mortality, and prolong hospitalization durations in both pediatric and adult populations2,3. A multicenter observational cohort study reported that 30% neonates in neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) develop AKI4. What are causes of AKI in neonates? Neonates exhibit immature physiological features including heightened vascular resistance, increased vulnerability to hypoperfusion, elevated plasma renin activity, and diminished reabsorption of sodium in the proximal tubules5, which is worse in preterm neonates. One fundamental question is whether preterm neonates undergoing surgeries is more frequently suffered from AKI.

It has been well established that neonates undergoing cardiac surgery are at the highest risk of AKI6,7. The reported incidence of AKI in neonates following noncardiac surgical procedures can reach 30%8. Severe necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) is a type of disease that often necessitates surgical intervention. The overall incidence of severe AKI was reported to be 32.6% in neonates with NEC9. Risk factor of AKI in neonates with a severe NEC was identified as outborn, antenatal steroids, and positive blood culture sepsis9. Little is known about risks of preterm neonates undergoing a noncardiac surgery.

Preterm neonates often couple with low birth weight. Both low gestational age and low birth weight have been established as significant predictors for perioperative AKI in this vulnerable population4. However, perioperative risk factor associated with preterm neonates undergoing a noncardiac surgery remains unclear. Given that preterm neonates determine an immature state of organs, those populations exhibit a susceptibility to developing sepsis, hypoxemia, hypotension, and congenital heart disease. Therefore, these characteristics linked to preterm neonates should be took into account for investigating risk factors for preterm neonates undergoing surgery. Although the prevalence of AKI is well established in neonates facing critical illness or undergoing cardiac surgery10,11, there is a significant knowledge gap regarding the incidence and perioperative risk factors associated with AKI in preterm neonates undergoing noncardiac surgical procedures. This study aimed to investigate and analyze the incidence of AKI and the underlying risk factors in preterm neonates who underwent noncardiac surgery.

Methods

Study design and population

We conducted a retrospective observational study at a single center where we gathered perioperative data from an electronic medical records system. Our cohort comprised preterm neonates born before 37 weeks of gestation who underwent noncardiac surgical procedures between January 1, 2020, and February 28, 2023, at Fujian Children's Hospital. This institution, recognized as a tertiary-class hospital in China, serves as a major referral center for neonatal cases and is affiliated with an academic institution. The neonatal population we examined consisted of a substantial number of preterm neonates with complex medical needs, often requiring high acuity and specialized care. This study was approved by the ethics committee of Fujian Children’s Hospital Review Board (IRB #2021KLR09069), and the requirement for informed consent was waived.

The inclusion criterion was preterm infants younger than 41 weeks of gestational age who underwent noncardiac surgical procedures in our operating theater. The exclusion criteria were defined as follows: infants with a gestational age of 41 weeks or older at the time of surgery, those who experienced cardiac arrest during the surgical procedure, those with a previous diagnosis of AKI or who required renal replacement therapy before the surgery, those who passed away within 7 days postsurgery, those who underwent two surgeries during their hospital stay with an interval of less than 7 days, those requiring sedation for vitreous chamber injection, those lacking preoperative creatinine data, those with severe cardiac malformations, and those whose data were insufficient for meaningful analysis.

Perioperative characteristics

The preoperative characteristics of the infants included parameters such as their preoperative weight, gestational age at the time of surgery, sex, history of asphyxia, ventilator dependency, presence of sepsis, congenital heart disease, hemoglobin levels, serum creatinine (SCr) levels, plasma albumin levels, C-reactive protein levels (CRP) levels, and serum bilirubin levels. Intraoperative characteristics included the duration of anesthesia, duration of the surgical procedure, mean arterial pressure (MAP), the lowest intraoperative temperature, oxygen saturation level, and the administration of blood transfusions and vasopressors. Postoperative infant characteristics included the SCr level, the duration of mechanical ventilation, the length of hospital stay, and the associated hospital costs.

Determination of Variables

The diagnosis of AKI was based on the modified Acute Kidney Injury Network (AKIN) criteria, which do not rely on urine output and instead utilize a change in serum creatinine (SCr) levels from a previously established baseline to determine the presence and stage of AKI (Table 1)12,13. The baseline SCr level was defined as the lowest recorded SCr value within the 3 days preceding the surgical intervention. The postoperative SCr level was defined as the highest recorded SCr value during the initial 7 days following the surgery. AKI was classified according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) stages, including AKI stages 1, 2, 3, or no AKI. The primary outcome of interest was the occurrence of AKI. Hypoxemia was characterized as a decrease in pulse oxygen saturation falling to less than 90%, as recorded in an anesthesia information management system (AIMS). Ventilator time was defined as the cumulative duration of mechanical ventilation from the postoperative period until discharge. Hypothermia was defined as a pharyngeal temperature below 35 °C, and hypotension was defined as a mean arterial pressure (MAP) below the infant's gestational age in weeks. Several variables were further stratified into subvariables as follows. Gestational age in weeks was categorized as < 32 weeks or ≥ 32 weeks. Infant weight in grams (g) was divided into three groups: < 1500 g, 1500–2500 g, and ≥ 2500 g. Anesthesia duration, measured in hours, was categorized as < 3 h or ≥ 3 h. Operation duration, also measured in hours, was divided into < 2 h or ≥ 2 h. C-reactive protein (CRP) levels, expressed in milligrams per liter (mg/l), were categorized as ≤ 40 or > 40 mg/l. Serum bilirubin levels, measured in micromoles per liter (µmol/l), were classified as < 60 or ≥ 60 µmol/l.

Statistical analysis

Numerical data are represented as counts or percentages. Normally distributed quantitative variables are expressed as the means with their corresponding standard deviations (SDs), while nonnormally distributed variables are presented as medians along with the interquartile range (IQR) denoted as p25–p75. To explore the relationship between these variables and the incidence of AKI, we conducted cross-tabulations using appropriate statistical tests. The chi-square test was employed for the analysis of two categorical variables with Yates correction when necessary, and Pearson's chi-square was used for variables with more than two categories. If any of the observed values in the contingency tables were less than 5, the chi-square test was substituted with Fisher's exact test. In the single-factor analysis, all variables exhibiting a P value of less than 0.1 were considered for inclusion in subsequent multivariate analysis via logistic regression. The results of the regression analyses are reported as odds ratios (ORs) accompanied by their corresponding 95% confidence intervals. Statistical significance was defined as a P value less than 0.05. The entire data analysis process was performed using IBM Corp.'s IBM SPSS Statistics software, Version 23.0 Armonk, NY.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study was approved by the ethics committee of the Fujian Children’s Hospital Review Board (Approval ID: 2021KLR09069). All methods were carried out in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations.

Results

Sixty-one neonates from the initial cohort of 167 preterm neonates who underwent noncardiac surgery between January 1, 2021, and February 28, 2023, were excluded from the analysis. The exclusion criteria were a gestational age of 41 weeks or older, a preoperative diagnosis of AKI, the occurrence of cardiac arrest during the surgical procedure, the administration of sedation for vitreous chamber injection, and a lack of preoperative creatinine data. This resulted in a study population of 106 preterm neonates, all of whom were included in the subsequent analysis. Among these preterm neonates, 25 preterm neonates were diagnosed with AKI, as depicted in Fig. 1. In this study, the neonates had a median gestational age of 35+6 (27+5, 40+6) weeks. Their median weight was 2180 (1618, 2625) g, and 58.8% of them were female. A substantial majority, approximately 79.8%, had congenital heart disease. Nearly half of the neonates, specifically 49.1%, presented with preoperative hypoproteinemia, while approximately one-third, or 33.0%, had anemia before surgery. Preoperative mechanical ventilation was required for 42.5% of the neonates. Preoperative asphyxia occurred in 12.3% of the neonates, and preoperative sepsis was reported for 20.8% of the neonates. The durations of surgery and anesthesia averaged 90 (65, 145) min and 160 (116, 215) min, respectively. The rates of intraoperative hypotension and hypothermia were 46.2% and 27.4%. It is important to note that only one out of the AKI patients (8%) received renal replacement therapy (RRT), which was administered in the form of acute peritoneal dialysis.

Risk factors associated with AKI

The risk factors associated with AKI, with a significance level of P ≤ 0.1, included a gestational age less than 32 weeks [OR: 5.88; 95% CI (1.80–19.19)], a birth weight less than 1500 g [OR: 4.26; 95% CI (1.16–15.62)], preoperative anemia [OR: 2.33; 95% CI (0.93–5.85)], preoperative hypoproteinemia [OR: 4.61; 95% CI (0.93–5.85)], preoperative sepsis [OR: 56.55; 95% CI (2.35–18.30)], intraoperative hypotension [OR: 4.15; 95% CI (1.56–11.06)], and the use of vasopressors [OR: 4.61; 95% CI (1.66–12.76)] (Table 2). Discussion

The present study revealed that 23.6% of preterm neonates who underwent noncardiac surgery experienced AKI. Notably, a low gestational age (less than 32 weeks), preoperative sepsis, and intraoperative hypotension emerged as robust independent predictors for the development of postoperative AKI in this specific population of preterm neonates. Additionally, AKI was associated with an extended hospital stay and increased medical costs. These findings underscore the common occurrence of AKI in preterm neonates, emphasizing the critical importance of preventing intraoperative hypotension to mitigate the risk of postoperative AKI, particularly in those neonates with a gestational age of less than 32 weeks and a history of preoperative sepsis.

In our study, the rate of AKI observed among preterm neonates was in line with the 30.3% prevalence noted in premature infants with a gestational age of less than 30 weeks admitted to NICU. However, this finding significantly contrasts with 52% higher reported prevalence in infants following pediatric cardiac surgery. Notably, the prevalence of AKI in this study is nearly four times greater than the equivalent prevalence found in neonates undergoing noncardiac surgery, which is estimated to be 8.3%11. Neonatal SCr levels are known to decrease at varying rates over a period of days to weeks, which can be influenced by gestational age14. The neonatal KDIGO definition hinges on establishing the baseline value by referencing the lowest prior serum creatinine level for subsequent comparisons. Inserting a urinary catheter is frequently impeded by the narrow urethra in neonates. Additionally, premature neonates display a limited ability to concentrate urine, and even during an AKI event, their urinary output might not decrease to be less than 0.5 mL/kg/h15. As a result, we opted for the AKIN criteria instead of the KDIGO criteria to define neonatal AKI.

In the present study, we found a significant association between AKI in preterm neonates with a gestational age of less than 32 weeks. This observation aligns with the findings of analogous studies conducted in neonatal populations16,17. Nonetheless, it is worth noting that Jetton et al. reported a somewhat different perspective, indicating that the incidence of AKI exhibited a U-shaped distribution when categorized by gestational age. They found the highest rates of AKI in the youngest neonates (48% in those aged 22 weeks to less than 29 weeks category) and the oldest neonates (37% in those with a gestational age of more than 36 weeks)13. We found that sepsis significantly contributed to the development of AKI, which has been reported in other shudies18,19. This observation can potentially be explained by the fact that sepsis triggers significant physiological alterations, including hypotension and systemic inflammatory responses, which has been suggested to directly impact renal function in some studies. Furthermore, a substantial body of literature has consistently highlighted the strong associations between intraoperative hypotension and the occurrence of AKI. As such, there is a growing consensus within the literature that emphasizes the critical importance of avoiding intraoperative hypotension as a crucial measure for preventing postoperative AKI20,21. Grocott's research findings demonstrated that interventions aimed at optimizing blood flow and maintaining adequate blood pressure during surgery, which may involve invasive hemodynamic monitoring, did not yield a difference in short-term mortality. However, they did significantly reduce the incidence of kidney injury22.

In some previous studies, low birth weight (< 1500 g) has been shown to be associated with AKI in infants20,22. In our investigation, we observed such an association in the univariate analysis but did not find it to be a significant factor in the multivariate model. This discrepancy may be attributed to the fact that infants with low birth weights are more susceptible to developing severe infections, necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), and hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE). Consequently, some of these infants might face challenging circumstances, leading to a decision to abandon or discontinue therapy. Another plausible explanation could be that the sample size in our study was insufficient to detect differences through multivariate analysis. Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) has been previously identified as a risk factor for AKI15. Unfortunately, the limitations of our study prevented us from conclusively establishing a causative relationship between NEC and postoperative AKI. It is conceivable that preterm neonates with NEC are at a greater risk of experiencing severe infections and preoperative hypotension, both of which can exert a more pronounced influence on renal function. Surgical intervention may mitigate the adverse effects of the disease on renal function, leading to a significant decrease in postoperative serum creatinine levels.

We wish to acknowledge the principal limitations of our study. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a small sample size, resulting in be difficult to eliminate potential biases. Consequently, the findings may not be fully representative of the broader population of preterm neonates in NICU, particularly as most preterm neonates in NICU do not require surgical interventions. Second, our definition of AKI did not incorporate urine output criteria, potentially resulting in an underestimation of the true incidence of AKI. Third, our study did not include an evaluation of the short- and long-term consequences of neonatal AKI, although such an analysis is of significant interest and warrants exploration in future research. Finally, despite our efforts to identify and quantify major potential covariates, there may be additional factors, such as exposure to other nephrotoxic medications, fasting duration, intravenous fluid regimens, urinary tract infections, specific urologic conditions, and others, which may have influenced our study results. In future, prospective research with a large sample size is needed to be conducted to demonstrate the association between those risk factors with AKI in preterm neonates undergoing noncardiac surgery.

Conclusion

Independent risk factors for AKI in preterm neonates undergoing noncardiac surgery were low gestational age, preoperative sepsis, and intraoperative hypotension. Preterm neonates who developed AKI were more likely to have longer hospital stays and increased medical costs.

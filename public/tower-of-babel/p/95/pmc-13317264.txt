Clinical anatomy of distal femur and its implications in total knee arthroplasty: a radiological study
Authors: Jakub Pękala, Monika Stępień, Jonasz Tempski, Maciej Jędrychowski, Jędrzej Bartoszcze, Konrad Malinowski, Przemysław Pękala, Tadeusz Popiela, Jerzy Walocha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoarthritis (OA) of the knee is a chronic disease affecting joints and its tissues, leading to gradual damage of articular cartilage and surrounding synovial tissues. The treatment of choice in case of end-stage knee osteoarthritis, total knee arthroplasty (TKA) is currently one of the most commonly performed surgeries. However, in many cases, as high as 20% there may be dissatisfaction, due to inability to recreate the normal knee biomechanics. For that reason this study aimed to provide the most comprehensive morphometric data of distal femur, as well as their implications in TKA.MethodsThe computer tomography (CT) of total 500 knees, randomly selected patients, without significant knee pathologies (i.e. severe osteoarthritis, traumatic injury, endoprosthesis) were included from 2023 to 2025 This morphometric data was imported and calculated in SPSS software and included intercondylar notch max height and transverse width, epicondylar width, medial and lateral condyle transverse width, femoral mediolateral and anteroposterior distal end dimension, as well as notch shape index.ResultsThere was no statistically significant correlation between the side of the limb and the morphology of distal femur. On the other hand all obtained morphometric parameters in relation to sex and age were found to have statistically significant correlations.ConclusionClinicians, especially orthopedic surgeons must understand the complex anatomy of the distal femur. It can provide crucial information during total knee arthroplasty(TKA) and especially during the positioning of the intramedullary alignment rod.Keywords: Femur, Tibia, Total knee arthroplasty (TKA), Knee, Morphometry, Computed tomography

Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) of the knee is the progressive, degenerative disease affecting the joint tissue leading to its damage,and even to severe loss of articular tissue and worsening of the symptoms such as pain and ultimately loss of mobility. OA affects over 500 million people worldwide and direct healthcare costs are estimated 1–2,5% national gross domestic product (GDP) [4]. The treatment of choice in the case of end-stage OA of the knee is total knee arthroplasty (TKA), which is one of the most common, cost effective procedures used in orthopedic surgery. Its goal is to replace the damaged knee articular joint with artificial components. It is most often suited for the patients with end-stage knee osteoarthritis. Its incidence is projected to increase substantially through the upcoming years due to many reasons, some of which include ageing, as well as increased obesity index in many populations [13]. That is why it is no wonder why it is predicted that the number of primary TKA procedures is predicted to grow by 85% (1.26 million procedures) by 2030 [7].

The TKA requires high accuracy during bone resection, especially in case of femur, to match the shape between cut-off part and femoral compartment.

Unfortunately, it is reported that almost 20% of patients undergoing TKA still report dissatisfaction after surgery [6, 8, 10].

This may be a result of many factors, if we exclude infection and periprosthetic fractures, the most dissatisfaction are due to inability to recreate normal anatomical conditions and biomechanics. The endoprosthesis and implants should reproduce physiological anatomy of the human knee joint, however this anatomy is highly variable and often influences the functioning of the artificial joint.

In the case of the malalignment it is usually strictly connected with component loosening, which increases the wear of the components causing the anterior knee pain [2, 3].

We should always take into account the sexual prediction during prosthetic design, as well as geographic one. The majority of current, most contemporary TKA prostheses are made, with the use of the data from Caucasians knees, which have been reported to be ineffective in Asian populations [7, 9, 11, 12, 15].

The aim of this study was to provide the most comprehensive morphometric data regarding distal femur and its possible correlations, as well as implications in TKA.

Material and methods

Data extraction

Two/three independent reviewers JP, MJ and JB reviewed a sample of 500 CT scans of lower extremities of European descent, which were randomly selected and automatically anonymized. The data was obtained from the Radiology Department of The University Hospital in Krakow with regard to the bioethics consent process. This study was performed in line with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of Jagiellonian University Medical College (1072.6120.288.2020). After preliminary screening 176 scans were excluded due to not meeting inclusion criteria(e.g. osteoarthritis, traumatic injuries to the knee, knee endoprosthesis or other knee implants). In total we included 324 limbs—125 male (51 under 65 years old and 74 over 65 years old age) and 199 female (68 under 65 years old and 131 over 65 years old age).

The data extracted in this study was based on distal morphometry of femur, therefore we provided the definition for each of the projections below.

The intercondylar notch max height was defined as the maximal measurement of the distance from the superior part of the intercondylar notch to a line defined by the dorsal aspects of the femoral condyles.

The intercondylar notch max transverse width was defined as measurement of the most extensive horizontal distance from the intercondylar notch of femur, at the level of anterior and posterior cruciate ligaments crossing.

The epicondylar width was defined as the widest distance between two epicondyle of femur.

Medial condyle transverse width was defined as maximal distance between the medial femoral condyle, from its inner surface to its outer-lateral surface.

Lateral condyle transverse width was defined as maximal distance between medial and lateral part of lateral femoral condyle.

Femoral mediolateral distal end dimension was defined as the maximal width of distal end of femur, from the most medial to lateral point.

Femoral anterioposterior distal end dimension was defined as maximal length of the distal femur, from the most anterior point to the most posterior point of the condyles.

Notch shape index the ratio of the intercondylar notch width to its height.

Eligibility assessment

Eligibility was assessed by three independent reviewers: JP, MJ and JB. 500 CT scans, randomly selected where displayed in order to measure morphometric data of the distal femur. Exclusion criteria involved: lower limb pathologies and implants (i.e. osteoarthritis, traumatic injuries to the knee, knee endoprosthesis or other knee implants). For each limb the measurements of the morphology of the distal femoral epiphysis were carried out twice, by three independent observers with a month's time gap between first and second measurement.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed by JP using IBM SPSS Statistics. On the basis of the measurements, descriptive statistics (t student test) and intraobserver reliability (ICC = 0,86) were calculated for the studied sample.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that there were statistically significant correlations between sex and age and all the morphometric parameters measured included in the present study. As for laterality and morphology of distal femur there were no correlations found.

The reliability of measurement was examined with the use of intra-class coefficient(ICC) analysis. In case of this study it was rather high (ICC = 0,86) indicating the very high reproducibility of the data.

In case of femoral mediolateral (ML) length and anterioposterior distal end dimension (AP) in male population were as follows 87 ± 3.7 mm and 66.1 ± 3.7 mm, as well as 76.5 ± 4.4 mm nd 59.9 ± 4.5 mm in female subgroup. Other parameters including notch shape index, medial and lateral condyle transverse width, intercondylar notch max height and transverse width, as well as epicondylar width show similar trend, with male population indicating higher distal femur parameters. These results are consistent with other studies performed [15]. Additionally it was confirmed that ML length was much higher than AP width.

As for the age similar trend was observed, however it was not so dominant as in case of sex correlations. In the case of the lateral condyle width, femoral AP end dimension and notch shape index they were 26.3 ± 3.5 mm, 62.7 ± 5.8 mm,1.17 ± 0.15 mm for the group under 65, and were much higher than for those over this age. This can be explained by the fact that, over the age of 65 majority of the group included female population.

All of the aforementioned data should be taken into account during the restoration of the mechanical axis in TKA and implant design, as it usually correlates with longevity, as well as clinical symptomatology. It can be easily explained as component malposition in either excessive varus or valgus will lead to uneven force distribution and higher failure rate [2, 3].

Many authors have concluded that correct alignment of the prosthesis was correlated with clinical success in TKA. Moreover, improper positioning of components during surgery may lead to early loosening of the implant due to impingement between the cam and the post in posterior stabilized systems [6, 10, 14].

For that reason femoral component sizing may be used, either as anterior or posterior referencing technique, all of which are based on anterior–posterior(A-P) diameter. The problem with the aforementioned technique may be due to high variability of the medial–lateral length of distal femur. Due to that there is a possibility in which by properly measuring AP data the surgeon creates either the under- or overhang, during femoral component selection.

The thorough knowledge of distal femoral geometry is necessary for correct rotation during femoral component TKA [5]. A proper understanding of knee morphology, especially distal femur can not only increase the success rate of TKA, but also improve the overall design of the implants [5, 9]. As we can expect not all implants are suitable for every population [5, 9]. Similar postulates can be made regarding the gender and specific ethnic groups [1, 5]. Our results correlate with all of the aforementioned authors, as the measurements and data reported in our study, showed statistically significant correlations between all morphometric parameters in relation to sex and additionally the age.

Lastly, the majority of the design in case of TKA implants is based on the anatomy of Caucasian population [11], therefore we should always keep in mind different factors, which may result in TKA failure.

At the time of conducting this study, authors found it to be the first one to pool all the distance measurements of distal femur, as well as provide background and specific implications on TKA in Polish populations.

The main limitations of this study is sole domination of radiological data and possible differences in measurements during data collection. Additionally, patients with early stage OA may exhibit local thickening, resulting in similar to physiological values of femoral thickness.

Conclusion

We found that there were statistically significant corrections between all obtained morphometric parameters in relation to both sex and age. For that reason manufacturers of TKA components should take this data into account and consider a gender specific prosthesis.

Similarly, the clinicians, especially orthopedic surgeons, should remember about those discrepancies, as improper choice of implant may lead to devastating sequelae.

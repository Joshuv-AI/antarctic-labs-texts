Enhancing Maize Drought Resilience with Agrichar Soil Amendments and Nanobiochar Foliar Sprays: Effects on Physio–Biochemical Responses
Authors: Muneeba Shabbir, Summera Jahan, Atif Kamran, Ayesha Saeed, Graciela Dolores Avila-Quezada, Elsayed Fathi Abd_Allah, Abeer Hashem
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Drought stress poses a threatening impact on maize growth, demanding the need for sustainable strategies to enhance plant resilience. This study investigated the effect of agrichar (AC) and nanobiochar on three maize plant hybrids subjected to three water levels (100%, 80%, and 60%), with treated groups receiving nanobiochar foliar spray (NBFS) with or without soil AC doses (0 t/ha (T), 5, and 10), while the control group received no NBFS and AC. Results demonstrated that the 5 and 10 T AC improved membrane stability index­(MSI) by 35% and 82%, respectively. The combined treatment significantly enhanced antioxidant activities as superoxide dismutase activity was enhanced by 11% in NBFS combined with agrichar treatment compared to no-NBFS and AC. Application of NBFS significantly enhanced catalase activity under 100%, 80%, and 60% water level (WL) by 9.1%, 7.5%, and 7.1%, respectively, compared to no-NBFS and AC. The enhancement in yield kg/ha with 5 and 10 T AC supplemented soil by 23% and 43.7%, respectively, at 60% WL. The combined application led to improvement in growth parameters, photosynthetic pigments, and grain yield. The improved agronomic performance observed under AC and NBFS application was further supported by molecular docking, which revealed a strong binding affinity of tartaric acid (an active metabolite present in plants as well as in AC) with antioxidant enzymatic protein (ascorbate peroxidase). In conclusion, these findings demonstrated that the combined application of 10 T/ha AC and NBFS caused an average improvement of 40% in maize yield under limited irrigation.

Introduction

High frequencies of drought stress adversely impact the development, growth, and productivity of agronomic crops. The future of agriculture and global food security is threatened by the tremendous population growth and an increased frequency of drought events due to global warming. According to the United Nations (2018), the increasing global population demand of water is expected to grow up to 50% by the year 2050. Pakistan is particularly prone to droughts because of inadequate drought preparedness and the heightened risks associated with recent climate change. Severe drought episodes have devastating effects on national economies, agriculture, and human well-being. Most of the agricultural land in Pakistan is arid, characterized by high temperatures and minimal rainfall. The elevated rates of evaporation and plant transpiration in these regions cause reduced soil moisture availability, making farming hard and leading to major crop losses. Prolonged and extreme drought periods also disturb plant phenology, degrade soil structure, upset ecosystem carbon balance, and result in reduced soil fertility. −

Zea mays refers to the C4 cereal crop that serves as a staple for both humans and livestock. However, it is highly sensitive to water-deficit conditions. An early physiological response to drought is closure of stomata to conserve moisture, but it also reduces the rate of CO2 uptake and restricts photosynthetic activity. Consequently, drought during critical growth stages severely reduces the maize yield. Water scarcity can cause maize yield losses ranging from 30 to 90% with greatest losses occurring when water scarcity occurs during flowering and grain filling stages.

One possible strategy for alleviating drought stress is the use of biochar, a low-cost carbonaceous material produced by the pyrolysis of organic wastes such as animal feedstock and crop residues under oxygen limited conditions. Slow pyrolysis is an effective technique for producing biochar. , Biochar, especially derived from wood, contains various oxygen-bearing functional groups on its surface such as carboxyl–COO from lignin, salinol (SiO), hydroxyl (O–H), and carbonyl (CO). These functional groups enhance moisture and nutrient retention in soil. The properties of biochar can be further improved by increasing its surface area and porosity. , Additionally, combining biochar with organic compost worm tea and perlite to form agrichar (AC) is a cost-effective and eco-friendly strategy to boost its effectiveness. For example, AC (biochar enriched with organic matter) has about 17 times greater pore volume and 2.5 times higher surface area than raw biochar, which in one study increased soil moisture content from 31 to 41%. The application of agrichar also improves soil physical properties by increasing aeration, available nutrients, water holding capacity, and stimulating microbial activity. ,

Advancements in nanotechnology have enabled the transformation of biochar into nanobiochar (<100 nm in size) which has a much higher surface area-to-volume ratio, improved physicochemical properties, enhanced mechanical and thermal stability, all of which can contribute to improved drought mitigation. , Nanobiochar is commonly synthesized through ultrasonication or ball milling and emerged as an effective remedy against drought stress. , Studies have shown that drought stress typically reduces chlorophyll content and compromises membrane stability, whereas the application of nanobiochar can increase photosystem II efficiency, enhance metabolic activity, and promote plant growth, ultimately improving crop productivity under drought stress. Once nanoparticles are absorbed by plant roots or leaves, they can trigger various physio–biochemical and molecular changes within the plant. They also enhance light-driven splitting of water to O2, protons, and electrons, which elevate photosynthetic efficiency by facilitating electron transport chain. , Additionally delivering nutrients in nanoparticle forms reduces nutrient losses and improves nutrient availability to plants.

Molecular docking is used to predict binding affinity of a ligand molecule to a receptor protein and calculate the associated free energy of binding to the target protein. , It is performed to provide in silico mechanistic evidence for the agronomic observations. The overall docking process involves the three-dimensional structure of both the ligand and protein, identifying the binding site and then using specialized software to sample and score different docking poses. Spectroscopic analyses such as Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) and X-ray diffraction (XRD) have identified multiple functional groups in AC. Of these, tartaric acid was selected as a representative ligand. Tartaric acid (2,3-dihydroxybutanedioic, C4H6O6) is the most important organic acid which act as a nutraceutical, vitamin, and antioxidant. It is a common organic acid associated with AC, also present in plants to stimulate the metabolic pathway under drought stress, has the ability to be docked with antioxidant enzymes, and known for its potential role in redox regulation and stress response in plants. Although AC does not directly engage in plant metabolism, its organic constituents likely participate in antioxidant signaling, possibly mediated by microbial pathways in APX pool. , The presence of meso-tartaric acid in both and its ability to dock with antioxidant enzymes highlight its role in redox regulation and stress adaptation. To verify this, in-silico docking was performed between the plant APX protein (PDB ID: 8DJX) and tartaric acid, revealing a strong interaction that may alter APX activity through ligand-induced signaling, thereby improving drought tolerance.

To the best of our knowledge, none of the previous research investigated the dual approach by combined effects of nanobiochar foliar spray (NBFS) and AC as a soil amendment on the yield and growth of maize under full and limited irrigation conditions. Based on the synergistic potential of NBFS and AC, it is hypothesized that enriching the soil with AC, due to its high surface area and porosity, together with NBFS will enhance the capacity of maize plants to absorb nutrients and to hold water in dry conditions. Furthermore, given the targeted delivery capacity of the nanoparticles, NBFS (taken up by the leaves and delivered to roots) is expected to provide a continuous supply of essential nutrients, thereby sustaining plant growth and productivity even during drought condition. This work aimed to assess the effects of soil-applied agrichar and foliar-applied nanobiochar on the productivity of maize under drought stress conditions. Drought stress resilience was observed by estimating antioxidant enzyme activities. The findings were validated by binding interactions between tartaric acid and the APX enzyme for supporting the stress resilience in the plant.

Materials and Methods

Biochar Production and Agrichar Synthesis

Air-dried wood cuttings (2–3 cm) from the Arabic tree (Collected from the University of Agriculture Faisalabad, 31°25′50″N 73°03′39″E, 185 m, Faisalabad District, Punjab Province, Pakistan) were utilized to produce biochar. Pyrolysis was carried out by using a fixed-bed reactor under an oxygen-limited environment. Nitrogen gas was used as a carrier during initiation of pyrolysis. The biomass underwent three h of slow pyrolysis with a gradual increase in temperature from 10 °C/min up to 500 °C using batch pyrolysis temperature-controlled equipment. Perlite (w/w), organic compost worm tea (v/w), and biochar (w/w) were combined in equal proportions (1:1:1) for the production of agrichar. The organic combination enhanced the agrichar’s saturation percentage to 56%.

Nanobiochar Synthesis

Nanobiochar was prepared by Song et al.’s (2019) procedure. 40 g of finely ground biochar was added to 1600 mL of distilled water. After 2 min of stirring, the solution was sonicated for 30 min, then again stirred for 5 min, and then kept undisturbed for 12 h. According to Stokes’s Law, particles more than 100 nm settled down in a 12 h duration. The solution’s top layer was collected the following day and centrifuged for half an hour at 3500 rpm. After being collected in a beaker, the supernatant was heated on a hot plate at 60 °C. The dried nanobiochar coating was scraped off using a scratcher.

The biochar yield (%) was obtained by the following equation: Yield=weightofthebiocharweightofthewoodenmaterialbeforepyrolysis×100

The nanobiochar yield (%) was obtained by the following equation: Yield=nanobiocharweightbiocharweight×100

Nanobiochar Foliar Application

Nanobiochar was sprayed on the leaves (0% NBC + 0T, 5T, and 10T AC) and NBFS (0.1% NBC + 0T, 5T, and 10T AC concentration). After 40 days of plantation, the initial treatment was administered, and it was repeated every 7 days. The spray treatments were supplemented with a 0.1% (w/v) NBC and 0.1% (v/v) Tween-20 (polyoxyethylene sorbitol ester) combination. The suspension was homogenized using magnetic stirring and sonication to ensure a uniform nanoparticle dispersion. Tween-20 enhances nanoparticle adhesion on the leaf surface. Deionized water with a comparable amount of surfactant was used to treat control plants without the addition of NBC.

Experimental Design

To perceive the possible impact of AC and NBFS on maize yield under different water levels, the experiment was conducted from August 2024 to November 2024. The field experiment was conducted at the University of the Punjab’s botanical garden in Lahore, at a height of 22.63 m, latitude 31.500314N, 74.301062E. The experiment used a randomized complete block design (RCBD) with a split–split–split plot configuration (4 × 8 square feet). The study included three different hybrids of maize: NMH-8352 Winner, LD Cobra, and Suraj 1045 (plant-to-plant space 6 in.) with three replication blocks. The agrichar was amended at a rate of 0 tons, 5 tons, and 10 tons per hectare (T). Three different water levels, i.e., deficit (60%), moderate (80%), and full water level (100%), were applied during maize growth. The crop evapotranspiration was recorded by CROPWAT-08, and the soil moisture content was maintained to field capacity by using a soil moisture meter (Lutron PMS-714) before each irrigation.

Characterization of Nanobiochar

40 g of crushed biochar yielded 2.3 g of nanobiochar. The pH and EC of AC and NBC were determined by combined pH and EC meters (pH2603). Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) coupled with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX; JSMS910 JEOL, Japan) was used to analyze the surface morphology and elemental composition of AC and NBC. Samples were gold-coated prior to imaging, and observations were made at magnifications ranging from 5000× to 20,000. EDX analysis was conducted on the same regions to identify the elemental distribution.

Soil Analysis

For estimation of pH and EC of soil, Rayment and Higginson (1992) method was used. For organic matter (OM) determination, the method of Trautmann and Krasny was used.

Plant Physiological and Biochemical Attributes

Analysis of Leaf Water Relation

The relative water content (RWC) was determined by Wheatherley’s (1950) method and calculated by the following formula: Relativewatercontent(RWC)=LFW−LDWLTW−LDW×100 The LFW indicates leaf fresh weight, LDW represents the leaf dry weight, and LTW indicates the leaf turgid weight.

Water retention capacity is the ratio of turgid weight to dry weight of the plant material. WRC determined by following formula of Sangakkara et al.: Waterretentioncapacity(WRC)=LTW(mg)LDW(mg)

Water uptake capacity (WUC) was recorded by the following equation: Wateruptakecapacity(WUC)=(LTW−LFW)LDW

Relative saturation deficit (RSD) as described by Turner and Ashraf et al. Relativesaturationdeficit(RSD)=[(LTW−LFW)LTW]×100

Determination of Sugar and Malondialdehyde Content

0.5 g of leaf material was crushed in 10 mL of distilled water. 0.1 mL of supernatant was added in 1 mL of phenol (5% v/v). After 1 h, 5 mL of concentrated H2SO4 was added to the test tube. The absorbance of the sample was noted at 420 nm. The amount of sugar in a particular leaf sample was determined using the glucose solution standard curve (10–50 mg mL–1). The Prochazkova et al. method was used for the estimation of malondialdehyde content (MDA).

Membrane Stability Index and Protein Content

The procedure of Sairam was followed for the determination of MSI in maize leaf. Bradford’s 1976 procedure was used for the determination of protein content in the leaf sample.

Antioxidant Activity Analysis

The activity of superoxide dismutase (SOD) was examined by Beauchamp and Fridovich’s procedure. Catalase activity (CAT) was examined by Teranishi et al. Peroxidase (POD) activity was measured by the Gorin and Heidema method. The antioxidant enzyme activity was expressed as units per milligram of protein (U/mg). Ascorbate peroxidase (APX) activity was assessed by the Nakano and Asada method. The reaction mixture has 50 μL of enzyme extract, 2.25 mL of 0.1 M phosphate buffer, 0.2 mL of 0.5 mM ascorbate, and 0.2 mL of H2O2. The reduction in absorbance by oxidation of ascorbic acid at 290 nm was noted using a spectrophotometer. One unit of enzyme activity is the amount of enzyme needed to oxidize 1 μ mole of ascorbic acid per minute, as noted in change in absorbance at 290 nm. Absorbance(A)=ε×l×c where A indicates the light amount absorbed by the sample, ε represents a molar extinction coefficient of 2.8 mM, and l and c represent the distance covers by light and absorbing specie concentration, respectively.

Yield Parameters

The yield parameters, including the number of cobs/plot and yield kg/ha, were measured manually, while cob weight and thousand seed weight were recorded by analytical weighing balance (Model FA2204E, China). The following formula was used for kg/ha yield estimation. Yield=(kgha)=seedyield/plant×plantingdensity

Molecular Docking

For molecular docking, tartaric acid was selected as the docking ligand because it is one of the primary organic acids in plants that has an ability to be docked with antioxidant enzymes. The protein structure of ascorbate peroxidase (pdb_00008djx) was obtained from the protein data bank (https://www.rcsb.org/) and downloaded in pdb file format. The ligand molecule ((2R,3S)-2,3-dihydroxybutanedioic acid) was obtained from pubchem (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), and protein visualization and cleaning was performed from software discovery studio, which gave detailed information about protein 2D structure and ligand molecule visualization. Molecular docking of the target protein and ligand molecule was performed with help of Amdock software (assisted molecular docking with Autodock v4.2.6. and Autodock vina). The protein structure (APX) was prepared using AutoDock Tools, and a grid box was generated to cover the active catalytic site, with dimensions 60 × 60 × 60 Å and a grid spacing of 0.375 Å, centered on the enzyme’s active pocket. The resultant docked molecule was opened in python-based pymol, which is a molecular graphic tool for the visualization of protein 3-dimensional structure to use in the editing of protein structure and exported molecule in gz format. Later on, complete information on the protein–ligand bonding pattern and bond forces with structure visualization was obtained from the Web site Protein–ligand interaction profiler (PLIP) (https://plip-tool.biotec.tu-dresden.de/plip-web/plip/index).

Statistical Analysis

Minitab software (ver. 21.2) was used to perform the statistical analysis of the collected data. A four-way factorial analysis of variance (ANOVA) based on a mixed model design was applied to evaluate the interactive effects of treatments on growth, physiological, biochemical, and yield parameters. Tukey’s pairwise comparison test was employed at a 95% confidence interval to separate treatment means. Regression modeling and heatmap visualization were carried out using Python (version 3.11) with the pandas, statsmodels, and matplotlib libraries.

Results

Characterization of Agrichar and Nanobiochar

EC and pH of Agrichar and Nanobiochar

The pH of nanobiochar and agrichar was 8.9 and 8.7, respectively. The electrical conductivity of agrichar and nanobiochar was 0.4 and 2.8 mS cm–1, respectively.

EDX Analysis of Biochar, Agrichar, and Nanobiochar

The SEM–EDX analysis revealed (Figure and Supporting Information S1) that biochar has the highest carbon content (60.06%), ideal for carbon sequestration and soil structure improvement. The NBC, though slightly lower in carbon (47.01%), is significantly richer in essential nutrients, including nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium, magnesium, sulfur, copper, and chlorine, making it more effective for enhancing plant growth and stress tolerance. The AC contains notable amounts of silicon (4.17%) and aluminum (0.91%), which support plant strength and influence soil chemistry. Overall, nanobiochar offers a more nutrient–dense profile, while AC contributes to long-term carbon benefits.

EDX analysis of agrichar, nanobiochar, and biochar.

Soil Analysis

In soil analysis, pH, Ec, and soil organic matter were increased compared to those with no AC supplement. The combined application of nanobiochar and AC caused a slight rise in pH, indicating that there is less influence of combined application on change in the pH of the soil (Figure a). A significant increase in pH was observed by 18.9% and 29.7% in soil amended with 5 T AC and 10 T AC/ha, respectively, as compared to 0 T AC. Figure b illustrates that the measured soil EC was 2.1% improved with NBFS as compared to an individual treatment. Figure c indicates that the organic matter of soil was enhanced by 0.08%, 0.1%, and 0.1%, respectively, in the treatment of 100%, 80%, and 60% water level (WL) of NBFS as compared to no NBFS and AC. In the soil supplemented with 5 and 10 T AC/ha, organic matter was 54% and 92% higher, respectively, in contrast to nonsupplemented soil.

Biochemical and Physiological Analysis

The 4-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test at a 95% confidence interval (Table ) indicated that the combined NBFS + 10 T AC/ha treatment increased RWC by an average of 3.7% across all hybrids. In soil treated with 10 T AC/ha at 100% WL, the hybrid Winner exhibited a RWC 12% higher than that of Suraj. As shown in Table , 5 and 10 T AC/ha increased WRC by 7.7% and 19.8%, respectively, compared to 0 T AC-amended soil under 100% WL, with the highest WRC observed at 10 T AC/ha. Application of NBFS further enhanced WRC by 2.7% relative to that of the untreated (control). The water uptake capacity (WUC) decreased by 7.4% and 34% in maize grown with 5 and 10 T AC/ha, respectively, compared to control. Under limited irrigation, the WUC increased by 25% and 17.5% at deficit and moderate WL, respectively, compared with those receiving full irrigation. Maize plants receiving NBFS showed a 9% lower relative stress damage than those without NBFS or AC. The combined application of NBFS and AC (5 and 10 T AC/ha) improved RWC and WRC, while reducing WUC and RSD under deficit irrigation compared with the plants grown without NBFS and 0 T AC/ha.

Discussion

Drought stress adversely affects cellular membranes, plant–water relations, and physio–biochemical processes, ultimately inhibiting growth, photosynthetic activity, hormone signaling, and seed germination. It reduces cell elongation by decreasing cell turgor through water loss. Previous studies have demonstrated that biochar addition improves the microbiological and physicochemical properties of soil. Recent observations indicate that the incorporation of AC enhances the availability of alkaline metals (Na, Mg, and Ca) and base cations, thereby increasing soil pH from acidic to near-neutral levels. Our findings are consistent with those of Rashid et al. (2023), who reported that the foliar application of nanobiochar caused minimal changes in soil pH compared with the control. The slight increase in pH and EC observed in soils treated with AC and NBFS in this study may be attributed to the soluble salts present in the ash fractions of biochar.

The RWC, a key indicator of plant water status, was enhanced by the combined application of NBFS with 5 and 10 T AC/ha under water-limited conditions. The improvement in RWC and WRC may result from increased osmotic adjustment and xylem vessel diameter, which facilitate water uptake in AC-amended soil. Nanoparticles further improved leaf hydration by maintaining a balance between root water absorption and transpirational loss. Conversely, Wakabayashi et al. reported reduced WRC under drought due to lower hemicellulose and cellulose accumulation in cell walls. In the present study, the combined application of AC with NBFS treatment lowered RSD values, indicating reduced stress, whereas higher RSD without NBFS and AC treatment reflected a severe drought response. Under water deficit, reduced RWC and increased WSD and RSD confirm progressive dehydration stress in maize hybrids.

Compared with no-NBFS treatment, the MSI improved notably with the combined application of NBFS and AC, consistent with previous findings showing enhanced MSI through nanoparticles and biochar use. Under limited irrigation, nanoparticles stabilize cellular water status and membrane integrity. The observed increase in protein and sugar contents in nanoparticle-treated plants may result from improved osmotic regulation, protein stabilization, and stimulation of defense-related enzyme synthesis. Biochar and nanobiochar promote accumulation of stress proteins, soluble sugars, and amino acids by supplying nutrients and functional groups (O–H, CC, CO, and N–H), which enhance osmotic protection and water potential under drought. Prolonged water scarcity reduces sugar levels; however, AC combined with NBFS supports carbohydrate adsorption and accumulation, improving moisture availability even under stress. Drought stress elevates ROS, which increases cellular viscosity and inhibits Calvin-cycle enzymes, particularly RuBisCO, thereby intensifying photorespiration and oxidative stress. − According to Hafiz et al. (2020), biochar alleviates oxidative stress by enhancing antioxidant activity, while Mubashir et al. (2023) reported that nanoparticles further reduce ROS and boost antioxidant enzyme responses. The application of NBFS in this study led to a decrease in MDA content, indicating reduced oxidative damage caused by ROS. Although lipid peroxidation and proline accumulation typically increase under water-deficit conditions, the incorporation of biochar significantly them by improving soil water retention.

Cob weight, 1000-grain weight, and grain yield in maize hybrid typically decline with reduced water availability. Numerous studies have reported that biochar and nanomaterials improve grain quality, biomass accumulation, and yield in maize. The present findings align with those of Song et al., who observed improved wheat production following the application of TiO2 nanoparticles, highlighting their effectiveness even under the water-deficit condition. Variations among maize hybrids may reflect differences in genetic makeup and stress-responsive traits. In particular, the Winner hybrid consistently outperformed others across physiological parameters, likely due to its enhanced antioxidant defense and superior root architecture.

Molecular docking further supported these physiological outcomes. A strong binding was observed between ascorbate peroxidase and meso-tartaric acid ((2R,3S)-2,3-dihydroxybutanedioic acid) under drought conditions in maize, indicating that ascorbate peroxidase plays a central role in ROS detoxification, while tartaric acid contributes to redox balance and antioxidant activation. , Through soil application, AC improves the water-holding capacity and releases bioactive organic compounds, while foliar NBFS directly activates stress-signaling pathways in leaves. This synergistic effect enhances APX activity, providing a biochemical basis for improved drought tolerance. Docking results confirmed a strong interaction between the APX active site and meso-tartaric acid, supporting the proposed antioxidant mechanism. The association of APX with tartaric acid suggests enhanced metabolism under drought through conversion of tartaric acid to oxaloacetic acid, couple with APX-mediated removal of ROS. , Docking analysis further showed stable hydrogen-bond formation with residues His-42 and Arg-172, key catalytic sites regulating H2O2 detoxification, , potentially increasing APX efficiency and accelerating ROS turnover under stress. While these results provide compelling molecular-level validation, the study is limited to in-silico and physiological analyses and requires in vivo confirmation of the underlying genetic mechanisms. Overall, this work highlights nanobiochar as a scalable, cost-effective, and environmentally sustainable strategy for improving drought resilience and long-term climate-smart crop productivity.

Conclusion

This work demonstrates that combining nanobiochar foliar spray (NBFS) with soil-applied agrichar (AC) offers a dual-pathway strategy to enhance maize growth, physiology, and yield under both optimal and drought conditions. The combined synergy between NBFS and AC improved soil water retention, nutrient availability, and organic-matter content, thereby strengthening stress signaling pathway within plants by activating antioxidant system (APX activity, a central enzyme in ROS detoxification) and induce resilience against drought. The 10 T AC/ha combination with NBFS treatment produced the highest agronomic gains. Molecular docking revealed a strong interaction between tartaric acid and ascorbate peroxidase (APX), providing molecular-level validation that links AC chemistry to ROS detoxification and NBFS-mediated antioxidant enhancement. These findings identify a cost-effective and scalable approach for improving drought tolerance and advancing climate-smart agriculture. Broader adoption of AC-based amendments and nanotechnology-assisted foliar inputs could contribute to sustainable soil fertility, efficient water use, and long-term food security.

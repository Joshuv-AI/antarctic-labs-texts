Prenatal Diagnosis of Aorto–Left Ventricular Tunnel
Authors: Tuyen Kim Le, Truong Huy Hoang, Mark S Sklansky
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphical abstractKeywords: Aorto–left ventricular tunnel, Fetal echocardiography, STIC, Aortic regurgitation, Congenital heart disease

Introduction

Aorto–left ventricular tunnel (ALVT) is an uncommon congenital cardiac anomaly characterized by an abnormal extracardiac communication between the ascending aorta and the left ventricle (LV).1,2 Prenatal diagnosis is rare and challenging because findings may mimic primary aortic regurgitation (AR) or coronary artery fistula.3,4 Advanced volumetric techniques such as spatiotemporal image correlation (STIC) may enhance anatomical understanding and diagnostic confidence.5, 6, 7 Early recognition of ALVT is critical, as it may present with severe fetal AR and significant hemodynamic consequences. This case illustrates the incremental value of three-dimensional (3D) STIC in confirming the prenatal diagnosis of an ALVT.

Case Presentation

A 26-year-old primigravida was referred at 22 weeks’ gestation for evaluation of fetal cardiomegaly. Two-dimensional (2D) fetal echocardiography in the apical 4-chamber view demonstrated cardiomegaly with relative dilatation of the LV compared with the right ventricle (Figure 1). The cardiothoracic area ratio was measured at 0.40 at end diastole, confirming cardiomegaly (Figure 2).

Transabdominal 2D fetal echocardiogram, apical 4-chamber systolic view, demonstrates a dilated left ventricular cavity with normal RV, LA, and RA. LA, Left atrium; RA, right atrium; RV, right ventricle.

Transabdominal 2D fetal echocardiogram, diastolic frame, demonstrates cardiomegaly with a cardiothoracic area ratio of 0.40. LA, Left atrium; RA, right atrium; RV, right ventricle.

Two-dimensional fetal echocardiography demonstrated left ventricular dilatation (Figure 1, Video 1) with suspicion of severe AR. Continuous-wave Doppler obtained in the apical 5-chamber view revealed a characteristic bidirectional “to-and-fro” spectral profile at the level of the aortic root, consistent with severe AR (Figure 3). Left ventricular systolic function appeared normal at the time of fetal assessment.

Transabdominal fetal echocardiogram, apical 5-chamber view with continuous-wave Doppler, demonstrates a bidirectional “to-and-fro” spectral profile across the aortic root consistent with severe AR.

Color-flow Doppler imaging in the apical 5-chamber view demonstrated severe AR directed from the ascending aorta into the LV (Video 2). The regurgitant jet was markedly eccentric, directed anteriorly toward the anterior mitral leaflet, mimicking a paravalvular-like regurgitation pattern rather than a central valvular jet, raising suspicion for an extracardiac communication.

In the long-axis (left ventricular outflow tract) view, the origin of regurgitant flow at the level of the aortic root was further delineated, supporting the presence of an extracardiac communication (Video 3). Right ventricular outflow tract imaging demonstrated normal ventriculoarterial alignment with normally related great arteries (Video 4).

Pulsed-wave Doppler interrogation of the aortic arch demonstrated normal antegrade systolic flow with holodiastolic flow reversal, reflecting the hemodynamic significance of AR (Video 5).

Spatiotemporal image correlation datasets were acquired, and 3D reconstruction demonstrated an extracardiac tunnel arising from the ascending aorta above the aortic valve (AV) and entering the LV (Figure 4, Video 6), supporting the diagnosis of ALVT.

Three-dimensional STIC reconstruction demonstrates an extracardiac tunnel arising from the ascending aorta above the AV and entering the LV, consistent with ALVT. Ao, Aorta; Asc Ao, ascending aorta.

Postnatal transthoracic echocardiography confirmed the diagnosis (Video 7). Intraoperative findings demonstrated an extracardiac tunnel originating above the AV (Figure 5, Video 8). Surgical repair was successfully performed, with a favorable early postoperative outcome.

Discussion

Aorto–left ventricular tunnel is a rare congenital cardiac anomaly characterized by an abnormal extracardiac communication between the ascending aorta and the LV. The condition leads to significant AR and progressive left ventricular volume overload. Prenatal diagnosis remains challenging because the imaging findings can mimic primary AR or coronary artery fistula.1, 2, 3, 4

In the present case, severe eccentric fetal AR with disproportionate left ventricular dilatation raised suspicion for an abnormal communication between the aorta and the LV. Conventional 2D fetal echocardiography was able to demonstrate the hemodynamic consequences of the lesion, including cardiomegaly and severe regurgitant flow. The presence of diastolic flow reversal in the aortic arch further supports the hemodynamic significance of the regurgitant lesion. Holodiastolic flow reversal in the aorta is a well-recognized marker of severe AR. Prior studies have demonstrated similar hemodynamic findings in peripheral arteries, supporting the concept that significant regurgitant volume leads to retrograde diastolic flow within the arterial system.8 These observations further reinforce the hemodynamic significance of the findings in our case. However, the precise anatomical origin of the abnormal flow was not clearly defined using standard imaging planes alone.

Three-dimensional STIC provided incremental diagnostic value by allowing volumetric reconstruction of the fetal heart. This technique enabled visualization of an extracardiac tunnel originating above the AV and entering the LV, confirming the diagnosis of ALVT. Spatiotemporal image correlation reconstruction can improve spatial orientation and facilitate recognition of extracardiac communications that may be difficult to appreciate on conventional 2D imaging alone.5, 6, 7

Recognition of this entity during fetal life is clinically important. Severe AR in the fetus may lead to progressive ventricular dilation, myocardial dysfunction, and eventually fetal heart failure. Early prenatal diagnosis allows appropriate perinatal planning and postnatal surgical repair, which is associated with favorable outcomes when performed early.

An additional noteworthy finding in this case was the echogenic appearance of the left ventricular endocardium, which may reflect subendocardial stress or relative ischemia related to the combined effects of severe volume overload and potential coronary steal from the tunnel. Recognition of such secondary myocardial changes may provide further clues to the severity of the underlying hemodynamic disturbance.

For fetal echocardiographers and sonographers, unexplained severe AR associated with disproportionate left ventricular dilation should prompt careful evaluation for extracardiac communications, including ALVT.

Conclusion

Severe fetal AR should prompt consideration of ALVT. Three-dimensional STIC imaging provides important incremental diagnostic value in confirming the diagnosis and guiding perinatal management.

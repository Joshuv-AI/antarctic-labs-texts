Integrating mechanistic research and emerging technologies to advance clinical translation in orthopaedics
Authors: Qiuli Qi, Ling Qin
Published: 2026

We are very pleased to present the March 2026 issue of JOT, comprising 7 review articles, 13 original articles, and 3 sets of correspondence. This issue highlights the latest advances in the mechanisms, diagnostic techniques, and therapeutic strategies for common orthopaedic degenerative diseases, trauma- and sports-related disorders, and metabolic bone disorders. It showcases the application of emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence and 3D-bioprinted materials in orthopaedic translational research and clinical practice, with the aim of accelerating the efficient translation of research discoveries to enhance the precision management of orthopaedic diseases.

For common orthopaedic degenerative diseases, Yan et al. demonstrated that the development of osteoarthritis (OA) could be attenuated by targeted knockdown of Piezo1 in synovial macrophages via inhibiting activation of the DRP1–cGAS–STING pathway [1]. Meng et al. reviewed how bidirectional crosstalk between degraded cartilage and sensitized sensory nerves drives osteoarthritis pain and outlines key molecular mediators, biomarkers, and model systems to enable mechanism-based phenotyping and personalized therapies, thereby advancing clinically relevant OA pain stratification [2]. In the aspect of spine and disc disorders, Xu et al. revealed that magnesium ions promote osteogenic differentiation and intervertebral fusion by enhancing METTL3-mediated m6A methylation of RhoA mRNA and activating the RhoA/ROCK pathway, providing an epigenetic rationale for optimizing magnesium-based spinal implants [3]. In terms of diagnosis, Tang et al. summarized the current applications and key challenges of medical large language models in spinal diseases and proposed a practical implementation roadmap and framework to guide the integration of safe and effective AI tools into spinal surgery workflows under strict regulatory and data privacy requirements [4]. Suo et al. introduced a multimodal AI-based radiomics model that integrates paraspinal muscle MRI features with clinical data to accurately predict pain intensity in chronic nonspecific low back pain, enabling early risk stratification and supporting personalized intervention [5]. Additionally, an article focusing on congenital spinal deformities is also included in this issue, Zhang et al. reported LBX1-mediated disruption of the polyamine pathway in paraspinal muscles and the consequent reduction in circulating spermidine as a driver of curve progression in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis, and demonstrated that oral spermidine can mitigate scoliosis-like deformity in a robust mouse model, pointing to a novel therapeutic target and biomarker [6]. Regarding other degenerative diseases, this issue also includes a review on proteomic biomarkers in rotator cuff disorders by Parrotta et al., highlighting synovial fluid proteins such as IL-6, MMP-1, MMP-13, and TGF-β1 as key mediators of inflammation and extracellular matrix remodeling with potential translational value for future multi-analyte diagnostic panels and disease stratification [7]. Faure et al. revealed that CSE-derived hydrogen sulfide inhibits lysyl oxidase (LOX) – driven pathological calcification and preserves tendon integrity, thereby identifying LOX as a therapeutic target and H2S as a promising treatment strategy for calcific tendinopathy [8]. This issue also includes a commentary by Harariya et al. and the author's response on recently published work investigating CMTM6, providing a critical appraisal of its mechanistic role in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) pathogenesis and further delineating its potential as a biomarker and therapeutic target within the synovial inflammatory microenvironment [[9], [10], [11]].

Regarding the recent advances in orthopaedic trauma- and sports-related disorders included in this issue, Wu et al. reported that Tramiprosate could restore mitochondrial metabolism in myelin debris–induced foamy macrophages by upregulating Shmt2, thereby reducing mtROS-driven cGAS-mediated inflammation and cellular senescence after spinal cord injury, highlighting its potential as a neuroprotective therapy [12]. Hu et al. identified TRIM59 as an endogenous suppressor of neuronal ferroptosis after spinal cord injury by promoting K48-linked ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of ANXA2, thereby mitigating secondary neuronal damage and improving functional recovery [13]. Zhou et al. reported that HIF1A transcriptionally activates CDKN1A to amplify ferroptosis in skeletal muscle ischaemia-reperfusion injury, indicating the HIF1A–CDKN1A axis as a potential therapeutic target for ferroptosis-focused intervention [14]. Yoshizato et al. developed 3D-bioprinted, scaffold-free cartilage constructs derived from adipose tissue-derived MSCs, which could effectively promote the healing of critical-size bone defects by recapitulating endochondral ossification [15]. Elvin et al. systematically reviewed the current applications, challenges, and future directions of metallic biomaterials in foot and ankle surgery, emphasizing emerging innovations, such as 3D-printed and surface-engineered implants, to improve reconstruction outcomes aligned with patient- and site-specific requirements [16]. Jin et al. systematically reviewed the recent advances in flexible wearable pressure/strain sensors, demonstrating how innovations in materials, sensing mechanisms, and intelligent signal processing enable real-time, high-resolution motion monitoring in orthopaedic sports medicine, with promising applications in injury assessment, rehabilitation, and human–machine interaction [17]. Regarding the original study by Mischler et al. entitled Preclinical validation of finite element models for predicting in vivo residual plate bending using continuous implant sensor data [18], the two Letters to the Editor by Tang et al. [19] and Yang et al. [20] and the authors' reply [21] highlighted that sensor-validated finite element modeling significantly advances in vivo implant behavior prediction but requires refinement in specificity, patient-specific bone-implant interface mechanics, physiological loading, and robustness through broader validation. Integrating continuous sensor feedback, advanced modeling, and AI-enabled workflows is crucial for a reliable, adaptive "digital twin" paradigm in personalized fracture care. The Letter to the Editor by Lee et al. [22] regarding the study by Zhang et al. [23], along with the authors’ reply [24] included in this issue, discusses the biomechanical significance and biological safety of the magnesium block design. Both sides agree that further validation, for example, through fatigue testing, in vivo studies, and more systematic mechanistic investigations, is necessary before clinical translation.

This issue also features the following articles on common metabolic bone diseases. Zou et al. identified the ET-1/ETBR signaling axis as the key driver for osteoporosis by promoting ROS-mediated skeletal stem cell senescence, revealing a mechanistic link between vascular ageing and skeletal degeneration, and proposing that ETBR blockade is a novel treatment strategy for postmenopausal and age-related osteoporotic bone loss [25]. Li et al. revealed that overexpression of SFRP1 in BMSCs from pediatric B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) patients leads to bone loss by inhibiting Wnt/β-catenin signaling and demonstrated that delivering shSFRP1 via E7 peptide-modified, BMSC-targeted liposomes can restore osteogenesis and mitigate leukemia-associated bone loss, highlighting clear translational therapeutic potential [26]. Luo et al. developed multifunctional Gel@ALN@E gelatin microspheres that sequentially modulate the osteoimmune microenvironment, suppress osteoclastogenesis, and restore balance in bone remodeling, thereby markedly enhancing regeneration of osteoporotic bone defects and highlighting a promising translational strategy for irregular clinical bone defects [27]. Mudiyanselage et al. systematically reviewed that the deficiency of vitamin D is a widespread, modifiable risk factor linking musculoskeletal weakness, frailty, cognitive decline, autoimmunity, infection, and orthopaedic morbidity, and argue for targeted supplementation, dietary fortification, and optimised sun exposure as key translational strategies despite current trial limitations [28]. Zhang et al. reviewed the integrated “gut–X axis” concept to highlight how gut microbiota and their metabolites orchestrate bone aging–related disorders—spanning the gut–bone, gut–joint, and gut–disc axes—and propose gut-targeted interventions as emerging translational strategies [29].

The study on osteosarcoma by He et al. included in this issue reports that ACSS1 drives osteosarcoma metastasis by maintaining mitochondrial acetyl-CoA homeostasis and activating SCD-dependent lipid metabolism, indicating that targeting this axis may serve as a complementary strategy to conventional chemotherapy for metastatic disease [30].

We gratefully acknowledge our authors, reviewers, and readers for their steadfast commitment to orthopaedic translation, and we look forward to continuing this shared journey toward innovations that meaningfully improve patient care.

Modification of the existing maximum residue level for flonicamid in honey
Authors: EFSA (European Food Safety Authority), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Luis Carrasco Cabrera, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Luna Greco, Samira Jarrah, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Stefanie Nave, Hermine Reich, Silvia Ruocco, Alessia Pia Scarlato, Marta Szot, Elena Taglianini, Anne Theobald, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the applicant ISK Biosciences Europe N.V. submitted a request to the competent national authority in Finland to modify the existing maximum residue level (MRL) for the active substance flonicamid in honey. The data submitted in support of the request were not found appropriate to derive an MRL proposal for honey. The assessment was complemented by an analysis of the monitoring data available from the EU monitoring programmes (conducted during 2009–2023). The results from the monitoring data analysis suggest that the current MRL is still sufficient to account for the residue uptake in honey resulting from plant protection products uses of flonicamid on melliferious crops. Adequate analytical methods for enforcement are available to control the residues of flonicamid in honey according to the residue definition as the sum of flonicamid, TFNA and TFNG, expressed as flonicamid at the validated limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg for each compound. Based on the risk assessment results, EFSA concluded that the short‐term and long‐term intake of residues in honey at a level of 0.05 mg/kg according to the monitoring data is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health.

SUMMARY

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, ISK Biosciences Europe N.V. submitted an application to the competent national authority in Finland (evaluating Member State, EMS) to modify maximum residue limit (MRL) for the active substance flonicamid in honey.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format, was submitted through the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) Central Submission System on 25 January 2024. The appointed EMS Finland assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 7 May 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 22 May 2024 to 12 June 2024. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the EMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report, in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and to EFSA on 14 June 2024. On the basis of existing authorised indoor use of flonicamid on cucurbits, which was identified by the applicant as the worst‐case EU GAP for residues in honey, the EMS proposed to raise the existing MRL in honey from the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.05–0.15 mg/kg.

On 18 June 2024, the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

Based on the conclusions derived by EFSA in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC, the data evaluated under previous MRL assessment and the additional data provided by the EMS in the framework of this application, the following conclusions are derived.

The metabolism of flonicamid following foliar spray applications was investigated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review and the MRL review in crops belonging to the groups of fruit crops (peaches, peppers), root crops (potatoes) and cereals/grass (wheat). In the crops tested, the parent compound and the two metabolites 4‐(trifluoromethyl)pyridine‐3‐carboxylic acid (TFNA) and N‐[4‐(trifluoromethyl)pyridine‐3‐carbonyl] glycine (TFNG), were found to be the main residues.

Studies investigating the effect of processing on the nature of flonicamid (hydrolysis studies) demonstrated that the active substance and the metabolites TFNA and TFNG are stable under standard hydrolysis conditions.

Investigation of carry‐over of residues in honey from rotational crops was not necessary as flonicamid and its metabolites TFNA and TFNG show low persistence in soil and therefore no residues are expected in rotational crops.

Based on the metabolic pattern identified in metabolism studies, the results of the hydrolysis studies and the toxicological relevance of metabolites, the residue definitions for plant products were proposed as ‘sum of flonicamid, TFNA and TFNG expressed as flonicamid’ for both enforcement and risk assessment. These residue definitions are applicable to primary crops and processed products.

In the absence of specific metabolism studies with honey bees and considering that the metabolic profile of flonicamid in all primary crops is similar and no degradation products are formed under pasteurisation conditions, EFSA concludes that the same residue definitions which are derived for plant commodities are applicable for honey.

A sufficiently validated analytical method based on high‐performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC–MS/MS) detection is available to quantify residues in honey according to the enforcement residue definition. The method enables quantification of residues at or above the individual LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for each compound included in the residue definitions (combined LOQ of 0.03 mg/kg). It is noted that the existing enforcement LOQ in honey is 0.05 mg/kg.

The data submitted in the context of this MRL application were not found appropriate to derive an MRL proposal for honey reflecting the plant protection uses of flonicamid on melliferous crops in Europe. EFSA disagrees with the selected critical GAP by the applicant and is of the opinion that the use pattern investigated in the submitted residue trials support neither the critical GAP identified by the applicant nor other authorised EU outdoor uses on melliferous crops.

In order to see whether the existing EU MRL at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg is sufficient to account for residues in honey, ESFA supplemented the current application with an assessment of monitoring data submitted in the framework of Article 32 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (official national control programmes). 1 The analysis of monitoring data suggests that at the 99.5th percentile and at the 95th percentile of the data population at the 95% confidence level residues in honey occur at levels ranging from 0.05 to 0.051 mg/kg, thus at the level of the existing EU MRL, for which the identified non‐compliance rate was very low (≤ 0.2%). EFSA therefore proposes to maintain the existing EU MRL at the level of 0.05 mg/kg, but without an asterisk (*). It is noted that the proposed MRL in honey covers the currently authorised uses for flonicamid according to the results of monitoring data and might need to be revised if new uses on melliferous crops are authorised or in case new residue trials representative of existing uses of flonicamid on melliferous crops become available. Further risk management considerations are required.

The toxicological profile of flonicamid was assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review under Directive 91/414/EEC and the data were sufficient to derive an acceptable daily intake (ADI) and an acute reference dose (ARfD) of 0.025 mg/kg bw (per day). The metabolites included in the residue definition are not of higher toxicity than the parent active substance.

The consumer risk assessment was performed with revision 3.1 of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo). The short‐term exposure assessment was performed for residues in honey and did not exceed the ARfD, accounting for less than 2% of the ARfD. For certain commodities (pears and peaches) not included in the present assessment, an exceedance of the ARfD due to differences in the applied PRIMo versions (2 vs. 3.1.) was observed. Refinement options for the acute intake calculations could not be identified based on the available data and may be further investigated in future assessments.

The long‐term exposure assessment performed with PRIMo 3.1 in the most recent EFSA reasoned opinion published after the MRL review was updated with the proposed MRL value in honey supported with monitoring data. The highest estimated long‐term dietary intake was 31% of the ADI (NL toddler). The contribution of residues of flonicamid in honey to the overall long‐term exposure was below 0.1% of the ADI.

EFSA concluded that residues of flonicamid occurring in honey at the level of 0.05 mg/kg will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference values and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health.

The EU pesticides peer review for the renewal of the active substance in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 is ongoing and therefore the conclusions reported in this reasoned opinion might need to be reconsidered in the light of the outcome of the peer review.

ASSESSMENT

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) received an application to modify the existing maximum residue level (MRL) for flonicamid in honey. The current MRL application is not linked to one specific good agricultural practice (GAP) but is related to the existing uses in crops that might be attractive to bees and that are a potential source for residues of flonicamid in honey. The critical GAP identified by the applicant is reported in Appendix A.

Flonicamid 2 is the ISO common name for N‐(cyanomethyl)‐4‐(trifluoromethyl) pyridine‐3‐carboxamide (IUPAC). The chemical structures of the active substance and its main metabolites are reported in Appendix A.

Flonicamid was evaluated in the framework of Directive 91/414/EEC 3 with France designated as rapporteur Member State (RMS); the representative uses assessed were foliar spray applications on potatoes, wheat, apples and pears in northern and southern EU and on peaches in southern EU. The draft assessment report (DAR) prepared by the RMS has been peer reviewed by EFSA (EFSA, 2010). Flonicamid was approved 4 for the use as insecticide on 1 September 2010. The process of the EU pesticides peer review for the renewal of the first approval is currently ongoing.

The EU MRLs for flonicamid are established in Annex II of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005. 5 The review of existing MRLs according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (MRL review) has been performed (EFSA, 2014) and the proposed modifications have been implemented in the MRL legislation. After completion of the MRL review, EFSA has issued several reasoned opinions on the modification of MRLs for flonicamid. The proposals from these reasoned opinions have been considered in recent MRL regulations. 6

In accordance Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 and following the provisions set by the ‘Transparency Regulation’ (EU) 2019/1381, 7 the applicant ISK Biosciences Europe N.V. submitted on 25 January 2024 an application to the competent national authority in Finland (EMS), alongside the dossier containing the supporting data using the IUCLID format to modify the existing MRL for flonicamid in honey.

The appointed EMS Finland assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 7 May 2024. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation run from 22 May 2024 to 12 June 2024. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the EMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report, in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and to EFSA on 14 June 2024. On the basis of existing authorised indoor use of flonicamid on cucurbits, which was identified by the applicant as the worst‐case EU GAP for residues in honey, the EMS proposed to raise the existing MRL in honey from the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.05 to 0.15 mg/kg.

On 18 June 2024, the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

EFSA based its assessment on the evaluation report submitted by the EMS (Finland, 2024), the DAR and its addendum (France, 2005, 2009) prepared under Directive 91/414/EEC, the Commission review report on flonicamid (European Commission, 2010a), the conclusion on the peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance flonicamid (EFSA, 2010), as well as the conclusions from previous EFSA opinions on flonicamid (EFSA, 2010, 2015, 2016a, 2017a, 2017b, 2018b, 2018c, 2018d, 2019b, 2020b, 2023, 2024), including the MRL review (EFSA, 2014) and the subsequent evaluation of the confirmatory data on the MRL review according to Article 12 of Regulation No 396/2005 (EFSA, 2020a). It is further noted that the import tolerance proposals derived by EFSA in 2020 (EFSA, 2020b) are not yet implemented in EU Regulations, and the MRL proposal assessed by EFSA in the most recent output (EFSA, 2024) are included in in the draft regulation PLAN/2024/791 which is not yet published in the EU Official Journal. However, conclusions reached in these EFSA outputs (EFSA, 2020b, 2024) although not yet taken over in the MRL regulation, will be considered for the present assessment.

For this application, the data requirements established in Regulation (EU) No 544/2011 8 and the guidance documents applicable at the date of submission of the IUCLID application are applicable (European Commission, 1997, 2010b, 2018, 2021, 2023; OECD, 2011). The assessment is performed in accordance with the legal provisions of the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and the Authorisation of Plant Protection Products adopted by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011. 9

As the EU pesticides peer review for the renewal of the active substance in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 is ongoing, the conclusions reported in this reasoned opinion may need to be reconsidered in the light of the outcome of the peer review.

RESIDUES IN PLANTS

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in plants

Nature of residues in primary crops

Information on the nature of residues in primary crops, particularly in plant parts subject to bee foraging, is relevant to address the nature of residues in honey in the absence of specific metabolism studies with bees (see Section 3.1).

The metabolism of flonicamid in primary crops belonging to the group of fruit crops (peaches, peppers), root crops (potatoes) and cereals/grass (wheat) following foliar application has been investigated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review and the MRL review. In the crops tested, the parent compound and the two metabolites 4‐(trifluoromethyl)pyridine‐3‐carboxylic acid (TFNA) and N‐[4‐(trifluoromethyl)pyridine‐3‐carbonyl]glycine (TFNG) were found to be the main residues (EFSA, 2010, 2014).

EFSA concludes that residues in melliferous parts of primary crops will consist of parent flonicamid and its metabolites TFNA and TFNG.

Nature of residues in rotational crops

As bees will forage also on crops that are grown in rotation with treated primary crops, information on the nature of residues in rotational crops is relevant to address the nature of residues in honey (see Section 3.1).

However, according to the soil degradation studies evaluated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review, flonicamid and its relevant metabolites, TFNA and TFNG exhibit low persistence in soil (DT90 values ranged from 1.5 to 8.7 days) (EFSA, 2010) and therefore studies investigating the nature of residues in rotational crops are not required (European Commission, 1997).

Nature of residues in processed commodities

Information on the degradation products formed during pasteurisation conditions is relevant to investigate possible impact of processing on the nature of residues in honey (see Section 3.1).

The effect of processing on the nature of flonicamid was investigated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2010) and in previous MRL applications (EFSA, 2014, 2018c). These studies showed that flonicamid, TFNA and TFNG are hydrolytically stable under standard processing conditions.

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

As the current MRL application is on honey, evaluation of analytical methods for enforcement of residues in primary crops is not required.

Storage stability of residues in plants

As the current MRL application is on honey, investigations of storage stability in primary crops are not required.

Nature of residues in honey

Honey is produced by bees from sugary secretions of plants (floral nectar mainly) through regurgitation, enzymatic conversion and water evaporation and followed by storage in the bee hives for a certain time period.

In the absence of specific metabolism studies with honey bees, studies investigating the nature of residues in primary crops and rotational crops and studies investigating the degradation during pasteurisation should be considered to determine the nature of residues in honey (European Commission, 2018). It is likely that the nature of residues in pollen and nectar, as well as in honey (resulting from the residues in floral nectar), is the same as in primary and rotational crops.

Considering that sufficient data investigating the metabolic profile of flonicamid in primary crops and the degradation of the active substance under standard hydrolysis conditions are available, no further information is required for the current application according to the guidelines. However, it would be desirable to further investigate whether enzymatic processes involved in the production of honey occurring in the bee gut or during the storage in the beehive have an impact on the nature of residues in honey.

Analytical methods for enforcement in honey

In the framework of the present assessment the applicant submitted validation data for a new method for enforcement of flonicamid and metabolites TFNA and TFNG residues in honey (Finland, 2024). The method, based on high performance liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry HPLC–MS/MS (HPLC–MS/MS), is considered acceptable according to SANTE/2020/12830 rev. 2 (European Commission, 2021). Validation was conducted for two mass transitions for flonicamid and its metabolites except for TFNA where the same mass transition was monitored twice and for confirmation a different stationary phase column was used. An independent laboratory validated study (ILV) was also submitted.

The method enables quantification of flonicamid and its metabolites TFNA and TFNG, included in the residue definition for enforcement, at an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for each analyte expressed as flonicamid (combined LOQ of 0.03 mg/kg) in honey.

Information on extraction efficiency of the analytical methods for enforcement of residues in honey is not required according to the guidance document on extraction efficiency (European Commission, 2023).

Storage stability of residues in honey

The storage stability of residues of flonicamid and its metabolites TFNG and TFNA, included in the residue definition for enforcement and risk assessment, was investigated in honey samples stored under frozen conditions in new studies, submitted under the current MRL application (Finland, 2024).

It was demonstrated that residues of flonicamid, TFNG and TFNA were stable for at least 6 months when stored at −18°C in honey.

Magnitude of residues in honey

In support of the current MRL application, the applicant submitted four independent residue trials performed in northern (two trials) and southern (two trials) Europe under semi‐field conditions. The active substance was applied on Phacelia tanacetifolia (surrogate crop) in one single foliar spray application at a rate of 124.5–132.0 g a.s./ha before flowering (BBCH 59: first petals visible but flower buds still closed).

According to the applicant, the application rate and treatment pattern tested in the residue trials was considered sufficiently representative of the worst‐case authorised GAP identified by the applicant (see Appendix A) (Finland, 2024).

Honey samples were collected when honey reached its commercial maturity (water content below 20% or after comb closure), 13–22 days after the application. According to the assessment of the EMS, the methods used are sufficiently validated for the quantification of residues of flonicamid and metabolites TFNG and TFNA in honey and are fit for purpose (Finland, 2024).

The samples of these residue trials were stored under conditions for which integrity of the samples has been demonstrated (Finland, 2024). The resulting residues in honey, determined as sum of flonicamid, TFNA and TFNG, expressed as flonicamid, were < 0.03, 0.058, 0.062 and 0.069 mg/kg. On the basis of submitted residue trials, an MRL of 0.15 mg/kg would be required in honey.

However, EFSA is of the opinion that the use pattern (application rate, number of applications and application timing) investigated in the submitted residue trials support neither the critical GAP identified by the applicant nor other authorised EU outdoor uses on melliferous crops. EFSA identified several uncertainties regarding the submitted residue trials: Choice of the GAP. The trials were based on an EU authorised indoor use that represents a higher critical application rate in comparison to other GAPs without accounting for whether bees would be exposed under the proposed GAP conditions (indoor) potentially leading to an overestimation of residues in honey which is not in line with the ALARA principle (see Section 3).Application rate. The honey trials were not conducted according to application rate of the selected cGAP (3 × 80 g a.s./ha, interval 7days). Instead, a single application of 128 g a.s./ha was performed. This application rate was calculated by applying a multiple application factor (MAF) of 1.6 to a single dose of 80 g/ha, according to the Guidance Document on Risk Assessment for Birds and Mammals (EFSA, 2009). EFSA does not consider appropriate the use of a MAF as it is currently not foreseen in the current technical guidance document in honey (European Commission, 2018).Number of applications and application timing. A single application before flowering at BBCH 59 (first petals visible but flower buds still closed) was performed on the surrogate crop, while, according to the authorised use patterns of flonicamid on melliferous crops, applications can also occur during flowering. Furthermore, it is noted that flonicamid is considered to have systemic properties, therefore, applications performed before and during flowering are regarded as relevant, in line with the technical guidance document in honey (European Commission, 2018). The impact of timing of application is considered not fully investigated in the submitted trials.

Choice of the GAP. The trials were based on an EU authorised indoor use that represents a higher critical application rate in comparison to other GAPs without accounting for whether bees would be exposed under the proposed GAP conditions (indoor) potentially leading to an overestimation of residues in honey which is not in line with the ALARA principle (see Section 3).

The trials were based on an EU authorised indoor use that represents a higher critical application rate in comparison to other GAPs without accounting for whether bees would be exposed under the proposed GAP conditions (indoor) potentially leading to an overestimation of residues in honey which is not in line with the ALARA principle (see Section 3).

Application rate. The honey trials were not conducted according to application rate of the selected cGAP (3 × 80 g a.s./ha, interval 7days). Instead, a single application of 128 g a.s./ha was performed. This application rate was calculated by applying a multiple application factor (MAF) of 1.6 to a single dose of 80 g/ha, according to the Guidance Document on Risk Assessment for Birds and Mammals (EFSA, 2009). EFSA does not consider appropriate the use of a MAF as it is currently not foreseen in the current technical guidance document in honey (European Commission, 2018).

Number of applications and application timing. A single application before flowering at BBCH 59 (first petals visible but flower buds still closed) was performed on the surrogate crop, while, according to the authorised use patterns of flonicamid on melliferous crops, applications can also occur during flowering. Furthermore, it is noted that flonicamid is considered to have systemic properties, therefore, applications performed before and during flowering are regarded as relevant, in line with the technical guidance document in honey (European Commission, 2018). The impact of timing of application is considered not fully investigated in the submitted trials.

Overall, EFSA is of the opinion that the honey trials provided do not reflect a residue situation in honey if flonicamid is used on melliferous crops according to the authorised EU GAPs. Moreover, the critical indoor GAP selected by the applicant is not relevant for honey production. At the current stage it is not possible to conclude whether residues measured in honey from the submitted trials are over‐ or under‐estimated and, therefore, no MRL proposal is derived in honey.

In the section below, EFSA additionally investigated whether residue levels determined in honey according to EU national monitoring programmes indicate a need for the existing residue levels in honey (currently at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg) to be modified.

Magnitude of residues from EU national monitoring programme

In the framework of Article 32 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (official national control programmes) 11 , monitoring data were submitted to EFSA. For the present assessment, an extraction of these data was performed by EFSA to retrieve the results for flonicamid residues in honey.

A total of 1617 honey product samples originating from EU Member States and measured according to the residue definition for enforcement (sum of flonicamid, TFNA and TFNG expressed as flonicamid) have been taken in the time period from 2009 to 2023. Samples reported as processed (10 samples) or measured in royal jelly (three samples), pollen (four samples), honeydew honey (nine samples) or other edible agricultural products (two samples) were disregarded (28 samples in total).

The LOQ of enforcement of the laboratories of the 1589 samples that were retained ranged from 0.001 to 0.05 mg/kg.

No samples were available for the year 2016 while only one sample was taken in 2015 and two in 2009. In general, an increase in the number of samples can be observed from the years 2018 to 2023 (1285 samples, 81% of the total dataset) compared to the previous years 2009 to 2017 (304 samples, 19% of the dataset). The year with the highest sampling number was 2022 with 421 samples. A boxplot with the residues of flonicamid in honey per year is available in Figure B.3 of Appendix B.3.2.2. In addition, it should be noted that the data retrieved from the year 2023 (194 samples) have not been validated yet by EFSA. Nevertheless, this dataset was included in the analysis for information purposes.

From the overall dataset, EFSA separately analysed the residue data for the last monitoring cycle, that corresponds to the years 2020 to 2022 (dataset 1). This was done to identify the short‐term trends of residue occurrence in honey from recently authorised uses of plant protection products (PPP) on melliferous crops and to see the impact of more sensitive analytical methods. The overall data set, covering the monitoring years of 2009 to 2023 (dataset 2) was analysed to capture the long‐term dynamics of residues in honey and to reflect any differences compared to the most recent data. Consequently, a total of 698 samples were available for data set 1 and 1589 samples were available for data set 2.

In Appendix B.3.2.2, EFSA reported the statistical indicators that are generally used to derive MRLs from monitoring data using the methodologies developed by FAO (FAO, 2016a, 2016b) and the rate of MRL exceedance for both datasets. EFSA calculated the residue concentrations in honey corresponding to 99.5th percentiles and the concentration corresponding to the 95th percentile of the data population at the 95% confidence level (EU approach laid down in Regulation (EU) No 283/2013 12 ). All the statistics were performed using the upper bound approach, therefore considering the LOQ values for those samples where the residue concentrations were quantified below the LOQ. The analysis shows that the 95th percentile of the data at the 95% confidence level (EU approach) as well as the 99.5th percentile of the data (FAO, approach) corresponds to residue values of flonicamid in honey falling within the range of 0.05–0.051 mg/kg for both data sets. This indicates that nearly all values for both data sets are clustered at or below the residue level of 0.05 mg/kg and there are no big differences between both datasets. In fact, a closer examination of the histograms of the datasets (see Figures B.1 and B.2 in Appendix B.3.2.2) reveals that the histograms are left‐skewed, with most samples concentrated around 0.01 to 0.03 mg/kg and very few values exceeding 0.05 mg/kg.

Residue levels in all honey samples (data set 2) ranged between 0.001 and 0.135 mg/kg. Only in 35 honey samples (2.2%) residues were quantified above the various LOQs of enforcement laboratories. Exceedance of the existing EU MRL (set at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg) was observed in only 3 samples: two samples from 2018 (0.135 mg/kg and 0.124 mg/kg) and in one sample from 2022 (0.089 mg/kg). This corresponds to 0.14% of the samples in dataset 1 and 0.2% of the samples in dataset 2. In addition, in one sample taken in 2022 residues were quantified at 0.05 mg/kg.

Overall, monitoring data indicate that existing EU MRL of 0.05 mg/kg (LOQ) is sufficient to account for flonicamid residues in honey from the existing authorised uses of flonicamid on melliferous crops in Europe.

Proposed MRLs

The residue trial data submitted under the present MRL application were not considered sufficiently representative to assess the magnitude of flonicamid residues in honey in support of the authorised flonicamid uses in Europe on melliferous crops. Thus, the MRL proposal of 0.15 mg/kg for flonicamid in honey derived by the EMS and the applicant is not supported. The current MRL for flonicamid in honey is set at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg.

In order to see whether the existing EU MRL at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg is sufficient to account for residues in honey, EFSA further investigated the monitoring data submitted to EFSA in the framework of the official national control programmes (2009–2023). These data indicate that the existing MRL set at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg may lead to a non‐compliance rate in only 0.14 to 0.2% of cases. Furthermore, the statistics based on the 99.5th and on the 95th percentile of the data population at the 95% confidence level confirm an MRL of 0.05 mg/kg.

In the framework of this application the applicant submitted a new method for enforcement that allows quantification of flonicamid residues in honey according to the enforcement residue definition at a lower LOQ of 0.03 mg/kg. However, the lowering of the existing enforcement LOQ is not proposed under the current assessment since monitoring data confirm that residues at a level of 0.05 mg/kg could occur in honey. Thus, EFSA proposes to maintain the existing EU MRL at the level of 0.05 mg/kg, but without an asterisk (*).

It is noted that the proposed MRL in honey represents the monitoring data and might need to be revised if new flonicamid uses on melliferous crops are applied for or new residue trials, representative for existing uses of flonicamid on melliferous crops, become available. Further risk management considerations are required.

In Section 4, EFSA assessed whether residues in honey at the level of 0.05 mg/kg are likely to pose a consumer health risk.

It should be noted that currently, MRLs set for honey are not applicable to other apicultural products following Commission Regulation (EU) 2018/62. 13

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

EFSA performed a dietary risk assessment using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018a, 2019a). This exposure assessment model contains food consumption data for different sub‐groups of the EU population and allows the acute and chronic exposure assessment to be performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology for pesticide residues (FAO, 2016a).

The toxicological reference values for flonicamid used in the risk assessment (i.e. ADI and ARfD values) were derived in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (European Commission, 2010a, 2010b). The metabolites included in the risk assessment residue definition were considered less toxic than the parent compound (EFSA, 2010).

Short‐term (acute) dietary risk assessment

The calculations were based on the input value of 0.05 mg/kg in honey. The list of input values for other commodities can be found in Appendix D.1. The acute exposure calculated for residues in honey accounted for less than 2% of the ARfD.

Regarding commodities not included in the present MRL applications, it should be noted that the short‐term exposure was found to exceed the ARfD for pears and peaches (105% and 114% of ARfD, respectively) as already identified in previous assessments (EFSA, 2019b, 2020a, 2020b, 2023, 2024). This exceedance is due to differences in the revised PRIMo versions compared to the version used in the MRL review 14 (EFSA, 2014). Further refinement following the use of unrounded highest residue (HR) values of the residue trials reported in the MRL review (EFSA, 2014) still lead to a slight exceedance of the ARfD for pears and peaches (102% and 113% of ARfD, respectively). Therefore, options for further refinement of the risk assessment shall be explored, e.g. in the process of the renewal of the approval or under a specific mandate or to consider lowering the existing MRLs for pears (0.3 mg/kg) and peaches (0.4 mg/kg) to the LOQ (EFSA, 2024).

Long‐term (chronic) dietary risk assessment

The comprehensive long‐term exposure assessment performed in the framework of the MRL review (EFSA, 2014) was updated with the STMRs derived in EFSA assessments of MRL applications issued after the MRL review and considering acceptable Codex MRLs (CXLs) (EFSA, 2015, 2016a, 2016b, 2017a, 2017b, 2018b, 2018c, 2018d, 2019b, 2020a, 2020b, 2021, 2023, 2024).

Moreover, in the context of this application, the MRL value of 0.05 mg/kg for honey was included in the calculations. The contributions of commodities for which no GAP was supported in the framework of the MRL review and in the EFSA opinions issued after the MRL review were not included in the calculation. The input values used in the exposure calculations are summarised in Appendix D.1.

No long‐term consumer intake concerns were identified for any of the European diets incorporated in EFSA PRIMo. The total calculated intake accounted for a maximum of 31% 15 of the ADI (NL toddler diet) (see also Appendix B.3). The contribution of residues expected in honey was < 0.1% of the ADI.

EFSA concluded that the long‐term and short‐term intake of residues of flonicamid in honey proposed at the level of 0.05 mg/kg according to monitoring data is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health.

For further details on the exposure calculations, a screenshot of the Report sheet of the PRIMo is presented in Appendix C.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The data submitted in the context of this MRL application were not found appropriate to derive an MRL proposal for honey reflecting the plant protection uses of flonicamid on melliferous crops in Europe. EFSA disagreed with the selected critical GAP by the applicant and the choice of residue trials design.

In order to see whether the existing EU MRL at the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg is sufficient to account for residues in honey, ESFA supplemented the current application with an assessment of monitoring data submitted in the framework of Article 32 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 (official national control programmes). The analysis of monitoring data submitted during 2009–2023 and in the last monitoring cycle (2020–2022) suggests that the 99.5th and the 95th percentile of the data population at the 95% confidence accounts for flonicamid residues in honey at a range of 0.05–0.051 mg/kg, which is at the level of the existing EU MRL, for which the identified non‐compliance rate was very low (≤ 0.2%). This indicates that nearly all values are clustered at or below the 0.05 mg/kg residue level with very few values exceeding this threshold. EFSA therefore proposes to maintain the existing MRL in honey at the level of 0.05 mg/kg, but without an asterisk.

It is noted that the proposed MRL in honey covers the currently authorised uses for flonicamid according to the results of monitoring data and might need to be revised if new uses on melliferous crops are authorised or in case new residue trials reflecting the existing critical uses of flonicamid on melliferous crops become available. Further risk management considerations are required.

EFSA concluded that the short‐term and long‐term intake of residues resulting from the potential transfer of residues of flonicamid into honey is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health. This conclusion may need to be reconsidered in light of the outcome of the ongoing EU pesticides peer review for the renewal of approval of the active substance.

In addition, although not related to the food commodities assessed within the present MRL application, the short‐term exposure was found to exceed the ARfD for pears and peaches. Possible short‐term intake concerns for some food commodities were already identified in previous EFSA assessments and are due to the use of a new version of PRIMo. Refinement options for the acute intake calculations could not be identified based on the available data for the current assessment and may be further investigated in future assessments.

Postoperative pain evaluation in patients undergoing endodontic treatment subsequent to working length determination with and without integrated apex locator endomotor: Randomized control trial
Authors: Gajender Rawat, Pravin Kumar, Vinay Kumar Chugh, Arun Kumar Duraisamy, Karishma Pathak, Rajat Sharma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Precise working length control during chemomechanical preparation is one of the primary factors for successful endodontic therapy.Aim:The study aims to evaluate the postoperative pain after determining working length with an integrated apex locator-endomotor and a separate apex locator-endomotor.Subjects and Methods:One hundred and twenty subjects with a diagnosis of symptomatic irreversible pulpitis and preoperative pain score Visual Analog Scale (VAS) >50 were allocated into Group I with nonintegrated apex locator and endomotor and Group II with integrated apex locator endomotor, further treated by single endodontist. Postoperative pain was assessed with a VAS chart on days 1, 3, and 7. The necessity of analgesic intake if required was considered as the highest VAS score.Statistical Analysis Used:Pre- and post-operative VAS scores between the groups were compared using the Mann–Whitney and one-way ANCOVA tests.Results:On days 1 and 3, Group II showed significantly lower values of postoperative pain and lesser frequency of rescue medication when compared with Group I (P < 0.05). On day 7, none of the patients reported postoperative pain.Conclusion:Simultaneous working length determination during instrumentation leads to significantly lesser postoperative pain.

INTRODUCTION

The most common and reliable method for measuring working length is by using apex locators, which complete and integrate the concept of anatomic and radiographic apex, allowing the clinician to achieve exact results.[1] According to Ricucci and Langeland, the most suitable location for the termination of endodontic procedures should be at or near the apical constriction.[2] The cemento-dentinal junction is an ideal place for termination but to detect this junction clinically is a challenge therefore apical constriction is seen as an appropriate ending for canal preparation.[3] Conventional methods of canal preparation aim to preserve apical canal constriction as a natural barrier between the apical tissue and the root canal, given its well-established role in root canal therapy.[4]

A dentist’s primary goal of achieving pain relief through root canal therapy is compromised when they merely use tactile and radiological methods for determining the precise working length.[5] Manual method of working length determination includes separate working length determination and canal instrumentation, which relies on rubber stopper against the coronal reference point. Whereas integrated apex locators endomotor have been demonstrated to be equally reliable and accurate in maintaining the working length concurrently instrumentation of root canal. These endomotor with in-built apex locator have an apex setting auto-reverse function in which the motor will stop automatically when the instrument reaches the apex resulting in less debris extrusion and lesser irritation of the periradicular tissues.[67]

The incidence of postoperative pain ranges from 3% to 58%[8] caused by irritation of periradicular tissue, which results in the release of chemical mediators which alters local adaption and periapical tissue pressure. Therefore, it is technically difficult to strike a balance between under/over instrumentation and total debridement.[910]

A recent study demonstrated that controlling the working length during canal preparation may significantly reduce postoperative discomfort on the 1st day.[7] However, results were inconclusive due to the limited sample size.

Hence, this study was designed to evaluate the postoperative pain and frequency of pain medication after determining separate working length with apex locator before instrumentation and with simultaneous integrated apex locator-endomotor. Another key point is that using the same manufacturer’s apex locator might yield more dependable and consistent findings than using various manufacturers.

The null hypothesis was that there was no significant difference in postoperative pain after determining working length with separate apex locator before canal instrumentation and simultaneous working length determination during canal preparation.

SUBJECTS AND METHODS

Following institutional ethical committee approval, the clinical trial was registered (CTRI/2022/09/045539). A total of 120 patients were enrolled from December 2022 to September 2023. Each subject got a patient information leaflet, and their written consent was obtained. Subjects were included from the patients referred to the Section of Conservative Dentistry and Endodontics, Department of Dentistry, All India Institute of Medical Sciences, Jodhpur. The minimum sample size was calculated using open Epi software, is 56 subjects per group considering 80% power and a significance level of <0.05. However, three patients were lost to follow-up.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Healthy subjects between the age of 15 and 65 years; with symptomatic irreversible pulpitis with healthy periodontium without any periapical lesion in mandibular first and second molar having Visual Analog Scale (VAS) score of preoperative pain >50 were included. Patients with systematic illness, local swelling, sinus tract, severely damaged tooth, absence of opposing tooth to the related tooth, endodontic retreatment, root resorption/fracture, with parafunctional habit and who have taken analgesics during past 24 h were excluded.

Block randomization was done to allocate individuals to two groups randomly using a software (available on www.randomizer.org).[11] Envelopes containing colored cardboard pieces assigned to a particular group was created by an assistant. This study is double-blinded as neither the primary investigator nor the operator was aware of the treatment protocol. Once the patient signed consent, the operator picked one of the envelopes and performed the treatment procedure accordingly. The color-coded cardboard pieces represented the following groups:

Group I (Red) – Working length was determined with apex locator, then chemo-mechanical preparation was done using TruNatomy single file systemGroup II (Green) – Simultaneous working length determination group. Chemomechanical preparation was done using endomotor integrated with apex locator.

By using an electric pulp test (Vitality Scanner Model 2006, Sybron Endo, New York, U.S.A.) and a pulp sensibility test with a cold refrigerant spray (ROEKO Endo-Frost, Coltene/Whaledent Inc., Cuyahoga Falls, U.S.A.), the clinical history of persistent, spontaneous, or referred pain suggestive of symptomatic irreversible pulpitis was confirmed. After the endodontic access, the diagnosis was confirmed by the presence of bleeding from the pulp tissue.

Clinical procedure

Standard operating procedure for each clinical steps was performed by one operator. Access to pulp chamber was obtained under rubber dam isolation after the administration of local anesthesia (2% lignocaine with 1:80,000 epinephrine). Canal patency was achieved by #10K file (Dentsply Maillefer, Ballaigues, Switzerland). Coronal preflaring was done using TruNatomy orifice modifier rotary file (Dentsply Maillefer, Ballaigues, Switzerland) used at a speed of 500 rpm and torque of 1.5 Ncm and further irrigated with 3% sodium hypochlorite (Percan, Septodent Healthcare Pvt India Ltd., India) using a side vented 27G irrigation needle. Glide path was prepared by using ProGlider rotary file (Dentsply Maillefer, Ballaigues, Switzerland).

Group I: A #10 K-file attached to an apex locator (CanalPro Apex Locator Compact, Coltene, Germany) was introduced into a root canal. When the red bar limit was reached, the file was withdrawn, and the working length was measured. Working length radiograph was taken and TruNatomy file system was used to prepare the canals according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Group II: The TruNatomy instrument was attached to the handpiece of the integrated endomotor (CanalPro Cl2i, Whaledent, Germany). The clip of the endomotor for the integrated apex locator was clamped to the instrument and preparation proceeded. The instrument automatically stopped when it reached the apex, because of the its integrated working length determination feature automatically controlling the working length and finishing the canal preparation.

Each canal was irrigated with 5 mL of NaOCl, dried and access cavity was sealed with a temporary filling material. Patients were instructed to take Ibuprofen 400 mg only in case of severe pain. Subjects were instructed to complete pain dairy for the evaluation postoperative pain and frequency of analgesic intake at specific intervals (i.e., 24 h, 72 h, and 7 days). Subjects were recalled after 7 days for return of pain diary and for clinical evaluation of complete endodontic therapy. Postoperative VAS score was considered highest for data analysis when subject taken analgesic for pain relief.

Assessment of postoperative pain

Postoperative pain was evaluated using VAS. The VAS is a 100 mm horizontal ruler with markings at every 10 mm. The subjects were instructed to draw a line at the position on the VAS that depicts the intensity of their pain, perpendicularly. The pain was categorized as follows: Mild pain (5–44 mm), moderate pain (45–74 mm), severe pain (75–100 mm), and no pain (0–4 mm).

Statistical analysis

The data were assessed for homogeneity by the homogeneity of variance and normality by the Shapiro–Wilk test. The Chi-square test was used to assess the data on gender and analgesic consumption following the procedure. To compare pre- and post-operative VAS score data between the groups, Mann–Whitney test and one-way ANCOVA test were used. Taking into account the covariate (preoperative pain) that will influence the postoperative pain value, ANCOVA test is administered.

RESULTS

There was 37.60% (44/117) male and 62.40% (73/117) female subjects included in this study. No statistically significant differences were found between the groups in basic demographic data (age, gender, and tooth number) (P > 0.05). Preoperative VAS value was more in Group II in comparison to Group I. However, statistically significant differences were found between the groups in terms of preoperative pain (P < 0.05) [Table 1]. At days 1 and 3 follow-up, postoperative VAS value was significantly less in Group II than Group I (P < 0.05). As a result, rather than having much higher preoperative pain, Group II patients have significantly less postoperative pain than Group I patients [Figure 2].

DISCUSSION

The goal of endodontic therapy is to eliminate diseased and necrosed pulpal debris as well as microorganisms from the pulpal space.[1213] The periapical region may repair and restore itself if the pulpal space has been thoroughly cleaned and disinfected. Under all conditions, extreme caution must be exercised to ensure that no infective material, medicament, or obturating material extends beyond the point of greatest apical constriction into the periapex; otherwise, various complications may occur, all leading to significant postoperative discomfort.[1415] As a result, it is critical to establish and maintain an appropriate working length during endodontic treatment to keep our canal preparation instruments and obturation materials contained up to the maximum apical constriction.[161718]

The CanalPro Cl2i motor has three function modes: Apex locator, endomotor, and combination. In the latter mode, simultaneous length control while instrumentation can be achieved, i.e., controls the working length automatically by stopping the instrumentation when the instrument reaches the working length. This dynamic feature may result in less periapical tissue damage, and therefore, less postoperative pain.[1920]

VAS was used to measure the pain intensity.[2122] Integrated apex locator group showed significantly less postoperative pain values on day 1 and significantly less frequency of analgesic intake in cases of postoperative pain. Group II showed significantly less postoperative pain level and none of the subjects required analgesic on day 3 follow-up. Despite having a greater prevalence of preoperative discomfort in Group II, it resulted in decreased postoperative pain when compared to the control group. None of the patient had postoperative pain at the end of the 7-day follow-up shows that debridement and disinfection of canals were complete with the resolution of local inflammatory changes. Furthermore, the apical constriction might not have altered or less damaged, resulting in less apical debris extrusion. Less postoperative discomfort is associated with less debris extrusion.[23]

With an off-centered parallelogram cross section and regressive taper, the recently developed TruNatomy file system preserves more peri-cervical dentin than reciprocating files while retaining the original root anatomy. Pericervical dentin plays a major role in maintaining the healthy biological status of the periodontal tissues and is responsible for the distribution of masticatory stress uniformly to the root. The file system is thermally treated after manufacturing.[2425] By combining the use of integrated endomotor apex locator and single file system, an appropriate balance between volume (less aggressive) and extent (length) of dentin removal is accomplished to mitigate a hospitable environment for periapical healing.[2627]

Due to less periapical extrusion, the integrated apex locator group showed less postoperative discomfort and required significantly less analgesics. As a result, these teeth may heal/repair faster than the control group.

CONCLUSION

Within the limitation of this study, it was concluded that simultaneous length control during root canal preparation is a useful strategy for preventing postoperative discomfort and lowering the need for analgesics.

ABCC2 rs2273697 is an independent determinant of tacrolimus intra-patient variability during the first year after kidney transplantation
Authors: Jia You, Hongxia Liu, Yunyun Yang, JiaZhao Fu, Mingxing Sui, Wenyu Zhao, Zhilong Yuan, Tao Ma, Zhuo Wang, Xuebin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTacrolimus is a critical immunosuppressive agent in kidney transplantation, but its pharmacokinetic variability, especially intra-patient variability (IPV), can lead to suboptimal outcomes. Genetic polymorphisms in CYP3A5 and ABCC2 may influence tacrolimus metabolism and transport, affecting dosing requirements and IPV. However, their combined impact during the first year after kidney transplantation remains insufficiently characterized.AimThis study aimed to evaluate the influence of CYP3A5*3 (rs776746) and ABCC2 (rs3740066, rs2273697) polymorphisms on tacrolimus exposure, IPV (C0-IPV and C0/D-IPV), and short-term renal allograft function in kidney transplant recipients, with a focus on the first post-transplant year.MethodA prospective cohort of 60 kidney transplant recipients was followed for one year. Tacrolimus trough concentrations (C0) were measured on postoperative Day 7, Day 14, Month 1, Month 3, Month 6, and Year 1. IPV was assessed using C0-IPV and dose-adjusted C0/D-IPV. Genotyping was performed using the SNaPshot platform. Multivariable regression models identified predictors of IPV and renal allograft function was evaluated by estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) at Year 1.ResultsCYP3A5*3 expressers required significantly higher tacrolimus doses than non-expressers from Day 7 through Month 3 (P < 0.05) and showed correspondingly lower C0/D values during this period (P < 0.01). ABCC2 rs3740066 was associated with C0/D at Month 6 (P = 0.037) and with the daily dose at Year 1 (P = 0.045). In the multivariable analysis, the ABCC2 rs2273697 variant (β = 8.429, 95% CI: 1.301–15.557, P = 0.021) was independently associated with tacrolimus C0-IPV. No significant associations were observed between IPV (C0-IPV or C0/D-IPV) and eGFR at Year 1.ConclusionCYP3A5 genotyping remains valuable for optimizing initial tacrolimus dosing, while ABCC2 polymorphisms, particularly rs2273697, contribute to tacrolimus C0-IPV during the first post-transplant year. Personalized dosing strategies using pharmacogenetic data could improve tacrolimus exposure stability. Larger studies with extended follow-up are needed to assess the long-term clinical implications of these findings.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s11096-026-02117-z.Keywords: Tacrolimus, Genetic polymorphism, Kidney transplantation, Cytochrome P-450 (CYP3A5), ATP binding cassette transporter Subfamily C Member 2 (ABCC2), Treatment outcome, Intra-patient variability

Introduction

Kidney transplantation is the most effective treatment for end-stage renal disease, providing superior long-term survival and quality of life compared with dialysis [1, 2]. The success of transplantation depends heavily on the maintenance of adequate immunosuppression to prevent allograft rejection. Tacrolimus, a calcineurin inhibitor, remains the cornerstone of immunosuppressive regimens in kidney transplantation [3]. Its narrow therapeutic window and substantial inter- and intra-individual pharmacokinetic variability necessitate careful dose individualization through therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) [4–6]. Subtherapeutic exposure increases the risk of acute rejection, whereas excessive concentrations may cause nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity, and other adverse effects [7].

Conventional TDM relies primarily on trough concentrations (C0) [8]. Despite TDM's utility, many patients experience unpredictable fluctuations in tacrolimus levels, adverse drug reactions, or rejection episodes [9], highlighting the limitations of trough-based monitoring. Intra-patient variability (IPV) of tacrolimus exposure has therefore emerged as a complementary pharmacokinetic marker [10–12]. High IPV, reflecting instability in drug absorption, metabolism, or adherence, has been associated with the development of donor-specific antibodies (dnDSA), chronic allograft injury, and inferior graft survival [10].

The pharmacokinetics of tacrolimus are largely determined by metabolic and transporter pathways. The cytochrome P450 3A (CYP3A) family, particularly CYP3A5, plays a dominant role in metabolism [13, 14]. The CYP3A5 rs776746 (*3) variant produces a non-functional protein, and individuals carrying at least one *1 allele (expressers) typically require higher doses to reach therapeutic concentrations than *3/*3 non-expressers [15–17]. In addition to metabolism, tacrolimus disposition is influenced by drug efflux transporters such as P-glycoprotein (ABCB1) and multidrug resistance-associated protein 2 (MRP2; encoded by ABCC2) [18–20]. Functional polymorphisms in ABCC2, including rs3740066 and rs2273697, may modify transporter expression or activity, thereby affecting tacrolimus absorption, distribution, and clearance [20].

Although the effect of CYP3A5*3 on early dose requirements and C0/dose ratios is well established [8], its relationship with long-term tacrolimus IPV, particularly during the first post-transplant year, remains insufficiently characterized. Likewise, evidence regarding the influence of ABCC2 polymorphisms on tacrolimus variability is limited and inconsistent, and the clinical significance of genetically determined IPV in graft function (e.g., estimated glomerular filtration rate, eGFR) is unclear. Importantly, few studies have examined the combined contribution of CYP3A5 and ABCC2 variants, and such data are particularly scarce in Chinese kidney transplant recipients.

Aim

This study evaluated the impact of CYP3A5 (rs776746) and ABCC2 (rs3740066, rs2273697) polymorphisms on tacrolimus pharmacokinetics—including C0, dose-adjusted concentrations (C0/D), and IPV—and explored their potential association with renal allograft function during the first year after kidney transplantation.

Method

Study design and participants

This prospective cohort study enrolled patients who underwent kidney transplantation at the Shanghai Changhai Hospital between October 2021 and December 2023. A total of 60 recipients were included, of which 59 had successful genotyping. Tacrolimus trough concentrations (C0) were measured at six standardized time points: postoperative Day 7, Day 14, Month 1, Month 3, Month 6, and Year 1.

Inclusion criteria were (1) provision of written informed consent, (2) first or second kidney transplantation, and (3) maintenance of a tacrolimus-based immunosuppressive regimen. The exclusion criteria were multi-organ transplantation and discontinuation of tacrolimus during follow-up.

This study was exploratory. No formal priori sample size calculation was performed because the genotype frequencies and anticipated effect sizes for tacrolimus IPV in this population were unknown.

Immunosuppressive regimen

All the participants received tacrolimus-based maintenance therapy. Induction therapy consisted of basiliximab or rabbit anti-human thymocyte globulin. Maintenance immunosuppression included tacrolimus, mycophenolate derivatives, and glucocorticoids.

Initial tacrolimus doses were weight-based and subsequently adjusted to achieve target C0 ranges: 10–15 ng/mL in the first postoperative month; 8–12 ng/mL at 3 months; and 5–12 ng/mL from 6 to 12 months. Dose adjustments were performed following routine TDM and clinical assessments.

Tacrolimus concentration measurement

Whole blood samples were collected immediately prior to the morning tacrolimus dose (trough level). Tacrolimus concentrations were quantified using a chemiluminescent microparticle immunoassay on an ARCHITECT i1000SR platform (Abbott Diagnostics).

Genotyping procedures

Genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood using a commercially available DNA extraction kit. PCR amplification was performed for CYP3A5*3 (rs776746), ABCC2 rs3740066, and ABCC2 rs2273697, using locus-specific primers. The PCR products were enzymatically purified (FastAP, Exo I), followed by single-base extension using an ABI SNaPshot Multiplex Kit.

The extension products were separated via capillary electrophoresis on an ABI 3730xl Analyzer. Genotypes were analyzed using GeneMapper 4.0 (Applied Biosystems).

Haplotype analyses were explored but were not included in the final analysis because of insufficient sample size; therefore, only individual SNP analyses were presented.

Data collection and variable definitions

Clinical and laboratory data

Demographic characteristics, donor type, immunosuppressive therapy, and laboratory parameters were extracted from the hospital information and TDM systems. Renal function was evaluated using serum creatinine (Scr) and eGFR calculated using the Modification of Diet in Renal Disease (MDRD) equation for Chinese patients:: eGFR (mL/(min*1.73 m2) = 186 × (Scr)−1.154 × (age)−0.203 × (0.742 if female) [21].

Tacrolimus pharmacokinetic metrics

For each TDM time point, the daily tacrolimus dose (mg and mg·kg−1) and corresponding C0 value were recorded. The dose-adjusted concentration (C0/D) was calculated as: C0/Dose/Weight (ng·ml−1/mg·kg−1).

Intra-patient variability (IPV)

The IPV was evaluated using two indices. C0-IPV was defined as the coefficient of variation (CV%) of all tacrolimus trough concentrations (C0) obtained from Day 7 to Year 1, IPV was calculated in recipients with at least 3 trough measurements. C0/D-IPV was defined as the CV% of dose-adjusted concentrations (C0/D), calculated by dividing the trough concentration (ng/mL) by the body-weight adjusted daily dose (mg·kg−1). Both indices were calculated using the formula CV (%) = (standard deviation / mean) × 100% [22]. C0-IPV described variability in clinically observed trough levels, whereas C0/D-IPV provided a dose-normalized measure.

Statistical analysis

Allele and genotype frequencies were tested for Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium using PLINK (v2.0). Due to the limited sample size, haplotype and interaction analyses were not performed further. Continuous variables were evaluated for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test and presented as mean ± SD or median with interquartile range (IQR), as appropriate. Categorical variables are expressed as counts and percentages. Comparisons of tacrolimus C0 and C0/D across genotypes were conducted using the Kruskal–Wallis test (three groups) or the Mann–Whitney U test (two groups). Comparisons of IPV between two genotype groups were performed using an unpaired t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test, as appropriate.

Tacrolimus IPV was expressed as the CV% and analyzed as a continuous variable. To identify its predictors, a two‑stage regression approach was used. First, univariate linear regression was performed for each candidate predictor (including demographic, clinical, and genetic variables). Variables showing an association with IPV at P < 0.10 in univariate analysis were then entered into a stepwise multivariate linear regression model (forward selection, entry P < 0.05, removal P > 0.10) to identify independent factors. Model assumptions were checked using residual plots and variance inflation factors (VIF < 5). Results are presented as the unstandardized regression coefficient (β) with its 95% confidence interval (CI). A two‑tailed P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 27.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), and figures were generated using GraphPad Prism version 10.2.

Ethics approval

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Shanghai Changhai Hospital (CHEC2021-133, approval date 2021-08-28).

Impact of CYP3A5*3 and ABCC2 polymorphisms on tacrolimus exposure

CYP3A5 expressers required higher tacrolimus doses than non-expressers throughout the early postoperative phase, with significant differences observed on Day 7, Day 14, Month 1, and Month 3 (P < 0.05). Correspondingly, C0/D values were significantly lower in the expressers at these time points (P < 0.01). These differences diminished by Months 6 and Year 1 (Fig. 3, Supplemental Table 2).

Association between IPV and Renal Allograft Function

Neither C0-IPV nor C0/D-IPV correlated with eGFR at Year 1 (Spearman’s correlation, P > 0.05, Supplementary Figure S1). Consistently, when recipients were grouped into high- and low-IPV groups based on the median IPV value, eGFR at Year 1 did not differ between groups.

Multivariable Analysis

In univariate linear regression analysis, deceased‑donor grafts, CYP3A5 expresser status, and ABCC2 variants rs3740066 and rs2273697 were significantly associated with tacrolimus C0-IPV at a threshold of P < 0.10 (Table 2). No other demographic or clinical variables reached the pre‑specified threshold for inclusion in the multivariate model.

A stepwise regression analysis revealed that only ABCC2 rs2273697 was identified as an independent predictor of higher tacrolimus C0-IPV (β = 8.429, 95% CI: 1.301, 15.557, P = 0.021). None of the other variables, including donor type, CYP3A5 genotype, or ABCC2 rs3740066, were retained in the final model (P > 0.05). Using C0/D-IPV as the dependent variable, no independent predictors were identified (P > 0.05).

Discussion

This prospective cohort study evaluated the influence of CYP3A5 (rs776746) and ABCC2 (rs3740066, rs2273697) polymorphisms on tacrolimus exposure and IPV during the first year after kidney transplantation. As expected, the CYP3A5 genotype significantly affected tacrolimus dose requirements and dose-adjusted concentrations, particularly in the early postoperative period. We also identified independent associations between ABCC2 rs2273697 and C0-IPV. In contrast, no significant relationship was observed between the IPV and renal allograft function at Year 1.

Consistent with previous studies [23], carriers of at least one functional CYP3A5*1 allele required higher tacrolimus doses during the early postoperative phase to achieve therapeutic concentrations, reflecting the well-established effect of CYP3A5*3, a loss-of-function allele associated with reduced or absent CYP3A5 activity [24, 25]. CYP3A5 expressers exhibit increased tacrolimus clearance and lower C0/D values, as observed in our cohort from Day 7 to Month 3. Although genotype-dependent differences diminished after Month 3, likely due to dose adjustments guided by TDM, our findings reinforce the utility of CYP3A5 genotyping for initial dose optimization [26]. Similarly, Pasari et al. reported that CYP3A5 expressers required higher doses from 6 months onward, while non-expressers showed consistently higher dose-adjusted concentration ratios [27]. These results underscore the importance of CYP3A5 genotype in dosing, but also highlight that effective dose adjustment and monitoring can mitigate its impact on IPV and early graft outcomes, emphasizing the multifactorial nature of tacrolimus exposure stability.

We also observed time-dependent effects of ABCC2 rs3740066 on tacrolimus pharmacokinetics. ABCC2 encodes MRP2, an efflux transporter that contributes to biliary excretion and intestinal drug disposition, and thus may influence tacrolimus absorption and clearance [18, 28, 29]. Although P-glycoprotein (encoded by ABCB1) is widely recognized as a relevant transporter in tacrolimus disposition, it was not included as a primary objective in this study. In our preliminary analyses, ABCB1 variants showed no clear association with tacrolimus C0, C0/D and IPV (C0-IPV, C0/D-IPV) in this cohort. Moreover, prior reports on the relationship between ABCB1 and tacrolimus exposure remain inconsistent and their clinical relevance controversial [30, 31]. In contrast, evidence for ABCC2 variants—particularly regarding their contribution to IPV and their joint influence with the major metabolic determinant CYP3A5—is comparatively limited. We therefore focused on CYP3A5 and ABCC2 to provide complementary and potentially novel insights into genetic determinants of tacrolimus exposure stability. Existing data on ABCC2 polymorphisms and tacrolimus pharmacokinetics are mixed. Cheng et al. reported no significant association of ABCC2 rs2273697 or rs3740066 with tacrolimus C0/D in Chinese kidney transplant recipients[32], whereas a subsequent study by Cheng et al. identified ABCC2 rs3740066 as an independent predictor of early tacrolimus C0/D, with TT carriers exhibiting a 14.42% higher C0/D than CC carriers (P < 0.05) [33]. Our finding suggests a potential role of rs3740066 in modulating long-term tacrolimus clearance, particularly at later time points. However, these findings warrant further validation given the small sample size and the possibility of time-varying drug–drug interactions or differences in hepatic function.

While prior studies typically calculated IPV during stable-dose periods to minimize the impact of dose adjustments [22, 34], we calculated IPV using all available trough concentrations from Day 7 to Year 1, covering both dose-adjustment and stable phases. This strategy was intended to better reflect real-world tacrolimus management, in which dose adjustment is frequent and unavoidable during the first post-transplant year. However, this inclusive approach may inflate IPV estimates compared with calculations limited to stable-dose periods, as it incorporates variability introduced by clinical interventions. Thus calculated, C0-IPV captures overall exposure fluctuations resulting from the combined effects of pharmacokinetic variability, clinical dose adjustments, and possible non-adherence. To partially account for the influence of dose changes, we additionally calculated C0/D-IPV as a dose-normalized metric that may better reflect variability in drug clearance. Consistent with our previous work [35], we consider C0-IPV a clinically relevant indicator of exposure variability in routine practice. Together, C0-IPV and C0/D-IPV provide complementary perspectives on tacrolimus variability and underscore the importance of clearly defining IPV calculation methods when interpreting and comparing results across studies.

Our study identified a significant association between the ABCC2 rs2273697 polymorphism and tacrolimus C0-IPV in kidney transplantation. To our knowledge, this relationship has been reported for the first time. This novel finding suggests that ABCC2 plays an important role in influencing IPV in tacrolimus exposure, particularly during the first year post-transplantation. Although prior studies have examined the influence of ABCC2 variants on tacrolimus pharmacokinetics, none have specifically linked them to IPV. A retrospective cohort study in living‑donor liver transplantation found no significant association between several ABCC2 polymorphisms (− 24C > T, 1249G > A, 3972C > T) and tacrolimus IPV [36]. Given that our findings newly implicate ABCC2 rs2273697 in tacrolimus IPV, further validation in larger, multicenter kidney transplant cohorts is warranted. Such studies would help clarify whether ABCC2 exerts a uniquely important role in renal transplant recipients and whether its pharmacogenetic profiling could enhance individualized immunosuppression management.

While CYP3A5*3 did not independently predict IPV, consistent with prior studies on kidney transplantation [13, 37, 38], our findings align with evidence suggesting that genetic contributors to IPV may differ by organ type and clinical context [39]. These data highlight the locus-specific and context-dependent nature of pharmacogenomic effects on long-term tacrolimus variability, and emphasize the need to consider multiple genetic pathways when developing individualized dosing strategies.

Contrary to earlier studies suggesting a link between high tacrolimus IPV and inferior graft outcomes [40], neither C0-IPV nor C0/D-IPV was associated with eGFR at Year 1. This may be due to effective TDM mitigating the potential negative effects of variability, the relatively small cohort size, or the limited follow-up duration. Long-term studies are needed to determine whether genotype- or donor-related variability contributes to chronic allograft dysfunction.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the modest sample size (n = 59 genotyped patients) limits statistical power, particularly for multivariable analyses, and may result in unstable regression estimates. Second, the subgroup of living-donor recipients was small (n = 9), and conclusions regarding donor type should therefore be interpreted with caution. Third, the one-year follow-up duration may be insufficient to detect long-term associations between IPV and graft outcomes. While we included all trough concentrations, including those during dose adjustments, this approach might have introduced additional variability, which, although reflective of real-world practice, could confound the interpretation of long-term stability. Future large-scale, multicenter studies with extended follow-ups are needed to confirm these findings and better understand the clinical implications of ABCC2-related tacrolimus variability.

Conclusion

This study confirms the value of CYP3A5 genotyping in guiding initial tacrolimus dose optimization and identifies ABCC2 polymorphisms, particularly rs2273697, as independent determinants of tacrolimus intra-patient variability. These findings support the potential utility of incorporating pharmacogenetic profiling into individualized tacrolimus management to enhance exposure stability during the first posttransplant year. However, the absence of an observed association between IPV and short-term allograft function demonstrates the need for larger studies with extended follow-up periods to clarify the long-term clinical impact of these pharmacokinetic variations.

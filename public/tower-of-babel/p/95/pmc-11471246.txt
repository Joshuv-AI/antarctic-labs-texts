The impact of environmental, social and governance disclosure on stock prices: Empirical research in Vietnam
Authors: Nhi Thanh Thao Ha, Thanh Cong Nguyen, Nguyen Thai Bao Ho
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The aims of this research is to evaluate the impact of Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) information disclosure (using both financial and non-financial information) on stock prices of businesses in Vietnam; From there, propose recommendations to promote transparent practices and information disclosure about ESG of businesses related activities; Contribute to developing sustainable investment goals in businesses, and the financial market in general. Based on the (Ohlson, 1995) [40] valuation model, and developed by Barth and Clinch (2009) [41] the research was conducted empirically in 30 listed companies in Vietnam in the period 2019–2022. Research results based on the GLS method show that ESG positively impacts stock prices. Additionally, this paper conducted an in-depth analysis of each individual pillar and found that the environmental pillar was considered the most meaningful to market participants. However, the correlation between ESG and stock prices is weakened for environmentally sensitive businesses. This study also makes a valuable contribution to the current literature on non-financial information reporting by using manually collected data and investigating the effects of ESG reporting on market participants in the public system voluntary information disclosure system of an emerging market like Vietnam.

Introduction

In recent years, the problems of environmental pollution and climate change have become increasingly serious. Therefore, many countries around the world have agreed on the importance and urgency of sustainable development to solve this problem. In response to global concerns about sustainability, the United Nations (UN) founded the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Along with that, businesses operating in the financial market also play a crucial role in promoting the execution of the SDGs. Furthermore, promoting sustainable development in businesses particularly and in the financial market generally also contributes to the comprehensive implementation of the SDGs through the development of green capital and green finance [1]. On that basis, sustainable development considerations are also increasingly enhanced and play a prominent role in financial markets [2].

Sustainable investing or Socially Responsible Investing (SRI), according to the Principles for Responsible Investment (PRI), is an investment method that takes into account environmental, social and governance (ESG) factors when making investment decisions [3]. According to data from Bloomberg Intelligence, as of the end of 2020, ESG assets worldwide reached $40 trillion and are expected to increase to $53 trillion by the end of 2025 [4]. This shows that ESG is emerging as a trend and being adopted by investors as an investment philosophy.

On the other hand, research by Ref. [5] also points out that stock prices are evidence of the level of investor reaction to the topic surrounding ESG information. The primary cause is that investors are interested in information disclosure and this impacts their decision-making to invest in the corporation, thereby creating price fluctuations in the market. In addition, transparent disclosure of ESG information also brings benefits to businesses such as improving financial efficiency [6].

Until now, achieving efficiency in sustainable investing, the topics of integrating environmental, social and governance into financial platforms are an issue of concern for investors and investors management as well as academic researchers around the globe are interested [5,7,8]. However, the experimental findings of the research remain inconsistent. Some find that ESG has a positive impact on stock prices [5,9]; while a few articles reported negative evidence [10]; there are even authors who say that investors are completely uninterested in ESG practices [11].

Besides, businesses in Vietnam are not left out of this trend. However, currently, reporting of ESG activities by businesses is still voluntary and there is still no framework for disclosing specific ESG practices. This leads to the problem of incomplete data, which remains a barrier in the process of evaluating ESG practices and disclosures. A [12] study conducted in Vietnam showed that 71 % of companies do not have enough knowledge about the necessary data to be able to report. Therefore, research related to ESG performance in businesses in Vietnam is still limited and the lack of ESG data is an important issue. On that basis, recognizing the importance of practicing and disclosing ESG by businesses along with the desire to fill this academic gap in Vietnam, the study decided to build a scale and method ESG scoring for businesses in Vietnam to assess the impact of ESG information disclosure on stock prices at 30 companies with the highest capitalization and liquidity that are representative of the Vietnamese market in the period 2019–2022.

This research contributes to theory and practice in five aspects. Firstly, combine three theories comprising of legitimacy theory, stakeholder theory and signaling theory in explaining the market reaction expressed through stock prices in terms of sustainable investment. Secondly, having a comprehensive view of the Environment - Social - Governance of the three aspects that create the "golden triangle" of sustainable development in businesses is the foundation for achieving the goal of developing a sustainable economy within the framework of the sustainable development in Vietnam from 2019 to 2022. In addition, the research found a positive impact of disclosing ESG information from overall to individual levels on financial performance, specifically corporate stock prices based on the modified Ohlson model by the GLS method. Furthermore, evidence for a positive correlation between ESG information disclosure and stock prices is found to decline in companies in environmentally sensitive industries in the Vietnamese market. Finally, propose recommendations appropriate to the current state of ESG in an emerging economy like Vietnam based on empirical research results.

The composition of this article is structured into five distinct sections. Subsequent to this introduction, the second portion expounds upon the literature review. The third section deliberates upon methodology. Research findings and conclusions are sequentially unveiled within the fourth and fifth sections, respectively.

Literature review

Theoretical perspective

This study employs a theoretical framework that integrates the theories of signaling, stakeholder, and legitimacy. Similar combinations have already been examined in a number of notable articles provided by writers [13,14]. According to research by Ref. [15], a company's legitimacy is based on the idea that its actions are desirable and in line with the norms, values, beliefs, and meanings that society has developed. Thereby, under the lens of legal theory, it shows that the practice and disclosure of ESG information is used by corporate managers as a self-protection strategy to reduce politically relevant costs. For example [16], demonstrated that disclosing social responsibility activities to demonstrate corporate legitimacy contributed to decreasing asymmetry information between managers and investors, helping in lowering the cost of equity capital and raising the company's market value. Stakeholder theory also supports this opinion based on the perspectives of investors and business stakeholders. As [17] stated, to the expectations of the partners (stakeholders) with whom the company engages through a network of different joint ventures will ultimately determine the success of the product and service [18]. also point out that social responsibility reporting to stakeholder groups contributes to reducing information asymmetry between shareholders and the company, helping them make more informed decisions. Meanwhile, signaling theory is said to be a tool used to minimize information asymmetry between companies and stakeholders, especially investors [19]. Based on signaling theory, disclosing a company's ESG information is considered a strategy to ensure that investors will respond to the signal that this is good information to take into account when assessing the company's market value. Therefore, ESG disclosure is expected to help increase the investment willingness of shareholders in the market [20]. Overall, these theories are related and mutually supportive in explaining how ESG disclosure practices can impact a company's stock price.

ESG and stock price

The notion of ESG was initially introduced in the United Nations Principles for Responsible Investment (UNPRI) report. From then on, ESG has emerged as a reliable tool promoted by many organizations and non-governmental organizations (NGOs). In general, the overall ESG includes three main pillars Environmental, Social and Governance that are combined in the corporate's internal activities to ensure sustainability.

Conducting ESG activities contributes to reducing environmental pollution problems, improving financial performance as well as satisfying stakeholders [9], with specific benefits such as reducing costs [21] or attracting customers [22]. These factors make the market expect improved profits and cash flows in the future of the business [21], consequently affecting its market value [23]. Furthermore [24], found that the transparency of ESG factors also helps enhance shareholder confidence and creates a favorable connection between the level of transparency and the company's worth. In addition, this information also helps avoid risks and uncertainties thus not causing a sharp drop in stock prices [25].

However, the above studies provide mixed results in analyzing the association between information disclosure and share prices. Some studies exist that suggest that ESG disclosure has an adverse effect on a company's market value [10,26]. Most recently, research by Ref. [10] was conducted on 600 listed companies in Europe during the period 2014–2020, a leading market economy in implementing ESG but resulting in a negative relationship between ESG disclosure and stock prices. This is explained by investors saying that the company's investment in ESG is considered a waste of resources and reacts negatively to the announced information, thereby causing the stock price to decline. Furthermore, research by Ref. [11] found that there was no correlation between ESG information disclosure and stock prices.

A literature review revealed very few studies that have explored ESG information on stock prices in emerging countries like Vietnam. Although many efforts are made to find evidence for the relationship between ESG information and stock prices, studies have largely encountered data difficulties. In 2022, the report on ESG Readiness in Vietnam in 2022 conducted by PwC pointed out that 44 % of businesses have made ESG commitments, including companies listed on the financial market accounting for 35 % [12]. From there, it can be seen that although it is still quite new, the concern of the financial market has created motivation for listed companies to implement ESG. Therefore, in order to fill the literature gap, the study exploring the impact of ESG information disclosure on stock prices was conducted in the context of the Vietnamese stock market.

ESG in environmentally sensitive industries

The level of ESG practices and disclosures for each company may vary depending on the business environment and fluctuations in the macroeconomic environment [27,28]. Therefore, investors' use of ESG information in stock valuation may vary between specific industry groups. Therefore, to evaluate the level of difference, the author considers the relationship between ESG information disclosure and stock prices at businesses operating in fields that are sensitive to the environment. Industries classified as environmentally sensitive include those in mining and quarrying, energy (gas and oil), chemicals, electronic utilities, steel manufacturing and construction [[29], [30], [31]]. According to Ref. [32], companies operating in environmentally sensitive fields will be at risk of facing an increase in environmental-related litigation and legal issues in the future. Therefore, these companies often have higher overall ESG scores because they are exposed to a higher level of risk [28] and there is social concern and scrutiny in these companies more stringent [32]. This leads to a powerful link between ESG information disclosure and stock prices of businesses operating in environmentally sensitive sectors.

Empirical models

The objective of the study is to investigate the relationship between non-financial information disclosure and the value of company stocks. In order to accomplish this goal, this study uses the [40] and modified by Ref. [41].

The [40] model explains the value of a company as a function in terms of both its financial (specifically book value and accounting earnings) alongside non-financial information. However, the author has not yet specified what this non-financial information includes. Therefore, in this study, the ESG score value is used as a proxy for non-financial information, based on previous empirical studies [11,14]. Additionally, the study utilizes the model developed by Ref. [41], which aims to address issues of scale effects and heterogeneity. Therefore, financial variables such as book value and earnings will be scaled based on the total of common shares outstanding. Furthermore, control variables such as firm size [13,26], debt leverage [14,42], and firm age [5,43] are incorporated into the model to control for potential bias effects which may skip.

To investigate the influence of overall and individual ESG practices on a company's stock market price, the author proposes the following research models:(1)Pit = β0+β1EPSit + β2BVPSit + β3Eit+ β4SIZEit+ β5LEVit+ β6AGEit+εit(2)Pit = β0+β1EPSit + β2BVPSit + β3Sit+ β4SIZEit+ β5LEVit+ β6AGEit+ εit(3)Pit = β0+β1EPSit + β2BVPSit + β3Git+ β4SIZEit+β5LEVit+ β6AGEit+ εit(4)Pit = β0+β1EPSit +β2BVPSit + β3ESGit+β4SIZEit+β5LEVit+ β6AGEit+ εit

The dependent variable in all models is Pit, which stands for the stock price of business i three months after the end of fiscal year t. The book value per share (BVPS) and earnings per share (EPS) of business i in year t are the independent variables in finance. Where Eit represents the environment pillar score of company i in year t; Sit represents the social pillar score of company i in year t; Git represents the governance pillar score of company i in year t, and ESGit is the company i's overall disclosure score in year t. The subscripts i and t denote a particular company i at the end of year t within the analysis period (2019–2022), and ε is the residual; β1, β2, β3, β4, β5 and β6 are the coefficients.

Finally, this study expands the model to examine whether there is a correlation between ESG disclosure and higher stock prices for companies in environmentally sensitive industries. As argued by Ref. [44], including the interaction between the ESG score and a dummy variable for environmentally sensitive firms can help identify any differences in this relationship. Therefore, the model is expanded to include the following:(5)Pit = β0+β1EPSit+β2BVPSit+β3ESGit+β4SIZEit+β5LEVit+β6AGEit+β7ESGitxES+εitWith the inclusion of the ESGitxES as the interaction variable between the ESG disclosure score and the company's sensitivity, Model (5) is an expansion of the baseline models. If the business is in an ecologically sensitive industry, the dummy variable ES gets on the value 1, and if not, it gets on the value 0.

Analytical techniques

A number of techniques, including multiple regression methods like ordinary least squares (Pooled OLS), fixed effects models (FEM), and random effects models (REM), have been used in this work to assess the influence of ESG disclosure on stock prices using panel data [13,45]. In particular, after the process of testing model violations, if violations of errors such as heteroskedasticity, autocorrelation,etc appear, the GLS method is proposed by many studies to be used [43,46]. In addition, there are also many studies using the two-stage least squares method (2SLS) to avoid bias during the simultaneous estimation of models [47] or the GMM method to account for potential unobserved heterogeneity and homogeneity problems [48]. However, in this research, the author chose to use Pooled OLS, FEM, REM and GLS methods to estimate the impact, overcome model defects, test the hypotheses and achieve results of the research. An important reason for choosing the method is also because of the characteristics of the data set with 30 companies and context in Vietnam. Furthermore, since the study utilizes the VN30 index, which includes a diverse range of industries as analytical data, the results obtained through Pooled OLS may be limited due to the influence of specific factors within each individual industry. In addition, this study uses GLS to overcome heteroskedasticity errors and autocorrelation detected. Finally, research techniques were performed using Stata 18.0 software.

The very important first step in the multiple regression estimation process is to check for multicollinearity in the model. Therefore, the variance inflation factor (VIF) is used. Running results from the software show that all models do not suffer from multicollinearity because all VIF coefficients are under 5. Next, Pooled OLS, REM and FEM methods are used respectively to estimate the impact between the company's composite ESG score and stock price. After running regressions of all three methods OLS, REM, and FEM, model selection tests continue to be conducted. Starting with the Breusch-Pagan LM test, the results are prob > chi and statistically significant, so the REM model is considered better than OLS. Next, the Hausman test is used to choose between FEM and REM. Finally, based on the FEM results, the best model is selected. Then, the Wald and Wooldridge tests were conducted, resulting in the detection of heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation, leading to estimates that were not highly accurate in the model. For this reason, the study decided to use the GLS method to overcome the problem [49].

Descriptive Statistics.

Table 4 also denotes the current status of sustainable development information disclosure for the 30 companies with the largest market capitalization listed on the Vietnamese stock market from 2019 to 2022. The statistical results reveal that the average ESG value is 56.12 %, with a standard deviation of 15.43. This reveals that the overall ESG performance in the VN30 index is not particularly high and is relatively concentrated. This could be ascribed to the actuality that Vietnam is a growing country and the dissemination of ESG information is still in its early stages. Furthermore, Vietnamese businesses tend to prioritize disclosing management-related information rather than social and environmental information. This is evident in the governance pillar (G) score, which has the highest average value of 80.75 %, while the environment pillar (E) score has the lowest average value of 32.27 %. This suggests that companies place more emphasis on governance structures and senior executive roles in their overall sustainability efforts. However, the average social pillar (S) score is 72.73 %, indicating that businesses are making efforts to incorporate employee welfare policies and contribute to social community service activities as part of their business strategy.

Moving to the control variables, the average enterprise size (SIZE) is 32.77, with a low standard deviation of 1.22. The range of values for SIZE is from 30.53 to 35.29, indicating a relatively symmetrical dispersion of scale. This is due to the fact that the selected companies are the largest in terms of capitalization and are representative of Vietnam. The minimum value for leverage is 2.18 % and the maximum value is 95.09 %, which is nearly 44 times the minimum value. This implies that the financial leverage ratios of the listed corporations varied significantly from one another. The average age (AGE) of the 30 selected businesses is 30.8 years old. The youngest company is Vincom Retail Joint Stock Company (VRE), which was founded in 2019 and is only 7 years old.

Finally, the dummy variable ES represents the environmentally sensitive industry characteristics of the enterprise and has an average value of 0.17. This indicates that out of the 30 enterprises being considered, 17 % operate in environmentally sensitive fields.

Pairwise correlation

Table 5 displays the results of the Pearson correlation matrix for the variables in the model. The data suggests that the majority of the independent variables are statistically significant (prob<0.05), indicating a linear relationship with the dependent variable (stock price). However, the main independent variables, such as social pillars (S) and governance (G), have a test coefficient of t > 0.05, suggesting that they are unlikely to have a significant impact on company value. Model 1 evaluates the impact of environmental information disclosure (E) on stock prices, using it as a proxy for non-financial information. The results indicate a positive correlation between environmental factors and stock prices, with a significance level of 5 %. This finding is in line with stakeholder theory, as shareholders and stakeholders value corporate environmental information disclosure in today's context of increasing concern about environmental issues [50]. Therefore, the results are consistent with theory and similar findings from other studies [31,51]. Overall, the analysis of the pillars suggests that the environment has the most significant impact, with a higher level of significance compared to the other pillars. In contrast, the social pillar (S) is considered the least important, as shown in Model 2 with the smallest t-stat value. The estimated coefficient shows that when the company discloses 1 % more information related to society, the stock price will increase by 0.14 thousand VND at the 10 % sense plains. This positive relationship is consistent with the findings of previous studies [31,52]. Model 3 demonstrates the impact of corporate governance efficiency (G). The results indicate that the estimated coefficient for governance is positive and statistically significant at the 10 % level. This finding is supported by Ref. [43], who found that companies with strong governance systems experience market development, specifically in terms of their stock market price. However, this positive association did not have a significant impact (prob<0.1). This may be due to the already well-established and strict corporate governance disclosure framework. As a result, when additional information related to governance is disclosed, shareholders and stakeholders may not place as much value on it and may not react as strongly.

After analyzing each aspect individually, the results show a positive correlation between ESG information disclosure and stock prices (Model 4). To be special, there is a statistically significant increase of 0.368 thousand VND in stock market price for every 1 % increase in information disclosure, with a confidence level of 99 %. This suggests that companies that actively practice and disclose ESG information can enhance their market value, as shareholders place a high value on this information. The signaling hypothesis, which contends that the market interprets ESG data as a positive signal and encourages more shareholder investment, also lends credence to this conclusion [20]. Additionally, stakeholder theory also supports this result [18].

Finally, the findings show that both overall ESG disclosure (ESG) and the interaction between ESG information disclosure and sensitive industries (ESGxES) are statistically significant at the 1 % sense level. This implies that businesses who operate in ecologically sensitive sectors and those that do not differ significantly. Even if the interaction variable's estimated coefficient is negative, overall ESG still has a very good effect. This suggests that the valuation of ESG information in the stock prices of environmentally sensitive companies is reduced (0.348–0.278). The findings in Model 5 contradict the findings of the majority of previous studies [31,42,44]. This is explained according to Ref. [53] because the cost of resources used in ESG activities in environmentally sensitive industries is very large. According to the same viewpoint [54], also reasoned that businesses that have an impact on the environment are probably going to come under more pressure from stakeholders, which would result in more expenses. These could be large investments in clean technology, waste treatment or R&D in measures to minimize environmental impact. Therefore, in the initial period, investors' expectations of stock profits decreased due to increased costs, leading to a decline in the motivation to increase stock prices. This is also a typical finding for a developing market context like Vietnam. As [55] demonstrated, the impact of ESG performance in environmentally sensitive businesses in emerging countries is often weaker than in developed countries. Talking about the impact of control variables in the model, the results in Table 6 illustrate that company size (SIZE) has a negative impact detected in most models. The control variable business age is always positive at the 1 % sense plains in all models. However, in contrast to size and age, the leverage ratio (LEV) was not found to be statistically significant in most models.

Conclusion

This research seeks to offer an appropriate perspective on the value of Environmental - Social - Governance (ESG) information through using a version of the [40] valuation model; developed by Ref. [41], research was conducted experimentally at 30 listed enterprises in Vietnam during the period 2019–2022. The study employed the GLS estimation method, after optimizing the data file through the use of Pooled OLS, REM, and FEM methods. Additionally, a model was developed to evaluate the effect of non-financial information on stock prices in the market, drawing on three legitimacy theories, stakeholder theory, and signaling theory.

The study looks at how Vietnamese stock market values are affected by financial and non-financial information, including ESG. To assess each pillar's impact on stock valuation, the writers thoroughly examine the environmental, social, and corporate governance aspects separately. The findings show a positive correlation between stock prices and ESG disclosure, with investors accepting more comprehensive ESG data. Furthermore, there is a favorable association between stock prices and the information on each individual pillar, especially when it comes to environmental information. This implies that market participants' appraisal and investment decisions heavily rely on non-financial information. However, the advantages of ESG data are lessened for companies in environmentally conscious industries. The empirical findings have also confirmed the suitability of legitimacy theory, stakeholder theory and signaling theory in a backdrop of the Vietnamese stock market.

The research's findings provide several implications. First, the positive effect of ESG information on stock prices has created a driving force for companies to actively incorporate ESG practices in an effort to enhance their market value, as investor interest has increased. Secondly, based on those results, it also provides investors the significance of taking this information into consideration when making decisions. This is especially relevant in the current market, where businesses that have implemented ESG practices have shown potential for price growth. This further emphasizes the value of ESG information for both companies and investors. Lastly, it is essential to take note of the distinctions in sensitive enterprises in Vietnam. In order to promote economic development while also protecting the environment, the government should introduce policy frameworks that mandate the practice and disclosure of transparent ESG information in this company. Additionally, offering green financial support packages specifically targeting highly "sustainable" firms can also be beneficial in promoting ESG practices in the market.

Overall, the research has made significant contributions both theoretically and practically, but there are still gaps for future development. Therefore, the potential for future research to develop is very wide open through expanding the sample size or using more standardized data collection methods. Besides, consideration of potential endogeneity issues also needs to be paid attention to and implemented in related studies in the future.

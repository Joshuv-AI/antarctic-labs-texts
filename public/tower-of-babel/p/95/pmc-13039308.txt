Alkali metal-induced modifications in the electronic and NLO properties of triselenasumanene complexes through DFT study
Authors: Vahdat Rafee, Saeedeh Kamalinahad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In this study, the optimized structure, electronic properties and nonlinear optical response (NLO) of TSSUM nanostructure and its complexes with alkali metals (M = Li, Na, and K) in the gas phase and solvent medium were investigated using density functional theory. The interaction energy of the complexes in the gas phase was calculated between − 10.20 to -10.75 kcal/mol, and in solvent medium as Li: -15.68 to − 24.30 kcal/mol, indicating higher stability of the complexes in the presence of solvent. QTAIM and NBO analysis also showed that the highest electron density and charge transfer to the metal occur in Li/TSSUM and the effective force in this system is higher than Na and K. UV–Vis spectral analysis and TD-DFT calculations showed that the main absorption of TSSUM in the gas phase is around 320 nm. The addition of alkali metals caused the absorption to shift to longer wavelengths (between 614 and 704 nm); indicating a decrease in excitation energy and an increase in charge transfer capability. Hyperpolarizability (β₀) values increased notably, with higher values in the solvent than in the gas phase, showing the medium’s effect on stability and charge distribution. For Li/TSSUM, Na/TSSUM, and K/TSSUM, β₀ ranged from 2242 to 4260 a.u in gas and 8246–12,032 a.u in solvent. The decrease in the energy gap Eg and the increase in β₀ indicate that the M/TSSUM complexes have significantly higher nonlinear optical response than pristineTSSUM and can be considered as suitable candidates for optoelectronic and photonic applications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-42303-7.

Introduction

Nonlinear optical (NLO) materials have attracted much attention from the scientific community due to their important applications in optoelectronics. The design and synthesis of this type of functional materials are continuously reported in scientific articles and references1–8. Molecules with high values ​​of first-order hyperpolarizability (ß₀) are considered a prerequisite for the identification of NLO materials. This category includes crystals lacking a center of symmetry9, donor-π-acceptor (D-π-A) molecules10,11, organometallic compounds12,13, octupolar molecules14,15, intramolecular charge transfer (ICT)16,17 and metal-doped structures5,18. Recently, theoretical calculations have shown that structures with scattered excess electrons exhibit a prominent NLO response19–21. Alkali metal atoms are known as potential sources of excess electrons due to their electropositive nature (tendency to lose electrons). On the other hand, organic materials that are active in nonlinear optics have higher electron permeability, higher molecular polarizability, and faster response times than inorganic materials. These features increase the efficiency and sensitivity of their nonlinear optical responses. The flexible molecular structure of organic materials also allows for more precise optimization of optical properties22.

The organic framework of triselenasumanene (TSSUM), which has attracted attention in the field of optical materials due to its unique structure and electronic properties, is a derivative of the classical sumanene structure by introducing hetroatom on sumanene and has a conjugated π system with a core containing selenium atoms, and has D3h symmetry. This structure provides special capabilities in charge transport, structural stability and chemical modification and, therefore, is known as a suitable basis for the fabrication of functional optoelectronic materials. This structure also has an asymmetric electron distribution and strong intramolecular charge transfer (ICT), which are key factors in enhancing nonlinear optical responses (NLO)23–25. Among the important advantages of TSSUM, we can mention high thermal stability, the possibility of changing energy levels, symmetrical charge distribution and flexibility in chemical synthesis by adding functional groups at the periphery. Also, the presence of selenium atoms increases the polarizability, improves the electric charge movement and strengthens the intermolecular bonds, which are very useful for applications in electron donor and electron transfer materials. All these properties have made TSSUM an ideal nanostructure for the design of multifunctional molecules in the new generation of optically active materials.

Previous key studies on sumanene-based NLO systems have emphasized the C₂₁H₁₂ bowl-shaped core with C₃v symmetry, which provides high hyperpolarizability through π-conjugation and intramolecular charge transfer, such as TTF-annulated with calxspirols, which offer switchable redox-active conformations to enhance nonlinear optics responses26,27. Sumanene derivatives, like triazole-linked receptors, act as allosteric effectors for metal cations (such as Cs⁺), making fluorescence quenching and tunable photophysical properties ideal for NLO sensors and amplifiers28. Nitrogen-containing sumanene bowls as electron donors in D-A architectures reduce the HOMO-LUMO gap and exhibit superior electro-optical performance, while metal-doped variants (such as B₃₆ clusters) break the symmetry and enhance the NLO via charge transfer, outperforming traditional organic materials. Additional electron strategies also complement scattered Rydberg electrons by integrating sumanene and alkaloids for hyperpolarizabilities29,30.

The optoelectronic properties and charge transport ability of triselenasumanene-based organic molecules were investigated using electronic structure simulations to evaluate their performance as hole transport materials (HTM) in perovskite solar cells (PSC) and as electron donors in organic solar cells (OSC). The results indicate that the newly designed TSSUM-based nanostructure molecules are highly suitable as hole transport materials with improved efficiency for application in PSCs25.

Based on the above data, in this study, the triselenasumanene molecule (TSSUM) has been used as a new option for use in the optical and light industries. In order to improve the nonlinear optical properties of this molecule, the solution of its association with alkali metals has been used. The main goal of this study is to investigate the effect of the interaction of alkali metal atoms with the TSSUM nanostructure on its electro-optical properties. Our results show that the first hyperpolarizability of the TSSUM nanostructure decorated with lithium, sodium and potassium atoms has increased significantly compared to the pure TSSUM nanostructure. Considering the unique physical and chemical properties of the TSSUM nanostructure, the findings of this research can pave the way for the design and fabrication of nanostructures with tunable electro-optical properties.

Computational details

In this study, the B3LYP functional31, which is widely used in the study of nanoscale molecular systems32,33, is used in combination with the new modified DFT-D3(BJ) method presented by Grimm et al.34. Since in the previous work35, in order to investigate the electronic and optical properties of the TSSUM nanostructure in more detail, geometric optimization calculations and time-independent functional theory (TD-DFT) calculations were performed for the pristine TSSUM nanostructure. These calculations were performed using various functionals such as APFD36, B3LYP31, CAM-B3LYP37, LSDA38, MPW1PW9139, WB97XD40 along with the 6-31G(d, p) basis set, in the gas phase, and the results are summarized in Table (1). Since the interactions between alkali metals and nanostructure are investigated in this study, dispersion effects are taken into account by applying Grimm corrections (DFT-D3(BJ) correction) to improve the accuracy of interaction energies. In addition, different basis sets were also evaluated; in one of the calculations, the 6-311G(d, p) basis set41 was used for carbon and hydrogen atoms and the LANL2DZ42 was used for selenium atoms using the keyword GenECP. The results presented in the Table 1 show that, considering previous reports that the absorption peak of this structure is located around 312 nm23, and also considering the closeness of the computational results and the reasonable computational cost, the B3LYP-D3(BJ) with the standard 6-311G(d, p) basis set was chosen as the best computational method to continue this study.

To simulate the effect of solvent on molecular properties, the polarizable continuum model (PCM) model43,44 was used, considering chloroform (ɛ=4.81) as the solvent. This model allows for an accurate representation of solvent interactions with the optical and electronic properties of molecules. The correlation values ​​for the spin multiplicity 2 in the M/TSSUM nanostructures (where M is Li, Na, and K) were obtained to be about 0.750 and 0.764, respectively. These values ​​are in remarkable agreement with the value of 0.750 for the pure doublet states. It can therefore be concluded that the amount of spin contamination is negligible and the computational data is of good reliability and accuracy. In addition, the harmonic vibrational frequencies were also calculated at the same theoretical level to ensure that all frequencies are real and to confirm the stability of the optimized geometry.

All quantum calculations were performed using the Gaussian 09 software package46 with standard convergence criteria.

Results and discussions

Optimized structure and NLO response of TSSUM nanostructure in the gas phase and solvent medium

The TSSUM nanostructure was optimized in both gas phase and solvent medium using the B3LYP-D3(BJ)/6-311G(d, p) method. The optimized TSSUM nanostructure is presented in Fig. 1a, S1. In Fig. 1a is shown that the optimized molecule structure is completely planar with D3h symmetry, and also, key values ​​of geometric parameters are indicated. The obtained findings are in significant agreement with the data and results reported in previous studies23. The electronic and optical properties of this molecule have also been investigated and analyzed in detail. The values ​​obtained for the energies of the frontier molecular orbitals (εH and εL) as well as the energy gap (Eg) calculated for the studied nanostructure in the gas phase and solvent medium are presented in Table 2. The energy gap of this molecule is 4.22 and 4.24 eV in the gas phase and solvent medium, respectively, which is very close to the values ​​reported previously59. The visual representation of the distribution of HOMO and LUMO molecular orbitals and the total density of states (TDOS) diagram and of the TSSUM nanostructure in the gas phase are shown in Fig. 1b, and 1c. In addition, the calculated values ​​of polarizability (α) and total hyperpolarizability (β₀) of TSSUM in both phases are also reported in Table 2. The findings indicate that due to the low value of the first hyperpolarizability, this molecule does not have a significant optical nonlinear response.

Geometrical characteristic and interaction properties of M/TSSUM (M = Li, Na and K) in the gas phase and solvent medium

In this study, alkali metal atoms are investigated as adsorbed species on the exterier surface of the TSSUM nanostructure in the gas phase and solvent medium. To investigate the adsorption process, several different initial positions are considered, including the top of different carbon atoms, selenium atom, and C–C and C-Se bonds on the concave and convex sides of the TSSUM nanostructure. The aim of this section is to determine the most stable energy configuration among all the M/TSSUM (M = Li, Na and K) nanostructures shown in Fig. 2 and S2.

After performing a complete optimization of the systems using the B3LYP-D3(BJ)/6-311G(d, p) method, a stable structure with real vibrational frequencies was obtained for each of the M/TSSUM complexes (M = Li, Na, K). After interaction with metals, the TSSUM nanostructure changes symmetry and becomes C3V, and the alkali metal atom is located in the concave position of the TSSUM nanostructure and interacts with it, which can be seen in Fig. 2 and S2.

Key geometric parameters are presented in Fig. 2 and S2. In these complexes, the interaction distances are in the range of 3.33 to 4.25 Å in the gas phase and about 3.38 to 4.25 Å in solvent medium. It is observed that with the increase in the size of the alkali atom, the vertical distance between the metal atom and the TSSUM nanostructure also increases in both mediums, so that its order is as follows: Li/TSSUM < Na/TSSUM < K/TSSUM. Therefore, it is expected that the lithium atom in the concave position can establish a stronger interaction with the TSSUM nanostructure. On the other hand, according to the figures and results, it is clear that the interaction distance between the metal and the nanostructure in the solvent medium is greater than in the gaseous phase. It can be said that in the solvent environment, the dielectric screening effect weakens the short-range electrostatic interaction component between the alkali metal and the nanostructure, and as a result, the potential energy minimum is transferred to larger distances compared to the gaseous phase. It should also be noted that the structure of the TSSUM nanostructure has undergone local changes under the influence of the interaction with alkali metals. The results show that the length of the C–C bonds in all complexes has increased compared to the initial state of the molecule. These structural changes can significantly affect the electro-optical properties of the investigated nanostructures. The stability of the M/TSSUM complexes was evaluated by analyzing the interaction energies (Eint) and the results are presented in Table 3. The interaction energy values ​​for the Li, Na and K/TSSUM complexes were calculated to be -10.75, -10.20 and − 10.72 kcal/mol in the gas phase, -24.30, -15.68, and − 19.41 kcal/mol in solvent medium, respectively. These data indicate that the Li/TSSUM complex with Eint of -10.75 kcal/mol in the gas phase and − 24.30 kcal/mol in solvent medium is the most stable structure and the lithium atom in the concave position of the TSSUM nanostructure has the strongest and most effective interaction. According to the results in Table 3, it is clear that the interaction energy in the solvent state is higher and the structures obtained in the solvent phase are more stable than in the gas phase. It can be stated that, in the solvent environment, the metal-nanostructure interaction energy increases (becomes more negative) due to the effective stabilization of the complex by the solvent, so that despite the increase in the equilibrium distance, the.

contribution of the energy due to solvation overcomes the direct decrease in the interaction and the overall interaction becomes more stable.

The thermodynamic potential of the interaction of different metals with the TSSUM surface was comprehensively and systematically investigated. For this purpose, the changes in the energies ΔHint and ΔGint in different interaction complexes were calculated, using the vibrational frequency calculations given in Eqs. (13) and (14) and detailed in Table 3. The results show that the ΔHint values ​​for the adsorption of Li, Na and K atoms are − 11.45, -10.84 and − 11.13 kcal/mol in the gas phase, and − 27.43, -19.46, and − 22.55 kcal/mol in solvent medium, respectively, and the ΔGint values ​​are − 5.48, -4.66 and − 5.01 kcal/mol in the gas phase and − 19.24, -11.67, and − 16.51 kcal/mol in solvent medium, respectively. These negative values ​​confirm that the interactions between these metals and the TSSUM surface are strong and stable. It is observed that the stability of the obtained complexes is greater in the solvent medium than in the gas phase.

The total density of state (TDOS) and frontier molecular orbitals (FMO)

The electronic properties of the investigated complexes have also been carefully analyzed in the gas phase and solvent medium; the values ​​of the frontier molecular orbital energies (ɛH, ɛL) and energy gaps (Eg) of these complexes are presented in Table 4. The results indicate that the interaction of alkali metals with the TSSUM nanostructure leads to a significant reduction in the energy gaps in both mediums. Table 4 shows that the Eg value decreases from 4.22 to 4.24 eV in the gas phase and solvent medium, respectively, for the original TSSUM nanostructure to 1.16, 1.23 and 0.80 eV in the gas phase and 1.28, 1.27, and 1.28 eV in solvent medium, for the Li, Na and K/TSSUM complexes. In order to provide a comprehensive and efficient analysis of the electronic structures of these complexess, the total density of states (TDOS) and partial density of states (PDOS) diagrams for these systems are shown in Fig. 2b, d, f, and S3. According to these diagrams, it is clear that the interaction of alkali metal atoms with these complexes leads to the creation of new energy levels in the higher energy region as HOMO and new intermolecular LUMO levels, which are actually located between the HOMO and the main LUMO of the TSSUM nanostructure. These new levels effectively create higher and lower energy levels, which ultimately leads to the reduction of the Eg value in these interacted compounds. The PDOS results show that the interaction between alkali metal and TSSUM in M/TSSUM complexes is clearly confirmed. Furthermore, these results indicate that alkali metals play a key role in the creation of the new LUMO level (Fig. 2 and S3), such that the energy gap (Eg) of the complexes is reduced by 72.47%, 70.77%, and 81.14%, in the gas phase, and − 63.81%, -70.05%, and − 69.81% in solvent medium, respectively, leading to significant changes in the electrical conductivity properties of the system. The significant reduction in the energy gap can be attributed to the overlap of the alkali metal valence orbitals with the TSSUM acceptor orbitals, which leads to the formation of new hybrid states in the energy gap region. These intermediate electronic states facilitate the charge transfer pathway and are responsible for the significant changes in the electronic properties of the complexes.

NBO analysis

Alkali metals such as Li, Na and K can attract negative charges upon interactions with various surfaces, especially in systems such as M/TSSUM complexes in the gas phase. In these complexes, electron transfer from the TSSUM surface to the alkali metal atoms causes the metal atoms to become relatively polarized. This process results in the transfer of additional electrons from the TSSUM to the alkali metal, which can change the electronic and even nonlinear optical (NLO) properties of the systems under study. In the gas phase, the main determinant of the direction of charge transfer is the relative position of the energy levels of the frontier orbitals and the chemical potential difference between the interacting components.

In the gas phase, the TSSUM nanostructure has a higher energy HOMO than the alkali metals due to its extended π-electron nature and the presence of highly polarizable selenium atoms. In contrast, the alkali metals have low-energy empty orbitals and a high tendency to accept electron density. Consequently, the effective overlap of the HOMO of the nanostructure with the acceptor orbitals of the alkali metal leads to electron transfer from the TSSUM to the metal, so that the total energy of the system is minimized. Since there is no dielectric stabilization for ionic states in the gas phase, this charge transfer occurs mainly as a partial and localized transfer and is associated with shorter distances and more direct metal-nanostructure interactions. These changes are particularly significant in systems where both electronic and optical properties are important.

In M/TSSUM complexes in the solvent medium, strong dielectric stabilization increases the local charge separation between the interacting components. The parameters provided include the electron density ρ(rc), the Laplace of the electron density ∇²ρ(rc), the kinetic energy density G(rc), the potential energy V(rc), the total energy H(rc) = V(rc) + G(rc), and the ratio |V(rc)|/G(rc). It is worth noting that the Laplace of the electron density is an important tool for measuring the local distribution and concentration of the electron density ρ(r). The sign ∇²ρ(r) indicates that at a given point, the electron density is locally concentrated (∇²ρ(r) < 0) or reduced (∇²ρ(r) > 0). Also, the Laplace relation to the local components of the total energy is established through the local expression of virial’s theorem: (1/4)∇²ρ(r) = 2G(r) + V(r). The sign of the Laplace determines which of the energy components—kinetic or potential—is dominant over the virial average (2:1). When ∇²ρ(r) is negative, the electron density at the point in question is highly concentrated and the potential energy dominates the local total energy H(rc) and the virial ratio. Conversely, when ∇²ρ(r) is positive, the electron density is locally reduced and the kinetic energy dominates. According to Bader and Essen60, the former is associated with shared electron interactions and the latter with closed-shell interactions.

The QTAIM topological analysis shows that the electron density at the critical points (ρ(rc)) for Li•••C interactions is about 0.0038 a.u., which is higher than the values ​​for Na•••C (0.0031 a.u.) and K•••C (0.0017 a.u.). This difference indicates a greater concentration of electrons around Li atom and, consequently, its stronger interaction with the nanostructured carbon atoms. The positive values ​​of the Laplace density (∇²ρ(rc)) indicate that the interactions are mainly of the closed-shell type and a relative decrease in electron density is observed at the critical points. Interestingly, for sodium the value of ∇²ρ(rc) is slightly higher (0.0048 a.u.), indicating a slightly higher local concentration than for lithium and potassium.

Examination of the local energies also provides valuable information. For Li•••C, the kinetic energy G(rc) is 0.0011 and the potential energy V(rc) is -0.0013, so the total energy H(rc) = -0.0002, slightly negative, indicating a relatively stable interaction with a tendency towards electron concentration. The ratio |V(rc)|/G(rc) for Li is 1.18, indicating that the potential energy dominates over the kinetic energy and that the interaction has a weak shared character. This ratio is 0.92 and 0.86 for Na and K, respectively, indicating a decreasing potential energy contribution and a fully shelled nature of these interactions.

In summary, it can be concluded that the strength of the interaction and electron concentration increase with decreasing atomic radius of the metal. Lithium, with the highest density and the highest |V|/G ratio, exhibits a stronger and slightly shared interaction, Na is at an intermediate level, and K shows the lowest density and a ratio less than 1, indicating a weak and closed-shell interaction. This trend indicates that the size and effective charge of the metal play a decisive role in the intensity and nature of the interaction with the TSSUM nanostructure, and is in complete agreement with the results of the QTAIM analysis and local energies.

Noncovalent interactions (NCI) analysis provides a detailed view of the type and intensity of intermolecular and intramolecular forces in a system57. The NCI diagram is an effective tool for distinguishing weak intermolecular forces from strong, localized intramolecular bonds. The analysis uses the RDG index, which allows the identification of weak van der Waals forces, repulsive forces, and useful attractive forces with specific color schemes (green, red, and blue).

Significantly negative values ​​of ρ(λ2) indicate strong attractive forces, while values ​​close to zero indicate the presence of van der Waals forces. The red areas in the diagram indicate regions of noncovalent repulsive overlap with positive ρ(λ2), while the blue and green areas indicate strong attractive forces and weak interactions, respectively. The sudden increase in RDG is associated with regions of low electron density and RDG values ​​from zero to medium, which are characteristic of weak interactions in the complexes.

As shown in Fig. 5, several distinct peaks are observed in the scatter plots of the studied complexes. The higher density of scattering points in each section indicates a higher electron density and a more effective role in the overall interactions. In this figure, we observe that the M/TSSUM complexes have significant strong attractive and van der Waals forces, such that for strong attractive interactions the values ​​of ρ(λ2) range from − 0.05 to − 0.02 and for van der Waals forces from − 0.02 to + 0.01 a.u.

In the solvent environment, in addition to this effect, the stabilization of the charge and scattered electrons by the solvent also plays a decisive role. By screening electrostatic interactions and increasing charge separation, the solvent environment increases the mobility of excess electrons and, as a result, a stronger enhancement of the optical nonlinear response. The different trends of hyperpolarizability and interaction energy in the gas phase and solvent medium can be explained by the role of the environment and the nature of the excess electron. In solution, the solvent stabilizes separated charges. It also weakens Coulomb interactions. As a result, the valence electron of the alkali metal becomes more delocalized over the nanostructure. This enhanced delocalization strengthens charge transfer. Consequently, stronger interactions lead to a larger redistribution of electron density and a higher nonlinear optical response. For this reason, the hyperpolarizability trend in solution follows the interaction energy.

In the gas phase, the situation is different. Here, hyperpolarizability depends less on interaction strength. It is mainly controlled by the mobility of the excess electron. Sodium, despite its weaker interaction energy, provides an electron that is more mobile and responsive. This behavior is related to its suitable ionization energy. It also arises from the broader delocalization of its s electron. As a result, sodium shows the highest hyperpolarizability without forming the strongest interaction with the nanostructure. Finally, the presence of an additional scattered electron causes the M/TSSUM (M = Li, Na, K) complexes to exhibit a significantly nonlinear optical response compared to the original TSSUM.

TD-DFT study

Charge transfer in the critical transition stage plays a crucial role in determining the NLO properties. To calculate the critical excited states, oscillator strengths (f0) and excitation energies (ΔE) of these systems, TD-DFT calculations were performed using the TD-B3LYP-D3(BJ)/6-311G(d, p) method in the gas phase and solvent medium. The obtained results are presented in Table 9.

TD-DFT calculations show that in the gas phase, the placement of alkali metals on the TSSUM nanostructure causes a significant decrease in the excitation energy and a shift of the absorption band towards longer wavelengths compared to the pristine structure. Based on the values ​​obtained, the excitation energy in the Li/TSSUM complex (1.80 eV) is lower than that of Na/TSSUM (2.02 eV), and this is accompanied by a significant increase in the absorption wavelength of Li atom compared to Na atom. Such behavior indicates that the interaction of Li with the TSSUM electronic framework in the gas phase can create lower-energy transitions, although the intensity of these transitions is not very high.

In this phase, the K/TSSUM complex shows the lowest excitation energy and the longest absorption wavelength among the investigated structures, indicating a softer electronic response and higher charge mobility due to the interaction of K with the nanostructure. The high value of the oscillator strength (f0= 0.3293) in this system also indicates that electronic transitions are more allowed and the metal orbitals overlap more appropriately with the TSSUM orbitals. In contrast, although the Li and Na/TSSUM complexes are in a favorable state in terms of excitation energy, the lower values ​​of the oscillator strength indicate that the intensity of transitions in these structures remains more limited.

In the solvent medium, the excitation energies in all systems increase and the absorption shifts towards shorter wavelengths, however, the difference between the metals is still discernible. Under these conditions, the Li/TSSUM complex exhibits the lowest excitation energy and a relatively longer wavelength than Na and K/TSSUM, which can be attributed to the enhanced interaction of Li with the TSSUM nanostructure in the presence of a solvent environment. Overall, the simultaneous changes in excitation energy, wavelength, and oscillator strength clearly indicate that the type of metal and the intensity of its interaction with the TSSUM nanostructure play a key role in determining the electron transport properties and hyperpolarizability behavior of these systems in the gas phase and solvent medium.

The UV–visible absorption spectra calculated for TSSUM nanostructures and M/TSSUM complexes in the gas phase and solvent medium, shown in Fig. 6, indicate the significant influence of alkali metal interaction on the behavior of the electronic transitions of the nanostructure. In the gas phase, the pristine TSSUM nanostructure exhibits a main absorption band at around 320 nm, which is mainly attributed to the HOMO→LUMO transition. With the addition of alkali metals, this band shifts noticeably to longer wavelengths and the absorption appears in the visible region. In particular, in the Li/TSSUM and Na/TSSUM complexes, the main absorptions are observed at 688 and 614 nm, respectively, which are due to transitions involving orbitals higher than the LUMO. The largest red shift is observed for the K/TSSUM complex at a wavelength of about 704 nm, indicating a decrease in the energy gap and an enhancement of the charge transfer between K and the TSSUM electronic framework.

In the solvent medium, the spectral behavior of the complexes is somewhat different. In this phase, the absorption of the pristine TSSUM nanostructure is shifted to slightly larger wavelengths and appears at about 322 nm, indicating a decrease in the excitation energy in the presence of the solvent. However, in the M/TSSUM complexes, absorption bands are still observed in the near ultraviolet region and the wavelengths are in a relatively close range (about 324 to 329 nm). The nature of these transitions mainly involves transitions from lower orbitals such as HOMO − 1 or HOMO − 3 to higher empty orbitals, indicating that the solvent medium causes a change in the distribution of energy levels and a decrease in the role of very low-energy transitions. Overall, comparison of UV–Vis spectra in the gas and solvent phases shows that the interaction of alkali metals with the TSSUM nanostructure is the main factor causing the shift towards the visible region, while the presence of solvent leads to a more compact transitions and their confinement to the ultraviolet region.

Conclusion

In this study, the optimized structure, electronic properties and nonlinear optical response (NLO) of TSSUM nanostructure and its complexes with alkali metals Li, Na and K in the gas phase and solvent medium were comprehensively investigated. The optimization results showed that the metals were located in the concave position of TSSUM and formed significant interactions with it; the M/TSSUM interaction distance was Li/TSSUM < Na/TSSUM < K/TSSUM, respectively, and the Li/TSSUM complex with an interaction energy of − 10.75 kcal/mol in the gas phase and − 24.30 kcal/mol in solvent medium showed the most stability. UV–Vis spectral analysis and TD-DFT calculations showed that the main absorption of TSSUM is located at 320 nm, but the addition of metals caused the absorption to shift to longer wavelengths (Li/TSSUM: 688, Na/TSSUM: 614 and K/TSSUM: 704 nm), indicating a decrease in the energy gap and enhancement of charge transfer. Also, the hyperpolarizability values (​​β₀) increased dramatically; in the gas phase, β₀ = 2242, Na/TSSUM = 4260 and K/TSSUM = 3509 a.u were measured for Li/TSSUM and in the solvent medium, 12,032, 8246 and 11,450 a.u, respectively. NBO and QTAIM analyses showed that the highest electron density and charge transfer towards the metal occur in Li/TSSUM and its interaction strength is higher than that of Na and K. The significant reduction in the energy gap and the increase in β₀ indicate that the M/TSSUM complexes have a higher nonlinear optical response than pristine TSSUM, and these properties make them suitable candidates for optoelectronic and photonic applications.

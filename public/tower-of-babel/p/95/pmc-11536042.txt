Miniaturized buried low-frequency acoustically actuated magnetoelectric antenna for soil moisture adaptive underground communication
Authors: Xi Chen, Xiaozhou Lü, Weiqiang Zhang, Chengming Xue, Xiangwei Zhu, Weimin Bao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe low-frequency (LF) magnetoelectric (ME) antenna is promising for underground communication, especially in the Underground Internet of Things (UIoT). However, soil moisture and its variations significantly impact the data transmission rate and efficiency. Therefore, it is imperative to thoroughly investigate the communication characteristics of the ME antenna in soil environments with different humidity levels. This paper proposes a miniaturized LF ME antenna based on Terfenol-D/PZT/Terfenol-D layered structure. The proposed ME antenna can sense changes in the soil moisture without moving the wireless network sensor node while also enabling LF underground communication, which achieves soil moisture adaptive underground communication. It can operate continuously for an extended period under the set driving conditions (Pin = 152 mW). This research illuminates the potential of a miniaturized LF ME antenna for enabling adaptive underground wireless communication for soil moisture monitoring, which is expected to provide a new solution idea for the UIoT communication.Subject areas: Natural sciences, Physics, Applied sciences

Introduction

The Underground Internet of Things (UIoT) can effectively monitor and manage underground environmental parameters and data. The UIoT is widely used in various fields including traffic control, pipeline monitoring, environmental monitoring, and smart farm construction.1,2,3 For the development of smart farm, reliable underground communication technology enables stable and accurate data transmission, leading to intelligent irrigation systems. Communication technology is divided into wired and wireless methods. The wired communication methods are widely used. However, they require long cables, limited sensing areas, and are easily damaged. Wireless communication technology mainly utilizes ultrasound, magnetic induction (MI), and electromagnetic waves (EM). Compared with ultrasound and MI communication, EM communication technology is dependable and mature, having clear advantages in underground communication studies.4,5,6,7 Unlike conventional antennas, the antennas used by the UIoT are buried underground. Soil composition, humidity variations, and burial depth affect the absorption, reflection, and refraction of EM waves, which significantly impacts the efficiency of the wireless communication system.2,8,9,10 Low-frequency (LF) EM waves are better suited for underground wireless communication systems than high-frequency (HF) signals due to their smaller propagation loss and stronger signal penetration.11,12 Therefore, it is crucial to research miniaturized LF antennas that are suitable for underground communication to advance the development of the UIoT.

Mechanical antennas that generate electromagnetic field radiation through the mechanical movement of charges or magnetic dipoles offer a promising solution for miniaturizing LF antennas.13,14 These antennas can be categorized into four types: electret,15 permanent magnet,16,17,18 piezoelectric resonance,19,20,21 and magnetoelectric (ME) resonance.22,23,24,25,26,27,28,29,30 Zhang et al. proposed a rotating permanent magnet antenna array and achieved directional communication at a distance of 5 m across concrete walls.31 However, the rotational speed of the motor constrains the effectiveness and efficiency of radiation. Compared with the piezoelectric LF transmitting antenna that uses the direct and inverse piezoelectric effects of piezoelectric materials to generate radiation, the ME antenna combines the piezoelectric and the magnetostrictive effects to simultaneously transmit and receive EM waves.32,33,34,35 Xiao et al. proposed an LF dual-driven ME antenna for underground communication, demonstrating the strong underground penetration ability of LF magnetic fields.26

In the design and deployment of underground wireless communication systems for intelligent irrigation systems, it is essential to consider the humidity of the underground environment to guarantee the reliability of the wireless communication link. Traditional soil moisture communication systems comprise a wireless communication network integrated with soil moisture sensors, enabling real-time data monitoring and communication.2,4,8,36 Kim et al. proposed a wireless underground sensor system utilizing a magnetic coil antenna and a surface acoustic wave humidity sensor, which used magnetic induction to monitor soil humidity changes.2,4 This underground sensor system includes surface acoustic wave (SAW) sensors and two magnetic coil antennas, and the complexity of the communication link limits the communication study of the system. The study of antennas that enable soil moisture sensing is beneficial to optimize the soil moisture communication system. Salam et al. established a theoretical model of a dipole antenna and verified that the high dielectric constant of soil and moisture content significantly affects the performance of buried antennae.9 Wireless communication systems are essential for the transmission of encoded data in the UIoT.8 However, soil moisture and its variations can impact the transmission of EM wave signals and consequently the data transmission rate and efficiency of the ME antenna communication.9 Therefore, it is imperative to investigate the communication characteristics of ME antennas in soil with varying humidity levels in order to develop an effective soil moisture adaptive communication system. In addition, Chu et al. suggested that unlike the quasi-static application of ME composite structures in magnetic sensors, high electric field excitation leads to rapid heating of the ME device, which may degrade the radiation performance of the ME antenna and lead to deterioration of the communication.29 Considering the practical operational requirements of the prepared ME antenna for soil moisture adaptive underground communication, it is necessary to investigate the continuous working performance of the ME antenna.

In this work, an acoustically actuated Terfenol-D/PZT/Terfenol-D sandwich structure ME antenna is proposed, which can realize soil moisture adaptive underground wireless communication. The proposed antenna employs the ME coupling effect to transmit and receive EM waves at the resonant frequency. The proposed ME antenna can simultaneously realize humidity sensing and communication, which avoids the system complexity of the traditional soil moisture communication systems integrated by the humidity sensing module and communication module. The designed ME antenna can be buried underground to monitor and detect changes in soil moisture, and communication methods vary depending on the humidity environment. Multiple ME antennas can be buried in the ground as individual sensor nodes of an underground communication system to achieve communication links between nodes. In addition, the continuous working performance of the ME antenna is investigated. The results of this work are of great practical importance in the field of UIoT and the proposed ME antenna shows great potential for deployment in the development of smart agricultural systems.

Results and discussion

Antenna principle and characterization

Figure 1A shows the schematic diagram of the ME antenna structure and physical image. The ME antenna was constructed using a Terfenol-D/PZT/Terfenol-D laminate structure. The ME coupling effect of the laminated ME composite structure is employed to generate and radiate electromagnetic waves and realize the conversion of physical quantities of “electric-mechanical (force, magnetism)-magnetic”.22,27,29,32,33 The commercially available Terfenol-D with a size of 14 mm × 6 mm × 0.5mm and high magnetostrictive coefficient was selected as the magnetostrictive (M) layer. The PZT-5H with a size of 14 mm × 6 mm × 0.5mm, high piezoelectric coefficient, and low cost was selected as the piezoelectric (P) layer. In order to obtain better magnetoelectric coupling effect and meet the application requirements of underground LF communication, the dimensions of the materials were referred from the study by Jia Y et al.37 The 6 mm diameter and 2 mm thickness rubidium magnet was implemented to ensure a large ME coupling effect within the DC-biased magnetic field (HDC).25,38 The sandwich structure of M-P-M, which was free to vibrate lengthwise at one end and constrained by the magnet at the other end, was obtained by bonding and curing epoxy resin (Devcon 14250).

The signal generator produces a sinusoidal voltage signal and amplifies it through the OPA541 power amplifier module. The amplified signal (40Vpp sine signal at 94.5 kHz in all tests) excites the Tx antenna to generate an EM wave. The Coil Rx antenna receives the EM wave and the output signal is measured and recorded by a spectrum analyzer (N9020A, Agilent Inc). For near-field communications testing, the lock-in amplifier (SR850, Stanford Research Systems Inc.) is connected to the Rx antenna to demodulate the output signal at 94.5 kHz.

The ME Rx antenna receives a rectangular wave with periodic 0 and 1 digital information. To evaluate the maximum bit rate of the ME antenna LF communication system, the ASK modulation technique was used to perform periodic communication tests with bit rates from 20 to 800 bps. Figure 3D shows the correlation between the peak-to-peak voltage of the demodulated signal (ΔVdemod) and the ASK rate. Communication performance peaks at an ASK rate of 80 bps. As the ASK rate increases, the peak-to-peak voltage of the demodulated signal ΔVdemod gradually diminishes. The ASK rate has a significant impact on the amplitude of the demodulated signal due to the ME coupling effect of the acoustic drive, which is because the stabilization time of the received voltage gradually exceeds the bit rate.11,25 Moreover, the maximum bit rate is limited by the sampling frequency of the lock-in amplifier (512 Hz).

Moreover, the increase in the soil moisture content causes the spaces between soil particles to be filled with water that has a dielectric constant, leading to a decrease in the speed of EM waves and signal attenuation.8,39 The change in transmission medium has a significant effect on the performance of wireless communication systems. The binary ASK baseband signal with 0 and 1 information is loaded into a 40Vpp, 94.5 kHz carrier signal for the ME antenna near-field LF communication test in underground. Figure 4B shows the demodulation signals corresponding to different time constants at 10% moisture content soil and 60 bps modulation rate. The time constant responds to the low-pass filter bandwidth of the lock-in amplifier. It can be seen that with a long time constant (time constant is 3 ms), the demodulator cannot respond quickly to changes in the signal, causing distortion. Conversely, with a short time constant (time constant is 100 μs), the demodulated signal is noisy and unreliable. A stable and less noisy demodulated signal is obtained at 300 μs time constant. This means that there is a trade-off between data stability and response speed to select the appropriate time constant.

To ensure a reliable communication test, it is critical to select the correct demodulation parameters to improve the stability and accuracy of the demodulated signal. Table 1 shows the results of the near-field LF communication test (the input power is 152 mW) of the ME antenna at different ASK rates (2 bps∼800 bps) in soil with different moisture contents (10%–30%). The basis for selecting time constants at different ASK rates during the testing process is to obtain stable and low noise demodulated Rx signals. The ASK rates in the table are the modulation rates corresponding to the change of the time constant. It can be seen that the time constant needed to be set for demodulation becomes smaller with increasing ASK rate. At higher modulation signal rates, a smaller time constant allows a faster response to follow changes in the signal more quickly. The ME antenna communication ASK rate decreases with soil moisture content for the same time constant. This is due to increased humidity causing a weakening of the received signal.

By selecting an appropriate demodulation time constant in the 20 bps ASK rate modulation experiment, the demodulated signal of the communication system decreases with increasing humidity, as shown in Figure 4C. The results are consistent with the sensing characteristics in Figure 4A. Figure 4D shows the ΔVdemod at different ASK rates in the underground communication experiment with a soil moisture content of 10%. The 800 bps ASK demodulated signal exhibits characteristics of a triangle wave, deviating from a rectangular wave which distorts the encoded data. The selection of an appropriate modulation and demodulation technique may mitigate the problem of acoustic coupling relaxation effects in communication systems.14 The prepared ME antenna can be buried in the soil to monitor soil moisture and match the optimal communication mode under different humidity conditions. This can help to design an effective and intelligent communication system for the UIoT.

The proposed LF ME antenna has significant advantages in terms of size and application compared to the underground antenna in Table 2. The LF ME antenna has a significant size advantage over microstrip antennas, dipole antennas, and Coil magnetic induction antennas.4,9,40 Compared with mechanically rotating antennas based on electrets and permanent magnets, which generate magnetic field radiation through mechanical rotation,31 the speed of the motor limits the effectiveness and efficiency of the radiation. The magnetic flux density of the prepared ME antenna under the condition of higher frequency and the good conductor transmission medium is similar to that of the ME antenna in air which also uses a magnet-integrated DC magnetic bias.30 Unlike existing studies on underground antennas,26,30,31 we investigate the effect of humidity on the communication performance of the prepared ME antenna and can achieve both humidity sensing and communication applications. In addition, multiple ME antennas can be buried in the ground as individual sensor nodes of an underground communication system to realize underground wireless communication network. This work provides valuable insight for using ME antennas in the realm of underground LF wireless communications and smart UIoT applications.

Continuous working performance of the ME antenna

Due to the potential impact of heat dissipation and losses on real-time humidity detection,29 the continuous working performance of the antenna must be considered. Figure 5 shows the results of the continuous working performance test within 90 min of the ME antenna, including the radiation intensity, transmission, and communication performance at the optimal modulation rate of the ME Tx antenna, with a distance of 15 cm between the antenna and the receiver. It is evident that the radiation intensity, received transmission test signal, and 40 Hz ASK modulation demodulation signal of the ME antenna remain stable during continuous operation. The antenna can operate continuously with a 40 Vpp driving voltage. The receiving end shows less than 2 μV signal fluctuation, which has little effect on the humidity sensing of the ME antenna (with a humidity sensing sensitivity of 6.37). The chosen magnetostrictive material, Terfenol-D, has good temperature stability and the test showed that the surface temperature of the ME antenna device remained at around 25°C during continuous operation, which is a key factor in maintaining stable performance.41 The ME resonator heats up quickly under high field excitation due to its strong nonlinear dynamic characteristics, which can lead to communication deterioration during continuous operation.29,42 The prepared ME antenna can operate continuously for an extended period under the set driving conditions (Vpp = 40 V, Pin = 152 mW).

Limitation of the study

The prepared ME antenna has problems such as low radiation efficiency and communication relaxation, which need to be addressed. At the Rx end, more sensitive Rx antennas are used; at the Tx end, the radiation efficiency and the communication distance can be improved by using ME antenna arrays or by improving the material and device structure. The selection of an appropriate modulation and demodulation technique may mitigate the problem of acoustic coupling relaxation effects in communication systems. This communication channel will be different for every installation. If the application environment is changed, it needs to be recalibrated. The universality of the soil moisture adaptive communication system is challenging.

Method details

The ME antenna was constructed using a Terfenol-D/PZT/Terfenol-D laminate structure, with a rubidium magnet attached to one side of the device to provide a biased DC magnetic field. The laminated structure was bonded and cured with epoxy resin (Devcon 14250) to form an MPM sandwich structure. The dimensions of the Terfenol-D magnetostrictive layer were 14 mm × 6 mm × 0.5mm, and the piezoelectric layer was 12 mm × 6 mm × 0.5mm. The cylindrical N35 neodymium magnet had a diameter of 6 mm and a thickness of 2 mm. The Coil antenna parameters were as follows: 135 turns, 32 mm length, 38 mm diameter, 0.2 mm wire diameter. For Near-field LF Communication Test of the ME Antenna, Soil Moisture Adaptive Underground communication, and Continuous Working Performance of The ME Antenna, the lock-in amplifier (SR850, Stanford Research Systems Inc.) was used to analyze the demodulated signal of the RX antenna.

The quality factor mentioned in Figure 2A of the Antenna Principle and Characterization section is calculated as the ratio of the resonant frequency of the ME antenna to the 3dB bandwidth of the frequency response diagram of the ME antenna.25,28

The coil transfer function mentioned in Figure 2C of the Antenna Principle and Characterization section is calibrated using a standard 1 cm circular probe antenna.28 Specifically, the relationship between the magnetic field strength and the magnetically induced output voltage of the Coil antenna was calibrated using a standard 1 cm loop probe antenna, and the test result was a ratio of magnetic field strength (in nT) to magnetically induced output voltage (in mV) of 13.55.

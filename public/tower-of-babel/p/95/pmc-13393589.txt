Acute Encephalopathy in an Infant
Authors: Julia R Klein, Rebecca Goldstein, Nicholas J Sausen
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

A 7-week-old infant presented to the pediatric emergency department with one day of lethargy, shallow breathing, and apneic episodes lasting 8 seconds. Parents also noted back arching and intermittent left esotropia. She had not received vitamin K prophylaxis at birth and had no trauma history. Vitals showed intermittent sinus bradycardia to the 80s and blood pressure of 113/74 mmHg. Laboratory analyses revealed direct hyperbilirubinemia (1.73 mg/dL), hemoglobin of 9.2 g/dL, platelets of 752,000/mcL, and international normalized ratio (INR) of 2.12. Head computerized tomography (CT) demonstrated ventriculomegaly with multifocal intracranial hemorrhages (Figure 1, Figure 2, Figure 3, Figure 4). She received vitamin K and was admitted to the intensive care unit, where an external ventricular drain was placed, later converted to a ventriculoperitoneal shunt.

Diagnosis: Late-Onset Vitamin K Deficiency Bleeding

Late-onset vitamin K deficiency bleeding can occur from 1 week to as late as 6 months of age and carries up to 20% mortality.1 Elevated INR reflects a functional deficiency of vitamin K-dependent clotting factors. The patient exhibited Cushing triad (bradycardia, hypertension, respiratory irregularity), indicating increased intracranial pressure (ICP) from hemorrhage and obstructive hydrocephalus.2 Intermittent esotropia suggests sixth cranial nerve palsy, common with raised ICP, given the nerve’s long intracranial course.3 Acute onset esotropia should be presumed to represent abducens paresis from raised ICP until proven otherwise. Similarly, back arching, or opisthotonus, may indicate meningeal irritation or brainstem compression.4 Coagulopathy requires urgent correction before neurosurgical intervention. Parental refusal of vitamin K prophylaxis for neonates is rising, up 77% from 2017 to 2024, greatly elevating the risk for potentially fatal, preventable hemorrhage.5

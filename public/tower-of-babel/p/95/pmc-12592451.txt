FZD6 mediates the contributing role of miR-199a-5p in the adipogenesis of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells derived from aplastic anemia
Authors: Xianning Zhang, Huanhuan Zhang, Saisai Ren, Wenjuan Zhang, Qian Huang, Wenhui Huang, Lulu Liu, Mingkang Yao, Haihui Liu, Hao Zhang, Mingtai Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccumulating evidence indicates that enhanced adipogenic differentiation of bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BM-MSCs) derived from aplastic anemia (AA) could contribute to the excessive fat accumulation in the bone marrow. However, the underlying molecular mechanism remains to be determined. Here in this study, we further characterized the contributing role of miR-199a-5p in adipogenesis of AA BM-MSCs through specific inhibition of miR-199a-5p expression. Subsequently, we performed a systematic screening of the downstream targets of miR-199a-5p through web tool prediction, profiling data analysis, and experimental validation, with FZD6 and CELSR1 identified as the most promising targets of miR-199a-5p in the adipogenesis of AA BM-MSCs. Finally, we mainly focused on FZD6, which presents prominently decreased expression in adipogenic differentiation and AA BM-MSC samples and negatively correlates with miR-199a-5p expression. Besides, FZD6 is demonstrated as a negative regulator of adipogenesis of BM-MSCs, and overexpression of FZD6 alleviates the facilitating role of miR-199a-5p in the adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs. Overall, our findings provide further mechanistic insights into miR-199a-5p-mediated enhanced adipogenesis of AA BM-MSCs, implicating miR-199a-5p and FZD6 as potential intervention targets for improving BM-MSC-mediated microenvironments.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-22732-6.Keywords: Aplastic anemia, Bone marrow mesenchymal stem cell, Adipogenic differentiation, MiR-199a-5p, FZD6Subject terms: Anaemia, Mesenchymal stem cells

Introduction

Aplastic anemia (AA) is generally considered an immune-mediated bone marrow failure syndrome, characterized by hypoplasia, pancytopenia, and fatty bone marrow1,2. During the past decades, though great progress has been made about the clinical diagnosis and therapy of AA, immunosuppressive therapy (IST) and hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) remain the standard first-line treatment strategies3. In recent years, the combination of various TPO receptor agonists (TPO-RAs) and cyclosporine has been considered as a promising therapeutic option in relapse and refractory AA patients or those who are not suitable for HSCT4,5. However, there is still an urgent need to improve the therapeutic efficiency and long-term survival of AA patients by revealing the molecular mechanism of AA pathogenesis and developing novel therapeutic strategies.

Bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BM-MSCs) have self-renewal capacity and could differentiate into adipocytes and osteoblasts in the bone marrow, all of which constitute the essential cellular components of the hematopoietic microenvironment, responsible for stimulating and enhancing the proliferation of HSCs by secreting regulatory molecules and cytokines6,7. Increasing evidence indicates that BM-MSCs derived from aplastic anemia display a variety of characteristic alterations, including decreased proliferation capacity, impaired immunosuppressive effect, and imbalanced adipo-osteogenic differentiation, which mediate the bone marrow microenvironment changes and further contribute to the pathogenesis of AA8,9. The enhanced propensity of AA BM-MSCs towards adipogenic differentiation at the expense of osteogenesis is closely associated with the adipocyte accumulation in the bone marrow and dysfunctional hematopoietic support10,11. Cotransplantation of umbilical cord-derived MSCs with HSC for the treatment of AA facilitates the hematopoietic reconstruction in the bone marrow12,13, highlighting the importance of simultaneously improving the bone marrow microenvironment in AA therapy. However, whether targeting BM-MSC-mediated microenvironment changes could serve as a combinational therapeutic strategy still needs to be further investigated. There have been reports that levamisole presents a suppressive effect on adipogenesis of AA BM-MSCs and has demonstrated promising therapeutic outcomes in severe AA when combined with cyclosporine11,14. PPARγ is a master transcriptional factor in adipogenesis, whose inhibitor has been reported to attenuate mouse bone marrow failure15, further implicating that improving the BM-MSC-mediated hematopoietic microenvironment by inhibiting adipogenesis may represent a novel therapeutic direction for AA16. Thus, it is of great importance to develop more effective intervention targets for AA therapy by revealing the detailed molecular mechanism underlying the enhanced adipogenesis of AA BM-MSCs.

Here in this study, we further demonstrated the facilitating role of miR-199a-5p in adipogenic differentiation of AA BM-MSCs through sponge-mediated specific inhibition of miR-199a-5p expression on the basis of our previous work. FZD6 is identified as a downstream target of miR-199a-5p, which negatively correlates with miR-199a-5p expression in AA BM-MSC samples, serves as a negative regulator of adipogenesis, and mediates the role of miR-199a-5p in the enhanced adipogenic differentiation of AA BM-MSCs. These findings provide further mechanistic insights into miR-199a-5p-mediated enhanced adipogenesis of AA BM-MSCs and implicate miR-199a-5p and FZD6 as potential intervention targets for improving BM-MSC-mediated microenvironments.

Results

Inhibition of miR-199a-5p expression impairs the adipogenic differentiation of AA BM-MSCs

To further validate the role of miR-199a-5p in the adipogenesis of AA BM-MSCs, we make use of the recombined lentiviruses expressing a short RNA containing three consecutive binding sites of miR-199a-5p (miR-199a-5p sponge) or vector control to infect AA BM-MSCs and inhibit miR-199a-5p expression specifically, followed by adipogenic induction for 9 days. Then the expression of miR-199a-5p and adipogenic differentiation markers was detected by qRT-PCR, and the lipid droplet formation was evaluated by oil red O staining. Infection of miR-199a-5p sponge significantly inhibited the expression of miR-199a-5p (Fig. 1A), which markedly decreased the mRNA expression of adipogenic differentiation markers (C/EBPα, PPARγ, LPL, PLIN1, and FABP4) (Fig. 1B) and suppressed the lipid droplet formation (Fig. 1C) compared with the vector control (lenti-vector). The results indicated that specific inhibition of miR-199a-5p expression impaired the adipogenic differentiation of AA BM-MSCs, providing further experimental evidence for the important regulatory role of miR-199a-5p in the adipogenesis of BM-MSCs.

AA BM-MSCs were infected with lenti-vector or lenti-miR-199a-5p-sponge and induced towards adipogenic differentiation for 9 days. (A) qRT-PCR analysis of miR-199a-5p expression. U6 was used as an internal control. (B) Adipogenic differentiation markers (C/EBPα, PPARγ, PLIN1, LPL, and FABP4) were detected by qRT-PCR. GAPDH was used as an internal control. Systematic screening of downstream targets of miR-199a-5p in BM-MSCs

TGFBI was previously identified as a target of miR-199a-5p and mediates the role of miR-199a-5p in regulating adipogenic differentiation of AA BM-MSCs17. Considering that the binding site of miR-199a-5p is located in the coding region of TGFBI mRNA and miRNAs mainly function by binding to the 3’UTR of the target mRNAs, we systematically screened the downstream targets of miR-199a-5p through combined web tools prediction and profiling data analysis. We first predicted the potential targets of miR-199a-5p using TargetScan and PicTar web tools and analyzed the significantly down-regulated genes after miR-199a-5p overexpression using the profiling data (GSE97824) from the GEO database. Based on the above data, 34 candidate genes were screened out, which were predicted by both TargetScan and PicTar and also presented decreased expression upon miR-199a-5p overexpression (Fig. 2A, Table S1). Our previous data indicated that miR-199a-5p presents increased expression during adipogenic differentiation17. Next, we analyzed the expression of the 34 candidate genes in the adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs using the profiling data (GSE199466) we previously performed and focused on the ten genes (SULF1, PODXL, CELSR1, MYH9, CDH2, FZD6, IPO8, PXN, NLK, and UBAP1) that exhibited decreased expression not only after miR-199a-5p overexpression but also in adipogenic differentiation according to the profiling data (Fig. 2B). Subsequently, we performed experimental validation on the ten selected genes by use of qRT-PCR in both miR-199a-5p overexpression and adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs. As expected, the mRNA expression of SULF1, PODXL, CELSR1, MYH9, CDH2, FZD6, and IPO8 presented significant downregulation upon lentivirus-mediated miR-199a-5p overexpression in BM-MSCs, with PXN, NLK, and UBAP1 expression not in agreement with the expression profiling data (Fig. 2C). Except for PODXL, the other nine genes all presented significantly decreased expression in adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs (Fig. 2D). Collectively, the candidate genes, which presented prominent downregulation both in adipogenic differentiation and miR-199a-5p overexpression, were SULF1, CELSR1, MYH9, CDH2, FZD6, and IPO8.

FZD6 is validated as a bona fide target of miR-199a-5p

To further validate the downstream targets of miR-199a-5p, the four genes (FZD6, CELSR1, SULF1, and CDH2) presenting the most significant differential expression (P < 0.01) in both adipogenic differentiation and overexpression of miR-199a (Fig. 2C and D) were selected for subsequent luciferase reporter assay. The 3′UTR of FZD6, CELSR1, SULF1, and CDH2 containing miR-199a-5p binding sites were shown in Fig. 3A and cloned into the pmiR-Report vector (Fig. 3B). Dual luciferase reporter assay indicated that the 3′UTR of FZD6, CELSR1, and SULF1 mediated the significant down-regulation of firefly luciferase activity after miR-199a overexpression, while no obvious changes were observed about CDH2-3′UTR-mediated luciferase activity (Fig. 3C). We also introduced base mutation or deletion of the miR-199a-5p binding sites in the 3′UTR of FZD6, CELSR1, and SULF1, with the luciferase activity of FZD6 and CELSR1 significantly recovered in mutant and deletion groups compared with the wild-type group (Fig. S1), while no significant differences were observed about the luciferase activity of SULF1 between the mutant/deletion and the wild-type groups. Thus, FZD6 and CELSR1 may serve as the potential downstream targets of miR-199a-5p. Considering that the molecular weight of CELSR1 is more than 250 kDa and the accessibility of antibodies is limited, here in this work, we mainly selected FZD6 for subsequent validation of protein expression in control BM-MSCs after miR-199a-5p overexpression and inhibition. Consistent with the qRT-PCR result, lenti-miR-199a-mediated miR-199a-5p overexpression resulted in remarkable downregulation of FZD6 (Fig. 3D), while miR-199a-5p inhibition led to prominent upregulation of FZD6 (Fig. 3E). These results imply that FZD6 is identified as a bona fide target of miR-199a-5p.

FZD6 negatively regulates adipogenic differentiation of AA BM-MSCs

FZD6, also known as frizzled class receptor 6, is a member of the frizzled gene family and generally acts as the mediator of WNT canonical and non-canonical pathways18, whose role in adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs has never been reported. To investigate whether FZD6 is involved in the adipogenesis of BM-MSCs, we first detected the mRNA and protein expression of FZD6 by use of qRT-PCR and western blot. As shown in Fig. 4A, FZD6 presented decreased expression during adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs at both mRNA and protein levels, which is negatively correlated with miR-199a-5p expression as we previously reported in the adipogenic process and further implicates that it is a downstream target of miR-199a-5p. Then, we constructed an FZD6 overexpression plasmid and performed functional evaluation of FZD6 in AA BM-MSCs infected with lenti-FZD6 or lenti-ctrl. Overexpression of FZD6 was successfully achieved (Fig. 4B), which impaired the adipogenic capacity of AA BM-MSCs characterized by significantly decreased mRNA expression of adipogenic differentiation markers (C/EBPα, PPARγ, LPL, PLIN1, and FABP4) (Fig. 4C) and fewer lipid droplets (Fig. 4D). On the contrary, shRNA-mediated depletion of FZD6 in AA BM-MSCs (Fig. 4E) further facilitated the mRNA expression of adipogenic differentiation markers (C/EBPα, LPL, PLIN1, and FABP4) (Fig. 4F) and increased the lipid droplet formation (Fig. 4G). The results demonstrated that FZD6 mainly functions as a negative regulator of adipogenic differentiation of AA BM-MSCs.

FZD6 presents decreased expression in AA BM-MSC samples, and overexpression of FZD6 alleviates the facilitating role of miR-199a-5p in adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs

To demonstrate whether FZD6 mediates the role of miR-199a-5p in the enhanced adipogenic differentiation of AA BM-MSCs, we first detected the mRNA expression of FZD6 in BM-MSCs derived from AA patients and control samples. As shown in Fig. 5A, FZD6 presented significantly decreased expression in AA BM-MSC samples compared with the control samples, and a prominent negative correlation between miR-199a-5p and FZD6 expression in BM-MSC samples was also observed (Fig. 5B), which implicates that abnormal expression of the miR-199a-5p—FZD6 axis may contribute to the enhanced adipogenic capacity of AA BM-MSCs. To further verify this conclusion, we also performed a rescue assay using control BM-MSCs. miR-199a-5p overexpression reduced the mRNA and protein expression of FZD6 (Fig. 5C-D, b versus a) and boosted the adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs characterized by the increased expression of adipogenic differentiation markers and more lipid droplets (Fig. 5E and F, b versus a). Meanwhile, FZD6 overexpression restored FZD6 mRNA and protein expression (Fig. 5C-D, c versus b) and alleviated the facilitating role of miR-199a-5p in adipogenic differentiation, as evidenced by a significant decrease in the expression of adipogenic differentiation markers and a decrease in lipid droplets compared with the lenti-miR-199a infection group (Fig. 5E-F, c versus b). These results demonstrated that FZD6 mediates the role of miR-199a-5p in promoting the adipogenic process of control BM-MSCs.

Discussion

Acquired aplastic anemia is a life-threatening bone marrow failure disorder characterized by defective hematopoiesis, immune dysfunction, and fatty bone marrow19. Bone marrow-derived MSCs, as an important cellular component in the marrow microenvironment, have been reported to possess various functions, including hematopoietic support, immune regulation, and adipo-osteogenic differentiation20,21. Accumulating evidence indicates that BM-MSCs exhibit significant characteristic alterations and functional differences in aplastic anemia compared with those in healthy donors, although they share the very similar surface marker expression used to define MSCs22,23. The impaired proliferation potential, high apoptosis rate, and susceptibility to senescence of AA BM-MSCs were observed and reported24,25. The insufficiency of AA BM-MSCs in hematopoietic support was also indicated by reduced colony-forming capacity of CD34+ HSPCs after coculture with AA BM-MSCs, which is proposed to be associated with the decreased expression of VCAM-1 in AA BM-MSCs26. Besides, deficiency in the suppressive ability of BM-MSCs on T cell priming and activation in aplastic anemia and enhanced adipogenic differentiation of AA BM-MSCs at the expense of osteogenesis were also extensively studied and demonstrated25,27. These characteristic alterations and dysfunctions of AA BM-MSCs contribute to the bone marrow microenvironment changes and pathogenesis of aplastic anemia28,29, which could account for the current dilemma with existing treatment strategies and may represent a promising direction for future AA treatment30,31. Differential gene expression profiles have been characterized in AA BM-MSCs23,32. However, the detailed molecular mechanism underlying BM-MSC-mediated marrow microenvironment changes in aplastic anemia remains to be determined.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are short (20–24 nt) noncoding RNAs that negatively regulate gene expression at the posttranscriptional level and play crucial roles in various physiological and pathological processes33. There have been reports that miRNAs are involved in AA pathogenesis by regulating T cell activation and Th17/Treg balance34,35. Besides, aberrant expression of miR-30a-5p and miR-146b-5p has been demonstrated to induce adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs derived from aplastic anemia36,37, while miR-144-3p upregulation in AA BM-MSCs could suppress osteogenic differentiation38, further suggesting the critical role of miRNAs in BM-MSC-mediated microenvironment changes in aplastic anemia. In this study, we further validated the promoting effect of miR-199a-5p on adipogenic differentiation of AA BM-MSCs by specifically inhibiting miR-199a-5p. miRNAs often exert various functions by regulating the expression of multiple target genes through directly binding to the target mRNAs in a seed sequence-dependent manner39. Identifying the downstream target genes of miRNAs is of great significance to provide deep insights into the role of miRNAs in a specific pathophysiological process40. Considering that the binding site of the miR-199a-5p is located in the coding region rather than the 3’UTR of TGFBI mRNA, we systematically screened and identified the downstream targets of miR-199a-5p by use of TargetScan and PicTar web tools prediction combined with profiling data analysis. Both FZD6 and CELSR1, with miR-199a-5p binding sites in their 3’UTRs, were identified as potential targets of miR-199a-5p, whose correlations with miR-199a-5p were also previously mentioned in colorectal cancer41 and glioma42, respectively. However, their roles in adipogenic differentiation have never been reported. Although FZD6 is demonstrated as a negative regulator of adipogenesis and partly mediates the role of miR-199a-5p in facilitating adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs, further research is also needed to determine whether aberrant expression of CELSR1 and other genes screened but not validated in this work also participate in the adipogenic differentiation and mediate the role of miR-199a-5p in the enhanced adipogenesis of AA BM-MSCs.

Generally, the differentiation of BM-MSCs towards adipocytes and osteoblasts is in a balanced state, finely regulated, and plays a significant role in maintaining the homeostasis of the marrow microenvironment43. Imbalance in the differentiation of BM-MSCs leads to enhanced adipogenic differentiation at the expense of osteogenic differentiation, which has been reported not only in aplastic anemia but also in aging and osteoporosis44,45. This may result in excessive accumulation of adipocytes and reduction of osteoblasts in the bone marrow, impairing and deteriorating the marrow homeostasis and hematopoietic supporting microenvironment. Decoding the molecular mechanism underlying the imbalanced differentiation of BM-MSCs towards adipocytes and osteoblasts is of great significance to provide deep insights into BM-MSC-mediated disorders and potential therapeutic targets. miR-199a-5p is involved in various physiological and pathological processes, such as hematopoietic lineage differentiation, immune response, tumorigenesis, and cellular senescence46–49. There have also been reports about the role of miR-199a-5p in osteogenic differentiation of valve interstitial cells and MSCs, but opposite results were observed50,51, which may be due to the different cell sources used. TGFBI and FZD6 are two validated target genes of miR-199a-5p in adipogenic differentiation and have been reported to be associated with TGF-β and Wnt/β-catenin signal pathways52,53, respectively, both of which have been implicated in osteogenic differentiation54,55. However, it still remains to be investigated whether aberrant expression of miR-199a-5p and its target genes in AA BM-MSCs mediates the impaired osteoblast differentiation of BM-MSCs in aplastic anemia.

Limitation of this study

Our results demonstrated the regulatory roles of miR-199a-5p and its target gene FZD6 in the adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs derived from patients with aplastic anemia, providing further mechanistic insights into miR-199a-5p-mediated enhanced adipogenesis of AA BM-MSCs. Whether miR-199a-5p and its target genes could be used as intervention targets for AA therapy by attenuating the fat accumulation in the marrow still needs to be further determined with more in vivo experimental evidence. Besides, the influences of imbalanced differentiation of BM-MSCs on hematopoietic support and T cell activation in aplastic anemia, which results from aberrant expression of miR-199a-5p and its target genes, are also worthy of investigation to further implicate the important role of BM-MSC-mediated dysfunctions in AA pathogenesis.

Methods

Human samples and cell culture

Human bone marrow samples derived from AA patients and controls diagnosed with iron deficiency anemia (IDA), infectious fever, kidney disease, and lymphadenitis were collected from the department of hematology in the affiliated hospital of Jining Medical University. These control patients used in this study did not present pancytopenia and hypoplasia. Informed consent was obtained from the individuals examined, and the study was approved by the human research ethics committee of the affiliated hospital of Jining Medical University (approval no. 2021C085) and performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Seventeen AA patients (eleven male and six female aged 3–77) were enrolled in this study. The characteristics of AA patients and controls were listed in Table S2. BM-MSCs were isolated from the human bone marrow samples as previously described and cultured in high glucose DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco). Experiments were performed using BM-MSCs from individual patient samples, not pooled together. 293T cells were obtained from the Institute of Basic Medical Sciences in the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and cultured in high glucose DMEM with 10% FBS.

Adipogenic induction and oil red O staining

BM-MSCs in passage 3 to passage 6 derived from AA patients or normal controls were inoculated into 6- or 12-well plates. Upon reaching more than 90% confluence, the cells were incubated with adipogenic induction medium containing 0.01 mg/mL insulin (HY-P0035, MedChemExpress), 100 mg/mL indomethacin (53-86-1, Sigma), 0.5 mM 3-isobutyl-1-methyl-xanthine (28822-58-4, Sigma), and 1 µM dexamethasone (50-02-2, Sigma). After adipogenic induction for 9 days, the cells were rinsed with PBS in the plates, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (P1110, Solarbio) for 10 min, and washed again with PBS, followed by staining with oil red O (G1260, Solarbio) for 30 min. Lipid droplet formation was observed and photographed using an inverted fluorescence microscope (Olympus).

RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and quantitative RT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from the cell samples using TRIzol reagent, and the concentration was quantified by NanoDrop One (Thermo). 2 µg total RNA was used for the subsequent reverse transcription reaction, and the first strand of cDNA was synthesized using the RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (K1622, Thermo). A stem-loop reverse transcription primer specific for miR-199a-5p was used, and U6 was reverse transcribed using a strand-specific primer. Oligo(dT) was used for reverse transcription of mRNA. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed using SYBR premix (RK21203, ABclonal) and a BioRad CFX96 PCR machine. The primers used for reverse transcription and qRT-PCR were listed in Table S3.

Plasmid construction

The cDNA encoding FZD6 (NM_003506) was amplified and inserted into pmiRNA1 (System Biosciences). The pmiRNA1- and pcDNA6-based plasmids expressing miR-199a were constructed as previously described46. The short hairpin RNA (shRNA) sequences for FZD6 were synthesized, annealed, and inserted into pSIH1-H1-copGFP (System Biosciences). The miR-199a-5p sponge vector (pLV-hEF1a-EGFP-2 A-Puro-sponge-hsa-miR-199a-5p) was purchased from the Beijing Syngenbio company and could express an RNA containing three consecutive binding sites for miR-199a-5p. The luciferase reporter constructs containing miR-199a-5p seed sequence binding sites from the 3’UTR of FZD6, SULF1, CELSR1, and CDH2 were created using the pmiR-Report vector system. The sequences used for plasmid construction were listed in Table S4.

Lentivirus package and cell infection

293T cells were used for the production of recombinant lentivirus. The lentivirus packaging was performed by co-transferring 1 µg of pmiRNA1, pSIH1-H1-copGFP, or pLV-hEF1a-EGFP-2 A-Puro-based constructs with each packaging plasmid (p-VSVG, pRSV, pMDL) 0.6 µg into 293T cells in 6-well plates. After 48 h, the culture supernatants were collected and centrifuged (5 min at 1500 g) to discard the cell debris, followed by adding them into the 6-well or 12-well plates to infect BM-MSCs in high glucose DMEM medium with a final concentration of 5 µg/mL polybrene (28728-55-4, Sigma).

Dual luciferase reporter assay

293T cells were plated into 12-well plates and transfected with pcDNA6- or pcDNA-miR-199a-, pmiR-Report-based constructs (pmiR-Report or FZD6 or SULF1 or CELSR1 or CDH2), and pRL-TK. After 48 h, the cells were rinsed with PBS twice and lysed using 200 µl 1x passive lysis buffer (Promega) for 15 min. Then the cell lysates were collected, centrifuged (12000 g for 2 min), and the firefly and renilla luciferase activity were detected using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System in a GloMax® 20/20 Luminometer (Promega).

Western blot

Total cell protein was extracted using RIPA lysis buffer (P0013B, Beyotime), and the protein concentration was quantified using a BCA protein assay kit (P0010, Beyotime) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. 20 µg cell lysates were subjected to SDS-PAGE (10% separation gel) and transferred to a polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membrane (IPVH00010 Millipore). Then the membrane was blocked with 5% non-fat milk for 2 h at room temperature, followed by incubation with primary antibodies (FZD6, A10503, Abclonal; Actin, AC004, Abclonal) overnight at 4 °C. The horseradish peroxidase-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (ZB-5301, 1:5000) and goat anti-mouse IgG (ZB-2305, 1:10000) were purchased from ZSGB-BIO. Signals were detected using an ECL western blotting substrate (KF8001, Affinity) in the Tanon 5800 Chemiluminescent Imaging System. The original western blot pictures used in this study were also provided in the supplementary information.

Bioinformatic analysis

TargetScan and PicTar web tools were used to predict the potential targets of miR-199a-5p. The expression profile of the candidate genes was further analyzed after miR-199a-5p overexpression and in adipogenic differentiation of BM-MSCs using the profiling data from GEO datasets (GSE97824, GSE199466). Considering the upregulation of miR-199a-5p in adipogenic differentiation and negative regulation of miR-199a-5p on its target genes, the differentially downregulated genes (log2 normalized signal change < -0.5) after miR-199a-5p overexpression and in adipogenic differentiation (fold change < 0.5) were preferentially selected by bioinformatic analysis, while the P values were not considered simultaneously.

Statistical analysis

Three independent experiments were conducted, and a representative experiment was shown. The data were presented as mean ± SEM. GraphPad Prism 6.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) was used to determine the statistical significance (*P < 0.05 and **P < 0.01).

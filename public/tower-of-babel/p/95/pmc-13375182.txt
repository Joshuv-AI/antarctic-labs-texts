Robotic-assisted Y-V plasty for obliterative bladder neck contracture following transurethral resection of prostate
Authors: Saroj Kumar Yadav, Boris Kangabam, N. L. Bharath, Sidhartha Kalra
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Bladder neck contracture (BNC) is a recognized complication after transurethral resection of prostate (TURP) (0.5%–10%). While endoscopic treatment is first-line, obliterative BNC not amenable to guidewire passage often recurs. We report a 66-year-old male with BNC 1 year after TURP, initially managed with suprapubic cystostomy (SPC). As the contracture was complete, robotic-assisted Y-V plasty was performed. The bladder was mobilised, fibrotic tissue excised, and a vascularised anterior flap was advanced under cystoscopic and indocyanine green guidance. Recovery was uneventful. At 1 year, the patient had Qma × 22 mL/s, minimal residual urine, and full continence. Robotic Y-V plasty offers a durable, single-stage solution for refractory obliterative BNC.

OBJECTIVE

This video aims to present upfront, single-stage bladder neck reconstruction using robotic-assisted Y-V plasty for obliterative bladder neck contracture (BNC) following transurethral resection of prostate (TURP), thereby alleviating the risk of recurrence after endoscopic management.

BNC is a recognized complication following TURP, with a reported incidence of 0.5%–10%.[1] First-line management includes endoscopic dilatation, bladder neck incision/resection, with or without adjunctive agents such as mitomycin C or steroids.[2] More recently, alternative endoscopic strategies – including drug-coated balloon dilatation[3] and temporary stent placement[4] – have been described. However, severe, thick, obliterative contractures – particularly those in which a guidewire cannot be negotiated – are associated with high recurrence rates after repeated endoscopic interventions.[5] In such scenarios, definitive reconstructive techniques, including open or robotic V-Y/Y-V plasty, provide durable outcomes with acceptable preservation of continence.[6]

METHODS

A 66-year-old male with diabetes, hypertension, and stage 3 chronic kidney disease, with prior buccal mucosal graft dorsal onlay urethroplasty for bulbar stricture posttraumatic catheterization 3 years ago, presented with lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) progressing to acute urinary retention (AUR). Evaluation showed bladder outlet obstruction from benign prostatic enlargement with a normal urethra; he failed the voiding trial. He underwent an uneventful bipolar TURP for a 42 g Grade II prostate with a prominent median lobe (resection time 45 min). He voided well for 6 months, then had recurrent LUTS progressing to AUR and evaluation revealed obliterative BNC [Figure 1a]. Failed perurethral catheterization (PUC) led to emergency suprapubic cystostomy (SPC) placement.

Composite illustration of Y-V plasty for bladder neck obliteration. (a) Preoperative MCU showing complete bladder neck obliteration, (b) Exposed anterior bladder wall with markings for a reverse V-shaped bladder flap, (c) Opened lower anterior bladder flap prepared for Y-V plasty with identification of the distal urethra, and (d) Postoperative MCU demonstrating a widely patent bladder neck following reconstruction

The bladder was mobilized for neck exposure [Figure 1b]. A vascularized anterior V-flap to the prostatic urethra was created after dorsal venous complex ligation. BNC site was localized via intraoperative retrograde cystoscopy using cut-to-the-light technique similar to the description by Liu et al.[7] Fibrotic tissue was released and excised. Flap was advanced to Y-shape for wide, tension-free reconstruction [Figure 1c and Video 1] as per the standard technique,[8] with indocyanine green (ICG) confirming the vascularity. PUC and SPC were placed for drainage.

RESULTS

The postoperative course was uneventful. At 4 weeks, a MCU demonstrated a widely patent bladder neck [Figure 1d]. At 3 months and at 1-year follow-up, the patient was voiding comfortably with a peak flow rate of 22 mL/s, minimal postvoid residual urine, and complete urinary continence.

CONCLUSION

This video case demonstrates robotic-assisted Y-V plasty as an effective single-stage solution for intractable obliterative BNC. Robotics provides 3D magnified vision, superior articulation, and ergonomically efficient suturing, precise dissection, excision of fibrotic tissue, and tension-free anastomosis. Intraoperative cystoscopic guidance and ICG imaging enable accurate localisation and assessment of flap vascularity, reducing the recurrence and preserving continence. Early robotic intervention in select patients avoids repeated failed endoscopic procedures and morbidity.

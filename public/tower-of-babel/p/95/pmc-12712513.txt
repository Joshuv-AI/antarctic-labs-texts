NELL-1–Positive Membranous Nephropathy Following Inhalational Mercury Exposure
Authors: Yagmur Tahillioglu, Ozge Hurdogan, Ahmet Burak Dirim, Ayse Serra Artan, Abdulkadir Celik, Egemen Cebeci, Ozgur Akin Oto, Yasemin Ozluk, Halil Yazici, Aydin Turkmen, Savas Ozturk
Published: 2025

To the Editor:

Mercury-associated membranous nephropathy (MN) is a rare but recognized entity, usually linked to chronic dermal or oral exposure through traditional medicines or cosmetics.1,2 Inhalational exposure, however, is extremely uncommon. We report a biopsy-proven case of NELL-1–positive MN following inhalational mercury exposure.

A 44-year-old man with psoriasis developed burning dysesthesias in his feet, inner restlessness, and increased urinary frequency after inhaling heated mercury mixed with animal bile daily for 2 weeks as an unconventional skin treatment. Laboratory evaluation revealed nephrotic-range proteinuria (urine protein-to-creatinine ratio: 5.6 g/g), hypoalbuminemia (3.29 g/dl), and hyperlipidemia, with preserved renal function. Blood pressure was 156/101 mm Hg, without hematuria or edema. Autoimmune serology was negative, and complement levels were normal. Neurological examination showed distal sensory loss and hyperreflexia; small fiber neuropathy was suspected, and pregabalin was initiated.

Renal biopsy demonstrated diffuse glomerular basement membrane thickening, subepithelial deposits, and spike formation. Immunofluorescence revealed granular IgG and C3 deposits; PLA2R was negative, but immunohistochemistry showed NELL-1 positivity (Figure 1). Electron microscopy confirmed stage 1 to 2 MN. Heavy metal testing detected elevated mercury levels (serum: 19.6 μg/l [normal: 0–10 μg/l]; urine: 86.7 μg/l [normal: 0–20 μg/l]). No other nephrotoxin exposure or secondary causes were identified.

(a) Diffuse thickening of the glomerular basement membranes (hematoxylin and eosin stain; × 400). (b) Strong granular IgG deposits along the glomerular basement membranes (immunofluorescence microscopy; × 400). (c) No staining observed in glomeruli with anti-PLA2R antibody (× 400). (d) Diffuse granular staining of the glomerular basement membranes with anti-NELL1 antibody (× 400).

The patient was treated with i.v. and oral 2,3-dimercaptopropane-1-sulfonic acid (Dimaval), leading to a decline in mercury levels (to 4.5 μg/l within 2.5 months) and gradual improvement in neurological symptoms. At 6 months, proteinuria decreased to 0.8 g/g under renin–angiotensin system blockade and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibition, with normalization of serum albumin (Figure S1).

This case highlights 2 points. First, inhalational mercury exposure is an underrecognized but clinically significant cause of nephrotoxicity, emphasizing the need for careful environmental and occupational histories in MN evaluation. Second, NELL-1 positivity aligns with emerging evidence linking this antigen to toxin- or drug-induced MN.2,3 To our knowledge, this is the first reported case of NELL-1–positive MN following inhalational mercury exposure.4 These findings broaden the clinical and antigenic spectrum of mercury-associated MN and support routine antigen staining beyond PLA2R.

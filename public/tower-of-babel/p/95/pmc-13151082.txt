Neural tube defects burden from 1992 to 2021: a global, regional, and national age-period-cohort analysis of disability-adjusted life years
Authors: Yuhang Wu, Lizhang Chen, Tingting Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundNeural tube defects (NTDs) remain a persistent global health concern; however, the complex drivers of their epidemiological trends are not fully understood. This study investigated three-decade trends in NTDs-related disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) using an age-period-cohort (APC) modeling framework across global, regional, and national settings.MethodLeveraging data from the Global Burden of Disease Study 2021, we analyzed trends in the number, all-age rate, and age-standardized rate (ASR) of NTDs-related DALYs. We assessed the ecological association between ASR and the Socio-demographic Index (SDI) using Spearman’s correlation and employed an APC model to disentangle the underlying effects of age, time period, and birth cohort.ResultsGlobally, NTDs-related DALYs and their ASR declined by approximately 47% between 1992 and 2021. A significant inverse correlation was found between ASR and national SDI levels, yet the annual net drift revealed a striking disparity, being negative in low-SDI regions (-1.18%) but positive in high-SDI regions (0.78%). Furthermore, the burden of DALYs shifted over time from neonates towards older age groups (≥ 5 years). APC analysis confirmed that while the protective age effect (risk declining with age) was universal, period and cohort effects varied substantially across regions.ConclusionIn conclusion, despite a marked global decline in the NTDs burden over three decades, substantial disparities persist, closely linked to socioeconomic development. The shifting burden towards older ages and the divergent trends uncovered by APC analysis underscore the need for tailored public health strategies that move beyond one-size-fits-all approaches to effectively address NTDs in diverse settings.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12887-026-06587-y.

Background

Neural tube defects (NTDs) represent a group of severe congenital malformations of the brain and spinal column, ranking among the most frequent birth anomalies worldwide. In serious cases, they can lead to mortality or lifelong paralysis [1, 2]. In terms of both mortality and morbidity, NTDs are second only to congenital cardiovascular anomalies [3], contributing substantially to global public health challenges. An estimated 300,000 newborns are affected each year [1]. Despite advances in prevention and care, mortality associated with NTDs remains considerably high in numerous low- and middle-income countries. Notably, NTDs account for up to 29% of neonatal deaths in low-income settings [4]. Affected children often need lifelong multidisciplinary care, imposing considerable financial pressure on families due to direct treatment expenses and substantial indirect non-medical costs [5]. Overall, NTDs pose a persistent economic burden on healthcare systems, service providers, and society at large. Hence, precise assessment of the disease burden attributable to NTDs is essential for guiding equitable allocation of health resources and shaping effective policy interventions.

Earlier research has consistently underscored the considerable global burden of NTDs, emphasizing the continued importance of addressing this public health issue [6, 7]. Conventional epidemiological approaches, however, have largely been descriptive and are limited in their capacity to disentangle the complex interplay of factors, including age, time period, and birth cohort, underlying trends in NTDs burden. The age-period-cohort (APC) model provides a powerful analytical framework to separately identify the effects of age, period, and cohort on disease epidemiology, offering deeper insights into the drivers of NTDs burden and potential intervention leverage points. With the recent release of the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 dataset, it is now feasible to re-examine temporal and geographical trends in NTDs using more sophisticated statistical techniques.

In this study, we aim to compile data on disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) due to NTDs across the globe from 1992 to 2021, with visual stratification by age, sex, year, and geographic region. This enables an updated evaluation of the worldwide burden of NTDs. Furthermore, by applying the APC model, we seek to analyze long-term trends in NTDs DALYs through the lenses of age, period, and birth cohort, and to explore underlying factors driving these temporal changes, thereby addressing a current gap in NTDs burden research. We anticipate that the findings will offer strategic insights for tackling this significant public health problem and support evidence-based, targeted policy formulation.

Method

Data source

This study draws on data obtained from the Global Health Data Exchange GBD Results Tool (http://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-results-tool). The GBD 2021 dataset enables systematic investigation of diseases and injuries worldwide, using standardized metrics and rigorous methodologies to evaluate 371 diseases and injuries, along with 88 risk factors, across 204 countries and territories. NTDs were classified according to codes from the International Classification of Diseases, 9th Revision (ICD-9: 740–741.93, 742.0) and 10th Revision (ICD-10: Q00-Q01.9, Q05-Q05.9, Q07.01, Q07.03) [8]. Because the underlying data sources span several decades, the GBD collaboration first maps relevant ICD-9 and ICD-10 codes to a unified cause list and then uses cross-walk models to adjust for changes in diagnostic criteria and coding practices between revisions. This harmonization helps to ensure that temporal comparisons of NTD burden from 1992 to 2021 are not driven solely by the transition from ICD-9 to ICD-10.

We extracted data on the number, all-age rate, and age-standardized rate (ASR) of DALYs attributable to NTDs from GBD 2021, disaggregated by sex (female, male, and both), geographic location (204 countries and territories), age (0–94 years), calendar year (1990–2021), and socio-demographic index (SDI). DALYs quantify disease burden by summing years of life lost due to premature death and years lived with disability, offering a comprehensive measure of NTD burden across ages and settings. Because deaths due to NTDs occur predominantly in fetuses, neonates and young children, these age-specific mortality patterns are largely reflected in the YLL component of DALYs; for parsimony, we therefore concentrated our APC analyses on DALY rates rather than modelling mortality rates separately by age group and country. The SDI, a composite measure of development status, was derived from lag-distributed income per capita, mean years of education among individuals aged 15 and older, and the total fertility rate among females under 25 [9]. Countries and territories were categorized into five SDI groups (Table S1): high (> 0.81), high-middle (0.70–0.81), middle (0.61–0.69), low-middle (0.46–0.60), and low (< 0.46) [10]. All estimates are presented with 95% uncertainty intervals (UIs), calculated as the 25th and 975th values of 1,000 ordered draws [9]. The data used are publicly accessible and anonymized, with ethical review and informed consent waived by the University of Washington Institutional Review Board (study number: 9060).

Statistics analysis

Analysis of overall temporal trends in NTDs DALYs

We evaluated spatial and temporal patterns in the burden of NTDs from 1992 to 2021. Initial assessment involved estimating absolute numbers, all-age rates, and ASRs of DALYs, along with calculating relative percentage changes between 1992 and 2021. We also examined the association between national SDI values and age-standardized DALY rates using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (Rho). Furthermore, the age profile of NTDs burden was analyzed by dividing DALYs into five age categories (0–4, 5–19, 20–39, 40–64, and 65–94 years) and computing the proportion of total DALYs within each group.

Age‑period‑cohort modelling analysis of DALYs rate in NTDs

The APC model, grounded in Poisson distribution theory, was employed to uncover latent trends in NTDs burden. We used this model to decompose DALYs rates into independent effects of age, time period, and birth cohort. The 2021 GBD DALYs estimates for NTDs, alongside the population data from each country, were used as inputs for the APC model that implemented the intrinsic estimator (IE) method. In this study, the IE method was applied to address the identifiability problem inherent in APC models [11]. Additional technical details on the APC framework are described elsewhere [12]. To satisfy model requirements, we organized data into 19 five-year age groups (0–4, 5–9, …, 90–94) and selected six time points (1994, 1999, …, 2019) to represent distinct periods, thereby minimizing transient fluctuations such as those related to the COVID-19 pandemic. This structure also defined 21 overlapping birth cohorts, ranging from 1900–1904 (median 1902) to 2015–2019 (median 2017).

In this study, we concentrate on several key estimable functions. The net drift represents the overall average annual percent change in DALYs rate over time. Local drifts reflect annual percent changes specific to each age group, whereas the longitudinal age curve indicates fitted age-specific rates in a reference cohort, adjusted for period deviations. Period Rate Ratios (Period RR) reflects ratios of age-specific rates in each period relative to a reference period. Cohort Rate Ratios (Cohort RR) reflects ratios of age-specific rates in each birth cohort relative to a reference cohort. Statistical significance of parameters and functions was assessed using the Wald χ2 test, with all tests being two-sided. APC modeling was performed using the National Cancer Institute’s APC Web Tool (http://analysistools.nci.nih.gov/apc/), and figures were generated using R software (version 4.2.3).

Results

Global, regional and national trends in NTDs DALYs, 1992–2021

Between 1992 and 2021, the total global DALYs due to NTDs were 5,294,602.55 (95% UI: 4,326,463.67 to 6,485,533.17) in 2021, reflecting a 46.5% reduction from 1992. The ASR of DALYs in 2021 was 83.18 per 100,000 population (95% UI: 67.94 to 102.10), a decrease of 47.3% compared to 1992. According to the APC model, the net drift in the global NTDs DALYs rate over this period was −0.219% per year (95% confidence interval [CI]: −0.504 to 0.067) (Table 1). Regionally, the ASR of NTDS DALYs was highest in the low SDI (175.35 per 100,000 population, 95% UI: 131.63 to 224.19), while it was lower in the high SDI (15.50 per 100,000 population, 95% UI: 12.97 to 17.62) and high-middle SDI (21.70 per 100,000 population, 95% UI: 17.60 to 25.41) regions in 2021. Notably, the annual net drift of DALY rate ranged from −1.178% (95% CI: −2.062 to −0.285) for low SDI region to 0.779% (95% CI: 0.528 to 1.031) for high SDI region (Table 1).

At the national level, Nigeria reported the highest number of DALYs in 2021 (798,459.56; 95% UI: 467,807.31 to 1,168,733.07), followed by India (580,948.48; 95% UI: 405,631.75 to 860,754.76), Ethiopia (256,652.64; 95% UI: 162,940.30 to 392,948.79), and the Democratic Republic of the Congo (228,516.97; 95% UI: 130,236.50 to 344,821.20). Together, these four countries contributed nearly half of the global NTDs DALYs burden. South Sudan had the highest ASR (351.62 per 100,000; 95% UI: 191.14 to 579.97), while Taiwan (Province of China) recorded the lowest (2.67 per 100,000; 95% UI: 2.11 to 3.30). More than 90% of countries experienced a decline in ASR from 1992 to 2021. The annual net drift in DALYs rate varied widely across nations, ranging from −3.561% (95% CI: −6.040 to −1.016) in Rwanda to 2.318% (95% CI: 2.110 to 2.526) in the United States (Table S2, Fig. 1). Time trends in NTDs DALYs rate across different age groups

As shown in Fig. 3A, local drift values for NTDs DALYs rates varied considerably by age. Globally, younger and middle-aged groups (0–64 years) predominantly exhibited negative local drifts, whereas older age groups (≥ 65 years) showed positive values. A comparable pattern was observed in high-middle and middle SDI regions. In high SDI regions, positive local drifts were seen in ages above 20 years. By contrast, nearly all age groups in low-middle and low SDI regions displayed negative local drifts, indicating widespread declines in DALYs rates. Age, period, and cohort effects on NTDs DALYs

Figure 4 displays the estimated age, period, and cohort effects for global data and each SDI region. Age effects followed a consistent downward trend with increasing age across all regions after adjusting for period deviations, with the highest DALYs rates observed in children aged 0–4 years in the reference cohort (Fig. 4A). Period effects revealed distinct regional trajectories. In low-middle and low SDI regions, period risks showed a continuous decline, suggesting improving control of NTDs over time. In contrast, high, high-middle, and middle SDI regions, along with the global aggregate, experienced an initial decline followed by a subsequent increase in period-related risks. Cohort effects globally showed a slight rise in early birth cohorts, then a gradual decline, a pattern particularly evident in high and high-middle SDI regions. In middle, low-middle, and low SDI regions, cohort risks remained relatively stable before beginning to decline in more recent birth cohorts (Fig. 4C). When the APC models were stratified by sex, we observed nearly overlapping age, period, and cohort curves for males and females across SDI categories, supporting the lack of pronounced sex-related differences in temporal trends. Age-period-cohort effects in representative countries

To better illustrate trends across development settings, we highlight representative countries from different SDI categories (Figure S26). Lithuania (high SDI) showed a slow decline in cohort effects and a broadening age distribution of NTDs burden. China (high-middle SDI) experienced a 92.35% reduction in DALYs over the past 30 years, though its absolute burden remains substantial due to population size. Iran and Nigeria both exhibited declining DALYs rates across age groups (local drifts < 0), along with decreasing period and cohort risks. Rwanda showed the most favorable net drift (−3.561%) and consistently declining period and cohort effects.

The United States of America recorded the most adverse trends among all countries, with a net drift of 2.318%, worsening period effects, and elevated cohort risks. Brazil (middle SDI) saw only a 17.34% decline in DALY rate from 1992 to 2021, with total DALYs increasing by 45.18%, driven by rising period and cohort risks. Nearly all age groups in Brazil showed positive local drifts (except 0–10 years). Zimbabwe was the only low-middle SDI country with an increasing DALYs rate (30.75%) and total DALYs (58.26%), also associated with rising period and cohort effects.

Discussion

This study provides a detailed examination of the global NTDs burden from 1992 to 2021, highlighting an overall decreasing trend in DALYs during this period. Our analysis identified a significant negative correlation between the SDI and ASR of DALYs, indicating that regions with lower SDI are disproportionately affected by this health issue. While NTDs DALYs continue to be predominantly attributed to the neonatal age group, there has been an observed transition in the age distribution of DALYs toward higher age groups over the observation period. Additionally, the APC model underscored the complexity of the epidemiology of NTDs, reflecting profound health disparities at global, regional, and national levels in terms of age, period, and cohort effects. These findings highlight the need for targeted interventions and policies to address the unique burden of NTDs across populations and settings.

Our analysis confirms a general downward trend in NTDs DALYs from 1992 to 2021, with the smallest reductions seen in low SDI regions. This pattern aligns with existing evidence indicating that low- and middle-income countries with constrained health systems experience a disproportionately high NTDs burden. In low- and middle-SDI settings, NTDs remain an important cause of stillbirths and early neonatal deaths; recent analyses from the Child Health and Mortality Prevention Surveillance (CHAMPS) network and global prevalence studies have shown that NTDs account for a substantial fraction of stillbirths and under-five deaths, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia [13, 14]. By contrast, in many high-SDI countries, wider availability of folic acid fortification and supplementation, advanced prenatal diagnosis, and improved perioperative and neonatal care have increased survival of affected infants, such that a greater share of DALYs now arises from long-term disability in older children and adults rather than early mortality [15]. A notable observation is the apparent divergence between the positive net drift values and the declining ASR in high SDI regions. The positive drift may reflect underlying increases in NTDs risk, potentially driven by factors such as population aging, extended survival among affected individuals, and improved detection through enhanced birth monitoring systems [16–18]. The decline in ASR likely reflects the benefits of effective antenatal interventions and advances in perinatal care. This discrepancy suggests that, despite improvements in clinical management and long-term care, underlying risk factors for NTDs may be intensifying in certain populations.

Substantial variations in NTDs burden were observed across countries, likely influenced by a combination of genetic, biological, and socio-environmental factors. A consistent age effect was identified worldwide, with the highest DALYs rates occurring in early childhood and declining with age. This pattern may be partly explained by advances in prenatal screening, counseling, and fetal surgery, which have improved early detection and management of NTDs [3, 15]. Nevertheless, the persistence of NTDs-related disability into later life highlights the need for sustained clinical and supportive services beyond the neonatal period. Effective long-term strategies are essential to mitigate chronic complications and reduce lifelong burden [19]. Globally, period effects displayed an unfavorable trajectory, characterized by an initial decline followed by a rebound in high and high-middle SDI regions. This pattern may be linked to the rising prevalence of maternal obesity in developed countries, [20], a known risk factor for NTDs [21]. Advanced maternal age in these regions may also contribute, as several studies have reported associations between older motherhood and NTDs prevalence [22–25]. In contrast, birth cohort effects showed consistent declines across all SDI regions. Individuals born earlier have more cumulative risk factors, especially exposure to harmful environments such as global industrialization and urbanization [26], such as air pollution [27, 28]. At the same time, improvements in healthcare infrastructure and prenatal screening have likely reduced NTDs births over successive cohorts [29]. Notably, the broadly similar DALYs trajectories we observed in males and females suggest that sex-specific biological mechanisms are unlikely to be the main driver of the global distribution of NTDs burden, which is more plausibly driven by these contextual differences.

Using the APC framework, we further examined country-level patterns within different SDI strata. Lithuania exhibited a gradual decline in cohort effects, possibly reflecting sustained improvements in prenatal and neonatal care following the implementation of national perinatal and congenital anomaly prevention programs since the 1990s [30]. In China, the substantial decline in DALYs over the past three decades may be attributed to the former one-child policy, which reduced birth rates [31], and the national folic acid supplementation program initiated in 2009 [32]. Both Iran and Nigeria showed reductions in DALYs rates across age groups, accompanied by declining period and cohort risks. The low fertility rate in Iran, with annual population growth falling from nearly 4% to under 1% since the 1980 s, may have moderated NTDs incidence [33]. Nigeria, meanwhile, has increased health expenditures and raised healthy life expectancy in recent decades [34]. Rwanda demonstrated one of the most favorable trends, with strong declines in net drift and period and cohort effects, likely aided by the introduction of community-based health insurance and system-wide health reforms since 2000 [35]. By contrast, the United States showed adverse period and cohort effects, potentially reflecting gaps in folic acid fortification policies and disparities in healthcare access [36]. Socioeconomic inequalities, along with variations in lifestyle and environmental exposures, may also be contributing factors [37–39]. In addition, the United States has experienced marked increases in opioid use disorder and misuse of prescription medications in recent decades, and population-based studies suggest that periconceptional exposure to opioids and other recreational drugs is associated with a higher risk of NTD-affected pregnancies [40, 41]. In Brazil, although the DALYs rate declined, the absolute number of DALYs rose by nearly half. Although the Family Health Strategy has improved such as life expectancy, maternal and infant mortality [42], many regions reported an increase in the infant mortality rate after an economic downturn that evolved into a troubled period of political crisis [43]. Zimbabwe was the only low-middle SDI country with a rising DALYs rate, which may be related to the high regional prevalence of HIV and periconceptional antiretroviral use [44]. as well as a high rate of folic acid deficiency among women of reproductive age in Eastern Africa [45]. Concerns raised by surveillance studies about slightly elevated NTD risks with specific antiretroviral regimens, such as dolutegravir exposure at conception [46], further underscore the need for careful evaluation of drug safety during the periconceptional period.

From a policy perspective, our findings reinforce the importance of strengthening both traditional and emerging strategies for NTD prevention and long-term management. Established measures include ensuring adequate periconceptional folic acid intake through supplementation or food fortification and optimizing maternal nutrition, improving access to antenatal care, and reducing exposure to environmental teratogens such as indoor air pollution and persistent organic pollutants. It is also crucial to identify and avoid potentially teratogenic drug exposures and harmful patterns of drug use during the periconceptional period. Alongside these conventional approaches, rapid progress in digital health is transforming prenatal and surgical care. In particular, the introduction of the Internet of Things (IoT) into surgical and obstetric practice is enabling real-time interconnection of sensors, imaging devices, and communication platforms to support remote monitoring, teleconsultation, and telesurgery [47]. IoT-enabled prenatal health monitoring systems have been proposed to track maternal and fetal parameters in real time, especially in underserved areas, thereby facilitating early identification and referral of high-risk pregnancies [48]. For NTDs, such technologies could help coordinate delivery at centers capable of providing fetal or neonatal surgery, support postoperative monitoring and rehabilitation for affected children, and ultimately improve survival and quality of life. Although the GBD 2021 data do not yet capture the impact of these innovations, they may alter the balance between mortality and disability from NTDs in the coming decades.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to apply the APC model to analyze long-term trends in NTDs DALYs across global, regional, and national levels. Compared with earlier studies [49], our approach offers a more nuanced understanding of temporal patterns, providing valuable insights for epidemiological assessment and health policy planning. The use of net drift and local drift estimates helps characterize overall and age-specific trends in DALYs rates, while age, period, and cohort effect analyses offer a deeper understanding of regional and national variations, supporting targeted prevention and management strategies. Additionally, the GBD 2021 database employs standardized and adjusted estimates, enhancing comparability across populations. Several limitations should be acknowledged. Although GBD 2021 includes 204 countries and territories, estimates for many low-income settings rely heavily on modeling due to sparse primary data, which may affect the accuracy of APC estimates. Second, while the GBD framework uses crosswalk models and standardized cause definitions to harmonize data across ICD revisions and coding practices, residual misclassification related to the gradual transition from ICD-9 to ICD-10 and evolving diagnostic criteria for NTDs cannot be completely excluded; as a result, some period or cohort effects might partly reflect improvements in ascertainment rather than true changes in disease risk. Third, in regions with limited diagnostic capacity, underreporting, misclassification or incomplete registration of NTD cases may further introduce uncertainty. Finally, the structure of the GBD database does not support subnational analyses, limiting our ability to explore within-country heterogeneity.

Conclusion

In summary, our analysis reveals a declining global trend in NTDs burden from 1990 to 2021, though significant disparities persist across regions and socioeconomic groups. The findings underscore NTDs as a persistent child health challenge with lifelong implications, while demonstrating distinctive age-period-cohort patterns across different populations. These results advocate for implementing tailored public health measures that address both prevention and lifelong care, ultimately reducing the impact of NTDs across affected populations.

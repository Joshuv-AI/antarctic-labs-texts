Construction and validation of a machine learning-based prediction model for social isolation in patients with colorectal cancer after stoma surgery
Authors: Wenqian Zhang, Liangliang Qu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo investigate the current status and influencing factors of social alienation in patients with enterostomy after colorectal cancer surgery, and to establish a risk prediction model for social alienation in this population.MethodsA total of 507 enterostomy patients treated in the General Surgery (Colorectal Division), Oncology Department, and Wound Ostomy Clinic of a tertiary hospital in Jinzhou from March 2025 to January 2026 were enrolled by convenience sampling. The dataset was randomly divided into a training set and a validation set at a ratio of 7:3. Risk factors were screened by LASSO regression combined with Logistic regression. Five machine learning algorithms, including Logistic regression (LG), support vector machine (SVM), naive Bayes, K-nearest neighbor (KNN), and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), were employed to establish prediction models for social alienation. The predictive performance was evaluated and compared using the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision, and F1-score. Calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA) were used to assess model fitness and clinical utility.ResultsAmong 507 included patients, 211 developed social alienation, with a prevalence rate of 41.6%. The SVM model achieved the optimal performance among the five models, with an AUC of 0.835 (95%CI: 0.772–0.899), sensitivity of 0.698, accuracy of 0.782, precision of 0.759, specificity of 0.841, and F1-score of 0.727. The calibration curve indicated favorable consistency between predicted and observed probabilities. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test yielded a χ2 value of 3.091 (P = 0.929). DCA revealed that the model provided high net benefit across the entire threshold range, suggesting excellent clinical applicability.ConclusionThe SVM model shows superior performance in predicting the risk of social alienation in enterostomy patients after colorectal cancer surgery and can serve as a screening tool for early identification in clinical practice.Keywords: Machine learning, Colorectal cancer, Colostomy, Social isolation, Prediction model, Influencing factors

Colorectal cancer is an umbrella term for colon and rectal cancer, referring to a malignant tumor originating from the mucosal epithelium of the colon or rectum [1]. With the rapid development of China's social economy and the remarkable improvement of residents' living standards, profound changes have taken place in people's dietary structure and lifestyle, and the incidence and mortality rates of colorectal cancer have also shown a continuous upward trend [2]. Studies have shown that approximately 20% to 30% of patients with low rectal cancer require permanent enterostomy [3]. Although enterostomy can save patients' lives, it may cause physical discomfort, negative emotions, social avoidance and other problems [4], which in turn leads to social isolation and seriously impairs patients' quality of life and the recovery of their social functions [5]. Social isolation is defined as a phenomenon where individuals experience neglect or rejection from others during social interactions, find it difficult to establish or maintain normal social connections, thus triggering a series of negative emotions, and ultimately exhibiting passive behaviors such as apathy, avoidance and refusal [6]. Prolonged social isolation can result in patients' self-seclusion, exacerbate psychological distress, and may reduce the body's anti-tumor immunity, increase the risk of tumor recurrence and metastasis, thereby threatening patients' survival and prognosis [7]. Relevant studies have indicated that the incidence of social isolation among cancer survivors is approximately 38% to 59% [8]. In view of this, early identification of the risk factors for social isolation in patients with colorectal cancer after stoma surgery and the implementation of targeted interventions are of great significance for reducing the incidence of social isolation. Commonly used clinical assessment tools can conduct preliminary screening for social isolation, yet their predictive ability for this condition still has certain limitations. Machine learning, as an artificial intelligence-enabled health technology, has been widely applied in clinical practice. It presents remarkable advantages in disease prediction and diagnosis, and effectively improves the accuracy and reliability of prognostic evaluation [9], which can provide a more precise approach for the risk assessment of social alienation.This study aimed to construct prediction models for the risk of social alienation among patients with enterostomy after colorectal cancer surgery based on machine learning algorithms, and to compare the predictive performance of different models. The findings are expected to provide a scientific basis and practical reference for clinical medical staff to early identify high-risk patients with social alienation and deliver timely individualized intervention strategies.

Subjects and methods

Research subjects

A total of 507 stoma patients who visited the Department of General Surgery (Colorectal Surgery), Oncology Department and Wound Stoma Clinic of a tertiary Grade A hospital in Jinzhou City from March 2025 to January 2026 were selected as the research subjects by convenient sampling.

The sample size of the model was calculated according to the Events per Variable (EPV) [11], with the formula: N = p × EPV ÷ incidence rate of events. In the formula, p is the number of predictive variables, EPV is the number of events required for each variable, and the incidence rate of events is the probability of event occurrence in each sample. This study estimated that about 22 research variables would be included in the model. Combined with literature data, the incidence of social isolation in cancer survivors is about 38% to 59% [8]. According to the 1/10 rule, EPV was set to 10, and 10% invalid samples were considered at the same time. Therefore, the required sample size of this study was 22 × 10 ÷ 49% × (1 + 10%)≈494 cases, and the final sample size was 507 cases.

Investigation tools

Based on literature review, a questionnaire on risk factors for social isolation in patients with colorectal cancer after stoma surgery was self-designed through expert consultation and discussion of the research group. The investigation tools included the following parts:

General information

Gender, age, main caregiver, marital status, educational level, place of residence, living status, personality type, per capita family income, occupational status, type of medical insurance, complicated chronic diseases, clinical tumor stage (Stage Ⅰ, Ⅱ, Ⅲ, Ⅳ), time of tumor diagnosis, stoma status, time after stoma surgery, presence of stoma complications, and self-care status of stoma.

General Alienation Scale (GAS)

The Chinese version revised by Wu et al. [12] was used to measure the social isolation status of individuals. The scale includes 4 dimensions and 15 items, with a total score of 15 to 60 points (a higher score indicates a higher degree of social isolation). The Cronbach's α coefficient of this scale in this study was 0.751. With reference to previous literature, the median total score of 37.5 points was used as the boundary to divide alienation into high and low levels [13].

Social Impact Scale (SIS)

Compiled by Fife et al. [14] and revised into Chinese by Pan et al. [15], it is used to measure the stigma of patients with chronic diseases such as cancer. The Chinese version includes 4 dimensions and 24 items, with a total score of 24 to 96 points (a higher score indicates a greater perceived social impact, i.e., a more serious stigma). The Cronbach's α coefficient of this scale in this study was 0.944.

Perceived Social Support Scale (PSSS)

Compiled by Zimet et al. [16] and translated and revised into Chinese by Jiang [17]. The scale includes 12 items and 3 dimensions, with a total score of 12 to 84 points (a higher score indicates a higher level of overall perceived social support). The total score was divided into three states: low support (12–36 points), moderate support (37–60 points) and high support (61–84 points). The Cronbach's α coefficient of this scale in this study was 0.903.

Mental Vulnerability Questionnaire (MVQ)

Compiled by Eplov et al. [18] and revised into Chinese by Gong et al. [19] to evaluate the degree of psychological vulnerability of individuals. The scale includes 3 dimensions and 22 items, with a total score of 22 to 110 points (a higher score indicates a higher level of psychological vulnerability). The Cronbach's α coefficient of this scale in this study was 0.941.

Stoma Acceptance Questionnaire (SAQ)

Compiled by Bagnasco et al. [20] and translated into Chinese by Hu et al. [21]. The questionnaire includes 3 dimensions and 12 items (4 items for evaluating importance), with a total score of 12 to 48 points (a higher score indicates a higher degree of stoma acceptance). The Cronbach's α coefficient of this scale in this study was 0.889.

Data collection method

Through literature review, expert consultation and repeated group discussions, the variables included in this study were finally determined. Prior to data collection, unified definitions of all variables were formulated, and standardized training was conducted for all researchers involved in data collection. The training contents covered research objectives, survey requirements, as well as specifications for data collection and entry. All investigators were required to fully master assessment contents and scale scoring criteria. Strict adherence to inclusion and exclusion criteria was implemented to ensure the accuracy and reliability of collected data. Before data entry, all research materials were thoroughly inspected, and cases with abnormal values were verified by reviewing electronic medical records. To guarantee the authenticity and accuracy of research data, two independent researchers separately performed data entry and mutual verification, screened out unreasonable questionnaires, and supplemented or eliminated invalid data in a timely manner.

Model development and validation

The data were randomly divided into a training set (355 cases) and a validation set (152 cases) at a ratio of 7:3. The training set was used for model training and hyperparameter tuning, while the validation set was used for independent external evaluation of the models. Based on the feature variables screened by LASSO regression and multivariate Logistic regression, five predictive models were constructed, including Logistic Regression (LR), Naive Bayes (NB), K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN), Support Vector Machine (SVM), and eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost). To optimize model performance and reduce the risk of overfitting, tenfold cross-validation was employed for internal validation and hyperparameter tuning of all models on the training set. Specifically, the training set was randomly partitioned into 10 mutually exclusive subsets; 9 subsets were sequentially used as training data and the remaining 1 subset as validation data, with 10 iterative repetitions. The optimal hyperparameter combination was selected using the mean AUC of the cross-validation set as the evaluation metric for model performance. A fixed random seed (set.seed(123)) was set throughout cross-validation and model construction to ensure result reproducibility. The optimal hyperparameters and preprocessing methods for each model are as follows:

Logistic Regression (LR)

An elastic net regularization model was adopted, with the optimal regularization parameters alpha = 0.1 and lambda = 0.0032 determined by cross-validation. Feature preprocessing involved centering and standardization.

Naive Bayes (NB)

The Gaussian Naive Bayes algorithm was utilized, with the optimal parameters (Laplace smoothing = 0, adjust = 1) identified via cross-validation.

K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN)

The optimal number of neighbors (k = 19) was determined by cross-validation, and feature preprocessing included centering and standardization.

Support Vector Machine (SVM)

A radial basis function (RBF) kernel was employed, with the optimal hyperparameters (cost = 1, gamma = 0.1) determined by cross-validation. Probability output mode was enabled to generate predicted probabilities.

eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost)

The optimal hyperparameters identified by cross-validation were max_depth = 3, eta = 0.1, gamma = 0.1, colsample_bytree = 0.7, min_child_weight = 3, and subsample = 1. A Bernoulli distribution was used to fit the binary classification problem.

After model construction, the discrimination performance (AUC), accuracy, precision, sensitivity, specificity, and F1-score of the models were evaluated on the independent validation set. Meanwhile, the calibration of the models was assessed using the Hosmer–Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test and calibration curves, and the clinical application efficacy was evaluated via decision curve analysis (DCA).

Statistical analysis

SPSS 25.0 and R 4.5.1 software were used for data analysis. The original data set was randomly divided into a training set and a validation set at a ratio of 7:3. The normal distribution of continuous variables was analyzed by the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Measurement data conforming to normal distribution were expressed as mean ± standard deviation ( s), and inter-group comparison was conducted by the t-test. Measurement data not conforming to normal distribution were described by median and interquartile range M[(P25,P75)], and inter-group comparison was conducted by the Mann–Whitney U test. Qualitative data were described by percentage (%), and inter-group comparison was conducted by the chi-square test. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Considering the pursuit of model simplicity and robustness, as well as the concern of overfitting, lambda.1se was selected as the final screening criterion. Ultimately, eight variables were retained, including gender, residential location, personality type, stoma status, stigma, social support, psychological vulnerability, and stoma acceptance.

Construction and performance comparison of prediction models

Seven predictive factors screened by multivariate Logistic regression, namely gender, residential location, stoma status, stigma, social support, psychological vulnerability and stoma acceptance, were taken as input variables, and the occurrence of social alienation was defined as the outcome variable. Five machine learning algorithms were adopted to establish prediction models, which were further validated in the validation set.

The results revealed that the SVM model achieved the optimal performance with an AUC of 0.835 (95%CI: 0.772–0.899). Its sensitivity, accuracy and F1-score were superior to those of other models. The radial basis function kernel was applied in the SVM model. By setting the parameter probability = TRUE, the built-in Platt scaling probability conversion mechanism was activated to map the decision function values of SVM into predicted probabilities ranging from 0 to 1, which was applied for model calibration and efficacy evaluation. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test yielded a χ2 value of 3.091 and a P value of 0.929, indicating no significant difference between predicted values and actual incidence rates, and confirming satisfactory model calibration. Decision curve analysis (DCA) demonstrated that within the clinical threshold probability range of 0.05 to 0.75, the established prediction model presented significantly higher net clinical benefit than the treat-all and treat-none strategies, suggesting promising prospects for clinical decision-making. Notably, the SVM model maintained a relatively high overall net benefit in this interval, possessing favorable clinical application value.

Discussion

Incidence of social isolation in patients with colorectal cancer after stoma surgery

This study included 507 patients with colorectal cancer after stoma surgery, and the incidence of social isolation was 41.6%, which was slightly higher than the research result of Yang [22]. This difference may be due to the fact that the subjects in this study were stoma patients after colorectal cancer surgery, and the changes in body image, defecation mode and stigma caused by stoma are more likely to lead to social isolation [23]. In addition, differences in sample sources, evaluation time points and sample sizes between the two studies may also be important reasons for the inconsistent results.

In conclusion, the incidence of social isolation in patients with colorectal cancer after stoma surgery is at a moderate level. Clinical workers should pay close attention to the psychological and social adaptation of such patients, predict the occurrence of social isolation as early as possible and take targeted intervention measures, so as to reduce the risk of social isolation and improve the quality of life of patients.

Influencing factors of social isolation in patients with colorectal cancer after stoma surgery

Multivariate Logistic regression analysis identified 8 independent risk factors for social isolation in patients with colorectal cancer after stoma surgery: gender, place of residence, stoma status, presence of stoma complications, stigma, social support, psychological vulnerability and stoma acceptance. The relevant discussion is as follows:

Gender

Female patients had a higher risk of social isolation, which was consistent with the research results of Zhang et al. [24]. The reason may be that female patients pay more attention to self-image and physical integrity, and the physical changes after stoma surgery are easy to cause body image disturbance and reduced self-worth. In addition, females are more delicate and sensitive in emotional perception, and their unmet demand for social support and emotional companionship is more likely to lead to psychological gap and social avoidance [25].

Place of residence

Rural patients had a higher risk of social isolation. The insufficient medical resources and imperfect rehabilitation support system in rural areas make it difficult for patients to obtain continuous stoma nursing guidance and psychological support after discharge [26]. At the same time, limited by educational level and health literacy [27], rural patients have a low level of disease cognition, which is easy to cause disease uncertainty and further aggravate social isolation.

Stoma status

Patients with permanent stoma had a higher risk of social isolation than those with temporary stoma, which was consistent with the research of Wang et al. [28]. Temporary stoma has a clear expectation of stoma reversal, and the physical changes are transient, so the psychological pressure of patients is light and social avoidance behavior is less [29]. However, permanent stoma leads to irreversible changes in physical function and image, making patients bear long-term care burden and stigma, and thus more prone to social isolation.

Stigma

Stigma is an important psychological factor leading to social isolation. A qualitative study showed that the physical image changes caused by stoma make patients produce a strong sense of stigma and self-denial, and patients often reduce going out and avoid interpersonal interaction to avoid being discriminated against, thus showing social withdrawal behavior [30].

Social support

The present study found that a lower level of social support was associated with a higher risk of social alienation among postoperative stoma patients, which was consistent with the findings reported by Liao et al. [31]. As a crucial external social resource, social support can not only effectively enhance patients’ ability to cope with disease and adapt to stoma life, but also remarkably alleviate negative emotions and reduce psychological stress. Especially during the critical period of postoperative rehabilitation and social adaptation, adequate social support can lower patients’ sense of stigma and perceived discrimination, thereby relieving their state of social isolation and reducing the incidence of social alienation.

Psychological vulnerability

Patients with high psychological vulnerability have weaker emotional regulation ability and psychological endurance [32]. They are more sensitive to the negative impacts of stoma (such as peculiar smell, leakage, body image changes) and external evaluations, and are easy to produce frustration and stigma, thus reducing social interaction and leading to social isolation.

Stoma acceptance

Patients with low stoma acceptance are more likely to suffer from social isolation. According to psychological theories, patients with high stoma acceptance can calmly accept physical changes and reduce negative emotions through positive cognitive restructuring, thus maintaining active social communication [33]; while patients with low acceptance are accompanied by strong self-denial and tend to adopt social avoidance, which leads to social isolation.

Performance and application value of the prediction model

Based on the seven predictive factors screened by multivariate Logistic regression, this study constructed five machine learning prediction models, including Logistic regression (LR), support vector machine (SVM), naive Bayes, k-nearest neighbor (KNN), and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), and evaluated their performance in the validation set. The results showed that the SVM model had the optimal overall predictive performance, with an AUC of 0.835 (95%CI: 0.772–0.899) in the validation set. Its sensitivity (0.698) and F1-score (0.727) were higher than those of the other models, indicating that the SVM model had good discriminative ability and predictive value for the occurrence of social alienation in patients with enterostomy after colorectal cancer surgery.

The calibration curves of the LR model in both the training and validation sets closely fitted the ideal reference line. Combined with the results of the Hosmer–Lemeshow test (χ2 = 3.091, P = 0.929), it was further confirmed that there was no significant difference between the predicted probability and the actual incidence, indicating good model fit. The calibration curve demonstrated favorable consistency between the predicted probability and the actual probability of the model, and the decision curve analysis (DCA) suggested that the model maintained a high net clinical benefit across the entire threshold range, with excellent clinical application value. Therefore, the SVM model was selected as the optimal model for predicting the risk of social alienation in patients with enterostomy after colorectal cancer surgery.

In clinical nursing practice, comprehensive intervention strategies should be implemented: differentiated psychological counseling and body image adjustment should be carried out for patients of different genders; continuous nursing and health education in rural areas should be improved; long-term psychological support and adaptation guidance for patients with permanent stoma should be strengthened; stoma-related complications should be actively prevented and standardizedly managed; patients’ stigma should be reduced through health education and peer support; a multi-dimensional support system involving family, medical staff and society should be constructed; emotional regulation and psychological resilience training for patients with psychological vulnerability should be enhanced; and patients should be guided to gradually improve stoma acceptance [34], so as to promote their active adaptation to physical changes, improve interpersonal interaction and social participation, and effectively reduce the risk of social alienation.

Conclusion

This study included 22 candidate variables, and a total of 7 important variables were screened out through LASSO regression and Logistic regression analysis to be incorporated into 5 models, namely LR, SVM, Naive Bayes, KNN, and XGBoost. Among these models, the SVM model exhibited the best performance in predicting the risk of social alienation in patients with enterostomy after colorectal cancer surgery.

This model can assist medical staff in early identifying the potential population at risk of social alienation among patients with post-operative enterostomy, thereby reducing the incidence of social alienation and improving the quality of life of patients.This study is a cross-sectional study, which is unable to track the dynamic development of social alienation in patients with post-operative enterostomy after colorectal cancer surgery. In addition, the samples of this study were not selected from multiple hospitals, resulting in limitations in the quantity and representativeness of the samples, and no external validation was performed. In the future, the constructed model will be applied to patients with post-operative enterostomy in other medical institutions or communities, and more external validation data will be collected to improve the generalization ability of the model.

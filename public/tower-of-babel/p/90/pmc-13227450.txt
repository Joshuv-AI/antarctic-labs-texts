Evaluation of Neutrophil-to-Lymphocyte Ratio and Platelet-to-Lymphocyte Ratio as Biomarkers in Missed Abortions

Introduction

Missed abortion, a common complication in early pregnancy, is defined as the intrauterine death of the embryo or fetus without the immediate expulsion of the gestational sac [1]. Diagnosis typically relies on ultrasonographic findings and clinical criteria, though the underlying pathophysiological mechanisms are only partially understood [2]. Growing evidence indicates that systemic inflammation may significantly contribute to early pregnancy failure, leading to the investigation of inflammatory biomarkers as potential diagnostic and prognostic tools [3].

The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) have attracted considerable interest due to their accessibility, cost-effectiveness, and potential to reflect subclinical inflammatory processes [45]. These hematological indices, derived from complete blood count (CBC) parameters, have shown diagnostic relevance in various gynecological and obstetric conditions, including preeclampsia, ectopic pregnancy, and threatened miscarriage [67].

Previous research has emphasized the altered inflammatory environment associated with early pregnancy loss. For example, Yazdizadeh et al. [8] reported significantly elevated NLR and PLR ratios in patients experiencing threatened miscarriage compared to women with healthy pregnancies, suggesting their potential role as supportive diagnostic markers. Similarly, studies by Ahmedy et al. and Jiang et al. indicated that these ratios might reflect the systemic immunological dysregulation often seen in failed pregnancies [910]. Nonetheless, findings across the literature remain inconsistent, and there is insufficient evidence supporting the routine clinical use of these ratios in the evaluation of missed abortion, with some results even contradicting one another [11].

Moreover, while inflammatory indices such as NLR and PLR have been extensively explored in the context of systemic diseases and malignancies [512], their relevance to obstetric populations necessitates context-specific validation. The immunological landscape during early gestation is uniquely modulated to promote fetal tolerance, and any disruptions in this finely balanced state may contribute to spontaneous pregnancy losses. Therefore, investigating easily accessible biomarkers like NLR and PLR within this clinical context presents a valuable opportunity to enhance early diagnostic capabilities and improve patient management.

In light of these considerations, the present study seeks to evaluate the usability and clinical significance of the NLR and PLR in women diagnosed with missed abortion. By comparing these indices with those in uncomplicated early pregnancies, this research aims to elucidate their diagnostic value and potentially offer new insights into the growing body of literature addressing immunological markers in early pregnancy failure.

Material and methods

The study population was divided into two groups: missed abortions and healthy pregnancies. A total of 120 women participated, with 60 diagnosed with missed abortion and 60 with healthy intrauterine pregnancy.

The sample size for the study was determined using G power analysis. There are few studies in the literature regarding the evaluation of NLR/PLR parameters as biomarkers in missed abortions. Therefore, an effect size of 0.5 was selected. The significance level (Alpha) was set at 0.05, while the acceptable level for the second type of error (beta) was set at 0.20. The statistical power of the test was established at 0.80, and the effect size remained at 0.5. Based on these calculations, it was concluded that a total of 60 participants would be sufficient for both the patient and control groups.

This is a retrospective case-control study. Our study is an observational research examining data collected retrospectively. The data collection period spans from January 1, 2023, to December 31, 2024. The research was conducted in the obstetrics and gynecology clinic of a state hospital affiliated with the Ministry of Health in Turkey. Information for the study was gathered from patient file records. Demographic variables such as age, height, weight, previous pregnancies, parity, and the number of living children were obtained retrospectively from the hospital's data system. The control group consisted of healthy pregnant women attending routine antenatal visits. We aimed to include participants with gestational ages similar to those in the missed abortion group to minimize confounding; however, strict one-to-one gestational age matching was not performed, given practical recruitment limitations.

The exclusion criteria consist of multiple pregnancy, molar pregnancy, systemic disease history (diabetes, heart disease, and so on), chronic inflammatory disease (systemic lupus erythematosus, rheumatoid arthritis, familial Mediterranean fever [FMF] and so on), thrombophilia, active malignant disease, diagnosed fetal chromosomal disorder, history of 2 or more miscarriages, smoking, incomplete data, and those who refused to participate in the study.

Examined blood variables are age, white blood cell (WBC), neutrophil, lymphocyte, platelet, monocyte, eosinophil, basophil, mean platelet volume (MPV), neutrophil lymphocyte ratio (NLR), and platelet lymphocyte ratio (PLR).

The ethical approval for this study was obtained from the Ethics Committee. The study was reviewed and approved by the [Adana City and Research Hospital Scientific Research Ethics Committee] Institutional Review Board [05/12/2024 - 261). All stages of the study were conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Statistical analysis

The SPSS 15.0 for Windows statistical package program was used for statistical analyses. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median, categorical variables are presented as numbers and percentages. The conformity of the data to normal distribution and homogeneity of variances were examined using Levene test and Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. If the data were normally distributed and variances were homogeneous, Student t Test was used, otherwise Mann-Whitney U Test was used. The relationship between variables was analyzed with Pearson Correlation Coefficient for data that met the parametric test assumptions, and Spearman Correlation Coefficient for data that did not. Test results will be considered significant if p < 0.05. Hosmer-Lemeshow analysis was conducted to evaluate the fit and calibration of the logistic regression model with the data. This analysis assesses whether the observed event rates align with the expected event rates in subgroups of the model population. A model was developed from the data, revealing statistically significant differences in the analyses and showing multiple collinearities. Logistic regression analysis was conducted to assess the magnitude and direction of the relationships between the variables.

Results

The demographic data from our study are presented in Table 1, which outlines the case and control groups. The youngest participant was 23 years old, while the oldest was 42 years old. There was no significant difference in age between the two groups. Notably, neutrophil and lymphocyte values were found to be twice as high in the control group compared to the case group. In contrast, platelet values were similar in both groups, showing no significant difference. Additionally, monocyte, eosinophil, and basophil values were significantly higher in the control group than in the case group. Overall, a statistically significant difference was observed between the groups for all variables except age and platelet count.

Based on the analysis results, we developed a model that incorporated variables appropriate for logistic regression, considering the distribution differences between groups. Variables such as age and platelet counts were excluded from the model because their distributions were similar in both the case and control groups. To prevent multicollinearity issues, neutrophil, eosinophil, and basophil variables, which showed high correlations with other variables during the correlation analysis, were also removed from the model. Consequently, logistic regression analysis was conducted using the following variables: WBC, lymphocyte, monocyte, MPV, NLR, and PLR. A graphical representation of the variables included in the model can be found in Fig. 1. Examining the figure reveals that MPV, NLR, and PLR serve as variables showing extreme values in both the case and control groups, while monocyte serves as a variable with extreme values in only the case group.

Discussion

The main findings of our study can be summarized as follows: We identified the NLR and the PLR as significant predictors of missed abortion. In contrast, MPV was not found to be statistically valuable. Variables such as age and platelet count were excluded from the analysis due to their homogeneous distribution between the groups. Additionally, a logistic regression model that included white blood cells (WBC), lymphocytes, monocytes, NLR, and PLR produced a Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit score of 0.743, indicating a strong fit for the model. This supports the hypothesis that altered immune and inflammatory responses play a critical role in early pregnancy failure.

This study aimed to assess the clinical utility of the NLR and PLR as inflammatory biomarkers in the context of missed abortion and to determine their predictive value using logistic regression analysis. Our findings indicate that NLR and PLR, in conjunction with other hematological parameters such as WBC count, lymphocyte count, and monocyte count, effectively distinguish between women experiencing missed abortions and those with normal pregnancies. These results align with a growing body of literature that underscores the role of systemic inflammatory markers in the pathophysiology of early pregnancy loss.

By excluding variables like age and platelet count due to their homogeneity across groups, and removing neutrophils, eosinophils, and basophils to avoid multicollinearity, we enhanced the robustness and validity of our model. The model's performance, indicated by a Hosmer and Lemeshow goodness-of-fit statistic of 0.743, confirms the adequacy of the selected predictors, suggesting that we have developed a well-fitting logistic regression model for this clinical condition.

The findings from studies examining the relationship between the NLR, PLR, and miscarriage are inconsistent. Some research indicates that NLR and PLR values are significantly higher in the early pregnancy loss group compared to those who experience a term birth, which aligns with our study's findings [13]. Conversely, Yakiştiran et al. [14]. reported that NLR and PLR levels were lower in cases of miscarriage compared to abortions and term births. Additionally, another study indicated that NLR was a positive predictor while PLR served as a negative predictor for spontaneous miscarriages [15]. Ata et al. observed that the NLR levels were similar in both the case and control groups, concluding that there was no statistically significant difference between them [16]. A study investigating the relationships among healthy pregnant women at risk of experiencing miscarriage, as well as those who have undergone spontaneous miscarriage, found no significant difference in NLR and PLR values between the groups [17]. Similarly, Christoforakiet all [18] reported comparable results, indicating no significant difference in NLR and PLR values between the live birth and miscarriage groups.

There is a limited body of research concerning missed miscarriages. One study indicates that the NLR and PLR are elevated in the missed miscarriage group compared to healthy pregnant women [19]. Similarly, Kim et al. reported comparable findings, suggesting that NLR may serve as a prognostic factor for diagnosing missed miscarriages [20]. These results align with those from our study. Moreover, some research has shown that NLR and PLR values are similar between the missed miscarriage and normal pregnancy groups [21]. The discrepancies in research outcomes may stem from variations in methods and equipment utilized in studies of missed miscarriages. Furthermore, differences in participant numbers and analysis techniques may also contribute to this variability.

In our study, the mean MPV values in the missed miscarriage group were lower than those in the control group. This finding aligns with results reported by Liu et al. [11], Kaplanoglu et al. [22], and Biyik et al. [19]. However, some studies examining the relationship between miscarriage and MPV have found no statistically significant differences between the miscarriage and control groups [23]. This contradiction may be because the study was a meta-analysis, and there was clinical heterogeneity among the studies examined. Only specific populations were examined in the meta-analysis. One of the populations examined was similar to the population of our study, while the other was different. This may be another reason why the results contradict our study's results. The varying designs of these studies may have introduced confounding factors, which could explain the differing findings. Given the differences in pathophysiology between miscarriage and missed miscarriage, further research on this topic is clearly needed.

Moreover, the inclusion of WBC, lymphocyte, and monocyte counts in our final model, along with their statistical significance in predicting missed abortion, supports previous research highlighting that variations in leukocyte subpopulations can signify an underlying inflammatory state. For example, Humadi et al. [24] found that altered lymphocyte levels were associated with early pregnancy complications, underscoring their diagnostic importance.

While some studies found elevated NLR and PLR values in miscarriage cases—aligning with our finding that both parameters were significant predictors of missed abortion—other studies observed no differences or even reported inverse relationships. We now specify that these discrepancies may stem from differences in study protocols, participant selection, and timing of biomarker measurement. Gestational age, maternal health conditions, and inclusion of various miscarriage subtypes (missed, spontaneous, elective) likely modulate immune and inflammatory responses, leading to inconsistent biomarker profiles. Additionally, laboratory techniques and thresholds for NLR and PLR lack standardization, which may further explain conflicting results.

In our analysis, the significant predictive value of NLR and PLR in our logistic regression model (Hosmer-Lemeshow score: 0.743) supports the theory that abnormal immune and inflammatory responses drive early pregnancy failure, particularly missed abortion. In contrast, MPV, which lacked statistical significance in our study despite inconsistent literature reports, may be less dependable due to its sensitivity to pre-analytical factors, such as anticoagulant use and timing of measurement.

Study limitation

Despite the clinically relevant insights derived from this investigation, several limitations must be acknowledged. One of the limitations of our study is that it is a single-center study and the sample size is limited. Single-center studies may be affected by factors such as the characteristics of the participants, medical level, and specific testing methods. Therefore, it may not accurately reflect the situation of a larger population. The modest cohort size (n = 120) limits the generalizability of the findings and reduces the statistical power to detect smaller effect sizes or subtle differences between groups. The inherent limitations of retrospective analyses, including potential biases in data recording, unmeasured confounding variables, and the inability to establish causal relationships, weaken the interpretative strength of the outcomes. This study was conducted at a single healthcare institution, which may limit its external validity. The institutional protocols and patient demographics may not accurately represent broader populations. Biomarkers such as the NLR and the PLR were assessed at only one time point. Dynamic or longitudinal measurements could provide more accurate predictive modeling and reflect the progression of the underlying pathology. Although major comorbidities were likely controlled for, unrecorded or subclinical inflammatory conditions could have influenced hematological parameters, introducing residual confounding. The study did not incorporate progesterone levels, trends in β-hCG, or sonographic data, which could have helped refine the predictive model and provided a more comprehensive diagnostic approach. Only basic complete blood derived indices were included; the omission of other inflammatory markers such as IL-6, or TNF-α may have limited the study's diagnostic depth.

In the present study, our main aim was to investigate whether NLR and PLR are significantly associated with missed abortions, suggesting their potential role as easily accessible biomarkers. We did not aim to establish threshold values or perform ROC curve analyses, mainly due to the limited sample size and retrospective design. Deriving cut-off values from our dataset might not yield reliable or generalizable results for clinical practice. Instead, our findings offer preliminary evidence of the predictive potential of NLR and PLR. Larger, prospective, multicenter studies are needed to confirm these associations and determine robust cut-off values for clinical application.

One limitation of our study is the lack of strict gestational age matching in the control group, which could have influenced results through variation in hematological parameters. Future prospective studies matching gestational age are needed to confirm our findings.

It is recommended that the sample size be increased, multicenter studies be conducted, and the extrapolation of the study results be improved in new studies to be planned.

Prospective study designs can be considered in future research to better control confounding factors and clarify causal relationships.

Subsequent research should increase detection time points and conduct longitudinal studies.

It is suggested to include more relevant indicators in subsequent research and construct more comprehensive prediction models.

In conclusion, our study reinforces the hypothesis that inflammatory markers such as the NLR and PLR could serve as accessible and cost-effective indicators of early pregnancy loss. Given their predictive value in logistic regression models, these parameters may be integrated into clinical practice for early risk stratification in suspected cases of missed abortion. However, further multicenter and longitudinal studies with larger cohorts are necessary to validate our findings and establish clinically relevant thresholds for these biomarkers in obstetric care.

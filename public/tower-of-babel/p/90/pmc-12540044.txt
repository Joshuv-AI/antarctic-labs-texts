Pedagogy experiments for Ayush academia
Authors: Girish Tillu
Published: 2025

Indian philosopher, scholar, and statesman Dr Sarvapalli Radhakrishnan's birthday is celebrated as Teacher's Day (5 September). Dr Radhakrishnan played an important role in shaping modern Indian education. He introduced Indian philosophy to the Western world. Teacher's Day is an opportunity to introspect and remind ourselves the legacy of great teachers. Indian history was shaped by great teachers. Vishnu Sharma (3rd century BCE) was an exceptional teacher, who introduced Indian wisdom to the world. He not only introduced student centric learning but presented a brilliant example of ancient Indian pedagogy through his work, Panchatantra. His work is a great pedagogy lesson for changing times. His storytelling taught how deeper philosophical insights could be conveyed through an edutainment approach. Vishnu Sharma and Radhakrishnan's teachings are important especially when education reforms in Ayush sector are in process. The Ayush sector is at a unique crossroads when the world is ready to embrace Ayush for its holistic, sustainable, and personalized health approaches. This is the time we need to energise its academia. We need to build institutional capabilities to integrate, innovate, and communicate effectively with stakeholders of modern healthcare. Although policy reforms have provided many opportunities, the Ayush academia need to rise to address several expectations [1].

Ayush and National Education Policy

The National Educational Policy (NEP) document and the establishment of National Commission for Indian System of Medicine (NCISM) are important initiatives of the year 2020. Both led to a series of educational reforms in last five years, however there is an urgent need for Ayush education to get connected with NEP. NCISM aims aims to ensure transparency, accountability, and high-quality standards in AYUSH education and practice. It has implemented revised curricula for Ayurveda, Unani, and Siddha education. Another important initiative is research orientation for teachers and students.

The National Education Policy 2020 has made Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS) one of the pillars of curriculum design. IKS and interdisciplinary learning are opportunities to blend classical Ayurveda and Yoga with contemporary clinical and biomedical knowledge. Ayush colleges need to leverage this opportunity for bridging with local academic institutions. Such collaborations will be mutually beneficial. Ayush colleges will not only get access to laboratories and academic infrastructure, but most important is connect with young students of various knowledge disciplines. For decades, Ayurveda education has prioritized the rote memorization (Patha) of verses from classics over the deep comprehension (Avabodha) and rational application (Yukti) of their principles. The new paradigm must be integration when the meanings of verses will also be exercised (Anushthan) into teaching, practice, and research. This will be mainstreaming of Ayush in academic sector. A dialogue of Ayush academia with other disciplines is very crucial. We need several experiments for strengthening the dialogue. A few recent initiatives are worth mentioning in this context.

Innovative initiatives

The NCISM in collaboration with Indian Institute of Technology, Jodhpur (IITJ) conducted five workshops that involved 130 post-graduate students. The series of workshops helped bridge the gap between technology and Ayurveda for development of precision health and integrative medicine. Ayurveda students were trained in artificial intelligence, digital health, and multiomics technologies for development of evidence-based Ayurveda solutions. It was a series of workshops and hands-on training at IITJ conceptualized by senior scientist Prof. Mitali Mukerji. It was a unique 'Ayurthinkathon' where students were supposed to come with an Ayurveda concept for which they need a technology either as assistance to explain the principles of Ayurveda or to innovate any new method or module for Ayurveda treatment. The workshop enabled them to better understand what technology can offer for Ayurveda and after five days of thinking and ideation, they had to give group presentation on 5th day. The workshop resulted in a cohort of postgraduate students willing to get enabled with Ayurtech. They will be intrumental in developing an interface between traditions and technology. Such workshops indeed prove to be great avenue of capacity building of Ayush sector.

Along with ongoing academic programs, the Maharashtra University of Health Sciences (MUHS) is implementing 'Summer Internship Program' (SIP) for students of Ayurveda, medicine, and paramedical sciences. Under this program, they get opportunity to work in research institutions during summer break. This program provides experience of working on real life research projects also enables interaction between students of Ayush and biomedical sciences. It provides exposure for undergraduate students to interact with scientists working in health or biomedical research. This internship not only provides motivation but also directs their research career. The program was conceptualized by Dr. Madhuri Kanitkar, a visionary academician and Vice Chancellor of MUHS. The SIP has trained more than 1100 students through a network of 100 institutions. The exposure at student level will generate open-minded integrative medicine practitioners.

Another experiment is ongoing at the Ayush Centre of Excellence (Ayush CoE), Department of Health Sciences at Savitribai Phule Pune University (SPPU). The ‘Sankalp Internship Program’ provides a dynamic and open learning platform for science enthusiasts. This transdisciplinary interactive program has largely been experimenting to make complex science digestible to the younger students. The program includes students from diverse backgrounds to bring together on a single platform to ensure skill-based learning and enhance practical knowledge. Sankap program had interns from statistics, pharmacy, basic sciences, Ayurveda, bioinformatics, Sanskrit and various humanities and biomedical sciences. Even the students of 10th and 12th standard get an interesting exposure to the functioning of a research group. The researcher team at SPPU help channelizing every intern's journey towards finding their specific research focus. The internship includes innovative capacity building activities enhancing the team spirit, project management and scientific acumen. The learnings are executed through hands-on experiments in real life projects, debates, interactive science games, quizzes, lab meetings, presentations, etc. The internship program is ongoing for the last three years and has provided opportunities to more than 100 interns, including school students. The program also created a network of experts who are contributing to Ayurveda and interdisciplinary research.

‘Health, Illness and Healing’ is a recent initiative coordinated by an Ayurveda teacher, Prof. Kishor Patwardhan from Banaras Hindu University. This brings Ayurveda and contemporary medical sciences into a dialogue. It explores how health was understood, how illness was experienced, and how healing was practiced through reconstructions of imagined clinical cases and other material that might have been available to ancient physicians such as Charaka and Sushruta. It uses the reasoning and tools accessible to them to examine how their theories might have been formed. These reconstructions are not historical reproductions but imaginative exercises framed through dialogic and reflective pedagogy that connects classical insights with today's clinical contexts. The aim is to address enduring questions while fostering inquiry and critical thinking. The initiative is distinctive for its interdisciplinary collaboration across medicine, Ayurveda, the humanities, social sciences, and the arts. It advances a plural and reflexive pedagogy that emphasizes contextual understanding and the re-examination of medical knowledge across time (Further details are available at www.clinicaltheory.org).

Ayush academia is indeed evolving with an inclusive approach. The NCISM revised curricula have defined various teaching, learning and evaluation activities. It has certainly created a pull by implementing teachers’ training and institutional accreditation process. However, the change in mindset and transforming the environment of academic institutions is very crucial. The NEP has already sensitized mainstream education sector, now Ayush academia should act.

We are aware of various innovative activities ongoing in Ayush colleges. We need to share our learning and experiences. The Journal of Ayurveda and Integrative Medicine welcomes thoughts and the experiments. The journal has a section 'Initiatives' that publishes experiments and activities to advance Ayurveda or Integrative Medicine.

Now is the time to make the best use of changing educational scenario in India and abroad. There should be more motivation than monitoring and more creativity than criticism. This is the way to develop scholarship and continue the legacy of great teachers. We need Vishnu Sharma and Radhakrishan of modern times!

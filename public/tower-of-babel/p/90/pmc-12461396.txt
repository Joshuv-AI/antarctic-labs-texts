Synthesis of Nitrogen-Doped Carbon Dots and Their Effects on Crop Seed Germination and Seedling Growth
Authors: Zhihua Xu, Xuejing Wang, Yu Ma, Zhihan Niu, Jiaqi Chen, Min Zhang, Zhenghong Xu, Tongtong Zhang, Feng Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Nanomaterials are helping to transform agricultural production, playing a crucial role in achieving sustainable development, increasing crop yields, and addressing the safety of fertilizer use. The advantages of carbon dots (CDs) in the application of new agriculture are apparent. This study combined the advantages of CDs to explore the positive effects of CDs doped with essential elements on plant growth. In this regard, for the first time, we prepared nitrogen-doped CDs (N-CDs) with blue fluorescence emission by a microwave reaction with succinic acid using m-phenylenediamine (MPD) as the nitrogen source, and studied their effects on crop growth and development. The morphology, structure, and fluorescence properties of the N-CDs were analyzed using various characterization methods. N-CDs exhibited good stability with an average particle size of 2.20 nm. The optimal emission wavelength was 360 nm, the maximum fluorescence emission peak was 452 nm, and the absolute quantum yield (QY) was 35.60%. The results of the effect of N-CDs on crop growth and development showed that the highest germination rate (86.67%, 68.33%), vigor index (127.76%, 15.31%), and biomass in maize and tomato were observed after treatment with 0.010 mg/mL N-CDs. With increasing N-CDs concentration, the indices first increased and then decreased, with significant growth inhibition observed at excessively high concentrations. Therefore, appropriate concentrations of N-CDs can enhance the development of crop seedlings. This study lays the foundation for the nanofertilization applications of CDs, which could be further doped with potassium, phosphorus, and other important plant nutrients.

Introduction

With continuous innovation in science and technology, food production worldwide has been increasing. However, nearly 1 billion people still suffer from food crises, and increasing food production remains a global issue that needs to be addressed. A large number of pesticides and chemical fertilizers are used in agricultural production. Although pesticides and chemical fertilizers have contributed significantly to increasing food production, their long-term use has resulted in air and water pollution and damage to soil structure and biodiversity. Genetically modified crops have been developed to address food shortages; however, the application of this technology is controversial, and consumer acceptance of these crops remains low. Therefore, the development of green and safe methods to promote plant growth and development has become a popular research aim.

Nanomaterials are widely used in biology, medicine, and sensing owing to their unique physical and chemical properties. − In addition, nanomaterials are increasingly crucial for addressing the agricultural challenges posed by pesticide and fertilizer use, enhancing safety, sustainability, and crop yield. , The application of metal-based nanomaterials in agriculture has been the most extensively studied, which has verified the positive role of nanomaterials in promoting plant growth and development. With the advancement of research, researchers have come to realize that commonly used metal-based nanomaterials have significant flaws. Their inherent insolubility and small specific surface area result in low utilization rates for promoting plant growth and development. And, as a result of high toxicity, low the pervasive nature of biocompatibility of actual application of agricultural production and sustainable development difficulties. Numerous studies have proven that newly discovered carbon-based nanomaterials have lower toxicity and higher biocompatibility than metal-based nanomaterials, and their application in agriculture is becoming a research hotspot. Carbon-based nanomaterials exhibit lower toxicity and higher biocompatibility than metal-based nanomaterials. CDs are a class of quasi-spherical and carbon-based luminescent nanoparticles with sizes less than 10 nm, carbon-skeleton structures, and abundant functional groups. CDs are extensively researched due to their numerous advantages, including a wide range of carbon sources and a simple, inexpensive synthesis method that is easily scalable for industrial production. Moreover, the low toxicity, high biocompatibility, tunable fluorescence, and good water solubility of CDs enable various applications. The advantages of its application in agriculture are gradually emerging. Qu et al. used mung beans as the material to conduct the biocompatibility test of CDs. They proved that CDs played a role in the biomass accumulation of mung beans, improvement of root vitality, and Rubisco enzyme activity. Liang et al. used pea seeds as a model to characterize the surface morphology changes of seed coat treated with CDs, revealing the prominent characteristics of CDs on seed germination. In terms of promoting plant growth, the interaction between CDs and plants may stimulate multiple metabolic pathways through experimental verification, thus enhancing the antioxidant enzyme activity of plants and significantly improving root growth, photosynthesis, and biomass accumulation of seeds. It is worth mentioning that compared to metal-based nanomaterials, using CDs for seed nanotriggering compensates for the problem of excessive nanomaterials left in the environment. Subsequently, studies on the impact of CDs on crop growth and development were conducted. , The application of CDs prepared from urea to mung beans resulted in higher yields than the direct application of urea, confirming the potential of CDs as nanofertilizers. In addition to mung beans, researchers have used crop species such as microalgae, maize, and cucumber as model systems to explore the impact of CDs on crop growth. , The excellent characteristics of CDs have been demonstrated in various physiological processes in plants, such as seed germination, growth and development, and photosynthesis. , In summary, CDs play a unique role in improving agricultural production applications, and their impact on promoting seed germination and plant growth deserves further research.

A review of previous research highlights the unique role of CDs in significantly improving crop seed germination and seedling growth. However, there has been little further exploration of the application of CDs doped with essential elements for plant growth in agriculture. It is important to note that nitrogen is one of the three fundamental elements for improving crop growth, development, and yield and is involved in the entire life cycle of plants. Previous studies have indicated that CDs can significantly improve the germination rate of crop seeds and seedling growth. Existing studies have reported that N-CDs have better biocompatibility, stronger stability, and lower toxicity, making their application in agriculture safer and more environmentally friendly. Therefore, we considered combining the advantages of CDs to further study CDs doped with essential elements for plants and investigated the effects of N-CDs on plant growth and development. In addition, the rich nitrogen-containing functional groups on the surface of N-CDs can also serve as additional nitrogen sources for the transport of specific nutrients to more comprehensively supplement the potential of CDs in agricultural production. Moreover, maize and tomato are important cash crops in Xinjiang, so it is helpful to discuss the effects of N-CDs on their growth. This has a positive impact on improving crop yield and quality, enhancing economic benefits, and protecting the ecological environment in Xinjiang. Hence, it is crucial to investigate the effects of N-CDs on plant growth and development.

In this study, for the first time, N-CDs with blue fluorescence and high fluorescence quantum yield (QY) were prepared by a microwave reaction with succinic acid using m-phenylenediamine as a nitrogen source. The properties of the N-CDs were characterized using transmission electron microscopy (TEM), fluorescence spectroscopy, and X-ray diffraction (XPS). It was applied to agricultural production to study the effects of different concentrations of N-CDs on seed germination and early growth of maize and tomato. The research results can enrich the research content of CDs in agricultural applications and also provide new ideas and methods for developing innovative, efficient, and environmentally friendly nanoagricultural technologies.

Results and Discussion

Synthesis and Characterization of N-CDs

Blue fluorescence-emitting N-CDs were obtained from succinic acid and MPD using a microwave-assisted method. Furthermore, after centrifugation, filtration, and dialysis, the purified N-CDs powder was obtained via vacuum freeze-drying.

In this study, we analyzed the synthesized N-CDs by observing their solutions, UV–vis absorption characteristics, and fluorescent emission behaviors. Under natural light, the prepared N-CDs solutions were pale yellow and displayed blue fluorescence when exposed to UV–vis illumination (Figure A Illustration). UV–vis absorption spectra can be used to preliminarily analyze the composition and structure of the measured substances. The UV–vis absorption spectrum of the N-CDs solution (Figure A) shows absorption peaks attributed to polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) in the range of 200–320 nm. The distinctive absorption peak observed at 290 nm (curve a) is the result of n–π* transitions within the carboxyl group. The illustration in the lower right corner of Figure A shows a weak UV–vis absorption peak near 360 nm (curve d). Its appearance belongs to the nonbonding orbital n–π* orbital, which is usually the electron transition of –CN- and –NH2, indicating the successful preparation of N-doped N-CDs. Then, the optimal excitation and emission wavelengths of the N-CDs were evaluated using fluorescence spectroscopy. Under excitation at 360 nm (curve b), the maximum fluorescence emission peak was observed at 452 nm (curve c). In addition, we observed that the optimal emission peak of the N-CDs did not shift with a change in the excitation wavelength (Figure B). This characterization result indicated that the prepared N-CDs had a uniformly dispersed and good morphology, and the particle size of N-CDs did not affect their fluorescence emission. QY is used as a parameter for measuring fluorescence intensity and is of great importance for characterizing CDs. Currently, the QY of common CDs ranges from 5 to 20%, and improving the QY of CDs remains a challenge in CD preparation. The QY of the N-CDs synthesized in this study was 35.65%, which is high compared to those measured in previous studies. Nitrogen-doped N-CDs introduce wealthy nitrogen-containing functional groups, such as amino groups, which reduce surface defects and enhance the surface passivation effect, significantly increasing the QY of N-CDs. Moreover, the small particle size of the N-CDs makes the quantum limiting effect more significant. Quantum confinement effects can enhance the probability of electron transitions, thereby increasing the QY. The QY can also be improved by adjusting the bandgap energy to optimize the probability of electron transition. These mechanisms work together to ensure a high QY for N-CDs. Furthermore, the fluorescence characteristics of the N-CDs were consistent with the results reported in previous studies. The doping of N enhances the emission intensity of the blue fluorescence and also improves the quantum yield of the CDs.

TEM observations revealed that the N-CDs possessed a spherical structure and were well dispersed (Figure C). Further characterization by HRTEM showed an obvious interplanar distance of 0.21 nm (Figure C Illustration), matching the typical morphology of CDs with graphene (100) crystal planes. The particle size statistics for the N-CDs provided an average of 2.20 nm (Figure D), which is similar to the size of blue-fluorescent CDs prepared in other studies. The zeta potential of N-CDs was then analyzed, and the zeta potential was approximately 18.2 mV (Figure S1). The results show that the positive potential of N-CDs can be attributed to the rich amino and hydroxyl groups on their surface. In addition, a diffraction peak was observed at 2θ = 20.23° in the XRD pattern of N-CDs, which is consistent with the (002) crystal face of graphitic carbon (Figure A).

Structural Characteristics of N-CDs. (A) XRD spectrum of N-CDs; (B) FT-IR spectrum of N-CDs; (C) XPS spectrum of N-CDs; (D–F) High-resolution deconvoluted spectra of N-CDs: (D) C 1s, (E) N 1s, (F) O 1s.

The composition and surface chemical groups of the N-CDs were analyzed using XPS and FT-IR. FT-IR spectral analysis shows that the absorption peaks of N-CDs (Figure B) at 3450 and 3363 cm–1 are related to the O–H and N–H stretching vibrations, respectively. The characteristic peak of CO is at 1697 cm–1, while the absorption peaks at 1500 and 1606 cm–1 are the result of CC/CN stretching vibrations, respectively, and the stretching vibration of C–OH is caused by the peak at 992 cm–1. The N-CDs characterization results were consistent with those obtained in a previous study. The high-resolution XPS C 1s and O 1s peaks indicate that the synthesized material contains carbon and oxygen, respectively. By deconvolving the XPS spectra of each element, we determined the surface groups of the N-CDs, as well as common groups including C–C, CC, and CO. The XPS spectra depicted in Figure C indicate that the synthesized N-CDs contained three primary elements: oxygen (O), nitrogen (N), and carbon (C), with relative contents of 16.35%, 12.41%, and 71.25%, respectively. The C 1s, N 1s, and O 1s peaks were deconvoluted to further explore the surface functional groups of the N-CDs. Three peaks were deconvoluted and fitted from the C 1s peak (Figure D): CO/CN (288.3 eV), C–O/C–N (285.25 eV), and C–C/CC (284.5 eV). The deconvolution of the N 1s (Figure E) gave peaks corresponding to CN (398.9 eV, C–N–C (399.8 eV), and N–H (400.4 eV). Deconvolution of the O 1s peaks resulted in two peaks (Figure F): CO (532 eV) and C–O (531.2 eV). The XPS results obtained from the above analysis were consistent with the FT-IR results, confirming the presence of various functional groups in N-CDs. The functional groups and crystal structures of the N-CDs in this study were similar to those of N-CDs synthesized in a previous study. , In addition, successful doping of N was proven, which provides a basis for the application of N-CDs in crop seedling.

The plant system is relatively complex, and while studying the positive effect of N-CDs on it, we should also consider its stability to ensure its practicability. The fluorescence intensity of N-CDs at different temperatures, pH values, cationic solutions, and storage periods was tested to verify their stability. The effect of pH on N-CDs was investigated (Figure A), and it was found that they had good stability under a wide range of acid–base conditions. Even at pH 3 and 8.5, the fluorescence intensity did not decrease significantly. However, the fluorescence intensity surface of N-CDs, they interact at low and high pH, and vibrational coupling causes energy level changes and affects the fluorescence intensity. Generally, protonation occurs under acidic conditions and results in high fluorescence intensity. Deprotonation occurs under alkaline conditions, resulting in fluorescence aggregation quenching. The stability of N-CDs in the presence of different cations was further investigated. According to the characterization results (Figure B), N-CDs were not significantly affected, and the overall fluorescence intensity was good without significant attenuation. This indicates that the surface functional groups of the N-CDs did not undergo ionization. Under different temperature conditions (Figure C), the N-CDs exhibited excellent stability within the range of 50 °C, and their fluorescence intensity began to decrease beyond this temperature. This may be due to temperature-induced changes in the surface functional groups of N-CDs, resulting in particle aggregation and partial quenching of fluorescence. Moreover, N-CDs remained uniformly resuspended within 60 d of storage, and there was no significant difference in fluorescence intensity (Figure D). The N-CDs prepared in this study were subsequently applied to promote the growth of crops, and the test results of pH, temperature, and different cations were in a good stability range. We believe that N-CDs can adapt to the complex environment inside plants and the temperature required for crop growth.

Effect of N-CDs on Seed Germination

The most critical period in plant growth is seed germination, which begins with water uptake and ends with the extension of the hypocotyl from the seed coat. Thus, we studied the germination of maize and tomato seeds after soaking them in different concentrations of N-CDs for a period of 7 days. Subsequently, the germination rate (GR) and germination potential (GP) were calculated by observing the germination of the crop seeds.

In the sprouting trial of maize and tomato seeds treated with N-CDs (Figure ), GP and GR in the experimental group significantly differed from those in CK2. This indicates that the differences in maize seed germination characteristics were likely due to the presence of N-CDs. Moreover, we found that the germination of seeds in CK2 was not very good, which may be due to the high concentration of MPD causing excessive nitrogen content in the soaking solution, leading to seedling burning. The GR of maize (86.67%) was slightly higher than that of CK1 at an N-CDs concentration of 0.010 mg/mL, although the difference was not statistically significant (Figure A). When the concentration of N-CDs exceeded 0.050 mg/mL, the GR of the seeds decreased. The GP was slightly higher than CK1 in maize seed germination, and with the increase in N-CDs concentration, the overall concentration began to show a flat to lower than CK1. In the germination experiment of tomato seeds treated with N-CDs ((Figure B), the GP and GR of T1 were significantly higher than those of CK2, with the GR being roughly 2–3 times greater than that of CK2. However, with an increase in N-CDs concentration in tomato seed germination, GP was consistently lower than that of CK1, showing a gradually decreasing trend. The GR of tomatoes peaked at a concentration of 0.010 mg/mL N-CDs, but the difference compared to CK1 was not statistically significant. With the progressive increase in N-CDs concentration, the GR of the tomato seeds began to decrease. The results of the germination experiments indicated that the prepared N-CDs, when present at concentrations less than 0.05 mg/mL, had a notably positive influence on the germination of tomato seeds. As the concentration increased, the GR of the crop seeds tended to increase and then decrease; generally, low concentrations promoted germination, whereas high concentrations inhibited germination and seedling growth. These experimental results are consistent with those reported previously. For example, a study of CDs obtained from high-temperature carbonized peanut bran showed that the fresh weight of Italian lettuce cultured under an appropriate concentration of a CD solution was 24.30% higher than that of the control group, indicating a dose-dependent effect. Furthermore, a low concentration of graphene oxide-CDs promoted the germination of mung beans, but a high concentration had a significant inhibitory effect. Finally, low concentrations of green-synthesized spirulina-based CDs also promoted the germination of lentil seeds, whereas high concentrations inhibited germination.

The germination index (GI) is used to evaluate the vigor index (VI) of seeds and judge their activity. Seed VI is the sum of the seed GR, seedling emergence rate, seedling growth, plant stress resistance, and productive potential. Both the GI and VI are important indicators of seed quality. Tables and display the GI and VI values of maize and tomato seeds treated with various N-CDs concentrations, further exploring the germination of the seeds. The application of N-CDs promoted seed germination, as evidenced by the considerably increased GI and VI values of both treated seed groups compared to the CK2 group. The GI of the maize seeds was the highest for an N-CDs concentration of 0.010 mg/mL, surpassing CK1 by 20.70%. Similarly, the VI values of the maize and tomato seeds were the highest for an N-CDs concentration of 0.010 mg/mL, which were 51.90% and 15.02% higher than that of the blank group CK1, respectively. As the concentration increased, the promotional effect of the N-CDs on seed germination gradually weakened.

Based on the above research results, it has been demonstrated that N-CDs have a promoting effect on seed germination. Seed germination is a physiological reaction involving water uptake and an increase in volume. During this process, the nutrients required by the seeds are mainly derived from the endosperm. Thus, seeds can usually germinate as long as they are provided with appropriate germination conditions, such as suitable temperature and humidity. The influence of N-CDs on seed germination may be connected to their small particle size, large specific surface area, and abundance of hydrophilic groups on their surface. N-CDs penetrate the seeds and accumulate rapidly in the cotyledons, where they adsorb and store water and nutrients. When the seeds germinate, the adsorbed water and nutrients can be released slowly, providing the necessary growth conditions for the seeds. Then, the functional groups on the surface of N-CDs may interact with biomolecules within the seeds, regulate the metabolic pathways within the seeds, and further promote the growth and development of the seeds. The nitrogen element in N-CDs can be directly absorbed and utilized by seeds as a nutrient. Nitrogen is one of the essential elements necessary for plant growth. It can promote the synthesis of biological biomolecules in plants, thereby accelerating seed germination and seedling growth. In addition to their hydrophilicity and improved water absorption capacity, some CDs exhibit a photothermal effect. This provide seeds with more suitable temperature stimulation during seed germination. Moreover, the charge on its surface may play a specific adsorption role in water, allowing more factors to promote the breaking of seed dormancy and the improvement of the seed germination rate.

Influence of N-CDs on the Growth of Maize and Tomato Seedlings

The Growth of Maize Seedling

The effects of different N-CDs concentrations on plant seedling growth were also studied. The growth of maize seedlings was observed after 7 days in the solution containing different concentrations of N-CDs. The results showed almost no germination in the CK2 group. The CK1 and experimental groups grew well. As the concentration of N-CDs increased, seedling growth was inhibited in the experimental group compared to that in CK1 (Figure A). Maize seed root growth was also observed (Figure B). In addition to shoot growth, root growth was also inhibited when the concentration of N-CDs exceeded the ideal level. The experimental results for CK1, CK2, and T1-T5 were analyzed by one-way ANOVA, with the outcomes represented in Figure C–E. Compared with CK1, there were significant differences in the shoot length, root length, and biomass of maize seedlings after treatment with specific concentrations of N-CDs. N-CDs have a positive effect on the growth and biomass accumulation of maize seedlings. All indexes in the experimental group were markedly exceeded those for group CK2. The shoot length (6.99 cm), root length (19.55 cm), and biomass of maize seedlings were the highest when the seedlings were treated with 0.010 mg/mL of N-CDs. According to the experimental results in Table , the root tolerance index (RTI) reached the maximum value of 120.10% at a concentration of 0.050 mg/mL. At 0.010 mg/mL, the relative seedling height (RSH) reached a maximum of 125.91%. With the increase of N-CDs concentration, the differences between various indicators of the experimental group and CK1 gradually narrowed and showed a downward trend.

The Growth of Tomato Seedling

The growth of tomato seedlings was observed in the same manner as that of the maize seeds. As shown in Figure A,B, the growth conditions were similar to those of the maize seedlings, but the overall growth condition of tomatoes is not very good. With an increase in the N-CDs concentration, the growth of underground roots and shoots in the experimental group was inhibited. The experimental results for CK1, CK2, and T1-T5 were analyzed using one-way ANOVA (Figure C–E). A marked disparity was observed between the group T1 and CK2, indicating that N-CDs rather than the precursor solution caused changes in tomato seedling growth indicators. The growth indices of the tomato seedlings were the highest when treated with 0.01 mg/mL N-CDs and significantly higher than those of CK1. Root length, seedling fresh weight, root fresh weight, seedling dry weight, and root dry weight increased by 26.15%, 34.70%, 4.05%, 35.70%, 33.3%, and 66.60%, respectively, compared with the CK1. The RSH (126.52%) and RTI (135.86%) of tomato seedlings were the highest when treated with 0.01 mg/mL N-CDs (Table ). These consequences indicate that low concentrations (<0.010 mg/mL) of N-CDs can effectively promote the growth of tomato seedlings. In this study, the growth of maize and tomato seedlings treated with N-CDs was promoted at low concentrations but inhibited at high concentrations. This growth inhibition phenomenon may be due to the toxic effects of nanoparticle aggregation at excess amounts. The findings suggest that different crop seedlings have different optimal concentrations of N-CDs treatment. The impacts of varying N-CDs concentrations on the growth of tomato and maize seedlings were investigated to explore potential mechanisms. Successful seed germination is accompanied by the appearance of roots, where N-CDs adsorb onto the surface and penetrate root vascular bundles. Furthermore, researchers have speculated that their surface modifications determine the distribution form of N-CDs in plants. Carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen are the primary constituent elements of CDs and are essential components of living organisms. The elements required for plant growth include carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, and phosphorus. The N-CDs prepared are rich in N and contain numerous hydrophilic groups on their surfaces. The experimental results obtained from the optimum N-CDs treatment (0.010 mg/mL concentration) showed that the root and seedling water content of the maize and tomato seedlings was higher than those of CK1. The results indicated that N-CD was important in enhancing the GR of crop seeds and promoting seedling growth. Some studies have shown that nanomaterials can enhance seed water absorption and accelerate germination. When the seeds have finished germination, N-CDs may provide critical nutrients during seedling growth. Su et al. also concluded that the presence of hydrophilic groups on the CDs surface can preserve the water and nutrients required by peanuts in large quantities and for a long time. In particular, it can be made from the roots to the xylem of the slow release of moisture and nutrients to ensure a continuous supply of nutrients. The beneficial mechanism of CDs on plants can be attributed to the intermolecular interaction between their surface hydrophilic groups and minerals so that CDs can be easily absorbed through the plant biofilm system. The small particle size of CDs facilitates their constituent elements entering the plant body, thus accelerating the growth of crop seedlings. Moreover, the surface amino groups of N-CDs can form strong ionic bonds with the carboxyl groups of pectin in plant cell walls. In contrast, the surface hydroxyl groups can form hydrogen bonds with components such as cellulose and lignin. The interaction of these functional groups with plant tissues enhanced the adsorption and storage of N-CDs in plant tissues. Combined with the FT-IR and XPS characterization data of N-CDs, the mechanism of N-CDs promoting crop growth and development was proved.

Transport and Distribution of N-CDs in Seedlings

Using maize seedlings as the observation model, analyze the distribution of N-CDs within the crop (Figure ) and explore the mechanism of its effect on promoting crop growth. The fluorescence images show that the control group had a small amount of autofluorescent background, but the seedlings treated with N-CDs observed stronger fluorescence signals. Moreover, N-CDs’ fluorescence emission is predominantly observed in the root tissues, with comparatively lower signals detected in the stems and leaves. This is because the plant roots first take up the N-CDs, enter the plant body, and are then transported to the stems and leaves. , The confocal images also show that the N-CDs penetrated the cell walls and entered the vascular bundle system. Fluorescent signals have been observed in the inter- and intracellular regions of plant roots and stems. As reported in previous studies, N-CDs may be taken up by plant roots via the apoplast and symplast pathways. Additionally, given that water transport in plants involves both the apoplast and cellular pathways, N-CDs can be taken up by plant roots and transported with water to the aboveground parts under the influence of root pressure and transpiration pull. , The distribution of N-CDs in different tissues also indicates that they can provide continuous support for the stable growth of plants.

Conclusions

In this study, an optimum synthesis process for N-CDs using succinic acid as a precursor was proposed, and the prepared N-CDs exhibited high quantum yield and stability. The safety of N-CDs was validated through MTT toxicity testing. The superior characteristics of CDs have a positive effect on promoting plant growth. Then, for the first time, Xinjiang cash crops maize and tomato were used as the research objects of N-CDs application, and the effects of N-CDs on crop seedling cultivation were analyzed from seed germination and seedling growth. The abundant hydrophilic groups on the surface of N-CDs promote the absorption of water by crops, resulting in better germination and growth. The appropriate concentration of N-CDs has a significant promoting effect on crop seed germination, biomass accumulation, and seedling growth. The experimental results demonstrate that N-CDs, with adjustable properties, simple preparation, and high-cost performance, can replace traditional chemical fertilizers, thereby reducing the dependence on nonrenewable resources. This improves the stability and sustainability of agricultural ecosystems. N-CDs can enhance the research content of CDs in agricultural applications and will also provide new ideas and methods for developing innovative, efficient, and environmentally friendly nanoagricultural technologies. The use of CDs is more in line with the development concept of green agriculture, which promotes the coordinated development of agriculture and the environment.

Although N-CDs have shown broad application prospects in the field of agricultural sustainable agricultural materials, there are still many problems that need further study and solution. Research on the application of N-CDs in agricultural production is in its initial macroscopic exploration. In the future, specific analysis and research can be further conducted further to measure reactive oxygen species levels, nutrient uptake efficiency, or stress-responsive genes. Currently, the application of N-CDs in agriculture is primarily limited to laboratory research. Its effect and feasibility in large-scale field applications still need to be further verified, especially in terms of adaptability across different regions and planting patterns. Moreover, for environmental impacts, long-term studies on the biodegradation of N-CDs, bioaccumulation risks, and interactions with soil microorganisms are still a necessary research direction. In addition, this study primarily involves the doping of nitrogen; plant growth and development also require certain metal elements, such as phosphorus and potassium. Moreover, some elements may exhibit synergistic effects after doping. In future research, the effects of different doped and codoped CD on plant seed germination and seedling growth can be further explored. The above discussion provides ideas for promoting the further application of CDs in agriculture.

Materials and Methods

Synthesis of N-CDs

The preparation of N-CDs involved a microwave-assisted “bottom-up” synthesis strategy. The ratio of carbon and nitrogen sources, as well as the microwave time, were optimized to obtain the ideal synthesis process as follows. First, 0.59 g of succinic acid and 0.43 g of MPD, at their optimal 5:4 molar ratio, were weighed out and mixed with 10 mL of deionized water in a conical flask. The reaction vessel was placed in a microwave oven at 750 W for 2 min to obtain a stock solution of N-CDs. Finally, the N-CDs were purified by centrifugation (10,000 rpm, 10 min), filtration with a 0.22 μm filter, and dialysis (48 h) to obtain N-CDs with a high fluorescence QY.

Intervention Experiment of N-CDs on Seed Germination and Seedling Growth

Maize and Tomato Seed Pretreatment and Seed Immersion Experiments

Maize and tomato seeds were immersed in a maintained-temperature water bath at 55 °C, gently agitated, and left to soak (30 min). Poor seeds were removed, and the remaining seeds were dried, soaked in a 0.30% H2O2 solution for 3 min, washed, and dried for later use. Subsequently, the effects of N-CDs on the growth and development of maize and tomatoes were investigated, and the experimental design was modified based on previous studies. Twenty-one Petri dishes (9 mm diameter) were prepared, with 20 seeds placed in each dish. Petri dishes were divided into seven groups, including two control groups (CK1: water control and CK2: precursor control) and five experimental groups (T1, T2, T3, T4, and T5), with three replicates for each treatment. Each Petri dish was filled with 10 mL of the corresponding culture solution (Table S1), which was changed daily.

Determination of Germination Indices of Maize and Tomato Seeds

During the germination experiment, the number of seeds germinated daily was recorded. The seeds were sprouted until the radicle was more excellent than half the length of the seed as a sign of successful seed sprouting. Each treatment was repeated three times, and each replicate was counted three times and averaged. The GP, GR, and GI of maize, along with tomato seeds, under different treatments were calculated. The methods used to calculate the GP, GR, and GI were based on previous seed germination studies using the following formulas GP=G3N×100% GR=G7N×100% GI=∑GtDt In this context, N represents the total number of seeds in a Petri dish. Dt is the germination days of the seed. G3, G7 and Gt are the germination numbers of seeds on day 3, day 7 and day t, respectively.

Determination of Maize and Tomato Growth Indices

A ruler was employed to take measurements of the shoot and root lengths of the seedlings on the seventh-day postgermination. Maize is a whisker-root crop, and the sum of all taproot and fibrous root lengths was used as the root length (n = 3). Tomato is a taproot crop; for root length measurements, only the length of the primary root was measured (n = 3). The seedling VI was calculated during different treatments, and the RSH and RTI were calculated based on seedling height and root length using the following formulas, where S is the growth of the seedlings (cm). VI=GI×S RSH=treatmentbudlengthcontrolbudlength×100% RTI=treatmentrootlengthcontrolrootlength×100%

Second, on day seven of germination, a precision balance was used to measure both the fresh and dry weights of the seedlings’ shoots and roots. First, the aboveground parts (buds) of the seedlings were separated from the underground parts (roots) after germination with a scalpel. Wipe off surface moisture with absorbent paper. The masses of the bud and root were determined using the same balance. Subsequently, the buds and roots were dried in an oven (60 °C) to a stable weight, followed by weighing (n = 3).

Transport and Distribution of N-CDs in Plant Seedlings

In order to study the absorption and transport of N-CDs in the seedling growth stage, maize seedlings were used as the observation model. Maize seedlings were cultivated with 1.00 mg/mL N-CDs, and the roots, stems, and leaves sections of maize seedlings were obtained by confocal laser microscopy. A blank control group CK1 was set up, and the difference in fluorescence images of root, stem and leaf of maize seedlings in the experimental group was observed and compared. The transport and distribution mode of N-CDs in plant seedlings was analyzed.

Data Statistics and Analysis

SPSS software offers robust capabilities for processing complex data sets, facilitating a clear presentation of experimental data. Origin software can present data trends clearly and straightforwardly. Therefore, Microsoft Excel 2022 was selected for data integration, IBM SPSS Statistics 26 was used for one-way ANOVA, and Origin 2018 was used for data mapping. Second, we chose a significance level of P < 0.05, which is widely accepted in agricultural and biological research, to determine statistical significance and ensure the validity of our experimental data.

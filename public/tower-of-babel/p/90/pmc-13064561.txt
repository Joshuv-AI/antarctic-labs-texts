The adiponectin/leptin ratio as a biomarker of adiposopathy and visceral adipose tissue accumulation: sex-specific mechanisms
Authors: Aida Medina-Urrutia, Iván Torre-Villalvazo, Andrea Díaz-Villaseñor, Esteban Jorge-Galarza, Luz Graciela Cervantes-Pérez, Neftali Eduardo Antonio-Villa, Felipe Santibañez-Escobar, Gabriela Leal-Escobar, Brenda Marquina-Castillo, Froylan David Martínez-Sánchez, Juan Gabriel Juárez-Rojas
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAdipose tissue (AT) dysfunction can lead to increased visceral AT (VAT) and metabolic damage. The adiponectin/leptin ratio (ALR) has been proposed as a biomarker of AT functionality. However, its participation in VAT accumulation and the impact of sex on these associations have not been evaluated. In an analytical cross-sectional study, 54 adults (29 male, 25 postmenopausal females, aged 30–70 years, BMI 19–31 kg/m2) were analysed. Anthropometric data, fasting serum samples, and subcutaneous AT (SAT) biopsies were obtained. Morpho-functional AT characteristics included ALR, adipocyte size, macrophage content and AT insulin resistance (ADIPO-IR). Using multivariate and mediation models, we evaluated the associations of SAT characteristics with systemic IR (TyG index), systemic inflammation (C-reactive protein), and VAT area. In postmenopausal females, ALR was inversely associated with adipocyte size, macrophage number, TyG index, CRP, and VAT area. SAT inflammation and ADIPO-IR were independently associated with VAT, and a mediation model suggested ALR as a possible precursor of VAT. Among males, ADIPO-IR and ALR were independently associated with VAT. These findings emphasize the importance of considering sex differences in the prevention and treatment strategies for metabolic diseases among Mexican-Mestizo populations, although these results should be confirmed by prospective studies.KEYWORDS: Adipose tissue dysfunction, adiposopathy, adiponectin, leptin, visceral fat

Introduction

Adipose tissue (AT) is a vital organ that regulates whole-body energy homoeostasis. It is primarily distributed as subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) and visceral adipose tissue (VAT). Although SAT deposits are more abundant and contribute to higher levels of circulating adipokines and free fatty acids [1–3], VAT is more strongly associated with chronic degenerative diseases, which are major causes of disability and death worldwide [4,5]. Nevertheless, SAT dysfunction has been proposed as the first step in metabolic damage, commonly associated with insulin resistance (IR) and low-grade systemic inflammation [6,7].

The morpho-functional alterations described for AT dysfunction, also known as adiposopathy, include an increased proportion of hypertrophic adipocytes, infiltration by monocytes and other inflammatory cells, as well as deregulation of adipokine production and AT insulin resistance (ADIPO-IR) [8]. Adiponectin and leptin are adipokines mainly produced by AT. Adiponectin favours insulin action and adipogenesis while decreasing AT inflammation [9]. On the other hand, leptin has been related to promoting a local and systemic pro-inflammatory state [10]. Thus, an imbalance between adipokines reported as the adiponectin/leptin (ALR) or leptin/adiponectin (LAR) ratio, has been associated with adiposopathy and its metabolic consequences [11,12]. However, few studies have analysed the association of these ratios with other morpho-functional characteristics of AT [13], which may reinforce the role of this adipokine imbalance as a possible central biomarker of AT dysfunction in clinical practice.

As far as we know, no studies have analysed the association between the morpho-functional characteristics of SAT and early metabolic damage, and how this association could impact VAT accumulation. Moreover, although some studies have reported differences in AT distribution, adipokine production, and insulin action between males and females [14], the mechanisms by which AT dysfunction may differentially affect metabolic damage by sex remain unexplored. Therefore, the objective of the present study was to investigate, through multivariate and mediation models, the association of different morpho-functional AT markers (adipocyte area, ADIPO-IR, ALR, and macrophage AT content) with systemic IR and inflammation, as well as the impact of this association on VAT accumulation, in males and postmenopausal females.

Methods

Study population

This cross-sectional study included males and postmenopausal females between 30 and 70 years old, with a body mass index (BMI) of 19 to 31 kg/m2, without acute infections (C-reactive protein [CRP] <10 mg/L), diabetes, cardiovascular disease, or another diagnosed chronic-metabolic disease, thyroid, autoimmune disorders, or cancer. Participants should not use lipid-lowering or hormonal drugs, have extreme hypertriglyceridaemia (>400 mg/dL), or excessive alcohol consumption (>30 g/day). Three hundred fifty individuals were invited to participate in the study through phone calls and personal interviews [15]. Subjects were instructed to fast for at least 10 hours and to avoid intense physical activity for 24 hours before their appointment to obtain samples. Before signing the informed consent form, all subjects were inquired about the risks of the study, and any questions regarding the procedure were addressed. A total of 83 participants agreed to participate in the study. However, biopsies from 23 participants were insufficient or inadequate, 5 were premenopausal, and 1 had unstable cytokine and adipokine measures. Thus, these participants were not included in the final analysis. Considering that the present study aimed to evaluate the impact of different markers of morpho-functionality of AT on IR, systemic inflammation, and VAT, different sample size calculations were performed, including data from studies that analysed the relationship between adipocyte size and insulin resistance [16] and the ALR with the presence of metabolic syndrome [17]. Based on the results of these studies, a sample size of 23 subjects was determined to be sufficient to find a relationship between adipocyte size and the ALR with insulin resistance. Additionally, another sample size calculation was performed using data from a study that analysed the effect of the ALR on VAT area [18]. Based on this information, a sample size of 4 subjects was found to be sufficient to detect differences. The research protocol and the informed consent form were reviewed and approved by the institutional ethics committee (INCAR 17–1040/INCAR 22–1294), in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The writing of this work adheres to the STROBE reporting guidelines for cross-sectional studies (supplementary file).

Anthropometry and body composition

A certified nutritionist obtained anthropometric measurements. Weight (kg) was evaluated with a digital scale (Soehnle Professional Design 7830, Leifheit, Nassau, Germany), height (m) with a SECA 220 wall stadiometer (SECA GMBH and Co.KG, Hamburg, Germany), and waist circumference (cm) was measured with a non-stretch tape (Lufkin W606 PM 6 mm × 2 m, Zapopan, Jal. Mexico). BMI was calculated as the weight (kg) to height (m2) ratio. Total body fat percentage and visceral fat area (cm2) were estimated in the supine position using a multi-frequency bioimpedance device with eight electrodes (InBody S10, Seoul, Korea), Bioimpedance allows the identification of intracellular and extracellular water, as well as segmented body composition analysis of the arms, legs, and trunk, enabling the estimation of visceral fat [19,20].

Clinical characteristics

After 10 hours of overnight fasting and 20 minutes of rest in a sitting position, a venous blood sample was obtained from each participant. Concentrations of fasting glucose, total cholesterol, triglycerides, and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol were measured in serum in a COBAS c311 autoanalyzer (Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany) by conventional enzymatic methods (Inter-assay CVs < 6%). Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol was estimated using the Friedewald-modified DeLong formula [21]. Serum adiponectin was determined with a Bio-Plex Pro Human Diabetes Adiponectin Assay KIT (Bio-Rad Inc, USA, catalogue code: 171A7003M), while leptin, insulin, resistin, tumour necrosis factor-alpha (TNFα), monocyte chemoattractant protein (MCP-1), and interleukins (IL) 1β, 6, and 10 were quantified in undiluted samples, using a Bio-Plex panel (Bio-Rad Inc, USA, catalogue code: 171A7001M). All these assays were carried out in duplicate; the intra- and inter-assay coefficient of variances for these measurements were < 4% and 5%, respectively.

Metabolic damage indicators

Insulin resistance was estimated by the homoeostasis model (HOMA-IR = Glucose [mmol/L] × Insulin [μIU/L])/(22.5)) [22] and the triglyceride-glucose index (TyG = ln [(triglycerides [mg/dL] × glucose [mg/dL])/2]), which is considered a marker of systemic insulin resistance (SYST-IR) [23]. To estimate systemic low-grade chronic inflammation, high-sensitivity CRP was quantified by immunonephelometry in a BN ProSpec nephelometer (Dade Behring, Marburg Hesse, Germany). Metabolic syndrome was diagnosed according to the International Diabetes Federation and the American Heart Association harmonized definition [24].

Morpho-functional analysis of SAT

A biopsy was taken from each participant. It was obtained through an incision no larger than 1 cm around the umbilical region, under sterile conditions and local anaesthesia (lidocaine 2%) [25]. Biopsies were immediately washed with sterile saline, and visible vessels were removed. The tissue was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde in PBS. After 24 hours in the fixation solution, it was dehydrated in ethanol, clarified with xylenes, and embedded in paraffin.

Adipocyte size: Histological sections of 6–8 µm were obtained from each biopsy and stained with haematoxylin-eosin. Three digital images were randomly captured from each section using an Axiocam 208 camera (resolution 3840 × 2160 pixels) coupled to a Zeiss Primostar 3 microscope at 10X. The images were analysed using the ImageJ software along with the Adiposoft plugin. The average area (in µm2) of at least 150 adipocytes was calculated for each participant. All images were carefully examined to avoid broken cells or artefacts.

Macrophage content: Immunohistochemical analysis of adipose tissue histological sections was performed. As previously suggested, local inflammation was evaluated by total macrophage counts, using anti-CD68 monoclonal antibody (Santa Cruz, SC 20,060) [26,27]. Five digital images were captured from each slide using an Axiocam 208 camera (resolution 3840 × 2160 pixels) coupled to a Zeiss Primostar 3 microscope at 40X. Positively labelled cells with macrophage morphology were counted manually.

Adipose tissue insulin resistance (ADIPO-IR) was calculated as the fasting plasma insulin (µU/mL) to glycerol product (mmol/L) as previously defined [28]. Glycerol was quantified by an enzymatic colorimetric method (Sigma-Aldrich).

Adiponectin/Leptin ratio: As proposed by Gema Frühbeck, the adiponectin (µg/mL) to leptin (ng/mL) ratio was used as an AT function marker [11].

Outcome definition

Based on the literature [1,29], we hypothesized that VAT accumulation results from SAT dysfunction. Therefore, the VAT area measured by bioimpedance was the outcome measure in this study.

Statistical analysis

Continuous and categorical variables are summarized as medians (interquartile range) and absolute proportions, respectively. Differences between sex categories were evaluated using the Mann-Whitney U test or the chi-squared test, as appropriate. The univariate linear regression analysis was used to explore associations among AT morpho-functional characteristics (adipocyte size, ALR, ADIPO-IR, and macrophage number), as well as to evaluate their associations with metabolic damage indicators (TyG index and CRP levels) and the outcome variable (VAT area). Asymmetric distribution parameters were logarithmically transformed (ln) before being included in the analyses.

Multivariate linear regression analysis

It was performed using variables that showed statistically significant associations in the univariate linear regression analyses. Results are shown as β (confidence interval [CI] 95%) or standardized β. The quality of the multivariate models was assessed using plots of predicted values versus residuals to evaluate homoscedasticity and linearity, and the variance inflation factor (VIF) was calculated; it was less than 3 for all models. These analyses were performed with the SPSS statistical package (version 15.0), and p-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Mediation model analysis: According to previous reports [1,29], we hypothesized that the morpho-functional characteristics of AT may mediate the effects of IR and systemic inflammation on VAT accumulation. This hypothesis was proved by mediation models that estimate a total model effect (TME). The attributable direct effect (ADE) represents the association between the exposure and outcome variables. The attributable causal mediation effect (ACME) represents the contribution of the exposure and outcome variables. The mediation proportion was expressed as a percentage [Mediation = (ET-ADE) *100]. To estimate the 95% confidence intervals, a bootstrapping method with 1,000 resamples (B = 1000) was employed using the mediation package in R Studio (Version 4.1.2). The code is publicly available in a GitHub repository and can be accessed at https://github.com/MedinaUrrutia/Mediation_Adipocitos_Project.

Results

Characteristics of the studied population

The present study included 54 participants, 25 postmenopausal females and 29 males, with a median age of 57 (53–62) years and a BMI of 27 [24–31] kg/m2. Females had higher body fat percentages and VAT areas than males. Regarding the biochemical profile, males had marginally higher triglycerides, insulin, and HOMA-IR values. Metabolic syndrome was identified in 17% (9 participants) of the studied subjects, being more frequent among females and subjects with obesity (32%). Females had significantly higher concentrations of systemic inflammation markers, except for MCP-1 and PAI-1, which were higher among males (Table 1).

Morpho-functional characteristics of AT

Mean ADIPO-IR values were similar for males and females. However, female adipocytes tended to be smaller and had significantly higher adiponectin and leptin concentrations. Of note, differences in leptin were higher, which may explain the lower ALR observed among females compared to males. The number of macrophages was also higher in females (Figure 1).

Univariate linear regression analysis

The interrelation among AT morpho-functional markers showed that ALR and leptin were significantly associated with all AT markers in females (Table 2), while leptin was positively associated with IL-6 and CRP (Supplementary Table S1). Conversely, in males, ALR was associated only with ADIPO-IR, whereas there were no associations between leptin and systemic inflammation variables. The association analysis of morpho-functional AT markers with metabolic damage and VAT accumulation shows that in females, ALR was associated with SYST-IR, systemic inflammation (CRP), and VAT area, apparently due to leptin values. In addition, ADIPO-IR and SAT macrophage content were strongly associated with the VAT area. In males, ADIPO-IR was found to be associated with SYST-IR and the area of VAT, while ALR was associated with VAT (Table 3).

Multivariate linear regression analysis

Considering insulin resistance and inflammation as pathophysiological precursors of VAT, various multivariate linear regression models were conducted to identify potential independent predictors of VAT (Supplementary tables S2-S6). Table 4 shows the models that better explain the VAT area. In females, the models indicated that ADIPO-IR and the number of macrophages on SAT accounted for 76% of the variance in VAT. In males, ADIPO-IR and ALR explained 40% of the variance in VAT. Interestingly, although CRP was only marginally associated with VAT, it was necessary to include it in the model to enhance the significance of ALR. This suggests an endocrine modulation of systemic inflammation in males.

Causal mediation analysis

Based on our findings, we hypothesized that ALR could mediate the effects of ADIPO-IR, systemic (CRP) and local inflammation (SAT macrophages) on VAT accumulation. This hypothesis was tested using mediation models separately for males and females (Figure 2). Mediation models were non-statistically significant for males. Model A shows that ALR partially mediates (37%) the effect of ADIPO-IR on VAT accumulation. Regarding the inflammation pathway, model B suggests that ALR marginally explains the association between CRP and VAT area (48%). Finally, we found that the number of SAT macrophages directly impacts VAT accumulation (model C). These findings suggest that AT dysfunction could modulate SYST-IR and inflammation, thereby contributing to VAT accumulation.

Discussion

Although many studies have shown a link between excess body fat and metabolic diseases, recent findings suggest that early changes in AT function may contribute to the onset of these conditions [6,7]. Thus, the present study examined how AT morpho-functional properties participate in SYST-IR and inflammation, as well as their role in VAT accumulation. Despite the results showing that hypertrophic adipocytes and macrophage accumulation contributed to an imbalance in adipokine production in both males and females, sex differences in the mechanisms of AT dysfunction were observed. In females, the ALR correlated with other morpho-functional AT characteristics, metabolic abnormalities, and VAT area, suggesting that adipokine imbalance could mediate the association between metabolic damage and VAT accumulation (Tables 2 and 3). This hypothesis was supported by mediation models, which showed that the ALR partially mediates the effects of local IR on the VAT area, and that SAT macrophage content was directly associated with VAT accumulation (Figure 2). Results in males showed that a lower ALR and ADIPO-IR was associated with VAT. Multivariate regression analyses confirmed these variables as the main predictors of VAT accumulation. These findings highlight different patterns of AT dysfunction by sex and point out that primary preventive and intervention programmes, focused on obesity-related comorbidities, should pay special attention to these differences.

Sex impact on morpho-functional characteristics and AT distribution

Sex differences in body fat amounts, distribution, and morpho-functional AT characteristics have been previously described. Compared to males of similar BMI, females have more body fat, distributed mainly as SAT. Moreover, prior research has demonstrated that compared with males, females SAT exhibits greater adipogenic potential [14,30], resulting in a higher proportion of small adipocytes that are more insulin-sensitive during fasting and post-prandial stages and less prone to triggering inflammation [14,31]. Oestrogens partially explain those differences, as healthy adiposity characteristics are attenuated in postmenopausal females [30,32]. In line with this, postmenopausal females included in the present study exhibited a higher proportion of body fat and a larger VAT area than males. Moreover, compared to males, females showed slightly smaller SAT adipocyte size, similar ADIPO-IR, and a higher degree of SAT and systemic inflammation, which may be associated with their postmenopausal state.

On the other hand, information about the effect of sex on ALR is limited. A recent study including elderly subjects found that compared with males, females had lower ALR. Interestingly, the authors reported an inverse association between the ALR and insulin, CRP, and IL-6 among females. In contrast, males exhibited an inverse association between the ALR and insulin levels, but not with the inflammation markers [33]. These findings support our results, showing an inverse association between ALR and SYST-IR, and inflammation-harmful effects on VAT accumulation (Figure 2). In males, ALR was inversely associated with ADIPO-IR (Table 2), likely due to SYST-IR and VAT accumulation (Tables 3 and 4). This information suggests that an imbalance of adiponectin and leptin may be a pathway linking AT dysfunction and metabolic damage.

Adiponectin/Leptin ratio as an AT dysfunction marker

Given that adiponectin and leptin are primarily produced by adipocytes and are strongly associated with metabolic risk factors, ALR and LAR have been proposed as biomarkers of AT function [11,12]. It has also been observed that the association between the ALR ratio and SYST-IR is stronger than that of individual adipokines [34,35] A recent study examined AT biopsies from healthy females with a wide range of body fat percentages, finding that participants with larger adipocytes and lower ALR had more SYST-IR and VAT area [13]. In a similar design study, Tremblay et al. reported that the ALR was directly related to GLUT4 transporter expression, supporting the notion that lipolytic activity increases during adiposopathy and ectopic lipid accumulation [18]. Another study involving healthy, normal-weight males exposed to 8 weeks of overfeeding showed increases in small adipocytes in SAT and a higher proportion of VAT after the intervention [36]. This study also found increased leptin levels, while adiponectin remained unchanged, suggesting a lower ALR after overfeeding [29]. In line with these, females in the present study showed higher body fat depots, SAT characterized by marginally smaller adipocytes, and a lower ALR than males. These results support the hypothesis that the adipogenic potential may be conserved during the early stages of AT dysfunction. In contrast, the imbalance in adipokine production could trigger metabolic damage and VAT accumulation. All these findings highlight the potential of the ALR as a possible early AT dysfunction biomarker for clinical practice. Nevertheless, further studies are required to support this hypothesis and to clarify the mechanisms underlying the development of adiposity-related metabolic disorders.

Adiposopathy, systemic inflammation, and VAT accumulation

Circulating leptin plays a central role in the systemic immune response through diverse pathways [10]. Nevertheless, a recent intervention study reported that 5% weight loss induced by caloric restriction led to a 26% reduction in leptin without changes in adiponectin or inflammation. However, a more substantial weight loss (−16%) was associated with a 58% reduction in leptin, a 33% increase in adiponectin, and a 33% CRP decrease in CRP [37]. In agreement, the ALR has been considered a biomarker of AT inflammation, as it is inversely associated with serum amyloid A (SAA) and CRP [17]. In line with these results, our study reveals a significant inverse relationship between ALR and CRP concentrations, and we suggest, for the first time, that the ALR could be a mechanism underlying AT dysfunction and systemic inflammation. While our study does not establish temporality or causality, the results suggest that in healthy postmenopausal females, adipose tissue inflammation may contribute to systemic damage and visceral adipose tissue accumulation, as proposed regression models and mediation analyses (Table 4 and Figure 2).

ADIPO-IR impact on metabolic damage and VAT accumulation

It has been proposed that adipocyte insulin resistance triggers systemic damage, resulting in AT-related chronic diseases [38]. Furthermore, increased lipolysis mediated by insulin resistance has also been described as contributing to this association [39]. Thus, ADIPO-IR, which has been considered a good marker of insulin-lipolysis regulation, is a reliable, accessible, and useful method to evaluate AT function in clinical studies [40]. In a recent study including a Caucasian population, it was reported that ADIPO-IR values were higher among males with obesity than those for females. Additionally, the authors described that these male with obesity had lower insulin-mediated inhibition of lipolysis, increased lipolysis rates, and lower expression of insulin receptor substrate (IRS)-1 on SAT [39]. All these findings align with the present study’s results which show ADIPO-IR as the main contributor to SYST-IR and VAT accumulation among males. Moreover, to confirm the involvement of the AT-insulin signalling damage on this pathway and given that the GLUT4 transporter is responsive to insulin-lipolysis regulation [18,40], we additionally investigated the expression of this transporter in a sub-sample of our series (Supplementary Table S7). Interestingly, the results showed that ADIPO-IR significantly correlates with GLUT4 expression in males (r = −0.483; p = 0.049) and marginally in females (r = −0.398; p = 0.082). Of note, to investigate the early stages of AT dysfunction and metabolic damage, participants of the present analysis were mainly healthy subjects without obesity. Only 17% had metabolic syndrome, and one-third of them were in each BMI strata. Considering that Hispanic subjects have a higher susceptibility to obesity-related abnormalities [41], adipose tissue damage may even be initiated at lower BMI levels in our series. The latter can be reinforced by data indicating that hypertrophic adipocytes are associated with worse fasting and post-prandial lipolysis and VAT accumulation [38]. Consistent with this, males included in this study were characterized by a lightly small adipocyte size (Figure 1).

Hypothesis of the impact of SAT dysfunction on metabolic damage and VAT accumulation

Based on the results of the present study, we propose some possible mechanisms through which AT dysfunction may lead to metabolic abnormalities and VAT accumulation in females and males (Figure 3). First, among females, hypertrophy [1] and inflammation [2] of SAT were inversely related to ALR. This adipokine imbalance has a central role in promoting local [3] and systemic [4] IR alongside to systemic inflammation [5]. Additionally, ALR may be proposed as a mediator of the effect that systemic inflammation had on VAT accumulation [6]. Moreover, SAT inflammation was strongly associated with VAT accumulation [7] in postmenopausal females (Figure 3A). Second, among males, the size of SAT adipocytes [1] was inversely associated with macrophage content. This SAT inflammation was found to be negatively associated with ALR [2], thereby affecting local and SYST-IR [3]. Finally, multivariate models suggest ADIPO-IR [4] as the main associate of VAT accumulation among males (Figure 3B).

Strengths and limitations

The present study has important strengths. Compared with most studies evaluating AT function that include subjects with class III obesity or with surgical-related pathologies that allow collecting biopsies, participants of the present study were mainly healthy subjects without obesity. Additionally, we included a similar number of females and males, allowing us to expand our understanding of the impact of sex on AT function. Moreover, the comprehensive characterization of the subjects was key to suggesting a possible pathway by which AT dysfunction could promote early metabolic damage, and lead to chronic diseases. The present study also has some limitations. First, AT and SYST-IR were evaluated using ADIPO-IR and TyG estimators, which are not the gold-standard methods. However, these valid methods correlate well with the euglycemic-CLAMP, the gold standard for evaluating IR [42]. Second, VAT was evaluated by the multifrequency segmental bioimpedance method. Although computed tomography (CT) is considered a gold standard method for VAT evaluation, some studies have shown a strong correlation between multi-frequency bioimpedance and CT [43–45] Additionally, bioimpedance offers multiple advantages in terms of cost, simplicity, and portability, and avoids the use of radiation. Third, we measure adipokines, free fatty acids, and glycerol in serum and not directly from SAT. However, it has been reported that SAT contributes the largest proportion of circulating adipokines [2,46], glycerol, and free fatty acids [47]. In addition, although adiponectin and leptin levels were measured using Bio-Plex systems, this method has been shown to strongly correlate with high-sensitivity ELISA measurements [48] Thus, these serum analytes, as well as the methods used to evaluate them, could be useful for investigating SAT functionality. Fourth, although the small sample size of included females and males lead to some imprecision in the confidence intervals of regression models, the adequate fit of the models supports their validity. Finally, the cross-sectional nature of the present study allows us to suggests hypothesis regarding adipose tissue dysfunction and the directionality of its associations, but these should be confirmed by prospective or experimental studies.

Results of the present study suggests that in a Mexican-mestizo population ALR could be a biomarker of early AT dysfunction with a central role in VAT accumulation and differential effect by sex. In females, an ALR imbalance appears to mediate the effects of IR and inflammation on VAT accumulation. In males, this imbalance leads to higher ADIPO-IR, which seems to have a central role in VAT accumulation. Thus, the results highlighted that prevention and therapeutic programmes focused on metabolic disorders should consider sexual dimorphism.

Association between ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, dementia, and rs201118034 among general Taiwanese population
Authors: Yi‐Chia Liaw, Che‐Hong Chen, Shu‐Yi Hsu, Yi‐Ching Liaw, Yung‐Po Liaw
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractINTRODUCTIONWe investigated the association between the NOTCH3 rs201118034 variant and the risk of ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and dementia within a Taiwanese population.METHODSData from 114,233 individuals enrolled in the Taiwan Biobank were analyzed, integrating clinical diagnoses from the National Health Insurance Research Database and genetic information. INTRODUCTION

Cerebral autosomal dominant arteriopathy with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy (CADASIL) is one of the most common monogenic small vessel diseases. 1 It is primarily caused by mutations in the NOTCH3 gene on chromosome 19, which encodes a transmembrane receptor involved in vascular smooth muscle cell function. 1 These mutations lead to the hallmark manifestations of CADASIL, including migraine with aura, psychiatric symptoms, cognitive decline, ischemic stroke, and, less frequently, hemorrhagic stroke. 2 According to a previous research, the prevalence of NOTCH3 cysteine altering variant in the general Taiwanese population is around 0.9%, which is four‐fold higher than the U.K. population. 3 , 4 Moreover, the NOTCH3 p.R544C mutation on exon 11 encompassed a 70.5% of Han Chinese in Taiwan, making this mutation a specific hotspot among Taiwanese population. 2 While the symptoms associated with the p.R544C variant continues to be an area of ongoing researches, the interplay between genetic factors, cerebrovascular events, and cognitive presentations remained unclear. Recently, due to the progress of genetic studies, the association between the single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) rs201118034 and p.R544C has been established, 3 though studies linking rs201118034 to stroke and cognitive decline remained limited.

Ischemic stroke has been found to be an independent risk factor for dementia regardless of common vascular risk factors. 5 Moreover, the incidence and severity of ischemic stroke are correlated with an increased risk of dementia. 5 In a Chinese study, the researchers showed that dementia adversely affects long‐term survival after ischemic stroke. 6 This evidence illustrates the detrimental impact of stroke on cognitive health, and vice versa. The mechanisms underlying the relationship between stroke and dementia are complex and involve various factors. For example, the presence of vascular lesions and white matter hyperintensities, often observed in patients with ischemic stroke, has also been linked to cognitive impairment or dementia. 7

Though previous studies have observed the clinical phenotypes encompassing cognitive decline as well as stroke over NOTCH3 p.R544C mutation patients, the interactions between these phenotypes and the effect degree of the mutated p.R544C remained unknown. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the association between rs201118034, cerebrovascular events, and dementia in a large Taiwanese population cohort. Such research is crucial for developing tailored clinical management strategies that address the needs of individuals identified as carriers of high‐risk variants. Additionally, a synthesis of genetic information improves our understanding of CADASIL, especially the phenotypic variations related to p.R544C mutation.

RESEARCH IN CONTEXT

Systematic review: To investigate the association between the rs201118034 genetic variant in the NOTCH3 gene and the risks of ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and dementia, we conducted a comprehensive review of existing literature and data sources. We analyzed 114,233 individuals from the Taiwan Biobank, integrating clinical diagnoses from the National Health Insurance Research Database and relevant genetic data. Our search strategy was designed to identify studies linking genetic factors to cerebrovascular diseases, focusing on single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and their effects on stroke and dementia outcomes. Interpretation: Our findings reveal that the rs201118034 AG+AA genotype significantly increases the risks of ischemic (odds ratio [OR]: 1.50) and hemorrhagic stroke (OR: 2.21), while showing no significant association with dementia. This underscores the critical role of genetic and cardiovascular factors in stroke susceptibility. The results contribute to the understanding of how specific genetic variants can influence cerebrovascular health, emphasizing the need for SNP‐based risk stratification in clinical settings. Future directions: Future research should explore the underlying mechanisms by which the rs201118034 variant impacts stroke risk, including potential interactions with environmental factors and lifestyle choices. Additionally, studies should aim to confirm these findings in diverse populations to assess generalizability. Investigating the combined effects of multiple genetic variants on stroke and dementia risks will also be crucial for developing comprehensive risk assessment models. Finally, longitudinal studies should be conducted to evaluate the long‐term implications of genetic predispositions on stroke and dementia outcomes.

The genetic data were retrieved from the participants enrolled between 2008 and 2020 from the Taiwan Biobank (TWB) Database. The major criteria to be enrolled in the TWB are restricted to (1) Taiwanese residents (2) aged between 30 and 70 years old, and (3) those who are without a documented history of cancer. Data on lifestyle behaviors, environmental risk factors, and genetic information were collected retrospectively at enrollment. TWB participants underwent a physical examination and a structured interview with a trained researcher to report demographics, lifestyle behaviors, and environmental exposures via a questionnaire. Approval for this study was provided by the Institutional Review Board of Chung Shan Medical University (Institutional Review Board [IRB]: CS1‐20009).

Initially, we retrieved a total of 132,720 TWB participants from the database. After excluding participants with no genotyping information (N = 18,329) and/or incomplete questionnaires (N = 158), 114,233 individual genetic samples were included for analysis.

Definition of the variables

We utilized the International Classification of Diseases (ICD) codes to categorize the patients with the disease of interest. Since the Ninth Edition, Clinical Modification version (ICD‐9‐CM) was used before 2016, and the Tenth Edition (ICD‐10) has been in use since 2016, we included both ICD versions to maximize our participant pool. In this study, we focused on three major neurological disorders: ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and dementia. Patients were defined as having these disorders if they had either two outpatient visits or one hospitalization with the corresponding ICD code documented in the National Health Insurance Research Database (NHIRD) from 2000 to 2020.

Combination of clinical diagnosis and genetic data

In this study, smokers were defined as individuals who had smoked for more than 6 months. Alcohol drinkers were defined as those who reported drinking more than 150 mL of alcohol per week for over 6 months. Physical activity included any amount of exercise activity performed at least 3 times a week, lasting at least 30 min each time. The body mass index (BMI) was calculated as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared (kg/m2).

The clinical diagnosis from NHIRD, genotyping data, and lifestyle information from TWB were linked via the encrypted identification numbers of participants in accordance with the regulations of the Taiwanese government.

Statistical analysis

We used the PLINK 1.09 beta and SAS 9.4 software (SAS Institute, Cary, NC) for data management and statistical analyses. The chi‐squared test was used to compare the differences in discrete variables among different disease groups. Multiple logistic regression analysis was used to investigate the effects of the NOTCH3 gene variant (rs201118034) and risk factors on ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and dementia. The odds ratios (ORs) with their 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated. A two‐sided p‐value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

Table 1 presents the distribution of AG+AA and GG genotypes (rs201118034) across control, ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and dementia groups. We analyzed two genotype groups: GG (homozygous wild‐type) and AG+AA (heterozygous and homozygous for the variant allele). The AG+AA genotypes indicate the presence of the variant allele resulting in the p.R544C substitution in the NOTCH3 protein, which is associated with CADASIL‐like cerebrovascular pathology. Our findings reveal that the AG+AA genotype was significantly more prevalent in both the ischemic stroke (1.28% vs. 0.92% in controls, p = 0.001) and hemorrhagic stroke groups (1.82% vs. 0.92% in controls, p = 0.001). In contrast, no significant difference was observed in the dementia group (1.06% vs. 0.92% in controls, p = 0.563). These results suggest a potential genetic association between rs201118034 and stroke subtypes, but not with dementia.

Table 4 summarizes the multivariate logistic regression analysis for dementia risk factors. Unlike the stroke outcomes, the AG+AA genotype of rs201118034 did not significantly affect dementia risk (OR: 1.30, 95% CI: 0.77–2.18, p = 0.326), even after including covariates in the analysis model. This suggests that the genetic variant may influence cerebrovascular pathology without directly impacting cognitive outcomes in our cohort. We found no evidence of interaction effects between the genotype and lifestyle factors regarding dementia risk. Instead, traditional risk factors such as advanced age, low BMI, and cardiovascular comorbidities were the primary contributors to dementia risk, independent of rs201118034 status.

DISCUSSION

This study highlights the association between the genetic polymorphism rs201118034 and its potential influence on ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, and dementia among the Taiwanese population. Our findings demonstrated that the AG+AA genotype is associated with a significantly increased risk of both ischemic stroke and hemorrhagic stroke, but not dementia. These results provide new insights into the genetic factors underlying the pathophysiology of cerebrovascular diseases among CADASIL patients and emphasize the importance of rs201118034 as a genetic marker for stroke susceptibility.

The significant association found between the AG+AA genotype and both ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke underscores its potential genetic role in stroke susceptibility. This aligns with previous researches linking inflammatory genetic pathways and cerebrovascular events. 8 Identifying rs201118034 as a significant genetic marker could refine risk‐stratification strategies for cerebrovascular diseases, particularly in populations like the Taiwanese, where this polymorphism is prevalent.

Interestingly, the lack of association between rs201118034 and dementia in our population diverges from earlier research where NOTCH3 mutations have been implicated in vascular dementia. 9 , 10 In a Taiwanese study, a significantly lowered cognitive function was also noted among NOTCH3 p.R544C carriers using either Mini‐Mental State Examination (MMSE) or Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA). 11 This discrepancy may be due to the diverse etiologies of dementia within our cohort, which included mixed dementia patients. Future studies focusing specifically on vascular dementia are warranted to clarify this relationship and explore the potential influence of rs201118034 in this context.

Our findings also reveal that traditional risk factors such as advanced age, sex, and lifestyle factors significantly contribute to the risk of ischemic and hemorrhagic strokes, which have been reported widely in previous studies. 12 Notably, a lower BMI (< 18.5 kg/m2) was associated with an increased risk for all three conditions, suggesting that it may reflect underlying frailty or malnutrition, which predisposes individuals to both cerebrovascular events and cognitive decline. This “obesity paradox” highlights the need for a detailed understanding of weight and its implications for health outcomes.

While our study specifically focuses on NOTCH3 p.R544C mutation, other mutations such as p.R133C, p.R141C, and p.C174Y have also been reported to cause varying degrees of vascular pathology and cognitive impairment. 13 When compared to these mutations, rs201118034 appears to have a stronger association with stroke events. For instance, p.R544C mutation was reported to increase 3.4‐fold overall stroke risk in Taiwanese population. 14 In a recent meta‐analysis, compared with mutations such as p.R75P, p.R141C, and p.R182C, p.R544C owns a higher incidence of stroke event. 15 This differential impact suggests mutation‐specific pathogenic mechanisms within the NOTCH3 gene. Still, the impact of different mutation on cognitive function remained to be explored.

With regard to the implications to clinical practice, our study demonstrates a cost‐effective approach using SNP‐based genetic testing to identify individuals at risk for cerebrovascular diseases like CADASIL. By integrating genetic screening into routine clinical practice, we could achieve early diagnosis and targeted prevention. Moreover, the early intervention for public health initiatives could utilize this information to reduce the burden of stroke and its associated complications through precision‐medicine approaches.

Despite the strengths of our study, we acknowledge several limitations. First, the small number of individuals with dementia carrying the AG+AA genotype limited our ability to perform a detailed analysis of dementia subtypes. Second, the generalizability of our findings to other populations is uncertain due to the rare minor allele frequency (MAF) of rs201118034 in non‐Taiwanese ethnic groups. Lastly, as described earlier, the subtypes of the dementia group could not be subcategorized in this study, which impede from further detailed analysis. Future research should address these limitations for more comprehensive clinical phenotyping and include a more diverse population.

In summary, this study demonstrates that rs201118034 is significantly associated with ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke but not dementia, highlighting its relevance as a genetic marker for stroke susceptibility. These findings contribute to the growing body of evidence supporting the role of genetic factors in cerebrovascular disease and underscore the potential of SNP‐based screening in improving stroke prevention and management. Further research is needed to explore the mechanisms linking rs201118034 to cerebrovascular events and to investigate its broader implications in diverse populations.

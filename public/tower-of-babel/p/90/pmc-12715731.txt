A Non-Faradaic Impedimetric Label-Free Immunosensor Integrated with PCCODE Logic for Stratified Monitoring of Post-COVID Conditions
Authors: Georgeena Mathew, Sasya Madhurantakam, Annapoorna Hochihally Ramasubramanya, Jayanth Babu Karnam, Vikram Narayanan Dhamu, Sriram Muthukumar, Shalini Prasad
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The COVID-19 pandemic has presented significant challenges for the effectiveness of existing diagnostic tools in detecting and monitoring infections. Currently, there is an increased emphasis on the potential challenges faced by individuals during their postrecovery phase. The impact of post-COVID conditions (PCC) has substantially influenced perspectives on disease management, fostering a positive trend toward personal healthcare. Here, we report a ZnO-modified, non-Faradaic impedimetric biosensor for the rapid detection of TRAIL and D-dimer across clinically relevant ranges. The dual-analyte platform demonstrated great sensitivity (LOD: 3.4 pg/mL for TRAIL, 8.9 ng/mL for D-dimer), with high specificity in human plasma. Optimized surface chemistry and impedance analysis enabled reliable signal acquisition from 5 μL samples in 5 min. Beyond detection, we introduce the PCCODE (Post-COVID Co-dysregulation Evaluator) threshold-based classifier model using quantified concentration output values-TRAIL <50 pg/mL, D-dimer >1000 ng/mL-to encode biomarker signals in four binary states. This logic-driven system was constructed using exogenously spiked plasma samples and validated through signal-mapped heatmaps, allowing stratification of healthy, inflammation, immune dysregulation, and post-COVID categories. Together, the biosensor and classifier framework enable real-time, mechanism-informed stratification of PCC, marking a significant advance toward point-of-care diagnostics.

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic had a profound global health crisis, not only due to the direct effects of the virus but also due to the long-term consequences encountered by people recovering from the infection. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), there has been a decline of 1.8 years in global life expectancy, marking the most significant decrease observed between 2019 and 2021 as a consequence of the pandemic. In May 2025, more than 206,000 new cases of COVID-19 and more than 1000 deaths worldwide were reported, with admissions to the ICU and hospitalizations happening in multiple regions. , Beyond the acute intense phase of COVID, approximately 6% of diseased individuals develop post-COVID-19 conditions (PCC) also known as long COVID. Growing concerns regarding the long-term effects of the virus, termed SARS-CoV-2 infection (PASC) or post-COVID conditions (PCC) worldwide, have prompted extensive research on various mental, physical, and neurological difficulties that persist well after the initial infection. A series of symptoms including that include respiratory problems, cardiovascular complications, fatigue, and cognitive impairment that last weeks or months are recorded as conditions related to PCC. − The multifaceted characteristics of PCC and its impact on the quality of life of an individual pose significant challenges for healthcare systems, clinicians, and researchers. , Hence, it is of utmost importance to develop effective diagnostic and monitoring systems for post-COVID conditions.

The pathophysiology of PCC is complicated by persistent viral presence, autoimmunity, microvascular thrombosis, and immune dysregulation. Recent studies on post-COVID conditions have suggested that the TN-related apoptosis-inducing ligand (TRAIL) and a degradation product of fibrin (D-dimer) may be involved in the pathophysiology of PCC. , An elevation in D-dimer concentration levels in diseases reflects ongoing coagulation and fibrinolysis as observed in thrombotic events, while altered TRAIL concentrations are often associated with inflammation and immune dysregulation. , In a healthy individual, D-dimer concentrations are typically seen below 0.5 μg/mL and anything above these threshold levels is often associated as an indicator of hypercoagulation. According to WHO clinical guidelines, a paitient with COVID-19 with elevated D-dimer levels greater than 1 ug/mL is associated with higher mortality. , While the World Health Organization (WHO) does not specify specific levels of tumor necrosis factor-related apoptosis-inducing ligand (TRAIL), a decrease in TRAIL levels has been documented in peer-reviewed studies concerning COVID-19 and post-COVID inflammatory conditions. In healthy individuals, normal TRAIL levels are typically around 100–200 pg/mL. However, a significant reduction in these levels is observed during instances of immune dysregulation or systemic inflammation, which are prevalent symptoms of post-COVID conditions (PCC).

Current diagnostic methods for these biomarkers are mainly based on laboratory-based immunoassays such as chemiluminescence and ELISA. Although these methods are accurate, they are time-consuming, require skilled technicians, and are not ideal for point-of-care (POC) applications. , Thus, a need for accessible, cost-effective, and, more importantly, rapid diagnostics has shown that electrochemical biosensors can be potential candidates for monitoring PCC. − Electrochemical sensors offer several advantages over conventional methods. This technique is extremely sensitive, compact, and capable of delivering real-time results with very minimal sample preparation and volume. − While DPV has been widely used in COVID diagnostics, our platform employs non-Faradaic electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) to enable label-free capacitive sensing. This approach captures interfacial changes in dielectric properties without requiring redox-active species, making it ideal for dual-analyte detection and logic-based classification in complex matrices. Among the various electrochemical techniques, non-Faradaic electrochemical impedance spectroscopy has emerged as a reliable and attractive approach for the detection of biomarkers. Unlike the Faradaic methods, where the signal is based on redox reactions and electron transfer between the electrode surface and target market, non-Faradaic EIS operated via measuring the changes in the interfacial capacitance at the electrode–electrolyte interface. , When a target molecule binds onto the functionalized electrode surface, it results in alteration in the double-layer capacitance and its dielectric properties. These changes are detected as variations in impedance across a range of frequencies, resulting in a label-free and highly sensitive detection of PCC markers. It is well established that EIS sensors are well suited for POC diagnostics due to their portability, lower power requirements, and capability for miniaturization. In this study, we have used a two-electrode-based system, which further simplifies the overall design and minimizes the cost of production in compared to the traditional three electrode systems. ,, This minimalistic architecture is particularly advantageous for point-of-care applications, where portability and scalability are critical. In addition, the use of semiconducting materials such as zinc oxide (ZnO) as the interfacial layer on a gold electrode enhances the performance of the biosensors. Furthermore, ZnO offers a high isoelectric point of ∼9.5 that promotes favorable electrostatic interactions with antibodies, great biocompatibility, and excellent affinity for the immobilization of biomarkers through suitable cross-linkers (DTSSP). , Unlike bare Au, which supports quick thiol bonds, but lacks charge tunability and is reported to be prone to biofouling, especially when using biological matrices. The semiconducting nature and nanostructured morphology of ZnO help to amplify capacitive signal changes and suppress nonspecific adsorption. Hence, the combination of Au/ZnO is ideal for label-free detection of biomolecules using electrochemical methods, enabling stable biolayer formation and reproducible signal modulation. Together, the simplified electrode design and ZnO-enabled surface chemistry support the development of a compact, logic-driven diagnostic platform suitable for point-of-need applications.

In this research, we demonstrate a non-Faradaic EIS-based sensor array for the dual detection of D-dimer and TRAIL for PCC. The integrated sensor array consists of a ZnO-coated gold working electrode and a gold counter electrode in a two- electrode configuration. Our compact and scalable platform addresses the key limitations of conventional diagnostic methods and shows significant advancement in the development of point-of-care devices to monitor post-COVID health conditions. To address the diagnostic gap, we design and demonstrate a novel zinc oxide (ZnO)-modified non-Faradaic impedimetric immunosensor for dual, label-free detection of TRAIL and D-dimer from plasma samples. Employing a dual-marker detection scheme, the proposed sensor achieves picolevel sensitivity via the alteration of the interfacial capacitance, offering rapid and nondestructive measurement without the need for a redox reagent.

Building on this novel dual-marker sensor architecture, introduce a biosensor-integrated classifier logic, PCCODE (Post-COVID Co-dysregulation Evaluator), that uses biomarker concentration thresholds (TRAIL <50 pg/ml and D-dimer >1000 ng/ml), was introduced to stratify individuals by post-COVID status. Unlike previous studies that depend on detecting each biomarker individually, our system is designed for dual-marker monitoring, where each electrode is independently functionalized and read out to encode binary thresholds for clinical decision making. This approach allows for nonsimultaneous but parallel detection, enabling robust classification without requiring multiplexed signal deconvolution. Furthermore, this dual-axis mechanism reflects the biological co-dysregulation observed in the immune and thrombotic pathways, establishing a framework that bridges biochemical sensing and pathophysiological classification. This proof-of-concept and development of PCCODE for PCC not only enables rapid diagnosis, but also provides prognostic insight into the risk of persistent illness. The developed classifier model integrates the threshold concentrations of TRAIL and D-dimer to stratify the patients into diagnostic endotypes: healthy, immune-dominant, coagulation-dominant, and codysregulated (PCC). This concentration threshold-driven approach enables personalized risk prediction and accounts for biological variability. Unlike the symptom-driven diagnostics, PCCODE’s classifier system converts complicated biomarker patterns into translatable digital outputs within 5 min, aiding in timely clinical decisions and reducing the reliance on empiric treatments. In addition, PCCODE is compatible with miniaturized electronics and designed for intuitive user interfaces enabling self-triage and remote monitoring alongside other EIS-based sensing benefits over the conventional diagnostic methods. Importantly, this logic-driven PCCODE aids clinicians in making smarter decisions about the medications and lower the healthcare costs along with empowering the patient to self-monitoring their recovery in resource-limited settings, where access to centralized laboratory infrastructure and multiplexed assays may be constrained. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first biosensor platform to combine threshold concentrations of TRAIL and D-dimer for post-COVID diagnosis and prognosis using non-faradaic EIS. By validating both sensor performance and clinically relevant biomarker threshold, this study reports a new pathway to monitor post-COVID conditions, not just by symptoms and accounts for direct measurement of immune and coagulation signals, all in a format ready for real-world point-of-care use.

Experimental Section

Immunoassay Development

A chip comprising a two-electrode-based sensor array was fabricated on a small printed circuit board (PCB) FR4 substrate, with an ENIG gold-finishing copper-clad board (Taichung, Taiwan). The working electrode surface Au was modified with ZnO (ZnO/Au) by using an electron vapor deposition technique. An array of electrodes of 2 ZnO/Au working electrodes individually surrounded by Au counter electrodes was used for the sensing studies. All impedance measurements were recorded using a portable, non-Faradaic electrochemical impedance spectroscope (EIS) reader specifically engineered for label-free biosensing. The device outputs impedance magnitude (|Z|) as the primary signal, capturing capacitive changes at the electrode–electrolyte interface. Measurements were conducted across a frequency range of 80 to 1000 Hz, with 200 Hz selected as the classifier frequency based on its low coefficient of variation (%CV) and consistent signal modulation across analyte doses. No external redox probes were used, and all readings were performed in PBS or plasma matrices under ambient conditions.

Furthermore, the surface of the ZnO/Au working electrode was functionalized using DTSSP, cross-linker, and specific antibodies tailored to TRAIL and D-dimer proteins ( Figure ). Before the functionalization step, a separate cocktail solution of 80 μL containing 10 mM DTSSP with 10 μg/mL of TRAIL and 20 μg/mL of D-dimer antibody was prepared separately and stored in dark conditions in RT for successful conjugation of the antibody with DTSSP. After the conjugation period, 5 μL of the cocktail solution was added separately to the individual working electrodes and incubated for 30 min. Upon incubation, the free end of the cross-linker binds to the ZnO surface, and the unbound DTSSP and Ab were removed with a PBS wash step. Finally, a sucessful functionaization of the electrode surface (Au-ZnO/DTSSP/Ab) was achieved. The developed label-free dual-marker sensor was calibrated using 5 μL of physiologically relevant concentration ranges of TRAIL and D-dimer and validated using spiked pooled LiH plasma samples. Each spiked concentration was incubated for 5 min prior to the measurements. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy was used to measure the response of the sensor to the target analytes and to evaluate other parameters such as linearity, sensitivity, selectivity, reproducibility, and repeatability. A representative plot of impedance magnitude (|Z|) versus frequency and bar diagram for each pre- and post-surface modification step is included in the Supporting Information (Figure S1).

Results and Discussion

FTIR Analysis

To verify the functionalization process of the biosensor, Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was used to confirm the step-by-step attachment of a thiol-based cross-linker and the immobilization of antibodies targeting the D-dimer and TRAIL on the ZnO electrode surface. Initially, the surface was modified with DTSSP, a homobifunctional cross-linker that includes NHS ester and thiol-reactive groups, facilitating covalent bonding with primary amines on biomolecules. FTIR spectra (Figure A,B) demonstrate this sequential modification. Black spectra depict the DTSSP-functionalized ZnO substrate, revealing distinct peaks at 1042 cm– 1, which correspond to N–O–C ester bonds, and within the range of 1650–1670 cm– 1 , associated with C-N-C streching of NHS esters, characteristic of the DTSSP molecule. These peaks confirm the successful attachment of the crosslinker. After immobilizing antibodies specific for the D-dimer and TRAIL, a decrease in the intensity of the NHS ester peak is observed, indicating the consumption of NHS groups during the formation of stable amide bonds with antibodies. Further spectral changes after the introduction of their respective antigens (D-dimer and TRAIL) suggest successful antigen binding, as evidenced by shifts and alterations in transmittance in the same spectral regions. , These spectral transitions confirm biochemical modifications on the sensor surface. Therefore, the FTIR analysis provides convincing evidence of the successful immobilization of antibodies and their functional interaction with target antigens, confirming that the sensor platform is adequately prepared for sensitive detection.

Electrochemical Detection of TRAIL and D-Dimer

Following the successful binding of the DTSSP-Ab conjugate onto the Au-ZnO electrode, the electrochemical sensing performance of the developed Au-ZnO/Ab sensors toward TRAIL and D-dimer was studied. A non-faradaic electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) sensor was employed for point-of-need, label-free detection of clinically relevant TRAIL and D-dimer biomarkers associated with post-COVID conditions. The sensor architecture developed helps to measure the capacitive changes at the electrode–electrolyte interface, thus eliminating the need for any redox probes and helping in maintaining the biological integrity of the markers during measurements. TRAIL and D-dimer markers were selected on the basis of their vital roles in immune regulation and coagulation, respectively. The fabricated biosensor displayed a dose-dependent increase in the percent response for both target analytes. Electrochemical interactions at the functionalized electrode surface were measured as changes in the impedance response signal obtained for the target concentration with respect to the baseline (blank dose) in the LiH-pooled plasma. The dynamic ranges for TRAIL (15.63–500 pg/mL) and D-dimer (9.75–10,000 ng/mL) were chosen to expand the biosensor range according to the physiological and clinically relevant concentrations observed in the PCC cohorts. For TRAIL, a drop in concentration typically indicates lingering inflammation or immune issues; therefore, we focus on levels that start around the normal baseline and extend the range downward. In the case of the D-dimer, the range ranges from a healthy level to higher values that correspond to people with blood vessel damage or clotting issues. Figure A and Figure B illustrate a clear upward trend in the % response with increasing doses of TRAIL and D-dimer, respectively, at 200 Hz. This demonstrates consistent signal amplification across a wide dynamic range of detection. 200 Hz was selected as the optimal frequency to compute the calibration-dependent dose response due to the maximum signal-to-noise ratio and enhanced signal response.

Electrochemical response of the non-faradaic EIS sensor to TRAIL and D-dimer across clinically relevant concentration ranges. Furthermore, Figure C,D presents the corresponding nonlinear regression plots using a three-parameter logistic (3PL) model, displaying an excellent correlation with the measured signal: R 2 = 0.89 for TRAIL and R 2 = 0.97 for the D-dimer. The dose–response regression model was expressed as Y=A+(B−A)1+(XC)D where Y is the percent response, X is the corresponding concentration of the target, A and B are the minimum and maximum responses, respectively, C is the inflection point (Ec50 or KD), and D is the slope of the hill.

The dissociation constants (K D) values for TRAIL and D-dimer were derived from the fitted curves and calculated to be 0.44 and 190 pM, respectively. This highlights the high affinity and sensitivity of the developed sensors toward the target molecules, particularly in the low-picomolar range. Additionally, the lowest limit of detection (LOD = 3σ/m) and the limit of quantification (LOQ = 10σ/m) for TRAIL and D-dimer was calculated using regression fit. For TRAIL, the residual standard deviation (Sy.x) of 1.503 and the corresponding slope yielded an LOD of 3.3 pg/mL and an LOQ of 11 pg/mL, allowing sensitive detection of even subtle concentration fluctuations. In the case of D-dimer, LOD and LOQ were calculated to be 6.6 and 22 ng/mL, respectively, using a Sy.x of 2.877 and slope near the lower asymptote. This is well below the tested lower bound range of the D-dimer concentration. Comparison of the analytical performance with the previously documented different COVID-related biomarker sensor listed in Supplementary Table S5.

The calibration of the dose–response study establishes that the developed sensor has high sensitivity for both markers for a wider concentration range. This may be attributed to the specific functionalization as well as the unique sensor design, and non-faradaic design of signal collection ensures minimal perturbation of biological samples. The surface-engineered ZnO semiconducting nanofilm resulted in a high surface-to-volume ratio, leading to immobilization of a large amount of target molecules onto the electrode surface. Thus, the developed sensor showed a sensitive detection. Furthermore, it can be concluded that the developed sensor is potentially capable of distinguishing and monitoring healthy cohorts and patients.

Electrochemical Detection of TRAIL and D-Dimer

On the basis of the calibrated and reliable sensor with a wider sensing range, the accuracy of the dual-marker sensor was validated by spiking known concentrations onto the surface. The percentage response corresponding to each known dose increase was measured to deduce the unknown concentration from the established calibrated dose–response curve. Hence, the correlation between the spiked concentration and the recovered concentration from the established regression equation determines the flexibility and accuracy of the developed sensor. As shown in Figure A,B, a linear relationship between the spiked and recovered concentrations of TRAIL and the D-dimer was observed with R 2 = 0.99 for both the biomarkers across the tested dynamic ranges tested. This result confirms that the developed sensor is flexible to accurately quantify target biomolecules in complex matrices with minimal buffer effect interference.

To assess the specificity, TRAIL was introduced onto the sensor designed for D-dimer, and similarly, D-dimer was evaluated on the sensor designed for TRAIL. This examines the potential interference or false signal responses, ensuring that each sensor exhibited binding and detection toward its specific target marker. As the results indicate, the nonspecific signal response obtained for low, med, and high doses of D-dimer and TRAIL on the biosensor specific for TRAIL and D-dimer, respectively, was within ±20% error. The result establishes the ability of the developed dual-marker sensor to distinguish between TRAIL and D-dimer with minimal cross-reactivity. In addition, the specificity of the sensor was evaluated by introducing a cocktail containing both TRAIL markers and D-dimer onto each sensor surface. The measured signal response was compared to the response obtained from its intended marker to determine the influence of nonspecific binding or interference, as shown in Figure C,D. In both biomarkers, the signal from the combined condition matched the specific-only response, indicating minimal interference from nonspecific binding. The results demonstrate that the developed sensor has minimal cross-reactivity with other potential biomarkers present along with the target analyte. Reproducibility and Repeatability Studies

Reproducibility and repeatability are fundamental aspects in evaluating the reliability and performance of sensor platforms. In this study, these parameters were determined through both intra- and interassay variability, using the coefficient of variation (%CV) as a degree of performance consistency (Tables S3 and S4; Supporting Information). For repeatability in the intra-assay, where the consistency of the measurement within a sensor is observed, the %CV measures over n = 8 for both the TRAIL and the D-dimer at various concentrations. The %CV calculated values remained below 10% for both markers as seen in Figure S2A,B. The %CV values remained below 10%, which is well within the acceptable range as per the CLSI guidelines. This result reveals that the developed dual-marker sensor produces highly repeatable results within an individual sensor unit. For the interassay reproducibility, the variation between the signal response obtained from different sensor units was assessed. Figure S2C,D show that for both markers of interest, the %CV values were below 20% for different concentrations of the TRAIL and D dimer. A consistent value below 20%, which aligns with the CLSI standards, indicates minimal variability between sensor units and confirms the reproducibility of the portable sensing platform. Taken together, these data highlight the robust performance of the sensor platform and its ability to provide accurate and consistent measurements across multiple sensors.

PCCODE: Classifier Framework Grounded in Output Concentrations

Overall, the performance of the sensor was observed to be consistent across all of the experiments, demonstrating strong reliability, accuracy, and sensitivity while detecting TRAIL and D-dimer in plasma. The observed results met the CLSI recommended standards with minimal background noise or variability. With this solid analytical foundation, we now shift the focus to developing a classifier model for the PCCODE sensor that can turn sensor readings into meaningful diagnostic insights. To achieve this, we have considered the biological significance of these biomarkers looking at how lowered TRAIL and elevated D-dimer concentrations relate to the post-COVID conditions.

To move beyond univariate biomarker interpretation and toward mechanism-driven diagnosis, we developed a classifier framework that stratifies post-COVID health status based on the output of biosensor-derived biomarker concentrations. This helps to minimize the variation resulting from the factors known to influence the impedance signals, batch-to-batch variability, user handling, and buffer differences while reinforcing strengthening clinical relevance through interpretable thresholds.

As shown in Figure A, TRAIL and D-dimer reflect two distinct but interconnected post-COVID pathophysiologies: immune exhaustion and coagulation dysregulation, respectively. Thresholds were established using exogenously spiked plasma samples, where TRAIL and D-dimer concentrations were independently varied to reflect the biologically relevant conditions. TRAIL levels of 50 pg/mL and D-dimer levels of 1000 ng/mL were chosen to simulate immune exhaustion and coagulation dysregulation, respectively, pathways previously implicated in PCC. In particular, these biomarker changes align with mechanistic reports of apoptosis impairment and persistent thrombotic signaling in PCC patients (Table S6; Supporting Information). −

Diagnostic Stratification Using PCCODE-Derived TRAIL and D-Dimer Concentrations

To evaluate PCCODE diagnostic stratification capabilities, the PCCODE sensor was used directly on the same exogenously spiked plasma data set that supported dose–response calibration and spike-recovery validation studies. No separate simulation or clinical cohort data set was introduced for this study. This methodology ensured that diagnostic logic was anchored in controlled and biologically representative conditions by maintaining complete traceability between sensor output and classifier performance, thereby grounding diagnostic rules in such conditions. Classifier logic was based on threshold concentrations defined at TRAIL <50 pg/mL and D-dimer >1000 ng/mL, reflecting post-COVID immune exhaustion and coagulation dysregulation, respectively. Samples exceeding both thresholds were classified as likely post-COVID, while alternative combinations mapped to healthy immune dysregulation or recovered with thrombosis, depending on which biomarker deviated from baseline.

Visual Mapping of Diagnostic Logic: TRAIL vs D-Dimer Heatmap

To visualize and validate the mechanistic boundaries encoded within the PCCODE classifier, we specifically varied the biomarker concentrations for TRAIL and D-dimer across biologically relevant ranges using exogenously spiked plasma samples. The heatmap was constructed using Boolean-assigned values for each diagnostic zone: 0 (immune dysregulation, 1 (non-PCC), 2 (recovered + thrombotic risk), and 3 (probably post-COVID). As depicted in Figure S.3, the classifier correctly stratifies the biomarker landscape into four biologically interpretable zones. TRAIL concentrations below 50 pg/mL were consistently assigned to immune dysregulation (zone 0) in all D-dimer values <1000 ng/mL, while the same TRAIL range at elevated D-dimer levels (>1000 ng/mL) reliably indicated post-COVID likely (zone 3). On the contrary, TRAIL concentrations 62.5 pg/mL predicted non-PCC status (zone 1) under low D-dimer conditions but shifted to recovered + thrombotic risk (zone 2) when the D-dimer exceeded the 1000 ng/mL threshold. This orthogonal separation validates the monotonicity and adherence of the classifier boundary, enabling precise diagnostic assignment of raw biomarker inputs.

Conclusions and Future Perspectives

The development of a semiconductor-based biosensor platform, incorporating cross-linkers and antibody-based capture probes, was successfully validated by using electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). This dual-analyte sensor demonstrated exceptional sensitivity and specificity in detecting TRAIL and D-dimer biomarkers within human pooled plasma samples. The platform achieved notable detection limits (LOD), measuring 3.3 pg/mL for TRAIL and 6.6 ng/mL for D-dimer, facilitating the identification of clinically significant concentrations. The minimal sample volume requirement of the system (approximately 5 μL), rapid detection capability, and the user-friendly design make it ideal for real-time bedside diagnostics. This multiplexed biosensing strategy enables dual monitoring of inflammatory markers, providing valuable diagnostic insights into conditions such as COVID-19 and associated postviral complications. By integration of a concentration-guided classifier model based on the threshold for TRAIL and D-dimer concentration, the platform advances beyond simple detection-offering Boolean-encoded stratification into four diagnostic states that reflect biologically distinct post-COVID endotypes. Using exogenously spiked plasma samples across calibrated signal ranges, the classifier was developed to simulate disease-specific biomarker profiles and validate zone separation through heatmap-based mapping. This logic-driven architecture ensures robust binary classification between healthy, immune-dominant, coagulation-dominant, and codysregulated state-anchoring diagnosis in pathophysiological relevance rather than arbitrary cutoff points. By facilitating early detection and continuous monitoring of patients, the platform supports timely medical decision-making, which can significantly improve patient outcomes. In conclusion, this work represents a substantial advancement in the development of portable point-of-care diagnostic tools for rapid and sensitive biomarker detection. Integration of such biosensors into personalized medicine frameworks, particularly when combined with wearable or continuous monitoring systems, has the potential to transform patient care. These biosensors represent a significant advancement in enhancing diagnostic accuracy, enabling timely interventions, and improving disease management across a broad spectrum of clinical settings.

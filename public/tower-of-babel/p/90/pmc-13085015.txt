Successful full-term pregnancy after bilateral uterine artery embolization for a large intracavitary submucosal fibroid: A case report
Authors: Mohammed S Almasri, Fahad A Almejrad, Shaikha S Alsharari, Huda K Almayouf, Adeeb O Bazuhair
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractUterine artery embolization is an established treatment for symptomatic fibroids, yet concerns persist regarding reproductive outcomes, particularly with intracavitary submucosal lesions. The current report describes the case of a 31-year-old woman who presented with severe menorrhagia and transfusion-dependent anemia. Investigations, including pelvic magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), demonstrated a large intracavitary submucosal fibroid completely occupying the endometrial cavity. She underwent an uncomplicated bilateral uterine artery embolization using polyvinyl alcohol particles, achieving near stasis on the left side and complete stasis on the right. Sixteen months later, she achieved spontaneous conception and experienced an uncomplicated antenatal course and healthy full-term delivery. No evidence of fibroid expulsion, infection, sepsis, or obstetric morbidity was observed. This case suggests that favorable reproductive and obstetric outcomes may be achievable after uterine artery embolization even for large intracavitary fibroids. It contributes to the growing evidence supporting uterine artery embolization as an alternative for women seeking fertility preservation in cases with similar characteristics.Keywords: Uterine artery embolization, Submucosal fibroid, Fertility preservation, Interventional radiology, Pregnancy outcomes

Introduction

Uterine fibroids represent the most prevalent benign uterine neoplasm in reproductive-age women and are a major source of abnormal uterine bleeding and anemia [1]. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) provides superior delineation of fibroid morphology, vascularity, and intracavitary extension, which directly informs treatment strategy and reproductive counseling [1], [2], [3]. Submucosal fibroids are strongly associated with heavy menstrual bleeding and reduced fertility due to distortion of the endometrial cavity [4].

Uterine artery embolization (UAE) offers a minimally invasive alternative to myomectomy and hysterectomy, achieving significant symptomatic improvement with shorter recovery times and lower morbidity [5]. Despite these advantages, concerns about the impact of UAE on ovarian reserve and pregnancy outcomes persist [6], [7]. Evidence suggests that while transient changes in ovarian function may occur, reproductive outcomes in appropriately selected young patients remain favorable [8], [9]. Several studies document successful conception and live birth following UAE, although outcomes vary with patient age, fibroid characteristics, and embolization technique [5], [6], [10].

Large intracavitary submucosal fibroids remain a challenging subset, and data regarding fertility after UAE in these cases are limited [11], [12]. The present case describes a young woman with a large submucosal myxoid fibroid treated with bilateral UAE using polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) particles, who subsequently achieved an uncomplicated full-term pregnancy and healthy live birth two years after the procedure.

Case Presentation

Clinical Background and Diagnostic Workup

A 31-year-old woman presented with a long history of heavy menstrual bleeding that had progressively worsened over the preceding five years. Her symptoms were severe enough to result in chronic anemia, necessitating multiple hospital admissions and repeated blood transfusions. She was gravida 4 para 4 (G4P4), two children delivered vaginally and two by cesarean section.

Gynecological assessment revealed longstanding menorrhagia with pelvic pain and no urinary symptoms. Further laboratory investigations showed a low hemoglobin level of 9.1 g/dL (reference range, 12–16 g/dL), slightly elevated platelet count of 487 × 103/μL (reference range, 150–400 × 103/μL), and normal leukocyte count of 8.49 × 103/μL (reference range, 4–11 × 103/μL). Coagulation parameters demonstrated normal results; international normalized ratio (INR) of 0.99 (reference range, 0.8–1.2), a prothrombin time (PT) of 12.9 s (reference range, 12.3–14.7 s), and a normal activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) of 34.7 s (reference range, 26–40 s).

Pelvic MRI revealed a submucosal fibroid measuring 8.2 × 6.8 × 8.0 cm with a total volume of 231.9 cm3. The lesion demonstrated low T1 signal intensity, intermediate to high T2 signal, and heterogeneous post-contrast enhancement consistent with myxoid degeneration. The endometrial interface was preserved without evidence of disruption or myometrial invasion. Based on the FIGO leiomyoma classification system, the lesion was consistent with a FIGO type 0 submucosal fibroid, completely occupying the endometrial cavity without intramural extension. Both ovaries appeared normal [Fig. 1]. After multidisciplinary discussion, the patient was referred to interventional radiology for uterine artery embolization (UAE), which was performed as a treatment for severe menorrhagia.

Note the preserved surrounding myometrium and no evidence of myometrial invasion. The ovaries appear unremarkable.

The Procedure

The procedure was performed under local anesthesia using 2% lidocaine with conscious sedation. Access to the right common femoral artery was established in retrograde fashion under ultrasound guidance, followed by advancement of a 5 French sheath over a guidewire. A Berenstein (BERN) catheter was advanced to selectively cannulate the left uterine artery. Angiography demonstrated a pronounced tumor blush corresponding to the fibroid (Fig. 2, Fig. 3). To achieve effective fibroid devascularization while minimizing distal non-target tissue damage, embolization was performed using 500–700 μm PVA particles injected until near stasis was achieved (marked slowing of antegrade flow with persistent contrast column for several cardiac cycles) (Fig. 4). The right uterine artery was then catheterized, and 500–700 μm PVA particles were again injected till complete stasis of forward flow was achieved (complete cessation of forward flow within the embolized branches with no further antegrade contrast progression) (Fig. 5). Super-selective catheterization was not performed in this case, as both uterine arteries demonstrated large caliber on angiography. No significant ovarian arterial contribution to the fibroid vascularization was identified after embolization. Hemostasis was secured by an Angio-Seal vascular closure device. The procedure was successful, achieving bilateral uterine artery embolization without complications. The patient was transferred to the ward for monitoring and post-procedural care.

Post-embolization digital subtraction angiography of the left uterine artery showing near-complete resolution of tumor blush with contrast stasis in the embolized branches, consistent with a satisfactory embolization endpoint.

Pre-embolization digital subtraction angiography of the right uterine artery demonstrating a hyper-vascular intracavitary fibroid with prominent arterial feeders and dense tumor blush prior to embolization.

Post-embolization digital subtraction angiography of the right uterine artery demonstrating complete absence of tumor blush with contrast stasis in the embolized arterial branches, consistent with a satisfactory embolization endpoint.

Follow-Up

On follow-up, the patient was monitored on the ward, where she was tolerating orally, passing bowel motion, and maintained vitals within normal ranges. Pain was controlled with a single intramuscular dose of pethidine (100 mg) and intravenous paracetamol administered as needed.” The patient was safely discharged from the hospital a few days after the procedure and reported full symptomatic relief on outpatient follow-up basis with no reported expulsion, fever, or abnormal vaginal discharge.

Spontaneous conception was achieved approximately 16 months after the procedure. The antenatal course was uneventful throughout follow-up. Two years after uterine artery embolization, the patient delivered at 37 weeks of gestation by elective cesarean section due to her history of prior cesarean deliveries. No intraoperative or postpartum hemorrhage was observed. The newborn was a healthy infant weighing 2.93 kg, with APGAR scores of 8, 9, and 9. Both maternal and neonatal outcomes were favorable.

Discussion

Uterine artery embolization (UAE), first reported for fibroid treatment in 1995, is now a well-established minimally invasive uterine-sparing therapy for symptomatic uterine fibroids [13]. The efficacy and safety of UAE as a treatment approach is well documented in the literature, with shorter recovery times than surgery [14]. Despite procedural efficacy for symptom relief, the effect of UAE on fertility and pregnancy outcomes remains controversial. Some current guidelines emphasize the uncertainty surrounding fertility after UAE and consider the available evidence to be limited [15], while other recommendations advise caution and discourage the routine use of UAE in young patients with fibroids who are seeking conception [16].

This uncertainty is particularly relevant for submucosal fibroids, as these lesions often distort the endometrial cavity and are associated with impaired fertility and an increased risk of miscarriage. Removal of these lesions is likely to improve fertility outcomes [17]. Because myomectomy is a well-established first-line approach for many cavity-distorting submucosal fibroids, reports of successful pregnancy after UAE in a clearly intracavitary context help refine patient counseling regarding alternatives when surgery is undesirable or high-risk [18].

The present case is particularly notable because it combines two unfavorable features, namely a completely intracavitary location and a large fibroid volume, yet resulted in spontaneous conception occurring approximately 16 months after the procedure. When considering the time to conception, the interval observed in this case falls within the reported range after UAE [6]. Bucuri et al. reported a pooled mean time to conception of approximately 14.7 months following UAE in a meta-analysis. However, intervals approaching 18 months have been observed in patients with submucosal fibroids compared with intramural and subserosal types [6].

In addition, pregnancy rates have also been reported to be lower in these lesions compared with other fibroid types [17]. Pregnancy outcomes are influenced by multiple factors, including fibroid size, with lower pregnancy rates reported in patients with a total fibroid volume exceeding 200 cm3 [6]. In the present case, the fibroid volume exceeded 200 cm3. Nevertheless, the patient achieved a successful full-term pregnancy despite these unfavorable factors [6]. These findings should be interpreted cautiously, as confounders and study populations vary considerably across literature [19]. Karlsen et al. in a systematic review of 17 studies reported an approximately 50% pregnancy rate after UAE in randomized controlled trials, with variable rates in other study designs, and emphasized that the overall quality of evidence remains very low and the estimates uncertain [19].

The obstetric safety after conception has been investigated previously, with some studies suggesting that adverse obstetric events may occur more frequently after UAE [20]. A meta-analysis comparing completed pregnancies after UAE with age and fibroid location matched pregnancies in women with fibroids reported higher miscarriage, cesarean delivery, and postpartum hemorrhage rates, with similar rates of preterm delivery and fetal growth restriction [20]. In contrast, the present case demonstrated an uncomplicated antenatal course and postpartum period without hemorrhagic or placental complications, providing an additional favorable clinical observation in the literature, where risks remain debated and incompletely quantified for specific fibroid subtypes [15]. Although cesarean delivery was performed, it was primarily attributed to the patient's history of multiple prior cesarean sections and therefore cannot be directly interpreted as a UAE-related outcome.

Importantly, the intracavitary location introduces UAE-specific risks that make uncomplicated outcomes particularly informative. Reviews report a wide incidence range of fibroid expulsion after UAE and identify intracavitary submucosal lesions as a subgroup at higher risk [13]. Prior studies further highlight that these fibroids are susceptible to post-embolization necrosis with subsequent sloughing into the uterine cavity which can lead to infection and sepsis [13], [15], [16]. The absence of expulsion-related sepsis or obstetric morbidity in this case of a large intracavitary lesion supports the evolving evidence of the safety and efficacy of bilateral UAE in large submucosal fibroids using PVA [5], [12]. This case has several limitations, including the lack of documented contrast volume administered during the procedure, the absence of recorded procedural time, and the lack of post-procedural imaging follow-up.

Conclusion

While large intracavitary submucosal fibroids are frequently managed surgically, the present case demonstrates spontaneous conception and an uncomplicated antenatal course followed by healthy full-term delivery after bilateral UAE using PVA particles. The favorable maternal and obstetric outcome suggests that fertility may be preserved even in the setting of a large intracavitary lesion. Although conclusions regarding comparative safety or effectiveness cannot be drawn from a single case, this report adds to the growing evidence that UAE may represent an alternative treatment approach. Continued reporting of well-documented cases and prospective studies will further clarify reproductive outcomes and help guide patient counseling for women with similar fibroid characteristics.

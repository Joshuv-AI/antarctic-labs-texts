Comment on “The efficacy of artificial intelligence in predicting mortality rate and cardiogenic shock in acute coronary syndrome patients”
Authors: Venkata Reddy Medikonda, Nalavala Ramanjaneya Reddy, Ch Rajyalakshmi
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We read with great interest the study by Liastuti et al. [1]. synthesizing AI model performance in predicting mortality and cardiogenic shock in patients with ACS.

Of particular concern is the pooled mortality area under the curve (AUC) of 0.89, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.00–1.00. An interval spanning the full mathematical range of the statistic conveys no precision whatsoever; yet, this finding anchors both the abstract and the conclusion. The same uninformative interval recurred across nearly every model-specific AUC in the subgroup analysis, including logistic regression, random forest, and XGBoost. This pattern across independent subgroup calculations points toward a systematic limitation in how variance was estimated for the summary receiver operating characteristic model, most plausibly the insufficient extraction of standard errors or confidence bounds from primary studies, rather than isolated reporting errors [2]. A pooled estimate accompanied by a non-informative interval cannot support claims of improved risk stratification, regardless of the apparent magnitude of the point estimate.

This distinction has direct clinical implications. A clinician evaluating whether AI-based triage could supplement GRACE or TIMI scoring needs to know whether 0.89 reflects a precise, reproducible signal, or an artifact of unstable pooling [3]. As presented, the AUC findings cannot bear that interpretive weight, even though the pooled sensitivity and specificity estimates (with appropriately bounded intervals) retain their interpretive value.

Clarifying the source of this interval width and whether AUC-based conclusions should be withdrawn or substantially qualified would meaningfully strengthen this otherwise timely synthesis [4].

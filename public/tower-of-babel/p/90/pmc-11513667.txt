Introduction

Hydatidosis is a worldwide zoonosis caused by larval stages of Echinococcus granulosus [1]. This disease is endemic in various parts of the world [1] and represents a public health problem in Tunisia [2]. The brain is a rare site where this disease occurs, especially in adults, and most cysts are usually located in the supratentorial compartment [2], mainly in the parietal lobe in the area of the middle cerebral artery, while the occipital location is extremely unusual [2]. Cerebral hydatidosis is usually primary, solitary and can be unilocular or multilocular [3]. Multiple cerebral hydatid cysts are rare and usually occur secondary to a traumatic or surgical rupture [4]. However, primary multiple hydatidosis is extremely rare even in endemic areas and only isolated cases have been reported in the literature [4]. Here, we present the case of primary bilateral occipital hydatid cysts in a 36-year-old Tunisian man who underwent successful surgical excision with two craniotomies in a single procedure. The aim of this case report is to highlight the presentation, diagnostic process and surgical treatment of this rare entity.

The work has been reported in line with the SCARE criteria and the revised 2023 SCARE guidelines [5].

Case report

A 36-year-old Tunisian patient with no medical history from a rural area was admitted to our institution for three months of headaches and visual disturbances. He reported no seizures, vomiting, or fever. During the admission period, examination revealed that the patient was conscious, afebrile, hemodynamically stable, oxygen levels were normal, and ophthalmological examination revealed bilateral decreased visual acuity without papilledema. There were no abnormalities in his visual field, eye alignment, and binocular function. The biological examinations showed the following results: leukocyte count of 11,640 cells/mm3, eosinophil count was within the normal range, hemoglobin of 12.2 g/dL, platelets of 278,600 cells/mm3 and C-reactive protein of 8 mg/L. Serological tests for hydatidosis were negative. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain showed two lesions: one in the left occipital region measuring 5 × 5 × 5.5 cm, the other in the right occipital lobe measuring 7.5 × 8 × 7.5 cm. The appearance of the lesions was hypointense on T1-weighted image (WI) and hyperintense on T2-WI, with no enhancement after gadolinium injection and no surrounding edema (Fig. 1). Thoracoabdominal computed tomography (CT) was performed to search for other organ involvement, but no other location could be found.Fig. 1Axial sections of a brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) on T1-weighted imaging (WI) without contrast injection (A) and T2-WI (B) showing bilateral occipital hydatid cysts.Fig. 1

Due to this life-threatening and visually threatening position of the cysts, the patient underwent emergency surgery. Intraoperatively, bilateral occipital craniotomies were performed and the cysts were completely removed in a single procedure, with each cyst undergoing craniotomy. To deliver the cysts safely and without rupture, we performed wide exposure on each side and used the gentle hydraulic dissection technique of Arana-Iniguez [6] (Fig. 2), the effect of gravity and the Valsalva technique.Fig. 2Delivery of the cysts using the hydraulic dissection technique of Arana-Iniguez: Right (A) and Left (B).Fig. 2

Pathological examination of the cysts confirmed the diagnosis. There were two cysts (right and left) measuring 7.5 × 8 × 7.5 cm and 5 × 5 × 5.5 cm, respectively. When sectioned, the cysts were unilocular and filled with a white gelatin membrane (Fig. 3). Microscopic examination confirmed the diagnosis of hydatidosis. Immediate surgical follow-up was uneventful, no complications were noted, and the patient recovered with improvement in visual impairment in the early postoperative period. A follow-up brain computed tomography (CT) scan confirmed complete removal of the cysts and the appearance of the surgical site was satisfactory (Fig. 4).Fig. 3Separated cysts from the brain.Fig. 3Fig. 4Follow-up Computed Tomography scan confirming full removal of the cysts.Fig. 4

The patient was discharged from the hospital after 5 days. Medical treatment with albendazole was prescribed. The patient was followed up for 1 year and showed no side effects of medical treatment or signs of recurrence.

Discussion

Hydatidosis is a cosmopolitan zoonosis [1], it is considered epidemiologically endemic in many regions of the world and represents a public health problem in Tunisia [2]. This disease in humans is caused by the larval form of Echinococcus granulosus [1]. Generally, humans become infected by ingesting dog tapeworm eggs due to contaminated food and water [7]. The embryo penetrates the intestinal mucosa and reaches the liver via the portal circulation. Only embryos that manage to pass through the liver and lung filter systems reach the brain via the systemic circulation [4]. Hydatid cysts are usually found in the liver and lungs but may develop elsewhere. Cerebral hydatidosis is a very rare disease, especially in adults [2,3]. It is more common in children and young adults (50 to 70 % of cases) [2,7]. Most cysts are usually located in the supratentorial compartment [2]. The parietal lobe is the most commonly affected area when the occipital location is extremely unusual [2]. This rarity is explained by the existence of liver and lung filters that protect the brain [3,8].

Intracranial hydatid cysts can be primary or secondary. The primary cysts are usually solitary and arise from direct penetration of the larvae into the brain. Secondary cysts are usually multiple and arise from traumatic or surgical rupture of the primary fertile cyst [3]. Primary multiple bilateral cerebral cysts are extremely rare even in endemic areas [4]. They occur more frequently in children [9]. Each cyst has a separate peri cyst with brood capsule protoscoleces [10]. The pathogenetic mechanisms of this entity are not yet precisely known. Some authors proposed some theories by which primary multiple hydatid cysts may be caused [11]. The common theory is repeated ingestion of multiple echinococcal eggs. In addition, the presence of a patent ductus arteriosus, which allows blood flow from right to left bypassing the lungs, may be a predisposing factor for it [11]. Symptoms of primary multiple hydatid cysts vary depending on the area of the brain affected. When it comes to the occipital lobes, patients often experience neurological symptoms such as headaches and visual disturbances due to the mass effect of the cysts [12]. MRI and CT scans help in diagnosis and appropriate treatment. CT often reveals a rounded, isodense and well-defined cystic lesion without marginal enhancement and without surrounding edema [13]. In our case, MRI shows some aspects that were not clearly visible with CT, including multiplicity, perilesional edema, and the peri cyst. MRI is the gold standard and extremely beneficial for surgical strategy [13,14]. MRI appearance of intracranial hydatid cysts is characterized by well-defined round cystic areas containing fluid with intensity values similar to that of cerebrospinal fluid. Generally, the lesions do not display enhancement, but contrast enhancement of the compressed brain may be found, but this depends on the size of the cysts [14]. The differential diagnosis includes other cystic lesions such as arachnoid and porencephalic cysts, which are extra-axial and are not as round as hydatid cysts, cystic tumors, most often represented by pilocytic astrocytomas containing nodules that enhance after contrast injection and brain abscesses that have a thick wall which enhance after contrast medium injection and are surrounded by edema [15]. The differential diagnosis between primary and secondary cysts may not be clear. However, in our case, due to the bilateral location of the cysts and because there was no evidence of hydatid cysts in other organs, it can be categorically assumed that it was a case of multiple primary echinococcosis [16].

The treatment of a hydatid cyst is exclusively surgical. The most commonly used surgical technique is the Drowling technique, later refined by Arana-Iniguez, which consists of expelling the cyst in the block by gentle hydro dissection and introducing hypertonic saline under and around the cyst to avoid rupture [5,16]. In the case of multiple cysts, as in the case discussed here, the principles of the operation are aimed at removing intact cysts, removing each cyst, if possible, together with the peri cyst and the surrounding gliotic brain tissue to prevent recurrence [16]. The key to the successful outcome in this case lies in the wide exposure, gentle hydro dissection, the use of the effect of gravity in delivery and the application of the Valsalva technique, which represent an important factor in the safe delivery of the cysts [17]. Sudden injections of saline should be avoided. If a rupture occurs, the cyst contents should be carefully aspirated and the entire surgical area should be irrigated with scolicidal agents [18].

Although cerebral hydatidosis is considered a benign disease in most studies, multiple and bilateral cysts can cause neurological sequelae and postoperative complications and be a cause of morbidity and mortality [19]. Medical treatment with albendazole is generally used to treat intraoperative cyst rupture, recurrence, disseminated and primary multiple cysts as in our case [20]. Long-term follow-up and iterative imaging must be performed to screen for recurrence [20].

Conclusion

Primary multiple bilateral intracerebral hydatid cysts in adults are extremely rare even in endemic areas such as Tunisia and our literature search revealed only a few cases. The pathogenetic mechanisms of this disease are not yet well known and an accurate diagnosis is based on imaging techniques, particularly MRI, which is the gold standard. As in our case, the key to an optimal result lies in a careful surgical technique that avoids perioperative ruptures and complications. Postoperative administration of antihelminthics is crucial to prevent recurrence. With a careful surgical strategy and appropriate postoperative management, patients can recover without neurological sequelae.

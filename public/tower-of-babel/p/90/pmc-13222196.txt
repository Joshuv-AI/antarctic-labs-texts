Safety and Effectiveness Analysis of Dolutegravir and Dolutegravir/Abacavir/Lamivudine in Japanese People Living with HIV: 10 years of Post-Marketing Surveillance
Authors: Akemi Sebata, Takako Nagao, Tomoko Matsukawa, Yuko Maeno, Miwako Suzuki, Yoichiro Yoshikawa, Shiro Ibe, Akiko Fukuda, Dong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionDolutegravir (DTG), an integrase strand transfer inhibitor, is recommended as a first-line antiretroviral therapy (ART) for HIV/AIDS in international guidelines. In Japan, approved DTG-containing formulations include the single-agent DTG (Tivicay® tablets) and the fixed-dose combination of DTG, abacavir sulfate, and lamivudine (DTG/ABC/3TC, Triumeq® combination tablets). Although overseas clinical trials have demonstrated the favorable safety and effectiveness of these DTG-containing products, evidence specific to Japanese people living with HIV/AIDS (PLHIV) has remained limited. This report presents the final findings from a decade-long, prospective, post-marketing surveillance of single-agent DTG and DTG/ABC/3TC initiated in 2014.MethodsData on the real-world clinical use of the single-agent DTG and DTG/ABC/3TC were obtained through the HIV-Related Drug Cooperative Survey, a national joint post-marketing program overseen by Japanese manufacturers of HIV therapies. Adverse drug reactions (ADRs) were captured and classified using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities. Associations between ADR incidence and potential risk factors were examined. Effectiveness was evaluated by longitudinal changes in plasma HIV RNA copy number and peripheral CD4+ cell counts.ResultsAmong 2345 PLHIV included in the safety analysis, 652 patients (27.80%) reported ADRs. The most frequently reported ADRs were increased blood creatinine (4.78%), abnormal hepatic function (2.43%), and nausea (2.30%). ADR incidence was higher in participants with baseline comorbidities, those receiving concomitant medications, and ART-naïve participants, relative to their counterparts. Notably, no progressive increase in ADR incidence was observed during prolonged use > 2 years. Plasma HIV RNA levels demonstrated sustained improvements from baseline in both ART-naïve and ART-experienced participants. By 12 months after administration, the proportion of participants achieving plasma HIV RNA levels < 50 copies/ml was about 90% in ART-naïve participants and about 96% in ART-experienced participants. Additionally, CD4+ T cell counts also demonstrated sustained improvement from baseline in both ART-naïve and ART-experienced participants.ConclusionThis 10-year post-marketing surveillance confirms the long-term safety and effectiveness of single-agent DTG and DTG/ABC/3TC in Japanese PLHIV. Introduction

Human immunodeficiency virus/acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (HIV/AIDS) continues to represent a major global health challenge. Although AIDS-related mortality has steadily declined since peaking in 2004, approximately 630,000 deaths were still attributed to the disease in 2023, while an estimated 31 million individuals worldwide were receiving lifesaving antiretroviral therapy (ART) [1]. In Japan, the cumulative number of people living with HIV/AIDS (PLHIV) surpassed 35,000 by the end of 2023 [2], underscoring the ongoing clinical need for effective treatment. The widespread implementation of ART—based on combinations of multiple antiretrovirals (ARVs)—has markedly improved the prognosis of PLHIV [1, 3]. Nevertheless, the long-term success of ART remains constrained by challenges such as the emergence of drug resistance, adverse effects with prolonged therapy, and clinically significant drug-drug interactions [3, 4]. These limitations have prompted the development of new agents with improved safety and efficacy profiles.

Dolutegravir (DTG), an integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI), is one such agent that has become a cornerstone of HIV therapy [5, 6]. DTG was first approved as a single-agent tablet (Tivicay®, ViiV Healthcare) in the US in 2013 and subsequently in Europe and Japan in 2014. In the same year, DTG was also approved in combination with abacavir sulfate (ABC) and lamivudine (3TC) as a fixed-dose formulation (DTG/ABC/3TC, Triumeq®, ViiV Healthcare) in the US and Europe, with approval in Japan following in 2015. More recently, additional DTG-based combinations, such as DTG/rilpivirine (Juluca®, ViiV Healthcare) and DTG/3TC (Dovato®, ViiV Healthcare), have been licensed in several countries as of 2025. Clinical studies have demonstrated that DTG provides potent and durable virologic suppression, a high genetic barrier to resistance, limited potential for drug-drug interactions, and favorable tolerability [5, 6]. Consequently, DTG-containing regimens are recommended as preferred first-line treatment options for most PLHIV in international and regional guidelines, including those established in Japan [7–11]. The efficacy and safety of both single-agent DTG and DTG/ABC/3TC have been consistently demonstrated in ART-naïve and ART-experienced populations across numerous pivotal clinical trials [12–22].

However, prior to regulatory approval in Japan, no domestic clinical trials of DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC had been conducted, and evidence regarding their long-term safety and effectiveness in Japanese PLHIV remained limited. To address this gap, we participated in the nationwide HIV-Related Drug (HRD) Cooperative Survey, a post-marketing surveillance program designed to monitor the clinical use of these products in Japan. Interim results from this surveillance, covering a 6-year period, showed no new safety or effectiveness concerns in Japanese clinical practice [23]. The present report describes the final results of this 10-year surveillance, providing comprehensive evidence on the long-term safety and effectiveness of single-agent DTG and DTG/ABC/3TC in Japanese PLHIV.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

This investigation was a post-marketing, prospective surveillance study designed to evaluate the long-term safety and effectiveness of single-agent DTG and DTG/ABC/3TC in Japanese PLHIV.

This surveillance was conducted within the framework of the HRD Cooperative Survey, a nationwide joint post-marketing program organized by Japanese manufacturers of ARVs under the supervision of the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW) [23]. Participating sites were exclusively specialized facilities with HIV specialists and a high volume of patients. Data included in this report were obtained from 28 centers across Japan that contributed to the HRD Cooperative Survey and granted approval for publication.

The surveillance period extended over 10 years, beginning with the initial market release of single-agent DTG in Japan. Enrollment commenced on April 17, 2014, for single-agent DTG and on April 10, 2015, for DTG/ABC/3TC, and continued through March 31, 2022. All PLHIV registered to the HRD Cooperative Survey during this timeframe who received either product were eligible for inclusion. Participant follow-up was maintained until March 31, 2023, with the database locked on March 23, 2024. The treatment initiation date was defined as the first day of single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC administration documented on the survey forms.

This post-marketing surveillance was conducted by CMIC Holdings Co., Ltd., on behalf of ViiV Healthcare K.K. in compliance with the relevant Japanese regulations (Ministerial Ordinance on GPSP; MHLW no. 171 of December 20, 2004, the amended ordinance, and the related notifications).

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the PMDA, the Japanese regulatory authority, and subsequently approved by the ethics committees at each participating study site. Under the GPSP regulations, written informed consent from patients is not required in post-marketing surveillance. However, the protocol specified that aggregated and analyzed results may be disseminated in scientific reports. Before treatment initiation, each potential participant received a detailed explanation regarding the use of the single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC, and verbal consent for therapy was obtained from all individuals before enrollment. All collected data were anonymized.

Treatment

Participants received either single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC in accordance with the approved Japanese prescribing information. Both formulations could be administered without regard to food intake. The single-agent DTG formulation contained 50 mg of DTG per tablet and was prescribed in combination with other ARVs. For treatment-naïve or -experienced INSTI-naïve adults, DTG 50 mg was administered orally once daily. In patients with documented INSTI resistance, a regimen of DTG 50 mg twice daily was permitted. Single-agent DTG was also prescribed once daily to treatment-naïve children and adolescents aged ≥ 12 years with a body weight of at least 40 kg and to those previously treated with ARVs other than INSTIs. DTG/ABC/3TC was indicated exclusively for adults and was administered as a single fixed-dose tablet once daily, containing 50 mg of DTG, 600 mg of abacavir sulfate, and 300 mg of lamivudine.

Variables

Baseline and longitudinal variables collected during the surveillance included demographic and clinical characteristics (sex, pregnancy status, age, inpatient or outpatient status, prior prescription history of ARV, and race), route of infection, disease duration, comorbidities and their types, average daily dose of DTG, cumulative duration and dosage of DTG exposure, use of concomitant medications (number and type), disease severity according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) classification, and presence or absence of blood coagulation factor deficiency.

Throughout the observation period, all adverse events (AEs) were recorded with detailed documentation of onset date, clinical course, management, seriousness, outcome, and suspected causal relationship to underlying disease or study drug. Information on laboratory abnormalities was also collected when available. Adverse drug reactions (ADR) were defined as AEs suspected to be causally related to the use of DTG, with the more severe assessment of causality from either the reporting physician or the sponsor being adopted. When assessments of seriousness of ADRs differed between the physician and the sponsor, the more severe classification was adopted.

ADRs were coded using the preferred term (PT) and system organ class (SOC) of Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities, Japanese version (MedDRA/J), version 26.1. Closely related MedDRA/J PTs were consolidated into ADR groups, as presented in Supplementary Table S1. Safety analyses also included evaluation of important identified and potential risks predefined in the risk management plan. To account for treatment duration, DTG exposure of ≤ 2 years (defined as ≤ 730 days) was categorized as short term, whereas exposure of > 2 years was categorized as long term. ADR incidence was analyzed separately for these categories.

Effectiveness was evaluated by monitoring plasma HIV RNA copy number and peripheral CD4+ T-cell count, using measurement methods employed at each participating facility. The proportion of participants achieving plasma HIV RNA levels < 50 copies/ml was also calculated. Effectiveness outcomes were analyzed separately for ART-naïve and -experienced participants.

Statistical Analysis

The safety analysis set included all participants for whom survey forms were collected, with the exclusion of those who met any of the following criteria: (1) participants who did not return to the study site after their initial visit; (2) participants for whom administration of single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC was not confirmed after the date of initial marketing; (3) participants for whom the presence or absence of AEs was unknown and who could not be followed for AE outcomes.

The effectiveness analysis set comprised all participants in the safety analysis set, excluding those who received single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC as off-label therapy and those without available effectiveness data (defined as missing plasma HIV RNA copy number or peripheral CD4+ T-cell count) both before and after initiation of single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC. Analyses were conducted on pooled data from participants who received at least one of the two DTG-containing products, as well as separately for each product. Participants who received both products during the surveillance period were counted only once in the pooled dataset.

Associations between participant characteristics and the incidence of ADRs were assessed using Fisher’s exact test for dichotomous variables and the chi-square test for variables with three or more categories. All other analyses were descriptive in nature. Missing data were not imputed, and no adjustments were made for multiplicity. Statistical analyses were performed using SAS software, version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Regarding disease severity at baseline, approximately two-thirds of participants (66.95%) were classified as category A under the CDC criteria, while 23.80% were classified as category C, indicating severe disease. Comorbidities were present in 64.43% of participants. Additionally, 20.64% of participants had liver disorders, and 7.04% had renal disorders.

Long-term treatment exposure was common: approximately 80% of participants received single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC for > 730 days. A history of prior ART was documented in approximately half of the population. These baseline characteristics were generally consistent in the effectiveness analysis set. Furthermore, no substantial differences in demographic or clinical characteristics were observed between participants receiving single-agent DTG and those treated with DTG/ABC/3TC (Supplementary Tables S2 and S3).

Overall ADRs

Among the 2345 PLHIV included in the safety analysis set, 652 participants (27.80%) experienced at least one ADR. The incidence of ADRs was 23.55% (431/1830 participants) among those receiving single-agent DTG and 30.85% (253/820 participants) among those treated with DTG/ABC/3TC. Notably, single-agent DTG is always used in combination with other ARVs, with approximately two-thirds being combined with emtricitabine/tenofovir disoproxil fumarate or emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide, about one-third with lamivudine/abacavir sulfate, and the remaining few with other nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors (NRTIs) (data not shown).

The most frequently reported ADRs, occurring in at least 1% of participants, included blood creatinine increased, hepatic function abnormal, nausea, hyperuricemia, insomnia, hypertension, dyslipidemia, diarrhea, and headache (Table 2). These findings were consistent with the most common ADRs identified in separate analyses for single-agent DTG and DTG/ABC/3TC (Supplementary Tables S4 and S5).

Among the ADRs listed in Table 2, the most frequently reported serious events were immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (n = 20), syphilis (n = 20), diabetes mellitus (n = 17), and renal dysfunction (n = 17). Nine of the participants who developed syphilis had a history of infection, and recurrence is recognized as a possibility even in individuals with normal immune function [24]. Risk factors for syphilis, including sexual behavior, number of partners, condom use, socioeconomic status, history of intravenous drug use, and HIV infection, are well established [25]. A causal relationship between HIV infection or concomitant medications and the development of diabetes or renal dysfunction could not be ruled out. Only two participants with diabetes mellitus (both treated with DTG/ABC/3TC) and three with renal dysfunction (1 treated with single-agent DTG and 2 with DTG/ABC/3TC) were considered to have events potentially attributable to drug exposure alone. In this surveillance, all events were classified as ADRs unless both the reporting physician and the sponsor judged the causal relationship to be completely unrelated. Consequently, particularly for frequently observed serious ADRs, it is possible that the incidence rates reflect cautious attribution by physicians who did not fully exclude a potential contribution from single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC.

The incidence of ADRs classified under the SOC of psychiatric disorders was 3.54% (83/2345 participants) overall, 2.79% (51/1830) among single-agent DTG recipients, and 4.02% (33/820) among DTG/ABC/3TC recipients. For nervous system disorders, the overall incidence was 2.13% (50/2345), with rates of 1.58% (29/1830) for single-agent DTG and 2.93% (24/820) for DTG/ABC/3TC.

When stratified by hospitalization status, immune system disorders and blood and lymphatic system disorders were more frequently observed in hospitalized than outpatient participants. Specifically, immune system disorders occurred in 6/93 (6.45%) hospitalized participants compared with 25/2108 (1.19%) outpatients, while blood and lymphatic system disorders occurred in 3/93 (3.23%) hospitalized participants compared with 6/2108 (0.28%) outpatients. SOCs reported exclusively in participants with comorbidities included blood and lymphatic system disorders (10/1511, 0.66%), reproductive system and breast disorders (6/1511, 0.40%), endocrine disorders (4/1511, 0.26%), and pregnancy, puerperium, and perinatal conditions (1/1511, 0.07%).

Participants with renal disorders exhibited a relatively higher incidence of ADRs compared with those without renal disorders. In particular, increased frequencies were observed for eye disorders [3/165 (1.82%) vs 6/2068 (0.29%)], cardiac disorders [3/165 (1.82%) vs 12/2068 (0.58%)], and renal and urinary disorders [8/165 (4.85%) vs 22/2068 (1.06%)]. Similarly, participants with hepatic disorders exhibited substantially higher incidences of several SOCs than those without hepatic disorders, including endocrine disorders [2/484 (0.41%) vs 2/1680 (0.12%)], immune system disorders [14/484 (2.89%) vs 14/1680 (0.83%)], and vascular disorders [16/484 (3.31%) vs 17/1680 (1.01%)]. In addition, alanine aminotransferase elevation [4/484 (0.83%) vs 3/1680 (0.18%)] and aspartate aminotransferase elevation [3/484 (0.62%) vs 0/1680] were reported more frequently in participants with hepatic disorders.

In the separate analysis of single-agent DTG, the factors influencing ADR incidence were generally consistent with those in the overall population, except that hospitalization status and race did not reach statistical significance (Supplementary Table S6). Among participants treated with DTG/ABC/3TC, race, renal disorder, allergy, and number of concomitant ARVs were not significant predictors of ADR incidence (Supplementary Table S7). Across both products, no meaningful differences in ADR types were identified when comparing subgroups with or without the factors that showed statistical significance in the overall analysis. Within the single-agent DTG group, ADRs reported exclusively in participants with comorbidities included vascular disorders (21/1198, 1.75%), cardiac disorders (7/1198, 0.58%), respiratory, thoracic, and mediastinal disorders (6/1198, 0.50%), blood and lymphatic system disorders (6/1198, 0.50%), reproductive system and breast disorders (6/1198, 0.50%), endocrine disorders (3/1198, 0.25%), and pregnancy, puerperium, and perinatal conditions (1/1198, 0.08%).

In the DTG/ABC/3TC group, ADRs by SOC observed exclusively in participants with comorbidities included neoplasms benign, malignant, and unspecified (including cysts and polyps) (8/522, 1.53%), renal and urinary disorders (8/522, 1.53%), blood and lymphatic system disorders (4/522, 0.77%), eye disorders (3/522, 0.57%), injury, poisoning, and procedural complications (3/522, 0.57%), and endocrine disorders (1/522, 0.19%).

Safety Assessment Related to the Risk Management Plan

The incidence of ADRs categorized as important identified risks and important potential risks for each DTG product is presented in Tables 4 and 5. Most participants who developed ADRs classified as the important identified risks achieved recovery following continuation of single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC, discontinuation of single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC, or initiation of targeted therapy for the ADR. Effectiveness Analysis

In ART-naïve participants, plasma HIV RNA copy number declined markedly within 3 months of initiating treatment with either single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC and remained suppressed throughout follow-up (Fig. 2a and Supplementary Table S8). Peripheral CD4+ T-cell counts showed a sustained upward trend beginning at 3 months after treatment initiation (Fig. 3a and Supplementary Table S9). At later time points, a pronounced increase in CD4+ T-cell counts was observed beyond 75 months in participants receiving DTG/ABC/3TC and beyond 90 months in those receiving single-agent DTG. These findings were interpreted cautiously, as the magnitude of change was likely influenced by small sample sizes and considerable variability in the data.

Among participants with prior ART exposure, plasma HIV RNA copy numbers remained durably suppressed after initiation of single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC, irrespective of the previous regimen (Fig. 2b and Supplementary Table S10). Similarly, CD4+ T-cell counts were consistently maintained following initiation of either regimen, regardless of the type of prior ART (Fig. 3b and Supplementary Table S11). An apparent increase in CD4+ T-cell counts at 84 months in participants treated with DTG/ABC/3TC was also considered an artifact of limited sample size and high data variability.

The proportion of participants achieving plasma HIV RNA levels < 50 copies/ml exceeded 90% by 12 months, regardless of prior ART history, and this level of suppression was maintained through 36 months (Table 7). High rates of virologic suppression were also consistently observed across subgroups stratified by baseline HIV RNA copy number, baseline CD4+ T-cell count, and the presence or absence of concomitant medication use (Supplementary Tables S12–S14).

Discussion

In this 10-year post-marketing surveillance, we systematically evaluated the long-term safety and effectiveness of two DTG formulations—single-agent DTG and DTG/ABC/3TC—in Japanese PLHIV. Because DTG is used in combination with other anti-HIV drugs such as NRTIs, the observed AEs may not solely reflect the effects of DTG, and the incidence of AEs could be overestimated. Nevertheless, the overall incidence of ADRs (27.80%) in this population did not exceed the rates previously reported in overseas clinical trials conducted prior to regulatory approval (single-agent DTG, 32.3%; DTG/ABC/3TC, 40.4%) [12–20], and the safety profile remained consistent with information contained in the Japanese package insert. Furthermore, compared with the incidence observed in the 6-year interim analysis of this surveillance (24.65%) [23], no meaningful increase was detected over extended follow-up. Effectiveness outcomes, assessed by plasma HIV RNA copy number and peripheral CD4+ T-cell count, were favorable and sustained throughout long-term treatment. Collectively, these findings confirm the safety and effectiveness of both DTG products in Japanese PLHIV.

Among INSTIs, including DTG, one of the principal safety concerns has been the potential for neuropsychiatric AEs [27, 28]. In the present analysis, ADRs classified under psychiatric disorders occurred in 3.54% of participants, while nervous system disorders occurred in 2.13%. These rates were comparable to those observed in earlier DTG clinical trials [27, 28] and in the 6-year interim analysis of this surveillance (psychiatric disorders, 2.97%; nervous system disorders, 2.01%) [23]. Prior evidence has suggested a possible association between abacavir use and an increased risk of central nervous system or neuropsychiatric events in PLHIV receiving INSTI-based regimens [29]. Consistent with this observation, the present study found numerically higher incidence rates of psychiatric and nervous system disorders in participants treated with DTG/ABC/3TC compared with those receiving single-agent DTG.

The stratified analysis identified several participant characteristics associated with higher incidence of ADRs (Table 3). Factors reflecting poorer overall health status—including hospitalization, presence of comorbidities (particularly renal or hepatic disorders and allergy), and both the use and number of concomitant medications—were significantly associated with increased ADR risk. Regarding hepatic disorders, previous studies have reported that PLHIV coinfected with hepatitis B virus (HBV) or hepatitis C virus (HCV) and treated with DTG exhibited higher frequencies of alanine aminotransferase or aspartate aminotransferase elevation than those receiving control regimens [12–14, 17]. Consistent with these observations, the present surveillance demonstrated numerically higher rates of liver enzyme elevations among participants with pre-existing hepatic disorders. Taken together, these findings suggest that HBV/HCV coinfection and underlying hepatic disease warrant particular consideration when prescribing DTG-containing regimens in the Japanese PLHIV population. Other factors, including prescription history of ARVs, number of concomitant ARVs, and CDC disease classification, were also associated with ADR incidence, although no consistent or interpretable patterns emerged.

From a pharmacovigilance perspective, no new safety concerns were identified relative to the information available at the time of regulatory approval. Among ADRs reported at frequencies of ≥ 1%, hyperuricemia, hypertension, and dyslipidemia were not commonly described at approval but may plausibly be attributed to underlying comorbidities, concomitant medications, HIV infection itself, or DTG exposure. Particular attention has been directed to the potential association between DTG/ABC/3TC and cardiovascular risk, although the available literature remains inconclusive [30–33]. In this surveillance, heart failure and congestive heart failure (classified as important identified risks) and three ischemic cardiac events (classified as important potential risks) were reported. However, none of these events were judged to demonstrate a strong causal association with DTG/ABC/3TC. Furthermore, long-term exposure > 2 years was not associated with an increased incidence of ADRs, and no events were identified that could be clearly attributed to extended DTG use. Although the findings of this 10-year surveillance reinforce the established safety profile of DTG-containing regimens, ongoing pharmacovigilance remains essential to detect rare, delayed, or population-specific adverse outcomes. In addition, given the known potential for DTG resistance [34, 35], ongoing monitoring of INSTI resistance mutations remains important in the era of widespread DTG use.

DTG demonstrated sustained effectiveness throughout long-term use. In this final analysis, a larger and more comprehensive dataset was available compared with the 6-year interim report [23], allowing a clearer evaluation of trends in plasma HIV RNA copy number and CD4+ T-cell counts (Figs. 2 and 3). Both ART-naïve and -experienced PLHIV achieved durable viral suppression and immune reconstitution, maintained for > 3 years. In addition, the proportion of participants achieving plasma HIV RNA levels < 50 copies/ml was comparable to, or exceeded, that reported in overseas phase III trials even after 3 years of treatment (Table 7) [13–18, 20–22]. Collectively, these findings support the durability of DTG’s antiviral effectiveness and immunologic benefits in Japanese PLHIV, reinforcing its role as a cornerstone of long-term treatment.

This surveillance is subject to several limitations. First, because the analysis was restricted to participants from the 28 sites involved in the HRD Cooperative Survey, the findings may not be fully generalizable to the broader population of Japanese PLHIV. Second, the safety and effectiveness of DTG alone could not be evaluated fully as DTG is always administered in combination with other anti-HIV drugs such as NRTIs. Therefore, some ADRs may have been attributable to concomitant use of various NRTIs. Third, laboratory data were not consistently available at all time points (i.e., before, during, and after treatment) owing to the real-world clinical setting, resulting in missing values, and information related to treatment discontinuation was not comprehensively captured due to the design of the case report form. Fourth, comparisons with prior clinical trials were limited, as most earlier studies reported AEs irrespective of causality, whereas this surveillance classified all events as ADRs unless both the reporting physician and sponsor explicitly ruled out a causal relationship. This methodologic difference complicates direct cross-study comparisons. Despite these limitations, the findings of this surveillance provide valuable real-world evidence that complements clinical trial data and informs the safe, effective use of DTG in Japanese PLHIV.

Conclusions

This 10-year post-marketing surveillance represents the most comprehensive evaluation to date of single-agent DTG and DTG/ABC/3TC in Japan. The overall safety and effectiveness profiles were consistent with those reported in overseas clinical trials and with information available at the time of the regulatory approval, with no new safety concerns identified. Based on the higher incidence of ADRs observed among participants with comorbid hepatic or renal disorders and the known DTG resistance [34, 35], appropriate monitoring is warranted when prescribing single-agent DTG or DTG/ABC/3TC to PLHIV. Ongoing pharmacovigilance by ViiV Healthcare K.K. will ensure continued monitoring and collection of safety data to further inform clinical practice in Japan.

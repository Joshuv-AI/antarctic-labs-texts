Neurocognitive dysfunction among patients undergoing coronary artery bypass surgery—A prospective study
Authors: Thanathu Krishnan Nair Jayakumar, Kavitha Chandran Chandrakumari, Vinitha Viswambharan Nair
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveNeurocognitive dysfunction (NCD) remains a common complication after coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG); however, the trajectory of cognitive recovery and associated risk factors remain incompletely understood. This study evaluated the prevalence, early postoperative course, and predictors of NCD among patients undergoing elective CABG.MethodsIn this prospective observational study, 110 consecutive patients undergoing elective CABG between January 2025 and August 2025 at a tertiary care center were enrolled. Neurocognitive function was assessed using Addenbrooke's Cognitive Examination–III preoperatively, at hospital discharge, and at postoperative day 10 follow-up. Changes in cognitive performance were analyzed using repeated-measures analysis of variance with Bonferroni-adjusted pairwise comparisons. Transitions in categorical cognitive status were evaluated using the McNemar-Bowker test. Associations between patient characteristics and NCD were examined using multivariable analyses.ResultsA total of 110 patients undergoing CABG were included in the study, with a mean age of 57.5 ± 8.5 years; 79.1% were male, and 85.5% underwent off-pump CABG. Mean Addenbrooke's Cognitive Examination–III scores declined significantly from baseline (77.0 ± 9.1) to discharge (73.8 ± 9.9), followed by recovery at follow-up (77.1 ± 10.0) (P < .001). Bonferroni-adjusted comparisons demonstrated a significant decline from baseline to discharge (mean difference −3.2; 95% CI, −4.8 to −1.6; P = .001) and a significant improvement from discharge to follow-up (mean difference +3.3; 95% CI, 1.7-4.9; P = .001), whereas baseline and follow-up scores did not differ significantly (mean difference +0.1; 95% CI, −1.5 to 1.7; P = .865). NCD was present in 68.2% of patients preoperatively, increased to 80.0% at discharge, and decreased to 64.5% at follow-up. Multivariable logistic regression analysis identified increasing age as independently associated with NCD at follow-up (adjusted odds ratio, 1.08; 95% CI, 1.02-1.14; P = .007)ConclusionsIn this group of patients undergoing CABG, cognitive impairment was often seen before surgery. There was a temporary decline right after the operation, followed by partial recovery by the early follow-up. The link between older age and early postoperative cognitive issues indicates that older patients may benefit from cognitive screening around the time of surgery. More research is needed to look at long-term cognitive changes and the effectiveness of specific interventions.

Perspective

In this prospective survey of 110 patients who underwent CABG and were assessed with ACE-III, most had abnormal cognition preoperatively, a worsened cognition at discharge, and partial improvement by follow-up. Coronary artery disease remains one of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide, and coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) continues to be a cornerstone revascularization strategy for patients with complex coronary disease. Although advances in surgical techniques and perioperative management have substantially improved survival and cardiovascular outcomes, postoperative neurocognitive dysfunction (NCD) remains a clinically important complication.1, 2, 3

NCD encompasses deficits in attention, memory, executive function, language, and psychomotor performance that occur after surgery. Among patients undergoing cardiac surgery, reported rates vary widely because of differences in diagnostic criteria, timing of assessment, and cognitive instruments used. Early postoperative impairment has been reported in 20% to 80% of patients, whereas persistent dysfunction may occur in 10% to 30% of patients.1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6

The pathophysiology of NCD is multifactorial and incompletely understood. Proposed mechanisms include cerebral hypoperfusion, microembolic injury, systemic inflammatory activation, perioperative metabolic disturbances, and underlying cerebrovascular disease.2,4,5 Although cardiopulmonary bypass has traditionally been implicated in postoperative cognitive decline, increasing evidence suggests that cognitive dysfunction may occur even after off-pump CABG, highlighting the contribution of patient-related and procedure-independent factors.4,6,7

Several patient characteristics have been associated with increased susceptibility to NCD, including advanced age, lower educational attainment, preexisting cognitive impairment, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, and reduced cognitive reserve.8, 9, 10, 11 However, considerable heterogeneity exists across published studies, particularly regarding baseline cognitive status and short-term postoperative recovery patterns.

The present study was undertaken to characterize the prevalence and early trajectory of NCD among patients undergoing elective CABG in a contemporary Indian cohort. Specifically, we sought to evaluate cognitive performance before surgery, at hospital discharge, and during early postoperative follow-up and to identify demographic and clinical factors associated with adverse neurocognitive outcomes. This study contributes to the existing literature in 3 important ways: first, by evaluating neurocognitive trajectory rather than isolated prevalence estimates; second, by documenting the burden of baseline cognitive impairment in an Indian CABG population; and third, by examining outcomes in a predominantly off-pump surgical cohort, a population that remains underrepresented in the literature.4,7,12

Methods

Study Design and Setting

A prospective observational study was conducted in the Department of Cardiothoracic Surgery at Government Medical College Hospital, Kottayam, Kerala, India, between January 25, 2025, and August 15, 2025. The study population comprised patients admitted for elective CABG.

Sample Size and Sampling Technique

The sample size was initially calculated for prevalence estimation using the formula 4pq/L2, assuming a prevalence of 50%, an absolute precision of 10%, and a 95% confidence level. The minimum required sample size was 100 participants. After accounting for an anticipated attrition rate of 10%, the final sample size was fixed at 110 participants.

To address the longitudinal objective of the study, a post hoc repeated-measures power analysis was performed. Assuming a moderate effect size (f = 0.25), α = 0.05, power = 80%, and an estimated within-subject correlation of 0.5, the achieved sample size was adequate to detect statistically significant within-subject changes in cognitive scores across the study period. Eligible participants were recruited using consecutive sampling until the required sample size was attained.

Eligibility Criteria

Inclusion criteria

Patients were eligible if they (1) were scheduled to undergo elective CABG (on-pump or off-pump); (2) rovided written informed consent; and (3) were able to read and understand Malayalam.

Exclusion criteria

Patients were excluded if they had (1) known learning disability, dementia, or other neurologic or psychiatric disorders; (2) history of stroke within the preceding year; (3) left ventricular ejection fraction <30%; (4) preoperative intubation; (5) concomitant major cardiac or vascular procedures; (6) prolonged postoperative mechanical ventilation (>24 hours); and (7) perioperative cardiac arrest occurring before or after cardiopulmonary bypass. Patients requiring prolonged postoperative ventilation were excluded to minimize confounding resulting from critical illness, prolonged sedation, delirium, and other postoperative complications that may independently influence neurocognitive outcomes (Figure E1).

Ethical Considerations and Data-Collection Procedure

The study was approved by the institutional ethics committee and scientific review committee of Government Medical College Hospital, Kottayam (approval number SRC/CTVS//1/2023). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment. The informed consent process included consent for the collection, analysis, and publication of anonymized study data.

Neurocognitive assessment was performed using Addenbrooke's Cognitive Examination–III (ACE-III) by a trained neurologist at 3 predefined time points: (1) reoperatively (baseline); (2) at hospital discharge (postoperative day 5 or 6); and (3) during the first postoperative follow-up visit (postoperative day 10). The same assessor conducted all evaluations to minimize interrater variability. Postoperative day 10 assessment was selected to evaluate early cognitive recovery while maintaining feasibility within routine postoperative follow-up schedules.

Perioperative Management and Clinical Variables

Perioperative anesthesia, analgesia, and sedation were administered according to standardized institutional protocols. General anesthesia was induced using intravenous anesthetic agents and maintained with inhalational anesthetics and opioid-based analgesia. Postoperative pain management followed a multimodal analgesic approach incorporating opioid and nonopioid medications. Sedation during the immediate postoperative period was titrated according to clinical requirements using short-acting agents.

For patients undergoing cardiopulmonary bypass, transfusion practices aimed to maintain hemoglobin concentrations at approximately 10 g/dL within the venous reservoir as part of institutional practice to optimize tissue oxygen delivery. Intraoperative blood glucose levels were maintained between 140 and 180 mg/dL.

Clinical variables with potential relevance to neurocognitive outcomes were recorded, including age, gender, educational status, body mass index, comorbidities, duration of coronary artery disease, duration of cardiac medication use, cardiopulmonary bypass duration, aortic crossclamp time, blood and blood-product transfusion, renal dysfunction, preoperative anemia, history of moderate alcohol consumption, and preoperative unstable angina.

Study Instruments

Data were collected using (1) a sociodemographic and sociopersonal data sheet; (2) a clinical characteristics data sheet; and (3) the ACE-III. The ACE-III is a standardized cognitive screening instrument with a maximum score of 100 points, comprising 5 cognitive domains: attention (18 points), memory (26 points), verbal fluency (14 points), language (26 points), and visuospatial abilities (16 points).

On the basis of established validation studies of ACE-III, total scores were categorized as follows: ≥88: normal cognition; 83-87: inconclusive cognitive status; and <83: abnormal cognition. Although categorical classification was used for descriptive and transition analyses, continuous ACE-III scores were considered the primary measure of neurocognitive change.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 27.0 (IBM Corp). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize participant characteristics. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± SD, whereas categorical variables are reported as frequencies and percentages.

Changes in ACE-III scores across the 3 assessment time points were evaluated using repeated-measures analysis of variance. Bonferroni-adjusted post hoc pairwise comparisons were performed to compare baseline versus discharge, discharge versus follow-up, and baseline versus follow-up scores. To account for potential confounding factors, a linear mixed-effects model was additionally fitted with ACE-III score as the dependent variable and age, educational status, comorbidities, and selected intraoperative variables as covariates.

Changes in categorical neurocognitive classification over time were examined using the McNemar-Bowker test, which is appropriate for paired nominal data with more than 2 categories. Associations between NCD and sociodemographic or clinical variables were initially explored using the χ2 test. A multivariable logistic regression model was subsequently performed to identify independent predictors of NCD at follow-up.

Variables associated with NCD at the first postoperative follow-up were evaluated using multivariable logistic regression (Table E1). Increasing age remained independently associated with postoperative NCD (adjusted odds ratio, 1.08; 95% CI, 1.02–1.14; P = .007). Discussion

This prospective study evaluated the prevalence and early trajectory of NCD among patients undergoing elective CABG. Three principal findings emerged. First, a substantial burden of cognitive impairment was present before surgery. Second, neurocognitive performance deteriorated significantly during the immediate postoperative period. Third, cognitive function demonstrated partial recovery by early follow-up, although a considerable proportion of patients remained cognitively impaired.

Preoperative Cognitive Impairment

More than two thirds of participants exhibited abnormal cognitive status before surgery. This finding is consistent with previous reports demonstrating that patients undergoing cardiac surgery frequently have preexisting cognitive impairment attributable to advanced vascular disease, cerebrovascular pathology, and reduced cognitive reserve.13, 14, 15, 16 The high prevalence observed in the present cohort highlights the importance of baseline cognitive assessment when evaluating postoperative cognitive outcomes. Previous studies have demonstrated that pre-existing cognitive impairment is an important predictor of postoperative cognitive decline, delirium, prolonged recovery, and reduced quality of life.13,14,17

Early Postoperative Cognitive Decline

A significant deterioration in neurocognitive performance was observed at discharge, reflected by both categorical classification and ACE-III scores. These findings are consistent with previous investigations demonstrating postoperative neurocognitive decline after cardiac surgery.18, 19, 20, 21, 22 Potential mechanisms include cerebral microembolization, inflammatory activation, perioperative hemodynamic fluctuations, metabolic disturbances, and surgical stress.20,21 Although most patients in the present study underwent off-pump CABG, cognitive decline remained common, suggesting that factors beyond cardiopulmonary bypass contribute substantially to postoperative NCD.4,6,22

Early Cognitive Recovery

Improvement in cognitive performance was observed between discharge and follow-up. Mean ACE-III scores returned to levels comparable with baseline, and the proportion of cognitively abnormal patients decreased significantly. These findings are consistent with studies demonstrating partial recovery during the weeks after surgery.18,19 Nevertheless, nearly two thirds of patients continued to exhibit abnormal cognition at follow-up, indicating considerable heterogeneity in recovery trajectories and underscoring the need for longer-term surveillance.14

Association With Age and Educational Status

Increasing age emerged as an independent predictor of NCD at follow-up. Older participants demonstrated a greater likelihood of persistent cognitive impairment, consistent with previous evidence linking advancing age to reduced neuronal reserve, increased cerebrovascular burden, and greater vulnerability to perioperative neurologic injury.14,21 Older adults may have diminished capacity to compensate for perioperative physiological stressors, making them more susceptible to postoperative cognitive decline.14,21 These findings underscore the importance of enhanced perioperative cognitive monitoring and targeted preventive strategies in older patients undergoing CABG.14

Perioperative Strategies in High-Risk Individuals

In our clinical practice, we perform carotid artery Doppler evaluation in patients who are older than 65 years of age, have a history of transient ischemic attack or cerebrovascular accident, have significant left mainstem coronary artery disease (left main stenosis >50%), and have evidence of carotid bruit on auscultation or peripheral occlusive vascular disease. These subsets of patients are at high risk for perioperative neurologic events. In patients who had past neurologic events, a computed tomography cerebral angiogram and computed tomography scan of the brain also is performed and neurologic opinion sought. Patients with uncontrolled type 2 diabetes mellitus (hemoglobin A1c >8.5%) are counseled against immediate surgery provided they don't have critical left mainstem disease, New York Heart Association class III/IV symptoms, or significant left ventricular dysfunction.

Intraoperatively, off-pump CABG is preferred in these patients to avoid the added risk of cardiopulmonary bypass and hence will be performed by senior surgeons. The ascending aorta is evaluated by an epiaortic ultrasound and if found to be atherosclerotic, we prefer an anaortic CABG, where aortic manipulation is kept minimum to avoid embolization. Near-infrared spectroscopy monitoring is performed intraoperative to assess the cerebral oxygenation during the procedure. Hemoglobin of the patients is kept around 10 gm/dL, the intraoperative mean blood pressure is maintained at 75 to 80 mm Hg, and oxygen tension in arterial blood gas is maintained greater than 80 mm Hg to ensure adequate cerebral perfusion and oxygenation. Tight blood sugar control between 140-180 mg/dL is maintained.

Post operatively, we avoid muscle paralyzing agents and assess the neurological recovery as early as possible and try to extubate early. Mean blood pressure, oxygen tension, hemoglobin, and RBS will be maintained as in the intraoperative period.

Clinical Implications

The findings support the incorporation of routine perioperative cognitive assessment into CABG care pathways. The identification of cognitively vulnerable patients before surgery may facilitate risk stratification, informed counseling, and targeted perioperative interventions. These findings are aligned with contemporary perioperative brain health recommendations advocating systematic cognitive screening and monitoring in high-risk surgical populations.14,17 Such screening programs may facilitate early recognition of patients at risk for postoperative neurocognitive disorders and support the implementation of individualized perioperative management strategies. Future multicenter studies with longer follow-up periods are needed to determine the persistence of cognitive impairment and to evaluate interventions aimed at improving neurocognitive outcomes after cardiac surgery.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, it was conducted at a single center, which may limit generalizability. Second, neurocognitive outcomes were assessed only up to the early postoperative period, precluding evaluation of long-term cognitive trajectories. Third, residual confounding from unmeasured factors such as depression, frailty, and preexisting cerebrovascular disease cannot be excluded. Fourth, the predominance of off-pump CABG procedures limited comparisons between surgical techniques. Finally, cognitive assessment was based on a single screening instrument (ACE-III), and routine neuroimaging or cerebral monitoring was not performed. Despite these limitations, the prospective design and repeated standardized cognitive assessments provide valuable insights into the prevalence and early course of NCD after CABG.

Conclusions

In this single-center observational study, we found that neurocognitive impairment after CABG was common among our patient population, and many patients presented with cognitive impairment before CABG surgery. The study showed a decline in cognitive function in the immediate postoperative period that was improved and returned to near baseline by the 10-day postoperative follow-up. At follow-up, although average group cognitive scores improved, a significant number of patients remained in an abnormal cognitive state post-CABG, and it was found that their age was a risk factor for this adverse state. On the basis of these initial findings, we may consider the need for more cognitive assessment in the perioperative period, particularly in an older population. However, because this is a single-center study with a short follow-up, there is a clear need for more multicenter studies to show long-term cognitive recovery from CABG and to frame perioperative cognitive protection interventions.

Background

Periodontal surgery is among the most frequently performed dental outpatient procedures, aimed at managing the anatomical sequelae of periodontal tissue destruction. While the current standard of care involves delicate and complex interventions, it can lead to significant morbidity and high costs. Recently, intraoral endoscopic approaches have become available [1]. The use of an endoscope simplifies the procedure, decreases morbidity, and reduces surgical time, although its efficacy has not yet been firmly established.

A recent randomized controlled trial demonstrated that endoscopic assisted subgingival debridement (EASD) is a non-inferior treatment alternative to minimally invasive periodontal surgical approaches [2]. This study is designed to assess whether periodontal regeneration achieved through endoscopic periodontal surgery results in clinically noninferior outcomes compared with standard treatment with papilla-preservation flaps.

Contemporary minimally invasive periodontal surgical technique

Minimally invasive surgical techniques, MIST, and modified minimally invasive surgery technique (M-MIST) are widely adopted and applied as minimally invasive papillary preservation surgical approaches to periodontal defects and are often used in conjunction with periodontal regeneration [3, 4] (Fig. 1). The MIST and M-MIST demonstrated excellent early healing outcomes. In the case-series reports of MIST and M-MIST, almost all cases underwent uneventful healing, and primary closure was achieved in 12 of 13 cases treated with MIST and in all cases treated with M-MIST. A 4.8 ± 1.9 mm and 4.8 ± 2.4 mm gain in CAL was reported for defects treated with MIST and M-MIST, respectively [3, 4]. A 3-arm randomized controlled trial evaluated the treatment outcome of periodontal regeneration with M-MIST only, M-MIST with enamel matrix derivatives, and M-MIST with enamel matrix derivatives and bone mineral-derived xenograft [5]. It was reported that the mean gain in clinical attachment level was 4.1 ± 1.4 mm (M-MIST only), 4.1 ± 1.2 mm (M-MIST with enamel matrix derivatives), and 3.7 ± 1.3 mm (M-MIST with enamel matrix derivatives and bone mineral-derived xenograft), with more than 70% of intrabony defect radiographic fill. By far, MIST and M-MIST are considered among the most widely applied minimally invasive papilla-preservation flap approaches for periodontal regeneration procedures.

Papillary preservation technique: modified minimally invasive surgical technique

Current evidence on the flapless periodontal regeneration with enamel matrix derivatives (EMD)

Periodontal regeneration benefits from the application of minimally invasive surgical techniques. The flapless application of EMD is particularly attractive as it resembles an ultimate minimally invasive approach. Its noninvasive nature means the procedure does not require flap elevation for access, thereby avoiding impact on non-diseased sites. Additionally, the procedure does not require incision of the defect-associated interdental papilla, minimizing disruption to blood circulation. Maintaining the integrity of the interdental papilla over the defect area reduces the risk of flap collapse or necrosis.

The current evidence on the clinical efficacy of flapless periodontal regeneration with EMD is controversial. A split-mouth RCT had further explored the benefits of flapless application of EMD [6]. Twenty-two subjects with moderate to severe periodontitis who received Step 2 periodontal therapy were recruited, and two sites in the same jaw with matching tooth anatomy and intrabony defect morphology were randomly assigned to receive flapless application of EMD after scaling and root planing or scaling and root planing only. A blinded assessor collected baseline and 3-month clinical data. Both groups showed significant improvement from baseline to 3 months postoperatively; however, no statistically significant difference was observed in changes in PPD and CAL between the two groups. A recent publication that included 6 RCTs systematically evaluated the evidence on EMD as an adjunct to nonsurgical periodontal therapy as Steps 1 and 2 periodontal therapy [7]. It was reported that there was a weighted mean difference of 0.14 mm of gain in CAL and a 0.46-mm reduction in PPD in favour of nonsurgical periodontal treatment with flapless application of EMD. However, there is no additional clinical benefit of flapless EMD application compared to traditional nonsurgical periodontal therapy, and this evaluation was limited by the high heterogeneity of the available evidence.

Some recent studies have demonstrated positive outcomes following flapless application of EMD as Step 3 periodontal therapy. Graziani et al. evaluated this approach using a one-stage full-mouth debridement protocol [8]. Thirty-eight subjects with ≥ 3-mm interproximal bone loss in ≥ 2 nonadjacent teeth were randomly assigned to receive one-stage full-mouth debridement with (Test) or without (Control) EMD application. After 3 months of healing, the sites treated with EMD showed significantly greater reductions in PPD (Test: 3.22 mm vs Control: 2.11 mm) and gains in CAL (Test: 2.89 mm vs Control: 1.87 mm) in sites with initial PPD ≥ 6 mm, along with a significantly lower percentage of sites with residual pockets ≥ 6 mm in the test group (Test: 3.25 ± 4.95% vs Control: 7.09 ± 8.03%).

Similar findings were also reported in a multicentre RCT with a split-mouth design [9]. Forty-four patients with residual periodontal pockets after initial therapy were randomly assigned to receive root surface debridement with EMD (Test) or without EMD (Control) as Step 3 periodontal therapy. Data collected at baseline and 1-year follow-up showed a significant additional reduction in PPD (6 months: 0.79 ± 1.3 mm and 12 months: 0.85 ± 1.1 mm) and a higher frequency of achieving pocket closure (PPD ≤ 4 mm with no BoP) (Test: 80% vs Control: 42%) favouring the approach of repeated root surface debridement with flapless application of EMD compared to those receiving only repeated root surface debridement.

A 24-month randomized controlled trial (RCT) compared the flapless application of EMD after nonsurgical periodontal treatment (Test) versus the application of EMD after periodontal surgery using a minimally invasive surgical technique (Control) [10]. Thirty patients with residual periodontal defects ≥ 3 mm in depth were recruited and allocated to either the test or control group. After 24 months of maintenance care, the Test and Control therapies resulted in 3.6 ± 1.0 mm and 3.7 ± 0.6 mm PPD reductions and 3.2 ± 1.1 mm and 3.6 ± 0.9 mm CAL gains, respectively, with no significant differences between the two groups.

The same authors also compared the treatment outcome of flapless application of EMD after nonsurgical periodontal treatment (Test) and nonsurgical periodontal treatment alone (Control) in a 12-month RCT [11]. The Test therapy resulted in significantly greater CAL gain compared to the Control therapy (3.9 ± 1.1 mm vs. 3.0 ± 1.2 mm, p = 0.017) and substantially greater radiographic fill of the intrabony defects (3.0 ± 1.0 mm vs. 1.8 ± 1.5 mm, p < 0.01). It was also reported that significantly more sites under the Test therapy achieved the defined successful treatment outcome (post-operative PPD ≤ 4 mm and gain in CAL ≥ 3 mm) (82.6% vs. 52.2%, p = 0.028).

The clinical benefits observed in these studies may be attributed to the application of minimally invasive nonsurgical periodontal therapy protocols, different treatment protocols of flapless periodontal regeneration with EMD (as Steps 1 & 2 vs Step 3 periodontal therapy), and stringent root surface isolation during EMD application (Fig. 2).

Perioscope explorer

Current evidence on the use of periodontal endoscope

Stambaugh et al. were the first to report the visualization of the subgingival root surface and detection of calculus using a periodontal endoscope [1]. A periodontal endoscope is modified for intraoral application. One commercially available model, the perioscope (PerioVision Limited, USA), utilizes a CCD/LED camera and light coupling to provide imaging and illumination from the endoscope fibre through a controller (Fig. 3). The endoscope fibre-optic probe is a 1 mm × 1 m fibre that allows a 24–48 × magnified vision with a depth of 2–6 mm and a width of 3 mm. The fibres are encapsulated within a single-use perioscopy sheath designed to deliver constant water irrigation to keep the endoscope lens clear during subgingival operations.

Periodontal endoscope unit

The periodontal sheath is held within perioscope explorers during intraoral use (Fig. 2). The perioscope explorer consists of a hollow endoscope mounting receptacle, a tissue retraction shield, the apex of a sheath-covered endoscope, and a handle with an endoscope holding channel. During application, the perioscope explorer is inserted into the periodontal pocket. The tissue retraction shield retracts the periodontal soft tissue away from the root surface, allowing the optical fibre to capture a real-time image. Constant irrigation and illumination through the sheath tip help keep the surgical site clear for image acquisition (Fig. 4). The real-time image is processed and displayed on a monitor (Fig. 5).

A recently published single-blinded randomized controlled trial demonstrated the non-inferiority of EASD compared to periodontal surgery with the MPPT in managing residual periodontal intrabony defects [2]. Non-inferiority was shown in terms of gains in CAL, reductions in PPD, changes in REC, improvements in radiographic intrabony defects, and the percentage of sites with pocket resolution. Subjects in both groups reported low levels of intraoperative pain and hardship, while EASD offered advantages such as shorter operative time, a better Early Wound Healing Index, and the absence of suture removal. The study concluded that EASD is a non-inferior treatment alternative to MPPT for managing residual intrabony defects as part of Step 3 periodontal therapy.

The application of a periodontal endoscope requires additional overhead per operation. If this study demonstrates non-inferior treatment outcomes in managing residual periodontal defects compared with periodontal regenerative surgery, the flapless periodontal regeneration with endoscopic assisted subgingival debridement could potentially eliminate the need for periodontal surgical intervention. This substitution could offer financial benefits by reducing treatment overhead, minimizing post-operative morbidity, and lowering material costs for surgical procedures.

Therefore, there is a strong need for a study to demonstrate the non-inferiority of the clinical efficacy of flapless EMD application after EASD compared to EMD application with the papillary preservation technique in managing residual periodontal intrabony defects. This first non-inferiority randomized controlled clinical trial aims to compare these two treatment modalities in terms of treatment outcomes and patient-reported outcome measures. The results of this study will provide valuable information on the application of periodontal endoscopes in dentistry and potentially redefine the role of periodontal surgery in current periodontal disease management strategies.

Methods/design

This study aims to establish, as a proof of concept, an endoscopy-assisted, flapless protocol for periodontal regeneration and to demonstrate that the endoscopy-assisted flapless protocol is non-inferior to the current standard surgical protocol for periodontal regeneration. The primary objective is to compare the primary clinical outcome variable, i.e. change in clinical attachment level, and secondary clinical outcome variables at the study surgical site, i.e. residual periodontal defects with periodontal pocket depth ≥ 6 mm and an intrabony component ≥ 3 mm, treated by periodontal regeneration with EMD in conjunction with (a) endoscopic assisted subgingival debridement (EASD) (test therapy) or (b) periodontal surgery using papilla preservation technique (PPT) (control therapy). It is also designed to compare the patient-reported outcome measures during intraoperative and recovery periods and the time requirement between test and control therapies as the secondary objective.

This study hypothesizes that the flapless application of EMD in conjunction with EASD can achieve non-inferior clinical results compared to periodontal regeneration with EMD using papilla preservation surgical techniques in managing residual periodontal intrabony defects with a clinically relevant inferiority margin of 1 mm 12 months after receiving either test or control therapy.

Study design

This is a 12-month single-centred, non-inferiority, assessor-blind, randomized controlled clinical trial with parallel groups. The clinical trial will strictly follow the ICH-GCP as well as the Consolidated Standards Of Reporting Trials statement. The overview of the study is displayed in Fig. 6. Patient allocation

Patients will be recruited from those admitted to the Prince Philip Dental Hospital or from those referred to the Periodontology Clinic for periodontal surgical intervention. Patients will undergo standard hospital protocols for periodontal examination before assignment for care. A recruitment specialist (RS) will screen patients based on inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Screening appointment

During the screening appointment with the RS, patients will be invited to participate in the research if they meet the research inclusion criteria. Details of the research protocol will be explained, and patient information sheets and informed consent forms will be issued and collected.

Patients should have received initial periodontal therapy (Steps 1 and 2) with a minimum of 3-month healing time before periodontal re-evaluation. Patients will be recruited according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria listed below. Patients will be scheduled to receive further pre-surgical treatment, such as oral hygiene instruction, scaling and root surface debridement, or composite splinting of hypermobile teeth, to prepare them to meet the inclusion criteria as indicated. If patients need further nonsurgical periodontal therapy before re-evaluation for inclusion, at least 6 weeks of healing will be allowed before rescreening to confirm the persistence of periodontal defects.

Baseline data collection and surgical site pretreatment

Within 6 weeks of consent and before surgery, subjects will be reviewed by the blinded assessor (BA) to collect baseline clinical parameters. A presurgical periapical radiograph and a silicon custom-made occlusal guide using the paralleling technique will be taken.

Therapy assignment and delivery

On the day of operation, the research assistant (RA) will be responsible for assigning test/control therapy according to the later-mentioned randomization protocol. If more than one site requires surgical periodontal therapy, only one site will be included in the study per the randomization protocol. The BA will be blinded about this randomized assignment until the data analysis stage. Subjects will receive test/control therapy from a single clinical interventionist (CI) according to the random assignment. The oral cavity and dentition will be disinfected with 0.2% chlorhexidine gluconate mouth rinse (Ecolab Ltd., England), and the anticipated surgical site will be anaesthetized with 2% Xylestesin 1:80,000 adrenaline (3M ESPE) using a local infiltration technique with/without an interdental nerve block.

Periodontal surgery will be delivered according to the following protocol:

Test therapy—endoscopic assisted subgingival debridement (EASD) + EMD

The calculus and deposited biofilm at surgical site will be debrided through the use of slim tip for ultrasonic scaling machine (PS, PL4/5 tip, Piezon Master 700, EMS, Switzerland) with the aid of a periodontal endoscope (Perioscope, PerioVision Limited, USA) and a pair of ×2.5 magnification loupes in an atraumatic debridement through opening of periodontal pockets. The atraumatic debridement will follow several principles, i.e. instrumentation within the periodontal probing depth, maintaining the gingival margin and interdental papilla integrity, avoiding excessive soft tissue retraction, and removing subgingival root surface calcified deposit without intentional removal of cementum [12]. If tension is noticed around the soft tissue during the insertion of the periodontal endoscope tip and the ultrasonic tip, a small incision will be placed around the gingival margin to sever dentogingival fibres. After the procedure, the surgical wound is held in place by applying wet gauze with pressure until haemostasis is achieved. A periodontal dressing will be placed only if needed.

At completion of the EASD, intra-pocket injection of 24% EDTA (PrefGel®, Institut Straumann AG) will be administered from the base of the study pocket in a coronal direction. Caution will be taken to avoid damaging the underlying tissue. After 120 s, the pocket will be rinsed with saline. After irrigation, sterile gauze straps will be placed in the pocket to control bleeding. Once haemostasis is achieved, EMD (Emdogain® FL, Institute Straumann AG) will be applied to the root surface using a blunt needle. The flap will be secured with pressure with wet gauze to achieve close adaptation to the root surface.

Control therapy — papilla preservation technique + EMD

Open flap debridement with modified minimally invasive surgical treatment utilizing the modified papilla preservation technique/simplified papilla preservation flap design will be adopted. In case of a more extensive defect that requires additional access for cleaning, the principles of the single-flap approach (33) will be followed, with only a single-sided flap raised and the integrity of either the buccal or oral papilla maintained. The incision is strictly confined to an intra-sulcular incision on the buccal aspect to preserve the entire volume of the soft tissue whenever possible. The buccal full-thickness flap is elevated to expose the buccal bone crest and allow access to the intrabony defect. The defect-associated interdental papilla is separated from the defect granulation tissue with sharp dissection using a mini-surgical blade. In M-MIST, the interdental papilla is intentionally left unelevated, and the supra-crestal attachment of the healthy side of the defect-associated interdental papilla remains attached to the cementum of the adjacent tooth throughout the procedure to preserve the soft tissue stability. No displacement of the defect-associated interdental papilla and lingual flap is required. The mesial-distal extension of the incision is kept to a minimum as needed. The underlying granulation tissue will be removed by sharp dissection, with minimal curettage. The root surface will be cleaned with a hand instrument (Mini-Five Gracey Curette, Hu-Friedy, USA) or an ultrasonic instrument with a slim tip (PL-3, Piezon Master 700, EMS, Switzerland) to remove visible calcified deposits. The surgical site will be cleaned with saline irrigation, and haemostasis will be controlled by packing pressurized sterile gauze straps. After bleeding is controlled, the root surface will be conditioned with 24% EDTA (PrefGel®; Institute Straumann AG) for 120 s. After irrigating with saline, the root surface will be blotted dry with sterile gauze, and EMD (Emdogain® FL; Institute Straumann AG) will be applied to the root surface. The buccal flap will be repositioned and sutured with fine suture (6/0 Novosyn, polyglycolide-co-L-lactide, absorbable surgical suture, B. Braun Surgery, Spain). A pressurized wet gauze pack will arrest any bleeding.

Post-operative care

Subjects will be given 28 tablets of paracetamol (500 mg) to be taken as needed over 7 days, and they will be briefed on standard post-operative care. They will also be instructed to record the number of tablets taken and to complete the 14-day recovery log diary. Subjects will be instructed to avoid mechanical plaque control at the surgical site for the first 2 weeks, and chemical plaque control with 1-min mouth rinsing using 0.2% chlorhexidine mouthwash twice daily will be introduced.

Review and evaluation

Patients who have undergone test or control therapy will be reviewed by the CI on the 7th, 14th, 30th, and 60th days, until any incisional or suture marks in soft tissue resolve. Patients in both groups will receive supportive care and may receive non-study-related care provided by CI/BA during the study period. Protocol deviations and concurrent procedures will be documented. The suture in the PPT group will be removed on the 7th day. Surgical site disinfection with 0.2% chlorhexidine will be performed on the 7th, 14th, 30th, and 60th days. The Early Wound Healing Index will be assessed independently by an external BA using photographic documentation captured at the 7th, 14th, and 30th days. Any deposit on the root surface will be carefully removed.

CI and/or BA will review subjects on the 3rd, 6th, 9th, and 12th months after the surgery, when the wound closure should have been completed, and any trace of surgery should have disappeared. At each review appointment, supportive periodontal care, including oral hygiene instruction and professional mechanical plaque removal and debridement, will be performed by CI/BA according to the schedule. If a calcified deposit is noted, it will be removed. The BA will measure clinical parameters. At the 6th- and 12th-month reviews, periapical radiographs will be taken, and BA will measure the bone crest level.

Randomization, blindness, and concealment

Participants will be randomized using permutated block randomization with randomly varying block sizes to ensure balanced group allocation while preventing predictability. An independent statistician will generate the allocation sequence and prepare sequentially numbered, opaque, tamper-evident sealed envelopes (SNOSE) for therapy assignment (EASD or PPT therapy). An independent statistician will maintain the log documenting each allocation to maintain auditability. Envelopes will be securely stored in a locked location and opened by the RA on the day of the intervention, after baseline parameters are measured by the BA. If multiple eligible sites exist, the study site will be selected using a pre-specified, computer-generated binary sequence (0 = smallest FDI tooth number; 1 = largest FDI tooth number), prepared by an independent statistician and concealed in sealed envelopes. A research assistant (RA) will be responsible for therapy assignment and surgical site determination after baseline parameters measurement by the BA. CI will be responsible for providing both the test and control therapies. The test/control subjects will be reviewed by CI up to 60 days after the treatment, when traces of surgery should have disappeared. The blinded assessor (BA) will be responsible for the pre-/post-operative patient care, oral hygiene instruction, debridement, and baseline and post-operative clinical parameters measurement without knowing the therapy assignment. The BA will remain blinded to therapy assignments throughout the study.

Clinical outcomes

The BA will measure clinical outcomes at the 90-, 180-, 270-, and 360-day post-operative review appointments using case report forms. The primary endpoint was the change in clinical attachment level from baseline to the 12-month post-operation [13]. Six sites per tooth, a dichotomous full-mouth plaque score, and bleeding on probing (45), recession, and periodontal pocket depth will be measured with a periodontal probe (15UNC Color-Coded Probe PCPUNC156, Hu-Friedy, USA). Tooth mobility [14], Early Wound Healing Index of the treatment site [15], time for the surgery, and % of sites achieving therapeutic endpoints (i.e. no PPD > 4 mm with bleeding and no deep residual pocket PPD > 5 mm) will also be recorded.

Radiographic outcomes

Several landmarks are identified for radiographic measurements by BA (Fig. 7). The change in depth of the intrabony defect (ΔINFRA) and defect angle (the angle between the line BC-BD and the root surface) will be compared between baseline and 12 months.

Landmark for radiograph measurement. CEJ, cervical enamel junction (on test tooth). RA, radiographic apex (on test tooth). BC, bone crest (on neighbouring tooth). BD, bottom of defect. Positioned on test tooth where the PDL loses its even thickness (Bjorn criteria). CEJ-RA, root length (to correct for vertical distortion). CEJ-BC, alveolar bone crest of neighbouring tooth. CEJ-BD, distance from CEJ to the bottom of the defect. CEJ-BD–CEJ-BC, INFRA (intrabony component of the defect). Defect angle: The angle between the line BC-BD to the root surface

Patient-reported outcome measures

Intraoperative discomfort (immediately after surgery), 14-day recovery log diary period (recorded between 21:00 and 24:00 of each day on the following 14 days after the surgery), and condition-specific health-related quality-of-life instrument and OHIP-14 questionnaire on baseline and 3, 6, 9, and 12 months will be collected for further analysis of the patient-reported outcomes [16–18].

Calibration and training

The CI is a registered periodontist in Hong Kong SAR. The CI has received overseas training on minimally invasive surgical techniques in periodontology. The BA is a registered dentist who has received/is receiving taught postgraduate education and training in periodontology and will be responsible for all chair-side clinical measurements. Examiner alignment meetings will be held between the CI and the BA to discuss the details of the measurement principles. Calibration exercises will be conducted on patients with moderate untreated periodontal disease to measure inter- and intra-operator consistency between the blinded assessor and the CI using interclass correlation coefficients.

Sample size calculation

According to a previous 12-month RCT with parallel-arm design comparing flapless approach alone or in combination with EMD in the treatment of deep intrabony defect, it was reported that the group that received periodontal regeneration with EMD resulted in a CAL gain of 3.9 ± 1.1 mm after 12-month healing [11]. This study used a non-inferiority margin of −1 mm in the mean change of clinical attachment level. Sample size calculation was performed using Sealed Envelope Ltd. (Sealed Envelope Ltd., 2012, power calculator for a continuous outcome non-inferiority trial, [Online] available from https://www.sealedenvelope.com/power/continuous-noninferior/ [accessed Mon Nov 18 2024]). Setting a randomization ratio of 1:1, a one-sided alpha of 0.025, and a power (1-beta) of 90%, and anticipating the standard deviations observed in the above-quoted study (1.1 mm), a minimum of 26 subjects in each group are required to ensure that the lower limit of a one-sided 97.5% confidence interval (or equivalently a 95% two-sided confidence interval) is above the non-inferiority limit of −1. Considering a 10% attrition rate, 58 subjects will be recruited for this study.

Subject replacement policy

If drop-out rate is more than 10% of the sample size, a new randomization code will be generated for the newly recruited subjects to make up the sample size.

Statistical procedures

All data will be analysed using the IBM Statistical Package for Social Sciences, version 20.0 (IBM SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA). Standard descriptive statistics such as the mean of subjects’ age and frequency of gender and tooth type will be reported. Baseline characteristics will be summarized descriptively by treatment group. The primary analysis will adjust for baseline values using ANCOVA, and secondary longitudinal assessments will use linear mixed models with baseline values included as covariates. Standardized differences will be reported for baseline variables to assess balance.

The primary outcome and secondary outcomes will be compared between groups using analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) adjusted for baseline values, with treatment group as a fixed factor. For cross-sectional comparisons at individual timepoints, independent t-tests (or Mann–Whitney U if non-normal, assessed via Shapiro–Wilk tests) will supplement ANCOVA results. Homogeneity of variance will be verified via Levene’s test, and homogeneity of regression slopes will be confirmed by testing treatment-by-baseline interactions.

For longitudinal analyses across timepoints, linear mixed models (LMM) with random patient intercepts will be employed, including fixed effects for treatment, time, and their interaction. Model assumptions (normality, homoscedasticity) will be evaluated using residual plots and Q-Q plots.

Missing data will be handled via multiple imputation (m = 50 imputations, 10 burn-in iterations) under the missing-at-random assumption, incorporating baseline outcomes, treatment group, and other prognostic variables (e.g. defect characteristics) in the imputation model. Sensitivity analyses comparing intention-to-treat and per-protocol results will be reported.

Subgroup analysis will be performed to explore the effect of intervention among specific groups defined by baseline characteristics (i.e. infrabony defect depth, defect containity, and defect angle). Adjusted analyses will be performed by incorporating covariates into the analysis (i.e. infrabony defect depth, defect containity, and defect angle) to adjust for variables that may influence outcome.

Discussion

This study aims to produce data on the efficacy of periodontal regeneration with EMD in combination with EASD and PPT in the management of intrabony periodontal defects. If a non-inferiority relationship in the primary outcome, i.e. the change in CAL, can be demonstrated between the two approaches, it will validate that flapless periodontal regeneration with EMD in combination with EASD can be considered a treatment alternative to periodontal regeneration with EMD in periodontal surgery. The data on secondary outcomes will further elucidate the additional benefits of periodontal regeneration with EASD compared to PPT. The findings of this study will be published in peer-reviewed journals and presented at international conferences following the completion of analyses at specific time points.

Roles and responsibilities of the coordinating centre

The coordinating centre comprises investigators from the Division of Periodontology and Implant Dentistry at the Faculty of Dentistry, University of Hong Kong. They are responsible for finalizing protocols, regulatory compliance, safety reporting, ethics submissions, adverse event monitoring and reporting, centralized data management, coordinating site activities (training, protocol adherence), reviewing trial progress, and conducting statistical analysis. The clinical research centre provides operational support, including facilities, equipment, and administrative support and assists in recruitment, scheduling, and regulatory documentation. Given that this is a single-centre, low-risk trial with well-established procedures, oversight is managed by the principal investigator (PI) and the Institutional Review Board (IRB), with regular internal audits. A formal Trial Steering Committee was not deemed necessary, as the study design does not involve high-risk interventions or require independent adjudication. However, all critical decisions (e.g. protocol amendments, safety concerns) are reviewed by the IRB and the study’s data safety coordinator (a neutral periodontist clinician not directly involved in the trial).

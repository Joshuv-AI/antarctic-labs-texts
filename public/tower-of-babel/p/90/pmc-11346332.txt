Verapamil inhibits inflammation and promotes autophagy to alleviate ureteral scar by regulation of CaMK IIδ/STAT3 axis
Authors: Mingqiang Zeng, Zhiwei Zhu, Wuxiong Yuan, Zhengyan Tang, Zhibiao Qing, Qiang Lu, Xuecheng Wu, Junhuan He, Yuanwei Li, Zhuo Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundUreteral stricture (US) is a pathological stenosis in the urinary tract characterized by increased collagen synthesis and inflammation. Autophagy activation has been shown to ameliorate tissue fibrosis and protect against fibrotic diseases. Verapamil has beneficial therapeutic benefits on fibrotic disorders. The pharmacological effects of verapamil on fibroblast autophagy in US and the underlying mechanism need to be investigated further.MethodsUS patients were recruited to isolate scar tissues, hematoxylin-eosin (HE) and Masson trichrome staining were performed to analyze histopathological changes. The US animal model was established and administered with verapamil (0.05 mg/kg) in the drinking water. Transforming growth factor (TGF)-β1 was adopted to facilitate collagen synthesis in fibroblasts. The mRNA and protein expressions were examined by qRT-PCR, western blot, immunofluorescence and immunohistochemistry. ELISA was adopted to measure interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-6 levels. Molecular interaction experiments like dual luciferase reporter and chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays were performed to analyze the interaction between signal transducers and activators of transcription 3 (STAT3) and RNA polymerase II associated factor 1 (PAF1).ResultsHerein, our results revealed that verapamil activated TGF-β1-treated fibroblast autophagy and inhibited inflammation and fibrosis by repressing Ca2+⁄calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMK II) δ-mediated STAT3 activation. Our following tests revealed that STAT3 activated PAF1 transcription. PAF1 upregulation abrogated the regulatory effect of verapamil on fibroblast autophagy and fibrosis during US progression. Finally, verapamil mitigated US in vivo by activating fibroblast autophagy.ConclusionTaken together, verapamil activated TGF-β1-treated fibroblast autophagy and inhibited fibrosis by repressing the CaMK IIδ/STAT3/PAF1 axis.Keywords: Ureteral stricture, verapamil, autophagy, CaMK IIδ, PAF1

Introduction

Ureteral stricture (US) is defined as functional obstruction due to ureteral stricture, which often compromises kidney function (1,2). The most prevalent cause of the US is postoperative ureteral scar, accounting for approximately 35% of all cases (3). At present, the conventional treatment strategies for US are ureteral dilation and endoureterotomy (4). Although the therapy strategies described above are effective in the short term, the treatment effect is limited in the long term (3). Therefore, it is critical to develop innovative treatment strategies for US, and finding effective drugs to prevent US is the key to achieving this goal.

Autophagy is a process which cytoplasmic proteins or organelles are encapsulated into vesicles and fused with lysosomes to form autophagic lysosomes further degrade the contents contained in them, so as to meet the metabolic needs of cells and renew some organelles (5). Autophagy activities are well-recognized for improving tissue fibrosis and preventing fibrotic disorders (6,7). Notably, US is a fibrotic process with the characteristics of reduced extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation and increased collagen synthesis (8). It was previously revealed that autophagy activation alleviated hepatic fibrosis by inhibiting ECM production in hepatocytes (7). Therefore, it’s speculated that activation of autophagy may promote ECM degradation and thus alleviate ureteral scar fibrosis.

Ca2+⁄calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II (CaMK II)δ, as a multifunctional serine/threonine kinase activated by Ca2+, functions in promoting inflammation and fibrosis (9,10). Ca2+ and CaMK II are recognized as major regulators of autophagy (11,12). As reported by Dong et al. Ca2+/CaMK II activation promoted cell proliferation and inhibited autophagy by activating the Akt/mTOR pathway (13). Transforming growth factor (TGF)-β1 is an inducer of fibroblast activation and collagen production and is involved in US pathogenesis (14). Our previous study demonstrated that CaMK II could facilitate fibroblast collagen production induced by TGF-β1 (15). However, whether CaMK II promotes collagen production in the US by regulating fibroblast autophagy remains unclear.

RNA polymerase II associated factor 1 (PAF1), as an RNA polymerase II-binding transcriptional regulator, plays a role in transcriptional extension and histone modification (including ubiquitination and methylation) by interacting with RNA polymerase II (16). PAF1 is an autophagy-negative regulator that suppresses glucose starvation-induced autophagy via transcriptional inhibition of autophagy-related genes (ATGs) (17). The signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) pathway is related to the occurrence and progression of organ fibrosis (18,19). Meanwhile, STAT3 activation contributes to inflammatory responses in various diseases, including US (20,21). In addition, STAT3’s role in regulating autophagy has also been extensively studied. Activated STAT3 performs an anti-autophagy function by up-regulating negative autophagy regulatory factors (22). Herein, predicted by hTFtarget database, PAF1 had potential binding sites to STAT3. Therefore, it’s speculated that STAT3 activation inhibits fibroblast autophagy to promote ureteral scar fibrosis by targeting PAF1, which deserves further research.

Verapamil, a calcium channel blocker, presented anti-fibrosis effects in vitro (23). It was revealed that Verapamil administration could inhibit fibroblast collagen production mediated by TGF-β1 in our previous paper (21), revealing the potential therapeutic effect of verapamil on the US. However, the function of Verapamil in regulating fibroblast autophagy in the US hasn’t been fully clarified.

It’s speculated that Verapamil promoted fibroblast autophagy and inhibited inflammation by inhibiting the CaMK IIδ/STAT3 pathway, thereby alleviating ureteral scar fibrosis. Our paper provided a reliable substance for US treatment and explained its mechanism.

Materials and methods

Fibroblast isolation, culture and treatment

US patients were recruited to collect the ureteral scar tissues and normal ureteral tissue at the Department of Urology, Hunan Provincial People’s Hospital, The First Affiliated Hospital of Hunan Normal University. All patients signed informed consent forms and the ethics protocol approval number is 2022-30. The scar tissues were washed, cut to about 1 mm3 and cultured in a 25 mL culture flask containing complete medium at 37 °C with 5% CO2. The third-generation fibroblasts were used for experiments. Cell morphology was observed, and Vimentin expression was examined. Fibroblasts were subjected to TGF-β1 (20 ng/ml, Sigma-Aldrich, MO, USA) for 24 h. Fibroblasts were subsequently treated with Verapamil (100 μM) (Sigma-Aldrich) for 24 h. Fibroblasts were incubated with CaMK II activator (A23187, 5 μM) (Sigma-Aldrich) for 10 min to activate CaMK IIδ. For STAT3 activation, fibroblasts were subjected to STAT3 activator (Colivelin, 5 μM) (Sigma-Aldrich) for 30 min.

Cell transfection

Cells were cultured in 6‐well plates (1 × 106 cells/well) for 24 h and transfected with the overexpression plasmid of PAF1 (OE-PAF1) and its negative control using Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Invitrogen, CA, USA). The above plasmids were obtained from GenePharma (Shanghai, China).

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

The total RNA was extracted using TRIzol (ThermoFisher Scientific, MA, USA). The cDNA was synthesized by the Reverse Transcription Kit (Toyobo, Tokyo, Japan) and subjected to qRT-PCR assay with SYBR (ThermoFisher Scientific). GAPDH was used as the reference gene. The data were analyzed with the 2−ΔΔCT method. The primers were listed as follows (5′-3′):

PAF1 (F): CTCACAGCATTACAGCAAACC

PAF1 (R): GTCTCTTCTACAGGCAGGAAAT

GAPDH (F): GGAGCGAGATCCCTCCAAAAT

GAPDH (R): GGCTGTTGTCATACTTCTCATGG

Western blot

The proteins were isolated with RIPA, which were further transferred to a PVDF membrane (Millipore, MA, USA). The membranes were incubated overnight with antibodies against CaMK IIδ (Abcam, Cambridge, UK, 1:1000, ab181052), p-CaMK IIδ (Abcam, 1:1000, ab171095), STAT3 (Cell Signaling Technology, MA, USA, 1:1000, #9139), p-STAT3 (Cell Signaling Technology, 1:2000, #9145), PAF1 (Abcam, 1:1000, ab137519), p62 (Abcam, 1:1000, ab109012), LC3 (Abcam, 1:1000, ab192890), Beclin 1 (Abcam, 1:1000, ab207612), Collagen I (Cell Signaling Technology, 1:1000, #72026), α-SMA (Abcam, 1:1000, ab5831) and GAPDH (Abcam, 1:10000, ab8245), then hybridized with secondary antibody (Abcam, 1:10000, ab7090) for 60 min. The blots were visualized by GEL imaging system (Bio-Rad, CA, USA) and subsequently analyzed with ImageJ software. Three experiments were repeated in each immunoblotting experiment

Hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining and Masson trichrome staining

The scar tissue was fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde overnight and cut into 4 μm thick. The sections were then dehydrated with gradient alcohol, washed and embedded in paraffin. The sections were subsequently stained with HE (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, C0105S) and Masson trichrome (Solarbio, Beijing, China, G1340). The sections were observed using a microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan).

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The scar tissue sections were deparaffinized and antigen retrieved (Dako, CA, USA), then blocked and incubated overnight with antibodies against p-CaMK IIδ (Abcam, 1:200, ab171095), p-STAT3 (Abcam, 1:200, ab267373) and PAF1 (Abcam, 1:200, ab137519). The sections were subsequently incubated with an appropriate secondary antibody (Abcam, 1:500, ab150077) for 1 h. The sections were stained with DAB and then counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated and mounted. The images were taken using a microscope (OLYMPUS).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

The secretion levels of interleukin (IL)-1β and IL-6 were examined by human IL-1β ELISA kit (Solarbio, SEKH-0002) and human IL-6 ELISA kit (Solarbio, SEKH-0013), respectively. All operations were performed according to the manuals.

Immunofluorescence

After fixed using 4% paraformaldehyde, cells were blocked and incubated with antibodies against vimentin (Abcam, 1:100, a92547). Then cells were incubated with the corresponding secondary antibody (Abcam, 1:3000, ab150113) for 1 h. The nucleus was stained with DAPI (Sangon, Shanghai, China). Sections were sealed with the sealing liquid and observed under a microscope (Nikon).

Dual luciferase reporter assay

The PAF1 promoter fragment containing STAT3 WT/MUT binding sites was amplified by PCR, and inserted into the pmiRGLO vector (Promega). Cells were co-transfected with PAF1 promoter-WT or PAF1 promoter-MUT and Colivelin or DMSO by Lipofectamine™ 3000 (Invitrogen). The luciferase activity was subsequently assessed.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay

Cells were fixed and quenched. DNA was fragmented by sonication. The cell lysates were incubated with anti-STAT3 (Abcam, 1:100, ab171361) or anti-IgG (Abcam, 1:100, ab172730) at 4 °C overnight. DNA was enriched and subjected to gel electrophoresis analysis.

Animal experiments

A total of 15 female Sprague Dawley rats (12 weeks old, 300‐330 g) were obtained from Charles River (Beijing, China). All experimental protocols on animals were approved by the Department of Urology, Hunan Provincial People’s Hospital, The First Affiliated Hospital of Hunan Normal University, and the ethical number is 2022-30. After acclimation for one week, rats were randomly divided into four groups (n = 5): sham group, US group, and US + verapamil group. The US model was established as previously described (24). In brief, under anesthesia, the left ureter was visualized through a flank incision and clamped with sterile microscopic vascular clamp (15 mm; Mingmou Medical Apparatus Instruments, Jiangsu, China) at the upper ureter just below the lower pole of the left kidney. All the clamps were removed via the initial incision at 6 h after surgery. Rats in the sham group had their left ureters exposed but not clamped via a flank incision. Starting from the first day to week 4, verapamil (0.05 mg/kg) was administered in the drinking water in the US + verapamil group. After that, rats were sacrificed, and the ureter tissue was collected.

Statistical analysis

All data were obtained from three independent experiments, analyzed by SPSS 19.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY) and presented as means ± SD. Between-group differences and multi-group comparisons were determined using Student’s t-test and one-way ANOVA, respectively. The p-value lower than 0.05 was referred as significant.

Results

CaMK IIδ, STAT3 and PAF1 were markedly upregulated in ureteral scar tissues

US patients were recruited to isolate ureteral scar tissues, and it was observed that PAF1 was highly expressed in ureteral scar tissues compared with that in normal ureteral tissues (Figure 1A). Western blot results showed that p-CaMK IIδ, p-STAT3 and PAF1 protein levels in ureteral scar tissues were markedly upregulated compared with those in normal ureteral tissues (Figure 1B). As revealed by HE and Masson trichrome staining, a large number of compact collagen fibers were observed in ureteral scar tissues (Figure 1C). In addition, IHC also revealed that p-CaMK IIδ, p-STAT3 and PAF1 protein levels were markedly elevated in ureteral scar tissues compared with those in normal ureteral tissues (Figure 1D). Collectively, CaMK IIδ, STAT3 phosphorylation and PAF1 upregulation were associated with US progression.

CaMK IIδ, STAT3 And PAF1 were markedly upregulated in ureteral scar tissues.

(A) PAF1 expression in tissues was detected using qRT-PCR, (n = 10). (B) p-CaMK IIδ, p-STAT3 and PAF1 protein levels in tissues were examined using western blot. (C) HE and Masson trichrome staining were employed to analyze histopathological changes (Scale bar = 100 μm). (D) IHC was adopted to measure p-CaMK IIδ, p-STAT3 and PAF1 protein levels in scar tissues (Scale bar = 100 μm). The measurement data were presented as mean ± SD. All data were obtained from at least three replicate experiments. ** p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001.

Verapamil inhibited CaMK IIδ, STAT3 and PAF1 expressions in TGF-β1-treated primary fibroblasts, meanwhile activated fibroblast autophagy and inhibited inflammation and fibrosis

Under the microscope, the primary fibroblasts isolated from ureteral scar tissues showed typical primary fibroblast morphology (Figure 2A). As displayed in Figure 2B, the isolated fibroblasts were positive for vimentin (the marker of primary fibroblasts). TGF-β1 was used to facilitate collagen synthesis in fibroblasts (15), and cells were subsequently treated with verapamil. As demonstrated in Figure 2C, TGF-β1 treatment increased PAF1, p-CaMK IIδ and p-STAT3 protein levels in fibroblasts, which were reversed by verapamil treatment. Autophagy activation can improve tissue fibrosis to prevent fibrotic diseases (6). Herein, TGF-β1 stimulation resulted in increased p62 level and reduced LC3II and Beclin1 levels in fibroblasts, while these changes in autophagy marker proteins mediated by TGF-β1 were eliminated by verapamil (Figure 2D). In addition, Collagen I and α-SMA protein levels in fibroblasts were markedly increased by TGF-β1, which were abrogated by verapamil treatment (Figure 2E). Moreover, TGF-β1 increased IL-1β and IL-6 secretion levels in fibroblasts, which were reversed by verapamil administration (Figure 2F). Taken together, verapamil treatment inhibited TGF-β1-induced p-CaMK IIδ, p-STAT3 and PAF1 upregulation in fibroblasts, meanwhile activated fibroblast autophagy and reduced inflammatory response and fibrosis.

(B) Immunofluorescence was performed to analyze vimentin expression in primary fibroblasts (Scale bar = 25 μm). (C-E) PAF1, p-CaMK IIδ, CaMK IIδ, STAT3, p-STAT3, p62, LC3, Beclin 1, Collagen I and α-SMA levels in fibroblasts were detected by western blot. (F) IL-1β and IL-6 secretion levels were assessed using ELISA. The measurement data were presented as mean ± SD. All data were obtained from at least three replicate experiments. ** p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001.

Verapamil activated TGF-β1-treated fibroblast autophagy and inhibited inflammation and fibrosis by inactivating CaMK IIδ

To investigate the function of CaMK IIδ in verapamil-mediated biological effects on the US, TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts were treated with verapamil and A23187 (CaMK IIδ activator). It was observed that A23187 treatment further increased p-CaMK IIδ protein level in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts and could abolish the amelioration effect of verapamil on TGF-β1-mediated increase in p-CaMK IIδ level in fibroblasts (Figure 3A). In addition, CaMK IIδ activation increased p62 level and decreased LC3II and Beclin1 levels in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts; Furthermore, CaMK IIδ activation abolished verapamil’s amelioration effect on TGF-β1-changed fibroblast autophagy (Figure 3B). As revealed in Figure 3C, Collagen I and α-SMA protein levels in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts were further increased by A23187 treatment, and the amelioration effect of verapamil on TGF-β1-mediated Collagen I and α-SMA upregulation in fibroblasts was abolished following A23187 treatment. It also turned out that A23187 treatment further promoted IL-1β and IL-6 secretion levels in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts and could abrogate verapamil-induced inhibition on IL-1β and IL-6 levels in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts (Figure 3D). Collectively, CaMK IIδ activation was associated to increased inflammation and fibrosis and reduced autophagy in US, and verapamil treatment could block this pathway.

TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts were treated with verapamil and A23187 (CaMK II activator). (A-C) p-CaMKIIδ, CaMK IIδ, p62, LC3, Beclin 1, Collagen I and α-SMA levels in fibroblasts were examined using western blot. (D) ELISA was performed to detect IL-1β and IL-6 secretion levels. The measurement data were presented as mean ± SD. All data was obtained from at least three replicate experiments. *p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001.

Verapamil activated TGF-β1-treated fibroblast autophagy and inhibited inflammation and fibrosis by suppressing CaMK IIδ-mediated STAT3 activation

As demonstrated in Figure 4A, Colivelin (STAT3 activator) increased p-STAT3 protein level in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts and could abrogate verapamil-mediated inhibition on p-STAT3 level, while did not affect p-CaMK IIδ level, suggesting that STAT3 might be a downstream regulator of CaMK IIδ. It was subsequently revealed by western blot that Colivelin administration increased p62 level and reduced LC3II and Beclin1 levels in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts and could reverse the regulatory effect of verapamil on these autophagy-related proteins in fibroblasts (Figure 4B). Additionally, Colivelin treatment further enhanced the promoting effect of TGF-β1 on Collagen I and α-SMA levels in fibroblasts and abrogated the inhibitory effect of verapamil on Collagen I and α-SMA levels in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts (Figure 4C). It was also turned out that STAT3 activation further increased IL-1β and IL-6 secretion levels in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts and could abolish verapamil’s inhibition effect on TGF-β1-mediated IL-1β and IL-6 upregulation (Figure 4D). In conclusion, CaMK IIδ-mediated STAT3 activation was related to increased inflammation and fibrosis and reduced autophagy in US, and verapamil treatment could block this pathway.

Verapamil activated TGF-β1-treated fibroblast autophagy and inhibited fibrosis by repressing the CaMK IIδ/STAT3/PAF1 axis

The mechanism of STAT3 in regulating fibroblast autophagy in the US was subsequently investigated. As displayed in Figure 5A-B, it was predicted that STAT3 had a potential binding site to the PAF1 promoter by using the JASPAR (https://jaspar.genereg.net/) database. ChIP assay revealed that STAT3 bound to the PAF1 upstream promoter region (Figure 5C). Meanwhile, it was subsequently demonstrated that STAT3 overexpression increased WT PAF1 promoter reporter activity but did not affect MUT activity (Figure 5D). To investigate the function of PAF1 in verapamil-mediated biological effects on US, TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts were treated with verapamil and OE-PAF1. It was observed that OE-PAF1 transfection increased PAF1 protein level in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts and could abrogate verapamil’s repression on PAF1 level, while did not affect p-CaMK IIδ and p-STAT3 levels (Figure 5E). In addition, PAF1 overexpression increased p62 level and reduced LC3II and Beclin1 levels in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts and could abolish verapamil’s regulatory effect on these autophagy-related proteins in fibroblasts (Figure 5F). Furthermore, PAF1 overexpression further increased Collagen I and α-SMA levels in TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts and could reverse the inhibitory effect of verapamil on Collagen I and α-SMA levels in cells (Figure 5G). In summary, verapamil activated fibroblast autophagy and inhibited fibrosis by inactivating the CaMK IIδ/STAT3/PAF1 axis.

Verapamil mitigated US in vivo by activating fibroblast autophagy

To investigate the effect of verapamil in vivo, the US model was established and administered with verapamil (0.05 mg/kg) in the drinking water. As revealed by HE and Masson trichrome stainings, a large number of compact collagen fibers were observed in ureter tissues of US rats, while these pathological changes were eliminated by verapamil treatment (Figure 6A). It was subsequently showed that p-CaMK IIδ, p-STAT3 and PAF1 levels were markedly elevated in ureter tissues of US rats, whereas after administrated with verapamil, the expressions of p-CaMK IIδ, p-STAT3 and PAF1 were reduced (Figure 6B-C). In addition, increased p62 level and reduced LC3II and Beclin1 levels were observed in the ureter tissues of US rats, while verapamil administration reversed the expression of autophagy-related proteins in US rats ureter tissues (Figure 6D). Moreover, verapamil inhibited the increased levels of Collagen I and α-SMA in ureter tissues of US rats (Figure 6E). In summary, verapamil mitigated US in rats by activating fibroblast autophagy.

Discussion

US is a functional obstruction resulting from ureteral stricture usually caused by iatrogenic injury, with the characteristics of fibroblast activation, resulting in increased collagen synthesis and subsequent pathological injury (14). The fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition plays a crucial role in the pathogenesis of various fibrotic diseases, including myocardial fibrosis, pulmonary fibrosis, and renal fibrosis (24–26). The increase in ECM secreted by myofibroblasts increases tissue hardness and promotes the progression of fibrosis (25). During the formation of US, TGF-β1 induces fibroblasts differentiate into myofibroblasts, and myofibroblasts can promote wound repair by contracting the edge of the wound. However, sustained myofibroblast activity can lead to the accumulation and contraction of collagen ECM, resulting in ureteral fibrosis (26,27). Therefore, fibroblasts are closely related to US progression. In recent years, with the increasing use of ureteroscopy, the incidence of US has been growing (28). Autophagy is a catabolic process that occurs as a stress response in eukaryotic cells, allowing cells to survive and maintain cell homeostasis by degrading and recovering cellular components. Recent studies have demonstrated autophagy is a key regulator of tissue remodeling and fibrogenesis (29). And autophagy activation has been proven to improve tissue fibrosis to prevent fibrotic diseases (6,7). Therefore, it’s suggested that autophagy activation may alleviate ureteral scar fibrosis. Calcium channel blocker verapamil has been shown to induce cellular protective autophagy (30). In addition, as revealed by Dong et al. Ca2+/CaMK II activation promoted cell proliferation and inhibited autophagy by activating the Akt/mTOR pathway (13). Herein, our results revealed that verapamil could activate fibroblast autophagy and inhibit fibrosis by repressing the CaMK IIδ/STAT3/PAF1 axis to relieve US.

As reported, Ca2+ transient is an important factor in the occurrence and development of fibrosis, specifically TGF-β1-induced Ca2+ transients contribute to enhanced collagen synthesis in pulmonary fibrosis (31). Verapamil, a calcium channel blocker, can prevent fibrosis and scar formation (32,33). Nevertheless, the role of verapamil in regulating fibroblast autophagy remains unknown. Herein, it was observed that verapamil treatment could activate fibroblast autophagy and inhibit inflammation and fibrosis. CaMK II, a major regulator of Ca2+ transients, can stimulate collagen synthesis during fibrosis (34). CaMK II is identified as an important regulator of autophagy (11). Notably, as reported by our previous paper, verapamil repressed TGF-β1-mediated fibroblast collagen production through inactivating CaMK II (15). Nevertheless, as an isoform of CaMK II, the role of CaMK IIδ in verapamil-mediated fibroblast autophagy activation remains unclear. Herein, our results displayed that CaMK IIδ was activated in scar tissues and TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts, and verapamil markedly repressed CaMK IIδ activation mediated by TGF-β1 in fibroblasts. Meanwhile, Verapamil mitigated US in vivo by activating fibroblast autophagy. In addition, CaMK IIδ activation abolished verapamil’s regulatory effect on TGF-β1-treated fibroblast autophagy, inflammation and fibrosis, suggesting that verapamil activated TGF-β1-treated fibroblast autophagy and inhibited inflammation and fibrosis through inactivating CaMK IIδ.

STAT3 is closely related to cell survival and autophagy. Nevertheless, persistently abnormally activated STAT3 causes a variety of clinical symptoms (35). STAT3 is associated with inflammation and fibrosis in scar tissues (35,36), and its inhibition could inhibit fibrosis process (37). In addition, STAT3 signaling has been shown to be engaged in the autophagic process, with STAT3 mostly performing an anti-autophagy role by up-regulating negative autophagy regulatory factors (22). However, the role of STAT3 in regulating fibroblast autophagy, inflammation and fibrosis during US progression remains unknown. Herein, STAT3 was activated in scar tissues and TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts, and verapamil could remarkably inhibit TGF-β1-induced STAT3 phosphorylation in fibroblasts. Notably, our work revealed that CaMK IIδ-mediated STAT3 activation promoted inflammation and fibrosis in fibroblasts and reduced autophagy during US progression, and verapamil treatment could block this pathway. All these results suggested that verapamil activated TGF-β1-treated fibroblast autophagy and inhibited inflammation and fibrosis by inhibiting CaMK IIδ-mediated STAT3 activation.

PAF1, as a transcriptional elongation factor, promotes transcriptional elongation and histone modifications associated with active transcription (38). Meanwhile, it has been revealed that PA1F also inhibits the expression of downstream targets (39). As proof, PAF1 inhibited mitophagy through repressing ATG32 expression (17), revealing the inhibition effect of PAF1 on autophagy. Herein, it was observed that PAF1 was upregulated in scar tissues and TGF-β1-treated fibroblasts, and verapamil markedly repressed TGF-β1-mediated PAF1 upregulation in fibroblasts. It was subsequently revealed that STAT3 activated PAF1 transcription in fibroblasts. And PAF1 upregulation abolished verapamil’s regulatory effect on fibroblast autophagy and fibrosis during US progression. It was concluded that verapamil activated TGF-β1-treated fibroblast autophagy and inhibited fibrosis by inactivating the CaMK IIδ/STAT3/PAF1 axis.

Taken together, our data suggested that verapamil alleviated ureteral scar fibrosis by regulating CaMK IIδ/STAT3 axis-mediated fibroblast inflammation and autophagy. Our research proved that verapamil had certain therapeutic effects on US preclinically and clarified its mechanism, providing a candidate for the therapy of US.

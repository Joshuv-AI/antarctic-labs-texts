Authors' Response to the Letter on “The Added Benefit of Intra‐Arterial Thrombolysis After Successful Recanalization by Endovascular Treatment: A Systematic Review and Meta‐Analysis of Randomized‐Controlled Clinical Trials”
Authors: Lina Palaiodimou, Nikolaos M Papageorgiou, Georgios Tsivgoulis
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

We thank Frol and colleagues for their thoughtful letter regarding our recent meta‐analysis on intra‐arterial thrombolysis (IAT) after successful recanalization by endovascular treatment (EVT) in large‐vessel occlusion (LVO) stroke [1, 2]. We agree that their observations highlight the broader therapeutic implications of IAT beyond the LVO setting.

Our analysis demonstrated that adjunctive IAT improves the likelihood of excellent functional outcomes at 90 days without increasing symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage or mortality [2]. These results support the concept that microvascular reperfusion is a critical determinant of recovery and that angiographic recanalization alone is insufficient to guarantee favorable outcomes. By targeting residual distal thrombi and improving tissue‐level reperfusion, IAT may help bridge the well‐recognized “angiographic–clinical gap” in acute stroke care.

The key question, raised by Frol et al., is whether similar benefits could extend to medium or distal vessel occlusion (MDVO) strokes. This is particularly timely in view of the neutral findings of recent MDVO EVT trials, in contrast to the unequivocal efficacy of EVT in LVOs. In our systematic review of MDVO stroke, EVT was not associated with improved functional outcomes compared with best medical therapy, while it increased the risk of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage and mortality [3]. These findings underscore both the heterogeneity of MDVOs and the limitations of extrapolating LVO evidence to this distinct population [4].

Several patient‐related factors may influence treatment response in MDVO. Our analysis showed that higher baseline stroke severity, as reflected by NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS) scores, was associated with relative benefit of EVT for achieving good functional outcome [3]. However, subgroup analyses by NIHSS thresholds failed to confirm a consistent effect, suggesting that more refined stratification is required. Age is another determinant, with older patients generally experiencing less favorable outcomes regardless of reperfusion status. Furthermore, vessel location matters: occlusions of proximal M2 segments may behave more like LVOs, whereas distal M3–M4, anterior cerebral, or posterior cerebral artery occlusions are technically more challenging and carry higher risks of complications [4, 5].

Taken together, these complexities highlight the inadequacy of a “one‐size‐fits‐all” approach. Adaptive trial designs provide a promising framework to address this challenge. Such designs can continuously refine patient selection (e.g., by age, NIHSS strata, or vessel territory) while simultaneously comparing different therapeutic strategies: EVT, IAT, or best medical therapy alone. Importantly, Bayesian adaptive platforms allow underperforming interventions to be dropped early, thereby reducing patient exposure to ineffective or harmful strategies. This dynamic methodology may be especially well suited to MDVOs, where treatment effects are likely heterogeneous and conventional parallel‐group randomized‐controlled clinical trials may be underpowered to detect clinically meaningful differences.

In conclusion, we appreciate the opportunity to extend this discussion. Although current data support IAT as a safe and effective adjunct in LVO stroke, its role in MDVO remains unsettled. Future adaptive trials, leveraging stratification by clinical severity, age, and vascular territory, will be essential to determine whether IAT can help close the persistent therapeutic gap in this challenging stroke population.

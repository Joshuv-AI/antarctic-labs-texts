Short-term outcomes of aflibercept biosimilar SB15 in macular edema secondary to retinal vein occlusion
Authors: Jaejin Cho, Sang Min Park, Chul Gu Kim, Jae Hui Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo evaluate the short-term outcomes of SB15, an aflibercept biosimilar approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, for the treatment of macular edema secondary to retinal vein occlusion (RVO).MethodsThis retrospective study included 29 treatment-naïve patients diagnosed with macular edema secondary to branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO)(n = 24) or central retinal vein occlusion (CRVO) (n = 5). All patients received intravitreal SB15 injection as the initial treatment and were followed up for a mean of 7.1 months. Additional injections were administered as necessary. Best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and central retinal thickness (CRT) were evaluated at baseline, after the first injection, and at the final follow-up in both the BRVO and CRVO groups.ResultsIn the BRVO group, the mean logMAR BCVA improved from 0.64 ± 0.36 at baseline to 0.45 ± 0.26 after the first injection and to 0.41 ± 0.32 at the final follow-up (P < 0.001). The mean CRT decreased from 527.1 ± 181.9 μm to 261.3 ± 95.8 μm and 285.1 ± 99.5 μm, respectively (P < 0.001). In the CRVO group, the mean logMAR BCVA improved from 1.29 ± 0.26 at baseline to 0.90 ± 0.54 after the first injection and to 0.78 ± 0.61 at the final follow-up (P = 0.022). The mean CRT decreased from 897.0 ± 344.5 μm to 213.2 ± 24.4 μm and 439.4 ± 268.9 μm, respectively (P = 0.015).ConclusionsSB15 demonstrated short-term anatomical and visual improvements in patients with RVO-associated macular edema, suggesting that it may have some therapeutic effect in this condition.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: Aflibercept, Biosimilar, Macular edema, Retinal vascular occlusion, SB15

Introduction

Intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) injection is an effective treatment for macular edema associated with retinal vein occlusion (RVO) [1, 2]. Aflibercept has been widely used anti-VEGF agent, demonstrating significant visual and anatomical improvements in patients with central retinal vein occlusion (CRVO) in the COPERNICUS study and with branch retinal vein occlusion (BRVO) in the VIBRANT study, establishing its efficacy in treating these conditions [3, 4]. However, aflibercept is an expensive agent, presenting a substantial financial burden to patients [5].

Biosimilars are biological agents that demonstrate equivalent efficacy to the originator drugs [6]. With the recent expiration of the patent of aflibercept, the development of biosimilars has accelerated. SB15 (Afilivu®; Samsung Bioepis, South Korea) is an aflibercept biosimilar approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in May 2024 and the Korean Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (KFDS) in February 2024.

Although equivalence trials for SB15 were conducted only in patients with neovascular age-related macular degeneration (AMD) [7], regulatory approvals have been granted for other indications originally approved for aflibercept through the process of extrapolation [6]. Macular edema secondary to RVO is one such indication.

Biosimilars, with their relatively lower cost than that of originator drugs, are known to reduce patient and societal financial burdens, thereby contributing to more efficient treatment delivery [8–11]. Aflibercept is the most widely used anti-VEGF agent globally, and aflibercept biosimilars are likely to emerge as the leading biosimilar products in the anti-VEGF market [12]. Therefore, studies evaluating treatment outcomes with aflibercept biosimilars are clinically important. To the best of our knowledge, no clinical reports have described the treatment outcomes of aflibercept biosimilars for RVO-associated macular edema.

The present study aimed to evaluate the short-term outcomes of SB15, an aflibercept biosimilar, for the treatment of macular edema secondary to RVO, a condition for which clinical trials have not previously been conducted.

Methods

This retrospective observational study was conducted at a single center, Kim’s Eye Hospital (Seoul, South Korea). The study protocol was approved by the IRB of Kim’s Eye Hospital (#2025-04-006).

Patients and treatment

This study was conducted on treatment-naïve patients who were diagnosed with macular edema secondary to CRVO or BRVO and received intravitreal injections of SB15 (2.0 mg/0.05 mL) between May 2024 and December 2024. SB15 had received approval for clinical use at our center prior to the initiation of the study. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) history of vitreoretinal surgery, (2) follow-up period of < 4 months after SB15 injection, or (3) presence of other macular diseases that could significantly affect visual acuity, such as macular holes. This study was not an experimental study, as it was conducted using a medication approved for clinical use.

After SB15 injection, the patients were followed up at 4–10-week intervals. During the follow-up period, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) was measured, and central retinal thickness (CRT) was assessed using optical coherence tomography (OCT). If intraretinal fluid (IRF) or subretinal fluid (SRF) completely resolved after the first injection, patients were observed without additional treatment. If the retinal fluid persisted or recurred after complete resolution, additional injections were administered at the treating physician’s discretion.

Outcome measures

Included patients were divided into two groups based on the type of RVO: BRVO and CRVO. Within each group, the BCVA and CRT measured at the time of initial diagnosis were compared with those measured at the first post-injection and final follow-up visits. The occurrence of adverse events, including endophthalmitis, retinal tears, and intraocular inflammation, was also assessed. Using OCT images, we evaluated and compared the frequencies of IRF and SRF at diagnosis, after the first post-injection visit, and at the final follow-up. In cases where IRF and SRF completely resolved after a single injection, the time to first recurrence of IRF or SRF was additionally evaluated.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software ( version 12.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). Visual acuity was converted to logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution (logMAR) values for analysis. The Friedman test was used to compare outcomes across the three time points, while the Mann-Whitney U test was applied for comparisons between the two groups. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 29 patients (29 eyes) were included in the study (Table 1). The mean patient age was 65.4 ± 11.5 years, with 24 patients (82.8%) diagnosed with BRVO and 5 (17.2%) with CRVO. The mean interval between RVO diagnosis and the first SB15 injection was 10.7 ± 15.7 weeks (11.9 ± 16.9 weeks for BRVO and 4.8 ± 3.1 weeks for CRVO). Three BRVO cases had intervals exceeding 24 weeks. The mean interval between the first SB15 injection and the first post-injection visit was 5.0 ± 2.1 weeks (range, 4–10 weeks), and the entire follow-up period was 7.1 ± 2.3 months (range, 4–11 months). During the follow-up period, patients received an average of 1.8 ± 1.2 SB15 injections (range, 1–5 injections).

In patients with BRVO (n = 24), the mean interval between the first SB15 injection and the first post-injection visit was 4.9 ± 2.0 weeks (range, 4–10 weeks), and the entire follow-up period was 6.8 ± 2.4 months (range, 4–11 months). During the follow-up period, patients received an average of 1.7 ± 1.0 SB15 injections (range, 1–5 injections). The mean logMAR BCVA was 0.64 ± 0.36 at baseline, 0.45 ± 0.26 at the first post-injection visit, and 0.41 ± 0.32 at the final follow-up (Fig. 1A). A significant difference in BCVA was observed between the three time points (P < 0.001). At the final follow-up, an improvement of ≥ 0.2 logMAR in BCVA was observed in 11 patients (45.8%), whereas a deterioration of ≥ 0.2 logMAR was noted in 1 patient (4.2%). The BCVA remained stable among the remaining 12 patients (50.0%).

Changes in best-corrected visual acuity (A) and central retinal thickness (B) after SB15 treatment in branch retinal vein occlusion. LogMAR = logarithm of minimum angle of resolution

The mean CRT was 527.1 ± 181.9 μm at baseline, 261.3 ± 95.8 μm at the first post-injection visit, and 285.1 ± 99.5 μm at the final follow-up (Fig. 1B). A significant difference in CRT was observed between the three time points (P < 0.001). Before treatment, IRF was observed in all 24 patients (100%), whereas SRF was observed in 12 patients (50.0%) (Fig. 2A). At the first post-injection visit, the incidences of IRF and SRF decreased to 20.8% (five patients) and 6.9% (one patient), respectively. At the final follow-up, the incidence rates of IRF and SRF were 33.3% (eight patients) and 8.3% (two patients), respectively. After a single SB15 injection, complete resolution of retinal fluid was observed in 19 eyes (79.2%), of which 11 experienced fluid recurrence during follow-up. The mean time to recurrence after the SB15 injection was 4.6 ± 1.6 months. Complete resolution was observed in 15 of 18 eyes (83.3%) with a first post-injection visit within 6 weeks and in 4 of 6 eyes (66.7%) with visits at 6 weeks or longer.

In patients with CRVO (n = 5), the mean interval between the first SB15 injection and the first post-injection visit was 5.2 ± 2.7 weeks (range, 4–10 weeks), and the entire follow-up period was 8.4 ± 1.8 months (range, 6–10 months). During the follow-up period, patients received an average of 2.4 ± 1.7 SB15 injections (range, 1–5 injections). The mean logMAR BCVA was 1.29 ± 0.26 at baseline, 0.90 ± 0.54 at the first post-injection visit, and 0.78 ± 0.61 at the final follow-up (Fig. 3A). A significant difference in BCVA was observed between the three time points (P < 0.022). At the final follow-up, an improvement of ≥ 0.2 logMAR in BCVA was observed in four patients (80.0%), while BCVA remained stable in the remaining one patient (20.0%).

Changes in best-corrected visual acuity (A) and central retinal thickness (B) after SB15 treatment in central retinal vein occlusion. LogMAR = logarithm of minimum angle of resolution

The mean CRT was 897.0 ± 344.5 μm at baseline, 213.2 ± 24.4 μm at the first post-injection visit, and 439.4 ± 268.9 μm at the final follow-up (Fig. 3B). A significant difference in CRT was observed between the three time points (P = 0.015). Before treatment, IRF was observed in all five patients (100%), while SRF was observed in four patients (80.0%) (Fig. 2B). At the first post-injection visit, the incidences of IRF and SRF decreased to 0% and 20.0% (one patient), respectively. At the final follow-up, the incidence rates of IRF and SRF were 60.0% (three patients) and 40.0% (two patients), respectively. After a single SB15 injection, complete resolution of retinal fluid was observed in four eyes (80.0%), of which two experienced fluid recurrence during follow-up. The mean time to recurrence after the SB15 injection was 5.5 ± 3.5 months.

In one patient with BRVO, mild anterior chamber inflammation was observed after SB15 injection, which subsequently resolved after treatment with topical steroids. No other adverse events, such as retinal detachment or endophthalmitis, were noted throughout the follow-up period. Discussion

In a comparative clinical trial targeting neovascular AMD, SB15 demonstrated visual outcomes equivalent to those of the original aflibercept, with a mean improvement of 6.7 letters at 8 weeks compared to 6.6 letters with the original agent [7]. At 34 weeks, the results remained comparable, with the SB15 group showing a mean improvement of 7.6 letters and a reduction of 110.4 μm in CRT, compared to a 6.5-letter improvement and a 115.7 μm CRT reduction in the original aflibercept group. Moreover, comprehensive structural, physicochemical, and biological analyses confirmed that SB15 exhibited properties similar to those of the originator drug [13].

Ranibizumab biosimilars were introduced earlier than aflibercept biosimilars. However, only a limited number of studies have reported their outcomes in RVO. In a study using Razumab, a ranibizumab biosimilar developed in India, the mean visual acuity improved from logMAR 0.89 at baseline to 0.41 at 48 weeks, and the mean CRT decreased from 527.25 μm to 307.47 μm over the same period [14]. Similarly, in a study conducted in South Korea using CKD-701, the mean BCVA improved from logMAR 0.58 at baseline to 0.48 at 6 weeks after injection, and the mean CRT decreased from 534.8 μm to 367.8 μm [15]. These findings suggest that biosimilar agents are effective in the treatment of RVO.

In the present study, SB15 treatment for macular edema secondary to BRVO resulted in improved visual acuity and anatomical outcomes. At the first follow-up after a single injection, intraretinal and subretinal fluid completely resolved in 79.2% of the eyes, demonstrating a good initial anatomical response. A similar trend was observed in patients with CRVO; however, because the analysis included only five eyes, definitive conclusions could not be drawn.

In Alforja et al.’s study reporting the long-term outcomes of anti-VEGF therapy in BRVO, the visual acuity improvements and CRT reductions during the early treatment period (within 12 months) were maintained up to 36 months, with an approximate gain of 11 letters [16]. The mean number of injections during the first year was 7.7. Among the treatment groups, the aflibercept group showed a greater mean CRT reduction (147 μm) than the bevacizumab (114 μm) and ranibizumab (128 μm) groups, indicating relatively superior anatomical outcomes.

In a short-follow-up study by Sakanishi et al., BCVA improved from mean logMAR 0.49 to 0.27 at 1 month after aflibercept injection for BRVO [17]. In a study by Pichi et al., which involved 12 months of follow-up, patients received a mean of 2.6 injections. Visual acuity improved from a mean logMAR value of 0.58 to 0.20, and mean CRT decreased from 498 μm to 204 μm [18]. In another study by Park et al., which employed a treat-and-extend regimen over 1 year, patients received an average of 6.7 injections, resulting in a mean CRT reduction of 326.2 μm and a mean visual acuity gain of 23.6 letters [19].

In a study by Hykin et al. involving patients with CRVO, a mean of 10 injections was administered over a 100-week study period, resulting in a mean CRT reduction of 378 μm and a mean visual acuity gain of 13.4 letters [20]. In another study by Chatziralli et al., conducted over 18 months, a mean visual acuity gain of 7.4 letters was observed [21]. At 12 months, the mean CRT reduction was 241 μm, and at 18 months, it was 234.6 μm. Additionally, macular edema completely resolved in 42.9% of the patients.

In the treatment of macular edema secondary to BRVO, aflibercept has demonstrated better outcomes than ranibizumab or bevacizumab in some cases. In a study by Spooner et al., patients with limited responses to prior therapies achieved better anatomical outcomes after switching to aflibercept [22]. Similarly, Khurana et al. reported that switching to aflibercept prolonged the duration of the edema-free status [23]. These findings underscore the high efficacy of aflibercept in managing this condition.

Despite its proven efficacy, bevacizumab has historically been the most commonly used agent [24]. This trend was likely influenced by the high cost of the original aflibercept formulations. Therefore, reducing the financial burden associated with treatment could promote the broader adoption of aflibercept-based therapies. In the present study, a mean visual acuity improvement in logMAR 0.22 was observed approximately 5 weeks after SB15 injection, and the mean improvement measured at 7 months was logMAR 0.28. These short-term visual outcomes were comparable to those reported by Sakanishi et al. [17] and Pichi et al. [18], suggesting that the aflibercept biosimilar demonstrates similar efficacy to the originator drug in treating macular edema secondary to RVO.

In our study, 57.9% of eyes in the BRVO group that achieved complete edema resolution after a single injection did not experience recurrence during the short-term follow-up, whereas the remaining eyes showed recurrence after a mean of 4.6 months. These findings may provide useful information for patient counseling and the adjustment of follow-up schedules when planning more efficient treatment strategies using SB15.

Considerable variability was observed in the timing of the first post-injection follow-up visit in this study, ranging from 4 to 10 weeks. Previous studies have reported that the mean duration of VEGF suppression with aflibercept is relatively long, at approximately 71 days [25]. However, clinical trials in patients with neovascular age-related macular degeneration have shown a slight increase in central retinal thickness at 8 weeks compared with 4 weeks after injection [26], suggesting a partial attenuation of treatment effect in some patients before 8 weeks. In our study, complete resolution was observed in 15 of 18 eyes (83.3%) with a first post-injection visit within 6 weeks and in 4 of 6 eyes (66.7%) with visits at 6 weeks or longer. Although the difference was modest, a relatively higher rate of fluid resolution with shorter follow-up intervals suggests that the timing of follow-up may have influenced the observed anatomical outcomes.

A key strength of this study is that it is the first to demonstrate the efficacy of SB15 for the treatment of macular edema secondary to RVO. These findings may serve as valuable reference data for future evaluation of treatment outcomes involving aflibercept biosimilars.

However, this study had several limitations. First, given the limited sample size and lack of a control group or comparative analysis with existing anti-VEGF agents, this study is exploratory in nature; therefore, the results should be interpreted with caution. Second, the follow-up durations and visit intervals varied among patients, and there was no standardized protocol for administering additional injections. Third, this study evaluated relatively short-term outcomes and, therefore, does not reflect long-term efficacy. Given that SB15 received regulatory approval from the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety of South Korea in February 2024, a limited follow-up period was required at the time of this study. Therefore, further long-term studies are warranted. Fourth, only five patients with CRVO were included in the analysis, making it difficult to draw definitive conclusions regarding treatment outcomes. Lastly, loading injections were not administered. Because previous reports have suggested that initial loading injections lead to better treatment outcomes than pro re nata dosing strategies [27], the present study may not fully reflect the outcomes achievable with loading injection protocols.

In conclusion, this study evaluated the short-term treatment outcomes of SB15, an aflibercept biosimilar, in patients with macular edema secondary to RVO. In the BRVO group, patients received an average of 1.7 injections over a mean follow-up period of 6.8 months, resulting in significant improvements in visual acuity and anatomical outcomes. Complete retinal fluid resolution was achieved with a single injection in 79.2% of eyes. These findings suggest that SB15 may have therapeutic potential in BRVO; however, given the limitations of this study, further studies, particularly those including a larger number of patients with CRVO, are warranted to confirm the study results.

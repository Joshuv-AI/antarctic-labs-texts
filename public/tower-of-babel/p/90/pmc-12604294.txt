Reliability and validity of the modified Chinese version of the Barratt Impulsiveness Scale (BIS-CV): a psychological autopsy study in rural China
Authors: Xinyu Bai, Guoxiang Chen, Qiqing Mo, Huiming He, Yourong Cao, Cunxian Jia, Liang Zhou, Zhenyu Ma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe modified Chinese Version of the Barratt Impulsiveness Scale (BIS-CV) has been widely used in China, but it lacks validation among older adults with completed suicide. Background

Globally, approximately 720,000 people die by suicide every year, and 73% of all suicides happen in low- and middle-income countries [1]. One of the Sustainable Development Goals targets a one-third reduction in the global suicide mortality rate by 2030 [2, 3]. According to a standardization analysis, older adults only account for 8.9% of the total population, while older adult suicides represent 38.2% of the total suicide cases in China during 2013–2014 [4]. The Chinese population is aging rapidly, and the number of older adult suicides will increase further [5]. In China, the suicide rate is higher in rural areas than in urban areas and highest among older adults compared to any other age group [6–10].

Impulsivity is a predisposition of rapid and unplanned reactions toward internal or external stimuli without considering the negative consequences of these reactions for oneself or others [11]. Most studies have demonstrated that high impulsivity is strongly associated with suicide. A survey of 347 patients found that individuals with impulsivity traits are at risk for suicide attempts, regardless of psychiatric diagnosis [7]. A meta-analysis showed that multiple facets of impulsivity are associated with suicidal behaviors among young individuals [12]. Among older adults, impulsivity is more broadly related to suicide-related ideation than hopelessness [13].

The Barratt Impulsiveness Scale (BIS) is widely used to measure impulsivity in the field of psychiatry. Barratt’s team compiled this scale in 1959 and revised it into BIS-11 in 1995 [14]. The BIS-11 has been developed in various languages and used in many countries [15–24]. The BIS-11 was revised and translated into Chinese version by Li et al. and showed good reliability and validity in China [25–28]. The BIS-CV has been widely used in China. Many studies have demonstrated good reliability and validity of this scale for measuring attempted suicide among community residents and college students [29–31]. However, the BIS-CV has not been validated among older adults with completed suicide in rural China.

To address such a research gap, we conducted the current study to test the reliability and validity of the BIS-CV among older adults in rural China. Considering the well-established strong association between impulsiveness and suicidality, we also aimed to test whether the BIS-CV could distinguish older adults with or without suicide. Therefore, we used a psychological autopsy case-control design to compare the BIS-CV score between the suicide group and the living controls. Case-control psychological autopsy is one of the most efficient ways used in suicide research to explore risk factors for completed suicide [32–35]. It has become a standard method that is widely used in the context of China and has good effectiveness and reliability [36–38]. In this study, we aimed to verify the reliability and validity of the BIS-CV among older adults in rural China based on psychological autopsy.

Methods

Participants and research design

Two-stage stratified random sampling based on the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) was applied to select 12 counties from Guangxi, Hunan, and Shandong Provinces as the research sites. From June 2014 to September 2015, older adults aged 60 and over who died from suicide were identified from the death certificates obtained from the county level of the Center for Disease Control and Prevention. For each suicide case, a living control was selected by matching age (± 3 years), gender, and living location of residence (living in the same community). The sample size was calculated based on the standard recommendation for instrument validation, which requires at least 5 participants for each item. The BIS-CV has a total of 30 items, indicating at least 150 participants are needed. In this study, we recruited 242 cases and 242 living controls, which satisfies the minimal sample size requirement.

The psychological autopsy technique was used to collect data. For each suicide case and living control, two informants were identified and interviewed. Additionally, the living controls were also interviewed. Each informant and living control was interviewed separately by a trained interviewer. The details of the study procedures have been described previously [39].

Measurements

General characteristics

The observed general characteristics of participants included sex, age, marital status, education, employment, family annual income, left-behind status, and physical health conditions. Older adults who were married and living together were classified as “stable,” whereas those who were unmarried, divorced, separated, or widowed were classified as “unstable.”

Mental disorder

The Chinese version of the Structured Clinical Interview for the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fourth Edition, was used to diagnose mental disorders.

The Chinese version of the Barratt impulsiveness scale (BIS-CV)

Impulsivity was measured using the BIS-CV [29, 31], which was revised from the BIS-11. The BIS-CV includes 30 items under three dimensions: non-planning (10 items), motor(10 items), and attentional impulsiveness (10 items). Each item is scored on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (none) to 5 (always), however, the items in the non-planning and attentional impulsiveness dimensions are reversely scored. The total score ranges from 30 to 150, with a higher score indicating a higher level of impulsiveness.

Statistical analysis

For both the living control group and the suicide group, we used proxy data from informants for the statistical analysis. A descriptive analysis, the Chi-Square test, and t-tests were used to compare general information and the scores of the BIS-CV between the suicide and control groups. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

In the living control group, we used the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) to evaluate the agreement between the data of the subjects and their proxy-based data from the informants. The ICC values were determined as follows: greater than 0.75 (excellent), 0.60–0.74 (good), 0.40–0.59 (fair), and less than 0.40 (poor) [31, 40]. Corrected correlation coefficients, Cronbach’s alpha, and split-half reliability were used to analyze internal consistency reliability, with recommended values of above 0.70 [41, 42]. We used a t-test comparing the BIS-CV scores between the two groups to confirm the discriminant ability. Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was used to analyze the structural validity and factor loading of the BIS-CV. The fit was acceptable if the CFI and TLI ≥ 0.9 [43], RMSEA ≤ 0.08 [44], and CMIN/DF < 3 [45].

Confirmatory factor analysis was performed using AMOS, and other statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 23.0 software.

Subject-proxy agreement on BIS

Based on the ICC value of 0.475 (P < 0.001), there was a fair agreement between the living self-reported and informants’ proxy data of the BIS-CV.

Reliability analysis

Corrected item-total score

In the suicide group, the correlation coefficient between each item and the total score was 0.242–0.837 (all correlations were significant at P < 0.01). The correlation coefficients between the total scale and its three subscales of non-planning, motor, and attentional impulsiveness were 0.835, 0.665, and 0.855, respectively.

In the control group, the correlation coefficient between each item and the total score was 0.029–0.772, with statistical significance for all items except for item 29. The correlation coefficients between the total scale and its three subscales of non-planning, motor, and attentional impulsiveness were 0.859, 0.636, and 0.879, respectively.

In both the suicide and control groups, item 30 was negatively correlated with the total score and its subscale, while the other items were positively correlated. A weak correlation was found between item 2 and the total score and between item 29 and the total score, with correlation coefficients < 0.4.

Internal consistency reliability

The split-half reliability was 0.939 in the suicide group and 0.927 in the control group. In the suicide group, Cronbach’s α was 0.910 for the BIS-CV, 0.878 for non-planning impulsiveness, 0.889 for motor impulsiveness, and 0.843 for attentional impulsiveness. In the control group, Cronbach’s α was 0.894 for the BIS-CV, 0.863 for non-planning impulsiveness, 0.780 for motor impulsiveness, and 0.794 for attentional impulsiveness.

Discriminant ability

As shown in Table 2, the suicide group had significantly higher scores on the total BIS-CV and its subscales than the control group. These findings suggest that the suicide group had a higher level of impulsiveness, indicating the good discriminant ability of the BIS-CV in differentiating the suicide and control groups.

Confirmatory factor analysis

The structural validity of the BIS-CV was tested using confirmatory factor structure analysis in both groups. The results supported the three-factor model of the BIS-CV. In the suicide group, the fit indices factors were CMIN/DF = 2.195, RMSEA = 0.07, CFI = 0.871, and TLI = 0.909. In the living control group, the fit indices factors were CMIN/DF = 1.616, RMSEA = 0.050, CFI = 0.908, and TLI = 0.901. Discussion

This study aimed to examine the reliability and validity of the BIS-CV in an older adult population in rural China. The BIS-CV score in the suicide group was found to be higher than that in the control group. This finding was consistent with previous studies showing significantly higher impulsivity scores in those with suicide attempts than in the control group [31]. This scale can be applied to assess impulsive personality traits in individuals with mental disorders or behavioral problems in future studies.

Each item of the BIS-CV is related to the overall score and its corresponding dimension. However, item 30, “I can always think of new ways to solve the difficulties,” is negatively correlated with the overall score. It may be that people with a low level of education have a biased understanding of this item, suggesting the need for further modification in the future. Item 2, “I do things without thinking,” and item 29, “I think of something, and I do it right away,” were weakly correlated (r < 0.4) with the total score, suggesting that these two items also require further modification.

Proxy data from informants were used for data analyses in both the suicide and living control groups. The agreement between the living targets and proxy respondents was fair, with an ICC value of 0.457. In previous studies, the BIS-CV had an ICC value of 0.80 for suicide attempters and 0.83 for the control group with proxy data [31]. The information proxy data for the BIS-CV is a better reflection of the targets’ genuine situation. The internal consistency of BIS-CV was good in the suicide and control groups, with Cronbach’s alpha coefficients and split-half coefficients greater than 0.7 for the total scale and all three subscales. Similar results were obtained in previous studies on the use of the BIS-CV in college students and community populations [29].

A Chinese study revealed that the structure of the BIS-CV was quite different from the original scale [26]; studies in other countries showed that the three-factor model of the BIS-11 is not ideal [23, 46]. However, our study confirmed a three-factor structure of the BIS-CV, including non-planning, motor, and attentional impulsiveness. This finding was consistent with the theoretical conception of the original scale, suggesting that the structural validity of the scale was well adapted to the suicide and control groups of Chinese rural older adults. However, some items had factor loadings of less than 0.5, indicating further modifications may be needed in the future.

Conclusions

Using the psychological autopsy method, this study showed that the BIS-CV had good reliability and validity in measuring impulsiveness in Chinese rural older adults with and without suicide. Specifically, the BIS-CV demonstrated a high Cronbach alpha coefficient and split-half reliability. CFA supported a three-factor structure including non-planning, motor, and attentional impulsiveness. Additionally, the BIS-CV score was significantly higher in the suicide group than in the living controls, indicating good discriminant ability. Our findings suggest that the BIS-CV is an effective tool to measure impulsiveness in Chinese rural older adults and has the potential to identify those at high risk of suicide, which carries significant implications for further development of suicide prevention programs.

Limitation

Information bias is inevitable in psychological autopsy methods. Problems were found with some of the items in the scale in this study, but no further exploration of modifying the relevant items was made. This study was a survey of older adults in rural China, and it is not yet possible to generalize the results to other groups.

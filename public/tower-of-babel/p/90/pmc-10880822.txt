Ergonomic and Anthropometric Evaluation of Locally Manufactured Vehicle Seats
Authors: Vikas Kumar, Kindu Oumer, Eshetu A. Merso, Rohit Sharma, Jaspreet Hira, Barun Haldar
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Most of the cars are imported as cargo vehicles and then converted into commercial passenger vehicles in Ethiopia. Seats are locally manufactured and installed as part of the conversion to passenger cars, with little regard permitted for the standard specifications required.Objective:A descriptive survey, an ergonomics assessment of passenger seats was conducted, and subjective evaluations of seat comfort were collected.Methods and Materials:For long journeys, there are problems of discomfort, back pain, and human fatigue. Anthropometric measurements of respondents and locally manufactured seats were used to conduct an objective evaluation of seat attributes.Results:The findings revealed that the passengers’ body proportions and the seat dimensions differ significantly and are not given any preference during design and fabrication of the seat.Conclusion:This study recommends the anthropometric dimensions of the Ethiopian passengers for the design and manufacturing of the vehicle seats.

INTRODUCTION

For the first vehicle, the first vehicle seat was adapted from a horse-drawn carriage without padding. The road rock springs were crude and seat modifications were not addressed. The development of extremely contoured seats that reduced the possibility of passengers being flung as the automobile pitch improved road safety. In general, little thought was given to it.[1] Comfortable automobile seating is no longer considered as a luxury but rather a need nowadays. A comfortable seat may have poor dynamic qualities which makes it uncomfortable on the road for the passengers. It is also possible that the best seat for one vehicle is not the best seat for another vehicle.

While evaluating the quality of the vehicle experience, both static and dynamic comfort must be considered. In the literature, very less studies have been available and concentrated on automobile seat comfort in Ethiopia. The anthropometric data of Ethiopians were not taken into account when designing automobile seats for Ethiopian passengers, particularly passenger seats for commercial vehicles. The ergonomic seat is critical for long-distance rides because it affects the passenger’s comfort; therefore, seat comfort needs to be thoroughly investigated for the targeted population.[2]

The static pressure distribution on the seat cushion is the feature to investigate in a static condition (vehicle remains stationary). Properties of the dynamic seat cushion and structure differ from same properties in the static conditions.[3]

Body structure evaluation, interface pressure distribution, and other typical ergonomic methodologies are used to assess static comfort.[4] Vibration on the surface of passenger chairs can be used to determine dynamic comfort and physiological parameters.[56] Whole body vibration causes discomfort, activity discomfort, and changes in physiological parameters of the passengers and drivers[78]

In Ethiopia, the mid-sized buses are preferred as the primary mode of transportation for people to commute from place to place. The bus passengers are the ones who complain the most about body pains after a lengthy ride. This research was conducted primarily to investigate the ergonomic evaluation of fabricated passengers’ seats using anthropometric measurements of passengers, as well as to compare seat dimensions to required standards, particularly on modified mid-buses from Isuzu trucks in the local bus bodybuilding industry.

The seat manufacturing industry will have an enormous contribution to make, furthermore, providing comfortable passengers transportation if it is well addressed and supported with eye-catching incentives.[9101112] Ineffective implementation of the specifications from road and transport authorities, shortage of sufficient testing tools, and seat structure bending machines to test the product and deficiency of trained manpower are the main problems observed in the local seat manufacturing sector.[13]

The study briefly covered the evaluation of the existing seat design for the comfort of passengers, transport standards of seat, deviation from the specification, and its influence on ride comfort.

METHODOLOGY

The major concern of this study, as indicated earlier, is to assess issues related to the ergonomics of locally fabricated passenger seats and passenger anthropometrics on modified Isuzu midi buses in Addis Ababa. A descriptive survey approach was employed mainly to reveal the current state of the problem and gather the data related to the participants in the study.

Subjective evaluation has been done with the help of analysis of the data together with passengers’ opinions on commercial vehicle seat comfort to realize the necessity of some of the questionnaires designed. Commercial vehicles, such as midi-buses, are intended to travel long distances, exposing passengers to fatigue rather than comfort.[1415] The purpose is to look into how people feel about the current seat layouts and to identify complaints and distress caused by long journeys. The survey also collects information on anthropometry among survey respondents and may aid in the investigation of any possible correlations between parameters.

Near a rest stop, travellers were asked to complete structured surveys by filling the questionnaire based on their journey and were asked to rate the seat features and the degree of body part discomfort on a scale of 1 to 5. The study’s target group ranged in age from 18-68 years with a mean of 42.5 years and a standard deviation of 12.3 years and who travel across Addis Ababa, encompassing all regions. The weight and height of the participants ranged from 45 to 88 kg and 148 to 190 cm with average weight 66.5 kg and the average height 168.2 cm, respectively.

The questionnaire consisted of basic questions and more particular questions that were written in a way that allowed the participants to respond to them. By deciding on an acceptable scale of evaluation, people had a strong desire to respond to queries. Such responses are advantageous and valuable for upgrading an automobile seat because they will reduce or eliminate discomfort even when sitting for long periods. The participants were required to write their body size metrics as a rough estimate; weight, height, gender, and age are all factors to consider.

Seat height, width, depth, backrest width, cushion softness, seat stability, backrest inclination, seat pitch, and overall discomfort are all factors to consider. On a five-point scale, participants were asked to rate each trait. Participants were asked to rate how uncomfortable specific body areas were during the voyage, such as neck support, lateral support, bum support, buttock comfort, thigh comfort, and footrest comfort.

For the subjective evaluation, 90 passengers were randomly selected (simple random sampling method) from eight mid-buses, and for the objective evaluation, 22 passengers and 24 seats were selected for the study.

After collecting and analysing subjective and objective evaluation data, descriptive statistics like means, standard deviations, and the fifth, 55th, and 95th percentiles of anthropometric as well as passenger seat variable measurements were generated using SPSS version 22.0.

The seat height, seat depth, seat breadth, and backrest height were all measured for the passenger seat. Because of the nature of the seat design, the seat width and backrest width were regarded to be the same. Hip breadth, shoulder breadth, buttock popliteal length, and popliteal height are the anthropometric measurements taken. Age, weight, and height were also acquired as extra information.

The Chi-square test at a 95% confidence level was done to see if there was a link between the dimensions of the seats and the passengers’ anthropometric measurements[16] and to see if there were any significant changes in the seat and passenger body proportions, an independent t-test (two-tailed) was used. All statistically significant differences were tested at the alpha value level of 0.05.

When anthropometric data are used to develop something, it must be representative of the population that will use it.[4] In many circumstances, the population of interest is made up of the general public, suggesting that the design characteristics must be able to accommodate a wide range of people. When products are made for specific groups (e.g., adult ladies, children, the elderly, etc.), data for such groups in the country or culture in question should be particular. However, there are several specific categories for whom no data are currently available. As a result, anthropometry is extremely important in the field of design.[18]

The summary of the anthropometric dimensions of the passengers in terms of means and standard deviations is presented in Table 4.

It is nearly impossible to design workspaces, equipment, or any other material to meet the needs of all users (the biggest, tallest, and widest, as well as the tiniest, shortest, and narrowest). As a result, meeting the needs of the majority by selecting 5%, 50%, or 95% of the population must be adequate. A similar issue exists in the automobile industry.[14]

To determine design values for an application, anthropometry uses percentiles of various body dimensions. The fifth percentile (lower percentile) of the population’s popliteal height is commonly recommended for seat height, so that a bigger proportion of the population may be accommodated.

Similarly, seat depth is calculated using the fifth percentile of buttock-popliteal length. However, to accommodate as many people as possible, the 95th percentile of hip width is normally advised when designing seat widths, allowing a fat person to use the seat. The critical parameters for the design of a passenger seat are the seat surface height, seat depth, seat breadth, backrest height, and backrest width.

MCH-PAN: gastrointestinal polyp detection model integrating multi-scale feature information
Authors: Ling Wang, Jingjing Wan, Xianchun Meng, Bolun Chen, Wei Shao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The rise of object detection models has brought new breakthroughs to the development of clinical decision support systems. However, in the field of gastrointestinal polyp detection, there are still challenges such as uncertainty in polyp identification and inadequate coping with polyp scale variations. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a novel gastrointestinal polyp object detection model. The model can automatically identify polyp regions in gastrointestinal images and accurately label them. In terms of design, the model integrates multi-channel information to enhance the ability and robustness of channel feature expression, thus better coping with the complexity of polyp structures. At the same time, a hierarchical structure is constructed in the model to enhance the model’s adaptability to multi-scale targets, effectively addressing the problem of large-scale variations in polyps. Furthermore, a channel attention mechanism is designed in the model to improve the accuracy of target positioning and reduce uncertainty in diagnosis. By integrating these strategies, the proposed gastrointestinal polyp object detection model can achieve accurate polyp detection, providing clinicians with reliable and valuable references. Experimental results show that the model exhibits superior performance in gastrointestinal polyp detection, which helps improve the diagnostic level of digestive system diseases and provides useful references for related research fields.

Introduction

With the continuous changes in human dietary habits, gastrointestinal polyps have become an increasingly common digestive system disease, posing a significant threat to the lives and health of numerous patients1,2. While most polyps are benign, the potential risk of malignant transformation should not be overlooked, making timely detection and treatment crucial3,4. Although conventional endoscopic examination methods are commonly used, their reliance on the experience and skills of medical professionals, coupled with the possibility of misdiagnosis, no longer suffice to meet the growing diagnostic demands. Therefore, developing an accurate, rapid, and user-friendly means of detecting gastrointestinal polyps has become an urgent task in current medical research5,6.

In recent years, deep learning technologies have achieved remarkable success in the field of computer vision, opening up new possibilities for medical image analysis7–9. However, in the field of polyp detection, a series of challenges still exist, such as the multi-scale problem, structural complexity, and the diversity in the appearance of polyps.

In addressing the multi-scale issue, various studies have proposed methods involving the fusion of multiple scale features10,11. However, these approaches have not entirely resolved the challenge of scale adaptability. Regarding structural intricacies, some research has introduced attention mechanisms to enhance the model’s focus on target structures12,13, yet capturing subtle differences remains a limitation. As for the diversity in the appearance of polyps, the current models still have limited generalization performance, making it difficult to effectively handle various complex polyp morphologies, which leads to suboptimal detection results in practice.

Against this backdrop, we propose a novel and powerful object detection framework called the “Multi-Channel Hierarchical Path Aggregation Network” (MCH-PAN). This framework enhances performance significantly in polyp detection tasks by efficiently integrating multi-channel information, constructing a hierarchical structure, and introducing a channel attention mechanism. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:Designing the Channel-Guided Feature Integrator (CGFI) to enhance the model’s channel feature expression capabilities. This mechanism makes the network more flexible in adapting to different representations of target features;Introducing the Scale-Guided Aggregator (SGA) to aggregate information from different scales, enabling the network to comprehensively understand polyp target features. This enhances the model’s adaptability to representations of multi-scale targets, thereby significantly improving overall performance;Designing the Hierarchical Attention Integrator (HAI) to guide attention to crucial targets at different levels of the network. This hierarchical attention strategy aids in capturing subtle differences in target structures, enhancing the precision of polyp detection.

Related work

In the early stages, the detection of gastrointestinal polyps primarily relied on advanced techniques such as endoscopic polyp detection and radiological detection. Through endoscopy, physicians can visually inspect the interior of the patient’s colon, facilitating the identification of polyps. Radiological detection encompasses traditional X-ray barium enema examinations and more sophisticated techniques like computerized tomography (CT) virtual colonoscopy, which utilize various imaging technologies for polyp detection.

In recent years, with the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence and machine learning technologies, image processing and machine learning-assisted detection have emerged as research focal points to enhance the accuracy of traditional polyp detection14–16. The application of deep learning technology in the field of polyp detection has made significant progress17–19, mainly due to its sophisticated algorithmic models. These models enable feature extraction and classification of medical images20,21, thereby improving the accuracy and efficiency of detection. Currently, there are three main types of deep learning technologies in polyp detection: traditional Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) technology, two-stage object detection technology, and single-stage object detection technology.

Traditional convolutional neural network method

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are among the popular structures in the field of deep learning, particularly well-suited for image recognition tasks. In the context of polyp detection, CNNs leverage their robust capabilities to automatically learn and extract crucial features from medical images, eliminating the need for tedious manual annotations or predefined feature extraction rules. This unique advantage enables CNNs to perform exceptionally well in handling complex gastrointestinal images, accurately identifying and distinguishing between normal tissues and polyps. Thus, CNNs provide robust technical support for the accurate detection of gastrointestinal polyps.

Tajbakhsh et al.22 proposed a polyp detection system based on a hybrid context feature method. This system utilizes contextual information to exclude non-polyp structures and, at the same time, utilizes shape information to precisely locate polyps with curved boundaries, significantly improving the accuracy of polyp detection. Urban et al.23 developed a deep convolutional neural network architecture for real-time polyp detection in colonoscopy videos. Leveraging large datasets and advanced neural network architectures, the framework demonstrates substantial potential in increasing the adenoma detection rate (ADR) without altering the colonoscopy examination procedure itself. Byrne et al.24 employed a deep learning model for standard colonoscopic video analysis, enabling real-time differentiation between adenomatous and hyperplastic small colorectal polyps. This method exhibits high sensitivity and specificity, streamlining the colonoscopy examination process.

Additionally, Shin et al.25 introduced a region-based Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) approach that combines deep CNN models and transfer learning to achieve automatic image detection of colorectal polyps. They also proposed post-learning methods such as automatic false positive learning and offline learning, overcoming obstacles in polyp detection and the scarcity of polyp images, thereby enhancing detection performance. Mohammed et al.26 presented a video polyp detection method named Y-Net, comprising two encoder networks and one decoder network. By ingeniously utilizing pre-trained and untrained models along with innovative summation skip-connection operations, they effectively improved detection performance. Ozawa et al.27proposed an automatic detection and classification method for colonoscopy images. Through training a CNN model, this method achieved high sensitivity and positive predictive value in polyp detection, demonstrating excellent performance across different types of colorectal polyp images.

Two-stage object detection method

Two-stage object detection methods, represented by R-CNN and its variants, initiate the detection process by first generating candidate regions for potential targets. Subsequently, these candidate regions undergo precise classification and bounding box regression. This approach typically excels in terms of accuracy, especially in scenarios that demand precise localization of polyp positions.

Among them, Qadir et al.28 proposed an improved two-stage polyp detection method. This method first utilizes a CNN to accurately detect regions of interest, and then combines temporal dependency information to effectively reduce the false detection rate, overcoming the limitations of CNNs. Qian et al.29 also introduced a polyp detection method based on Faster R-CNN. This approach first enhances image quality through preprocessing techniques to highlight polyp features, and then feeds the processed images into the Faster R-CNN model for automatic polyp detection. Experimental results show that this method significantly improves performance in colorectal polyp detection tasks.

Additionally, Wang et al.30 proposed an improved Faster R-CNN network for gastric polyp detection. This network enhances pooling operations and the loss function, using soft-NMS instead of traditional NMS. The improved Faster R-CNN achieved better performance in detecting gastric polyp images. Liu et al.31 introduced a lightweight Faster R-CNN framework for medical image object detection, which cleverly combines encrypted sharing and edge computing technologies to enhance computational efficiency under privacy protection. Through the careful design of interactive protocols and improved secure computation subprotocols, it achieved efficient and secure object detection.

One-stage object detection method

One-stage detection methods like SSD and the YOLO series can directly predict the category and location of targets in an image without the need for pre-generating candidate regions. The advantage of these methods lies in their speed, making them particularly suitable for real-time or near-real-time medical image analysis applications. By reducing computational redundancy and accelerating the inference process, single-stage methods quickly identify and locate areas of pathology, providing timely auxiliary diagnostic information for medical professionals.

Among them, Sharma et al.32 proposed a method called YcOLOn, which combines local and global channel context by introducing a multi-scale channel attention (MS-CAM) component and integrating it into the attention feature fusion (AFF) module. Four AFF modules are then embedded into the neck structure of the model, replacing traditional concatenation methods, which enhances the model’s ability to locate polyps. Zhang et al.33 introduced a polyp detection method called SSD-GPNet, which reuses data lost in the pooling layers by connecting it as an additional feature map. Simultaneously, they connected low-level feature maps with those obtained from upper-level deconvolution to enhance information interaction across different layers. Chou et al.34 proposed a single-stage colorectal polyp detection method that combines feature extraction and data augmentation techniques. This method uses discrete wavelet transformation to extract polyp texture features, strengthening subtle features. By extending the training dataset through GAN, the detection accuracy of small polyps is significantly improved. Shuang et al.35 presented a real-time detection method for colorectal adenomas based on YOLOv5s. They incorporated a channel attention mechanism into the backbone network of YOLOv5s and used BCEWithLogitsLoss as the loss function to achieve more efficient and accurate real-time detection of colorectal adenomas.

Additionally, Liu et al.36 utilized the SSD framework combined with the InceptionV3 feature extractor for polyp detection in colonoscopy videos. Experimental results demonstrate that this approach outperforms existing methods, achieving real-time detection. Doniyorjon et al.37 proposed an automatic detection method for wireless endoscopic images based on YOLOv4-tiny. This method enhances model accuracy by replacing the backbone structure and using data augmentation techniques to improve data quality. Compared to the original model, it effectively reduces detection localization errors in disease regions of wireless endoscopic images. Wu et al.38 introduced a polyp detection model named PATM-YOLO. This model improves attention to small polyps by constructing a small polyp detection head and integrating an improved PATM attention module. It also enhances feature richness by using an improved ASFF module to fuse multi-scale information and incorporates Swin Transformer into the network to optimize its position for improved detection accuracy. Lalinia et al.39 proposed a polyp detection system based on the YOLOv8 network for polyp recognition in colonoscopy. This system achieves a balance between accuracy and computational efficiency by constructing a diverse dataset and performing extensive evaluations.

Comparison of related methods

In polyp detection, traditional convolutional neural network methods, two-stage object detection methods, and single-stage object detection methods each have their own characteristics and advantages.

Traditional convolutional neural network methods primarily rely on the combination of convolutional layers, pooling layers, and fully connected layers to detect polyps by sequentially extracting and abstracting features. These methods typically exhibit high accuracy and stability, but they may face challenges in handling multiscale polyps or complex backgrounds, leading to issues such as missed detections or false positives.

Two-stage object detection methods adopt a staged strategy. Firstly, a set of candidate target regions is generated, and preliminary screening and filtering are applied to these regions. Then, refined classification and localization are performed on the filtered regions. The advantage of this method lies in its ability to more accurately identify and locate polyps, especially in complex backgrounds. However, due to the need for processing in two stages, its computational complexity is relatively high, resulting in slower detection speeds.

One-stage object detection methods aim to complete polyp detection and classification in a single forward pass. These methods typically exhibit higher detection speeds, making them suitable for scenarios with high real-time requirements. However, as they omit the candidate region generation and filtering steps, their accuracy may be slightly lower compared to two-stage methods.

Despite the numerous methods that have emerged in the field of polyp detection, existing technologies still face challenges such as missed detections and poor performance in multi-scale detection. To address this challenge, this paper innovatively proposes a polyp detection algorithm. The core of this algorithm lies in the fusion of multi-scale features. By optimizing feature extraction and integration mechanisms, the algorithm aims to significantly improve the accuracy and robustness of polyp detection, providing more reliable technical support for clinical diagnosis and treatment.

Method

To enhance the performance of colorectal polyp target detection models, this paper introduces an innovative network architecture based on YOLOv8s, named Multi-Channel Hierarchical Path Aggregation Network (MCH-PAN), as illustrated in (Fig. 1). In comparison to the traditional PAN structure40, MCH-PAN achieves a significant improvement in model performance through the design of three key components: Scale-Guided Aggregator (SGA), Hierarchical Attention Integrator (HAI), and Channel-Guided Feature Integrator (CGFI).Fig. 1Architecture of the MCH-PAN.

Hierarchical attention integrator

The introduction of the Hierarchical Attention Integrator (HAI) is primarily aimed at establishing an effective attention mechanism between local features and high-dimensional features. The input to this module comes from the feature maps at positions N3, N4, and N5 in the model’s neck section. After processing by the CGFI, the output is then transmitted to higher layers, namely positions H4 and H5. The pseudo-code for SGA is shown in Algorithm 3.

Experiment

Dataset and experimental environment

The dataset used in this study includes images of gastric and intestinal polyps, comprised of the Kvasir-SEG41 dataset and a gastric polyp dataset created by Zhang et al.33. In total, there are 1,758 endoscopic images containing polyp targets, which are divided into training, validation, and test sets with a ratio of 0.75:0.15:0.10.

Although the Kvasir-SEG dataset provides bounding box information for polyp categories, this information cannot be directly used for training and validation of YOLO series models. To meet the requirements of YOLO series models, the bounding box information provided in the Kvasir-SEG dataset was extracted and normalized in conjunction with the image size. Some samples of the dataset and their labels are shown in Fig. 2, where the rectangular boxes indicate the detected polyps.Fig. 2Dataset labeling.

Dataset labeling.

The dataset from Zhang et al.33 consists of images from 215 patients who underwent endoscopic examinations at Shao Yifu Hospital in Zhejiang Province, China, from January to June 2015. The dataset was augmented by rotating the original images by 180°. The Kvasir-SEG dataset41, on the other hand, was collected by the Vestre Viken Health Trust (VV) in Norway, which includes four hospitals providing medical services to a population of 470,000. The Bærum Hospital’s large gastroenterology department provided the training data. The combination of these two datasets enhances the model’s applicability across different populations and polyp targets.

A detailed analysis of the processed dataset is provided in Figure x. Figure x (left) shows the distribution of bounding box center points, indicating that the targets are distributed in various directions, which is highly consistent with clinical experience. Figure x (right) illustrates the sizes of the bounding boxes, showing that most are rectangular and primarily represent small to medium-sized polyps. The dataset also includes some large polyp targets, which aligns with clinical observations. These distribution characteristics help the model effectively capture polyps of different sizes and positions during training, improving the model’s generalization performance.

To ensure the reliability of the results, all experiments were conducted on two Tesla V100 16G GPUs using the PyTorch 2.0.0 framework. To guarantee efficient training and optimization of the model, CUDA 11.8 and Python 3.9 were configured for the experiments. The Adam optimizer was used with a batch size of 16, and the training was carried out for 200 epochs with an initial learning rate of 0.01. Input images were uniformly resized to 640 × 640 pixels to ensure that the model could effectively process and recognize polyps of various scales. These hardware and software configurations ensured the performance and stability of the model.

This hierarchical attention strategy helps the model more precisely locate targets and capture subtle structural differences, thus improving detection accuracy. CGFI enhances the model’s channel feature representation capability, making it more flexible in adapting to various target feature expressions. These three key mechanisms collectively drive the superior performance of the MCH-PAN model in gastrointestinal polyp detection tasks.

Discussion

The proposed Multi-Channel Hierarchical Path Aggregation Network (MCH-PAN) framework has achieved significant success in the field of gastrointestinal polyp detection. However, like any method, it comes with both strengths and weaknesses that require thorough consideration.

MCH-PAN’s primary strengths lie in its comprehensive scale adaptability and effective feature integration capabilities. By introducing the Smart Scale Guidance Mechanism (SGA) and the Hierarchical Attention Integrator (HAI), MCH-PAN can better understand features across different scales and complex target scenarios, enhancing its performance in gastrointestinal polyp detection. This provides a novel and powerful solution for multi-channel, multi-scale tasks.

In clinical practice, MCH-PAN can be deployed in endoscopic examination equipment to perform real-time analysis of endoscopic images and automatically identify polyp targets. This deployment approach not only aids doctors in quickly locating suspicious polyp areas during the examination but also reduces human error, enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of diagnosis. Additionally, doctors can obtain immediate image analysis results during the procedure, supporting more precise treatment decisions.

The application of MCH-PAN is not limited to real-time detection on a single device; it can also serve as a post-processing tool to analyze archived endoscopic image data. This approach is suitable for large-scale screening and follow-up, helping medical institutions process a vast amount of imaging data in a short period, thereby improving operational efficiency.

However, MCH-PAN still faces certain challenges in practical applications. Its computational complexity is slightly high, which may limit its application in resource-constrained environments. Additionally, tuning hyperparameters can be relatively complex, requiring deeper research and domain-specific expertise.

Future research directions can focus on addressing these challenges. Firstly, for the issue of computational complexity, exploring lightweight strategies can ensure the efficient operation of MCH-PAN on a broader range of computing devices. Secondly, introducing adaptive hyperparameter optimization methods can simplify the tuning process, making MCH-PAN more readily applicable to various tasks and datasets. Finally, additional clinical validation work will contribute to further confirming the effectiveness of MCH-PAN in real medical settings.

By addressing these issues, MCH-PAN is poised to become a crucial tool in the field of gastrointestinal polyp detection, enhancing the accuracy and efficiency of medical image analysis. This lays a solid foundation for future research and practical applications.

Conclusion

This paper introduces an innovative object detection framework - the Multi-Channel Hierarchical Path Aggregation Network (MCH-PAN), successfully applied in the field of gastrointestinal polyp detection. With the incorporation of key components, the Scale-Guided Aggregator (SGA), and the Hierarchical Attention Integrator (HAI), MCH-PAN demonstrates significant performance improvements in multi-channel and multi-scale tasks.

Through the experiments and discussions presented in this paper, we summarize the primary advantages of MCH-PAN, including comprehensive scale adaptability and effective feature integration capabilities. The introduction of SGA and HAI enables the model to perform exceptionally well under various lighting conditions, distinctiveness of target features, and in multi-target scenarios. This provides a more flexible and accurate solution for gastrointestinal polyp detection tasks.

While MCH-PAN has achieved positive results in practical applications, we acknowledge that it still faces some challenges, including computational complexity and the difficulty of hyperparameter tuning. Future research directions should focus on optimizing the framework for lightweight operations, adaptive hyperparameter optimization, and conducting more extensive clinical validation work.

MCH-PAN demonstrates significant potential for widespread application in medical image analysis. Through continuous improvement and optimization, we aim to propel it towards practical applications in gastrointestinal polyp detection. This will elevate the standard of medical image processing, providing doctors with more reliable and efficient diagnostic tools.

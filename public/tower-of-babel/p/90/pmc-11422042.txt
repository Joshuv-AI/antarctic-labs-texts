An unusual case of primary melioidotic prostatic disease: Misdiagnosed as benign prostatic hyperplasia
Authors: Yangbo Cai, Hongtao Jiang, Tao Li, Dengke Luo, Peng Li, Yi Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractMelioidosis is a fatal infectious disease caused by Burkholderia pseudomallei. Its clinical manifestations are so varied that it has been called a great mimic. The primary melioidotic prostatic disease usually presents as an abscess of the prostate. This is the only documented case misdiagnosed as benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) because it does not show as a prostate abscess. The patient was a 66 years old male with a history of hypertension and alcoholism. Symptoms of difficulty urinating and fever had persisted for three days. Laboratory tests of the patient showed a marked increase in inflammatory markers. The first urine culture was performed to isolate Acinetobacter lwoffii, not Burkholderia pseudomallei. And CT showed prostatic hyperplasia with calcification. Thus, the preliminary diagnosis was BPH, urinary tract infection, and pulmonary infection. Cefmenoxime and Piperacillin were given for anti-infective treatment, but both of the effects were not satisfactory. Then, the second urine culture and multiple hemocultures were performed to isolate Burkholderia pseudomallei. The final diagnosis was primary melioidotic prostatic disease, BPH, pulmonary infection, bacteremia and multiple organ damage. The patient was treated with imipenem for two weeks and cured. Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) was administered orally for twelve weeks after discharge. Therefore, clinicians need to have an understanding of primary melioidotic prostate disease to avoid misdiagnosis and mistreatment to improve the cure rate and survival rate of patients.Keywords: Primary melioidotic prostatic disease, Melioidosis, Burkholderia pseudomallei, Benign prostatic hyperplasia, Case report

Introduction

Melioidosis is endemic in Southeast Asia and is a fever disease caused by Burkholderia pseudomallei. Clinical manifestation from chronic to local infection is characterized by acute abscess septicemia involving multiple organs, and the most common clinical presentation is pneumonia [1]. Doctors easily misdiagnose the disease as tuberculosis, common pneumonia, or other pyogenic infection due to the diversity of its clinical manifestations and lack of specificity; melioidosis is named “the great imitator” [1,2]. Prostatic abscess due to Burkholderia pseudomallei is rarely reported in China, especially when misdiagnosed as benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH). Woo et al. published cases of melioidosis in 30 patients with prostate abscesses in Hong Kong, China [3]. We report an unusual primary melioidotic prostatic disease misdiagnosed as BPH in Hainan, China.

Case report

A 66-year-old male patient from Lingshui, Hainan, China, who had been engaged in infrastructure construction for a long time, was hospitalized in the Second Affiliated Hospital of Hainan Medical University for dysuria accompanied by fever for three days. The patient had a long history of alcohol abuse. However, the patient had no family history of the disease. Body examination did not reveal any skin wounds, and there is no history of skin damage recently. Based on a digital rectal examination, the palpable prostate was enlarged and had a medium-sized and smooth surface, and the central sulcus disappeared without apparent tenderness. The CT results revealed prostate hyperplasia (62 mm × 53 mm × 45 mm) (Fig. 1a) and infected lesions at the apex of both lungs and the anterior segment of the right upper lobe (Fig. 1c). T-PSA was 54.713ng/ml and F-PSA was 11.130ng/ml. The first urine culture was performed to isolate Acinetobacter lwoffii, not Burkholderia pseudomallei (Supplementary Table 1). The changes in inflammation indexes during hospitalization is showed by Supplementary Fig. 1. As we can see from these numbers, inflammatory markers were significantly elevated at the beginning of hospitalization and remained high during the following seven days. After two weeks of imipenem treatment, inflammatory indices dropped to near-normal levels. The preliminary diagnosis was BPH, urinary tract infection, and pulmonary infection. Finasteride and Tamsulosin Hydrochloride were used to treat BPH. Cefmenoxime (1.5g, q8h) was given for anti-infective treatment, but the effect was not satisfactory. After three days, Cefmenoxime was changed to Piperacillin (2.25g, q8h) according to the drug sensitivity results of the first urine culture (Supplementary Table 1a). After two days of treatment, the patient still had a fever, with the highest temperature reaching 39.9 °C. Electrocardiogram monitoring showed that heart rate was 110 beats/min and blood pressure was 91/63 mmHg. What's more, due to the impaired liver and kidney function of the patient, there is multiple organ damage. So, the sofa score was more than 2 points. The patient was transferred to the Intensive Care Unit (ICU) with hemoculture indicating Burkholderia pseudomallei (sensitive to imipenem) (Supplementary Tables 1a and b). The final diagnosis was primary melioidotic prostatic disease, BPH, pulmonary infection, bacteremia and multiple organ damage. Imipenem (1 g/time, pump injection, 25 ml/h, q8h) was given. After treatment for two days, the patient was transferred back to the urology department and continued to receive imipenem (1 g/time, ivgtt, q8h) for twelve days. After two weeks of imipenem treatment, the CT results revealed the BPH was smaller than before (50 mm × 42 mm × 40 mm), and calcification was the same as before (Fig. 1b) and inflammatory exudation of both lung apices and the anterior segment of the upper lobe of the right lung was absorbed better than before (Fig. 1d). The patient's hemoculture showed negative results. Inflammatory indices dropped to near-normal levels (Supplementary Fig. 1). Reexamining T-PSA was 5.824ng/ml, and F-PSA was 0.949ng/ml. After the patient's condition improved, he was discharged and given oral Trimethoprim-Sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) for three months, with continuous follow-up. He was subsequently seen in three months, six months, one year, and three years after discharge, at which times he was asymptomatic with no biochemical and imaging evidence of melioidosis. The patient's entire treatment process is showed by Supplementary Fig. 2.

Discussion

Burkholderia pseudomallei is a Gram-negative bacillus primarily transmitted through the skin, inhalation, and ingestion pathways [1]. The patient is a construction worker who often works their fields barefoot so that infection can be acquired through the skin. Burkholderia pseudomallei is mainly prevalent in southern China, including Hainan, Guangdong, and Guangxi, with Hainan being the most severe [2,4]. Risk factors for melioidosis include male gender, middle age and elderly, diabetes, alcohol abuse, immunosuppressive status, and frequent contact with soil or water in endemic areas, among which the most crucial factor is diabetes [5]. Kozlowski et al. found that the most common clinical risk factor for patients with melioidosis prostate abscess is excessive alcohol intake [6]. This case was a 66-year-old male, a construction worker in Ling Shui, Hainan province, who has a long history of alcoholism, consistent with some high-risk factors for melioidosis.

After admission, the patient was preliminarily diagnosed with BPH according to his clinical manifestations and related examinations. Besides the first urine culture was performed to isolate Acinetobacter lwoffii, not Burkholderia pseudomallei (Supplementary Table 1). We speculate that the first urine culture either had multiple infections or was contaminated. Corresponding measures had been taken, but the effects were still not good. The digital rectal review helps detect changes in the prostate, but it does not distinguish between prostate abscesses and other causes of acute prostatitis [7]. In this case, it was difficult to distinguish between primary melioidotic prostatic disease and BPH using rectal digital examination because there was no obvious prostate tenderness observed in the patient's rectal examination. In addition, CT scans of patients with primary melioidotic prostatic disease can indicate multiple low-density mass shadows, usually in the shape of a compartment, septum, and lobe. However, the CT scan of this patient did not show various low-density mass shadows, only suggesting BPH. A review of the patient's CT scan after two weeks of imipenem treatment indicated that the volume of the hyperplastic prostate was significantly reduced compared with the previous one. Therefore, in future diagnosis and treatment, attention should be paid to distinguishing BPH and prostatic melioidosis, and the possibility of the simultaneous existence of both cannot be ruled out. When necessary, a prostate puncture biopsy is feasible to confirm the diagnosis. Therefore, a limitation of this report is that the patient did not receive a needle biopsy for a prostate abscess.

The 2020 edition of Darwin melioidosis treatment guideline for treating melioidosis indicated that treatment is mainly divided into intravenous antibiotic strengthening and oral antibiotic eradication stage [8]. In addition, in a randomized controlled trial of an oral eradication agent for melioidosis, Chetchotisakd et al. found that TMP-SMX was as effective as TMP-SMX plus doxycycline during the oral phase of melioidosis treatment, with superior patient safety and tolerability [9]. Therefore, Ceftazidime, meropenem, or imipenem is recommended for two weeks in the intensive phase of intravenous antibiotics [10]. Subsequently, TMP-SMX was recommended for at least twelve weeks during the antibiotic intensification and oral antibiotic eradication phases. The patient, in this case, had positive hemoculture many times, and the diagnosis of prostatic melioidosis complicated with bacteremia was established. Therefore, hemoculture as a molecular method can be used to improve diagnosis and therapy outcome. imipenem was given in the intensive stage. The inflammation index significantly decreased after one week, did not drop to the normal range, and may appear even higher again (Supplementary Fig. 1). After two weeks, these indicators returned to normal and did not rebound. Overall, intensive intravenous treatment with imipenem for at least two weeks and oral radical treatment with TMP-SMX for twelve weeks were required to minimize the likelihood of melioidosis recurrence, consistent with Darwin's guidelines.

Conclusions

In summary, Burkholderia pseudomallei is increasingly recognized as an important human pathogen worldwide. However, its clinical manifestations are atypical and non-specific, and it is easy to be misdiagnosed and mistreated. Therefore, clinicians need to have an understanding of primary melioidotic prostate disease to avoid misdiagnosis and mistreatment to improve the cure rate and survival rate of patients.

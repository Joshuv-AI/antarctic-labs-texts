Comparison of 40% Glucose Solution and Autologous Blood Patch Pleurodesis for the Treatment of Postoperative Air Leak After Lung Resections: A Prospective Randomized Controlled Study
Authors: Piotr Jerzy Skrzypczak, Tomasz Dobiecki, Monika Rozmiarek, Piotr Gabryel, Mariusz Kasprzyk, Cezary Piwkowski
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesPostoperative air leak is among the most common complications in thoracic surgery. Current management strategies are often suboptimal. Intrapleural administration of a glucose solution has emerged as a novel therapeutic approach; however, its clinical value has not been thoroughly investigated. This study aimed to compare the effectiveness of intrapleural 40% glucose solution vs autologous blood patch pleurodesis in managing postoperative air leak.MethodsThis prospective, randomized, controlled study enrolled patients who underwent anatomical lung resections (segmentectomy, lobectomy, or bilobectomy) at the Department of Thoracic Surgery, Poznan University of Medical Sciences, between November 2023 and December 2024. Patients with postoperative air leak were randomized to receive either 40% glucose (study group) or autologous blood (control group). The primary end-point was cessation of postoperative air leak. Secondary end-points included incidence of residual air space, chest pain, and reoperation.ResultsA total of 110 patients were included: 47 in the glucose group and 63 in the blood group. Glucose administration resulted in a higher rate of postoperative air leak resolution (95.7% vs 82.5%, P = .033; 95% CI: 2.2% to 24.2%). The glucose group had a shorter postoperative hospital stay (7 vs 9 days, P = .036). Chest pain occurred more frequently in the glucose group, but the difference was not statistically significant (10.6% vs 4.8%, P = .24). No major infectious complications were observed. Residual air space occurrence and reoperation rates were comparable.ConclusionsIntrapleural 40% glucose solution achieved a higher resolution rate of postoperative air leak compared to autologous blood patch pleurodesis, with a comparable safety profile.Clinical Registration NumberNCT06936969 (ClinicalTrials.gov).Keywords: postoperative air leak, autologous blood patch pleurodesis, 40%, glucose solution, anatomical lung resections, ABPP

INTRODUCTION

Postoperative air leak (PoAL) remains one of the most frequent complications following pulmonary resection, occurring in approximately 5%-25% of cases.1 It is typically defined as the continued air loss from the residual lung parenchyma beyond the fifth postoperative day. The underlying cause is often an alveolar-pleural fistula, which is an abnormal connection between the alveoli and the pleural space. It may result from parenchymal laceration, bronchial stump leakage, or surgical dissection. Risk factors include pleural adhesions, incomplete interlobar fissure, emphysema, or reduced lung function (low preoperative FEV1 values).2–5

Persistent PoAL prolongs chest drainage, restricts mobility, and increases the risk of complications such as pneumonia, empyema, or respiratory failure, particularly in patients with pre-existing pulmonary disease. Effective management of PoAL has the potential to reduce morbidity, improve quality of life, shorten hospitalization, and lower treatment costs.6–8

Management strategies for PoAL include surgical techniques and non-surgical approaches, particularly chemical pleurodesis. While various agents and protocols are used to promote pleural adhesion and seal air leaks, the quality of supporting evidence remains low.6 Most studies are small, heterogeneous, and observational, and clinical practice is often based on local protocols or surgeon preference rather than standardized recommendations. A key knowledge gap includes the lack of documented superiority of any of the methods, as well as a systematically analysed safety profile of the most popular conservative treatments for PoAL.

The aim of our study was to compare the efficacy and safety of intrapleural instillation of 40% glucose solution with the autologous blood patch pleurodesis (ABPP) in the treatment of PoAL following anatomical lung resection.

METHODS

Study design and ethics

This prospective, single-centre, open-label randomized controlled trial was conducted at the Department of Thoracic Surgery, Poznan University of Medical Sciences, Poland. The study was approved by the local Bioethics Committee (No. 765/24) and registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06936969). Patient recruitment occurred between 22 November 2023 and 14 December 2024. All participants provided written informed consent before enrollment.

Adults (≥18 years) who underwent anatomical lung resections (segmentectomy, lobectomy, or bilobectomy) were eligible. Exclusion criteria were: non-anatomical procedures (eg, wedge or sleeve resections, lung transplantation), active infection or sepsis, inability to provide 120 mL of peripheral blood, necessity for immediate reoperation or other interventions interfering with pleurodesis, cognitive or psychiatric conditions limiting consent or compliance, or failed to complete 3 pleurodesis attempts due to consent withdrawal.

Sample size calculation, randomization, and blinding

The required sample size was calculated, based on prior data. The anticipated incidence for 40% glucose solution vs ABPP was assumed to be 95% vs 80%, respectively, with alpha = 0.05, power = 70% and enrollment ratio = 1. The group size was calculated as 59 participants in each group. Patients were randomized 1:1 postoperatively using a web-based random number generator (www.graphpad.com), without stratification. Due to visible differences between agents and blood collection in 1 group, blinding was not feasible. The study was conducted as an open-label randomized controlled trial.

Outcome measures

The primary end-point was the resolution of PoAL after a maximum of 3 administrations of ABPP or a 40% glucose solution, assessed during the initial hospitalization. Resolution was defined as a sustained air leak rate <20 mL/min for at least 8 hours, followed by successful drain removal, no pneumothorax on a control chest radiograph (taken 3 hours after drain removal), and discharge. Follow-up continued for 90 days to capture secondary outcomes. The secondary outcomes included;

The study was reported according to the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) recommendations (Supplementary Material).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using Statistica 13.0 (StatSoft, Dell, Round Rock, TX, USA) and R software (version 4.3.2) with RStudio (version 2023.12.1 + 402; Posit PBC). Categorical data were analysed with the chi-squared test and the Fisher’s exact test for small sample sizes. The unpaired t-test analysed the data with normal distribution and homogeneous variances. The normality of the distribution was tested with the Shapiro-Wilk test, and the equality of variances was checked with Levene’s test. The data that did not follow a Gaussian distribution were analysed with the Mann-Whitney U-test. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was used to assess time from surgery to hospital discharge, using the survival and survminer packages in R. The number-at-risk table was included in the graphical outputs. All results were considered significant at a P-value below .05. Time from surgery to hospital discharge was analysed using survival analysis methods.

Intention-to-treat analysis

The PoAL resolution was analysed using the intention-to-treat (ITT) approach, including all randomized patients. Protocol deviations (eg, discharge with chest tube, incomplete pleurodesis, consent withdrawal) were classified as treatment failures. A secondary per-protocol analysis included only patients who completed the full protocol (up to 3 administrations).

Administration of the 40% glucose solution was significantly more effective in resolving PoAL than ABPP (95.7% vs 82.5%, P = .033; 95% CI: 2.2% to 24.2%) (Figure 3).

Distribution of Patients with and without Successful Pleurodesis Following the Use of 40% Glucose or Autologous Blood Patch Pleurodesis (ABPP). Bars indicate the number of patients in whom the intervention was effective or ineffective for each treatment group

The median hospital stay was 7 days in the glucose group and 9 days in the ABPP group. The difference in medians was −2 days (95% CI: −3.49 to 0.49), and the difference was statistically significant (P = .036).

The 40% glucose solution was more frequently effective after a single administration compared to ABPP (61.7% vs 41.3%, P = .034), and fewer patients in the glucose group required 3 procedures (2.1% vs 15.9%, P = .017).

Post-procedural chest pain requiring administration of intravenous non-opioid analgesics was more frequently reported in the glucose group, but the difference was not statistically significant (10.4% vs 4.7%, P = .24; 95% CI: −4.2% to 18.9%, P = .20).

There were no major infectious complications in either group. In the 40% glucose group, median blood glucose increased by 71 mg/dL at 30 min post-administration. This elevation was clinically insignificant and required no insulin therapy. No glucose elevation was observed in the ABPP group. The incidence of RAS ≥ 30 millimeters was 14.9% in the glucose group and 12.7% in the ABPP group (P = .638; 95% CI: −10.7% to 15.8%), and reoperation rates were 2.1% vs 1.6%, respectively (P = .609; 95% CI: −3.9% to +9.2%). Continued pleural drainage beyond 48 hours after the third pleurodesis attempt was more common in the ABPP group (14.3% vs 2.1%, P = .023; 95% CI: −23.7% to −3.8%). In 13 patients, triple pleurodesis failed to resolve the air leak. Two of them required reoperation (1 in the ABPP group and 1 in the 40% glucose group), while 11 required continued drainage, beyond 48 hours after the final pleurodesis attempt. Of these, 5 patients were discharged with a Heimlich valve (3 in the ABPP group and 2 in the 40% glucose group); in all such cases, the chest drain was removed within 30 days postoperatively (presented in Table 2 as “continued drainage after third pleurodesis”). Detailed outcome data are summarized in Table 2. A comparison of hospital stay duration is illustrated in Figures 4 and 5. Time-to-discharge analysis using Kaplan-Meier curves further confirmed the advantage of the glucose group in reducing hospitalization duration (Figure 5).

In the ITT analysis, including all randomized patients, PoAL resolution was achieved in 90.0% of the glucose group and 77.6% of the ABPP group (P = .078). Although not statistically significant, the trend aligned with the per-protocol results.

DISCUSSION

To our knowledge, this is the first prospective randomized study evaluating 40% glucose solution in the treatment of PoAL. Our findings demonstrate that 40% glucose administration was safe, with no major complications or excess reoperations, and more effective than ABPP. Additionally, it more often resolved air leak after a single administration and required fewer repeats, indicating a faster onset of action for glucose.

Current PoAL management includes both surgical and non-surgical strategies. Intraoperative techniques—such as staple line reinforcement, the fissureless technique, or pleural tenting—may reduce risk. However the postoperative PoAL remains common and often unpredictable, especially when alveolar-pleural fistulas go undetected intraoperatively.6 In some cases, PoAL requires reoperation, which is often technically more demanding than the initial surgery and may lead to additional complications and psychological distress.9–11

Among pleurodesis methods, commonly used agents include ABPP, medical talc, povidone-iodine, or doxycycline.12,13 Talc pleurodesis, widely used for spontaneous pneumothorax and recurrent effusions,14 has also shown efficacy in treating PoAL, with success rates exceeding 90%.15 However, concerns remain due to the lack of long-term human studies assessing potential systemic side effects. This is especially relevant for younger patients, as animal studies have shown talc deposits in various organs.16

ABPP remains widely used and is often favoured due to its simplicity and low cost.17 The exact mechanism is not fully understood. Intrapleural blood is believed to promote sealing of air leaks via clot formation (“patch effect”)18 and by inducing an inflammatory response that enhances fibrin deposition and pleural adhesion.1,19 Reported efficacy ranges from 70% to 90%,8,20–23 though most studies are small, retrospective, or heterogeneous. Only a few small prospective trials exist2,6,7 along with 1 large retrospective analysis using propensity-score matching.17 Several studies emphasize the benefit of early ABPP—within 5 days post-surgery—which may reduce both drainage time and hospital stay following anatomical lung resections.17,24

The use of hypertonic glucose for pleurodesis has been rarely reported, mostly in small Asian case series.25–27 In Western settings, it remains uncommon, with only isolated reports on early PoALs28 or intraoperative use during mesothelioma surgery.29 Reported success rates exceed 80%, with minimal complications. The only comparative study, by Hong et al,27 retrospectively analysed 100 patients (64 ABPP, 36 glucose). After propensity score matching, both methods showed similar effectiveness and safety. However, the study had limitations, including small sample size, unequal agent volumes (50 mL blood vs 200 mL glucose), and a heterogeneous surgical cohort. One case report from China (1992) described pneumonia after 50% glucose.30 Due to glucose’s irritant properties, intrapleural lignocaine is recommended prior to instillation27—consistent with our findings, where glucose-treated patients more often reported chest pain.

The authors acknowledge several limitations of the study. First, due to visible differences between agents and the need for blood collection, blinding was not feasible. To limit bias, only objective end-points were used. Second, simple 1:1 randomization without stratification led to numerical group imbalance. Although no baseline differences reached statistical significance, this asymmetry could affect perceived comparability, and the study might have benefited from block randomization. Third, timing of intervention varied slightly between patients but showed no group difference in Kaplan-Meier analysis. Future studies may benefit from competing risk or parametric survival approaches. Finally, although the sample size was modest, this is the largest randomized study on conservative PoAL management. A complementary ITT analysis, classifying protocol deviations and early discharges as failures, was also performed and showed results consistent with the per-protocol analysis, supporting robustness of the findings.

Based on the authors’ experience, several practical considerations should be highlighted. The administration of a 40% glucose solution is simple, sterile, and can be performed by either a physician or a trained nurse. In contrast, ABPP typically requires both a physician and a nurse, as well as the collection of 120 mL of peripheral blood—which may be challenging in some patients. ABPP is also contraindicated in cases of active blood-borne infection,8 while 40% glucose has no such restrictions and is acceptable for patients who are Jehovah’s Witnesses.10

Effective PoAL management improves outcomes and reduces hospital burden. Further research is needed to assess treatment efficacy and predictors of success. A safe, low-cost, widely available agent could improve care and support future guidelines.

CONCLUSION

Intrapleural administration of 40% glucose solution is a safe, simple, and clinically superior alternative to ABPP for managing PoAL. It shortens hospital stay and reduces the need for continued drainage or reoperation. Its practicality, safety profile, and accessibility make it a promising candidate for standard conservative treatment of PoAL.

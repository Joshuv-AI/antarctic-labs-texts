Real–time digital prescriptions unlock influenza dynamics: evidence from 21 million transactions
Authors: Rui Shen, Xueying Xu, Lei Yang, Zixing Wang, Rongrong Yang, Xiao Liu, Zilu Xu, Hao Jiang, Na Sun, Dayan Wang, Hongbo Liu, Luzhao Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTraditional influenza surveillance suffers from 1–2 week reporting delays that compromise outbreak response. We analyzed 21.08 million digital prescription transactions from China’s largest on-demand medication delivery platform across 31 provinces (2022–2024) and demonstrated that prescription data provide causally validated epidemic proxies. Digital prescriptions exhibited 2-week predictive lead time (convergent cross-mapping skills ΔρCCM = 0.339; p < 0.001; 28/31 provinces), substantially exceeding environmental predictors (ΔρCCM = 0.032–0.196) while matching online search indices (ΔρCCM = 0.349). Critically, prescriptions demonstrated bidirectional causal coupling with laboratory-confirmed influenza positivity (forward: 28/31; reverse: 22/31 provinces). This dynamical signature was absent in online search (reverse: 2/31) and environmental variables (reverse: 0–5/31), distinguishing validated disease signals from confounded correlates. Prescriptions also exhibited greater environmental sensitivity compared with laboratory surveillance (air pollutants: 14–26/31 versus 3–7/31 provinces). Leveraging this validated proxy, a spatiotemporal deep learning framework integrating GNN, Mamba, and LSTM achieved 96-day forecasting of daily prescription rates (mean absolute error (MAE) = 1.166; MAE < 3.0 in 29/31 provinces). Digital prescriptions thus enable both immediate epidemic detection (24 h data availability) and actionable long-range forecasting, providing an additional validated data stream for multi-source epidemic surveillance. Subject terms: Diseases, Health care, Mathematics and computing, Medical research

Introduction

The digital revolution has fundamentally transformed human health behaviors. Medical care, once confined to clinical settings, now extends into everyday digital interactions through online consultations, digital prescriptions, and instant medication delivery1. This transformation is most pronounced in China, where over 418 million users accessed online medical services by 20242, with the online pharmacy market projected to reach $26.32 billion in 20253. The convergence of heightened health awareness, ubiquitous mobile connectivity, and same-hour pharmaceutical delivery has generated a digital health ecosystem that captures population-level therapeutic decisions invisible to traditional surveillance.

The use of novel digital data streams for public health surveillance has generated substantial interest, with studies exploring the potential of internet search queries4, social media activity5, and electronic health records6. However, the trajectory of this field has been marked by both promise and pitfalls. The cautionary tale of Google Flu Trends, which overestimated influenza prevalence by 140% at its peak in 2013, illuminated how search-based surveillance can conflate information-seeking behavior with disease burden, generating signals influenced by media attention rather than actual transmission7. Pharmaceutical transaction data may offer distinct advantages through inherent transactional barriers—economic commitment and, for prescription drugs, clinical authorization8. Yet critical questions remain: do these filters create more robust epidemic signals? Does the relationship between prescription purchasing patterns and laboratory-confirmed disease activity represent causal coupling or spurious correlation driven by seasonal confounding? These knowledge gaps constrain the operational deployment of prescription-based surveillance.

Seasonal influenza emerges as the optimal proving ground for digital prescription surveillance. With approximately 1 billion infections annually yielding 3–5 million severe cases and 290,000–650,000 respiratory deaths globally9, influenza’s burden is both massive and measurable. Its predictable seasonal patterns enable robust validation against established surveillance benchmarks. Yet traditional systems fail precisely when speed matters most: reporting lags of 1–2 weeks4, combined with healthcare-seeking rates of merely 24–31% among symptomatic individuals10, create blind spots that compromise outbreak response. When rapid detection is critical, for emerging pathogens or severe seasonal epidemics, these delays fundamentally limit the window for pharmaceutical stockpiling, resource allocation, and targeted intervention.

We analyze 21.08 million digital prescription transactions from China’s largest online pharmacy platform across 31 provinces (2022–2024) to evaluate their utility for epidemic surveillance (Fig. 1). We assess temporal correspondence between digital prescriptions and laboratory-confirmed influenza positivity, and apply Convergent Cross-Mapping to distinguish causal coupling from spurious correlation, benchmarking against online search indices and environmental indicators. We further develop a spatiotemporal deep learning framework that achieves 96-day forecasting of daily prescription rates across most provinces. This work establishes digital prescription data as a complementary stream within multi-source surveillance frameworks, providing real-time epidemic intelligence without laboratory reporting delays.

Framework for digital prescription-based influenza surveillance.

Laboratory-based surveillance requires 1–2 week reporting delays with limited population coverage, whereas digital prescriptions provide real-time data availability (21.08 million transactions, 31 provinces, 2022–2024). Two-stage validation establishes prescriptions as epidemic proxies: temporal alignment confirms correlation with laboratory surveillance, and Convergent Cross-Mapping with surrogate controls confirms bidirectional causal coupling and 2-week early detection capability. Validated proxies are integrated with environmental factors, online search indices, and mobility data through spatiotemporal deep learning (GNN, Mamba, LSTM), enabling 96-day forecasting horizons.

Results

Spatiotemporal distribution of digital prescriptions and influenza activity

To assess whether digital prescriptions track epidemic dynamics, we analyzed 21.08 million transactions across 31 Chinese provinces from January 2022 to December 2024. Daily prescription rates varied 37-fold (0.22–8.22 per 100,000 population), with eastern provinces consistently exceeding western regions (Supplementary Fig. 1). Annual transaction volumes of 1.27 million (2022), 11.69 million (2023), and 8.12 million (2024) demonstrated 9-fold variation corresponding to major epidemic waves. Heatmap visualization revealed strong temporal synchronization between digital prescriptions and laboratory-confirmed influenza positivity across multiple epidemic cycles (Fig. 2). Provincial-level correlations were consistently significant (Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient ρ = 0.29–0.80; all p < 0.05; Supplementary Fig. 2). This population-level correspondence across China’s diverse geographic and socioeconomic contexts suggests digital prescriptions may serve as epidemic indicators.

Multi-domain analysis of influenza early indicators

To distinguish causal coupling from spurious correlation, we applied convergent cross-mapping (CCM) to test whether digital prescriptions exhibit genuine dynamical linkage with laboratory-confirmed influenza positivity, using online search indices and environmental factors as comparative benchmarks (Fig. 3A and Supplementary Table 1). At 2-week lead time, digital prescriptions demonstrated strong predictive coupling (ΔρCCM = 0.339; p < 0.001; 28/31 provinces significant), comparable to online search (ΔρCCM = 0.349; p < 0.001; 30/31 provinces significant) and substantially exceeding environmental predictors (ΔρCCM = 0.032–0.196). Air pollutants showed limited provincial coverage (NO2: 7/31, O3: 5/31, CO: 4/31, SO2: 4/31, PM2.5: 3/31, PM10: 3/31), as did meteorological factors (relative humidity: 6/31, wind speed: 6/31, precipitation: 3/31, temperature: 2/31). These patterns remained consistent across 0–2 week lead times, with digital prescriptions maintaining stable performance (ΔρCCM = 0.323–0.339; 28/31 provinces at all lags) while environmental factors showed persistently weak associations.

Province-specific standardized S-map coefficients quantifying state-dependent effects revealed contrasting spatial patterns at 2-week lead time (Fig. 4A–L and Supplementary Table 1). Digital prescriptions showed consistent positive effects across provinces (median = 0.030), with the strongest impacts in western and northern regions where pharmaceutical access is limited. Online search exhibited negative effects (median = −0.025) with substantial provincial variation, potentially reflecting media-driven searches. Environmental factors demonstrated geographic clustering: temperature showed negative effects concentrated in northern provinces (median = −0.047), while relative humidity showed positive effects nationwide (median = 0.025). Similar spatial patterns persisted at 0–2 week lead times, though effect magnitudes attenuated with increasing lag (digital prescriptions: 0.056 at 0 weeks to 0.030 at 2 weeks; online search: −0.007 at 0 weeks to −0.025 at 2 weeks).

Digital prescriptions uniquely exhibited bidirectional relationships with influenza positivity at 2 weeks (forward: 28/31 provinces; reverse: 22/31 provinces), indicating reciprocal information flow between medication purchasing and disease dynamics (Supplementary Tables 1 and 3). In contrast, online search showed predominantly unidirectional relationships (forward: 30/31; reverse: 2/31 provinces), while environmental factors remained strictly unidirectional with negligible reverse causality (0–5/31 provinces). This bidirectional causality, present in prescriptions but absent in online search and environmental variables, establishes digital prescriptions as validated epidemic proxies: signals that both respond to and anticipate disease dynamics, rather than merely correlating with them (Supplementary Fig. 3).

Determinants of digital prescriptions

Having established prescriptions as validated epidemic proxies, we next examined their determinants to characterize the environmental sensitivity underlying their early detection capability (Fig. 3B and Supplementary Table 2). At 14-day lead time, online search showed the strongest correlation (ΔρCCM = 0.318; p < 0.001; 30/31 provinces significant). Strikingly, environmental factors demonstrated substantially greater effects on digital prescriptions than on laboratory-confirmed influenza positivity: air pollutants affected 14–26/31 provinces versus 3–7/31 for influenza positivity (NO2: 26/31, CO: 21/31, SO2: 19/31, PM2.5: 17/31, PM10: 17/31, O3: 14/31), while meteorological factors affected 6–21/31 versus 2–6/31 provinces (relative humidity: 21/31, wind speed: 16/31, precipitation: 7/31, temperature: 6/31). These patterns persisted across the 0–14 day window, with online search maintaining strong associations throughout (ΔρCCM = 0.318–0.369; all p < 0.001).

S-map coefficients revealed spatial heterogeneity in determinant factors at 14-day lead time (Fig. 4M–W; Supplementary Table 2). Online search exhibited negative effects (median = −0.013), evolving from positive effects at day 0 (median = 0.005), a transition from contemporaneous correlation to anticipatory signal. Air pollutants showed positive effects concentrated in industrial provinces (NO2: median = 0.004; PM2.5: median = 0.003). Temperature (median = −0.003) and O3 (median = −0.004) showed negative effects concentrated in northern regions.

Bidirectional causality between online search and digital prescriptions was evident (forward: 30/31; reverse: 27/31 provinces), contrasting with unidirectional environmental influences (reverse: 0–3/31 provinces), a pattern consistent with known epidemiology, validating the CCM framework’s directional specificity (Supplementary Tables 2 and 4; Supplementary Fig. 4). These findings establish digital prescriptions as composite sensors integrating health-seeking behavior with environmental exposures undetectable by traditional laboratory-based surveillance.

Spatiotemporal forecasting performance

With prescriptions validated as epidemic proxies and upstream drivers identified, we developed a spatiotemporal deep learning framework to forecast daily prescription rates, leveraging 24-h data availability to circumvent laboratory reporting delays (Fig. 5A). This dual-stream architecture integrates local temporal dynamics with inter-provincial transmission patterns: the Local Temporal Module processes multi-source time series (digital prescriptions, online search, environmental variables) through province-specific embeddings and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, while the Spatial Transmission Module captures dependencies via graph neural networks (GNN) and Mamba state-space models.

The model was evaluated across six forecast horizons (7, 14, 28, 56, 72, and 96 days) (Supplementary Tables 5 and 6). At the 96-day horizon, the model achieved MAE of 1.166, outperforming LSTM (1.380; 15.51% reduction) and Transformer (1.261; 7.53% reduction) (Fig. 5B; Supplementary Fig. 5 and Supplementary Table 5). Root mean square error improved to 2.038 versus 2.452 (LSTM) and 2.154 (Transformer). MAE < 3.0 was achieved in 29/31 provinces (93.55%), with strong correlations between predicted and observed values (Pearson’s r = 0.804; 28/31 provinces with r > 0.7; Supplementary Fig. 5).

Performance exhibited marked geographic heterogeneity (MAE range: 0.193–8.055), with superior accuracy in several western and central provinces, including Xizang (0.193), Jiangxi (0.342), Qinghai (0.418), Shanxi (0.429), and Guizhou (0.491). Metropolitan areas showed higher errors, Beijing (3.521) and Tianjin (8.055), potentially reflecting complex healthcare infrastructures and diverse care-seeking behaviors. Despite elevated errors, the framework reduced MAE in Beijing by 39.67% (from 5.836 to 3.521) and in Tianjin by 22.22% (from 10.357 to 8.055) relative to LSTM. Collectively, these results establish that prescription-based models can deliver reliable 96-day forecasts for most provinces, extending epidemic surveillance well beyond the 7–14-day horizons of traditional systems.

Discussion

Analysis of 21.08 million digital prescription transactions establishes these data as validated early warning proxies for influenza surveillance. Through information-theoretic causal inference, we identified bidirectional coupling between prescriptions and laboratory-confirmed influenza positivity across most provinces, a dynamical signature absent in online search data and environmental variables. This validated equivalence confers three operational advantages, including immediate availability (24-h updates versus 1–2 week laboratory reporting delays), enhanced environmental responsiveness (daily-resolution data capturing environmental and behavioral influences exceeding traditional surveillance sensitivity), and extended forecasting capacity (reliable 96-day predictions across most provinces). These findings support the integration of digital prescription data as a complementary stream within multi-source epidemic surveillance frameworks.

Regarding real-time surveillance capabilities, digital prescriptions outperformed environmental variables in 2-week detection. Environmental factors provide only indirect signals, absolute humidity explains approximately 15% of transmission variance, temperature 13%11, PM2.5 increases risk by 1.5% per 10 μg/m312. In contrast, each digital prescription undergoes a multi-stage clinical validation process, including symptom presentation, physician assessment, prescription authorization, and transaction completion13. These sequential filters ensure that only verified medical decisions enter the surveillance stream. Although online search activity exhibited comparable predictive power, it reflects health information-seeking behavior rather than confirmed therapeutic actions.

Digital prescriptions also exhibited greater sensitivity than laboratory-based surveillance. Environmental variables influenced prescription patterns in 14–26/31 provinces for air pollutants, but affected laboratory-confirmed influenza positivity in only 3–7/31 provinces. This difference arises from two structural characteristics. First, daily-resolution data capture immediate behavioral responses that weekly reports cannot detect. Second, digital prescriptions capture a broader spectrum of symptomatic cases, including mild infections that do not reach laboratory testing. The combination of temporal granularity and expanded case ascertainment enables detection of early epidemic signals not captured by laboratory-based systems.

Beyond predictive performance, the causal structure itself provides mechanistic insights. CCM analysis identified bidirectional causal coupling (information-theoretic) between digital prescriptions and laboratory influenza surveillance. Forward causality indicates digital prescriptions can predict influenza activity, whereas reverse causality confirms that both systems capture the same epidemic dynamics. This bidirectional relationship reflects complementary rather than redundant surveillance mechanisms. Digital prescriptions capture mild-to-moderate symptomatic cases, accounting for approximately 70–80% of symptomatic individuals14 who do not seek laboratory testing, thereby providing broad population coverage, while laboratory-based surveillance focuses on hospitalized severe cases requiring clinical confirmation, offering diagnostic specificity. The observed 2-week temporal advantage thus represents a detection lag arising from differences in case ascertainment rather than true predictive foresight. We therefore forecast prescription rates, rather than laboratory cases directly, to leverage 24-h data availability while addressing regional heterogeneity through population normalization and province-specific model embeddings.

In contrast, online search behavior showed predominantly unidirectional relationships with a negative association with influenza activity, reflecting a mixture of health anxiety, media-driven searches, and genuine symptom inquiries15,16. Environmental factors remained strictly unidirectional, functioning as external forcing variables that influence transmission dynamics but are not affected by epidemic conditions. This hierarchical causality structure reveals distinct surveillance characteristics. Digital prescriptions both track and are tracked by epidemic dynamics, indicating true coupling. Online search partially reflects disease activity, representing a confounded signal. Environmental factors influence without being influenced, functioning as external drivers.

The distinction between prescription and search-based signals merits further examination. Search-based surveillance systems, exemplified by Google Flu Trends’ 140% overestimation of 2013 influenza prevalence, can conflate information-seeking behavior with disease burden when media attention amplifies query volumes independent of actual transmission7. Search queries capture an undifferentiated mix of genuine symptoms, media-induced worry, and general health interest, producing signals that reflect public attention rather than disease burden7,17. Pharmaceutical transaction data may offer advantages through sequential structural filters, economic commitment, clinical authorization, and therapeutic action, that attenuate noise while preserving signals associated with genuine treatment demand8.

This filtering advantage manifests empirically across multiple healthcare systems. Prescription-based surveillance, whether using offline pharmacy data in Japan18, United States19, and Canada20, or the online platform data analyzed here, achieves robust correlations with laboratory-confirmed cases without requiring continuous algorithmic recalibration. This convergence suggests that systems capturing validated therapeutic decisions provide more reliable epidemic tracking than those reflecting information-seeking behavior. Where Google Flu Trends required ongoing algorithmic intervention to maintain accuracy, prescription transactions track disease burden rather than disease awareness through structural filtering.

The spatiotemporal model achieved operational threshold (MAE < 3.0) in 29 of 31 provinces across 96-day horizons, with marked geographic variation (MAE range: 0.193–8.055). Several western and central provinces achieved low prediction errors, whereas major metropolitan areas exhibited higher errors. This heterogeneity likely reflects regional differences in epidemic dynamics and data characteristics. Metropolitan areas exhibit more complex transmission patterns due to higher population mobility21, and diverse healthcare-seeking behaviors22,23, whereas western provinces with lower urbanization may generate more stable surveillance signals. The 96-day forecasting capacity extends surveillance beyond real-time detection into medium-term planning, supporting pharmaceutical stockpiling, resource allocation, and vaccination campaign timing.

CCM distinguished causal coupling from seasonal correlation through state-space reconstruction and randomized surrogates. Cross-mapping skill convergence provided quantitative causal evidence impossible with traditional correlation24, revealing the bidirectional relationships that validate digital prescriptions as true epidemic indicators rather than spurious correlates. The spatiotemporal deep learning framework integrated GNN for inter-provincial transmission, Mamba for long-range dependencies, and LSTM for temporal dynamics. Our framework outperformed LSTM and Transformer, with particularly strong improvements in high-complexity metropolitan areas where multiple pharmaceutical channels and diverse healthcare-seeking behaviors create unique prediction challenges. Performance gains derived from selective state-space models addressing LSTM gradient limitations25 and local-global attention reducing complexity from O(n2) to O(nlogn)26. Beijing’s error decreased 39.67% and Tianjin’s 22.22% compared to LSTM, demonstrating that the proposed architecture achieves greater relative improvements in metropolitan settings despite their inherent forecasting complexity. These methodological advances enabled reliable 96-day forecasting across 93.55% of provinces, demonstrating that careful integration of causal inference and deep learning can transform commercial data into actionable surveillance signals.

Several limitations warrant consideration. Users of digital prescription platforms tend to be younger and urban, potentially underrepresenting elderly and rural populations who are less engaged with online healthcare services27. Moreover, this analysis focused exclusively on prescription antivirals, excluding over-the-counter (OTC) medications and mild self-managed infections, thereby capturing only influenza cases requiring formal medical consultation and prescription approval. The framework’s dependence on China’s integrated digital health ecosystem, characterized by dominant platforms28,29 with seamless prescription-delivery integration and widespread smartphone penetration, may limit international generalizability.

Three research priorities emerge from these limitations. First, multimodal integration combining digital prescription transactions with clinical records, genomic surveillance30, and wastewater monitoring31 could provide a more comprehensive view of influenza dynamics that captures both treated and untreated infections. Second, prospective validation is needed to evaluate the real-world impact of digital prescription-based early warnings, specifically, whether such alerts improve outbreak response and reduce disease burden. Third, mechanistic studies examining physician prescribing behaviors and patient purchasing dynamics during epidemic periods would further elucidate the behavioral foundations of the digital prescription signal.

Despite these constraints, the core principle of integrating commercial health transactions into epidemic surveillance remains broadly applicable. As the global digital pharmacy market is projected to reach USD 364–484 billion by 2030–203232,33, this framework offers an adaptable approach for implementing data-driven epidemic surveillance across diverse healthcare systems.

Digital prescription transactions, validated through bidirectional causal coupling with laboratory surveillance, represent a new class of epidemic proxies distinct from confounded correlates. By providing real-time epidemic intelligence without laboratory reporting delays, this approach offers a scalable complement to traditional surveillance systems. As digital healthcare infrastructure expands globally, such causal validation will be critical for harnessing commercial health data for epidemic surveillance.

Methods

Digital prescription transactions data

Province-level digital prescription records were obtained from Meituan Health, China’s largest on-demand medication delivery platform. The platform serves over 300 million users through partnerships with approximately 250,000 pharmacies, representing approximately 70% of China’s digital pharmacy market share28,29. Meituan Health was established as an independent business unit in 2020, following the December 2019 amendment to China’s Drug Administration Law permitting online prescription drug sales under specified conditions. Standardized data collection began in January 2022. The dataset comprised 21.08 million prescription-only antiviral medication transactions (oseltamivir and baloxavir marboxil) spanning January 1, 2022, to December 31, 2024, across 31 Chinese provinces. Transactions required physician authorization via electronic prescriptions per Chinese pharmaceutical regulations, with prescriptions issued following clinical assessment of influenza-like illness, laboratory confirmation, or post-exposure prophylaxis indications. Digital prescription rates were calculated as daily unique prescription purchasers per 100,000 residents, yielding 33,976 observations (1096 days × 31 provinces). All data were aggregated at the provincial level with no individual identifiers retained.

Laboratory influenza surveillance data

Weekly laboratory-confirmed influenza positivity rates were obtained from the Chinese National Influenza Center’s sentinel surveillance network (https://www.chinacdc.cn). The surveillance system reports the proportion of specimens testing positive for influenza among all respiratory specimens tested. For analysis requiring continuous variables, proportional data were bounded to [ε, 1 − ε] where ε = 10−6 to prevent undefined values at boundaries, then logit-transformed: logit(p) = log(p/(1 − p)).

Environmental and online search covariates

Meteorological variables (temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, precipitation) were obtained from the China Meteorological Data Service Center (http://data.cma.cn) at daily resolution for all 31 provinces. Air quality indicators (PM2.5, PM10, CO, NO2, O3, and SO2) were retrieved from the China National Environmental Monitoring Centre (https://www.cnemc.cn/). All environmental measurements underwent within-province z-score standardization to ensure comparability across regions with varying baseline levels.

Online search activity was quantified through Baidu Index (https://index.baidu.com), China’s dominant search engine, capturing about 70% of the domestic search market34. We monitored search volumes for eight influenza-related terms selected based on their epidemiological relevance and search frequency: influenza, influenza A, influenza B, flu B, fever, sore throat, oseltamivir, and Tamiflu35. Daily search volumes were summed across all eight terms for each province, then normalized using Baidu’s proprietary algorithms to adjust for regional differences in internet penetration.

Inter-provincial human mobility was quantified using the Baidu Migration platform (https://qianxi.baidu.com), which tracks population movements through location-based services from over 1.1 billion mobile devices. Daily migration rates were calculated following established methodology: Migration rate = (M × OD × Coefficient/P) × 105, where M represents migration scale index, OD denotes directional flow proportion between provinces, and P indicates origin provincial population36.

Convergent cross-mapping analysis

We employed Convergent Cross-Mapping (CCM) to quantify directional causal relationships among environmental factors, online search activity, digital prescriptions, and laboratory-confirmed influenza positivity. Convergent Cross-Mapping (CCM) is grounded in Takens’ embedding theorem: if variable X causally influences Y, information about X becomes encoded in Y’s dynamics, allowing X to be recovered from Y’s reconstructed state space (shadow manifold)24. This framework detects information-theoretic causality—the transfer of predictive information between variables—rather than interventional or mechanistic causality. Crucially, reconstruction accuracy improves as library size increases, a property termed “convergence” that distinguishes true causal coupling from spurious correlations driven by shared forcing or confounding. Cross-mapping convergence was quantified as ΔρCCM = ρmax_library − ρmin_library, where ρ represents the Pearson correlation between observed target values and cross-mapped estimates. Positive ΔρCCM indicates convergence, providing evidence that predictor states contain recoverable information about target dynamics; larger values reflect stronger dynamical coupling.

For multi-domain analysis of influenza early indicators (weekly resolution), shadow manifolds were reconstructed from weekly influenza surveillance data using lagged-coordinate embeddings, M(t) = [X(t), X(t − τ), …, X(t − (E − 1)τ)], with a unit time delay (τ = 1 week). Optimal embedding dimensions E were determined independently for each province-variable combination via simplex projection, maximizing one-step-ahead forecast skill ρ through leave-one-out cross-validation. The search range E ∈ [2, 6] was applied to 156 weekly observations per province, with upper bounds constrained to prevent overfitting in finite samples. Twelve predictor variables—digital prescriptions, online search indices, temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, precipitation, PM2.5, PM10, CO, NO2, O3, and SO2—were tested against clinical influenza activity across prediction horizons tp ∈ (0, −1, −2) weeks, spanning the reporting latency characteristic of laboratory-based surveillance systems. Library sizes for cross-mapping were conservatively bounded as L ∈ [E + 2, 156 − |tp| −E − 5]. Convergence was assessed using 50 random library subsamples. To distinguish genuine causal coupling from shared seasonality, 500 Fourier phase-randomized surrogates were generated. Surrogates preserved seasonal periodicity (T = 52.18 weeks) and autocorrelation structure while disrupting true causal dependencies. Trend decomposition used smoothing splines (spar ∈ [0.1, 1.5]), with boundary continuity enforced via tripled concatenation [Xt−52, Xt, Xt+52].

For determinants of digital prescriptions (daily resolution), daily digital prescription data were analyzed under the same embedding framework, with expanded dimensions E ∈ [2, 8] to capture higher-dimensional dynamics across 1,096 daily observations per province. Eleven predictor variables, online search indices, temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, precipitation, PM2.5, PM10, CO, NO2, O3, and SO2, were evaluated at prediction horizons tp ∈ (0, −1, −3, −7, −14) days, encompassing immediate behavioral responses (0–1 day) through sustained environmental effects (14 days). Library sizes were defined as L ∈ [E + 2, 1096 − |tp| −E − 5]. ΔρCCM was computed using 30 random library subsamples, with 200 seasonal surrogates (T = 365.25 days) for null distribution construction.

CCM analyses were conducted in both forward and reverse directions for all variable pairs to establish causal directionality. This bidirectional approach validates: (1) causal asymmetry—stronger CCM in the hypothesized direction indicates predominant information flow; (2) temporal precedence—early indicators should predict targets at negative lags; and (3) potential feedback loops—significant bidirectional CCM suggests mutual coupling. Independent optimization of embedding dimensions was performed for each direction (E ∈ [2, 6] weekly; E ∈ [2, 8] daily), respecting the distinct dynamical properties of each variable.

Continuity corrections were applied: p-values of exactly zero were set to 5 × 10−4; instances with ΔρCCM ≤ 0 were assigned p = 1. Spatiotemporal deep learning framework for influenza prediction

We developed a dual-stream spatiotemporal fusion deep learning architecture that integrates local temporal dynamics with spatial transmission patterns through iterative cross-module updating. The architecture consists of two main components: (1) a Local Temporal Module for province-level time series analysis, processing digital prescription rates, environmental variables (meteorological factors: temperature, relative humidity, wind speed, precipitation; air quality indicators: PM2.5, PM10, CO, NO2, O3, and SO2), and online search indices; and (2) a Spatial Transmission Module capturing inter-provincial dependencies through human mobility networks. These two modules interact through iterative information exchange, where temporal representations inform spatial aggregation and spatial context refines temporal predictions. This dual-module design comprehensively captures the spatiotemporal dynamics of epidemic spread and significantly improves overall predictive accuracy.

Multi-source time-series data establishes a local model. We start by tokenizing the time series through a linear layer. The study period (1 January 2022 to 31 December 2024; 1096 days × 31 provinces) was partitioned chronologically to prevent information leakage. After reserving input (48 days) and evaluation (96 days) windows, 70% of the remaining 952 days and input window were allocated to training, yielding 714 training days (1 January 2022 to 15 December 2023; 22,134 province-days). The evaluation period (16 December 2023 to 20 March 2024) encompassed the 2023–2024 winter epidemic peak.

The input sequence length was set to 48 days, capturing approximately three population-level serial intervals of influenza transmission and providing sufficient temporal context for seasonality learning. The prediction horizon of 96 days spans a complete influenza season in China (mid-November through late February), matching operational planning cycles for vaccination, pharmaceutical stockpiling, and healthcare capacity allocation. The model generates 96-day forecasts through recursive propagation. Given input sequence [t−47,t], the model predicts day t+1; this prediction replaces the oldest observation to forecast day t+2, continuing through day t+96. Ground-truth values from the forecast horizon are not accessed during prediction. The sliding window advances daily as observations become available. Accuracy was assessed using mean absolute error and root mean square error; temporal agreement was evaluated using Pearson’s r. Baseline architectures (LSTM, Transformer) received identical inputs and training protocols.

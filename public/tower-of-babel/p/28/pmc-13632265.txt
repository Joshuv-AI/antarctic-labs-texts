Nasopharyngeal classical Hodgkin lymphoma: a case report
Authors: Suhaib Eid, Somaya Al Kiswani, Hossam Salameh, Nidal Almasri, Nooreddin Saifi, Omar Sawafta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHodgkin lymphoma is a malignant lymphoid neoplasm that most commonly originates in lymph nodes, particularly within the cervical, mediastinal, and axillary regions. Extranodal involvement is relatively uncommon, and primary nasopharyngeal Hodgkin lymphoma defines a rare clinical phenomenon. Diagnosis may be delayed because its clinical presentation resembles more common nasopharyngeal conditions.Case presentationA 51-year-old Palestinian woman presented with progressive nasal obstruction and snoring. Endoscopic examination revealed a mass in the nasopharynx. Histopathological analysis confirmed lymphocyte-rich classical Hodgkin lymphoma, staged as IIA. The patient was treated with four cycles of ABVD chemotherapy followed by involved-site radiotherapy using CT simulation with a total dose of 30.6 Gy delivered in 17 fractions, resulting in complete resolution of the lesion. At 12 months of follow-up, the patient remained disease-free with full clinical recovery.ConclusionThis case highlights the diagnostic challenges posed by rare extranodal presentations of Hodgkin lymphoma and illustrates the value of maintaining a broad differential diagnosis and performing timely histopathological evaluation in patients with persistent nasal symptoms.

Introduction

Hodgkin lymphoma (HL) is a relatively uncommon malignancy, accounting for approximately 10% of all lymphomas and about 0.4% of all cancer cases in the U.S [1]. It is characterized by the presence of neoplastic Reed–Sternberg cells within a reactive inflammatory background.

Classical HL (cHL) comprises the vast majority of cases and is further subtyped into nodular sclerosis, mixed cellularity, lymphocyte-rich, and lymphocyte-depleted variants, with nodular sclerosis being the most common [2]. The lymphocyte-rich subtype is less frequently encountered and often presents with early-stage disease and favorable outcomes [3].

Nasopharyngeal involvement in HL is rare, and when present, may mimic other more common entities such as nasopharyngeal carcinoma, extranodal non-Hodgkin lymphoma, or infectious/inflammatory processes [4]. The clinical presentation can include nasal obstruction, post-nasal discharge, or otologic symptoms, which may be nonspecific and delay diagnosis [5].

Accurate diagnosis requires a combination of imaging, histopathologic examination, and immunohistochemical analysis. Imaging modalities such as CT and (positron emission tomography–computed tomography) PET–CT are essential for initial staging, treatment planning, and response assessment. Histologically, lymphocyte-rich cHL is characterized by a predominance of small lymphocytes with scattered classical Reed–Sternberg cells, often positive for CD30 and CD15, and negative for CD45 [6].

Management involves combined modality therapy in early-stage disease, with chemotherapy, most commonly the ABVD (doxorubicin, bleomycin, vinblastine, dacarbazine) regimen. For patients with limited or intermediate-stage disease, combined modality treatment consisting of a brief chemotherapy (ChT) followed by radiation therapy is still the standard approach, also in case of PET-guided approach [7].

This report presents a rare case of lymphocyte-rich classical Hodgkin lymphoma initially presenting with nasopharyngeal symptoms, following completion of therapy and total remission.

Case presentation

A 51-year-old Palestinian woman, known to have hypothyroidism and osteoporosis, with no baseline dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DEXA) scan available, initially presented in January 2022 with nasal congestion and snoring. These symptoms, initially mild, progressively worsened over the following year, eventually prompting further investigation. In February 2023, an endoscopic examination in the clinic showed a large, smooth, pinkish-red mass occupying most of the nasopharynx (Fig. 1). An magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the neck was performed (Fig. 2), revealing a lobulated mass centered at the nasopharyngeal roof and posterior wall, completely obscuring the nasopharyngeal air column. The mass was isointense on T1-weighted images, hyperintense on T2, and showed enhancement post-contrast, with restricted diffusion. A nasopharyngeal biopsy was performed on 15 March 2023, and histopathology revealed classical Hodgkin lymphoma of the lymphocyte-rich subtype (Fig. 3). Histological sections showed scattered large, atypical Hodgkin and Reed–Sternberg (HRS) cells embedded within a background of small mature lymphocytes. Immunohistochemistry showed that the neoplastic cells were positive for CD30, CD20 (dim), CD15, PAX5, and Epstein–Barr virus (EBV), and negative for CD45. The morphology and immunoprofile were consistent with lymphocyte-rich classical Hodgkin lymphoma. Despite dim CD20 expression, the diagnosis of lymphocyte-rich classical Hodgkin lymphoma was supported by the presence of classical Reed–Sternberg cells, CD30 and CD15 positivity, weak PAX5 expression, and absence of CD45 expression. A staging PET/CT performed in April 2023 (Fig. 4) showed an fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG)-avid adenoidal mass in the nasopharynx with bilateral retropharyngeal extension (3.1 × 1.7 cm; SUVmax 4.45), along with mildly FDG-avid bilateral cervical lymph nodes, the largest being a right level II node (1.6 cm; SUVmax 3.1). Additionally, mild diffuse thyroid uptake was observed, considered likely physiological. A contrast-enhanced CT scan of the neck (Fig. 5) confirmed the presence of the nasopharyngeal mass and cervical lymphadenopathy, with no evidence of distant disease. This established a diagnosis of stage IIA classical Hodgkin lymphoma. A bone marrow biopsy was not performed, as there were no clinical or imaging findings suggestive of bone marrow involvement on initial staging, including PET/CT. Given her early-stage disease, she was started on combination chemotherapy with the ABVD regimen (doxorubicin, bleomycin, vinblastine, and dacarbazine). She completed four cycles of ABVD without significant toxicity. Interim PET/CT performed post-chemotherapy showed a complete metabolic response with complete resolution of the previously seen hypermetabolic nasopharyngeal mass and cervical lymphadenopathy.

Following chemotherapy, the patient received involved-site radiotherapy (ISRT) to the nasopharyngeal and upper cervical regions, planned using CT simulation. A total dose of 30.6 Gy was delivered in 17 fractions. Image fusion was not used. A follow-up PET/CT done post-radiotherapy demonstrated sustained complete remission, with no evidence of residual or recurrent disease.

Approximately 3 months after completing radiotherapy, the patient developed acute abdominal pain and signs of bowel obstruction. A contrast-enhanced CT scan of the abdomen and pelvis revealed intestinal obstruction with inflammatory enteritis (terminal ileitis and duodenitis), without radiological features of radiation-induced bowel injury or evidence of recurrence or new malignancy. The etiology of the enteritis remains unclear but may relate to prior chemotherapy or an unrelated infectious process. No specific pathogen was identified. Conservative management led to a gradual improvement of symptoms.

In October 2023, she presented with bilateral hip pain. Pelvic X-rays and a subsequent MRI of the pelvis revealed insufficiency fractures involving both sacral alae and pubic rami, attributed to underlying osteoporosis and corticosteroid use during chemotherapy. Bone densitometry confirmed low bone mineral density, and the patient was managed with antiresorptive therapy and physiotherapy. Pre-treatment bone density was not assessed; therefore, the contribution of corticosteroid exposure versus pre-existing osteoporosis cannot be quantified. Her condition gradually improved.

She continues to be followed up regularly with interval imaging and clinical assessment, including CT and PET/CT scans during and after treatment. Follow-up was conducted through periodic clinical evaluations and scheduled imaging at defined intervals throughout the treatment and post-treatment period. Notably, her most recent PET/CT and CT scan (Fig. 6), performed at 12 months from the time of diagnosis, confirmed ongoing complete metabolic remission. Discussion

Nasopharyngeal involvement by classical Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL) is exceedingly rare, accounting for approximately 0.32% of all Hodgkin lymphoma cases. This presentation is reported almost exclusively in adults, further highlighting the unique nature of the clinical profile. Previously reported cases describe patients presenting with nonspecific nasal symptoms such as nasal obstruction with prolonged duration before diagnosis, and are commonly identified at early stages (I–II) with favorable outcomes. Our case is consistent with previously reported cases, which are typically diagnosed at early stages and show good response to combined modality therapy [4]. A larger literature review of extranodal head-and-neck Hodgkin lymphoma identified 357 reported cases, including 109 cases involving the nasopharynx. The median age of the overall cohort was 45 years (range, 5–89 years). Within the nasopharyngeal subgroup, 63 of 109 cases (58%) were stage I–II, whereas 46 cases (42%) were stage III–IV [8]. However, primary nasopharyngeal HL remains much rarer, with only about 20 cases described in a previous review [4]. In contrast, most cases of Hodgkin lymphoma arise in nodal regions, particularly the cervical, mediastinal, and axillary lymph nodes [2]. Nasopharyngeal presentation poses a significant diagnostic challenge due to its nonspecific symptoms and overlap with more prevalent pathologies such as nasopharyngeal carcinoma, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and chronic inflammatory or infectious conditions [9]. In this case, these differential diagnoses were excluded based on histopathological findings and immunophenotypic profile, particularly the presence of Reed–Sternberg cells and the characteristic CD30+, CD15+, CD45− staining pattern. The patient’s initial symptoms, progressive nasal obstruction and snoring, were insidious and nonspecific, resulting in delayed diagnosis.

The histopathologic subtype, lymphocyte-rich classical Hodgkin lymphoma (LR-cHL), is among the least common variants and is typically associated with early-stage presentation and favorable prognosis [10]. Its morphological similarity to nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma (NLPHL) or reactive lymphoid hyperplasia requires careful histological and immunohistochemical evaluation. In this case, immunoprofiling confirmed the diagnosis with HRS cells positive for CD30, CD15, PAX5, and EBV, and negative for CD45, aligning with known patterns in LR-cHL [11]. EBV positivity is frequently observed in classical Hodgkin lymphoma, although its prognostic significance remains variable.

Imaging studies, particularly PET/CT, played a crucial role in both staging and response assessment. The use of ABVD chemotherapy followed by involved-site radiotherapy is consistent with current treatment guidelines for early-stage, non-bulky cHL and has been associated with excellent outcomes [11]. The patient’s complete metabolic response post-therapy, sustained over a year, highlights the favorable prognosis associated with early-stage LR-cHL even when presenting in rare anatomical sites.

Interestingly, the post-treatment complications in this case, intestinal obstruction and insufficiency fractures, underscore the importance of comprehensive long-term follow-up. Notably, the radiotherapy field involved the nasopharyngeal and upper cervical regions, whereas the insufficiency fractures involved the sacral alae and pubic rami. These complications were not related to disease recurrence but are likely attributable to treatment-related factors, including corticosteroid exposure and underlying comorbidities such as osteoporosis. Overall, they are likely multifactorial, reflecting the combined effects of chemotherapy, corticosteroid use, and the patient’s baseline comorbidities.

Conclusion

This case underscores the importance of maintaining a broad differential diagnosis in patients presenting with nasal obstruction and nasopharyngeal masses. It reinforces the need for histopathological confirmation in atypical presentations and demonstrates that with timely diagnosis and standard-of-care treatment, even rare presentations of Hodgkin lymphoma can achieve excellent clinical outcomes.

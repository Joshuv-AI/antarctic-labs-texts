Final Analysis of Neoadjuvant Sintilimab Plus Chemotherapy in IB–IIIA Non‐Small‐Cell Lung Cancer: Phase 2 neoSCORE Trial
Authors: Miner Shao, Jie Yao, Lufeng Zhao, Ling Zhu, Baizhou Li, Lili Li, Bangyi Zhou, Yixin Zhang, Huiying Liu, Xiaoke Chen, Zuqun Wu, Zexin Chen, Junqiang Fan, Fuming Qiu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe optimal number of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy cycles for resectable non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) remains uncertain. The randomized phase 2 neoSCORE trial (NCT04459611) conducted a comprehensive final analysis, comparing the outcomes of two versus three cycles of neoadjuvant sintilimab combined with platinum‐doublet chemotherapy in 60 stage IB–IIIA NSCLC patients, among whom 55 underwent surgery. After a median follow‐up period of 56.9 months, no statistically significant differences in disease‐free survival (DFS) or overall survival (OS) were observed between the two‐cycle and three‐cycle groups. Specifically, the 4‐year DFS rates stood at 53.8% and 55.2% while the 4‐year OS rates were 76.9% and 75.9%, respectively. Major pathological response (MPR) was strongly associated with improved DFS (HR = 0.26, 95% CI: 0.08–0.85, p = 0.026) and OS (HR = 0.15, 95% CI: 0.03–0.81, p = 0.028) on multivariable Cox analysis. Exploratory analysis revealed that maintenance immunotherapy was associated with longer DFS among patients who achieved MPR, whereas no clear DFS or OS advantage was observed among patients without MPR. In summary, no superiority of three cycles over two cycles was demonstrated in this study. MPR stands out as an independent prognostic indicator, and maintenance immunotherapy seems to be associated with long‐term prognosis in patients who achieved MPR, necessitating further prospective validation. Introduction

Lung cancer tops the list of cancer killers globally, with non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) making up 80%–85% of cases [1]. While surgery offers the best cure for resectable NSCLC [2, 3], the threat of recurrence and metastasis persists, with 5‐year survival rates varying from 45% for stage IB to a mere 24% for stage IIIA [4]. This stark reality has spurred extensive research into perioperative treatments to cut recurrence risks and enhance long‐term survival.

The emergence of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting the programmed cell death protein 1 (PD‐1) and its ligand (PD‐L1) has revolutionized NSCLC treatment [5, 6]. For metastatic cases, numerous phase three trials have cemented PD‐1/PD‐L1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy as the first‐line standard, showing substantial and enduring survival gains over chemotherapy alone [7, 8]. Leveraging these achievements, immunotherapy has swiftly advanced into curative treatment for resectable NSCLC.

The pivotal CheckMate 816 phase 3 trial offered the top‐tier evidence for neoadjuvant therapy in resectable stage IB–IIIA NSCLC, showing that three cycles of nivolumab plus chemotherapy notably improved event‐free survival (EFS) (hazard ratio [HR] = 0.63) and pathological complete response (pCR) rates (24.0% vs. 2.2%) compared to chemotherapy alone [9]. Recent mature follow‐up confirmed sustained overall survival (OS) benefits (HR = 0.71), establishing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy as a standard treatment [10]. Similarly, the KEYNOTE‐671 trial demonstrated significant enhancements in both EFS (HR = 0.59) and OS (HR = 0.72) with perioperative pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy followed by adjuvant treatment [11]. These findings are echoed in other large trials, such as CheckMate 77T [12], AEGEAN [13], Neotorch [14] and RATIONALE‐315 [15], all highlighting the effectiveness of chemoimmunotherapy in improving pCR rates, EFS, and OS.

Despite these advances, important questions regarding treatment optimization remain unresolved. Among the most clinically relevant is the optimal number of neoadjuvant treatment cycles. Clinical trials and practice vary, using two to four cycles without direct prospective randomized comparisons. A shorter course could lower toxicity, prevent surgery delays, and reduce preoperative disease progression risk. Conversely, extended treatment could potentially enhance tumor downstaging, improve pathological response rates, and provide more robust systemic control of micrometastatic disease [16].

Sintilimab, a fully human anti‐PD‐1 antibody, has demonstrated robust efficacy and favorable safety in advanced NSCLC and is increasingly being investigated in earlier‐stage disease [17, 18]. NeoSCORE (ClinicalTrials.gov ID NCT04459611) was a prospective, open‐label, single‐center, randomized phase 2 study, evaluating two cycles versus three cycles of neoadjuvant sintilimab combined with platinum‐doublet chemotherapy in patients with resectable stage IB–IIIA NSCLC. This is the first randomized trial to assess the efficacy and safety of different cycles of anti‐PD‐1 therapy plus chemotherapy for resectable NSCLC in the neoadjuvant setting, and we demonstrated that the three‐cycle arm presented a numerically higher major pathological response (MPR) rate than the two‐cycle arm (41.4% vs. 26.9%, p = 0.260) [19].

Here, we present the neoSCORE trial's final analysis of disease‐free survival (DFS) and OS after a minimum 4‐year follow‐up, shedding more light on the best neoadjuvant treatment duration for resectable NSCLC patients on chemoimmunotherapy.

Material and Methods

Patients

NeoSCORE was a prospective, open‐label, single‐center, randomized phase 2 study that evaluated different cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy for resectable NSCLC. We enrolled patients (aged 18–75 years) with cytologically or histologically proven NSCLC. Other inclusion criteria included previously untreated stage cIB‐IIIA NSCLC (American Joint Committee on Cancer [AJCC, 8th edition] [20, 21]), at least one measurable lesion (Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors [RECIST, version 1.1] [22]), adequate organ function, life expectancy more than 6 months and Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 0 or 1 [23]. Key exclusion criteria included previous checkpoint inhibitor therapy, a history of malignancy within the past 5 years, serious systemic diseases, and active autoimmune disease requiring systemic treatment.

The trial received approval from the local independent ethics committee and adhered to Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki. An independent data safety monitoring board (DSMB) monitored the study and reviewed the data of the prespecified interim analyses. All patients provided written informed consent before enrollment.

Study Design and Treatments

In this trial, eligible patients were randomized to the two‐ or three‐cycle arm in a 1:1 ratio. A stratified‐block randomization design was used, with a block size of six, and the stratification factor was PD‐L1 expression (≥ 1% vs. < 1%). The patients received neoadjuvant intravenous sintilimab (200 mg; Innovent Biologics, Suzhou, China) plus chemotherapy (carboplatin AUC 5 plus nab‐paclitaxel 260 mg/m2 for squamous NSCLC or pemetrexed 500 mg/m2 for non‐squamous NSCLC) every 3 weeks. Surgery was performed within the fourth week (±7 days) from the last dose of neoadjuvant treatment. Post‐surgery, patients received one or two additional cycles of the same regimen to complete four doses of perioperative therapy. Radiotherapy was administered at the discretion of the investigators based on the patient's clinical situation and pathological staging. Maintenance treatment with sintilimab (every 3 weeks) for up to 1 year was allowed based on the patient's decision.

Endpoints and Assessments

The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients achieving an MPR at the time of surgery. Secondary endpoints included the pCR rate; objective response rate (ORR), defined as the proportion of patients with complete or partial response; 2‐year DFS rate, the proportion of patients without disease recurrence, metastasis, or death at 2 years; 2‐year OS rate, the proportion of patients alive at 2 years; and safety. Exploratory endpoints included novel immune biomarkers and the impact of sintilimab maintenance on the 2‐year DFS and OS.

Patients underwent computed tomography scans at the baseline, within 2 weeks before surgery, 1 month after surgery, and then every 3–6 months until 2 years after surgery. Comprehensive imaging examinations were performed at the baseline to exclude patients with distant metastases. The radiological response was assessed by the radiologists using RECIST. The radiologists were not aware of the grouping information.

The pathological stage was evaluated from the resected specimens according to the AJCC. The local pathologists without knowledge of the grouping information reviewed the resected specimens to assess the percentage of residual tumors. The samples were stained with hematoxylin–eosin using routine procedures. MPR was defined as 10% or less residual viable tumor in the surgically resected specimen. pCR was defined as no viable tumor in the resected specimen, including all the sampled regional lymph nodes [24, 25, 26].

In this study, DFS was defined among patients who underwent surgical resection as the time from surgery to disease recurrence, distant metastasis, or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. This differs from EFS, which usually includes preoperative progression, inability to undergo surgery, recurrence, or death.

Adverse events (AEs) were monitored during the study period. The National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (version 5.0) was used to assess the severity of each AE. During the first 30 days after the operation, postoperative complications and mortality were monitored and stratified using the Clavien‐Dindo classification [27].

PD‐L1 Immunohistochemistry

Tumor tissue samples were obtained to assess PD‐L1 expression before randomization. PD‐L1 immunohistochemistry was performed with a PD‐L1 immunohistochemistry E1L3N assay kit (Amoy Diagnostics, Xiamen, China). TPS was used to determine PD‐L1 expression. TPS was defined as the percentage of tumor cells with partial or complete membrane staining.

Next‐Generation Sequencing

A customized 16‐gene next‐generation sequencing panel covering hotspot regions of key lung cancer driver genes (including EGFR, KRAS, BRAF, PIK3CA, MET, ERBB2, PTEN, ALK, RET, ROS1, NTRK1/2/3, POLE, TP53, and CD274) was used for library preparation. Sequencing was performed on the Illumina NextSeq 550 platform with paired‐end 150 bp reads at a mean depth of 1000×. Samples with insufficient DNA yield or mean sequencing depth < 200× were excluded from analysis.

Statistical Analysis

According to a three‐stage group sequential randomized trial design, a sample size of N = 102 (51 per arm) was required to detect an improvement in the MPR rate from 50% in the two‐cycle arm to 70% in the three‐cycle arm, considering a 10% attrition rate. The target power was 80%, with a two‐sided type I error of 0.05. The PASS (version 11.0) was used to calculate the sample size.

Two interim analyses were planned when 33.3% and 66.7% of patients were recruited. The O'Brien‐Fleming group sequential design was used with p‐values of 0.000207, 0.01189, and 0.037903 in the two planned interim and final analyses, respectively. To account for futility, an unplanned interim analysis was performed after 60 patients were enrolled. The interim results were reviewed by the DSMB in December 2021. The DSMB recommended termination of the trial because the MPR rates were lower than predicted. It was unlikely that the three‐cycle treatment led to a significant increase in MPR compared with the two‐cycle treatment using the planned sample size.

The efficacy population included all randomized patients who underwent surgery. The safety population included all patients administrated one or more doses of the study treatment. The Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare continuous variables, and the χ2 test or Fisher's exact test was used for categorical variables. The Clopper‐Pearson method was used to estimate the associated 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Survival curves were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and median survival outcomes with corresponding two‐sided 95% CIs were calculated. Hazard ratios and confidence intervals were estimated using stratified Cox proportional hazards models. p < 0.05 indicated statistical significance. Statistical analyses were conducted with SPSS (version 24.0) and GraphPad Prism (version 9.0).

Results

Patients and Treatments

From June 2020 through September 2021, 64 patients were screened, of which 60 were enrolled and were randomized to receive either two (n = 29) or three (n = 31; Figure S1) cycles of neoadjuvant sintilimab plus chemotherapy. The characteristics were similar between both treatment groups at baseline (Table S1). In the total population, the median age at enrollment was 64.5 years, most of the patients were men (48/60, 80.0%) and had smoking histories (37/60, 61.7%). Thirty‐two patients (53.3%) had stage IIIA disease, and 36 (60.0%) had squamous cell histology. All patients were assessed for PD‐L1 expression (≥ 1%, 51.7%).

Surgical Outcomes

Among the 60 randomized patients, 89.7% (26/29) in the two‐cycle and 93.5% (29/31) in the three‐cycle group underwent surgical resection. The surgery‐related data are presented in Table 1. Among the 55 patients who underwent resection, minimally invasive surgery was attempted in 54 (98.2%) patients, including video‐assisted thoracic surgery (VATS) in 12 (22.2%) and robotic‐assisted thoracic surgery (RATS) in 42 (77.8%). Open thoracotomy was planned in 1 (1.8%) patient. No conversion from minimally invasive surgery to thoracotomy occurred in either treatment group. The distribution of surgical approaches was not significantly different between the two treatment groups. All patients in both groups achieved R0 resection. Median operation time was 120 min in the two‐cycle group and 110 min in the three‐cycle group, and median intraoperative blood loss was 20 and 25 mL, respectively.

Response

As previously reported [19], 55 of the 60 enrolled patients successfully underwent surgery and were evaluated for pathological response. MPR was achieved in 19 patients (34.5%), with MPR rates of 26.9% (7/26) in the two‐cycle group and 41.4% (12/29) in the three‐cycle group (p = 0.260). PCR rates were similar between the two groups, at 19.2% (5/26) and 24.1% (7/29), respectively (p = 0.660). The ORR were 50.0% (13/26) and 55.2% (16/29), respectively (p = 0.701) (Figure S2).

Survival Outcomes

At the data cutoff date for the final analysis (February 12, 2026), the median follow‐up was 56.9 months (95% CI: 55.7–58.1). Disease progression was reported in 34.6% (9/26) of patients in the two‐cycle group and 37.9% (11/29) in the three‐cycle group. Salvage therapies administered following disease progression are summarized in Table 2. Seven patients (26.9%) in the two‐cycle group died, including four (15.4%) due to disease recurrence and three (11.5%) from causes unrelated to lung tumor progression (one [3.8%] from bronchial hemorrhage, one [3.8%] from pneumonia, and one [3.8%] from respiratory failure). In the three‐cycle group, seven patients (24.1%) died, comprising five (17.2%) due to disease recurrence and two (6.9%) from non‐cancer‐related causes (one [3.4%] from pneumonia and one [3.4%] from cardiac arrest). The non‐cancer‐related deaths were reviewed and were not considered late‐onset immune‐related adverse events.

Association Between Survival and Pathological Response

Patients who achieved MPR demonstrated improved DFS compared to those who did not achieve MPR (HR = 0.297 [95% CI: 0.133–0.664], p = 0.018). Consistently, patients who achieved MPR also demonstrated a trend toward improved OS, with a 70.5% reduction in the risk of death compared to non‐MPR patients (HR = 0.295 [95% CI: 0.099–0.878], p = 0.089) (Figure 2). Similarly, patients achieving pCR showed a trend toward improved DFS (HR = 0.430 [95% CI: 0.172–1.077], p = 0.158) and a marked reduction in the risk of death compared to non‐pCR patients (HR = 0.257 [95% CI: 0.074–0.892], p = 0.158) (Figure 2).

Among MPR patients, two deaths (10.5%) occurred, none of which were specifically attributed to lung cancer, whereas among non‐MPR patients, 12 deaths (33.3%) were reported, including nine (25.0%) due to lung cancer. Disease recurrence was reported in two MPR patients (10.5%) compared to 18 non‐MPR patients (50.0%).

Multivariable Cox regression analysis revealed that, after adjusting for treatment cycle, clinical stage, PD‐L1 expression level, age, histology, EGFR/ALK mutation status, and smoking status, only MPR remained significantly and independently associated with improved DFS. Specifically, patients who achieved MPR had a 74% reduction in the risk of disease progression or death compared to non‐MPR patients (HR = 0.26 [95% CI: 0.08–0.85], p = 0.026). In contrast, treatment cycle (3‐cycle vs. 2‐cycle: HR = 1.58 [95% CI: 0.66–3.81], p = 0.307) did not show an independent association with DFS (Figure 3A). Similarly, Cox regression analysis for OS demonstrated that only MPR remained significantly and independently associated with improved survival. Patients who achieved MPR had an 85% reduction in the risk of death compared to non‐MPR patients (HR = 0.15 [95% CI: 0.03–0.81], p = 0.028), while none of the other factors showed independent associations with OS (Figure 3B).

Survival According to Immunotherapy Maintenance

Among the 55 patients who underwent surgical resection, 13 received maintenance immunotherapy with sintilimab. Compared to those who did not receive maintenance therapy, patients in the ICI maintenance group achieved superior 2‐year DFS rates (92.3% vs. 64.3%) and 4‐year DFS rates (76.9% vs. 47.6%), with consistent advantages observed in OS rates (100% vs. 81.0% at 2 years; 100% vs. 69.0% at 4 years) (Figure 4A,B). Subgroup analysis revealed that the benefit of maintenance immunotherapy was closely associated with pathological response status. In the MPR and pCR subgroups, ICI maintenance was associated with significantly improved DFS (MPR subgroup: HR = 0.125 [95% CI: 0.018–0.894], p = 0.038; pCR subgroup: HR = 0.108 [95% CI: 0.011–1.053], p = 0.055) (Figure 4C–F). In contrast, for patients who did not achieve MPR or pCR, maintenance therapy failed to confer significant DFS or OS benefits (Figure 5).

Driver Mutations

NSCLC tumor samples with sufficient tumor tissues (96.4%, 53/55) were detected by next generation sequencing (NGS) of driver mutations. Driver mutations were detected in 28 patients, with some harboring multiple concurrent alterations. The identified driver mutations included EGFR (13/53, 24.5%), ERBB2 (4/53, 7.5%), PTEN (4/53, 7.5%), PIK3CA (4/53, 7.5%), MET (2/53, 3.8%), KRAS (2/53, 3.8%), ALK (1/53, 1.9%), RET (1/53, 1.9%), and POLE (1/53, 1.9%). No EGFR mutations were detected in patients with squamous cell carcinoma. Among the 23 patients with non‐squamous NSCLC, no statistically significant differences in DFS (HR = 0.710, p = 0.536) or OS (HR = 0.460, p = 0.382) were observed between EGFR‐mutant and EGFR wild‐type patients (Figure S3). Specifically, 7 of the 13 EGFR‐mutant patients (53.8%) received adjuvant targeted therapy (osimertinib, n = 1; almonertinib, n = 1; icotinib, n = 5).

Safety

The safety outcomes observed in all treated patients were consistent with previously reported results. Treatment‐related adverse events are summarized in S2 and S3. No neoadjuvant treatment‐related deaths occurred in either treatment arm. Grade ≥ 3 immune‐related adverse events included colitis in one patient and pneumonia in two patients. The present analysis focused on late‐onset adverse events, particularly serious immune‐related adverse events, none of which were reported after the completion of treatment in this study.

Discussion

In this final analysis of the randomized phase 2 neoSCORE trial with a median follow‐up of 56.9 months, we found that extending neoadjuvant sintilimab plus chemotherapy from two to three cycles did not result in a statistically significant improvement in DFS or OS in patients with resectable stage IB–IIIA NSCLC. The 4‐year DFS rates were 53.8% in the two‐cycle group and 55.2% in the three‐cycle group (HR = 1.048, p = 0.906), with corresponding OS rates of 76.9% and 75.9% (HR = 0.905, p = 0.852). To our knowledge, neoSCORE is the first randomized trial to directly compare different neoadjuvant cycle numbers, and superiority of three cycles over two cycles was not demonstrated in our final survival analysis.

Our findings are consistent with recently published meta‐analyses and real‐world studies. A systematic review and meta‐analysis by Ye et al. [28], which included 29 studies with a total of 1331 patients, similarly demonstrated that increasing the number of neoadjuvant immunotherapy cycles from two to three did not significantly improve pathological response rates. Further support for our conclusions comes from real‐world evidence. A real‐world study by Yang et al. [29] enrolled 184 patients with stage IB‐IIIB NSCLC and compared the efficacy of two cycles versus three to four cycles of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. The results showed no significant differences between the three‐to‐four‐cycle group and the two‐cycle group in terms of MPR rate (68.0% vs. 69.4%, p = 0.90), pCR rate (36.0% vs. 38.8%, p = 0.82), or long‐term survival.

This study does not demonstrate a survival benefit of three cycles over two cycles; however, it is not adequately powered to exclude a clinically meaningful difference, and it is crucial to interpret these findings with caution. The trial was terminated early due to futility regarding the primary endpoint (MPR), resulting in a small sample size that is underpowered to definitively exclude a meaningful survival difference between the two groups. Therefore, our survival analysis should be considered exploratory, and the absence of a significant difference does not equate to therapeutic equivalence.

In the analysis of prognostic factors, our study showed that MPR was associated with improved DFS and OS. Multivariable Cox regression analysis demonstrated that, after adjusting for multiple covariates, only MPR remained significantly and independently associated with improved DFS (HR = 0.26, p = 0.026) and OS (HR = 0.15, p = 0.028). This finding is consistent with conclusions from landmark trials such as CheckMate 816 and KEYNOTE‐671 [9, 10, 11], further establishing pathological response as a valid surrogate endpoint in neoadjuvant immunotherapy studies. It also suggests that patients without a deep pathological response may need more effective follow‐up treatments. Despite these findings, given the limited number of events relative to the number of covariates, our multivariable Cox models may be unstable and should be interpreted cautiously.

With regard to exploratory endpoints, our study revealed that in the MPR subgroup, maintenance immunotherapy significantly improved DFS (HR = 0.125, p = 0.038), whereas no significant long‐term survival benefit was observed in non‐MPR patients. These results suggest that, for patients who achieve MPR after neoadjuvant therapy, subsequent immunotherapy maintenance may provide long‐term benefits, whereas those without a pathological response derive little apparent benefit from maintenance alone and may require alternative or combination strategies. Nevertheless, only 13 patients received maintenance therapy and treatment allocation was administered according to patient decision. Patients who recovered well from neoadjuvant therapy and surgery had better performance status, or experienced fewer complications may have been more likely to receive maintenance therapy. This introduces substantial selection bias and confounding by indication. Therefore, these findings should be considered exploratory and warrant validation in prospective, randomized trials.

Our genomic analysis showed a driver mutation profile similar to prior reports in Chinese NSCLC patients, with EGFR being the most common alteration. Notably, no significant survival differences were observed between EGFR‐mutant and EGFR wild‐type patients in the non‐squamous subgroup. Notably, the sample size (13 EGFR‐mutant patients) is too small, and more importantly, 7 of these 13 patients (53.8%) received adjuvant EGFR‐TKI therapy, which represents a major confounding factor. Thus, current results are insufficient to support meaningful conclusions regarding the prognostic or predictive impact of EGFR mutation status. The detection of mutations in PIK3CA, PTEN, and other PI3K pathway genes in a subset of patients warrants further investigation, as these alterations may influence both pathological response and long‐term outcomes, though the low allele frequencies in some cases necessitate cautious interpretation [30, 31].

This study has several limitations. First, as a single‐center phase 2 trial, the sample size is modest (55 patients who underwent surgery), which limits the statistical power for survival analyses, particularly for subgroup comparisons. Second, the decision to receive maintenance immunotherapy was not randomized but based on patient preference, introducing potential selection bias. Therefore, the observed benefits of maintenance therapy should be considered exploratory. Third, the generalizability of our findings to other ICI agents or chemotherapy backbones (e.g., cisplatin‐based regimens) requires further investigation.

Despite these limitations, the neoSCORE trial provides the first randomized evidence on optimal neoadjuvant cycle duration of chemoimmunotherapy for resectable NSCLC and offers mature survival data with over 4 years of follow‐up. Our results did not demonstrate superiority of three cycles over two cycles of neoadjuvant sintilimab plus chemotherapy, but clinically meaningful differences cannot be excluded. MPR was associated with improved DFS and OS and may serve as a clinically relevant prognostic marker, although the multivariable analyses should be interpreted cautiously given the limited number of events. These findings may help inform clinical decision‐making and future trial design in the perioperative immunotherapy landscape for NSCLC, although prospective validation in larger cohorts is required.

Diagnostic challenge of occult ER-positive breast carcinoma within bone marrow indolent B-cell lymphoma and response to CDK4/6 inhibitor: a case report
Authors: Denise Drittone, Monica Mariniello, Luisa Esposito, Niccolò Noccioli, Gianluca Maiorana, Giacinto La Verde, Federica Mazzuca, Simona Pisegna
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBone marrow metastasis from hormone receptor–positive breast cancer can be challenging to detect, especially in patients with concurrent hematologic malignancies, where isolated epithelial cells may be hidden by a dominant lymphoid infiltrate. This situation requires careful histopathologic and immunophenotypic evaluation to prevent misdiagnosis.Case presentationWe report a 74-year-old White woman with Waldenström’s macroglobulinemia carrying the MYD88 L265P mutation and bone marrow infiltration by indolent B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma. She was evaluated for suspected skeletal disease progression due to rising tumor markers and inconclusive PET findings. Bone marrow biopsy revealed small clusters of epithelial cells positive for estrogen receptor (ER), cytokeratin AE1/AE3, and GATA3, consistent with metastatic breast carcinoma within a lymphoid background. Notably, ER positivity was the only marker indicating epithelial origin. The patient had previously received empirical aromatase inhibitor therapy after the initial detection of scattered ER-positive epithelial cells in 2018. Following disease progression in December 2024, she was treated with palbociclib and fulvestrant, while the indolent lymphoma was managed with active surveillance. At 6-month follow-up, the patient showed clinical stability, a biochemical response, and radiologic evidence of stable disease.ConclusionsThis case emphasizes the importance of maintaining a high level of clinical and pathological suspicion when assessing bone marrow infiltration, especially in patients with a history of malignancy. The presence of isolated ER-positive epithelial cells within a lymphoid marrow environment should raise suspicion of occult breast carcinoma metastasis. Due to the rarity of this coexistence, the underlying mechanisms and optimal management strategies are still unclear. To our knowledge, this is one of the few reported cases of occult ER-positive breast carcinoma presenting solely as bone marrow involvement in a patient with indolent B-cell lymphoma, with documented response to CDK4/6 inhibitor-based therapy.This case report was prepared in accordance with the CAse REport (CARE) guidelines.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) constitutes nearly one-third of all female malignancies and remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, accounting for approximately 15% of all oncologic deaths among women [1]. Bone metastases occur in more than 70% of patients with metastatic breast cancer and are associated with substantial morbidity and reduced quality of life. [2]. Symptomatic bone marrow metastases (BMM) are rare in breast cancer and, although more commonly associated with hematologic malignancies, may also occur secondary to solid tumors, particularly breast, lung, and prostate carcinomas [3, 4]. BMM typically presents with hematologic abnormalities, most commonly anemia and thrombocytopenia. Owing to its associated poor prognosis, the clinical features and therapeutic implications of symptomatic BMM remain insufficiently defined [5]. The prevalence of multiple primary malignant neoplasms (MPMNs) varies widely across populations and study methodologies, with reported rates ranging from 0.7% to 13% [6–10]. Among these, the coexistence of breast carcinoma and non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) remains uncommon, with only a few cases reported in the literature [11, 12]. Even more exceptional is the presentation described here, in which recurrent breast cancer was revealed solely through bone marrow involvement in the absence of a detectable primary breast lesion, making the case rare and diagnostically challenging. This case illustrates the complexity of identifying metastatic ER + BC in the context of an indolent B-cell lymphoma involving the bone marrow. Notably, the metastatic component was characterized by sparse epithelial cells expressing estrogen receptor in the absence of other definitive clinical or radiologic signs of recurrence. The diagnosis required a high index of suspicion, integration of clinical history, and reliance on immunohistochemical profiling to distinguish epithelial elements from the dominant lymphoid background. We report this case to highlight the need for heightened vigilance in similar clinical contexts, where bone marrow metastasis may be the sole or initial manifestation of recurrent breast cancer, particularly in patients with known hematologic comorbidities.

Case presentation

A 74-year-old White woman with a good performance status is a current smoker with an 8.3 pack-year smoking history. She has a family history of uterine carcinoma in her mother, melanoma in her father, and melanoma in her brother. Her past medical history includes arterial hypertension, dyslipidemia, valvular heart disease of unspecified cause, moderate-to-severe chronic kidney disease, and a prior ischemic stroke in 2017. She was evaluated for suspected disease progression. Since 2012, she has been monitored by hematologists for IgM kappa monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (IgM-MGUS), which subsequently progressed to Waldenström’s macroglobulinemia with MYD88 L265P mutation. Molecular analysis of deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) extracted from bone marrow aspirate using Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) detected a monoclonal IGH gene rearrangement. In October 2018, she presented with mild cytopenia and diffuse bone pain. A comprehensive radiological evaluation, including contrast-enhanced total-body Computed Tomography (CT) performed in November 2018, showed no evidence of visceral malignancy and revealed only osteosclerotic bone lesions of uncertain significance. In this context, a broad tumor marker panel was initiated, which demonstrated elevated levels of carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) (30 ng/mL) and cancer antigen 15-3 (CA 15.3) (80 U/mL). An off-target bone marrow biopsy on November 11, 2018, revealed heterogeneous cellularity (30–40%) comprising trilineage hematopoietic precursors, with evidence of dyserythropoiesis and focal megakaryocytic dysplasia. The specimen showed interstitial and paratrabecular infiltration of small-sized lymphoid cells, making up approximately 25% of the total cellularity, with an immunophenotype consistent with indolent B-cell NHL (CD20 + , CD3−, CD79a + , CD5−, CD23−, CD10−, BCL6−, BCL2 + , CD138−, Ki-67 < 5%). Additionally, scattered atypical epithelial-like cells with enlarged nuclei, prominent eosinophilic nucleoli, and abundant clear cytoplasm were observed within fibrotic stromal areas. Immunohistochemical analysis showed strong cytoplasmic positivity for cytokeratin AE1/AE3 and EMA, as well as for CK7, E-cadherin, and estrogen receptor (ER), and was negative for CD30, ALK1, CD15, CK20, TTF1, and CDX2. These findings suggested a diagnosis of metastatic carcinoma of mammary origin. Histopathological images from the 2018 bone marrow biopsy were not available for review; however, the diagnosis was supported by detailed pathological reports documenting the presence of ER-positive epithelial cells within a lymphoid background. These findings represented the first indication of occult metastatic breast carcinoma, despite the absence of radiologically detectable primary disease. Given the histologic findings, the patient underwent bilateral mammography, breast ultrasound, and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) on November 14, 2018, which showed no evidence of a primary breast tumor. Consequently, she was empirically started on first-line endocrine therapy with an aromatase inhibitor, given the lack of identifiable primary disease and the absence of robust evidence or established guidelines addressing this uncommon presentation. After a period of clinical stability, in September 2024, she reported diffuse bone pain, coinciding with a significant rise in tumor markers (CEA 264 ng/mL, CA 15.3 319 U/mL). In September 2024, an integrated Fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography/computed tomography (FDG PET/CT) demonstrated multiple newly detected skeletal lesions with low FDG avidity, confirming progressive osseous disease (Fig. 1A, B). A bone biopsy performed on October 1, 2024, (Fig. 2A, C) showed diffuse interstitial and paratrabecular infiltration by a lymphoproliferative process composed of small CD20 + , CD79a + , Cyclin D1– B lymphocytes, compatible with the known indolent B-cell lymphoma, accounting for approximately 40% of the total cellularity with a skewed κ/λ ratio (4:1). Given her history of IgM-MGUS, a sample of bone marrow was analyzed using PCR with detection of the MYD88 L265P mutation, thus confirming Waldenström’s macroglobulinemia. Importantly, rare scattered epithelial cells immunoreactive for cytokeratin AE1/AE3 and GATA3 were identified within the lymphoid aggregates. These cells were also estrogen receptor positive and negative for CD30, ALK1, CD15, CK20, TTF1, and CDX2, supporting the diagnosis of metastatic carcinoma of mammary origin. Immunostainings confirmed the coexistence of lymphoid (CD20 +) and epithelial (AE1/AE3 +) populations within the same marrow compartment. Based on morphological and immunophenotypic features and considering the patient’s prior history, these findings were interpreted as bone marrow involvement by metastatic breast carcinoma, coexisting with indolent B-cell lymphoma. In December 2024, after multidisciplinary discussion, the patient was started on second-line endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor (palbociclib 125 mg orally, days 1–21 of a 28-day cycle) and fulvestrant (500 mg intramuscularly, per the standard schedule). This regimen is supported by the American Society of Clinical Oncology as the standard of care for HR-positive, HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer, including cases with bone marrow involvement, based on evidence of improved progression-free survival and a favorable safety profile compared to endocrine therapy alone [13, 14]. Subsequently, laboratory workup in March 2025 showed a significant biochemical response (CEA 97.7 ng/mL, CA 15.3: 106.0 U/mL). A contrast-enhanced total-body CT scan performed on March 21, 2025, demonstrated stability of known osteosclerotic lesions, with no new skeletal findings (Fig. 3). The patient exhibited biochemical and radiographic disease stability with preserved performance status and only mild (grade 1) fatigue and pruritus, consistent with the expected tolerability of this therapeutic combination. Discussion

We have described a rare case of a patient with double primary malignant neoplasia comprising Waldenström’s macroglobulinemia (WM) and malignant solid tumors. Metastatic involvement of the bone marrow by breast carcinoma is rare and often difficult to diagnose, particularly in the context of concomitant hematologic malignancies such as indolent B-cell lymphomas [3, 15–17]. The reported incidence of multiple primary malignant neoplasms ranges from 0.73 to 11.7%, with most studies showing that metachronous tumors are more common than synchronous tumors [18]. Despite advances in diagnostic techniques, synchronous malignancies remain uncommon, particularly in patients with non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and their optimal management remains poorly defined [19]. Clinical presentation is often nonspecific, including cytopenias, fever, or unexplained hematologic changes [3, 15, 17]. Diagnosis depends on careful morphological evaluation and the use of immunohistochemical markers, particularly ER and pancytokeratin, which help distinguish metastatic epithelial cells from hematopoietic elements [16, 20, 21]. The literature also emphasizes the frequent mismatch in receptor status between primary tumors and bone or bone marrow metastases, making immunophenotypic reassessment essential when clinical suspicion is high [20]. The management of synchronous breast cancer and NHL remains poorly defined, and treatment is usually tailored according to the biologically dominant disease [12]. A similar case described in the literature involves a 68-year-old woman with a two-year history of indolent B-cell lymphoma under active surveillance who presented with worsening anemia and thrombocytopenia. Physical examination revealed no lymphadenopathy or palpable masses. Laboratory tests confirmed progressive pancytopenia. PET/CT showed diffuse bone marrow uptake without focal extramedullary lesions [22]. Bone marrow biopsy demonstrated hypercellularity with a lymphoid infiltrate consistent with indolent B-cell lymphoma, along with rare isolated atypical cells that morphologically resembled histiocytes or signet-ring cells [16, 21]. Immunohistochemistry showed CD20 and Bcl-2 positivity in the lymphomatous component, while the atypical cells were negative for hematopoietic markers and strongly positive for pancytokeratin and ER, confirming their epithelial origin and the diagnosis of metastatic breast carcinoma [16, 20]. The patient had no prior history of breast cancer; subsequent breast MRI revealed a 1.2 cm lesion in the upper outer quadrant of the left breast, which was confirmed by core needle biopsy as ER-positive, HER2-negative invasive lobular carcinoma. Bone marrow metastases from breast carcinoma, particularly of the lobular subtype, may be clinically silent and mimic reactive or hematologic processes [3, 16, 17]. Marrow infiltration by breast carcinoma is frequently associated with cytopenias and may be the sole manifestation of metastatic disease, especially in ER-positive subtypes [3, 16, 17, 23]. Differential diagnosis with hematologic malignancies requires comprehensive morphological evaluation and extended immunohistochemical panels [16, 21]. Isolated epithelial cells showing ER positivity in the bone marrow should always prompt consideration of metastatic breast carcinoma, even in the absence of a known oncologic history [16, 20]. Therapeutic management must be individualized: for ER-positive breast carcinoma with bone marrow metastases, endocrine therapy is the first-line approach and is associated with improved survival compared to chemotherapy alone [3, 15, 17]. In indolent B-cell lymphomas, treatment may be deferred in the absence of symptoms or progression, but severe cytopenias may necessitate a combined approach [24, 25]. Prognosis in patients with dual pathology depends on disease extent and response to systemic therapy [3, 15, 17, 24, 25]. Zhan et al. reported that 4.46% of patients with non-Hodgkin lymphoma developed double primary malignant neoplasms, with breast cancer representing one of the most common associated solid tumors and being associated with more favorable overall survival outcomes [11]. This case highlights the diagnostic challenge posed by the coexistence of two bone marrow–infiltrating malignancies. Bone marrow biopsy and immunohistochemistry were essential for identifying occult metastatic breast carcinoma despite negative breast imaging. An individualized multidisciplinary approach, combining active surveillance for the indolent lymphoma with palbociclib and fulvestrant for metastatic breast cancer, achieved durable clinical, biochemical, and radiologic disease control.

Conclusion

In summary, the coexistence of indolent B-cell lymphoma and bone marrow metastasis from breast carcinoma is extremely rare and may be obscured by morphological similarities, leading to delayed or missed diagnosis. This case emphasizes the crucial role of immunohistochemical profiling, including ER, GATA3, and cytokeratins, in detecting hidden metastatic disease within a lymphoid marrow environment. Equally important is the involvement of a multidisciplinary tumor board to ensure hematologists, oncologists, and pathologists collaboratively interpret complex findings and develop a customized treatment plan. Since current evidence mainly comes from case reports and small retrospective series, there are no standardized protocols for prognosis assessment or therapy decisions in such dual pathologies. This highlights the need for systematic long-term follow-up and for creating registries or prospective studies to gather similar cases. Such initiatives would facilitate the development of evidence-based algorithms to enhance diagnostic accuracy and patient management in cases of synchronous hematologic and solid tumors. Until then, a personalized treatment approach—tailored to the biological behavior and stage of both tumors, as well as the patient’s overall condition—remains essential, with the aim of achieving sustained disease control and optimal outcomes.

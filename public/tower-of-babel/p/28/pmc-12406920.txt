Mental health disorders among people living with HIV in sub-Saharan Africa: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Waheed Adeola Adedeji, Adeyinka Ganiyat Ishola, Olufisayo Elugbadebo, Olayide Muideen Oladepo, Tinashe Mudzviti, Malinda Kaiyo-Utete, Nicola Willis, Dhikrullah Adeyemi Adebayo, Takudzwa Joylyn Mtisi, Kudzai Fortunate Vongai Chokuona, Qing Ma, Raymond Cha, Alan Hutson, Man E Charurat, Oyindamola Bidemi Yusuf, Charles Maponga, Fatai Adewale Fehintola, Oye Gureje, Gene D Morse
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe mental health of people living with HIV (PLWH) is a growing concern globally, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa (SSA), where there is limited access to mental healthcare, with evidence showing high levels of depression, anxiety and neurocognitive disorders among this population. While Mental Health Disorders (MHDs) can impede HIV care and promote adverse health outcomes, there is limited literature on MHDs among PLWH. This scoping review will explore the existing literature on the burden and factors associated with MHDs among adults living with HIV in SSA.Methods and analysisArksey and O’Malley’s methodological framework will guide the search of this scoping review. Relevant original research articles published in English from 1 January 2000 to 31 May 2025 on MHDs among PLWH in SSA will be identified through searches in the African Index Medicus, African Journal Online, PubMed and Embase databases. Four independent reviewers, working in pairs (one reviewer and one verifier), will screen the titles, abstracts and later the full texts, adopting the population, concept and context framework. Other coauthors will serve as tiebreakers whenever there is disagreement on the eligibility. The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews flowchart will be presented. We will perform a narrative synthesis to report our findings.Ethics and disseminationThis scoping review protocol does not require ethical approval, as it relies solely on publicly available existing data and does not involve human participants. We will disseminate the findings from this review through peer-reviewed publications and presentations at local and international conferences.Trial registrationThe protocol was registered in the Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/8ymqu).

Introduction

HIV persists as a public health problem, affecting about 90 million people and leading to more than 40 million cumulative deaths globally.1 The WHO African Region remains the most severely affected and accounts for the majority of the AIDS-related deaths.2 In 2023, about 1.3 million people were newly infected with HIV, compared with about 3.3 million people with new HIV infections in 1995.3 Though there is a reduction in new rates of HIV infections, recently, the total number of people living with HIV (PLWH) has increased slightly due to a prolonged life expectancy emanating from efficient antiretroviral therapy.2 The HIV-associated opportunistic infections and attendant morbidity and mortality have significantly reduced.4 However, the increased life expectancy of PLWH has also increased their risk of acquiring non-communicable diseases (NCDs).57 Mental health disorders (MHDs) are closely associated with NCDs and are among the most prevalent comorbidities in PLWH globally.8

PLWH have a higher risk of developing mental health problems than the HIV-negative general population.9 It has been estimated that about half of the PLWH meet the criteria for the diagnosis of one or more forms of psychiatric disorders.10 Studies have reported a high burden of MHDs among PLWH. A systematic review of common MHDs in adults living with HIV globally by Hoare et al reported that 28%–62% of PLWH have mental health symptoms.11 Of the 142 studies in the final review, only 27% were conducted in sub-Saharan Africa (SSA).

Worldwide, MHDs among PLWH are linked to factors such as unemployment,12 low or decreased social capital and support,1315 food insecurity,1618 ineffective coping strategies, limited family and community support,14 HIV-related stigma, low self-esteem,15 more negative life events19 and chronic HIV-related stress.20 In Africa, a systematic review reported that being female, having poor health, lack of family and social support and receiving substandard health services were associated with more significant psychiatric morbidity among PLWH.10 Similarly, a recent review reported a higher burden of MHDs among females living with HIV and AIDS in SSA.21 Depression,11 22 anxiety,23 24 cognitive impairment,25 26 substance use disorders,27 suicidality,28 post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)29 and psychosis3032 were reported to be more prevalent among this population. Among the PLWH, depression was the most prevalent MHD reported.11 22

In addition to the critical role that MHDs play by increasing the risk of HIV acquisition by several folds, mental disorders contribute significantly to poorer healthcare behaviours across the HIV care continuum, resulting in adverse HIV health outcomes.30

To achieve the 95-95-95 targets for all by 2025 and end the AIDS pandemic by 2030,33 there is an urgent need to integrate mental health screening and care services into all HIV treatment services.32 The integration of behavioural and mental health screening and care will improve HIV prevention and care outcomes and enhance global access to mental healthcare.30 To achieve this, it is necessary to review the common MHDs and the effective treatment available, consider the available resources and prioritise accordingly.

SSA remains the centre of the HIV pandemic, and current estimates show that progress towards reducing its spread is not meeting the targeted goals.34 35 Also, due to effective HIV management and increased longevity for people with HIV,36 SSA is expected to have a high burden of MHDs among PLWH.37 Despite the rise in the intersection between HIV and mental health, a crucial knowledge gap still exists in the systematic mapping of evidence on MHDs among PLWH in SSA. Although some reviews10 12 have addressed MHDs among PLWH in SSA, an updated comprehensive review is needed. Brandt’s10 review covered studies from 1994 to 2008, requiring updates due to changes in HIV/AIDS epidemiology and management. Boakye et al21 focused only on depression and anxiety among females living with HIV/AIDS, while Hoare et al’s11 global review (2018–2020) reported depression, anxiety and suicidality. Therefore, a comprehensive review of the common MHDs among the PLWH in SSA is desirable.

This scoping review will systematically review studies conducted between 2000 and 2025, aiming to document the burden and factors associated with the common MHDs among adults living with HIV in SSA. The findings of this study will inform future research and the development of targeted interventions for improving mental healthcare for PLWH in SSA.

Methods and analysis

The protocol was registered in the Open Science Framework (https://osf.io/8ymqu).

This scoping review will adopt the Arksey and O’Malley framework.38 39 The review will follow five stages: (1) identifying the research question, (2) identifying the relevant studies (search strategy), (3) selecting the eligible studies, (4) charting the data and (5) collating, summarising and reporting the results40 (table 1). The search criteria will be limited to published original research studies conducted in SSA between 2000 and 2025.

Information sources and search strategy

This review will search four databases: African Index Medicus (AIM), African Journal Online, PubMed and Embase. The Joanna Briggs Institute guidelines39 will be considered, the framework recommended by Arksey and O’Malley40 will be followed and we will adhere to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) for Searching (PRISMA-S) extension.22 24

The search strategy will focus on terms associated with MHDs and PLWH. We will draft and refine the strategy through team discussions and search electronic literature in three steps.

Step 1: conduct a preliminary search of PubMed for MHDs, HIV/AIDS and SSA. We will identify the keywords used in the titles and abstracts of the identified articles. We reviewed search terms from similar MHD systematic reviews10 11 21 to ensure our strategy was comprehensive.

Step 2: a thorough review of the keywords identified in the first step and a compiled list of terms to guide our detailed literature search in the selected databases will be used. Search terms will include Medical Subject Headings and keywords related to mental health, MHDs/diseases, HIV, HIV/AIDS, PLWH and SSA. The key search terms will include, ‘Mental disorders’, ‘Depression’, ‘Anxiety’, ‘Substance use’, ‘Neurocognitive impairment (NCI)’, ‘Suicidality’, ‘Post-traumatic stress disorders (PSTD)’, ‘Psychiatric disorders’, ‘Psychosis’, ‘Schizophrenia’, ‘Adults’, ‘People Living with HIV (PLWH)’, ‘HIV’, ‘AIDS’ and ‘Sub-Saharan Africa’, which will be combined by the Boolean operator ‘AND’. The synonyms of each of the key search terms will be combined with the Boolean operator ‘OR’. We will translate the final PubMed search string with the Embase translator for the Embase search. Online supplemental file 1 contains the search strategy for PubMed and Embase. The search terms used in PubMed will be modified to meet the specifications for AIM and African Journal Online.

Step 3: we will manually search for eligible articles in the references of relevant articles and other reviews.

The search will be confined to studies published in the English language. The final search results of this review will be exported into EndNote V.20. The comprehensive scoping is set to begin in September 2025, with completion anticipated by February 2026.

Population: the study population is adults living with HIV in SSA.

Concept: the concept is the burden of common MHDs, including depression, anxiety, psychosis, substance use disorders, neurocognitive impairment (NCI), suicidality, PTSD and psychotic disorder/psychosis/schizophrenia.

Context: the context is SSA. The articles that will be included are studies that describe the association between HIV (PLWH) and MHDs, published as full-text, peer-reviewed papers in English and published between 2000 and 2025. Studies of any original research, such as quantitative studies, randomised clinical trials, cohort studies, case-control studies and cross-sectional studies, will be included. The studies would be ineligible if the study population did not include PLWH, were conducted outside SSA, were secondary literature (scoping reviews, literature reviews, systematic reviews and meta-analyses), were letters to the editors, protocol, case series or studies that did not report the mental health outcomes among PLWH. However, we will review the references of any identified secondary literature for eligible articles.

Study selection process

The data will be managed in Covidence software. The retrieved publications will be imported into Covidence, which automatically identifies and removes duplicates. We will access the import history page of Covidence to view the number of duplicates and review for any potential issues. Two reviewers (WAA and OMO) will independently evaluate the title and abstract of each retrieved publication to determine its suitability for full-text review. The same reviewers will assess the full text of any relevant citation for eligibility. At this stage, we will document the reasons for exclusion, and any discrepancies will be resolved either by consensus or by review from a third independent reviewer (AGI or OE).

Data charting process

We will develop the data extraction template in Covidence using variables that align with our review. The included variables will encompass study characteristics (first author’s name, year of publication, country of study, state of study, rural or urban location, study design, sample size); population characteristics (gender distribution (male, female/transgender) and mean (SD) age of participants in years); MHDs (depression, anxiety, NCI, suicidality, substance use, PTSD or psychosis/schizophrenia); screening/diagnostic tools; the proportion of PLWH with MHDs and additional comments (key findings, including factors associated with MHDs). Two reviewers (WAA and OMO) will independently extract and chart the data, discuss the results and continuously update the data-charting form based on feedback from discussions and consensus.

Collating, summarising and reporting the results

Synthesis of results

The data obtained will be exported to SPSS V.27 for analysis. A table, with a narrative summary of the information extracted from the included studies, will be provided in relation to the scoping review question. Basic descriptive statistics (frequencies with proportions) will be used to summarise data on the common MHDs among PLWH in SSA. The pooled prevalence, with a 95% CI, of individual MHDs (depression, anxiety, NCI, suicidality, substance use, PTSD and psychosis/schizophrenia), will be calculated. Data will be presented in text and tables. The flow diagram of the PRISMA extension for Scoping Reviews will be used to display the review process. Also, tables will be used to show the prevalence estimates of each MHD (depression, anxiety, NCI, suicidality, substance use, PTSD and psychosis/schizophrenia), indicating the first author, year of publication, location, study design, sample size, gender, diagnostic/screening tools and prevalence. Conclusions will be drawn from our review documenting the available literature on MHDs among PLWH. We will identify knowledge gaps and provide recommendations for future research.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public will not be involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

Ethics and dissemination

This is a scoping review protocol and does not require ethical approval. We will disseminate the findings from this review through peer-reviewed publications and presentations at local and international conferences.

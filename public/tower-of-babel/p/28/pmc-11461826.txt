Exploring topological indices and entropy measure via rational curve fitting models for calcium hydroxide network
Authors: Rongbing Huang, Muhammad Farhan Hanif, Muhammad Kamran Siddiqui, Muhammad Faisal Hanif, Mazhar Hussain, Fikre Bogale Petros
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis work uses rational curve fitting models to investigate the complex link between topological indices and entropy metrics in calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)2) networks. Entropy measurements shed light on the complexity and disorder of these networks, whereas topological indices are essential instruments for comprehending the structural characteristics of chemical substances. Our goal is to find new patterns and connections by merging these two fields, improving materials science’s prediction capacity. We calculate many topological indices, such as the Randic, Balaban, and Zagreb indices, and examine the relationship between them and entropy measurements obtained from (Ca(OH)2) structural arrangement.Keywords: Topological indices, Zagreb type indices, Forgotten index, Augmented Zagreb index, Redefined type indices, Calcium hydroxide, Shanon entropySubject terms: Physical chemistry, Applied mathematics

Introduction

A basic area of mathematics called graph theory uses the concept of graphs to investigate the intricate interactions that exist between things. A graph G consists of a vertex set V and an edge set E. In a graph, vertices represent entities, and edges show relationships or interactions between them1,2. A graph’s size represents how many edges it contains, whereas its order tells how many vertices it has. On the other hand, the number of edges incident to a vertex is represented by the vertex degree ς(u). Graph theory is a vital tool for modeling and understanding a wide range of real-world events because it offers a methodical framework for research. Basic terms in graph theory, such as paths, which define edge sequences, degree, which quantifies vertex connection, and connectivity, which characterizes a network’s overall connectedness, simplify in-depth study. Moreover, a more in-depth analysis and comprehension of graph structures is made possible by exploring several properties of graph theory, including trees, cycles, polarity, and coloring3.

Numerous areas can benefit from the extensive use of this mathematical framework. One significant use of graphs is network analysis, which uses them to depict complex systems like social networks, computer networks, and transportation networks4–6. For instance, social networking sites like Facebook and Twitter may be seen as graphs where people are the vertices and their relationships are the edges. Analysts can get important insights into network topologies, information distribution patterns, and influential nodes by applying graph theory. This may result in enhanced decision-making and system optimization7–10.

Chemical graph theory is a subfield of graph theory that focuses on modeling and analyzing chemical compounds using graphs. In chemical graph theory, atoms are represented as vertices and chemical relationships between atoms as edges. This abstraction enables the use of graph-theoretic concepts and methods to understand many aspects of chemical compounds13,14. One field that makes extensive use of chemical graph theory is computational chemistry. In computational chemists people employ graph models of molecules to study their properties, behaviors, and interactions, they consider atoms as vertex and bonds between atoms as edges15. By analyzing the topological structure of chemical graphs, scientists may predict molecular properties such as molecular stability, reactivity, and biological activity. This information is essential for drug development as computational methods are used to evaluate large databases of chemical compounds16. Structure of calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)2)

Calcium hydroxide, also referred to as hydrated lime or slaked lime, is a chemical substance having the formula Ca(OH)2. It is a powder that is odorless, white, and has a low solubility in water. Calcium hydroxide crystals are made of a layered crystal lattice, which usually forms hexagonal or rhombohedral crystals. Six hydroxide ions (OH-), grouped in an octahedral form, surround each calcium ion (Ca2+) in this lattice. Each hydroxide ion is shared by three calcium ions. A three-dimensional crystal lattice structure is produced by this configuration. When combined with water, calcium hydroxide forms a suspension known as lime water, but its density is only slightly soluble in water (2.21 g/cm3). When it reacts with acids in the neutralization processes, it acts as a potent base and produces salts and water44. It also produces a calcium hydroxide solution when it reacts exothermically with water. Moreover, it has the ability to take in carbon dioxide from the atmosphere to create calcium carbonate, which coats the surface of the calcium hydroxide in a protective coating.

Many sectors use calcium hydroxide, including building (for mortar, plaster, and cement), agriculture (for soil amendment), water treatment (for softening hard water and eliminating impurities), and food processing (for pH adjustment and processing)45. Although calcium hydroxide is generally thought to be safe when handled correctly, it can irritate the respiratory system and cause skin and eye irritation when inhaled in dust form. So, when handling calcium hydroxide, the proper safety precautions should be followed, such as wearing gloves, and goggles, and preventing dust inhalation46.

Analyzing the rational curve fitting (Rcf) relationships between indices and their respective entropies (ΔS)

Examining the rational curve fitting (Rcf) correlations between indices and their associated entropies is necessary to comprehend the relationship between these variables. By applying methods for rational curve fitting, researchers may model the functional relationships between indices and entropies and so provide insight into underlying patterns and correlations. Rational curve fitting allows researchers to identify the mathematical formulae that most properly represent the link between changes in entropies and changes in indices. This method makes it feasible to find the optimal coefficients or parameters to characterize the relationship between the two variables.

An important stage in the Rcf analytical procedure is to identify the optimal rational function that best describes the connection between the indices and entropies. In this procedure, selecting the appropriate rational function form (linear, quadratic, or cubic) and determining the coefficients or parameters that minimize the error between the fitted curve and the actual data points are usually necessary. Important metrics may also be used by researchers to evaluate the rational curve’s goodness-of-fit to the data. These metrics include things like the coefficient of determination (R2), the sum of squares error (SSE), the adjusted R2, the root mean square error (RMSE), and the Akaike information criterion (AIC). These measures may be used to assess how well the rational function takes into account data variability.

Furthermore, the interpretation of the connection between entropies and indices in terms of theoretical or physical significance is made possible by Rcf analysis. The coefficients of the rational function and their statistical significance allow researchers to ascertain the direction and strength of the relationship between the variables. Strong foundation provided by Rcf analysis allows for the investigation of complex correlations between entropies and indices, leading to a substantial comprehension of the underlying mechanisms controlling the system’s behavior.

Discussion and conclusion

We used edge partitioning to calculate the degree-based topological indices for Ca(OH)2, as well as entropy and closure formulas. Numerical calculations and graphical representations were used to conduct a comparative examination of these estimates. For the numerical calculations, MAPLE was used, and MATLAB was used for the graphical representation. To determine the correlations between different indices and the volatility values that corresponded with them, curves were fitted. Despite differences in parameterization values, the logical approach consistently produced the best outcomes, according to an evaluation of the performance of entropy metrics. When it came to connecting mobility with indices, logarithmic regression models showed the highest level of precision. This study clarifies the complex connections between molecule structure and characteristics, providing new understandings for advances in chemistry, materials science, and related domains. For future work we will apply logarithmic regression models for certain chemical structures. Through our research we predict chemical properties without experiment.

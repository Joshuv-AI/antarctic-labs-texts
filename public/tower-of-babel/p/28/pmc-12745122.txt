The state of educational research in cardiothoracic surgery: A review of the literature
Authors: Amirtha Shekar, Victoria Hart, John Treffalls, Travis J. Miles, Todd K. Rosengart, Marc R. Moon, Joseph S. Coselli, Lauren M. Barron, Shawn S. Groth, Shanda H. Blackmon, Ravi K. Ghanta, Dawn S. Hui, Subhasis Chatterjee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveEvolving training paradigms in cardiothoracic surgery (CTS) are changing the educational needs of integrated residents and traditional fellows. Educational research is important for evaluating specialty training. This study assessed CTS education articles to analyze their prevalence, content, and reach.MethodsIn a cross-sectional observational study of education articles published on North American CTS programs from January 2013 through December 2023, articles were identified by querying PubMed, other databases, and journals. Articles that passed an initial abstract review underwent full-text review. Articles were organized by content into 9 categories: curriculum, simulation, trainee assessment, program evaluation, wellness/burnout, diversity/inclusion, mentorship/career, outcomes, and trainee perceptions. Data were analyzed with univariate statistics and the Mann-Kendall test.ResultsA total of 124 CTS education articles from 20 journals were identified, comprising 0.15% of the 84,005 articles published across these journals. The greatest volume of articles came from Annals of Thoracic Surgery (41%) and Journal of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery (26%). PlumX metrics revealed that each article had a median of 11 interaction citations (interquartile range, 6.0-22.0; n = 113) and 23 captures (interquartile range, 11-47; n = 101). Common topics were curriculum (n = 39, 32%) and simulation training (n = 19, 15%). Manuscripts with ≥11 citations had greater PlumX metrics (P < .001). The annual CTS publication volume of educational articles averaged 11.3 ± 4.0; excluding pandemic years, this volume showed no significant increase from 2013-2019 to 2023 (P = .80).ConclusionsApproximately 10 articles per year are published on CTS education and training. Increased efforts in the CTS community are needed to encourage and disseminate education research.

CTS education literature by the numbers, a high-level view.

Central Message

Despite an evolving CTS training landscape, the volume of education literature remains limited and predominantly centered on residents, program curricula, and simulation training.

Perspective

By outlining characteristics of the existing CTS education literature, such as article content and journal contributions, this study establishes the need to increase the number and diversity of these publications. It identifies gaps in the literature, with the goal of encouraging scholarly work that serves the specialty by promoting trainees’ needs and the development of highly capable CT surgeons.

Surgical training requires rigorous review to ensure trainees are prepared for independent practice. Concerns about traditional evaluation methods have driven major changes in surgical education, shifting from quantitative benchmarks to comprehensive assessments of competence across American College of Graduate Medical Education−defined domains.1 The first 6-year integrated (I-6) cardiothoracic surgery (CTS) program, introduced in 2007 and modeled after neurosurgery and plastic surgery pathways, shortened training and provided earlier specialty exposure (postgraduate year 1 vs postgraduate year 6). Growing interest in CTS education has prompted comparisons among training pathways2 and between the qualifications of CTS I-6 applicants and other subspecialty applicants.3 Nevertheless, scholars note that this increased focus has produced few well-designed studies.4

Despite growing interest in surgical education, most of the 6.8 articles published annually focus on general surgery rather than subspecialties.5 However, specialty-specific technology and minimally invasive techniques highlight the need for research on simulation and other adjuncts to guide training.6 Although such work exists in vascular7 and orthopedic surgery,8 it remains limited in CTS.

The scope and quality of CTS education literature are not well defined. We therefore reviewed the prevalence, content, and impact of CTS education publications to characterize the field, assess changes over time, and identify priorities for future research.

Methods

Study Design

A scoping review was conducted and reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for scoping reviews guidelines (Online Data Supplement 1).9 The design and methods for this study were guided by similar scoping reviews of education articles in vascular7 and orthopedic surgery.8 A scoping review was selected to broadly assess CTS education literature, map current knowledge, and identify research gaps.10

We analyzed PubMed-indexed full-length articles (January 2013 to December 2023) to assess trends in CTS education research, focusing on trainees and program approaches. January 2013 was selected to capture publications since the first I-6 class graduated. Authors involved in similar scoping reviews (J.R.T.) and expert opinions (S.C.) selected 9 Medical Subject Heading terms to use in the PubMed query: (“cardiothoracic surgery” OR “thoracic surgery”) AND (training OR education OR teaching) AND (trainee∗ OR student∗ OR program OR residency). The OVID MEDLINE, EBSCO, Embase, and Cochrane databases were queried in a similar manner (final search: April 11, 2024).

In a secondary review, references were screened, and 5 journals were individually scoped: Annals of Thoracic Surgery (ATS), Journal of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery (JTCVS), Journal of Surgical Research, Journal of Surgical Education, and Thoracic Surgery Clinics. Journal selection was based on expert opinion (S.C.) and relevance to CTS education, broadening the search to capture additional articles not identified initially.

Articles were included if they reported US or Canadian studies on CTS training for medical students, residents, fellows, or faculty. All CTS subspecialties (congenital, adult cardiac, general thoracic, vascular) were eligible. Excluded were letters, abstracts, editorials, and studies in which CTS education was a secondary focus of broader surgical research.7 Institutional review board approval was waived because this study involved no human subjects and was exempt under institutional review board criteria.

Reviewer Reliability

All articles identified from the initial database query were imported into Covidence (Veritas Health Innovation). Covidence automatically excluded duplicates and nonempirical studies (eg, commentaries, protocols, case reports, editorials), streamlining screening. Abstract/title screening and full-text reviews were performed independently by 2 reviewers (A.S., V.K.H.) using predefined inclusion/exclusion criteria; disagreements were resolved by a third reviewer (J.A.T.). Eligible articles proceeded to data collection. Interrater reliability, assessed at the full-text stage with the Cohen kappa, was 0.65, indicating substantial reviewer agreement.

Journal Evaluation

For full-text review, journals were assessed for CTS education publication volume over the 11 years. Article totals were counted using date-range filters across databases. Each journal's most recent impact factor was retrieved directly from the journal's website.

Article Evaluation

Articles were evaluated by year, conference presentation, subject type, study design, article category, and PlumX metrics (captures, citations, mentions).7,8 These metrics reflect article engagement but vary across journals as the result of differing use of social media and platforms. Subjects were classified as “residents,” “medical students,” “faculty,” or “multiple.” Study design was categorized as single-institutional, multi-institutional, or neither (eg, National Resident Matching Program or meeting data). Content categories, adapted from vascular surgery education and full-text review themes, comprised 9 areas (eg, wellness, inclusion; Figure 1).Figure 1Topics of cardiothoracic education articles from 2013 to 2023.

Topics of cardiothoracic education articles from 2013 to 2023.

Author Evaluation

We collected first and senior author names, senior author affiliation (academic/community), and gender. To minimize implicit bias or inaccuracies, gender was assigned using Gender-API software (Gender-api.com), which applies machine-learning algorithms.7 For our dataset, predicted gender accuracy exceeded 90%, minimizing classification bias in author identification.

Statistical Analysis

Data were evaluated with R studio (A.S.) and Jamovi (J.A.T.), focusing on article impact (using citations as a surrogate). Categorical data are reported as percentages; continuous data are reported as mean ± standard deviation (normal) or median (interquartile range [IQR]) (nonnormal). Normality was assessed with the Shapiro-Wilk test. Univariable analysis was conducted using Pearson χ2, Student t, or Mann-Whitney U tests as appropriate. The Mann-Kendall Test evaluated annual CTS education publication counts to detect changes over time. Analyses were exploratory and descriptive, aimed at benchmarking publication trends rather than establishing causation.

Results

Journal Characteristics

A systematic search identified 3617 CTS education articles published over 11 years. Automated tools removed 2411 as duplicates or ineligible (Figure 2). The remaining 1206 articles underwent abstract and title screening, of which 112 advanced to full-text review. Twenty-one were excluded for non-CTS focus,11 non-North American data,1 or correspondence format.11 An additional 33 articles were identified through journal and reference screening, yielding 124 CTS education articles from 20 journals (Online Data Supplement 2).Figure 2Selection process for articles included in the study.

Selection process for articles included in the study.

High-impact CTS journals dominated: JTCVS and ATS contributed 67% of included articles (Online Data Supplement 3). In these journals, CTS education accounted for 0.33% of publications (83/25,001), exceeding the 0.15% rate across all 20 journals (124/84,005). The greatest relative proportions were in Thoracic Surgery Clinics (0.95%), ATS (0.42%), and Journal of Surgical Education (0.33%). The median impact factor of the 20 journals was 2.5 (IQR, 1.7-3.1).

On average, 11.3 ± 4.0 CTS education articles were published annually. The Mann-Kendall test showed no significant increase over time (P = .13; Figure 3, A). When pandemic years (2020-2022), which saw a temporary surge across specialties, were excluded, no significant change in publication rates was found between 2013-2019 and 2023 (P = .80; Figure 3, B).Figure 3Yearly cardiothoracic education articles from 2013 to 2023. Article Interactivity

PlumX metrics showed a median of 23 social media captures (IQR, 11.0-47.0) per article; captures declined over time (P < .001), probably as social media engagement accumulated. Spearman correlation analysis showed that greater-impact journals (P = .038) and highly cited articles had more captures (P < .001). Nineteen percent were presented at conferences, with presentation frequency not differing significantly between highly cited and less-cited studies (P = .643).

Author Characteristics

Senior authors were almost exclusively academic (99%). First authors were 63% male; senior authors, 81%. Male senior authors were evenly distributed by citation level (79.7% vs 80.8%, P = .88). Gender distribution of first (P = .92) and senior authors (P = .46) showed no significant temporal change.

Education Content

Curriculum and teaching was the most common topic (32%), followed by simulation training (15%), trainee perceptions (14%), diversity/inclusion (11%), and career/mentorship (9%). Less common were trainee assessment, case-outcomes, program evaluation, and wellness and burnout topics (Table 1). Content distribution did not differ significantly by citation level (P = .379): career/mentorship and trainee assessment were more frequent among less-cited articles, whereas simulation training and diversity/inclusion were more frequent among highly cited articles (Table 1). Content distribution remained stable over the 11-year study period.

Discussion

The field of CTS is evolving rapidly. Multiple training pathways and advances in minimally invasive and endovascular techniques require faculty and residents to acquire new skills.12,13 Ideally, such changes would be guided by robust educational research to identify the most effective training strategies and methods for disseminating new technical skills. Instead, these developments have largely been driven by societal pressures and practice demands rather than evidence, representing a lost opportunity to critically evaluate how best to prepare future surgeons.

In our review of 1200+ CTS education articles over 11 years,we found that although expert opinions and commentaries are common, original research on training models and trainee experiences remains sparse. This gap is concerning because education research guides curricular reform and ensures that training programs adapt to evolving needs. To our knowledge, this study provides the largest benchmark of CTS education trends to date.

CTS education publication volume has remained low and static. Across 20 journals from 2013 to 2023, only ∼10 CTS education articles appeared annually, a tiny fraction of total output. Small trainee numbers and, possibly, lower acceptance rates may contribute, but the larger issue is the inherent difficulty of conducting education research, which is therefore rarely pursued. Growth has been inconsistent and limited, representing missed opportunities to improve training.

Comparable fields show similar gaps: education publications comprise 0.2% in vascular surgery7 and 0.5% in orthopedics,8 yet CTS output is the lowest (0.15%). By contrast, 3% of general surgery publications address education.5

Three journals have contributed the most CTS education articles: JTCVS, ATS, and Journal of Surgical Education. These leading journals provide structure for readers to follow the evolving CTS education landscape. JTCVS features dedicated education sections across congenital, cardiac, and thoracic subspecialties, whereas ATS includes an Education and Professional Development section. Thoracic Surgery Clinics has published entire volumes devoted to education. Despite recognition from major societies such as the Society of Thoracic Surgeons and the American Association for Thoracic Surgery, stronger emphasis on and incentivization for education research are still needed. Although international journals such as the European Journal of Cardio-Thoracic Surgery publish education content, they were excluded from this analysis to maintain focus on North American training; future work may expand globally.

Most CTS education studies focused on residents, consistent with other surgical subspecialties.3,7 Mixed-population studies often compared outcomes between trainees and attendings, showing that, with proper supervision, trainees achieve results comparable with faculty.14 Further work is needed to balance patient safety with operative exposure. Outcomes after aortic15 and mitral valve surgery have been examined,16 but few recent studies address other complex cardiac operations, highlighting an important area for future research.

CTS education articles have predominantly focused on curriculum/teaching (30%) to standardize training and address variable operative exposure, as many graduates feel additional training is needed for competency.16 Simulation studies (15%) were common but focused mainly on coronary techniques; further work is needed in congenital and general thoracic surgery to broaden skill assessment. Although curriculum reform in CTS is essential, attention to trainee wellness is equally critical. Only 2 articles addressed wellness or burnout, despite burnout being the leading predictor of surgical attrition,17,18 underscoring the importance of wellness curricula tailored to CTS residents’ needs.

Barriers to CTS education research include few trainees per program, limited single-center studies, and insufficient funding. Organizations like the Thoracic Surgery Directors Association and the Thoracic Surgery Residents Association provide multicenter models. The Thoracic Surgery Directors Association’s Thoracic Education Cooperative Group already promotes collaboration, and such platforms should be leveraged further to conduct large-scale, high-quality studies across diverse training programs.

This study has limitations. Despite a comprehensive search, some articles without indexed terms or absent from queried databases may have been missed. Abstract exclusions could not be characterized in detail due to software constraints. Interrater reliability was substantial but imperfect, reflecting subjectivity in inclusion decisions. Only 5 journals were manually reviewed, selected by expert consensus and precedent from other scoping reviews. Although journals such as Annals of Cardiothoracic Surgery, Innovations, Seminars in Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery, and Journal of Chest Surgery are relevant, a 2023 audit showed they rarely publish eligible CTS education articles. We prioritized a reproducible, database-driven approach supplemented by targeted manual review.

This study highlights that CTS education articles remain few and narrowly focused despite evolving training dynamics. More scholarly work is needed from CT surgeons and associations to address gaps, strengthen curricula, and improve training quality to meet the evolving needs of future surgeons and their patients.

Conclusions

This 11-year analysis of CTS publications revealed that education-related articles consistently remained a small fraction of total output. By highlighting this persistent gap, this study aims to motivate broader research across educational domains and reinforce the importance of evaluating training models to ensure that future surgeons are prepared for both routine and complex care. These statistical comparisons were exploratory, designed to contextualize publication trends rather than to imply definitive associations.

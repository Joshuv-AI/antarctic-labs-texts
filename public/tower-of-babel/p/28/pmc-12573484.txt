Oxandrolone treatment prevents muscle atrophy and retraction after rotator cuff injury: an experimental study in rats
Authors: Polychronis Papadopoulos, Cangyu Wang, Felix H Savoie, Zongbing You, Dongxia Ge
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundChronic rotator cuff (RC) tear leads to muscle atrophy, fatty infiltration, and intercellular fibrosis of the torn muscle-tendon unit. Prevention of muscle degeneration appears desirable to allow further treatment options to be successful. Pharmacologic prevention of muscle changes incurred by chronic retraction has not been extensively investigated. This study aimed to determine the effect of preoperative oxandrolone treatment on preventing rotator muscle atrophy and retraction after RC tears and promoting RC healing after repair surgery.MethodsOld male CD Sprague Dawley IGS rats (1-1.5 years old) underwent a procedure where the tendons of the supraspinatus and infraspinatus were severed to simulate RC tears; 2 weeks after RC cut, animals received oral placebo or oxandrolone treatment for 2 weeks; 4 weeks after RC cut, RC was repaired by suturing the cut tendons to the humerus bone; after RC repair, the animals were followed up for 6 weeks. Changes in the supraspinatus and infraspinatus were captured by microcomputed tomography, followed by histologic analysis of myofiber size, fatty infiltration, and fibrosis.ResultsThere was no statistical difference between the placebo and oxandrolone groups in body weight, wet weight of muscle, muscle volumes, and fatty infiltration. The fibrosis fraction (%) of the supraspinatus belly was less in the oxandrolone treatment group and the healthy-control oxandrolone treatment group than in the placebo treatment group and the healthy-control placebo treatment group (P = .016 or .030, respectively). The fibrosis fraction (%) of the supraspinatus origin was less in the healthy-control oxandrolone treatment group than in the healthy-control placebo treatment group (P = .016). The fibrosis fraction (%) of the infraspinatus origin was less in the oxandrolone treatment group than in the placebo treatment group (P = .017). The mean cross-sectional areas of the muscle fiber of supraspinatus and infraspinatus were larger in the oxandrolone treatment group in both the surgery shoulder (P < .0001 or P < .001, respectively) and the healthy shoulder (P < .001 or P < .0001, respectively) than in the placebo treatment group in both the surgery shoulder and the healthy shoulder. There was no statistical difference in muscle retraction in the oxandrolone treatment group before and after treatment (P = .098), but muscle retraction increased in the placebo treatment group (P = .005).ConclusionTwo-week oxandrolone treatment prevents muscle retraction and muscle atrophy by reducing fibrosis and increasing muscle fiber cross-sectional area after RC tears.Keywords: Rotator cuff, Fatty infiltration, Fibrosis, Muscle atrophy, Anabolic steroids, Rat

Rotator cuff (RC) tear (RCT) is one of the most common causes of shoulder pain and disability.30 A patient may not always be aware of the onset of a RCT. Even if such an event is known, surgery may not always be clinically necessary or practical at the time of the injury. Chronic RCT leads to muscle atrophy, fatty infiltration, and intercellular fibrosis of the torn muscle-tendon unit.13,17,18 Prevention of muscle degeneration appears desirable to allow further treatment options to be successful. Pharmacologic prevention of muscle changes incurred by chronic retraction has not been extensively investigated. Several pharmacological substances influence muscle development, growth, and repair mechanisms, namely, anabolic steroids, growth hormones, β-2 agonists, heparan sulfate, xanthine derivatives, creatine, and vitamin D.16 Among these substances, anabolic steroids seem best suited for clinical application. Anabolic steroids enhance the synthesis of muscle proteins,7 induce hypertrophy of both type-I and II muscle fibers,27 and impact satellite cell replication and activation28 as well as contractile strength.1 Nandrolone decanoate administration in the phase after tendon release prevented fatty infiltration of the supraspinatus muscle and reduced functional muscle impairment caused by myotendinous retraction in a rabbit RC model.9 In the United States, oxandrolone is categorized as a Schedule III controlled substance approved by the Food and Drug Administration for treating bone pain associated with osteoporosis, aiding weight or muscle gain following surgery or physical trauma, during chronic infection, or in the context of unexplained weight loss, and counteracting the catabolic effect of long-term corticosteroid therapy. A human clinical trial, Oxandrolone Rotator Cuff Trial (ORCT, NCT03091075), is ongoing to test the postoperative use of oral oxandrolone in RC repair (RCR) healing. The outcomes of this trial will likely be known later in 2024. In a recent study,10 the application of an anabolic steroid or insulin-like growth factor in sheep with RCTs demonstrated that neither an anabolic steroid nor insulin-like growth factor contributes to the regeneration of the muscle once degenerative changes are established. Preoperative oxandrolone use in RCR has not been investigated. Therefore, we propose to test a hypothesis that preoperative oxandrolone treatment prevents rotator muscle atrophy after RCTs and promotes healing after RCR surgery in old male rats because male patients aged 45-70 are more frequently treated in the clinics.32

Materials and methods

Retired male breeder rats (1-1.5 years of age, CD Sprague Dawley IGS Rat; strain code 001) were acquired from Charles River Laboratories (Wilmington, MA, USA). Old male rats were used in this study because male patients aged 45-70 are more frequently treated in the clinics. The rats had an average body weight of 379.1 ± 15.7 g. The rats' approximate human ages were about 45 years.25 Twenty male rats were assigned to the standard housing condition, in which they were pair-housed in clear plastic cages (42 × 22 × 20 cm) lined with wood-based bedding. All animals had free access to food and water and were maintained on a 12:12-h light: dark cycle and a room temperature range of 23 ± 2°C. Animals were observed daily for their activities, as well as food/water intake, and weighed weekly. Estimation of animal number is based on a power analysis for sample size calculation, assuming a standardized design treatment effect size of at least 1.20 (ie, the difference between the control and treatment groups is at least 1.10 units of standard deviation) and with a one-sided test, a sample size of n = 10 animals per group has a statistical power of 80% to detect the difference between groups at a significance level of α = 0.05. To mimic the in vivo response of RC muscles to oxandrolone treatment, rats were used because their RC muscles are large enough to perform RCR.29 All animal studies (Protocol#1804) were approved by the Animal Care and Use Committee of Tulane University, which complied with the US Department of Health and Human Services Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and the regulations of the Department of Agriculture.

Surgical technique and experimental protocol

All 20 rats received a RC cut on the right shoulder on the same day. Two weeks before RC cut surgery, all animals were scanned using a Quantum FX microcomputed tomography (micro-CT) system (PerkinElmer, Hopkinton, MA, USA) after 7-10 days of acclimatization in the animal facility. Both shoulders were scanned, and 3-dimensional images were generated to quantify the total volumes of the supraspinatus and infraspinatus muscles on both shoulders. Animals were ranked based on the total volume of the supraspinatus and infraspinatus muscles (hereafter referred to as the total muscle volume) on the right shoulder (planned for RC cut) from the highest volume to the lowest volume. Animals were paired based on the absolute difference between their total muscle volumes. Each block consisted of 2 animals with the most similar muscle volumes. Within each block, a simple randomization method was used by flipping a coin to assign one animal to the control group and another to the experimental group. This randomization method ensured that each group consisted of 10 animals and that the total muscle volumes of the RC cut shoulders in both groups did not differ significantly, as confirmed by an analysis of variance statistical analysis.

Surgical protocols and oxandrolone treatment

Rats were anesthetized with isoflurane inhalation (4%-5% for induction and 1%-2% for maintenance at an O2 flow rate of 300-600 ml/min). Rats were laid in a prone position on a warm pad at about 37°C with both eyes treated with ophthalmic ointment. Buprenorphine was given subcutaneously at a dose of 0.05 mg/kg body weight at the time of surgery, and another dose was given 6-8 hours after surgery. During RCT surgery, a 2-cm skin incision was made over the craniolateral aspect of the right scapulohumeral joint (the left shoulder was not operated on and served as a healthy control). The deltoid muscle was detached sharply from the posterior, lateral, and anterior acromion and split distally for 1-1.5 cm. The site of the supraspinatus and infraspinatus tendon insertion was located. The muscle insertions were located using a periodontal curette, and the muscles were grasped with a double-armed 5-0 proline suture using the Mason-Allen technique. After grasping the muscles with the sutures, a cut was made below the insertion of the muscles. After confirming both muscles were cut, the ends of the sutures were cut, and about 4 cm of sutures were kept with both ends to be used in the secondary RCR surgery. The overlying detached deltoid muscle was sutured back to the acromion and trapezius muscle with a 4-0 Vicryl suture (Ethicon, Raritan, NJ, USA). The skin was closed using an intradermal suture with a 4-0 Vicryl suture.

The RCR surgical procedures were modified from the previously published procedures.29 Anesthesia and medication were applied in the same way as in RCT surgery. The surgical approach was the same. The incision was done on top of the previous one. The nonabsorbable prolene sutures were found on the deltoid muscle. The deltoid was split with extreme caution, ensuring that the sutures were not cut. While pulling the sutures, we confirmed that the muscles were cut and free to move. No spontaneous healing or attachment was observed. With an 18-gauge needle, we pierced the greater tubercle at the tendon insertion, and with a free needle, we passed the proline sutures that were left at the time of the previous RCT surgery. Confirming that the tendons were sutured at the footprint, we reattached the deltoid muscle and closed the skin wound with 4-0 Vicryl sutures.

Ten mg of oxandrolone (Millipore Sigma, Saint Louis, MO, USA, cat# SML0437) was dissolved in 0.5 mL of dimethyl sulfoxide12 to prepare a 20 mg/mL stock solution. A 0.5 mL stock solution was diluted with 199.5 mL ultrapure water to make a 0.05 mg/mL working solution. For the placebo solution, 0.5 ml of dimethyl sulfoxide was diluted in 199.5 ml of ultrapure water.

Two weeks after RCT surgery, the experimental group received oxandrolone orally by gavage at a dose of 0.1 mg/kg/d, equivalent to a standard dose of 20 mg/d used in men.5 The control group received a placebo solution by gavage. Two weeks after oxandrolone or placebo treatment, micro-CT scanning was repeated to quantify the total muscle volumes of the supraspinatus and infraspinatus on both shoulders. At this time (4 weeks after RCT surgery), all animals received RCR surgery as described above. Three and 6 weeks after RCR surgery, micro-CT scanning was repeated to quantify the total muscle volumes of the supraspinatus and infraspinatus on both shoulders. In total, micro-CT scanning was conducted 5 times: 1) before RCT surgery (baseline level), 2) two weeks after RCT surgery/before oxandrolone/placebo treatment (2 weeks post-RCT), 3) two weeks after oxandrolone/placebo treatment/before RCR (4 weeks post-RCT), 4) three weeks after RCR, and 5) six weeks after RCR/immediately before endpoint euthanasia. The endpoint is 6 weeks after RCR surgery, immediately after the final micro-CT scanning. Comparisons were made among the normal (healthy) shoulder, the control shoulder (RCT/RCR with placebo treatment), and the experimental shoulder (RCT/RCR with oxandrolone treatment). Micro-CT assessment of muscle volume and muscle retraction changes

Rats were anesthetized with isoflurane inhalation (4%-5% for induction and 1%-2% for maintenance at an O2 flow rate of 300-600 mL/min) and placed in the prone position in the micro-CT scanner. Micro-CT scanning was performed according to the established protocols4,15,21 using the Quantum FX micro-CT system (PerkinElmer, Hopkinton, MA, USA). Briefly, the field of view was 72 mm with a voxel size of 144 μm; the scan mode was high-resolution, taking 4 minutes. The voltage was 90 kV, with a current of 88 μA and a radiation dose of 560 mGy. The micro-CT data were analyzed using the program Analyze V.14 provided by the manufacturer of the micro-CT system. The supraspinatus and infraspinatus muscle volumes were measured in a blinded manner 5 times as planned. Muscle retraction was measured 2 weeks after the RCT surgery and 2 weeks after the placebo/oxandrolone treatment. In brief, the distance between the muscle cutting end and the muscle attachment point on the bone was measured using micro-CT with the program Analyze V.14 (AnalyzeDirect, Overland Park, KS, USA) provided by the manufacturer of the micro-CT system.

Muscle harvesting and wet muscle weight

All rats were sacrificed with carbon dioxide inhalation, followed by chest opening with scissors to ensure death at 6 weeks after the RCR surgery. To assess muscle atrophy, wet weights of bilateral supraspinatus and infraspinatus muscles were measured immediately after harvesting.

Necropsy of animals

Necropsy of the euthanized rats was performed by the researchers. The supraspinatus and infraspinatus muscles of both shoulders and major organs (brain, heart, lungs, liver, kidneys, spleen, and intestines) were examined visually.

Histological assessment

The supraspinatus and infraspinatus muscles of both shoulders were harvested by dissection and cutting at the tendon-bony junction. The wet weight of the muscles was measured. Three segments of each muscle were used for histological examination: a segment from the middle point (belly), a segment one-third from the proximal origin (origin), and a segment one-third from the distal insertion (insertion). The muscle segments were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and embedded in the Optimal Cutting Temperature blocks in such a way that cross-sections of the muscle fibers (perpendicular to the long axis of muscle fibers) were cut into 10-μm tissue sections with a Cryostat Microtome (CM 1500; Leica, Nussloch, Germany) for staining and examination. Muscle sections were stained with Masson's trichrome staining (Richard-Allan Scientific LLC, Kalamazoo, MI, USA) and Oil Red O staining (Newcomer Supply, Inc., Middleton, Wisconsin) according to the manufacturer's instructions. All photomicrographs were captured using the EVOS FL Auto Imaging System (software EVOS; Life Technologies, Carlsbad, CA, USA), which was equipped with ×4, ×10, ×20, and ×40 objective lenses. Pictures were analyzed using image analysis software (ImageJ; National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA). All photos were assessed by 2 reviewers in a blinded manner. The fraction of fibrosis was calculated as the aniline blue-stained collagenous fibrotic area divided by the entire sample area. Similarly, the fraction of fatty infiltration was assessed as the Oil Red O-stained area divided by the whole sample area. Muscle fiber areas were measured in the cross-sectional muscle fibers of the belly part with Masson's trichrome staining. The cross-sectional areas of 10 random single muscle fibers were measured for each section. Myofibers with unclear boundaries were excluded to eliminate the possibility of measuring multiple myofibers.

Statistical analysis

Prism 10 software (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) was used for statistical analyses. The data are reported as mean ± standard deviation or as specified. Intergroup comparisons were performed using a two-sided Student's t-test. Multigroup comparisons were performed using one-way analysis of variance to compare muscle volume when grouping the rats, with two-sided tests unless otherwise noted. Statistical significance was determined as P < .05.

Histological examination results

Masson's trichrome staining can distinguish collagen fibers from muscle fibers: collagen fibers are stained blue, nuclei are stained dark brown, and muscle fibers are stained red. Based on Masson's trichrome staining, we calculated the fraction of fibrosis (%) as shown in Figure 5 and Tables V and VI. After the oxandrolone treatment, the fibrosis fraction (%) of the supraspinatus belly was lower in both the surgery shoulder and healthy shoulder compared to the placebo treatment. The supraspinatus origin's fibrosis fraction (%) was less in the oxandrolone-treated healthy shoulder compared to the placebo-treated healthy shoulder. Oxandrolone treatment decreased the fibrosis fraction (%) of the infraspinatus origin in the surgery shoulder. Mean muscle fiber areas were increased in the oxandrolone treatment group in both the surgery shoulder and the healthy control shoulder (Fig. 6 and Table VII). Based on Oil Red O staining, the fraction of fatty infiltration (%) was calculated and shown in Figure 7 and Tables VIII and IX. There was no statistical difference between the placebo group and the oxandrolone treatment group in the fraction of fatty infiltration in either supraspinatus or infraspinatus.

Discussion

The present study aims to investigate whether oxandrolone treatment can prevent rotator muscle atrophy and retraction and then promote healing after RCR surgery. We found that a 2-week preoperative oxandrolone treatment had no effect on animal body weight, muscle weight, muscle volume, or fatty infiltration in the supraspinatus and infraspinatus muscles. However, it increased the cross-sectional area of muscle fibers and inhibited muscle retraction and fibrosis.

In the United States, oxandrolone is the only anabolic androgenic steroid that is approved for restitution of weight loss after severe trauma, major surgery, or infections, malnutrition due to alcoholic cirrhosis, and Duchenne's or Becker's muscular dystrophy.20 The therapeutic use of oxandrolone has received limited investigation in the field of orthopedic surgery. In the last 2 decades, preclinical and clinical research has started to identify promising applications of oxandrolone in the perioperative period. Preoperative oxandrolone use in RCR has not been investigated. Patients with RCTs are often associated with fat accumulation in the injured muscle and muscle atrophy and subsequently suffer from a high retear rate after RCR.3,34 The development of new treatments to reverse these degenerative changes may reduce the retear rate and obviate the need for ultimate shoulder replacement surgery. Therefore, we studied whether preoperative oxandrolone treatment can prevent rotator muscle atrophy, fibrosis, fatty infiltration, and retraction after RCTs.

Previous studies have shown that oxandrolone increased lean body mass in severely burned children23 and young men.26 There was a study27 showing that testosterone-induced skeletal muscle hypertrophy in healthy older men (60∼75 years old) after 20 weeks of treatment. In our rat RCT model, oxandrolone treatment did not increase the animal body weight, supraspinatus/infraspinatus muscle weight, or supraspinatus/infraspinatus volume. The reasons might be that we used retired breeder rats (1-1.5 years of age) who were not young, and the rats were treated with oxandrolone for 2 weeks. The rats' old age and short-term treatment may not be able to cause dramatic changes in body weight, muscle weight, or muscle volume.

We quantified the muscle fibrosis and fatty infiltration using Masson's trichrome staining and Oil Red O staining. Oxandrolone treatment decreased the fibrosis fraction (%) of the supraspinatus/infraspinatus belly and origin. In the insertion segments of the muscles, there was a trend that the muscle fibrosis was less in the oxandrolone treatment group, but there was no statistical significance, which might be caused by the large intergroup variations as indicated by the error bars. There was relatively higher collagen content in the muscle insertion than in the belly and origin segments. The sectioning planes were different and might cause intergroup variations. Unlike the insertion segment, the belly and origin segments were primarily composed of muscle fibers with fewer collagen fibers, and the tissue components were more homogeneous, resulting in more consistent results. Both muscle atrophy and fatty infiltration are independent predictors of unfavorable outcomes after RCR11,19 We found that oxandrolone treatment increased the cross-sectional areas of muscle fibers, suggesting that oxandrolone prevents muscle atrophy. Short-term oxandrolone administration may stimulate skeletal muscle protein synthesis and improve intracellular reutilization of amino acids. The mechanism for this stimulation may be related to an oxandrolone-induced increase in androgen receptor expression in skeletal muscle.5,6,26 However, oxandrolone treatment did not affect the fatty infiltration, which is perhaps caused by the short-term nature of the treatment.

Irreparable RCTs are common conditions seen by shoulder surgeons, characterized by a torn and retracted tendon associated with muscle atrophy and impaired mobility.31 Direct fixation of the torn tendon is not possible due to the retracted tendon and lack of healing potential, which results in poor outcomes. Treatment options for irreparable RCTs are numerous and growing, but no treatment has been deemed superior.14 Our study showed that there was no dramatic increase in muscle retraction in the oxandrolone treatment group (P = .098), while in the placebo treatment group, the muscle retraction was increased (P = .005). This result suggests that the muscle retraction was prevented by oxandrolone treatment. We also found that oxandrolone treatment reduced fibrosis, which may contribute to the inhibition of muscle retraction. These findings suggest that administering oxandrolone before RCR surgery may facilitate the fixation of the torn tendon by preventing muscle retraction.

There are some limitations in this study. First, it was a pilot study with a limited number of animals, which may explain why the statistical analysis of some datasets was underpowered. Second, we only studied male rats due to budget constraints. Since there may be some sex differences in the responses to oxandrolone treatment, we will include female animals in future studies. Third, the oxandrolone treatment timeframe was arbitrarily chosen, which might not be optimal. In our future studies, we will compare different treatment periods, investigate more sensitive markers of muscle atrophy, such as Atrogin-1 and MuRF1, which have been shown to be upregulated prior to the onset of atrophy,2,8,22 and examine the signaling pathways involved in muscle atrophy.24,33

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that a 2-week oxandrolone treatment did not alter body weight, wet weight of the supraspinatus/infraspinatus muscles, supraspinatus/infraspinatus volume, or fatty infiltration. However, oxandrolone treatment prevented muscle atrophy, fibrosis, and retraction, which may facilitate the RCR surgery and reduce the possibility of irreparable RCTs.

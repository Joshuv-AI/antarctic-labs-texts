A nationwide register study on medicines exiting the reimbursement system in 2010–2022
Authors: Leeni Minkkinen, Hanna Koskinen, Kati Sarnola, Heini Kari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNational medicines reimbursement systems play a key role in supporting access to medicines. Although inclusion in these systems is important for patients to obtain necessary treatments at affordable costs, medicines exit reimbursement each year. However, research on the later stages of the medicine lifecycle, including reimbursement discontinuation, remains limited. Therefore, we evaluated medicines exiting the national reimbursement system in Finland.MethodsData on all market-authorized medicinal products that exited the reimbursement system for at least one full calendar year between 2010 and 2022 were retrieved from a nationwide database maintained by the Social Insurance Institution of Finland (SII). Reimbursement discontinuation was validated using data from the Pharmaceuticals Pricing Board and the SII’s Pharmaceutical Price Register. The study was conducted at the level of active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), focusing on whether the entire API exited the reimbursement system. Therapeutic categories, age, price levels, criticality, and market availability after reimbursement discontinuation were examined. Prices per treatment day were calculated using Defined Daily Doses where available. APIs were categorized into short- and long-term use based on expert assessment supported by literature. Descriptive analyses were conducted.ResultsA total of 150 APIs exited the reimbursement system. The most common therapeutic categories were alimentary tract and metabolism (15%), cardiovascular system (15%), and nervous system (11%). Of all identified APIs, 49% were intended for short-term use 51% for long-term use. Overall, 6% of the APIs, including phenoxymethylpenicillin, were listed as critical medicines by the EU. After reimbursement discontinuation, 62% of the APIs remained on the market without reimbursement, whereas 38% were immediately withdrawn. APIs remaining on the market were older than those withdrawn (median age 31 vs. 28 years) and had a lower median price per treatment day (€0.90 vs. €1.50).ConclusionsAPIs, even some classified as critical in the EU, exit the national reimbursement system each year. APIs that remain on the market tend to be older and less expensive than those leaving the market. These findings highlight the importance of monitoring changes in the range of reimbursable medicines and their potential implications for availability and affordability.Keywords: Reimbursement status, Discontinuation of reimbursement, Medicine, Price, Market withdrawal

Background

The aim of the medicines reimbursement system is to enable patients’ access to necessary medicines at an affordable cost. The reimbursement status and price of a medicine can significantly influence patients’ treatment options as well as pharmaceutical expenditure [1]. In addition, reimbursement status may affect the number of issued and dispensed prescriptions [2–5]. The lifecycle stage of a pharmaceutical product may also influence its price and availability [6]. For new and often more expensive medicines, being included in the reimbursement system is essential for market entry and availability [7, 8]. In contrast, older medicines are typically priced lower, making them more affordable for patients [9].

Even though being included in the reimbursement system is essential for availability and patients’ access to outpatient medicines, several medicines exit the system every year [7, 8, 10]. The reasons for reimbursement discontinuation are diverse and may relate to actions by pharmaceutical companies or regulatory decisions.

Across countries, the processes and mechanisms underlying reimbursement exit may vary. In this context, company-driven mechanisms may include voluntary withdrawal from the reimbursement system, decisions not to apply for or renew reimbursement status, pricing strategies that do not align with payer requirements, and product discontinuation for commercial or strategic reasons [9, 11]. In addition to company-driven actions, reimbursement discontinuation may also result from policy decisions targeting specific medicine groups. For example, in France, mucolytics and expectorants were de-reimbursed, which led to significantly declined prescription rates for mucolytics and a concurrent increase in the prescription rates for antitussives and bronchodilators [2]. In the Netherlands, the de-reimbursement of benzodiazepines has been examined in several studies [3, 4, 12], showing reductions in dispensed prescriptions and doses [4]. However, previous studies have primarily focused on specific medicine groups, and information on which medicines exit the system more broadly, and at what stage of their life cycle, remains limited.

In Finland, new medicines applying for reimbursement status and a reasonable wholesale price undergo health technology assessment under the responsibility of the Pharmaceuticals Pricing Board (PPB) [13]. In contrast, there is no systematic mechanism for reassessing older medicines or evaluating their continued role within the system. Consequently, there is little structured information on the later stages of the medicine lifecycle, including factors related to potential reimbursement discontinuation or market exit. This highlights the importance of studying the later stages of the medicine lifecycle, including which medicines exit the reimbursement system, at what stage, and with what characteristics.

The aim of this study is to evaluate the medicines exiting the reimbursement system (reimbursement discontinuation) over a 13-year period (2010–2022) using national register data. Furthermore, therapeutic areas, the age of medicines, price levels, and the criticality of the medicines exiting the reimbursement system are examined, as these reflect relevant aspects of medicines’ lifecycle and market context. The aim is also to examine which medicines will be completely withdrawn from the market and which will remain on the market without reimbursement, as these different pathways may have distinct implications for patients’ access to and continuity of pharmacotherapy.

Methods

Setting

In Finland, all permanent residents (approximately 5.6 million) are entitled to reimbursements under the National Health Insurance (NHI) for listed outpatient medicines prescribed for the treatment of an illness [14]. The list of reimbursed products is administrated by the Pharmaceuticals Pricing Board (PPB), which operates under the Ministry of Social Affairs and Health [13]. The PPB is responsible for determining which medicinal products are included in the reimbursement scheme, and these decisions are always made at the level of individual products and not, for example, at the level of an active pharmaceutical ingredient (API). In this context, medicinal products applying for reimbursement status and a reasonable wholesale price are subject to a health technology assessment assessing their therapeutic value and cost-effectiveness. The PPB is also responsible for setting appropriate reimbursement categories and reasonable wholesale prices for the reimbursed products. These decisions are made based on the marketing authorisation holder’s application for reimbursement status and wholesale price. If the reimbursement status has not been applied for, or the PPB has not affirmed it, the marketing authorisation holders can set the price themselves. However, in Finland, prices of prescription medicines are the same throughout the country.

All prescriptions issued for outpatient setting and dispensations made by community pharmacies are recorded in the nationwide Prescription Centre [15]. Reimbursed dispensations are registered in the Dispensations reimbursable under the NHI register [16]. Of all outpatient prescription medicine purchases made in 2022, approximately 80% were reimbursed [17]. Over 60 million medicinal purchases were reimbursed under the NHI scheme in 2022, and the paid reimbursements amounted to approximately €1,800 million [18].

A medicine’s reimbursement status can be discontinued in various ways. The reimbursement status for a new medicine is usually set for a specific time span, and the reimbursement will not continue if an extension is not applied or granted. The marketing authorisation holder can also withdraw their product from the reimbursement system by submitting a notification to the PPB at least three months prior to the desired date of discontinuation. In case of market withdrawal or shortage, a product is not automatically removed from the list of reimbursable products, even though no purchases are made at the pharmacies, as the product is not available. In specific cases, the PPB can also end the reimbursement of a product. However, there is no comprehensive public list of the discontinued reimbursements.

Data and statistical analysis

Data on all market-authorised medicinal products that exited the reimbursement system for at least one full calendar year between 1.1.2010 and 31.12.2022 were retrieved from a nationwide database maintained by the Social Insurance Institution of Finland (SII). The data were classified at the levels of API and therapeutic group using the WHO Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) classification system [19]. A full calendar year without reimbursed purchases for an API was set as the intake criterion for discontinued reimbursement. The discontinuation of the reimbursement status was then validated by cross-referencing the results with publicly available records of the PPB [20], and the SII’s Pharmaceutical Price Register, which contains historical data not publicly available, ensuring that the identified cases corresponded to actual reimbursement discontinuations. Possible ATC code alterations were reviewed and duplicates were excluded based on the overview by WHO Collaborating Centre for Drug Statistics [21]. In addition, basic ointments, shortages, and special permit products were excluded from the final dataset. Basic ointments were excluded because they are not considered medicinal products, as they do not contain an active pharmaceutical ingredient, even though they may be reimbursed in the treatment of certain conditions. For the reference to the number of APIs that exited the system, the statistics of APIs included in the reimbursement in 2022 system were collected [22].

For the identified and validated APIs, the Defined Daily Doses (DDDs) [23] were collected as well as median and average prices at the beginning of each month from the database maintained by the SII. When available, the DDDs were used as a proxy for a daily dosage, from which the cost of one treatment day was calculated. APIs without an assigned DDD value were excluded from these calculations but included in other analyses. The data were completed using publicly available data sources containing information on the date of first marketing authorisation [24], and inclusion in the EU list of critical medicines [25]. The age of an API was calculated based on the first marketing authorisation in Finland and the time the API exited the reimbursement system. The last month of reimbursement was set as the reference point for price comparison. The price range was set by calculating the medicine’s cost for 30 days. Based on the prices six different price categories were established.

The APIs were also categorised for short-term and long-term medications by two researchers (HeK and LM). The classification of APIs into short-term and long-term medications was first performed independently by the researchers. After the independent review, the researchers held a consensus discussion to resolve any discrepancies and ensure consistency in the classification. The classification was based on the researchers’ expertise in pharmacotherapy (PhD Pharm and BSc Pharm), and the researchers also drew on relevant literature as well as summaries of product characteristics (SmPCs) to support the assessment.

Descriptive analyses were conducted using R (version 4.1.3) and Microsoft Excel (version 2108).

Results

Characteristics of the APIs exiting the reimbursement system

Between 2010 and 2022, a total of 150 APIs exited the Finnish reimbursement system (Table 1). The yearly number of APIs exiting the system varied from 4 in 2022 to 26 in 2013. Among the APIs identified in this study, the most common therapeutic areas were alimentary tract and metabolism (15.3%), cardiovascular system (15.3%), and nervous system (10.7%). Of all the APIs that exited the national reimbursement system, 49% were for short-term use, while 51% were primarily for long-term use.

After exiting the reimbursement system, 62% of the APIs remained on the national market as non-reimbursable medicines, while 38% exited the market entirely and immediately. More than half (58%) of the APIs that left the market immediately after the change in the reimbursement status were intended for long-term use. In the group of APIs that remained on the market, however, the share of long-term medications was 46%. On average, APIs that stayed on the market were older (median age 31 years) than those leaving the market immediately (median age 28 years).

The three top API groups from which medicines exited the reimbursement system, alimentary tract, cardiovascular system, and nervous system, were also among the four most used therapeutic areas in the national reimbursement system in 2022 (Fig. 1). While antineoplastic and immunomodulating agents was the biggest group in terms on individual reimbursed APIs in 2022 (n = 134), it was only the sixth largest (9.3%) among groups with APIs exiting the reimbursement system. In most API groups, a majority of the APIs exiting the system remained on the national market. However, in three API groups (antineoplastic and immunomodulating agents, alimentary tract and metabolism, and blood forming organs) the APIs most often exited the market immediately after the change in their reimbursement status. In the other ten API groups, a majority of APIs stayed on the market immediately after the change.

Therapeutic areas and marketing status of active pharmaceutical ingredients exiting the reimbursement system 2010–2022

Of all APIs identified, 20 were combination products, i.e. medicinal products containing more than one active ingredient. Some of these APIs were part of the reimbursement system only for a few years during the study period. These included, for example, combination products such as lansoprazole, tetracycline and metronidazole, and metformin and rosiglitazone.

Of all the APIs exiting the reimbursement scheme, 6.0% (n = 9) were included in the EU list of critical medicines (Table 2). Within this six per cent, seven different therapeutic groups were represented. The group of anti-infectives for systemic use (J) was the only therapeutic group with more than one API (n = 3) included in the EU list of critical medicines as well as being found to exit the national reimbursement system. Most of these APIs (8 out of 9) also stayed on the national market after the change in their reimbursement statuses. The APIs that stayed on the market included all three APIs in group J, including phenoxymethylpenicillin (J01CE02), which is a commonly used narrow-spectrum antibiotic.

Affordability of the APIs exiting and APIs staying on the market after the change in their reimbursement status

APIs that stayed on the market after exiting the reimbursement system were found to be more affordable, on average, than those that left the market, with median prices of €0.90 and €1.50 per treatment day during the last month of reimbursement, respectively (Table 1). These results are based on APIs with an assigned Defined Daily Dose (DDD) (n = 120). Among the APIs that stayed on the market, there was also a relatively higher number of APIs with a lower price level (55.2% of the APIs less than €30 for 30 days) compared to those that exited the market immediately (35.8%) when the cost per DDD of the last reimbursed month was examined in both groups.

APIs in both groups had a similar price development prior to the change in the reimbursement status (Fig. 2). Compared to the last 24 months before the change in the reimbursement status, the price level was lower near the change in both groups. Neither group included APIs with a higher price during the last month before the change in the reimbursement status.

Prices of the last reimbursed month compared to other months during the API’s life cycle

After the change in the reimbursement status, the prices of the APIs that stayed on the market generally rose, the median cost per treatment day being €0.90 prior to the change and €0.92 in the first month after exiting the reimbursement scheme (Fig. 2). In this group of APIs, the number of APIs was observed to decrease over time. When 24 months had passed after the intervention month, only 57.6% of the APIs that had stayed on the market were still available.

Discussion

Many of the previous studies on medicines exiting national reimbursement systems have been conducted following regulatory or policy decisions related to the reimbursement schemes in different countries [2–4, 12]. The focus of these studies has been, for example, on the changes in the prescription rates, dispensed prescriptions, and doses of specific APIs that have been removed from the reimbursement system [2, 4]. This study, however, focused on a complete selection of APIs that exited the national reimbursement system. It was found that 150 APIs exited the Finnish reimbursement system over the 13-year period between 2010 and 2022. For comparison, 734 APIs were included in the national reimbursement system in 2022 [22]. Of the 150 APIs, approximately three fifths stayed on the market as non-reimbursable medications, and approximately two fifths left the national market immediately. This result produces new information in the field of reimbursement systems, as it differs from previous studies where the studied APIs always stayed on the market even after the change in their reimbursement statuses [2–4, 12].

When examining the number of APIs in each group, the largest groups of APIs exiting the reimbursement system correspond reasonably well with the largest groups of APIs included in the system in Finland [22]. In 2022, antineoplastic and immunomodulating agents (L), nervous system (N), alimentary tract (A), and cardiovascular system (C), in this order, were the four largest groups of APIs being reimbursed in Finland. Of these API groups, the latter three were the largest also in terms of the number APIs leaving the system. However, the consumption of the three API groups (A, C and N) was quite large in comparison to group L [18].

On average, APIs exiting the reimbursement system are older than those in the system. In our study, at the end of 2022, the median age of APIs exiting the reimbursement system was 30 years, while the median age of APIs in the reimbursed medicine range was 23 years (average age 25 years), based on unpublished data from the SII. Given the age distribution, many of the APIs examined in this study are likely in the off‑patent phase, which may influence their market dynamics [11]. We also found that some APIs were included in the reimbursement system only for a few years during the study period. These APIs included, for example, combination products such as lansoprazole, tetracycline and metronidazole and metformin and rosiglitazone. While combination products have been associated with improved treatment adherence [26], their relatively short reimbursement duration may reflect multiple factors, including safety concerns, regulatory actions, or market-related considerations [11].

Supply shortages and availability of medicines have been a huge concern during recent years [27–29]. This has led to the development of the EU list of critical medicines to avoid shortages of these products [25]. In this study, it was found that most of the APIs that exited the Finnish reimbursement system were not part of the EU list, which includes just over 200 APIs. However, our analysis showed that 9 of the 150 APIs that exited the national reimbursement system were also part of the EU list. Although the APIs on the EU list are listed as critical, one of the nine APIs (methylergometrine (G02AB01)) also exited the Finnish market immediately after exiting the national reimbursement system. On the other hand, one of the nine APIs was phenoxymethylpenicillin (J01CE02), which has been the third most commonly prescribed and used outpatient antibiotic medication in Finland in recent years [30, 31].

The prices and price development of the APIs were also studied and it was found that the APIs that stayed on the market were, on average, lower in price than those that exited the market immediately after reimbursement discontinuation. According to Pousi (2023), more expensive medicines appear to leave the market more easily, even though affordability, and thereby availability, are crucial for patients’ engagement to outpatient care medicines [10]. Conversely, prices of medicines that remained on the market showed increasing prices following the change in their reimbursement status. For example, Stolk et al. (2008) [32] found that the termination of reimbursement and an increase in the prices may lead to medication discontinuation and switching to less expensive medication options if such are available. The discontinuation and switching patterns after a change in the reimbursement status have been discovered in other studies as well [2, 3, 5, 33].

The price of a medicine is also important from the perspective of its overall availability in a country. In 2013, the Finnish reimbursement system underwent a minor reform in which the prices of medicines included in the reimbursement system were reduced by 5% [34]. This reform coincided temporally with changes observed in exits from the reimbursement system, although present study does not allow causal interpretations. Even though several medicinal products, and therefore many APIs, exited the reimbursement system in 2013, only 5 of the 26 APIs exiting the system also exited the market.

One of the core strengths of our study is the extensive nationwide database maintained by the SII, which enabled the identification of the APIs that exited the reimbursement system during the study period from 2010 to 2022. The study provides new insights into the life cycle of medicinal products, particularly its later stages, which have been less studied. Previous research has, among other things, focused on market entry and post-patent competition [35, 36], whereas less is known about reimbursement discontinuation and market exit. Although our analyses are descriptive, the findings improve understanding of patterns related to reimbursement discontinuation and medicine availability after discontinuation, and can thereby inform the development of reimbursement systems both internationally and nationally. However, this study also has some limitations. First, the study focused on changes in a single country. Nevertheless, to our knowledge, this study is one of the first ones to comprehensively describe reimbursement discontinuation at the level of all APIs within a national system over a long follow-up period. Second, the Finnish reimbursement system underwent some reforms during the study period. However, the reforms were minor, and they are a natural part of the development of reimbursement systems. Third, not all data were available for all APIs; for example, Defined Daily Dose (DDD) values were unavailable for some medicines, which may introduce some uncertainty when interpreting the findings at the overall level.

While the aim of the study was to map all APIs that exited the national reimbursement system, the study does not specify the reasons behind the change in the reimbursement status. Some of the APIs may have been withdrawn from the market due to safety issues, which would naturally lead to termination of reimbursement, while others may have been discontinued by pharmaceutical companies for commercial or strategic reasons [1, 11]. However, irrespective of the underlying reason, the reimbursement discontinuation may affect patients’ access to medicines through changes in out-of-pocket costs, as some patients may no longer be able to afford necessary treatments if reimbursement is withdrawn and costs increase. Previous research has shown that the cost of medicines may pose a financial burden to patients even in countries with universal health systems, suggesting that increases in out-of-pocket costs may reduce affordability for some patients [37, 38]. From a patient perspective, this may have important implications for access and, if the medicine is no longer available on the market, may further impact the continuity of pharmacotherapy, particularly when no alternative treatment options are available [39]. From a broader system perspective, understanding which APIs remain in use after reimbursement discontinuation and which are no longer available provides insight into how treatment options evolve within therapeutic areas. A more comprehensive evaluation would require examining how the overall landscape shifts when certain products exit, including the availability of substitute therapies, the balance of options across treatment categories, and the role of critical medicines as well as the availability of alternatives to them. In line with this, examining additional predictors such as generic competition and the impact of price competition on reimbursement discontinuation would be an important area for future research.

Importantly, the study provides novel descriptive information on the number, characteristics, and therapeutic groups of APIs exiting the reimbursement system, which has not previously been systematically available and may support the evaluation of reimbursement policies and the lifecycle of medicines within the system. The reasons behind the changes in the reimbursement statuses should be explored in future studies. Future research should also assess potential changes in patients’ purchasing behaviour after the change in the reimbursement status, especially because the prices appeared to increase after the change, and examine implications for treatment patterns and adherence.

Conclusions

Each year, APIs, including even some considered critical in the EU, exit the national reimbursement system. APIs that remain on the market after the end of reimbursement tend to be older and more affordable than those that leave the market immediately after the reimbursement ends. These findings underscore the importance of monitoring changes in the range of reimbursable medicines and their potential implications for availability and affordability.

Interested parties may however apply for permission to access the data from the Social Insurance Institution of Finland (Kela), https://www.kela.fi/web/en/data-permits-and-data-requests; email: tietoaineistot@kela.fi; and Findata, https://www.findata.fi/en/; email: info@findata.

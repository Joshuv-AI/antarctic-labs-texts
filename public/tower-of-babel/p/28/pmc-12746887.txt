The positioning of cephalon medullary nailing correlated with hidden blood loss during the perioperative period in patients with intertrochanteric fractures: A retrospective study
Authors: Yao Chen, Shaobo Zhang, Zhiqi Liu, Jiashan Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeHidden blood loss (HBL) during the perioperative period significantly impacts postoperative recovery and complications, yet it is frequently disregarded. This study aimed to investigate the effects of tip-apex distance (TAD) and calcar-referenced tip-apex distance (calTAD) on HBL in the treatment of intertrochanteric fractures utilizing proximal femoral nail antirotation (PFNA). The study also seeks to evaluate the possible decrease in HBL subsequent to PFNA treatment by optimizing nail positioning.MethodA historical cohort study was conducted from January 2020 to December 2022. Patients diagnosed with unilateral acute closed femoral intertrochanteric fracture and who underwent PFNA internal fixation surgery met the inclusion criteria, and were grouped according to the value of calTAD and TAD. The participants were divided into low TAD group (TAD<20 mm) and high TAD group (TAD≥20 mm); low calTAD group (calTAD<7.625 mm) and high calTAD group (calTAD≥7.625 mm), respectively. The primary outcome measures were intraoperative blood loss (including HBL, overt blood loss, and total blood loss). Continuous data were analyzed using an independent sample t-test or Mann-Whitney U test, and categorical data were analyzed using the Pearson Chi-square test. Univariate analysis was used to evaluate the association between various indicators and perioperative HBL. A stepwise multiple linear regression analysis model was used to determine the independent factors affecting perioperative HBL. A p value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.ResultsA total of 131 patients were initially included, of which 80 were assigned to the calTAD group (with 61 in the high calTAD group and 19 in the low calTAD group), and 80 were assigned to the TAD group (with 34 in the high TAD group and 46 in the low TAD group). The average HBL for the low TAD group was 772.85 mL, whereas for the high TAD group it was 919.68 mL (p>0.05). The average HBL for the low calTAD group was 611.42 mL, whereas for the high calTAD group it was 904.97 mL (p<0.05). Subsequent analysis revealed that the patient's height, preoperative hemoglobin levels, changes in hemoglobin and hematocrit levels from pre- to post-surgery, and calTAD are independent risk factors influencing HBL.ConclusionIn summary, our investigation revealed a significant correlation between the positioning of nails in PFNA and HBL during the perioperative period. By optimizing the placement of the cephalic nail, specifically by ensuring a calTAD of less than 7.625 mm, a significant decrease in HBL can be attained. Additionally, we identified that height, preoperative hemoglobin, differences in preoperative and postoperative hemoglobin and hematocrit, and the positioning of the cephalic nail were independent risk factors for HBL.Keywords: Intertrochanteric fracture, Proximal femoral nail antirotation, Hidden blood loss, Location of the cephalic nail, Calcar-referenced tip - apex distance

Introduction

Intertrochanteric fractures are prevalent injuries in the elderly population,1,2 and the trend is intensified by the rising population of elderly individuals.3,4 At present, proximal femoral nail antirotation (PFNA) is the favored technique for internal fixation in the management of intertrochanteric fractures. The issue of blood loss in these fractures is garnering increasing attention, with hidden blood loss (HBL) identified as a significant factor.5, 6, 7 Notably, PFNA surgery is significantly linked to substantial HBL.8

The study by Yu et al.9 indicates that the enlargement of the medullary cavity during intramedullary fixation may damage the medullary canal, resulting in increased intramedullary pressure and the dissemination of fat particles into the circulatory system. This process may lead to hemolysis and worsen anemia. The influence of the cephalic nail's position on blood loss has not been thoroughly examined. Moreover, the placement of PFNA nails is crucial in postoperative complications associated with intertrochanteric fractures.

Tip-apex distance (TAD) (Fig. 1) and calcar-referenced tip-apex distance (calTAD) (Fig. 2) are presently significant metrics utilized for evaluating nail positioning.10, 11, 12 The correlation between the nail placement and HBL following PFNA surgeries has been insufficiently documented. This study seeks to investigate the relationship between calTAD and HBL induced by PFNA, enhancing comprehension of this association.

Methods

The study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Huzhou Central Hospital with the number 202309009-01. The Medical Ethics Committee of Huzhou Central Hospital waived the need for informed consent in the study.

Participants

The retrospective study included patients with intertrochanteric fractures who underwent orthopedic surgery at Huzhou Central Hospital from January 2020 to December 2022. Surgical procedures were performed by 4 trauma surgeons or senior residents.

All patients received PFNA treatment within 3 days of admission and underwent clinical follow-up with pelvic anteroposterior (AP) and hip lateral radiographs within 1 week post-surgery. Surgery took place 4–10 days after injury. Electronic medical records of all patients were retrospectively reviewed, recording variables such as age, gender, height, weight, body mass index, preoperative and postoperative fibrinogen levels, D-dimer levels, blood glucose levels, hemoglobin level, hematocrit levels, intraoperative blood loss, and fracture classification.

Inclusion criteria and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria are: (1) patients diagnosed with unilateral acute closed intertrochanteric fracture; (2) provide the following information: height, weight, blood routine before and after 2 or 3 days; (3) closed reduction and PFNA fixation of fractures; (4) the amount of postoperative fluid should not exceed 2000 mL per day; (5) prothrombin time and activated partial thrombin time were within the normal range; (6) no serious liver or kidney disease, and no history of gastrointestinal bleeding.

The exclusion criteria are: (1) patients with pathological fracture; (2) lower limb arteriosclerosis and occlusion; (3) internal fixation failure; (4) patients with multiple injuries or fractures; (5) delayed diagnosis of intertrochanteric fractures exceeding 1 day.

Calculation of HBL TAD and calTAD

The participants' age and gender were extracted from the medical records. The patients' radiographs were accessed from the institution's picture archiving and communication system.

The TAD in the AP view (TADAP) is measured by determining the apex of the femoral head using a guideline through the midline of the femoral head, and then measuring the anterior-posterior distance. The TAD in the lateral (LAT) view (TADLAT) measures the distance from the screw apex to the femoral head. The TADAP+TADLAT=TAD (Fig. 1).13

The method of calculation of the calTAD in the LAT view uses the same measurement technique as the TADLAT. The calTADAP is measured by moving the guideline of the cephalic nail to be adjacent to the medial cortex of the femoral neck and then measuring the distance between the 2 lines. The calTADLAT+calTADAP=calTAD (Fig. 2).14

All patients received thromboembolic prophylaxis: 0.4 mL of Enoxaparin sodium injection was given on admission day, Enoxaparin sodium injection was withdrawn 12 h before the operation, and restarted on the first day after surgery (at least 6 h postoperatively).

Statistical analysis

All the calculations were done using the SPSS version 23 software. For categorical data, the Pearson Chi-square test was used. Continuous data were analyzed using an independent sample t-test or a Mann-Whitney U test.

The association of various indicators with perioperative HBL was evaluated using univariate analysis. A multiple linear regression analysis model (stepwise) was used to identify independent factors affecting perioperative HBL. A p<0.05 was considered to be significant.

Results

General information

During the study period from January 2020 to December 2022, Huzhou Central Hospital treated 131 patients with intertrochanteric fractures. Out of these, 51 were excluded due to inadequate postoperative hematocrit and intraoperative blood transfusion data, resulting in 80 patients who satisfied the inclusion criteria (Fig. 3). Perioperative blood loss

Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis indicated that calTAD (p=0.0213, area under curve=0.6497, 95% CI: 0.5295, -0.7700) served as an independent predictor of HBL in PFNA, with a cut-off value of 7.625 mm (sensitivity=0.357; specificity=0.895) (Fig. 4). Nonetheless, the receiver operating characteristic curve for TAD yielded a p value >0.05, indicating the absence of an optimal cut-off value, so the average value was designated as the cut-off point. The findings demonstrated that TAD length correlated with TBL (p=0.035) and OBL (p=0.003). No correlation was observed between TAD length and HBL (p=0.124). And the calTAD length exhibited a significant correlation with TBL (p=0.021) and HBL (p=0.007). No correlation was noted between calTAD length and OBL (p=0.076). (Table 2, Table 3).

Factors influencing perioperative blood loss

Univariate correlation analysis revealed that HBL showed significant positive correlations with several variables: patient age ((77.79±10.45) years; p=0.006), height ((1.60±0.08) m; p<0.001), preoperative hemoglobin ((108.75±14.78) g/L; p<0.001), preoperative hematocrit (33.07±4.09; p<0.001), and preoperative–postoperative changes in hemoglobin (p<0.001) and hematocrit (p<0.001). HBL also correlated positively with calTAD length ((10.00±3.32) mm, p=0.002), indicating increased blood loss with greater calTAD. Additionally, males exhibited significantly higher HBL than females (p<0.001) (Table 1, Table 4). Subsequent multiple linear regression analysis revealed that height, preoperative hemoglobin levels, variations in hemoglobin levels, variations in hematocrit levels, and calTAD independently affected HBL (β=0.147, 0.299, 0.176, 0.226, and 0.187; p=0.004, 0.015, <0.001, <0.001, and 0.001, respectively) (Table 5). To address collinearity concerns among the variables, ridge regression was employed to alleviate these issues.

Discussion

Trochanteric fractures are becoming more common in the elderly population, reflecting the demographic transition towards an aging society.16 This fracture type is especially susceptible to HBL, the primary contributor to perioperative blood loss. Excessive blood loss can trigger coagulation mechanisms, heightening the risk of thrombosis, and may also hinder the rehabilitation of postoperative limb functionality. The increasing recognition of HBL highlights its clinical importance.17 Anemia and the necessity for blood transfusions are recognized as linked to adverse outcomes in hip fracture cases.18 It is essential to monitor and quantify perioperative HBL. Due to its minimally invasive characteristics, uncomplicated procedure, and diminished intraoperative hemorrhage, the PFNA has become the preferred option for internal fixation in these instances.19,20

The influence of the cephalic nail's positioning in PFNA on perioperative blood loss in patients with intertrochanteric fractures is inadequately recorded. Traditionally, most orthopedic surgeons have used TAD and calTAD to evaluate the position of the cephalic nail after PFNA surgery.10 Our study demonstrated that in the context of PFNA, TAD primarily affected overt blood loss (higher TAD resulted in increased overt blood loss, p=0.003), whereas calTAD significantly influenced HBL (lower calTAD resulted in decreased HBL, p=0.007). Correlation analyses of multiple variables revealed significant positive correlations among patient demographics, blood parameters, and calTAD with HBL. Furthermore, males demonstrated higher HBL during the perioperative phase in comparison to females (p<0.001).

For the above-mentioned significant correlation factors, multiple regression analysis indicated that the patient's height, preoperative hemoglobin levels, variations in hemoglobin and hematocrit levels pre- and post-surgery, and calTAD were independent factors positively correlating with HBL. Elevated values for these factors (including elevated preoperative hemoglobin concentrations) correlated with increased HBL. Therefore, preoperative blood routine analysis is crucial for predicting potential substantial HBL, especially in patients with high preoperative hemoglobin levels. Future exploration is necessary to ascertain the precise extent of HBL impacting postoperative recovery and complications through extensive data analysis, alongside the implementation of perioperative monitoring.

The patient's height and changes in preoperative and postoperative hematocrit levels were significantly associated with HBL, in accordance with the Gross equation. Of the identified risk factors, only calTAD was correlated with intraoperative HBL. A higher calTAD correlated with increased HBL (β=0.187, p=0.001). This paper demonstrates that when calTAD is less than 7.625 mm, the HBL in patients is significantly reduced compared to when calTAD is greater than or equal to 7.625 mm (p=0.007). We contend that optimizing the placement of the cephalomedullary nail during surgery, specifically with a calTAD of less than 7.625 mm, is essential to reduce HBL in the perioperative phase. Furthermore, in accordance with prior research, to achieve fracture healing with minimal failure risk, it is advisable to limit calTAD to less than 35.2 mm to decrease the likelihood of cut-out.10,21

The etiology of perioperative HBL in patients with intertrochanteric fractures remains ambiguous. Current literature widely agrees that intertrochanteric fractures are classified as metaphyseal fractures, noted for their rich blood supply. The insertion of an intramedullary nail may cause internal bleeding during the widening of the medullary canal. Furthermore, the intramedullary cavity and intermuscular lacunae may serve as reservoirs for HBL.9 Additional factors contributing to HBL from intramedullary fixation include: firstly, the traversal of the gluteus medius during the fixation procedure, which may jeopardize the peritrochanteric arterial network; secondly, the insertion of an intramedullary device requires drilling into the greater trochanter, thereby disrupting neoangiogenesis within the bone marrow.22 We propose that minimizing calTAD and refining nail placement may avoid significant vascular structures, modify blood flow direction, and diminish blood inflow into interstitial spaces, consequently decreasing the volume of HBL.

Fundamentally, diminishing calTAD may significantly reduce HBL and the frequency of cut-out, warranting careful consideration and possible endorsement by clinicians to improve fracture healing outcomes, enhance surgical success rates, and decrease adverse events.

There are several limitations to this study that must be acknowledged. Firstly, since the different lengths of the intramedullary nails may affect the accuracy of HBL, we excluded the cases with nails longer than 20 cm in our study. However, this also resulted in the article's results not applying to those PFNA patients with overly long intramedullary nails. Secondly, this study was conducted in a single research institution, and the sample size is relatively small. Further research is needed in the future to re-evaluate the results of a larger sample.

In summary, the placement of the nail may have an impact on HBL during the PFNA surgery. By optimizing the placement of the nail during the operation, HBL can be effectively reduced.

In conclusion, previous research on decreasing HBL primarily concentrated on the preoperative management of intertrochanteric fractures, including the prevention of preoperative anemia and the prompt administration of prophylactic anticoagulants to decrease the occurrence of postoperative thrombosis in the lower extremities. There are limited reports on the decrease of HBL during surgery. Our study constitutes the inaugural examination of the correlation between calTAD and HBL, with possible ramifications for the clinical management of intertrochanteric fractures. However, it requires additional verification whether repositioning the cephalic nail during clinical procedures, such as decreasing calTAD, can effectively reduce HBL. Currently, robotic orthopedic surgery is gaining increasing popularity. In the future, can we contemplate modifying the ideal placement of the nail in the preoperative planning of robotic surgery to enhance the surgical strategy?

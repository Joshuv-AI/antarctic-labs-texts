Comprehensive comorbidity assessment for the ECOPD: a long-term multi-centre retrospective study
Authors: Hui Lian, Lei Kou, Xiaozhen Han, Zhu Rui, Sun Dong, Xin Zhang, Liukai Zhao, Qianyu Yue, Xiaomeng Hou, Baiqiang Cai
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundExacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (ECOPD) results in severe adverse outcomes and mortality. It is often associated with increased local and systemic inflammation. However, individual susceptibility to exacerbations remains largely unknown. Our study aimed to investigate the association between comorbidities and exacerbation outcomes.MethodsWe included patients with the primary discharge diagnosis of exacerbation for more 10 years in China. Data on all comorbidities were collected and analysed to determine the impact of the comorbidities on 1-year exacerbation readmission, length of hospital stay, and hospital cost. Univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed, and predictive models were developed.ResultsThis extensive investigation evaluated a total of 15,708 individuals from five prominent locations in China, revealing notable variations in the prevalence of comorbidities and healthcare expenses among different regions. The study shows that there is a high rate of readmission within one year, namely 15.8%. The most common conditions among readmitted patients are hypertension (38.6%), ischemic heart disease (16.9%), and diabetes mellitus (16.6%). An extensive multivariable study revealed that age, gender, and particular comorbidities such as malnutrition and hyperlipidemia are important factors that can significantly predict greater readmission rates, longer hospital stays or increased healthcare costs. The multivariable models show a moderate to good ability to predict patient outcomes, with concordance index ranging from 0.701 to 0.752. This suggests that targeted interventions in these areas could improve patient outcomes and make better use of healthcare resources.ConclusionsThe results regarding the association between severe exacerbations and systemic disease status support the integration of systematic evaluation of comorbidities into the management of exacerbations and the intensification of treatment of important comorbidities as a appropriate measure for prevention of further exacerbations. Our models also provide a novel tool for clinicians to determine the risk of the 1-year recurrence of severe ECOPD in hospitalised patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12890-024-03257-6.

Background

Globally, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is one of the five leading causes of disability-adjusted life years [1]. ECOPD (ECOPD) is important in the management of COPD because it can negatively impact health status, disease progression, hospitalisation, and readmission rates [2]. Acute ECOPD has been defined as the acute worsening of respiratory symptoms that results in additional therapy [3, 4]. According to the 2023 update of the guidelines published by the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease Committee (GOLD), ECOPD is characterised by increased dyspnoea and/or cough and sputum that worsens in 14 days, which may be accompanied by tachypnoea and/or tachycardia, and is often associated with increased local and systemic inflammation caused by infection, pollution, or other airway insults [5]. The significant change in the definition shows that the prevention, early detection, and treatment of ECOPD are clinically significant because of its negative impacts on quality of life, pulmonary function, and disease progression [3, 6]. ECOPD accounts for the majority of the total COPD cost [7, 8]. However, individual susceptibility to exacerbations of COPD remains largely unknown. The number of ECOPDs that patients have had in the previous year is still the strongest predictor.

COPD is a systemic disease due to its systemic manifestations, including cardiovascular comorbidities, cachexia and muscle dysfunction, osteoporosis, anaemia, clinical depression, and anxiety [9–11]. Chronic comorbidities affect the health outcomes of patients with COPD [12–14].

Prediction models for ECOPD have been developed. However, after evaluating the 27 most used prediction models, Beniamino and colleagues concluded that none of the existing models fulfilled the requirements for personalised ECOPD care [15]. Therefore, we aimed to identify the characteristics of patients with ECOPD, the role of comorbidities in ECOPD, and disease-related expenses. We focused on comorbidities such as malnutrition and hyperhomocysteinemia due to their significant impact on COPD outcomes as indicated by preliminary studies. Data collected included demographic information, comorbidity status, and hospital stay details. Patients with incomplete records or those transferred to other facilities were excluded to ensure data consistency and accuracy. This study is distinguished from previous research by its large-scale dataset, comprehensive analysis of comorbidities, and focus on multiple centers across China. The extensive multi-center cohort allows for a detailed examination of regional differences in comorbidity prevalence and their impact on COPD exacerbations. We used integrated comorbidities to develop a efficient predictive model which further demonstrates the important role of comprehensive comorbidity assessment in ECOPD management and prevention.

Methods

This study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Peking Union Medical College Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (No. S-K844), and due to the retrospective nature of the study, informed consent was waived. All methods were performed in accordance with institutional and national guidelines and regulations.

Study population

All patients diagnosed with ECOPD from five different tertiary hospitals in five provinces in China were included: Peking Union Medical College Hospital in Beijing (BEIJING), Chengdu Fifth People’s Hospital in Sichuan Province (SICHUAN), Hebei Chest Hospital in Hebei Province (HEBEI), The First Affiliated Hospital of Kunming University of Science and Technology in Yunnan Province (YUNNAN), and People’s Hospital of Zhengzhou in Henan Province (HENAN). The data were collected before the COVID-19 pandemic. The study period was from January 1, 2008, to September 30, 2019. We focused on comorbidities such as malnutrition and hyperhomocysteinemia due to their significant impact on COPD outcomes as indicated by preliminary studies. Data collected included demographic information, comorbidity status, and hospital stay details. Patients with incomplete records or those transferred to other facilities were excluded to ensure data consistency and accuracy. Data on all patients with a primary discharge diagnosis of ECOPD from the International Classification of Diseases, ninth or tenth revisions (ICD-9/ICD-10 codes J44.1) were collected at different centers over a continuous observation period ranging from 4.9 to 11.6 years.

Data collection

We collected data from the computerised home pages of discharge medical records obtained from the medical records department of each participating hospital. The main discharge diagnoses were coded according to ICD-9/ICD-10. The clinical team checked and signed the final discharge diagnoses for each patient. Thus, we included all comorbidities of each discharged patient with ECOPD. An ECOPD stay was defined as the principal diagnosis of COPD exacerbation. Index hospitalisation was defined as the first ECOPD stay during the study period when the patient was discharged alive, excluding transfer to other acute care hospitals and discharge against medical advice.

Variables

All comorbidities of each patient with a primary discharge diagnosis of ECOPD during the observation period were collected, and the frequency of each comorbidity was ranked. Twenty-five of the top 100 most frequent comorbidities with high clinical value were manually sorted and double-checked by the authors (X. H. and H. L) based on the ICD system and were included as variables alongside age and sex. The comorbidities included anaemia, hypothyroidism, diabetes mellitus (DM), malnutrition (severe malnutrition with marasmus and low weight), hyperhomocysteinemia, hypoproteinaemia, hyperlipidaemia, anxiety and depressive, obstructive sleep apnoea/hypopnea syndrome (OSAHS), hypertension, ischaemic heart disease (IHD, coronary stenosis, myocardial infarction, coronary revascularisation, angina, or coronary atherosclerosis), arrhythmias (any type, including unspecified arrhythmias, irregular heart rhythm, atrial tachycardia or fibrillation or flutter, ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation, or conduction disorders), heart failure, cerebrovascular disease (cerebral infarction, occlusion and stenosis of precerebral arteries, cerebral atherosclerosis, cerebral haemorrhage), peripheral artery disease (PAD), varicose veins of lower extremities, chronic rhinitis or sinusitis, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), constipation or functional diarrhoea, gout, osteoporosis, chronic kidney disease (CKD), prostate hyperplasia, abnormal glucose tolerance, and malignant neoplasm history. Outcome variables

The length of initial hospital stay (LOS), hospital cost of initial stay (total cost), and ECOPD readmission within the following year for each patient were extracted during the observation period. We defined readmission as a return to the hospital for the principal diagnosis of ECOPD (ICD-9/ICD-10 code J44.1) within 1 year of discharge.

Statistical analysis

Normally distributed measures are summarised as mean (standard deviation), non-normally distributed continuous measures as median (quartiles), and categorical factors as frequency (percentage). The Shapiro-Wilk test was conducted for continuous variables to assess normality. A t-test was applied to compare normally distributed continuous variables, the Wilcoxon rank sum test to compare non-normally distributed continuous variables, chi-square test to compare categorical variables, and Fisher’s exact test to compare categorical variables when the cell frequency was < 5.

Univariate and multivariate analyses of the impact on outcomes were performed for 25 comorbidities along with age and sex. Then, univariable and multivariable logistic regression analyses were employed to predict 1-year ECOPD readmission. Univariable analyses were conducted to narrow down the predictor list, and all predictors with p-values of < 0.05 were included in the multivariable full models. The step-down method was applied to obtain reduced models. The selection criterion was the concordance index (C-index). Bootstrapping was used to internally validate the reduced model’s performance over 200 iterations. The concordance index (C-index) measures the predictive accuracy of the models, with values ranging from 0.5 (no predictive power) to 1 (perfect prediction). Bootstrapping is a resampling technique used to assess the stability and accuracy of the models’ estimates by repeatedly sampling with replacement. The models’ discrimination ability was assessed using the C-index, which was identical to the area under the curve. The calibration of the models was assessed graphically using a smoothed scatter plot of the predicted probabilities and observed outcomes. All tests were two-tailed and performed at a significance level (p) of < 0.05. Similar analyses were conducted for LOS and total cost outcomes. For these two outcomes, predictors with p-values of < 0.1 in univariable analyses were included in full multivariable models. R version 4.0.3 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) was used for all statistical analyses. Missing data were managed using multiple imputation methods to minimize bias and enhance the robustness of the study findings. Variables with missing values were imputed based on the observed data to ensure comprehensive analyses.

Results

Demographic and clinical characteristics

The study comprised 15,708 patients, with the majority from Sichuan (10,311; 65.6%), followed by Hebei (2,076; 13.2%), Henan (1,553; 9.9%), Yunnan (1,426; 9.1%), and the fewest from Beijing (342; 2.2%). Males were predominant, representing 68.4% of the total participants. The one-year readmission rate was 15.8% overall, with the highest rate in Sichuan at 18.0% and the lowest in Beijing at 8.2%. The median length of stay (LOS) for the entire cohort was 10 days, while a small fraction (2.2%) experienced prolonged LOS of more than 30 days. Financially, Beijing’s patients faced higher costs, with 36.5% spending over 30,000 RMB, contrasting with Henan, which had the lowest costs among the regions studied. The three leading comorbidities across the participants were hypertension, ischaemic heart disease (IHD), and diabetes mellitus (DM), underlining the burden of chronic conditions in the patient population (Table 1).

Prevalence of comorbidities in regional medical centers across China

In the analysis (Table 2; Fig. 2) of comorbidity prevalence across five hospital centers, the study documented 15,708 patients with varied conditions. Hypertension was the most common comorbidity, especially in Yunnan (42.4%), while ischemic heart disease (IHD) was markedly prevalent in Henan (45.8%). Diabetes mellitus (DM) and prostate hyperplasia were uniformly distributed conditions, each affecting 16.6% of the total population. In contrast, malnutrition was significantly reported in Sichuan (8.7%) compared to other regions. Notably, Beijing reported higher proportions of patients with costly comorbidities such as chronic kidney disease (CKD) and heart failure, indicating potential regional differences in disease burden or healthcare access. The prevalence of conditions like osteoporosis and hypothyroidism varied significantly, with a 10.2% prevalence of osteoporosis in Yunnan and a 4.7% prevalence of hypothyroidism in the same region, both considerably higher than other regions. Overall, the data suggests significant regional variation in the prevalence of comorbidities, which may reflect underlying demographic, environmental, or healthcare service differences.

ECOPD readmission within 1 year

In the univariable analysis, several factors were associated with an increase in the 1-year ECOPD readmission rate, including older age, DM, malnutrition, hyperlipidaemia, heart failure, cerebrovascular disease, osteoporosis, prostate hyperplasia, and so on. However, in the multivariable analysis, only older age, sex (female: male), DM, malnutrition, hyperlipidaemia, heart failure, cerebrovascular disease, osteoporosis, GERD, arrhythmias, and prostate hyperplasia remained significant predictors. These variables were predominantly associated with increased readmission rates. The C-index for this model would indicate its discriminative ability, with a value of 0.701 suggesting moderate discrimination (Table 3; Fig. 3).

Length of initial hospital stay

The univariable analysis identified multiple factors that could potentially increase the length of stay (LOS), such as older age, DM, malnutrition, hyperlipidaemia, heart failure, cerebrovascular disease, osteoporosis, hypoproteinaemia, and IHD. The multivariable analysis refined this to older age, sex (with females having shorter LOS compared to males), hyperlipidaemia, hypoproteinaemia, IHD, cerebrovascular disease, osteoporosis, prostate hyperplasia, history of malignant neoplasm, and CKD as significant factors. Nearly all identified factors in the most suitable multivariable model were associated with a prolonged LOS. The C-index for the multivariable model, 0.752, suggests good predictive ability (Table 3; Figs. 4 and 5).

Discussion

The therapeutic landscape for Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) has undergone a paradigm shift over the past two decades, with a focus broadened from merely treating symptoms to improving lung function, maintaining stability, and preventing exacerbations. The significance of this approach is underpinned by evidence demonstrating that exacerbations of COPD (ECOPD) accelerate the decline in lung function, correlating with decreased physical activity, poorer quality of life, increased mortality, and higher disease-related costs [16–20]. Furthermore, as COPD progresses, exacerbations become more frequent and severe, suggesting the existence of a distinct phenotype characterized by susceptibility to exacerbations. This phenotype represents an area ripe for further investigation across various degrees of disease severity to tailor more effective management strategies [21]. This susceptibility trait requires further investigation across a spectrum of disease severity.

COPD’s association with low-grade systemic inflammation is pivotal, serving as both a consequence of and a link to other systemic manifestations of the disease. This inflammation may extend beyond the lungs, indicating that COPD exacerbations are not only local pulmonary events but also markers of systemic disease [14, 22]. The heterogeneity of these exacerbations, marked by impaired airway function and systemic inflammation, underscores the complex interplay between COPD and its comorbidities [23]. Common comorbid conditions, such as cardiovascular diseases, diabetes mellitus (DM), anxiety, depression, obstructive sleep apnea-hypopnea syndrome (OSAHS), gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), and osteoporosis, significantly influence COPD’s trajectory. These comorbidities may follow independent courses or be intricately linked with COPD, impacting disease management and patient outcomes [23–26]. Some diseases occur and progress independently of COPD, whereas others are related.

The complexity of managing COPD alongside multiple comorbidities presents substantial challenges in clinical practice. This complexity is compounded by the potential for conflicting therapeutic objectives across different conditions, making it difficult for clinicians to prioritize treatments [27, 28]. To address this, comorbidity indices like the Charlson and Elixhauser indices have been developed to predict outcomes such as ECOPD exacerbations, hospitalization, and mortality. However, these indices may not capture the full spectrum of comorbidities affecting individuals with COPD. Our study leverages a cross-national multicentre database to collect comprehensive comorbidity data confirmed by clinicians during hospitalization, offering a unique perspective on the relationship between COPD and its comorbidities [29, 30]. In this study, using data from the cross-national multicentre database, all comorbidities confirmed by clinicians during hospitalisation were collected. The frequency of all comorbidities in patients with COPD was determined to objectively evaluate the weight of various complications. Combined with manual sorting, we could comprehensively and accurately identify the comorbidities related to the ECOPD prognosis.

In our univariable analysis, statistical analysis revealed that many factors were associated with an increase in the 1-year ECOPD readmission rate. They are classified into nutritional metabolic diseases, including diabetes, osteoporosis, hypothyroidism, malnutrition, hypoproteinaemia, hyperlipidaemia, and anaemia; cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, including hypertension, IHD, arrhythmias, heart failure, and cerebrovascular disease; local factors, including OSAHS, chronic rhinitis or sinusitis, and GERD; and others, such as anxiety, depression, CKD, prostate hyperplasia, and malignant neoplasm history. Some of these comorbidities may share the same risk factors as COPD, whereas others may be related to COPD by one disease, thereby increasing the risk of other diseases [25, 31–34]. This underscores the multifaceted nature of COPD, which not only is a pulmonary condition but also shares risk factors with and is impacted by systemic diseases.

In the multivariate analysis of this study, we demonstrated that patients with combined diseases, such as heart failure, cerebrovascular disease, osteoporosis, DM, and malnutrition, had an increased risk of readmission for severe COPD exacerbations the following year. The model can effectively be used to predict the occurrence of severe ECOPD readmission within 1 year for individual patients. This model provides a novel tool for clinicians to determine the risk of the 1-year recurrence of severe ECOPD in hospitalised patients. The results of our study suggest that ECOPD is closely related to systemic disease status, further proving that systemic inflammation plays an important role in the occurrence of exacerbation. Therefore, accurate evaluation and treatment of certain comorbidities may improve the prognosis of patients with ECOPD [13].

COPD’s economic burden is substantial, with longer hospital stays, higher healthcare costs, and increased readmission rates exacerbating the personal and socioeconomic impact of the disease [35, 36]. Longer hospital stays, higher hospital costs, and increased hospital readmission frequency increase the personal and socioeconomic burden of COPD [37]. Our multivariate analysis highlights the potential of certain comorbidities to predict hospitalization length and costs effectively. Notably, the analysis identified two conditions, chronic rhinitis or sinusitis and GERD, that were negatively correlated with overall costs, suggesting that their management during hospitalization could mitigate healthcare expenditures and improve patient outcomes.

Our study has several strengths. First, compared to previous studies, we included all comorbidities from discharge diagnosis instead of already developed indices, which made the analysis between comorbidities and disease prognosis more comprehensive and objective. Second, the predictive models we built were based on multicentre, large sample size, long-term, and accurate diagnostic data, which could provide individualised prediction results. The models provide a novel tool that is more convenient and efficient for making clinical decisions for patients with COPD. Third, as a cross-sectional study, we offered a national overview of the comorbidities, costs, and readmission rates of patients with ECOPD. Finally, the multivariable model in our study clarified the contribution of each comorbidity to one exacerbation, helping doctors target treatment.

Our study offers a comprehensive and objective analysis of the comorbidities associated with ECOPD prognosis, enabled by the use of a multicentre, large-sample, long-term database. The predictive models developed from this analysis provide clinicians with a novel and efficient tool for personalized COPD management, based on individual risk assessments. This approach not only enhances clinical decision-making but also offers insights into managing comorbidities to improve patient outcomes and reduce healthcare costs. Our predictive models for ECOPD can aid clinicians in identifying patients at higher risk of readmission and tailoring management strategies accordingly. These models may also inform healthcare policies by highlighting the importance of comorbidity management in reducing healthcare costs and improving patient outcomes. Future research should focus on validating these models in diverse populations and exploring the integration of predictive analytics into routine clinical practice. Future research should aim to explore the associations between COPD, its comorbidities, and exacerbations in greater detail, with a focus on designing multicentre prospective studies to verify the causal relationships identified in this study.

As the study provided significant outcomes there are some limitations as well. Our study’s focus on a population exclusively from China may limit the generalizability of our findings to other regions with different healthcare systems, environmental factors, and population demographics. The prevalence of comorbidities and outcomes in ECOPD could vary significantly across different regions. Future studies should aim to include diverse populations to validate and expand the applicability of our findings. Additionally, the retrospective nature of our study and reliance on pre-COVID-19 data may not reflect recent changes in ECOPD management practices or outcomes, potentially influenced by the pandemic and advancements in medical care. The selection of hospitals and patient populations could introduce selection bias, potentially affecting the generalizability of our results. Differences in documentation accuracy or coding practices across different hospitals may also introduce biases in the data. Acknowledging these potential biases is crucial for interpreting our findings and their applicability to broader settings. Potential confounders, such as varying levels of healthcare access and socioeconomic factors, might have influenced our results. These were controlled through multivariable analyses; however, some residual confounding may persist. We acknowledge that unmeasured confounders could affect the study outcomes and interpretation. The reliance on medical record data can also introduce the possibility of biases related to documentation accuracy or inconsistent coding practices across different hospitals, which may affect the study’s results. While comprehensive, the follow-up period in this study may not fully capture the long-term progression of comorbidities and their impact on COPD exacerbations. Future studies with extended follow-up periods are recommended to evaluate long-term outcomes more accurately.

Conclusion

The updated GOLD standard 2023 guidelines predict clinical outcomes separately for each comorbidity. However, this interaction and therapeutic paradox are unavoidable in clinical practice. We evaluated 15,708 patients with ECOPD from five different centres in China and established an optimal prediction model considering all comorbidities. Currently, applications based on our prediction model are available online. The results regarding the association between severe exacerbations and systemic disease status support the integration of systematic evaluation of comorbidities into the management of exacerbations and the intensification of treatment of important comorbidities as a appropriate measure for prevention of further exacerbations.

Neoadjuvant camrelizumab combined with chemoradiotherapy and watch-and-wait strategy versus neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy followed by surgery in locally advanced oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma: study protocol of a randomised controlled trial (PALACE-3)
Authors: Xipeng Wang, Chengqiang Li, Yuqin Cao, Xiaozheng Kang, Xuefeng Leng, Yan Zheng, Bin Zheng, Wei Guo, Hui Jing, Yongtao Han, Chun Chen, Yin Li, Hecheng Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCombining immunotherapy with neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (neoCRT) has been shown to be safe, achieving a pathological complete response (pCR) rate of 56% in patients with locally advanced oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) in the PALACE-1 trial. This high pCR rate encourages us to explore the feasibility of postponing surgery after immunotherapy combined with neoCRT under active surveillance. This study aims to assess the efficacy, safety and patient-reported quality of life (QOL) of camrelizumab combined with neoCRT and watch-and-wait strategy versus neoCRT followed by surgery in locally advanced resectable ESCC.Methods and analysisThe PALACE-3 trial is a multicentre, open-label, randomised non-inferiority trial expected to recruit 356 patients from six high-volume centres in China. The study is planned to start in May 2024 and end in December 2028. Eligible patients will be randomly assigned (1:1 ratio) to either camrelizumab combined with neoCRT and watch-and-wait strategy or neoCRT followed by surgery (standard surgery). In the active surveillance group, patients achieving a clinical complete response (cCR) to camrelizumab combined with neoCRT will undergo active surveillance, while those with residual disease or locoregional recurrence will undergo immediate surgery. Patients in the standard surgery group will proceed to surgery after neoCRT. The primary endpoint is the 3-year overall survival (OS) rate. The secondary endpoints include cCR rate, salvage surgery incidence, objective response rate, adverse events during the neoadjuvant therapy, pCR, tumour regression grade, R0 resection rate, lymph node ratio, perioperative complications, disease-free survival (DFS) and 3-year DFS rate, OS and health-related QOL.Ethics and disseminationThis study has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine Affiliated Ruijin Hospital (Shanghai, China), as well as the ethics committees of the following participating centres: National Cancer Centre/National Clinical Research Centre for Cancer/Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences and Peking Union Medical College (Beijing, China); Sichuan Cancer Hospital and Institute, Sichuan Cancer Centre, School of Medicine, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China (Chengdu, China); The Affiliated Cancer Hospital of Zhengzhou University, Henan Cancer Hospital (Zhengzhou, China); Fujian Medical University Union Hospital (Fuzhou, China). Complete information about the study status, relevant events and results will be regularly updated on the project’s webpage on ClinicalTrials.gov. Written informed consent (Supplemental Material) will be obtained from each participant. All research outputs will be published in peer-reviewed journals and presented at national or international conferences.Trial registration numberThe trial was meticulously registered in advance on 1 April 2024 and can be accessed through the following link: https://www.clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06339060. Introduction

Oesophageal cancer ranks 11th in incidence and 7th in mortality among malignancies worldwide, with approximately 511 000 new cases and 445 000 deaths estimated in 2022.1 Approximately 50% of new global cases occur in China, of which more than 90% have oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC).2 Due to non-specific symptoms at an early stage, most patients presented are diagnosed with locally advanced disease, resulting in an estimated 5-year survival rate of only 30%.3 Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (neoCRT) followed by oesophagectomy has been recommended as the standard treatment for locally advanced oesophageal cancer.4,8 However, radical oesophagectomy is highly invasive, associated with substantial morbidity,9 and decreases patients’ quality of life (QOL).10 11 Moreover, despite radical procedures, approximately 60% of patients develop recurrent disease, with a 5-year survival rate of 47%. These findings underscore the need for less invasive treatment strategies with proper patient selection.

Immunotherapy, particularly with immune checkpoint inhibitors, has been used in the metastatic and adjuvant settings for oesophageal cancer.12,14 Preclinical studies have demonstrated synergy between immunotherapy and chemo-/radiotherapy through several mechanisms.15,17 Clinically, our previous phase Ib PALACE trial showed the safety and promising activity of pembrolizumab combined with neoCRT for ESCC in the neoadjuvant setting.18 The phase II PALACE trial is ongoing to evaluate the efficacy and safety in resectable ESCC.19 The improved pathological complete response (pCR) rate to immunotherapy combined with neoCRT encourages us to explore the feasibility of postponing surgery in the context of active surveillance.

An organ-sparing approach with active surveillance has been proposed for clinical complete response (cCR) to neoCRT. Retrospective studies and individual patient data meta-analysis suggest that active surveillance after neoCRT might not compromise overall survival (OS), and postponed surgery can be done safely.20 21 The recently published phase III SANO trial compared neoCRT followed by active surveillance versus standard oesophagectomy in patients with oesophageal cancer. The trial demonstrated that active surveillance in patients who achieved cCR after neoCRT was non-inferior to immediate surgery in terms of 2-year OS.22,24 Therefore, we hypothesise that combining immunotherapy with neoCRT and a watch-and-wait strategy will result in non-inferior survival and improved QOL compared with neoCRT followed by surgery in locally advanced ESCC.

Camrelizumab, an anti-programmed death 1 (PD-1) inhibitor, has been approved in China for advanced ESCC.25 26 The recent phase III ESCORT-NEO trial showed a superior pCR with neoadjuvant camrelizumab plus chemotherapy.27 The PALACE-3 trial is designed to evaluate the efficacy, safety and patient-reported QOL of camrelizumab combined with neoCRT and watch-and-wait strategy versus neoCRT followed by surgery in locally advanced ESCC.

Methods and analysis

Hypothesis

Combining immunotherapy with neoCRT and the watch-and-wait strategy leads to non-inferior survival and improved QOL when compared with neoCRT followed by surgery.

Study design

The PALACE-3 trial is an investigator-initiated multicentre, open-label, randomised non-inferiority trial. The study is planned to start in May 2024 and end in December 2028. Recruitment is so far planned in six high-volume centres in China (Shanghai Ruijin Hospital Affiliated to Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine; Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences; Union Hospital Affiliated to Fujian Medical University; The First Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine; The First Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University; The First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University). The trial has been prospectively registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06339060).

Randomisation

This study will use stratified block randomisation, stratified by centre, with block randomisation within each centre. The sponsor (Ruijin Hospital Affiliated with Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine) will employ professional statisticians to generate a random number table and random envelopes. After each centre sends the basic information of the enrolled patients to the sponsor, the sponsor will perform randomisation by centre (using the envelope randomisation method), assigning patients to either the experimental group (active surveillance) or the control group (standard surgery) in a 1:1 ratio. The enrolment number and group information will be sent back to each centre via email within 1 day.

Interventions

Experimental group (active surveillance)

The trial intervention consists of camrelizumab combined with neoCRT, followed by active surveillance. Oesophagectomy will be offered only when there is a high suspicion or proof of residual or recurrent locoregional disease. The neoCRT will follow the CROSS regimen, which includes intravenous nab-paclitaxel (50 mg/m² once per week for 5 weeks) and carboplatin (AUC of 2 mg/mL/min once per week for 5 weeks), along with concurrent radiotherapy (1.8 Gy per fraction, 5 fractions over five consecutive days per week, to a total dose of 41.4 Gy). Camrelizumab will be administered intravenously at a dose of 200 mg every 3 weeks for two cycles, specifically on the day of chemotherapy administration during weeks 1 and 4.

Four to 6 weeks after completing neoadjuvant therapy, all patients will undergo a comprehensive clinical response evaluation, including gastroscopy with at least six tumour-site biopsies, contrast-enhanced CT of the neck, chest and abdomen, and positron emission tomography (PET)-CT to assess both distant metastasis and locoregional metabolic activity. Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) will be performed if technically feasible and indicated, particularly in cases where tumour depth is unclear. If gastroscopy cannot be passed due to stenosis, this will be considered indicative of residual disease. Biopsy samples showing high-grade dysplasia or indefinite for malignancy will be interpreted conservatively as possible residual tumour. In cases with suspected cervical or locoregional lymph node involvement on imaging, ultrasound-guided fine-needle aspiration (FNA) will be performed to confirm or exclude malignancy (table 1). A cCR will be defined as no visible tumour on endoscopy, negative biopsies for malignancy, no suspicious lymph nodes on imaging or positive FNA, and no metabolic activity suggestive of residual disease on PET-CT. Patients with no residual cancer are deemed to have achieved a cCR, which should be confirmed at least 4 weeks apart before committing to active surveillance. Patients with residual disease after neoadjuvant therapy, as well as those with locoregional regrowth during surveillance, will undergo an immediate minimally invasive McKeown or Ivor Lewis oesophagectomy. Control group (standard surgery)

The control intervention follows the CROSS trial protocol, consisting of neoCRT followed by oesophagectomy. CT restaging, PET-CT (recommended) and other necessary tests will be conducted 4 to 6 weeks after the completion of neoadjuvant therapy to evaluate clinical efficacy and surgical indications. Patients without progressive disease or distant metastases, who are eligible for surgery, will undergo an immediate minimally invasive oesophagectomy using either the McKeown or Ivor Lewis approach.

Pathology

All biopsies and resection specimens will be assessed by expert pathologists. The resection specimens will be assessed according to the 8th edition of the UICC TNM cancer staging.28 Tumour regression grade (TRG) will be reported as complete response (TRG0), near complete response (TRG1), partial response (TRG2) and poor or no response (TRG3), according to the criteria adopted by the College of American Pathologists (CAP)/National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines.29

Follow-up

For patients who undergo surgery, postoperative follow-up will be performed in accordance with the NCCN guidelines: 1 month after surgery, every 6 months for the first 3 years and annually thereafter. Patients who undergo active surveillance will undergo close monitoring according to a structured follow-up schedule: every 3 months in the first year, every 4 months in the second year, every 6 months in the third year and annually from the fourth year onward. At each follow-up visit, patients will undergo clinical evaluation, including contrast-enhanced CT scans of the chest and abdomen to assess for locoregional or distant recurrence, as well as cervical lymph node ultrasonography. FNA of cervical lymph nodes will be performed if suspicious findings are detected on ultrasound. Gastroscopy with deep biopsies at the original tumour site will be routinely performed at each visit. Additional diagnostic tests such as PET-CT, EUS or other imaging modalities will be carried out when clinically indicated—such as the emergence of suspicious lesions on routine tests, new symptoms (eg, dysphagia or weight loss) or elevated tumour markers. Active surveillance will continue until locoregional recurrence is detected, at which point patients will undergo salvage oesophagectomy. If distant metastasis is identified, patients will receive palliative treatment or individualised therapy following multidisciplinary team discussion.

Recruitment and interim analysis

Patient recruitment is scheduled to begin on 1 May 2024, and conclude on 1 March 2028. An interim analysis is planned for 2026, after 178 patients (50% of the target sample size) have been enrolled and followed for a minimum predefined period. The interim analysis will be conducted based on the O’Brien-Fleming method to control for type I error. The primary objectives of the interim analysis are to assess safety and futility and to determine whether early termination is warranted due to clear evidence of benefit, harm or lack of efficacy.

All principal investigators from the participating centres will have access to the interim results. A pre-designated independent monitoring committee, composed of clinical and statistical experts not involved in patient enrolment or treatment, will review the interim data and provide recommendations. The final decision to continue or terminate the trial will be made collectively by the steering committee, based on these recommendations and in accordance with the pre-specified stopping rules.

Data collection and management

All study data will be recorded in Case Report Forms (CRFs) by the treating investigator in accordance with the study protocol. Investigators are responsible for ensuring that data are complete, accurate and entered in a timely manner. Any corrections must be signed and dated without obscuring the original entry. Completed CRFs will be reviewed and signed by the site investigator before being submitted for data entry.

A dedicated database (Integrated clinical research data science) network will be used for this trial. Trained staff will conduct double data entry, with two independent data entry personnel entering the data separately to ensure accuracy. An appointed data manager will oversee the entry process and perform routine data quality checks.

Any data discrepancies or missing information identified during data review will be documented using data query forms. These queries will be returned to the site and resolved in writing by the investigator. The data manager is not permitted to modify original data without proper documentation.

Data validation will include checks for completeness, logical consistency, protocol deviations and outliers. A final review will be conducted jointly by the principal investigator and the trial statistician. Once validated, the database will be locked. No changes will be allowed post-lock unless predefined procedures are followed and the changes are approved and documented. Data will be securely stored with regular backups, and access will be restricted to authorised personnel only.

Statistical analysis

Sample size calculation

The sample size calculation was reviewed and validated by an experienced biostatistician. According to data from the CROSS and NEORTEC5010 trials, the 3-year OS rate for patients who underwent surgery after neoCRT is approximately 66%.4,8 A non-inferiority margin of 15% is adopted, that is, the 3-year OS rate for patients in the active surveillance group should not be lower than 51%. To this end, a minimum of 314 patients (157 per group) will have 80% power to declare non-inferiority with a type I error of 5%. Considering a 12% dropout rate, a total sample size of 356 patients, 178 in each group, will be required for randomisation.

Data analysis

All analyses will be performed on an intent-to-treat basis. The primary endpoint analysis will be performed by estimating the difference in the 3-year OS rate using the Kaplan-Meier method. The secondary time-to-event endpoints will be analysed using the Kaplan-Meier method. Baseline variables, tumour response, toxicities and other binary variables (eg, R0 resection rate) will be reported using usual descriptive statistics. QOL will be analysed using linear mixed regression models to estimate the mean differences between the groups. Prespecified subgroup analyses will be performed on the major primary and secondary endpoints according to baseline patient characteristics (age, sex, body mass index, prior therapy, clinical stage, primary tumour location and PD-1 and PD-L1 expressions) and centre. The non-inferiority test will be performed for the primary endpoint using a one-sided test with an overall significance level of 0.025, while other tests will be performed using a two-sided test. All statistical analyses will be performed using R software.

Discussion

The PALACE-3 is a randomised controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy, safety and patient-reported QOL of camrelizumab combined with neoCRT and watch-and-wait strategy versus neoCRT and surgery in patients with locally advanced ESCC. The neoCRT followed by surgery has been the standard treatment for locally advanced ESCC. However, the improved pCR rate to neoCRT versus neoadjuvant chemotherapy did not translate to the long-term survival benefit when followed by oesophagectomy.32 33 Given the increased morbidity and mortality associated with surgery, as well as a lasting impact on patients’ QOL, especially for patients with no residual tumours after neoadjuvant therapy, an organ-sparing approach with active surveillance seems attractive as an alternative to standard oesophagectomy.

Patients with ESCC will be included in this study, as ESCC is more sensitive to chemoradiotherapy when compared with adenocarcinoma of the oesophagus. The CROSS trial reported a significantly higher pCR rate with neoCRT in ESCC patients than in those with oesophageal adenocarcinoma (49% vs 23%).4 Moreover, the PALACE-1 trial showed a pCR rate of 56% (10/18) with pembrolizumab combined with neoCRT in ESCC patients,18 which was higher than that of 25% (10/40) with atezolizumab combined with neoCRT in patients with oesophageal adenocarcinoma in the PERFECT trial.34 This high pCR rate to immunotherapy combined with neoCRT in ESCC provides a strong rationale for us to explore the organ-sparing approach with active surveillance.

One major concern of combining immunotherapy with neoCRT is the risk of increased toxicity. The conventional CROSS regimen with a relatively low radiation dose (41.4 Gy), rather than a high radiation dose (50–60 Gy) of definitive chemoradiotherapy, has been used in our PALACE-serial trials. Previous randomised trials demonstrated similar morbidity and mortality in patients who underwent minimally invasive oesophagectomy following neoCRT versus neoadjuvant chemotherapy.32 33 Moreover, early-phase clinical trials, like PALACE-1 and PERFECT trials, showed acceptable safety of immunotherapy combined with neoCRT that was generally comparable to those reported in the CROSS trial.18 19 However, the number of patients included is small. Our ongoing phase II PALACE-2 trial has successfully recruited 143 patients with locally advanced ESCC. The preliminary analysis showed promising efficacy and manageable safety of pembrolizumab combined with neoCRT (Data not published). The PALACE-3 trial will help to better understand the efficacy and safety of immunotherapy combined with neoCRT using standard neoCRT followed by surgery as control.

This study also has some limitations. For example, the choice of a 15% non-inferiority margin for the primary endpoint (3-year OS rate) seems rather wide, which was based on both clinical and statistical considerations. We acknowledge that this margin may appear relatively wide; however, it reflects a balance between clinical acceptability and the feasibility of detecting non-inferiority with adequate power in a large, multicentre trial.

Accurate clinical response evaluation should have the ability to detect residual disease at an early stage to justify active surveillance and to postpone surgery safely. In this study, clinical response evaluations will be performed periodically during the active surveillance using a set of diagnostic modalities (endoscopy with biopsies, PET-CT and endoscopic ultrasonography) to accurately detect residual disease.35 In patients with residual or recurrent locoregional disease, immediate surgery will be offered. Data from the PALACE-3 trial will provide more information on this important issue, which will affect patient stratification for active surveillance in clinical practice when non-inferiority is approved.

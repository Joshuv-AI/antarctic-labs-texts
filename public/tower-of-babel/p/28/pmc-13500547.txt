Fluorofenidone ameliorates pressure overload-induced heart failure by stabilizing SERCA2a in male mice
Authors: Quan Liu, Huayang Li, Jiantao Chen, Qiushi Ren, Tongxin Chu, Li Luo, Zeyu Li, Suiqing Huang, Liqun Shang, Adilai Maihemuti, Zhuoming Zhou, Zhilin Miao, Kaizheng Liu, Jinyu Pan, Kangni Feng, Mengya Liang, Zhongkai Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractReduced expression and activity of cardiac sarco/endoplasmic reticulum Ca2+-ATPase 2a (SERCA2a), causing intracellular calcium dyshomeostasis, are regarded as hallmarks of heart failure (HF). However, effective SERCA2a-targeted therapies for HF treatment have not been identified. Here, we show that fluorofenidone, previously reported to enhance cell shortening, contraction and relaxation of cardiomyocytes by nearly 100%, prevents the progression of pressure overload-induced HF. Combining drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS), cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA) and surface plasmon resonance (SPR) approaches, we identify SERCA2a as a direct binding partner of fluorofenidone. Mechanistically, fluorofenidone attenuates the binding of WW domain-containing E3 ubiquitin protein ligase 1 (WWP1) to SERCA2a and inhibits WWP1-mediated K27-linked polyubiquitination of SERCA2a. Moreover, Gln758, Asp812 and Glu917 are essential for the fluorofenidone-SERCA2a interaction. SERCA2a knockdown markedly attenuated the cardioprotective effects of fluorofenidone on pressure overload-induced HF in male mice. Together, these findings highlight the therapeutic potential of fluorofenidone for HF treatment. Nevertheless, further preclinical and clinical studies are required to fully evaluate its in vivo efficacy and safety before clinical translation.Subject terms: Cardiac hypertrophy, Target identification, Cardiovascular diseases

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a complex clinical syndrome that mainly results from persistent pathological pressure overload due to hypertension1. Cardiomyocytes from the failing heart show calcium dyshomeostasis and energy deficit, attributable to alterations of a collection of Ca2+-handling proteins and impaired mitochondrial oxidative metabolism, respectively, which together contribute to cardiac systolic and diastolic dysfunction2,3. However, present recommended drugs4 have no direct effect on these aspects, which may be one of the reasons why their therapeutic effects are unsatisfactory.

SERCA2a (sarco/endoplasmic reticulum Ca2+-ATPase 2a), predominantly expressed over other isoforms on cardiomyocytes, is an essential pump maintaining intracellular calcium homeostasis. On the one hand, SERCA2a is responsible for the reuptake of cytosolic calcium during diastole, having a key role in controlling cardiac relaxation5. On the other hand, SERCA2a ensures the calcium content of sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR), required for ordered cardiac contraction5. At present, reduced expression and activity of cardiac SERCA2a have been regarded as the hallmarks of heart failure6. Although SERCA2a has emerged as a key therapeutic target for HF, therapeutically relevant SERCA2a-targeted therapies for the treatment of HF have not been identified.

Protein ubiquitination is a dynamic post translation modification of proteins where ubiquitin holds a covalent binding with substrates on lysine in a three-step cascade. Firstly, E1 ubiquitin activating enzymes form an unstable thioester bond with ubiquitin to activate it. Secondly, the activated ubiquitin is transferred from E1 to the active site of E2 ubiquitin conjugating enzymes. Finally, E3 ubiquitin ligases catalyze the covalent binding of ubiquitin to the target protein after recognition7. The single ubiquitin can be attached to one or multiple lysine residues on substrate (mono/multiubiquitination), or give rise to an isopeptide-linked polyubiquitin chain on a lysine residue of substrate (polyubiquitination). All seven lysine (K6, K11, K27, K29, K33, K48 and K63) and one N-terminal methionine (M1) residues are able to constitute linkage points during polyubiquitin chain elongation7. K48-linked chains, the predominant linkage type in cells (often > 50% of all linkages), direct substrates to proteasomal degradation, while K63-linked chains, the second most abundant linkage type, have non-proteolytic functions8. However, the cellular functions of ‘atypical’ polyubiquitin chains (M1, K6, K11, K27, K29 and K33) still remain less well characterized.

WW domain-containing E3 ubiquitin protein ligase 1 (WWP1), a member of highly conserved C2-WW-HECT E3 ligase family, is expressed in almost all human tissues. It contains a C-terminal HECT domain with E3 ubiquitin ligase activity, a N-terminal C2 domain related to calcium-dependent phospholipid binding and four WW domains in the middle for recognizing and binding to proline-rich motifs (e.g. PPxY, PPLP, PPR, pS/pT-P) on substrates9–11. Possessing a key catalytic cysteine (C890 for human), WWP1 can ubiquitinate multiple substrates and regulate diverse cellular processes including protein trafficking, degradation, and cell signal transduction9. Generally, WWP1-mediated polyubiquitination are K48-linked, promoting proteasomal degradation of substrates12. Under certain circumstances, WWP1 directs substrate degradation through the lysosome way after adding other types of polyubiquitin chains13. In addition, WWP1 has been found to increase the stability of substrate proteins via promoting ‘atypical’ polyubiquitination14. These diverse effects of WWP1 may be due to different substrates or ubiquitination types in different scenarios12.

Fluorofenidone (also named as AKF-PD), a novel derivative of pirfenidone, shows equivalent antifibrotic activity, lower toxicity and longer half-life than pirfenidone15. So far, fluorofenidone has been demonstrated to have remarkable efficacy in attenuating fibrosis in kidney16, liver17, heart18 and lung19 via regulating several signaling pathways. Besides, its functional impact on the physiology of adult rat cardiomyocytes has lately been investigated. Avila20 reports that fluorofenidone can enhance the percentage of cell shortening and rates of both contraction and relaxation by nearly 100%. However, the targets involved and the underlying mechanisms have not been identified. Meanwhile, fluorofenidone pharmacological potential has not been tested in models of cardiac disease.

In the current study, we explore the effect of fluorofenidone on phenylephrine (PE)-induced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and attempt to determine the therapeutic target and the underlying mechanism. Furthermore, we subject wild type (WT) mice and adeno-associated virus 9 (AAV9)-cardiac troponin T (cTnT)-small hairpin RNA targeting Atp2a2 (AAV9-cTnT-shAtp2a2)–injected mice to transverse aortic constriction (TAC) surgery to corroborate the cardioprotective effects of fluorofenidone. We discover that fluorofenidone prevent the progression of heart failure (HF) induced by pressure overload by stabilizing SERCA2a via inhibition of WWP1-catalyzed K27-linked polyubiquitination. Therefore, fluorofenidone has high potential as a candidate for the treatment of cardiac hypertrophy and HF.

Results

Fluorofenidone alleviates PE-induced hypertrophy of cardiomyocytes

Neonatal rat cardiomyocytes (NRCMs) and H9C2 cells were stimulated with PE to induce cardiomyocyte hypertrophy in vitro. Over time, the mRNA level of hypertrophy response genes Nppa, Nppb and Myh7, together with the protein level of β-myosin heavy chain (β-MyHC), encoded by Myh7, elevated gradually (Fig. 1A-D). Therefore, in our further studies, NRCMs and H9C2 cells were treated with PE for 72 h to induce cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and dysfunction.

Recently, fluorofenidone has been reported to be able to increase the percentage of cell shortening and rates of both contraction and relaxation by about 100% in adult rat cardiomyocytes20. Thus, we envisaged fluorofenidone might attenuate cardiomyocyte hypertrophy induced by PE. Based on the result of cell viability assay (Figure S1A in the Data Supplement), 1, 2, and 4 mM fluorofenidone were chose to carry out subsequent studies. As results showed, the enhanced mRNA levels of Nppa and Nppb as well as the elevated protein levels of atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) and β-MyHC in PE-stimulated NRCMs were dose-dependently suppressed by fluorofenidone (Fig. 1E–G; Fig. S1B in the Data Supplement). Besides, fluorofenidone evidently decreased PE-induced β-MyHC expression in H9C2 cells in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1H). To visually observed the effect of fluorofenidone on hypertrophic cardiomyocytes, rhodamine-phalloidin was used to stain the actin cytoskeleton. In concordance with these findings, the increased cell size of PE-stimulated NRCMs was lessened by fluorofenidone (Fig. 1I).

Taken together, cardiomyocyte hypertrophy induced by PE was mitigated by fluorofenidone.

Fluorofenidone targets SERCA2a and stabilizes it via inhibition of polyubiquitination

The loss of myocardial calcium homeostasis, attributed to alterations of Ca2+-handling proteins, leads to impaired contraction and relaxation happened in cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure21. Nowadays, the reduced expression and activity of SERCA2a which mediates calcium reuptake from the cytosol into the sarcoplasmic reticulum are regarded as hallmark features of heart failure22. In line with previous researches, both the mRNA and protein level of SERCA2a were significantly down-regulated after PE treatment (Fig. S2B, S2C in the Data Supplement). Herein, we further explored the effect of fluorofenidone on SERCA2a. Surprisingly, dose-dependent inhibition of fluorofenidone against PE-induced SERCA2a expression in NRCMs and H9C2 cells was found, while even 4 mM fluorofenidone could not change the mRNA level of Atp2a2 (Fig. 2A, B; Figure S2A, S2D in the Data Supplement). Besides, we observed a concentration-dependent escalation of SERCA2a activity by fluorofenidone using sarcoplasmic reticulum microsomes isolated from NRCMs (Fig. 2C).

To examine the direct combination of fluorofenidone and SERCA2a without resorting to chemical modifications, drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS) assay, capitalizing on the decreased protease susceptibility of the target protein upon drug binding, was employed23. The result showed that the proteolysis of SERCA2a by pronase was attenuated in the presence of fluorofenidone (Fig. 2D, E). To further validate the interaction between fluorofenidone and SERCA2a in intact cells, another label-free method — cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA) which takes advantage of the increased thermal stabilization of proteins upon ligand binding was performed24. According to the fitted melting curves, the melting temperature (Tm) and apparent aggregation temperature (Tagg) for SERCA2a in H9C2 cells were increased from 49.56 °C and 53.54 °C to 61.80 °C and 68.26 °C by fluorofenidone, respectively (Fig. 2F, G). In other words, fluorofenidone pronouncedly delayed the thermal degradation of SERCA2a. These findings suggested that SERCA2a was the direct binding partner of fluorofenidone. To further corroborate the direct binding of fluorofenidone to SERCA2a, purified SERCA2a protein was employed for the surface plasmon resonance (SPR) assay. We found that fluorofenidone exhibited a discernible concentration-dependent binding and dissociation association with SERCA2a. The dissociation constant (KD) for fluorofenidone / SERCA2a was determined to be 248 nM (Fig. 2H, I), confirming a robust binding interaction between them. To identify whether the protective effects of fluorofenidone on PE-induced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy were SERCA2a-dependent, we transfected NRCMs with the small interfering RNA (siRNA) to silence expression of SERCA2a and found that SERCA2a knockdown pronouncedly inhibited fluorofenidone from reversing the cell size enlargement (Fig. 2J) and reducing the increase of β-MyHC expression level (Fig. S2E–S2G in the Data Supplement). In other words, fluorofenidone alleviated cardiac hypertrophy via regulating SERCA2a.

Since fluorofenidone dose-dependently upregulated the protein level and activity of SERCA2a while had no effect on its mRNA level, we speculated that fluorofenidone affected its degradation rather than synthesis. To test our hypothesis, we exposed H9C2 cells to cycloheximide (CHX), a eukaryotic translation elongation inhibitor25, with or without fluorofenidone and found that after blocking synthesis for 24 h, 48 h and 72 h, SERCA2a contents in fluorofenidone-treated H9C2 cells were, respectively, 1.46, 1.71, and 3.36 times as high as that in vehicle-treated H9C2 cells (Fig. 2K). As evidenced by these findings, fluorofenidone remarkably delayed SERCA2a degradation.

Presently, the ubiquitin-proteasome system and the autophagy-lysosomal pathway are regarded as the two major intracellular proteolytic mechanisms by which mammalian cells remove misfolded, aggregated or damaged proteins to maintain protein homeostasis26,27. It’s been reported that the intracellular degradation of SERCA2a is conducted in an ubiquitination-dependent manner28. Therefore, we hypothesized that fluorofenidone might prevent the intracellular ubiquitination of SERCA2a. To confirm this hypothesis, MG132 was used to potently block the proteolytic activity of the 26S proteasome complex in H9C2 cells. As expected, exposure to PE significantly increased the amount of polyubiquitinated SERCA2a, whereas co-treatment with fluorofenidone effectively reduced SERCA2a polyubiquitination (Fig. 2L), indicating that fluorofenidone enhanced SERCA2a stability by inhibiting its polyubiquitination.

WWP1 directly interacts with SERCA2a and is involved in SERCA2a polyubiquitination and degradation in human failing heart

In the processes of proteins polyubiquitination and degradation, E3 ubiquitin ligases determine the specificity and the modification sites of substrates, catalyzing transfer of ubiquitin from an E2 ubiquitin conjugating enzyme to substrate lysine residues29. To explore the E3 ubiquitin ligase that interact with SERCA2a and determine whether fluorofenidone affects their interaction, immunoprecipitation-mass spectrometry(IP-MS) was performed (Fig. 3A, B). Among differentially expressed proteins identified based on the iBAQ (intensity-based absolute quantification) values, a total of four candidate E3 ubiquitin ligases were revealed (Suppl. Data 1). The comparison of the iBAQ values between groups for these four proteins showed that treatment with fluorofenidone decreased the number of WWP1 binding to SERCA2a by nearly 90.72%, while the number of HACE1, NEDD4, and MYCBP2 were only decreased by 31.54%, 16.28% and 0.92%, respectively (Suppl. Data 1). Therefore, WWP1 piqued our interest. To verify our discovery from IP-MS, lysates of H9C2 cells were first immunoprecipitated with an anti-SERCA2a antibody and probed with an anti-WWP1 antibody. Inversely, the H9C2 cells lysates were also immunoprecipitated with an anti-WWP1 antibody and probed with an anti-SERCA2a antibody. This reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assay suggested that these two endogenous proteins were complex in H9C2 cells and fluorofenidone could efficiently inhibit their interaction (Fig. 3C).

WWP1 is a HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligase that interacts with proteins having proline-rich motifs9. Considering that SERCA2a harbors two PPR motifs, we assessed whether these two PPR motifs are recognized by WWP1 via a physical interaction. Human embryonic kidney–293 T (HEK293T) cells were transfected with overexpression plasmid coding deletion-mutated SERCA2a (Flag-SERCA2a-P601_R603del or Flag-SERCA2a-P819_R821del) or wild-type SERCA2a (Flag-SERCA2a-WT) together with WWP1 wild-type plasmid (Myc-WWP1-WT). We found that the immune complexes of Flag-SERCA2a-P819_R821del group had a less intense band of Myc-WWP1 (Fig. 3D), indicating that the PPR motif (P819P820R821) of SERCA2a plays a vital role in the interaction with WWP1. We next attempted to evaluate whether their binding was direct. After constructing the prokaryotic expression plasmids, recombinant GST-WWP1 and GST were expressed and purified (Fig. S3A–S3C in the Data Supplement). Then purified GST-WWP1 or GST together with Flag-SERCA2a expressed and purified from HEK293T cells (Fig. S3D in the Data Supplement) were utilized to carry out GST pull-down assay. The results showed that GST-WWP1 and Flag-SERCA2a constituted a complex in vitro, however, GST did not (Fig. 3E). Collectively, these data demonstrated that WWP1 directly interacted with SERCA2a and raised a possibility that WWP1 regulated the polyubiquitination of SERCA2a.

To investigate the regulating effect of WWP1 on SERCA2a in the progression of chronic heart failure (HF), we analyzed the binding degree between WWP1 and SERCA2a as well as the polyubiquitination level of SERCA2a in left ventricular myocardial tissue of patients with HF. Co-IP assay revealed that although HF patients differed sharply from one another in these two parameters, the polyubiquitination level of SERCA2a showed significant positive correlations with the binding degree between WWP1 and SERCA2a (Fig. 3F). Meanwhile, we detected ANP expression in left ventricular myocardium to reflect heart failure severity and found that the protein level of ANP was significantly negative correlated with the content of SERCA2a while positive correlated with both the polyubiquitination level of SERCA2a and the binding degree between WWP1 and SERCA2a in patients with HF (Fig. 3F). Hence, it’s reasonable to point out that WWP1 was correlated with SERCA2a ubiquitination and degradation during HF.

WWP1 destabilizes SERCA2a through catalyzing K27-linked polyubiquitination

Given the findings obtained in failing myocardium, we next investigated whether WWP1 regulated SERCA2a polyubiquitination and degradation. H9C2 cells were transfected with siRNA targeting Wwp1 to knockdown WWP1. Our results indicated that Wwp1 silencing notably increased the protein level of SERCA2a (Fig. 4A). Simultaneously, overexpression of WWP1 in HEK293T cells increased SERCA2a polyubiquitination and decreased its protein level, whereas the expression of a catalytically inactive mutant of WWP1 (WWP1-C890A)30 abrogated this effect, indicating the indispensable role of the catalytic activity of WWP1 in triggering SERCA2a polyubiquitination and degradation (Fig. 4B). Together, these results supported a crucial role of WWP1 in promoting SERCA2a polyubiquitination and degradation.

To confirm the functional domain of SERCA2a recognized by WWP1, we used IntrePro Database (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/interpro/) to clarify the functional domain divisions of SERCA2a. As shown in Fig. 4C, we constructed four truncated SERCA2a mutants denoted as Δ1 [ATPase_P-typ_cation-transptr_N, Del (AA3-77)], Δ2 [E1-E2_ATPase, Del (AA123-329)], Δ3 [P-typ_ATPase, Del (AA330-752)] and Δ4 [ATPase_P-typ_cation-transptr_C, Del (AA783-986)]. Plasmids coding Flag-tagged truncated SERCA2a mutants (Δ1, Δ2, Δ3 and Δ4) were separately transfected into HEK293T cells along with Myc-tagged WWP1 plasmid. All four truncated proteins detected in the whole cell lysates were accompanied with varying degrees of polyubiquitination and only Δ4 group had a clear band of truncated SERCA2a with the slightest polyubiquitination (Fig. 4C). In addition, the level of Myc-WWP1 in the immune complexes of Flag-tagged truncated proteins was much lower in either Δ3 group or Δ4 group than that in Δ1 group and Δ2 group, and the Δ4 group was the lowest (Fig. 4C). Compared with naturally occurring cellular proteins, truncated proteins were inclined to misfold because of their lacking of normal amino acid sequences, and thus likely to be ubiquitinated and degraded. Despite a lower level of Myc-WWP1 in immune complexes in Δ3 group, the expected band of truncated SERCA2a was not obvious in it, and the most intense polyubiquitination occurred (Fig. 4C). Consequently, it was reasonable to suggest that the majority of Δ3 were rapidly polyubiquitinated and degraded after synthesized. Taken together, these results indicated that WWP1 recognized and bound to the ATPase_P-typ_cation-transptr_C domain of SERCA2a.

Ubiquitin is a highly conserved small protein containing seven lysine residues (K6, K11, K27, K29, K33, K48 and K63) and a N-terminal methionine residue (M1), ubiquitously expressed in all eukaryotic cells. Successive rounds of E3-catalyzed reactions can produce substrates with isopeptide-linked polyubiquitin chains31. All seven lysine and one methionine residues can constitute linkage points during polyubiquitin chain elongation, and all possible linkage types are able to co-exist in cells7. To reveal the type of polyubiquitin chain generated on SERCA2a by WWP1, we substituted each of the lysine residues on ubiquitin with arginine individually or all at the same time (K6R, K11R, K27R, K29R, K33R, K48R, K63R and K0) and evaluated their effects on SERCA2a polyubiquitination in HEK293T cells transfected with Flag-SERCA2a, Myc-WWP1 and HA-Ub plasmids simultaneously. The result of Co-IP assay showed that WWP1 indeed catalyzed the polyubiquitination of SERCA2a and only when lysine residue at position 27 or 29 mutated into arginine, the WWP1-mediated SERCA2a polyubiquitination was impeded significantly (Fig. 4D). Moreover, there was no significant difference between K27R group and K0 group in the degree of SERCA2a polyubiquitination. Then, we retained one lysine residue while removed the other six lysine residues with arginine substitution (K6, K11, K27, K29, K33, K48, K63) and compared their effects on SERCA2a polyubiquitination. Although the degree of SERCA2a polyubiquitination in K63 group was significantly higher than that in K0 group, it was still remarkably lower than that in WT group. Only the K27 group had the degree of SERCA2a polyubiquitination comparable to the WT group (Fig. 4E). In conclusion, these results demonstrated that WWP1 destabilized SERCA2a through promoting its K27-linked polyubiquitination.

Considering that K27-linked chains were atypical linkage type and SERCA2a had a long half-life (Fig. 2K), we next further explored the subsequent proteolytic mechanism of WWP1-mediated K27-linked polyubiquitinated SERCA2a. MG132, 3-Methyladenine (3-MA), Chloroquine (CQ), Aloxistatin (E64d) and Calpeptin were utilized to inhibit corresponding degradation pathways. Intriguingly, after transfecting HEK293T cells with HA-K27, Myc-WWP1 and Flag-SERCA2a plasmids, we found that E64d and Calpeptin had no effect on SERCA2a degradation while MG132, 3-MA and CQ could slow down the degradation rate to varying extents (Fig. 4F). Collectively, these results suggested that both the proteasome system and the lysosome pathway were involved in WWP1-mediated K27-linked SERCA2a degradation, and the latter was dominant.

Fluorofenidone attenuates the binding of WWP1 to SERCA2a and SERCA2a polyubiquitination catalyzed by WWP1

To predict potential binding sites of SERCA2a where fluorofenidone might interact, we then carried out a molecular docking and simulation study using MOE (2023). We detected four binding pockets on SERCA2a suitable for fluorofenidone binding (Fig. 5A). These four pockets were selected as docking regions to perform molecular docking with different conformations of fluorofenidone, respectively. After conducting SERCA2a-fluorofenidone docking via induced fit docking, a total of 20 optimal docking modes from four binding pockets were reserved. Based on the binding energy, we further analyzed these interaction modes and determined the best mode, of which the docking score was estimated to be −5.36 kcal/mol. In this mode, fluorofenidone occupied the third binding pocket and formed hydrogen bonds with Gln758, Asp812 and Glu917 (Fig. 5B). Given that binding energy of less than −5 kcal/mol indicated a strong affinity between donor and acceptor32, it was rational to suggest that fluorofenidone could spontaneously and stably bind to Gln758, Asp812, and Glu917 in SERCA2a.

We then mutated Gln758, Asp812, and Glu917 simultaneously to alanine to corroborate their involvements in fluorofenidone-SERCA2a interaction. Plasmid coding mutated SERCA2a (Flag-SERCA2a-Q758A/D812A/E917A) or Flag-SERCA2a-WT plasmid together with Myc-WWP1-WT plasmid and HA-Ub plasmid were transfected into HEK293T cells. Forty-eight hours after transfection, HEK293T cells were exposed to MG132 with or without fluorofenidone. Reciprocal Co-IP analyses indicated that fluorofenidone significantly weakened the interaction between SERCA2a and WWP1 and the polyubiquitination level of SERCA2a; moreover, fluorofenidone no longer inhibited the interaction and SERCA2a polyubiquitination when Gln758, Asp812 and Glu917 were mutated to Ala simultaneously (Fig. 5C; Fig. S4A–S4E in the Data Supplement). To further confirm the key residue among them, we substituted Gln758, Asp812 and Glu917 with alanine separately (Flag-SERCA2a-Q758A, Flag-SERCA2a-D812A and Flag-SERCA2a-E917A) and carried out reciprocal Co-IP assay after the same treatment as above. The results showed that the mutation of Gln758, Asp812 or Glu917 could reduce the inhibitory effect of fluorofenidone to differing extents, however, only when they were substituted together could this inhibitory effect be eliminated (Fig. 5D; Fig. S4F–S4J in the Data Supplement). Thus, Gln758, Asp812 and Glu917 contemporaneously participated in fluorofenidone-SERCA2a interaction. In addition, we employed purified mutant SERCA2a protein (SERCA2a-Q758A/D812A/E917A) to conduct SPR assay and found that the KD value of fluorofenidone / SERCA2a-Q758A/D812A/E917A was 8.61 μM (Fig. 5E, F), which was 34.7 times as large as that of fluorofenidone / SERCA2a, indicating a substantial reduction in binding strength after mutation.

Fluorofenidone protects against TAC-induced heart failure

In an effort to determine whether fluorofenidone could prevent heart failure induced by pressure overload, we subjected WT mice to TAC surgery. Fluorofenidone treatment was initiated 1 day after performing TAC and maintained for 8 weeks. At Day 56, transthoracic echocardiography was used to determine the effect of fluorofenidone on cardiac function in TAC mice. Echocardiographic analysis showed conspicuous decreases in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and left ventricular fraction shortening (LVFS) in TAC mice, while treating with fluorofenidone significantly alleviated such changes (Fig. 6A, C), suggesting that fluorofenidone treatment lessened TAC-induced cardiac function deterioration.

Next, we focused on examining the effect of fluorofenidone on TAC-induced cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis. In comparison with the sham group, the heart weight/tibia length ratio (HW/TL) and the heart weight/body weight ratio (HW/BW) in the TAC group markedly increased by 50% and 61%, respectively (Fig. 6D, E). Besides, wheat germ agglutinin (WGA) staining revealed that the cross-sectional area of the cardiomyocyte was augmented remarkably by TAC (Fig. 6F). These alterations were all normalized by fluorofenidone. In line with these, the elevated left ventricular end-systolic internal diameter (LVIDs) and left ventricular end-diastolic internal diameter (LVIDd) caused by TAC were attenuated notably by fluorofenidone treatment (Table 1). Consistent with our in vitro findings (Fig. 1A, B), the significantly enhanced left ventricular myocardial mRNA levels of Nppa and Nppb in TAC mice were markedly suppressed by fluorofenidone (Fig. 6G, H). Meanwhile, WB revealed that remarkably increased protein levels of ANP, brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) and β-MyHC as well as decreased protein level of α-myosin heavy chain (α-MyHC) in pressure-overloaded left ventricle could be significantly reversed by fluorofenidone (Fig. 6I; Fig. S5A–S5D in the Data Supplement). These results indicated that fluorofenidone protected the heart from TAC-induced cardiac hypertrophy. The mitigation of TAC-induced cardiac fibrosis by fluorofenidone was evidenced by Masson’s trichrome (MT) staining and real-time quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) assay. MT staining showed that the myocardial fibrotic area in the TAC group was 3.61 times larger than that in the sham group, and this increase was significantly attenuated by fluorofenidone (Fig. 6J). Besides, RT-qPCR assay showed that fluorofenidone reduced TAC–induced upregulated mRNA levels of Col1a1, Col3a1, Fn1, and Postn in left ventricular myocardium (Fig. 6K–N).

To confirm our in vitro study findings (Fig. 2A, B; Fig. S2A and S2D in the Data Supplement), we examined mRNA and protein levels of SERCA2a in left ventricular tissues of mice. As expected, pressure overload markedly decreased the mRNA and protein levels of SERCA2a in heart tissues by 90% and 41%, respectively. After fluorofenidone treatment, SERCA2a contents in left ventricular myocardium were returned to a comparable level as that in the sham group, whereas its mRNA level showed no significant change compared with the TAC group (Fig. 7A, B). Additionally, the interaction between WWP1 and SERCA2a as well as the polyubiquitination level of SERCA2a was notably reduced by fluorofenidone, consistent with our in vitro study (Fig. 7B, C; Fig. S6A–S6D in the Data Supplement). These discoveries validated that fluorofenidone upregulated the protein level of SERCA2a via inhibiting its degradation rather than promoting its synthesis. Interestingly, ultrastructural analysis by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) showed that TAC mice had a significantly increased number of double membrane-bound autophagic vacuoles than sham group mice, which was decreased by fluorofenidone (Fig. 7D). A significant increase in the microtubule-associated protein 1 light chain 3 beta II (LC3B-II) to LC3B-I ratio and a dramatical decrease in the abundance of sequestosome 1 (SQSTM1, also called p62) were found in TAC mice, while fluorofenidone treatment pronouncedly reversed both trends (Fig. 6I; Fig. S5E, S5F in the Data Supplement), suggesting fluorofenidone might attenuate TAC-induced autophagic influx.

In view of calcium reuptake function of SERCA2a, we recorded intracellular calcium transient and sarcomere contraction in isolated single left ventricular myocyte to explore the effect of fluorofenidone on calcium handling. In comparison with cardiomyocytes isolated from sham-group mice, cardiomyocytes from TAC mice displayed a diminished calcium transient amplitude (Fig. 7G) and a prolonged time constant (τ) of the calcium transient decay (Fig. 7H), implying the reuptake of calcium into SR was impaired. Accompany with the impaired calcium cycling, reduced sarcomere peak shortening (Fig. 7J) and decreased maximal rate of contraction (Fig. 7K) and relaxation (Fig. 7L) occurred in pressure-overloaded cardiomyocytes. As anticipated, fluorofenidone pronouncedly improved the impaired calcium cycling and sarcomere contractility in TAC mice (Fig. 7E–L).

Altogether, these results demonstrated that fluorofenidone regulated SERCA2a to protect against pressure overload-induced heart failure.

Discussion

In the present study, we delineate how fluorofenidone protects against cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and HF. Mechanistically, in response to hypertrophic stimulation, WWP1 recognizes, combines with and then destabilizes SERCA2a by catalyzing its K27-linked polyubiquitination, contributing to the progression of pathological cardiac hypertrophy and HF. Whereas, through forming hydrogen bonds with Gln758, Asp812 and Glu917 in SERCA2a, fluorofenidone disrupts the interaction between WWP1 and SERCA2a, thus suppressing the polyubiquitination of SERCA2a and enhancing SERCA2a stability and activity. Besides, SERCA2a knockdown blocks the protective effects of fluorofenidone.

As terminally differentiated cells, individual cardiomyocytes undergo pathological hypertrophy, accompanied by the defects in the regulation of Ca2+-handling proteins, in the presence of pressure overload or volume overload33,34. Due to sympathetic overactivity, voltage-gated L-type Ca2+ channel (LTCC) is phosphorylated and downregulated35,36, ryanodine receptor 2 (RyR2) is hyperphosphorylated37, SERCA2a expression and activity are diminished5 and Na+/Ca2+ exchanger 1 (NCX1) expression and activity are enhanced38, causing the loss of calcium homeostasis in failing heart. Dysfunction of these Ca2+-handling proteins not only decreases Ca2+ release initiated by the action potential but also reduces Ca2+ reuptake and leads to the diastolic sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca2+ leak, thus seriously impairing myocardial systolic contraction and diastolic relaxation and resulting in HF progression. Among these proteins, SERCA2a pump is responsible for around 71% of the calcium turnover in human cardiomyocytes39, arousing particular interest. Owing to the promising results of previous overexpression studies in vivo and in vitro40–42, AAV1/SERCA2a gene therapy advanced to clinical trials. Whilst the results of calcium upregulation by percutaneous administration of gene therapy in cardiac disease (CUPID) 1 trial were encouraging43, a larger CUPID 2 trial, which was a randomized, multinational, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 2b trial, failed to replicate the positive outcomes observed in CUPID 1 trial44. The exact reason for the failure of AAV1/SERCA2a gene therapy in CUPID 2 trial has not been fully understood, but the low proportion of empty viral capsids, the presence of neutralising antibodies and the inefficient delivery method have been proposed as contributing factors to the neutral results45. Besides, SERCA2a expression and activity can be regulated by transcription factors (e.g., Sp146), non-coding RNAs (e.g., lncRNA ZFAS147 and circITCH48), binding proteins (e.g., PLN49, DWORF50 and S100A151) and post-translational modifications (e.g. Ubiquitination52, SUMOylation53, Acetylation54 and Glutathionylation55), thus SERCA2a overexpression might be insufficient to positively impact the HF progression. Therefore, it is of great clinical significance to elucidate the mechanisms that regulate SERCA2a expression and activity.

Polyubiquitination is a versatile post-translational modification. Generally speaking, K48-linked polyubiquitin chains transfer substrate proteins to the proteasome for degradation, whereas other types of polyubiquitin chains perform proteolytic as well as nonproteolytic functions7,56. In line with previous studies28, we find that SERCA2a content in left ventricular myocardium is negative correlated with heart failure severity and the polyubiquitination level of SERCA2a is positive correlated with heart failure severity. Yang et al.57 and Ye et al.28 report that SERCA2a is degraded by the 26S proteasome after the formation of K48-linked polyubiquitin chain. However, the results of our study show that WWP1-mediated K27-linked polyubiquitinated SERCA2a is degraded dominantly through the lysosome pathway. Of note, the proteasome system mainly degrades short-lived proteins while the lysosome pathway mainly degrades long-lived proteins58, and these two pathways in some cases can synergistically degrade the same substrate through ubiquitination59. Intriguingly, we find that SERCA2a content in cardiomyocytes only decreases by 36.03% after CHX treatment for 24 h, which is consistent with the study by Moshal et al.60, suggesting the slow degradation rate of SERCA2a. Besides, WWP1-catalyzed ubiquitin-mediated lysosomal degradation of substrate has already been reported13. In this study, E3 ubiquitin ligase WWP1 is identified to interact with SERCA2a directly and destabilize SERCA2a by catalyzing K27-linked polyubiquitination. Meanwhile, the polyubiquitination level of SERCA2a positively correlates with the binding degree of WWP1 and SERCA2a in patients with HF. Zhao and colleagues have shown that WWP1 deficiency suppresses pressure overload–induced pathological cardiac hypertrophy, and they owe the protective effects to the inhibition of WWP1-mediated K27-linked ubiquitin multichain assembly on DVL214. One possibility to explain the distinct findings is that the effects of WWP1-mediated K27-linked polyubiquitination vary on different substrates. More studies are needed to determine whether these two mechanisms exist cross talk. In addition, further studies to confirm the exact mechanism through which the interaction between SERCA2a and WWP1 is regulated during the HF progression are needed to be done as well.

After CUPID 2 trial terminated early, some researchers begin to conduct drug therapy trials targeting SERCA2a in HF61–63. Despite having been proven preclinically effective, istaroxime, a Na-K ATPase inhibitor with the unique property of SERCA2a activation, has no significant effect on myocardial relaxation and a moderate improvement of cardiac contractility in clinical trial64. PST3093, a long-lasting metabolite of istaroxime with entirely selective for SERCA2a, and compound 8, a derivative of PST3093, have already been shown to improve cardiac function in a rat model of streptozotocin (STZ)-induced diabetic cardiomyopathy, respectively65,66. These compounds are defined as SERCA2a agonist, for they enhance SERCA2a activity through relieving its inhibition by PLN while they have no influence on the protein level of SERCA2a. However, in light of the fact that SERCA2a expression and activity are reduced together in failing myocardium6, the long-term efficacy of SERCA2a agonists may be questionable in patients with HF. Fluorofenidone, a derivative of pirfenidone with equivalent antifibrotic activity, lower toxicity and longer half-life15, has turned out to alleviate renal, hepatic, cardiac and pulmonary fibrosis via regulating diverse signaling pathways (e.g. PI3K/Akt/mTOR16,19, MAPK17, TGF-β1/Smad18, RIPK3/MLKL67, Fas/FasL68, NF-κB69, GSK-3β17, eIF3a70 and STAT371). Herein, we find that fluorofenidone can increase the protein level and activity of SERCA2a in vivo and in vitro. Furthermore, using DARTS, CETSA and SPR approaches, we identify SERCA2a as a direct binding partner of fluorofenidone at cellular level. Moreover, the key residues participating in fluorofenidone-SERCA2a interaction are tentatively explored by molecular docking, Co-IP, and SPR assays. Consistently, we also find that cardiac function deterioration, myocardial hypertrophy, sarcomere contractility impairment, and calcium dyshomeostasis induced by TAC are improved by fluorofenidone. Further, these cardioprotective actions of fluorofenidone were attenuated in varying degrees by SERCA2a knockdown. Interestingly, TAC-induced upregulation of cardiomyocyte autophagy, representing a maladaptive response to hemodynamic stress72, is attenuated by fluorofenidone. It has been reported that the disruption of calcium homeostasis can trigger ER stress which typically activates autophagy via inducing the unfolded protein response (UPR)73,74. In view of above results, we deem that the reduction of autophagy by fluorofenidone should stem from restoring calcium homeostasis via its targeted protection of SERCA2a, which in turn further stabilizes SERCA2a. However, more research is needed to confirm this conclusion.

Our study identifies SERCA2a as a previously unrecognized target of fluorofenidone, but it is not the only one. As mentioned above, fluorofenidone has multiple known molecular targets and pathways of action, which might be independent of its influence on SERCA2a. In other words, our data establish SERCA2a as an essential target through which fluorofenidone exerts cardioprotective effects, however, it is unlikely to be the sole mediator. Besides, although fluorofenidone shows lower toxicity and higher activity than pirfenidone (PFD)75, a relatively high daily dose is still required for it to take effect due to its short biologic half-life76. In addition, our present study initiates fluorofenidone treatment from the first day after TAC, failing to observe whether fluorofenidone can reverse severe cardiac remodeling. Of note, all in vivo experiments were conducted exclusively in male mice to eliminate the confounding cardioprotective effect of estrogen. However, this sex-specific design limits the generalizability of our findings to female patients. Therefore, before clinical translation, future studies with adequate sex-based subgroup analyses are warranted to determine whether sex-specific dosing or treatment strategies are needed. Even though fluorofenidone may not be applied immediately in clinic, it can be used as a basis for further developing other drugs.

In conclusion, our present findings provide convergent evidence that fluorofenidone stabilizes SERCA2a by inhibiting its WWP1-catalyzed K27-linked polyubiquitination and prevent pressure overload-induced heart failure. We identify fluorofenidone as a cardioprotective agent which acts through targeted inhibition of SERCA2a polyubiquitination, thereby enhancing its stability and activity. This SERCA2a-targeted small-molecule drug may provide a promising therapeutic strategy for treating cardiac hypertrophy and failure.

Methods

Animals

Our animal experiment protocol was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Sun Yat-Sen University (Approval No. SYSU-IACUC-2024-000398). Wild-type C57BL/6 mice (male, 22–25 g, 5–6 week old) were purchased from the Guangdong Province Animal Center (Guangzhou, China), housed in the experimental animal center of Sun Yat-Sen University (Guangzhou, China), a specific pathogen free (SPF)-grade facility under a 12 h light–12 h dark cycle with ambient temperature (24 ± 1 °C) and relative humidity (50–60%). All perioperative care and surgical procedures were in strict accordance with the animal care protocols of Sun Yat-Sen University (No. 2017-692) and conformed to the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals, published by the National Institutes of Health.

All mice were allowed ad libitum access to food and water. For anaesthesia, pentobarbital was administered to mice via intraperitoneal injection at a dose of 50 mg/kg body weight. For euthanasia, mice were first injected with pentobarbital intraperitoneally at a dose of 200 mg/kg, and then euthanized by cervical dislocation. Death was confirmed by the absence of a heartbeat and reflex.

On one hand, wild-type mice were subjected to sham operation or TAC surgery after being randomly divided into three groups: 1) Sham group: sham operation + vehicle, 2) TAC group: TAC + vehicle, 3) Fluorofenidone group: TAC + 250 mg·kg−1·day−1 fluorofenidone. From 1 day after the operation, fluorofenidone or vehicle was administered to mice intragastrically for 8 weeks.

On the other hand, for rescue experiment, wild-type mice were divided into five groups: 1) S group: AAV9-cTnT-shNC + sham operation + 0 mg·kg−1·day−1 fluorofenidone; N- group: AAV9-cTnT-shNC + TAC operation + 0 mg·kg−1·day−1 fluorofenidone; N+ group: AAV9-cTnT-shNC + TAC operation + 250 mg·kg−1·day−1 fluorofenidone; A- group: AAV9-cTnT-shAtp2a2 + TAC operation + 0 mg·kg−1·day−1 fluorofenidone; A+ group: AAV9-cTnT-shAtp2a2 + TAC operation + 250 mg·kg−1·day−1 fluorofenidone. To knock down SERCA2a, mice were injected with AAV9-cTnT-shAtp2a2 via the tail vein 2 weeks before operation. From 1 day after the operation, fluorofenidone or vehicle was administered to mice intragastrically for 8 weeks.

Human heart samples

Human left ventricular myocardial tissues were obtained from failing hearts at the time of heart transplant surgery. The primary etiology of these HF patients (the left ventricular ejection fraction < 35%) is dilated cardiomyopathy. The use of human left ventricular myocardial tissues for the present study was approved by institutional review board of the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients. Our study protocol conformed to the ethical guidelines that govern the use of human tissues outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. These tissue samples have been conserved for further analysis in the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University, and can be shared with other researchers by contacting the corresponding authors.

Recombinant adeno-associated virus 9 construction and delivery

Adeno-associated virus 9 (AAV9) bearing shRNA against Atp2a2 under the cardiomyocyte-specific cardiac troponin T (cTnT) promoter (AAV9-cTnT-shAtp2a2) and AAV9 bearing scramble shRNA (AAV9-cTnT-shNC) were constructed and generated by Vigene Biosciences (Shandong, China). The sequences of scramble shRNA (NC) and Atp2a2 shRNA oligos are as follows: NC (5’-CAGGCAGAAGTATGCAAAGCAT-3’) and Atp2a2 (5’- CTGGGTTTCCTGAGGCTTTAATTAGTGAAGCCACAGATGTAATTAAAGCCTCAGGAAACCCAA −3’).

The average viral titers were 6.3 × 1013 and 1.67 × 1014 vector genome (vg) particles/ml for the AAV9-cTnT-shAtp2a2 and AAV9-cTnT-shNC, respectively. Viral solution was diluted with sterile saline. A total of 5 × 1012 vg/ml AAV9-cTnT-shNC or AAV9-cTnT-shAtp2a2 in 100 µL was intravenously injected into the mouse tail vein using a 29-gauge insulin syringe.

Transverse aortic constriction (TAC)

TAC was performed as described in our previous study77. Briefly, after anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital (50 mg/kg), mice were fixed in a supine position and ventilated (RoVent Jr, Kent Scientific Corp., Torrington, CT). The thoracic cavity was entered from the second intercostal space. Then the thymus was pushed aside gently by retractors to expose the aortic arch. A 6-0 monofilament polypropylene suture that had been threaded through a blunted 26-gauge syringe needle was passed under the aortic arch. The suture was tied around the transverse aorta quickly with a 3 mm-long blunted 27-gauge needle between the innominate and left carotid arteries. After ligation, the needle was withdrawn to generate a constriction of 0.4 mm in diameter. The surgical procedure of sham operation was identical except for the ligation.

Transthoracic echocardiography

Two dimensional guided M-mode transthoracic echocardiography was performed 8 weeks after TAC using Vevo 2100 System (VisualSonics, Toronto, Canada). Mice were lightly anesthetized with isoflurane (1.5%–3%) via continuous inhalation and maintained with spontaneous respiration at 37°C. Left ventricular (LV) end-diastolic internal diameter (LVIDd), LV end-systolic internal diameter (LVIDs), LV end-diastolic posterior wall thickness (LVPWd), LV end-systolic posterior wall thickness (LVPWs), interventricular septum end-diastolic thickness (IVSd), and interventricular septum end-systolic thickness (IVSs) were measured in the short-axis M-mode view at the level of papillary muscles. The LV end-diastolic volume (LVEDV), the LV end-systolic volume (LVESV), the LV ejection fraction (LVEF) and the percentage of LV fractional shortening (LVFS) were calculated as follows, respectively: LVEDV = [7.0/(2.4 + LVIDd)] × LVIDd3, LVESV = [7.0/(2.4 + LVIDs)] × LVIDs3, LVEF = [(LVEDV – LVESV)/LVEDV] × 100% and LVFS = [(LVIDd - LVIDs)/LVIDd] × 100%. All parameters were measured over the course of at least three repeated cardiac cycles by a technician ignorant of grouping.

Tissue harvesting and cardiac weight indexes

Mice were weighed and then euthanized by cervical dislocation under anesthesia. The hearts were harvested promptly, and then the left atrium and right atrium were cut off. Heart weight (HW) and tibia length (TL) were recorded. Then the heart weight-to-body weight ratio (HW/BW, mg/g) and the heart weight-to-tibia length ratio (HW/TL, mg/mm) were calculated.

Morphological analysis

A ring of heart tissue cut at the level of the papillary muscles was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and embedded in paraffin. Five μm thick sections were stained with Masson’s trichrome (MT) and wheat germ agglutinin (WGA). MT staining was used to evaluate cardiac fibrosis. For each sample, eight random fields were captured under 200 magnification. Positive staining areas were analyzed using Image Pro Plus (Version 6.0, Media Cybernetics, Silver Springs, MD). Fibrotic area = (positive area/total area) × 100%. WGA staining was used to determine the cross-sectional area of cardiomyocytes. For each sample, the cross-sectional areas of one hundred cardiomyocytes were measured.

Isolation of neonatal rat cardiomyocytes (NRCMs)

Neonatal rat cardiomyocytes (NRCMs) were isolated from 1–3-day-old Sprague-Dawley rats, as described previously78. Briefly, the minced ventricles of 1–3-day-old Sprague-Dawley rats were dissociated with trypsin and collagenase in succession. At last, the cell suspension was plated in dishes and allowed to attach at 37°C for 1 hour to reduce the number of nonmyocytes. The unattached cardiomyocytes were collected and plated into new culture plates for in vitro experiments.

Cell culture and treatments

NRCMs, H9C2 cells and Human embryonic kidney–293 T (HEK293T) cells were all cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM, Thermo Fisher Scientific, No. C11995500BT) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Thermo Fisher Scientific, No. 10270106), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 U/mL streptomycin (penicillin-streptomycin, Thermo Fisher Scientific, No. 15140163). NRCMs and H9C2 cells were serum starved for 24 h before 50 μM phenylephrine (PE) treatment (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, No. P6126) with or without different concentrations of fluorofenidone (1, 2, and 4 mM) for 72 h.

Plasmids, siRNAs and cell transfections

All plasmids used in our present study were designed and obtained from Miaoling Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China. The eukaryotic expression plasmids were transfected into HEK293T cells with Lipofectamine 3000 reagent, according to the manufacturer’s protocol. All small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) used in our present study were designed and obtained from Umine Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Guangzhou, China. The siRNA sequence targeting rat SERCA2a is 5’-CGAGCAGUUUGAAGACUUAdTdT-3’. The siRNA sequences targeting rat WWP1 are 5’-GGACUUGAAACAAGUGCUGUUdTdT-3’ and 5’-UUGGCUGUUGAAGGUACUAUUdTdT-3’. Transfections of NRCMs and H9C2 cells were carried out using the Lipofectamine RNA-iMAX transfection reagent (Invitrogen, CA, USA, No. 13778-150) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol.

Cell viability by cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

Cell viability was assessed by the Cell Counting Kit-8 (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. C0039) assay following manufacturer’s protocol. After serum starvation for 24 h, H9C2 cells were treated with different concentrations of fluorofenidone (0.5, 1, 2, 4 and 8 mM) for 72 h. After CCK-8 was added, the 96-well plates were incubated at 37 °C for an additional 2 h. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (Sunrise, TECAN, Switzerland). Cell viability = (absorbance of the treated cells - absorbance of the blank)/(absorbance of the untreated cells - absorbance of the blank) × 100%.

Rhodamine phalloidin immunofluorescence staining

After treatment, NRCMs were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100, and stained with rhodamine-phalloidin (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. C2207) at room temperature in the dark for 1 h. Nuclei were stained with the DAPI (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. C1002) at room temperature in the dark for 5 min. Immunofluorescence staining was viewed and captured using the inverted microscopy (Leica DMI8, Wetzlar, Germany).

Extraction of sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) and measurement of SERCA2a activity

After treatment, the extraction of sarcoplasmic reticulum from NRCMs was performed with endoplasmic reticulum extraction kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China, No. EX2690) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Then the SERCA2a activity was detected using an inorganic phosphorus colorimetric method with an ultramicro-Ca2+-ATPase assay kit (Jiancheng, Nanjing, China, No. A070-4) following manufacturer’s protocol.

Protein extraction and western blot

Proteins were extracted from NRCMs, H9C2 cells, HEK293T cells or the left ventricular myocardial tissues of human or mice by Cell lysis buffer for Western and IP (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P0013) containing proteinase inhibitor (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P1046-1) and phosphatase inhibitor (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P1046-2). Bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein quantification kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P0012) was used to determine the protein concentration. Protein samples were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) in 10% gels and transferred to PVDF membranes (Merck Millipore, Billerica, MA, No. ISEQ00010). After being blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) at room temperature for 1 h, the membranes were incubated with specific antibodies at 4°C overnight. After incubated with appropriate horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-linked secondary antibody at room temperature for 1 h, the protein bands were visualized with chemiluminescent HRP substrate (Merck Millipore, No. WBKLS0100). The grayscale of the bands was quantified by Image J Software. Antibodies used for western blot in our present study were as follows: α-MyHC (1:5000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, No. 22281-1-AP), β-MyHC (1:2000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, No. 22280-1-AP), ANP (1:100, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, CA, USA, No. sc-515701), BNP (1:500, Affinity Biosciences, Jiangsu, China, No. DF6902), SERCA2a (1:5000, Abcam, MA, USA, No. ab150435), WWP1 (1:100, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, CA, USA, No. sc-390897), GAPDH (1:3000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, No. 60004-1-Ig), β-actin (1:5000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, No. 60008-1-Ig), Ubiquitin (1:200, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, CA, USA, No. sc-8017), FLAG tag (1:5000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, No. 20543-1-AP), GST tag (1:1000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, No. 10000-0-AP), Myc tag (1:1000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, No. 16286-1-AP), HA tag (1:5000, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, No. 51064-2-AP).

RNA extraction and real-time quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

The total RNA was extracted from NRCMs, H9C2 cells or the left ventricular myocardial tissues of mice by TRIzol reagent (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, No. T9424) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. And the reverse transcription was performed using the PrimeScript RT reagent Kit (Takara, Tokyo, Japan, No. RR036A) following the manufacturer’s protocol. The RT-qPCR was carried out by SYBR-Green Master mix (Takara, No. RR820B) in LightCycle480 II (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). GAPDH was used as the internal reference gene for the normalization. Drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS) assay

The drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS) assay was performed as described previously23. Briefly, H9C2 cells were harvested and lysed in NP40 buffer (Solarbio, Beijing, China, No. N8032) containing proteinase inhibitor (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P1046-1) and phosphatase inhibitor (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P1046-2) for 15 min on ice. The cell lysates were separated into two tubes. One tube was incubated with 4 mM fluorofenidone and the other one was treated with an equivalent amount of DMSO at room temperature for 1 h. After incubation, these two groups were further subdivided into different subgroups (5 aliquots, respectively). Then these tubes were added with pronase (Roche, Basel, Switzerland, No. 10165921001) at a final concentration of 0, 1:300, 1:600, 1:1200 or 1:2400 (pronase: total protein) and incubated at room temperature for 30 min. These protein samples were then analyzed by western blot.

Cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA)

The cellular thermal shift assay (CETSA) was performed as described previously24. Briefly, fluorofenidone or equivalent amount of DMSO was added to cell culture medium to get a final concentration of 4 mM. After a 24 h incubation, H9C2 cells were digested and collected. Cell suspensions of these two groups were respectively divided into 12 aliquots and subjected to a temperature gradient from 37 °C to 70 °C, with a 3 °C interval. After being heated at designated temperature for 3 min, tubes containing the cell suspensions were removed immediately and cooled at room temperature for 3 min. After that, the heat-treated cell suspensions were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Then the frozen cell suspensions were thawed quickly. After three freezing and thawing cycles, cell lysates were centrifuged to collect the supernatants. The supernatants collected were subsequently subjected to western blot.

Molecular docking and simulation study

The three-dimensional structure of SERCA2a was constructed by AlphaFold algorithm, followed by protonation and structural optimization. The molecular structure of fluorofenidone was constructed through the builder tool of MOE (Version 2023), and then the three-dimensional structure of fluorofenidone was generated based on the energy minimization. Subsequently, molecular docking was performed by MOE (Version 2023). Four binding pockets suitable for fluorofenidone were determined by the SiteFinder plugin of MOE. Fluorofenidone was docked into these four pockets separately to generate 30 optimal docking modes in each docking area. The induced-fit algorithm was employed to optimize these docking modes and then the binding energies of the optimized docking modes were calculated by the GBVI/WSA δG scoring function. The lower the binding energy is, the more stable the docking mode is. Finally, based on the result of molecular docking, the key residues for fluorofenidone-SERCA2a interaction were identified.

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) assay

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) assays were performed with Biacore 1 K (Cytiva, Uppsala, Sweden) to study the binding kinetics of fluorofenidone with wild-type or mutated SERCA2a protein. Purified wild-type or mutated SERCA2a protein was bound to an activated carboxymethylated 5 (CM5) sensor chip (Cytiva, Uppsala, Sweden, No. BR-1005-30) using the amine coupling method. Fluorofenidone was diluted with running buffer for six concentrations and then injected over the immobilized surface in a continuous flow of running buffer at 25 °C (flow rate, 30 μl/min; association time, 60 s; dissociation time, 60 s). The association rate constant (Ka), dissociation rate constant (Kd) and equilibrium dissociation constant (KD) were calculated.

Coomassie blue staining

Proteins were loaded onto the electrophoresis system. Following electrophoretic separation by SDS-PAGE, the gel was stained with Coomassie brilliant blue solution (Solarbio, Beijing, China, No. P1305) in accordance with the manufacturer’s protocol.

Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assay

H9C2 cells, HEK293T cells or the left ventricular myocardial tissues of human or mice were lysed with Cell lysis buffer for Western and IP (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P0013) containing proteinase inhibitor (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P1046-1) and phosphatase inhibitor (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P1046-2). About 5% of the total lysates were used as the input. The remaining total lysates were incubated with specific primary antibodies or control immunoglobulin G (IgG) at 4 °C on a rotation wheel overnight to form the immunocomplex. The lysates and antibody (immunocomplex) solutions were then incubated with protein-A/G magnetic beads (Selleck, TX, USA, No. B23202) on the rotation wheel at room temperature for 1 h. The resulting protein complexes were washed with lysis buffer more than five times and then subjected to immunoblotting. The antibodies used for immunoprecipitation in our present study were as follows: SERCA2a (1:100, ABclonal, Wuhan, China, No. A11692), WWP1 (1:100, FineTest, Wuhan, China, No. FNab09534), FLAG tag (1:250, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, No. 20543-1-AP), GST tag (1:250, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, No. 10000-0-AP), Myc tag (1:250, Proteintech, Wuhan, China, No. 16286-1-AP).

Immunoprecipitation-mass spectrometry (IP-MS)

After PE treatment with or without 4 mM fluorofenidone for 72 h, H9C2 cells were lysed with reference to the part of Co-immunoprecipitation (Co-IP) assay above. Total lysates were incubated with anti-SERCA2a antibody. The resulting protein complexes were eluted and subjected to SDS-PAGE and analyzed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) (Shanghai Applied Protein Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, P.R. China).

Expression and purification of recombinant protein

The pGEX-4T-1 and pTac-GST-Wwp1 were transformed into E. coli BL21 (DE3). Then recombinant proteins were induced using 0.1 mM isopropyl-β-D-thiogalactoside (IPTG) at 16 °C for 16 h. After induction, bacteria were collected by centrifugation and resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing proteinase inhibitor (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P1046-1), followed by ultrasonication. Recombinant GST-WWP1 and GST proteins were purified by immobilized glutathione beads (Sangon Biotech Co. Ltd., Shanghai, China, No. C600911-0005), and then confirmed by western blot.

HEK293T cells was transfected with overexpression plasmid coding wild-type SERCA2a (Flag-SERCA2a-WT) with Lipofectamine 3000 reagent, according to the manufacturer’s protocol. After overexpression, cells were lysed with Cell lysis buffer for Western and IP (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P0013) containing proteinase inhibitor (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P1046-1) and phosphatase inhibitor (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P1046-2). Recombinant FLAG-SERCA2a protein was purified by Flag-tag protein ip assay kit with agarose gel (Beyotime, Shanghai, China, No. P2202M), and then confirmed by western blot.

GST-pull down assay

GST-pull down assay was performed as described previously14. Briefly, GST or GST-WWP1 proteins expressed in E. coli BL21 were purified using immobilized glutathione beads (Sangon Biotech Co. Ltd., Shanghai, China, No. C600911-0005) and incubated with purified FLAG-SERCA2a protein solution from HEK293T cells transfected with Flag-SERCA2a-WT overnight at 4 °C. After wash, the proteins were eluted at room temperature for 15 min, followed by western blot.

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

Fresh left ventricular tissues were harvested and cut into 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm pieces. Then samples were fixed in 2.5% buffered glutaraldehyde at room temperature for 2 h, followed by dehydration with an ascending concentration gradient of ethanol. Samples were then embedded, cut into 50-nm thick slices, and stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate. Ultrathin sections were imaged by transmission electron microscope (Hitachi HT7700, Japan) at 80 kV.

Isolation of adult cardiomyocyte and cardiac physiology

Adult mouse ventricular cardiomyocytes were enzymatically isolated as described previously79. Briefly, after sacrifice, the heart was quickly removed from the chest and perfused in a Langendorff apparatus with a calcium-free Tyrode buffer at 37 °C for 3 min. Then, the heart was perfused with a calcium-free Tyrode buffer containing 1 mg/mL type Ⅱ collagenase (Worthington, NJ, USA, No. LS004176) for 20 min. Heart was mechanically separated using forceps. Dissociated ventricular cardiomyocytes were gradually exposed to Ca2+ (from 12.5 to 900 µM Ca2+ over 20 min) and plated in glass slides. Then the cardiomyocytes were loaded with 1 μM Fura-2, AM (Invitrogen, CA, USA, No. F1221), the calcium indicator, in the dark at room temperature for 20 min. The cardiomyocytes were electrically paced at 1 Hz with a MyoPacer EP stimulator (IonOptix). Quiescent rod-shaped cardiomyocytes with well-defined striations that reacted to electrical stimulation were regarded as meeting the requirements. Baseline calcium, calcium amplitude, calcium decay tau, peak cell shortening, maximal rate of contraction, and maximal rate of relaxation were recorded and analyzed by IonWizard 7.4 software (IonOptix Co, MA, USA).

Sex as a biological variable

Considering that Estrogen has been shown to be a protective factor against pathological hypertrophic remodeling in pressure-overload, we carried out experiments only in male mice to establish stable and pronounced TAC model. It is unknown whether the findings are relevant for female mice.

Statistics and reproducibility

All quantitative data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, Inc., La Jolla, CA). The data were normalized to the control group, and no data were excluded from any of the experiments. Comparisons between two groups were performed using two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test. Comparisons among groups were performed with one-way ANOVA, followed by Bonferroni’s post hoc test for multiple comparisons. Pearson correlation coefficient (R2) was used to analyze the correlation between 2 variables. P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

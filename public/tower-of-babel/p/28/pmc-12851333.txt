Introduction to the 13th pediatric intestinal failure and rehabilitation symposium (PIFRS) special edition
Authors: Jeffrey Rudolph, Simon Horslen, George Mazariegos
Published: 2025

PIFRS 2024

The 13th biennial Pediatric Intestinal Failure and Rehabilitation Symposium (PIFRS) was held September 26–28, 2024 in Pittsburgh, PA. Situated to occur on the years opposite the Congress of the International Intestinal Rehabilitation and Transplantation Association (CIIRTA), the meeting is designed to bring together experts in the multidisciplinary care of the pediatric intestinal failure patient. The 2024 event boasted 373 registrants from 20 countries spanning 5 continents. Staying true to its multidisciplinary roots, there was roughly an even split between physicians/surgeons and allied health professionals as well as 8 patients/families. There were 63 separate talks split between the traditional “primer” running concurrently with the Allied Health Professional program and two days of the symposium. A total of 65 abstracts were presented in either oral or poster formats, which was the highest number submitted to date.

The theme of PIFRS 2024 was Intestinal Failure 360, ambiguous on purpose, and meant to capture the many facets of pediatric intestinal failure care from different perspectives. For example, talks ranged from prenatal and neonatal care to transition issues facing our young adult patients. The full spectrum of intestinal failure was examined, from reversible to chronic irreversible intestinal failure and beyond with a continued focus on intestinal transplantation. Multiple talks were directed at the burden of intestinal failure from different perspectives such as nutrition/growth and neurodevelopmental outcomes as well as more pragmatic issues including integration into schools/community, cost of care, and the effect of drug shortages. There were sessions directed towards cutting edge research in both intestinal failure and transplant. Perhaps the most compelling discussion were led by patients and families who were willing to share their perspectives on this devastating disease state. While one could argue that none of the topics received the time necessary to fully examine the subject matter, taken as a whole, the symposium encompassed the breadth of intestinal failure and hopefully inspired consideration of the whole picture and collaboration between disciplines.

In this special issue of Intestinal Failure, completed manuscripts originally derived from selected abstracts presented at PIFRS 2024 are presented in full to continue the propagation and sharing of information that is so critical to advance the field. The Directors of PIFRS 2024 would like to express their gratitude to Intestinal Failure in this collaboration and extend an invitation to the 14th PIFRS to be held in Omaha, Nebraska in 2026.

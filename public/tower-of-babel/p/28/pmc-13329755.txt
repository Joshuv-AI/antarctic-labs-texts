TCAD investigation of β-Ga2O3 junction barrier Schottky diode with p-Cr2O3 heterojunction wells
Authors: Phuc Hong Than, Tho Quang Than, Yasushi Takaki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Although β-Ga2O3 Schottky barrier diodes (SBDs) are promising for high-voltage power applications, the lack of effective p-type doping severely limits the realization of junction-based devices. In this paper, TCAD simulations are employed to numerically investigate a vertical β-Ga2O3 junction barrier Schottky (JBS) diode incorporating an interconnected grid of p-type chromium oxide (p-Cr2O3) wells. The wide-bandgap p-Cr2O3, which exhibits a favorable valence-band offset of 2.65 eV and a reported ideality factor as low as 1.07 at the Cr2O3/Ga2O3 heterojunction, enables the formation of effective p-type regions without requiring p-type doping in β-Ga2O3. The simulated β-Ga2O3 JBS diode exhibits a turn-on voltage of approximately 0.8 V, a forward current density of 52.8 A cm−2 at a forward voltage of 3.0 V, and a low reverse leakage current density of 2.0 mA cm−2 at 800 V. Compared with a conventional SBD, the simulated JBS diode shows an increase in breakdown voltage from 390 V to 950 V with only a slight increase in specific on-resistance (from 45.4 to 47.8 mΩ cm2), resulting in a Baliga's figure of merit of 18.88 MW cm−2. The simulation results further indicate that optimal device performance is achieved at a p-Cr2O3 acceptor concentration of NA = 5.0 × 1016 cm−3 and a p-well spacing of WS = 4 µm, for which the electric field distribution becomes more uniform within the drift region. Temperature-dependent simulations predict stable unipolar operation up to 450 K. This predictive design framework provides valuable physical insights and design guidelines for the experimental realization of Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 JBS rectifiers, demonstrating that the proposed heterojunction architecture effectively bypasses the p-type doping bottleneck and offers a viable pathway toward next-generation high-voltage and high-temperature β-Ga2O3 power rectifiers.

Introduction

Beta-gallium oxide (β-Ga2O3) has emerged as one of the most promising ultra-wide-bandgap semiconductors for next-generation power electronics. This is due to its outstanding intrinsic material properties, including an ultra-wide bandgap of approximately 4.8 eV and an exceptionally high critical electric field exceeding 8 MV cm−1.1–8 These properties result in an extremely high theoretical Baliga's figure of merit (BFOM), substantially surpassing those of conventional silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride (GaN) power semiconductors. In addition, the n-type conductivity of β-Ga2O3 can be readily controlled over a wide carrier concentration range (1015–1020 cm−3) through intentional donor doping. Another important advantage is the availability of large-area native substrates fabricated by melt-growth techniques, enabling low-defect crystal growth and providing a feasible route toward scalable manufacturing and commercial implementation. As highlighted in recent comprehensive reviews by M. Higashiwaki,4 further advances in edge termination engineering are essential for mitigating electric field crowding, particularly at the electrode periphery. This remains a major challenge in realizing reliable high-voltage β-Ga2O3 power devices. Consistent with this view, M. Labed et al.8 demonstrated that the implementation of optimized electric field control techniques together with buffer layers can minimize electric-field crowding effects, leading to enhanced breakdown voltage and improved device reliability. Consequently, a variety of vertical β-Ga2O3 power devices, including heterojunction p–n diodes, Schottky barrier diodes (SBDs), high-electron-mobility transistors (HEMTs), and metal–oxide–semiconductor field-effect transistors (MOSFETs), have been developed and demonstrated promising performance.7–25 Among these devices, Schottky barrier diodes are particularly attractive for high-frequency power conversion because of their majority-carrier operation, negligible reverse recovery, and low forward voltage drop. However, under high reverse electric fields, image-force barrier lowering and the relatively limited Schottky barrier height often induce excessive leakage current and premature breakdown. In contrast, conventional p–n diodes provide superior voltage blocking capability and lower leakage current, but they suffer from large forward voltage drops and slow switching speeds caused by minority-carrier injection and charge storage effects. To bridge this trade-off, the junction barrier Schottky (JBS) diode was developed by integrating p-type regions beneath the Schottky contact. Under reverse bias, the depletion regions originating from the p–n junctions laterally expand and electrostatically shield the Schottky interface from excessive electric-field crowding, thereby suppressing leakage current and enhancing the breakdown voltage. Under forward bias, current transport remains dominated by the low-barrier Schottky conduction path, preserving low turn-on voltage and fast switching characteristics. As a result, JBS diodes effectively combine the advantages of both SBDs and p–n diodes for high-voltage and high-frequency applications. However, the realization of JBS structures in β-Ga2O3 is fundamentally hindered by the absence of reliable p-type β-Ga2O3.

To address the absence of reliable p-type β-Ga2O3, significant research efforts have been directed toward heterojunction-based JBS designs utilizing external p-type oxide semiconductors, which form p–n heterojunctions with β-Ga2O3 and enable the depletion-induced electrostatic shielding necessary for leakage-current suppression and breakdown-voltage enhancement.26–31 Among these candidates, NiO has been the most extensively investigated due to its favorable band alignment with β-Ga2O3, which enables a high energy barrier height. However, recent studies have revealed that the NiO/β-Ga2O3 heterointerface suffers from thermal instability when operated at elevated temperatures. In particular, under prolonged thermal stress (e.g., up to 550 °C during thermal cycling), interfacial reactions and intermixing occur at the NiO-Ga2O3 interface, leading to the formation of a ternary compound—the spinel NiGa2O4. This thermally induced degradation has been found to increase leakage current and decrease forward current, ultimately causing degradation of the electrical properties and device reliability.32 Cu2O presents another attractive option owing to its p-type conductivity and thermal stability, enabling an ultra-low turn-on voltage as low as 0.78 V in Cu2O/β-Ga2O3 JBS diodes.23,29 However, the polycrystalline nature of Cu2O films and the presence of interface defects can lead to trap-assisted tunneling (TAT) as the dominant carrier transport mechanism at room temperature, while thermionic emission becomes more important at temperatures above 400 K.33 SnO, on the other hand, exhibits a hole mobility that is approximately one order of magnitude higher than that of NiO. In SnO/β-Ga2O3 heterojunction junction barrier Schottky (HJBS) diode configurations, these devices have achieved a high breakdown voltage of 1375 V with a power figure of merit (PFOM) of 0.37 GW cm−2, while maintaining a reverse leakage current below 2 µA cm−2 at 1250 V.30 However, the p-type conductivity of SnO is highly sensitive to oxygen stoichiometry, and non-optimal growth or annealing conditions can lead to the formation of secondary phases such as metallic Sn or SnO2, which degrade device performance.34,35 Additionally, while SnO has demonstrated outstanding thermal stability up to 300 °C, it undergoes a disproportionation reaction (2SnO → SnO2 + Sn) when annealed above 250–300 °C, which severely deteriorates its electrical properties and limits its processing window.34,35 Consequently, a major materials-design challenge remains the identification of a p-type heterojunction material capable of simultaneously providing a wide bandgap, strong electrostatic shielding, chemical stability, and robust interface reliability under extreme electric-field and temperature conditions. In this context, p-type chromium oxide (α-Cr2O3, hereafter denoted as p-Cr2O3) has recently emerged as a highly promising candidate owing to its excellent thermodynamic stability and favorable chemical compatibility with the Ga2O3 lattice.36–43 As a wide-bandgap oxide semiconductor with intrinsic p-type conductivity, Cr2O3 enables stable operation even at temperatures approaching 600 °C. Mashiko et al.36 reported indirect and direct bandgaps of approximately 3.0 and 3.2 eV, respectively, for α-Cr2O3 based on optical absorption measurements. Furthermore, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy studies by Ghosh et al.40 revealed that the α-Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterojunction exhibits a type-II staggered band alignment with a valence-band offset (ΔEV) of approximately 2.65 ± 0.2 eV and a conduction-band offset (ΔEC) of approximately 0.9 ± 0.2 eV. The large ΔEV effectively suppresses hole injection from the p-Cr2O3 region into the n-β-Ga2O3 drift layer under forward bias, thus preserving majority-carrier-dominated conduction. Ghosh et al. also reported non-commutativity of the band offsets at the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 interface, where the ΔEV varied depending on the deposition sequence because of interface dipoles arising from differences in electronegativity and interfacial bonding configurations.40 Since p-Cr2O3 is deposited on n-β-Ga2O3 in the present work, the experimentally reported ΔEV value of 2.65 eV was adopted in the TCAD simulation. Experimentally fabricated p-Cr2O3/n-β-Ga2O3 heterojunction diodes exhibited an ideality factor as low as 1.07, which is among the closest to unity reported for β-Ga2O3-based heterojunction rectifiers.41 This near-unity ideality factor indicates a high-quality heterointerface with minimal trap-assisted recombination. In addition, capacitance–voltage measurements revealed negligible frequency dispersion from 10 kHz to 1 MHz,41 suggesting a low interface-trap density. The quality of this heterointerface is particularly critical for JBS architectures because excessive interface traps can locally distort the electric-field distribution and promote trap-assisted leakage under reverse bias. Compared with previously reported oxide heterojunction systems, the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 interface therefore appears to provide a more stable electrostatic environment for high-voltage operation. The thermodynamic stability of Cr2O3 is also superior to that of NiO when integrated with β-Ga2O3. Callahan et al.39 calculated the pO2–T phase diagram of the Ga–Cr–O system and experimentally demonstrated stable operation of Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterojunction diodes at 600 °C for 140 h and after 30 thermal cycles between 25 °C and 550 °C without severe phase degradation. The calculated phase diagram confirmed the stable coexistence of Cr2O3 and β-Ga2O3 over a broad range of temperatures and oxygen partial pressures. Moreover, the p-type conductivity of Cr2O3 has been experimentally verified. Jagadish and Kekuda38 reported p-type Cr2O3 thin films deposited by DC reactive magnetron sputtering with hole concentrations approaching 1018 cm−3 and hole mobilities ranging from 1.48 to 10.29 cm2 V−1 s−1 depending on the deposition conditions. The p-type conductivity was attributed to Cr3+ vacancies within the Cr2O3 lattice. Other studies reported lower hole concentrations of approximately 1 × 1016 cm−3 with hole mobilities of approximately 0.64 cm2 V−1 s−1,41 indicating that the electrical properties of Cr2O3 strongly depend on growth conditions and can therefore be tuned over a relatively wide range for heterojunction device optimization.

Furthermore, experimentally demonstrated p-Cr2O3/n-β-Ga2O3 heterojunction diodes achieved breakdown voltages approaching 1.96 kV together with reverse leakage currents below 10 µA cm−2, highlighting their strong potential for extreme-environment power applications.41 Despite these promising characteristics, previous Cr2O3/Ga2O3 studies mainly focused on conventional p–n heterojunction diodes,41–43 which inherently suffer from relatively high forward turn-on voltages due to minority-carrier injection. By contrast, introducing an interconnected array of Cr2O3 p-wells beneath the Schottky anode can simultaneously preserve low-barrier majority-carrier conduction during forward bias while enabling electrostatic field redistribution and Schottky-interface shielding under reverse bias. Nevertheless, the electrostatic modulation mechanisms, localized field-crowding behavior, and geometry-dependent design limits of such Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 JBS architectures remain largely unexplored. Therefore, in this work, a vertical β-Ga2O3 junction barrier Schottky (JBS) diode incorporating interconnected p-Cr2O3 heterojunction wells is investigated using technology computer-aided design (TCAD) simulations. The proposed device is compared with a conventional β-Ga2O3 Schottky barrier diode having identical structural dimensions and doping concentrations. Particular attention is given to elucidating the electric-field redistribution mechanism introduced by the p-Cr2O3 heterojunction barriers under reverse bias, as well as to evaluating their effectiveness in suppressing electric-field crowding while maintaining low-loss unipolar conduction. Furthermore, the effects of the p-well width, p-well spacing, and p-Cr2O3 acceptor concentration on the breakdown voltage and specific on-resistance are systematically analyzed to establish design guidelines for high-voltage and high-temperature β-Ga2O3 JBS rectifiers.

Structure and mechanism

The cross-sectional structure of the β-Ga2O3 JBS diode employed in this work is shown in Fig. 1. All device characteristics were obtained through numerical simulation using commercially licensed TCAD software packages, accessed through the authors’ current and previous institutional affiliations and collaborative research environments, ensuring full compliance with all applicable software-licensing requirements. The adopted device configuration and simulation framework are further supported by prior experimental work, including one author's previous study on normally-off β-Ga2O3 MOSFETs with nitrogen-doped channels.44 An 8 µm-thick n−-β-Ga2O3 epitaxial drift layer with a donor concentration of 1.0 × 1016 cm−3 was designed on a 650 µm-thick n+-β-Ga2O3 substrate doped to 1.0 × 1018 cm−3, which serves as the cathode. A substrate thickness of 650 µm was used in the TCAD simulations, consistent with experimentally fabricated β-Ga2O3 wafers, to ensure that the simulation model accurately reflects the physical device dimensions. On the top surface, a 1 µm-thick p-Cr2O3 layer with a doping concentration of 1.0 × 1017 cm−3 was introduced beneath the Schottky contact, which was selected to ensure adequate depletion-region extension and numerical stability. The JBS region consists of periodically distributed p-type segments laterally embedded under the Schottky contact, with each p-region width (WP) and adjacent spacing (WS) both fixed at 5 µm. Furthermore, a 50 µm-wide p-Cr2O3 junction termination extension (JTE) was incorporated beneath both edges of the Schottky anode to improve electric field distribution and minimize field crowding near the device periphery during reverse operation. The Schottky contact has a diameter of 150 µm and serves as the active area for extracting the current density–voltage (J–V) characteristics, as indicated in Fig. 1. This geometry allows the depletion regions originating from the p-type segments to laterally expand under reverse voltage and effectively shield the metal/semiconductor interface from the peak electric field without significantly constricting the forward current path. For a fair comparison between the JBS diode and the conventional Schottky barrier diode (SBD), the drift layer thickness, doping concentration, and overall device geometry were kept identical. The only structural difference is the introduction of the p-type junction barrier beneath the Schottky contact in the JBS configuration. The electrical characteristics of the proposed β-Ga2O3 JBS diode were evaluated using a physics-based TCAD simulation framework. The carrier transport behavior was analyzed by self-consistently solving the Poisson equation and carrier continuity equations based on the drift–diffusion model under both forward and reverse bias conditions. The Schottky contact was modeled using a thermionic-emission-based contact model with an appropriate metal work function, while the cathode contact was assumed to be an ideal ohmic contact. To improve the physical accuracy of carrier transport under high electric field conditions, electric-field-induced barrier lowering and tunneling-assisted transport mechanisms were incorporated into the simulations. In addition, concentration-dependent and electric-field-dependent mobility effects were included to account for impurity scattering and high-field transport behavior in β-Ga2O3. Shockley–Read–Hall (SRH) recombination was included to account for carrier lifetime effects and defect-assisted recombination processes. The temperature-dependent electrical characteristics of the device were evaluated over a temperature range from 300 K to 450 K to investigate the thermal stability and high-temperature operation capability of the proposed structure. Avalanche breakdown characteristics were analyzed using the Selberherr impact-ionization model with parameters calibrated according to previously reported β-Ga2O3 power-device studies.

Cross-sectional schematic of the proposed β-Ga2O3 junction barrier Schottky (JBS) diode incorporating periodically distributed p-Cr2O3 heterojunction wells and p-Cr2O3 junction termination extensions (JTEs).

The material parameters of the α-Cr2O3 and β-Ga2O3 layers employed in the TCAD simulations are summarized in Table 1. All parameters were carefully calibrated against established experimental data available in the literature.3,15,36–43,45–47 For the α-Cr2O3 layer, the bandgap was selected based on experimentally measured values reported for sputtered Cr2O3 thin films, which typically range from 3.0 to 3.4 eV.36–38 This variation arises from differences in deposition conditions, crystalline quality, phase purity, oxygen stoichiometry, and residual strain, all of which can influence the electronic structure and optical properties of the material. The electron affinity was subsequently estimated from the valence-band offset (ΔEV) of the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterojunction. Photoelectron spectroscopy measurements have shown that ΔEV at the interface is approximately 2.65 ± 0.2 eV.40 Based on this value, the electron affinity of Cr2O3 was calculated using χCr2O3 = χGa2O3 + Eg,Ga2O3 − Eg,Cr2O3 − ΔEV, yielding a range of 2.55–3.35 eV. In the TCAD simulations, we set ΔEV to 2.65 eV and adjusted the Cr2O3 parameters within physically reasonable ranges reported in the literature to maintain this band alignment, thus providing a consistent framework for evaluating the proposed JBS diode. The key physical models, material parameters, calibration procedures, and bulk-trap configurations employed in the TCAD simulations have been described in detail in our previous works.46,47 In particular, the β-Ga2O3 drift layer was modeled with deep-level electron traps located at EC −0.6 eV, EC −0.75 eV, and EC −1.05 eV, corresponding to the experimentally observed E1, E2, and E3 levels.48–53 Building upon this previously validated framework, the present study incorporates simplified interface-related effects at the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterointerface through fixed interface charge and interface recombination mechanisms. Although interface defects may exist, previously reported Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterojunction diodes exhibited a near-ideal ideality factor of approximately 1.07,41 suggesting relatively good interfacial quality and limited interface-recombination effects. In addition, temperature-dependent electrical measurements in Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterojunctions revealed Poole–Frenkel-emission-related trap behavior with an extracted barrier height of approximately 0.19 eV,39 indicating the presence of trap-assisted transport under reverse bias. However, the physical origin, spatial distribution, and energy distribution of these traps remain unclear, and experimentally calibrated interface-trap parameters for the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterointerface are still scarce in the literature. Therefore, a comprehensive energy-distributed interface-trap model was not implemented in the present work. Instead, additional sensitivity simulations were performed by systematically varying the interface-trap density (Dit) over the range of 1.0 × 1010–1.0 × 1012 cm−2 eV−1 and the fixed interface charge density (Qf) over the range of 1.0 × 1010–1.0 × 1013 cm−2, spanning multiple orders of magnitude and encompassing values typically reported for oxide-semiconductor heterointerfaces. Negligible variations were observed in the reverse leakage current, electric-field distribution, and breakdown voltage. These results indicate that the reverse-blocking performance of the proposed JBS diode is governed primarily by depletion-induced electric-field redistribution generated by the p-Cr2O3 heterojunction wells. Therefore, the principal conclusions and design trends reported in this work remain robust against reasonable interface non-idealities.

The practical viability of this material combination is strongly supported by experimental breakthroughs,39,41,42 confirming that the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 junction sustains low defect density and excellent thermal tolerance under high-voltage conditions. Accordingly, the physical assumptions and carrier-transport behaviors adopted in the TCAD model remain consistent with experimentally demonstrated device characteristics. Furthermore, the predictive accuracy of our TCAD platform was validated in our previous investigations of vertical p-GaN/n-β-Ga2O3 heterojunction devices, where the same numerical methodology successfully captured carrier transport and breakdown behavior.31,46 The relevance of our modeling of β-Ga2O3/GaN power heterostructures has been recently cited by A. Ho et al.54 and Q. Chang et al.55 as part of the existing literature context in this comparatively underexplored research direction. In their work, they demonstrated the successful fabrication of lateral p-GaN/n-β-Ga2O3 heterojunction diodes and a vertical β-Ga2O3 heterojunction barrier Schottky (HJBS) diode with sputtered p-GaN. The consistency between the present simulation results and previously reported experimental and numerical studies further supports the applicability of the TCAD platform for advanced β-Ga2O3 heterostructure design. Therefore, despite the prospective nature of the proposed interconnected JBS configuration, the material system and numerical methodology employed in this study are based on experimentally reported material properties and established device physics.

Results and discussion

Electrical characteristics

The simulated forward current density–voltage (J–V) characteristics of the conventional Ga2O3 SBD and the proposed Ga2O3 JBS diode are presented in Fig. 2(a) and (b) on linear and logarithmic scales, respectively. As shown in Fig. 2(a), both devices display nearly identical forward conduction, with turn-on voltages (Von) of 0.75 V for the SBD and 0.8 V for the JBS diode. This indicates that the JBS design retains the unipolar transport mechanism without noticeably altering the turn-on behavior. In contrast, conventional p–n heterojunction diodes (HJDs) based on p-Cr2O3/n-β-Ga2O3 structures39,41,42 typically exhibit higher turn-on voltages (1.65–1.9 V) due to the large built-in potential of the full p–n junction. In the proposed JBS configuration, the p-Cr2O3 regions are patterned as interconnected wells rather than a continuous film. Thus, at low forward bias the current is still primarily conducted through low-barrier Schottky paths. As a result, the device maintains a turn-on voltage of 0.8 V, which is comparable to that of a standard Schottky diode, while at the same time avoiding the forward-conduction degradation often observed in full-coverage heterojunction rectifiers. The specific on-resistance (Ron,sp), extracted at a current density of 100 A cm−2, is 45.4 mΩ cm2 for the SBD and 47.8 mΩ cm2 for the JBS diode. These values are approximately one order of magnitude higher than those reported in our previous work,31 where a smaller Schottky contact diameter of 50 µm was used, whereas a larger diameter of 150 µm was employed in this study. Although the Schottky contact diameter is increased, the forward current (IF) shows little variation. This suggests that the forward current is not primarily limited by the Schottky contact area in the present device structure. As a result, the extracted Ron,sp becomes higher than that in ref. 31. This behavior was further investigated using TCAD simulations. The results show that the forward current is predominantly limited by the bulk resistance of the 650 µm-thick substrate rather than by the Schottky contact area. Analysis of the current density distribution reveals significant current crowding near the edges of the Schottky contact, while the thick drift and substrate region acts as a transport bottleneck. Therefore, the increase in the contact area does not lead to a proportional increase in the total current. To confirm this mechanism, additional TCAD simulations were performed using a scaled structure with a 2 µm substrate and an 8 µm drift layer. In this case, the forward current increases with the Schottky contact diameter, demonstrating that the expected area-dependent current scaling can be restored when the bulk resistance is reduced. These results suggest that although the intrinsic current transport depends on the contact area, the large series resistance and current spreading in the thick substrate of the present devices limit the current increase, resulting in a higher extracted Ron,sp. In addition, the current density at a forward voltage of 3.0 V is 57.3 A cm−2 for the SBD and 52.8 A cm−2 for the JBS diode. The slightly lower current density of the JBS diode is attributed to the presence of the p-Cr2O3 regions, which reduce the effective Schottky conduction area, consistent with our previous reports.31 Unlike conventional p–n junctions, the large valence-band offset (2.65 eV) at the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterojunction suppresses hole injection from these p-Cr2O3 segments under forward bias, so that they act primarily as current-blocking regions and carrier transport occurs mainly through the Schottky regions between adjacent p-type segments. However, the difference in the forward current density between the SBD and the JBS diode is small, indicating that the incorporation of the p-Cr2O3 junction barrier introduces only a minor penalty in forward conduction performance. Therefore, the turn-on voltage and specific on-resistance of the JBS diode remain close to those of the SBD.

Simulated forward current density–voltage (J–V) characteristics of the conventional β-Ga2O3 SBD and the proposed β-Ga2O3 JBS diode shown on (a) linear and (b) semi-logarithmic scales.

Fig. 3(a) and (b) display the temperature-dependent forward and reverse J–V characteristics of the conventional Ga2O3 SBD and the proposed Ga2O3 JBS diode, respectively. The electrical characteristics were evaluated at 300 K, 400 K, and 450 K. As shown in the forward characteristics, the turn-on voltage of both devices decreases as the temperature increases from 300 K to 450 K. The knee voltage shifts toward lower values with increasing temperature, which is consistent with thermionic emission due to the reduction in the effective Schottky barrier height at elevated temperatures. At higher forward voltages, the current density decreases with increasing temperature in both devices, suggesting that the forward conduction in this region is limited by the series resistance of the n−-Ga2O3 drift layer. As the temperature increases, the electron mobility decreases due to phonon scattering, resulting in a reduced current density. The temperature dependence of the JBS diode is very similar to that of the SBD over the entire forward voltage range, further confirming that the p-Cr2O3 junction barrier does not alter the majority-carrier transport through the Schottky regions. In the reverse bias region, the leakage current of both devices increases with increasing temperature, which can be attributed to thermally activated carrier transport across the Schottky barrier. However, the reverse leakage current of the JBS diode is lower than that of the SBD at all temperatures because the depletion regions formed by the p-Cr2O3 segments beneath the Schottky contact reduce the electric field at the metal/semiconductor interface and suppress carrier emission. Furthermore, the present Cr2O3-based JBS diode exhibits lower reverse leakage current than the previously reported GaN/β-Ga2O3 JBS diode31 over the entire temperature range. At 450 K, the leakage current is approximately two orders of magnitude lower. This improvement is attributed to the larger valence-band offset (2.65 eV) at the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterointerface, which more effectively suppresses thermally activated carrier transport and trap-assisted leakage current under reverse bias. These results highlight the effectiveness of the p-Cr2O3/n-β-Ga2O3 heterojunction in suppressing leakage current and maintaining stable operation at elevated temperatures, which is crucial for practical power devices facing challenges related to excessive leakage current and poor thermal stability. The temperature-dependent forward voltage (VF) for both the conventional Ga2O3 SBD and the proposed Ga2O3 JBS diode was evaluated at a constant current density of 50 A cm−2, with the results depicted in Fig. 4. The temperature dependence of VF is similar for both devices, and VF increases as the temperature rises from 300 K to 450 K. For the SBD, VF increases from approximately 2.75 V to 4.1 V, while for the JBS diode, it increases from approximately 2.9 V to 4.3 V over the same temperature range. However, the forward voltage drop of the JBS diode is slightly higher than that of the SBD at all temperatures due to the partial replacement of the Schottky-active region by the p-Cr2O3 wells.

The simulated reverse current–voltage characteristics of the conventional SBD and the proposed JBS diode are illustrated in Fig. 5. The breakdown voltage (Vbr) of the JBS diode is higher than that of the SBD. The conventional SBD exhibits breakdown at approximately 390 V, whereas the JBS diode can sustain reverse bias up to nearly 950 V. As seen in Fig. 5, the reverse leakage current of the JBS diode is lower than that of the SBD over the entire pre-breakdown region. The improvement in the breakdown voltage and the reduction in leakage current are attributed to the presence of the p-Cr2O3 regions. Under reverse bias, the depletion regions originating from the p-type segments expand laterally, redistributing the electric field away from the Schottky interface and thereby enhancing the breakdown capability while suppressing leakage current. To further evaluate the device performance, the Baliga's figure of merit (BFOM = Vbr2/Ron,sp) was calculated. The JBS diode exhibits a BFOM of 18.88 MW cm−2, which is approximately 5.6 times higher than that of the conventional SBD (3.35 MW cm−2). This improvement indicates that the JBS structure provides a better trade-off between breakdown voltage and on-resistance.

Fig. 6(a) and (b) present the electric field distributions of the conventional SBD and the proposed JBS diode at breakdown, respectively, while the corresponding line profiles beneath the Schottky interface are shown in Fig. 6(c) and (d). For the conventional SBD, a pronounced electric field spike of approximately 1.75 MV cm−1 occurs at the anode edge at Vbr = 390 V. This severe field crowding at the Schottky periphery initiates premature avalanche breakdown, while the electric field in the central drift region remains underutilized. In contrast, the JBS diode sustains a much higher reverse bias of about 950 V by effectively redistributing the electric field. As seen in Fig. 6(d), the peak field at the Schottky interface is significantly suppressed. Instead of a single edge spike, the field is spread laterally across the periodically arranged p-Cr2O3 segments, resulting in multiple moderated peaks. These p-type segments introduce additional depletion regions under reverse bias, which extend laterally and reshape the field distribution. This junction-assisted modulation reduces local stress at the contact and shifts the maximum electric field deeper into the drift region. As a result, image-force barrier lowering is suppressed and the reverse leakage current is reduced. Consequently, the JBS diode achieves a substantially higher breakdown voltage and a 5.6-fold improvement in BFOM while maintaining nearly unchanged forward conduction characteristics. These findings confirm that the interconnected p-Cr2O3 junction barrier successfully enhances voltage blocking capability while preserving the low-loss advantages of the Schottky architecture. It should be noted that the absolute breakdown voltage obtained from the β-Ga2O3 TCAD simulations may depend on the selected impact-ionization coefficients and avalanche breakdown models because the impact-ionization behavior of ultra-wide-bandgap β-Ga2O3 is still under active investigation. Therefore, the primary objective of the present study is not to establish an exact breakdown voltage value but to evaluate the relative breakdown enhancement mechanism introduced by the p-Cr2O3 heterojunction barrier under identical simulation conditions and physical assumptions. The incorporation of the p-Cr2O3 regions redistributes the electric field, reduces electric-field crowding near the Schottky contact edge, and shifts the peak electric field deeper into the drift region, thereby improving the breakdown characteristics. To assess the sensitivity of the simulated breakdown voltage to uncertainties in the β-Ga2O3 impact-ionization parameters, an additional analysis was performed by varying the impact-ionization B coefficients by ±20% from their nominal values. The results are summarized in Table 2. For the conventional SBD, the breakdown voltage remained nearly unchanged at approximately 390 V for all coefficient variations. In contrast, that of the proposed JBS diode varied from 650 V to 1260 V. This variation reflects the response of the simulation to intentionally perturbed impact-ionization coefficients and was used solely to evaluate model sensitivity. Such a parameter sweep does not represent actual device operating conditions; rather, it provides a quantitative measure of the uncertainty associated with the impact-ionization model. With the calibrated parameter set adopted throughout this study, the reference JBS diode exhibited a breakdown voltage of approximately 950 V. The weak dependence of the SBD breakdown voltage on the impact-ionization coefficients indicates that its breakdown behavior is primarily governed by severe electric-field crowding at the Schottky contact edge. In contrast, the p-Cr2O3 heterojunction barrier redistributes the electric field into the drift region, making the breakdown behavior of the JBS diode more strongly influenced by the avalanche process and therefore more sensitive to the selected impact-ionization parameters. Nevertheless, the proposed JBS diode consistently exhibits superior reverse-blocking capability compared with the conventional SBD throughout the entire range of parameter variations. These results demonstrate that the breakdown enhancement mechanism associated with the p-Cr2O3 heterojunction barrier remains physically valid despite the uncertainty in the impact-ionization coefficients.

Effects of JBS structural parameters on device performance

As already discussed, the proposed JBS diode shows improved breakdown characteristics while maintaining comparable forward conduction. However, because the device performance depends critically on the structural design of the p-Cr2O3 regions, the dependence of the breakdown voltage (Vbr) and specific on-resistance (Ron,sp) on these parameters was investigated. The p-well width (WP), spacing (WS), and doping concentration (NA) affect the depletion behavior and electric field distribution under reverse bias. The introduction of the p-type regions also reduces the effective conduction area, resulting in a trade-off between Vbr and Ron,sp. A systematic analysis of these parameters was therefore performed to determine the optimal design conditions, beginning with the effect of the lateral p-Cr2O3 well width (WP). For this analysis, all other structural parameters, including WS and NA, were kept constant.

Fig. 7(a) and (b) show the dependence of Vbr and Ron,sp on WP, respectively. When WP increases from 2 to 10 µm, both Vbr and Ron,sp increase. The breakdown voltage increases from approximately 940 V to nearly 975 V. The increase arises from the enhanced electric field shielding effect of the wider p-Cr2O3 regions, which reduces the electric field concentration at the Schottky interface. In contrast, Ron,sp increases slightly from 47.4 to about 48.2 mΩ cm2. Although increasing WP reduces the effective conduction area, which tends to increase Ron,sp, the change remains small because the total on-state resistance is mainly determined by the bulk resistance of the 650 µm-thick β-Ga2O3 substrate. In addition, unlike conventional JBS structures, where excessively large p-well widths often induce severe depletion-induced pinch-off effects and significant degradation in the forward conduction characteristics, the depletion regions formed by the p-Cr2O3 wells in the proposed structure remain relatively well confined under forward-bias conditions. Therefore, the proposed device maintains stable unipolar conduction behavior over a relatively wide WP range. However, although Vbr continues to increase as WP increases beyond 5 µm, the improvement gradually becomes smaller. In particular, increasing WP from 5 to 10 µm improves the breakdown voltage by only approximately 2.6%, from about 950 V to 975 V, indicating that the electric-field redistribution effect approaches saturation at larger p-well widths. Meanwhile, increasing WP causes the p-Cr2O3 regions to occupy a larger portion of the active area, thereby reducing the effective Schottky conduction area available for majority-carrier transport. Consequently, excessively large WP values become less attractive for practical large-area device implementation. The simulated electric-field distributions further suggest a tendency toward more pronounced localized electric-field concentration near the bottom corners of wider p-Cr2O3 wells. Although this does not significantly affect the breakdown voltage, a more uniform electric-field distribution is generally preferred from a device-reliability perspective. In addition, wider p-Cr2O3 regions may introduce additional fabrication complexity in future large-area implementations. Therefore, considering the trade-offs among breakdown-voltage enhancement, preservation of the effective Schottky conduction area, electric-field uniformity, fabrication practicality, and device scalability, WP = 5 µm was selected as a balanced design point in the present work.

Next, the influence of the p-well spacing (WS) was analyzed. The electrical characteristics as a function of WS are shown in Fig. 8. When WS increases from 4 to 15 µm, both Vbr and Ron,sp exhibit noticeable variations. The breakdown voltage decreases from approximately 955 V at WS = 4 µm and then approaches a nearly constant value of about 935 V for WS ≥ 10 µm. The reduction can be attributed to the weakened electric-field shielding effect as the spacing between adjacent p-wells increases. With a larger WS, the depletion regions formed around the p-wells become less effective in merging, so that the Schottky interface is exposed to higher electric field concentrations, making the device more susceptible to premature breakdown. A similar behavior has been reported in GaN-based junction barrier Schottky diodes (JBSDs), where the p-well spacing plays a critical role in determining the effectiveness of the pinch-off effect.56 When the spacing is sufficiently small, the merged depletion regions can effectively shield the Schottky contact, whereas a larger spacing reduces this protection capability. A notable difference between the present device and the GaN JBSD lies in the underlying mechanism responsible for the breakdown-voltage enhancement. In the GaN JBSD, the depletion width is sufficiently large to enable complete pinch-off at small p-well spacing, which significantly reduces the effective Schottky area and suppresses leakage current.56 In contrast, complete pinch-off is not achieved in the present β-Ga2O3 JBSD structure within the investigated WS range. This is due to the relatively narrower depletion width, which arises from the higher drift layer doping concentration and the specific band alignment of the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterojunction. Nevertheless, the breakdown voltage is still substantially enhanced, as the depletion regions from the p-type segments expand laterally to redistribute the electric field and shift the peak field away from the Schottky interface, even without full merging. This leads to a more gradual dependence of Vbr on WS compared with that observed in the GaN JBSD device, where the pinch-off effect produces a more abrupt transition. In contrast to the breakdown voltage, Ron,sp shows a slight decrease from 48.0 to approximately 47.0 mΩ cm2 as WS increases. This trend can be explained by the increase in the effective conduction area of the n-type region when the spacing between p-wells expands. However, the change in Ron,sp is relatively small, reflecting the fact that the series resistance of the 650 µm-thick substrate remains the dominant contribution to the total on-state resistance. Therefore, the influence of WS on the on-resistance is limited compared to its impact on the breakdown characteristics. Both Vbr and Ron,sp saturate for WS > 10 µm, suggesting that further increasing the p-well spacing does not significantly improve conduction performance. Instead, it continues to degrade the electric field management capability of the device. From these observations, optimization of WS is essential for achieving a proper balance between breakdown voltage and conduction loss. In this study, an optimal value of WS = 4 µm is identified, where the electric field shielding is most effective while maintaining a comparable on-resistance, consistent with the concept of a critical spacing reported in high-performance power diode structures.56

Finally, the effect of the p-Cr2O3 acceptor concentration (NA) on the device performance was evaluated. The dependence of Vbr and Ron,sp on the p-Cr2O3 well doping concentration (NA) is plotted in Fig. 9(a) and (b). While Ron,sp remains remarkably stable at approximately 47.6 mΩ cm2 across the entire range of NA, the breakdown voltage exhibits a distinct non-monotonic behavior. As NA increases from 1.0 × 1016 to 5.0 × 1016 cm−3, Vbr rises sharply from 550 V to a peak exceeding 1053 V, marking a shift from surface-limited to bulk-limited breakdown. The electric-field distributions for different NA are shown in Fig. 10. At a low doping concentration (1.0 × 1016 cm−3), Fig. 10(a) reveals intense field crowding at the edge of the anode electrode due to insufficient shielding, leading to premature surface breakdown. When NA reaches the optimal concentration of 5.0 × 1016 cm−3, a balance between surface shielding and uniform bulk field distribution is achieved. As illustrated in Fig. 10(b), the pinch-off effect becomes most effective at this stage, allowing the peak electric field (Epeak) of 2.04 MV cm−1 to be distributed more broadly within the epitaxial layer. This distribution maximizes the voltage blocking capability of the device. However, beyond this threshold (NA > 1.0 × 1017 cm−3), Vbr gradually decreases and stabilizes around 900 V. This degradation originates from excessive charge accumulation within the p-wells, which triggers localized electric field crowding. The simulation results in Fig. 10(c) indicate that the electric field becomes constricted at the bottom corners of the p-Cr2O3 strips, leading to premature edge-triggered breakdown. The fact that Ron,sp remains constant across the entire investigated range further highlights that while p-well doping is critical for tailoring the depletion region and field profile in the off-state, it exerts a negligible influence on carrier transport within the n-Ga2O3 conduction channel during on-state operation. Before proceeding to the benchmark comparison, the breakdown voltage of 950 V reported in the abstract and in the comparison with the conventional SBD (Fig. 5) corresponds to the reference device geometry (WP = 5 µm, WS = 5 µm, NA = 1.0 × 1017 cm−3) described in Section 2. The peak value of 1053 V discussed above is obtained by optimizing NA alone to 5.0 × 1016 cm−3, representing the theoretical maximum under ideal conditions. The reference device with Vbr = 950 V was selected as the main design point for the benchmark comparison in Table 3 and Fig. 11.

Lin et al.60 Nevertheless, the results demonstrate that integrating p-Cr2O3 heterojunction wells into β-Ga2O3 SBDs can significantly enhance the breakdown voltage while preserving unipolar conduction characteristics. Several reported β-Ga2O3-based HJDs exhibit significantly higher breakdown voltages and lower specific on-resistances. For instance, J.-S. Li et al.64 reported a vertical NiO/Ga2O3 heterojunction rectifier with a Vbr of 8.9 kV and an Ron,sp of 7.9 mΩ cm2, corresponding to a BFOM of 10.2 GW cm−2. Similarly, other HJDs based on NiO/Ga2O3 and Cu2O/Ga2O3 have also demonstrated superior reverse-blocking capability with substantially lower conduction losses. However, these heterojunction diodes rely on bipolar p–n conduction, which inherently results in a higher turn-on voltage (Von) because of the built-in potential of the heterojunction. In contrast, the investigated device maintains the low Von characteristic (0.8 V) of the Schottky transport mechanism despite the incorporation of p-type heterojunction regions. This behavior is attributed to the large valence-band offset at the p-Cr2O3/n-β-Ga2O3 interface, which effectively suppresses hole injection under forward bias and maintains majority-carrier-dominated conduction. While the above discussion highlights the transport mechanism responsible for the low Von characteristic, it is also important to evaluate how the p-Cr2O3/n-β-Ga2O3 heterojunction compares with other p-type materials employed in Ga2O3-based heterojunction JBS rectifiers. To further benchmark the proposed Cr2O3-based JBS diode against previously reported devices, its performance is compared with four representative p-type materials employed in Ga2O3 heterojunction JBS rectifiers, namely NiO,26–28 Cu2O,29 SnO,30 and GaN.31 To facilitate a meaningful comparison, the selected devices were chosen because they employ similar drift-layer doping levels (1.0–2.0 × 1016 cm−3) and drift thicknesses in the range of 5–10 µm. Among these devices, the GaN-based JBS diode achieves the highest breakdown voltage (up to 4335 V) and the highest BFOM (2.77 GW cm−2).55 The NiO-based JBS diode exhibits the second-highest performance, achieving breakdown voltages of up to 2395 V27 and BFOM values approaching 0.93 GW cm−2.26 However, the NiO/β-Ga2O3 heterojunction suffers from thermal instability when operated at elevated temperatures, such as up to 550 °C, because of interfacial reactions and intermixing at the NiO/β-Ga2O3 interface, leading to the formation of a ternary NiGa2O4 spinel compound.32 Cu2O-based devices exhibit an ultra-low Von of 0.78 V,29 but the polycrystalline nature of Cu2O films and the presence of interface defects can promote interface recombination and trap-assisted tunneling as dominant transport mechanisms at room temperature.33 The SnO/β-Ga2O3 HJBS diode in ref. 30 achieves a Vbr of 1375 V and a PFOM of 0.37 GW cm−2; however, SnO is highly sensitive to oxygen stoichiometry and undergoes disproportionation above 250 °C.34,35 Meanwhile, the GaN-based JBS diode31 provides a lower Ron,sp but has been reported to exhibit significantly higher leakage current at elevated temperatures. Within this context, the Cr2O3-based JBS diode proposed in this work occupies a unique position. Although the device does not achieve the highest Vbr or the lowest Ron,sp among the reported JBS structures, yielding a BFOM of 18.88 MW cm−2 because of the thick substrate resistance and the absence of advanced edge termination techniques, it exhibits superior thermal stability compared with previously reported counterparts. In particular, Cr2O3 is the only p-type material among the five candidates that has been experimentally demonstrated to operate stably at 600 °C for 140 h and withstand 30 thermal cycles,39 while maintaining a reverse leakage current at 450 K approximately two orders of magnitude lower than that of the p-GaN counterpart.31 These comparisons highlight an important trade-off between breakdown capability, conduction loss, and thermal reliability in Ga2O3-based heterojunction JBS rectifiers. Unlike previous studies that mainly focused on maximizing Vbr or minimizing Ron,sp, the present work highlights the potential of Cr2O3 for high-temperature and low-leakage operation under harsh environments. Therefore, although the proposed device exhibits a moderate Ron,sp, the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterojunction appears particularly suitable for power electronics applications requiring high thermal stability and suppressed leakage current. Furthermore, unlike conventional JBS structures that strongly depend on complete electrostatic pinch-off between adjacent p-regions, the present device enhances the breakdown voltage mainly through electric-field redistribution. Consequently, the device characteristics exhibit strong tolerance against variations in the spacing and geometry of the p-rings, as discussed in Fig. 7–10. This relaxed design constraint provides an important technological advantage for future fabrication and structural optimization. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first TCAD investigation of a Ga2O3 JBS diode employing p-Cr2O3 heterojunction wells. The proposed device demonstrates the simultaneous realization of enhanced breakdown capability, low turn-on voltage, and excellent thermal stability. These results confirm that the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterojunction is a promising candidate for next-generation high-voltage and high-temperature power electronics applications.

Conclusions

In summary, we have proposed a β-Ga2O3 junction barrier Schottky (JBS) diode employing p-Cr2O3 heterojunction wells to overcome the lack of p-type doping in β-Ga2O3. The large valence-band offset (2.65 eV) suppresses hole injection under forward bias, thereby preserving unipolar conduction while enabling electric-field redistribution under reverse bias. As a result, the proposed device achieved a breakdown voltage of 950 V, more than twice that of a conventional Schottky barrier diode (390 V), while maintaining comparable forward characteristics with only a slight increase in specific on-resistance. Compared with our previously reported p-GaN/n-β-Ga2O3 JBS diode, the Cr2O3-based structure exhibited two orders of magnitude lower reverse leakage current at 450 K, indicating superior high-temperature stability. The preferred design point identified in this study corresponds to a p-well width of 5 µm, spacing of 4 µm, and acceptor concentration of 5.0 × 1016 cm−3. A Baliga's figure of merit of 18.88 MW cm−2 was achieved while maintaining stable operation up to 450 K. The significance of this work extends beyond the device-performance improvements achieved by the proposed structure. By utilizing thermodynamically stable p-Cr2O3 wells, this study provides a practical heterojunction-based pathway for circumventing the long-standing p-type doping bottleneck in β-Ga2O3 and enabling junction-assisted power rectifiers without requiring p-type β-Ga2O3. Unlike conventional JBS structures that rely on complete pinch-off to suppress leakage current, the proposed diode enhances the breakdown voltage primarily through electric-field redistribution, which remains effective even without full depletion merging. This relaxed design requirement provides greater tolerance to variations in p-well spacing and geometry, offering a practical advantage for device fabrication. The results further indicate that the Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 heterojunction provides favorable band alignment and interface characteristics for suppressing leakage current at elevated temperatures. Benchmarking against previously reported β-Ga2O3 rectifiers confirms that the proposed Cr2O3-based JBS diode occupies a distinct performance region characterized by moderate conduction loss, exceptionally low reverse leakage current at elevated temperatures and robust high-temperature operation. These findings represent an important step toward the realization of reliable junction-based power devices in ultra-wide-bandgap semiconductors where native p-type doping remains elusive. The present simulation study provides useful physical insights and design guidelines for the experimental realization of Cr2O3/β-Ga2O3 JBS rectifiers. Further improvements may be achieved through optimization of the p-Cr2O3 heterojunction design, edge termination design, and drift-layer engineering to extend the breakdown voltage into the multi-kilovolt range while maintaining a low specific on-resistance. The excellent thermal stability of Cr2O3 also suggests the suitability of the proposed heterojunction concept for high-temperature and high-power applications. Furthermore, the design strategy developed in this work may be extended to other β-Ga2O3 junction-based device architectures, including merged PiN Schottky diodes and heterojunction field-effect devices. Based on the results obtained in this study, it is concluded that p-Cr2O3 heterojunction wells provide an effective approach for improving the reverse blocking capability and high-temperature performance of β-Ga2O3 power rectifiers while providing a practical pathway toward reliable junction-assisted β-Ga2O3 power rectifiers.

MRI-based radiomics signatures for predicting the efficacy of targeted therapy with lenvatinib in hepatocellular carcinoma: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Kun Huang, Haiyan Ma, Haikuan Liu, Jia Yuan, Xiaolan He, Yuancheng Liu, Zhouli Zheng, Rongpin Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTargeted therapy with lenvatinib is a preferred option for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma, however, predicting its efficacy remains challenging. This study aimed to build a nomogram integrating clinicoradiological indicators and radiomics features to predict the response to lenvatinib in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma.MethodsThis study included 211 patients with hepatocellular carcinoma from two centers, who were allocated into the training (107 patients), internal test (46 patients) and external test set(58 patients). Radiomics features were extracted using a Pyradiomics-based system. Biopsy specimens were used for immunohistochemical staining of epidermal growth factor receptor. Risk factors for predicting treatment response were screened out to construct models by logistic regression analysis. The performance of models was evaluated with receiver operating characteristic and decision curve analyses.ResultsA total of 9370 radiomics features were extracted from five sequences. Subsequently, seven radiomics features were identified for modeling. Intratumoral vessel and expression level of epidermal growth factor receptor were independent risk factors for predicting treatment response to lenvatinib and were used to build a clinical model. No difference was found in predicting performance between clinical model and radiomics model. The combined model, integrating intratumoral vessel, epidermal growth factor receptor and radiomics features, had better predicting performance with areas under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.908, 0.877, and 0.870 for the training, internal test and external test sets, respectively.ConclusionThis study underscored the significant potential of radiomics features combined with clinicoradiological indicators in the prediction of treatment response to lenvatinib in patients with hepatocellular carcinoma.Keywords: Hepatocellular carcinoma, Lenvatinib, Epidermal growth factor receptor, Radiomics

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most common malignant tumors. Unfortunately, most of HCC are diagnosed in the advanced stages, missing the opportunity for radical surgery(Llovet et al. 2021; Reig et al. 2022). Systemic therapy is the preferred option for advanced HCC, among which targeted therapy with lenvatinib is currently one of the most commonly used in clinical practice (Llovet et al. 2018; Kudo et al. 2018). Lenvatinib is a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor that targets vascular endothelial growth factor receptors, fibroblast growth factor receptors, platelet-derived growth factor receptor, and so on, thereby blocking downstream signaling pathways and inhibiting angiogenesis and tumor cell proliferation(Catalano et al. 2021). Although lenvatinib improves the survival prognosis of some patients with HCC, its efficacy varies among different patients, and a considerable number of patients will develop resistance to lenvatinib (Jin et al. 2021; Buttell and Qiu 2023). For drug-resistant patients, lenvatinib not only fails to control tumor progression, but also carries the risk of side effects such as liver function damage, hypertension, proteinuria, and increases the economic burden on patients. Therefore, it is of great clinical significance to screen the advantageous patients who are effective against lenvatinib before treatment.

Activation of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) is considered one of the important mechanisms of lenvatinib resistance (Jin et al. 2021). EGFR mutations or amplification activation will increase EGFR protein expression, thereby activating the EGFR-PAK2-ERK5 signaling pathway, and promoting tumor cell growth and lenvatinib resistance (Jin et al. 2021; Buttell and Qiu 2023). Research found that lenvatinib was ineffective in patients with high EGFR expression, when EGFR inhibitors were combined with lenvatinib, a synergistic effect could be achieved, resulting in better therapeutic outcomes (Hindson 2021; Hu et al. 2022). Therefore, EGFR level has important reference value for predicting the efficacy of targeted therapy with lenvatinib. For patients with high EGFR expression level, it can be suggested to treat combined EGFR inhibitors with lenvatinib or switch to other treatment options.

With the development of computer technology, radiomics has emerged, which converts digital medical images into high-dimensional data that can be mined, and extracts high-throughput quantitative features hidden in the images and provides more dimensional information for the evaluation of lesions (Bera et al. 2022). Radiomics can be used to non-invasively reveal potential pathophysiological information contained in medical images and evaluate the therapeutic efficacy of patients with HCC(Harding-Theobald et al. 2021). Several studies have explored the possibility for predicting early postoperative recurrence, efficacy of interventional therapy and targeted immunotherapy in patients with HCC using radiomics(Ji et al. 2021; Kong et al. 2021; Yu et al. 2024). However, there were only a few studies on prediction of the efficacy of lenvatinib for HCC using radiomics. Bo et al.(Bo et al. 2023) used machine learning algorithms to construct a radiomics model to predict the efficacy of lenvatinib in patients with HCC. The model was constructed with 25 radiomics features and had high predictive power. Xu et al.(Xu et al. 2023) compared the predictive efficacy of models built by clinical pathological data or radiomics features for predicting the objective response rate of patients with HCC treated with lenvatinib plus immune checkpoint inhibitors. The results showed that the radiomics model was superior to the clinico-pathological model. In summary, it is feasible to predict the efficacy of lenvatinib using radiomics. However, the above-mentioned radiomics models were mostly constructed based on a single type of data, with limited predictive power. Multimodal model built by integrating multiple type of data could analysis tumor heterogeneity from various dimensions and more accurately predict the therapy efficacy(Boldanova et al. 2021). Studies combining radiomics signatures with EGFR as a prognostic marker have not yet been reported.

Therefore, this study aims to construct a multimodal model by integrating clinioradiological characteristics, EGFR level and radiomics features, and to provide an efficient tool for accurately predicting the efficacy of lenvatinib in patients with HCC.

Methods

Patient selection

A total of 258 HCC patients from two medical centers (center1: Guizhou Provincial People’s Hospital, center 2: The First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University) between January 2020 and December 2023 were considered for inclusion in this study. The Institutional Review Board of Guizhou Provincial People’s Hospital approved this study. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) pathological diagnosis by biopsy puncture before treatment, (2) underwent gadolinium-ethoxybenzyl diethylenetriamine pentaacetic acid enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) within 2 weeks before treatment, (3) treated with lenvatinib and followed up for at least 3 months. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) combined with other cancer-related treatments, (2) history of other malignancies, (3) poor image quality. Therefore, the final analysis included a total of 211 patients in the study cohort. Patients from Guizhou Provincial People’s Hospital were randomly allocated with a ratio of 7:3 into the training and internal test cohorts. And patients from The First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-Sen University were used as the external test cohort (Fig. 1).

Treatment and follow up

Lenvatinib (Eisai, Eisai, Japan): Oral administration of 12 mg (body weight ≥ 60 kg) or 8 mg (body weight < 60 kg) daily for at least 3 months. All patients were followed up once a month and rechecked imaging examination after 3 months of treatment. The objective response was evaluated using the modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST) (Llovet and Lencioni 2020).

Immunohistochemical staining and analysis

Tissue specimens were obtained through biopsy before treatment. Immunohistochemical staining for EGFR was performed using mouse anti human monoclonal antibody (66455-1-lg, dilution ratio 1:2000, Proteintech, Chicago, USA). The immunohistochemical slides were scored by the product of staining intensity and positive cell counting(Dong et al. 2019). The staining intensity was divided into four categories: negative, 0 point; faint yellow, 1 point; yellow, 2 points; brown or dark brown, 3 points. The positive cell count was the percentage of positive cells in 100 cells in × 10 high magnification, which was was classified as follows: 0–10%, 0 point; 11–25%, 1 point; 26–50%, 2 points; 51–75%, 3 points; >75%, 4 points. Considering the median score as a dividing line, the EGFR expression level was divided into high-expression and low-expression.

MRI acquisition

MRI images including T1-weighted image, T2-weighted image, arterial, portal venous, delayed and hepatobilliary phases, were acquired from two medical centers. Patients underwent MRI scanning using Siemens Magnetom Trio 3.0T MRI scanner from Germany. All patients performed gadolinium-ethoxybenzyl diethylenetriamine pentaacetic acid enhanced MRI examination, and acquired axial images at 13–43 s, 50–80 s, and 95–120 s to obtain arterial phase, portal phase, and delayed phase images. Hepatobilliary phase images were collected twenty minutes after intravenous injection of contrast agent. The scanner parameters were set as described previously (Dong et al. 2019).

Clinicoradiological characteristics collection

Clinical data were recorded, including age, sex, levels of preoperative serum α-fetoprotein (AFP). MRI images were assessed independently by two experienced radiologists. MRI features including portal venous tumor thrombus, extrahepatic metastasis, maximum tumor diameter, tumor number, pseudocapsule, necrosis, hemorrhage, intratumoral vessel, tumor margin, peritumoral enhancement, peritumoral hypointensity were evaluated as described previously (Dong et al. 2019).

Radiomics features extraction and selection

Regions of interest were manually delineated on five phases of axial MRI images slice by slice using ITK-SNAP software (version 4.0.1) from the University of Pennsylvania and the University of Utah. Two radiologists (reader 1 and reader 2) performed the segmentation in 20 randomly selected cases, and reader 1 repeated the segmentation after twenty days later to obtain robust radiomics features with inter-observer and intra-observer intraclass correlation coefficients greater than 0.8. Image preprocessing and radiomic features were extracted using a radiomics system (ultra-scholar, ShuKun Technology, Beijing, China) based on a third-party Python library called Pyradiomics(van Griethuysen et al. 2017). The original features were extracted from twelve types of filtered images, including exponential, original, logarithm, square root, square, log-sigma, lbp-3D-m2, lbp-3D-m1, lbp-3D-k, lbp-2D, gradient, and wavelet. Finally, a total of 1874 radiomics features from each MRI sequence were extracted, including first-order statistics, shape-based, gray level size zone matrix, gray level run length matrix, gray level dependence matrix, gray level co-occurrence matrix, and neighborhood gray tone difference matrix features (Huang et al. 2024). In addition, radiomics features were further standardized using the z-score method. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression was used to reduce the dimensionality redundancy and pearson’s correlation analysis was used to select robust features.

Model construction and validation

Logistic regression was used to construct models. We included the selected radiomics features to construct radiomics model. Independent clinicoradiological characteristics (including EGFR level) were used to build the clinical model. All the data mentioned above were integrated to construct a combined model. Internal test set and external test set were used for model validation and optimization.

Statistics

Statistical analysis was performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (version 26.0), GraphPad (version 9) and Python (version 3.12.0). Logistic regression was used to analyze the effect of each variable on the objective response rate and to screen variables for modeling. The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was used for comparison of model performance. Decision curves were used to assess the clinical utility. P value less than 0.05 (two-tailed) was considered a statistically significant difference for all analyses.

Results

Patient characteristics

This study included 211 patients finally, among of which, 153 patients were from center 1 and 58 patients came from center 2 (Table 1). The patients from center 1 were divided into a training set consisting of 107 patients and an internal test set consisting of 46 patients. The 58 patients from center 2 were used for an external test set. The cohorts included 190 males and 21 females. About 66% of patients were older than 50 years. The analysis of immunohistochemical staining for EGFR demonstrated that 93 patients were high expression, and 118 patients were low expression. According to mRECIST criteria, 138 patients responded to treatment, and the objective response rate was about 65%. Table 1 listed the clinical variables, which did not significantly differ among the training, internal test and external test sets (P > 0.05).

Radiomics features screening

A total of 9370 radiomics features from five MRI sequences were extracted and finally 7 radiomics features correlated with therapy efficacy were screened out. The radiomics score was calculated using the following formula: radiomics score = (− 0.532×EOB_exponential_gldm_DependenceEntropy) + (− 0.785×T1_lbp-3D-k_gldm_HighGrayLevelEmphasis) + (− 0.027×T2_wavelet-LHL_firstorder_Uniformity) + (0.492×AP_squareroot_firstorder_Variance) + (1.185×AP_original_gldm_HighGrayLevelEmphasis) + (0.601×VP_log-sigma-1-0-mm-3D_ngtdm_Busyness) + (0.339×EOB_lbp-3D-m2_ glrlm_RunLengthNonUniformity). Univariable logistic regression showed that radiomics score was a risk factor of therapy efficacy for patients in HCC treated with lenvatinib(P < 0.001). The higher the radiomics score, the higher the objective response rate.

Model construction and validation

Multivariable logistic regression showed that intratumoral vessel (P = 0.001), EGFR level (P < 0.001) and radiomics score(P < 0.001) were independent risk factors of therapy efficacy for patients in HCC treated with lenvatinib (Table 2). Intratumoral vessel and EGFR level were used to build a clinical model, radiomics score was used for construction of a radiomics model, and intratumoral vessel, EGFR and radiomics score were integrated to construct a combined model (Table 3). The results indicated that AUCs of clinical model were 0.788(95% CI 0.695–0.881), 0.808(95% CI 0.660–0.955), and 0.742(95% CI 0.604–0.880) in the training, internal test and external test sets, respectively. The AUCs of radiomics model were 0.768(95% CI 0.675–0.861), 0.786(95% CI 0.647–0.925), and 0.712(95% CI 0.582–0.841) in the training, internal test and external test sets, respectively. The combined model revealed AUCs of 0.908 (95% CI 0.849–0.967), 0.877 (95% CI 0.756–0.999) and 0.870(95% CI 0.775–0.965) in the training, internal test and external test sets, respectively. Paired ROC analysis revealed no statistically significant difference in AUCs between clinical model and radiomics model (P > 0.05), and the AUCs of the combined model were significantly higher than that of the clinical model and radiomics model(P < 0.05). The radiomics signatures, independent clinicoradiological factors and EGFR level were combined to create a nomogram. Decision curves suggested that the combined model resulted in powerful clinical utility(Fig. 2).

Discussion

Currently, lenvatinib shows promising potential in the management of unresectable HCC. However, due to the heterogeneity of HCC, the clinical benefit of lenvatinib is limited(Al-Salama et al. 2019; Kalasekar et al. 2021). It is essential to identify potential patients who will respond to lenvatinib. In this study, we investigated whether a combination of clinicoradiological indicators and radiomics features could be predictors of therapy efficacy in patients with HCC treated with lenvatinib and proposed an easy-to-use tool to predict objective response rate of lenvatinib.

Previous studies have explored the value of various variables in predicting the efficacy of lenvatinib. A previous study found that the continuous reduction of AFP was closely related to the effectiveness of Lenvatinib(Saeki et al. 2020), but our study did not find that the level of AFP before treatment was related to the objective response rate of lenvatinib. There were also some studies explored the correlation between imaging signs and the efficacy of lenvatinib. Studies showed that heterogeneous enhancement pattern in contrast-enhanced CT images and 18 F-fluorodeoxyglucose uptake in PET/CT could be as useful predictors of response to lenvatinib(Kawamura et al. 2020a, b). In our study, hepatobiliary specific contrast-enhanced MRI was used, which had higher tissue resolution and would be helpful to identify more valuable imaging features of tumor. Differently, our study did not find that the enhancement pattern was related to the objective response rate of lenvatinib treatment, which might be due to the sample selection bias, and further validation of studies with larger sample size was needed. In addition, our study found that the intratumoral vessels correlated closely with the objective response rate of lenvatinib treatment. As we all known, the mechanism of lenvatinib treatment is to inhibit tumor angiogenesis and normalize tumor blood vessels, so as to control tumors(Zhao et al. 2020). Therefore, we speculated that lesions with intratumoral vessels were more likely to enable lenvatinib to exert its mechanism of action, so the objective response rate would be higher.

Lenvatinib blocks downstream signaling pathways and inhibits angiogenesis and tumor cell proliferation through targeting some tyrosine kinases (Catalano et al. 2021). Based on this, some studies have attempted to predict the therapeutic efficacy of lenvatinib using molecular markers. For example, Yamauchi et al. (2020) utilized the FGFR4 to predict the efficacy of lenvatinib and the results showed that FGFR4 level was an independent predictor of response to lenvatinib. In our study, we found that EGFR level was an independent risk factor of response to lenvatinib. High EGFR level would activate the EGFR-PAK2-ERK5 signaling pathway, promote tumor cell growth and lenvatinib resistance (Ladd et al. 2024). Our results confirmed that the objective response rate of patients with high EGFR level was low, while the objective response rate of patients with low EGFR level was high. EGFR might be an important molecular marker for the prediction of the efficacy of targeted therapy with lenvatinib.

Although many promising biomarkers predicting response to lenvatinib have been identified, there still needs a reliable predictive model to guide clinical decision-making in HCC. Radiomics has been widely used to predict treatment response, and prognosis of HCC (Fang et al. 2021; Shan et al. 2019). In our study, we used multi-phase enhanced MRI images for radiomics feature extraction and screening, including T1-weighted image, T2-weighted image, arterial, portal venous and hepatobilliary phase. Five MRI sequences contained a large amount of imaging information, which could enhance the potential of radiomics information mining, among which information from arterial and hepatobiliary phase play a great role in management of HCC. Radiomics features such as first-order statistical features, gray level dependency matrix, neighbourhood gray-tone difference matrix, and gray level run length matrix were screened out to construct models. The first-order statistical features were used to describe the overall distribution of voxel intensity. The gray level dependency matrix, neighbourhood gray-tone difference matrix, and gray level run length matrix features were used to quantify the spatial distribution relationship between a voxel and other voxels within the region of interest and had high sensitivity in revealing spatial relationships. Biological behaviors such as tumor necrosis, neovascularization, and uneven growth would cause complex internal structures, resulting in varying degrees of heterogeneity in the tumor microenvironment, which could be identified by analyzing voxel intensity and spatial distribution relationships.

The radiomics model had similar performance in predicting response to lenvatinib with the clinical model constructed by intratumoral vessels and EGFR level, while the prediction efficiency of the combined model was significantly better than that of the radiomics and clinical model, showing the complementarity of various types of data. The combined model constructed in our study could provide significant directions in the selection of patients for lenvatinib therapy. For patients who were expected to have a good response to lenvatinib, the administration of lenvatinib should be considered. For patients who were expected to have a negative response to lenvatinib, it should be recommended to switch to other first-line treatment as soon as possible.

Our study had some limitations. First, this study was a retrospective study with a limited sample size, which might have selection bias. Therefore, further prospective studies based on a larger cohort would be necessary to verify the results. Second, the segmentation of region of interest was manually delineated by radiologists, which was time-consuming and might affect the results due to changes in users. Third, the model in our study was used to predict the response to lenvatinib monotherapy, but lenvatinib seemed to be less frequently used alone in clinical practice. In future studies, we would expand our study to predict the efficacy of combination therapy for patients with HCC.

Conclusions

We constructed a combined model based on radiomics features of five MRI sequences, clinicoradiological characteristics and EGFR level. This model had good prediction efficiency and might provide a new tool to guide clinical.

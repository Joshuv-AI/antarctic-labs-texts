Effect of internet usage on the life satisfaction of the elderly spousal caregivers of disabled elderly
Authors: Jia Li, Nan Liang, Yue Wu, Xiangcao Ma, Zangyi Liao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This empirical study examines the impact of internet usage on the life satisfaction of spousal caregivers responsible for elderly individuals with disabilities. Utilizing data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), released in November 2023, we analyzed the health status and life satisfaction of these caregivers in the context of the recent rapid development of the internet. A stratified Proportionate to Size Sampling (PPS) approach was employed in this investigation. We assessed whether the life satisfaction of spousal caregivers is associated with internet use and whether internet adoption can moderate intergenerational relationships, interpersonal interactions, and political engagement among these caregivers. The study analyzed a sample of 760 individuals, with an average age of 60.31 years, and rural households comprising 77.14% of the sample. Our findings indicate a positive correlation between internet usage and the life satisfaction of spousal caregivers. This result enhances our understanding of the relationship between internet usage and life satisfaction in this context. Furthermore, this study supports the advancement of internet access for the aging population, assists elderly individuals in bridging the digital divide, and provides a theoretical foundation for the development of targeted, elderly-friendly internet policies by the government.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73298-8.

Introduction

Improving the life satisfaction of the elderly is a critical issue for ensuring social security1. According to statistical data, the population aged 60 and above in China reached 267.36 million in 2022, accounting for 18.90% of the total population (The National Bureau of Statistics of China, 2022). It is projected that the number of disabled elderly individuals in China will increase to 61.68 million by 2030 and 97.50 million by 20502. Due to the declining birth rate, home-based care has diminished, leading to increased responsibility and caregiving burdens for the spouses of disabled elderly individuals3.

Both practitioners and academics have emphasized the significance of internet usage on the life satisfaction of older spousal caregivers. According to the 48th Statistical Report on Internet Development in China released by the China Internet Network Information Center (CNNIC), as of June 2021, the total number of internet users in China was projected to reach 1.011 billion, with individuals aged 60 and above constituting 12.2% of this population. The proportion of elderly individuals utilizing the internet continues to exhibit growth. For older adults, internet use can enhance interpersonal interactions, increase access to community resources, and serve as a bridge for social participation. However, due to variations in individual circumstances, living environments, and educational levels among older adults, they may encounter numerous challenges when navigating the online realm. Consequently, these individuals, referred to as “digital refugees,” require increased societal support to effectively harness the benefits of the internet and seamlessly integrate into the “information age”4.

Aging is an inevitable process in human life. The elderly are not isolated individuals but are interconnected with specific social environments. Consistent engagement with family, friends, and the surrounding social environment is essential for them to establish a proper role identity and effectively demonstrate their individual value. Particularly for older spousal caregivers, the continuous concern for their disabled partners often results in a sacrifice of personal freedom and a diminished ability to participate in social activities5. This can lead to a decline in their sense of identity within societal roles6. For these individuals, the internet serves as a practical tool for facilitating social interaction among elderly spouses from diverse backgrounds.

According to the Activities of Daily Living (ADL) Scale, the term “disabled elderly” refers to individuals aged 60 years and above who require assistance with at least one daily activity, such as eating, dressing, getting in and out of bed, using the toilet, navigating indoors and outdoors, and bathing. The term “spousal caregivers of disabled elderly” refers to spouses aged 60 years and above who provide unpaid care, financial support, and emotional assistance to disabled elderly individuals.

This study contributes to the existing literature in five key ways. First, it advances our understanding of the relationship between internet use and life satisfaction among spousal caregivers of disabled elderly individuals. Second, it investigates the impact of internet use on life satisfaction, considering intergenerational relationships, interpersonal relationships, and social participation as mediating variables, thus highlighting their influence. Third, it explores the contextual factors surrounding internet use among older spousal caregivers by examining their social backgrounds and accounting for both the spouses’ and caregivers’ educational levels. Fourth, it proposes a model that illustrates how three distinct pathways affect the life satisfaction of spousal caregivers of disabled elderly individuals. Finally, by analyzing the effects of internet use on life satisfaction within this group, the study offers strategies to enhance their internet utilization, along with data-driven insights and policy recommendations for decision-makers.

The paper is structured as follows: Section “Literature review” presents a literature review, section “Methods” outlines the methodology applied, section “Results“ analyzes the results, and section “Discussion” discusses strategies for promoting internet use among spousal caregivers of disabled older adults.

Literature review

To gain a comprehensive understanding of the current state of research and identify existing knowledge gaps, we examine the relevant literature related to our topic. Three key streams of studies are particularly relevant to our research, as outlined below.

Connotation and influencing factors of life satisfaction

Life satisfaction is an overall subjective evaluation based on a specific standard of living7. It is a perceptual factor that constitutes a crucial component of subjective well-being6. As a key indicator of subjective well-being, life satisfaction reflects an individual’s assessment of the overall quality of their life8, encompassing both material and spiritual dimensions.

The life satisfaction of older spousal caregivers is influenced by various demographic variables such as age, gender, education level, and living environment9. For older adults, particularly certain special groups within this population10, the fulfillment of individual social needs is a prerequisite for realizing personal value and engaging in social participation11,12. Compared to the happiness levels reported by disabled elderly individuals themselves, their spouses tend to experience lower levels of life satisfaction13,14. The primary stressors faced by these spouses arise from their own declining health and the excessive mental pressure associated with year-round caregiving responsibilities at home15. In addition to receiving support from their children within the family context, spouses caring for disabled elderly individuals also require external social support networks16,17. Access to psychological support within society is essential for these individuals to regulate their physical and mental well-being and alleviate stress levels18.

Purposes of the internet for the elderly

Elderly individuals have increasingly recognized the value of the internet in facilitating timely and intimate contact with their children, relatives, and friends through its social functionalities19,20, thereby addressing their social needs21. Data from Europe and the United States indicate that 64% of older adults use the internet for daily social interactions. Many seniors view the internet as a crucial tool for maintaining their physical well-being22,23, as it allows them to access health information online and acquire knowledge relevant to their daily lives. Additionally, online health consultations via digital platforms24 support their health maintenance objectives25. The internet is increasingly playing a significant role in daily communication practices26,27, providing abundant information and resources that enhance the lives of seniors and offer them greater convenience28.

The relationship between internet use and life satisfaction of the elderly

Current research indicates that internet use among elderly individuals can enhance their life satisfaction29,30. A primary factor contributing to low life satisfaction among elderly individuals living alone is their profound sense of loneliness31. However, the advancement and widespread adoption of the internet32have provided more opportunities for elderly individuals to access it33. Through internet use, elderly individuals can maintain timely and intimate connections with their children34, relatives, and friends35, acquire positive emotional experiences36, and enhance their interpersonal communication skills37. This suggests a potential positive correlation between increased internet usage and reduced feelings of loneliness among elderly individuals38. Moreover, the internet can augment social capital for older adults4while encouraging greater engagement in social matters39. Many older adults utilize the internet as an essential tool for maintaining physical health by accessing health-related information for both learning purposes and practical application in daily life40. Consequently, internet use can improve self-rated health levels among older adults.

In intergenerational relationships, older individuals must establish meaningful connections with their family41, friends, and the broader social environment to develop a proper role identity and showcase their personal worth42. Consequently, fostering mutual support and companionship between parents and children is advantageous for both family harmony and the psychological well-being of elderly individuals7. The internet serves as a bridge that enhances intergenerational connections, mitigates feelings of loneliness among caregivers of the elderly population, and boosts their overall life satisfaction31. In interpersonal relationships, caregivers can create value and receive rewards by effectively utilizing available resources while also benefiting from interacting with peers who serve as positive role models38. By eliminating time and space constraints, the internet provides social channels through which caregivers can enhance interaction within their interpersonal networks, alleviate emotional loneliness, and maintain good mental states, ultimately leading to increased levels of satisfaction43. From the perspective of social participation, the development of the internet has expanded opportunities for communication, dissemination, information sharing, and expression; it has revolutionized people’s lifestyles while significantly impacting the lives of elderly individuals37. The internet has emerged as an effective tool for promoting age-friendly development, thereby positively influencing caregivers’ attitudes toward enhancing life satisfaction among disabled elderly spousal caregivers.

Summary

The emergence of the internet has provided older people with more avenues for social participation, enriching their lives and enhancing their satisfaction44. At the same time, technological advancements have prompted older people to actively utilize the internet to meet diverse needs28. Most existing studies have focused on the direct impact of internet use on the life satisfaction of older adults while neglecting the mediating variables that contribute to this impact45,46. Therefore, this study aims to consolidate relevant theories into two key aspects: intergenerational relationships47, interpersonal interactions, and social participation. Building on this foundation48, a theoretical analysis framework is established. Consequently, we pose the following questions:

RQ1: What’s the relationship between internet use and elderly caregivers’ life satisfaction?

RQ2: Do intergenerational relationships, interpersonal connections, and social engagements mediate the relationship between internet use and elderly caregivers’ life satisfaction?

Methods

Database selection

The data used in this study were extracted from the 2020 China Health and Aging Longitudinal Survey (CHARLS), which captures micro-level characteristics of the middle-aged and elderly population across 28 provinces, municipalities, and autonomous regions in China. The CHARLS2020 questionnaire includes key variables relevant to this study, such as internet usage over the past months, depression scale, self-rated health, cognitive status, number of outpatient visits and associated costs in the previous month, and hospitalizations and related expenses in the preceding year. With these variables, the questionnaire provides a robust foundation for research endeavors. After reviewing the data and excluding samples with missing variables, a total of 760 samples were retained for analysis. These samples met the criteria of caregivers aged 60 years or older who care for disabled elderly spouses, with an average age of 60.32 years. We employed a range of variables, including gender, household registration status, average education level, economic status, intergenerational relationships, interpersonal relationships, and social participation, to examine internet usage among the target group. Leveraging the rapid development of the internet in recent years, continuous follow-up visits enabled a more comprehensive exploration of health status and life satisfaction among spousal caregivers of disabled elderly individuals. This research received ethical approval from the School of Public Administration, Dongbei University Of Finance And Economics, ensuring its scientific validity, rationality, and ethical compliance.

Variable setting

In this study, the dependent variable is life satisfaction, categorized into three levels based on responses to the “my life satisfaction” question in CHARLS’s (2020) questionnaire. The specific categories are as follows: unsatisfied = 1 (participants who responded with 1 or 2), average = 2 (participants who responded with 3), and satisfied = 3 (participants who responded with 4 or 5).

The independent variable in this study is internet use. A dichotomous variable, “whether to use the internet,” is employed to examine internet use. Respondents who answer “Yes” are classified as internet users and assigned a score of “1,” while those who answer “No” are classified as non-internet users and given a score of “0.” Additionally, variables measuring intergenerational relationships, interpersonal relationships, and social participation among disabled elderly individuals are considered as mediating factors in assessing the impact of internet usage on life satisfaction. Control variables such as age, gender, education level, and family economic status are also included in the model to ensure data integrity. This study employed the Probability Proportional to Size (PPS) sampling method, which ensures that each unit has a sampling probability proportional to its size by utilizing auxiliary information. This approach optimizes the use of sample data to enhance both estimation accuracy and efficiency. The implementation of PPS sampling is complex and requires precise data accuracy. In this study, samples were drawn from the nationwide tracking survey conducted in 2020, ensuring high data quality and representativeness, thus making it suitable for analyzing issues related to population aging in China. PPS sampling increases the number of observations from larger groups or units within the sample, which more accurately reflects the structure and characteristics of the population. As a result, this improves the representation of the population in Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) analysis, leading to enhanced accuracy and reliability in model parameter estimation. Results

Our final sample consisted of 760 participants, aged between 60 and their 80s. The participant distribution was nearly equal between genders, with agricultural caregivers accounting for 32 (28.51%) individuals and non-agricultural caregivers comprising 80 (71.49%). The majority of the caregivers considered themselves financially secure, with an equal proportion of economically disadvantaged and affluent caregivers (25.45% and 23.30%, respectively). This study employs a generalized structural equation model to analyze the data, utilizing robust standard errors to estimate the economic impact of the variables. The impact of internet use on mediating variables

The results from Table 3 indicate that the regression coefficient for Internet use is 0.338, with a highly significant p-value (0.000) at the 1% level of significance. Similarly, the regression coefficient for the interpersonal relationship pathway is 0.330, also significant at the 1% level. Additionally, the regression coefficient for social participation is estimated at 0.616 and remains statistically significant at the 1% level. These findings suggest that Internet usage positively influences intergenerational relationships, interpersonal relationships, and social participation among older caregivers to varying degrees. Furthermore, it strengthens these aspects by enhancing pathways related to social participation.

The influence of mediating variables on life satisfaction of elderly caregivers

In terms of intergenerational relationships, elderly caregivers who have more frequent contact with their children via the Internet experience significantly greater life satisfaction compared to those with less frequent contact (p = 0.076 at the 10% significance level). Regarding interpersonal relationships, elderly caregivers with general or good interpersonal relationships consistently exhibit a positive impact on life satisfaction, which is significant at the 1% level (p < 0.001). From the perspective of social participation, an increase in political participation correlates positively with improved life satisfaction among elderly caregivers (correlation coefficient = 0.616, p < 0.001), with significance at the 5% level.

Path analysis

In the context of “intergenerational relationships,” an increase in contact frequency with children is positively associated with improved life satisfaction among elderly caregivers, as indicated by a significant regression coefficient of 0.025. Similarly, Internet use also contributes to higher contact frequency between research participants and their children, with both factors being statistically significant. Regarding “interpersonal relationships,” Internet use enhances interpersonal communication skills and fosters better relationships, as evidenced by a significant regression coefficient of 0.061. Additionally, improved interpersonal relationships facilitate greater Internet usage among spouses caring for disabled elderly individuals, with a statistically significant effect at the 1% level (regression coefficient = 0.272). Concerning “social participation,” Internet use leads to increased enthusiasm and active engagement in social activities, as demonstrated by a highly significant regression coefficient of 0.723 at the 1% level. Furthermore, social participation positively impacts life satisfaction among spouses caring for disabled elderly individuals, with a statistically significant effect at the 5% level (regression coefficient = 0.224). Robustness test

We utilized data from the 2015 China Health and Aging Longitudinal Survey (CHARLS 2015) to validate the model. The findings demonstrate that Internet usage exerts a significant impact on intergenerational relationships, interpersonal connections, and social engagement. Specifically, employing the Internet can effectively enhance intergenerational relationship status (regression coefficient = 0.544, p < 0.01). Furthermore, Internet utilization positively influences interpersonal relationships at a statistically significant level of 1% (regression coefficient = 0.247). The improvement in interpersonal connections significantly contributes to the life satisfaction of elderly caregivers. Additionally, political participation exhibits a notable enhancing effect on the life satisfaction of elderly caregivers (regression coefficient = 0.109, p < 0.10).

The findings suggest that the impact of Internet use on intergenerational relationships, interpersonal interactions, and social participation among older caregivers remains consistent despite the influence of COVID-19. These effects are consistently positive at a statistically significant level of 1%, although the regression coefficients vary slightly. This implies that “intergenerational relationships,” “interpersonal interactions,” and “social engagement” each have distinct but positive influences on the life satisfaction of elderly family caregivers. Discussion

This study assessed the impact of Internet usage on the life satisfaction of spouses caring for disabled elderly individuals. We observed that Internet use positively influenced intergenerational relationships, interpersonal connections, and social engagement among these spouses, all of which significantly affected their well-being. In contrast, spouses who did not use or could not access the Internet had limited contact with their children, relatives, and friends, as well as lower levels of social participation. Our findings suggest the following:Enhancing aging policies for spousal caregivers by addressing infrastructure gaps and improving the availability of aging-related industries and services can increase Internet adoption rates among older adults. Promoting a societal environment conducive to active aging and healthy lifestyles is crucial for improving life satisfaction among spouses caring for disabled elderly individuals.

To address the issue of population aging in China, it is imperative to embrace the concept of active aging and enhance the life satisfaction of spousal caregivers for disabled elderly individuals. This approach is essential for tackling the challenges faced by these caregivers. In terms of future research directions, both government and society should prioritize efforts to promote Internet literacy among elderly caregivers, advance Internet infrastructure development, and bridge urban-rural divides. Education on digital skills for the elderly should be prioritized to facilitate their online activities while minimizing associated risks. Families also play a role in providing digital support and encouraging elderly relatives to learn about and engage with technology, thereby enhancing intergenerational connections. Additionally, diversified pension services delivered through online platforms, such as “online communities” or “cloud pensions,” can help broaden social circles for disabled elderly individuals and their spouses, thereby improving overall happiness levels.

Limitation and future direction

The investigation conducted in this study into the mechanisms through which Internet use impacts the life satisfaction of spousal caregivers for disabled elderly individuals is not yet complete, particularly with regard to the understanding of mediating effects. Additionally, it is important to note that this study utilizes CHARLS data from 2020, which may differ significantly from current conditions, thus posing a limitation to our research.

Future studies should explore three key aspects: First, when considering individual factors, it is crucial to simultaneously focus on social influences and analyze micro-level individuals in conjunction with macro-level policy analysis. Second, future research should assess both the impact and effectiveness of intergenerational relationships, interpersonal relationships, and social participation as mediating variables affecting caregivers’ life satisfaction. We speculate that as disability or deteriorating health conditions worsen among elderly or spousal recipients, caregivers may reduce their usage of internet technology. Thus, the influence of internet usage on caregivers’ life satisfaction may exhibit dynamic characteristics. Third, in terms of expanding mediating variables, further exploration should consider other potential factors influencing the relationship between internet usage and caregivers’ life satisfaction. Future research could also focus on the design and optimization of electronic products and network services tailored to the needs of the elderly population.

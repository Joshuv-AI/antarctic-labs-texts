Investigating the psychometric properties of pathological concern questionnaire in Indian emerging adults
Authors: Shubham Gupta, Priyanka Tiwari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:The Pathological Concern Questionnaire (PCQ) measures thoughts, emotions, and actions based on attitudes, including repression of personal needs and overinvestment in satisfaction of others’ needs. The PCQ is widely used in the literature, but its psychometric properties are not well-established in the Indian population.Aim:The current study aimed to examine the reliability and validity of PCQ among emerging adults in India.Materials and Methods:The data were collected from 252 emerging adults, aged between 18 and 29 years using PCQ. Confirmatory factor analysis was conducted to analyse the data. Internal reliability was computed using Cronbach’s alpha and correlational analysis was conducted to examine the convergent and discriminant validity.Results:The statistical findings supported an acceptable goodness of fit for PCQ in India. PCQ showed an adequate internal consistency (α = 0.903). In addition, correlational analyses supported the convergent and discriminant validity of the PCQ.Conclusion:Our findings suggest that the PCQ is a valid and reliable instrument to measure pathological concern among Indian emerging adults.

INTRODUCTION

Pathological concern is a “phenomenon which is characterised by two attitudes—denial and repression of one’s needs and overinvestment in satisfying others’ needs.”[1] It is important to consider pathological concern seriously because it directly or indirectly results in intrapersonal and interpersonal concerns like loneliness, cognitive decline, attachment insecurities, interpersonal conflicts, reduced well-being, hopelessness, and emotional and cognitive suppression among individuals.[123] These concerns if not treated can result in the development of psychopathology.

The psychological problems are observed profoundly during the emerging adulthood since this developmental period is marked by transformation in social and family system in India causing a threat to interpersonal processes in emerging adults.[4567] Emerging adulthood is a developmental phase where transition between adolescence and young adulthood occurs. It includes exploration of one’s identity (in various areas of life including love, work), increased responsibility towards self and others, taking major decisions independently, and financial independence.[89] Emerging adults, thus, have an increased responsibility toward their careers, their families, and interpersonal relationships, and failure to fulfil these responsibilities may result in higher stress, lower psychological well-being, and higher psychological distress among them.[1011121314] The emerging adults may suppress their own thoughts and feelings to perform the responsibilities toward their careers and others. Intriguingly, habitually suppressing thoughts and feelings results in physiological problems including cardiovascular problems and metabolic dysregulation.[1516] In addition, emotional suppression is also associated with mental health concerns, such as lower social well-being, higher stress, impaired decision making, impaired sleep quality, anxiety, and depressive symptoms.[17181920] The long-term consequences also include development of mental health disorders like anxiety and depressive disorders.[212223]

Alarmingly, some studies have reported that the detrimental nature of components of pathological concern tend to result in mental illnesses in emerging adults including depressive disorders, eating disorders, and anxiety disorders.[2425] This is a matter of concern since the occurrence of these disorders in Indian emerging adults is also raising at an alarming rate.[262728] The India State-Level Disease Initiative in a study in 2020 also reported the substantial increase in the occurrence of depressive, anxiety, and eating disorders among younger population in India.

The pathological concern has been studied in different cultures, and Pathological Concern Questionnaire has been validated across some cultures.[29] The theoretical model from which PCQ is derived has its base in psychoanalysis. Individuals tend to have an ability to associate themselves and others as either objects or subjects, and individual differences are present in this ability wherein they have to relate to self and others simultaneously. This is usually innate, and it originates from experiences early in life where the capacity to acknowledge mutually was either reinforced or discouraged. The pathological concern tends to occur in interpersonal situations where self is considered as an object and other individual as a subject. This concern is distinctive by having two key features: repressing and denying one’s self-related needs and investing excessively in meeting the needs of others.[1] In India, as shared above, suppressing of one’s feelings tends to have adverse consequences including anxiety issues and mental disorders.[30313233343536] Understanding the sensitivity of pathological concern and its consequences in other cultures, it is important to understand this construct to work on specific areas internally and externally (like emotion regulation, interpersonal relationships) for the better course of development during the emerging adulthood in Indian population. Once studied, specific interventions can be planned and implemented to reduce pathological concern and strengthen healthy concern among the emerging adults.

As there is currently no instrument in India that can give a reliable and valid measure of pathological concern, the current study thus examined the psychometric properties of pathological concern on emerging adults in India.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Setting and participants

The inclusion criteria of the study included nonclinical individuals ranging from 18 to 29 years, belonging to Delhi/NCR region, can comprehend Hindi and English language. A convenient sampling method was used for collecting data. The sample size was calculated using G power software. The study is quantitative, and cross-sectional design was used for this research. The participants below 18 years and above 29 years were not included in the study. This research was conducted after the approval from the Ethical Committee of Manav Rachna International Institute of Research and Studies.

Tools

Sociodemographic data sheet

The data sheet is used to collect age, gender, state, city, religion, education, occupation, relationship status, family type, residence, family income, presence of physical or mental illness, and presence of psychiatric treatment or counselling.

Pathological Concern Questionnaire (PCQ)

The PCQ[1] is an 18-item self-report questionnaire of two facets of pathological concern: denial of one’s needs, feelings, and overinvesting in fulfilling and satisfying other peoples’ needs. Each item is rated on a Likert scale (7-point) of 1 (not at all) to 7 (very much). This scale has high reliability where Cronbach α is 0.89.

Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale-Short Form (DERS-SF)

The DERS-SF[37] is an 18-item self-report instrument assessing concerns regarding emotions in six facets including lack of awareness of emotions, lack of emotional clarity, lacking acceptance of emotional responses, difficulty in engagement in goal targeted behaviour, limited repertoire of strategies to manage emotions, and difficulties in handling impulses. The assessment is conducted using a Likert scale which ranges from 1 (almost never) to 5 (almost always). The scale shows good psychometric properties where Cronbach’s α lies in a range of .78 to .91.

Cognitive Flexibility Inventory (CFI)

The CFI[38] is a self-report measure having 20 items measuring two facets of cognitive flexibility, control (tendency or ability to perceive difficult circumstances as controllable), and alternatives (ability to perceive alternative reasons or solutions to difficulties). The items are measured using a Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree). This scale has adequate psychometrics where Cronbach’s α for alternative is .91 and for control is .89.

Measure of Attachment Style (MOAS)

The MOAS[39] is a self-report scale having 27 items which measures attachment patterns of people. It uses a Likert scale which ranges from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The psychometric properties of this tool are fine where Cronbach’s α is .80.

Procedure

The study was conducted in various public and private universities, offices, and residential areas in Delhi/National Capital Region (NCR). Emerging adults who met the inclusion criteria as shared in setting and participants were invited to participate in the study and those who agreed, completed the PCQ and other psychometric measures. They were informed about the study, confidentiality was ensured, and written informed consent was taken from the participants. Data were collected using convenience sampling technique after the approval from the Institutional Ethical Committee, Manav Rachna International Institute of Research and Studies. Participants completed the instruments independently. About 277 participants completed the instruments out of which 25 were removed who did not met the inclusion criteria and a final sample of 252 participants was analysed. The data were then screened for missing values, and no missing values were observed. The obtained data were then coded and proceeded for data analysis.

Statistical analysis

After receiving participants’ responses, data were checked and statistical analyses were conducted using confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) through Amos Software version 26.0. Descriptive statistics, internal consistency, and correlational analysis were examined using SPSS Version 25.0.

Reliability of PCQ

The internal consistency reliability of PCQ was assessed, and the results showed that the reliability of PCQ in Indian emerging adults was high (Cronbach α =0.903).

Convergent and discriminant validity of PCQ

For determining the convergent and discriminant validity, DERS-SF, MOAS, and CFI were administered. Convergent validity was evaluated by studying the relationships between PCQ, DERS-SF, and MOAS. As shown in Table 3, PCQ was significantly correlated with DERS-SF indicating that a person with higher pathological concern on PCQ was associated with higher difficulties in regulating emotions on DERS-SF. In addition, PCQ was positively linked with all the subscales of DERS-SF (including strategies, impulse, goals, clarity, nonacceptance) except awareness with which it was negatively linked. Furthermore, the research has also found that pathological concern was positively linked with ambivalent attachment style and avoidant attachment style. PCQ was also significantly correlated with cognitive control and alternative subscales of CFI but had insignificant correlation with CFI as a whole construct.

Moreover, discriminant validity is “the extent to which measures of theoretically distinct constructs are unrelated to one another,”[42] which was examined by calculating correlations between PCQ, CFI, and MOAS. The discriminant validity was confirmed by nonsignificant correlations between pathological concern and secure attachment style (r = 0.016; P = 0.80). In addition, the relationship between pathological concern with cognitive flexibility was also found to be insignificant (r = 0.049; P = 0.43).

DISCUSSION

Pathological concern is a problematic concern which can lead to reduced cognitive control and hinders processing of affective processes.[343] It also results in attachment insecurities, anxiety, loneliness, stress, depression, and other psychological issues and illnesses.[128] It is important to see pathological concern as an important clinical phenomenon that needs tools to measure and identify that will help the mental health professionals and doctors to recognize its presence early and provide appropriate treatment and interventions to deal with it.

The current research is the first study to examine the psychometric properties of PCQ in Indian emerging adults. Confirmatory factor analysis results showed that the demonstrated model of PCQ fitted the data and turned out to be an acceptable and a good fit. The reliability of PCQ was observed to be 0.903 indicating a high and a good internal reliability. The internal consistency is in line with the results of the original version[1] and versions established in different regions of Israel where Cronbach’s alpha were found to be 0.87 in two studies[244] and 0.88 in another study.[29] Moreover, the original version has explained pathological concern and its two components using only one factor. However, in our study, pathological concern has been examined using two factors, explaining the two components or closely linked attitudes: one related to self where there is denial of one’s feelings and second related to others where an individual tends to overly invest in fulfilling the needs of others. All the items in both the factors showed high loading (loading > 0.40), and both the factors resulted in a model having an acceptable fit as shown in Table 2 and Figure 1. In addition, Cronbach’s alpha was estimated to be 0.903 instead of 0.89 as in the original version, indicating an excellent internal consistency as reported by previous studies.[45] Thus, the bifactor model in our study thus fitted the data better than its original version.[1]

Bifactor model of PCQ

Hence, our results encourage the use of pathological concern questionnaire in Indian population. As we are aware that India is a collectivistic culture, and it is seen that collectivistic cultures tend to have an external locus of control where people usually strive towards social harmony,[46] and this in turn can lead to dependence on others and may influence one’s personal needs negatively.[47] Self is also defined in context with others in collectivistic societies and is linked with reciprocity, empathy, dependency, and belongingness. In these societies, relationships with other people are primarily focused while personal space and autonomy are considered secondary.[4849] Our study also found that the PCQ correlated with all the subscales of DERS-SF reflecting that higher levels of pathological concern can result in higher problems in managing emotions. Similar findings have been reported in the literature.[50] The correlation of PCQ with ambivalent and avoidant was significant as well which is in line with the findings from previous studies.[144] Surprisingly, PCQ was not found to be correlated with CFI but showed significant correlations with its subcomponents, control, and alternatives. It is possible that entire cognitive flexibility may not show significant correlations since higher cognitive flexibility may or may not lead to pathological concern directly. Previous studies have also supported this fact where a direct connection between both the constructs was missing.[5152]

From our findings, it is evident that the current measure helps in understanding the existing problems among the Indian emerging adults in relating to oneself and one’s identity in various relationships. In addition, owing to a collectivistic culture, most Indians are usually taught to prioritize relationships over oneself,[4849] and so they tend to cater to the feelings and needs of others before themselves. This is turn may result in overlooking one’s own feelings and needs, and consistently prioritizing others over self may lead to psychological problems including lower emotional well-being, lower positive affect as reported by few studies.[53]

Thus, the psychometric properties of PCQ are established in some cultures, but its validity and reliability in India has not been examined. Our study is the first to study the factor structure of PCQ in Indian emerging population. This reliable and validated tool is a credible measure to assess pathological concern in emerging adults. However, certain limitations need to be considered while interpreting the findings. Most of the participants were nonclinical samples. Previous studies also have not examined the efficacy of this construct in clinical population. The reliability and validity of PCQ in clinical samples will help in understanding its efficacy in this population. In addition, the participants were emerging adults. Future studies can focus on incorporating a more diverse distribution of participants to assess the age-based differences. Moreover, the data were collected from few states of India. Data can be collected from different areas of India for generalizing the results of the study.

Considering the findings and limitations of the study, PCQ can find its application in clinical and nonclinical settings which can help in identifying pathological concern, formulating interventional plans focusing on intrapersonal needs and emotional processes for the betterment of one’s well-being and development.

CONCLUSION

The confirmatory factor analysis conformed the reliability and validity of PCQ in a sample of emerging adults in India. Future studies can focus on developing interventions focusing on intrapersonal problems in interpersonal relationships.

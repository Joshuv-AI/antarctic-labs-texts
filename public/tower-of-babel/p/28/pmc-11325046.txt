MVP enhances FGF21‐induced ferroptosis in hepatocellular carcinoma by increasing lipid peroxidation through regulation of NOX4
Authors: Jinkun Xia, Boqi Fu, Zhe Wang, Gaolin Wen, Quanshui Gu, Dayu Chen, Haozhen Ren
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFerroptosis is a novel, iron‐dependent regulatory cell death mainly caused by an imbalance between the production and degradation of intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS). Recently, ferroptosis induction has been considered a potential therapeutic approach for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) is a new modulator of ferroptosis; however, the regulatory role of FGF21 in HCC ferroptosis has not been investigated. In this study, we explored the role of FGF21 and its underlying molecular mechanism in the ferroptotic death of HCC cells. We identified Major vault protein (MVP) as a target of FGF21 and revealed that knockdown of MVP inhibited the lipid peroxidation levels of HCC cells by decreasing NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4, a major source of ROS) transcription, thereby attenuating the effect of FGF21‐mediated ferroptosis. On the other hand, MVP overexpression showed the opposite results. Mechanistically, MVP binds to IRF1 and thus interferes with the interaction between IRF1 and the YAP1 promoter, leading to an increase in NOX4 transcription. Importantly, forced expression of IRF1 or downregulation of YAP1 partially reversed the effect of MVP overexpression on HCC ferroptosis. Furthermore, the results in xenograft tumor models suggested that overexpression of MVP can efficiently increase the level of lipid peroxidation in vivo. Taken together, these results provide new insights into the regulatory mechanism of ferroptosis in HCC.

INTRODUCTION

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the main histological subtype of liver cancer, is a highly heterogeneous disease with poor survival due to a high recurrence rate and a lack of effectual treatment options. 1 Most HCC patients are diagnosed at intermediate or advanced disease stages and have no opportunity for surgical treatment. 2 Sorafenib, a multikinase inhibitor and the standard first‐line drug for advanced HCC, has increased the survival rate of progressive HCC. 3 Unfortunately, drug resistance to sorafenib can lead to unsatisfactory efficacy. 4 To date, the five‐year survival rate of liver cancer patients in China is only 14.1%. 5 Therefore, it is urgent to seek effective new drugs and explore the underlying molecular mechanism of HCC to open new avenues for cancer therapies.

Ferroptosis, a new type of programmed cell death process, is described by iron overload, lipid reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation and lipid peroxidation. 6 Ferroptosis is distinct from other major traditional forms of regulated cell death (RCD) and has unique morphological and bioenergetic features. 7 , 8 Ferroptosis can be characterized into three major components: lipid peroxidation substrates, lipid peroxidation drivers, and the anti‐ferroptosis system. 9 Disruption of the balance of these elements, such as excessive accumulation of ROS and activation of lipid peroxidation, is linked to ferroptosis‐dependent membrane rupture and affects the final consequences of cell death. 10 Induction of ferroptosis alone or in combination with other drugs exerts considerable potential during cancer therapy, especially for malignant tumors that are resistant to conventional treatments, including HCC. 11 , 12

Fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF21) is an endocrine hormone that is synthesized and excreted mainly by the liver and has an essential role in the modulation of glucose, lipid, and energy metabolism in the organism. 13 , 14 Administration of FGF 21 in rodents or nonhuman primates can alleviate a range of obesity‐related metabolic complications by improving oxidative stress and reducing lipid peroxidation, including reduced fat mass and hyperglycemia, insulin resistance, dyslipidemia, cardiovascular disease, and nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH). 15 Moreover, FGF21 appears to play a part in preventing the progression of advanced pathologies, such as pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) or HCC. 16 Recently, FGF21 has been identified as a regulator of ferroptosis 17 ; however, there is still a lack of evidence regarding whether FGF21 is implicated in the regulation of ferroptosis in HCC.

In this research, we confirmed Major vault protein (MVP) as a target of FGF21. Knockdown of MVP expression inhibited lipid peroxidation levels in HCC in vitro and in vivo, while MVP overexpression showed the opposite results. Mechanistically, MVP triggers ROS accumulation and lipid peroxidation by enhancing NADPH oxidase 4 (NOX4) protein expression through the IRF1‐YAP1 axis. Overall, we demonstrate that the activation of MVP contributes to the FGF21‐mediated ferroptosis process and that targeting this pathway has the potential to provide new strategies and opportunities for the treatment of HCC.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Human subjects study

Ten pairs of primary liver cancer and normal tissue specimens were provided by the Affiliated Drum Tower Hospital of Nanjing University Medical School (Nanjing, China). The Ethics Committee of Affiliated Drum Tower Hospital of Nanjing University Medical School approved this study, and signed informed consent forms were obtained from all the patients enrolled in this study.

Cell culture and treatment

The cells were cultured in DMEM (Gibco) or RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco), penicillin (100 U/mL), and streptomycin (100 mg/mL) and maintained at 37°C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2. When the cell density reached 60%–70%, the cells were treated with ferrostatin‐1 (Fer‐1, S7243), or N‐acetyl‐L‐cysteine (NAC, S1623), which were obtained from Selleckchem (Houston, TX, USA).

Bioinformatics analysis

The transcriptomic data of MVP in normal and HCC samples were extracted from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets. Gene set evaluation analysis (GSVA) was used to reveal the potential biological function of MVP based on the analysis of the relevant TCGA database. The GeneMANIA database was utilized to make predictions of potential interactions between multiple proteins.

Lentiviral infection and small interfering RNA (siRNA) transfection

The FGF21‐overexpressing lentiviral GV492 vector, MVP‐overexpressing lentiviral GV492 vector, IRF1‐overexpressing lentiviral GV492 vector, MVP small hairpin RNA (shRNA) lentiviral GV493 vector (target sequence: TGGAGGAGCTGCTGAACAA), p53 shRNA GV493 vector (target sequence: CACCATCCACTACAACTACAT) and their corresponding control lentiviral vector (named negative control, NC) were constructed and generated by GeneChem (Shanghai, China). siRNA against YAP1 and control siRNA were obtained from TSINGKE Biological Technology (Nanjing, China), and the interferin reagent (Polyplus, USA) was used based on the protocol. We referred to the instructional method to construct the above stable knockdown and overexpression cell lines. The siRNA sequences of YAP1 are as follows: si‐YAP‐1: 5′‐GGUCAGAGAUACUUCUUAATT‐3′, si‐YAP‐2: 5′‐GAGUACAGACAGUGGACUATT‐3′, and si‐YAP‐3: 5′‐GAAUGAACAUAGAAGGAGATT‐3′. The siRNA sequences with the highest silencing efficiency (si‐YAP‐1, named si‐YAP) were screened through experiments for further gene function research.

Quantitative real‐time polymerase chain reaction (qRT–PCR)

Total RNA from liver cancer cell lines and human normal cells was extracted by TRIzol™ (Life Technologies, USA), and cDNA was synthesized by using HiScript II RT SuperMix for qPCR + gDNA wiper (Vazyme Biotech, Nanjing, China), followed by real‐time PCR analysis using SYBR Green Premix Ex Taq (yeasen, Shanghai, China). Human β‐actin was used as the internal reference gene, and the relative mRNA expression was calculated by the 2−ΔΔCT method. The primers used for qRT–PCR are listed in Table 1.

Western blot analysis and coimmunoprecipitation (co‐IP)

Total proteins were derived from HCC tissue samples or cells using RIPA buffer (KeyGEN BioTECH, Nanjing, China) with phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF). Protein concentrations were calculated utilizing a bicinchoninic acid (BCA) protein assay kit (KeyGen BioTECH, Nanjing, China). Western blot and co‐IP analyses were carried out as described previously. 18 Antibodies against NOX4 (ab154244), NOX2 (ab129068), N‐cadherin (ab76011), Claudin‐1 (211737), YAP1 (ab205271), IRF1 (ab191032), ACSL4 (ab155282), GPX4 (ab125066), p53 (ab26), β‐actin (ab8227), and GAPDH (ab181602) were purchased from Abcam (Cambridge, MA, USA). Antibodies against Vimentin (60330‐1‐Ig), FTL (10727‐1‐AP), β‐catenin (51067‐2‐AP), and GSK3beta‐S9 (67558‐1‐Ig) were provided by the Proteintech group (Wuhan, China). Antibodies against MVP (sc‐23916) were supplied by Santa Cruz Biotechnology (Shanghai, China). The bands of Western blots were normalized to GAPDH or β‐actin and were processed by Image J.

Immunohistochemical staining

HCC tissues and paired normal tissues were formalin‐fixed, paraffin‐embeddedas described previously. 19 Those tissues were incubated with anti‐MVP (1:200 dilution) and were scored independently by two clinicopathologists for staining intensity (0, negative; 1, weakly expressed; 2, moderately expressed; 3, strongly expressed) and the percentage of stained area of MVP‐positive cells (0, 0%; 1, 1%–25%; 2, 26%–50%; 3, 51%–75% and 4, 76%–100%). The resulting score was calculated based on the proportion of positive tissue multiplied by the staining intensity (range 0–12: high expression, staining index score ≥5; low expression, staining index score <5).

Measurement of intracellular ROS and MDA

ROS levels were measured using a ROS Assay Kit (Beyotime, China) with the DCFH‐DA oxidation method. Briefly, Huh7 HCC cells were incubated with 10 μmol/L DCFH‐DA probes for 30 min in a 37°C incubator to allow the fluorescent probe to diffuse into the cells. Afterward, the cells were washed with PBS three times to remove excess dye. DCFH‐DA was sequestered intracellularly by de‐esterification and then oxidized by peroxides to generate the fluorescent compound DCF. DCF fluorescence was determined using a flow cytometer, and data were processed with FlowJo software. Malondialdehyde (MDA), an end product of lipid peroxidation, is positively correlated with ferroptosis. The MDA concentration in HCC cells was detected using a cell MDA assay kit (S0133S, Beyotime, Shanghai, China) following the method indicated in the product manual.

Xenograft mouse model

Approximately 5 × 106 of the indicated Li‐7 or Huh7 cells were subcutaneously injected into the axilla of male BALB/c nude mice (3–4 weeks, n = 5–6 per group) (GemPharmatech, Nanjing, China) to build the xenograft mouse model. The tumor length (L) and width (W) were measured twice per week, and tumor volume (V) was calculated as follows: (3.14 × L × W2)/6. Finally, the mice were anesthetized and euthanized by intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium 150–200 mg/kg and dissected immediately to measure the tumor weights. The Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Drum Tower Hospital of Nanjing University Medical School approved the animal experiments.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0. All data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were compared using Student's t‐test or one‐way ANOVA. The relationship between MVP expression and pathological parameters was analyzed using Pearson's Chi‐square test with SPSS 22.0 (SPSS, USA). p < 0.05 was considered significant (*p < 0.05; **p < 0.01; ***p < 0.001).

RESULTS

FGF21 is a novel ferroptosis regulator, and its activation induces ferroptosis in HCC cells

To reveal the potential biological functions and regulatory mechanisms of FGF21 in HCC, Huh7 cells were infected with lentivirus carrying FGF21 to stably express FGF21 (Figure 1f), and RNA sequencing (RNA‐Seq) was performed to identify FGF21 target genes. By analyzing the data with a high cutoff value (|log (2) fold change > 2, p < 0.05), 264 upregulated genes and 75 downregulated genes were identified and displayed in the volcano plot (Figure 1a). Subsequently, gene functional enrichment was performed by gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) and Wikipathways (Figure 1b,c). Our results indicated that FGF21 was significantly linked to oxidative response damage, ferroptosis, dysregulation of Hippo signaling, etc. To determine whether FGF21 plays a role in ferroptosis regulation in HCC, we detected ROS and lipid peroxidation levels, which are the leading cause of ferroptosis, in FGF21‐OE cells (Figure 1g,h). The results demonstrated that FGF21 overexpression markedly enhanced ROS and MDA levels in HCC cells. To further verify this observation, transmission electron microscopy (TEM) was utilized to reveal morphological changes in HCC cells with or without FGF21 overexpression (Figure 1i). Compared with control cells, FGF21‐OE cells showed diminished mitochondria, reduced or vanished mitochondria crista, and concentrated mitochondrial membrane densities, which are hallmarks of ferroptosis caused by disruption of mitochondrial membranes. These findings suggest that FGF21 overexpression triggers ferroptosis by inducing lipid ROS in HCC cells.

MVP is a target of FGF21, and increased MVP expression predicts an unfavorable prognosis in HCC

MVP, the main constituent of a ribonucleoprotein complex, is one of the proteins significantly upregulated in HCC. Intriguingly, GSVA analysis was conducted to explore the biological pathways of MVP through the TCGA‐LIHC database, and the results showed that MVP expression is tightly associated with the ROS pathway (Figure 2a). Hence, we hypothesized that the MVP‐associated ROS pathway may be involved in FGF21‐mediated regulation of ferroptosis in HCC. To investigate the expression pattern of MVP in HCC, the TCGA, and GEO (GSE45267 and GSE112790) data portals were utilized (Figure 2b,c). The results suggested that MVP mRNA expression was markedly enhanced in HCC tissues compared with control tissues. MVP mRNA and protein expression was also higher in most liver cancer cell lines (LM3, SUN387, HepG2, Hep3B, PLC/PRF/5, Li‐7, and Huh7) than in a nonmalignant cell line (LO2) (Figure 2e). Furthermore, we used immunoblotting to examine the expression levels of MVP in HCC tissues from 10 fresh HCC patients and observed a similar result: MVP expression was upregulated in HCC tissues (Figure 2d). A representation of images with distinct MVP expression levels is displayed in Figure 2f.

Suppression of MVP decelerates FGF21‐mediated ferroptosis in HCC cells

To investigate whether MVP was involved in ferroptosis regulation in HCC, we examined ROS production, lipid peroxidation levels, and mitochondrial morphology in MVP knockdown and overexpression cell lines (Figure 3c–e). The results showed that overexpression of MVP triggered the accumulation of ROS and activation of lipid peroxidation levels, as well as shrinkage of mitochondria (Figure 3f,g), while the absence of MVP had the opposite effect. NOX2 and NOX4, two enzymes that generate and activate ROS production, are considered to be essential for the regulation of ferroptosis. 20 , 21 We first assessed the mRNA and protein expression levels of NOX2 and NOX4 in FGF21‐OE cells (Figure 1d,f). The results indicated that FGF21‐OE increased the expression levels of NOX4 but not NOX2. Moreover, we found that FGF21 overexpression partially reversed the downregulation of NOX4 levels caused by ferrostatin‐1 (Fer‐1, an inhibitor of ferroptosis)‐treated HCC cells (Figure 1e). Western blotting was also carried out to detect the protein expression levels of the ROS‐generating enzymes NOX2 and NOX4 in MVP knockdown and overexpression cells, respectively. Similarly, we found that overexpression of MVP could only activate the expression of NOX4 but not NOX2, while knockdown of MVP inhibited the transcription levels of NOX4 (Figure 3a). To investigate the crucial role of MVP in the process of FGF21‐mediated ferroptosis, we transfected MVP shRNA into Li‐7 cells with stable FGF21 overexpression. Intriguingly, we found that downregulation of MVP reduced the expression level of NOX4 but not NOX2 in FGF21‐OE cells (Figure 3b). Additionally, we observed that the deletion of MVP could partially reverse the accumulation of ROS and the upregulation of lipid peroxidation levels caused by FGF21 overexpression (Figure 3d,e). We subsequently obtained a more visual microscopic morphology by TEM. Consistent with the previous results, TEM results showed reduced mitochondria and increased membrane density with vestigial cristae in FGF21‐overexpressing HCC cells, and these effects were partially reversed after MVP knockdown treatment (Figure 3g). Taken together, these findings indicated that downregulation of MVP weakens FGF21‐induced ferroptosis in HCC cells.

MVP activates the transcription of NOX4 by regulating the IRF1‐YAP1 axis in HCC cells

The Hippo pathway has a significant impact on modulating ferroptosis; furthermore, regulating YAP/TAZ, which are the important effectors of the Hippo pathway that activate ferroptosis, is a new strategy to conquer drug resistance in cancers. 22 YAP/TAZ target genes modulate the process of ferroptosis in many manners, such as modulation of PUFA‐PL synthesis (ACSL4), intracellular iron availability, and generation of ROS (NOX2/NOX4). Enrichment analysis suggested that the expression of FGF21 was associated with an abnormal Hippo‐Merlin pathway (Figure 1b); however, whether they were involved in the regulation of ferroptosis in HCC is still unknown. Interestingly, we found that MVP can directly regulate the transcription of the interferon regulatory factor (IRF) family, 23 , 24 and IRF1, a tumor suppressor gene induced by interferon‐gamma (IFN‐γ) that plays an important role in cell death, is a potential transcription factor of YAP1. 25 Therefore, we suspect that MVP may affect YAP1 signaling by interfering with the expression of IRF1. We first queried the potential interactions between MVP and IRF1 by the GeneMANIA database, and the results are displayed in Figure 4a. To confirm this prediction, we further conducted co‐IP assays and demonstrated that MVP physically interacted with IRF1 (Figure 4b). Moreover, the interactions between IRF1 and YAP1 were confirmed (Figure 4c). Considering that MVP negatively regulates the expression of IRF1 (Figure 3a), we subsequently transfected IRF1‐overexpressing lentivirus into Huh7 cells with stable MVP overexpression. Western blot analysis indicated that forced expression of IRF1 could partially reverse the regulation of ferroptosis‐related genes, including YAP1, NOX4, and FTL, by MVP overexpression (Figure 4d). Consistent with these results, immunofluorescence indicated that MVP overexpression enhanced the transcriptional level of YAP1, while this effect was partially reversed by treatment with IRF1‐overexpressing lentivirus (Figure 4f). The enhanced ROS and MDA levels in MVP‐OE HCC cell lines were also decreased after transfection with IRF1‐overexpressing lentivirus (Figure 5g,h). Given that MVP positively regulates YAP1 expression (Figure 3a), we adopted siRNA to suppress YAP1 and transfected YAP1 siRNA into stable MVP‐overexpressing HCC cells, and deletion of YAP1 also partly abolished the regulatory effect caused by MVP overexpression on ferroptosis‐related genes, including YAP1, NOX4, and FTL, but not NOX2 (Figure 4e). These results demonstrate that MVP can interact with IRF1, and subsequently, IRF1 binds to the promoter of YAP1, thus leading to the activation of NOX4. Therefore, we inferred that MVP activates ROS accumulation in MDA levels by upregulating NOX4, and this phenomenon is partially mediated by regulation of the IRF1‐YAP1 axis in HCC cells.

MVP positively regulates the transcription level of NOX4 in a p53‐independent manner

To further demonstrate whether there was an underlying connection between ROS generation caused by MVP overexpression and the alterations in NOX4 regulation in HCC cells, we pretreated HCC cells with the ROS inhibitor N‐acetylcysteine (NAC, an antioxidant). We found that NAC inhibited the expression of NOX4, while MVP overexpression partially restored the expression of NOX4 (Figure 5a,b). A previous study demonstrated that MVP can induce the degradation of p53, a crucial modulator of ferroptosis. 24 Immunofluorescence results confirmed that MVP overexpression in lentiviral‐transfected or NAC‐treated HCC cells could contribute to the downregulation of p53 (Figure 5b). To further determine whether MVP regulation of NOX4 occurred in a p53‐dependent manner, we constructed a p53 knockout Huh7 cell line (Huh7sh‐p53) and overexpressed MVP in Huh7sh‐p53 cells (Figure 5c). We detected that MVP overexpression could also mediate NOX4 upregulation and ROS accumulation even in the absence of p53 (Figure 5d,e), suggesting that MVP regulation of NOX4 could occur independently of p53. Interestingly, p53 knockdown significantly suppressed the expression of glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4), the cornerstone of the antioxidant defense system, in MVP‐overexpressing HCC cells, indicating that p53 may interfere with MVP‐mediated ferroptosis regulation by an alternative mechanism.

MVP overexpression increases lipid peroxidation in HCC in vivo

Finally, to confirm the effects of MVP on the tumorigenicity of HCC in vivo, a subcutaneous xenograft model was established. As presented in Figure 6a, neoplasms generated by MVP‐downregulated cells were generally smaller and lighter than those generated by control HCC cells, suggesting that MVP knockdown was effective in restraining tumor growth in vivo (Figure 6b,c). In contrast, the tumor volume and weight were increased by cells with high MVP expression (Figure 6d). Compared with tumors induced by cells with high MVP expression, a significant reduction in the volume and weight of subcutaneous tumors was obtained in MVP‐OE cells transfected with IRF1‐overexpressing lentivirus, suggesting the important role of IRF1 in MVP‐mediated tumorigenesis (Figure 6e,f). To visualize the potential effects of ferroptosis, we performed IHC staining of subcutaneous tumor sections with 4‐hydroxynonenal (4‐HNE), a sensitive marker for lipid peroxidation reactions. 26 Consistent with the previous results, IHC staining displayed lower expression of 4‐HNE in the sh‐MVP‐treated HCC cells than in the control group, while MVP overexpression had the opposite effect (Figure 6g). In addition, forced expression of IRF1 partially abolished the high 4‐HNE expression in MVP‐overexpressing cells. Therefore, MVP overexpression increases lipid peroxidation in HCC in vivo, and IRF1 is implicated in this process.

DISCUSSION

Sorafenib is currently an effective first‐line therapeutic agent in advanced HCC; however, resistance to sorafenib is becoming increasingly prevalent. 27 Several studies have suggested that the induction of ferroptosis may be a potential strategy for overcoming sorafenib resistance 28 ; thus, elucidating the underlying mechanism of ferroptosis in HCC is necessary. Recent studies have revealed that FGF21 is a novel ferroptosis suppressor and that FGF21 induction may offer a promising strategy for the management of iron overload‐induced liver injury and fibrosis. 17 In this study, we revealed that FGF21 activated MVP expression and further upregulated the expression of NOX4 via the IRF1‐YAP1 axis, thus leading to ROS production and high levels of lipid peroxidation. This finding suggests that FGF21‐mediated regulation of ferroptosis may vary depending on the type of disease and the molecular mechanisms involved, and we hypothesized that targeting this pathway to augment FGF21‐induced ferroptosis offers a promising therapeutic option to conquer drug resistance in HCC.

The unique and evolutionarily highly conserved MVP is the main component of unique cellular ribonucleoprotein particles. 29 It has been shown that MVP plays a crucial role in multidrug resistance, autophagy and cell signaling in many tumors and is therefore a potential therapeutic target for many types of tumors. 30 , 31 Here, we identified MVP as a crucial regulator of FGF21‐mediated ferroptosis. We revealed that MVP knockdown weakened FGF21‐induced ferroptosis in HCC cells by inhibiting ROS production and lipid peroxidation levels. Conversely, lipid peroxidation levels were increased in MVP‐overexpressing cells in vitro and in vivo. These findings indicated that MVP may have an essential role in FGF21‐induced ferroptosis by activating lipid ROS levels.

The Hippo pathway is an evolutionarily conserved pathway that contributes to a variety of biological functions, including cell proliferation and regulation of organ size. 32 Recently, ferroptosis was observed to be sensitive to Hippo signaling‐modulated cell density. 33 Mechanistically, high‐density proliferative tumor cells activate intracellular Hippo signaling through E‐cadherin‐mediated cell–cell contact interactions, which inhibits the transcriptional coregulatory activity of YAP1. 34 At low cell density, the Hippo pathway is closed, and unphosphorylated YAP/TAZ translocates to the nucleus and further regulates downstream ferroptosis‐related genes. 34 Nonetheless, inhibition or induction of ferroptosis by YAP1/TAZ signaling varies based on disease category, cell type, and molecular mechanism. NOX4 is a novel genetic determinant of ferroptosis regulated by Hippo signaling, and elevation of NOX4 increases ferroptosis‐dependent cytotoxicity by causing oxidative stress‐induced lipid peroxidation. 35 In the present study, we observed excess ROS production and MDA accumulation by MVP‐induced NOX4 activation. We identified IRF1, a transcription factor that interacts with the YAP1 promoter, as one of the downstream regulatory molecules of MVP. IRF1 overexpression or YAP1 knockdown both partially attenuate MVP‐OE‐mediated NOX4 upregulation, suggesting that MVP transcriptionally regulates NOX4 partly through the IRF1‐YAP1 pathway.

p53 plays a key but complex role in ferroptosis regulation by modulating both the canonical (GPX4‐dependent) and noncanonical (GPX4‐independent) ferroptosis pathways. 36 MVP has been reported to trigger p53 degradation. 24 We hypothesized that MVP‐mediated ferroptosis may be correlated with the status of p53. Interestingly, we found that in the p53‐deficient state, the activation of NOX4 by enforced expression of MVP was not affected, but the expression of GPX4 (core factor antagonizing ferroptosis) was significantly inhibited. This suggests that the MVP‐mediated activation of lipid peroxidation occurs independently of p53.

There are certain limitations to the present study. First, this study did not perform ChIP‐seq/ChIP‐qPCR experiments or dual luciferase assays to demonstrate that MVP blocked IRF1 transcription, and refinement of these results would more strongly confirm the regulation of downstream protein transcription by MVP. Furthermore, this study has not yet elucidated how lipid peroxidation of MVP/NOX4 promotes HCC progression; and it is unclear whether there is a connection between the two different phenotypes of MVP/NOX4 promoting lipid peroxidation and MVP/NOX4 regulating HCC proliferation. More experiments/markers for ferroptosis should be tested to validate the overall ferroptosis levels in HCC cells, and co‐IP experiments should also be performed in HCC cell lines, instead of HEK293 cells; these results need to be verified further in subsequent studies.

In summary, our results reveal a novel role of MVP in the modulation of FGF21‐mediated ferroptosis by modulating lipid peroxidation levels, and modulating the MVP/IRF1/YAP1/NOX4 cascade to induce ferroptosis may be utilized as a potential therapeutic approach to overcome therapy resistance in HCC (Figure 6h).

Association between blood pressure and metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease among adults without hypertension: a cross-sectional analysis of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey 2017–2018
Authors: Shujing Wu, Ning Chen, Mengzhang Wu, Yongquan Wang, Zhaoming Huang, Jie Zhang, Jianan Li, Demin Xu, Lihua Guan, Junbo Ge
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractObjectiveHigh blood pressure (BP) is one of the optional components of metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD). However, whether subclinical elevations in BP are related to MAFLD even in the absence of hypertension remains unclear. We investigated the association between BP and MAFLD among adults without hypertension.Design and settingA nationwide, multicentre and cross-sectional study of the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES).Participants2022 US adults without hypertension were recruited in the 2017–2018 cycle of the NHANES.ExposureSystolic BP (SBP) and diastolic BP (DBP).Outcome measuresMAFLD was defined by the novel consensus of diagnostic criteria in 2020.ResultsMAFLD is common among US adults even at the ‘normal’ BP ranges, with a prevalence of 38.8%. We observed a positive, linear relationship between SBP and MAFLD down to 90 mm Hg, and between DBP and MAFLD down to 50 mm Hg. The MAFLD risk was elevated significantly with increasing SBP level (per 10 mm Hg increase: adjusted OR, 1.59 (95% CI, 1.21 to 2.09)) in the range of 90–129 mm Hg. The relationship between DBP and MAFLD was not evident (per 10 mm Hg increase: adjusted OR, 1.42 (95% CI, 0.96 to 2.21)) in the range of 50–79 mm Hg. Similar findings were identified in the sensitivity analyses.ConclusionsIn a population without hypertension, SBP is significantly associated with MAFLD, indicating potential early intervention to prevent MAFLD.Keywords: Blood Pressure, Chronic Disease, Hepatology

Introduction

Elevated blood pressure (BP), even within the normal range, is commonly used to identify individuals at high risk of cardiovascular diseases (CVDs).1 2 Furthermore, emerging evidence suggested that the raised BP remained as one of the foremost modifiable risk factors for chronic kidney diseases and other cardiometabolic diseases.3 4

Metabolic dysfunction-associated fatty liver disease (MAFLD), a novel concept that clearly highlights the role of metabolic dysfunction in this disease,5 affects up to a third of the global population and accounted for the vast majority of chronic liver diseases.6 The diagnostic criteria for MAFLD are hepatic steatosis in addition to one of the following three types of evidence: type 2 diabetes mellitus, overweight/obesity or metabolic dysregulation.5 Hypertension often coexists with other metabolic abnormalities in MAFLD,5 which makes it difficult to verify whether elevated BP is independently associated with MAFLD. Moreover, the association between BP level and the risk for MAFLD among healthy individuals without hypertension remains uncertain.

Therefore, using data from the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), we selected participants without hypertension to explore the association between BP level and the risk for MAFLD and whether there are thresholds below which systolic BP (SBP) and diastolic BP (DBP) levels are no longer associated with increased risk for MAFLD.

Methods

Study population

The study sample consists of participants from 2017 to 2018 in NHANES, which assessed liver function using ultrasound and vibration-controlled transient elastography (VCTE) for the first time in the survey. NHANES is an ongoing, multistage, cross-sectional and nationally representative survey of the civilian population in the USA. All data and materials have been made publicly available at the National Center for Health Statistics website (https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/index.htm).

Of the total 9254 participants enrolled in this survey cycle, adults without hypertension who were included in the study were required to provide complete information on BP levels and MAFLD components. Therefore, 6589 were excluded for the following reasons: (1) aged <18 years (n=3398); (2) self-reported or screen-detected hypertension (n=3217); (3) potential hypotension (n=83); (4) missing information on BP levels (n=409) and MAFLD (n=125). Finally, a total of 2022 eligible individuals were included in the final analyses (online supplemental figure S1).

Definition and assessment of BP and MAFLD

BP measurements were taken at the Mobile Examination Center during the NHANES examination visits. After resting quietly in a sitting position for 5 min, BP was measured three times, and the average readings of SBP and DBP were used for analysis. Screen-detected hypertension was defined as SBP≥130 mm Hg and/or DBP≥80 mm Hg, according to the 2017 American College of Cardiology (ACC)/American Heart Association (AHA) hypertension guideline.7 Self-reported hypertension included people who gave a yes response to the question: ‘Ever told you had high blood pressure?’. Participants were categorised according to SBP levels 90–109, 110–119 and 120–129 mm Hg or according to DBP levels 50–59, 60–69 and 70–79 mm Hg.

MAFLD was diagnosed as hepatic steatosis confirmed by ultrasound or VCTE, with at least one of the following components: overweight/obesity (body mass index (BMI) ≥ 25 kg/m2), diabetes mellitus or at least two metabolic risk abnormalities, including (1) waist circumference ≥102 cm in men and ≥88 cm in women, (2) fasting plasma triglycerides 150 mg/dL or specific drug treatment, (3) high-density lipoprotein cholesterol <40 mg/dL in men and <50 mg/dL in women or specific drug treatment, (4) prediabetes (fasting glucose 100–125 mg/dL, 2-hour postload glucose levels 140–199 mg/dL or haemoglobin A1c 5.7%–6.4%) and (5) homeostasis model assessment of insulin resistance score ≥2.5 and (6) high-sensitivity C-reactive protein level >2 mg/L.5 The FibroScan 502 Touch device was used to evaluate participants for VCTE. Controlled attenuation parameter (CAP) was a validated indicator for liver steatosis in patients with fatty liver disease. Significant steatosis was defined as CAP ≥248 dB/m.8

Covariates

Information on age, sex, race/ethnicity, education, smoking and drinking status and physical activity was obtained using standard questionnaires. Race/ethnicity was categorised as non-Hispanic white, non-Hispanic black, Mexican American and other. Education was grouped into less than high school and high school education or above. Smoking/drinking status was categorised into self-reported current smokers/drinkers or non-smokers/non-drinkers.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were performed accounting for the complex survey design. Characteristics of the study participants were described in weighted means (95% CIs) for continuous variables and weighted percentages (95% CIs) for categorical variables. The weighted linear regression was used to compare continuous variables, and a weighted χ2 test was used to compare categorical variables across BP categories.

To delineate the BP levels and the possibility of being MAFLD, the restricted cubic splines with 3 knots at the 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles were fitted, with reference values of 90 mm Hg for SBP and 50 mm Hg for DBP. We used survey-weighted logistic regression models to assess the associations of BP categories with MAFLD and its individual components. Restricted cubic splines and logistic regression models were adjusted for age, sex, race (non-Hispanic white, non-Hispanic black, Mexican American and other), education (with/without high school education or above), current smokers (yes/no), current drinkers (yes/no) and physical activity.

We also conducted several sensitivity analyses to evaluate the robustness of our primary findings. First, we repeated the weighted logistic regression models in participants aged <50 years and ≥50 years in both men and women. Interactions between BP levels and age or sex categories in associations with MAFLD were estimated by including the product term in the models. Second, we additionally evaluated the associations between different SBP categories and MAFLD.

Statistical significance level was set at p<0.05. All the statistical analysis was conducted using the survey package in R V.4.3.1 (http://www.r-project.org).

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans of this research.

The multivariable-adjusted restricted cubic spline analyses revealed a positive, linear relationship between SBP/DBP and MAFLD (figure 1). The event numbers and ORs of MAFLD in each SBP and DBP group are presented in table 2. With respect to MAFLD prevalence, it increased from 30.1% in individuals with an SBP of 90–109 mm Hg to 49.7% in those with an SBP of 120–129 mm Hg, and from 33.5% to 44.0% in individuals with a DBP of 50–59 mm Hg to a DBP of 70–79 mm Hg, with an overall prevalence of 38.8%. Compared with participants with an SBP of 90–109 mm Hg, the multivariable-adjusted risk of MAFLD increased by 90% and 134% in participants with an SBP of 110–119 mm Hg or 120–129 mm Hg, respectively (table 2). Using SBP of 90–99 mm Hg as the reference, similar associations were observed in different SBP categories as those in the primary analyses (online supplemental table S1). For every 10 mm Hg increase in SBP, the odds of developing MAFLD increased by 59% (table 2). Compared with participants with a DBP of 50–59 mm Hg, higher DBP groups were associated with a numerically higher but not statistically significant risk of MAFLD (DBP of 60–69 mm Hg: adjusted OR, 1.22 (95% CI, 0.68 to 2.21); DBP of 70–79 mm Hg: adjusted OR, 1.87 (95% CI, 0.79 to 4.41); per 10 mm Hg increase in DBP: adjusted OR, 1.42 (95% CI, 0.96 to 2.21); table 2). The sensitivity analyses stratified by age and sex did not change the pattern of results (all P for interaction ≥0.05; figure 2).

Discussion

In the present research, we investigated the association between BP levels and MAFLD in US adults without hypertension, using nationally representative data. We found a linear relationship between SBP/DBP and MAFLD prevalence. Using SBP of 90–99 mm Hg or 90–109 mm Hg as the reference, the MAFLD risk was elevated significantly with increasing SBP level as high as 129 mm Hg. Below 80 mm Hg, we did not have enough evidence suggesting associations between DBP and MAFLD. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to report the relationship between BP levels and MAFLD among adults without hypertension.

Hypertension, along with prehypertension and other hazardously elevated BPs, remains one of the most common chronic diseases and the leading cause of CVDs and death worldwide.9 10 Even among participants within the normal range (either updated <130/80 mm Hg or traditionally <140/90 mm Hg), elevated BP alone was reported to increase CVD risk.2 11 High BP often coexists with other metabolic diseases, such as diabetes, obesity and fatty liver disease.12 13 Of note, fatty liver disease, which remains a rising global epidemic, appeared to share a bidirectional relationship with hypertension.14 BP status has been reported to be associated with a progressively higher risk of liver steatosis.15 Previous studies revealed that, on the one hand, increased BP levels were considered risk factors for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)15 16; and on the other hand, NAFLD was also associated with the development of hypertension.17 MAFLD is now suggested as an updated definition for NAFLD to reflect more accurately the clinical features of fatty liver disease and help with risk stratification for clinical management.18 As is well established, high BP is a key component of MAFLD. Nevertheless, the relationship between ‘normal’ BP levels and MAFLD remains unclear. Moreover, the BP status determined in previous reports is heterogeneous,15 16 as hypertension was often defined by BP levels or the use of antihypertensive medications, regardless of whether the target BP was achieved or not.

Among this representative population without hypertension, we observed a positive, linear relationship between SBP and MAFLD, while the relationship between DBP and MAFLD was not significant. In line with other clinical outcomes, such as cardiovascular and renal diseases,19 SBP (range 90–129 mm Hg) was continuously related to MAFLD, indicating that more intensive SBP management might be beneficial for MAFLD prevention. In contrast, the influence of DBP (range 50–79 mm Hg) on MAFLD was not evident, which demonstrated that SBP might have a greater effect on MAFLD than DBP across the ‘normal’ BP spectrum.

Several mechanisms have been proposed to account for the role of BP in MAFLD. First, both MAFLD and high BP are closely linked with lifestyle and metabolic risk factors, such as drinking, obesity and insulin resistance,20,23 with insulin resistance being linked strongly to both high BP and MAFLD. A previous observational study reported on the prevalence of fatty liver disease in the setting of hypertension and the possible role of insulin resistance in this interplay by comparing non-obese, non-diabetic and hypertensive patients with healthy individuals.24 Although the role of hypertension in the setting of insulin resistance has not been fully delineated, hypertension was considered a predictor of insulin resistance in clinical evidence,25 26 which in turn was found to be associated independently with fatty liver disease.27 28 Moreover, the pathogenesis of MAFLD is complex, and the direct interplay between the clinical and molecular components linked to MAFLD development remains only partly understood. Elevated BP induces oxidative stress and inflammation,29 which is thought to play a vital role in developing MAFLD.30 Finally, the activation of the renin-angiotensin system (RAS) in elevated BP might contribute to MAFLD.31 32 Several studies underline the proposed role of RAS imbalance in promoting hepatic fibrogenesis.32,35 Angiotensin I receptor (AT1R) activation by angiotensin II induces hepatic stellate cell contraction and proliferation, which results in the induction of oxidative stress, endothelial dysfunction, cell growth and inflammation.33 34 In addition, AT1R blocker administration led to a reduction in inflammation and improvement of hepatic fibrosis.35

The current study has several limitations. First, temporality between BP levels and MAFLD cannot be inferred owing to the cross-sectional design and needs to be addressed in future prospective longitudinal studies. Second, the current study relied on several self-reported covariates that may result in misclassification and recall biases. Third, another problem concerned the lack of 24-hour ambulatory BP monitoring (ABPM) to provide a more accurate assessment of BP, which might result in misclassification bias. In addition to confirming the diagnosis of hypertension, ABPM can be used to identify BP phenotypes, including masked and nocturnal hypertension.36 Fourth, although multivariable-adjusted logistic regression models were used, residual or unmeasured confounding, such as other metabolic risk factors, remains possible. Finally, the generalisability of these findings to other geographic regions and ethnic populations may be limited.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our study demonstrates that MAFLD is highly prevalent in the US adult population without hypertension, raising concerns for future BP management in this condition. The elevated MAFLD risk started even at a ‘normal’ SBP range, while the positive relationship was not evident between ‘normal’ DBP and MAFLD. Controlling SBP intensively may be beneficial in preventing MAFLD.

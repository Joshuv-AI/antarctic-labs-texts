1. STUDY OF MICRONUCLEAR FREQUENCY AND ITS CORRELATION WITH CYTOLOGY, HISTOPATHOLOGY, AND IMMUNOHISTOCHEMISTRY (p53 AND p16) IN ORAL POTENTIALLY MALIGNANT DISORDERS AND ORAL SQUAMOUS CELL CARCINOMA: AN OBSERVATIONAL STUDY

Asmita Chakrabarti, Moumita Maiti, Pranabashish Banerjee, Gopinath Barui; Department of Pathology, Nil Ratan Sircar Medical College and Hospital, Kolkata, West Bengal, India

Introduction: Oral potentially malignant disorders (OPMDs) and oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) remain major health challenges in India, where delayed diagnosis contributes to poor outcomes. Micronuclear (Mn) frequency in exfoliated buccal epithelial cells is a simple marker of genomic instability and may serve as a useful adjunct in the early detection of dysplasia and malignancy. Aims: The present study evaluated Mn frequency in buccal smears of OPMDs and OSCC and examined its correlation with cytological and histological evidence of dysplasia and malignancy, as well as with immunohistochemistry (IHC) findings of p53 and p16. Materials and Methods: A descriptive, cross-sectional, observational study was conducted on 100 patients (56 OPMDs, 44 OSCC). All underwent scrape cytology followed by biopsy. Cytology and histology were assessed for dysplasia and malignancy using WHO criteria. Mn frequency per 100 cells was counted according to Tolbert’s criteria. IHC for p53 and p16 was performed on 64 biopsy samples. Results: Cytology and histology detected dysplasia in 55.4% and 69.6% of OPMDs respectively. Mn frequency was significantly higher in dysplastic versus non-dysplastic lesions in both cytology [2 (2-3)] vs. 1 (0-1)] as well as histology [2 (1-3) vs. 1 (0-1)]; (P < 0.05). In OPMDs, Mn frequency correlated with p53 positivity [2 (1.75-3)]; (P = 0.03) and showed a near significant association with p16 expression (P = 0.051). In OSCC, Mn counts were higher in cytologically malignant smears [2 (2-3)]; (P = 0.033). Mn frequency showed significant association with p16 expression (P = 0.007) but association with p53 was not significant (P = 0.259). Cytology demonstrated sensitivity of 66.7% to detect dysplasia in OPMDs and 95.5% to identify malignancy in OSCC. ROC analysis of OPMDs and OSCC showed good diagnostic accuracy of Mn frequency in OPMDs and OSCC in predicting dysplasia and malignancy respectively. Conclusion: Mn frequency is a simple, non-invasive biomarker for detecting oral epithelial dysplasia and early oral malignancy. Its association with p53 and p16 highlights its potential as a marker of genomic instability, complementing cytology in early detection and risk stratification.

2. DIAGNOSTIC UTILITY OF NUCLEAR MORPHOMETRY IN FINE NEEDLE ASPIRATES OF BREAST LESIONS

Bishakha Deb, Sushma Ramraje, Megha Kinake; Department of Pathology, Grant Government Medical College and Sir JJ group of Hospitals, Mumbai, Maharashtra, India

Introduction: Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology (FNAC) is a routine diagnostic modality for breast lesions but is subject to interpretive subjectivity, especially in the gray zone cases between benign and malignant lesions. Nuclear morphometry using computerised image analysis may enhance diagnostic accuracy by providing objective measurements of the FNAC smears. Aim: To evaluate the diagnostic utility of computerised nuclear morphometric assessment of FNAC smears in distinguishing benign and malignant breast lesions. Materials and Methods: A cross sectional study was conducted at a tertiary care teaching hospital over a period of 18 months. A total of 94 breast FNAC cases with subsequent histological diagnoses were included in the study, classified per the International Academy of Cytologists Yokohama System 2020 and further studied using Computerised Image Morphometry software (Morphonuclei v6.1). 50 nuclei per case were studied and the following 8 nuclear morphometric parameters analysed digitally:

Nuclear perimeter (Henceforth referred to as the Perimeter),

Nuclear perimeter

Area of the cell,

Maximum radius of the nucleus,

Nuclear density,

Nuclear area,

Nuclear Circularity

Nuclear elongation,

Chromatin texture

Statistical analysis was done using SPSS v29 software and included the Independent T – test and ANOVA. Observations and Results: Nuclear morphometric parameters specifically nuclear perimeter, area of the cell, maximum radius of the nucleus and nuclear density significantly differentiated between benign and malignant categories (P < 0.05). Chromatin texture abnormalities increased from benign to malignant lesions. Conclusion: Computerised nuclear morphometry on FNAC is a promising adjunct for improving objectivity particularly in diagnostically challenging cases. Further, it shows potential for integrating artificial intelligence with routine cytopathology for reporting of breast lesions and mass screening of malignancies.

3. ENHANCING ZIEHL-NEELSEN CYTOSMEAR TRIAGE WITH RESIDUAL ATTENTION AND CHANNEL FUSION

Lavleen Singh1, Venkateswaran K Iyer1, Deychen Myes2, Sherrin Jacob1, Partha Pratim Roy3, Aparajita Khan4, Parshiv Kapoor5, Yash Bansal5; 1Department of Pathology, All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), New Delhi, India, 2Department of Pathology, AIIMS-CAPFIMS, New Delhi, India, 3Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee, Roorkee, Uttarakhand, India, 4Department of Computer Science and Engineering, Indian Institute of Technology (BHU) Varanasi, Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh, India, 5Department of Biosciences and Bioengineering, Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee, Roorkee, Uttarakhand, India

Introduction: Manual screening of Ziehl–Neelsen (ZN) cytomears for acid-fast bacilli (AFB) is labor intensive and prone to fatigue-related oversight, especially when bacilli are sparse and stain variability is high To reduce review time while maintaining diagnostic sensitivity, we propose an AI triage system that prioritizes bacillary morphology (shape, edges) over color alone. Methods: Our framework, MorphoChanNet, analyzes 80 × 80 patches using a six-channel input: lightness, saturation, and value (LSV) plus their Sobel edge maps. Channels are selected based on reconstruction fidelity, ensuring preservation of bacillary contours across staining conditions. Features are processed through residual attention blocks and fused using reconstruction-guided channel weighting. Training employs a focal loss variant with added penalty for false positives, limiting artifact-driven alerts. A curated dataset of 200 annotated ZN images (100 AFB-positive, 100 AFB-negative; 1368 × 776 px) supported training and evaluation. Hard negatives were sampled from background, overstains, stain transitions, and bacillus-like debris. Interpretability was assessed via Grad-CAM. Workload was quantified with review fraction (RF), the proportion of patches flagged for pathologist review at high recall. Results: On held-out data, MorphoChanNet achieved F1 ≈ 97.2% and accuracy ≈97.6%, matching or exceeding deeper networks with fewer parameters. Grad-CAM localized to slender, rod-like structures, supporting face validity. At sensitivity-tuned thresholds, review fraction was reduced to a minority of patches, indicating practical workload savings. Discussion and Conclusions: By addressing stain variation, artifact confusion, and computational constraints MorphoChanNet translates accuracy into reduced screening burden. It enables pre-filtering of low-yield areas while preserving sensitivity, supporting adoption in high-volume and resource-limited labs. Future work will extend to pixel-wise localization and prospective workflow validation.

4. TRANSFER LEARNING IN CONVOLUTIONAL NEURAL NETWORK TO DIFFERENTIATE FOLLICULAR ADENOMA VERSUS FOLLICULAR CARCINOMA OF THYROID ON ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY MATERIAL

Dipanwita Biswas1, Pranab Dey2, Rajiv Savala1; 1Post Graduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, 2Department of Cytology and Gynecologic Pathology, Post Graduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, Currently Professor of Oncopathology, Homi Bhabha Cancer Hospital and Research Centre (Tata Memorial Center), New Chandigarh, India

Aims and Objectives: In the present paper, we tried to build a basic convolutional neural network model and a transfer learning model on it to distinguish follicular adenoma (FA) and follicular carcinoma (FC) of thyroid in fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC). Materials and Methods: We selected histopathology proven cases of FA (25 cases) and FC (29 cases). In each case, the best May-Grünwald-Giemsa-stained smear was selected and 10 to 11 representative microphotographs were taken with Olympus microscope (Olympus DP74) in 40X objectives from the most representative areas of the smear. There was total 347 microphotographs in FA and 229 microphotographs of FC. The images were divided into training (406 images, 70%), validation (89 images, 15%) and test set (81 images,15%). The basic (scratch) model of convolutional neural network (CNN) was built in Python 3.11.11. We subsequently also used a transfer learning model by a pre-trained MobileNetV2 in the same images. We ran the model in Jupyter notebook for 15 epochs with 13 steps. Results: The sensitivity and specificity of the base model were 72.41% and 88.46% respectively. The Accuracy, precision and F1 score were 82.71%, 77.78% and 75.00%. The area under the curve (AUC) of Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) was 0.85. The sensitivity and specificity of transfer learning model are 82.76% and 92.31% respectively. The Accuracy, precision and F1 score were 88.89%, 85.71% and 84.21%. The area under the curve (AUC) of Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) is 0.94.Conclusions: We built a CNN base model and transfer learning model (MobileNetV2) and successfully distinguished FA and FC in thyroid FNAC. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study of CNN in thyroid follicular tumours.

Keywords: Artificial neural network, CNN, Follicular tumour, Thyroid, Transfer learning

5. EGFR MUTATION, EGFR AND VEGF EXPRESSION IN FINE NEEDLE ASPIRATION CYTOLOGY SAMPLES IN METASTATIC ORAL SQUAMOUS CELL CARCINOMA

Jennifer Kimnuntluangi1, Meeta Singh1, Nidhi Verma1, Arun Kumar Rathi2, Binita Goswami3; 1Department of Pathology, Maulana Azad Medical College, New Delhi, India, 2Department of Radiotherapy, Maulana Azad Medical College, New Delhi, India, 3Department of Biochemistry, Maulana Azad Medical College, New Delhi, India

Background: Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is a major health burden in India, with most patients presenting at advanced stages and poor survival. Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) are important in tumor growth and angiogenesis, and their expression may influence prognosis. Fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) offers a minimally invasive method for diagnosis and biomarker evaluation. Aim: To assess EGFR mutations and EGFR/VEGF expression in FNAC samples of lymph nodes from metastatic OSCC and correlate findings with therapy response. Methods: A one-year prospective study of 117 metastatic SCC cases (61 oral cavity, 56 other/unknown) was conducted; 12 oral cases were excluded due to inadequate CB, and histopathology was available for 31 of the remaining 49, which were included for correlation. FNAC smears and cell blocks were examined for cytomorphology, immunohistochemistry (IHC) of EGFR and VEGF, and droplet digital PCR for EGFR mutations (T790M, L858R). Clinical data, treatment, and six months follow up information were recorded. Results: Patients (mean age 47.7 years; M:F = 9.3:1) most commonly had tongue primaries (25.8%). EGFR expression was positive in 87.1% and VEGF in 74.2%. EGFR mutations were rare (6.4%), with two cases positive. EGFR and VEGF expression significantly correlated (P = 0.008) and were associated with poor therapy response (EGFR: P = 0.001; VEGF: P = 0.016) and adverse outcomes (EGFR: P = 0.008; VEGF: P = 0.003). Conclusion: EGFR and VEGF overexpression are common in metastatic OSCC and predict poor response and outcomes. FNAC is a practical tool for IHC and mutation testing, supporting its role in prognostic evaluation.

6. EVALUATION OF DERIVED HEMATOLOGICAL MARKERS ACROSS VARIOUS GROUPS OF BETHESDA CERVICAL CYTOLOGY

Kamali, Rachagiri Suneel, Jyotsna Naresh Bharti, Prashant Joshi, M. Rajeshwari; Department of Pathology, AIIMS Mangalagiri, Mangalagiri, Andhra Pradesh, India

Background: Pap smear screening is cornerstone in cervical cancer screening and also in identifying premalignant conditions and various infections. Various haematological parameters have emerged as a potential biomarker in reflecting immune response and systemic inflammation. This study assesses the relationship between Bethesda cervical cytology categories and the haematological parameters. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to assess its significance across the Bethesda cervical cytology groups. A new composite marker CII Cervical Inflammation Index is also assessed in this study. Methods: It is a retrospective record-based study. Bethesda cervical cytology categories were divided in 7 groups (NILM, NILM with dense inflammation, NILM with BV, NILM with TV, NILM with candidiasis, Low-risk lesions and high-risk lesions). Haematological parameters like inflammatory ratios [Neutrophil Lymphocyte ratio (NLR), Monocyte Lymphocyte ratio (MLR), Platelet Lymphocyte ratio (PLR), Eosinophil Lymphocyte ratio (ELR)], Composite indices [Systemic Immune-Inflammation Index (SII), Pan-Immune Inflammation Value (PIV), Cervical Inflammation Index (CII)] and platelet indices [Plateletcrit (PCT), Mean Platelet Volume (MPV) and Platelet Distribution Width (PDW)] were assessed for significance across the Bethesda groups. A total of 455 cases, with 65 cases in each group were included after applying the exclusion criteria. Results: Data is not normally distributed hence represented by median with IQR. Overall statistical significance was observed in NLR, MLR, SII, PIV, MPV and PDW across the Bethesda groups (P = 0.041, 0.004, 0.047, 0.032, 0.019, 0.003 respectively). Pair wise comparison of NLR, MLR, SII and CII revealed high risk lesion group is significantly higher than low risk lesion group. MLR also showed significance between high-risk lesion group and NILM with BV group. PDW was significantly higher in both high risk and low risk lesion group when compared to NILM with BV group. ROC analysis of NLR, MLR, SII and CII showed significant predictive value in differentiating low and high-risk lesions with MLR showing highest AUC of 0.658 (P = 0.001). MLR and PDW showed better prediction between NILM with BV and high-risk lesion with AUC of 0.672 (P < 0.001) and 0.712 (P < 0.001) respectively. It also demonstrated acceptable discrimination between NILM with BV and low-risk lesion with AUC of 0.668 (P < 0.001). Conclusion: Derived haematological parameters can show distinct patterns across the Bethesda cervical groups with several markers elevated in high-risk group. Hence these markers can serve as a potential biomarker in stratifying patients and identifying patients with greater risk of cervical premalignant and malignant lesions.

7. TARGETED MICRORNA AND GENETIC ANALYSIS IN THYROID FNAC SPECIMENS WITH BETHESDA INDETERMINATE CYTOLOGy

Sharma Meghal1, Rohilla Manish2, Srinivasan Radhika2, Uma Nahar3, Divya Dahiya4, Harish Bhujade5, Sourabh Patro6, Anish Bhattacharya7; 1Departments of Pathology, Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, 2Departments of Cytology and Gynaecological Pathology, Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, 3Department of Histopathology, Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, 4Department of General Surgery, Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, 5Department of Radiodiagnosis, Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, 6Department of Otolaryngology (ENT), Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India, 7Department of Nuclear Medicine, Postgraduate Institute of Medical Education and Research, Chandigarh, India

Introduction: Thyroid cancer is the most common endocrine malignancy. TBSRTC indeterminateCategories (IntC) III–V pose diagnostic challenges, and Molecular testing is recommended to reduce the frequency. Aim: To determine the utility of miRNA expression and mutational analysis in differentiating benign and malignant TBSRTC indeterminate thyroid nodules. Material and Method: The study includes sixty FNAC thyroid cases of IntC (III and IV), out of which 30 were retrospective with known histopathology and 30 were prospective cases. Nucleic acids (DNA and RNA) were extracted from FNAC smears by the Cytoscrape method, and BRAF V600E/RAS gene mutations and miRNA (miRNA146b, miRNA221, miRNA222) were performed by qPCR and RT-qPCR, respectively. Results: BRAF V600E/RAS mutational analysis was successful in 55/60 (91.6%) cases (21-CatIII, 39-CatIV), and 27/55 (49.1%) had BRAF/RAS mutations. The most common mutations were NRAS and HRAS (12cases each), followed by dual KRAS and NRAS (2cases), and BRAFV600E mutation in only one case of PTC. These findings indicate potential roles for these miRNAs in differentiating the neoplasm from non-neoplastic benign lesions. Conclusion: Molecular testing is feasible in FNAC and is a useful adjunctive tool in the evaluation of indeterminate thyroid-nodules. BRAF V600E/RAS mutation testing has high specificity, and miRNA differentiates between neoplastic and non-neoplastic thyroid nodules with high accuracy. Combining cytomorphology with molecular tools improves diagnostic precision and avoids unnecessary thyroid surgeries.

8. DIAGNOSTIC UTILITY OF BRAFV600E IMMUNOCYTOCHEMISTRY ON THINPREP CYTOLOGY SMEARS IN INDETERMINATE CATEGORIES (IV–VI) OF THE BETHESDA SYSTEM FOR REPORTING THYROID CYTOPATHOLOGY.”

G Preethi Nissy, Jyotsna Naresh Bharti, Jyoti Verma, Meenakshi Yeola, Hemant Singh; AIIMS Mangalagiri, Near Vijayawada, Andhra Pradesh, India

Background: Fine-needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) is the cornerstone for evaluating thyroid nodules; however, indeterminate categories of the Bethesda System (IV–VI) often pose diagnostic challenges. The BRAFV600E mutation, a key activator of the MAPK pathway, is strongly associated with papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC). Immunocytochemistry (ICC) on ThinPrep liquid-based cytology provides a practical adjunct for mutation detection. Aim: To assess the diagnostic utility of BRAFV600E ICC on ThinPrep cytology in Bethesda IV–VI categories and correlate expression with histopathological and clinicopathological parameters. Methods: A prospective study was conducted on 48 thyroid FNAC samples (Bethesda IV–VI) processed by ThinPrep. ICC using VE1 monoclonal antibody for BRAFV600E was performed and compared with immunohistochemistry (IHC) on surgical specimens as the reference standard. Diagnostic performance indices were calculated, and correlations with clinicopathological parameters were analyzed. Results: BRAFV600E ICC positivity showed a stepwise increase with Bethesda category: 17.6% in Category IV, 80.0% in Category V, and 81.0% in Category VI (χ² = 17.93, P = 0.000128). ICC demonstrated high concordance with IHC, with sensitivity 87.1%, specificity 94.1%, positive predictive value 96.4%, negative predictive value 80.0%, and diagnostic accuracy 89.6%. BRAFV600E positivity was significantly associated with Bethesda category (P = 0.000128) and malignant outcome (P = 0.001), but not with age, gender, laterality, or other histopathological risk factors. Expression was largely restricted to PTC and its subtypes, while all benign tumors and NIFTP were negative. Conclusion: BRAFV600E ICC on ThinPrep cytology is a reliable, cost-effective adjunct in thyroid cytology, with strong diagnostic utility in Bethesda V and VI lesions.

9. UTILITY OF IMMUNOCYTOCHEMISTRY IN CEREBROSPINAL FLUID OF PEDIATRIC B-ALL PATIENTS FOR CNS INVOLVEMENT: A PILOT STUDY

Sherrin Jacob, Lavleen Singh, Venkateaswaran K Iyer, Rachna Seth; Department of Pathology, AIIMS, New Delhi, India

Background: Central nervous system (CNS) involvement in pediatric B-acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) significantly impacts relapse risk and survival. Conventional cytomorphology (CM) of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) is rapid and specific but has low sensitivity due to limited cellularity and morphological overlap with reactive lymphocytes. Objective: To evaluate the diagnostic utility and resource efficiency of immunocytochemistry (ICC) as an adjunct to CM in detecting leukemic infiltration of CSF in pediatric B-ALL. Methods: In Cohort 1 (n = 45), all CSF samples underwent CM and ICC using CD3, CD79a, and TdT. In Cohort 2 (n = 44), ICC was applied selectively to samples with pleocytosis (>4 cells/µL) based on a triage algorithm. Results: In Cohort 1, ICC upgraded a subset of “doubtful” CM cases to definitive leukemic infiltration, particularly with CD79a and TdT. CD3 reliably confirmed reactive T-cell morphology, enhancing specificity. In Cohort 2, selective ICC use maintained diagnostic yield, detecting blasts in doubtful cases while conserving slides and resources. Together, ICC demonstrated added diagnostic value, especially in morphologically equivocal and higher-cellularity specimens. Conclusion: ICC complements CM in CSF evaluation for pediatric B-ALL, improving sensitivity and supporting a pragmatic triage strategy. A combination of CD3 and CD79a is optimal when two smears are available; with limited material, marker choice should be guided by morphology. This cost-effective workflow enhances detection of CNS involvement where flow cytometry or molecular assays are not readily available.

10. DIAGNOSTIC UTILITY OF CK19, CD56 AND Ki-67 IMMUNOCYTOCHEMISTRY FOR THE CYTOPATHOLOGICAL DIAGNOSIS IN THE AMBIGUOUS CATEGORIES OF THE BETHESDA SYSTEM FOR REPORTING THYROID CYTOPATHOLOGY

Tarinishree Nedunchezhian, Neelaiah Siddaraju, Bhawana Ashok Badhe, Rashmi P Joshi, Debasis Gochhait; Department of Pathology, Jawaharlal Institute of Postgraduate Medical Education and Research, Pondicherry, India

Background: The utility of immunochemistry in diagnosing the ambiguous categories of the Bethesda System for Reporting Thyroid Cytopathology (TBSRTC) has been extensively studied in the literature using various markers; however, it has yielded conflicting results. Aims: To assess the accuracy of CK19, CD56, and Ki-67 for predicting the cytopathological and histopathological diagnosis among the ambiguous categories of the Bethesda system for reporting thyroid cytopathology. Settings and Design: A diffuse and strong expression of CK19, loss of CD56 expression, and a higher Ki-67 index have been observed in malignant thyroid lesions. Our cross-sectional analytical study was done at a tertiary care center in India. Materials and Methods: Our study included 70 cases of FNA preparations, categorized as III, IV, and V of TBSRTC, with corresponding biopsies (study period: six years). Immunocytochemistry (ICC) and immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining were performed and evaluated, with cut-offs of >30% for CK19 and CD56, and >1% for Ki-67. Due to certain limitations, both ICC and IHC were performed in 55/70 cases using CK19, 51/70 cases using CD56 and 49/70 cases using Ki-67. Statistical Analysis: The association between the three markers and the cytopathological and histopathological profiles was assessed using Fisher’s exact test (P < 0.05). The sensitivity, specificity, PPV, and NPV were assessed. Results and Conclusions: CD56 was the most sensitive of the three markers on cytological (ICC) and histological (IHC) samples, with a high PPV. The 3-marker panel ICC could improve the pre-therapeutic cytodiagnosis, especially in c-PTC cases.

1. EVALUATION OF THE DIAGNOSTIC ACCURACY OF BRONCHIAL BRUSH CYTOLOGY AND BRONCHOALVEOLAR LAVAGE FLUID CYTOLOGY FOR DIAGNOSIS AND SUBTYPING OF LUNG CARCINOMA

Indira Biswas, Indranil Chakrabarti, Sujaya Mazumder, Mahesh Ravikumar Jansari; All India Institute of Medical Sciences, Kalyani, West Bengal, India

Introduction: Lung cancer accounts for 5.9% of all cancer cases and 8.1% of cancer-related deaths in India. Early and accurate diagnosis remains a challenge, with various cytological techniques demonstrating variable sensitivity and specificity. Objectives: This study aimed to evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of bronchial brushings and bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) fluid cytology in detecting and categorizing lung carcinomas, using histopathological examination as the gold standard. Cytological findings were also correlated with clinical, radiological, and bronchoscopic diagnoses. Materials and Methods: A hospital-based cross-sectional study was carried out over 2 years in the Department of Pathology and Laboratory Medicine, AIIMS Kalyani. Cases with available bronchial brushings, BAL fluid samples, and corresponding endobronchial biopsies were included. Cytological evaluation was performed according to the WHO Reporting System for Lung Cytopathology, 5th edition. Histopathological diagnoses were confirmed with immunohistochemistry when required. Data were analyzed using appropriate statistical methods. Results: A total of 45 cases were studied, predominantly in the 60–80 years age group, with a male-to-female ratio of 8:1. The major presenting symptoms were breathlessness, chest pain, and cough. Bronchoscopic findings most frequently revealed mass lesions, followed by irregular mucosa and cavitary lesions. Among 45 BAL fluid samples, 34 were negative for malignancy, 6 were suspicious, 4 were reported as atypia of undetermined significance, and 1 was non-diagnostic. In contrast, bronchial brush cytology yielded 24 positive, 15 negative, and 4 atypical cases. Bronchial brush cytology demonstrated a sensitivity of 66.67% and a positive predictive value (PPV) of 100%. BAL cytology also showed a PPV of 100% but had lower sensitivity. Conclusion: Bronchial brush cytology provides a higher diagnostic yield compared to BAL fluid cytology. Maximum accuracy in diagnosing lung carcinomas can be achieved when bronchial brushings are combined with histopathological biopsy.

4. BRIDGING THE GAP: CYTOLOGY TO THERAPY THROUGH AXILLARY LYMPH NODE FNAC AND HISTOPATHOLOGY CORRELATION IN PRIMARY BREAST CANCER

S Sandhiya, Punam Prasad Bhadani, Tarun Kumar, Amber Parwaiz, Surabhi, Ruchi Sinha, Avinash Singh; Department of Pathology, AIIMS, Patna, Bihar, India

Introduction: Breast cancer (BC) is the most prevalent malignant tumor among women, occurring at approximately twice the rate of other solid tumors. The prognosis of breast cancer is closely linked to the stage at which the disease is detected. Consequently, assessing axillary lymph node involvement is essential in determining the appropriate treatment strategy and predicting patient outcomes. Fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC), often performed under clinical or ultrasound guidance, has become a widely used practice in many breast clinics to evaluate suspicious lymph nodes. Axillary lymph node FNAC can reduce the need for SLNB by confirming the presence of tumor in lymph nodes, thus avoiding the more invasive SLNB procedure and its associated costs and potential complications. Aim: The aim of this study was to assess the safety, feasibility and efficiency of axillary lymph node fine needle-aspiration cytology (FNAC) as a diagnostic tool for predicting metastatic lymph node in patients with primary breast cancer. 2. ENHANCING DIAGNOSTIC ACCURACY IN EFFUSION CYTOLOGY: COMPARATIVE EVALUATION OF THREE CELL BLOCK TECHNIQUES WITH MORPHOLOGICAL AND IMMUNOHISTOCHEMICAL INSIGHTS”

Lata, Shaivy Malik, Sachin Kolte, Navpreet Kaur, Epsita Das, Charanjeet Kaur, Mukul Singh; Department of Pathology, Vardhman Mahavir Medical College and Safdarjung Hospital, New Delhi, India

Introduction: Effusion cytology is widely utilized for diagnosing benign, inflammatory, and malignant processes, yet conventional smears are often hampered by scant cellularity, poor architecture, and limited scope for ancillary testing. Cell block (CB) techniques improve diagnostic utility by preserving tissue architecture and enabling immunohistochemistry (IHC). However, comparative data on different CB methods in effusion cytology remain limited. This study systematically evaluated three CB methods—direct sedimentation, plasma–calcium chloride, and formalin–alcohol—with respect to cellularity, morphology, diagnostic yield, and IHC performance. Materials and Methods: In a prospective study over six months, 98 effusion samples (pleural, peritoneal, pericardial, synovial, bronchoalveolar lavage) were processed into smears and CBs using the three methods. Parameters assessed included cellularity, morphological preservation, detection of malignancy, interobserver agreement, and IHC staining quality (CK7, EMA, WT1, TTF-1, Calretinin, MUC-1). Statistical analysis employed ANOVA, Chi-square, and κ statistics, with P ≤ 0.05 considered significant. Results: A total of 98 effusion specimens were analyzed. Formalin–alcohol cell blocks consistently outperformed other methods, yielding the highest cellularity (46% Grade 3) and malignant detection rate (97%), with well-preserved vesicular chromatin and conspicuous nucleoli. Plasma–calcium chloride demonstrated intermediate performance (85.3% malignant detection), while direct sedimentation showed the lowest diagnostic adequacy (70.5%). Immunohistochemistry revealed strong, reproducible staining with formalin–alcohol, moderate with plasma–calcium chloride, and weak or patchy with sedimentation. Interobserver agreement was excellent for formalin–alcohol (κ=0.88), good for plasma–calcium chloride (κ=0.71), and moderate for sedimentation (κ=0.56). Overall, formalin–alcohol proved most robust and diagnostically reliable. Conclusion: Formalin–alcohol CB significantly outperformed the other methods in cellularity, morphological preservation, malignant detection, IHC quality, and reproducibility, establishing it as the most robust and clinically valuable approach in effusion cytology.
